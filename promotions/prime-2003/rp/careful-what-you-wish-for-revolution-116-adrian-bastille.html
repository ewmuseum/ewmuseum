Careful What You Wish For (ReVolution 116 - Adrian Bastille)

Date: Timeless It was an aura he had. Emanating like heat from an inferno it surrounded him like a force field, a lordly and dangerous air that precluded annoyance. Fans and crew backstage would grin at the sight of his saunter, but from a wise distance, knowing his character was more exaggeration than invention, suspecting terrible repercussions for idle chitchat or a begged autograph. Out in the arena, on their stage, they would cheer or revile him, sometimes both within the span of ten seconds, entertained, repulsed, or knowing they were about to be. Anywhere else fear dominated every feeling, petrifying all emotion save a wary awe. It resulted in a reverse Doppler effect; the closer he got, the less sound there was. He was The Devil’s Don. Some claimed that named him Satan’s right hand man, others that his malevolence and cunning were unmatched even on Hell’s throne, others still that he’d made the devil his bitch. Where the dispute died was at how well it fit, that when he joined the mob of the underworld he would be their king, that he lived merely to practice for that inevitability. Oil envied his slickness. He was the Cadillac of Wrestling. Classic and stylish, held in high regard even by the newest and fanciest models. So skilled in the squared circle that pundits tossed around words like juggernaut and unconquerable, even in the wake of his rare defeats. Most of the best lost more in one year than he had in his career. The history books belied the theory, but it was said if you could beat him you were made, that you would usurp his position and power, crowned in the annals of wrestling lore. He was The Deville himself. Those who defeated him needed to be very careful, because the only time he defined magnanimous was when he read the dictionary. Above all else, he was on a level by himself, an island-cloud, and while allegiances were sometimes made, the only counsel he took was his own. Necessity made it so. Paranoia made it necessity. A lifetime of betrayal rationalized the paranoia. You become what you’re exposed to, no matter how valiantly you resist. The only way to fight it is to avoid it, as Nietzsche once more eloquently observed. ~><>…<><~ How it happened he didn’t know. Trying to summon the memory of the mistake he made, the misstep he took, his mind mocked him, denying even that minimal comfort. He thought he’d been prepared, that he’d taken every precaution, that he’d aligned himself with destiny, and through it, victory. But as Gamble’s arms coiled around his neck, tightening, doom skittered in the pit of his stomach. His own left arm was trapped, and as ambidextrous as he practiced to be, he really wished Gamble had taken his right. It happened beyond his vision, but he didn’t need to see it – he could feel The Grin’s fingers locking together. In desperation Deville launched back into the ropes, knowing it was a fool move long before and immediately after he did it, and cringing internally as he felt the grip tighten, as he felt the legs constrict around his torso. Thoughts swirled through his mind faster than he could decipher them, beleaguered by adrenaline and disbelief, by the excruciating night he’d already had and the failure now dawning. Flip him! Flip over the ropes! He turned toward the outside to do just that, but as he tried to bend forward Gamble reefed back, drawing him stumbling toward the centre of the ring. Crush him! There was little hope of jarring him enough to weasel free, but that was all he had at this point. Kicking out his legs he landed on Gamble with all the force he could muster, wrenching at his wrist as they hit the canvas, twisting his head and tucking his chin to ease the escape. No! "Come on, Deville." The derisive whisper slithered into his ear like a red hot poker, twisting in his brain. "Tap out. Don’t deny the inevitable." His whole life had been in defiance of the inevitable. He responded the only way he could, making his voice much calmer than he felt. "Fuck you." "Come on, Deville," the taunting repeated, already sounding more like the taunted than either would dare believe. "Tap out. There’s no shame in it." For some there wasn’t, and he’d said the same thing to countless prone prey. He drove an elbow into Gamble’s ribs, trying to jerk free once more, punctuating it with a sharper "Fuck. You." This wasn’t happening. It couldn’t be. "If you make me put you to sleep . . ." He sounded bored! As if he couldn’t believe Deville would force him to go through the motions of victory, as if it was already over save the ceremony of raising his hand. "FUCK! YOU!" Gamble actually sighed. "Have it your way . . ." Impossibly, the grip tightened, squeezing like a cold hand around his heart. "FUCK YOU!" he managed to cry out, rasping. The raucous sound of the crowd was now coming in waves, alternating with the throbbing in his temples. Calm the fuck down! He’d let himself be baited, and the hook was deep, but there was still the matter of fighting. He’d escaped worse than this before. Relax . . . Forcing the precariousness of the situation out of his mind, The Deville went limp, closing his eyes as he began to time his breathing, slowing it. He was already light-headed, but he knew if he could slow his pulse, if he could induce a trance-like state, he could frustrate his wily would-be apprentice. Maybe desperation would seize him, maybe— Relax. He did. He could feel his tempo receding, could feel the throbbing diminishing. Let him think you’re slipping into unconsciousness. Counting his slow breaths, meditating, he pushed everything else into sensing respite, into feeling for any slackening or weakness in The Grin’s grip. There was none, but it was early yet. A clammy hand took his own, feeling cold and numb to Deville because of his own state, not Bernie’s. He let that float straight out of his mind, refusing to let it compute. The shout was deeper than it should have been, exaggerated and distant, like Deville was underwater. "ONE!" Slower. Calm. Breathe in, two, three, four, five, six . . . Breathe out, two, three, four, five, six . . . Again Bernie let his hand drop, and again he heard the faraway shout. "TWO!" Gamble was shaking and had been for a while, but it began to reverberate with something new. Relief. He thought it was over. His grip tightened for the last, aching to release and be done with it. When Roberts lifted his hand the third time, Deville rocketed to life, planting his feet and driving backward, hoping to catch Gamble off-guard and score the pin. But Bernie, apparently channelling Deville’s counting pace, only made it to two before Gamble reasserted control. Worse, he locked the hold in tighter. Calm. Let him think that was the last you had. That was the last I had! Grimacing, unable to feel his extremities, he surged once more, forcing Gamble’s shoulders to the canvas. In his mind he swore he heard the hand hit the mat the third time, swore he felt it reverberate through his body, but there was no bell, and he could feel Gamble wriggling to a better position. Closing his eyes to block out the tunnel vision, Deville cut off cursing the zebra and focused again on serenity. Like a thief, the idea of tapping slipped into his mind. Other people have done it. It didn’t kill them. We’ll get him anoth—NO! He couldn’t give up. He’d abandoned principles, friendships, every virtuous thing he ever knew to make himself untouchable, to elevate beyond submission. He’d razed and salted everything but pride. It was all he had left. Rapidly it slipped away, only to return a moment later with dulled yet frantic panic. The realization that his arm was in the air, that he didn’t feel it being lifted, scared Deville like nothing else. He couldn’t really feel his arm, but he could feel gravity’s pull, could feel the lull of sleep, could feel Succubus enticing him to her bed. Let it fall. Never. He thought he flexed his arm – he tried – but he wasn’t sure. It was a struggle to remember where he was, what he was doing. All he wanted was to roll over and close his eyes. Why was that too much to ask? Re . . . lax . . . And he did. Sinking into the limbo between worlds, he dreamt of blissful death. Of release, freedom from his haunting sickness. He heard cutting laughter. He heard his own unsung sobs. What he didn’t hear was the bell, or Howard proclaiming Gamble victorious, marking The Deville’s first loss in PRIME singles competition. Instead, he dreamed of himself as a genie, obligated to a lamp clutched in the hands of a man who wouldn’t stop grinning at him, a man growing foggier by the second, absorbing like a sponge, reflecting like a mirror. He knew what The Grin wanted, and now he had no option but to oblige him, no method of convincing him of the sacrifices it entailed. Gamble would learn the second lesson. More, he would get his wish, what no man knowing should want. ~><>…<><~ He had no idea what he was doing. . . . Alright, that was a fib. Even when The Deville wandered aimlessly there was a blueprint for the journey buried somewhere in his brain. And he knew that fact better than anyone else. In this instance he simply chose to deny it, to mask his motivation from his ever-prying mind. "A little girl’s smile," he whispered, eyes darting from the black leather of the steering wheel to find their oceanic reflection in the rear-view, narrowing as if they might find a lie. "Why can’t I make a little girl smile?" With a scowl he tore away from his own face, kicking the door open and straightening smoothly, oblivious to the crisp winter air. The package – a shiny silver deal decorated with reindeer and busy elves, complete with red ribbon and billowing bow – made him duck back into his car, but a moment later he was striding in his sure, purposeful manner up the walk. Before him loomed a stately apartment building, swirling black and white as if he’d designed it himself. It bore brick instead of his favoured marble, but it was impressive just the same. The stark lack of Christmas decorations inspired a longing for his childhood, when people would celebrate openly and happily, without do-gooder organizations trying to demonize the occasion and paint Santa Claus as a villain. He knew it wasn’t a matter of them having been taken down after the holiday – it was only New Year’s Eve – they’d never been put up in the first place. He could hear his booming knock echo within, and as he waited, a different uneasiness washed over him. It was anticipation, not dread, but in a man supposedly long since culled of emotion neither was welcome. Only a superhuman effort kept his face from heating, crushing the sensation with an inhuman spirit. For a moment, standing there clutching that present, he’d felt like a fool, and despite his denials, he knew above all else what made men fools. The door opening snapped him back to attention. He glared, freezing the doorman’s smile halfway onto his face, pushing past him into the lobby before he could blink or sputter or even offer some generic greeting. There was no doubt the man recognized him, but even if he hadn’t, job or no job, Deville doubted he would have called him back and forced him to follow protocol. He was used to that. The sooner someone saw his back the better, even if at first they didn’t realize it. As a general rule from a life supposedly left behind him, The Deville avoided elevators. They were too easy to rig, ideal for an ambush. Stepping in, cringing immediately at the tuneless music, he told himself it was because he simply wasn’t in the mood to climb thirty flights of stairs. In some small way that was true. When the doors spread with that grating bing! he merely stood there, staring vacantly, an argument raging in his mind, half of him needing to stay, the other adamantly demanding he go. She deserves more -- went the stronger argument -- than to become the next Karina. Deville bit his lip, raising a shaky hand to the panel, finger hovering hesitantly over the G button. You know how it will end. It’s how it always ends. Her bitter and full of hatred, wanting to see you only for the chance to kick your head off. He knew it all too well. Eventually she would come to hate him even without his scheming. As if he could go without. There was no sense in delaying the inevitable, but this time . . . this time he vowed he would, no matter how hopeless the journey. He didn’t want to push her away, so the less she saw of him the better. He stabbed the button, making the elevator chime at him as the doors began sliding shut. That’s when her voice hit him, confused: "Angelo?" His breath caught. Forcing his arm through the doors, he restrained himself from silent cursing. Sardonic laughter mocked him in his head. Inevitable. Stepping through the once-more-parting doors, he schooled his face to a winning smile. It was a façade only until his eyes found her, then it spread genuinely in spite of the persistent ache in his chest. Eyebrows drawn together, Eva eyed him quizzically, head tilted. "Benny called and said you were on your way up." Fucking Benny. Her lips twitched. He thought it was with amusement. "Said you seemed to be in a foul mood." An easy shrug no-sold that, morphing into a gesture toward the elevator. "Tell him to fix that damn elevator. Thing nearly took off my arm." She snorted, then noticeably brightened as she scanned him up and down, eyes locking on the present and going wide. "Another present?" Quickly taking a scolding cast, her face again found his. "You’re going to spoil her!" The laughter came unbidden. "Nonsense." He grinned, eased merely by her presence. "Nothing of yours could ever spoil." "Uh-huh." Rolling her eyes, she chuckled and turned, beckoning him to follow her into the penthouse. "Well come on in. She’s sulking in her room, but I’m sure she’ll be happy to see you, especially bearing gifts. Drink?" "No." He clamped his mouth shut, not having meant to answer so roughly, cringing as her back stiffened. Clearing his throat, he amended with "Sorry. I’m just a little on edge today. I’ll have to pass on the drink, though. I can’t stay long . . . I’m in town on business. Just wanted to pop in to drop this off." She didn’t look back, but her hair bobbed as she nodded, considering. "Business? Today?" He could hear the doubt in her voice. She knew he was lying, she was simply too polite to call him on it directly. Deciding it was best not to answer, he followed her in, internally complimenting himself on already paving the road to loathing with lies. Relax. The barrage of yuletide decorations soothed him, and the white tree was beautiful enough he didn’t even get mad about it being made of plastic. Somehow the scent of pine still permeated the air. "Theresa!" Eva called, her voice ringing like dulcet chimes. "You have a visitor!" Stomping footfalls sounded, eventually ushering in a miniature version of Eva. She spared a glare for her mother, then softened upon spying Angelo, or more accurately, upon finding the package tucked under his arm. Crimson flushed her cheeks as they lifted in a smile. "Is that for me?" The Deville nodded. "Santa must have left it at my house by mistake. I on--" "Oh come off it," Eva interrupted sharply, though without malice, addressing her daughter. "You know perfectly well it’s for you." Theresa affected not to have heard, walking over to Deville. "Santa, huh? Looks like Santa’s been working out." Deville grinned back. "He’s gotta keep fit for Mrs. Claus. She was growing tired of hugging that bowl." Again with the eye-rolling from Eva. "Alright Deville. Stop flirting with my twelve-year-old daughter." Before Deville could even gape, Theresa whirled on Eva with a mischievous eyebrow raised. "Jealous?" "Oh please." Wholly unfamiliar with mother-daughter relationships, Deville shifted uncomfortably, wondering how much of the animosity in the air was serious. "Unfortunately," he interjected, clearing his throat and holding out the gift, "I really have to run." Theresa accepted the present with an "Awwww!" and bounded to the loveseat, plunking herself down and neatly slicing the tape with a fingernail. "Enjoy," Deville said, backing toward the door. "I will! Thanks!" Eva snapped her mouth closed, apparently having been on the verge of asking Theresa what she was supposed to say. She turned it into an innocent lick of her lips and a smile for Deville. "I’ll see you out." Deville waited, then walked with her side-by-side. "I’m sorry about running out like this," he opened honestly, before reverting to the lie, "I really do have business to attend to." He vowed silently to himself that he would make business in the city, reasoning in his own delightful way that that would erase the deceit. "It’s okay," Eva soothed, patting his arm. "It was sweet of you to come by." His eyes darted sideways. Because of the present? Or coming to --Don’t! Again he cleared his throat. "I’ll give you a call in a couple of days . . ." She turned to him as they reached the door, brow piquing. ". . . we need to go over the plans for the next ReV. Maybe I’ll fly b--" "WAIT!" It was Theresa. Both Eva and Angelo whipped around, alarmed. "What’s wrong?" they demanded in unison. Apparently nothing. She was grinning devilishly. "Look up." When they did The Deville’s stomach clenched, excited and panicked butterflies warring to eat him alive. Mistletoe. Ever so slowly he lowered his eyes to Eva, who was staring deep into him, wide-eyed, mouth opened just a pinch. "You have to kiss!" Theresa shouted gleefully. Don’t fucking do it! He grinned in spite of himself. "It is a sacred rule." Eva blinked, then hummed and nodded. "It is." The voice rebelled in Deville’s head, warning that this was the end, slicing him for taking the road to ruin so soon, but thoughts of Eva, her scent, her pretty face filling his vision, choked out that nonsense. He snaked an arm around her lower back, pulling her snug into him, and descended, waiting until she closed her eyes before doing the same. When they finally pulled apart, panting slightly, Theresa was giggling behind both hands. "Wow," Deville uttered, breathless, that grin sliding easily back onto his face. "Maybe . . . I think I’ll have to find my way back here for midnight." The smile stole over to Eva’s, but she said nothing as he wrenched the door open and swaggered down the hall. Unable to help but wonder what she was getting into, Eva watched him for a moment before closing the door, then turned to find Theresa grinning at her. Her eyes clenched as Theresa broke into giggles again, pointing at her mother’s red face. "You liked it!" ~><>…<><~ He was ten kinds of fool. The worst, painted variety. He knew it well as his key slid into his front door, but his inner voice hammered it home relentlessly, just in case he might try to forget for a moment. It was stupid of him to have gone with the present, and that was an eureka moment next to kissing her with passion. He ended up fleeing New York well before midnight, succumbing to the niggling doubt and self-revulsion, completely oblivious to the idea that in trying to prevent the inevitable he was hastening its arrival. Well, not oblivious to it, just helpless to stop himself. He could still fly back with hours to spare, but at the same time he knew he couldn’t. Eva might be hurt from him standing her up, but it wasn’t like he’d guaranteed anything, and besides, that pain would be a pinch next to the cardiomyopathy she would suffer if he allowed his tainted touch to infect her heart. Knowing him – interacting with him – opening up to him – raped people of their innocence. There was a long list of victims to provide the proof. He never affected anyone for the better. Never. Not Jason O’Neil. Not Karina Wolfenden. Certainly not Chris Vega. It was always for the worse, and with time they realized it. With time they came to hate him as much as he hated himself. He wanted to spare Eva from joining that list. He needed to. Needed it as strongly as he knew he couldn’t avoid it. Making his way to his extravagant kitchen, he moved numbly to one of the dining tables and sat, staring at nothing. The other five chairs at the table were more ornamental than anything else. He couldn’t remember the last time someone besides himself had sat in one. He had no friends. Those he hadn’t murdered long ago were eventually chased away, either by his own design or out of fear or hatred, often a combination of those things and worse. Rollins saw him as an obstacle overcome. Snow couldn’t care one way or the other. Nova hated him without knowing it, burying it beneath the good times they’d shared. Eva . . . With Eva he wanted more, and that damned them to so much less. He was damned to make sure of it. All of his acquaintances collected a paycheque from him, or on account of him. Nobody trusted him, and rightly so. He didn’t trust himself. The world he’d abused would breathe a quiet sigh of relief when he left it. His legacy was that of a tyrannical parasite, blessed with the wit and skills to make him the envy of any man, cursed with an empty soul unable to strive in the right direction. He was Angelo Deville. Little to live for, nothing to lose, loved by no one. Alone. It was an aura he had. Destroying from the inside like a cancer, it surrounded him like a cell. It was a hard lesson to learn. It was absolute hell to teach.
