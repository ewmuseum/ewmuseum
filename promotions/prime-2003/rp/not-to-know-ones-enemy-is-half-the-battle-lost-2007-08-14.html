Not to Know One’s Enemy is Half the Battle Lost

14 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 14/08/2007 Chapter I: Bad Idea… It wasn’t just a bad idea, it was a fucking stupid idea. Jonathan Winters knew that now, but the revelation had come far too late. He should be kicking himself, he should be kicking his illustrious employer, Cornelius Stakiri, for putting him up to it, but it was too late for that too. Now, all that he wanted was to survive. He just wanted to see Aeryn’s beautiful face again. He just wanted to witness her smile light up the room and everyone in it all over again. Winters gritted his teeth and, blocking out the pain the best he could, crawled to what he perceived to be the nearest exit. Chapter II: Battle Cry… “The enemy have made their first mistake,” Stakiri announced, shooting his broad grin all around the conference room, “they’ve shown their hand.” His illustrious employer was being his ever dramatic self, but it was also the happiest Winters had seen him in a long time. Stakiri wouldn’t admit it to anyone, but Winters could see the too this little territory war with this relatively new enemy was having on him and to him, this seemed like the light at the end of the tunnel. But Winters wasn’t entirely convinced. A week back, Winters was purposefully goaded into action in an elaborate operation orchestrated by the enemy with the intention of turning against his employer, but they’d misjudged him. He’d been through too much now to even consider the notion. A white-haired albino motherfucker had confronted him at a freshly demolished bar, with bodies strewn in a gruesome and bloody pile in the far corner of the drinking hole and Winters got the sickly feeling at the pit of his stomach that the crazy fuckwit did it all by himself and needless to say it made Winters take Stakiri’s supposedly ‘good news’ with a pinch of salt. “Now we have to utilise their mistake to our advantage,” Stakiri continued. Winters glanced around the room and noted how the rest of his inner circle were eating it all up with a spoon. He knew then this wasn’t going to end well. “We have to draw them out of hiding again and take this to streets. They’ll never know what hit ‘em!” Cheers surrounded the entire room, but he couldn‘t bring himself to join in. Stakiri had rallied his troops well, but Winters had to entertain the notion that they were all being played again, just like last time. Just like they did with Aeryn. No. This definitely wasn’t going to end well. Chapter III: Misgivings… “What the hell was that?” Stakiri yelled. The rest of the inner circle had been gone for the better part of half an hour, but Stakiri had deemed it necessary for Winters to stay behind and now he realised why. Winters shrugged, pretending to play dumb. “What?” “You know this is the first break we’ve had in the better part of six months,” Stakiri told him. Winters hated being told what he supposedly knew. “You could at least show it.” Winters shook his head. “I don’t know. I don’t like it.” Stakiri raised both eyebrows speculatively. “What’s to fucking like about any of this?” “You didn’t see him, Stakiri,” Winters replied. “I just don’t think it’s the break you envision it to be.” Stakiri rubbed the bridge of his nose and chuckled to himself. “Are you scared of this guy, Jonathan?” “No,” Winters lied. “And stop changing the subject. They wouldn‘t reveal this guy unless he was seriously protected or had the ability to disappear at the drop of a hat and we both know it. We‘re being played here, Stakiri.” “You underestimate yourself, Jonathan,” Stakiri responded. “They though you were worth the risk, they simply misjudged you and that was their real mistake in all of this and you need to realise that.” Winters brow furrowed, he didn’t like where this was headed one bit. “Just what exactly are you getting at, Stakiri?” Stakiri slumped down in his chair and produced a toothy grin. “We got lucky.” “Lucky?” “We’re lucky that this motherfucker looks so strange,” Stakiri added. “We found him.” “You found him?” Winters asked, shaking his head in disbelief. “Really?” “With all the resources at my disposal,” Stakiri began, extracting a cigar from the inside of his jacket pocket, “how could I not?” Winters shook his head. “It can’t be this easy.” Stakiri lit his cigar and took a long drag of it, instantly stinking up the room. “Sometimes, Jonathan, you just have to have a little faith.” Winters knew he wasn’t going to like the answer, but he asked the question anyhow. “So, I take it you have a plan.” Stakiri’s grinned widened and he blew a smoke ring at Winters face. “Of course I do, my boy.” Chapter IV: Into the Breach Dear Friend… The partially demolished parking lot, had rubble scattered all around and as Winters entered deeper into the lion’s den, he made sure to make note of some of the more useful pieces - perhaps potential weapons in the making. “You’re a brave man, Mr. Winters,” the familiar voice boomed, echoing throughout the parking lot ominously. “Certainly a braver man than I.” Winters spun around on the spot, desperately trying to pinpoint the source of the voice. In the end, he noted the silhouette of the perpetrator up ahead and drew closer toward it and realising his cover was blow, the albino man with the dreadlocks appeared from a crack in the wall. “You know why I’m here,” Winters told him, “so let’s talk.” “Ah,” the albino began, “but do I? At our previous encounter you seemed pretty adamant that you were not the least bit interested in my little proposal.” “That was then,” Winters told him, “this is now.” The albino nodded. “Yes. You did seem a little…upset at the time, but I came to believe that I gravely misjudged you, Mr. Winters. You’re not the cold hearted bastard you report to be.” Winters prepared himself the best he could for a fight. He knew virtually nothing about his opponent and he hated it. “I wouldn’t bank on it, snow white.” “Ah,” the albino nodded knowingly. “Trying to goad me into a confrontation, eh? A commendable tactic. Is that the true purpose of this meeting on your part?” “Are you so sure you’ve not misjudged me again?” Winters asked. “Or are you trying to talk yourself out of something you know you’d lose?” Goading the man wasn’t a particularly great idea on Winters part, but it served to get the man angry, it would at least give him a chance. Or so he hoped. He hated going into confrontations blindly and he had to admit, he grouped the upcoming PRIME Rev one-three-six match-up right in along with it. The albino man chuckled. “I’m afraid you think of me as some sort of honourable foe, Mr. Winters.” He clicked his fingers and four thugs appeared from the shadows. They surrounded Winters and he knew just by looking at them, each knew how to fight. “But that’s far from the truth.” “Fucking great,” Winters sighed, immediately springing into attack. The first one fell easy enough. A thrust to the throat that would keep the moron grounded for the foreseeable future. Surprisingly, Winters also managed to catch the second off-guard, almost taking his head off with a roundhouse kick. The third at least managed to pull a punch, but Winters parried it away with his forearm and brought his boot down hard into the thugs chest. The fourth one managed to pick his punch well, but sacrificed power for contact and ultimately the blow had little effect. Winters countered with a knee to the gut and an elbow to the back of the neck. Winters didn’t even have a chance to catch a breath, when the albino man caught him with a punch unawares. Winters felt himself lift off the ground and actually fly through the air, before landing on the hard concrete flooring. He had never hit that hard in his entire life. Winters struggled to remain conscious as the albino man chuckled to himself and slowly began his approach. “Now, did seem fair to you, Mr. Winters? Did that break your stiff code of conduct?” Winters managed to get to get on his hands and knees and, gritting his teeth crawled away, towards what he perceived to be the nearest exit, fighting the comforting embrace that unconsciousness brought to the table. “What’s this?” The albino man questioned. “The great Jonathan Winters running away from a fight?” Or so he made it appear. Winters made it to the edge of the rubble and then slowed right down. “What’s wrong, Mr. Winters?” The albino taunted. “Had enough?” Quickly, Winters reached into the rubble and extracted a thin copper pipe that had been savagely mutilated at the end and then lunged the sharpened thing directly into the albino’s thigh, sending it right through to the other side. The screams almost deafened him and, wiping away the blood from his mouth, he managed to smile as the albino man extracting the pipe from his own leg and ambled towards what HE perceived to be the exit. “What’s this?” Winters muttered, barely audible now. “The great albino fruit loop running away scared?” He chuckled to himself and propped himself up against the bricks and the mortar. Maybe this wasn’t such a bad idea after all. End
