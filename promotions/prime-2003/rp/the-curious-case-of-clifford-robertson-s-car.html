The Curious Case of Clifford Robertson's Car

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: ReVolution 167 "Two by two...hands of blue." - River Tam, Firefly, "The Train Job." * * * * * If you asked a magician to tell you their secrets, chances are, you wouldn't get a straight answer. Magic is all about giving the audience an illusion of control, the belief that, if they just look a little harder, the trick will sort itself out. Magic is the art of the left hand not knowing what the right hand is doing -- and meaning it to be that way. Magic is organized misdirection. But, then, so is a lot of life. And so is this story. * * * * * One hour ago Two women shared whispered threats in an ill-lit room. "Not making this easy for me." "Who said I was trying?" "You should give it a go. Am the one with the knife an' all, y'know." "About that. You've never really frightened me, you know. You're just a schoolyard bully given a sense of legitimacy by -- " The wet smack of flesh on flesh echoed oddly in the chamber. "Be respectful for once in your misbegotten existence." "Because you'll go running to Mother if I don't?" "Because I'll leave your pretty, pretty body in this room, strapped to that chair until ya wither away, leaving nothing but sallow skin, brittle bones, and silicon implants." * * * * * Ten Days Ago Memphis, Tennessee. They start Independence Day festivities early in this part of the country, Clifford Robertson noted with a wry smirk as he sped down the road. His Dodge Charger (it was orange with the Stars and Bars painted on the roof, because Dale really wanted to be Bo Duke -- Luke Duke could lick his nuts) purred with the satisfaction of moving so fast, and the bursts of crackling noise from firecrackers seemed almost like a fanfare announcing his arrival. Anyone who met Clifford was left with the undeniable impression that the unkempt man catered to stereotypes most of the South would like to forget existed. He was a beer-swilling, shit-talking, intolerant jackass; the kind of guy who'd buy a six-pack for "the missus" as an anniversary gift and never understand why she was upset. That Friday morning, Cliff was on his way to his storefront, where he'd sit in the back room and watch a tiny, crappy television set and hope -- just hope -- no goddamn whiny fucks would call him to bitch about their sink leaking, because he was not in the mood for that shit. On the list of shit he was in the mood for: a burger, some fries, maybe a Percoset. He was pretty sure the missus had a few left from having that filling put in. Murky brown eyes shielded from the sunrise by mirrored shades scanned the road ahead; Cliff was a good driver (two DUIs aside, he'd never gotten in a wreck, and he took extra care in the Charger). "Always scan three car lengths ahead," his daddy told him when he was sixteen. It was the only advice he ever gave his son, but it was sensible, nonetheless. So it was that he saw the blonde in the over-sized sunglasses standing along the side of the road, glittering pink thumb out. Clifford wasn't usually one for picking up hitchhikers; too many were dirty hippies who smelled of patchouli. But there was something about this girl that intrigued him. Actually, there were two things that intrigued Cliff, but they were barely visible under the Cubs windbreaker, wrapped lovingly in a red, white, and blue bikini top. The girl drifted to a halt as the Charger approached, lifting the honey-colored sunglasses from her eyes. Clifford leaned across the passenger seat, unrolling the window. "Hi," Cliff said, hoping he sounded friendly enough. "Where ya headed?" The girl turned on a positively cheeky grin as she leaned in the open window. "Anywhere but here," she said. "I'm, like, completely over Tennessee." "I'm headed as far as Little Rock, if you're interested in going to Arkansas." "Perf!" she squealed, clapping her hands. She pulled on the door; it didn't budge. "Uhm, your door is stuck." "It's welded shut," Cliff explained. "Just climb in through the window -- you'll be fine." "Are you sure? My shorts are kind of, like, shorts." "Really? Hadn't noticed." Is it even necessary to point out that Clifford Robertson had, in fact, noticed? "You'll be fine. C'mon, it's supposed to be a hot one." Cliff made a show of not paying attention as the long-legged blonde seemed to struggle with the logistics of entering a car through the window. There was a lot of leg, and, boy, she wasn't lying -- those shorts were something else. "Whew!" the woman chirped when she was finally inside. "That was a heckuva workout!" "Yeah, looked like," he said, easing away from the shoulder. "This might seem kinda skeevy, but you are awful limber. What is it you do fer a livin'?" Cozen grinned so widely, it appeared her head was in danger of flopping open like a broken Pez dispenser. "Oh, this and that," she said, crossing her legs at the ankle. She stretched out in the cramped muscle car as best as she could, pointing her toes. "I always wanted to become a ballerina, though." She turned a sunny smile that wasn't her own onto the man. "What about you? Other than having kind of the best car ever, what do you do?" If there was just one thing in the world that Clifford Robertson liked...it was boobs. If there were two things, they'd be boobs and beer. But if there were three things the plumber enjoyed, they were boobs, beer, and talking about himself. So there was no lack of conversation for the duration of their trip. Which never made it all the way to Little Rock. * * * * * Eight Days Ago New Orleans still sees an impressive amount of tourist traffic, even after Hurricane Katrina ravaged the city. Certain areas (the French Quarter, for example) had the feel of a city struggling to rise out of the mire of its misery, a malaise that gave the place the kind of maudlin beauty that attracted a certain kind of visitor. Plus, the Big Easy had some of the best restaurants in all of the southeastern United States -- such as the café one would find two women laughing softly last Monday afternoon. The terrace dining room was picturesque, all wrought iron tables and antique chairs. It was the perfect place to enjoy a balmy summer evening. "Do you really think this will work?" asked the woman PRIMEates called "Siena van der Wal." "How do you know I won't just turn you in to the Mother Superior?" The blonde across the table from the Auburn-Haired Harpy delicately dipped a shrimp that belied the term into a white China bowl. "The very fact that you call her 'Mother Superior' is a decent indication of where you stand, I'd say." Dr. Sarah Riley smiled with a good deal more warmth than she probably felt. "You and I aren't that different, Siena. We both enjoy playing her games, but you...you're kind of sick of being the marionette on her strings, aren't you?" Sarah Riley had quite a soothing, mild voice -- one that entirely lacked the kind of malicious weight the woman the two were speaking so mildly of. But there was no missing the implications of her words. Siena's teacup paused, scant centimeters from her plum-colored lips. "You have no idea what you're talking about." "Probably not." Sarah pulled together another affable grin. "Whatever you did, I know you're the only one of her students she doesn't like." "I really doubt she likes anyone whose name isn't Kathryn-with-a-Y." "That may be the case, but she's kind of harsh with you and Dandy Andy." "Oh, please -- not you with the nickname, too." Sarah's laugh matched her tone -- light and airy, as if the two weren't talking around plotting against the most dangerous woman -- perhaps the most dangerous person -- either had met. "Oh, come on -- it's adorable." "So I'm in the doghouse?" "You're lucky you're not in the mausoleum." There's no symbolism in Sarah's eating, as there might be with the Spider -- she's not biting their heads off or anything noteworthy. Really, this could be something from Sex and the City, if they weren't talking about talking about treason. "Either this means," Dr. Riley continued. "That you're powerfully desperate to crawl back to her -- in which case, I was pretty much screwed the moment I picked up the phone." "Or?" Siena's answer was the very definition of noncommittal. "Or you're looking for a way to kick her in the chops and remind her why she picked you in the first place." Dr. Riley meticulously wiped prawn guts from her fingers, never once meeting Siena's eyes. The other woman swirled the dregs of her tea in its cup, focusing her sea-green gaze down at the bottom of the teacup. There is an old saying: The first one who speaks, loses. Siena lost, this time. "And you need Cozen for this?" "Well..." Sarah hedged her bets. "I doubt the right word is 'need'. The girl is gifted." Siena nodded, once, sitting up straighter in her seat. "What do you need me to do?" "For now?" Sarah pulled her clutch bag onto her lap, fishing out an egg-white business card. "Keep in touch. There's no such thing as being too careful, so stay alert." Dr. Riley slipped to her feet, adjusting her wide-framed eyeglasses. "I really must be going, sweetie. It's been a blast." "I know!" she squealed, fully back into the brainless archetype she overemphasized on television. "The food here was to die for." "Let me at least cover my half?" Sarah turned her lips down in a perfect pout. "Don't be mad, girl! You found the place, the least I can do is pay." The exchange was for show; neither woman knew for certain they weren't being watched. Dr. Riley nodded briefly, almost raising a hand to brush the hair behind her ears, before letting her hand fall. "You have my cell number. Stay in touch! Mwah!" Siena topped off her teacup, careful to not watch Sarah's departure. * * * * * Sarah Riley's expression quirked as she passed a gaggle of waitresses, including the too-bubbly brunette who'd served them. The young girl flicked dark eyes away from her compatriots' story, turning over her shoulder to glance back at the table. Siena van der Wal stared off into space, almost blissfully. The young girl tossed a questioning look back to the yellow-haired doctor. But the brief glance between the tall girl and the petite doctor was the only one they shared. The Good Doctor slid open the gate and stepped onto the sidewalk. "Sorry, guys, I have to check in on Table Six." The waitress picked up her tray, bouncing away from the trio of servers. A passing of words between the three others; they all use different words, but it's the same meaning. "Does Mila seem weird you?" * * * * * Four Days Ago Outside Baton Rouge. "People do some pretty fucked up things," the old man said to the young one. Clifford Robertson's orange Dodge Charger was abandoned nose-first in a ditch when the green Jeep eased to a halt. Two men (a fresh-faced kid and his grizzled-to-the-point-this-seems-like-an-action-movie partner) pulled out heavy-handled flashlights to investigate. "Damn shame," the elder man, whose name was Neil Higgins, muttered. "Fine automobile." The car appeared to be completely empty, keys left in the ignition. Neither Neil nor his son found a bag or even a discarded fast food cup to indicate the car had even been driven. "This is almost too clean" young Henry Higgins said, shining his light in through the passenger window. "Like someone ran it through a car wash and then crashed it in a ditch." "People do some pretty fucked-up things," Neil said, repeating it like a mantra. The crunch of tires on the dirt road drew the aged officer's attention. He raised a hand to squint past the headlights of the oncoming vehicle. "The hell?" he muttered. "Kid, are those fuckin' feds? Already?" Two people in suits clambered out of the vehicle, faces locked into severe, curling frowns. The man drove; he was built like an LSU linebacker, chestnut hair yanked back a ridiculously tight ponytail, which had the effect of pulling the skin of his face so taut his expression looked like it pained him. The woman who joined him was hardly half his size, looking somehow less comfortable in her suit than he did in his. With her spray of blond hair and sharp, almost elfin features, the woman didn't scream, "government spook!" like her partner -- an impression that wasn't lessened when she opened her mouth. "Evenin', chaps," the blonde said pleasantly, a slight Irish accent staining her words. "Name's Guinevere Maddox, this is my partner, Ian Ogden." She flashed some kind of badge; Neil couldn't make it out in the light. "Neil Higgins," the elderly man said. "This here's m'boy, Henry. We were drivin' home, found this car face-up in the ditch. Weren't here this morning when we drove into town." Ogden circled around to the far side of the car, where Henry was trying to jimmy open the trunk. The big man didn't say a word. "Tell me, Mister Higgins." Maddox smiled wide as she approached. It was the kind of open expression that lent itself to reciprocating. "Tell me ye called this into yer local authorities." "Can't get no cellphone reception out here." "I've noticed, aye." In contrast to the big man, it seemed the small woman couldn't shut up. "You were..." Her laugh was as sudden as it was bright. "You were thinkin' o' keepin' the bloody thing, weren't you? You knave!" "Someone abandons a car like this on the side of the road." Henry poked his head up from the other side of the car, a little bit of a grin on his face. "An' they leave the keys? Ma'am, it's like they wanted it took." "Now, what if I told ye this was part'a the investigation Agent Ogden an' I're conductin'?" Her smile was relentless. "I'd say that's fine, Miss Maddox." Neil was only halfway listening to the woman. "Just so long as my boy and I get it before it goes to some kinda auction." "Wow." Guinevere glanced past the older man to her partner. "That's pretty reasonable of you. Isn't it reasonable, Ian?" The other agent nodded, but his smile just served to counter his frown, leaving his lips a thin line. "We try to be courteous around these parts, Miss Madd--uhlk!" Neil Higgins hadn't heard the song of Guen Maddox's knife opening; his son hadn't seen a glint int the moonlight. Neil felt the blade, however -- a sharp pain in his left side. "Dad?" Henry peered up over the roof of the car as the elfin woman cupped a hand over his father's mouth. He hadn't even had a chance to react when the silent giant grabbed him by the long, stringy hair at the back of his head and smashed his face against the heavy steel of the car. "Shh, shh, now me bairn." Guinevere eased the old man down to the dirt road with remarkable kindness, considering she'd plunged her blade into his side. "I've gotcha. It's okay. It's okay there." A few noisy minutes later, Guinevere Maddox rose to her feet, brushing off her knees. "I hate it when they're nice," she said, more to herself than anything -- it wasn't like Ogden was going to respond. "Feels more like murder then." She tore her gaze away from the fallen man. "Call it," she said. "I don' want this man's sacrifice t'be in vain." Neil Higgins was right: people do some pretty fucked up things. * * * * * Eight Days Ago Siena barely remembered the young waitress speaking to her. "Ma'am?" See believed her name was "Mila" -- there was some bad joke about "My name is Mila and I'll be serving your meal, eh?" when she'd introduced herself. The girl kneeled beside the table. "Miss, can you hear me?" The girl's brown eyes were wide with concern. Siena tried to turn her head to focus on the girl, but the effort was almost insurmountable. Part of Siena knew she'd been drugged; this part was screaming at Sarah Riley's betrayal. But the rest was in a warm, kind of hazy place. "Nng." Her mouth tasted like cotton. "We're called for a cab for you," Mila explained. "Think you can walk?" Siena tried to respond, to scream, "The lying blond bitch did this -- go get her!" But her lips wouldn't form any coherent words, and the miserable sounds she was making only drew more pity out of the waitress' soulful brown eyes. She tried to lift her hand, to point or signal, but it felt like fifty-pound barbells were strapped to her forearms. "Geez, what happened to her?" One of Mila's colleagues -- a spiky haired blond kid named Tad -- had wandered over. "I think it's some kind of allergic reaction," Mila said. "Maybe she's on something," Tad countered. "We should call the cops." In her head, Siena threw her eyes open and frantically shook her head. In reality, she moaned softly and flinched. "My daddy said you could sleep anything off. Jack already sent for a cab. Says this isn't our problem." She didn't see whether Tad frowned. "We should at least help her outside, though. State she's in, she'll be like a bull in a china shop." The brunette girl and the blond boy each threw one of Siena's arms over their shoulder, pulling her to her feet and guiding her out the door. At Mila's suggestion, they used the service entrance -- no sense in humiliating the girl further. There was no yellow cab outside the restaurant (nor was there a cab of any other color). Mila frowned. "Where the hell are they?" she murmured. "I called, like, twenty minutes ago." "Should be here then, yeah." Tad nodded. "You want me to yell on them?" "Would you?" "Sure. Who'd you call?" Mila itched the right side of her eye. "Discount, I think." Tad's hand dropped to his hip for his phone, only to come up empty. "Huh, that's weird." He tapped Mila on the arm. "I think I'm gonna have to go in and make the call. I don't have my cell." "No prob," Mila said absently. "I think the drunken sailor here and I will be fine for a couple. Tell them I want a free fare." Tad grinned, yanking the side door open and slipping into the restaurant. "Be careful, ladies." Mila glanced after him only briefly, noting the door closed. "Hrm," she said hrmingly. "I think Tad likes me. What do you think?" Siena tried to shrug noncommittally, in the hopes that the other woman would at least shut up. All she managed to do was flip her hand over. "Here's the problem, right? The name! Tad. Tad the Tadpole. Tadpoles are yucky, and they do not taste very good. How do they become frogs? Why can't they be happy as tiny frog-fish? What about you? What do you think about the frog question, Miss Siena?" Siena turned her head to the other woman, eyes widening as much as she could manage. "N-no," she mumbled. The tall girl pushed off the car, her looping smile in place. "I think he's been gone long enough," the waitress -- whose name wasn't Mila -- said. "Let's get you all snug as a bug in a rug in my car. We have soooo much to do before Wednesday!" Cozen didn't struggle with Siena's weight as she lifted the slight woman, making for Mila Robertson's car. She was humming, Siena realized faintly as she faded in and out of consciousness. Soon, the Faceless Fighter added words to her tune. "What do you do with a drunken sailor? Early in the morning..." * * * * * When Siena was next startled awake, they were outside a pawnshop. It was the sound of glass shattering that had stirred her, and she blinked away as much of the nagging sleep as she could. Cozen emerged from the storefront, heedless of the alarm going off, clutching a small, handheld camcorder in her hand. The Auburn-Haired Harpy watched the grin spill onto Cozen's face as the Crazy turned the camera over in her hands. (Admittedly, "Cozen's face" is a relative term -- she had shed the bubbly, too-nice waitress sometime while Siena slept). "It still works -- yays!" The girlish happiness in Cozen's words chilled Siena to the bone, but that was all the Asian woman remembered before whatever she'd been dosed with rushed back up to claim her. * * * * * Fiberglass bathtubs are not nearly as comfortable when they're empty, Siena realized as she came to again. The shower curtain blocked her view of the room, but she heard the chipper tones of her charge. "Goodie, you're up! I thought you would sleep all day, lazyface" Siena was only dimly aware of the rest of Cozen's promo. She remembered weakly protesting, trying to convince the Faceless Fighter she'd need assistance for the week's match with Devin Shakur and she'd have the bruises to show for her impertinence. The Harpy fought off the doldrums as Cozen wound up her video ("And I'm never giving it back...) and prowled out of the room. She sat up as best she could in the tub to watch -- "know your enemy" was Madame Desade's first rule. The Crazy hadn't closed the door behind her, allowing Siena a glimpse of the motel room beyond. Cozen flopped down, face-first, on the quilted bedsheets, kicking her feet up in the air. Long arms stretched to snatch up the TV remote control; several seconds of aimless flipping eventually brought the cheery sight of Spongebob Squarepants to the screen. Cozen was humming the song softly to herself when the stillness in the room was broken by a quiet voice -- Siena applied her lessons quickly, pegging the speaker as a forty-or-so-year-old man, but his flat, expressionless tone kept his nationality hidden, and she didn't see him again. "Why do you do that?" the man asked. Cozen rolled onto her side, fingers buried in her tumbledown of tarry curls, hand propping her head up. "Do what?" she asked. "Buy into your own lies." The man's words were clipped, disgusted. "Because Spongebob Squarepants is kind of great." "Mm." The man didn't sound convinced. "Have to take your word for it." "Uh. Dude, he lives in a pineapple, but under the sea." "I've heard that. Must save on rent." "Absorbent and yellow and porous is he? You can't seriously expect to top that." It was like she was honestly trying to convince her guest of the cartoon's greatness. "Or you could not take my word for it -- pull up a chair and sneak a peek." "I'll pass." Siena distinctly heard the sound of liquid being poured. "You really think this'll work?" "Well, obviously not, because you have no taste in cartoons." Her looping smirk twisted into something less kind; was that an edge of the Troyslash? The Impressionable Impersonator's guest had a frown that carried into words. "Oh, come on: Ren & Stimpy is still the standard-bearer -- but that's not what I was talking about and you know it." "It'll rattle their cages, shake the bars. Awaken the monkeys that walk as men. But she won't be sore. She doesn't like the shrew, wants her tamed." The Crazy's intentionally vague use of pronouns made it difficult for Siena to follow. "And I'm damaged. Like a stained glass window rife with cracks; the lines draw another picture over the saint." "No, you're necessary. She wants what you can do. So does the Good Doctor." "So do you. Everyone wants what I can do. It's why I'm popular -- because I put out." "Everyone but you." Joints creaked as the man stood. "Just remember which of us can help you." Siena would never be sure whether or not she heard that last sentence correctly. She shifted in the tub as best she could without alerting the Skrull, desperate to get a look at the visitor. All she saw was a man with a shaved head, in a terribly swishy coat before the door swung closed. A silver flask dangled from the man's bony fingertips (nails painted black). Some kind of sigil -- a cobalt-blue ankh -- was etched into the side of the vessel. The television's light reflected off the flask oddly, and Siena tilted her head slightly, trying to place the symbol. She didn't have much time to study it before the man was gone. She had less before the show's end credits led to the Faceless Fighter scooting up off the bed, still singing. "Spongebob! Squarepants!" Cozen's expression -- or the expression of the haunted, haunting face she'd chosen -- was devoid of joy or, indeed, any emotion as she sat down on the edge of the tub. "Hiya," the Crazy said. "We need to talk. Away from even the prying eyes." Her grin hooked. "No, that's not a setup to Xavier Kannon you. Girl talk, sister, come on -- let's dish." * * * * * Forty-Three Minutes Ago If it sucks to be kidnapped once by a crazy bitch, imagine how bad it is the second time. Siena van der Wal was bound to a chair in a...she was pretty sure it was a warehouse. Smelled like one to store fish. Her ankles were chained to the floor, her hands manacled behind her. Guinevere Maddox and her partner, the silent giant, Ian Ogden, stood nearby. One was impassive...the other less so. "An' then, less twenny-four hours later," Guen prompted; her accent became more pronounced as she became agitated. "Y'came to the arena an' ye hadnae any hard feelin's fer the lady who putcha behind a wall fer thirty-six hours. How d'ye explain that?" "I can't." "Cain't? Or won't." Siena tried to sneer; she wasn't sure if she pulled it off. "Pick one." "Tha's the thing with the lot of ye what work for Pierce. No regard for history. Yer all tits an' legs -- a God's piss bordello of bad folk. Don't understand there's always consequences, 'cause she's got of scot-free fer so bloody long." "And you're here to change that, I take?" The doubt in Siena's words could have put Thomas to shame. "You, Edward Lambert, Jon Rhine and Lindsay fucking Troy?" "Nay, lass." Maddox turned towards the small table at her left. "Those folks ye mentioned? They're good sorts, real white hats." She plucked a dentist's pick from a leather-wrapped bundle unrolled on the table. "I'm an Inquisitor -- we're the kind of thing what gives even the boogeyman nightmares." The elfin blonde approached the Auburn-Haired Harpy with a placid smile on soft pink lips. Despite being bound to the chair, Siena tried to shrink away. "Now, sister, how 'bout y'tell me all about that tall drink'a crazy ye're watching over for the fucking Spider?" Siena van der Wal did so, and at length.
