Finding Yourself

Date: ReV 149 "How do you feel today, Craig?" The questions swirls around in his mind for a minute while he sits there, wearing a pair of khakis, and a fitted long sleeve shirt to protect him against the slight breeze in the city on this day. The woman sitting across from him is Dr. Silvia Young, a psychologist. He glares at her briefly, playing around with the question in his mind. Craig: Today? Today, I feel like an idiot for even considering coming back here. Silvia: I assumed you'd feel like that. Our first go-around wasn't... that great. Craig: That would have to be an understatement. She smiles at his comment. Being here was going against everything that Craig knew, but to keep his job, he had to; he had to suffer through this painful process. Silvia: [chuckles] It might be. Craig: I threw a book at your head. Silvia only nods her head, confirming that he did indeed throw a book at her head last time. She recalls the moment vividly, the book coming mere inches away from her face. The notes for that day read not to talk about his adolescence and sexual encounters. Silvia: [calmly] You did. I'm actually surprised that you called back for a second appointment. Craig: You're surprised? I'm surprised you even took the appointment. Silvia: [concerned] Why did you make the appointment? Craig: Well, I guess-- wait, no, I made it because... The question, though, even trips up Craig inside his own mind. Sure, he could put the blame on his job once again, but he had already used that excuse once. He sighs and resigns himself to telling the truth. Craig: I made it because I need help. Silvia: Well, that's a step in the right direction. Before, you had said it was for your job. Craig: [interjecting] To be honest, it was. Silvia: But, you're actually going to get something out of it now. Craig holds up his hands, not wanting to get her hopes up too much. Craig: Let's take this one step at a time. I wouldn't say all of that quite yet. Silvia: [backs off] Good point, and I'll take that into consideration. Now, last time, you talked about the recent affairs in your life. The suicide inside of your own house, your girlfriend as well as your best friend walking out on you when you needed them the most. Craig: [shaking his head] I don't need them. Silvia: Why're you lying to yourself? Craig: I'm not. Silvia: You are and you do need them. Nicole is the first person since you're amnesia, and this is what you told me, that you felt like you could get close to and trust again. You even told me that Katie pulled you out of the darkness after the death of your daughter. She takes a look at him, hoping for a response, but it's obvious that he needs a moment. He closes his eyes as he ponders her words and scratches his forehead lightly, more so to buy him time instead of getting rid of an itch. Craig: [sighs] Yeah, I guess I did say those things. Silvia: So, why belittle them? Craig: [quickly and bitter] Because they left me! They walked out that door and left me there! Silvia: After they found out that you weren't being entirely truthful to them. They had just gone through a traumatic situation and were in shock. Yet, on top of that, Nicole found out about your dark past while Katie found out that you hadn't been truthful with her. What did you honestly expect? Craig shrugs his shoulders as he sits back in the chair. Craig: I don't know, understanding maybe? A chance to tell my side of the story. Silvia: Do you think it would've mattered much? Craig: I don't think so. Silvia: For what it's worth, I don't think so either. They need time and if they truly care about you then they'll come back. It's not going to be quick though. It's going to take some time for them to trust you again and want you involved in their lives. You just have to be patient and wade it out. It's not going to be easy. Trust me when I tell you it's going to be a difficult process, but it's going to be well worth it. Craig: [sighs] I hope so, I really do. Silvia: I know you're not the most optimistic person on the planet, Craig. Craig: [interrupts] We should add that to the pile of understatements. Silvia: [laughing] Yeah, we probably should. Try this though. Give each of them a call. Odds are that it'll go to voicemail and just leave them a voicemail. Sooner or later, that anger will vanish and they'll listen to that voicemail. When they do, I think they'll come back. Craig: [skeptical] Yeah? Silvia: I think so. What do I know? Craig: Who honestly knows? Silvia: Just remember, you're the one paying me. Craig: I guess I'm the sucker then. Silvia: And with that, you're fifteen minutes are up. Craig glances over at the clock and rolls his eyes as he sees that they're indeed up. The fifteen minutes were a stipulation for Dr. Young to take the appointment though, to ensure that Craig didn't throw another book at her head. Craig: Thanks for fitting me in on such short notice. Silvia: It's honestly not a problem. Next time, thirty minutes. I'll see you in three days, Craig. She stands up and the two shake hands before she walks to her office door and pulls it open to let Craig out. As he begins to walk out though, she stops him with one final thought. Silvia: Craig, I want you to do me a favor for next time. Think past your adolescence, and when you were with your parents. I want you to remember memories if you can, okay? Craig: Yeah, I guess I can try that. Silvia: Good. Craig then walks out as the door is closed behind him and he begins to feel a little awkward. Opening up and talking about his feelings were two things that he couldn't stand doing, but even after this session, he was beginning to feel better about some things. He begins to walk out of the building as he grips his cell phone and looks at it. He thinks for a second about calling Katie or Nicole, but holds off on that thought as he walks outside and towards his car. As he opens the door, his cell phone begins to go off, and he answers it not even checking the caller ID display but still thinks that it might be Katie or Nicole. Craig: Hello? Woman: Craig? This is Nurse Carol from Dr. Green's office. Craig: Oh, hey. Did the tests come back in? As he speaks into his cell phone, he turns on his car, and backs out of the parking lot. Nurse: Well, Craig, I'm just returning your phone call to let you know that some of the tests came in. Craig: And? Nurse: Doctor Green just finished looking over them and he says there isn't anything conclusive from these reports. Craig takes a look at his cell phone for a brief second and nearly chucks the thing out of the window before putting it back against his ear, hoping that a police officer isn’t around him at all. Craig: What?! Oh, come on! Nurse: I understand your frustration sir, but-- Craig: [interjects] I don't know if you DO understand my frustrations! Listen, I've been headaches for the past four months and there's no end in sight. Fatigue, nausea, joint paints, you name it, I have it right now. Why would you call me without any answers?! Nurse: I understand your frustrations, sir. Craig: Good! Because your doctor told me that he would get an answer for me discreetly. He's been telling me this for 6 weeks now! I've had a spinal tap and a lumbar puncture while giving you guys enough blood to fill the Red Cross up by this point. Nurse: Yes, sir, and he would like to extend his apologies. He just needs one more test to come in. He has a list of things it could be and while this eliminated some things on the list, it didn't eliminate everything. Craig: And when do you think that will be? Nurse: Either tomorrow or the day after, sir. Craig: Okay, will you or the doctor give me a call when it does? Nurse: We will sir. I just wanted to respond to your phone call. Craig: Thanks. Nurse: You have a good one, sir. Craig: Thanks. Craig then hangs up the phone before slamming his hands into the steering well a few dozen times. After screaming out a few expletives, he realizes that he's pulled up to his house. He sits in the car though for a few moments with his eyes closed as he hopes that the results of this final test doesn't signify something seriously wrong with him. Tears begin to well in his eyes for a moment, but they vanish just as quickly as they came. He opens the door to this car and gets out. As he looks around, he grabs his cell phone and begins dialing a phone number and places the phone to his ear once again. After it rings a few times, it goes to voicemail. Craig sighs, but decides to leave the message. Craig: Hey, Katie. It’s me. Look, you know I’m not the kind of person to be rather verbose, so I’ll get straight to the point. He begins walking up to his front door and opens it with no problem at all. Craig: I’m sorry. I’m sorry for not being honest with you and keeping you away from something so important in my life. I just didn’t know how to tell you to be honest. But, I need you. I need you more than ever and with you gone, it’s starting to kill me. For me to admit that to you, you know it’s serious; because that’s never easy for me to admit. He closes his front door and begins to walk towards the living room. Craig: So, please call me back. You can yell at me and— As he walks into the living room though, he notices a woman sitting with his back towards him on his couch. He lowers the phone from his ear as he wonders who the person sitting there is. Craig: Who the hell are you and what the hell are you doing here?! The woman turns around slowly, her hair moving almost in slow motion, but still spinning like a tornado from all of the curls in it.
