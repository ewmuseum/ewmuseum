War (Part III)- Cynthia's Day

11 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 11th “There are things in life we don't necessarily want to do, but we have to do them.” March 11th, 2009 8 PM Location: Shakur's Basement A cold wave of emptiness hovers over Devin Shakur's basement as each member of the Crew prepares for a task nothing short of inhumane. Tonight, they exceed the barricades of traditional warfare and enter into a realm of sickness only a sliver of the population has the credentials to confine into. Tonight they end the life of four year old Cynthia Connors. Steven Connors made the decision to take away two members of their family, two people that meant the world to each of the seven individuals suiting up for another grotesque round of battle. So Christian Daniels has elected to shatter the Connors' entire world with one swift cut. The Biker reaches into his denim trench coat and pulls out the twelve-inch knife known as 'Big Slick'. He stares down at the blade that has gotten him through plenty of intense brawls and reminisces about all the blood he's washed off. In his view every cut has been justified, so he never had a single regret when standing over the kitchen sink removing the crimson from his prized possession. Tonight, Big Slick could be permanently stained, no amount of water able to remove the blood. The patriarch in Christian knows that the verdict must be carried out. Helping Matthew and Brandon mature into the men they eventually became rank as two of the proudest accomplishments in Christian's life. When two people considered children are murdered, their deaths must be avenged without question. Demonic brown eyes stare down at a left arm littered in tattoos. Christian is quite proud of his collection of skulls, bones, quotes, and Death emblem that don this particular arm. But underneath the intimidation of fire and skulls lies an aberration in the artwork of Christian Daniels, which can only be observed if one looks hard enough and doesn't become captivated by the wealth of designs. Christian doesn't have to look hard because the ink was his first. A five-letter word, Caleb Christian hasn't seen the child he helped conceive in 17 years and that is the one thing holding him back, the one thing keeping everybody in the basement longer than necessary. Nobody wants to speak out because Christian's struggle is exclusive. None of the crew have had the privilege of bringing a child into this world or the heartbreak of leaving one behind. Everybody hopes that Cynthia is asleep so Christian can accomplish the task without having to get into anything complicated. Externally, his face will show the macabre snarl that has made him into one of the most violent individuals to ever walk the planet. Internally, when he looks down at Cynthia, knowing that he has to end her life will make him sick to his stomach. Hanging his head and looking deeper into the tattoo, Christian whispers a single phrase, “Forgive me, Caleb...You know I don't wanna do this.” A long moment passes before Christian deeply exhales and turns around to look at his fellow brethren. Six pairs of eyes meet his glance and each crew member gives a nod of the head. An extra bit of somberness escapes Christian's voice when he asks, “Everybody know their role?” “I take out the alarm and phone on the right side of the house.” Jason Hardy says, showing a pair of wire cutters. Michael Blaze points to Mallory Daniels and himself, “We watch the roads, make sure no pigs happen to stroll by. If they do, we call you.” “I drop you guys off, keep the car around the corner, wait for my signal, and get us out of there.” Chris Smith says. “I sneak in the back of the house, kill the guardians, let you guys inside.” Rachel says. “And you and me CD...” Devin says, raising his eyebrows in a matter of fact way, “...Yeah.” Christian shakes his head in disgust while groaning, “Let's get this over with.” The patriarch leads the march up the stairs and out of the home, heart heavier than a ton of bricks. March 11th, 2009 Time: 9:45 PM Location: Bosch Residence “Good night Auntie Michelle and Unkie George!” Cynthia says nestled comfortably underneath her Spongebob Squarepants covers. Michelle and George Bosch never suspected they would be caring for Cynthia over an extended duration, but they've managed to be model guardians ever since Larry dropped her off after the funeral. On the positive side, Larry called about two hours ago and said he would be picking Cynthia up on Friday. Michelle moves forward, arms extended, threatening to tickle Cynthia and start another fit of laughter that has resonated throughout the home for the past two weeks. They've managed to avoid any tense questions by saying her mother had to go away on international business for a few weeks. When Cynthia was dropped off, the group concluded that Larry will explain what really happened when he retrieves his daughter. “Sleep tight and don't let the bed bugs bite.” George says, pointing at Cynthia and shutting off the overhead light. A Barbie night-light illuminates enough of the room to make Cynthia feel safe. Once outside, Michelle wraps her arms around George and the two head for their bedroom just down the hall, eager to get some sleep, having been up for fifteen hours straight entertaining Cynthia and planning for tomorrow's big work day. A beep resonates through the custom black Sedan waiting just off the Bosch property, sending the passenger door vertically upward. Jason Hardy leaps from the seat, streaming across the driveway and around to the right side of the house. He pauses in place, waiting a moment as a precautionary measure. They picked tonight for the simple fact that everybody is supposed to be attending a housewarming party two blocks over. However, Jason knows that somebody could have stayed behind and must play this by the book. When no lights pop on, the former CIA and FBI consultant goes to work. Chris punches a number on the phone, attached to an in car charger, and gets the voice of Michael Blaze. “We good to go?” “Tell Rachel she better be quiet and wait for Jason.” “You know how quiet I am, Chris.” Rachel responds, opening the passenger side door on Michael's car and beginning her trip toward the back of the home. Michael backs up and heads down the street, messing with the scanner and adhering to his role as a lookout. Throwing the covers over their bodies, George and Michelle share a good night kiss before George reaches over and scans the alarm clock for five straight seconds. Michelle has been trying to rid him of the OCD that has plagued him for the past three years, but every now and then he has relapses. He will regret having the disorder and the constant need to perfect his lawn every single day of the week. Even Michelle can't eliminate his longstanding passion for lawn care. It's a habit that annoys the hell out of his neighbors and gives him the nickname 'Leaf Man' in the autumn months. With a perfectly groomed lawn under her feet, Rachel drops from the fence without making the faintest of sounds. She's been trained to do this for years upon years and knows that one single mistake can ruin any operation. Bolting across the yard with the speed of a cheetah, she easily avoids anybody spotting her in the five hundred foot distance to the back deck, and tiptoes up the stairs. All of the lights on the first floor are off. Exactly what she wanted. Her ready hands reach inside the lock picking kit and wait for Jason's signal. A moment later, he peers his head into her line of vision and gives the thumbs up. The assassin takes out her tools and swiftly manipulates the sliding glass door in front of her while Jason leans up against the back right corner of the home. He needs to remain guard just in case something goes awry. Seconds later, Rachel puts the accessories back into her coat, carefully sliding open the door and walking into the home. Turning her petite frame around, Rachel gently slides the door shut and reaches into her pocket for the 9 millimeter, attaching a silencer on with ease. George's head shoots up out of bed, eyes wildly moving around the room and settling upon Michelle, “What was that? I think I heard something downstairs.” “George, it was probably just something outside.” Michelle nonchalantly responds, barely rolling over to acknowledge her husband's worry. “I'm going to go down and check.” George firmly responds. He yanks the covers down and grabs the flashlight he keeps in the top drawer of his nightstand. Michelle rolls her eyes and sits on her left elbow, rubbing her eyes with her right hand. “Let it go, honey. We've got to get up early tomorrow and you can't be roaming around every time you hear something. Our alarm system works fine.” George turns on the nightstand light and glares over at his wife. “You can't be too careful, Michelle.” Sighing, Michelle throws her arms up and turns her head away, “Alright, one time, while you are up make sure Cynthia is still tucked in.” “Was intending on it.” George replies, throwing open the bedroom door and flashing the bright light down at his front door. If his blinds were open, he would see the black Sedan with three people patiently waiting outside his property. Rachel freezes like a deer in the headlights. How could someone have heard her? She didn't make a sound. Unaware of George's OCD habits, she quietly backs into the kitchen and hides behind the corner. If he decides to come down the stairs, there's only one door he can enter through and she'll be waiting for him. The circular light continues haphazardly around the ground floor and into the living room. Nothing appears to be out of place, but something doesn't feel right to George. He can sense a difference and walks down the ten steps, clutching the flashlight as a weapon while waving it around. When he hits the ground, Rachel calculates the distance between George and herself. He can't be more than 50 feet away and he's closing in. The light becomes brighter and brighter, reflecting against the sliding glass door that she entered. George swings the light back and forth five times before entering the kitchen. He makes the grave mistake of turning his head to the left first. Rachel puts a gloved hand over his mouth and a bullet into the side of his head. Instincts kicking in, she sticks her foot out and catches the flashlight nanoseconds before the object slams into the ground, which would no doubt alert Michelle. Turning the light off, Rachel bends down and checks George for a pulse. Finding none, she places the light on a kitchen table and moves toward the front door, undoing the bolt and motioning for Christian and Devin to enter. “GEORGE, DON'T YOU DARE GO OUTSIDE!” Michelle shouts from the bedroom. Now she doesn't have a choice but to get up and confront her husband. Rachel hears the footsteps and waits at the base of the stairs, gun pointed upward. Meanwhile in the car, Christian and Devin exchange a glance at one another. Christian pats his pocket and feels the outline of Big Slick against his leg. Chris checks over his shoulder and raises both back doors on the Sedan. “Sure you gotta do this? I can make the cut if you need me to.” Devin says, a concerned glance in his eyes. Rarely has he seen Christian in such a fragile and vulnerable state. “Don't worry bout me man,” Christian says, trying to exude confidence but failing. “Just...Make sure I don't lose focus. That's why you are there.” “She ain't gonna fight it man, I can do it.” Devin responds, extending his hand out to acquire Big Slick. Christian shakes his head and looks over at his adopted brother, putting a reaffirming hand on his shoulder, “Dev, your like one of my kids, man. I can't let you do it. This was directed at me. This is my call. I gotta do it.” Devin scrunches his face up and just as the two step from the vehicle, he reaches out and grabs Christian by the arm, pulling him close. “He's not going to be ashamed of you, you know that right?” Christian nods twice and lingers in the car for a second, putting the same hand on Chris's shoulder. “Wait until Rach gets out and then gun it, 10 on the nose, come back.” “Got it big man.” Chris says, looking over to his right and observing Rachel walking down the driveway. If the house warming party wasn’t consuming the neighborhood, she would never boldly step out even knowing that both guardians are dead and the phone line has been cut. Christian and Devin pass by and walk into the home while Rachel drops into the Sedan. All the doors shut and Chris drives away. Cynthia Connors hasn't heard a sound, the sign that Rachel did her job properly. Her eyes are shut, mind completely at ease. Devin leads the way up the stairs while Christian straddles behind, doing his best to get into character. He could have changed his mind and taken out Steven just as easily, but he wants to create an impact that will resonate with both brothers and anybody else that might get involved. This is a statement killing. They have to show that no border or boundary is above their reach, that they will go through any crevice and take any shortcut in order to achieve complete liberation from the people chasing them. Reaching the second floor, Devin sees the open master bedroom and Michelle's dead body. He points over to the next room and walks to the door, placing his hand against the handle, applying a minimal amount of pressure to check and see if the door is locked. He looks over at Christian and shakes his head. The Biker looks down at the floor for a moment, clinching his fists and having to lean against the rail for support. Devin tiptoes back over to Christian and tilts his head up. “We can still get out. Her waking up to see this will be pretty fucking traumatizing.” Forcefully putting his head back down, Christian responds, “Make sure she is ready. I need one more minute, Dev.” Devin nods and goes back over to the door, grasping the handle and nudging his way inside. Cynthia is still fast asleep, unaware of any abnormality. He notices the night-light brightening the room and halts his movement. When running through the plethora of possible scenarios, the primary objective was not to wake Cynthia. If she gets startled, they were going to be in trouble and faced with a tough decision. Christian removes his arms from the rail, causing Cynthia to take her hands out from under the covers and rub her eyes, convinced that Michelle or George are watching her sleep. She can't understand why elders relish watching their children sleep. She is drawn into a momentary stare with Devin who mutters, “Fuck” under his breath and charges ahead to cover Cynthia's mouth. She squirms vehemently, but Devin places his knees atop the covers to prevent an escape. Her small hands swat against his muscular arms and provide no luck in freeing her from the dangerous situation she finds herself in. Hearing the squirming, Christian is left with no choice but to enter the room and find Devin holding Cynthia down. Devin and Christian each give one another incredulous looks. Christian is angered to find the situation compromised and Devin is glaring because Christian was the one who did the compromising. “...The rail!” Devin shouts at Christian, throwing one arm into the air while keeping the other over Cynthia's mouth to stop her from screaming. The Biker grunts and goes to swear, but refrains from doing so. He doesn't want to do so in the presence of a minor. Even one he's going to have to kill. In anger, Christian leans against the wall and turns on the overhead light. There is no going back now. She'll eventually tell somebody and the eyewitness testimony of a child in court is going to hold a massive amount of weight. Lawyers could try to persuade, but the jury will be convinced. She's seen both of their faces before and could identify them with unflinching certainty, locking them up for the rest of their lives with no possibility of freedom. Christian needs to calm the situation and continues the unconventional method of doing so, going over to Cynthia and staring down at her. His eyes are ones that hold concern, something that even the little girl can pick up on. She's still frightened because Devin hasn't let go and is putting a finger over his mouth to ensure Cynthia's silence. Christian goes to speak, but words aren't coming out. His first phrase would be a lie, so he gives a quick glance to Devin. “We're not going to hurt you Cynthia,” Devin says, removing his hand from her mouth and rising off her. Cynthia tries to sneak away, but Devin's reflexes are a little too fast and he puts a powerful arm over the covers. “But we've gotta tell you the truth about some things. We've got to tell you the truth about what happened to Mommy.” Continuing her resistance, Cynthia shakes her head “No” and makes another attempt to slip past Devin, his knee slamming against the wall to stop her. Devin leaves her no choice but to back against the wall and move her eyes over to Christian. The two become stuck in a trance, the one that Christian wanted to avoid. His stomach turns to knots and his body tenses up. Devin shifts his look back and forth between the two, sensing the trance as a good distraction. Christian is playing a pivotal role even if he doesn't realize it. The child is not screaming or crying out for Michelle and George. Devin rolls off the bed and blocks Cynthia's last path of escape. With the bed against the wall, she's locked in a proverbial cage with no where to go. Christian has the west wing covered and Devin has the south locked. Finally, perhaps realizing that her options are limited, Cynthia replies to her soon to be murderers, “Mommy is working.” Devin looks over at Christian and smacks him on the shoulder. The Biker told him his sole objective was to make sure focus wasn't lost. But he's still lost in Cynthia, seeing too much of Caleb in her eyes. Devin winds up and lands a stiff shot into Christian's back, awakening him from the state and causing him to shoot a menacing stare at Devin. He is met with raised eyebrows and a point at Cynthia. Fully realizing where he is at and who exactly is in front of him, Christian has finally found his voice, “...No Cynthia...She's not at work.” “Then where she is?” The child quips back, eyes scanning the room perhaps to look for another outlet to run away. Christian bites his lips and plops down on the bed, sliding Cynthia up against the wall. His legs swing around to block the south exit, cornering her. Devin takes this as his cue to exit the room. Christian folds his arms into his lap and looks over at the child. He takes a special interest in trying to make Cynthia comfortable, wiping the strands of hair away from her face and offering her a treat, one he packed as his own precautionary measure, a lollipop. With Devin gone from the room, she indulges in the treat. “Has Daddy ever told you about Heaven?” Cynthia's eyes look up and see Christian's worn jeans. She can't maneuver around his massive frame, so she responds, “It's the place where God lives.” “That's right, God lives there. It's also the best place anybody can go to. Do you know why?” “Cuz you are happy forever there.” “Very good, Cynthia.” Christian says, cracking a genuine smile down at the girl. He's trying to ease himself into explaining to her that Mommy is there without having to delve into the fact that the man who killed her is listening only feet outside the room, enamored with the way Christian is speaking. Devin has never heard Christian be so...polite. “Well...Mommy had a bit of an accident one night and...God decided that it was time to call Mommy up to Heaven.” A puzzled expression comes across Cynthia's face, she doesn't entirely fathom what was just told to her. “...Mommy is in Heaven?” “Yes, Cynthia. She did such a good job of making sure that you were a good little girl, keeping out of trouble, and God felt he needed her by his side and that she could serve him better.” “But I can still see her Friday, right?” The Biker pauses and wipes his brow, which has filled with sweat ever since sitting down next to his victim. He's aware that children of her age haven't been introduced to the concept of finality and death. For a moment, he ponders how to answer the question. Cynthia looks at The Biker with inquisitive eyes, clinging onto his words. He has made her curious and she needs the inquiry answered. “No, Cynthia...She can't come back. When someone goes to Heaven, they stay there forever.” A shock wave hits Cynthia and Christian puts his finger over her mouth to quell her forthcoming scream. He puts his rugged fingers underneath her eyes and dries tears, rocking her back and forth. The parenting gene he's never shown anybody else ultimately kicks in. “Cynthia...God knew you would be upset...But he's smart, just like you. And it's not like she's completely gone...She's watching over you.” Christian stops rocking Cynthia and allows the child to look up into his eyes. He continues to dry her tears. “She's...watching me?” With all the sincerity that Christian can muster, he brings his head down close to Cynthia. “God wouldn't have it any other way. Every time the sun shines, that is Mommy telling you Good morning. Every time the wind blows, that is Mommy checking on you. Every time it rains, Mommy wants you to stay inside because something bad is happening outside and she is protecting you. When the moon comes up, that is Mommy telling you good night. If you look at all the stars in the sky, they are Mommy's smile. She's proud of you, Cynthia.” To say that Devin is flabbergasted would be an understatement. Even when finding out about what his father did to him throughout twelve years of childhood, Christian never spoke to him in that tone, with that kind of compassion. “But...” Cynthia stops, still not capable of fully grasping the situation before her. She can't figure the exact manner in which to phrase the next question that she has. “But what?” Christian responds, putting a finger underneath her chin. “I dunno” “You do, Cynthia, just ask me.” A bit frustrated with her lack of understanding, Cynthia asks, “...Why would God leave Daddy without Mommy?” “Because he knows Daddy loves you with all of his heart. He knows that Daddy will take care of you. Daddy will never let you out of his sight.” “...But Daddy and Mommy love each other.” “That's right, they do.” “Why would God break them apart if they are happy?” Christian has to pause again and ponder. He's refused to lie to the child under any circumstances. Dropping back against the wall, his brain ticks away trying to conjure up the best possible answer. This is the conversation that Larry himself wanted to prepare for and murder afterward wouldn't be involved. “God has a weird way of working, Cynthia...Everything I told you about Mommy, the sun, moon, and the rain, remember that?” “Yeah” “Daddy knows that too. He's upset also, but he knows that Mommy would never leave either of you two alone. You are a family, one that will never be broken. Even though God took Mommy away, she'll always be there for you whenever you or Daddy need her.” “Really?” “God wouldn't let me lie to you.” “Are you a messenger from God?” Christian can't help but chuckle and smile at the remark, “In a way...Yes, yes I am.” Seeing Christian smile eases Cynthia for the slightest moment, “Will I see Mommy again?” Devin grimaces, wishing that she didn't ask that question. He desperately wants to poke his head into the room and see what Christian's expression is after coming into contact with the comforting side that he never knew existed. If Devin turned the corner, he would find Christian's smile still present, but underneath a hint of malice is forming. He's gotten comfortable with Cynthia, which is the worst possible scenario for the girl. “I have a feeling Cynthia...God is going to call you to see Mommy very soon.” Cynthia looks up at Christian with a puzzled look, and is only slightly reassured by his smile. His tone is a little different in that response. Taking a bite from her lollipop, she is unaware that his right hand is uncovering the knife lodged in his pocket. “What about Daddy?” Christian turns his body to Cynthia and gives a full on smile. He takes a little too much pleasure in the prospect of putting Larry Connors six feet under. “In due time...” With a cobra's speed, the blade emerges from his pocket and slices Cynthia's throat in one swift motion. He turns his head away, not wanting to see what reactions occur, even though he can picture them in his mind. “...In due time.” With the child going through the stages of death, Christian rises from the bed, his back still to Cynthia. He gives a long glance at the blade, trying to keep his eyes from shifting backwards and watching her go to heaven. His blade now covered in blood, Christian places it back into the cover and slowly walks out of the room, turning off the light and shutting Cynthia's door. He looks over at Devin and motions for them to leave the house. Dropping down the stairs rapidly, the duo walk around into the kitchen, stepping over George's body and putting themselves in front of the sliding glass door. Devin puts a hand out and stops Christian from touching the handle. “How much of that did you mean?” Christian looks over at his brother, blinking rapidly, and pulls his hand down. The intimidating monotone has resurfaced. “We know differently, but I never lied to her.” Throwing open the glass door, Christian steps out onto the back porch while Devin shuts the door behind him. Jason is there to greet his fellow crew members with a pat of the shoulder. The job has been done. Christian can only imagine the rage that Larry Connors will go into when he finds out that his daughter's life has been taken.
