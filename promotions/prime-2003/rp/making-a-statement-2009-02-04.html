Making a Statement

4 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 4th, 2009 Malibu Marriott Malibu, California 6:19 AM January 29th, 2009 Tommy O’Connor stands facing the world, as it seems, seven stories above sea level in a tortured hotel room. He looks out of his balcony door to the crashing tide and strokes his long, red beard thinking about the night prior to this sobering moment. Stepping over empty bottles of whiskey, beer, and prescription pain killers, Tommy finds a path to fresh air. He reaches the doorway, filled with ‘Rippa Wins!’ streamers. He tears them down, revealing sunlight to the room. His eyes dart toward the beach, spotting what seems to be the ‘morning birds’ who take pride in their 6:30 AM, 3-mile journey up and down the beach. Tom takes his hand away from his face, picking up a glass of ice and whiskey. He looks over the situation and clinks the ice off the sides of the glass a few times. He lifts his eyebrows nonchalantly and takes what should not be the end of the glass down in one swig. His face puckers up. He looks down to the runners again, holding up his empty glass. “Cheers.” Setting the glass down, Tom sits in one of the balcony chairs of his seventh floor suite, crushing all the empty boxes of Camel and Marlboros. Pulling out a pack of Camel’s from underneath him, he flicks open his stainless-steel Zippo and takes the first drag. He lies back, resting his head in his hand and looks to the sky. He watches the cloud drift away as he lets his mind run adrift… -------------- Ryan’s Pub Boston, Mass. New Year’s Day 1988 The clutter and trash in the alley behind a local pub makes some racket as young boy staggers around in the afternoon shadows. His hair is red. His eyes are blue. This is the young Tommy O’Connor. The young Scrap. He is wearing an olive green jacket and fingerless, black gloves. His hair is red and long, tied up underneath a black cap. He wanders around aimlessly in the alleyway filled with trash cans and dumpsters. Suddenly the back door of the Ryan’s Pub slams open, echoing throughout the alley and sending the boy into hiding. Pouring into the alleyway is a trio of trouble; one man wearing a silk floral dress shirt and two men in suit jackets, chasing him. He turns down the alley past the dumpsters but one man pulls out his chain and whips it out, grabbing the neck of his mark. “Got ‘im, Bobby!” The man yanks and drops the victim. Bobby, the bigger of the two goons, stalks closer toward with a mean look in his eye. He clubs down with his fist and forearm on the man’s face, busting his mouth and nose open. “Heh. Goin’ somewhere, pal?” He kicks with his right foot and nails him right in the stomach. The other man continues to pull on the chain while he tries to pry it away from his neck, desperately. Bobby pulls out his phone. “Just two- two more weeks and I’ll have it. The money, money’ll be there. I swear.” Bobby looks away from the man and to his partner. The door opens up one more time and a man walks out in a much nicer suit. He ashes his cigarette out and approaches the situation. “Boss. This is Stew Lemons. He ain’t got what you want.” The Boss’ eyes lock on to his employee Paul’s. He motions his hand around in a circle a few times. Paul lets the chain off from around Stew’s neck. He lets out deep breaths and drops down to all fours. “Stew Lemons… the fisherman, right?” He bends down and grabs the chin, tight, and lifts it up so they are eye to eye. He nods his head, disappointed-like. He drops the man’s head after Paul loosens the chain. As he walks around he scowls down at his reason for a bad taste in his mouth. So he spits on him. “There are better ways to handle this, Stewie. A lot better ways. But you picked the worst way. The hard way.” The man kicks his right foot, his big right foot, into the stomach of Stew Lemons. He laughs a bit and looks over at Bobby. After a long two second exchange, the big right hand of the Boss comes out, palm up. Bobby looks at it then back at his chief in command. “Boss?” “The piece, kid.” “Boss?” “You think I’m kidd’n? Gimme the piece, Bobbo.” Bobby, uncontrollably it seems, reaches into his jacket and pulls out a small six-shooter, capable of close range damage. He holds it out. “These days, it’s all about power statements. Making sure that people like this nitwit don’t get any fancy ideas, y’know?” Bobby steps back with a look of horror over his face. His arms go back and he falls against the silver trash cans on the wall. He looks down and spots the long, red locks of a young boy. “Huh?- Hey!” “Pipe down, Bobbo.” CRACK Before that can happen, the sound of a gunshot against bone and brain is made. Bobby stops and looks back to his Boss, holding Stew Lemons’ limp corpse by the collar. He drops it and cowers down at it. He straightens the lapel on his jacket and looks to Paul, tossing the gun to him. “Get rid of it.” Bobby is making a racket and jumps off of the trash cans. “Boss! Boss! There’s a- a kid! Under the cans. He-“ Before the bumbling idiot finishes, the cans fly upward and onto the trio of gangsters. Out of them comes a small storm of red hair and white socks. The kid kicks Paul in the gut and pushes him over. He falls down and hits his head on the dumpster. Tom grabs one of the cans and dumps it over the Boss’ head, pushing him into the brick wall and down to the ground. He steps on something hard and metal. He looks down, reacting, not thinking. The man uppercuts Tom and sends him to the ground with a throbbing nose. Once again, Tom reacts before he thinks. “Whoa kid.” Before he knows it, Tom is staring at Bobby, who is staring down the barrel of his own six-shooter. He looks down the gun, down the arm, and then down the nose – to the eyes of this young, bright-eyed kid whose hands were beginning to shake from the nerves. The sound of the bullets rattling inside the chamber could be heard throughout the alley. “Just… don’t…” The rattling stopped and Tom looks directly at Bobby. He grins a bit. “What are you doin’, kid? Put the gun down-“ “Just drop the talkin’, okay? I’m just out here, strollin’ through and you try to bring me into this mess you call a hit? I’m here to tell you, you’re boss, and whoever that guy was - it’s not just about power statements.” “Huh?” “It’s about showing people what can happen.” The click of the hammer going into position is heard. Sweat begins to roll off Bobby’s forehead and nose tip. Frantically, he glances back and forth between the kid and the piece. The kid winks before shoving the cold barrel into the side of Bobby’s face. “Showing them that bad things can happen all the time. Bad things can happen to bad people. Bad things can happen to good people, too. But the truth is, Bobbo…I make bad things happen… all the time.” The kid smashes the gun over Bobby’s head and then spins around, kicking him in the stomach and toward the dumpster with a wild grunt. The big man crashes into it, doubling over backward into the trash bin and letting out all sorts of unpleasant sounds. Tom raises the gun over his head, emptying the gun of anymore lead. He tosses the gun in the dumpster and turns toward the street. Tom lights up a Camel, takes a drag, and exhales a large cloud over his head. The blood trickle from his nose grows longer and thicker. He wipes it, smiling at the lovely surprise. These days it’s all about making power statements. -------------------- Danny Dingo’s Dance Room Atlantic City, NJ June 4th, 1997 11:42 PM A black sedan waits behind the well-lit part of Atlantic City. In side sit three men, hidden by the shadows, and all smoking cigarettes. The smoke clouds each pop out as they take their turns talking. A large cloud from the back emerges. “This is takin’ for-fuckin’-ever.” “Relax. He’ll be here.” The engine of the sedan bumbles under their whispers and the street life sounds of horns, sirens, and high-heels. The men in the car sit, idle, and wait. Another car slowly pulls into the alleyway, blocking off any other entrance. It rolls to a stop. “Whodafuckisthat!?” The three men adjust around, looking for answers. They all stop once the other car’s door opens and closes. Footsteps can be heard and then nothing. The headlights ignite, blinding the opposite car and those inside it. A silhouette appears, startling the men. The darkness of the alleyway is impossible to make out a face. “That him?” “How the fuck should I know? Can I seen ‘im?” “That ain’t him. Too small.” The passenger exhales smoke but stays still. The backseat passenger sits up trying to get a better look. He squirms around in any attempt at sight. The front-door pops out and opens up. The passenger motions to get out, just before having his sleeve jerk him back into the car. The driver glares at him, concerned. “Make it quick.” “Heh,” The passenger takes another drag off his smoke and flicks the ash to the ground. He looks back to his colleague. “I make ‘em all quick, Don.” The figure steps out of the car, standing about six-three or so. He straightens his jacket, closes the door, and slowly makes his way to the front of the car. The figures stand there for a few seconds. The driver of the black sedan turns his head toward the backseat. “Say, Greggy; what was the score of the Yanks’ game?” “Three to one. Ol’ Jeter made the-“ Before the conversation goes on anymore, the hood is dented in with the body of their third pal. His head hits the windshield with a slight crack, causing the others in the car to jump. They both begin to scatter for their handles. The back door opens. Seeing the opportunity, the man tries to get out. Before he can, though, a heavy hand slams the door on his head and back, crushing the man into a motionless pile of bones. “Ray?! What the hell is this?” Pulling out his knife, the man from the front begins screaming obscenities and threats. He kicks out the door window and crawls through. When he hits the wet asphalt, he finds himself staring down a pair of black combat boots. The man, picking himself off the ground, wipes whatever liquid on his pants and looks at the face of the mystery man. “What the fuck, Tom? Late night stroll? Where’s the money!?” The man, smoking a cigarette and wearing a black headband, grins wryly at his accuser. He smiles wide and exhales the smoke into the man’s face. “Money, money, money… that’s how it always is with you guys, isn’t it?” “When you owe as much as you do… Yeah. It’s a hot topic.” The redhead laughs a bit to himself. He looks around some more, seemingly wasting time. He runs a hand over his beard and long hair. “Funny.” “What is?” “I’m the hot topic?” The man turns around, lifting his arms and such in a mysterious manner and not really paying any attention to the conversation. “What’s that smell? Why am I wet?” “Well that depends. Are you… excited?” Smiling, the redhead’s eyes grow wide and he takes another drag off of his cigarette. Without warning, though, he flicks it toward the chest of the driver. The orange, burning ashes hit his black leather jacket and drop toward the ground. The alleyway suddenly lights up, as if commanded to. The flames engulf the black sedan like the ocean would a rock and the three men involved seemingly vanish into a pile of metal, glass, and screams. The redhead takes a step back. He watches the fire while the flames’ reflection glistens off his blue eyes. A small, sadistic smile spreads over the man’s face. He strolls, casually, away from his inferno and walks toward the back door of the building. Looking back, he grins through his thick, red facial hair. The brightness of the flames causes him to shield his eyes a bit and throw on a pair of black sunglasses. He walks into the back of the bar. The first big man dressed in black sets his big paw in the middle of the man’s chest. “Name?” Looking down at the impolite gesture, the man’s moustache turns up on its’ ends and he shrugs, inconsequently. “You mean you forgot?” The big palm quickly is turned around and sent back from whence it came. The large thud causing people to move is that of the big body, dropping down a steel staircase and pouring out onto the crowded dance floor. The redhead scans the room with a sharp eye and quickly makes his way to the bartender. He nudges in next to a man in a big, green suit. The two exchange glances. “How ‘bout some space, pal?” The offending party looks down at the club’s newest nightmare and motions for his lady friend to get in. Tom enjoys checking out the reason for rudeness as he stares for quite some time, ultimately making the woman feel uncomfortable. He looks back at the other man, who is wearing a magnificent green blazer, and grins while admiring the jacket. “Sure, bub. Nice threads.” With that, the man orders his drinks and then makes his payment. He hands them to the lady and heads away from the bar, toward the bathroom, in succession. Tom is quick to follow. The dor closes behind the green-jacket man. Tom hesitates, slightly. “Hey! The guy from the bar right? Love that jacket!” Scrap rushes the man from behind before he knows it. The elbow that incapacitates the man in the green suit comes out of nowhere, it seems, and goes unheard. His head falls into the stall-door and rolls into the toilet like yesterday’s special. The redhead nudges himself into the stall even further, trying to conceal the incident. He throws on a fake, plastic-like smile that is sure to attract everyone’s unsuspecting eye. Kneeling down, he swipes the wallet out of his latest victim’s wallet. He pulls out an American Express, reading over the name. Thanks, Phil. Strolling briskly away from the restroom, Tom nuzzles his way back in next to the woman and his prior spot. He stands up, holding up his index finger and donning a slick new green blazer. She stares at it. He glares at her. She looks in his eyes but sees nothing. Tom looks away and grins. “DRINKS ARE ON ME, PEOPLE!” These days it’s all about power statements. ------------- Rip City Lodge Portland, Oregon 8:31 PM February 4th, 2009 “Here’s your key, sir. Room three-three-seven.” The desk tenant’s chirpy voice echoes over the clanging keys that are being put inside the jacket pocket of one Tommy O’Connor. The PRIME neophyte carries a black gym bag and dresses as if he’s been hired to take out the President. He blends in, too well. “And here is a message for you, Mr. O’Connor. It was received just before you arrived, sir.” He hands Tom a piece of paper. Tom, Please try and keep your head about you this week. Veljumin and I won’t be flying with you to Portland. We’ll be at the PRIME HQ running over the final paperwork. Remember why you’re here. Love, Ruby The blue eyes underneath stray strands of amber hair review the paper quickly. He crumples it up when he finishes and tosses it to the trash can. Strengthening the grip on his bag, he makes his way for his room. Walking through the lobby, Tom’s teeth begin to wrench and grind. She thinks I need a reminder? I know damn well why I’m here, Rube. You suckered my ass into this PRIME gig. Now I’ve got two meatheads to handle and she’s sending me reminders? Notes about my job? Get real, Tom. She’s just trying to play professional. Let her deal with notes, plans, and interviews. I’ll deal with the rest… Tom stands in front of the door reading ‘337.’ He slides his key in and the door clicks, opening up and revealing nothing as the lights are out. The doorway closes leaving the room completely black. The sound of scraping and rubbing is heard from the wallpaper. A click fills the room with light and shows PRIME’s latest addition just how much he’s working with. Tom grins, dropping his bag to the floor. He picks up a pamphlet sitting on the table. He looks it over and grins. Setting it down, Tom picks up the phone next to his bed. “Hi, I’m calling from three-three-seven. I need you to call me a taxi. Yes, I’ll hold.” He pauses and scans over the room some. His eyes lock on to the window and the outside lights of Portland at night. He picks his head up, taking note of the differences between here and home, wherever that was. His hand runs through his long red hair and he leans back. Looking at his shoes, he thinks back to what Ruby’s note said. He pulls out his lighter and waits for the other person on the phone “What for?” Scrap sits up, anger filling his head and blood rushing to his muscles. He smiles, abruptly. “I’ve got a statement to make.” Tom slowly drops the phone down to the receiver and hangs it up. His eyes intently study the scene of downtown Portland and a wry grin spreads over his face. He rubs his hands together. These days it’s all about power statements.
