Things Go South

6 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/6/06 Everyone remembers when things went sour. Titan St. James had been outside, doing what he called a “patrol of the perimeter.” Whether or not he was really patrolling anything was subject to your point of view. Scott didn't exactly care, nor did Van. Lacey believed it. Alyssa and Cathy, though, thought he was walking to have time to himself. It was actually the subject of discussion at the time. “No, I don't think so,” Cathy said, setting her coffee cup down on the solid kitchen table and handing the other plain, white mug to Alyssa. The matriarch of the Matthews clan reached into the refrigerator and removed a half gallon of milk. She tipped it up carefully, pouring a small amount to make her morning drink a rich brown instead of the deep black it had been. “I don't think it's so much time to think or be alone. I think it's something else.” Alyssa accepted hers and took a sip. Life on the road with Scott had made her eschew mixing things in in favor of the strong, dark flavor of a fresh pot. “What about mulling over things about the Cult?” Cathy put the milk back into the fridge and took a seat opposite her future daughter-in-law. “I think it goes further back than that.” “Israel?” “Yes,” she said, pushing her shoulder length brown hair over her shoulder. “It just seems to me that he's got some stuff from back then that he has to go off and deal with. I can't imagine what he had to deal with.” Alyssa shuddered in spite of the warm morning. “Living somewhere with the possibility of random explosions everyday....” she trailed off. “I can see why someone would have scars from that.” It was at this point that a bleary-eyed Scott shuffled into the kitchen, his bare feet thudding on the hardwood floor. He was dressed in a pair of mesh basketball shorts and not much else.... not all that different from his wrestling attire, really, just with shorter pants. He leaned down to kiss Alyssa on the cheek and picked up a coffee mug for himself. In contrast to Scott's slow morning start, Lacey and Van came bounding in behind him, laughing already. The two had a great friendship, making their time in seclusion far easier. Scott gave them a dour look, sitting down at the table with his oversized mug grasped in his fist. He took a sip and nodded. “Good and strong,” were the first words out of his mouth. Alyssa scooted her own mug over next to his and reseated herself on his thigh. “Your mother knows what she's doing, Scott,” she said, slipping an arm around his big neck. He just nodded in reply. Van and Lacey took the opportunity to pounce on the empty seats. Breakfast, they could smell cooking, and settled in to wait. Biscuits were in the oven, gravy on the stove and a bit of bacon sizzling in the clean, morning air. The comfortable morning in a place hidden away from the world was shattered when Titan burst through the kitchen door, his secure cell phone going back into its spot on his belt. “James Picot is dead,” he said simply, “and our position might be compromised.” * * * Scott swung the ax in his hands tirelessly. This wasn't an issue of needing firewood. Near the house, there were no large trees, but there were smaller trees and underbrush. Titan had put people to work... after breakfast. The man knew people had to be nourished to work, including himself. But now, Scott was clearing out every bit of cover within fifteen yards, or right up to the big treeline. Scott hacked smaller trees off with one swing, he pulled brush up by the roots and attacked larger trees with ferocity that was usually reserved for his in-ring exploits. But the way he moved was different than it had been a few weeks before. His arms didn't flail in trying to swing hard. They moved in precise arcs, bringing the edge of the blade across at precise, perpendicular angles. Titan gazed out at Scott, watching the man work. He was already moving differently, applying his strength differently. It was nowhere near as subtle a change as you might expect. Instead, it was very clear and Titan knew that as Scott became more accustomed to these methods, his strength could be applied even more effectively. Titan lowered his eyes back to his work. Holstered on his hip was the .357 Desert Eagle that had served him so well since he first joined the IDF. It was kept hot, with one round in the chamber and a full magazine. The sidearm was firmly fastened into the holster, a band of leather pulled tight across it and over the stud on the other side to keep it in place. On the folding table in front of him were a pair of rifles and a shotgun. One was small, a .22 caliber LR rifle, which he meant for either Cathy or Lacey to use should things get hairy. The other was large, a Winchester 308 semiautomatic, meant to take men down at range. Titan had owned both of them since he had come to live in the US and brought them to the cabin just in case they might have become compromised. The shotgun he was currently cleaning was a Mossberg 590A1 with cruiser grips, or a rear pistol grip and a loop on the pump. He had requisitioned this particular weapon just for this assignment. He had debated bringing buckshot or slugs for the weapon and opted to go for slugs. His training had always taught him to shoot for the high center of mass or the head, and with slugs there wouldn't be the random spread of shot as well as his accustomed nature to bullseye shooting with a solid round. That isn't to say that all the rounds he had were solid; the .22 was to be loaded with copper hollowpoint rounds, just about as sure to mess a target up as badly as anything else he had. The sound of shuffling on the roof didn't even raise his head. He had assigned Vangellus to the roof with a pair of binoculars to scan not only the road to the cabin, but the landscape all around. Van was actually fairly adept at moving softly when he wanted to, and managed to do well positioning and repositioning so that he could keep a sharp eye out. The hammer sound from inside the house also didn't concern him. Cathy had taken the news with a deep sigh and stated that she and Lacey would be boarding the windows. Titan admired this woman. She had an exterior that seemed rather soft, but it was wrapped around a core of solid iron. He knew that the windows were the weak point of the cabin. This particular place had been engineered to be a hostile safehouse, with a layer of steel inside the walls and doors. The basement would function effectively as a bunker and last holdout point. Titan took the shotgun and clipped the shoulder-strap on. He stood, slinging the weapon under his arm and looking around. At least here he knew that they would be coming to him. In Israel, you never knew where or who they were going to. * * *
