Ten Seconds. (ReVolution 158, Intense Match vs. Dusk for the Intense Title)

22 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 22nd, 2008 Omaha City, Nebraska "Guess PRIME ain’t too picky ‘bout their locations." The dark green ’95 Dodge Caravan inched along with the steady traffic, moving at rates just over turtle speed toward the small city of Omaha, Nebraska. "You’d think traffic’d be faster in a puny city like this…" grumbled Cliff, shifting his colossal frame in the driver’s seat, eyes squinted on his hairless face as the mid-day sun beamed through the windshield. "Small as it is, ain’t gon’ be any easier findin’ Ol’ Wade," mentioned Horace, peering out the passenger’s side window into the city, rubbing his prickly jawline. "He ain’t been stayin’ at any’ve the hotels the rest’ve the PRIME roster sleeps at. Been hidin’ somewheres else…" "Probably sleeps in his truck," piped Becky from the van’s bench seat in the back, shifting her beautiful body for optimal comfort, pulling a faded red John Deere baseball cap further over her eyes. "That’d make sense…" said Horace, reclining the passenger’s seat and resting his cowboy-footed feet on the dashboard and adjusting his tan cowboy hat. "…that’s how we found ‘im the first time…" * * * * * * * Somewhere Outside Jefferson City, Missouri August, 1992 "Horace, we are good an’ fucked." Smoke billowing from under the hood, drifting into the clear night sky, a small, silver Honda Civic puttered and bucked along the highway outside of Jefferson City, Missouri. The yellow lights of traffic whipped past the small car, offering their displeasure with a polite honk of their horns. "Just keep ‘er movin’, Cliff," said Horace from the passenger’s seat. "There’s a rest area up ahead, see if you can swing ‘er in." In nothing short of an automotive vehicle, Cliff rolled the car into a rest area drive, out of harm’s way on the interstate. With a last few gasps of life, the vehicle rolled into the rest area parking lot before backfiring and finally stalling into the mechanical after-life. "Now what?" asked Cliff, his huge body falling against his seat. "Now that," Horace replied, pointing past Cliff and down the parking lot. "Huh?" said a confused Cliff, turning his head. "What’re you tal…" Cliff stopped mid-sentence to see a red pickup truck far down the parking lot, just visible in the darkness. "You thinkin’ to steal it?" he asked. "Hell yeah!" said an enthusiastic Horace. "I can hot-wire the thing, easy as hell!" "I dunno, looks like a piece’ve shit." "Better’n what we’re drivin’ now. C’mon." The two swung their doors open and climbed out of the lifeless Civic, closing the doors quietly before marching toward the pickup truck. Horace made the universal "shut the fuck up" sign with an index finger to his lips, to which Cliff nodded. Approaching the car, Cliff ducked low (as low as a seven-footer can go) and scooted around to the truck’s driver’s side. Horace did the same, scuttling to the opposite end. The big man took a moment to peer around the area, making sure they were alone, before slowly rising upward and peering into the window. His eyes widened a bit as he noticed the window was rolled down, and they grew larger to see a man sleeping in the driver’s seat, a red John Deere hat covering his face. Cliff stood up, looking over to Horace. He tossed the "window’s down, there’s a guy in here, and he’s sleeping" signal by spinning his forearm in a window-rolling motion, pointing into the truck, putting his hands together and resting his head against them. Horace replied by grabbing his throat, the "choke the fucker out" signal. Cliff nodded, held his breath, and slowly reached his tree-trunk arm toward the sleeping man’s neck. In a flash, the man burst into life, grabbing Cliff’s wrist, and in an impressive display of strength wrenched the monster closer to the window before driving his right fist into his nose. "Ah, shit!" yelped Cliff, clutching his nose and reeling back. The man quickly swung the door open, slamming against Cliff’s body, toppling him to the ground. Diving out of the car came the startled young buck, quickly placing large, worn out boot against the big man’s neck. "Sonnova bitch!" hissed Horace from the other side of the truck, fumbling for the pistol sitting in the back of his jeans. Hearing his cursing, the man spun ninety degrees, revealing a large, silver, black handled .357 magnum revolver from the back of his dirty jeans, swiftly placing the bead between Horace’s eyes. Horace froze, his pistol pointed to the ground in an un-threatening manner. He smiled after a tension filled moment. "Hey there, feller, no need to…" "WHO THE FUCK’RE YOU!?" roared the man, interrupting Horace’s attempt to dispel the intense situation. He stood tall, his rugged frame tense with fright and anger, his blue eyes wide and wild, the tuft of brown hair on his chin jutting forward. "I ain’t nobody," said Horace calmly. "Just passin’ through…" "HORSE SHIT! YER FUCKIN’ BUDDY ‘ERE WAS LOOKIN’ TO TAKE THE AIR OUT’VE MY LUNGS!" A groan came from the fallen Cliff, who slowly tried to sit up. Without taking his eyes from Horace, the man kicked backward, clocking Cliff in the face and sending him back to his horizontal position. "Well, cain’t quite tell ya otherwise…" "GIMME ONE GOOD FUCKIN’ REASON NOT TO BLOW YER FUCKIN’ HEAD OFF!" Horace’s brain raced, but after a moment of calculating the options, (most of which resulting with, indeed, his fuckin’ head being blown off) a light bulb popped above his head. "We can help each other," he said with confidence. The man’s head tilted to the side. "’We can help each other,’" the man repeated. "Sorry, bub, but that aint’ fuckin’ good ‘nuff!" The young man pulled back the hammer, to which Horace threw up his palms in defense. "Whoa whoa whoa whoa whoa, hold yer horses, son!" he pleaded. "Just hear me out a moment!" "You’ve got ten fuckin’ seconds." "Where ya from?" "Alabama. Nine." "This yer truck?" "God-damn right. Eight." "Where ya live?" "Yer lookin’ at it. Seven." "How old are ya?" "Twenty. Six." "Jesus, just a pup! Got a job?" "Nope. Five." "Come run with us. We’ll improve upon yer sit’iation one-hundred percent." "Do fuckin’ tell. Four." "Lots to see." "Three." "Warm places to sleep." "Two." "Plenty’ve food an’ whiskey." "One." "An’ LOTS of fuckin’ money." He stopped counting. "Why should I believe you?" he asked, gun still fixed on Horace’s brain pan. "I can tell just by lookin’ at ya that you’ve been in the same tight spots as us," said Horace sincerely. "Yer a drifter, just like the two’ve us, just scrappin' along, just like us, an’ I know potential when I see it." The man remained firm a moment. Crickets chirped into the night along with roaring engines of cars on the interstate. He pushed the hammer forward, and lowered his gun. Horace exhaled heavily with relief. "Glad you saw the sens’ve things, son." "Don’t git too comfortable just yet. The moment I feel like yer fuckin’ me over is the same fuckin’ moment I put bullets in yer heads." "You ain’t lyin’!" laughed Horace. "Caught us with our thumbs up our asses out’ve a deep sleep!" Horace chuckled, the young man didn’t. He simply reached down to retrieve his red John Deere hat, setting it over his tousled brown hair. Horace extended a stout hand across the bed of the truck with a toothy, yellow grin. "Horace Wilks. That bear on the ground is Cliff." The man gripped Horace’s hand with a burly paw of his own, exchanging a rugged shake. "Wade."
