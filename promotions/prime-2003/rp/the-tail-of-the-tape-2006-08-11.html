The Tail of the Tape...

11 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/08/06 The Raymond James Stadium, Tampa Bay, Florida... 'What a sight' thinks Ignatius Lisieux, as he stares in wonderment at the gigantic pirate ship that veered outside of the arena, cutting across his view like the proverbial hot knife through butter. Raising his right hand up to rest below his chin in the most contemplative of manners, a small grin sneaks out over his lips as his mind flashed to thoughts of the last time he had been involved in PRIME's biggest event of the year... Colossus I - Main Event - PRIME Global Championship - Ignatius Lisieux (c) .vs. Tony Rolo Funnily enough, it seems a hundred years ago to him now. He had flown into New Orleans, Louisiana for the first ever Colossus with a swath of controversy flooding his every step. Rumours of his exit from the company were dogging his existence, his complicated friendship with Black Angel had ended and he and Adam were growing further apart with every passing moment. The 'Asian Invasion' stable was diminishing and Toshiaki Motoki was losing faith in his hand-picked protégé, a loss to Tony Rolo in the biggest match of PRIME's history would seal the deal. If Ignatius Lisieux lost at Colossus to Tony Rolo, his position in PRIME would become untenable - sound familiar? Unlike this time around, though, the 'MachiaVellian Protagonist' didn't have the majority of PRIMEates on his side, their support could not be underestimated, as he had found out in the 'Superdome', this time, he hoped, and thanks to them it would be a completely different story. A pained grimace swiftly replaces the half-smile as the French-Canadian takes a seat on the steps of the entrance to the stadium. Looking up at the night sky he sighs as he pulls a cigarette out of his pocket and rolls it between his fingers, the urge to go down that road again all-too-present in his current existence. When you have nothing else in the world, it’s easy to take the easy-way out – while temptation goads Lisieux he lays back a little - and then the memories continue to flash through his mind... Every blow that he had struck on Rolo had been greeted with jeering on a scale that he had never heard before, it was demoralising; it made him believe that the 'Specialist' could not be defeated. Every time the Illinoisan was in trouble, they had roared him into revival; every time Ignatius had looked to build an advantage they had shouted him down from the nosebleed seats to the front row and made him lose his focus. This time, though, he hoped that the opposite would be the case. Ego dictated that Lisieux did not believe Rolo could ever beat him in a wrestling ring; it was simply an impossibility in his own mind. But one thing that Tony had always had on his side was the PRIME fans - for the French-Canadian, they had always been the difference-maker. In wrestling, the good guy always tends to prevail... why? Look no further than the impact of thousands of rabid fans loudly yelling their support for their heroes with every last inch of their fibre and demoralising their opponents with nothing but pure loathing and hatred. Anybody who tells you that the PRIMEates don't make a difference does not speak the truth: at least not completely. Granted, there are those special men for whom the hatred acts as a fuel more so than a hindrance: Hoyt Williams beat Killean Sirrajin for the Universal Championship at Colossus II and he was perhaps as hated as Ignatius Lisieux had been the first time around... but maybe not for the same reasons. Hoyt knew that he functioned at his best with ravenous jeering bellowing into his ears and so he did his utmost to make it so. For Lisieux more than any, though, fan reaction has been both a huge burden and a huge help. While it was the fans who he could blame for his loss to Tony Rolo, he knew that they had seen him through against Ozric Mortimer and against Hoyt Williams: when the odds were stacked so heavily against him, he had relied on them to pull him through. Fast forward from 2004 to 2006 and here sits perhaps the most popular superstar on the entire PRIME roster. The 'Eternal Sunrise' flicks the cigarette down the steps and watches as it pings off the concrete and rolls to the paving below, he banishes the haunting memories of his loss to Tony Rolo from his mind and turns his attention towards the future: Colossus was around thirty-six hours away and he knew from here-on-in his thoughts could stretch no further than one woman: the 'Negasonic Lupine'... the 'K-Wolf'... Karina Wolfenden. The woman who had rode on the hype-wave into PRIME after the French-Canadian had paved the way for her ilk boiled his blood, quite simply. For all of his wrongs, for all of his regrets, he still regarded PRIME with a massive amount of respect and he still held dear the tradition of the company, he still recalled the men who had made it so. Alex Caine, Rock Startling, Tony Rolo, Ian English, Joey Troy, Black Angel, Adam... were all a huge part of PRIME while Wolfenden pranced around wherever the hell she was: Lisieux had never even heard of the bitch before she showed-up in his territory during his second run. Anyway, during that time, Karina was too pre-occupied with The HIT to pay any attention to the greatest wrestler in the history of her new company - and boy, with the benefit of hindsight, how Ignatius Lisieux preferred it that way. Culture Shock 2006 - Dual Halo III Tokyo, Japan had witnessed the first ever interaction between the two greats. In one of the iconic moments of the third Dual Halo contest, two times Ignatius Lisieux and Karina Wolfenden were given the opportunity to do battle, but two times they refused - be it out of mutual respect, self-preservation or whatever - and they even worked together on an occasion, as well. Oh how times change, huh? With only six competitors remaining, the 'K-Wolf' seized her chance... catching the French-Canadian off-guard - and taking advantage of his kindly perspective of her - she fired some sickening right-boots into his face and torso before planting him with her signature move and resigning him to his second sixth-place finish in two Culture Shock appearances: Ignatius Lisieux had refused to strike his female colleague... one particular instance where him taking the high road had done him no favours at all. His face reddens at the thought of that evening: to be so close to winning a Dual Halo contest only to be eliminated for having values is something that the 'MVP' doesn't ever expect to get over. Zipping-up his Atticus tracksuit jacket with a scowl he pushes down on his arms to get back to a vertical base and turns for one final glimpse of the Raymond James Stadium before putting his hands in his pockets and heading off back towards his motel room. Culture Shock 2006 had only been the beginning of a gradual decline in the fibre of Karina and Ignatius' relationship: not that there had ever been one anyway. The following weeks and months had seen tensions increase as the French-Canadian continued to resist the urge to attack the woman with the most PRIME wins of any superstar... until one night in July when she decided to expose Ignatius' intimate relationship with Johnny Noble's daughter on a live broadcast of ReVolution on the FX Network: that had been the initial breaking point. Karina Wolfenden had destroyed Ignatius' twenty-five year association with 'Mr. Incredible' within two minutes and the former double-champion has not seen Hayley since... Despite this, somehow, Wolfenden still found herself free of any physical retribution at the hands of Lisieux, who vowed to wait until Colossus to settle things the right way: inside of the squared-circle in a 'Hall of Fame Championship' match. For Iggy to snap, Kari realised more buttons had to be pressed... ReVolution 100 The centenary edition of PRIME's flagship show saw Karina doing battle with Tchu in a 'Colossus III' warm-up match, while Killean Sirrajin defended his ill-gotten Universal Championship against Lisieux... both of our protagonists were defeated... but that was not the major issue of the day, oh no... It was later revealed by Blaine Blair that the 'K-Wolf' had destroyed proposed contracts for the 'old guard' of the company to return: for one night only. In his mind, Karina could disparage him, she could destroy his friendships and relationships, all of that stuff was personal and he was able to rise above it, he would beat her at Colossus in retribution and rebuild those bridges later: but hurting the men who had built PRIME by depriving them of a night in the spotlight... depriving the PRIMEates of seeing their heroes one last time... that was the ultimate disrespect. Again, Ignatius Lisieux found himself confronted with a huge dilemma: stay true to his values by waiting until a sanctioned bout to get his retribution or go against everything he preached and go for her jugular... it was an almighty amount of self-control that eventually led the French-Canadian down the latter path. Karina Wolfenden again escaped unscathed, but she was not finished yet, not by a long shot... As Ignatius approaches the door to his motel room, the only audible sound is that of chirping crickets and the buzzing of the balcony light above his head... he looks up at the light with a snarl at its incessant grumbling and then puts the white keycard into the slot to release the lock on the door. The cool air-conditioned atmosphere of his habitat hits him instantly as he slumps backwards onto the double-bed with another sigh, tension overcoming him as Colossus encroaches rapidly. His attention is briefly caught by the slam of the door, but then his eyes switch to the golden 'HoF' title that rests on top of the television set before he recalls last week, when he finally broke... ReVolution 103 The 'K-Wolf' had tried everything - to no avail - it was time for her to go really low... Chet Worth's office was the scene and blackballing Ignatius Lisieux was the plan... it became the case that if Wolfenden was to prevail over the French-Canadian, then he would lose his spot on the PRIME roster. Much like at Colossus I, if Lisieux lost this time around, he was getting the boot from the company. That did it. In an explosion not seen since his early days, the 'MVP' lost it... and Adam found himself on the receiving end of much of his best friend's frustration. Karina Wolfenden had finally got what she wanted, but the Scotsman had prevented Ignatius from attacking her before the Colossus match, so things had not completely gone her way. She would still have to step through those ropes and face the greatest ever PRIME superstar one-and-one... and little did she know of the fire that she had ignited inside of the French-Canadian. For all that Kari had done to Lisieux; all it took was putting his PRIME position into jeopardy for her to get the required reaction out of him. The “K-Wolf” had finally found what meant more to him than anything else in the world. Colossus III This Sunday at Colossus III Ignatius Lisieux defends the unofficial 'Hall of Fame Championship' against Karina Wolfenden as part of a dual main event; if he loses the match he gets fired and If he wins the match he cements his status as the best ever in PRIME - it's as simple as that. Question is, do you really think he's going to lose with so much on the line? Chet told him to find the fire... and with a little help from Karina, found the fire he most certainly has… come Sunday, Karina Wolfenden is going to find out first-hand that actions speak a whole lot louder than words and that patience, the like as demonstrated by her opponent, most certainly, is a virtue.
