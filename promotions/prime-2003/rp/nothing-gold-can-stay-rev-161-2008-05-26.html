Date: May 26, 2008 This is not an identity crisis. For thirty-one years, I've had the same hair color, wore the same type of clothes while on the job, changed wardrobe styles away from the camera to fit with the times and very rarely compromised my principles for something other than what I believed in. Hell, I didn't even change my name when I got married. And, while that isn't so uncommon in today's 'buck the trend' society, I have always considered myself traditional. Individual. Unique. Many have tried to emulate what I do and what I say. All have failed, except for this one niggling irritant who thought my face, my clothes, my style would bring her the world. The world isn't big enough for two Lindsay Troys, doppelganger fake or no. I fear if I ever have children and they inherit some of my eccentric and nonpareil qualities. If I cannot have the Universal Title, than neither can she. If I cannot be myself, then Cozen cannot be me. If I am me, and Cozen is me, then I must reinvent myself. What was Original becomes New, what was Old will be Discarded, and nothing Gold can stay. This is not an identity crisis. PPS: Fucking (devil) Rays... ---- Monday, May 26, 2008; 10:30AM CDT Sheraton Chicago Hotel and Towers Chicago, Illinois In another life, she must have been a contortionist. With her long, long legs somehow tucked neatly on the smallish love seat, Lindsay Troy's thumbs flew effortlessly over the keys of her Blackberry Pearl, wisps of black cherry hair tickling her nose as they fell about her face. Her lower lip jutted out slightly and her lungs forced air out of her mouth to blow the strands away. When that effort failed, her fingers took a break from their task to help tuck the bothersome tresses behind her ear, then finished the curt message that was paused partway through. You're acting like my hair is the end-all, be-all, defining part of my person. "Will there be anything else, ma'am?" Lindsay looked over to the breakfast spread on the small, round table and gave a smile of approval to the staffer standing next to the dining card. "No, that's fine. Coffee's fresh though, right?" "Yes, ma'am." "And it's regular?" "Of course." She rose from her seat and slowly walked to meet him by the table, pulling folded bills from the pocket of her jeans. The money was slipped into the worker's palm, and Lindsay gave him the subtlest of winks. With a flushed face, the Sheraton's finest nodded, pushed the cart toward the door and took his leave. Once the door clicked shut, she set to making sure that the food was still steaming and the coffee piping hot. Not really much of a coffee drinker to being with, Troy only consumed the stuff when she needed to calm her nerves; ironic considering caffeine causes jitters. She poured herself a cup, watching the liquid splash inside the mug, and plucked three sugars from the tray beside the carafe. No sooner had she reached for the cream when a knock like a thunderclap echoed from behind her. One last check before making her way to the door and unlatching the sliding lock. "Well, you're a sight." "Ya ain't lookin' so peachy yerself." Lindsay smirked as Wade pushed the brim of his drifter's hat up with one hand, while the other held his own cup o' Joe. "Didn't expect you to actually take me up on breakfast. I figured you'd be halfway across the state by now." "That was the plan, 'til I opted t'sleep late." "Guess the old dog can be taught new tricks." She swung the door open wider. "Coming in?" Wade sauntered in and looked at Lindsay curiously as she shut the door behind him, then made a motion around his head with his free hand in reference to her new hair-do. "This is new." "The look on Coziekins face when she saw it made it worth every penny I paid, plus the tip for it being last-minute," she chuckled, something between proud and eerie. "You like?" "I ain't never been much fer hair dyin'...but yeah, it ain't a bad look." "Well, good. My sister hates it." "She does?" "Yeah, she gave me an earful in a voice mail and then gave me another one when I texted her instead of calling her back. Says my hair was my signature." She scoffed. "She doesn't understand the big picture. Anyway, I ask because I'd hate to think you're calling me names in your head about how I look like a punk ass cunt with fruity-colored hair." It was Wade's turn to chuckle as he followed her into the room, eyes still fixed on her hair. A sly smile formed on his lips as he watched her move to the table, then quickly wiped it away when she looked back at him. "I know you've got coffee, but this is fresh. And hot." "Yeah, I got this from downstairs 'fore I came up here. Wasn't very hot." He sat down at the table, took the second mug and poured his own coffee into it, then topped it off with what was in the pot. Lindsay took the metal covers off the plates and put them in a corner before plunking herself down at the table. "Food's not going to eat itself, you know." "Was waitin' fer you. Sometimes I remember that thing called manners." "Never thought I'd see the day." Wade snorted and the two dug into their food, conversation kept to a minimum, since it would have been impossible for Wade to get two words in in between shoveling eggs, home fries and bacon into his mouth. Lindsay merely sipped her coffee, taking small forkfuls here and there. Before long, his plate was clean and hers was two-thirds of the way there. "Ya ain't hungry?" Wade finally inquired. "I'll finish my stuff, don't worry," she stated, and leaned back in her seat. "Listen, Wade, there's something that I need to tell you." Wade looked at her blankly for a moment. Quickly, he retrieved his flask from his back pocket and started pouring his bourbon supply into his half-filled mug. "What're you doing?!" "Preparin'." "Oh, Christ..." Lindsay trailed off, her eyes lifting to the ceiling. She sighed. "Well, you'll probably need it." She leaned forward, her hazel eyes meeting his bright blues, and she had to look down at the table to compose herself. "I, um..." Eyes lifted again, then averted. "There's really no easy way to say this." "So just say it 'n be done with it. No sense beatin' 'round the bush." Lindsay forced herself to look at Wade dead-on, and when the words caught in her throat she made her vocal chords untangle them to force them out of her lips. "I was seeing Rayne. As in...pseudo-dating him. Secretly, without anyone knowing." Wade's eyes visibly stormed over, and his lips slowly curled inward. "Yer fuckin' kiddin' me." "No, Wade. I'm not." "Tell me yer FUCKIN' kiddin' me!" "You told me not to beat around the bush." Wade's head whipped around the room, like he's a mouse trapped in a cage. His attention turned quickly to his coffeebourbon, and he chugged the drink down. Lindsay rubbed her temples as he fumes. "How long," he said finally. "About a month and a half. It started right after I dropped the Uni title." "Jesus..." He stood up, removed his drifter's hat and rubbed his head. "Why? Why the FUCK would ya keep that from me?" "Because you can't stand him, that's why! And I didn't want anyone to know that I was getting involved with him. I didn't need another circus like I had when I was dating and then married to Melton. I just wanted things quiet for once." "And what'dya think would happen when I found out?!" he roared. "I wouldn't be so fuckin' piss an' vinegar if I were knowin' sooner! Shit, every part've my god damn conscience is tellin' me to grab my gun an' put a fuckin' hole in his god damn head!" "Oh stop being such a petulant child," Lindsay snapped. She rose to her feet and slammed her hands down on top of the table. Plates and silverware clattered and her empty coffee mug nearly fell to the floor. "You've kept plenty from me, like the time When Ninjas Attacked and you were all 'Oh, me and the cops don't see eye to eye', and the time when you said 'A blemish ain't nothin' on a record full've scars.'" "Don't play that fuckin' card! Anythin' I keep to myself is from my own past fuckin' transgressions. They were my problem then, they're my problem now, an' they ain't yer problem at all." "What the hell kind of logic is that? It'd make David Smith's head go asplodey because it's completely asinine." "You can't fuckin' know about it, Lindsay! Ya can't!" "Oh, can't I? Let me tell you something, hypocrite, you don't get to be all pissed off with me when you've pulled the same shit. I've never once asked you to expound on whatever it was you were alluding to and the only reason I'm even bothering to tell you about this now is because I need to get a somewhat clean slate for myself and start focusing on myself. Not on the baggage that I'm carrying around. Not on trying to fix everyone else's fucking problems that I continually let myself get dragged into. On me and on what I need to be doing. Wade takes a breath, visibly defeated, as the two share a odd silence. "Lindsay, I ain't got the cleanest of conscience. I used to be one fuckin' rotten cunt an' I used to run with even worse. But that ain't me no more, an' the last thing I need is you lookin' at me in a darker kinda light. I'm sure that further down the line you'll pull it outta me, but I can't let ya right now. I can't." "I don't care if you ever tell me. But I didn't tell you about this because I knew it would hurt you, even though Rayne was what I wanted." Another awkward silence. Wade sank back into his chair and sighed, heavily. "So, are ya done with 'im?" "Yeah. We're done." "Fer good?" "He hit you with his car. I kneed him in the balls. We're done, for good." He nodded, satisfied. "Ya can't be doin' that to me Lindz, I'm apt to run off an' blow someone in half." She laughed, half-heartedly. "An' honestly, what the hell'd ya see in that short fuckin' cunt anyways?" Lindsay sat back down as well, then reached for the coffee pot. She took her time pouring herself another cup, trying to push back the answer she didn't want to admit to anyone: from Sonny Silver to Vic Creed; from Matthias Warrington to Christian Sands; from Joey Melton to Tyler Rayne; personally and professionally, she had shitty, shitty taste in men. The coffee pot was placed back on the table, sugar reached for, cream placed close to hand and the lie created to replace the truth poured freely from her mouth. "Wade, I wish I knew..."
