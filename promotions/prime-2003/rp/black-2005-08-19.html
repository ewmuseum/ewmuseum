Black

19 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8.19.05 scene: The 2004 Maxima was hardly a Mercedes-Benz SLK but it was a rental, and got the job done. The blackness outside was streaked by the rare whites, reds, and yellows of other cars, but nothing more. The only thing Damien could see was headlight-lit road ahead of him, and the red-tinted world in his rear-view mirror. And he prefered it that way. The dark silence was meditative, and he was feeling very introspective. In fact, he had spent the days since walking into PRIME inside his own head. Questioning his motives, justifying to himself his qualifications, examining his place in the company. He spent a lot of time doing a lot of thinking. And none of it changed a damn thing. A week before, at Rev 72, he pissed himself in front of a sold-out crowd. Kano made an example of him, and dammit if he didn't fall right into line. He offered up minimal resistence, instead serving to validate Kano's return. This week, he made the trip all the way out to Fargo to find out that his second match was scrapped, and glossed over. His redemption would have to wait. Blackwolf was on his way to Turmoil; Collins and himself were in limbo. The video camera sat at his feet. He had avoided it since the night of his match, almost hoping that it would honestly fade into forgetfulness, but it didn't. I stared at him in the locker room, it called him on the plane ride home, it haunted him as he brushed his teeth before going to sleep. And when he was packing his bags for the next trip, he knew that he forget the camera in his gym bag, but somehow remembered to ask his driver to bring his extra pads which were tucked into the same gym bag. So, he relented. He decided to sacrifice to the unblinking eye, and put his frustration into words. With the camera sitting on the dashboard, he lit a rare cigarette, and opened up once more. D: I remember the last time we spoke, I was feeling like a virgin before the match. Now I feel like it was over too quick and ended with a transparent consolation remark and an empty promise to call me again some time. I feel like I was given a shot to earn my stripes, and I froze up. I blanked. I fell back on stupid habits that I thought I left back in Cielo. I found myself staring out at a crowd of people that had heard me, only hours before, talking about unrelenting aggression and brutal insight. And that moment of hesitation, that stasis of the spirit when I broke the enrance threshold keeps running through my head like a damn video loop. I stepped out of myself for that split second, and it was like the entire world shifted three inches to the left, and I never quite realigned. I felt heavy and sluggish. I felt clumsy and dim. I felt like I was walking through wet sand as I stepped down that walkway. And when I got into that ring and took a shot at Kano, I felt like I was trying something, anything to remind my muscles how it feels. But it never came together. So Kano, if you ever hear this, know that you gave me a hell of a wake-up call, but it's embarassing that I needed one.' scene: Damien Cruz tries to remember the last time he lost a match so easily. Sure, he had been double-teamed, blind-sided, beaten before he even stepped into the ring. He had been screwed, betrayed, and robbed. The Latin Assassin had taken beatings that literally threatened his career. He had, in every example he could think of, been handed the unjust defeat. But he could not remember the last time he was out-wrestled, and in over his head. Kano had given him a clean-cut beating, and there was not a damn thing he could say or do to change that. D: ' There is no way I will try to devalue your win by claiming that I held back. You saw my best. You saw what I brought to the table in that match. As far as you are concerned, you stared down the Latin Assassin, and I flinched. There is no amount of shit-talking or excuses I could pull out that will take that away from you. But I kick myself. I kick myself because I know what it takes to walk away on top and I let it slip between my fingers because I didn't have the balls to own it, to embody what I knew needed to be done. So congrats, John, and I hope that our paths cross again, and I give you the match you deserve.' scene: Damien tosses the cigarette from the window, and decides that a meal, even if it was at a roadside diner in the middle of Wisconsin, would do him good. He just needed to wait for the next exit. D: ' And I guess I missed the boat tonight. through some unknown force, my chance to step back into my own shoes was postponed. So the pep talk I gave myself before heading out to the Fargodome was pointless. And in retrospect, I question just how much of a difference it made. I literally stared at myself in the mirror like Dirk Diggler, trying to get it up. It was humiliating. And for what? Because I took a loss, and then had a chat with some burned-out hippie who watched the greatest minds of her generation destroyed by Reaganomics. And I knew that I had really sunk low when I envied her clarity. She put away three sticks while I was standing there, and who knows how many before I got there, and I envied her clarity. But I envied the fact that right or wrong, she knew where she stood. Damn, if only more of had that type of conviction. Hell, if I had conviction like hers, maybe I'd stop trying to validate my accomplishments by collecting praises from people who think I've done my people proud.' scene: seeig his exit, he habitually peeks over his shoulder, and readies himself for the Blue Plate heart attack he was about to receive. Wiscosin, the last time he checked, was not big on low-cal, low-carb, fiber-rich, heart-healthy, low-sodium, veggie-tofu burgers. But he'd take what they offered...as he usually did. He continued. D: ' ...Countless awards, accolades in every company I stepped in, respect from my peers, endless acclaim, and a wealth that hardly needs mentioning. What's there to do with all that promise when the thought of falling on my face paralyzes me? Who's there to offer me a congratulations when I've been used in a filler match, and lost? Or better yet, when the show doesn't even need the filler, and leaves it out altogether? How does one resist the urge to hang it up, fall back on their laurels, and retire with the satisfaction of true excellence among the mediocre?' scene: He tried to stay focused, by the questions came faster than he could articulate them. Damien Cruz, who had once wrestled for three hours with a torn cruciate ligament in his right knee could only speculate that there was something larger at hand. Damien Cruz, who had been wrapped in barbed-wire and suspended from the top of a steel cage in the Tokyo Arena, felt like there was more to his lifestyle than the cameras, the notiriety, and the money. And even though he kept the reality of the product close in mind, his sense of genuine pride was till nourished. The Latin Assassin, who had never tapped out in a match, but had blacked out and thereby forfeited, had the nagging desire to conquor every obstacle placed before him, but didn't know why. It had bitten him in the ass many times, but not as many times as it changed the face of the sport. So if there was an answer to be found, it was in those facts. The highway fell behind him, and he pulled into an almost empty parking lot of a greasy diner. With a sense of unfinished business, Damien pressed the STOP button, and stepped out of the car. Three steps toward the door, he could feel the pull from the camera calling him. But this time, he knew that he'd be back.
