Date: CVI And that’s when he swung, with an instinct he couldn’t shake… could never forget. His fist connected with jaw and the sound of bones cracking pierced his eardrums. When he pulled his hand away, it was trembling. Slowly, his vision began to clear, and when it did, he saw the familiar sight of the carnage caused by one well-placed blow. * * * * * * * * Monday, August 10th 2:53 PM The tiny pieces of crumbled asphalt sounded like slush under his feet.He'd woken that morning to a colorless sky and a rain that fell so steadily, it eventually didn't make a sound. Now, nearly eight hours later, it hadn't stopped, and as he crossed the parking lot, his shoes splashed in the random puddles that had pooled on the surface of the black top. Everywhere he looked, all around the complex, life was saturated beyond its limits, and when it could hold no more, the run-off spilled with little regard for where it was headed. He approached the apartment he was looking for, and his knuckles rapped across the door in a few quick knocks, each louder than the previous. Then he stood, and waited... waited for his sister to answer. But no answer came, and so he knocked again, harder this time, little droplets of water splashing from the door’s surface. A few seconds passed by, then a few more, till finally, he began to think of just heading back for the car, but before his mind had settled on a retreat, the door swung open. "Kaiser." "Bad time?" "No." Michelle stepped to the side, offering sanctuary in her apartment. "Sorry, I was just running a bath. Come in." "That’s ok." "Kaiser, it’s pouring." "I know. But this wont take long." He ran his hand down his face, trying to clear his water-logged vision. "I wanted to tell you that I’m sorry. I’m sorry that I wasn’t there for you. That I was too damn busy kickin’ myself in the ass." "Didn't need to do that." "I felt responsible for what Anth had done." "You weren’t." "Never was. Took me a while to figure that out. In the meantime, I wasn’t there for you when you were going through some tough times. I shoulda been there… talked to you, listened to you when you wanted to yell, given you a shoulder to cry on… or to punch." "Would have been nice." The words themselves had all the venom of a rattlesnake, but the soft smile on her face robbed them of any poison. "I just felt guilty, and I figured the best place for me to be was as far away from you as possible. But I was wrong, and I can’t apologize enough for that." "This is more than enough." "And I’m not doing this just to get my own head stra…" "I know." Michelle again stepped to the side, clearing the doorway. "C’mon in, Kaiser. Dry off. Have a cup of coffee or somethin’." "Next week. I promise. Right now, I have a plane to catch." "Off to New England?" "Yeah." Michelle stepped outside her appartment and wrapped her arms around his neck. He returned the gesture, squeezing her around the waist with his right arm. Two years. It’d been more than two years since he’d hugged his baby sister, and for a man who made his living on violence, he was surprised how much he’d missed the simple embrace of family. "Ok. I don’t want you to miss your flight. But I’m holdin’ you to that cup of coffee next week." "Good deal." Kaiser reached into the pocket of his jeans and struggled to pull free his car keys. "You flying straight through?" "No." Finally, the keys came loose, tearing free from some tangled threads. "I gotta long lay-over in Ohio." * * * * * * * * Monday, August 10th 9:14 PM "God damn." Kaiser climbed out of his rental car and rested an arm across its roof, staring at the large house in front of him. It looked like the sort of place a modest millionare would live in… the sort of home that belonged to a guy who had retired from his riches to dabble in an occupation that paid a lousy six figures. Closing the door to his car, Kaiser headed up the cobblestone driveway and toward the front door. From inside, he could hear the television, and armed with the knowledge that at least someone was home, he rang the doorbell. A moment later he was greeted by the welcoming smile of a beautiful woman. She was attractive in a simple way. The t-shirt and jeans she wore were the staple of a mother of two, not the supermodel wife he had always invisioned, though, he couldn’t quite be sure why he ever held such preconceptions. The doctor was a family man… and the woman in front of him seemed far more appropriate than any trophy wife. "Hello." "Hi. You must be Mary." He extended his hand. "I’m one of Dr. Ward’s patients…" "Kaiser." "Yeah." He smiled at the casual way in which she said his name, as if he stopped by every Sunday for brunch. "Thought the doc wasn’t supposed to talk about his patients." "He doesn’t. But pro wrestlers he’s mentoring… can’t get him to shuddup sometimes." Mary gave a small laugh, allowing Kaiser to ease into a chuckle of his own. "Please, come in." He stepped into their home and looked around, giving himself a quick self-tour. In the living room, he could see the doctor sitting on th couch, bouncing a young one on his knee, making all sorts of ridiculous noises to entertain the little girl. "She’s cute." "She’s a handful." Mary headed towards the human rollercoaster in the living room. "Sweetie, you’ve got company. Kaiser stood flat-footed in his spot, waited for Dr. Ward to look over towards the front door. "Kaiser." Matt steadied his leg and looked the little one in the eyes. "Ok, angel… Daddy’s old legs are done for the night. And it’s time for you to head to bed." "One more time!" "Not tonight, Georgie. Daddy has a visitor and it's past your bedtime." He kissed his daughter on the forehead and handed her off to her mother. "G’night, angel." "C’mon, kiddo." Mary hoisted Georgie high into the air and spun her around before heading off down the hall toward’s the child’s bedroom. With the living room emptied, Matt pushed himself off of the couch and took a step towards his protégé. "Surprised to see you hear. Thought you’d be on your way to Boston by now." "Flying out tomorrow." "What brings you on the detour to Ohio?" "I was hoping we could talk about a couple things." "Alright." Matt turned and looked over his shoulder, nodding towards the couch. "Have a seat. You want anything to drink? Got a couple of pretty good beers. Some Mountain Dew..." "No, thanks. I'm fine." "So, I take it whatever you wanna talk about must be a pretty big deal for you to show up at my front door at nine o'clock. Especially coming from Mississippi." Matt headed into the kitchen, his words growing fainter as he reached into the fridge and pulled out a Pacifico. "I talked to Michelle earlier today." "How'd that go?" "Good... real good." "You finally realized you weren't responsible for what your friend did?" "Pretty much." "And that's what she wanted to hear?" Matt returned to the living room and let himself collapse into the recliner adjacent to the couch. "Little ones wear me out." "I would imagine." Kaiser thought briefly of his looming fatherhood, but wiped the thought from his mind. It wasn't the subject for the evening. "Ya know, I think Michelle just wanted me to stop being a ghost. My family was always close growin' up, so having her big brother be so distant wasn't something she was used to. But I mean... yeah, it went real well." "I'm glad to hear it. I think that was one of the last steps towards getting back on your feet." "You think so?" "I do." "Good." Kaiser bit at his bottom lip, hungry... wanting to strike. But he waited, biding his time. "Your wife seems great." "Mary... has to be to put up with me." "You ever think about going back?" "What?" "To wrestling. You ever think about getting back in?" "No." Matt took a drink from his beer, eyes locked cautiously on Kaiser. "You ever think about getting out before all the years of wear and tear do their damage?" "No." "I think you might be lying." "I think you might be too." "What are you doing here, Kaiser?" Matt leaned foward, resting his elbows on his knees, rolling the beer bottle between his palms. "Your wife says you talk a lot about mentoring me as a pro wrestler. I just thought maybe you were trying to relive some of that glory." "Not hardly." "So I gotta ask then..." Kaiser rubbed his chin as he began to bounce his foot against the floor. "what's the deal?" "The deal?" "With Snow." Matt reached over and sat his beer down on a small table to the right of the recliner. The bottle shook a bit in his grip, nearly kicking the coaster out from underneath. "What are you asking me?" "With Colossus right around the corner, you took Jason Snow on as a patient." "The hell... how..." "Jesus, man... I saw it in the fucking book. Who cares. I wanna know why? Mentoring the 5 Star Champ not good enough for ya?" "Kaiser, I don't know how exactly you came up with all this, and God only knows why I'm not gonna throw you outta my house right now, but who I choose to take on as patients has nothing to do with you. Whether or not Jason has been in for a session... it has no bearing on what you and I have been doing." "First of all, doc... drop the 'if this' and 'if that' bullshit. I know for a fact he's been in to see you at least once. And how can you sit there with a straight face and say that it has no impact on what we're doing?" Kaiser's teeth released the bottom lip he'd been gnawing at, and now he pounced, straight at the throat. "This guy is the enemy. You agreeing to even listen to one word he has to say is a knife in my fuckin' back." "I've got a job to do, Kaiser. I'm not going to refuse to see a patient solely because they're a professional wrestler you might happen to have a problem with. I don't care if every last patient of mine is a wrestler... that's none of your concern. You don't get all my time. You aren't my pet project." "I was supposed to be your protege. I was supposed to be the guy who carried on the tradtion of the Inhuman Being." "You didn't even give a damn what I had to say half the time. I'd tell you "Do this" and you'd do that. But now you want to act like the star pupil... make it seem like I ripped the legs out from under you." Matt jumped out of the recliner, shaking his head, almost laughing at the thought. "You remember when I told you to play it slow, to cut back on the ego? The next week you went and called out Lindsay Troy. A Hall of Famer for Christ's sake. And a good friend of mine. But now you want to act like the star pupil... make it seem like I ripped the legs out from under you. Gimme a break, Kaiser." "That's exactly what you did." Kaiser rose to his feet, narrowing his eyes, tunnel vision blacking out the surroundings. At his right side, he clenched his hand into a fist, squeezing so hard the knuckles went white. "I was weeks away from the biggest match in history, and you had to stick yourself right in the middle of it all. You just lusted to be back in that Colossus main event. Any way it took." "You really believe the crap you're tellin' me right now?" Matt nearly spit as he asked the question. "I hope your wife knows you're cheating on her. Still sleepin with that whore you call PRIME." And that’s when he swung, with an instinct he couldn’t shake… could never forget. His fist connected with jaw and the sound of bones cracking pierced his eardrums. When he pulled his hand away, it was trembling. Slowly, his vision began to clear, and when it did, he saw the familiar sight of the carnage caused by one well-placed blow. It was the rush that caused him to swing a second time, chemicals flooding his brain where there should have been a thought process, little droplets of blood splashing from torn flesh. Not until he felt the pain shoot through his fist did he even realize he’d thrown another punch. Kaiser fell to the floor, hand cupped over his bloody mouth, already searching for the teeth he was sure were missing. "Matthew!" Her voice jolted his conscious and the rush dissipated, vanishing almost instantly. "Matthew... what the hell is going on?" Mary stood where the hallway met the living room, looking on with disbelief. "What's going on?" Matt didn't answer, didn't know how. It was Kaiser who spoke first, crawling up off the carpet, removing his hand from his face, and sucking back the bit of blood that seeped from his mouth. "Matt was just showing me how to throw a solid right hook." He wiped the back of his hand across his bottom lip and headed straight for the front door. "That's good stuff, Doc." "Are you kidding me?" Mary's eyes darted back and forth, first to Kaiser, then to her husband, who still stood frozen in the middle of the living room. "I should be going." "Yeah." She kept her gaze locked on Matthew while she spoke. "I think you probably should." Turning the knob, Kaiser stepped out into the muggy August night. He closed the door behind him, leaving Matt and Mary to have a talk he didn't care to imagine. Kaiser had assumed, after all this time, that Matt was at his limits. He'd just needed to pour on a little more... see where the run-off would take him. For the first time in the eighteen months they'd worked together, he'd seen the Inhuman Being. The proof was in that large, beautiful home, right there on the stained hands of a husband and a father. And a fucking liar.
