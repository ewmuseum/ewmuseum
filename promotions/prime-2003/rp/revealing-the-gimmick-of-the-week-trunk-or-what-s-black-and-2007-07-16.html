Revealing the Gimmick-of-the-Week Trunk; Or: What's Black and White and Ugly All Over?

16 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/16/07 SOUND AND THE FURY Hi, I’m a Private Dick…no, not Adam’s brother When we last left our dynamic duo, they were in Alabama trying to recruit their long list of supporting characters so they could feel comfortable rejoining the world of wrestling. They stopped in Alabama to employ the services of Tourettes-syndrome-inflicted slut Nate POTCROTCHDIRTMONKEY (that may or may not be her real last name) before their first bout against theUPstarts. Many things have changed since then. For example, last week our friends were superheroes, fighting to rid the world of bad guys, villains, and Starburst that was so chewy it locked your jaw up. Unfortunately, all of that stuff can continue to live in the world, because this week Sound and the Fury are… Well, you tell me. I don’t know what they are, I haven’t even faded up yet, give me a little time. Jeez. Fade up. We fade up to see Ian and Gildenstern sitting in a small room that, considering the bunk beds, they probably sleep in. Ian is playing a video game while Gildenstern walks around nervously. “When are we leaving for the next supporting character, Ian?” Gil asks impatiently, tapping his foot on the ground (as he walks, too. He’s talented like that). “As soon as I’m in a good spot to save.” Ian sticks out his tongue to help his body aerodynamically adjust to the angle he is moving his character in the video game. It’s an exact science, of course. “Come on, this is taking forever,” Gil complains, sitting on the bottom bunk bed and sighing. “It’s not like we have all day.” Ian continues to ignore his foster brother, moving his body this way and that in accordance with the game. “Lighten up, we’re in no rush.” He is interrupted by the sound of a clearing throat behind the camera. Yes, this is being filmed. Ian and Gil look up to the camera. “Actually, Gil is right. We don’t have all day,” says the man behind the camera, who using the wonderful power of the English language will be referred to as the cameraman from here on out. (the cameraman isn’t using the English language, though…argh, I hate misplaced modifiers!) “Why am I here, anyway?” Ian and Gil look at each other strangely. “Uh…so we can cut a promo?” The cameraman, although absent from our view, is probably looking confused at this point. “A what-o?” Ian pauses the game, staring at the camera with great intent and confusion (he can do both, because he’s a little bit cross-eyed). “A promo.” “What the fuck is that?” asks our scholarly cameraman. Gildenstern laughs almost as if he isn’t believing this conversation is happening, then tries his hand at it. “Where you sit there and shoot a film of us saying how we’re awesome, explaining how we’re going to kick our opponent’s ass, that sort of thing.” The camera actually moves a little in accordance with the cameraman’s dawning of reality. “Ah, a proooooomo. What are you, like 80? Nobody does promos anymore.” Ian and Gil stare at each other again, and Ian pouts the tiniest bit. Ian is a big fan of telling his opponents that they suck, so this is like taking away his favorite doll. This is even more disheartening because Gildenstern actually did take his favorite doll earlier in the day. “Can I at least tell Easton Hall and Titan St. James that they smell bad and we had sex with their mothers last night?” “I really wish you wouldn’t,” answers the cameraman. After a second of pause, Ian nods claps his hands. “Fine. Then since we have you here, even if we can’t say that Easton Hall and Titan St. James suck balls and how we’re going to totally win and all that...” “Even though he just did,” Gil interjects. “We’re going to reveal the secret of Sound and Fury’s success.” Ian digs under the bed and pulls out an ordinary looking brown chest. Putting his foot on it, he thrusts his arm in the air, proclaiming, “It’s time the truth be told!” His raised hand suddenly falls and he falls along with it. Recovering and standing back up, Ian looks at Gil. “How do we start?” Gil shrugs. “How about a little background?” Nodding, Ian sits down and looks at the camera, then points at the chest. “We made a trade for this chest with a hippie back in the 60’s for some mushrooms and a lava lamp…” Gil shakes his head. “Ian?” “Yeah?” “You weren’t alive in the 60’s.” “Right, right.” Ian nods, then gestures to the chest again. “We bought this chest – locked – from a thrift store in Alabama. It was sold to us by a crazy Iranian man named Ahmed, and we bought it because of the beautiful design on the side of the box.” The camera zooms in on the box to see no such design of which they mention. Gildenstern continues, “Ahmed told us that the chest ‘fell off of a circus truck.’” Picking up the reins of the noble laureate (that’s storyteller for people who don’t know whatever language that word originates from), Ian shrugs. “We busted open the lock and peered in on the treasure that was to befall on us. It was a scary moment, as we weren’t sure if it had a curse, or maybe we had opened Pandora’s Box, or even if there would be icky spiders in it.” Ian and Gil shudder vehemently. “Upon discovering it was a costume trunk,” says Gildenstern, “we used it for Halloween and the like.” “Until we came up with the brilliant idea of Gimmick of the Week!” says Ian, smiling. Gildenstern throws his arms in the air, shouting, “Now everyday is Halloween!” Gildenstern and Ian begin running in circles, laughing and shouting. This process lasts for about five minutes. Maybe less. It feels like it lasted forever. Eventually the cameraman clears his throat again, bringing Ian and Gil back to the real world. “Guys?” I remember when keeping them in line was my job. Damn this cramping novel style! Back in the day it was all about the script style, and man were those the days! We didn’t have to worry about connecting dialogue to things like “Ian says mysteriously” or “Gil interjects voraciously.” We just said the action, and they said the words, and the result was a snappy promo that would go fast and make everyone wet themselves in awe. Seriously, wet themselves! One time one of my friend’s brothers asked me, “What do you do for a living?” and I said, “I narrate a tag team,” and he was like, “Can I watch your promos?” and I was like, “Sure.” So we fired up the ol’ VCR and played one of the tapes (see, DVDs weren’t as big back then, they existed, but I hadn’t made enough money to get a player, because narrating a team, even tag team champions, isn’t where the money is) and he just watched it and WHOOSH, right out of his pants… Oh, I was supposed to be narrating, wasn’t I? Sorry. Gil mutters “You’re not the boss of us anymore, God,” as Ian looks to the cameraman with eyebrows raised. “Yes?” “Do I need to be here anymore, guys?” asks the cameraman politely. “I really have some place I need to be.” Here the cameraman wisely omits that the place he needs to be is an all-man bathhouse. “Hey,” he shouts, as the camera moves around, simulating him looking around in wild fear, “how did you –” I’m a narrator. It’s my job. The cameraman contends, “Whatever, I have to get out of here.” “Wait,” Ian says, “don’t you want to see what gimmick we’re going to be this week?” Sighing, the cameraman remains focused on Ian and the trunk. Ian nods to Gildenstern, who grabs the lid of the trunk and opens it, causing a long creaking noise. Gil looks away while he reaches into the trunk, sticking his tongue out, much like Ian did, to improve his chances of picking a good gimmick. Again, it’s an exact science. Lifting his hand out of the crate, the feed suddenly becomes black and white as Gil brings out a pad of paper and a Dick Tracy-style hat. Ian and Gil look at each other strangely, and then Ian nods, then starts speaking at an incredible speed: “Irememberthefirsttimeshecameintomyoffice…shewantedmetohelpherfindhermissinghusband…butithoughttomyselfasitookhercase…iwasgoingtohelpherforgethim…BYHAVINGTONSOFFACESEX!” Gil shrugs, saying, “They didn’t talk that fast, I’m sure.” Nodding, Ian tries to alter his speed. “Is-this-better?” “A little,” Gil answers before pausing, then asking, “Wait…FACE sex? What the fuck is face sex?” Cut. Ian and Gil are walking down a street, looking down intently at it. Ian is staring through a big magnifying glass and Gil is following him with a bigger flashlight. “It should be right around here,” says Ian in a slowed-down, yet still fast – and ominous – voice. Gil grunts in agreement. Sighing, Ian continues. “Day three of the rugged search, and no signs so far. It seemed as if there was nothing that will lead us to what we were looking for. It hadn’t been too long on the search, but the Sound and Fury detective agency was ready to close the books on this particular case – it was as if what we were looking for never existed at all.” Gil looks confused. “Wait…aren’t we looking for our supporting characters?” Ian shakes his head. “No, I employed a side project but it looks like we’re going to have cut our losses. We won’t ever find it.” “Well what were we looking for?” “Tom Walczak’s personality.” Gildenstern shuts off the flashlight. “Man, that was a losing battle to begin with.” Cut. We join our private investigator friends (for that’s what they are, if I didn’t explain it well enough) in black and white as they try to find the next side character. A low jazz line is playing in the background as the two slink down the dark streets. Ian is wearing a trench coat and the hat tucked over his eyes. Gildenstern is in nearly the same dress, but does not have the hat on. “Day five of our search for our supporting characters, and the trail was getting cold,” Ian says as a voice-over – except for the fact he’s actually speaking. “We had interviewed all the witnesses many times, checked and re-checked every lead until we were blue in the face.” Gil interjects, “Although in this tone it would show up as a dark gray.” Ian continues: “We were just about to give up when we saw a man holding a sign that called us to the scene: apparently, the sign says…” He pauses, then drops the act as we go back to color for a moment. “What?” Ian and Gil shuffle over to the sign and read it aloud: “I NEED HELP FROM PRIVATE INVESTIGATORS TO FIND AND MURDER SOUND AND FURY IN A MOST DEVILISH AND UNDERHANDED MANNER FOR THE TERRIBLE AND UNBEKNOWNST TREASONS THEY HAVE COMMITTED AGAINST MY PERSON.” Gil blinks, then says, “You write TINY.” Ian looks up at the big man, who is holding the sign, and arches his eyebrows. “Looking for a private dick, are you?” he asks as the black and white returns. “Maybe we can help you out. Why do you want to murder your targets? I hear that they’re okay guys.” “And great dancers!” Gil adds. “That may be,” says the big man, “but they also stole my idea for wrestling!” “What do you mean?” “Well, PRIME hired me to be in their development camp, and when I finally got on TV I was going to do a gimmick where every week I was going to come out as something different.” Ian makes a face. “Something different? Every week?” Gil shakes his head. “That’s retarded.” The man holding the sign gets angry. “I THOUGHT IT WAS A GREAT IDEA! But then Sound and the Fury got hired straight to TV, no development deal or anything! They stole my gimmick, and I got fired because of it! And I’m going to murder them in a most devilish and underhanded manner for the terrible and unbeknownst treasons they have committed against my person.” Ian furrows his brow. “You just told us you knew what they did to you. How can they be unbeknownst?” “Is that what that means?” the man asks, matching Ian’s confusion. “I thought it meant…uh…bad.” Nodding, Gildenstern puts his hand on the big man’s shoulders. “We’ll be glad to help you out, young man…for a fee. Now what’s your name?” “Bobby “The Dilapidated Asshat” Ottilio.” Ian puts his hand on his chin. “Dilapidated Asshat, you say?” The Asshat nods. Gil chuckles. “Are you sure you were fired because of Sound and Fury? Sure it wasn’t something to do with the name?” Ian elbows Gil and says, “What he means to say is…uh…what info do you have on this group of young, well-dressed, good-looking gentlemen?” “Well,” Bobby starts, putting the sign on the side so he can adequately talk with his hands, “they are getting ready to go around the country looking for their supporting characters. They are dressed as private investigators this week, and for some reason black and white color follows them everywhere they go, kind of like now…and one of them kind of looks like you,” he says, pointing to Gil, “and the other looks a little like you, and…” Dawning of realization in five… Four… Three… Tw… “HEY!” Bobby shouts. Looks like he’s quicker than I thought. “YOU GOT ME FIRED!” the big man yells, grabbing for Ian and Gil as they run away in fear. “Do you think this counts as a supporting character?” Gil asks Ian as they run. Ian shrugs while running (amazing he doesn’t fall down), saying, “It’s better than nothing, I guess.” Fade.
