Date: 25th March "All the old knives that have rusted in my back, I drive in yours." - Phaedrus. I "At this point in time, Bobby, I couldn't care less if it's in front of nothing more than five men and a dog," Aidan Chase said into his mobile phone as the elevator came to a dithering halt. "As long as there's four posts and a set of ropes, I'll do it." Slipping between a set of sliding doors that were in dire need of lubrication, Aidan struggled to keep the cell wedged between his shoulder and cheek from slipping to the floor. Carefully he walked through the modest hallway, dawdling passed the uniform brown doors that punctuated the peeling cream wallpaper. Concerned only with the telephone conversation and keeping the bag of groceries in his arms upright, Aidan didn't even notice as one of his neighbours breezed by offering him a passing "hi". As the distorted garble of the middle-aged wrestling promoter spewed from his antiquated phone, Aidan finally reached the end of the corridor. He gently knelt and placed the groceries on the rotting blue carpet. "Yeap, yeap. Long as it pays more than a couple hours at the store," he said, fumbling around in his pocket for keys. Suddenly his eyes widened as a forthwith mask of surprise landed on his youthful features. "Twenty-five?! Come on, Bob. I could earn more than that down at the mar-" Bobby's midwestern drawl interjected before he could utter another word. Pulling a bunch of keys from his navy blue slacks, Aidan sighed. "Alright. Well, if that's the best you can do, I'll take it. Bell time's seven? I'll be there for five, then." Slipping the key into the lock and twisting the knob, Aidan picked up the brown grocery bag and stepped clumsily into his low-grade apartment without looking where he was going. "Okay Bob, sounds fine. Sunday it is. Bye." Finally he snapped what he'd often thought to be the world's first flip phone (such was it's lack of functionality and aesthetic flair) closed and placed it on the worktop. With a tired wheeze the muscle-bound twenty-something set down the groceries and pulled the Walmart vest from his shoulders. Two days into the job and already the clean-shaven New Yorker was sick of it. Life on the North American indy scene meant he was used to long, unforgiving hours on the grindstore but nothing could've prepared him for the monotony of shuffling convoys of shopping trolleys around a parking lot for ten hours at a time. Still, with the abrupt death of his Dual Halo dream he needed to find another way to put food on the table, and the commercial giant seemed like the most readily-available option. Neglecting perishables for a moment, Aidan wandered around the worktop and pulled open the refrigerator. After grabbing a half-clean glass from beside the sink and a carton of Walmart's cheapest orange juice from the fridge he closed the door and took a look around his modest abode. The studio apartment wasn't much but it was just about the only place he could afford on his meagre paycheck that didn't involve sharing living space with at least five varieties of vermin. The living area – combining kitchenette, dining room and lounge in one very small space – provided no more space than you'd need to swing a cat, and the little floorspace that there was was taken up by a small, two-person dining table, an ancient couch and a television cabinet. The harsh acidity of the budget orange stung Aidan's tonsils as he cocked his head back and swallowed. He closed his eyes and leaned back against the fridge, allowing the cool citrus to dribble down his oesophagus. When his vision returned he was startled by the sudden presence of familiar figure across the room. "Jesus, Manda..." he said, pausing to catch his breath. "Scared the life ou-" "You're home early," she interrupted, her voice shaky and unsure. "I thought took an early lunch break," Aidan stated as he slowly padded across to the doorway in which is girlfriend stood. "Thought it would be a nice surpr-" his train of thought derailed when he caught glimpse of the stuffed suitcase sitting at his slender partner's feet. Curiously he looked upwards; Amanda Lee's face trembling with an apologetic look as her beau gave her the eye. "What's with the case?" Aidan asked. "I-..." she began, clenching tightly to the strap of the rucksack that was slung over one of her shoulders. "Aidan, I'm leaving." "You're what?" he blurted unintentionally as thoughts began to race passed his inner cortex at a thousand miles per hour. "It's for the best," said Amanda. Her pitch was wavering but she spoke very matter-of-factly. "Wait, no! Wait do you mean it's 'for the best'?!" he pleaded. Aidan grabbed Amanda by the shoulders, but the skinny redhead looked up and brushed his weak grip aside. "Amanda. This doesn't make any sense..." Without a word she reached into the pocket of her pink knitted cardigan and produced a folded A5 envelope. Subduing a sniffle, Amanda handed the already open document to the would-be professional wrestling. "Take a look." The first thing Aidan noticed was the familiar red and blue Bank of America header at the top of the page; the second was the printed balance towards the bottom that sprung from the sheet like a jack in the box. $12,697.29 Credit. "I can't go on living like this, Aidan." "Honey, come on. It's just money," he began his defence. "You know that I'll be able to pay this off as soon as the appearance fees start to roll back in!" "Are they going to 'roll back in' though? For Pete's sake, you're working in Wal-Mart!" her face grew red, her voice impassioned. "You'll never pay anything off on minimum wage, and we're going to supplement that with a $30 fee from some piss-pot wrestling show?" Eyes-arching, tear-ducts threatening to burst, Amanda reached down and pulled up the suitcase. "No, Aidan. I'm sorry. After your little PRIME fib, you've lost my faith." Amanda tried to get on her way but her step forward was blocked by Aidan. "How many times do I have to tell you, dammit?!" he barked sounding a lot harsher than he'd intended. "PRIME wasn't a lie! The offered me a contract!" he enthused, as if it would encourage her to reverse her decision and unpack her bags. "Oh yeah? Then why didn't you take it?!" Chase searched for words within the deepest recesses of his mind but he was without response. The question hung in the air as his facial muscles sagged and Amanda's tightened anger. She shook her head slowly. "Even now you don't have it in you to admit it. Goodbye, Aidan." Raising one of her dainty hands to cover the tears swelling in her eyes, Amanda strode out of the apartment without thinking of looking back for a second. Aidan, broken, dashed to the open door and called out, but by the elevator doors had slammed shut before his scream had reverberated to the end of the hallway. II He stood in the kitchen, Jason; forcing one final corner of toast into his mouth and washing it down with lukewarm filter coffee. Setting the crockery aside and pulling a black tee over his tattooed parchment of a torso, the PRIME grappler walked away from the kitchen towards the blaring LCD television just across the room. With a quick flick of the remote and little regard for the electricity bill Jason turned the screen onto standby when a feminine hiss beckoned him from the bedroom door. "Where are you going, tough guy?" Clothed only in under-starched white bedsheets, Jason's previous night's pleasure slinked across the room and wrapped her long arms around him. Almost serpentine in her movement, the raven-haired woman with hazel eyes so deep they were almost black leaned towards the rugged wrestler, whispering in his ear. "I think we have some unfinished business..." She paused, looking for some kind of response from The Anti-Superstar but Jason's stance remained as rigid as his facial expression. Pulling away, she saw the deep-rooted scowl etched across his chiselled face and felt her desire slowly begin to trickle down the drain. Raising his rough, calloused mitts, Jason grabbed her by the wrists and pushed her away. "Only business you've got's gettin' yer ass outta here b'fore I come back." Stepping around the abashed vixen, Jason pulled his scuffed leather jacket from the couch and pulled it on. "You know where the door is." He left, heavy Dr. Marten's thumping hard on the wooden floor with early weighty footstep. Aghast, she watched him pull open the front door and threw a barrage of obscenities at him, none of which Jason paid any attention to. Closing the door behind him, Jason told himself that if the bitch did trash the place while he was gone he would at least have enough in his next paycheck to replace the few valuable items that he possessed. He walked through the tenement hallway towards the stairwell. The grimy Bronx building didn't seem like the most obvious choice of living space for a man employed by the biggest pro-wrestling franchise in North America and Jason could easily have afforded something a little more up-market by now. Then again, it had never been his goal to join the ranks of the preening playboy piss-stains that he called colleagues, and shacking up in some mansion in The Hamptons would only take him one more step toward become Chandler Tsonda or Jason Snow than he was comfortable with. Eschewing the unreliable elevator in favour of the stairs, Jason began the slow fifteen-floor descent. The place was bland, uninspiring, sure; but at least nobody had ever bothered him there. Nobody ever expects a PRIME "superstar" to be holed-up in a building that was barely an improvement on a squat, and that suited Jason to a tee. Apparently, making a surprise comeback at one of the biggest pay-per-views and eliminating two of the biggest names in pro-wrestling history gives the press a massive fucking hard-on, and the last thing that The Anti-Superstar wanted was paparazzi turning up on his doorstep. Huffing and puffing his way down to the underground parking garage, Jason could not decide for love nor money if his life had taken a turn for better or worse. His Dual Halo showing was surprising to say the least. Entering the match full of piss and vinegar and with a point to prove to almost anybody possessing the ability of free-thought, he punched and kicked his way through the biggest match that the sport has ever seen with a newfound vigour. For a man with a record as hit-and-miss as Jason's, and eighth place Dual Halo finish was one hell of an accomplishment. Perhaps more shocking was that he managed to pin Lindsay Troy and rival Jay Phoenix along the way, two competitor's who's achievements Jason Natas could never realistcally hope to match. For so long Jason had revelled in his perpetual underdog status; even after toppling such foes as Dusk and Troy Douglas, he was never though of as more than an "also ran" in PRIME when compared with the likes of Bryan Dawkins, who had entered the company at the same time as him yet had left Jason in the dust when it came to rising up the card. Things were beginning to take an upswing though, he could feel it. If he could follow pinfalls on Troy and Phoenix with victory over Dawkins and Tsonda at Revolution then he would surely become a genuine threat to the upper echelons of the PRIME talent roster. He never wanted the glitz and the glamour, Jason. He never dreamed of owning a Rolls Royce and a mansion in the hills, no. Celebrity was never on the agenda, but if PRIME was to make him a superstar then he would reduce their world to rubble, one broken body at a time. Stepping into the cool, fresh air of the parking garage, Jason quickly walked towards his car – a typically undesirable white hatchback – which sat in the dimly lit reaches of the lot's far corner. With long strides it didn't take him long to get there, and soon he'd pulled the key from his pocket and slid it into the lock. Jason was just about to twist the door open when a sudden blunt force pressed between his shoulder blades. Groaning as a gloved hand force him against the side of his car, Jason clearly heard the telling metallic click of a safety switch. III "No, you listen to me you fuckin' fuck!" Nathaniel Loftman, red-faced, barked into his Nokia. "You work for me, not the other way around! Get this fucking bullshit sorted out before I replace you with somebody who can, okay?!" With a displeased groan he disconnected the caller and slammed the phone down on the wooden surface with more force than he'd anticipated. "Well, that sounded, uhhh, jolly..." his wife Cynthia said from the other side of the small dining table, her voice laced with sarcasm. Cynthia picked up her ham salad sub from it's plate and took a hearty chunk out of it as her blonde-headed husband ran two stressed hands through his shoulder-length locks. "What's wrong with you?" she asked between chews. "You've been awfully... well, angry recently." "Nothing. I'm fine." "But you've hardly touched your sandw-" "I said I'm fine!" he snapped, jarring his head upwards. Almost immediately he found himself ruing this decision. He sighed. "I'm sorry, Cynth. It's just some work stuff that's been grating on me. Nothing you need to worry about." "Well, obviously it is something that I should be worrying about, because you're not exactly leaving this anger at the workplace, are you?" she said, swallowing a mouthful of bread, ham and lettuce. Again Nathaniel exhaled heavily before taking a drink from his glass of chilled spring water. Extended conversations about a job that he wasn't, for the most part, particularly fond of were never his favourite thing in the world, and this case things were especially difficult. He shrugged, figuring that if he couldn't open up to his wife then there was no hope for him. "Just this client we have. He's completely impossible to work with." "Awkward celebrities. I thought that was just part of your job?" was the question she posed to the sports agent. "Well, yeah. But this guy..." he tailed off. "I don't know. There's just something about him. Well, that and the fact that I'm certainly he screwed us into renewing his contract." Cynthia's face twisted with confusion. "How so?" "We've been looking to replace him for a while. He's rude, disruptive, can barely stay on the right side of the law. Just an absolute nightmare to deal with, you know? So we put a contingency plan in place. We found a replacement; and he seemed like a solid enough kid. Young, enthusiastic and with a clean legal history..." "So what happened to him?" "He was all set to sign, to take that bastard's contract, but moments after he comes in for his final meeting he calls me up and says he doesn't want to take it anymore." "So he changed his mind? Isn't much you can do about that." "Yeah. It's just that I'm pretty certain that the guy he was supposed to replace had something to do with it," Loftman paused, not sounding so sure of himself. "I don't know. I think he got to him." His wife actually laughed. "He 'got' to him? Come on, Nath. This isn't The Sopranos." "Trust me. You don't know this guy. What he's capable of." Cynthia shook her head and took another bite out of her lunch. "So if this guy's such a problem, why didn't you just take his contract? Surely that would've solved all your problems." Loftman lowered his head again, refusing to meet the truth head-on. "It's not as simple as that." From his body language she sensed that prying further was not a good idea, so she chewed down the remains of the sandwich and thought through her next words. "And this is what's been getting you down? The fact that you've gotta work with this guy for however long his contract extends?" "Pretty much. I just... I dunno. I want to know what he did. He got to the kid, he twisted his arm. I'm sure of it." "Have you spoken to the 'kid' about it?" He raised his head again. "No." "Well, if you think it'll give you a little peace of mind, maybe you should," she shrugged. "I'm no psyciatrist, Nath, but it's not good for you to bounce this back and forth in your head all the time. Maybe if you find out what happened you'll feel a little better about it." "But what if I find out that he did get to him? What am I supposed to do then?" Cynthia shrugged. "That's a risk you'll have to take." Pondering his options, Nathaniel Loftman swallowed another mouthful of water. Cynthia's wasn't such a bad idea at all. IV "Shit, Jase. You are one tough motherfucker to track down, you know that?" the assailant croaked in a voice as rough as sandpaper. Tightening his grip on the shooter, the man grabbed Jason's skull and forced the side of his head into the vehicle. "Been lookin' all over. Elusive prick." Tightening his jaw to blot out the pain in his temples, Natas tried desperately to squirm free but the gun at his back told him that that wasn't such a bright idea. "Let fuckin' go of me!" Jason seethed through clenched teeth. "Uh-uh. Quit strugglin' like a bitch first, then I might consider it." More out of being referred to as a "bitch" than anything else, Jason tried to lash out, wildly throwing an elbow backwards. With his head against the car his aim was poor and the assailant dodged the wayward strike with relative ease. "Fuck did I just tell you?" his voice cackled. Pulling the gun from Jason's back, he raised his hand in the air and cracked the butt across the back of the PRIME wrestler's skull. "Ahhh! FUCK!" Not hard enough to knock Jason out but hard enough to leave a nasty lump. Stepping away from the pained Anti-Superstar, Jason's aggressor watched him drop to one knee, rubbing the palm of his hand through shaved dirty blonde hair to the impact area. He lifts his arm and points the black pistol at Jason Natas, checking over his shoulder for potential witnesses. "Get up. Come on." Jason did get up, but not because the assailant was telling him to do so. Rising to his full 6'4", Jason kept one hand on the back of his head to numb the pain. With his head drawn low and eyebrows furrowed, Jason glared down the barrel of the gun with a look that would have turned Medusa herself to stone. Without warning Jason strode forward as the red mist descended upon him. Bang! A crackle of smoke and gunpowder from the barrel as the aggressor pulled back on the trigger, sending a bullet downwards into the concrete at Jason's feet. Instinctively Natas jumped backwards. "Five more in the barrel if you're feel like dancin'," the man said through craggy vcal chords ravaged by years of excessive tobacco consumption. Relieved that the warning shot hand't hit him, Jason looked up and caught his first real glimpse of the man who'd attacked him. He was shorter than Jason by a couple inches but he was still big. A bulky, almost bodybuilder-like physique lay hidden under a long, black trenchcoat and the African American's weathered face bared scars common for a man who'd spent too many years hanging around with the wrong type of people. "Fuck d'you want?" Jason sneered, feeling a dreadful thump in the back of his head with every beat of his heart. The man focused his gaze. "Just a friendly reminder from Mr. Abraham, is al." "Hell you talkin' about?" "Hmph. Obviously you've forgotten the arrangement." "Obviously." Stoic, stern, Jason Natas was breathing slowly and not taking his eye off his assailant for a millisecond. Years of a life less fortunate had ensured his ability to compose himself whilst staring down the barrel of a gun. The man chuckled mockingly. "Damn, he said you were stupid. Didn't think he meant this fuckin' braindead, though." Watching carefully, Jason sized up his chances. The man, who was at least ten years his senior, had a broken nose and a face that suggested he was no stranger to a good old-fashioned brawl or two, and he was built like a pitbull. He wasn't showing any sign of wavering with that shooter, either. "Your debt. It still stands." "Fuck you," Jason barked. "I did what I was asked." "No. No you didn't." Slowly the man began to pace to the left, maintaining his aim. "You did two fucking fights in some stinky old basement. You think that that's gonna bring in anythin' close to what we're lookin' for?" "Hell with what yer lookin' for," Jason snorted. "I ain't doin' no more fights for you twisted fucks. Find some other punk to do your monkeywork." His tone hard and unwavering, Jason Natas was deadly serious. In his time out from professional wrestling a very influential former benefactor had forced him back to a lifestyle he'd thought he'd seen the back of, and his return to the New York City underground fight scene wasn't pretty in the slightest. The man stopped in his tracks. "You fail to grasp the meaning of the word 'debt'. Don't see how, it's pretty fuckin' simple. You wronged us – you wronged Mr. Abraham – and there's only two ways to make this go away. You can be a good boy and do what yer told until the boss decides enough's enough, or I can gun you down like a dog in this parking lot. Your choice." Jason swallowed. If he had anything but contempt for Cordell Abraham and his shady mob of associates then he may have considered the man's words – he was, for the most part, correct – but with a gun pointed at him Jason Natas is only thinking about one thing. He watched the man carefully, like a lion waiting to pounce on an unsuspecting wildebeest. The man walked slowly around him again, but the opening Jason was waiting for didn't materialise. "Fuck you. I don't owe you shit," Natas snarled, clenching a fist. "You can tell Abraham I said that, all I care." The man stopped. "Jason. You shot his son. Surely even a retard like you can realise why that might piss him off just a l-" "In the foot!" Jason interrupted. "Shot the prick in the foot. Hardly life-threatening, is it?" "Cordell Jnr. had lost three toes that night. He'll never walk again." His tone of voice changed suddenly, dropping an octave. Unfortunately he was talking to the wrong person if he wanted to work the whole "sympathy" angle. "Should'a thought 'a that b'fore he 'cided to break into my fuckin' apartment then, shouldn't he." Unable to comprehend the argument of someone who's position, in his eyes, was untenable, the man raised his eyebrows. "What?!" he quizzed in disbelief, looking at Jason as if he were talking from a hole in the side of his neck. "You're out of you mind, man. Ser-UGH!" The moment Jason had been waiting for materialised. The man's first mistake was his emotional attachment; his disbelief broke his focus and a split-second's lapse in concentration was all Jason needed to bounce and disarm the man. His gun dropped to the floor, the assailant was helpless to avoid the wide haymaker that crashed into the side of his jaw with the force of a sledgehammer. Staggering backwards, he belt Jason grab him by the collar and thrust a knee up into his nose. A crimson explosion erupted from the middle of his face and Jason allowed him to fall backwards as he knelt to the floor and claimed the gun for his own. "Your turn t' listen t'me now, fuck-o," Jason said as he slowly rose to his feet. With both hands he pointed the gun at what he thought was the man's forehead. "Tell yer boss an' his army of cunts t'stay the fuck away from me, else he's gonna have more t'worry about than his boy's three missin' toes." Slowly Jason walked backwards. He dropped one hand from the gun and fumbled around behind him, finding his keys still in the lock. "You won't get awa-" "Shut the fuck up," he snarled as the hired muscle raised his bloodied head from the ground. Blindly, Jason pulled the car door open. "You tell the fat fuck all 'bout what happened here, alright? Tell him next time I ain't gon' be so forgivin'". Sliding into the driver's seat and dropping the pistol at his feet, Jason slammed the door shut and flicked the ignition without buckling up. Watching helplessly as he propped himself up on his shoulders, the trenchcoat-wearing man shook his head and watched Jason Natas' car drive out of the garage. V The weight of a hundred dumbbells strung to his heart pulled the demolished man to the floor. Left alone with nought but his thoughts to keep him company, Aidan Chase quietly disappeared into himself. With puffy red eyes from interminable weeping and a mind as scattered as a jigsaw puzzle, he was ruined. Money had always been a struggle for the couple. With Amanda's very limited skill-set and Aidan's choice of career the two of them were never going to live like David and Victoria Beckham and, truth be told, Aidan couldn't remember the last time his bank account wasn't in the red, but never had he thought it would come to this. The Dual Halo. He remembered when he first told Amanda about it; they were both so excited. After years of toiling on the independent circuit he was to make the big step to potential superstardom. They began dreaming of a change in lifestyle beyond their wildest dreams as the glittering star of celebrity twinkled brightly on the horizon. She'd set her heart on it, Amanda; she built it up for weeks - months - and when their PRIME dream came unceremoniously crashing down her disappointment was crushing. The sudden collapse of something she'd put on a pedestal for so long tore Amanda to shreds, but she could've lived with all of that if only he'd told her the truth. Balling a fist and smashing it half-heartedly into the floor, Aidan felt like such a wet blanket. Cursing himself, damning his inability to admit to the woman he loved that his own cowardice had cost him the opportunity of a lifetime, Aidan sobbed and moaned incoherently. Raising a hand to wipe the tears from his stinging eyes, Aidan ran his fingers down the counters of his face to the small, healing burn wound at the base of his neck. He rubbed it not-so-gently; prying and prodding until it stung, remembering the face – the name - of the man who'd done this to him. His PRIME contract. His livelihood. His girlfriend. In one five-minute exchange of violence in one dingy Manhattan alleyway all of them had been taken away from him. It was then that he made his decision. Aidan Chase wasn't a wet blanket, no; no man had ever wronged him without later facing the repercussions no matter the extent of his grievance, and that wasn't a habit he was about to change any time soon. Jason Natas had taken everything that was dear to Aidan Chase, and he would suffer for it.
