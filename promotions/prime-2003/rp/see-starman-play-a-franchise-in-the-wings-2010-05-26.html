See Starman Play: A Franchise in the Wings

Date: May 26, 2010 See Starman Play: A Franchise in the Wings By William K. Spades Orlando Vacant Staff Writer Wednesday, May 26, 2010 HOLLYWOOD – “Let me tell you about my first experience with Syrian erotic wall paintings…” It was an odd conversation starter, but I was quickly learning that in a town where down is up and up is sideways and green is red is orange is Thelma Todd’s murdered lungs on an ice cream blonde marquee, well…an odd starter might just light your cigarette. “As opposed to your most recent?” I wanted to respond, but didn’t. Fear kept me from addressing Castor Strife incorrectly, though it was fear that led me deeper into his universe. And what a splendid universe it was. And by splendid, I mean get me the fuck out of here. The happy home that is Castor V. Strife Productions does, coincidentally, contain a room decorated with Syrian erotic wall paintings, and that’s exactly where Castor was leading me. The detour would prove to be infinitely more fascinating than the destination. He took me into rooms located (would ‘narrow corridor’ be too cliché a description?) down a narrow corridor, none of which included sleeping quarters. Come to think of it, where does this guy sleep? Hmm. Back to the ‘narrow corridor’ thing- perhaps my description was only partially correct. It was in fact a corridor that brought us to many a room, but each room had a separate exit door that would lead you to a round perimeter, which in turn led you to backdoor entrances to the other rooms of the corridor. In other words, Castor had an entire segment of his house/production studio laid out like an inverted traffic circle. You could travel along the perimeter and exit to various destinations located on it’s inside. If you wished to circumvent the entire corridor and simply exit at the top, you could. But with grand sights such as a full 1930’s replica bar, a room dedicated to dead hornets (Castor hates them; had a childhood phobia), and a prosthetic limb gallery, why would you want to? Best of all, he gave me a tour of his Nazi paraphernalia museum, which is to say I took a lap around a small living space dedicated to such. To his credit, it had all the bells and whistles you’d expect from one man’s tribute to his Nazi collectibles eBay spending spree. He even paid a full-time museum curator and/or tour guide- a blue eyed blonde woman with fair skin dressed in an actual (as in not replica) Luftwaffe SS uniform, though I suspect the black stockings and guarder belt were ripped straight from Rob Zombie’s “Werewolf Women of the SS” costume gallery. What’s with this new Nazi Chick fetish anyway? I prefer my doggy style fantasies not be laced with images of gas chamber horrors, but I may just be in the minority on that. Another thought: this entire room must have cost in the high six figure thousands. I kid you not, he has a used stein certified to be smashed on the night of the Beer Hall Putsch. How such a thing could be verified I have no idea, but it is and he’s got it. Other items include SS daggers, buttons, Hitler’s walking stick and his dinner plates (lots of those), and several Eva Braun’s film posters. “I’m currently in a bidding war over one of her bones” he tells me. As in skeleton bones? “That is correct. It is very rare and very expensive; highly illegal in seven countries, all of which carry lengthy prison terms for dealing in such memorabilia. You’d understand if I didn’t reveal which bone it is?” Sure, why the fuck not. Apparently he paid $15,000 for a christening present given by SS leader Heinrich Himmler to Luftwaffe Commander Hermann Goering’s daughter. “It is not ideological, it is an addiction,” he reassures me. Eventually we arrive at the Syrian erotic whatever-the-hell room, and the wall paintings are…disturbing, but not shockingly so. The images are fairly civilian for pornography, but what’s unsettling is that anyone would feel the need to purchase something so obscure and void of any monetary or historic value. They are a virtual highlight reel of sexual positions, ranging from missionary depictions of male on female, to female on female scissoring, and male homosexual acts. All this tells me something I knew within five minutes of meeting the man: Castor Strife is a weird cat. But it’s his type of weird, the obsessively eclectic quality of his every possession and creation, that nearly cripples my every advancing step with apprehension. I shouldn’t continue, but I do. This interview shouldn’t happen, but it does. “If you believe, as I do, that every corner of the world is starving for the truest representation of the human art form, then you export it wherever you can,” he says, sitting across from me on the patio outside of his home. “Like McDonald’s or Big Lots? Are you bringing the Whopper to PRIME, so to speak?” I say, immediately regretting I did. My comfort level rises and falls with the minutes, and I find myself both jumping over and pulling back from the line. “So to speak. Many in the community would find your reference appalling and insulting, but franchising is an important aspect of art. Of course, the quality speaks for itself: your films, paintings, plays, your…work, as it were, could be on every billboard and street corner from here to Manaus, and if it’s shit then it will be called shit in 50 different languages. That’s nothing new. If the quality is there, and it’s truly a world apart from your contemporaries, the learned public will know the mark of Castor Strife where they see it. They won’t even need a name identification.” “You mean your films?” I asked. “My films, my productions which include exhibitionist demonstrations, my paintings, and perhaps most prominently…my physical art.” A woman, one I haven’t met, silently approaches him, whispers, and hands him a folder that he immediately leafs through. “And the physical art, that would be professional wrestling?” I hope he’s still paying attention. “That’s right,” he says while reading. “An athletic endeavor that requires its elite to wear multiple hats- it is a star hungry industry. I have found success because I am a star with or without this business; a self-made sculpture. My competitors are worlds below me, here in PRIME and elsewhere. They want to share my billing, this Hush, this Roxie, but to do so is career suicide.” “Is it?” “That’s right,” he repeats. “I can outshine them at will. PRIME’s Universal Champion, their main event, has up and left. Another young star, Brandon Youngblood, has followed suite. Now do you think PRIME is hedging their bets on Roxie Sykes? Hush? Garbage Bag Johnny?” “I don’t know, I don’t watch pro-wrestling. Honestly, I’m only here because Dustin Diamond stood me up.” It’s true, I am. “Well I can assure you, THEY’RE NOT. If any other competitor were to win the Universal Title, it would be a disaster for the company. They brought me in to do what I do best,” he said, handing the folder back to the woman standing over his shoulders. As she walks away, I notice her jeans are wet. The fuck? “Be the star. And I will deliver without difficulty” he continued. Castor noticed my diverted attention, and caught on quick to what was happening. “You noticed her, didn’t you? Your feeling is correct: it’s urine.” “Does she have bladder control problems, or…? This isn’t part of the interview.” OK, now it is. “She has complete control over her bladder. But unlike you or I, she derives pleasure from auto-degradation, which is the act of degrading oneself, usually in front of a select person.” “She pees her pants in front of you?” “Only in front of me. The psychosexual complex is a peculiar paradise.” Peculiar paradise, he tells me. Truer words haven’t been spoken all week.
