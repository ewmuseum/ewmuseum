Homecoming [1]: For A Name (Rev 172 v. Xavier Kannon)

8 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 8th, 2008 September 8th, 2008; Providence, Rhode Island It had been a long, winding road for the Princes of New England as they got to this point. Since joining the PRIME roster, they've toured around the eastern side of the country and earned the scorn of many fans nationwide. It had taken several months and a couple of thousand miles to get to this point, but the two men named Simon Knox and Connor O'Reily had finally come home. For Connor, home was another hundred miles away, but it was almost the spirit of the land known as New England that caused him to believe that the city of Providence was his home as well. For Simon, Providence was his hometown... his place of birth, and his place of residence. Which was why Simon pulled the car into the Knox family's home on the outskirts of the city. The home was either a large house or a small mansion, it looked expensive to own but not to an outrageous degree. The lawn looked like it was somewhat well-maintained, but not at a professional quality. The building consisted of two wings, a west wing and an east wing. Much of the family lived in the west wing, while the east wing was more recreational and utility... even including a gym with a wrestling ring. To the Knox family, wrestling was everything. For some members of the Knox family, that still held true today. Either way, Simon Knox hadn't been back home in two years. He'd spent his time working for wrestling promotions such as the Squared Circle and, much more recently, PRIME. He decided to devote himself fully to the endeavor, not out of love for professional wrestling, but out of a desire to follow completely in his father's footsteps. It meant that, some day, Simon Knox would want to become the same sort of wrestler-actor his father was. He was not in the business to stay. Travelling with Simon Knox on his trip back home were Connor O'Reily, who was sitting in the backseat of the car, and Miranda O'Reily, who sat in the front passenger seat. Soren Knox had opted not to come, instead heading back to his own home to spend time with his girlfriend, Rachel. Connor looked at the large house as he got out of the car, and whistled. "Never knew your family was so well-off, Simon." Connor said. Wearing a red bandana and a black "PRIME" T-shirt with a pair of torn jeans, Connor looked way too much like a punk to actually warrant being anywhere near this house. Miranda had long given up on voicing her disapproval of her twin brother's choice of garments, but he could almost feel the annoyed gaze that she gave him. "My dad was a millionaire by the time he died, and everybody's been working to keep it going. So we've always been kind of well-off." Simon said, as he put his hands into the pockets of the black leather jacket he wore. The collared white shirt he wore underneath the jacket and the black slacks and loafers indicated that he cared just a little bit more about his appearance than his cavalier tag team partner. The fact that his long hair was perfectly groomed while Connor hid his underneath his bandana was another sure sign of this. "Connor and I haven't been so fortunate." Miranda O'Reily observed as she got out of the car. Her long brown hair was tied up into a bun, she wore a red blouse with a long black skirt, and a pair of black pumps. Much like Simon, she really cared about her appearance. She looked at the house, almost in that way girls would when they covet such things as a big house, and she frowned. "Yeah." Connor surmised, though he did not add to her comment. The three walked from their car to the front door, and Simon casually rang the doorbell. It took a bit of waiting, during which time, Miranda yanked off Connor's headband, handed him a comb, and pointed at his head. Connor muttered and grumbled and said various nasty things before he did as his sister told him to. Finally, the door opened and there stood a young girl. She was in her mid-teens, a short girl with hair dyed a sort of pinkish red color. She had several studs in her ears and a nose ring. She also wore a set of eyeglasses on her face, presumedly because she needed them. She wore a tattered, sleeveless white shirt over a short-sleeved red shirt, and a single white arm warmer over her left arm which looked like it had been made from a sock. She wore a tattered denim skirt, seemingly fashioned out of an existing pair of jeans, and a pair of striped stockings with black ankle boots. Her reaction to seeing Simon was simply a crossing of her arms. "Simon." she said. "Siobahn." was Simon's slightly surprised response. The last time Simon saw Siobahn, her hair was still in its natural color and she didn't have the ability to set off metal detectors with her face. Needless to say, his surprise was a little warranted. Siobahn Knox looked over at the company that Simon was keeping, and then looked back at Simon, "I take it that the circus is in town." "In a manner of speaking." Simon said. Most people might have been a little bit annoyed by the statement that essentially implied that wrestling really consisted of a bunch of circus freaks, but Simon Knox was not "most people". He just kind of brushed the comment off and kept his usual poker face, "Is Lieselotte here?" "Yeah, she is," Siobahn said, "Don't tell me you came all this way just to talk to your creepy German cousin." "Of course not," Simon said, "My friends and I need to stay somewhere tonight, and so we came here. It just so happens that I also need to talk to Liese, too. ......And my cousin is not creepy." There was a pause from Siobahn before she stepped away from the door and walked away, "I'll go tell mom." After Siobahn stepped away, Connor O'Reily whispered to Simon Knox, "Dude. Your sister's hot." Simon's poker face didn't change, but he did comment on Connor's statement, "Wait about three months before you decide to jump her bones, Connor." * . * After being invited into the house, the individuals that made up the Princes of New England ended up scattering around the house. This was primarilly because Simon Knox wished to talk to his cousin by himself, and therefore left the O'Reily twins to their own devices downstairs. Lieselotte Weber's room was the second door on the left on the second floor of the mansion's west wing. When Simon knocked on the door, he heard a young woman with a very faint German accent telling him "come in", so he did so. Lieselotte kept her room very simple, very tidy, and very plain. She hung nothing on the walls, she didn't have anything more striking than a bookshelf filled with language books and a television set, and she didn't even have a particularly interesting bedspread or set of curtains. It was likely because she had to fund things herself if she wanted her own things that she kept things simple. The girl herself sat in a chair by a desk that sat in front of the only window in the room and positioned right next to her bed. Her hair had grown very long and would now reach her thighs if she stood up at her full height. She was very short for her age, though, standing all of about five feet and an inch in height. She wore a black dress with a lot of frills, though she was barefoot. Lieselotte only glanced over her shoulder for a moment, content on just going back to what she was doing upon seeing the intruder in her room. She saw Simon, and then turned back to what she was doing. It probably took her only two seconds for it to register who was behind her before she suddenly shot up from her chair, banging her knee into the desk as she went, causing her to shout "OW!" in response. Pain aside, Lieselotte turned to see Simon Knox standing there. "Hey." was the only thing he said. Lieselotte's response wasn't anything verbal, it was more of a sudden rush forward followed by a hug. Were this something out of an anime, the term for the action might have been "glomp". Simon rested his arms on her shoulders, before she looked up at him with her blue eyes. "Why haven't you been back?" she asked. Gone was the broken English of seven years ago, and gone was the much heavier German accent from the same time period. She was every bit as normal as any other girl at this juncture, even with English as a second language. "I've been touring lately." Simon Knox said, simply, "It's a wrestling thing." "I see." was Lieselotte's response, as she let go of Simon and let him breathe. She invited Simon to sit in her chair, as she sat down on her bed. The two looked at each other for an awkward moment, neither really sure how to approach the other. For Lieselotte, she always had some sort of special place in her heart for Simon Knox, who was rather unintentionally the outcast of the family. And Simon felt that Lieselotte was the only person who really understood him. "So." they both said, roughly at the same time. The "talking at the same time" thing caused the two to just enter another long, awkward pause. "So," Lieselotte said, trying to break the nervous silence, "What did you want to talk about? Over the phone, you weren't willing to say anything about it except to my face. I'm curious about what it is." Simon Knox hesitated before he decided to go ahead and talk about it, "It's about dad." * . * Miranda O'Reily walked around the main lobby of the mansion, seemingly impressed with its size. Connor O'Reily, meanwhile, simply sat on the staircase and observed what his twin was doing at the moment. The first floor of the lobby was largely decorated mostly in flowers and plants, and they were quite well-maintained. The steps leading up to the second floor were covered in a red carpet, and ran halfway up before brancing off into separate staircases that took someone to the western and eastern wings. Siobahn Knox came into the lobby, herself, and saw the twins just idling around. She shrugged and decided to talk to them, if only to alleviate her boredom, "How long have you two known Simon?" "Two years." Connor responded, almost flatly. Miranda nodded in agreement, though her tone was not as flat as Connor's, it was more like fond reminiscence, "Two years." "He doesn't seem to have changed too much," Siobahn said, "He's still every bit the miserable fuddy-duddy he was when he left." Connor looked at Siobahn Knox, and gave her an odd look. Siobahn caught the odd look and decided to elaborate, "Okay. I'm going to assume you two know that Simon's the youngest son of some big wrestling star from the 90s, right?" The O'Reily twins only nod. "Well, my brother admired my dad. And then he died. And he's been miserable ever since." Siobahn said, "That whole poker faced indifference thing is just his way of dealing with his own misery. At least, that's what I think. I don't really understand him much, myself, any more." "You know... that's how he is around us," Miranda said, "We never know what he's thinking." Siobahn nodded, "There's one person who probably does know... Lieselotte." "His cousin?" Connor asked. * . * "You wish to know what I heard seven years ago?" Lieselotte asked. She had a hair brush in her hand, and she was dealing with the cascade of hair she had by using it. "I only recently remembered," Simon explained, "I know it was a long time ago, but... you couldn't tell me because you weren't good with English. Hell, I don't know if you even remember, yourself." Lieselotte looked up at her ceiling for a little bit, "Hmm." Her expression changed once she came to some conclusion, "Ah." "Did you remember?" Simon asked. He seemed impatient, but the details have been weighing heavy on him since remembering. He wanted to know this. He needed to know this. "I heard two voices, as I remember correctly. One was your father, the other was that... other man." Lieselotte said, a certain amount of disgust in her voice for the man she was describing, a Hollywood producer by the name of William "Willy" Wilkerson. Simon visibly grimaced upon her mention of the "other man", as he shared in her disgust of the snake-like producer. "I can't remember too clearly what happened. The other man said something about your father's 'secret' being... um, leaked." Lieselotte said. Her wording indicated that she wasn't completely sure, but the statement piqued the interest of Simon Knox, who leaned forward in the chair. "What secret?" Simon asked. "I don't know." Lieselotte said. The groan of disappointment from Simon Knox couldn't be more obvious, "That's all you know?" "Hmm." she thought, looking up at the ceiling again. Simon looked at Lieselotte for a while, waiting for her to finish thinking. It felt like a long time before she moved her head back down to face her cousin, "There was one puzzling statement that your father made. I couldn't figure it out." "What's that?" Simon asked. "He mentioned a name." Lieselotte said, simply. * . * "That stupid German girl won't talk to any of the rest of us, really." Siobahn said, looking up in the direction of Lieselotte's room. Her arms were crossed and her scowl was evident. She had an obvious dislike for her "creepy German cousin". "But around Simon, she's happy. I'll never understand that." she added, moments later. "Neither will I. Simon kinda sucks the joy out of the room when he's in a bad mood." Connor said, with a nod. His most recent encounter with a Simon Knox in a bad mood came the day right after Colossus, and it was an occasion he wouldn't soon forget. "He's like that," Siobahn said, "Has been ever since dad died. You know what really annoys me, though?" Miranda, perhaps in the interest of wanting to know more about the Knox family, decided to inquire, "What's that?" "After the funeral, Simon didn't say a word to any of us for almost a year. Not me, not mom, not Uncle Jeff, nor his brothers. We tried everything to coax a reaction out of him, but it only seemed to make him more distant," Siobahn said, before she sat down next to Connor O'Reily on the staircase, "The very first person he ever opened up to after that period of silence... was Lieselotte. Out of all of the people he could have opened up to, why did it have to be her? She didn't even start speaking proper English until three years ago!" "They're close, then?" Miranda asked. Siobahn laughed to herself at first, raising the eyebrows of the O'Reily twins, "You could say that." "Huh?" went both twins. "Nothing," Siobahn said, with a sigh, before she got up in a huff and started to walk away from the O'Reily twins, "You don't need my theories." * . * "Sarah Fulton." The name hung over the room like the Sword of Damocles. For a while, neither Lieselotte Weber nor Simon Knox said anything... Lieselotte because she was the one who spoke the name, and Simon because he was trying to process it in his head. "Sarah Fulton?" he finally asked. "Yes. That was the name I remember," Lieselotte said. She paused, and stood up from the bed she sat on so she could look out of the window, "I'm sorry, though. I don't know the name's meaning. The way he sounded, though, it sounded like it was someone dear to him." Simon tried to comprehend the words she spoke to him, but perhaps ironically, English was failing him at the moment and he could only repeat Lieselotte's last few words in question form, "Someone dear to him?" "It's just a thought, Simon... a passing thought. At the time, I could only understand people by their tone of voice. The way he sounded was sad, like he was remembering something that hurt him. Whoever this Sarah Fulton was, she hurt your father in some way." Lieselotte said. She then paused and turned to Simon, her hands behind her back, "Well, did that help any? I realize it isn't much, but..." "Don't worry," Simon said. Was that a hint of... care in his voice? "You gave me something to go on. You're as big a help as always, Lieselotte." Simon stood up, and Lieselotte was quick to walk in front of him. "If... if there's anything else I can do for you, let me know." she said. Simon looked down at his shorter cousin, before he simply wrapped his arms around her and gave her a hug. Lieselotte, in response, wrapped her own arms around her. "I'd still be in a completely pathetic state if you weren't around," Simon whispered to his cousin, holding her close, "You've done more than enough for me as it is." Finaly, the two awkwardly broke up their hug. "I, uh... I think I will go get some food." Simon said. He went to the door and opened it, but left Lieselotte with some parting words and a small smile on his face, "Thanks." Lieselotte offered her own smile, and quietly lamented to herself, "You're welcome." Around most other people, Simon Knox was a cold, indifferent jerk. A man who hardly listened to anyone but himself and could only really coexist with others in the wrestling ring. However, around just one person in particular, he showed his real emotions and feelings. One person understood Simon Knox, the feelings he had pent up over the years, and his motivations for today. And that was enough for him, however little it was.
