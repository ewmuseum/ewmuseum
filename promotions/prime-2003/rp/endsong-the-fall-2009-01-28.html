[Endsong] The Fall

28 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28th January 2009 Have you ever dreamt that you were falling? To be honest as that is probably a stupid question. Every human being who has ever lived, or will ever lied, has also dreamt. To do otherwise leads to nothing less than madness and nothing more than death. In a time where people are defined by their race, their creed or their sexuality and classified as a different race by the simply pigment of their skin one would think that a common denominator as simple, and wonderful, as the ability to dream would link us all together. After all, we all dream. Out of all of our dreams falling is, probably, the most common shared by all of mankind. Even if it is just once, barely remembered and never acknowledged, every single person has – or will have – dreamt of falling. Now, I don’t mean that they have dreamt of flying. Soaring above the landlocked mortals on wings formed from wishes and miracles; twisting and turning in the thermals with an eagle’s grace; or, most awesome of all, arms outstretched with nothing but our own power to hold us free from gravity’s tug. I also don’t mean floating, lonely as a cloud, while the Earth turns gently, sedately, below. A leaf, falling red and heavy with Autumn’s weight, may meander down to the welcoming grass below but that is something that is completely different. What I am talking about is the fall that never ends and, in the dream, sometimes never even starts. In that place between sleep and waking you can find yourself simply falling. Your stomach lurches, trying to escape through your mouth as your heart beats faster than you can count. The blood rushes around your body with a mounting pressure and you know that if – when – you hit there will be nothing but pain. Your body will crunch and fold inwards and outwards; angles that aren’t humanly possible being formed from bones that shouldn’t ever move that way. You know that you are screaming because, in that situation, who wouldn’t? You can’t hear the noise, though, as the wind that pulls your hair into a tangle of misery steals the noise and whips it away. Tears flood your eyes and streak along your face in a raging rivulet of fury as sweat forms under your arms, on your forehead and in that crevasse between your thighs. Your balls tighten and your bladder screams out as piss runs down your legs, up your body, and everywhere that you know it shouldn’t go; you were taught, at a young age, to control yourself – to pee before bed, to wash your hands and brush your teeth. The embarrassment of soiling yourself is almost enough to chase the fear away. Almost, but not quite enough. The fear of falling isn’t what scares you. It is the fear of what happens when the falling stops. When the air that hurtles pass you is replaced by the unforgiving ground and all that is left is the impact. And then you wake. If you only ever have the dream once then you may not realise this but, despite the fear, despite the terror, the fall never actually ends. Night after night, for months on end, you can wake up – screaming, crying, wet and shaking – and the fall will stop but, thinking back, night after night, for months on end, it will suddenly become clear and obvious that the dream – the nightmare – is always the same. You are falling. That is it. Falling. But it never ends - it never stops. You never, ever, stop. The impact, the pain itself, never comes. Only the dream ends. Until next you sleep. * * * Hawaii The awesome beauty of the vista took my breath away. I stood there, watching the waves dance and crash against the rocks so far below, the blues and greens a multitude of grandeur in the Creator’s own palette, and held my breath. The lush grass beneath my feet curled in and around my toes, tickling them and making me laugh. The sound never reached my mouth, of course but – on the inside – I was smiling. The Sun had set barely a minute ago and the horizon still burned with the embers of its memory. Where the Sun met the water, at the edge of my vision, nature warred in a never-ending battle that would be repeated each and every day. A single bird cried out – calling its defiance or joy to the passing of the day – and I felt its shadow cross over me. I shivered at its passing but didn’t look up. I didn’t need to. I knew what it was, after all. It was one of the lucky ones. It knew its fate, accepted its destiny and got on with it. Who wouldn’t, though, when blessed with the gifts it had? Standing there, the edge of the cliff so close that when I looked down it was as if I was floating with nothing to support me I couldn’t help but wonder why the Creator would give a bird the ability to fly high above the World and, yet, leave me standing there, kicking the ground with grass stained feet as it soared high above me, kissing the Heavens. I could feel my leg muscles protesting as I allowed my thoughts to wander. I had found the cliff edge earlier that morning, just before sunrise, and its view had captured my heart in an instant. The house and the people who owned it – and attempted to care for me – were miles away and nearly forgotten. I had told them that I was going for a run and, with gentle smiles and caring eyes, they let me go. It sickened me. The way they looked at me, the way they tried to help. They way that they cared. No matter what I did, no matter what I said to them, they cared. Rick most of all. It didn’t matter how hard I tried to push him away, to hurt him, he just smiled. He just took my hand. He cared. He understood. No matter what I did to him he accepted me. He loved me. Damn him to Hell! Leaving the house before he awoke was the only thing that I had thought about that morning. I couldn’t face him, not again, and know that we would go through the same routine, the same cycle. I couldn’t bear to have him look at me. I didn’t deserve to have him care, to have him accept. To have him love. I didn’t deserve to walk the same ground as him or breathe the same air. I had told him that, twice before. The first time – days that felt like years ago – in Detroit he had slapped me. He apologised, afterwards, and cried. It was the chock, he had said, and the horror. I suppose that when someone asks you to kill him or her it can be disconcerting. The second time, just the night before as the Hawaiian air breathed gently over us as we watched the stars fill the sky – sitting close together but miles apart – I had told him, simply and honestly how I didn’t want to live. Didn’t deserve to. He didn’t slap me, not this time. I think that he wanted to but, this time, he was prepared. This time he did something worse than hit me. He looked and me and forced me to look back. Then he talked to me. He told me that it wasn’t my fault – none of it – that I couldn’t have done anything to stop it from happening. He told me, in every detail he could remember, what he had done, what he had been through, to find me. To bring me back. He reminded me that there were people who cared about me, people who loved me. He asked me what it would do to my Grandfather, the man who raised me as his son, if I were to die. He asked me, so quietly, what it would do to him if I were to leave him again. He told me he loved me; he made me believe it and didn’t look at me again. He couldn’t. Even if he had stared at me he wouldn’t have seen me. For something so soft, so fragile, tears can blind. Standing up, hours into the night and hours closer to the morning, he reached out for my hand and told me to come inside, to please come inside. It was warmer inside, he said, and it was where he was going. He gave me the choice. I could take I his words and take his hand. I could accept his word and accept his hand. I tried my hardest, I really did. I reached out, slightly, knowing that I could do it. Wanting to. Oh Creator I wanted to take his hand and give him my heart. It would not be over, the pain would still be there, but I knew that it would be a start, at least. Looking up, looking past him, I saw a shadow in the sky and – inside my head, inside my heart and inside my memory – I heard Ember laugh. My arms wrapped around my chest, holding myself tight, and I ignored him. He walked into the house, giving me the space he knew that I craved, and left me to the night. It wasn’t long before I knew that the night was ending so I told the people that were awake that I was going for a run. I found the cliff and, standing there – my soul naked and torn – I watched the miracle of Creation itself unfold below me. Waves crashing, clouds rolling, birds crying out. A rainbow danced in front of me but I knew that it was my own tears that gave it life. It was beautiful, though, a perfect moment. A once in a lifetime moment that would be a great beginning or a great ending. It was easy, in that moment, to do it. I didn’t need to think – not anymore – I didn’t need to jump, or be pushed. I knew what to do. I simply fell.
