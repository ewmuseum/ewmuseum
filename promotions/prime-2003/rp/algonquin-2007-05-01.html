Algonquin

1 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 1, 2007 (21 Days Ago) "I think you’re overreacting a little." A full moon hangs over SoCal, adding a little extra light to a perpetually neon sky. The scene sounds like it looks, as literally hundreds of stars, star-fuckers and wannabes of both varieties crowd an outdoor bar near the beach. The general attire is “optional” by the looks of some of the women. Danny Ferguson tilts his sunglasses down to watch a pack of them stroll by his table. Pursing his lips but not making a sound, he slowly turns his attention back to his table and the closest thing he has to a confidant: Vin Diesel. “You’re not the first person to turn down a movie role, Danny,” Diesel says, taking a sip of a mixed drink and surveying the crowd. Fun-fact that you won’t find in any Vin Diesel fun-fact generator internet bullshit machine ass thing: Vin hates eye contact. Hates it. “I didn’t just turn this down, VD. I fucking 86’d that picture. ‘The Green Lantern’ will be sitting in development hell for at least three more years thanks to me.” “Man,” Vin drawls, giving a quick eyebrow-only shrug. He takes another drink. “What?” “Nothing,” he replies, despite there clearly being something. A veteran of action movie line delivery, the bald baritone lets the moment simmer before continuing. “Used to be you were proud of things like that.” A smirk, or rather a kind of ugly curl of the lip, crosses his face. “You’re getting soft, Fergie.” Danny dismisses it with a wave of his hand. “Fuck that. Easy for you to say when they toss franchise offers at your feet as you stroll down Hollywood Boulevard.” “What can I say?” Vin deadpans, the curl turning into a full smile. “I’m marketable.” He punctuates that by redirecting the smile to another pack of passing ladies. Scanning the crowd himself, Danny kills his own beer and drops the bottle next to the three others that came before it. “Who knew America wanted a mulatto Mr. Clean with Barry White’s throat?” “Don’t hate.” “Do you know I’ve never made a sequel, Vin? How can I call myself an action star when I haven’t even made a sequel? Or even a prequel?” “It’ll come, dude,” Vin assures. “Everyone in town wants to adapt a comic book or a video game. You’ve got a good agent. He’ll get you a franchise soon enough, and then you won’t have to waste my partyin' time by drinking yourself emo and cursing your damn luck.” Mock-insulted, Danny over-acts his pain, which for him is just so incredibly over the top you lost track of the top somewhere down there. “I didn’t know I was such a burden.” “I didn’t want to say anything.” “I’ll just go jump of a bridge or something.” “Couldn’t hurt.” “Heh, no shit. Probably boost my reviews when More Than Hardcore drops next month.” The two share a laugh that quickly dies off. A waitress shimmies by and both silently raise their hands for the next round. Danny finds his eyes wandering again. "Who did you say threw this party again?" "Irakoy Kincaid." "The UCLA guy?" "The Pittsburgh Steelers guy. Number seven overall pick, man." With his eyes fixed on a brunette girl in seven pieces of string passing as a bikini, Danny swallows hard. "That is a signing bonus well spent, my friend." "Easy, champ," Vin offers. "This ain't Hollywood tail. This is NFL tail. Dudes got the market cornered on everything these days." With a roll of the eyes, Danny looks over at his friend. The waitress brings the drinks over. "He's got you," Danny directs her, gesturing to Diesel. The bald man digs out his wallet, shoots Danny a look and laughs it off. "Come on, man!" Danny begs. "You've got the deals in development! You're Triple X! You're Riddick!" He waves to the waitress before she goes. "Did you know he was Riddick?" "Weren't you in The Pacifier?" the waitress replies, looking at Vin, of course. He nods. "You see?" Danny proclaims, "Something for everyone, this guy. Do you know who I am?" The waitress pauses, scrunching her face and nervously looking at Diesel. Vin says a silent prayer that she makes a positive ID and saves him from another hour of Ferguson's laments. "Aren't you the wrestler guy?" Danny's head hangs a little, and the waitress shoots Vin a concerned look. Diesel waves it off hands her a tip and checks out her ass as she walks away. "I'm the wrestler guy, VD," Danny says, slightly defeated. "Not the star of Four Is A Crowd - which, by the way, has landed more pussy than your discount James Earl Jones impression. Not even the star of the second-highest domestic grossing film of 2005, Hard Money, or even the critically acclaimed More Than Hardcore, coming soon to a theater near you. What am I? I'm the guy without a followup project. I'm the fucking WRESTLER GUY." Vin starts to diffuse the situation before the alcohol makes Danny do something the tabloids will make him regret. Before he can get a word out, he's interrupted by a third voice, less friendly and markedly less welcome. "Well, you're sure as shit not the Green Lantern..." The voice boils Danny's blood immediately, an almost Pavlovian reaction. His nostrils flare and his mask twists in disgust as Jason "The Transporter" Statham approaches. Note that "The Transporter" isn't his official nickname, but rather a memory jogger that I tossed in in case you were unfortunate enough to recognize him simply as Jason. The Brit strides up to Danny and Vin, sporting a grin that belongs at a shit buffet. He slaps Diesel on the shoulder and chuckles at his own joke. "Whassa word, gents?" Statham drawls. "Can't stay for long; too much of this lot would kill my career by 'ssociation." Vin rolls his eyes and settles them on something other than the Brit. "Something we can do for you, Jay?" "Eh, I was just havin' a laugh." "You're a fucking fad, Handsome Rob," Danny spits. "Wait 'til the three-picture deal is up and then see how many calls you get." "At least I got the deal and the calls then, right mate? Nobody's ringing you up lately, and your boy here's trying a Travolta after that mob movie gave his career cement shoes." Vin's eyes turn toward Statham. A low growl precedes his words. "Sidney Lumet directed that movie." "Yeah well, he directed it straight to video." Before things escalate, Danny deflects the comments with a jab of his own. "If things are coming up aces for you, then what's this I read about Olyphant getting Hitman? Variety called you a lock for that one." Statham's eyes widen and his jaw tenses. Any courses of action he considers, however, are dissuaded by Vin's similar mental state. The last thing any smart action star would do is enter a fight in which he could get his ass kicked. A quick close of the eyes, a deep breath and a stretch of the neck brings the Londoner back down from boiling. "I'd rather be passed over than to not be in the running to begin with," he chokes. "Be seeing you gits." He backs away and disappears into the crowd, leaving Danny and Vin alone again. A few quiet moments pass before Danny slams his beer down on the table. "FUCK." "Don't listen to him," Vin assures. "He hit a hot streak and he's all over himself." "He's right, though. Studio's aren't calling, VD. I'm persona non grata since this Lantern shit." "The town's got a short memory, Danny. You take your lumps for a bit, wait 'til they find another goat and then pull out the redemption project." "I'm in my prime, Vin." "And yet you're not even 30. You're almost a decade younger than every other action star or wannabe action star out there. You can come back from this." Ferguson doesn't respond, and just shakes his head, still going over Statham's words in his head. He kicks himself for a few missed comeback opportunities. Diesel feels for his boy's plight, but a check of the wristwatch beckons him elsewhere. "I gotta roll, Champ. I've got an appearance thing. Gotta shake hands and share drinks. You know, noth-" "Yeah, I know," Danny says. "Work stuff." Vin pauses after standing up from the table, letting a few more seconds tick away. "Yeah." Danny nods knowingly, the same way a jilted significant other nods to say 'that's what I thought.' Then he rises and sticks out a hand for his evacuating friend. "Take it easy, VD," he insists as the two shake. "You hear about any projects, you'll holler?" "You know it." The two part ways and Diesel pushes his way to the exit. Danny takes a few more minutes to check out the scenery. Across the way, he catches a pretty blonde in a g-string making eyes at him. He tips his sunglasses down again to return the gaze and she smiles and goes back to chatting with her group. Sensing blood in the water, figuratively, Danny grabs his beer and pushes himself up, ready to make his move. Unfortunately, that move started off with a brief period of lightheadedness and a stumble that carried him into another partygoer. "MOTHAFUCKA!" The scream silences everyone around them. Even the music from the nearby DJ fades a little as Danny hazily turns toward the source. He cranes his neck to look up at the Volkswagen-shaped black man in front of him, then he slowly lets his eyes drift down to check out the giant bluish stain on the man's shirt and the half-empty martini glass he still grips in his hand. "Whoops," Danny says. "Now look a' dis mothafucka right'ere!" the man responds, and Danny wonders if that was an appropriate response. "Ya put ya fuckin' drunkass shoulda inta my drank and all ya gats ta say is 'whoops.'" Ferguson, keeping his eyes on the blonde girl, who isn't so much returning the gaze as staring at the scene in horror (like everyone else), produces a wallet and peels out a couple hundreds. He reaches up to drop them in the man's martini glass, but his hand is slapped away. "Muthafucka I don't want you fuckin' money." "Then what the fuck do you want?" Danny snaps, retrieving his bills and smoothing them out. The crowd gasps and murmurs a little. From a distance, an equally large but not quite vehicle-shaped man calls out to the one with the ruined shirt. "Irakoy! C'mon, man! Irakoy!" Irakoy Kincaid holds up a hand to silence his apparent friend, then leans in to Danny a little. He smiles big, and his entire mouth of silver-plated teeth shimmers in the moonlight. "You tha wressler, aincha?" "My friends call me Danny," Ferguson responds, either avoiding the gravity of the confrontation or ignorant to it. "Yeah, well, if the stain on muh shirt ain't givin it 'way, I aincha friend, wressler." "Look, I'm sorry. I mea-" "It don't matter what you meant, it matter what you DID." Kincaid growls, bigging himself up for the crowd, his crowd. "You try to embarass Irakoy at his own fucking party?" "No, I-" "I know you used to playfighting and rollin around bein all gay an shit, but I ain't fuckin playin dat. You wanna bump, we can bump." His temper flaring but his brain, thankfully, keeping it together, Danny Ferguson, for once in his life, tries to diffuse a situation. "I don't think some sorry-ass muthafucka like you is gonna start shit with me," the football player says, and he gets in closer, begging, pleading for Danny to try something. Ferguson refuses. He'd heard this kind of smack before. There was no reason to let it get to him just because some punk is questioning his legitimacy. Although, on second thought... "Because if you try to come into my world, wressler," Irakoy threatens, pulling Danny's sunglasses off and looking him in the eye, "you will not like what you find." That was the last straw, but no one knew it yet. No one touches the fucking aviators. Not even at night. Irakoy, oblivious to the shot he had just fired, smiles and flares his nostrils, reveling in the scene he is causing. He holds the sunglasses out for Danny, but as the actor goes to take them, they're dropped to the ground. Ferguson reaches down and snatches them before they're stepped on. He stands up, back to the future NFLer, checking the lenses for scratches. "Don't think 'bout trying nothin'," Kincaid warns again. "Getting ya ass kicked ain't gon help the wrasslin career any." "You're right," Danny responds. He looks up from his sunglasses and sees every eye on him, wishing that he'd defy the big man, so bloodthirsty for a conflict in raw, human fashion. Wrestlers might thrive in this environment, but action heroes know how to make the most of it. "But I have no intention of getting my ass kicked." As he spun around and punched Kincaid in the mouth, Danny caught a reflection of the full moon in his extensive dental work. It was beautiful.
