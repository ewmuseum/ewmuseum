Beauty and the Mind Which Contemplates It

6 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1-6-10 The phrase, "Beauty is in the eye of the beholder" is one that many have heard. It is a phrase that hearkens back all the way to ancient Greece, and yet it still has much power and meaning today, as it always has. However, its translation has changed, and in it has come many, many subsequent iterations. David Hume wrote in 1742, "Beauty in things exists merely in the mind which contemplates them." To reiterate: Beauty in things exists merely in the mind which contemplates them. It means, therefore, that the mind itself is granted a great deal of power. For it is in the mind that beauty is created. It is in the mind, which contemplates beauty, does it so exist. Michelangelo, the great Italian painter, was one such mind who was able to contemplate beauty and bring it into being with the painting of the Sistine Chapel. Leonardo Da Vinci was another such man, whose painting of the Mona Lisa created a form of beauty that some would say was unsurpassed to this day. It is regarded as one of history’s most beautiful relics and it was contemplated, at first, within the mind of Da Vinci. This concept means that anyone, no matter how intelligent or foolish, no matter how rich or poor, no matter how talented or mundane, possesses the ability to find great beauty in something which they contemplate. The two aforementioned historical figures were gifted giants of artistic thought whose concept of beauty far exceeded that of their contemporaries. However, one must beg the question then: what is beauty if contemplated within a vastly different mind? ---- The news came quickly to Cyrus Sutherland, the thirty-seven year old failure of a man who had battled internal demons all his life and, after those thirty seven years had discovered that the totality of his life was nothing. Of course, were you to tell the fiery haired individual this assessment he’d just as soon knock your teeth out of the back of your head. Nonetheless, the news indeed came swiftly to Sutherland that, after all this time, he was formally being offered entrance into the wrestling federation known as PRIME. It came like a stroke of lightning from a clear sky. Truth be told, Sutherland wasn’t even sure just when he applied to the federation, but the formal letter of inquiry came just as quickly. They were interested. To say that Cyrus Sutherland was an atypical specimen of a wrestler would be a grand understatement. The man had only trained as a wrestler for a scant year, at the most, and that was only on his free time. Yet it came quickly to him and he picked up on it. His previous experience as a combatant during a time he would prefer not to recall helped set him up for his application into the world of wrestling. Cyrus Sutherland, standing at nearly six and a half feet tall and tipping the scales well towards three hundred pounds left little doubt that he was larger than life. The bright red hair that stood atop his head, to the point of the tips beginning to cascade down slightly, made him easy to spot in a crowd. Despite the typical flashy ring attire of contemporary wrestlers, Sutherland’s was particularly rudimentary. After all, to Sutherland, it didn’t really matter what you wore so long as you were able to garner the win in the end, and so he always tended to defer to functionality over form. Yet, even in his physical demeanor, one could see the cracks in Sutherland. Deep rooted cracks that held something far more sinister and damaging than one could ever have guessed. The man was in his prime, truthfully, and yet he wore suspenders all the same. Of course, say what you will about suspenders and their commonplace use in the old days of fashion, but now they were reserved primarily for businessmen in suits. To many, suspenders were a sign of a higher upbringing. Sutherland was farther from higher upbringing than one could ever have imagined. It was the PTSD that really transformed Sutherland into what he was now. The Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder had taken him from a slightly volatile child and turned him into, more often than not, a raving maniac. Violent outbursts, mood swings, failure to focus, and a want to cause as much mayhem as possible in as little time were frequent events in Sutherland’s wake. And wrestling, for better or for worse, seemed to be the best fit for such a demeanor. It was the PTSD that generated a great number of Sutherland’s previous failures. His attempts at becoming a police officer had failed because he refused to follow standard operating procedure. Sutherland didn’t know why you should read a man his Miranda Rights when you can just as well knock his front teeth out. A fireman required a cool head. That wasn’t about to happen. A bank teller? Forget it. There was even a time, a long time ago, when Sutherland wanted to be an astronaut but, though this was never formally said, one could easily believe that Sutherland wouldn’t even be welcome on the moon. Before one knew it, he’d reduce the moon into a dust cloud whirling around the Earth. But PTSD wasn’t the sole blame for Sutherland’s faults. No, it can help to be a catalyst, yes, but the true faults, or quirks if you will, would reside within the man himself: Cyrus Sutherland. The PTSD, perhaps it helped to feed him. Perhaps he somehow embraced it. He didn’t use it a as a crutch and never, ever would he acknowledge that there was something mentally wrong with him, but one could guess that Sutherland at least had an inkling that he was short of all his marbles. Those marbles he lost just weren’t worth keeping. And the voice! That cracking, grating, shrill voice when he laughed maniacally… it was capable of making dogs howl if the correct pitch was achieved. It was an uncanny ability, when he would get into an altercation, for Sutherland’s mouth to ramble and scream almost as much as the punches and kicks he was throwing. Frequently, his constant dogging of an adversary only helped to tip the scales in his favor. As if being mentally addled and physically overpowering was not enough, he was one hell of a trash talker. More than one opponent of Sutherland’s made a tactical error simply because the man wouldn’t shut the hell up. When you combine it all: the suspenders, the hair, the voice, the demeanor, and the mentality, you find something before you that is truly horrible to behold. It is a powder keg, a time bomb, which has been tweaked and honed with such a short fuse that, undoubtedly, when it blew it was going to be catastrophic. Prior to the inquiry from PRIME, the easiest way to thwart this time bomb was to avoid it. But if he got the job? There would be no avoiding him whatsoever. And yes, to us, Cyrus Sutherland was ugly. Some would say he was a handsome man, but beneath the exterior, there was a great ugliness. Even those who were attracted to something terribly dangerous could be repelled by the great sense of trepidation that hung over his shoulders like a dark, foreboding cloud. But to Cyrus Sutherland…? "Beauty in things exists merely in the mind which contemplates them." He was a thing of beauty. He never would call himself beautiful, for Sutherland was neither a vain nor poetic man, but he would say that he was calibrated to the absolute perfect level of physical and mental precision. Take the contemplation of beauty within the fractured mind of a maniac and suddenly such a simple phrase takes on a more sinister tone. Beauty was the destruction of his enemies. It was the rush of adrenaline that came just before a staggering blow was absorbed by Sutherland’s skull. It was the gasp of the crowd as Sutherland slammed his boot directly into the crotch of his adversary. Beauty, to Sutherland, was a crimson masque screaming in agony as he glared down upon his prey and pummeled it into submission with whatever implement he could find, and if none were handy, his own fists would do the trick. Mr. Sutherland was surrounded by blasé ugliness and it was only when that bell rang that the world could explode into a beautiful kaleidoscope of carnage and misery. And his victory was frequently bittersweet, for that brought the pandemonium to an end and the others got to live in their own, boring, beautiful world. It was in this moment that Cyrus Sutherland was hit with a hunger to cause more carnage. One could say he was addicted to it. They were truly colossal words that David Hume wrote over two hundred years ago. Little did he know how they could be attributed to one as dangerous as Cyrus Sutherland. And that inquiry from PRIME, the one where they wanted to see just what kind of a man Sutherland was? It was just the first step toward a decision many would learn to regret. For you see, my friends, Cyrus Sutherland was getting into PRIME, one way or the other… …and his beauty would be a terrible sight to behold.
