The Sinners And The Saints [1]

9 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/9/05 FYI: This is the Black Dog series renamed. The original title is only revelant to a certain portion of the story. The city is so hot that everything sticks. Humidity does that. It makes everything so dry but at the same time leaves a trace of liquid. Like a cock tease, getting you all worked up and then turning over and flicking off the light switch, the job half finished. It gets that way sometimes in Carcer. Especially before a big rain. Not that stuff that falls from the sky, warm as piss and evaporates before it hits the ground. No, we are talking a good hard rain. The cold stuff. Carcer only gets that a couple times a year, but when it does, things happen. Bad things. As if the rain is a beacon for every low life and crime lord to engage in war. The humidity goes down. The rain comes down. Hard, like bullets. It smacks into the roof of my car and it startles me. Guy like me needs to have nerves of steel, but I get jumpy sometimes. That's why I smoke. Calms the nerves down. Carcer is a beautiful city, if you are into the gothic style cathedrals and dead atmosphere. Carcer City is the worst place in America. Reason I say that is because I ain't never been anywhere else. I heard it was bad in Mexico. They take your kids and put them up for ransom or some shit. Crazy stuff. The second you get children involved, then there is something wrong. If you got two consenting adults willing to pistol whip each other's brains out, let em do it. If a body shows up all charred in the river, who gives a fuck? But if that body is a kid, then someone needs to pay. The CSI's or whatever need to do whatever the hell the city pays them to do. Not like I'd trust the cops in Carcer. Most of the bastards are too busy keeping their noses out of situations that would be for the public good because of bribes. There are only a few cops in this place who are not willing to do that. Not willing to cut corners. Those are the guys you got to worry about. You get pulled over by one of them guys and it don't matter who you know or how much you got in your wallet. You get thrown away, and the key gets swallowed. Carcer is like any normal metropolis. Growth rate shooting into the toilet, buildings being torn down to be replaced by new ones that'll be vacant in a couple months. A downtown that you'd have to be clinically insane to go down in the dead of night without a Beretta and pocket full of shells. There are bars with flickering neon signs. A dirty appearance over everything. People crammed together, treating each other like shit. What makes Carcer different is that nobody who comes here thinks of it as being any different. This ain't Los Angeles, where you might become a star if you bus enough tables. This ain't New York. You come here to either be part of the crime or to be part of the clean up crew. Biggest businesses I think are probably drugs, gambling, and anything to do with hospitals. We get some young kids coming through the force thinking they gonna change everything, but they learn. Either they get a bullet in the back or they protect the criminals. We are on the maw of Hell. I know it. Every single day I wake up and know that all Satan gots to do is stab his pitchfork hard into the ceiling and it's gonna come through the middle of Fifth and Macy. But I enjoy that. At least I ain't the baddest guy in the area. I love the neon landscape. It makes me know that the place is alive beyond the usual phony perfect crystal clear bullshit everyone puts up. The people here know who owns this town. They know who runs the streets once the sun goes down. And the only reason they go out is to pick up a broad or a six pack and some smokes. Or maybe all of them. The rain is pounding down hard. Not even an hour and the streets are flooding. The tires on the old Sedan squeak and squeal as I try to get myself to Westchester and Holland. The lamps flicker on and off as I try to make my way to her. To find the reason I'm out here on such a bad night in such a bad way. She has brunette hair. Many girls do. I can't stand that fake ass blonde shit myself. But I know a lot do, because the ratio is three blondes to every one redhead. Which is a cryin' shame, because the redheads are usually the best looking ones. They are real. Natural. But this brunette, she's a dame. I picked her up one time, and she smelled great. Like strawberries. Unlike me, unlike the rest of us, she don't deserve this. Carcer is a shithole, and while I love it, it's not pretty or passionate. It's soulless and cruel. It twists you into pieces and throws them away. Then you look at yourself in the mirror with a bunch of scars and half an ear gone and you don't know what the hell is goin' on. She doesn't deserve that. She deserves something better. Oh so better. She deserves to be free. To get a second chance. I'm gonna give her that second chance. I'm gonna tell her I love her. That she is safe. That whatever it is she has to hide from she don't have to hide no more. That I'm a hero and she is the damsel and we are all gonna live happily ever after in a beautiful castle. Or some shit like that. I see her. Hiding under an umbrella as the rain pounds away at the concrete and the buildings, taking little microscopic pieces of them with it. She's beautiful, in stiletto heels that hurt to look at. She's got a skirt that's too short, and a shirt that has the stomach cut out. She isn't like the rest of the whores. She's top quality, or so I was told. She's expensive because of her looks and skills. She looks like Audrey Hepburn in that movie Breakfast At Tiffany's. She ain't as elegant or whatever, but she could be if she wanted to. I cruise on by, playing it cool. I slow down, and reach over to roll down the window. I pull right beside her, and she stops, looking over to me. 'Mighty fine weather we got here, wouldn't you say?' She had spunk. Had a personality. I heard sometime some college student wanted to just talk, and they talked about philosophy for five hours. Ran the bill up good, but she did it for free because she has character. I blow out a smoke ring causally. My hands are shaking. 'Hey, even the farmers need some love sometimes.' My speech is different because of the cigarette in my mouth. She comes closer. I pull down my shades and look into her eyes. She recognizes my eyes. Everyone does. 'Rashard? What you doin' out on a night like this. You hate the rain?' She knows me too well. My hands still shake. 'Yeah, figured I would liven up your night. Figured nobody would be out in this shit weather anyway.' My hands don't shake anymore. They visibly tremble. 'What's the matter? Want me to hop on in and warm you up?' She's so sweet to me. Oh so sweet. 'Nah. I just got a message to deliver.' I wasn't a postman, but I was the kind of guy that could tell you something useful every now and again. This is going to be hard. I'm not sure if I can do this. I rehearsed it a hundred times in front of the mirror and I vomited into the sink twice. I'm never nervous. Not unless it rains. 'Yeah, and what is that? Mister Green loves Miss White? Come on, let's get out of here.' The cigarette falls off of my lips. Then, I tense up. 'Wolfenden sends his regards.' It goes away. The fear. The anxiety. She smells of strawberries. Shuuuuuuuuuuu! The silencer makes a whisper of the gunshot. She lingers there for a frozen second. Shuuuuuuuuuuu! I place another one right next to the hole in her head. I throw the Beretta on the driver side seat and put the pedal to the floor. I can hear the screech. I didn't want to stay. I saw her fall to the sidewalk becoming drenched. Lifeless. You stay around and you are likely to get revenged upon. She smelled like strawberries. She was the most beautiful girl in the whole world. A mile away, I light up another cigarette. I take a deep inhale as I think about what I did. I'll cash the check in the morning. *** The lights flash hues of red and blue in the night sky. The sirens scream and holler, taking away the element of surprise. The police car sped along, gaining speed as it went. It slowed down momentarily as it came off the street and pulled into an expansive drive way. The car was miles away from the downtown area, but for the men inside the vehicle, the stench of it was strongest here. The mansion was beautiful. White marble pillars and three stories. Almost blindingly white. The driveway was in a upside down u shape, and at the middle of the top was the walkway to the home. The car screeched to a halt. The siren still blared. And suddenly, two men got out of the vehicle and started walking. One looked of somewhat Hispanic origin, the other purely Caucasian. The Caucasian man had an tan old fashioned hat, with his work suit and brown trench coat over top of it. The Hispanic man was in a black sweatshirt and black khakis. Both men had badges on chains across their necks. Neither man said a thing. They didn't need to. The two men walked side by side, making their way quickly to the door. The Caucasian man pounded on the white door, something nobody had ever done. Stuff like that was treated with elegant care usually. 'Open up! This is Brandon Youngblood and Emilio Rage of the Carcer City Police Department! We have a warrant out for the arrest of Lawrence Wolfenden in association with the murder of Quinn Morgan! Open the damn door or we will have to use force!' Without a second given, Rage kicked the door open. Brandon looked over to him with a look of disgust on his face. Brandon was the kind of cop who did things strictly by the book. He never strayed away from the letter of the law. Rage, however, was new to the force. Still wide eyed, but he was rational. He knew he had to cut corners sometimes to nail the bad guys. And while his heart was pure, it never seemed that way to Brandon. The door being kicked in startled the people within. There were two kids just standing there. A young girl and a young boy. They glanced up to the pair as they made their way forward. Suddenly, an older woman in a robe came storming in, making sure to kick up a fuss as she came towards the two. The inside of the mansion was just as beautiful as the outside, but seemed to be bare. Looked like they had gotten there just in time to snatch up the last piece of the jigsaw puzzle that was the leading crime family in Carcer City. The Wolfenden's. 'What is the meaning of this? This damn witch hunt has gone far enough!' She belted out of her vocal cords. Most times, it would have startled the two. But not this time. They simply flashed their badges and went on their way. She decided to get in the way. Brandon had seen her on many occasions, being the mouth piece for all that was wrong in this dreadful city. He wasn't going to let he belt out angel tunes with a devil's heart. He shoved her. Hard. She ran into a nearby mirror with her arms outward to cushion the blow. The force shattered the mirror, causing cuts all along her arms and body. The pain took hold as blood started pouring out of her. Tears soon followed, and she just laid there, weeping and yelling. 'Oh god! Oh god! I'll have your fucking badges for this, you fucking pigs! Oh god!' Youngblood and Rage went force, looking for a sign. A clue. Something as to where Lawrence was. They saw a pair of hard oak doors closed, sealing off the study from the rest of the house. They had heard stories about how people could have a Mexican Standoff with that room. But this wasn't going to be a standoff. Brandon had nailed so many of the family members within Carcer City and no door was going to stop him for liberating the city from this trash. He kicked the door in, and looked over. Sitting at a desk was Lawrence, in a red robe smoking a corn cob pipe. He snickered as they came in, running his hand through his graying hair. 'You got nothin'. You stupid sons of bitches! I'll have your badges tomorrow morning and your heads by lunch!' The venom was powerful. Rage snarled. 'Lawrence Wolfenden, you have the right to remain silent. Anything you say can and will be used in a court of law-' Brandon was quickly interrupted. 'What am I being charged with? This is some bullshit witch hunt! I told you jackasses that I have no connection with the crime family.' Rage piped in. 'That's not what Clark said. He's pulled jobs for you before. And he came to us to nail your balls to the wall. He's shown us fifteen bodies you had him bury out by the harbor. And the rest of the family broke their silence too the second they heard that the hit man was spillin' the beans. Hacete coger!' Wolfenden's face went pale. He's stored a thousand secrets at that harbor. And they were all grabbing at him now, trying to pull him under. He laughed. Not out of humor, but of unbridled fear. 'You know something, Youngblood?' Brandon looked over to him. He felt no sympathy. 'What?' 'If I'm going to Hell, and I know I am, then I'm going to take you fuckers kicking and screaming all the way down with me.' He picked up a nearby shot glass as he stood up and downed it. The moon shined brightly. To brightly. BOOM! BOOM! BOOM! BOOM! BOOM! The metal of the gun reflected the light. The second he grabbed for the pistol on his desk, Brandon pulled a Colt single action army and cocked the hammer back, firing round after round into Lawrence Wolfenden. The last piece of trash to ruin Carcer City. He couldn't even get a shot off. He stood there for a second, before collapsing to the red carpet on his back. He looked at the ceiling, trying to make sense of everything. It was a split second decision. And Rage knew the consequences. So did Brandon. Brandon turned over to Rage, looking at him in his eyes with tears forming. 'GO! GO RUN!' Rage stood there for a moment. He couldn't move. Brandon brought the gun up and cocked the hammer back one more time. 'Better one life ruined than two! I got one bullet left Rage, and if you know what's what, you'll get the fuck out of here.' Rage back pedaled. He then took off, running as fast as his legs could. And he disappeared. The sirens disappeared. The flashing lights disappeared. Brandon Youngblood was alone. He looked over to Lawrence Wolfenden, looking at the slime choking on his own blood with his very last breathes. Justice was a cold hard bitch sometimes. But Wolfenden knew what he was doing. That son of a bitch knew what he was doing the entire time. Brandon walked over to his downed catch. He looked into the eyes of the man he wanted to bring to justice so much. And all he could do is choke out a laugh, blood spraying out. 'To your last breath, you'll pay. You'll fuckin' pay, you piece of shit cop!' Brandon couldn't stand it anymore. He pulled the trigger. BAM! Lawrence Wolfenden collapsed, completely devoid of life. His brains were splattered on the fine carpet it must have cost thousands to buy. That son of a bitch knew what he was doing the entire time. Brandon put the gun back in his coat, the chamber still warm. As he walked out of the study, the wife screamed and rushed into the room. All he could hear were her sobs. Brandon looked over the children he had left behind. The daughter was beautiful. She'd grow up to be a beautiful young woman. The boy would grow up to be a football player or something like that. But at this moment, their eyes were glazed over. They understood what had happened. But they were unable to speak. To move. If anything, those eyes pierced him, like daggers to the chest. He walked outside, looking over the great horizon. He pulled a pack of cigarettes out of his coat and lit one up, as he sat down on the front porch. The world was so full of possibility. The horizon stretched on forever and ever. The sound of sirens played in the background. The sounded like a crescendo. That son of a bitch knew what he was doing the entire time. That son of a bitch. *** The judge screamed of dignity and morals. It did not take long. No matter how many people went up to bat for him, no matter what Rage tried to tell the investigators, it made no use. Brandon Youngblood, former respected cop, killed an man in cold blood. In his own home. The fact that there was a warrant out for his arrest was never brought up, and the Carcer City Police Department never had a record of such a warrant being issued. The same judge who signed the nonexistent warrant was the one belting out the charges. It was ironic, in such a sick way. It showed just how far the sin went in Carcer City. The Wolfenden family was powerful in other areas of the country. They pooled their resources and strings to make the rat bastard cop who had shut down their operations in Carcer City go away for a long time. He was sentenced to life in prison. He had no fear. Only guilt and worry. Single dad. A daughter who was merely eight. She cried in a court room when she heard daddy was going away forever. Someone in the family would take care of her, sure. But it wasn't the same. She loved her father deeply. He was being punished for doing his job. For protecting and serving. Before being transported to his Hell, Brandon tried to explain that his daughter should always do the right thing. She could only sob and hold him tight while the armed officers took him away. Lindsay cried a lot after that. Her name was Lindsay. The daughter that got caught up in this whole mess. The bystander that got caught in the crossfire.
