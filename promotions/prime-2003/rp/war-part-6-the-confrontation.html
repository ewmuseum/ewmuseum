War (Part 6)- The Confrontation

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: I HATE TRAFFIC JAMS A thick amount of low, gray clouds hung over Raleigh, North Carolina, much to the chagrin of those in the weather business who deemed July 4th to be "another balmy Saturday evening". Almost every weather forecaster predicted sunny skies and temperatures to reach a high of 96 degrees. With heavy amounts of rain and violent thunderstorms expected through 2 AM, a lot of the firework shows were being canceled or rescheduled for later dates. Most just saw the unfortunate change of weather as only a coincidence, blaming the matter on meteorological phenomenon they couldn't fully comprehend. For Steven Connors, and anybody with the last name Daniels, nothing was ever coincidental, not in their lines of work. They each looked into the endless gray pool of awkward shapes and surmised their own conclusions. Christian Daniels The nefarious biker heard a clap of thunder in the distance and sighed, leaning back in his rocking chair atop the third floor balcony. He chugged a bottle of Jack Daniels straight and lit up an imaginary cigarette. Steven Connors had been quiet for about two months and the leader of Death knew the down on his luck detective would soon strike. The maelstrom that is his mind concocted every possible combination of retaliation for someone in Steven's mental state. He was one desperate son of a bitch, Christian thought, but he was also resilient, enough to savagely slaughter two members of his family after his brother's wife bit the dust. Who in the household would he target now that the only relevant person left in his life had fallen at their hands? How deep into his bag of tricks would he reach to acquire the proper vengeance? The second smack of thunder, powerful enough to knock out power in surrounding homes, indicated Steven was going for the Hail Mary pass. Christian patted his pocket three times, ensuring the two inch steel chains were ready in case someone came for his head. Steven Connors Today was either going to be the best or worst day of Steven's life. After the few phone conversations with Caleb Daniels, the two finally met face to face a week ago. Steven put on the acting performance of a lifetime, doing everything in his power to convince Caleb that ending his situation was a necessity. He told the young son of Christian many times that living in the shadows of death was impossible, and despite the transgressions committed against him, he was willing to put the past behind him and look toward the future. Every word that came from his mouth was a bold lie. In actuality, he wanted the shock value of Caleb coming back into Christian's life to leave the group vulnerable long enough for him to pick all of them off in rapid succession. He would have to act swift and without hesitation, otherwise the numbers game would overwhelm him. Christian would be the first casualty, a strategic tactic which was both outrageous and intelligent. If the snake was decapitated, how could the rest of the body continue forward? Everybody else would be sitting ducks, ripe for the picking. The massive bolt of thunder brought the former detective's focus outside. A fleeting moment passed before waves of rain descended from the sky. Steven picked up his blackberry, stuck on the weather report from the previous evening, and scoffed. Some forecast. He looked over toward the guns from his rack lined out on the kitchen table, all fully loaded. It didn't hurt to check a second time, though. Caleb Daniels The nightmare of meeting the man who brought him into this world haunted Caleb for as long as he could remember dreams. Often times, it was the same horrific scene: Caleb was in the desert, imitating the long walk of Jesus Christ. This was something he wanted to do so that his faith would shine through any shadow of doubt. It was Day 3 and the worn young man stalked, hills and blazing hot sun the only objects his eyes could see in any direction. Without warning, a massive wall of sand made its way toward Caleb. He looked around for an avenue of escape, but the wall encapsulated him, leaving him resigned to accept the fate. If this was what God wanted, he would endure for his almighty creator. The blockade stopped and a figure emerged into his line of sight, but it wasn't the holy father, rather it was the one known as the Devil. He stood seven feet tall and wore black garb from head to toe. A sinister smile donned his grizzled mug. In an instant, the temperature dropped from about 100 to 15, leaving Caleb shivering. Caleb's eyes darted back and forth as the figure came closer and placed a firm, tattooed hand on his shoulder. The power was enough to bring Caleb to his knees. "I come bearing a message, young Caleb. All your life, you have been mislead by the optimistic culture you live in. You have been told that you are the Chosen One and will lead many people to a better life. You have been told that your father is a great and wise man. However, everybody has lied to you. Your father does not reside in the heavens...but rather in the depths...of hell. Caleb, I am your father." The visions always shook Caleb awake, leaving him in fright. Today, he had to relive the moment one more time while sitting in his black Nissan outside the Smith residence. The thunder jolted Caleb and when the drops poured down from the heavens, Caleb knew they symbolized the sand from his dreams. It was surrounding him. This time though, he had to walk through it and face the Devil in his own palace. Caleb did the sign of the cross and said a silent prayer before opening the door and stepping into the rain. The Confrontation Discarding a bottle over his left shoulder into the trashcan, Christian Daniels walked toward his couch, intent on sitting there for a few hours and catching up on his DVR. The past few days were nothing short of criminal. Having been away from the business for almost seven years, Christian forgot how truly grueling a schedule those at the elite level of wrestling endured. Seldom did they get a moment's rest. An entire week off was even rarer. Despite the fact he wrestled once in a blue moon, media personalities wanted every waking moment of his time and the effects were showing. The Biker listened to the pops in his knees as he descended onto the leather cushion and searched for the remote. There was no use in taking the bike out for a ride in such weather considering traffic would still be hellacious. He'd call a few of the boys over and get a card game going, make the minions deal with the traffic. The knock on the door was light, almost timid, and Christian didn't hear it the first time. Nobody bothered installing a doorbell, so Christian went about his business until the second knock tilted his head. Muttering a swear under his breath, The Biker rose and mumbled about occupying the basement for the remainder of the evening so others would have to answer the door. The third knock caused his remaining patients to dissipate, "I'm coming goddammit, hold on." Grasping the knob, Christian threw open the door and saw a figure which made him glad he didn't release the knob, otherwise he would have fallen back in fright. Standing in front of him was someone equal in height and very close in weight, dressed in the same black garb Christian frequently wore. The only difference was the man on the other side of the door had a cloth around his neck. Christian didn't need a degree in rocket science to recognize the signs: the long weary face, raised forehead, big eyebrows, round complexion, and eternal five o clock shadow. He dreaded this day and hoped it would never come. "...Son" Caleb Daniels stood with arms folded across his stomach, a red Bible clutched tightly in his right hand. If Christian looked down, he would see the hands of his son slightly trembling. He didn't want to be here anymore than Christian wanted him there, but sacrifices had to be made during times of war. "Hello, father." The young man said, attempting to keep his composure. He was succeeding better than most. Years of working at the church hearing stories of great tragedy strengthened his resolve and kept his base sturdy when he needed it the most. Christian went to reply, but words wouldn't come out. Caleb was the child he abandoned to live the lifestyle that got him into the powerful position of today. He often thought about what became of the child and if he had managed to survive in the cruel world. The ink on his left wrist was indicative that The Biker never forgot about him though. Christian still had to show the strong alpha male presence and went to speak, "Wha..." Caleb reached out and placed a firm hand on his father's shoulder. The strength in his hand almost buckled Christian. "It's alright, Dad, you don't need to speak. May I come in?" "Of course. You uh...Want me to get you anything, a bee...Water, soda?" "I'm fine, Dad, help yourself though." Christian definitely needed a beer to relax his body. While The Biker gingerly walked toward the kitchen, Caleb observed the surroundings that his father's line of work brought him. The finest of luxuries were at every turn and Caleb knew none of it came through honest money. He felt a little disgusted sitting down on the leather couch and looking over at the 80 inch plasma television mounted on the wall. "You've done quite well for yourself, Dad." The way Caleb said his name sliced through Christian's heart. The last time someone called him "Dad" was many lifetimes ago, and the palindrome took some time to register. Christian shut the door on the refrigerator and walked over, carrying a bottle of Jack Daniels. He knew Caleb probably wouldn't be too fond of his father drinking in front of him, but Christian hadn't abandoned his nature for anybody and he wasn't going to start now. Christian looked at Caleb's cloth and gave a nervous nod of the head, "Same to you. Sure you don't want somethin, last offer." "I had a big bottle of water before coming over here," Caleb lied, "So I'll be alright for a while." "Fair enough..." Christian looked around for a place to sit. The recliner was too far away and he didn't want to further alienate his kid, but he didn't want to get too close. Christian couldn't handle being that close when Caleb just walked through his door. After two more seconds of deliberation, Christian opted to stand and cross his arms, bottle wedged underneath his right arm. "So...How's your mom?" "She's doing fantastic, still has the same state job and is now the boss of that entire printing department, been working there for twenty years." Christian cracked a half grin and sipped on his beverage, "Never figured her as the kind who'd hop around lookin' fer work." Both Christian and Caleb wish that sentence hadn't come out, and both emitted stereo grunts. Caleb's eyes shifted back and forth, looking over at the glass picture case next to the television. His eyes landed upon a picture of Devin Shakur with the Universal Championship, sitting in Bartman's seat at Wrigley Field. "Saw you on television with that Devin Shakur character. He live here?" With a loud sigh, Christian responded, "Yeah, he owns the place, we just mooch off him." "I thought this was the family home. How'd he come into it?" The situation was about to go from bad to worse. Abandoning Caleb was one thing, but taking Devin in was something Christian didn't desire elaborating about, especially considering the path he and the rest of Death took Devin down. Christian took another big swig from his bottle. He needed to coat this answer so drawing on the sympathy card was possible. "Well...Group kinda took him after findin' out that his father abused him. Kid is a wreck under that shirt, lemme tell ya, don't think any of yer holy remedies could cure the scars he's got." Caleb felt a genuine amount of remorse, even though he heard vile rumors about Devin through the grapevine of gossip. "That is unfortunate, nobody should have to go through that." "Got that right," Christian replied with a firm nod. It was the most conviction he uttered through the conversation so far. "What became of his father?" Christian instinctively shot his son a hard glare, considering that was the entire reason the Smith crew returned the United States. Upon seeing the fear in Caleb's eyes, the sternness dropped a few notches and Christian elevated his eyebrows. This was another question he really didn't want to answer. "...Wow." Caleb said, interlocking his fingers and shifting his head back and forth. He had been in the presence of murders before, but none of them held the impact that his father did. Not to mention the fact Caleb was on assignment from someone with strong associations to law enforcement. "Can we pretend I didn't ask that?" Caleb said "Long as you tell nobody." Christian replied. "You didn't admit to anything." "Damn right I didn't." From the side room, Devin Shakur walked down the hall and saw Christian standing by the couch. He made the outline of another person sitting on the couch and put a hand against his smooth face, pondering who it could be. Once he saw the eerie resemblance, Devin bolted around the corner and pressed himself against the wall with a stunned expression. He whispered "Holy shit" before tiptoeing back across the carpet, hoping no creaks would give away his position, and crept toward the stairs on the right side of the house. He had to tell everybody else even though nobody would believe him. Caleb tried to pick the conversation back up and change the subject, "So...Who all lives here, just you and Devin?" Christian walked over toward the wall next to the television and leaned against it, careful to avoid tapping the expensive plasma screen. "The whole gang lives here: Dev, me, Chris, Rachel at times, Chris's wife, girl he had a crush on since 2nd grade, kinda cute when you think about it...His best friend, Jason, and our financial consultant, Michael." "Must be some fun times you guys have." "All of us have busy lives so none of us're here much, but the poker games are pretty ridiculous, way better than what you see on TV." "I'm not particularly fond of television, too much glamorization of the negative." Caleb responded, reaching over and pushing a button that reclined the couch back, giving his long legs some room. "Ah, the church, right...How'd you...How'd you come into that?" Christian inquired. Caleb didn't want to give him the honest response. Ever since it was lucid that Christian wasn't coming back into his life, Caleb needed a rock in his life to guide him through the troubling times. The church was the best option available at the time and before long, Caleb became synonymous with Knightdale Methodist Church. So he chose to give his father the standard answer, "Got into it as a kid and never wanted to leave, heart kinda wouldn't let me." "Had to give that from Mama, because I sure ain't give it to you." Christian said, taking another massive swig from his bottle. Both gave a courtesy smile to one another and moved their heads. This wasn't going remotely well and when Caleb revealed his intentions, the conversation would likely become even murkier and more opaque. "You pretty much live out of the church?" "I've found myself doing that lately. The economy leaving so many people without direction has pretty much left me to sleep in my office, taking calls night and day." "Must be pretty rewardin." "Definitely. I enjoy it." Caleb said while nodding. "Good to find somethin' you like doin'." Christian said, hoping Caleb's eyes wouldn't jump toward any of the tattoos that decorated his arms. "So...Dad, I came here because there is something we need to talk about," Caleb said. He couldn't avoid the business at hand much longer. He didn't want to be sucked into a casual back and forth with his father. "I figured ye ain't come here without a reason. What's on yer mind?" Christian replied. Caleb blurted the reason out, quicker than he wanted to, "You and a man named Steven Connors have had some issues, and he asked me to come here and negotiate a truce between the two of you." Immediately after hearing the name Steven Connors, Christian's blood started to boil. He slammed the bottle of Jack down on the mahogany table, sending Caleb out of his seat and taking him aback. The possibility of having to live with someone who had such a short fuse entered Caleb's mind. Perhaps Christian did the right thing by leaving him with the more mature parent. Caleb continued, a little frightened. The man he was looking at now wasn't the same one who stuttered over himself answering the door, "He wants to stop the conflict that has been going on with you two. He says that he's tired of living with all the death that has happened." Christian's rage intensified and he started gritting his teeth, "Well, tell that little leech the only way I'd leave his sorry ass alone would be if he left me alone." Caleb responded, "He's told me that you and yours have done some things which require you to be brought to justice, so he can't fulfill that request." "Then I'm not agreein' to nothin'" Christian emphatically stated. "You murdered his brother and brother's wife. Someone who does that..." Caleb felt empowered, like he was teaching a sermon, "Someone like that-" "Someone like what?" Christian barked, "Look...Son, if you wanna bring up the death card, prick killed two of my own..." Christian gaged the look on his son's face and reasserted himself, "Yeah, I figured he left that little tidbit out when tryin'ta make you drink his fuckin' Kool-Aid." Caleb held up an apologetic hand, "I'm sorry, Dad, I didn't mean to anger you. Steven came to me and asked-" Christian interrupted his son again, "Yeah, I know exactly what he asked and I know exactly what he's tryin' to do..." Christian muttered to himself, "And people wanna call me sick." Caleb was on the defensive, and even though he despised the lifestyle his father had gone into, there was still a portion of him that had to respect the man pacing across the room, foaming at the mouth, and eager to give this day some very special fireworks of his own. "I really didn't mean to come here and start trouble. I'm sorry, Dad." Christian stopped pondering his evil schemes when he heard the validity escape from Caleb's lips. He was floored by the thought of his own son being used as a weapon against him, but none of the fault could be placed on the boy's shoulders. Steven could be a very convincing character if anybody gave him enough time to work his charm and magic. Caleb stood from his seat and walked toward the door. Christian's long arm reached out and grabbed his shoulder, stopping him in place. Christian moved around and looked his son eye to eye, something he couldn't have done give minutes ago. "I know you didn't son, and I ain't mad atcha." "I appreciate that..." Caleb said, nervously looking back for the door. "Perhaps one day I can come back and see you, we do a little more catching up and a lot less business?" "Door will always be open for ya, kid." Christian replied, dropping his paw for a hearty handshake before throwing open the door and ushering Caleb onto the 'Welcome' mat. The two had another long moment of silence before Caleb looked across at his father, "See ya around, Dad." "Take care, son." Christian replied. After exchanging a nod, Christian turned and gently shut the door. Caleb let out a thunderous sigh and walked toward the car. He swore off alcohol as a teenager, but his father had something right when he was swinging that bottle of Jack around. This would be a day he needed to forget. Christian kept his head down and walked out into the living room. Devin Shakur emerged from his hiding place and had Michael, Jason, and Chris trailing behind him. "That is so fucking sick." Devin said, walking over toward the kitchen and snagging a bottle of Pepsi from the refrigerator. "How does a man do that to another through the son?" Michael remarked. Jason chimed in, "I mean...Yeah, we've done some cruel shit to the guy, ruined his family and put his name through the mud, but you don't use someone's kid against them like that." Christian raised his head, cheeks full and bright red with uncontrollable anger. His breathing increased and he looked for anything that wasn't nailed down. The living room table got kicked over first, followed by the glass shelf next to the television. Fragments shattered all over the floor while Christian marched across and threw chairs around the kitchen. Devin and Jason ducked out of the way as a chair sailed overhead, leaving holes in the wall just above them. Nobody in the room wanted to say anything, even if they were all tallying the damages in their heads. They figured soon Christian would beckon them to battle and checked to ensure they had the essentials. Christian slung all the food off the kitchen island and dented the steel refrigerator. Still in a blithering rage, Christian stormed over to the phone and hit a few buttons. Steven Connors sat at his kitchen table when the phone rang. He barely glanced over, indifferent as to who was calling him. Nobody could bring him down from the high he would be on when Christian walked out of his home a shell of his former self. Steven reached out and grabbed the receiver. The voice on the other end was macabre, the sound of pure evil. "Count your fuckin' hours, bitch, cuz they are gonna have to use all the fuckin' search teams available in the whole fuckin' world to find your sorry ass when I'm done with you!" Christian slammed the phone down and assembled the troops. Well, maybe there was one person who could bring him down... Six feet down.
