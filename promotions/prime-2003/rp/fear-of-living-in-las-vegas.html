Fear of Living in Las Vegas

undated

Date: Late March, 2009 “…and then I killed Jay Phoenix.” The New England weather had mellowed in recent weeks. The last of the snow had melted, the ground had begun to thaw, and in some places a few shoots had begun to spring up through the earth. None of this made any difference to Jared Sykes; even if the temperature had dropped into the single digits it was not uncommon to see him lounging around outside in nothing more than shorts and a sweatshirt. The obscurity of his habits extended far beyond the ring, and far beyond the blue costume that now hung in his closet. The weather wasn’t the only change. Having made himself something of a hermit throughout most of the winter, Jared had once again started opening up to the people around him. He had started to mend a friendship he’d once thought irreparable, one whose absence only helped to diminish an already-fragile mental state. “There is no way in hell I’m going to believe that you killed Jay Phoenix.” Four months ago Mervin Humperdink was confined to a hospital bed, recovering a series of surgeries to correct a subdural hematoma, the result of injuries he had suffered at the hands of Alexandra Pierce and her Dead Man’s Hand, a penalty incurred for being too close to someone who had dared challenge the humanity of The Director. The attack had been designed to rip at the heart of Jared Sykes and in that it had been successful. Mervin did not blame his friend for any of this, having grown somewhat accustomed to the life of a wrestler after ten years in the business, and even though a part of Jared Sykes believed this it did not prevent him from letting the thought chew through him. The two now sat around the patio table of Jared’s new home, far removed from the small apartment in Cambridge where the group had spent so much time over the last decade. “I sure did,” Jared continued, taking a short sip from a nearby bottle of Pepsi, “Killed him with a shovel. See, it’s more economical that way. If you use a shovel, then not only to you have a handy weapon, but you’ve also got the means to dig a great big hole right there on the spot. It’s the ultimate in nefarious convenience. Honestly, I’m surprised more people don’t do it that way.” Conversations like this were not uncommon between the two before the accident, but only recently had Jared entertained the idea of being this absurd again. In the days leading up to his return to active competition, Infinite Gauntlet notwithstanding, he’d grown quiet and reserved. Only lately had the part of his personality he’d oft referred to as “the inner retard” begun to show itself. “Jared, seriously. You did not kill Jay Phoenix.” “I’ve got the shovel out in the shed. If you want I can show you. It’s right next to the treasure map, and…” “Jared!” “What?” “You did not kill Jay Phoenix. I saw him in Hawaii while you were wandering the parking lot after we arrived. He’s actually a pretty nice guy.” “Alright, fine,” Jared conceded, throwing up his hands, “I didn’t kill Jay Phoenix. He’ll probably live a long, healthy life, have lots of babies – or hatchlings, whatever, and continue to torture us with that fruity little theme song of his.” “Wait. The guy who makes us all dress like a blueberry - for the second time in a year,” Mervin’s words were deliberate, incredulous; a futile attempt at making a point, “is going to have the balls to call someone else ‘fruity’?” Jared Sykes, the man who wrestled under the guise of King Blueberry, sat unwavering. “Seriously, Merv’, the song’s fuck-awful.” “Well, I’ll make sure to tell Jay when I see him at,” Mervin paused, thinking for a moment, “where are we going again this week?” “Not we, I.” The liveliness in Jared’s voice began to fade, his demeanor gradually becoming colder. “Why just you? I thought that this was a team thing. The deal was that if you were back on the road that I was tagging along. I know you still think that there’s something wrong here, so I figured that…” Mervin was cut off before he could finish the thought. “Don’t.” “But I’m not sca…,” he pleaded, before he was cut off yet again. “Seriously, don’t. This time it’s just me. No you, no Lloyd, no Parker, no anyone.” Mervin slouched back in his chair, defeated. From the expression on Jared’s face it was clear that this was not an argument he was in any position of winning. “Alright. Whatever. Can you at least give me a reason as to why none of us are going along?” Jared steadied himself, cleared his throat, and leaned forward in his chair. “Because the show’s in Las Vegas, simple as that. You know what else is in Las Vegas? Sin City’s corporate headquarters, where the power is. And if that’s where the power is, then there’s probably a fair bet that Ale…,” he cut himself off this time and took a deep breath, trying to draw in the courage to say her name. “That’s where she is, or where she could be, and I’ll be fucked if I’m bringing anyone other than myself within arms reach of that again. Once in my lifetime is enough.” Mervin sat in silence, however Jared’s voice had raised, whether done consciously or not. “Once is e-fucking-nough!” - - - - - The scene at gate A-19 of Logan Airport was one of quiet panic. Jared had woken up early that morning, knowing full well that it would take a substantial amount of motivation to get out of the house and get on the plane. Since his conversation with Mervin only a few days prior the anxiety had started to take hold. He had no way of knowing whether he would run into Desade in Las Vegas, but the possibility was always there. It was the possibility that had dug deep into his subconscious and rooted itself firmly in place. He had called Sebastian Parker, a licensed psychiatrist and close friend, the day before he was set to depart and had begged for a refill on his prescription of Xanax, a prescription that Sebastian had been hoping to wean him off. Asking had turned into reasoning that quickly devolved into pleading, and only then did Sebastian give in (and against his better judgment). As he stood against the glass window of his gate, staring out onto the tarmac, Jared slowly turned the clear orange bottle in his pocket, debating whether to pop one before getting on board. The sounds of Kamelot coming from his other pocket indicated an incoming call, though it took him a moment to realize it. As he glanced at the screen to see who the call was coming from he pressed the volume button, muting the remainder of the ring. “Not now,” he whispered to himself. He waited for the ringing to complete, flipped open the phone, and shut it off before replacing it in his pocket. Exhaling, Jared leaned forward against the glass, the window cooling the sweat that had formed on his brow. “Now boarding rows one through seven for flight 286 with non-stop service to Las Vegas, Nevada.” The announcement caught Jared’s attention, and he slowly turned his head in direction of the counter. He shuffled through his pockets for a moment, producing his ticket and reading the flight information from it: first class, row 2. Slinging his carry-on over his shoulder he turned away from the window and started towards the gate, but not before downing one of the pills from plastic orange bottle. - - - - - The last few days leading up to Las Vegas were nothing compared to being there. By the time the sun had set on his first night in the city of sin, Jared Sykes had descended into a full-blown paranoia. From the moment he stepped off the plane his eyes darted from person to person, paying particular attention to scout any redheaded women he happened to see. The further away from the plane he got the worse he seemed to feel. During the cab ride to his hotel he sat in the back seat, his eyes closed and his fingers pressed against his temples, the fear of not making it to his hotel – of being rerouted down a side street and “dealt with” taking hold. He breathed a small sigh of relief when he finally arrived at his hotel and stepped foot in his room, but even that was short-lived as he quickly found himself tearing through room looking for any surveillance that may or may not be there. During his time working for SCCW he had heard rumors, none of which he had evidence to substantiate, but he had no desire to confirm any of them first-hand. By the time he was done satiating his paranoia, his room was in shambles, and he sat panting in the corner surrounded by blankets and covered in a cold sweat. The prescription bottle, full before he’d boarded the plane, was now half empty, its contents either consumed or spilled across the room. A knock at the door startled him to his feet. “Sir? Excuse me, sir?” the voice from the hallway asked. Jared slowly stalked his way to the door, and as he drew closer his eyes scanned the room for something, anything, that he might use as a weapon if the opportunity presented itself. “Sir? Is everything okay?” Jared disconnected the receiver from the telephone, holding it tight in his right hand. Peering quickly through the peep hole in the door, he disengaged the lock and opened the door just slightly. “Yeah? What’s up?” he said, exposing only part of his face to the young attendant in the hall. “Well sir, we’ve received a few complaints from some of the people on this floor, and we wanted to make sure that everything was okay.” It was obvious that the young man was trying to look beyond Jared and into the room, hoping to gauge the condition of the situation. In his paranoia Jared sensed this, positioning himself to better block the view. “Yeah, fine. I’m great. Everything’s great,” his words were short, manic. “Can I go now?” “Just please try to keep it under control sir. I’d prefer to not have to come back.” “Right. Got it. You won’t.” Before the attendant could finish his thought the door was closed and locked. Jared had pressed his back up against it and was inhaling deep, as though the young attendant posed some threat to his well-being. Jared darted for the bathroom, using the phone’s receiver that he still held to turn on the light before tossing it casually onto the countertop. The water from the sink felt cool on his hands, and he leaned forward to splash some of it onto his face. For a moment he forgot where he was, losing himself in the refreshing water. He stood up slowly, expecting to see the reflection of someone else in the mirror when he stood. Recognizing only himself was cause for a smile, letting the water drip from his face. “What the fuck is wrong with you, Jared?” he asked of his reflection, “And what the fuck are you doing here?”
