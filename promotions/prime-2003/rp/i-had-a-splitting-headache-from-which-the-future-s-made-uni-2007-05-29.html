I Had A Splitting Headache From Which The Future's Made (Uni Title Match vs. Nova, ReV 129)

29 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 29, 2007 "Lanni?" "Hey Lindz! Sorry, I just got back from a board meeting. One of the VPs...remember Leslie?...is about to go on maternity leave and she's talking about not coming back. Nice of her to let us know now when she's two weeks away from going. Ugh. Nightmare. So what's up with you?" "I think my iPod's possessed." The Amelia Earhart Expressway sounded like the Homestead Speedway on race day, with cars flying by on their way to and from the Louis Armstrong International Airport outside of New Orleans. It was that in-between break between lunchtime rush and the end of the workday crawl, and traffic was relatively light. Speed-demons and speed-limit abiders paid no attention to a sleek, black SUV that's pulled over to the side of the road with its hazards blinking on and off in a steady rhythm. Lindsay Troy sat in the driver's seat, hands on her thighs and a Bluetooth device in her ear. Her eyes wander from the tinted windows to the middle console of the Nissan Xterra, where a bottle of water and her black iPod, paused for the time being, rest comfortably in separate cup holders. The sound of leather being settled into came through the headset loud and clear, despite the outside rumble of motorists. "Did I just hear you right?" Alaina asked, quite confused as she sank back into her office chair. "You said your iPod's possessed?!" "Yeah, you heard me right." Lindsay glanced down at the seemingly innocent-looking music player. It mocked her. "Possessed as in...demons living inside the hard drive?" "Alaina..." came the exasperated reply in hopes that her sister would stop ribbing her and listen. "Alright, I'm sorry. But whatever would make you think that? And why are you calling me to tell me?" "Because I clearly can't call anyone else without them thinking that I'm completely off my rocker." "I'm not so far off from thinking you've gone off the deep end here, myself..." The growl on the other end of the line quickly silenced Alaina, and she grinned to herself. "OK, I'm done. I'm all ears here." Lindsay shifted in her seat, trying to get comfortable despite the uncomfortable situation. "It started on the plane, right before we landed: certain songs coming up one after another. I thought it was just coincidence until I got into the rental and synced the iPod's AirPlay to the radio so I could listen to it on the ride to the hotel and it kept happening. It's all so eerie. I don't know what to make of it." "Well, what songs are we talking about?" "'True Friends', 'All I Want', 'You're My Best Friend', 'You Don't Know What It's Like'...there were others, and they all started to run together until..." "Until what?" Lindsay gulped and lowered her voice for no other reason that the couldn't believe what was about to come out of her mouth. "...until a Celine Dion song started playing." Alaina dropped the phone and Lindsay winced at the audible "Shit!" on the other end of the line. "Lanni? You still there? Hello?" "I'm sorry," Alaina finally replied after a dramatic pause. "I didn't know how to react, and then the phone fell." "You mean you dropped it." "Same difference." "Look, this wouldn't be a big deal if I had put that song on there in the first place. But I didn't." "Are you sure?" "Alaina, I'm one-hundred percent positive I would have remembered putting a Celine fucking Dion song onto my iTunes. Let's remember who you're talking to here. I tolerated Melton's Rod Stewart LPs, but I draw a line when it comes to shit like this." "Point taken. Do you think Joey would have put it on there?" Lindsay sighed and rubbed her temples, trying to massage his name out of her head. She'd been trying to do this since January, to no avail. When you hardly speak to each other, and when you do it's never pleasant, the headaches are rarely cut off at the pass. "He barely knows how to turn on a computer, let alone figure out how to put music on one. No, it can't be him." "I've got no answers for you, sis. If you didn't put it on there, and he didn't put it on there, it must have gotten on there somehow." "I know, it's just..." "Don't give up on your faith. Love comes to those who believe it And that's the way it is..." The Bluetooth went flying against the dashboard, while Lindsay scrambled to turn the radio off. She could hear Alaina calling her name while she quickly grabbed the headset and jammed it back against her ear. "Alaina...it just turned on by itself..." ~**~~**~~**~~**~~**~ "This one! This one!" One tiny finger pointed to a song on an immense playlist, and the sound of laughter filled the kitchen. A little girl sat at an oak table, seated on the lap of a man barely thirty years old, and touched the screen of a laptop, as if to offer her babysitter extra help in highlighting the exact song she meant. He dipped his head low to look at her, jet black hair wildly gelled in all directions and a soul-patch goatee that tickled the top of her crown. With a click, the piano intro to Billy Joel's "She's Got a Way" softly trickled through the computer's speakers. "I think she's onto you, Jai." Jaime Thomaselli looked up from the computer and over to the kitchen's owner, who was standing in front of a window and looking out at the sky and the clouds that surrounded the home. Marco Strozzi held a glass of pinot noir in his right hand, and swiveled his head to look over his shoulder at his once future son-in-law with a mischievous gleam in his eye. "Hm?" "She knows something's up, kid. Better to stop now while you're ahead." He now turned his attention to the child. "What'dya say, jelly bean, you ready for some juice?" "Uh huh!" came the tiny reply, and the girl jumped off Jaime's knee and toddled over to the refrigerator. "I'll get it for her, Marco," an incoming Cecilia Strozzi said as she swept gracefully into the kitchen with a cup already in hand. The refrigerator door opened with ease and she poured a glass of apple juice from the bottomless pitcher. "Here you go, sweetie. Don't spill now." "I won't!" Jaime rose from his seat and walked over to Marco, slumping against the wall and belying his six-four height. Strozzi continued to stare outside, occasionally sipping his wine and listening to his wife coo over their tiny visitor. "What were you trying to prove with that, Jai? You finally trying to communicate with her after all this time?" "In a way," he replied and rubbed his goatee. Lindsay always liked that. "But I think my time for breaking through that barrier has passed." "It's only passed if you want it to, kid. Most of us haven't tried it yet." "Why haven't you and Cecilia?" "Hasn't been the right time." Marco smiled. "And we may never, y'know? With everything she's going through, and has gone through in the past...she needed to get through it on her own." "It's why you're questioning this now, aren't you?" A nod confirmed Jaime's suspicion. He took a glance out the window and saw Lindsay still talking on the phone to Alaina, her mouth forming the words, 'I know I haven't dropped it, Lanni. I don't know why it turned on like that. It's friggin creepy.' "You know the two of them need each other right now, Marco. They can't keep standing on that divide for too much longer before one of them falls too deep to climb back out. And you know it won't be her." "Who? Lindsay and Joey?" Jaime shook his head. "For once no, not her and Joey. Her and Caesar." "Daddy?" The men looked down at the little girl, who lowered her cup from her mouth and widened her eyes hopefully. Cecilia looked forlornly at her husband and Jaime, then redirected her attention. "No, Lulu, darlin', your daddy's not here." "Where's Mommy?" "Your mommy will be here soon, but she's not here yet either." Little Lulu Vega sniffled, then looked down into her juice cup. "She'll be here soon, okay sweetie? She just got delayed a little bit. But I hear she's coming real soon." "Okay..." "How about we go play with the piano for a little while?" Lulu nodded and let Cecilia take her hand. The two wandered out of the kitchen, leaving the two men by themselves. "How do you explain purgatory to a two year old?" "You can't, Jai. Especially when there were exceptions made in Ariel's case. I think the Big Man knew just how much she and Lulu need each other, and how sorry Ariel was that she had to leave. But you know, nothing good can happen until there's repentance." Jaime nodded, thoughtfully. "Lindsay's repented long enough for me, Marco. And my being here isn't her fault." "She'll always think it was, though. Lindsay will always feel a measure of regret and guilt over you, just like she's feeling regret and guilt over her crumbling marriage to Melton and feeling sorrow over Ariel's death, even though she never knew her. Caesar will always feel responsible for the death of his girls, and he's all alone to bear the burden." "She wants to help him, Marco. The pain is interconnecting their lives right now and through their friendship they can help ease it. They just ... need to stop being on opposite sides long enough to do so." Marco placed his arm on Jaime's shoulder and looked him square in the eye. "You can nudge her along, Jaime, but you can't nudge him. It's not your place to." "I can't think of a better person to nudge him than Lindz." "Neither can I." ~**~~**~~**~~**~~**~ RING! RING! RING! "New Orleans Marriott, this is Giselle, how may I help you?" "Hi Giselle, this is Lindsay Troy in room 433." "Good afternoon Ms. Troy. I trust you've settled in comfortably?" "I have, thank you, after a bit of a rough afternoon. I'm not currently in my room, though. Would you be so kind as to come to the lounge area? I have a rather urgent request that needs attending to and I rather it be done by the person I'm speaking with than someone who's sent along in your place. "I'll be there shortly," Giselle replied and placed the receiver back onto the phone. She looked to her left to Christine, the new girl, who was handing a couple their room cards. "Chris, I'll be right back. I just need to run to the lounge quick." "You're going to leave me here all by myself?!" Christine squeaked, looking nervous as the patrons moved off toward the elevators. "Don't worry, you're doing fine! And I won't be more than a couple minutes, I promise. Dorian's coming off break in a minute anyway. Don't look so scared." With a smile, she walked through a back door that would take her around to the front of the concierge station and down the hall to the lounge. Her heels clacked against the marble tiles and then became silent as she crossed a carpet that brought her into the hotel's lounge area. Sitting at the bar with a glass of red wine in front of her is the Queen of the Ring, writing quickly onto a sheet of paper. Lindsay finishes a sentence, folds the paper up and stuffs it into an envelope. A quick flick of her tongue seals the letter shut and she writes someone's name on the front. "Thanks for coming so quickly," she replied, looking up with a smile. Giselle couldn't help but notice that, for a woman who makes a living kicking the shit out of men without so much as a second thought, she was quite pretty and down-to-earth. "I'm about to head out to meet some friends for dinner, so I don't have time to run this upstairs myself. Could you bring this to Caesar Vega's room for me? If he answers and asks who it's from, don't say my name. It's better if he just reads this first without knowing." "Sure, I can do that." "Thank you. I appreciate it." Troy smiled at her again, then downs the rest of her wine. Giselle turned to go, but Troy's voice called her back. "Giselle? One more thing." "Yes, ma'am?" Troy slid off the bar stool, reached into her purse and slid a bill into the staff member's hand. "This letter is between you, Caes and I, alright?" "I can definitely do that." ~**~~**~~**~~**~~**~ Caesar-- I'm truly sorry about Ariel. I tried to think of a time that would have been more appropriate to say this, but in my dalliance to do so I lost several weeks that I cannot get back and was a participant in actions that neither one of us can reverse. I suppose I should have made a better effort to say something when things were still fresh and before you chair-shotted me across the face at UltraViolence, but even then I don't think anything I could have said would have stopped you anyway, so I try not to dwell on this event too much. But I can't say I blame you, either. The pain and the heartache is overwhelming, I know, and the way you lashed out wasn't completely inconceivable. There was a time in my life when I lost someone very dear to me as a result of things that were done and said, and that I still regret nearly five years later. Wrestling took him from me, and instead of succumbing to the pain I pressed forward with a change in attitude and a steely resolve that you've seen in me for almost two years now and that the rest of the world has seen since before then. I'm writing this to you now because you're falling into the void I almost let myself fall into, and I don't think anyone else will broach the subject with you. How many people that you know in this business know what pain like this feels like? Can you even fill one hand when you try to count? And if a person really doesn't understand the pain on a personal level, it makes it that much harder to relate to the person who is suffering. You can't bear this alone, as much as you want to, and our current situation makes it difficult for me to help you cope in any substantial way. But you're not alone in this. Unfortunately, the other burden you've had to hoist on your shoulder is that title you've had a stranglehold on. While it's difficult to help you in other ways, you can rest assured that I'll be more than willing to lift that golden cross and carry it on my back for a time...or around my waist, as I prefer it. Ah, you thought I'd be all sappy in this letter, didn't you? Consciences have a right to look out for themselves sometimes, too. But we'll get past this, Caesar. You have to lose a little before you can ever hope to truly start winning again. I guess we'll see just how well you fare on Wednesday. --Lindz
