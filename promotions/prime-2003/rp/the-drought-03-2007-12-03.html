The Drought (.03)

3 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/03/07 “Don’t even think about pussing out on me now.” Tyler Rayne took a long drag from his cigarette, turning eyes away from the ocean to watch the paper turn to ash. Smoke rolled from his nostrils as he considered the pulpy pink flesh at the tips of his fingers, just the slightest of sensitive pulp that had yet to be protected by the growth of new fingernails. The process had been more painful than he’d expected. His eyes turned back to the Pacific, water reflecting a sheen of pink beneath the deep purple backdrop of sunset. Heat emanated from the hood of his rental car, traveling up through the fibers of his jeans to warm his thighs as he leaned against the wheel well. He took a final pull of the cigarette before snuffing the remaining ash against the vibrant red of the Liberator’s fender. With a heavy sigh, Rayne pushed himself from the vehicle, turning his attention away from the picturesque scene of the ocean and over to the dilapidated establishment to his right. The place was more a shack than anything else, thatched roof looking as if it would cave at any moment. The wooden stairs and hand-built entryway, long since crafted by original owners twice removed, were water-worn and in desperate need of repair. One half of the saloon-style doors hung lazily from the frame, creaking softly as it listed in the unforgiving pull of gravity. Japanese speed metal wafted out from the open doors, seeping through the cracks of a building well past its prime. He looked back over his shoulder, through the dense tropical foliage that surrounded twelve miles of dirt road back to the main thoroughfare. From there, another twenty minute drive to anything remotely resembling a town. “You fuckin’ doin’ this thing or what?” With one final glance at the magnificent sunset, Tyler Rayne put boot to sand and marched his way up to the rotting stairs, across the creaking planks of the entryway and through the swinging, broken door into the gloom beyond. The bar, which neither needed nor had a name, was dim and filled with smoke. Though he could make out the bodies of maybe fifteen or twenty different people sitting amidst the tables and shadows, he couldn’t make out a single face from where he stood. He felt, rather than saw, each of them look at him in turn. Imagined as their eyes drifted from the still visible scars on his scalp down to the three days growth of stubble on his chin. The black leather jacket with the yellow racing stripes sliding down each arm. The dirty white t-shirt beneath it, equally matched by a pair of ragged jeans and scuffed boots that had certainly seen better days. He wondered if they would note the dozen or so bloodstains spotting his shirt and jeans. Perhaps there was too much grime to make out the difference. His own eyes wandered to the bar, meeting with the prejudiced gaze of the grizzled old Japanese sake slinger behind the counter. A lone man sat there at the bar, probably deep in discussion with the old bastard before Rayne had walked in. The neon lights behind the counter provided enough luminance to distinguish features on the figure, and Tyler made a point of sizing him up quickly. The man was probably no more than two or three inches taller than Rayne himself, which wouldn’t put him much above 6’ 4”. Not exactly monstrous when compared to some of the men Rayne faced off with, but by Japanese standards, the man was a giant. He had a shaved head and one long, nasty scar that traced from his left temple to the corner of his lip. He wore a dingy wife-beater and a pair of those black and white floral pattern swim trunks with sandals. From ankle to thigh, knuckle to shoulder and every inch between, the man was absolutely canvassed with bright, intricate tattoos. As Rayne approached the bar, he got a better feel for the man’s size. He was large, stocky as some might say. Not cut or defined in any way, so it was obvious that he didn’t work out. But mass alone, probably a good sixty or seventy pounds on Rayne himself, insinuated that the man did plenty of heavy lifting in the day-to-day. Probably the local strong arm, maybe even a direct hand of the Cousins themselves. Likely had a few small projects working on the side. Couple of extortion deals or some other mindless bullshit that allowed him to bully money from people without too much fuss. Rayne stepped up to the bar, resting his elbows only a few inches from those of the strong arm. He didn’t bother to match the glare that came from the man to his right, but rather met the shifty gaze of the bartender, who snarled at the sight of him. “We no serve gaijin here. Fuck out.” “Disrespectful little shit. Doesn’t even know who he’s fuckin’ with.” Tyler Rayne’s eyes never moved from those of the bartender. He matched the hardened gaze of the old man, barely even flinching when his right leg shot out to the side, kicking the wooden barstool from beneath the strong arm and dropping the Japanese giant chin first to the bar. Blood splattered across the bartender’s face and shirt, the tip of the giant’s tongue flopping uselessly to the counter. To his credit, the bartender was much faster than his feeble façade implied. He’d pulled the Desert Eagle from beneath the counter before Rayne had had too much time to react. But the distance between them was little, and no matter how fast the old man was, Rayne was faster. Rayne’s hand was wrapped around the gun before the old man could stop twitching enough to pull the trigger. He wrenched the hand cannon from the man’s weakened grip and in one swift motion turned the weapon on its master. The old man’s knee cap exploded out the back of his leg, sticky pieces of patella adhering to the cabinets behind the bar. Rayne’s hand was already moving back with the force of kickback, so he simply went with the flow and jammed the butt of the gun right across the strong arm’s nose. The giant staggered backward, but not before Rayne could spin ‘round, adding momentum to the point of the elbow that stunned the strong arm in the back of the head. The giant’s head slammed forward again, this time cracking and chipping teeth against the countertop. The strong arm buckled at the knees, holding himself up with only his forearms. So Rayne put him down to the ground with a .50 caliber bullet through the thigh before turning to face the shadowy on-lookers. “So much as breathe funny and I’ll splatter your brains all over the fucking floor. Month or so back, I was chained up in a warehouse in New York. Gettin’ used as a punching bag for about eight hours of my day. Good money says a few of you were doin’ the punching. Had my nails ripped out. Sliced and ripped with more razors than I care to recall. They hooked me up to a car battery one day. Didn’t stop twitching for almost seventy hours. Damn near drowned a couple times. Various drugs and poisons and whatnot injected into my arm. Some other shit I was too doped up or unconscious to remember.” He takes a handful of steps forward, drawing closer to the nearest table. Though he still can’t make out a face, he can see the frightened look in the eyes of the man sitting at that table. “I wanna know who ordered it.” A couple of the chairs creak as the denizens shift their weight. A few of those chair legs scratch against the wooden planks of the floor, though no one makes a quick move for Rayne or the door. Good. Other than that, though, there is silence. He smiles, stepping right up next to the table. From here, he can make out some more features. Enough to tell the kid can’t be more than twenty-two or three. No wonder he’s scared. Probably someone’s cousin or brother. Thought it would be cool to get in on the game. Nothing more than an errand boy. Odds of him knowing much more than jack shit are about nil. That doesn’t stop Rayne’s leg from snapping up, shin smashing nose as he knocks the kid to the floor. A hard stomp to the chest pins the kid to the ground, though the business end of a Desert Eagle pointed at his face would have probably kept him from going too far. “I asked you fucks a question. I know the Cousins didn’t pull this on their own. They're taking orders from up on high. And I want to know who.” Not even the creaking of chairs this time. “Do it. Fuckin’ do it and show these assholes you mean business.” He can hear the kid sobbing beneath him. But he doesn’t look. He can’t. He does his best to keep a steady gaze, looking from one pair of eyes to the other. No one flinches. His finger twitches nervously against the trigger. “C’mon you fuckin’ pussy. Do this shit. Just like the old days.” “Who. Gave. The fucking. Order.” The answer comes in the form of a loogie splashing against his jacket. “Do it!” The sound is like a mortar shell in the silence. He feels the body jump beneath his boot, rocked by the force of the bullet that just splattered the majority of some kid’s head across the floor of a dirty ass Yakuza bar in the middle of nowhere Hawaii. Some kid who watched one too many movies. Saw too many music videos on MTV and figured the risks would be worth it. Some kid who never knew what he was getting into and never had the chance to get out. For half a second, time froze. No one moved or spoke or even breathed. Smoke curled from the barrel of the Eagle. Pieces of skull came to rest in sticky puddles of brain and blood. The first man to move got the same treatment. His brain painted a modern art masterpiece against the back wall. The next to move was left with a gaping hole in his chest. Then it was chaos. Bodies came and his finger reacted, snapping shot after shot until there were no bullets left. He had someone in a front face lock. Kick their legs out. Body falls. Neck snaps. A punch whiffs by his face. Tyler grabs the wrist, spinning beneath the arm, bending to bring his leg up over his back in a scorpion kick that snaps the arm at the elbow. The sharpened edge of shattered bone serves as a vicious weapon for slicing throats. It’s all blurs and splatters of blood. He can hear the bones breaking around him. The screams and curses. None of it makes sense. There’s a knife. He can’t remember where it came from, but he has it, and there’s a man on the table with half a dozen puncture wounds. Just like that, quick as it began, it is over. Tyler Rayne shakes the fog from his head to survey the damage. Fifteen bodies lay broken and bloodied about his feet. Pink matter floating in puddles of crimson. Someone gurgling their last breaths through the gushing slit in their throat. He stumbles backward, tripping over one of the bodies. He turns on his stomach, staring right into the fettered remains of what used to be a young man’s skull. He wretches, a modicum of vomit dribbling from between his lips. Somehow he pushes himself to his feet, staggering away from the scene and out the saloon-style doors. He trips down the stairs and falls to his knees in the sand. Vomit. His head is pounding. Up again. Stumbling. Tripping. He crawls past the driver’s side tire of the rented Liberator. From the ground, his hand fumbles to work the door handle. Hot, searing tears stream down his cheeks. His hand turns to a fist. He punches the door. Again. Again. Skin splits from knuckles. He punches the door harder, sure he heard something crack and far too gone to care. His forehead slams against the cherry colored Jeep, body heaving with sobs. His palms, his face, slide down the cool metal frame of the vehicle. Tyler Rayne curls into a fetal position beneath the dark sky on a lonely Hawaiian beach and cries.
