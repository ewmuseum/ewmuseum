Chronicles of the Young and Untested.

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Today A seventeen year old boy’s entire world explodes into fragments as a result of a cataclysmic chain reaction at the planet’s core... graduation. Ushering their hopes and dreams in along with him, the young boy’s parents place him inside an experimental rocket ship and launch him toward the safety of another planet, college. Tossing his parents’ wishes aside almost as soon as he arrives, the boy sets his sights on his own dream, becoming a wrestler. Rooming with friends, the boy spends the years that follow syphoning tuition money from his parents to cover his expenses while honing and fine-tuning his particular powers on shabby, sweat stained canvases known for spawning legends. At small arenas, bingo halls, and high school gyms all across the state he performs wondrous feats under the guise of a mere mortal, leaping to vertiginous heights in running shoes(“they make me feel lighter”) before crashing down onto his unsuspecting opponents. Then turning cartwheels and back flips before crowd surfing after big wins. Little does he know, but the ever vigilant recruiting eye of PRIME is already upon him. Unbeknownst to him he’ll be there soon. Friends and peers tell the kid he’ll never make it if he dares to fly too high, too fast, so he works even harder. He becomes brassy, and strong, he fights with an uncontrolled violence that to this day still defies reasoned analysis. He does everything with a mischievous grin, and a vibe emanates from him that isn’t felt often enough. Joy. Pure, unadulterated joy. “I’m just a kid having fun.” he says. “Every day is a blessing, so I’m just out there giving it my all. Every night I’m out there saying ‘Come on, come and get me.” Numbers are punched. Phones ring. Word spreads. More numbers are punched. Fans tell other fans, promoters call other promoters, “Jackson Darilek” passes through innumerable lips, often accompanied by “You gotta see this kid!” Pure word of mouth, no sycophants or hyperbole. The wonder of the discovery hits everyone at the same time, and before long, his phone is ringing off the hook. “First come first serve.” he says, and proceeds to wow every venue. By the time he signs on the dotted line with PRIME, he’s the hottest thing on the independent circuit. So ends Chapter 1 of Jackson Darilek, Boy Wonder. Now, Darilek is poised to add a new chapter to the saga. Brandishing his trusty “Gravity Be Damned” double shooting star, Boy Wonder has worked hard to prove he belongs in PRIME, all but vanquishing two time 5-Star Champion Nova and picking up a convincing win over the top contender for the Alias Title. Still, like any good hero, Darilek is an “other” in a world that doesn’t quite appreciate his talents. Fueled by the crowd, and undermined by a devious alter ego, he sees himself locked in a never-ending struggle against evil forces intent on holding him back. Despite his attempts to bend space, time, perception, and most recently, his title situation- questions still remain about his true identity. Is Boy Wonder a budding hulk about to harness his burgeoning powers, or is he an out of control mutant who never will? Mentioning his less than impressive record unleashes a storm, because the only thing more wicked than Boy Wonder’s “Final Flash” is his memory. It’s a searing reminder of rookie mistakes, wrongs perpetrated, and above all else... a portent of retribution to come. “Ill get ‘em back...” he says “they’d never beat me now.” Probably so, as the majority of his losses came from cluster matches, many of which he had all but in the bag, only to lose due to losing track of where everyone was or taking one too many risks. Self restraint has never been his signature virtue. Boy Wonder transforms into a brooding Dark Knight in the blink of an eye, racking up injuries and collateral damage as he flies headlong into trouble. Following a particularly disheartening loss in a four way match, Jackson threw a chair backstage with such force that it put a hole in the wall. He then took a shower to cool of, still fully clothed... and tore out of the arena like a bat out of hell on his motorcycle still soaking wet. He stewed for days, but his anger didn’t blind him to the truth, “I wasn’t there yet, I wasn’t ready...” Since then he’s come to terms with what it’s going to take to be successful at the professional level, and he’s prepared to give it all he’s got. His once seemingly perpetual smile is all but gone now, eroded away by the belief that his happiness was being taken for immaturity, weakness. Though on rare occasions his megawatt glow still shines bright... every time a “W” is placed next to his name. Chapter 3 in Boy Wonder’s saga sets up to be the one in which he finally conquers all-and is recognized for the superhero he is. Wether or not that comes true will be seen when Jackson’s action-adventure plays out in what will be the biggest match of his young career. An epic clash between himself and two archvillains in the form of Peter Vetra and Tyler Lopez... Two months into his PRIME experience, Jackson Darilek is sitting in a tediously unoriginal hotel room near San Francisco’s Cow Palace Arena. His stark white Nike “uptowns” along with matching polo shirt and fitted cap are on the floor beside him. Modest diamonds sparkle from both ears as he talks, and working shows in half empty high school gymnasiums and bingo halls seems like a lifetime ago, yet yesterday at the same time. In conversation Boy Wonder bounces from subject to subject as zealously as he catapults himself into the air. For now though, his voice is rising and his hands are rapidly gesturing as he attempts to explain how fired up his is about his shot at the Alias Title on Overkill. His eyes beam and his rigid, sinuous muscles tense as he stumbles over words. He starts and stops several times over, aborting ideas mid-sentence as another thought bursts into the forefront. Then, in an instant, he stops. His eyes return to normal size, his mouth closes, and his muscles relax. Now, in a much more collected turn, he settles for “I’m just happy to be here.” Looking into his eyes it becomes evident that no truer words have ever been spoken. He wasn’t always, but at this point in time he couldn’t be happier. He spent the first month of his career second guessing himself and wondering wether or not he even belonged in PRIME, and his less than impressive showings made the query that much harder to answer. Then came the turning point, which ironically enough, was a loss. It came against two time 5-Star Champion Nova, in a match in which he was narrowly defeated. Though the losses that preceded it drove him to doubt his position, that one spoke to him. “It showed me that maybe I actually was good enough. I mean, if I could take Nova to the limit, I must be doing something right...” he starts, standing to his feet and wandering out onto the hotel room’s minuscule balcony(He has the worst time staying still). “After that I felt like I belonged, I had confidence.” People can smell when you’ve got it... and even more so when you don’t. The following week Boy Wonder was reeking of the stuff, going up against fellow cruiserweight, as well as top contender to the Alias Title, Peter Vetra. Showing the signature style and reckless abandon he showcased in his career on the independent circuit, Jackson put away his foe, almost too soon for the returning Everest, who was forced to intervene in order to make it more of a contest. He then proceeded to show Tyler Lopez that talk was indeed cheap by ending his night with a “Final Flash”. He hopes to be able to end his Overkill performance in a hail of flashes as well, those from cameras as he hoists the Alias Title high overhead. When asked what he’d name it, Jackson casually shrugs while opening the room’s small icebox. Removing a bottle of water from within, he begins “I don’t know...” Then taking a sip he continues “Gotta win it first.” with a wink.
