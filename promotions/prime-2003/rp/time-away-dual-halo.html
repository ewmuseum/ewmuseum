Time Away (Dual Halo)

28 February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Occured: 12/1/06, posted 2/28/07 “No, honey, really, it's okay. I honestly don't mind.” It was hard to reassure her, but I had to try anyway. Nicole had a hard time believing that I wouldn't mind if she took a trip to go visit her parents, even if I was home from PRIME for a while. The fact is, I wouldn't mind to have some quiet time to myself. It was nice to have a little solitude, a little peace so that I could sort things out in my head, watch some TV and just fall asleep on the couch. I looked at her and couldn't help but smile. God, I'm a lucky man. She's always been a vision: long, lithe, beautiful with her curly black hair that falls just beyond her shoulder blades. Those dancers' legs, that dazzling smile... I don't know how exactly she ended up with a fool like me. An ex-cop that left the state benefits package just to go and fight a different guy every week. I took the left easily, swinging us onto New Circle Road as we circled around. I had decided on the flight home to take her out for a quiet evening with some good food we didn't eat often, so I decided a trip to Miyako's would be in order. It's a little Hibachi place off of Richmond Road, and apparently the cooks there are MMA fans, or wrestling fans, or both. Regardless, they knew me when I sat down, and then go that extra mile to impress us and make the food really good. I don't go back often; I don't like that kind of celebrity status when I go out. My Wrangler took the turn like a pro. I've had this thing for years, finally got it tuned the way I want it, I'm used to its balance and acceleration. I pressed the pedal down and merged into the traffic. It's pretty heavy tonight, I don't know why. There's some cold rain coming down and the night is dark. And it's Thursday. What in the blue hell is going on with a Thursday night? I feel a hand rest on my knee and see her left hand resting there gently. As I drive, her engagement ring and wedding ring flash in the street lights as we go. Her gentle touch makes me think of everything married life has brought me. I'm just plain happier than I used to be. It's great to wake up in the morning with her there. To go to sleep with my arms around her. To come home and see her smile, or to smile at her when she comes home. To go out and eat together. It's all just so much better, and it's because she cuts my rough spots, she compliments my strengths and we're just so much better than we were. “Shawn,” she says quietly. Her voice has changed, it's softer and more hesitant. She's got something to tell me about. It has to be something important, especially if her voice is like this. My mind raced, trying to figure out what it could be. The gym is doing great, she's having great attendance for her dance classes. I had no idea what it could be. Might PRIME have called her before I got home and she just forgot to tell me? That could do it, but if that was the case... no, that fits. She gets so worried sometimes that I'm going to get mad because she didn't tell me something earlier when she meant to and it might possibly be time sensitive. “I'm sorry, but the PRIME office called, they wanted me to tell you if you didn't get ahold of them by tonight, you wouldn't be booked this week.” Her face was a mask concealed fear; not that I'd blow up, but that I'd be disappointed. She knows how much I love to compete. “Babe,” I say, taking a moment to smile at her, “it's okay. I don't exactly mind having a week to spend with you.” That's when I lost control of everything. My fists tightened on the wheel when the sledgehammer hit the side of my Jeep. I fought to keep the wheel straight as it bucked in my grip. I tried to right vehicle as we slid towards the guardrail. The loud squeal of tires slashed in my ears as I fought against the sudden intrusion into the quiet conversation I was having with my wife. I was happy, I was content, I was going to be just fine; all until someone just showed up and ruined a great evening. The impact was horrid. I've been hit a lot in my life. I mean, I've been hit a whole lot in the course of my lifetime. Nothing was quite like this. I don't know, maybe it was just that I was being slammed against something I was in, not being hit myself. My teeth ground when we were sandwiched between whatever hit us and the guardrail. Glass flew and I couldn't get my eyes to shut. I saw the tinkling shards reflect in the streetlights. Some hit me, some went past. I could only hope Nicole was alright. I couldn't even do anything to influence what was going on. The impact was so bad that when we collided with the steel barrier meant to keep folks on New Circle Road, we were shoved over and the Jeep began to roll. Side over side, we rolled down the gradual sloping grass. My seatbelt tugged at my collarbone and each jarring role jerked at my hands, still glued to the steering wheel. My grip is apparently better than I thought, given that I managed to snap the top third of the wheel off from between the two supports on the inside. We came to a rest upside down. The Wrangler was resting on its support frame, and I was pretty much hanging by my seatbelt on a very bruised collarbone. I looked at the piece of the steering wheel in my hand, but couldn't get my fingers to unclench. “Nicole, baby?” I managed to rasp out. “Are you okay?” “Yeah, Shawn,” I heard her reply. “I'm alright.” That right there let me breath so much easier. I let myself relax a little, and my shoulder hurt even more. I couldn't tell you what me being tightened up had done, but it had been keeping weight off my shoulder somehow. It was then I heard the rattling and banging of something. I whipped my head around... and watched as the bumper of the other vehicle flipped end over end down the hill and struck me full in the face and the world went that familiar kind of black. * * * Blood. So much blood. Shawn's face had been covered in it by the time the ambulance had arrived, but Nicole hadn't dared move him. She knew he could easily be hurt worse, no matter how careful her attempts, so as much as it broke her heart to do so, she let Shawn hang from his seatbelt. The EMT had told her he had bled so much from the impact to the face, hitting his nose, eyebrows and mouth. It was hard for her to believe, given all the fights she'd seen him in and he'd never bled this much. The hospital took more blood, gave him some back and so on and so forth. But when they tested hers, they ran her into an X-Ray machine and then she spent two hours sitting with Shawn before hearing anything about why. The doctors stood outside, conferring back and forth, always glancing in worriedly. Shawn had tried to wake up once, and it hadn't been pretty. He had taken a sharp blow to the head and was confused. He thought they were trying to hurt him, so there was one nurse with a black eye and one intern with a cracked jaw. It was horrible to see him in that condition, but she also felt some sort of pride at the fact that he was still in there, still fighting, even if he was swinging at the wrong targets. * * * “Is that really Shawn Stewart in there?” the intern asked. “Yesh,” came a reply from a man with a fattened lip and dried blood on his chin. “You wanna go find out? It'll be two hours until the anesthesia wears off.” “What's he doing in here?” It seemed so strange that someone as seemingly invincible as this local celebrity would be laid up in a hospital bed. Really, it was to monitor his brain activity and make sure there wasn't some sort of imbalance. But all the same, why? “Car wreck,” another chimed in, looking down his clipboard. “They got t-boned, rolled down the hill next to New Circle by the Harrodsburg Road exit and he got smacked in the face with the bumper of the car that hit him while he was hanging upside down. News has it on video from one of those stoplight cameras.” “Okay, so how's he doing?” Silently, the intern was hoping that he'd be back on his feet in no time, ready to go back to busting skulls in PRIME. “He seems to be doing okay. There was a bad disorientation from the wreck, that's why he went batshit on us until we got him sedated. The man woke up the instead a needle went in and just swung for all he was worth.” An uncomfortable silence hung in the air. “Alright,” the intern continued, “so why exactly are we all standing around looking like the dog died?” “It's his wife,” another doctor said, turning his back on the window into the room and taking a deep breath. “She was four weeks pregnant.” “Wait, what?” “Yeah,” he went on, “and she said they'd been trying to have a kid for about a year now.” “Oh my God...” “Well,” the other doc chimed back in, “what about the folks that hit them? How are they?” “The little jerkoff is fine,” replied the second. “Though I don't think he'll stay that way after Mr. Stewart wakes up.” “You think he's going to take the miscarriage that hard?” “He might,” he conceded. “The scary part is what will happen if he finds out why the car swerved across two lanes of traffic and made perpendicular impact with his car.” “Drunk driver?” “Eighteen year old college freshman getting road head from his girlfriend. To beat it all, she looked up right before they hit.” “So he didn't even up getting bitten or anything?” “Dumb luck, huh?” * * * Blackness still holds the sight when you come awake. You hear first, then it's followed by an awareness that you're coming around. I'm familiar with the feeling, but it wasn't like the normal morning wake-up or even coming back up from being knocked stupid. No, this was a slow recovery, thoughts coming together one word at a time. It was like this: I'm... waking up. I was... in a wreck. Nicole... she said... she was okay. I can feel... feet... hands... Slowly, I forced my eyelids open. It was a sheer force of will against curtains of lead. I managed to force them up and look around. The light burned my eyes for a moment, but I didn't close them back. I might go back to sleep. I wanted to know what was going on. I let my eyes roll around, then blinked some grit from them. I sucked air in through my nose and blew out through my lips. I seemed to be okay... Nicole was sitting next to my bed. Her face was red, her makeup all washed off. She was still beautiful, but she looked so upset. I lifted my hand, seemingly like a fifty pound weight and placed it on her hand. I smiled to her as I placed it carefully. “Hey, you,” I said in a gravelly voice. It felt like rubbing the inside of my throat on sandpaper. I swallowed and went on. “You come here often?” She gave me a small smile and gripped my hand. “Hey, honey. How do you feel?” “I'm not sure...” I replied. “I think I'm okay. I feel everything, nothing hurts, I think I'm okay.” “You had a concussion, honey,” she said back to me. “You were out for about six hours. They say the swelling is already going down. You'll be just fine, honey. But you should rest more.” “No,” I said, fighting off the feeling of a sedative tugging at my head again. I wasn't going to let myself go back under without finding out what was wrong. “You knew I was going to be okay. Why were you so upset?” “Later, baby, later. You need to get more rest.” “Not until you tell me what's going on,” I said. “I will not go back to sleep until you tell me what's bothering you.” I knew the steel in my voice was coming out, and she knew I'd do my damnest to live up to what I said. “Shawn...” she said, her lip trembling. “I was four weeks pregnant with a little girl. The wreck made me have a miscarriage.” I faded back into blackness. * * * The little girl smiled up, in her little jeans and her pink t-shirt. “Daddy, what are those?” she said pointing through the glass. It was adorable, almost more than I could really stand. Her dark, curly hair framed her face, accentuating those delicate features she'd gotten from her mother. “Those, honey, are panthers. They're big cats they find in Asia, according to this little panel they have here.” The black, elegant cats strode around the exhibit, licking at their chops and flicking their ears at whatever caught their attention. “Didn't they use to call you a panther, Daddy?” she said, one small hand in mine, the other against the glass. “Yes, baby, when I was still competing, they did call me that.” “Why? Did you wear a black suit or something?” “No, baby,” I replied, “why?” “You don't look anything like the panthers.” ...... “I know, Dad,” she told me. She was getting ready to head off to her first Prom, one of the few sophomores going. Her dress was a stunning red ensemble, showing her slim figure. Her hair was meticulously arranged, looking like it was just completely tussled. Those curls had straightened out a little bit, lengthening to long waves in beautiful black hair. “Honey, please don't get upset with me. You know your old dad is just protective.” I smiled and set my camera aside. In reality, I was mad that some little pervert was going to take my little girl off to some heathen dance, but I didn't say anything. “Dad, I know you are, but please don't try to run my date off,” she said sweetly, kissing my cheek. “Well, honey, he just has to come say hi to me in the living room.” “Dad!” she exclaimed. I knew it was exactly what she didn't want, but hey, I was the Mighty Dad. I made the rules about it. “Nope, honey, he comes to say hello. I'll be in the living room.” I smiled to my little girl as her mother fussed over her, adjusting things that had just been adjusted, and headed towards the living room. There was a reason I wanted him to come in. I sat down in my chair and leaned back with a copy of Black Belt Magazine with a copy of me on the cover. The wall behind me had pictures of the family, a few pictures of me with some good friends with me and, in the middle of the wall, the OSF title belt. I wanted this boy to know what he was dealing with. ...... I almost sobbed, I was so happy. The little girl had gone on to get a Masters Degree in occupational therapy, she had gotten into a great clinic, she was making more at twenty-five than her dad had until he hit the big circuits. And now she was getting married. No, not the punk that had brought her back late on that Prom night with her screaming at him. I scared the boy so bad he had ruined his rented tux, and that couldn't have been good for her seats... But now, here I stood, in the back of the church with my little girl looking like a vision in her white dress with a dazzling smile and looking so beautiful, I couldn't help but dab tears away from my eyes. “I love you Daddy,” she said looking up at me. “I love you, honey,” I said softly. “You'll always be my little girl.” * * * I clawed my way back up to consciousness. Nicole was still there. She had changed her shirt, but otherwise she looked the same. She looked at me, and smiled sadly. Her hand gripped mine. I struggled to sit up, my elbows and shoulders popping loudly as I pushed myself up. “Hey, honey,” she said, kissing my forehead. “How are you?” “I'm okay,” I said. “The police asked if we wanted to press charges,” she said quietly. I shook my head no. “They're going to be in enough without us. Just as long as we get the insurance payment and everything.” “Are you really okay?” she asked me quietly. I didn't answer for a bit. In my head, over and over, turned the one thought. Will I be alright? Will I be good to go again? Could I do it? “I'm going to be back for the Dual Halo,” I replied to her. “I'm going back for our little girl.” “I know.” “How?” I asked, genuinely shocked. “You were talking to her in your sleep.”
