He Gave Me a Name (Dual Halo - 2008, and singles match vs. Hoyt Williams)

7 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 7th, 2008 "I do not know this man. It's been nearly a year since we first met, but still, so much of his life remains a mystery to me. He'll tell the occasional tale of his early days, but there's this large gap in between then and now that's just... ...blank. But I can understand that. There's a whole lot to my own life that I'm unable to tell him, and there's nothing glorious about it. In fact, it's actually pretty nasty, but there's a part of me that knows his story is no different, which puts us on an even playing field. He's a conundrum. There are times when he'll give a stranger the very shirt off his back, but with the tip of a shot glass could just as soon smash their jaw into shards. I guess that's the remnants of an old time shining through, a time where honor and sin stood side by side. You don't see that in people anymore. He's the last of a dying breed. I think he knows it. But lately it's been more bad than good. He's drifting from the honorable roughneck to the terrible outlaw. It's obvious. He doesn't tell many stories anymore. He spends more and more time drunk, pissing people off and digging himself into holes he can't climb out of. There's a number of reasons why. Consequences of bad decisions coupled with feelings of loneliness and betrayal are pulling him in all sorts of directions, tearing him to pieces. I can only wonder how long it will take before he breaks. And the worst part is that I can't DO anything about it. I can only lend an ear. It's frustrating, watching him crumble, but I can't shake the feeling that HE'S the one to blame for his own downward spiral. Even now, as I watch him through the window of this truck, getting thrown out of a bar and onto the pavement, I can see the truth. As he stands and staggers toward me, lighting a cigarette and digging that flask of bourbon out of his pocket, I can see his destruction is self inflicted. This is not a good situation. Every part of my instinct says to leave him to his own demise before he drags me down with him. But this man gave me a second chance, and he gave me a name, and for that I owe him more than I could ever possibly offer. So I will remain at his side, hoping he'll pull through, hoping the stalwart drifter I used to know will resurface. It's all I can do to be this man's best friend. He gave me a name."
