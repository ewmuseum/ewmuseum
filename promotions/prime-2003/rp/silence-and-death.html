Silence and Death

Date: ReV 183 -January 6, 2009 Tokyo, Japan The quiet was always so gods damned unnerving. It settled on The Katana’s Point like a burial shroud. Oppressive and stifling and so… final. As if disturbing it in the slightest would be an affront against the gods and heaven above. The silence was unnatural. Unwelcome. It made his skin crawl. The soft click of the bathroom door was his two-second warning. He didn’t bother looking up when she stepped out. Those long legs slinked out from the escaping steam. Her arms, toned but slender, worked to tussle the towel wrapped loosely around her head. Hands fell to her sides. The towel lowered and damp red strands bounced around the edges of her face. She strode forward in her flannel pajama bottoms, a mischievous smile creeping across her lips. “I think that’s the first time I’ve ever walked in a room and you didn’t get all google-eyed on me. You sure you’re okay?” A jest, of course. Another witty jibe from Lindsay Troy. He shrugged but still refused to look up. The Queen’s smile quickly turned to a frown. “Tyler, I was just-“ “It’s cool. I’m…” The hesitation to indulge his thoughts was duly noted. Tyler Rayne finally turned to look at his… lady friend. Dark eyes descend the length of the flannel. “You’re walking.” “Yes. I know. It’s amazing, really. Learned it all by myself, too. Eight months old. A regular child prodigy.” “Smartass.” “The pot told the kettle.” The smile had returned, albeit with a little less mirth than before. She descended, lips pressing to his forehead for the briefest moment before she threw herself upon the bed, not far from where he sat in a hard-backed chair. The corners of his mouth lifted ever so slightly. She sat up and stared at his scarred torso, lost for a moment in an unexpected flood of sympathy. He’d been through so much… “What now?” Another shrug. “We sleep. You’re probably exhausted.” “I was talking about the… you know.” “I know.” He offered no more. Silence rushed in to fill the void again. Cold and unwelcome. An empty stillness where thoughts of recent events were wanting to play and dance, mocking them both with a carefree frivolity. Tragedy slapped him in the face. “You could have gotten me a better shirt.” She’d never quite noticed the interesting design of the tops until she actually put one on. Surprisingly comfortable and, even more surprisingly, an accurate size that fit almost perfectly. Almost. It would seem that the front of the tank top, right where the Rayne Dancer logo spread across her breasts, was about a cup size too small. “You could have thought to pack your own.” “You could have given me a bit more notice.” “You didn’t have to come.” “You didn’t really give me much choice. One minute we’re in Boston and you get this phone call… throw stuff in a bag, then we’re off to New York. Another phone call, no time to breathe and we’re in Seattle. Now you drag me out to Japan and-“ “Drag you, huh? So you didn’t want to come?” “I didn’t say that.” “You’re not denying it.” "I--“ She sighed. Her long legs slowly rose upwards, knees climbed to her chin and arms dangled along her muscular calves. Fingertips danced along the syndesmotic scar, once hidden by a cast on her right leg but now partially visible on skin not covered by fabric. "Memories, Tyler. That's all. Memories from long ago." Her eyes slid sideways to peek at him from their corners. "But I came regardless, because I think you needed me to." “I don’t know what I need right now. I feel like…” “Like you’re losing everything?” “Yeah.” The word was barely a whisper. “You still have me.” The response was no louder. “Do I?” “I… yes. You do.” The affirmation came with an authority her previous sentence was lacking; a forced confidence that betrayed the truth. The statement was too strong. Trying to convince herself as much as she was him. Probably more. There was a long beat as they both came to the realization at the same time. Even a beat was long enough. The silence returned. The quiet, blackened void. A great empty in which something must exist. In the absence of sound, of substantiality, there was memory. Recent struggles and pains once hoped forgotten rushed to fill the hole. True life nightmares came crashing through barriers both were far too tired to maintain. Defenses worn through exhaustion, the tide burst forward like an immense juggernaut. There would be no stopping the memories. They had no choice but to think about them. To dwell… So, in a moment of ravishing passion, Lindsay Troy and Tyler Rayne chose not to think at all. To give themselves to instinct and animal lust. Anything to avoid the pain. He leapt from the chair, making the first move, as was his nature. She crawled across the bed, relenting to his advances, as was hers. Their lips met… crashed… somewhere in between. He forced himself upon her only to find that she pushed back. The Queen did not gain her title from being so submissive. His fingers entwined themselves into her hair, searching for purchase until they closed into a fist, finding each other yet again. A handhold by which he could control her. She allowed him this small victory. Allowed him to guide her further back onto the bed. He was on top of her. His free hand moved across her thigh. He had yet to stop kissing her. She would not allow him to. She could feel the heat emanating from his skin. He could feel her heart beating rapidly beneath his hand. Barely centimeters away. It was too far. Even in recovery, Lindsay was strong. Her legs, though mending, were far from atrophied. The long limbs found little problem wrapping around his waist. Powerful thighs squeezed, drawing him in. She felt him against her. Fingernails plunged into his skin. She could not wait any longer. Now she acquiesced. Released him. Free from the grasp of her lips he wasted no time in returning the favor and freed her from that dreadful shirt. His eyes left her body once. An involuntary reaction to a stimulus. A flash of red. The alarm clock on the nightstand blinked at him angrily. Warningly. 12:01 a.m. 12:01 a.m. 12:01 a.m. He pushed himself upward with a sudden ferocity that took her completely by surprise. Her grip was broken. Tyler’s feet found the floor first. Then his knees. His palms. His elbows. His hands scrabbled beneath the bed. “Tyler. What the fuck?” Scientific theorem states that energy cannot be destroyed. Only transferred. Pain and sorrow had broken into passion. Lust. Now with that taken away, the intensity of the emotions shifted to rage. “Someone’s here.” He looked up at her with no apology. With no… there was nothing. No emotion whatsoever. Just a cold determination that chilled even her fiery rage. The small, metal box he pulled from beneath the bed was opened beside her. Opened toward him. She could not see inside. “You’re just being paranoid. How could you even--“ He nodded toward the blinking digital display. “The batteries must have died.” “It’s plugged in, love. ‘Sides, things lose power… don’t they usually start flashing twelve?” The numbers on the clock continued to flash. 12:01 a.m. 12:01 a.m. 12:01 a.m. The alarm clock spoke to her, but she did not understand the language. The alarm clock. Alarm clock. Alarm… “Stay here. Watch the door. Anyone comes through that’s not me or Milena… use these.” He turned the box to her now. She stared, somewhat dumbfounded, at the throwing stars lying within. When she looked back to him, she shivered. The calm ease with which he stood. Relaxed muscles. Controlled breathing. That hollow, emotionless lull in his eyes. Mostly, though, she shuddered at the thought of what he might do with the six inches of cool steel in his hand. Where it came from, she could not determine. “What are you going to do?” “What I should have been doing last week. Protecting my home.” Lindsay's lips moved, but no sound poured forth. Stricken with a sudden case of cotton-mouth, she looked from him back down to the box. The stars glinted dangerously in the room's low light. As her eyes watched her arm reach for them, she remembered something very important. "Tyler." There was urgency in the call. A hiss of a name that brought even his determination to a halt at the door. He looked back at her, eyes narrowed to viper's slits. She could feel her own orbs taking on a similar shape. "Could you give me back the shirt that you tossed God knows where?" ---- The quiet was too gods damned unnerving. It settled on The Katana’s Point like a burial shroud. Oppressive and stifling and so… final. The finality promised him some ease. Fortune smiled upon them this morning. Not only would they fulfill their goal in coming here… but the Horseman slept only just down the hall. He would not wake with the morning sun. In these dark hours, two goals would be accomplished, and the Son of Nine would wake to find himself unchallenged. There was a small lament in the circumstances. The Horseman would die quietly in his sleep. Without a fight. Without honor. He deserved neither, but for the assassin that did the deed… none could be earned. That was unfortunate. That it would be too easy. The silence was unnatural. Unwelcome. No sounds of struggle. No babbling pleas for a life made forfeit long ago. Fifteen years, to be exact. There was just the stillness of quiet. It made his skin crawl. He would die without a sound. Without a whimper. Without even knowing what had come. The palm pressed tight against the fabric of his mask, just above where the lips would be. Before his brain could process the threat and formulate a proper response, it was already too late. It began as a prick against the back of his neck. A tiny sting that grew to searing hot pain in seconds. The front of his throat split from the inside out. Blood splattered across the bare forearm of his attacker. His murderer. The blade twisted, wrenching an even larger hole in the neck, before it was removed. Tyler Rayne carefully set his victim down to the ground. For a moment he stared, not at the gaping maw ripped into the young man’s throat… but at the thick sheath of blood now coating his left arm. The body convulsed, just once, then lay still. Blood pooled around the would-have-been assassin. Collected around the edges of Tyler’s bare feet. He looked down at the body. The black-clad uniform he remembered from that evening in Boston many months ago. The ninja army of the Son of Nine. Their presence here was not coincidence. So close to the loss of his other homes. Perhaps without realizing it, the Son had just claimed responsibility for those acts. He wouldn’t have expected Tyler to be there in these early morning hours. He certainly wouldn’t have expected Tyler to protect his home with such ferocity. Tyler Rayne did not kill. Not anymore. He had given up that life long ago. He had become something better. No longer the monster of Yakuza legends. A boogeyman to all those who would do him and his kin wrong. Those days were behind him… or so he had thought. Now the memories were no longer past, but present. They lay in a black uniform, bleeding at his feet. The first victim of the evening. He had died without a sound. Without a whimper. Without even knowing what had come. Tyler padded off down the hall. The Katana’s Point was his oldest companion. They knew each other well. There were no other intruders between his room and Kairi’s. He could only assume the sentry from the hall was a scout. More would be coming soon. Kairi’s door was open when he approached, and Tyler took great care to slip through the space between wood and frame without disturbing either. His entry was completely silent. The room dark. A tomb. He saw the shadow moving within and crept forward. An assassin lingering over Kairi’s quiet form. Sleeping peacefully. She would die without a sound. Without a whimper. Never knowing what had come. Would have, if not for Tyler Rayne. The executioner rose from behind. Again his left hand wrapped around the mouth of his victim, silencing any screams before they could come. The combat knife stabbed deep, striking the kidneys. The assassin’s knees buckled and he slid forward. The knife slipped from his back. Soon found the soft flesh of his throat. That, too, would be torn away. More blood. Another body. Tyler left it where it lie. He focused his attention to the woman in the bed. A blood-soaked hand covered her mouth, stifling the startled cry that was sure to come. The Japanese beauty woke to find her oldest and dearest friend stained in blood. A finger held to his lips was her instruction. She nodded understanding. “Please tell me it’s not ninjas.” “Apparently they forgot to check our hours of business.” “Lindsay?” “Safe for now. I left her in my room. She can take care of herself.” “The office…” “Yours. I’m going after Drake. Be careful, Kai. I don’t know how many there are.” Neither moved immediately. A half dozen unasked questions bounced between them. The concern in her eyes. The cold determination of his. Answers and reassurance for them both. He squeezed her hand and left the room in a silent sprint. The halls remained dark. Quiet. He noticed no movement beyond his own. The club was essentially empty. That fact disturbed him more than any other. There was a purpose to this insurgence. A strategic and specific target they had not yet acquired. Something hardened in his stomach. Unease settled upon him. He rounded the corner and discovered two of the ninjas crouched in front of Drake’s door. Now he knew their purpose. So then, did he know his own. Tyler dashed forward with a guttural cry that startled both assassins. They turned, but not nearly fast enough. Tyler leapt on them. Tackled them both. The combat knife sunk between the ribs of his first victim. Straight to the hilt. He ripped it away and slashed at the second as he rose. The blade cut cloth but nothing more. The assassin lunged forward with his own weapon. Tyler leaned away from the short sword. His free hand grabbed at the wrist. The knife jabbed through the ninja’s forearm. Tyler used it as a fulcrum, along with his grip on the assassin’s wrist, to wrench the arm completely out of socket. Guided by the hand of Death, the knife came free of the forearm and sliced up through the bottom jaw, straight into the nasal cavity. The ninja fell to the floor, coughing his final breath. Tyler took the short sword and turned toward the door. He lunged again, rising from his knees to plunge the sword deep into an assassin’s abdomen. He tore the weapon free, continuing on with the momentum in an arc that kept the next two ninjas from approaching too quickly. Another lunge took him beyond the foremost assassin to the one behind. The sword slid easily into the neck, but a slash from the remaining ninja put Tyler on his heels. The gash cut across his ribs. Nasty, but not fatal. He dropped into a defensive stance and waited for the ninja’s next move. It came fast. A feint kick followed by a lunging strike. Tyler swatted the lunge low and stomped on the ninja’s elbow. He stomped hard. Tyler’s opposite leg came sweeping around, a knee that crashed into the assassin’s temple. His palm rushed forward. Even under the mask, he could feel the nose explode. Tyler saw the movement in the corner of his eye. Drake sat up on his bed, staring horrified at the scene laid before him. Tyler stepped over to his original victims and kneeled to recover his knife. “You good?” “I’m… the fuck just happened. Who are they?” “Doesn’t matter. They were coming for you.” “I’ve never seen--“ “Get up. We need to--“ The windows imploded. Shattered glass and splinters of wooden frame shattered down around the bed. Drake turned to watch the assassins pour in through the window. Four. Five. Seven. He scrambled to crawl from the bed, but three of them were already upon him. Tyler stood and marched forward. Ninjas rushed to meet him. They rushed to their deaths. Tyler dropped to a knee, below the sweeping blade of the first. The knife drove straight down into the foot. Tyler grabbed a handful of cloth pulled the assassin down. The knife slid through the ribs. Into the heart. “GET!” He rose and slashed at the same time, opening the throat of the next ninja to approach. “OUT!” A straight jab. The knife plunged right through the front of an assassin’s throat. “OF MY!” He stabbed deep into the stomach. Twisted the blade upward. And split the assassin open to the sternum. “HOUSE!” Drake threw a back elbow that staggered one of the ninjas. The assassin fell from the bed, and when he stood, Tyler stuck the combat knife through his ear. He grabbed the wrist of another that held Drake. Physically overpowering the assassin, he pulled the ninja straight into waiting arms. The neck snapped with surprising ease. The last of them had almost pulled Drake out the window when the combat knife shuttled through the air and drove right through his forehead. The ninja tumbled out the window and Tyler leapt forward to snatch Drake before he, too, fell to his doom. “Thanks.” “Tyler!” He turned. Three throwing stars glinted at him. Tyler’s head snapped to the side. One of the points sliced across the top of his ear. Otherwise, the stars flew true, pinning the arm of a would be decapitator to the wall. Lindsay Troy offered a cocksure smile. True, Tyler still had his head. He also had a katana sticking through his shoulder. Kairi and Milena both rushed forward. Lindsay’s mobility was still too limited for such a burst of speed. The window became a blackened shadow. Assassins poured through. Hands all snatching after Drake. Tyler slid from the blade. Fell into Kairi’s arms. Milena was on the bed, scrambling toward Drake. She reached for him. He reached for her. The ninjas were too many. Drake fell through the window, surrounded in their shadowy grasp. He was gone. Milena’s angered scream turned all their blood to ice. The one remaining ninja, arm pinned firmly to the wall, had little defense. Soon he had no sword. After that, he had no head. Lindsay gasped. Milena silently stalked from the room with her trophy, leaving but a simple statement in her wake. “We kill them all.” No one had much reason to argue.
