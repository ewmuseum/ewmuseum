Spanish Guitar and Metal = Problems

16 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/16/06 So, it’s El Vice and Gamble this week…We gotta be ready this time. The words of “Electric” Eric Evans echo as he picks a few loose notes on a Spanish style acoustic guitar. He looks to his left and there standing in all his glory is “The Bad Mutha Plucka” himself, “Jammin” Jason Bass. He is holding a large Spanish acoustic bass strung with horsehair. “Dude, Eric, this thing sucks, can I just play my bass?” Eric shakes his head and plucks a few more notes out. The method had to be there somewhere…he just knew it. The boys needed to prepare both physically and mentally this week, so they aren’t blindsided by a seemingly unstoppable foe. Luckily for them, El Vice and Gamble were both fairly new to the game, as were they. Maybe they could put out a good show this time…If only he had waited to rent the elephants. Those are the fortunes of war, Eric. “Jam, you gotta do it right.” He pauses and looks at the strangely strung guitar he has in his hands. “If we are going to win this week, we need to get into these guys head. So here’s the idea, we play Spanish guitars and smile a lot.” Now Jason Bass had heard Eric come up with a lot of goofy ideas, but this was pretty much the stupidest one yet. Yet, he went along with it because, well, Eric was a pretty cool dude, and he knew a lot about how the world works. Eric lead in with a fairly common Mexican song, La Cucha Racha, and Jason followed in with the bass. They played just far enough into it to get to the lyrics before Eric realized there was a tremendous problem. “La Cucha Racha, La Cucha Racha…el…soy…amigo…por favor.” Jason sings a high pitched verse, and Eric looks in confusion. He didn’t know very much Spanish, as Jason claimed to, but he knew enough to know that Cockroaches aren’t our friends. Evans was also fairly sure he wasn’t playing the song right, because every time he did, it sounded less like La Cucha Racha and more like Home Sweet Home ala Crue, circa 1991. “Whoa Jam, that aint right at all! Dude, we have a serious problem.” Jason stops plucking at the big instrument long enough to grunt and roll his eyes. “Eric, this sucks, I WANT my electric.” Eric calms him down a bit by waving a Twinkie through the air, and then sighs. There had to be some sort of method to the madness of their enemies. Evans, suddenly seems to strike upon a great idea, and holds a finger up. “Hey Jam, remember “Loud” Diamond Phillips?” Jason thinks hard for a bit. “Oh yeah, they were that metal band that played the tribute to La Bamba!” Eric nods and begins to play the beginning of La Bamba on the Spanish guitar. Pretty soon, Jason follows right in with the bass. The song sings out wonderfully on the old style stringed instruments, however, neither Bass nor Evans can claim the same thing. “Par Blaylock, La Bamba” Eric sings a little, followed by Jason. “Porque Bingo La Bamba” Again Eric chimes in. “Uno need-o el paco degrasse!” Jason suddenly stops playing and looks at “The Six String Sex Symbol” as he suddenly goes into the main rifts of For Whom the Bell Tolls. Now, a Spanish guitar is great for some things. Carlos Santana, Tito Santana, and El Mariachi all loved Spanish guitars. We, however, do not have to suffer hearing Eric playing Metallica on the Spanish guitar. “Yo Eric, I think you missed a chord…or like…a dozen.” Eric snaps out of his zone and blinks a few times. “You know Jam, for a minute there; I could almost see myself playing with Metallica.” Jason, being a jokester at times, and not being able to resist an open invitation to a joke, is quick with a comeback. “It sounded to me like you were playing with yourself.” Evans let out another long sigh. “Nice Jason…reaaaaal classy.” They sit idly for a minute or so. Jason shuffles in his chair a few times, obviously stuck on what exactly they are learning from this exercise. He leans back in his chair and plucks a couple low notes in thought. “Eric…” Evans harrumphs and holds a finger up. “Jam I swear to god, if you say you want your bass one more time, I am going to wrap this foul sounding contraption in my hand directly around that casaba melon you call a head.” Jason makes an odd face, not wanting to get hit…and half in wonderance what a casaba melon tastes like. “Now, tell me Jam, what was it you were going to say?” Jason shrugs. “I guess it wasn’t really all that important dude.” Eric smacks his forehead and leans down, resting his head in his hand. It just didn’t make sense, there had to be a way to train for these guys. They really didn’t know a lot about them, so the only practical solution was to try and get into the mindset of their enemies right? Jason, meanwhile, was thinking about a hero sandwich. That and the fact that this indeed did suck and he wanted his bass. He couldn’t remember the last time he had been out of site of the sweet 5 stringed bass machine. For that matter, he couldn’t remember when he had even played another one. “Hey Jam.” “Yo?” “You remember that time we were in Tijuana and there were these two chicks that wanted to come on the bus?” Jason thinks a bit, but can’t seem to recall that particular incident. “Uh, can’t say I do?” The Electric One smiles a bit. “Oooh yeah! That’s right that was just me. Sorry bud!” Jason rolls his eyes and tries to think happy thoughts. This was a serious problem. Here they were just days from another event, and another match, and they were in dire peril of not knowing how to prepare for their enemies. Eric: You know the stupid thing? Jam shrugs. Jason: What? Eric: I don’t think El Vice even plays a Spanish guitar. Jason suddenly gets a look on his face like that of an angry midget trying to do a chin-up. Jason: So what are we doing playing these things? “The King of Swing” picks a couple easy notes and thinks about his answer for a bit. Rushing into a conversation with an angry dude like Jason is not exactly beneficial to ones health. Eric: Well, I thought maybe it would help. Besides, what’s the worst that can happen? Jam stops plucking his bass and a thought seems to have struck him like a brick. Jason: The worst thing that can happen? We lose to a Mexican Elvis Impersonator. Eric stops all at once and they both have a dead serious look in their eyes. There is just NO way that they can put the reputation of the metal world, not to mention their fans all over the world, The Destroyers, to shame like that. No…this fight deserved some special attention. It was time to crank the amps and deal the pain. No way on earth could they lose this time. Last week was forgivable, they were new and they made some major mistakes. Not this time though. This time…it was musical. Eric: Okay, so what have we learned from this? Well, number one, Spanish guitars suck. Number two, we can’t lose to El Vice and Tony Gamble. Number three… Jam held up a finger to Eric, and “The Electric One” paused. He nodded as if to say “yes?” and Jason got a huge Tony Gamble Would Be Proud grin on his face. Jason: Eric, this sucks…and I want my bass.
