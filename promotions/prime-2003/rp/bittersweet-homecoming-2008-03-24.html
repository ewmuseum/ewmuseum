Bittersweet Homecoming

24 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/24/08 Monday, March 24, 2008 – Hilo, Hawaii Mere seconds after he stepped out of his limousine and back onto the streets of Hilo, Bryan Dawkins was met by a mob. The scene looked like one you would find at the Grammy or Emmy Awards red carpet, with the celebrity exiting his or her choice of transportation and entering the mad rush of fans, media, and in some cases, enemies. This group, however, was clear in their intentions: congratulate the new PRIME superstar, Bryan Dawkins. Family, friends, and tourists surrounded the limo that had arrived at the airport to pick Dawkins up, and were showing varying forms of admiration to the new PRIMEate. His mother met him with a firm hug and a kiss on the cheek, along with a tear of joy and an “I’m so proud of you, I love you son.” Various friends and family acquaintances, including former teammates of Dawkins on his high school wrestling team, his former high school wrestling coach, classmates of Dawkins through high school and college were all there to greet the newest superstar on the PRIME roster. There were also the various tourists who heard that a Dawkins was making an appearance in Hilo, and wore their Philadelphia Eagles and, more specifically, their Brian Dawkins jerseys to the meet-and-greet, but that was to be expected… …Bryan was used to by that now. What he was also used to was the absence of his father from the situation. Ken Dawkins, legendary wrestler and cornerstone for Hawaii Championship Wrestling, was not in attendance this afternoon. On the island, Bryan and Ken’s rocky father-son relationship was well documented by the wrestling gurus and smarks following HCW. So really, not seeing his father there was something that Bryan expected. He still wanted to talk to him, even though he could pretty much assume how the conversation would go. He consulted his mom about the situation. “Hey mom, you seen pop?” She nodded her head and whispered something into Bryan’s ear, causing one of those “I figured” looks to be painted upon his face. Dawkins turned and headed off away from the celebration and toward the beach. There were things that needed to be addressed… About an hour later… “Thanks for showin’ up today, pops…means a lot to me.” The sarcasm in Bryan’s voice was evident, as he addressed his father, who gazed, un-phased by his son’s words, out into the vast blue of the ocean. “There were a couple hundred people there to greet you. People who care about what you did last night. People who think you’re going to go far and do big things in PRIME. People who love and care about you. You act like you needed me there, son.” Those words stung. He had no good reason to not show up, yet he attempted to cover himself up using blatant lies. But Bryan wasn’t stupid, he knew. “…I figured you’d say somethin’ like that. Honestly, why can’t you just tell me you’re upset with me. Everyone else knows. I know, mom knows, everyone who even remotely follows wrestling knows. It’s not a big secret, pops. Ever since I left HCW to go to Risk, you’ve been pissed for no rea—“ “LOOK WHERE THAT GOT YOU, BRYAN. That place was dead after a few weeks. You left a place that was stable, a place where you were loved by the fans, and me. You left ME, Bryan. And now, you’re leavin’ again. You’re goin’ off to tour with this huge company where you’re just gonna get lost in the mix. It’s just gonna end the same way it did last time; with you, out of work, coming back home to waste away here in Hilo.” Again, the words stung, but there was some truth to what Ken was saying. Dawkins left a strong promotion to go join Risk, an up and coming organization based in the UK, where although Bryan started off strong and won a title on his first night there, the promotion folded not even three weeks after. By the time Bryan made his way back to Hilo after a couple months spent in the UK wrestling the independent circuit, HCW was dead. Although he’ll never admit it, Bryan’s jump from HCW to Risk sparked a mass exodus from HCW to larger companies, which Ken believes is what killed HCW. Bryan wasn’t about to hear it, though. “Whatever pops. I’ll be here on the island until tomorrow. If you need me, I’ll be around. If not, watch the television, you’ll be seein’ your boy do some amazin’ things.” Ken stayed silent, still peering out towards the horizon, as Bryan departed from his father. Later that day…at the Dawkins residence. “’Eyyyyyy bruh! Congrats on the performance in the Halo! You were really tearin’ it up in there, bruh!” Polunu was all smiles as he conversed with Bryan, who he had surprised a few moments earlier when he arrived at the front door. Polunu just couldn’t sit in Honolulu while his friend spent his last few days at home before leaving for PRIME’s hectic schedule. He hopped on a plane a mere thirty minutes post-Culture Shock and was waiting at the house when Bryan made it home Monday afternoon. “Yeah, I guess I did myself some good in there. Management said they liked my style in the ring, and offered me a full-time spot on the roster.” “Awww shit bruh, that’s awesome! Well here, I know you’re gonna be busy the next couple’a days, restin’ and packin’ and whatnot, so Im’ma give you these.” Polunu reached into a small gym bag and pulled out a coconut...with Brian Dawkins’ face carved into it, and handed it to Bryan. “But, uh…?” “Wait, there’s more!” “Oh great,” thought Bryan as Polunu again reached into the gym bag…and pulled out another carved coconut. This time, however, the correct face was carved into it and in the back, the word “PRIME” was carved neatly into the coconut. Bryan could do nothing but crack a grin upon receiving the second coconut. “Knock ‘em dead, bruh…we’ll all be watchin.” It was a kind gesture by Polunu, and Bryan loved it…but there was only one thing running through Bryan’s mind at the time. “No…you won’t ALL be watching.”
