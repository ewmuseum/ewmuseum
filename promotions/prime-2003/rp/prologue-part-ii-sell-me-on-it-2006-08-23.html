Prologue, Part II: Sell Me On It

23 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/23/06 It was time for another sit-down with my accomplice (or was he my victim? Or I his?), Chandler Tsonda. A triumph on the biggest stage in wrestling had provided me with new insight as to Chandler the competitor, but I still knew very little about Chandler, the human. And I was still working on his terms; I had to follow him around for a day and show him what I wrote. If he didn’t like it, he could rip it up and we’d never see each other again. And even with the knowledge that he would read these very words, I knew that Chandler Tsonda’s ego would force him to keep everything that I had written, good or bad. Bad publicity is worlds above no publicity. Because I wanted to have some semblance of my own life, I would stay in a separate hotel from Chandler, always. I didn’t want to be bogged down by the minutia of brushing teeth; I could afford a slightly grander scale of quotidian fare. So I was meeting him on a corner, where he pledged that he would start the day’s affairs by picking up a newspaper. And so that was the reason why I had been standing on said corner for roughly two hours. Truthfully, an hour and a half had been devoted to sitting in the nearby coffee shop playing Spider Solitaire and trying to get the number for Gil Lavenstein, Chandler’s agent. My efforts had been fruitless and the gnawing sense that I would miss him by mere seconds sitting inside brought me out into the creeping sunshine. A white Acura TL pulled up alongside the curb and parked illegally. Chandler Tsonda stepped out, grimaced at the sun pouring down and slid on a pair of sunglasses. He didn’t make any motion towards me, but procured his newspaper with the carefree attitude that would allow one to arrive two hours late in the first place. “Is this your idea of professional?” In our first encounter, I had given up trying to be the consummate professional nice-guy with Tsonda. He was a different breed, one who hoped and dreamed that you would rise to his bait. Arguing for the sake of riling one another up was an intoxicant to him. “How do you mean there, J-Bug?” The deity shot me a look from under his heavenly lunettes, clearly implying that I was his underling for the day, and perhaps for as long as I wanted to use him as my story. “You said that you start every day by getting a newspaper at this corner.” I tried to keep my voice at a reasonable level and I resisted the natural urge to let my fists clench themselves. Other than the fact that I was bound to whatever Chandler had in store for me, I would clearly not win in a fight. While not a physical juggernaut himself, he was certainly in better shape than me. Anything more physical than a thumb war placed the advantage firmly in his camp. “It’s eleven o’clock already!” “Yeah, guess it is.” The newspaper was far more interesting than anything I had to say. “I don’t really like to, ya know, confine myself to normal human standards. I’m always on one time zone: Tsonda Standard Time.” All of this came while Chandler perused the front page, perhaps holding out hope that there was some slight mention of his name. “Whatever, I’ve been here since nine.” There was not a hint of remorse on his part; and why should there be? In the eyes of the martyr, it was I who had invaded his daily routine. But I had never witnessed Chandler to be dumb; he knew the reciprocal nature of what I was doing. He just tried his damnedest to deny it. “Have ya know, small fry? That’s a bitch. Well, you can go sit in the car seat while Daddy finishes up some grown-up work. And hey, maybe if you’re a good boy, we’ll go to PlayPlace at McDonald’s later! Would my big guy like that?” “You want me to blow up and quit? It’s not happening. What’s more: you don’t want me to quit. You want me to prove myself like this and that’s the real reason you’re acting like the sophomore frat boy who just realized there’s someone new to abuse. So I’ll just sit in the car until you’re done, King Tsonda. At your leisure, m’lord.” I was furious. This bizarre type of interaction was still foreign to me, no matter how much I had told myself I was acclimatized to Tsonda. One meeting had not been enough to break me in. I would have to leave what my small, but occasionally ferocious, pride in its cradle at home. The decision had already been made for me; there was going to be no keeping of an objective, journalistic view of this story. Chandler was polarizing on every level and it would be a lie to try and convince you otherwise. I moved toward the car and snapped at the door handle. After fidgeting for ten seconds, I realized that I was only going to look like a fool if I continued. My point had been made and (I hoped) Chandler would have to take me seriously. He shrugged off the rant and, chuckling to himself lightly about my attempts to enter the car, used the keyless remote to unlock the passenger door. Fuming, but trying to settle myself, I pretend to read through some of my notes. After a couple more minutes, my colleague joined me without saying anything. He tossed the newspaper into the backseat, with the implication being that I had given up my newspaper-sharing privileges with my small explosion. Chandler started the car and fiddled with the radio controls for an inordinate amount of time. I thought this was another ploy. “We gonna go anywhere or are we gonna stall for two hours to keep some more reporters waiting?” “Easy on the controller, buddy. You’re not being hazed anymore so you can stop pretending you’re a prick and get back to being the quiet guy again. It suits you better.” Chandler could see through my façade of bravado; I was not quite the gunslinger that he was. Then again, he didn’t have to prove himself for the second time in a week. “Don’t you have some questions to ask me to, ya know, make it less awkward?” “The second you decide that you’ll give me full rights to do the story that I want, I’ll have all the questions you could imagine.” Perhaps it had slipped Chandler’s mind (I doubt it) that were both yet to commit to this story. I had explained the possibilities for us as a tandem, but we were presently working on his dangling caveat, his stipulation that he needed a sample of my work. Despite my confidence in my writing, I was more than happy to prove its merit. What worried me more was allowing the intrusion of Chandler Tsonda into my creative process; a realm in which he admittedly did not belong. However, like the two-hour wait, I was certain that everything was just part of a larger test. I can’t imagine what it’s like to be written about this precisely. I could understand, personally, why he wanted to scrutinize what would appear in millions of homes, God willing. The image Chandler had built was one of walking on a tightrope: he couldn’t appear too sympathetic to detract from how wrestling fans saw him but he would have to forsake the everyone-be-damned attitude if he sought widespread acceptance, a la Mick Foley. The day itself was one of the most painful I have ever endured in the name of writing. To try and detail each event would only bring upon my readers that same numbing sense of dullness, so I recorded only in my notebook all the places we visited: laundromat, Vietnamese café for lunch, clothes shopping, gym, Fresh City for early dinner. When we finally reached my hotel, I was a defeated prizefighter; drooling and barely able to stand. “So that’s your day? That’s the best you can do to show that you’re newsworthy?” I had to know just what had gone through Chandler’s head all day. No longer would I, or could I, pretend to understand the machinations of his mind. “What the hell do you want from me? I said you could follow me around and write shit down. It’s your job to make it interesting.” It was an otherworldly argument; the elfin young man with a notepad was trading barbs with the (relatively) brawny fashionista. And this argument was forcing my hand. “Maybe it turns out you’re just not interesting enough to base a story on.” Did I believe my own words? I’m not sure, but I knew, with a hundred percent certainty that Chandler would call my bluff.“I’m not interesting enough? J-Bug, I’m a fucking Encyclopedia Britannica of cool. It would bring that permanent morbid look off Jill Carroll’s face to even be around me. But…” Underneath the designer sunglasses, some horrid plan was evolving. Still, I held fast to the knowledge that Chandler would take this as a challenge to his chic. “…if you think there’s nothing to write, then that’s that.” My knees went a-buckling. My stomach felt weightless for a second and then everything felt like I was stuck in that liberating, terrifying elevator freefall. My story was crumbling and my only option was to watch the wind steal it, laughing, from my fingertips. The car began to pull away. And after an eternal fifty feet, the Acura stopped and plopped lovingly into reverse. There was no more emotion than before and, in the denouement to whatever movie was playing his head, Chandler leaned towards me. “You’ve got your magazine story, but not right away. Give me until ReV 105. Then we’re in business.” I hadn’t had the time to switch between one shock to the other so I suppose I took this new proclamation in stride. Suggested deadlines be damned, I could work during the many menial hours spent with Chandler during the day (if today was any indication of his life), but I would finish this story. “Okay, I’ll be in touch.” “I’m sure you will.” Not willing to let his movie conclude without his having the final word, Chandler took off. The Acura was no longer taking with it the breath out of my chest and so I turned back towards the hotel before it was out of sight. I made my way up to my room and passed out almost immediately. Dreaming, it seemed, took too much effort. My brain had been challenged enough for the day and was now going into hibernation. I wouldn’t need it until ReV 105, until the proverbial big break began to materialize.
