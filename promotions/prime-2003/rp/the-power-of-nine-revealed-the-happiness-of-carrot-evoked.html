Date: May. 2003, Mar. 2004, Jan. 2009 I've dealt with numbers all my life, of course, and after a while you begin to feel that each number has a personality of its own. A twelve is very different from a thirteen, for example. Twelve is upright, conscientious, intelligent, whereas thirteen is a loner, a shady character who won't think twice about breaking the law to get what he wants. Eleven is tough, an outdoorsman who likes tramping through woods and scaling mountains; ten is rather simpleminded, a bland figure who always does what he's told; nine is deep and mystical, a Buddha of contemplation... - Paul Auster, The Music of Chance ------------------------------------------- May 5th, 2003. The stream of thick, gray smoke rolled thunderous from his lungs. A home it held for nearly half a minute before, like volcan ash, it erupted from his pursed lips. Owen's pupils dilated, the dark brown of his irises dissapearing almost completely in the same way the sun might during a lunar eclipse. He considered how he'd eaten the mushrooms she'd given him and how the colors on the leaves were shifting from green to yellow and back to green. Farhat put little stock in the fact that his friends said four grams was too much for his first time. It was a fine thing to lay in the grass and watch his trip unfold, a bowl and a half oz. of mid-grade marijuana the only friends left to keep him company during his experience. Fields in South Georgia were all the same. Flat, ornate with animal shit and soft grass. These grasslands usually belonged to someone with a pair of overalls and a John Deere hat stuffed so full of money that they wouldn't fit properly anymore. Rarely, if ever, would these fat farm cats be seen prowling the terrains they rightfully owned. Most agreed you'd be more likely to catch them at Harrah's Cherokee Casino, gambling money and licking up the shots of scotch whiskey they spill between the tits of rent-a-whores than anywhere near their unworked property. His eighteen-year-old frame laid near motionless in that particular field as a result of the owner's known negligence. When Owen closed his eyes the strangest, sickest, depraved and beautiful things would flash into his sub-conscious. Some were pleasant feelings and colors, transcending the darkness of locked eyelids. A few were intense and burned into his retinas and forcing retained memory. Dark, decadent visions of nonsense. Owen felt like those visions were the kinds of daydreams that Eric Robert Rudolph had. They made him sweat and uneasy. Even worse, nausea was creeping in during the harsh parts which were so ugly, red and violent. Owen wished he was a child again. He thought he should try and be like a child, anyway. He wanted to be wide-eyed and love animals and believe in destiny and all of that. "That's not me though." Indeed. That was not him. The psilocybin was effecting his brain in such a way that he had lost control of his own thoughts. Where he was once zen and centered he was erratic, irrational and fearful. What was once a totally pleasant feeling now left him staring down the barrel of a bad trip. A long, lonely path that would change his life once he made it. If he made it. Farhat recoiled into a fetal position. He was resigned to his fate in that any moment he would hurl up something. He hated throwing up. Though perhaps he deserved it, Owen thought, for not listening to his friends who were all the wiser. Finally he could no longer contain his own stomach's ambitions, moving to all fours and facing the grass. At first he dry heaved and realized he may have been over-reacting. But then it came, a blast of what had been burned to liquid in his stomach. His eyes watered and tears escaped through shut eyelids if only for his own body's tension during his gut's mutiny. Visions of numbers blazing a trail through the darkness. "Ugh... fucking... GOD DAMNIT!" He rolled onto his back and opened his eyes. That was when the world w a r p e d. ------------------------------------------- March 20th, 2004 When Janelle Collins walked into her lovely home she found her worst nightmare staring her right in the face. Her three year old daughter, affectionately named Carrot, had a swollen face from weeping. She was out of breath, cries sounding like squeaks. Even still, she looked over her daughter and to the trail of brain matter, skull residue and blood. "Oh, my God..." Mrs. Collins scooped her daughter up in her arms and stepped outside, closing the door quietly behind her. Carrot was quietly sobbing with her face pressed hard against her mother's shoulder. Janelle was crying softly with her while digging in her pocket and retrieving a cellphone. She flipped it open quickly and began searching through the contacts list. "Sssh, Sssh. Carrot, it's okay baby. Everything's okay, I swear baby. I swear. Sssssh." Deep down, Janelle knew nothing was okay. Her tender, green eyes told the story. Not only was the love of her life dead, but she saw the gun near his hand. She knew he was depressed and their relationship was on the decline, but did he really feel so trapped as to kill himself? Would he really think it necessary to take his own life in order to escape her? Guilt creeped up her spine like a cat on edge. She pressed a button and held the phone to her ear. "C'mon..." Carrot had hushed a little but not enough for Janelle herself to calm down. She needed a cigarette. She needed a drink. She needed her husband. Her guilt was quickly replaced with anger. The selfish asshole! The fucker! Such audacity! "Mike. Hey... This isn't easy to tell you... I need you to take Carrot for a few days while I clear my head..." Carrot started crying, "PLEASE DON'T LEAVE ME MOMMY!" Janelle was talking to two people at once. "Carrot, be quiet... ...Your brother's dead, Michael... I said your brother's-- I SAID YOUR BROTHER'S DEAD MIKE!" Carrot screamed in the kind of way that only a child with a broken heart can. He was such an asshole. A wierd, less-likeable version of the asshole she was married to. The asshole laying dead in the living room. "I'm sorry for yelling. I know this is diffi-- I don't want to say anything right now with Carrot here, you should just come here and see for yourself if you have to... Okay. You're coming by? ...Right now? How long will it take?... Ten Minutes? Fine. Thanks Michael." Janelle pressed a button, flipped the phone back in and put it in her pocket. "Carrot, I want you to sit on the porch right here until your uncle gets here. I have to go inside and do some things, okay honey? I promise everything is fine." "Mommy please don't go! Please stay here! PLEASE!!!" Janelle hugged her daughter and kissed her. "I. Love. You," Janelle said while forcing a smile through her tears. "Don't ever forget that your mommy and daddy love you. Stay out here Carrot. Everything will be fine." Carrot didn't refuse. She did as she was told. Janelle stepped back inside, her eyes drifting to the small trail that led to the house phone, a beeping tone howling from the receiver to let whoever was in earshot know it was off of the hook too long. Her husbands body was slumped against the front of the couch. It seemed safe to assume he regretted his decision in the few seconds after he'd made it, she felt. Janelle was not like him. Not at all. She picked up the gun from the floor, making sure not to touch the hand that belonged to her late husband's shell. "I'm not like him," she told herself as she pointed the gun to her own temple. And she was not, for once she pulled the trigger she wasn't alive long enough to regret it. "To run away from trouble is a form of cowardice and, while it is true that the suicide braves death, he does it not for some noble object but to escape some ill." --Aristotle ------------------------------------------- The clouds above Owen began to look like waves in the ocean, moving forward towards him before a tide carried them backward and continuing in perpetual motion. The sun was as sharp as any knife to his eyes, causing him to squint and shudder at the imagery. He could not help his mind from drifting to thoughts about the sun. At 4.6 billion years old it was still considered an average star. Though it was average enough to become the Earth's eternal lover, whose children would worship him during several periods and throughout entire spans of civilizations. Though with a gravitational pull of 55x the Earth, it was no wonder that both her and her creatures were drawn so strongly to it. He held the kindof power that only the cruelest men on the planet dreamed of aquiring. Owen thought that, perhaps, the Sun was worthy of all of the worship he received. For an hour he dealt with the necessary demons while diving through introspective thoughts, an action forced on him by the psilocybin. No one likes to see their mistakes, their bad qualities... but this, this was something that Nine felt had come almost too late. He realized now that after all was said and done, he'd come out of this a better man. A more insightful human being on a planet he understood more of, if nothing else. This was not just a trip. Even through closed eye visions of shapes and knives and God and himself, he smiled and opened his eyes. The numbers were still dangling in his sight despite seeing his surroundings clearly. They burned bright yellow and purple, his mind shaping them from the rays of sunlight reaching his eyes. Though one number particularly was dominating the others, to the point it appeared to bully them. This did not surprise the teenaged Farhat. The poor girl was now in a pick-up truck riding with her Uncle Mike, after having seen policemen arrive on the scene shortly after him. He promised an officer he'd take care of everything, including Carrot, and they drove off shortly after. Mike did what he could to comfort her, and she had opened up just a little to him. Remarkable, he thought, for a three year old to speak well. Much less speak after something so traumatic. "Why did they do it?" Carrot finally asked. She wanted to know anything, everything. More importantly, her heart needed to know anything and everything. Mike answered carefully, "No one can know for sure, dear." He said through a few sobs of his own. "We do know they loved the heck out of you though." "But if they loved me, then why did they do it?" "Sometimes," Mike began. "Good people, they do dumb things. They do things that they wish they didn't do." There was a quiet that filled the car for most of the ride. Just before reaching their location, Carrot began. "Mike?" "Yeah hon'?" "Are you a good people?" "Yeah. Yeah... I'm a good person." "Do you do dumb things?" Mike shrugged his shoulders. "Yup. We all do dumb things." Her uncle parked the truck outside of a small trailer, the place that would become Carrot's own, personal Hell. The place she would first dream of her eventual savior and the place that would always haunt her, for the rest of her life. ------------------------------------------- January 5th, 2009. HAPPY 8TH BIRTHDAY CARROT!!!! The banner swayed above the door of the quaint, brick house proudly. Neighbors, elementary school friends of Carrots' and friends of Nine's had gathered for the party. What a party it was, too. Carrot had confessed that she hadn't had a true birthday party that she could remember except for her 3rd and her 7th. Though her 7th was kindof a last minute affair as she'd neglected to tell Nine until the day before. So Nine went out of his way to make this one as kickass as possible. No expense was spared on this day. Pony rides, a moonwalk, ice cream cake and a crapload of party games all added up to make a sum of Carrot's favorite day in her life thus far. She had survived her darkest hours, the tough little girl, and even the most skeptical psychiatrists were amazed at Carrot's progress. Though she had issues that would impact the rest of her life, they noted she was genuinely happy at this stage. If it were up to Nine, she wouldn't meet with a child psychiatrist at all. This was a decision made by the court of law, and so she was there twice weekly. But not that day. Nope. Though the party was over and she was stuffed full of cake, she was still wide awake as Nine and his friend Carlos chatted over a few beers and played with cards at the bar counter of the kitchen. She climbed onto a stool. "What are y'all playing?" Carlos grinned, "We're playing War. Know how to play?" Carrot chuckled, "Yeah I played at school." They played another round of War, with Carlos winning both of them. She played coy for a just a moment before coming right out with it, "Can I play?" "Of course," Nine said. "But I must warn you, Zanahoria," Carlos spoke in a joking tone. "I am a War pro. I've never lost a game, and an eight year old like you doesn't stand a chance!" "I'll show you," she yelped excitedly. "Deal the girl in, Nine. I gotta show her who's who." Nine did as told by his friend. All three of them shared laughs and played cards well into the night. Carlos took note of the fact that both Nine and Carrot never seemed happier. ------------------------------------------- "When we feel love and kindness for others, it not only makes others feel loved and cared for, but it helps us also to develop inner happiness and peace." --H.H.
