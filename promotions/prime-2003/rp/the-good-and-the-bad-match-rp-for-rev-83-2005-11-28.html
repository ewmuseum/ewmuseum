The Good and the Bad (match RP for ReV 83)

28 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/28/05 Doctor’s Orders ~Tuesday, November 28, 2005. 2:19P.M.~ “You’re looking better. Still not a hundred percent though. I’d take it easy for another week, maybe two.” “I’ve got a match tomorrow night.” Matt pushed himself off of the seat and headed over to a small mirror in the doctor’s room, checking the still healing cut across his forehead that he had suffered two weeks ago at the hands of Hoyt and the Imperium crew. The wound was showing improvement, but it was clear that one wrong blow could possibly bust it open again. Then there was the general pain that flowed through his blood, inflicting its will on his entire body. It was clear by the continuing ache he felt any time he twisted his torso that Hoyt and his buddies had done relatively severe damage. “I suppose Wednesday night is too early to step back in the ring, Greg?” “Probably.” Dr. Steinhilber said it with a half-hearted sigh, most likely tired of athletes overruling his decision with their own stubborn stupidity. For the life of him, he couldn’t figure out why they even took the time, or effort, to drag themselves in for an appointment when they knew they weren’t going to give a bit of respect to his professional, and well educated, advice. Instead, they just hopped in their car and drove to his office, all the way feeding themselves with delusions of their own destiny, which they would then hurl on his diagnosis, paying it not the least bit of attention. The doctor folded his arms, shooting a bit of an annoyed look at his patient. “But, I don’t suppose that’s going to keep you from wrestling.” “Nope. Big match this week. Main event against a newly crowned champion. Helluva competitor. With a lot of heart. I’m not missing that. I’m wrestling at ReVolution.” “So tell me, Matt… why even bother coming in for this check up if you’re just going to take my recommendation, toss it out the window and do the exact opposite? You could have saved yourself some time and money, ya know.” “Greg, it’s not like I don’t value your opinion. Otherwise, I’d have switched doctors. Besides, you were nice enough to overlook your policy of ‘no children under twelve’ and you’ve been great with Georgie since she was born. I value your advice. I just can’t always take it.” “Ha” Dr. Steinhilber chuckled under his breath, “care to give me an example of advice you could take?” “Well, if I were to come in here looking to know the status of my condition, and you were to tell me that I shouldn’t wrestle because wrestling could likely get me killed if I got hit or fell just the wrong way… then I would probably take your advice.” “Probably?” “No promises.” “You’re quite an interesting individual, Mr. Ward.” Greg shook his head, a part of him still perplexed by the situation. “I’ve been told that before.” “So, I tell you to take it easy for at least another week, and you’re gonna step into the ring tomorrow night and let some guy kick your ass?” “Actually the goal is not to get your ass kicked.” “All the same. The fact remains, a handful of days after just narrowly avoiding having your head shredded to pieces, and with a still healing gash above your brow… you’re going to shrug your shoulders, say ‘oh well!’ and risk having your face beaten.” “Actually, in this particular case, I’m more concerned with the possibility of having my face eaten.” “What?” “Nothing, Greg.” “Like I said, you’re an interesting guy, Matt.” “Thanks.” Matt walked over to the corner of the room and grabbed his jacket from the back of a chair. He held it in his right hand, letting the end of the sleeves drag the ground, not bothering to put it on. “I’ll probably be back in here soon… unfortunately. Take care, Greg.” “You too.” Before Greg could let his mind trail on to the next appointment on his list, it occurred to him to stop Matt before he had left the office. “Hey, by the way, how’s Mary doing?” In the previous weeks, the question would have stopped Matt like slamming on the emergency brake while barreling down a back country road. Now, instead, he slowly came to a pause in the hallway just outside the office and he turned back towards Greg with a content look on his face. “Things have been good the last couple of weeks. Well… good, considering the circumstances. Things have just been much more peaceful around the house.” “That note you were telling me about when you were in here a couple weeks ago… the one she made for herself after you told her what was going on… you took it from her, right?” “No, I took it from the top of the microwave, where she had left it.” Matt smiled, switched his coat over to his left hand, and gave a quick wave. “Thanks again, Greg.” * * * Examining the Opponent: The Favorite Creation He couldn’t remember on what day it had been when God created man, he knew it didn’t matter. What he did know, was that whatever day it had been, had certainly been a bad day for the creator of all things. God should have stopped while he was ahead… with the dolphins and gorillas and all things not human. Man, as it turned out, was somewhat a failure. Where the fish swam effortlessly, bothered by nothing, the great cats hunted as needed… only as needed, and the apes grazed peacefully and cleansed one another without thought… man messed everything up, turning his world into one of confusion, violence, and turmoil. The truth might have been as simple as man being so smart that he was stupid. God had smiled, thinking he had produced his finest creation, and he had laid everything at man’s fingertips, only to watch him destroy and tear apart what he should have held and caressed. It must have made God sad, then likely angry, to watch his favorite wreck everything he had worked so hard to make perfect. Mankind had been the end of peace. Where they should have worked together, respected one another like the thousands of other species that shared earth as home with the humans, all mankind could do was rape and pillage the world they had been given, then turn around and rape and pillage one another. And, where for quite some time Matt had considered himself entirely different, the recent months had taught him otherwise. While Mary struggled with an unfortunate and terrible situation, he found ways to distance himself from a situation he should have held softly in his hands. It was nothing to be terribly proud of, nothing that God could smile about, and the only thing that kept that fact from breaking him down was that he found he didn’t care. He just didn’t care. God likely didn’t appreciate the way Matt was handling the situation, likely didn’t appreciate the type of husband and perhaps even the type of father he was being… but Matt didn’t appreciate the tragedy God had allowed to strike Mary. That was bullshit… and he knew it. God wasn’t meant to interfere in the life of mankind… and somewhere deep in the pit of his being, Matt probably believed that… but recent events had caused a part of him to buy into a vengeful God, something he didn’t much care for. So he had stopped giving a damn what God might think of him. Who cared what a bitter, angry God thought? Though, even if rather emotionally apathetic about God and his approval, the way of God’s vengeance did appeal at least to general curiosity. It seemed unlikely that God would not be irate over watching his supposed greatest creation wreck everything he cared for with an ignorance that seemed unbelievable. Certainly the vengeful God that man was to fear, yet love and respect (and that idea itself was enough to kill Matt’s last bit of caring) would never allow mankind to continue to destroy and murder without suitable punishment. The end of the world would see to that, but the end of the world would be the end of all things. Surely, a truly angered and vengeful God would seek more payback towards mankind than the simple ‘end of all things’. Every last member of mankind had their flaws, and every last one would have given God at least one moment of disappointment and disgust, and so it seemed that every last one was fair game in God’s plan of revenge. Who could say? Maybe God wanted to spite him, and that’s why Matt found himself waking up every day next to a beautiful girl who had no idea they had shared any yesterdays in the last few months. How fitting it was that at The Great American Nightmare, God would be in his opponent’s corner. Then again, maybe God was setting Hoyt up for disaster. It wouldn’t be that hard… to fool him… not that Hoyt was ignorant of his own liability… it was merely that Hoyt was human and guilty of his own moments of disappointment… and mankind had proved its stupidity to God countless times before. Matt knew that anything was possible. He and Hoyt were simply members of mankind… God’s greatest failure. Matt started to turn into his driveway, but stopped, leaving his car sitting in the street at an awkward angle. A new vehicle occupied the spot, blocking his destination. * * * Walking with an Old Friend ~Tuesday, November 28, 2005. 2:46P.M.~ “It’s been quite a while since you’ve stopped by to pay a visit. We’re usually the one’s coming to visit you.” Matt closed the front door behind him and hung his jacket carelessly on the coat rack in the entrance way. “Yeah, but that was usually when you came to visit me in the hospital.” Rising from his seated position on the couch next to Mary, the owner of the vehicle which had forced Matthew away from his own driveway and relegated him to the street in front of his own home made his way over to his old friend. “So that doesn’t count.” “C’mon Seph… now you’re just nitpicking.” The two men shook hands, Seph adding a small nod as he did so. “How’re ya doin’, buddy?” “I’m good. Mary and I were just talking about Georgie. That’s quite a cute little baby you have there. Must be one proud dad.” “Absolutely.” Matt smiled as he looked around Seph, over towards his wife and child. He saw Mary stand, Georgie in her arms, and head across the living room, acknowledging the two as she left. “Well, I’ll leave you two boys to yourselves. I’m gonna try and get Georgie to take a nap. It was nice seeing you again, Seph.” She smiled and nodded, then disappeared into the bedroom. “So what brings you hear, Seph? This is a little out of the ordinary for you.” “Why don’t you grab your coat, Matt. We’ll go for a walk or something.” Seph didn’t bother to wait for Matt to grab his jacket, he reached over and pulled it off the coat rack himself, handing it to his friend. “What’s the matter, Seph? Don’t like my living room? I actually think the couch is pretty comfort…” “Living room is fine. Nice place all around. Just feel like a walk on a cool November Day.” “Sure” Matt wasn’t entirely sure what to make of Seph’s odd obsession with autumn weather, but he figured it wasn’t anything that actually had to do with the magnificent changing of the leaves or any of that bullshit. “After you.” He opened the door and let Seph exit the house first, the followed a step behind. “So what’s up?” “Just thought I’d stop by and check in on you guys.” As Seph spoke, he made no attempts to actually move his legs, a necessity to the process of walking he had just moments ago expressed so much interest in. “See how things were going. I know you said a couple weeks ago that things had been rough, ever since you had told Mary about the wrestling… the amnesia… and she had written it all down.” “Yeah, it was pretty nasty. Seph… you do realize that you aren’t actually walking anywhere for our little walk, right?” His sarcastic, though good natured, remark was not met with the same humor. Seph turned around quickly and grabbed Matt by the collar of his coat, slamming him against the house, the cool brick penetrating Matt’s skin even through the jacket, its sting matching the pain of the impact. “You took the note from her, didn’t you?! You found it sitting around the house, and taking advantage of the fact that she can’t remember that she left herself a note and has to just stumble across it… you took the damn thing and hid it from her so you could once again keep her in the dark!” “Seph, you can only cry and scream and curse at each other over the same god damn issue so many times before you just break! I didn’t want to break.” “You’re using her amnesia to your advantage. You’re making use of her ignorance! That’s pathetic, man. How about dealing with the problems, how about retiring and staying retired so that there’s nothing for you to even hide in the first place. There’s a million ways you could handle this.” “You think I fucking like the fact that my wife can’t remember anything that’s happened over the last few months?! How sick do you think I am? I took the note because it was tearing us apart. I took the fucking note because I didn’t want Georgie to grow up with parents constantly screaming and cursing at each other. I don’t want my daughter to grow up with a single parent, cuz Mom and Dad split after the three hundredth argument about the same god damn issue! I want Mary back… the way I remember her. I want me back… the way I was. But right now, that ain’t happening” Matt paused long enough to reach up between Seph’s arms and push them off, breaking the hold. “and for you to imply that I took that note for any reason other than the fact that it was only causing us to run in a viscious circle going nowhere… is bullshit!” “Matt…” “I want nothing more than Mary to get back every last bit of her memory. I want the memory loss to go away. I want her to somehow defy what we know about the human mind, and recall her memory of every single day she’s lost thanks to this amnesia. I want her to know I’m wrestling. I want her to know I lied and that I wasn’t a true hero and knight and shining armor during all this. And I want to be able to move past that… to apologize… to make up for it. But I can’t do that right now, and that note wasn’t helping anything good happen. All it was doing was causing us to blow up at each other and hold us in place, not letting us move forward. That note was nothing more than prison for both of us, so your damn right I took it. And not because I’m a complete son of a bitch, but because… contrary to what you might believe…but because I still love her more every day than the day before… and I’m not about to let go or lose my family because of a piece of paper.” “So then why don’t you just quit wrestling, Matt?” Seph took a step back, his anger clearly extinguished by the passionate, though equally enraged, reply he had just received. “What would it help, Seph? I’m home most of the time. I feed Georgie. I roll around on the floor with her. I tuck her into her crib. I kiss my wife. I tell her I love her. Wrestling hasn’t changed all that. But it has given me the one night of escape that I need. I get to be inhuman in the ring, and I do it so that I can be human at home, with my family, where it matters. If I quit now, I’ve still lied to Mary. When…if… she gets her memory back, I still have to tell the truth, and the truth would still be that I broke my promise. Meanwhile, I lose my escape, I lose my release of pressure and confusion and pain and all that shit. And I run the risk of becoming an asshole in my own home.” “Yeah…” Seph didn’t know much of what he should say, or if he should even really open his mouth to say anything at all. “I guess so.” “Guess so? Don’t you get it, Seph. There’s no debate here. Watching Mary walk around like some half of a person, remembering how I jumped at the chance to join PRIME the moment it happened… it reminds me how messed up I can really be. And every time I step into that ring, it reminds me just how beautiful and how dangerous being that messed up can be. I go in there every week with no intentions of proving myself to the world. I step into the ring to spare my wife and child from this fucking sickness I’m inflicted with.” “Right. Proving your dominance to yourself, not to anyone else.” “No, understanding my faults won’t get the best of me. At the end of all those evenings, when I stand over a fallen, beaten, equally inflicted individual… it lets me know that I have it in me to fight through anything. And when I leave the arena that night, and hop on a plan and fly back home… that’s the real victory. It’s not about being 7-0 or the King of Kings. Those are symbols of what its really about. Face Eater thinks I want to step into that ring tomorrow night and prove myself the new face of PRIME… the hot newcomer who will be the future of the federation. He’ll probably step into that ring to face me expecting me to want to crush him so that I can shout “8-0” at the top of my lungs. He’ll expect that I’ll want to prove that I deserve my shot at the Universal title. And he’ll probably think this is about sending a message to Hoyt, or about proving myself as the dominant figure in the company, defeating his newly crowned self as the message. And those are all side effects.” “Side effects?” Seph rubbed the back of his neck, the conversation turning into more than he had bargained for. “The Illustrious Face Eater has his own special train of though and motivations. But in many ways, its always the same no matter who the opponent. Either they’re all out to prove themselves to everyone, or they think that’s what you’re out to do. When I got my start in this business in N4O, I took one thing away from that experience that I’ve carried with me to this day. Know who you are and don’t be out to prove anything to anyone. In the middle of all his craziness, Jace was damn right about that.” “Yeah, he was.” “And now I realize that in a way, everybody who climbs into that ring is alike. We’ve all got some sickness driving us, it just varies from person to person. When I step into that ring with anybody, you, Killean, Jace, Face Eater… its about understanding that my inhuman side is powerful enough to control when it needs to, and give way when it should. Victory lies in knowing that I fought through my sickness where the other guy couldn’t. That’s 7-0. That’s being King of Kings. That’s success. The difference between winning and succumbing.” “So you keep wrestling, so you can keep knowing you can come home and be a decent guy…” “Seph, I know Mary doesn’t deserve to be lied to, but does she deserve to wake up next to a monster? Next to something cold and inhuman?” “No.” “Yeah, exactly. I let PRIME deal with the inhuman being… so I can let my wife and child deal with the human being. It’s best that way.” The door behind them opened, and Mary leaned out, trying to avoid the chill as much as possible. “Everything ok out here?” “Isn’t it always?” Matthew turned and smiled, giving Mary the assurance she needed to head back to Georgie. He turned his attention back to Seph. “I gotta go. Mary and I always do better getting Georgie to fall asleep as a tag team.” “Thought you despised tag teams.” “This one is an exception.” Matt laughed as he turned and stepped inside the house. “I told you, I’m not that bad of a guy.” Matt closed the door. The TV was humming in the corner of the living room. Mary was singing in the bedroom. Georgie was still awake.
