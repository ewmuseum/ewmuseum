Allow me to reintroduce myself

3 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 3rd, 2010 Perhaps you are surprised to hear the news that Steve Starr is returning to PRIME. After all, it has been nearly seven long years since Steve Starr first made an impression, winning the Intense Championship while appearing to be the next big hit in professional wrestling. What happened next is a tale known all too well, Steve Starr pushed himself to the limit and suffered a serious injury that threatened his career with PRIME. After returning to full health, Steve returned but with a new emphasis; making money. Many observers are quick to label him a sell-out, referring to his contract with Nike becoming their first ever professional wrestling representative. It was a bold move by Nike, but not as bold as the next move that Steve was pushed into believing was solid. Steve Starr left PRIME for good and operated as an independent professional, charging a fee for one-time appearances which led him on a circuit akin back to the grassroots era of professional wrestling. Steve had somehow believed that he had the kind of clout to someday charge $1 million dollars per appearance. Those were lofty expectations for a competitor without any sort of World Championship on his resume, but then again Steve Starr was all about taking risks. For years he lived his life on the edge, consistently finding himself in horrible situations and never seeming to learn anything from previous exploits. With a guy like Steve Starr and his kind of potential, many always hoped that one day he would figure it out. After all, Steve was only his mid-20’s when he debuted in PRIME, he is now approaching his 30th birthday and seems to finally be putting things into proper perspective. “I’m like the cagey veteran that signs with the powerhouse team in just about every sport” Steve concedes, “in that I’m all about winning. I don’t care if I have to come off the bench, or in my specific case off the top turnbuckle. I know I am capable of doing things that people can only dream about.” There’s that confidence, that swagger. But you also hear a man that is not driven by the almighty dollar. Steve used to be controlled by cash, a harsh reality for someone who grew up basically having to be the father/brother to his two younger sisters. Life is much more pleasant than Steve can ever remember, he’s financially secure, his girlfriend of three years gave birth to his first child, a baby girl just over a year ago. “Life is pretty much taking a more traditional route then what I have grown used to” Steve says. His past is what it is, do not expect him to make excuses for it. What does all of this have to do with PRIME, and why is Steve committed to a return? “It’s like this, PRIME is where it is at. When I was here, you had to bring it every single week. There were no off-nights when you laced him up here. So when you actually do when something here, and I’m not just talking about a few matches, I mean titles. First of all let me be clear about something, I do not get excited about winning a few matches. I have won hundreds, maybe thousands of those. I wake up in the morning to be a champion.” This does not sound like the guy people may be familiar with. This is the same guy who once said “I’ll kick anyone’s ass anywhere if they money’s right”. No one ever doubted whether or not Steve Starr had talent, that was apparent. There were questions about his dedication to the craft, did he train? Steve was known for spending long nights out on the road, not getting enough sleep, but still having the ability to put on a show. For a while the critics wondered if that was all he cared about, being popular and making money. Unknown too many, the guy so may have turned the corner and actually a learned a thing or two. That will not be known for quite some time, but for now it is time to get reintroduced to Steve Starr. Take a seat and strap in, this could be quite a trip. The following are 7 key facts that you need to know before Steve Starr makes his triumphant return. 1. Steve Starr made his debut in PRIME in 2003. While his total time with the organization was brief, it was not without impact. As previously mentioned, he captured the Intense Championship after defeating “The First” on November 24, 2003. He also was the runner-up in the inaugural “Jewel In The Crown” Tournament, losing to Ian English who then went on to capture the now defunct Global Title. 2. Steve Starr is a tremendous athlete. This is not a hyperbole, the kid was a dual sport superstar in high school excelling in basketball and baseball. He was good enough in baseball that the Kansas City Royals selected him in the later rounds of their amateur draft. “I never really cared about baseball, my dad loved it so I stuck with it in his honor. I loved hoops, but I was kind of short.” A career with the Royals never materialized as Steve Starr suffered two off the field injuries; one while playing a pick-up game of hoops and another while “competing in my first wrestling match, what are the odds of that” Steve confirms. 3. Steve Starr got his professional wrestling career started with the North American Wrestling Federation, better known as the NAWF. It was a very small promotion with a huge following in the North/Atlantic region of the United States. A few interesting tidbits about Steve’s career, as told by Steve. “Umm… let’s see a buddy of mine from high school who was a wrestler was in the NAWF. He calls me up and says they were offering $1000 a show to some guys to lose to a few of their up and comers, so basically I began my career as a jobber. I could have cared less I was getting paid in addition to the minor league gig. After getting cut, I said screw baseball and turned my services full time to the NAWF. It was after a breakout performance against one of their supposed new studs that I was given a full-time contract. Never looked back.” 4. Steve Starr used to be the ultimate “Prima Donna” in professional wrestling. “I was terrible” Steve recalls. “I had an agent, signed a sponsorship deal with Nike. I was a complete sell-out. I really did not care about being the best. I knew I could show up and just off natural talents that I could contend. However, to be great in competition, especially individual competition, one must know himself and the opponent equally.” 5. Speaking of which, Steve Starr actually trains now. It is crazy. “I’m working about 5 days a week, waking up at 6 AM. I like to get my workouts in early, because I believe while I’m up going hard there’s some chump at home asleep or out partying. He does not believe in me so I must train hard to prove him wrong.” Steve Starr even watches film on his opponents, provided that film exists. “You better believe I have been scouting everyone in PRIME. I wanna know everyone’s tendencies, everyone’s style, I wanna see all of their weaknesses. I plan on knowing my opponent so well that the match is going to be decided even before the bell rings because I will know everything about him.” 6. Steve Starr’s return to PRIME has nothing to do with his finances, or a perceived lack thereof. “I’m not broke any means” Steve admits. “I’m damn near 30 years old. I have been at this taking care of myself thing for lord knows how long. The reason I am ready to get back into the ring has nothing to do with money. I know how difficult that may be for some to believe, but I truly miss the competition. Although it is nice to have that steady cash flow again, I cannot wait to actually hit the ring and perform for the people. 7. Steve Starr is not making a return to be some push over. “This will be the safest bet that any of you can make; but I’m here to make an impact. I’ve studied long and hard and waited for this opportunity. You better believe that I’m bringing the pain.” And with that said, PRIME-ates, prepare to be Starr Struck all over again.
