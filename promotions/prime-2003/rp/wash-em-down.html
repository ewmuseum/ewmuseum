Wash 'em Down

Date: May Sixth May 6th, 2010 Ten, maybe twelve inches in front of his face, he watched his hand as it trembled, despite his best efforts to keep it still. It wasn’t the first time he’d seen it. Everyone had struggled at some point or another to hold their hand steady. It could come from any number of events… the anxious anticipation of a job interview, the worried nerves of a final exam. Once, he’d been so concerned over a mid-term that he’d found his hand shaking so much, he hadn’t even been able to properly shade the blocks on the scan-tron; a mess of graphite spilled outside the lines, like it’d been filled by a child still indifferent to the idea of boundaries. Nothing about the manifestation of this shake was any different than those hundred other moments, but unlike all the times before, this was the first tremble he couldn’t discern the cause of. That wasn’t to say there seemed no rational explanations, but instead, too many possibilities. The nerves of a big fight. They’d struck before, and it was hard to argue that a restructuring of PRIME and a tournament to crown the new Universal Champion wasn’t a big career moment. For some individuals, it was a chance to capture their first great success, a first trip to the top of the mountain. For a highly accomplished, grizzled, borderline washed-up wrestlers like himself, it was a chance, despite the lack of that new-car smell, for something more. Maybe nothing’s as sweet as the first time… except the last. Then again, after ten years, desensitized, anxious nerves before big fights were becoming rarer by the day. So maybe it was a fearful trembling, terrified, even in his mid thirties, of losing his father. No word was as scary and suffocating as ‘cancer’, and the cool exterior he worked so hard to maintain in front of his father and mother left him exhausted and prone; the tired old man behind the curtain. Or perhaps it was just self-loathing, anger at his inability to be half the father and husband his old man had been. Maybe, hanging there in the air, his hand trembled in revolt, disgusted that it was attached to a cowardly shell of a man. Sitting on that steel folding chair, staring as his fingers danced with the slightest movements, Matt Ward didn’t know what was causing the shake. A knock at the door snapped him from his trance, and he turned his head, stretching to see over his shoulder. The small frame of a PRIME backstage-hand stepped into the locker room, waving a sheet of paper, scribbled names and times across white surface. “Showtime. You’re up.” “Already?” “End of the show. Main event goes on in five.” “Ok. I’ll be out in one minute.” The man nodded briefly, presumably in approval, then stepped out, closing the door behind him. Matt leaned forward, grabbing at his boots, attempting to lace them up, but try as he might, he couldn’t get them to cross. The trembling was getting worse. He was in no condition to wrestle, certainly not in a way that he had come to expect from himself. But that could be fixed. He pushed himself out of his seat, the legs of the chair screeching across the floor. Walking over to his locker, Matt grabbed his gym bag, and tore it open, rummaging through its contents. He pushed aside clothes, tossed around medical tape, and dug to the bottom, pulling out a small bottle. His hands still shook, but he was able to twist off the cap and pop two pills... two pills his father wouldn't take no matter how bad the pain was. Matt took a deep breath, instantly feeling a stillness blanket his body. As the nerves settled, he felt comfortable that he would be able to perform well enough, at least, to not embarrass himself, though he wasn't counting on much more. Again, Matt reached down and attempted to lace his boots, this time finding success. While he slipped the laces through their guide holes, crossing them back and forth, a thought occurred to him and he laughed. He was feeling so good all of a sudden, that if he just had a Blue Moon, it might even be enough to win the match.
