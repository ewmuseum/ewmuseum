The Target

Date: G.A.N After all of the heartbreak, bad decisions, and tragedies Adam Garcia had faced throughout his life, it seemed to finally be heading in the right direction. He never would have imagined that life could feel good again. As a child he always believed that life was a journey full of adventures. But somewhere down the road; he’d lost his way. “Adam,” a beautiful young woman sitting next to him called out. “Hello? Earth to Adam, is anyone there?” She spoke with a Spanish accent and had a lisp that was both cute and endearing. Her long brown hair which was her trademark and a big topic of conversation amongst many, sat in curls upon her shoulders. Adjusting the straps on her red dress, she leaned forward on the bar stool and waved her hand in front of Adam’s face. Receiving no response and realizing that he did this often, all she could do was chuckle. “I’m sorry,” he said suddenly. “What were you saying again, Veronica?” Shocked by his response and taken back a few years, she corrected him. “You mean, Ana.” Being Adam’s triplet sister she could usually sense what he was feeling; but that caught her completely off guard. You could see the tears began to well up in her eyes and she looked as if she wanted to cry. Veronica was Adam’s deceased wife who died along with his two children many years ago. That painful memory, the car crash in which he caused, continued to haunt him to this day and it usually affected his health and positive state of mind. But Ana was slightly confused. Adam seemed to be in good spirits. “Adam. Umm, are you okay,” she couldn’t help but ask. Adam sighed heavily and brushed his long brown hair back into a soft and silky-like ponytail. He placed his hands into his pockets and nervously fiddled with his car keys. Searching for the words, he stared blankly into his glass. For once, he felt at peace—with the world and with himself. Life was good. A coy smile surprising crept upon that big face of his. “Actually,” he hesitated at first. “I am. I’ve never been better.” He reached forward and grabbed the shot glass full of Apple juice and took it straight to the head as if it were alcohol. Ana laughed and shook her head at the comedian her brother had always been. It felt good to see his old happy self returning. He blushed innocently and wiped his chin with the back of his hand and smirked. “So what’s so important you had to leave Mexico to tell me again? Wait, it can’t be.” His mouth dropped slightly to overdramatize the act and he pointed one of his bony fingers directly at her. “You’re pregnant, I’m telling mommy!” Ana burst into hysterical laughter at the realization of what he had just said, “Oh God, no!” She covered her mouth with her right hand and giggled gleefully. “We’re going to have to knock on wood at that one!” She knocked on the bar and mistakenly got the barkeeps attention. Shrugging through continued laughter, she said, “Oh, why the hell not… another strawberry margarita, please!” As she continued to laugh, Adam glared darkly at her, relief washing over his face. The barkeep nodded and looked Adam’s way, “And for you, sir?” Not really wanting anything he replied, “Pepsi on the rocks my good man,” he paused. “You know what? Make it a double.” “Coming right up!” “So what is it then,” he asked anxiously. “Marriage or did you finally decide to break into wrestling like the rest of us?” “Lemme stop you while you’re ahead, little brother.”Ana was born three minutes before both Adam and their twin Alex. She was the second oldest in the Garcia family and normally referred to the both of them as her “little brothers.” Blowing a loose bang from in front of her eyes, she replied, “It’s about dad. He’s running a tournament back home in Uncle Chuck’s honor. We’re all getting involved; Carlos, Alex, Manuela, Guillermo and even mom. I think it’d be good if you came back and participated. They all really miss you.” The bartender returned with their drinks and placed them on the two empty coasters in front of them. Ana slid him a twenty from her pocketbook and whispered, “Keep the change.” “I don’t know,” Adam responded. “I haven’t been home in years. I don’t think I can ever step foot on Mexican soil again; at least not now anyway.” He turned the glass up and drank it before slamming the bottom back down to the bar, almost breaking it. He hadn’t been home since serving time for the accident. Ana slid her finger around the rim of her glass, making it resonate. “Oh come on Adam,” she begged. “We’d all love it if you contributed, especially dad. You don’t even have to wrestle. Just show up, your fans would love it!” “I haven’t talked to dad since I left,” he said through gritted teeth clearly bothered by the mention of his name. “If he cared so much about me, why doesn’t he call?” Ana sipped on her margarita and batted her green eyes at Adam thinking of what to say. “He’s tried many times, Adam. You know how hard you’re to reach. I had to call your job just to get your new phone number. After a while a person just stops…well trying, ya’ know?” Adam nodded and understood. His eyes shifted from confusion to clarity. “Yeah, I hear ya’, it’s just…just,” he stuttered as he tried to find the right words. “I made a fool of the Garcia name. You guys didn’t deserve that. I was a horrible person-“ She interrupted making him wonder why she even asked, “You were young Adam and everyone makes mistakes. When you realize that, you will finally be able to go on and live your life fully again. I miss the old you. I know it’s hard baby bro, but you got to stop beating yourself up about it.” She kissed him on the cheek and embraced him. “You did your time and you got help. Look at you now. You’re happy, sober and living your dreams by wrestling for PRIME!” She shook him gently and said softly, “Look me in the eye. You’re PRIMEtime son! Tell me, who wouldn’t want to be in your shoes right now?” “I know but-“ Disrupting him again she looked directly into his blue eyes. “No buts about it, mister! There are a lot of people pulling for you back in Mexico whether you know it or not,” she informed him. “You have to give back and you can never forget where you come from.” She finished the margarita with one big gulp and got up from the bar stool. “Do it for Uncle Chuck and Mexico, Adam. Even if you don’t want to do it for papa, just think about it.” She kissed him on the forehead this time and started towards the door. She turned slightly and whispered, “We love you” to him and left. ~~~~~~~~~~~~ As he watched her leave, he observed his surroundings for the first time since he entered. It was actually quite depressing. The little bar in New York was dark, dank, filled with clouds of smoke, and reeked of stale beer. The sawdust on the floor not only made his black shoes dusty but also the bar as a whole. The floor was wet—just waiting for some drunken person to slip—and it emitted a rotten smell. Adam looked down at his watch and decided that he’d been there far too long. “Leaving so soon, sir?” Adam turned back to the bar and noticed the bartender wiping up Ana’s spot. “Call me Adam, please,” he quipped through a broad smile. “And, yeah. I’m not a drinker as you can see, and it’s getting pretty late.” The bartender chuckled holding his potbelly which reminded Adam a lot of Miles Long, his manager and maybe only friend. “Understandable. Believe it or not I don’t drink either.” He removed Adam’s glass from the table and placed it into the sink behind him. “That was some women you were with earlier. She was all over you and very attractive if you don’t mind me saying. You lovebirds from ‘round here?” Adam’s face turned red in embarrassment and he faked a smile. He ran his fingers through his eyebrows and adjusted his black suit jacket. He always dressed to impress, no matter where he went. “That was my sister. We’re from Mexico and I haven’t seen her in a while.” He giggled, “We’re a loving family.” “Ah,” he nodded perceptively. “I know what you mean. My sister and I are in our forties and she still kisses all over me.” He noticed a woman trying to get his attention from down the table. “I have to get this. But how’s about you stay a while and mingle. There’re a lot of foxy ladies in here tonight,” he jabbed Adam with his elbow gently and fluttered his graying eyebrows. “If you know what I mean.” Cougars were more like it. Most of the women there looked to be at least twice his age and Adam wanted no parts of it. He should have known taking bar advice from his older brother; Carlos was always into older women. He did decide to stay, just for a little while. Adam had no real friends of his own and it wasn’t often that someone wanted him around. Unlike the man he portrayed on television, Adam was shy, reserved and insecure. “You’re not from around here are you?” “Huh? You talking to me?” The brunette standing in front of him smiled as a hint of red came into her cheeks. Her blue eyes glimmered in the faint light as she took a seat next to him. Unlike the rest of the women in the bar she was interesting and pretty as a picture of a summer’s day. More importantly, she was young. “Yes, silly.” “What gave me away,” he chuckled nervously playing with his hands, “my accent?” She smiled. “The suit is more like it. Your kind doesn’t usually show up to places like this. However, you look strangely familiar. Have we met before?” “Uh… I don’t think so.” She caressed her chin, appearing to be in thought. “I’m sure I’ve seen you somewhere.” And then it hit her. “Oh wait, I know! You’re a wrestler aren’t you? You’re that Adam Garcia character from PRIME, right?” He smiled. “Guilty as charged.” “What are you doing at a place like this?” “Meeting my sis-…I mean just catching up with some old friends.” He wanted to come off innocent but not desperate. She waived for the bartender. “I’m Eva. Can I get you something to drink?” “Naw, I’m good,” he replied. “I don’t drink anymore-“ She punched him on the shoulder gently. “Oh come on, just one drink you little girly! What’s that going to hurt?” Adam placed his head into his hand and just shook. He knew what one drink would do to him but at the same time he didn’t want to disappoint her. He hadn’t had much success with the ladies lately and this could have been his chance to get back out there, so to speak. Besides, he remembered what his sister just said and maybe a drink would be good. A toast to a new beginning. “Live on the edge for once!” But then he remembered. “I’m a recovering alcoholic. I’ve been sober for five years.” Eva gasped, “Oh, my God!” Her mouth opened wide and she said with a voice full of concern, “I’m so very sorry. I didn’t mean to make fun.” She wanted to put her foot in her mouth. “Isn’t this place a little tempting? No offense, but I mean… if I was in your shoes this would be the last place I’d want to be.” That hadn’t hit him. The temptation of alcohol didn’t bother him much anymore, in fact the thought of it made him sick. The bartender returned. “What can I get for you?” “I’ll take a rum and coke, please. And get my friend here whatever it is he’s been drinking on all night.” “Coming right up.” She smiled at Adam and he smiled back. There was something off about her, he thought. Why was she so interested in him? She was beautiful and could surely have any man she wanted and Adam was just, well Adam. It didn’t quite seem right with him. And how did she know who he was? He’d only wrestled one match and he was nowhere near famous. Maybe he was just thinking too much about it. The bartender returned with the rum and brought Adam another Pepsi. He winked at him and gave him what seemed like an, “I told you so” kind of look. She nudged him. “What do you say we get out of here?” Adam almost choked on his Pepsi. He wasn’t ready for that, not yet anyway. He didn’t want to seem too hopeless so he went with the flow. Tonight he was going to be Adam Garcia the wrestler, and not Adam Rivera the uninteresting man he really was. “What did you have in mind?” “I have a place right up the street, what do you say?” He wanted to say “hell yeah,” but settled with a “sure sounds fun.” Deep in his gut something told him not to go but he ignored his conscious and decided that a little trouble wouldn’t hurt. Eva threw a ten on the bar table and took him by the hand and lead him toward the exit. Adam turned and looked back at the bartender who was trying to get his attention but Eva had already pulled him out. “Wait here,” Eva instructed him. “I have to make a call and make sure my girlfriend isn’t home.” Adam nodded in agreement and did what he was told. Eva journeyed into the middle off the darkened streets, cell phone pressed deeply to her ear. It was the first and last mistake she’d ever make. “John, we got him. The target is locked in.” "Good. I knew you wouldn't disappoint." She accidently dropped the phone and taking her attention off of her safety she bent down to pick it up. Such a stupid move but she was so excited for some reason. At that very moment a blue Monte Carlo with a white lightning bolt on the side zipped down the street with no regard for anyone in it. Poor Eva looked like a deer caught in the headlights. “EVA, WATCH OUT!” Adam came running. But it was too late. The car flattened her. He dropped to his knees and covered his head as the car skidded off. The bartender who watched this from out of the bar window came running. He went into the middle of the street and felt Eva’s neck for a pulse. The crowd inside was sure to follow. She was dead. A muffled voice came from the broken cell phone but it was inaudible. At that moment Adam didn’t know what to think. He was at a lost for words. Why him? Why tonight? He was just in good spirits, why this now? Why didn't God want to see him happy? Was Ana trying to warn him, or did she herself have a hand in this little fiasco? She couldn't have. Why did that car look so familiar? He's sure he's seen it somewhere. Come to think of it, just who in the hell was "Uncle Chuck"? And who was this mysterious woman? ...
