The Things He Wanted

17 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/17/07 This is not a story about drugs. This is not a story about gangs. Not everyone is involved with those things. This isn’t a story about violence and tragedy. This is a story about the little things in life… the small things everyone tries not to sweat, but somehow manage to eat at every nerve, leaving a person plenty exhausted for when the real fucking bomb drops. * * * Saturday He wanted to be like Scotty. He wanted to have fun and somehow manage to live a good life without ever having to deal with that terrible thing called responsibility. He wanted to not have to succeed or fail. As Matt kicked the soccerball around the backyard with his younger brother, he couldn’t help but feel a little jealous. Inside the house, Scott’s fiancee was on a business call. With his free time on a Saturday afternoon, the younger Ward had decided a cool breeze and a light rain was the perfect weather for some soccer (sorry… realfootball) and had somehow convinced Matt to join him. “Scotty, shouldn’t you be inside cleaning the house, or starting on dinner? Something to help out your better half. The question was probably best directed in another direction, something more his own, and as he ignored this fact, Matt slammed the side of his right foot into the bottom of the ball, launching it towards his little brother in annoyed frustration. “Whoa!” With a quick response, Scotty controlled the pass with a rising knee, dropping it to his feet. “Dude, no shooting at my boys! And no… I shouldn’t be inside doing anything. She’d probably just kick me out of the house so she could concentrate anyways.” “I’m beginning to realize that there’s a part of you… a larege part… that will never grow up.” “Dude, you sound jealous.” Scott booted the ball back towards his brother, who stuck out his foot, but failed to stop the pass, the ball sliding off his shoe. “You’re slippin’, bro. That was lame.” “Lame?” “Yeah. That was lame.” “Cut me slack. I haven’t played since I was fourteen. Besides, its slick out here cuz of this damn, cold rain.” “Geez, you’re gettin’ old. Listen to you bitching about the weather.” Through the falling drops, Scott thought he saw a bit of smoke escape Matt’s nostrils as he snorted a response. “Some of us can’t stay eleven forever, Scott.” “Again with the jealousy.” Scotty shook his head and laughed. “There’s no jealousy.” That was a lie. “It’s more… nerves.” That was the truth. “I’m anxious.” “Anxious to…?” “To make on last, hopefully successful, run in the business. Anxious to move on to bigger things, like raising a family.” Matt ignored the ball at his feet, leaning against the wet metal of the chain ling fence that enclosed their backyard turned playing fielf. His mind was clearly no longer on the game, as his blank stare locked onto a pointless patch of grass in the middle of the lawn. “So what the hell are you nervous about?” “Neverous I’m gonna fail to de either. Ya know, it can still be a bit overwhelming… to stop and think that I’ve got a kid.” “You’ll be fine, bro. Remember, I’m the one in the family who can’t do anything right. I’ve got that role covered. You always land on your feet one way or another. Stop worrying so damn much.” While Scotty finished his attempt at a motivational pick-me-up speech, Matt’s cellphone jolted to life in his pocket. Wiping the rain from his eyes, Matt answered the call, listening carefully to every word. After a minute, he said “I’ll be home in ten minutes. I love you.” Then flipped the phone closed. “Mary?” “Yeah. Wanted to know if I was okay with just ordering pizza for dinner. Sais she wasn’t feeling too well, so I think I’m gonna head home just to make sure she’s doin’ alright.” “See… you’re gonna do fine at the whole ‘family man’ thing.” “And what about the wrestling? “Dude, you just won the dual halo. You looked great. I don’t think you’ve lost even a step.” Scotty motioned for his brother to kick him the ball. Matt obliged, scooting it away from the fence and launching it with a powerful right foot. The ball sailed ten feet to Scott’s left and slammed into the grill on the patio, causing a bottle of steaksauce to crash onto the concrete below. “Soccer, on the other hand…” “Shut up.” Matt shook his head and cursed under his breath as he opened the gate and left the yard, heading for his car. He was just stepping into the stree when he heard Scotty, back in the yard, complaining to no one in particular. “That was even lamer.” * * * Monday A slow sigh escaped her lips as she put a hand on her stomach. She couldn’t decide if the pain was from hunger or God only knew what sort of virus. She’d not felt well for the past several days and had been trying not to make too big of a deal about things, but the more it persisted, the more concerned she became. Was it the flu? Something milder? Something worse? Mary pulled a glass from the cabinet and filled it with some tap water, taking a few small sips. “Where’d I put my phone?” Georgie didn’t seem to have the answer, and so Mary began to tear her way through the kitchen, searching under every piece of junk mail and old newspaper that littered the countertops. For a moment, she thought it might be better if she couldn’t find it. She hated having to call Matthew and ask him to come home… he’d already left Scott’s early on Saturday because she’d been feeling under the weather, and she didin’t want to bother him again. But, as Mary continued to rummage through the kitchen, she knew she needed to lay down and take a nap and someone needed to watch Georgie. If Matthew could get home within a half an hour, she would have time to lie down and get in a couple hours of sleep before dinner. Finally, she located her cellphone lying next to the napkin holder and she flipped it open, deciding to text him in case he didn’t answer his phone in the middle of the meeting. Her thumbs seemed to ache as they pressed each button, spelling out the words. Baby, not feeling well again. Can you come home? Satisfied with its simplicity, she pressed the ‘Send’ button, then threw the phone out of the kitchen, watching it crash onto the couch. Dragging her feet, Mary trudged into the living room and bent over to kiss Georgie on top of the head. “Daddy should be home soon.” She smiled and took a few small steps over to the sofa, then, much like her cell phone, tossed herself down into the cushions. * * * Monday He wanted to be likeBrandon Youngblood. He wanted to be like Tony Gamble. He wanted to be a heel. For the better part of his entire career, he’d felt it in his blood. It was his natural existence. In the squared circle, he was a machine… cold, calculating, unrelenting… earning hiim the title of inhuman. Even in his life away from the ring, he’dbeen a ‘bad guy’. Bad friend. Bad husband. Bad father. And yet, he always seemed to find himself face to face with being a face. Circumstances tended to land him more often than not in the role of the hero. But the truth, however, was that he was no more a hero than Youngblood was a mild-mannered soul, or Gamble was a pretty face. Under the label of anti-hero, he still felt uglier than his role. It made Matt wonder sometimes… just how big of a jackass did you have to be to get yourself booed standing in the ring with him? Probably a rather colossal jackass. The fact remained, however, that he had always struggled accepting his role as the good guy. In the world of professional wrestling, there was a forgiveness for such a struggle. Crossing the line would get you cheered. Disregarding the well-being of others was common place. But, in the real world… a place he planned on spending a little more time in down the road… those shortcomings weren’t acceptable. Crossing the line with Mary would get him a lot of things, cheered wouldn’t be one of them. Disregarding the well-being of his family would make him the kind of father and husband he wanted desparately not to be. That was fact. In the beginning, he had intended wrestling to be his outlet. It was supposed to be a place for him to play the role of the bad guy. But, as the old saying went… the best laid plans of mice and men.... Despite his struggle to fully adapt to, and embrace, his role as the babyface, it seemed something positive had perhaps managed to work its way into things. If nothing else, he’d been forced to adjust to a high level of responsibility and expectation, something that would benefit him… “AND another autograph signing next Friday.” The words interrupted him mid thought. Though to be fair, it had been a rather long thought. “What?” Matt turned his attention towards Sara, a member of PRIME’s Public Relations staff. She had made the trip to meet him at his office to discuss his schedule from ReVolution 125 to UltraViolence. He’d been warned that it was ‘a little busy’, but that warning was quickly becoming one of the great understatements of all time. “Another autograph signing? That makes three, right?” “Yeah. Plus, Saturday, the day before UltraViolence, there’s gonna be a little photo shoot, and they want you to be a part of that.” Sara rifled through a stack of papers, whisking them one by one from her right hand to her left, apparently reading their contents as the speed of some awesome metaphor that means ‘fast’. “Jesus, that’s a lot of appearances. Three autograph signings, three radio interviews, a magazine interview, a photo shoot…” “Plus, You’re doing Conan O’Brien’s show next week to promote the PPV.” “Oh that’s cool.” Matt leaned over and tried to find the sheet that had his name booked for Late Night. “That one I’m ok with. The rest gotta go. I don’t want to do all that stuff. That’s too much.” “You’re main eventing UltraViolence. You just won the Dual Halo. PRIME is doing its best ever ratings. Business is a hit… what did you expect?” “I dunno. A chance to breathe?” “You can breathe after UltraViolence.” Sara looked up from her papers and smiled. “Unless you win the Universal Title.” “That’s about enough to make a guy want to throw a match.” “I don’t think the PRIME executives would like that much.” “Yeah, well there’s just another irritant in life.” Matt leaned back in his chair and tilted his head towards the lights in the ceiling, rubbing his eyes hard with the back of his hands. “So there’s no way to get out of some of these appearances?” “I wouldn’t count on it. Maybe we can cut one of the radio interviews, but I think you’re stuck with this for the most part.” “Damn it! See, this is why I always wanted to be a heel… you don’t have to do nine hundred goddamned special appearances!” “Not a fan of the fans?” Sara seemed a bit startled by his brief explosion. “Love the fans, just not forty times a week. And I doubt they’d want to see that much of me either.” He shifted forward, resting his forearms on the desk. “Haven’t you guys in the PR department heard of ‘over-exposure’?” Before Sara could think up an appropriate response, Matt’s cell phone beeped, letting him know he had a text, and he grabbed it from the table to check the message. “Again? Somethin must be wrong.” He closed the phone and dropped it into his pocket as he pushed his chair back and rose to his feet. “I have to go. You know you’re way out. Make some of that shit go away.” He motioned towards the stack of papers, then turned to leave. While Sara sat at her desk, beginning to sort through schedules, wondering what she could cut, he headed home… hoping all his little headaches were the worst of his concerns.
