A New Addition

17 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 17, 2006 Her hand cut through the vanilla-scented smoky darkness, her mind still floating in that space between consciousness and the dream-like slumber she struggled to reach on a near-nightly basis. The vivid recollection of the tragedy in her third year of existence slipped slowly back into the recesses of her mind while her hand fumbled atop the bedside table to grasp her cell phone, which was blaring ‘Zero’ by the Smashing Pumpkins. The one night she forgot to put her phone on silent... “Dan, we’re not in the same time zone, and it’s five-thirty in the morning...” Troy’s cotton-mouth mumbled the words into the tiny microphone, her free hand rubbed her eyes to make sure she wasn’t seeing things. “No, scratch that. Four-thir...” “Alaina’s going into labor,” Dan Ryan, Lindsay’s brother-in-law, interjected on the other end of the line. The Empire Pro Wrestling owner was making a valiant effort at remaining calm. “Her water broke on her way back from the bathroom.” ”JESUS, DAN....OWWW..!” was clearly heard and picked up on in the background. Lindsay bolted upright in bed upon hearing her sister’s cries. “Oh God...Oh God, it’s really happening.” “Your sister’s frantic enough, don’t you fall apart on me too,” Ryan’s Texas drawl commanded. “The EMT’s are getting her stable and I’m following them to the Woman’s Hospital in Houston.” ”Sir, we’re getting ready to move her now.” “OK...OK...Lindz, she needs you here...” “We’re on the first plane, Dan. Just...” “You’re breaking up, Lindz...” “Just make sure they take care of her,” Troy said as quickly as she could. “You know she’s high-risk.” “I know. They will, don’t worry.” “I can’t...I can’t lose...” “You won’t. We won’t...” ************* I was three years old when I first experienced the cruelties of fate and the unjust nature of the world we live in. I was too young to understand it, though, and knowing what I know now it wouldn’t be the last time I was a victim of an unjust circumstance along these lines. When a child is that young, barely having enough time to bask in the accomplishment of being able to read, they’re not expected to comprehend the tragedies that befall the human existence. Everything...life...is so much simpler to them. The things that adults worry about aren’t even a thought in their minds; their world revolves around what Big Bird and Grover talked about on Sesame Street, how much they don’t like peas because they look icky and what story they’ll be read at bedtime. I don’t remember much about my mother, being such a young age when I lost her to a brain aneurysm while she was delivering my sister Alaina into the world. What I do remember is the way she used to pull the blanket over my tiny body as she put me to bed. I can remember reaching up to her with tiny hands that would one day cut across the flesh of the person I was booked to fight on a particular night and I can remember her saying to me, ‘If you keep reaching, mia bella ragazza, you’ll one day touch a star.’ Where my mother’s life was taken from me, my sister’s life brought about the innocence of another child who didn’t understand the heaviness of mourning and the finality of death. I would not understand these, mourning and death, in toto until I reached the age of ten and then felt it, once again, at the age of twenty-five. Alaina never knew our mother, and when we were younger she would always ask me about her. Some of the questions she posed I had no answer to, because my mother’s death made me forget the things that made her who she was. I remembered the color of her hair and I remembered her smile and the gentleness of her voice. Answers like ‘she was nice’ and ‘she used to smell like spices on Sundays’ yielded no conclusive evidence that made my mother...my mother. The stories that my father told us about her helped alleviate the fuzziness that the questions made, but there’s only so much we could have felt when there were so many holes to patch. Alaina was classified as a high-risk pregnancy given my mother’s and my father’s medical histories, but inheriting the Troy stubbornness didn’t prevent her from going about her day-to-day duties. With the family business moving headquarters from Tampa to Houston and Dan being on the road the majority of the time, there was no one there to really keep an eye on her. But Alaina wasn’t stupid; she followed the doctor’s orders as instructed. She just refused to stay confined to her bed if Dan wasn’t home to keep her there. If you know what I mean... ************* The top of the cell phone snapped shut and Lindsay replaced it on top of the table. Taking a deep breath, she adjusted the thin strap of her tank top and looked over her shoulder to a sleeping Melton, who was making a run at Guinness’ record of loudest recorded snore. “Joey...” came the gentle call as Troy scooted over closer to him. No response. ”Joey, wake up...” she said, a little more forcefully and accompanied the command with a touching of his shoulder. A noise resembling a ‘mmmmph’ was made, and Troy scowled. “I swear to God, if you’re faking being asleep again I’m putting you outside with the dogs.” Troy started to forcefully shake him, pulling Joey back to the land of the awake. “But Ryan, I don’t want to job to Cammy again....” “JOEY!” “GAHHHHHHH!” It was Melton’s turn to sit bolt-upright in bed, wide-eyed and confused. “Lindsay, what the hell?!” “Come on...” her fingers clenched around his wrist and she started to pull him off the bed. “Babe it’s five...” Melton looked at the clock. “...four-forty in the morning. Where are we going?” She tugged harder, managing to somehow get Melton’s upper body to hang off the bed. “Time isn’t of consequence right now, we have to go.” “I don’t see what...wait a minute.” “No, we can’t wait a minute.” “I get what this is about,” Melton’s pearly whites flashed a mischievous smile while his free hand wrapped around Troy’s two. “You want to go...” “What?” Lindsay felt herself being pulled back to bed and didn’t have enough time to dig her heels into the carpet. She went flying back on top of the mattress while Joey rolled over to meet her. “You know, this always was a fantasy of mine with you...” “No, damnit! That’s not what I meant!” Her hands attached themselves to Melton’s shoulders, keeping him at bay. “Dan just called, Alaina’s having the baby and we need to get to Houston...now.” It took a moment for Melton’s brain to process this; after all, it was five...four forty-five in the morning. “Alaina....my sister...the blonde lady with the big round belly...” Lindsay encouraged; the man’s not a Rhodes scholar by any means and especially not at this early hour. “You mistakenly hit on her three years ago at our Fourth of July party...” Joey looked down at her with crystal blue eyes that, even in the darkness, seemed to shimmer. “I’m gonna be an uncle...” “And you’re gonna be in big trouble if you don’t get off me so I can book plane tickets,” Troy responded with a kiss to Melton’s cheek and a hint of playfulness in her voice. ************* Troy pulled her long legs closer to the chair so the child running around the waiting room unsupervised wouldn’t trip and crack his head open on the tiles. “You’d think after being in one of these at least once every few months I’d get used to the atmosphere.” “I didn’t know you frequented the maternity ward’s waiting area on your walk-in visits,” came Joey’s mumbled reply as he flipped through a Baseball Digest. “Smartass,” she said before standing and walking over to the nurse’s station to address the woman behind the desk. “Have you heard anything yet?” “Nothing yet, ma’am,” the orderly said with a smile and a heavy accent. “But don’t worry, they’ll come out and tell you when the baby’s born.” Heaving a sigh, Lindsay returned to her seat and was greeting with a nudge by Joey. “Going up there every fifteen minutes isn’t going to make Alaina squeeze the baby out any faster.” “I’m scared, you know.” Silence. Melton closed the magazine, put it in his lap, and looked at her. “I know, it’s a big shock that I’m scared about something,” the statement was accompanied by the wringing of her hands. “I’m scared that I’m going to hear the same words from Dan that I heard from my father, that I’m going to be three years old again and I’m not going to understand why any of this happened and why my life is being rewound and played back, except I’m older now and supposedly wiser because of everything that’s happened, and...” Placing his hands on her cheeks, Joey tried to stop the emotions from tumbling out of her mouth. “Going a mile a minute and getting yourself worked up about this is going to land you in a bed here in a different part of the building.” “I lost my mom this way,” tears escaped from the well behind her eyes. “ I can’t lose my sister too.” “You’re not going to.” “Ms. Troy?” Both looked at the man in the teal-colored scrubs who was standing at the doorway to the waiting room. “I have some news on your sister...” ************* “We named her after mom,” Alaina (Troy) Ryan said as Lindsay sat in the chair next to her bed. The sleeping baby was wrapped in a white blanket, white booties and a white hat. “My water broke and she refused to come out. The doctors had to do a C-section to get her out.” “And then your blood pressure started to drop?” “Yeah...they got it stable again afterwards. Dan said he could feel himself turning white. They’re going to keep me here a few extra days to make sure I’m fine.” “We were worried. I nearly had a breakdown in the waiting room.” Lindsay smiled at her sister while she readjusted the baby’s position in the crook of her arm. “I called Brent and Elise, they’re going to fly in tomorrow.” The elder Troy sighed and looked down at the stirring bundle. “Little Cecilia Marie, you sure gave your mommy and daddy some trouble today. Didn’t you?” Cecilia replied with a gurgle. “If she’s still stubborn when she grows up, I’m blaming you.” “Oh come on now, Lanni,” the protest was half-hearted. “You’ve inherited the genes too.” “You’re worse, and you know it.” Alaina held out her arms for her daughter and her sister obliged her. “I think we’re all going to be OK.” Lindsay gave her a kiss on her head and looked down at her newborn niece. “Yeah, I think so too.”
