The Road To Redemption: The Evil Within (1.2)

7 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 7, 2009 “I don't see how you can trust the guy, Ty.” Marquis Peeples stood in front of the large window of Tyrell Dawkins' office, his arms folded across his chest. If there was a shirt made to look any tighter, Marq had yet to find it. The sheer white shirt did nothing to hide the curves and definition of his toned arms, his mocha brown skin bleeding through the fabric that looked as if it were a second layer of skin. It wasn't like he had the body of a genetic freak, but the man took care of himself and you could tell. Tyrell Dawkins was looking out the window too, but from the comfort of the black office chair he was leaning back in. While not as toned as his younger counterpart, it was clear that Tyrell has done his fair share of training. Not one to flaunt his physique, he was dressed comfortably in a red two button polo shirt with a small logo of his gym over his left breast. “We wouldn't even be here if it weren't for my trust in the goodness of people's hearts, Marq. Or did you forget about that?” Marquis turned to face his partner, his lips curled into a bit of a frown. “I'm not a damn child molester.” “Neither is Jeremy,” Tyrell replied. “Not yet,” Marquis grumbled. “And putting him out there with the kids isn't going to make things any easier for him to keep his hands clean either.” “We all succumb to temptation at one time or another, Marq.” Tyrell attempted to defend the man he found following one of the young women that considered his gym her home away from home. “Jeremy knows what he was about to do was wrong, and he knows he has a problem. Now that he has admitted that there is a problem, he needs someone to walk beside him and pull him by the collar if he starts to stray.” “So what happens when you're not there to keep him from straying,” Tyrell's attention was back on the gentleman sweeping the floors. “What are you going to do then?” “That isn't going to happen.” Tyrell interlocked his fingers and rested his elbows on the desk in front of him. “You're sure about that?” Marquis watched as Jeremy's attention was drawn toward a young woman skipping rope, but he also notices how quickly he turned his attention back to his duties. “Have a little faith, Marq,” Tyrell smiled. “People tend to surprise you.” “That's what I'm afraid of.” “Oh ye of little faith,” Tyrell said with a shake of his head. “I can see the good in him, Marq, and with a little time you will be able to see that too.” “I know you want the center to stay open while we're away, but this guy isn't the answer.” “The answer?” Tyrell curled his lips in interest. “I don't remember there being a question.” “How can you expect me to focus in the ring when I'm going to be thinking about the kids' safety?” Marquis walked over to the front of the desk and placed his hands palm down on the oak finish. “This week is important, and I don't know if I can concentrate with this in the back of my mind.” “Then let me worry about it,” Tyrell answered with a finality in his voice, as if the discussion was over. “It is my decision and mine alone. You have told me how you feel about it, and I've taken it in consideration, but my decision is the final one and I've already made up my mind.” Marquis already knew the answer, and his doubts about the man's trustworthiness had fallen on deaf ears. And so would the words of his partner. “Jeremy stays.” ---- Hundreds of cars sat quietly in the parking lot of the large church, a sign that he would not be alone when he walked in. He was amazed at the beauty of the church's external appearance. A professional landscaping crew had to be on the payroll. The lawn in front of the church doors was perfectly manicured, an island of green amongst the gray asphalt of the parking lot that circled it. Jeremy Johnson swallowed hard, but the lump in his throat had nothing to do with anything being stuck there. He was nervous. He hadn't been in a church since he was a child, choosing to have his marriage done by a justice of the peace so no one would notice the extra inches of girth his wife had gained. He knew what everyone would think, but it did not matter because he knew in his heart that he cared for her. Cared. He should have known that he loved her, after seven years he owed her that much, but he was not sure he knew what that felt like. Growing up in a home where the amount of love you had for a family member was shown by how much you could beat them before passing out in a drunken stupor, instead of holding them in your arms and saying those three little words everyone longs to hear from their parents. Jeremy grew up thinking his name was 'That little bastard', since that was how his father addressed him in conversations. Love is not something that comes easy to him, but standing in front of the church all he wants to do is feel the love of his Father. Not the one who made the donation, but the one he was told was a Father to all. How he longed to feel that special love only a choice few were aware of. Was he worthy of that love? Had the decisions made in his life ruined his chances to understand peace of mind? Would he ever be forgiven for the horrible thoughts that almost became a reality? There was only one way to find the answers to the questions running free like stallions through his mind, so with a firm grip on the handle of the door he pulled it open. It would be a lie to say that all of his questions were answered, but at least it was a start.
