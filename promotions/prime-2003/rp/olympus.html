Date: Colossus V & Aftermath August 9, 2008 "Now, brace yourself, because this might sting a little bit." The syringe hovered over Troy's head as PRIME's resident physician, Dr. Victor Fihlguud, readied to deliver the latest anesthestic injection in a long, long line since he joined up with the then-fledgling wrestling group nearly five years back. After patching up wrestler after wrestler over his distinguished career as the man who singlehandedly kept the PRIME locker room ticking, the gash atop the recently deposed Intense Champion's skull was nothing remarkable. Victor had seen cuts like this before on an everyday basis, especially among those competitors crazy enough to pursue the Intense Title, but the look on Troy Douglas' face was different than that of almost any man or woman he'd ever treated backstage at a PRIME show. Beneath the patina of already drying blood and sweat was a look that Victor Fihlguud could barely describe. He'd seen pain, he'd seen disappointment, apathy, ecstasy, just about every emotion listed in Webster's Dictionary etched on the face of any number of PRIME wrestlers, but this was something almost entirely new. Emptiness. Troy Douglas had only been around the PRIME locker room for five months, but considering his somewhat legendary medical history, the physician had come to know wrestling's resident workhorse rather well. Normally, when the 32-year-old came knocking on Fihlguud's door, the one always-present thing was a spark in his eyes. He'd identified it immediately, because he so rarely saw that spark in the eyes of so many jaded prima donnas that had grown to occupy his list of patients. But, in Troy Douglas, Victor Fihlguud saw someone who actually was willing to endure inhuman amounts of physical pain and extreme medical treatments to keep on wrestling. Not tonight. This time around, when Troy Douglas staggered into Fihlguud's office and collapsed against the training table, there wasn't no vibrant spark, just vacancy. It was almost as if he had the spirit beaten out of him, and given the circumstances he'd just endured, Victor couldn't begrudge him one bit. The opening match at Colossus V hadn't yielded any major injuries, so Victor was able to sit back and watch the spectacle that was the Intense Title match. As a man with a long history in the medical field, he'd never had a weak stomach, but when he saw Troy Douglas, leaking blood from numerous spots on his head and screaming in agony while locked in Kaiser Vashaun's Texas Cloverleaf, Victor could feel the blood draining from his face. The fact that, ten minutes later, the man whose surgically repaired back had failed him and forced him to pass out from the pain actually WALKED into his makeshift training room under his own volition was nothing short of astounding. "Umm, you going to actually stick the needle in, or should I try and guide it in with my deadly mind powers?" In his reverie, Victor had completely forgotten about his job. It wasn't something the doctor did often, though, at times, he was prone to a little philosophical speculation as to why his patients put themselves through an endless Roman gladiator routine. "Just trying to find a clean spot," Victor said, trying to find an excuse that wouldn't make him seem like an idiot for failing to attend to the rather grievous head wound. "Relax yourself, it'll be over in a second." "Fire away, doc." Then, with the surgical precision borne from thousands upon thousands of procedures, Dr. Victor Fihlguud did his best to numb at least some of the physical pain of the man who had to be hurting much more in places where no medicine could help. ... After a moment, Troy felt the familiar pinch of the needle against the flesh atop his head. When it released, he could feel the familiar numbness instantly set in as the drugs did their work and Victor Fihlguud set to his job of stapling up the gash that Kaiser Vashaun had opened up. He couldn't feel the warmth of that blood anymore, but as he looked at the plasma and sweat stains on his frayed wrist tape, the reality of the war he'd just been through was still fresh in his mind. In front of his home state, in front of family and friends, Troy had poured absolutely everything he had into this one match. He fired against the Next in Line with everything he had, drawing as much blood from his opponent as Kaiser drained from Troy. But, in the end, that old chestnut popped up one more time. Mentally, he had plenty left in the tank, and no matter the pain, he wasn't going to give Vashaun the satisfaction of submitting. His surgically repaired back, however, had other ideas, and told Troy's brain in was time to shut down. Unfortunately for Troy, his back won out over his heart, and after shrugging off medical attention in the ring, he'd stumbled backstage minus ten pounds of leather and gold in addition to the generous amount of bodily fluids he'd left on the mat. He appreciated the statement from his home crowd and the fact that they respected his determination. That didn't change his opinion that, right now, he was broken. Troy was ready to keep going, but as it had so many times in his career, his body betrayed him. He was only 32, but his injuries and nine years of being on the receiving end of nightly ass-kickings probably qualified his body to receive Social Security benefits. And at this moment, having lost his title in front of people he would have died to impress, Troy Douglas wasn't sure his body was ready to get back in that ring. "All done," came Victor Fihlguud's voice as he patted Troy on the shoulder. Stick a question mark at the end of that statement and you've got a pretty good idea at what was racing through Troy's mind right now. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ The next night... The last place in the world Troy Douglas wanted to be was backstage again at Bank of America Stadium. But, being the good soldier, the loyal employee, here he was, trying to make himself as scarce as humanly possible. PRIME had mandated all its performers be at Night Two of Colossus V, but Troy was among the only ones not watching the spectacle taking place on the football field outside. Sure, he'd applauded the new Hall of Famers and glanced back and forth as Chandler Tsonda and Devin Shakur did battle for the Universal Title, but mainly, he just sat there. He wanted to be a million miles away from here, far away from where anyone had ever heard of PRIME or the Intense Title or Troy Douglas. He just wanted to get away and not have to think about the sobering fact that pain, the one thing he'd learned to deal with over nine years, had finally beaten him. Kaiser Vashaun may have been wrong in calling Troy a fraud, but the Next in Line had found something that people had been searching for over Troy's entire career. A breaking point. Troy hadn't been pinned, forced into submission or knocked out, but he had been broken. And to a man who'd made a living off of his never-say-die reputation, there could be nothing worse. This time, he didn't overcome the odds. This time, he didn't stand up in the face of adversity. This time, he didn't keep getting back up. And he wasn't sure he would want to get up again for quite a while. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Later that night... Nearly all of the PRIME crew had filed out of the stadium for the lavish afterparty, but Troy couldn't bring himself to leave. He just paced around the backstage area, pausing every few moments to watch the enormous set being struck or to see the lighting crew pulling down their gargantuan rigs. Then, from the production truck, he heard a building clamor. For a moment, he just walked by, not caring at all. Soon enough, his curiosity got to him, and he limped to the truck and peeked in the door. Ten or twelve of the remaining production crew were huddled around an HD screen, watching swimming's most glamorous race, the 4 X 100 men's freestyle relay. "What's going on?" "French are pulling away, and they've got the world record holder ready to anchor." Looking down, he saw the French, one lane above the Americans, begin to open up a lead as the 6-foot-5 powerhouse Alain Bernard flung himself headlong into the pool. A moment later, America's veteran leader, Jason Lezak, powered into the water himself, desperately trying to keep gold medal hopes alive. 46 seconds later, not one person could believe what they'd seen. Bernard, the fastest swimmer in HISTORY, had opened up a seemingly insurmountable gap. But, Lezak, the 32-year-old team captain, began eating up water like Jaws, gaining ground with every stroke, every kick. In one last, desperate reach, the man who said he and his countrymates were going to "smash" the Americans was eclipsed by a man who'd been overshadowed by far more talented teammates. The entire Water Cube in Beijing, 17,000 strong from all around the world, exploded. On NBC, Dan Hicks and Rowdy Gaines were shouting in a blind euphoria, in awe of what had just happened. On the deck of the pool, nobody was more elated than Michael Phelps, whooping in joy, his dream of eight golds in one Olympic Games still improbably alive. In PRIME's production truck, half a world away, Troy Douglas backed away from the mass of shouting men slapping hands and merely smiled. 32 years old, against insurmountable odds. Seemed oddly familiar. His back still ached, his title was still gone, and he was never sure of how long he had left, but Troy Douglas owed it to himself to go headlong back into the ring and see what happened. After all, he could accomplish something downright Olympian.
