Date: Various One month ago... It wasn't three seconds after he'd taken the first drag off his cigarette that Tommy Wade heard the unmistakable sound of a gun cocking. Truth be told, it's not all that hard a sound to divine, a faint click of metal slicing through a chilly Boston night. Didn't hurt that he'd heard the exact same gun in a frighteningly similar situation just a few weeks before. So similar, that if he were the type of person who believed in deja vu, about right now would be the point when Tommy would start fumbling in his pocket to dial Miss Cleo. Thomas Gabriel Wade didn't believe in deja vu. He spun around, already knowing who he'd see attached to the handgun. It was the sound that gave it away. It wasn't that he was an expert in the field of firearms, far from it, it was the imprint of a memory. The imprint of two weeks before, when he heard the same safety on the same gun click into the "off" position as he opened his apartment door. Now, just as before, he turned around. Just as before, he was greeted by the scowling visage of Colin Ross. On his own, the lanky, stringy-haired Ross presented little to be intimidated by, even with his wispy blonde goatee, yellowing teeth and sour expression. In that sense, it's a wonder how much pointing a loaded handgun at somebody's face will elicit a far different reaction than normal. "Jesus, Colin! What the fuck are you doing here?" Ross deepened his sneer, beckoning Tommy against the wall with another wave of his gun. Not wanting to trip Colin's hair-trigger temper, Tommy backed away. "What the fuck am I doing here? You fuckin' serious?" Colin Ross, it should also be mentioned, lived his life at a pace so frenetic that if the folks from Red Bull could isolate and bottle it, every raving caffeine junkie in the country would be banging at the factory doors for a taste. That added pep had the side effect of causing him to wave his gun around like a dyspeptic baboon, which had the side effect of causing Tommy's knees to start dancing a syncopated samba. "I'm serious, Colin," Tommy answered, trying not to focus on the gun that flashed in front of his face every few seconds. "What the hell are you doing outside my restaurant?" "Your restaurant, huh? Pretty sure this is Mr. Boyle's restaurant." Tommy Wade had a tendency to try and charm his ways out of sticky situations. Right now, he was failing. "And ... umm, Mr. Boyle sent you here?" "Exactly." "Why would that be?" Colin's screwed up face went slack for a second, resembling something that, if Tommy had ever seen it on his oppressor's face before, might have been recognized as a dumbfounded expression. To Colin Ross, the answer couldn't be more obvious. "The twenty fucking thousand fucking dollars, that's what!" "Twenty what where with the who now?" A fierce jab of firearm towards face told Tommy that had been the incorrect response. "Mr. Boyle just got last week's statements in, and there's a $20,000 dis ... dis ..." "Discrepancy?" Colin Ross wasn't the sharpest bulb in the drawer, either. There was a reason he ran around Greater Boston as a gofer for a man the likes of Raymond Boyle. "Yeah, that," he said. Tommy ran his hands against the cold, coarse brick of the wall behind him, as if somewhere in the little chinks and chips of missing brick and mortar he'd somehow find a magic piece of paper with the answer to his current conudrum. No such luck. "I didn't take that money, Collie." "Bullshit." "I'm only fucking working here because Boyle threatened to dump my torso in the Charles and my legs in the Harbor if I didn't provide a legitimate front for his little money laundering operation." Befitting a man of his mental and emotional state, Colin scratched his chin with the butt of his gun, trying to surmise a solution. "Thing is, Mr. Boyle thinks you took it, and if I don't deliver you or the money, he'll think I took it, and them I'm going to be the one going for a fucking swim." Tommy Wade thought for a moment, then said something he'd immediately regret. "Better you than me, pal." Once again, Tommy Wade was staring at the barrel of a gun. "How's this, then? You've got one month. I don't see the money by then, you and this gun are going to become real good friends. Got it?" He didn't. "You'll have the money, Colin. Now, can I run my fucking restaurant?" Finally, mercifully, Colin holstered his gun in his ratty Levi's and started to turn away, but not before one final thuggish missive. "One month, Tommy." As Colin Ross jitterbugged away up the alley, Tommy took the long overdue second drag off his Marlboro and looked longingly at Telemachus' kitchen door. One month. In one month, he'd be a ghost. ***** Last night... "Troy, can you check on the chicken? It should be just about done." Samantha Wade's voice had been piping up from the bedroom down the hallway on and off for the past 35 minutes. Troy was absolutely wild about his fiance, but she was a bit of a pack rat, and this Saturday evening had turned into a particularly irksome quest for a certain pair of earrings, or a necklace, or whatever it was Sam had misplaced in one of the dozen boxes littering her bedroom floor or in the Mount Everest of clutter currently occupying the left half of her walk-in closet. Today, Troy was more than content to let her search for knick-knacks while he searched for something worth watching on TV. Catching a whiff of the chicken currently roasting to golden brown deliciousness in the oven, Troy pulled himself up off his well-worn divot in Sam's plush sofa and hopped towards the kitchen. He was just about to embark on a hunt of his own for a meat thermometer, when he heard Sam's intercom buzz. "Hello?" "Troy? It's Tommy. Can you let me up?" "Come on in." Troy rifled around a shelf, finally locking onto his quarry and jumping up like a 260-pound 12-year-old who'd just spotted Santa Claus on Christmas Eve. A quick check told him the chicken needed a few more minutes to get up to temperature. By the time the oven was closed, there was the expected knock on the door. When Troy looked at what was on the other side, he knew the chicken would probably end up a little on the dry side. Tommy Wade, to be frank, looked like grim death. His face was ashen, his posture was slouched, his hair was lank and the bags under his eyes were so heavy they might as well have been toting lead weights along with all that stress. Not to mention that his well-worn grey hooded sweatshirt had been soaked to near-black with snow and rain. "Tommy, what the..." "Jesus CHRIST!" Sam had finally finished her search, and as soon as she saw her twin brother standing in her doorway looking like a street urchin out of a Dickens novel, she nearly doubled over in shock. "Sam, I didn't know where else to go." "Where else to go?" she asked, fury and empathy rushing out of her all at once as she struggled to hold back tears. "What the hell is going on?" "I'm in a bit of trouble." That was saying something. ***** Four hours later... "But, why didn't you tell anybody? Why didn't you go to the police?" Sam and Troy had probably asked some variation of that question 100 times since Tommy had shown up on their proverbial doorstep looking like he'd spent a week manning the dunk tank at the carnival in Hell. Thus far, they'd yet to receive a satisfactory answer. "I couldn't." "Bullshit you couldn't," was all Sam had to say as she stared right back at her brother, matching pairs of hazel eyes locking onto each other. It was a side of his fiance that Troy hadn't seen all that much of. Dead serious. Intense. Taken aback. Troy didn't have to initiate any "Good Cop, Bad Cop" routine. Sam was playing both roles here with Oscar-winning gusto. Every time Tommy backed away, curling up in the sweater and blankets Troy and Sam had provided, the indomitable Ms. Wade reached her hooks in even further, trying to pry more information out of her brother. "We can help you, Tommy, if you just let us," she said. "We're getting on a plane back to Boston tomorrow morning, and you're going to the cops and spilling on all this. I'll be damned if I let you burn for what these bastards are doing. "Now get some sleep. For god's sake, you need it." There was nothing more to be said. Troy slipped his arm around his fiance's waist and pulled her tired body towards the bedroom. Tommy slumped under the blankets, hoping that somehow he'd dream of something that wouldn't remind him of what he was facing. A nice cabernet, maybe. ***** The next day... Troy tossed his car keys onto the dining room table and made a beeline to the sofa. Even on a Sunday, the traffic out to JFK was somewhere on the level of the Bataan Death March, and the ample tension in the car did little to alleviate that. Sam and Tommy were still on tenterhooks as Troy dropped them off at the terminal. He had a flight of his own back to Greensboro tomorrow before heading on to Birmingham, so joining the Wade Family Circus as they attempted to take down organized crime wasn't in the cards. He wanted to be there for Sam and her family, but sometimes, he'd be glad for a little less stress in his life. Almost ready to sit down and do his best imitation of kicking back and relaxing, Troy felt the cold squish of soaked sock against already cold feet. A rather unfriendly puddle outside the parking garage could count itself responsible for that. Socks. Must change socks. Peeling the wet socks from his feet, he tossed them into the laundry basket before even reaching the bedroom, then reached into his drawer for a fresh pair. That's when he felt something. Rather, he didn't feel something. He'd only just put them there a couple of days before. In fact, Sam didn't even know he'd bought them yet. He was planning it as a little Christmas surprise. Not anymore. The wedding rings were missing.
