Crash Course: Part I - The Lost & The Damned

2 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02-11-09 18th October, 2009 Waco, TX "You're not going anywhere until you get your act together, lady!" Bob Wilson's stance was military, muscles tensed and eyes bugging out at the girl in front of him. His presence was a literal black mark against her and her surroundings, a room decorated and furnished in all shades of pink and complimented with lace and silk, feathers and hearts, teddies and pictures of her posing like a supermodel. All of it was marred by this forty-something hard-ass with a greying crew cut and a bland cardigan-and-slacks combo that only accentuated his prehistoric outlook on life. She sat stiffly on the bed in a Winnie the Pooh nightie, knees up and back pressed against the headboard trying to lose herself in the text messages from her boyfriend while her dad went on yet another rant. He was never satisfied with any of her decisions in life and seemed to be on a mission to upset her every chance he got. He was conditioned for perfection after a stint in the army in his younger days. His bad attitude stemmed from a bung knee; the result of a training exercise gone wrong. Instead of the War Hero he dreamed of being he had to make do working in a general store behind the repair desk. He couldn't fulfil his dreams of some day being on the front line, and now his daughter was throwing away the chance of making something of herself, and it boiled his blood to think of her lax attitude to life. "Do you hear me Christy?" He boomed, forcing the words out with such haste and force as to assert his authority that she felt the hardwood vibrating through her spine. "Yes!" She snapped back, mouth agape and empty blame shimmering in her big blue eyes. She shirked back, ruffling her Playboy duvet with her feet and twisting them under cover in a feeble attempt to somehow shield herself from her dad's wrath. No matter how hard she tried she couldn't concentrate on the message she was composing, instead caught in that furious tractor-beam stare of his. "It'll be a cold day in hell before I see my daughter's education gone to waste because of some...some poser in a glorified biscuit tin!" He seethed. "You don't even know him!" She glared accusingly, "You're too busy being pissed off at nothing to give a crap what I do or who I'm with!" "You don't have a clue." He hissed back, dropping his arms and clenching his fists, "You don't take any responsibility for yourself. No, you're happy to let someone else nanny you while your own life goes down the shitter! If it's not me or your mother it's that prick..." "Just shut up!!" Christy bawled, still frozen in place as big bold tears plopped off her eyelashes. "He's not a prick! You don't even care about me! JUST LEAVE ME ALONE!!" She shrieked, and the ringing in his ears only intensified his hatred of her failings. He grit his teeth and paused for a moment before taking an intimidating step forward and thrusting a finger out at her, sending a chill through her bones. "Your mother and I only want what's best for you, but you're too dumb and too lazy to know any better. That's the problem with your generation, you think the world owes you a fucking favour!" He said, glancing away and cursing in a hushed tone so that Christy knew his ire was directed solely at her. "MOOOM!!" Christy yelled in retort, holding her knees as they began shivering. Her dad narrowed his eyes, grumbling something under his breath before storming out of the room. His thudding footsteps echoed down the hallway, stopping abruptly for a moment as his harsh tone mingled with a soft concerned muttering as her mother reluctantly answered her call. Tears continued dripping down her face as she sent out her next text like a plea for help. She mentioned nothing of her dad in it, she just wanted a response from someone who wasn't out to get her. "What are you two arguing about now?" Her mom asked, peeking meekly around the jamb before stepping into the bedroom and closing the door behind her. Dora Wilson was a a proud woman, the product of a strict upbringing. While other girls were getting into the hippy scene and exploring their freedoms, she studied home economics and busied herself at home. When she met Bob at a local dance they fell in love and she became the perfect housewife. Now in her forties she still had a flawless youthful complexion, and anyone familiar with the mantra that a mother's looks determined her daughter's in later years could see that Christy would, like a fine wine, better with age. "I hate him." Christy sobbed, glancing up at her mom. "Hey..." She replied sympathetically, sitting beside her daughter and putting an arm around her. "Your father's just...he wants what's best for you, darling. He doesn't mean to be so harsh but he's set in his ways, he's always been stubborn like that." "Stubborn." Christy scoffed. "Now, now." Dora whispered, holding her daughter close, "You know you could have done better on your Midterms, and gallivanting around with Derek doesn't do you any favours..." "That's got nothing to do with it, Mom." Christy sighed guiltily while thumbing in another text message, "I do try, it just doesn't seem to be good enough for him." Dora regarded her daughter as she sent out another message to Derek. She had no problem with the relationship, as long as Christy was happy. Many times she found herself siding with her daughter over her husband, if only to defuse a situation and put his roaring anger into a more gentle perspective for her to understand. "You need to concentrate more on your studies," She began, instantly garnering a huff from Christy, "now I'm not saying abandon Derek, but let him understand your education is more important than these auto shows you're always going to. Nobody ever got famous sitting in the passenger seat their whole life." Christy couldn't help but smile. She knew her mother was right but she just didn't understand what a nineteen year old girl had to go through these days. Keeping up appearances was key, and society quickly overlooked the shirkers and the meek in favour of the characters and the freaks. Even if she wanted to buckle down and focus on her studies she couldn't ignore the eyes all around her, of the crowd that shunned education and intelligence in favour of hedonism and sexuality. Christy was naturally beautiful, a typical blonde bombshell; her little button nose and perma-pout lips complimenting her flawless skin and delicate jawline. She had big innocent eyes that, with a single flutter of those long eyelashes, could shame the devil. She was the spitting image of Dora at that age, but back in her mother's day sex and all the trappings that came with it weren't flaunted as they were in the 21st century. She didn't understand how to deal with it and the advice she fell back on from her own mother and her own generation was lost on Christy. "So will you try a little harder? Finals are in December, honey. Do it for your momma?" Dora continued after a moment. "Yeah, I'll try mom. I'm sorry." Christy replied, dropping her phone and cuddling her mother. "I'm sure Dad would appreciate it if you said sorry to him. I know you don't feel like you should but it'd put his mind at rest." "Sure. I'll do it tomorrow when he's cooled off a little." Christy said with a reluctant smile. If it gets you off my frickin' back, as an afterthought. Dora kissed her daughter on the forehead and ran a hand through her fragrant blonde locks. She gave her pride and joy one final hug, drying her eyes before getting up and pressing the creases out of her skirt. At the door she turned back and smiled sweetly at Christy, who returned one of her own, before closing the door over. Immediately Christy began to sneer and grabbed her phone from the duvet, texting furiously even as it vibrated and a reply from Derek came through. Already she could hear the argument emanating from the den as her dad chastised her mom for consoling their wayward daughter. "...I don't care, Dora! She could have gone to Houston, and now she's flunking out of some two-bit beauty college!" "Don't be hard on her Robert, she is trying..." "...Trying my damn patience. She's quite content to mooch off of us and spend all her time with that deadbeat. The way I see it she can just get the hell out if that's her plan." "Robert Gregory Wilson! That's our daughter! She needs support from us, not a lynching! How can you even think of casting her out of our home?" "It's coming Dora...if she doesn't shape up, she can ship out. We've done our best to raise her good but if that no-good kid is going to undo all our hard work then she-......" Christy tuned out, plugging earphones into her cell and letting her playlist drown out the rest of the world to the beat of Pitbull, among other hip pop artists. A lingering sense of guilt and dread still haunted her thanks to the berating from her parents. Unable to shake the negative vibes she sent one last text and curled up on her bed, closing her eyes and drifting off into a slumber. ---- Earlier that day "What're ya doing with this then Deek?" asked a voice from beneath the hood of the Celica. "Scavenge anything worth keeping then sell it on man, ain't no good to me if I can beat it in the Eclipse." came the reply from the driver seat. Derek Hamilton sat up, a bundle of wiring held in his greasy hand under the steering column. After every race he meticulously scoured his winnings for valuable equipment while ensuring it was fit for resale, even back to the unlucky opponent he won it from. First the wiring and electronics were studied and tested before he moved on to the engine and finally all the cosmetics that could perhaps increase the total performance of his Mitsubishi Eclipse. All the work was carried out in the garage belonging to his old buddy Dustin, who always took pride in helping his friend fix up his car before every race. Waco might as well have been a ghost town for all Dustin could care, but having worked for years alongside his father in their family-run auto shop, when Derek acquired his Eclipse Dustin jumped at the chance to work with him on it. The arrival of that mechanical beast on the streets changed the attitude of the boy racers around town who pitted their souped up abominations against each other on the streets, clad in neon and stickers and grotesque spoilers and body kits while most of the tech underneath the hoods barely improved the drive past stock performance. When Derek introduced the Mitsu to the roads everyone took notice and upped their game to try and thwart him and take his precious car from him. "What's the good word under there?" Derek asked his friend, still rooting around under the hood. "Pfft, there ain't none. Looking at this set-up man he was lucky to even keep pace with ya. There's nothing worth salvaging, shit the turbo is about the only decent piece of kit on here but it's nothing like what you've got. Shit is after-market." Dustin replied, straightening up and cracking his back. Derek got out of the Celica and stood alongside Dustin, taking off his baseball cap to scratch his head. Both his car and the prize he won at his last race were parked over pits; hoods up, doors open and guts hanging over the work space below ready for inspection. Glancing between the hoods of the Toyota and his Mitsubishi he saw immediately what Dustin had assessed and sighed. He drew a gaze over his own engine and all the modifications he had made to make it the best in town. When he bought it the first-generation car was completely stock, with factory-issued turbo and the ever-handy AWD for extra pull. The ruby red paint job set it off nicely and while the insides were completely Frankensteined, Derek kept the stock appearance to give himself a deceptive edge. That deception was blown away with his first race, and in one quarter-mile the whole town was talking. He started on the engine, replacing the pistons, connecting rods and crankshaft with forged aluminium counterparts allowing the rotating assembly to handle the power of the upgraded Garrett T-3 turbo. The cylinder heads were ported and polished and he gave it a valve job with new, stiffer springs. The intake was HKS custom built, as was the exhaust manifold which was then mounted with the T-3. A 4" stainless steel exhaust replaced the stock piece and the catalytic converter was removed. To keep from blowing the engine apart from the pressure of the turbo in the pipes he obtained a blow-off valve that hissed when he shifted gears. When Derek first got into the game he heard that it was essential and sought one out almost before the rest of the kit. A Greddy intercooler, MSD ignition system and an electronically controlled stand-alone fuel management system custom designed to bypass the stock computer completed the engine build, which took the best part of two months to complete. When it became common knowledge that this subtle but sporty Mitsubishi was tooled to the nines, Derek decided to improve on the bodywork; replacing the hood, front fenders, door skins and tail gate with carbon fibre. The doors themselves were absent of handles, covered over and replaced with door poppers activated by a fob on Derek's keys. Dustin suggested replacing the glass in the windows with lexan, much to Derek's surprise. While he had an extensive knowledge of mechanics Derek didn't imagine that he would ever contemplate something that most people of the culture might miss. This wasn't Los Angeles and Dustin Garrity was definitely no Dominic Torretto. With the carbon fibre fitted, Derek decided to re-spray the body with the stock ruby red. The grey weaved graphite gave the Eclipse a gaudy look, like ill-fitting patches. The next step was to strip the interior of all the excess weight; the carpeting, speakers and even the dashboard had been removed leaving only the several gauges around the OEM racing wheel. Two matte black bucket seats with gold harnesses occupied the empty interior, but that's all Derek needed. He didn't even have a rollcage, preferring to forego the added weight to allow Christy to ride shotgun during some races. In comparison the Toyota Celica he won was a bag of bones. It was set up with similar kit but hadn't been tuned as expertly as the Eclipse. Running a slick of grease through his short spiked brown hair, Derek clucked and looked up at Dustin. He resembled Monstar Blanko from Space Jam, and even had a snaggletooth to compliment his cartoonish features. "You know what?" He grumbled, "Just punt it Dusty. It ain't worth shit to me." "You mind if I keep it then, and work on it some?" Dustin asked. "Knock yourself out. I'm gonna get cleaned up. Taking Christy out tonight." A broad smile spread across Dustin's face. His looks and slow nature could never bag him a girl like Christina Wilson, but he had a soft spot for her nonetheless, mainly because Derek would always talk about what a little slut she was in the sack and back it up with grainy cellphone videos of her sucking him off in the Eclipse or getting railed doggy style over the hood. When they hung out together Dustin would melt at the sight of her and do whatever she asked, mistaking her politeness as teasing behaviour. He was well aware that she was his friend's property and while jealous of Derek's boyish good looks and cocky attitude snagging him a gem like Christy he never let hormones get between friends. "So you'll be busy tonight?" He asked coyly. "You knows it. Gonna take a drive up around the Lake." "Oh, alright." Dustin sighed, smiling half-heartedly. Most nights they hung out and had a few drinks, and on the nights Derek was busy with his girlfriend, Dustin either busied himself at the shop or spent the time quietly cooped up in his bedroom in front of the TV. Lately though Derek had been spending more and more time with her, and Dustin was feeling left out. "Yeah, I'll catch you later man." Derek called out as he wiped the grease off his hands and lay a fresh tarp cover over the driver seat before getting in. The engine roared to life, warbling gutturally as Derek slowly rolled over the pit and out of the garage. Dustin's father exited the office as he drove out, and they exchanged a wave before Derek began to pull out of the yard. As he did so a tremendous boom like thunder clapped through the air as a pearlescent blur skidded into the parking lot in front of him and spun ninety degrees to a halt just a couple of feet in front of him. "What the fuck?!" He yelled, jumping out of his seat, leaning on the door jamb and glaring at the Acura Integra which was blocking his exit. Both custom Lambo-style doors opened upwards like unfolding wings. Two figures emerged; one a petite and buxom raven-haired girl with a snarling expression permanently etched on her face, the other a young man in his mid-twenties, the same age as Derek. He wore a trilby over long gelled spikes falling across his pale face. His leather jacket and jeans were all-black, with an oriental dragon stitched into the back of the jacket. At first Derek thought he was being sneered at before he realized the oaf was in fact Asian. "What's you're fucking deal dude? You trying to cause an accident??" He yelled at the stranger. Dustin and his father looked on from inside the garage, but left Derek to handle it himself. In the beginning Dustin's dad opposed all the boy racers speeding around, but when Dustin and Derek began bringing in business he began to embrace the culture. When morons like the guy in the Acura pulled up like posers in their flash cars he let the boys do the talking and just let the cash roll in. "You Deek Hamilton?" the Asian asked, eyeing up the Eclipse and glancing at Derek, ignoring his angry protest. "Yeah, what of it?" He replied, ired by the Asian's lack of respect for the road. "You took that Celica off my boy Hyatt last night." He pointed accusingly. "Haha, no shit. I spent about a half hour looking for the punchline on that joke-mobile. Dude was crazy to race me. If he wants it back it's gonna cost him. A lot." Derek chuckled wryly. "Nuh-uh. You an' me are gonna race for it, and you can put that piece of shit Mitsu up too." "No offense dude but I don't just pick up challenges off the street like some whore," Derek replied, eyeing up the Asian's girlfriend who subsequently snarled back at him. It don't matter where I'm from, what matters is you're taking killer whips off my guys and selling them on with second-rate tech." "What I do ain't nobody's business but mine, Cheech." Derek said, narrowing his eyes. "Well it's my business now. So we gonna race or are you gonna bitch out?" The Asian asked, cocking an eyebrow. Derek regarded the Integra. It was custom designed similarly to the Eclipse, but with a Neese behind the wheel of an Acura it was obvious there was going to be a trick or two up the exhaust pipe. There was nothing more pretentious than an Acura or its driver, that was common knowledge throughout the culture. "Let me see under the hood first, then we'll talk." Dustin cautiously approached the glittery abomination, studying the fishtail spoiler and the glittery paint job coating the aerodynamic body kit. Clutching the straps on his overalls he hummed a low sigh of concern. "Deek. Look at the engine code." Derek was already fixated with the modified Type-R engine, his eyes glued to the sharp italics that read "B-18c". Japanese specs, given away by the symbols etched over it, told him this kid knew his shit. The B-18c was a 1.8l, but the Type-R version produced 220 horses; very powerful for its size. That however was the stock power, and the modded version could be anywhere over it. His Eclipse was fitted with a 2.0l 4g63 motor, and the last time he had it on the dyno it pushed 400hp, hard. Flashbulbs were going off all around her. Stood atop a rotating podium she struck sensual poses and felt her pride swell with every click of the cameras. Beyond the frantic photographing her name was being called out by a horde of shadows beyond the light, bobbing and dancing rhythmically to a hip club beat. The front tyres on the Integra spun up smoke before taking off, while the Eclipse's AWD gave it enough traction to simply power away. In the first split second of the race starting Derek acknowledged that he had the lead and shifted into second as Eddie pulled parallel with him. Christy, engulfed by her harness, kept her eye on the hood of the Eclipse as it bobbed back and forth with the Integra. Occasionally her eyes flitted to the roadside, anxiously expecting the ditch to swallow them up as the Eclipse's Pirelli tyres thundered over the tarmac. In the Integra, Eddie was in the zone, eyes locked on the road as he shifted into fourth. Derek noticed the change in the way the Integra lurched and knew immediately Eddie had changed a second too soon. Jamming the accelerator down he roared ahead a few feet longer before switching into fourth. Christy let out a whoop as the Eclipse began pulling away from the Acura, taking the definitive lead. "Fucker..." Eddie muttered to himself as the tail lights of the Eclipse shone in his eyes. "Hahaha, what'd I tell you baby! Unstoppable!" Derek yelled, caught up in the adrenaline rush. ---- At the end of the strip, Dustin and the Frank Cobalt driver were standing in the yard of an old abandoned farm on the corner of Tirey and Concord. The road was quiet. In the distance the faint roar of the Integra and the Eclipse became louder with every passing second. "Here they come." said Dustin. "Na man, here comes Eddie. Yer boy is gonna be trailin' bad." replied the Cobalt driver. "You're in for a shock, dude." Dustin added, under his breath. ---- "Where in the hell am I?" He asked himself, sighing and peering ahead into the darkness. Tapping the sat-nav he grumbled as the route marker suddenly changed and a female voice barked out for him to make a U-turn at the earliest convenience. He had only just left Waco having been medically cleared after the show and was now lost on its outskirts. "Goddamn GPS." He muttered, cursing the device over a good old-fashioned road map. The Ford F-150 continued along Williams Drive as he pushed on looking for the correct route. In the distance the rusted corrugated roof of a barn belonging to an old abandoned farm loomed out of the darkness. ---- "Come on baby! Push it!" Christy screamed, slapping her thighs and giggling. Derek was focused. The Eclipse had a 0.12 second lead and the Integra wasn't gaining. Trees creeping overhead shook in the wake of the two motors careering past them, while clouds of dust whirled up out of the dirt. In the Integra, Eddie was tapping his free foot against the floor anxiously, as though waiting for something. The Acura was holding pace with the Eclipse but at this rate he knew he couldn't catch up before the end. Then he saw the crossroads ahead where Tirey met Williams Drive before a final short sprint to the end of the road where it met Concord Road. Laughing deviously he extended a finger behind the steering wheel and pushed a small red button hidden discreetly near the centre. A burst of blue light fired out of the exhaust of the Integra. Vaporized nitrous oxide began significantly cooling the intake air, and as the air's density increased the cylinders were provided with yet more oxygen, propelling the Integra forward like a bolt of lightning. Eddie felt himself being pulled back into his bucket seat as the Integra roared wailed straight past the Eclipse. "Motherfucker!!" Derek screamed, spittle flying from his mouth. "What?!" Christy yelled, "What is it??" "He's got fucking NOS!! That cheating fuck! He must've hidden the fucking lines!" "Oh my God." Christy gasped, remembering how much Derek hated the idea of NOS in a drag race. Glancing at the speedometer she gasped; the Mitsu was pushing 135mph. God only knew what the Integra was doing. The Integra was three car lengths ahead before it passed the first junction. Derek barely had time to register the situation before he spotted two beams of light speeding towards the crossroads. "Shit." He managed, before the Ford F-150 pulled out in front of him, and his world went blacker than black. ---- Peeking out from the yard, Dustin and Frank watched as the Acura Integra roared past the first junction. Smoke billowed out from underneath the vehicle as Eddie applied the brakes and brought his car to a roaring halt over another spray painted line in the road in front of them. "Haha! Fuckin' told ya dude! You're guy is dea-..." A thunderous boom echoed through the night followed by a shower of fire and sparks as the headlights of the Eclipse seemingly exploded. As Eddie got out of his car celebrating he jumped in fright as the sound of twisted metal burst through his ears. Dustin looked on in horror as he tried to adjust his eyes to the dim light. He could make out a second shape colliding with the Eclipse, and another set of headlights exploding in a shower of light. "...Fuck." He managed. Eddie, Frank and Dustin were frozen to the spot as they watched the second vehicle tip up onto its side and roar as though in agony. Skidding several feet towards them it stopped, the momentum causing the truck to tip again onto its roof as it began spinning. As it did this a burning flare flipped through the air over it and crashed through the fence into the field on their left. Shards of metal tore through the night sky as the Eclipse slammed into the ground. Dustin could only remember one occasion when he heard a sound like what the Mitsubishi made as it came down hard; a couple of years ago an old factory was scheduled for demolition in an industrial estate where he and Derek used to watch other drivers race. The explosion, followed by thousands of tonnes of concrete, steel and iron falling to the earth was a cataclysmic sound that shook him to the core and that he didn't think he'd ever hear again. Now, as he stood frozen to the spot he remembered the booming wreckage as Derek's car, with him and Christy inside, spun to a stop in the middle of the field, a hundred yards or so away from them. "DEREK!! CHRISTY!!" He yelled, vaulting over the fence and charging across the field to the twisted heap of smoking metal that was the Eclipse. Sprinting over to the car he thrust his hand into the empty window space and tore the door prcatically off its hinges. Dropping down he looked inside and breathed a sigh of relief. There, held firmly in place by their harnesses, Derek and Christy were hanging upside down in their seats. Both were breathing, Derek was groaning while Christy was hanging on to the harness straps for dear life. "Are you hurt?" "I don't know." Christy whimpered, "Get me out of here." "Derek, you alive man?!" Dustin asked, adrenaline overriding his fear. "......Mhm...yeah...get Christy..." Derek replied faintly. While Dustin went to work freeing them from their restraints, Eddie and Frank were still at the farmyard, aghast at the scene unfolding before them. "Holy shit dude." Frank wheezed. Eddie jumped back into his Integra and tore off towards the other vehicle. Frank dialled the cops and got into his Cobalt, taking off after his buddy. "Hello?!" Eddie yelled as he brought the Acura to a stop and leapt out, racing towards the wrecked truck. No response. "Hello?!! Somebody talk to me!" ...uhhh... The groan was barely human, and almost inaudible over his own beating heart. But he heard it. "Hello!" He yelled, frantically dancing around the cab through the smoke, searching for any signs of life. A bloody arm reached up through the driver side window. Shaking horribly the large hand grasped at the air before falling to the ground. Dropping to his knees Eddie grabbed it and followed it up the wrist to the shoulder, and felt a cold wet mess where the face should have been. Shivering, he slowly crept as far into the cab as he could. Suddenly he came face to face with a giant. In the low light he could just make out the face covered in blood while a beard, bloody and tangled, hung limply off his chin. "...Help." He managed, before his eyes rolled into the back of his head and he passed out. Eddie was sure he recognized the man. Frank pulled up and got out, running over to the truck as Eddie talked to the unconscious man, reassuring him even though he probably couldn't hear a word of it. Frank turned to the bed, its tonneau cover ripped clean off and lying behind the wreck. Cases of clothes, kit bags and other containers were spilled out over the road. Among the strewn mess he noticed a large, shiny gold belt shining in the glare from the headlights pointed at the Ford. Approaching the bed he bent down and picked up the belt, holding it up to read the inscription on it and gasped at what he read... PTC Elite Champion HESSIAN "Oh shit." He muttered, as the smoke cleared and the unconscious and bloodied frame of the giant Von Kelsig appeared before them. To Be Continued.
