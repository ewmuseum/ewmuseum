The Lost Year: Discovery (UV RP)

September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 2009 There's something to be said for priorities. I've always felt that my first two priorities in life were my job and my family. I can't even say that there is a clear-cut 1 and 2 in that order. More like a 1 and a 1a. There've been times where my job has been at the forefront of my life. There've been times--one blinding, debilitating time comes to mind--where I've forsaken my family for the betterment of my career. But more often than not, when there's a family crisis staring me head-on, family will come first, no matter what. Professional losses, I can deal with...for the most part, anyway. The beginning of 2009 didn't exactly see things going my way. Big time losses to eventual big time names would put a black mark on my fairly-dominant record in PRIME. Hessian would go on to an Elite Championship run. Jason Snow would run roughshod over the company. Kaiser Vashaun would live up to his "Next In Line" moniker. Christian Daniels would become an enforcer like PRIME hadn't seen in many years. The autographs never stopped being written, despite all that. The pictures never stopped being taken. Questions from fans never stopped being asked. Invitations to talk shows and hounding from paparazzi didn't cease either. I rolled with the punches, took my licks and never saw any adverse affect on my career-driven popularity. It wasn't until injuries took their horrifying toll that the back of my brain quickly and adamantly called for a reassessment. First it was Tyler Rayne. Then it was my uncle, Brent. I couldn't even tell you how many miles I logged in air travel between Minneapolis, where Tyler was, and Tampa, where Brent was. Tens of thousands, maybe? Every time I boarded a plane, I felt like my responsibilities to my job were a little less important and my desire to return was diminished. Hospital rooms replaced hotel rooms, replaced arenas, replaced my home in Cambridge. Doctors and nurses became stand-ins for the administration and the fans, and I found myself championing their efforts to speed up the healing process of these two very important people in my life. When I no longer had a father, Brent filled that familial void. As insufferable and presumptuous as Tyler Rayne can be, he was nothing short of understanding once we turned that corner into something more than friends and I eased myself into my first relationship post-divorce. I've wanted nothing more than a return to normalcy following those bleak and dreary days on the roads to recovery. Tyler would be able to walk again, following therapy and not without using a cane for a time. Brent was not so lucky; the stroke he suffered claimed the use of his legs, but not his spirit. Just because he could no longer demonstrate the martial arts he taught, he said, didn't mean he was any less of a teacher. Days rolled into weeks, into months, and it wasn't long before things, as different and foreign as they were, began to return to a state resembling ordinary. Or so one would think, anyway. ~**~**~**~ September, 2009 Clay Darcy flipped open his legal folder and extracted a pen from the inside of his suit jacket. He twisted the top to reveal the pen's tip and scribbled something on the pad to get the ink flowing. "Now, as we're all aware, the purpose of this meeting is to review the final will and testament of Brent DiPetrillo, at the request of Brent himself. Here with him today are his wife Elise, his niece Alaina and his other niece--" Clay looked around the dining room table and frowned. "Where's Lindsay?" "She'll be here," Alaina replied. "She may have been running late from the airport." "She usually calls if something's come up," Elise said. "Maybe she can't talk right now. Traffic wasn't that great getting here today." "We can wait." Brent leaned against the back of his wheelchair and smiled. "You don't have anything else planned the rest of the day, right Clay?" "You're my last order of business before a weekend of golf." "Good. I don't figure this will take too long anyway. You'll be on the greens before you know it." "Is this even necessary, Brent?" his wife asked. "I can't foresee there needing to be any changes made." "Well, probably not," Brent agreed, "But given my recent spells of bad health, I figure it can't hurt to just go over it one more time. Be on the safe side." Elise reached over and patted her husband's hand. This was the second stroke he'd suffered in a five year span and it was, by far, the most debilitating. "I'm going to go get a refill. Anyone else need one?" Alaina stood up from the table and took her glass to hand. Everyone shook their heads and she walked back into the kitchen. Condensation rolled down the pitcher of iced tea as Alaina set her glass down on the top of the island and slid her hand in-between the body of the pitcher and the handle. She began to top off her drink when the sound of a car motoring up the driveway halted her in mid-pour. "I think she's here!" Alaina hollered into the dining room. She left her glass and the pitcher on the counter and strode out into the front foyer to greet her sister. No sooner had she made it to the hallway closet when the front door burst open and Lindsay Troy stepped inside, looking like a tornado spit her out of its funnel. Alaina stepped back in shock. "Lin? What is it?" "I need to talk to you," Lindsay said brusquely. Her curly hair, pulled back in a ponytail, was matted to her head. Her hands trembled slightly. There was no sign of luggage or a purse; just the keys to her car in her hand. "Lindsay? Is that you?" "Yeah, Aunt Elise it's me! I just need to talk to Lanni for a minute, then I'll be in," she yelled back. "Grab a glass of iced tea before you do!" Lindsay grabbed Alaina's hand and pulled her outside to the front walkway. Alaina managed to shut the door on her way out and stumbled after her older sister. "Lindsay. WHAT is going on?" "I'm late." "I know you're late," Alaina yanked her hand out of Lindsay's and checked her watch. "Forty minutes. Did you forget that in the twenty-first century, we use cell phones?" "No, Alaina. I'm late. LATE late." The suggestive pause hung heavily in the humid Floridian air. Alaina stared at Lindsay in disbelief. Words formed on her tongue but she couldn't force them out in any sense of coherence. "I knew I missed one, in July, during Brent and Tyler's recoveries. But it happened before; when Brent had that first stroke four years ago," Lindsay said and began to pace. "Back then, the doctor said it was just stress. I figured, same deal this time. They both got out of the hospital, things started to calm down a little bit, not a biggie. But this is two in a row, Alaina." "Oh...oh shit. Lindz. Oh my God!" Alaina squealed. "Do you know for sure? Did you take a test? Does Tyler know?" "That's why I'm late getting here. I drove back to my place here and took a test. Three tests. To be sure," she rambled, "And no, Tyler doesn't know. I mean, I called him and left messages for him to call me, that I need him to come back home, but his phone is off. He's in Japan right now. I don't know what time it is over there. He could be sleeping or in a meeting for his club or on a plane or...shit, Alaina. Fuck. FUCK!" Lindsay threw her hands up to her head and crouched on the ground. Her breaths came rapidly and when she started to wheeze, Alaina knelt down next to her and rubbed her back. "You're hyperventilating. Lindz. You need to calm down. Breathe easy. In and out, come on. In...out...in...out...." It took a handful of minutes but eventually, the eldest Troy regained some semblance of composure. She sat down on the pavement and heaved a heavy sigh. "It's a shock, I know." Alaina sank down next to her. "I know that when I found out I was pregnant with CeCe, it wasn't in my plans at the time. But it happened. There's nothing you can do about it happening. I know that I'm so glad that it did." "I need Tyler back here," Lindsay replied. "He doesn't deserve to hear it over the phone. I can't tell him long-distance." "Do you know when he's coming back to the States?" "Not for certain, no. A week? Two weeks? He said he had business over there. He wasn't sure how long it was going to take. He's been gone for a couple weeks now as it is." Alaina draped her arm over her sister's shoulders and eased Lindsay into leaning against her for support. "Lindz, there's nothing you can do right now. But I know Brent, Elise and Clay are going to worry about what's going on out here. Let's just get you inside where you can sit down and we can talk about this when we're in air conditioning. Alright?" Alaina kissed her forehead then stood up, stretching her hands out to help her to her feet. Once standing, Alaina pulled her sister in for a hug and held her tight. "I want to keep it." Alaina lifted her head back and looked Lindsay square in the eyes. "I--I don't know what Tyler wants and I don't know if this will change things but I want to keep it. Whether we raise it or I raise it or someone else raises it, I don't know right now. But I don't want to toss it away like it never happened or that it doesn't matter. It matters." "I know, Lindz," Alaina replied. "You've never done anything in your life that didn't matter." Lindsay nodded, wiped her forehead and then her eyes. She took Alaina's hand and lead her forward to face their unsuspecting family with an unexpected surprise and an unknown future before her.
