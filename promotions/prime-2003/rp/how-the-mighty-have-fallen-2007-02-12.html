How The Mighty Have Fallen...

12 February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/12/07 The sounds of glasses clinking together as toasts are raised, laughter some at jokes, the rest at the expense of others. Voices fill the room, but to the man seated in the corner it as if he is alone in the room. Tom Walczak sits on a barstool, his shoulders spreading wide enough to cover the stools that stand next to him. It is a Friday night and the bar is packed, but the area surrounding Walczak remains empty except for the bartender’s trips to refill the big man’s mug. While the action in the bar heats up only a couple of feet away from Walczak, he remains in his own world. Every couple of seconds he lifts his beer mug to his lips taking a sip, but outside of that he remains motionless, staring blankly at the display of bottles that are in front of him. Why did I come back? Second guessing seems like the only place to go for Walczak. He had the life he always wanted back in Poland, but he came back. For What? To get his ass handed to him by Nitz Donnelly, to be embarrassed in front of the people he despises the most? He could have been worshipped in any organization in Poland, but he came back to PRIME to be a bottom feeder. Where did I go wrong? PCW Rising Star Champion, OSW World Heavyweight Champion. I was at the top, but how did I fall so far? It’s funny how people always seem to look back on their lives when they are at there lowest points. You never see someone looking at what they have accomplished when they are on top of the world, no at those times they live in the moment. However, if you take all the glory, all the accomplishments, away from a person all they can do is look back, live in the past. It wasn’t to long ago that Walczak was mopping the floor with Tyler Rayne, but so much has changed in a couple of months. Tyler Rayne is now the 5-Star Champion, and Walczak is opening up cards against newcomers looking to get their big break. SLAP A hand comes across the back of Walczak startling him, and even causing him to spill a trickle of beer out of his mug. The Pole turns around a look of anger plastered on his face waiting to see who would be stupid enough to piss him off on a day like today, but when he finally catches a glimpse of the offender his look turns to one of confusion. “Why aren’t you up there?” The question does not register with Walczak. He is still awkwardly looking at the gentlemen across from him. The smell coming off of him gave away the fact that he was drunk long before Walczak turned to look at him, but the glazed over look on the man’s face only further clarifies this fact. Stains cover the pink, button down shirt, and hair clumsily overflows the top unbuttoned area of the shirt. His chinstrap beard and large diamond like earrings only add to Walczak’s distaste for the man, but the glasses resting on top of the gentlemen’s over gelled hair seal the deal for Walczak. “I said why you aren’t up there?” Walczak follows the finger of the man towards the television above the bar only feet away from him. It shows a commercial promoting the upcoming PRIME Revolution. There are numerous shots of Lindsay Troy, Tyler Rayne, Nitz Donnelly, and Tony Gamble, but as the commercial comes to a close Walczak realizes that he is in fact not represented. “I don’t know, guess I haven’t been at too many events recently.” Walczak turns away taking another extended sip from his beer, hoping that this will be the end of the confrontation, but like other recent events it doesn’t go the way Walczak intended. “Yeah or maybe you just suck.” The words come out slowly as the man tries hard not to slur his speech, and as soon as the sentence is completed he turns around foolishly smiling at no one in particular, proud of the fact that he believes he has just gotten one over on the Polish Punisher. “Yeah that could be it.” WHAT!?!?!? What has Walczak become that he just lets a comment like that go. If this were even two months ago, this douche bag would have been on the floor not knowing what had happened, but now all that happens is he goes back to his mug, almost hoping that the man behind him just leaves him alone. “Man, you are pathetic.” The man finally does make his way out of Walczak’s area, but not before giving the big man a less than playful nudge on the back. Walczak doesn’t react at first doing nothing more than once again turning to his beverage for solace. However, as the last drop falls from the mug into Walczak’s mouth it seems to cause an instant transformation. His eyes light up as he turns around and grabs his condescending new friend by the collar bringing him into the air before firmly planting him on the floor. The bar goes silent as the music cuts out, and all people turn their attention to Walczak’s corner a place they had previously ignored. Looks of shock and awe now fill the bar, but no one does anything to confront the cause of all this. Walczak pays no attention to those looking at him, and looks down at his fallen opposition almost smiling as he writhes around the ground grabbing the back of his head. After a couple more seconds of staring at his opponent, Walczak heads back to the bar raising the empty mug towards the bartender as if nothing had changed. The intense look disappears from Walczak’s face, but for that one second we got a glimpse of what the Polish Punisher once was. Have I really deteriorated that much that some two bit punk can come up to me and talk to me like that? When I used to walk into a room people used to clear the way because they were afraid of me, but now it is almost as if they clear the way because of how pathetic I am. They no longer fear me, they look down on me with disgust and pity. This must change Indeed Walczak needs to change, but looking in the past will not help him accomplish this, and before Walczak can change the opinions of others he must regain the swagger that once made him the most dominant force in wrestling.
