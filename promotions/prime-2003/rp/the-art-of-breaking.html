The Art of Breaking

Date: ReV 136 It’s the kind of evening that would make lovers swoon. The temperature steadies somewhere in the perfect medium, a nexus of non-extremes where shorts and jackets exist in the same plane of acceptable and comfortable. The night sky is devoid of clouds, a perfectly dark canvas for the twinkle of a hundred beautiful stars. A cool breeze tugs at the loose hollow of his sleeve, Tyler Rayne’s precarious perch upon a residential rooftop affording him a fantastic view of both the stars and the lavish little ranch house down the road. For hours he has been watching the house, mental notes of intricacies and finite details amassing within his mind. Mapping. He carefully places the binoculars on a shingle, a momentary break from the reconnaissance to sip at a thermos resting near his boot. It’s peaceful. Quiet. The faintest scent of lotus blossoms tickles his olfactories. “Find ‘er?” There is a rustle within the leaves of a tree, nothing more than the wind at play. The creak of a branch, something almost weightless passing along the bough, pressing wood and foliage against the roof. Somewhere in the dense black of the tree, a small glint, not unlike the twinkle of the stars above. The moonlight reflected in her eye. Milena Takahashi steps from the darkness with all the grace and fleet-footedness of a ballerina, nary making a sound as she tiptoes onto the shingled roof. She is covered, neck to toe, in skin-tight black material. Enveloped in night. Thin, gloved fingers reach to pull at the edges of her hood, revealing delicately beautiful features and releasing a wave of soft, luxurious hair. “Arkansas.” Milena is infamous within their circle for short, straightforward responses. She’s an eccentric woman, as could be said of many in Rayne’s life. A native of Japan, it was the wrestling industry that first brought the femme fatale to the States, where she befriended Katterina Wylde, and in turn, Tyler Rayne. The business held little appeal to the woman. She left the spotlight before most had had chance to realize she’d been there. Which, given her usual line of work, was indicative of exactly how the woman preferred to operate. When in the States, she insisted on speaking English, though both close friends were more than fluent in her native tongue. Thus, her responses varied only in the use of one or two words, leading to the misbegotten assumption that Milena’s English skills were less than satisfactory. As it turns out, she speaks English quite well, particularly when intoxicated. She just chooses not to elaborate unnecessarily. “The fuck? What’s she doing in Arkansas?” The same cannot be said for her current counterpart. “Concert.” He scoffs, pulling another sip from the thermos. “Tea?” He nods. The recipe dates back, supposedly, to feudal Japan. An ancient, mystical concoction created to relieve the pains of war and prolonged battle. He can attest, no matter the origins, that it does in fact do as advertised. Tastes better than pain killers, too. “That him?” She does not point. Barely moves. Simply leaves herself unshrouded, allowing him to follow the line of her concentrated stare. Her assassin’s gaze falls upon the ranch below, the private residence he’s been watching for some time now. There, in the large patio window, a pair of swollen eyes peer through blond tresses, scouring the street and nearby properties for indications of a threat to come. The resident, unfortunately for him, never thinks to look above, to the roofs and trees of his suburban neighbors. Again, Tyler nods, a small stone of discomfort forming within the pit of his stomach. Milena, while an apt conversationalist when appropriate, rarely goes out of her way to attempt such. She speaks when spoken to, and when the need arises. She does not, however, speak for the sake of hearing her own voice. That’s something Tyler would do. “What’s up?” He asks the question as nonchalantly as possible. It’s impossible to tell whether she noticed how much he had to force the calm. “A question. If I may.” Between the three of them, they’d saved each other’s asses more times than he’d ever care to count. There was friendship. There was love. And there was deep, deep respect. It was something he still had trouble adjusting to. Given his career choice, Tyler Rayne had become accustomed to certain amounts of respect and idolatry. Came with the job. But there was something so…sincere, so very personal about the way Milena presented herself, respectfully, that it unnerved him in ways he could never admit. He knew who he was. Cursed at the man every morning he looked in the mirror. That man, he didn’t deserve the kind of respect Milena offered. Never would. “Shoot, kid.” “Do you love her?” He had expected something along those lines. Still, even anticipating a question of that genre, the simple bluntness of her query struck him at the core. He had grown accustomed to dealing with people somewhat more tactful. Dancing around questions and problems until everyone was far too exhausted to bother or care about the answers. Milena was a killer, through and through. Straight for the jugular every time. “I did.” “You do not now?” It was something he hadn’t thought about in quite some time. Roxy Phoenix was an annoying little wench of a wrestling prodigy. Hot little number with more talent than Peter North has cock. Beautiful little red-head. Absolutely lethal in the ring. That’s what had caught his attention first. Sheer, unbridled passion for the business. For kicking ass. She didn’t even care about the names. At the time, she was involved with someone else. His initial interest had been in taking her on as a protégé. A padawan. Things…evolved, as they tend to do. One thing led to another. She tried to kill him. Broke his sternum. Nearly crushed his skull. Instead, she kissed him. Then she fucked him. Damn, could that girl grind. She left the business shortly thereafter. Left him. Without a word. Without a warning. Up and disappeared. Just like the last one. Just like Michelle. And Di, before her. He laughs, the kind of quiet snort one does when realizing the obvious truth in front of them. “No. I guess I don’t. Hadn’t thought about it until now.” “Then why persist?” He shrugs. “Just wanna know she’s okay, I guess.” “Roxy is more than capable of caring for herself. As capable as Dionne, I would suspect.” Miss Dionne. The name still made him laugh, though he supposed it wasn’t any more ridiculous than calling yourself the Icon, or abbreviating your name to LA3. Apparently “Triple H” was already under copyright. She was the first woman he’d loved since… well, it had been awhile. Truly, honestly fallen in love with. It lasted for quite awhile, especially as far as relationships in the wrestling business go. Few years before it finally cracked. The business had become corrupted. More political than usual. Lost all its fun. She left and never looked back. For Tyler, though, wrestling courses through his veins. Hell, one could argue it practically pumps his blood. A reason to live. He chose the business over love, a decision that is difficult to live with some five years later. “I do not understand.” A short, simple admission that brings Tyler Rayne from his thoughts. Milena’s eyes remain fixated on the residence, noting the placement of shadows, hints of movement within. “What is it, exactly, that you don’t understand?” “Why you loved them instead of me.” For the second time in a single evening, Tyler Rayne is taken aback. “I…shit, kid. Just…fuck. I didn’t know you were that serious.” There was a time, probably three or four years back, when Katterina had confided that Milena did, in fact, have something of a crush on Tyler. He’d dismissed the notion at first. Though, later, he had considered pursuing the interest, the timing just never worked out in their favor. Milena still does not pull her gaze from the house. She does, however, offer a short smile. “That was long ago. I am over it. It is…curious, is all. There are these women you have loved. Women who have left you. Then, there is Katterina. There is myself. We have, in all this time, never once abandoned each other. Yet neither of us has been worthy of that type of love. Not even of consideration.” “I think I like you better when you don’t talk.” She obliges. The two of them sit quietly for a long while, just staring at the ranch house, neither actually looking at all. He sips his tea. The silence lingers. Continuing the uncharacteristic nature of the conversation, Milena is the first to speak. “If I may…” He laughs, though it’s a sound deprived of all amusement. “By all means.” “It has occurred to me that perhaps you seek these women. That you intentionally fall for someone who will inevitably leave you behind.” “That’s an interesting concept. And why, oh great Japanese sage, the fuck would I do somethin’ like that?” “Once she is gone, you’ll never have to worry about watching her die in your arms.” The assessment was more crushing than a sledgehammer to the chest. It was a sudden, quick pain that stole the breath from his lungs and wrenched at the fibers of his heart. It was all he could do to choke back tears. “Lin.” Milena sat no more than a foot away, but the choked utterance of her sacred name was a whisper even her keen senses could barely hear. He sighed, a finger rising to wipe the tear from his eye. “Perhaps we should change the subject.” “Little late for that, kid.” “I…I am sorry. It was not my place.” “Nah. It’s cool. What makes you so talkative all a sudden, anyway?” “Loneliness.” “Yeah.” The pause is shorter, but no less silent than the last. A moment for each to regroup. “Alright, so you’re the chatty one, what do you wanna talk about?” Her brow furrows, a likely attempt to think of a less depressing conversation topic. “How was the match? The important one.” “That Colossus thing?” “Yes. That.” “I lost. To a rookie.” “Oh.” Silence. He can feel the tension radiating from beneath her black garb. She’s nervous. “It happens. Happens a lot, actually. The losing.” “You have recently returned. It is common, I understand, for there to be a readjustment period. What is the term?” “Ring rust?” “Yes. You have ring rust.” “If that’s the case, I’m the gorram Tin Man and Dorothy’s little oil can ain’t nowhere to be fuckin’ found. Two months, kid. If I was gonna hit my stride, I’d have done so by now. Barely off to a fuckin’ crawl in this place. I mean, Munson’s a good guy and all, but damn. Fuckin’ green. It’s just, you know, couple years ago I would’ve walked over the kid without lookin’ back. Him and Polish. It’s like…I dunno. Fuck. Maybe I just don’t have it anymore.” “I would doubt that.” “I would have, too. Did. But lookin’ at things now…shit. It’s just not the same. I’m not the same. Not in the ring, anyway. Guess I should’ve left the boots hangin’. Or maybe I should put ‘em back.” “You would quit?” “No. …Maybe. I dunno. I’m happy for the kid. It’s his first big break. Probably go on to do some great things. But…far as this place goes, just doesn’t look like there’s a break left for me. This shit’s just…I’m losin’ my fuckin’ mind here. Years ago, you couldn’t have paid me to listen to these fuckin’ smarks out there. They did this stupid online poll, right? Usual ‘who’s gonna win’ bullshit. Kinda shit me and Katt just laugh off over a couple drinks. This time, though, shit got in my fuckin’ head. It’s like, there’s a whole internet out there, and not a single person on it expected me to win.” “You lost because of this?” “Fuck no. I lost because Munson was the better man that night. Or because I’m just the suck, who fuckin’ knows. It’s just, I’ve been here two months, and I’ve already sunken into this abyss. I’m that guy. Tries hard. Entertains the fans. It’s like I’m Leonard Aarons, but you know, with the trying and entertaining portions. So, kinda nothing like Aarons. But this weird inside joke that everyone gets to laugh at except me.” One by one, the lights within the ranch house are switched off. Tyler’s focus narrows on the movement of shadows inside, a silhouette betrayed by the moonlight. “I have never heard you lacking in confidence.” “And I’ve never heard you talk this much without emptying a bottle of Tequila first.” He stands, cautiously slipping across the shingles toward that tree. He may not be as feather light as the Japanese assassin, but for a man of his size, quite impressively quiet and weightless when he chooses to be. He pauses, hidden within the shadows of the tree. “Maybe you’re right. About the whole love thing. I never thought about it.” “Why would you have?” “I wouldn’t. Got another job, if you’re interested.” “I do enjoy your money.” “Shiny. Some new queen in Tokyo. Calls herself the Jade Scorpion.” Even one as disciplined as Milena cannot keep herself from laughing. “That’s what I said. Sent a messenger, personally to me. Said she knows something about Se7yn.” “Do you believe her?” “Not in the least. But I can’t pass up the opportunity. Need you to check ‘er out. Lemme know what I’m getting into.” “This sounds like an easy task.” “They’re never easy, kid. You should know that by now.” “I suppose I should. Thank you, Tyler. For speaking with me.” “Nothin’ doin’, kid. Not exactly one to turn down a beautiful woman, now am I?” He winks, though it’s impossible to tell if she could even see it through the shadows. The hour grows early, and there is business yet to be done. The tree is already an afterthought, Tyler Rayne slinking across shadows and darkness as he carefully closes on the ranch house. The denim jeans reflect light too easily. A wardrobe choice that most would not make. He finds them comfortable. Relaxing. And it is much easier, if caught, to look like an unassuming citizen when not dressed like you’re trying to infiltrate Fort Knox. He’s in the house within minutes, silently sliding through the patio doors on his way to the kitchen. D-20, a young hacker with a reputation for unsavory dealings, digs through the meager options of his refrigerator, internally cursing his lack of adequate munchie food. “You heal quick, Dice. Can barely tell I kicked your ass a week ago.” Startled is an understatement. He jumps, the crown of his head cracking against the fridge frame. Vision blurs for the few seconds it takes to pull himself out of the cool appliance, a shocked and unbelieving expression turned on his intruder. Survival instincts kick in, and D-20 throws himself forward with a quick succession of kicks that catch Rayne completely off guard. The kid moves in, a sliding shoulder block that doubles his intruder over. From such a position, it’s only a matter of leverage and minimal effort that throws Rayne over his shoulder and to the cold linoleum below. Unbeknownst to those who may have witnessed last week’s encounter, Dice is actually a second degree black belt in kempo karate. Unbeknownst to the young would-be gangster, Tyler came with a handgun stuffed into his waistband. A .45 caliber pistol that now rests comfortably in his grasp. Before complete realization of his utter fuckedness takes his mind, a bullet rips straight through Dice’s patella, exploding out the back of his knee and making a dreadful mess of the kitchen floor. Tyler calmly rises to his feet, setting the gun up near the oven. Out of reach but easily within sight. Tyler’s hands move to the stove controls, turning a knob that ignites the right front burner. “You…you fuckin’ shot me!” “Just because I can kick your ass, kid, doesn’t mean I wanna waste the time to do it. ‘Specially if you’re gonna fight back this time. Sit tight for a sec, eh?” He leaves the kitchen. Dice closes his eyes, a large intake of breath intended to flush the pain. He attempts to stand, though the tiniest ounce of pressure on that leg drops him immediately back to the floor. Tears in his eyes, Dice begins to drag himself across the linoleum, hands stretching for the gun though he’s nowhere near close to it. The pistol is his chance at retribution. At escape. It is all he can think about. All his concentration pours into getting that god damn gun. Which is why he never notices Tyler coming up behind him, a swift but powerful swing of metal cracking against the hacker’s vertebrae. Dice screams, his entire body wrenching with uncontrollable pain. Tyler steps over him without so much as a glance. Like he were nothing more than a piece of debris on the sidewalk. “Never been shot before, have you?” Through the searing red pain blurring his eyes, D-20 can see that the object in Rayne’s hand is a fire poker. He can also see that Rayne is heating the tip of the poker over the oven. “You’re probably thinking the cops’ll be here soon, right? Someone must have called by now. Gunshot in the middle of the night. Your pussy ass screamin’ like a little girl.” Rayne pulls a cigarette from his pocket, igniting it with the heat of the oven burner. “Probably be true if you hadn’t bought a place in Mafioso central. I’ve been watchin’ your house for two hours now. Sittin’ on some lawyer’s roof. Cop watched three of DeAngelis’ men beat and murder this woman, right. Goes to court, this lawyer gets ‘em all off, scott free. No fuckin’ idea how he pulled that shit. Lady lives two houses down? Madame for a brothel, rents out to local thugs and such. Mostly underage girls. Couple underage boys, too.” He takes a long drag of the cigarette, turning the fire poker in his hand to examine the tip. Not quite ready. “You bought this place ‘cause they wouldn’t ask questions, right? Figure people like that, tryin’ to get in good with the Fam, they’re not gonna call the cops when some shady characters show up on your doorstep in the middle of the night. Not gonna ask questions as to why some punk kid can afford to live in a place like this. Nah, they’re gonna mind their own business. Let you take care of yours. Works out pretty well, ‘til one of those shady characters turns up to shoot you in the knee cap.” “Fuck…y-y-you.” Without moving the poker from the flame, Tyler turns, a quick snap of his leg dropping boot sole on Dice’s face. Splitting lips. Breaking teeth. Blood splatters on the stainless steel refrigerator door. “I bet they told ya I wouldn’t kill ya. If it’s any comfort, that’s true. I won’t kill you. Not the killing type.” Tyler now turns to fully face his victim, red-hot fire poker gripped firmly in hand. Dice attempts to crawl away, but his body moves only so fast. Tyler stalks the hacker easily, slowly lowering himself to the floor. He firmly plants a knee in Dice’s chest to hold him in place, then proceeds to cauterize the gunshot wound. Smoke swirls from the wound. The smell of burning flesh fills the air. Dice begins to scream, but a quick punch to the Adam’s apple ends that before it really begins. Once both the entry and exit wounds have been sealed, Tyler tosses the poker away, throwing himself against a nearby cabinet. He sits on the floor next to the hacker, a moment’s pause to light another cigarette from his pocket. “Turns out there’s all sorts of sick fuckin’ shit I can do that won’t kill ya.” He doesn’t speak another word. Just sits on the floor, casually smoking a cigarette. He finishes, smoldering the flame and ash against Dice’s calf. Hair singes. The skin blackens and bleeds. Tyler stands, once again leaving the kitchen and his victim. Minutes pass with nothing but the broken whimpers of a hacker on his kitchen floor. The sound of boot falls. Tyler returns, a small hammer now in hand. Again, he does not speak. Does not acknowledge the muted sobs of his victim. He simply shoves the hammer into Dice’s mouth, claw placed around either side of a tooth. A quick flick of the wrist pulls the tooth out at the roots, a gush of blood pouring from Dice’s upper gums. “It’s the wrestling thing, isn’t it? Makes you think I’m stupid. Or maybe you’ve got one of those superiority things, what with you being a hacker and all. Like, you’re smarter than all of us. But seriously. Did you really think I wouldn’t notice it was a little too convenient how you happened to be in Oregon only days before I was to show up in Seattle?” “P-Please…” “Who’s setting me up?” “I…I don’t know. Please, I don’t…know.” Another tooth. “At the moment, you can still kinda talk. Assuming you don’t puss out on me and pass out from the pain. So I need to know who told you to be there, specifically, at that time. I need to know why, after weeks of hopping from one end of the country to the other, you very easily popped up on the radar only a handful of hours from my show.” “I told you. Renaissance. These…three guys. Yakuza. Call themselves… Michelangelo…Raphael… …Leonardo. Get a real kick out of it. Said you’d come… come looking for me. Promised a big pay off…if I…tipped you to them.” “And they’ve got the Roberts kid?” “Yeah.” “Where?” “I dunno.” “Why me?” “I…I don’t know, man. Just… just fuckin’ let me go. C’mon, man.” He begins to cry. Sobbing, really. Tyler doesn’t care. He repositions himself, a firm knee to the back that presses Dice’s blood-soaked and broken face into the linoleum. Tyler takes him by the wrist, stretching the left arm down to the floor. “Spread your fingers.” A whimper. The hammer is a pendulum of pain, a blinding blow to the kidney. He almost passes out. A knuckle grinding into the cigarette burn jolts enough pain through his leg to keep him conscious. “Spread. Your fucking. Fingers.” He does as instructed. The sobbing is uncontrollable. Tyler bends his arm at the elbow, hammer suspended above his shoulder. It falls with all his fury. With the momentum of gravity and the twisted will of some macabre and pagan god. The thumb is its target, and it obliterates without remorse. The force of the impact divots the linoleum beneath. The thumb itself literally pops at the knuckle. Tyler removes the lighter from his pocket, torching another cigarette before he moves the flame to the bloody stump of a thumb. Cauterize another wound. Keep him from bleeding out. “I’ve got this anger thing. Guess they didn’t tell ya that. My job, man, it’s fuckin’ gettin’ to me. Just really fuckin’ grindin’ my nerves. And then…I hate Yakuza. They probably didn’t tell ya that, either. But I fuckin’ loathe those cunt drip sonsabitches. Despise their shit. Ya know… the wrestling thing. I keep losing. It’s pissin’ me off somethin’ fierce. But nothin’ pisses me, just fuckin’ infuriates my ass more than some cock suckin’ Yakuza bastards tryin’ to fuck with me. Just got some fuckin’ Wolverine, berserker rage shit goin’ on right now, and—” “Hawaii. They’re from…fuckin’ Hawaii. Alright? That’s where they took him. Just…fuckin’…Hawaii. Cool? Fuckin’ good enough, you sick cunt!” “Why me?” “They’re gonna kill me.” “I’m not.” The index finger doesn’t explode quite like the thumb did. Of course it’s crushed under the might of the hammer, gnarled and twisted in a dozen tiny fractures. But at least it’s still in one piece. “Why me?” “A cousin, okay? They’ve got a…got a cousin. Runs the shit…down…down in Hawaii. Knew you’d come for the kid. Wants you fuckin’ bad, man. Just…fuckin’… god…wants your head. I don’t know why. I swear to fuckin’ Christ I don’t know why.” “What’s his name?” “They never told me. I swear to you, they never fuckin’ told me a name.” Tyler skips to the end of the hand, opting to demolish the pinky. Dice screams, a primal, inhuman cry for help. No one cares. “I believe you.” He tosses the hammer aside, a warm, almost friendly tap of his palm against Dice’s cheek. Tyler walks to the sink, washes the blood from his hands. “Lucky you caught me in a forgiving mood, kid. Most times I’d keep going, what with that little secret you got in the basement and all. But I’m givin’ you a pass. Chance to clean up. Your leg’ll heal. Might not ever run a marathon in your life, but you’ll walk again. Teeth are replaceable. Dentures don’t exactly get ya the ladies, though. The hand…well, you’re kinda fucked on that. That’s just fuckin’ busted. But I’m sure you can learn to beat off with the left. It’s not personal, kid. Maybe a little. The basement thing. But mostly, just business. See, last time Yakuza and I got into it, someone very close to me died. Did a lot of really heinous shit in return. Shit makes what I did to you look like a paper cut. I’m hopin’ maybe they see you, jogs the memory. Reminds them I’m not the type to be fucked with. If you’d be so kind as to pass the message, I’d like to settle this amicably. I don’t want shit to get out of control. Go too far. I’m gettin’ to old for this ‘Hard-Boiled’ racket. Tell ‘em I want the boy. Might even be willing to work out a deal where they don’t all get crippled.” That’s how he leaves him. Friendly words of advice to a broken, beaten young man bleeding on his own kitchen floor. A helpless victim of circumstance. Tyler Rayne opens the front door of a lavish ranch home in an expensive suburban neighborhood, and admires the wonderful evening that lay before him. The kind of perfect lover’s dream of. He pauses to light a cigarette before pulling the door closed behind him. In seconds he’s on the sidewalk, the house and its owner long forgotten. He’s just another man in a black t-shirt and jeans. A comfortable citizen out for a pleasant stroll beneath a beautiful starry sky.
