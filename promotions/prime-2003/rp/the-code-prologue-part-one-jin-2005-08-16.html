The Code (Prologue / Part One: Jin)

16 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-16-05 THE CODE: PROLOGUE Bushido For the uninitiated, a code of ethics that prevailed among the warrior class in feudal Japan, and truly formalized during the beginning of the Tokugawa Shogunate...it was the code of the samurai. For truly the best definition of Bushido, one might refer to Inazo Nitobe, the author of Bushido: The Soul Of Japan. “Bushido...is not a written code, at best it consists of a few maxims handed down from mouth to mouth or coming from the pen of some well-known warrior or savant. More frequently, it is a code unuttered and unwritten, possessing all the more the sanction of veritable deed, and of a law written on the fleshly tablets of the heart.” And what does this have to do with Kyle Lamen, rock-star wannabe and pro wrestler? Well...he's always called himself, since his association with Kensuke Suzahara, the “Six-String Samurai.” Though Kyle originally thought it sounded kind of cool, he was unaware of the fact that this statement tended to be truer than he originally intended. It was Hiroshi Sennosuke, the man that taught Kyle the art of “shinsakuto” (the traditional method of Japanese sword-forging), that agreed with Kyle, when he called himself that, and explained a little more behind the code of Bushido, that defined the samurai. Firstly, he told Kyle that Miyamoto Musashi, perhaps the greatest swordsman to ever walk the earth, said that Bushido was a twofold way of the pen and the sword. Kyle, who devoted himself both to writing and performing his music (the pen), and engaging in professional wrestling (the sword), was already on the path of the Samurai. Then, he explained the seven associated virtues of Bushido. Jin (Benevolence)Rei (Respect)Chuugi (Loyalty)Gi (Rectitude)Yu (Courage)Meiyo (Honor)Makoto (Honesty) Finally, he agreed that Kyle was a lot like Musashi, partially because he was so tall, and had taken to wearing the sword hung over his back, as Musashi was said to. Kyle laughed, and would eventually come home to America. He may have forgotten the precepts of Bushido...but they did not forget him. Like the warrior-poets of feudal Japan, Bushido was as much a part of Kyle as his arm, his sword, his soul. It's what made him who he is. * * * * * THE CODE: JIN 08.16.05. Seattle, Washington. The offices of KS Sports Management were like the offices of any other high-powered sports management firm... ...except they were smaller, dirtier, less well-staffed, and not high-powered in the slightest. But that didn't stop the man from behind the rich, uh...generic-particle-board desk. He had hair similar to Kyle's, except that it was a rich onyx. He's Kensuke Suzahara, former CCW/OSW/PCW/PURE wrestler. Oh, and he is most definitely Japanese, high cheekbones, and naturally quite handsome..if you ask the young woman sitting opposite him at the desk. The young woman in question is Sarah McIntyre, Kyle Lamen's lovely personal assistant. And next to her is her charge, the Six-String Samurai, Kyle Lamen. He absently picks at the neck of his t-shirt. In addition to the three bodies in the office, there wasn't much else. Considering KS Sports Management was founded a month ago, for one...Kensuke put what everyone in the room was thinking into words. “Uh, we're getting the new offices next week. I have blueprints, if you want to see them...” Kyle shakes his head, his blue spikes jittering slightly. “No thanks, man.” He looked askance at Sarah, who was her usual nervous self around her bosses. Little beads of sweat formed in the hollow of her cleavage, displayed in a tight black shirt to attract the attention of Kensuke. Was she actually squeezing her chest together? Kensuke appeared oblivious. Sarah appeared to be glistening. Kyle was already a little bored. “So, Kyle.” Kensuke started in his 'professional' tone, which was basically the tone that he had copied from watching Tyler Nelson video footage. His voice was too high, but no matter. “Sarah says you've been doing quite well in PRIME so far...and you've got a match against Hoyt Williams this week?” Kyle nodded a little, and then leaned back dangerously in his chair. “Yeah, but you knew that already. Is there a REAL reason we're here?” Kensuke rolled his eyes and sighed. “Fine, fine. As your manager, I'm informing you of a few business matters. One, we've already been asked to renegotiate your contract in PRIME...and your first endorsement deal just needs your signature.” Kyle kind of scoffed, brushing some invisible lint off of his shirt. Kensuke's office seemed dusty to him, and he didn't like it. “Yeah, whatever. The local Tropical Smoothie thing, right?” Sarah winced at the mention of her former place of employment, which Kyle and Kensuke had “rescued” her from after getting her fired...but that's a story for another time. “Kyle, just shut up and listen for a moment, okay?” Sarah said with more than a mild hint of malice. She'd never liked Kyle, and still didn't, despite being his personal assisstant. Kensuke looked at Kyle for a moment, and then shook his head disbelievingly. “Man, after you get those contracts finalized this week, plus your PPV payout...you're going to have money. Lots of it.” Kyle just nodded, not really comprehending. “Cool.” Kensuke and Sarah shared a look, almost disbelieving, crow's feet curling up in unison. “Kyle, you don't understand,” Kensuke said. “A whole shitload of money.” He held up a piece of paper, and produced a Sharpie from his pocket. He drew one horizontal line, then five zeroes after it. Kyle's eyes kind of widened in shock. “You're kidding, that much a year?” Sarah buried her face in her hands. “No, you moron. That's your bank account. You'll be pulling around three hundred fifty thousand dollars a year-” Before Sarah could finish, Kyle immediately passed out. * * * * * 08.17.05. North Dakota. “Would you like to make that a meal?” Kyle still had kind of a glazed look in his eyes, remaining from his discussion with Kensuke and Sarah the prior day. But now, it was just Sarah and Kyle, sitting in a rental car, in the drive-thru to a McDonalds. Everywhere has a McDonalds, even North Dakota. “Fuck no!” Sarah McIntyre insisted. In response, a few garbled words made their way out of the squawk box, and the rental car, a Buick, made it's way forward in the drive-thru lane. Sarah, as always, was thinking about money. “Hey Kyle, can I have a raise?” she asked, putting on her “sweet face.” Kyle looked at her like she had eaten a week's worth of crazy pills. “No, I don't pay your paycheck anyways. Ask Kensuke.” Sarah stuck her lower lip out, pouting a little. “So what are you going to do with all that cash, then?” she asked, running a hand through her dark locks. Kyle stared out the window. “Dunno. What do other wrestlers with this money do?” Sarah shrugged. “They save it.” Kyle nearly spit at the thought. “Lame. Like they need to worry about tomorrow. Why save it when you might not be around tomorrow?” Sarah shook her head, hardly being able to contemplate Kyle's strange logic. “Other than that, I dunno, Kyle. Some of them travel, like Karina. I'm sure the ones like Ellis Easton and Danny Ferguson spend it on fast cars and whores. And you know about Hoyt and his...” Kyle's eyes finally light up, and Sarah mouth's an 'oh, shit.' “That's it! I know exactly what I'll do!” Sarah, confused, offers up her best guesses. “Buy gaudy ring robes?” Kyle shakes his head. “Hire low-card talent to follow you around?” Kyle shakes his head. “Buy all of your own merchandise, in order to falsely boost your popularity?” Kyle shakes his head. “Try and rip off his RP style, and not do it as well as he does?” Kyle is confused, but shakes his head. “Make fun of Aimz? Honestly...I don't really know what else the guy does...” Kyle shakes his head a final time. “Well then, what is it?” Kyle smiles a real, beatific, smile. No hint of guile, or irony, or smarm. Just a real, jubilant smile, like you see on sitcoms that don't star El Spiffy. “I'm going to give it away.” Sarah is completely horrified, and she raises a hand to her lips, and spazzes out, pushing the gas pedal and scraping the front of the rental car on a curb with a screeching sound. “You're kidding me, Kyle.” Kyle shakes his head. “Come on, what do I need all that money for? And you said I'd be making all that money a year...quick, name a charity!” Sarah throws her hands up, before trying to put the car in reverse and back away from the curb. “I don't know, Kyle. AIDS research? Make-A-Wish foundation?” Kyle thinks. “How about cancer research?” Sarah kind of scoffs. “Come on, why that, Kyle?” Kyle looks out the window, avoiding looking at Sarah. “My dad's got melanoma.” Again, the scraping of metal on concrete, as Sarah freaks out and hits a curb. “Kyle, I'm sorry. Jesus...” Kyle smiles a little bit, and looks at Sarah with a little happiness. “Jesus doesn't have anything to do with it, Sarah. It's not all about religion. Sometimes we can make our own miracles, y'know?” Sarah just sighs to herself, and pulls the car forward, spurred on by the thoughts of “wasted money” and fast food.
