Date: This week Bad to the bone, raised in the gutter, Not exactly a motherfucking role model. - Children of Bodom His muscles rife with aches and pains, Jason pushed up from the bench with the palms of hands rougher than a badger’s arse. Raising his thick, tattooed arms to the heavens and pushing his size fourteen boots hard into the concrete floor, Jason could think of a million other things he’d rather be doing than stretches at the moment. He knew there was no avoiding them though; if he didn’t do a proper warm-down he’d wake up the next day feeling ten times as bad, and with a second chance in the Jewel in the Crown tournament on the horizon that wouldn’t be ideal. It had been a long time since he’d felt this tired, this exhausted. Sonny Silver had been working tooth and nail to try and transform Jason Natas from a scrappy street fighter into a finely honed wrecking machine, bit never before had Jason ever been through anything like this. He’d been in the ring for five hours straight, five fucking hours. For three hundred consecutive minutes he must have locked up or sparred with every over-confident meathead in Seattle. Sure made those ten/fifteen minute bouts in PRIME seem like a walk in the park. And the best part? Sonny Silver wasn’t even there! The head of the academy, Mr. Silver Lining himself, was out of town, leaving the academy to the mercy of the other trainers. Typical. Maintaining the hold for a few seconds, Jason soon dropped his limbs and shook them loose. Reaching over, he grabbed his grey Silver Lining Academy hoodie from the hook and slid his arms inside. Simultaneously, he stuffed a pair of long, black shorts and a half-empty bottle of water into his bag before slinging the holdall over his shoulder. The dull ache that seemed to consume his entire body slowed his pace and he shambled towards the locker-room door at a near-glacial pace. He had one hand on the door handle when he realised he was the last “student” left in the Academy tonight, so he paused and flicked the lights off. Jason couldn’t understand why Sonny was so anal about something as petty as leaving a light switched on. Nonetheless, knowing the reputation of the PRIME Hall of Famer, he thought it best not to be the one guy to violate the “last one out” rule. Head hung low as he rummaged around in his pocket for his MP3 player, Jason cruised through the door with all the grace of a baby elephant standing on a balloon. Continuing to pace through the main gym area of the Silver Lining Academy, he finally pulled out the small white I-Pod. “Jason. Good work tonight.” Bobby Bosch caught Jason off-guard. The Anti-Superstar stopped, cocked his head to the side, and offered the trainer a polite nod. “S’what I do, Bob.” “No, I mean it, Jase,” Bosch continued in an accent that was far too West coast for Jason’s liking. “Fists are looking quick and the ducking and diving’s right up there. Sure there’ll be a few kids goin’ home tonight with a couple more bruises and loosened teeth than they came in with, but ya know.” Slowly Bosch drew his bulbous physique closer and closer to Jason. The man stood at least 6’2” and his shoulders were like the back end of the bus. A former professional boxer, the man must have been quite the intimidating presence in his heyday what with his yellowed teeth and acne-scarred cheeks, but the years hadn’t exactly been too kind on Bobby Bosch. His short greasy morass of black hair was making a hasty retreat at the temples towards an ever-growing bald patch, and what was once a finely chiselled upper-body was now round and flabby, belying his chosen profession. Physical misfortunes aside, Bosch was quite the dab hand when it came to training. Stern but fair, harsh but just, he knew how to push his students to the threshold of their physical capabilities. He might not have been able to glide around the ring like he used to, but while retirement had sullied his physical state to the point of ridicule, he carried with him a wealth of striking knowledge that a competitor of Jason Natas’s ring style would surely find invaluable. “Well, fuck ‘em. They can’t take a beatin’ then they should seriously be thinkin’ ‘bout changin’ careers,” proclaimed Jason in a low, tired tone of voice. Bobby stopped a couple of feet from him, pulling a pack of Camel’s from the pocket of his black denim jacket. After sparking up, he pointing the carton in Jason’s direction. “Cig?” “Nah. Not in the mood,” Jason said, raising his hand and shaking his head. “Sure you should be smokin’ that thing in here? If Sonny finds out-“ “Fuck’s he gonna do? He ain’t even here!” Bosch said with a crooked, gleeful smile. He took a quick drag on the cancer stick as Jason chuckled. Silver had barely been gone for twenty four hours and already one of his trainers was acting above the law. “’Sides, its fucking cold outside. You think I’m gonna freeze my nuts off just for a cigarette, you’ve got another thing coming.” Losing interesting momentarily, Jason set about untangling the wiry mess surrounding his MP3 player. “So here’s something I don’t get,” Bosch piped up, blowing a plume of rancid cigarette smoke between puckered lips. “You’re in PRIME, right? Biggest fuckin’ wrestling show on the planet, and yet you’re here? What’s the deal with that?” Sighing at a low decibel, Jason ceased untangling the headphone wires and glanced up. “Made some mistakes, pissed off a couple of the wrong people. Fuckin’ agents thought it’d be a good idea…” “Bah. Fucking suits,” Bobby shook his head. “Everywhere you go. They’ll strangle this god damn sport. But shit,” he chuckled, “you must’ve done somethin’ pretty awful for ‘em to send you all the way over here, ‘cause let’s face it; Sonny ain’t exactly a fluffy little bunny.” “Yeah, well. Let’s just say I ain’t the choirboy they were hopin’ for.” “Heh, ‘choirboy’. I like it,” Bosch forced a phoney smile across his weather features and pointing the cigarette towards Jason. “Fuckin’-A man, I hear you. I know what it’s like; seems like they wanna stop you from enjoyin’ yourself, you know? Put a curfew on you, a ball and chain. Guy like you’s gotta play it safe sometimes though, Jase. I mean, thousands and thousands of kids watch you on television every week, hell, bet there’s even a few look up to you.” Jason snorted. “Fuck that. I leave that part to Chandler and Rayne. Me? I step into the ring, make sure the other guy knows he’s been in a fight, an’ roll on out with a nice, big paycheque. What I do on my own time ain’t nobody’s business but my own.” Clearly disappointed by Jason’s words, Bosch took a step forward and looked the PRIME grappler straight in the eye. “Be careful, Jase. Look, I know more about you than you think, but I know adaptin’ to a lifestyle in the public eye ain’t the easiest thing in the world, but you’ve been doin’ this for a while, you know? Fact is, you’re one of the most-watched people on television, whether you like it or not, and you can’t be out getting drunk and breakin’ people’s noses every night. You gotta set a good example…” “Back up. ‘Set a good example’?” Jason raised an eyebrow and curled his lip. “What, you expectin’ me to help out in the community? Tell the kids the stay in school and eat a balanced diet? Shit.” “No no no, nothing like that. Just sayin’, you gotta be careful.” Jason sighed and bowed his head, finally managing to fully untangle the wire. Tucking the headphones in behind his hoodie, he slid the I-Pod into his pocket. “Look, Bob. You’re all right. You ain’t so bad. But don’t go lecturin’ me on shit I don’t wanna hear, ‘kay?” The Anti-Superstar glared at Bosch under a furrowed brow. The former boxer turned wrestling trainer parted his lips. “Bu-…” he gave up. It wasn’t the first time that Jason had rejected his advice, but still he found himself stupefied. “All right. G’night, Jason.” Offering the portly trainer a salute, Jason trundled off through the gym ready to hit the sack. Disappointed, Bosch shook his head and turned away as Jason thumbed at the click wheel on his MP3 player. Jason Natas, set an example? That’ll be the day. * * * * * “Okay cunt muscle, listen, I’m having an incredibly stressful day, and talking to you isn’t helping matters. What? Yeah, yeah. Ask me if I give a fuck. Listen, until I have nothing better to do than to exchange idle chit-chat with a fuckstick whose last notable ‘job’ was in the fucking PTC mail room, I’ll give you a call. Until then, I don’t want to be bothered unless there’s a scud missle headed straight for New York, okay?” Nathaniel Loftman was having a bad day. He slammed the phone down with such force that he almost cracked the plastic, leaned back in his insanely comfortable office chair and gently massaged his temples. Loftman had heard enough incessant whiney voices to last him a lifetime, so he quickly switched his Voicemail divert on to prevent himself from wanting to pull out even more of his blonde locks. Loftman could have sat there all day without having to deal with yet another self-important parasitic client, but in his line of work spare moments were few and far between, so he quickly grabbed onto the mouse and pulled the cursor across his computer screen. Clicking on a spreadsheet in the taskbar, he brought up his day’s schedule and ran his eyes across it with haste. What his eyes latched onto necessitated the grabbing of the small, yellow stress ball on his desk. 4:30: ARRANGE HOTEL FOR JASON (SAVANNAH). Fucking Jason. That jumped-up street urchin thug. Since his partnership agency, Potemkin, had taken Jason Natas on as a client Mr. Loftman had never seen the appeal of the Anti-Superstar. Rough, unkempt and with an attitude problem to make Russell Crowe seem like Hannah Montana, Jason had caused his organisation nothing but headaches with his overly stiff wrestling style and often illegal antics in his personal life. Where his partners, Najera and Jewel, were still holding onto the faint hope that the New Yorker might finally turn his career around and start bringing them some serious fiscal benefits, Loftman had completely given up hope on the vile perisher. But none of it would be so bad if it weren’t for that god damned poker game. Until last week, despite his general resentment, Loftman could quite easily have gotten on with his life, blagging his way through his minimal contact with Jason Natas, but that one night last week had really taken the biscuit. Not only had Jason taken Loftman’s money and his car, but he’d put a serious dent in Loftman’s pride. This swine, this prick had gotten the better of him. He’d made him look like a fool in his own game of poker, and the worst part? His smile as he dangled the keys to his new Lexus in front of Loftman’s face, now permanently etched in his psyche. And now he was supposed to book a hotel for the prick? Hunched over his table, Loftman held his head in his hands and quietly growled. He needed to get back at Jason for what he’d done, no doubt about it. The real question was just how the hell would he do it? Of course, he could just “forget” to book the hotel, that would piss him off. He’d have to stroll round late at night in an unfamiliar city that would no doubt be crammed to the rafters with PRIME in the house. Could prove pretty tricky to get a hotel on such short notice… No. What was he thinking? He couldn’t do that. Najera and Jewel would know exactly what he was up to, and the last thing he wanted to do was to get on the bad side of his business partners. He exhaled heavily and reluctantly grabbed the receiver. This one was going to take a bit of thought. * * * * * No sooner had Jason parked his mountainous form at the bus stop than the larger, mostly-empty double decker flew right past him with no intention of stopping. With a mixed bag of anger and despair, Jason watched helplessly as the vehicle disappeared around the corner at a dangerous velocity. He lacked a watch to confirm it, but surely that bus wasn’t due for another five minutes at least. No point in disputin’ it, Jason thought as he carefully examined the timetable attached to the shelter. The next number twenty-seven wasn’t for another thirty minutes, and he wasn’t in any mood for waiting, so he set off by foot. Climate-wise, Seattle wasn’t exactly the most exciting place in the world to be at any time of year, let alone early November. A cold chill had descended upon the city and Jason had to pull his hood up and jam his hands into his pockets just to keep himself from turning into a human popsicle. “Fuckin’ miserable place,” he muttered as generically aggressive hardcore punk rock belted through his headphones. While Jason Natas wasn’t exactly in any position to refer to anything as “miserable,” he certainly had a point. It was just gone five ‘o clock and it was getting dark already and a thick layer of grey fog hung above street level like a grave spirit. It wasn’t an entirely bad city, per se, but the shoddy weather and downtrodden grey buildings didn’t exactly help it’s image. Jason picked up the pace; if he stopped for too long the cold would get into his already sore muscles, making the journey back to the apartment all the more laborious. He walked purposefully and yet carefully so as not to slip on the layer of ground frost that was slowly accumulating on the pavement. Turning a corner, his steps slowed to a more hesitant pace. A gang of youths lingered just up ahead, lurking with a foreboding intent. Seattle wasn’t exactly a city known for its gang culture, but Jason knew to be careful all the same. As he drew nearer he could see that they were all dressed pretty much identically. Baggy jeans hung around the hips, garishly coloured sneakers and big, puffy parka jackets. A quaint army of clones. One of them sat on a BMX bike, gently rolling back and forth, while the other six or seven stood on the sidewalk, hanging around like a pack of hyenas. They wouldn’t pay any attention to him, right? He was nothing much to look at, after all, and with his piercings and tattoos he wasn’t exactly the most obvious target for a mugging… Jason pulled tightly on the cords around his hood and his world became a small, cloth-lined porthole as he lowered his head. Conversation seemed to cease as Jason approached. Shit. This wasn’t going well. Eyes turned to the Anti-Superstar as he increased the pace, shifting through the gang of extras from a 50 Cent video, almost knocking a couple of them off their feet in the process. Over the top of his music he heard a voice. “Hey, watch the fuck where you’re going, asshole!” Fuck. * * * * * “Are you sure you’re okay, honey? You’ve hardly touched your chicken.” Loftman sat idly at the dining table, pushing torn chunks of gravy-drenched chicken around his plate with the end of his fork. His hair dishevelled and the top few buttons of his work shut undone, he exuded none of the spunk and confidence you’d expect from one of the most successful sports agents in New York City. “What’s wrong? You’ve been acting like this all week,” his with, Cynthia, said in a soft, soothing tone of voice. She parked her petite frame beside him at the table, watching with concern as her husband disappeared further into his cocoon. Was he ignoring her? She placed her hand on his shoulder, gently massaging the joint. “Nathan?” “I’m fine, Cynth,” he sighed. “Seriously.” “Look at me,” she said firmly. Finally he found himself able to drag his attention away from the cold plate of food sitting before him. “Look, I can tell when you’re lying to me. Is it work?” Again he sighed. “Yeah. Kind of.” “I thought as much. Anything in particular I should know about… “Ehhh, not really. Nothing special.” Mr. Loftman was particularly convincing; his wife flashed him one of those “puh-lease!” looks. “Alright, it’s just this client we have. Ungrateful shit. It’s nothing though, I can deal with it.” “I seriously hope so, Nathan,” and again she reached out, rubbing her twig-like fingers into the side of his shoulder. She was genuinely concerned for her husband; it had been a long time since she’d seen him in such a negative frame of mind. Where was the confidence? Where was the panache? Whatever it was that was rattling Mr. Loftman’s cave, it must’ve been pretty bad. She didn’t want to probe too much, but she had a good idea where to start. “Wait, it’s not that guy that you ‘gave’ our car too, is it?”. Cynthia gave him a look of disapproval. Nathaniel had fobbed her off on that one and she knew it. Well, let’s face it, you wouldn’t exactly be keen to explain to your wife that you’d lost an expensive, top of the line Lexus in something as stupid as a game of poker, would you? “Yeah. Jason.” Nathan cocked his head back, swallowing a mouthful of water. “Nathaniel, what are you letting that guy inside your head for? You’re so much better than him. Seriously. He’s pondlife.” “But he too-“ “Never mind that,” she interrupted. “Look, he dented your pride, sure. But you’ve got to rise above it. Besides, surely it won’t be too long before the asshole manages to make a monumental fuck-up again, anyway…” For the first time in a long while, Nathaniel Loftman felt a smile creeping across his face. She was right; he was better than Jason Natas on every conceivable level. Or so he thought. “You’re right, honey. And when he does, there’s no way I’m letting him get away with it. Not this time. Not after he took our car and our cash. I’ll flatten him.” A warm sense of satisfaction consumed Loftman’s senses. What better way to get back at Jason Natas than to exploit one of his own faux-pas? He could not think of a sweeter form of revenge. Now all that was left to do was for Jason Natas to slip up. And he would slip up. He always did. * * * * * “I’m talkin’ t’you, bro! Don’t you walk away from me!” Jason tried his best to ignore the little punk, but he couldn’t blot him out. Reaching into his hood he pulled his earphones out as the sanctimonious little bastard jogged up to him, friends in tow. “Hey! You! Listen to me.” Huffing through his nose, Jason kept up the brisk pace, hoping the shake them off. At another time in another place he might just have knocked the kid’s head off his shoulders by now. Time to set that “good example”? “You don’t want none of this, trust me boy,” Jason mumbled coherently enough for the parka-wearing youth to hear. “’Ey yo! Who you callin’ ‘boy’?!” The look on the kid’s youthful face suddenly soured. He pushed both of his hands out, forcing them into Jason’s arm, momentarily knocking the Anti-Superstar off-balance. Feeling the heat of his blood suddenly increasing, Jason stopped and pulled his hood back. A collective mocking call of “ohhhhhhhh!” escaped the lips of the gathered mob as Jason stood firm, staring a hole through the kid. Tall and wiry, this guy was way out of his depth but he clearly didn’t realise it. Jason had seen his type before, all “thugged out” with baggy clothing and bullish bravado, as if they’d been plucked straight from a Los Angeles ghetto. And yet here they were. Seattle, Washington. Fucking posers. The boy had a certain level of mischief in his eyes. He seemed to be getting some kind of kick out of this, and, spurred on by the presence of his friends, he was all too keen to stick his nose in Jason’s business. “Suggest you be movin’ on swiftly. Don’t wanna go back to mommy all bloodied and bruise, do ya?” Jason balled a fist that he had no intention of using. He just wanted to scare the kid. Besides, if he did hit the guy his posse of morons would no doubt set upon him. “Fuck you gonna do, hmmm? Fuck you gonna do,” the kid pulled up his jacket to reveal a large, black hunk of metal protruding from the top of his jeans. “How ‘bout you gimme all your dough before I BLAST you, mo-fucka, hmmm? How you like that?” Things were getting tricky. The kid had a gun. What if he was bluffing? Shit, he couldn’t have been a day older than eighteen. He must be bluffing… With a shake of the head Jason turned to walk away. Click “What did I tell yo’ ass ‘bout walkin’ away from me, huh?!” The Anti-Superstar knew immediately that the barrel of the gun was pointed at his head, and he acted quickly. Throwing a forearm at the boy’s hand, Jason sent the gun scraping across the asphalt. Looks like those extra hours at the Silver Lining Academy were paying off after all. Instinctively he grabbed the boy by the throat and thrust his body against a locked shop door, suspending him a good few inches from the ground. As the writhing “gangsta” struggled to release Jason’s iron grip, the New Yorker clenched a fist and pulled his hand back, ready to squish the kid’s head between his knuckles and the door. A flash of blue and a quick siren burst, all of a sudden. Behind him two car doors opened. “FREEZE ASSHOLE!” The cops. Shit. “HANDS WHERE I CAN SEE ‘EM!” Seattle’s finest wasted little time in darting over to Jason, who released his grip on the boy and held his hands behind his back, a position he was all too familiar with. The familiarly cold sting as the cuffs snapped around his wrists filled him with disgust as the young mob stood by, laughing their heads off. Cheeks flush with red anger; Jason was pulled round by the overly aggressive police officer. “Bet you think you’re a real tough guy, huh? Picking on a helpless child,” Jason wanted to laugh at the words; this guy really didn’t have a clue what was going on. “Yeah, we’ll see how tough you are behind bars. Get the fucking in the car.” Lead towards the cruiser and hastily stuffed in the backseat, Jason watched as the crowd goaded him, mocked him, but refused to give them the satisfaction of seeing his rage. As the car sped away towards the station, Bobby Bosch’s round, acne-ravaged face popped into his head, puffing away on that cigarette. “See? What’d I tell ya?” the trainer asked. Setting a good example? Mightn’t have been such a bad idea after all.
