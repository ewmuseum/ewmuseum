Date: Timeless Pedestrian ordeals such as shopping were torments The Deville rarely suffered. With an entire staff dedicated solely to keeping his freezer and refrigerator stocked, a very select few people gracing his gift lists, and tailors making housecalls on the regular, his delicate olfactory was perpetually spared the onslaught of perfumes overzealous store owners pumped out to spur purchases, not to mention the pungent lack thereof that constituted public gathering. Even when he did go out to buy things for people, his journey took him to specialist boutiques and jewellers, not the vulgar rabble-grounds known as malls. However, two days removed from getting pinned by Brandon Youngblood on payperview, for a number one contender spot only he could have hoped to capitalize on, the general hubbub of the mall suited his need for anonymity. He didn’t realize where he was driving to until he pulled into the parking lot, but when he pushed through the old-fashioned manual doors, it made sense to him. He felt . . . common. A book store distracted him from where he was going as he stalked down the broad hallway, so he didn’t see the grungy man that bumped him until the jostling had already took place. Deville turned, startled and indignant, considering calling the man down. Riffraff had no business brushing up against him; he wasn’t feeling that common. Muttering about class, Deville turned back and continued his dejected trek. The mall was as unremarkable as he remembered it from his days of adolescence, when he and his friends occasionally hung out in the food court to hit on girls in their ridiculously obtuse way. Every third store was a shoddy clothiers, and as often as not they were flanked on either side by a bank or a two-bit jewellers. Sometimes both. Deville shook his head as he heard some reject haggling over a sixty-nine dollar watch, but his inner mockery of the pauper stopped short when his eyes fell on something in the window of the adjacent store. Intrigued, Angelo entered, barely restraining a cringe as the two on-duty employees perked up and began a race for his commission. Waving a dismissive hand before they could take two steps, Deville turned his back on them and inspected the item that had piqued his interest. The mostly-black leather motorcycle suit caressed the mannequin’s slight curves, but it wasn’t “her” boyish figure The Deville envisioned inside. The flames licking up the legs would certainly suit her, even if she wouldn’t admit it. Hell, she’d probably just roll her eyes and announce she’d sold her bike. Why I even . . . The tirade died prematurely as his eyes traced down the legs and noticed an infant-sized replica of the get-up and mannequin both. “Awww,” Deville cooed, bending down to get a better look, “how cute.” There’s no way she could argue with that. And when-- Bolting upright as if his spine had morphed into a shard of ice, Deville snapped his head back and forth, then fled in horror. Did I just fucking say “how cute”? . . . Did I just fucking coo? ~><>…<><~ The Deville didn’t know what had happened to him – what was happening to him, but one thing he was suddenly sure of was that he didn’t like it. Fuck the high road. Fuck piety. Fuck morality and benevolence with a three-foot-long barbed dildo. Somehow he’d convinced himself that they were making him stronger, but they weren’t. They’d turned him into a pandering pussy. He needed some time away from Karina, from relationships and love and the vulnerability that snuck in with them. The thoughts recently infecting his mind were unconscionable, the type of prancing nonsense that was going to get him hurt further, or perhaps measure him for a pine box. He needed to get back to his roots. For the next two months his manor would go untouched, unmolested by touch or sight or any human indiscretions. His final visit prior to venturing out in search of his cunning malevolence was to provide alternate tasks for all of the staff save the guards at the gate, pack a small fortune into a briefcase, and to resist the urge to take his cell phone with him, not to mention use it before he left. The temptation to make that call steeled his backbone for the necessity of his flight all the more. Never again would he report to someone his every thought and action. Never again would he be a slave to permissions and expectations. Never again would he be leashed as a dog. The only other thing disturbed by his visit was a large patch of soil in the far reaches of his backyard. The whole time he was gone, the hole remained open like a festering wound, the unfilled grave howling its despair over the departed dead. ~><>…<><~ It had been far too long since Angelo had enjoyed the calming rattling of the rail. Having travelled exclusively by plane and automobile for years, trusting only to his own devices, it struck him as a nice change of pace to break the monotony he’d been lulled into. Beyond that, he had no idea where he wanted to go, or what he was going to do once there. On the train he could simply ride until inspiration struck. His briefcase occupied the window-seat beside him, but sadly, while the passenger car wasn’t crowded, neither was it empty. There was a haughty debutante straight out of some ridiculous romance novel, a pudgy man in a business suit, a pair of young jocks in basketball jerseys, a needle of a man in a Sherlock Holmes cap, and some other guy it would be a waste to describe. Holmes had tried to spark a conversation with him earlier, but some cutting mockery about his hat lacking a propeller quelled that quickly enough. Having said it loud enough that everyone heard, and following it up with scornful laughter, he’d succeeded in alienating himself from the rest of the riders. The jocks had laughed and nudged one another, pointing at the hat, but it was the nervous sort, done only to soothe their machismo. No one would even look in his direction now. Smiling, Deville snapped open one of the complimentary newspapers and began scanning for stories of interest. The moment he found one, however, just to spite him, the train entered a tunnel, swathing the car in darkness. “For fuck’s sake,” Deville growled, dropping the paper and glaring overhead where lights should have been. “Knew I should have waited for another train. Cheap assholes. No wonder I’m surrounded by these hapless derelicts.” He heaved a sigh and waited less-than-patiently for the darkness to subside. Finally they emerged from the tunnel, and Deville reached for his paper. Just as his hands found the edge, the debutante let forth a piercing wail of purest fright, making Deville jump. His wits returned instantly, bringing ire along for the ride. “If you spotted a spider,” Deville began, rising, “I swear to Chri—” He cut off mid-sentence, mouth agape, as his eyes found the source of her horror. The man beneath description was slumped forward in his seat, the handle of a knife protruding from between his shoulder blades. Everyone stood and watched with ill-concealed curses as the bloodstain slowly encroached across the back of his shirt. Then they began to eye one another suspiciously. No one had been close to the man when they entered the tunnel, and everyone was still in their places when they came out the other side. There had been no sounds of struggle. Between Deville’s cursing and the woman’s scream, there had been no sounds save for those of the train itself. “Well,” Holmes said, plucking a pipe from the breast pocket of his plaid jacket and sticking it in his mouth, slurring his words as he continued, “isn’t this a quaint little pickle.” Rocking back and forth on the balls of his feet, he offered a thin smile around the pipe-stem. Everyone regarded him as if he was retarded. He affected not to notice. “You beasts!” cried the mademoiselle, clutching at her plump bosom. “Which of you did this foul deed?” Deville only shook his head and turned around, stalking to the door at his end of the car. A blessed string of vulgarity erupted at finding it locked. Ploughing through the other passengers, ignoring their protests of innocence, Deville tried the door at the other end. It too was locked. Holmes puffed his unlit pipe. “Fascinating,” he observed, then began pacing, obviously on the verge of offering his insight. A backhand from Deville pre-emptively solved that annoyance. “Alright,” Deville boomed, angrily surveying the panicked faces of all but Holmes. For his part, Holmes was glaring petulantly, muttering under his breath about how this was supposed to be his show. “Obviously” – a cutting glance for Holmes – “one of us is the killer, and even more obviously—” Another tunnel. “Oh for!” Silence reigned but for the woman’s blubbering, which made Deville strongly consider making sure she was the next to go. When they came out the other side, she screamed again, as did one of the jocks. The other jock was sprawled out on the floor, another knife planted in his chest. Thankfully, this knife had a note attached. While the others sobbed, save Holmes, Deville bent down and snatched the note, then rolled his eyes. “Welcome to Mystery Train~!” he read disparagingly. From out of nowhere, the notes of an organ sounded, three of them, each deeper than the last. Deville sighed and shook his head. “Christ, what a horrible joke.” Rather than repeat what they all knew, Deville scanned the survivors. The fat man was trembling like a nude Santa Claus, the debutante was crying and shaking so bad she couldn’t have cut a cheque, let alone brandish a knife, and the jock was black. Since it wasn’t murder by bullet, to Deville’s exceedingly racist mind, that ruled him out. “I’m looking at you, Holmes.” Holmes gaped. The pipe fell to the floor. He pointed to the fallen jock. “But he was my son!” Deville blinked. “Oh. Nevermind then.” Scanning again, Deville stopped on the fat man. “I’m looking at you, then, fat man!” Before the gelatinous blob could respond, Holmes, pipe recovered, interjected with a “Not so fast,” as if he was hurt at being discluded. “Him being my son doesn’t necessarily rule me out, as any good detective knows.” Deville sighed. “The way I see it,” Holmes went on, puffing, “all of us are suspects. No one is beyond suspicion. The trick is determining who has a motive, who—” Holmes cut off as darkness draped the car again. They weren’t in a tunnel, but the curtains had been pulled. Angelo counted to five in his head, then pulled the opposite string to open them all again. Everyone looked at him in horror. Well, everyone except the other jock, who had his throat cut. “Motherfucker,” Deville muttered. Holmes was flabbergasted. “What do you think—” The curtains swept shut once more. When Deville opened them this time, it was the young woman who had been made a victim. “Stop that!” Holmes yelled. “We need to figure this out! It’s what I live for! This is a huge puzzle that can only be pieced together with blind guessing, interrogation, and huge leaps of logic!” Deville sighed. Then pulled the shades. “I prefer elimination,” Deville whispered into the darkness. “That’s no way to get answers!” Holmes declared. “It takes--!” He cut off when Deville pulled the drapes again, and the fat man mourned missing his last meal. Holmes and Deville stared at one another in silence for a long moment. “But that means . . .” he trailed off as terror gripped him, backing away from The Deville, who was grinning and nodding. “What can I say?” Deville asked, then pulled out his piece and blew Holmes’ head off, obliterating the ridiculous hat. “Of course it was me.” Sirens and bells and buzzers polluted the air, making Deville cringe. The door at the opposite end of the car burst open to admit a spokesman-looking guy with a corduroy suit and a bleached white smile. Trailing him were two smiling bimbos in bikinis. “Congratulations!” the man bellowed enthusiastically, making his way toward Deville. “You solved the Mystery Train! You’ve now achieved the rank of full detective, and—” Too horrified to shout or protest, Deville turned the gun on himself and fired, blowing his own brain matter all across the walls. Some things were worse than death. … T3h D3v1ll3 jerked awake with a loud groan, then heaved a relieved sigh at discovering it had all been a dream. Eyeing the other passengers warily, Deville patted his chest to make sure his gun was there, just in case The Olsig Zone struck again, this time to infect his reality.
