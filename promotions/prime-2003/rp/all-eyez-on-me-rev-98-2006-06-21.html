Well, if you include scruffy (we prefer ‘rustic’), off-putting (please, ‘provocative’ is more appropriate), unwashed (that is a damn lie) professional athletes (yes, I’m done now) under the “Decorations” category, then welcome to the fuckin’ Champagne Room, baby. Nova lies on his back on the double-sized bed, not that it matters, arms folded behind his back. A slow-turning fan above his bed plays tricks with the shadows provided by the moonlight seeping through the slats in the Venetian blinds of the window by the corner. The light/fan combo makes a neat effect with the smoke drifting off a cigarette poking like a flagstaff out of his mouth. He takes a drag and sets it on the ash-tray next to his bed. He pours Maker’s into a shot glass and knocks it back before leaning back against the pillows fl00fed around his head. A small grin spreads across his face because shots of Maker’s rock face, but the goofy smile belies the seriousness of the thoughts coursing through his head. Serious thoughts, indeed, as Nova rolls back over towards the nightstand, grabs a handheld tape recorder in one hand, and the Maker’s in the other. He leans the bottle over, quickly filling a shot glass and spilling a little bourbon on the night table before turning the bottle upright. He knocks back the little vial of contentment, gives himself a shake, and grabs a cigarette before flopping back onto the bed with somewhat diminished grace. “Ugh,” he murmurs, feeling that twirk your stomach gets as your saliva glands are suddenly working overtime, “hoo boy.” He takes a drag and holds the recorder to his mouth. “Every…whew…thing seems to be in order for Monday.” Pause. Drunkenly yammering to yourself alone in a hotel room, and recording it might seem pathetic on the surface. But let’s examine further. We don’t always make the most brilliant decisions when we’re wasted. NOVA, AGE 16, 1996 “HEEWUUUUGGHH!!!” A much younger Nova attempts to hold his hair out of his face as he leans against the side of a house in a suburban neighborhood. A loud party “Chris, are you okay? Let me drive us home,” a girl standing behind him pleads. “Gimmeedakez,” Nova manages in a single expulsion of air before lurching around to dry-heave. “No, you’re trashed,” the girl replies, “please? I’m not giving you the keys.” “Uh-wait,” Nova says quickly with a grin, reaching into his pocket and removing a keychain, “gottanuddaset.” “Chris…” But Nova has already waddled to the car. He wrenches open the driver-side door and tumbles in. With his legs still dangling out the door on the gravel driveway, he starts the car, throws it accidentally into reverse, and mashes on the accelerator with his left hand. The car roars backward and slams into the front of another one. “CHRIS!” Four boys wearing varsity jerseys pile out of the offended car, a plume of ganja smoke emanating from within the confines of their automobile. “Oohhh,” Nova groans, still dangling half out of his own car, “oohhhh, shit.” With a bit of blood trickling down the side of his face, he reaches under the front seat and removes a black revolver. That, for instance, was a terrible decision. Even after they discovered that Nova was waving a cap gun (which, to his credit, took them at least a minute), they kicked the living shit out of him. I have to believe that alcohol played some role in that whole ridiculous debacle. But ’96 wasn’t his year in general. I DIGRESS! What I was talking about was alcohol and stupid decisions. I brought this up not to give myself the opportunity to tell another ‘When I Was Wasted’ story (well, maybe a little), but to use it as foil for the situation we’re playing witness to right now. THE ACTION THUS FAR FOR THOSE JUST TUNING IN (AND SHAME ON YOU FOLKS): Nova is drunk, alone in a hotel room, talking to himself and recording it on videotape. And to think you could be watching cartoons or counting BBs or doing really anything else more productive. Yes, alcohol makes us say and do stupid things…but there are those times when you’re well past a little warm in the face where you break the walls down and just say it like it is. Just put it out there, whatever “it” is. Well, “saying it like it is” implies truth. That’s not necessarily true. You certainly aren’t more prone to saying true things when plowed, but you are more inclined to say how you really feel about things, really interpret them. It’s a release, and a major part of the reason people get drunk in the first place. It’s like a good cry without acting like a big, sloppy pussy. You criers out there know what I’m talking about. In yo’ face! This is what Nova sets out to feel. As close to real as it comes. The problem about all these thoughts, feelings, etc. is that much like that life-changing acid trip, after its over one tends to forget exactly how it all went. Raise your hands if you’ve said or felt something while obliterated that you couldn’t specifically remember the next morning. It doesn’t have to be profound. He’ll settle for what he can get. That’s what I thought. I can’t see the coat rack in the back of the room. Put your hands down. Hence, why Nova has a sturdy grip on the recorder hovering above his mouth. And the shot of Maker’s draining into his gullet. Firewater, sang the Indian girl. “I’m all set for what I wanna do. I gotta…gonna go after Facey and take it to him. Take it to his house!” He chuckles a little to himself at the absurdity of the cliché and drags from the cigarette. “I-dunno why I even try to get involved-indese things. They…never end up too well for me. Nova’s got a point. Over the years, Nova has ended countless show nights face-down on the ground at the hands of his enemies, and he’s taken the high road (mostly) in fighting back, preferring to play out the beef in the ring. It hasn’t worked out very well for him. Almost every major feud has ended in Nova’s ultimate defeat. Rock Startling, quite possibly the worst asshole ever to be born out of the chemical combination of C-rate gangster movies and 150 CC’s of retarded, pinned Nova’s shoulders to the mat in a crushing, much-hyped defeat in the beginning of 2003. “Might gonna…wind up on the mat again. Li…Youngblood.” This time last summer, Nova was gearing up to face the fiercest challenge to his 5-Star Title yet…Brandon Youngblood. Warring over the summer, Youngblood spared no expense in underhanded tactics, and Nova waited (with saintly patience, if I don’t mind saying so) until the night of Colossus II and their showdown to unleash on “The Only Diamond in the Sport.” But the end of the match (the longest singles match in PRIME history) saw Youngblood’s hand raised. Failure again. Another shot now of Maker’s. “Maybe not worth it. Too much hassle.” Hassle had always been treated with a Zero Tolerance policy by the Rising Star…part of the reason that after almost two years, he still had few real friends-or enemies-on the roster. Too much hassle. But as much as Nova shrugged it off on ReV 98, mostly because of the bigger picture he was waiting to make painfully apparent to parties involved, Facey had gotten under his skin. He wanted to instill some humility in the little cocksmooch. Of course, there were ways to do this without all the trouble of stepping between the ropes… ...he could shoot Facey in the kneecaps. He could hang Facey on a hook and dust off his limbs with a chainsaw. He could sign Facey up to be PTC Competition Complaints Office supervisor. But Nova knows that despite his record of failure whenever he chases the “bad” guy, and all the times he’s been made the asshole, the victories are so freaking sweet that the CII’s don’t seem so towering. Please sir, may I have another…one of those Maker’s shots? Yes you can! “Got my…plan for Wenzdy. Got my ‘fessional life turnin’ ‘round, now a-gotta work on-mah perssnull life back.” Nova wakes up the next morning. As soon as he blinks, his eyes widen in pain before shutting, and he grips his head. The tape recorder is still in his hand. Nova hits <<REW and gropes for a glass of water. PLAY. “Got my ‘fessional life turnin’ ‘round, now a-gotta work on my perssnull life back.” Sometimes Nova hates listening to these tapes. Times like these. But he also knows that he’s put off doing anything about his situation with Ariel for long enough. Time to throw them bones, and that means ‘throwing dice’ in street-smart slang, not anything about people having carnal relations. Nighttime (the Right time!), Detroit, MI, some time later… Nova stands at the end of a very familiar driveway and takes a deep breath, a bouquet of red roses dangling by his left leg. His lips recall the 10,000 things he’s thought about saying when he saw his ex-wife again. They all seem horrifically stupid all of a sudden. Weird how you can be shy about seeing the woman you spent years with, and had a child with. He takes a last drag and flicks his cigarette away before beginning his approach to the house. “Hi, Ariel,” he mutters under his breath, “Hello, Ariel. How’s it…no. Hey, Ariel…Hello ther-” Nova comes to a sudden stop, his breath catching in his chest. Ariel comes into view in a front window, her back to the glass as she appears to be moving something…a piece of furniture? She stops, and moves to the side, revealing a man on the other side of the couch helping her. He smiles at her, and she crosses the divide and collapses into his arms, craning her neck up to kiss him. The roses fall silently and bounce off the bright green grass. Nova takes a step back and turns to run… …and wakes up, wincing from pain as soon as he blinks his eyes. The tape recorder is still in his hand. Nova hits <<REW and gropes for a glass of water. Nighttime (the real Right time!), Detroit, MI, two days later… The bouquet of roses shakes in Nova’s hand as he stands at the edge of the driveway to his old home. It’s been an eternity. His daughter seems completely different every time he manages to see her. There are cars in the driveway. She’s home, no doubt about that. Nova ponders for a moment if part of him wasn’t hoping she wouldn’t be. He gets close enough to see through the front windows, and instinctively closes his eyes. Please don’t be moving the couch. He opens his eyes…and no one is there in the window. He breathes a sigh of relief and continues walking. Confidence renewed, he jogs up the steps onto the porch and gives a friendly knock on the door. Pause. His hand relaxes. The roses fall silently and bounce off the bright green grass. Nova wakes up, wincing as his eyes blink, tightening the vice-grip of the booze’s consequences around his head. He rolls over and grabs the tape recorder, hits the <<REW and lets it play. “Got my ‘fessional life turnin’ ‘round, now a-gotta work on my perssnull life back.” He could go to Detroit. See Ariel face-to-face and hope that something happens. Or at least see his daughter in person. … Yeah. … Start with a phone call. Tomorrow. Baby steps.
