Date: various Date: 11/30/2005 10:43 PM Location: 248th Street, Queens, New York A man in a black trench coat runs up the stoop of a building directly in the middle of the street. A single light near the door reveals the light blue siding of the house as the man presses the doorbell directly beneath the light. You can hear the echo of the bell inside of the house, but nothing else. A couple of seconds pass and then a flurry of footsteps is heard as someone runs down the stairs. The footsteps get louder and louder until they stop and the door swings open from the inside. We see Tom Walczak, but we don’t see past him as he takes up the entire doorway. “Who the fuck are you? Do you know what time it is?” Walczak continues to stare at the man, who seems to be second guessing his decision to ring the doorbell. He swallows his breath, and straightens the glasses resting on the bridge of his nose. “Mr. Walczak, my name is James Rogers, and I work for immigration services. I am here with regards to your status in the country.” “What status?” “It’s actually something I believe that you already know about. You seem to have used illegal means to enter the country. All of your paperwork is forged, and you are going to have to leave the country.” “What the fuck are you talking about? I went to the goddamn US consulate to get my papers, and if you are saying that they messed up the paperwork than that’s a problem that your government made, and I am not going to be buried with that shit.” “Mr. Walczak, you can believe whatever you want to believe, but we have looked over your paperwork more than once, but the person who filled it out was never authorized to give you your permanent residency status.” “Okay so what you’re saying is that someone who worked for your government is at fault? So what I propose you do is take it up with him.” “I am not looking for a confrontation of any sort, but I think that you know that paperwork is bogus. We have a police escort coming to take you away, and all your property is going to be seized by the US government. You have five minutes to get some clothes and whatever else you think is necessary, but you will be taken away and extradited to Poland where your trial will take place.” Walczak clenches his fists and grits his teeth thinking what to do next. “Get the fuck off of my property or you aren’t going to like what happens next.” Walczak slams the door in the man’s face, and the man immediately gets on his cell phone. Within minutes lights brighten the darkened street, and sirens blast away the silence of the Queens’ street as the scene fades away. Date: 5/25/2007 9:56 PM Location: 118- 06 236th Street, Queens, New York Walczak sits in a corner of a nearly empty room with a half empty bottle of vodka in his hands. His lip is protruding, with some tobacco spilling out. He spits on the floor into a slowly growing puddle. His once clean cut goatee is no longer visible through a now thick beard, and his bald head is covered with unkempt patches of hair. He seems to be going in and out of consciousness as his head pops up instantly every time it slides to his shoulder. This position has become one of habit ever since Walczak came back to the United States. Walczak lifts up the bottle of vodka, and takes a huge gulp emptying half of the remaining vodka. Walczak gazes at the bottle and a drunken smile comes over his face. Ah, Krolewska Wodka, I have never had many friends in my life, but you are the one that has been there from the beginning. For nearly two years they had tried to keep us out of this country, but we are back. I will never forget the way in which I was treated by this country. Every night I think about it, I cannot stop. It consumes me and I will not rest until they realize the how great of an injustice that night actually was. Walczak once again takes a gulp of the vodka this time emptying the bottle. Walczak’s hand goes limp and the bottle rolls out and clanks into a pile of already empty ones. Walczak can no longer hold onto consciousness, and he rolls flat onto the floor almost immediately erupting into snoring. Date: 5/26/2007 10:14 AM Location: 118- 06 236th Street, Queens, New York The scene from last night remains nearly unchanged as Walczak lies on the ground. The room reeks of vodka, and light comes through the shades. Walczak begins to rummage on the floor, and emits a loud yawn. He sits up and shakes his head trying to get the cobwebs out. Walczak slowly gets to his feet, and takes out the tobacco from his mouth before making his way over to the kitchen. Opening up the cabinet he takes out a bottle of aspirin. He fills a glass with water as he pours some pills into his mouth. He downs the water and seems to be a new man. “I have got to stop doing this shit. What time is it?” Walczak looks up at the clock and notices that it is almost 10:30. A panicked look comes over his face as he rushes off towards the bathroom. He emerges from the bathroom fifteen minutes later putting a white t-shirt on. “Of all the fucking days to be late.” Walczak grabs his keys off of the kitchen counter and runs out of the door. He hops into his white 2003 Yukon Denali, and speeds off down the street. As he gets to the end of the street he turns on his radio blasting 36 Chambers by Wu-Tang Clan. Soothing music. This is the one respite that this country offers me. Walczak eases back into his seat as he makes his way towards Madison Avenue. He is heading towards the Polish Consulate waiting to meet with Krysztof Blachowicz in regards to his previous problems with the US Government. BEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEP!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!! Walczak’s hand slams on the horn as a Black Acura TL pulls out nearly slamming into Walczak’s Denali. He rolls down his window and tilts his enraged face outside. “DO YOU EVEN HAVE FUCKING EYES?” “Sorry, sorry, you were in my blind spot.” Walczak shakes his head as the car pulls away from him. That’s the problem with these American’s they are always in a fucking hurry. Every day that I spend in this country I run into inconsiderate douche bags like that. Even if he did see me coming he wouldn’t have stopped because these people only look out for themselves. Walczak shakes his head one more time before once again driving off. Date: 5/26/2007 11:35 AM Location: 233 Madison Ave, Polish Consulate An imposing brick building with large brown doors is Walczak’s destination. Walczak walks up the stairs and grabs one of the golden door handles swinging the door open. Inside a janitor buffs the already impeccably clean floor, and a receptionist sits behind a large wooden desk. “Hello, is there anything that I can help you with today?” “Yes, I am here to meet with Jaroslaw Toczek.” “Oh, you’re Mr. Walczak. Mr. Toczek is still in a conference call but you could make your way up to his office I’ll notify him that you’re waiting.” “Thank you.” Walczak walks up the spiral staircase in an unusually good mood. He sees an empty seat outside of the office, and makes his way over. He sees a Polish Flag hanging from the wall and a full blown smile appears on his face. I feel completely different inside of this place. It is as if I am back in my home country, and out of this god forsaken one. Even walking into this place the differences are seen immediately. The floor inside is spotless, and yet the janitor continued to work on it making it even better. I was greeted immediately, and got right to where I needed to be. I would never have received that kind of service in any government agencies run by Americans. “Mr. Walczak, you could come in now.” Walczak walks into Mr. Toczek’s office and his smile only grows larger. The room is a shrine to all that is Polish. Pictures of Lech Walesa, Jaroslaw Kaczynski, and Kasimir Pulaski hang behind his desk, and a picture of Pope John Paul II sits on the man’s desk. “Now, I understand that you wanted to find out exactly what went wrong with your papers the last time you were in this country.” “Yes, I went through all of the proper paperwork, and it took me months to get everything in order, but in the end I was still thrown out of the country.” “Well, all the paperwork that I have received seems to indicate that you had indeed falsified your paperwork.” “That is why I am here, because I know that I did everything correctly. I was already punished for coming into the country once, and I didn’t want it to happen again. Someone tampered with my paperwork, and I want to find out whom.” “It seems like you are grabbing at nothing here. It could have been a simple filing error, but I don’t think that someone would intentionally try to get you.” “You could try and find another explanation, but I don’t see any other way. I went to the American Consulate in Warsaw almost everyday for nearly four months. It was hell I had to interact with useless imbecile Americans on a daily basis, in my own country. I used to think of Poland as a place where everything in the world is right. It was a place where I had no problems, and for three months that was taken away from me.” “While I truly feel for you Mr. Walczak I think that your view of American’s is way to cynical.” Walczak’s smile quickly falls from his face, and a completely emotionless look takes over. Than Walczak catches a glimpse of something that takes him completely by surprise, sitting on a bookshelf is a picture of President George W. Bush, someone whom Walczak’s feelings for are less than caring. “Sorry for bringing this up, but is that a picture of President Bush sitting on your bookshelf?” “Oh, yes, yes it is. I know the man gets a lot of crap, but this is an extremely difficult country to run, and I for one do not envy his position.” “It just seems out of place in this office. You are surrounded by pictures of great men from the greatest country that man has known, and to put him in that company, well, it just seems wrong.” “You are over exaggerating just a little bit wouldn’t you say. Working in this country for as long as I have you gain a great appreciation for it.” “I have been in this country for quite some time as well, and my feelings have yet to change. I thought that I was going to get someone to help me who really understood what it was like to live in a foreign land. Listen, I don’t want to waist your time and I feel like I know where this meeting is heading. Give me a call if you make any headway, but I’m not expecting anything.” Walczak gets up from his chair not thanking the man with meeting with him, nor saying goodbye. Walczak’s exit is cold, and emotionless, a complete turnaround from his entrance just minutes ago. Date: 5/26/2007 2:42 PM Location: 130-13 McGuiness Blvd, Greenpoint, NY The predominantly Polish neighborhood is bustling with people. There are little children leaving school, butchers loading up vans with deliveries, and a variety of people just going about their everyday business. Walczak’s Denali is seen in the distance, and is slowly making its way down the busy street. It pulls into a relatively empty parking lot, next to a decrepit looking building. A sign hangs in front of the building saying; “Pod Zdzijah, Polska Jedalnia.” Coming out of the parking lot seconds later Walczak heads into the building. A rich aroma fills the air as an array of Polish foods is being cooked inside. “TOMEK! It has been quite a while since I have seen you.” An older lady welcomes Walczak as he enters the restaurant. “Ewa, you always greet me as if it has been years since you last saw me.” “In this case it has been, has it not?” Walczak realizes just how long he has actually been absent from the United States. He had just gotten back into the country, and this was in fact his first visit back into his once, favorite restaurant. “I had yet to realize that fact to be true. I guess you just make me feel every time I am here.” Ewa comes over to Walczak and rubs him on his head, and while being toched by others is not something that Walczak always appreciates he does nothing to stop her. “You seem to have let yourself go.” “What do you mean? I work out everyday, and take very good care of my body.” “Well, your hair is patchy and unwashed, and your have bags under your eyes.” “Ah, that’s just life for you. Life sucks, and it doesn’t seem like it’s gonna get any better.” “No use in complaining here. I’m here to take care of you, now what can I get you?” “Let me get a couple of kotlety, and a bowl of ziemiaki.” “Anything to drink Tomus?” “You didn’t forget the answer to that one did you Ewa?” “Two pints of Zywiec and keep them coming.” Walczak nods, and rests his hands on the table, waiting for his food. Good food, good drink, good people, this is what the world should be like. Yet, even coming back here reminds me just how numb I have become to everything. Two times I had been kicked out of this country, two times I was sent to prison, and twice I have been humiliated by this country. All I live for is to prove to the world that America is not what it is portrayed to be. Walczak sees his a plate being carried out towards him, steam comes off the plate as Walczak rubs his hands together in anticipation. Date: 5/26/2007 8:40 PM Location: 118- 06 236th Street, Queens, New York The faint ringing of keys is heard outside of the door. They clank into the door over and over again until finally Walczak stumbles through the door. Dzisiaj, w niedzielę, spotkamy się z Rysiem w kościele Ja trochę kasy mam, on brzuch jak Mobby Dick I on, i ja pójdziemy piwo pić Ja trochę kasy mam, on brzuch jak Mobby Dick I on, i ja pójdziemy piwo pić Potem wpadnie Ela-Ela zawsze jest, kiedy jest niedziela A z Elą narzeczony i jego koleś Krzyś I on, i ja pójdziemy piwo pić A z Elą narzeczony i jego koleś Krzyś I on, i ja pójdziemy piwo pić Potem wpadnie Janek-Janek zawsze ma szlugi i pianę I koleżków kilku ma-oni będą razem z nim I on, i ja pójdziemy razem w dym Walczak bellows out Bedziemy Piwo Pic, a beloved Polish Drinking song. The off rythym singing continues as he makes his way over to his refrigerator. He opens up the fridge, and reveals that it is filled with nothing but bottles of vodka. He pulls one out and unscrews the cap. “Oh….my…friend…it looks as if we will end this night… just as any other…”
