Date: May 13th, 2007 The town of Bear Creek, Alaska was a quiet town. It was so quiet it usually was left off most maps. With a populous just under 1,800, the people of Bear Creek had a way of life that was routine, orderly; a life with no adventure, no surprise. Not even the Bear Creek high school had any action unless it was athletics or girl’s locker room gossip. --- April 19th, 1996 Bear Creek, Alaska 1:15pm A group of three teachers at Bear Creek high School flocked around the glass doors peering into the school’s outer limits. They were not the only ones watching, however. Many students passing through the halls and in and out of the doors were keeping their eye on whatever was going on too. "He’s just been sitting out there?" Lisa Ross, a math teacher at Bear Creek High, stood next to two other women. "For how long?" Helen Witkowski asked, crossing her arms. She was the English Lit. teacher at Bear Creek "Since my second smoke break," scratched out one of the cafeteria monitors; the kids all call her Mrs. A and she hates to ‘get jiggy wid it.’ Approaching was John Peters, the assistant principal and lacrosse coach. He went unnoticed as he approached the crowd of female staff. "Wow, that’s a long time. When did all this happen?" Mrs. Witkowski asked, wishing to know everything about anything. "Don’t you check your emails Helen?" The sound of a whip crack was heard as the two met eyes then had a little ha-ha moment. "About two hours ago, that’s when they found-" "Let’s not make this out to be more than it is people. We’ve still got a job to do." Peters said as he fastened his tie, expressing that he was about to be in some pretty deep water. He gave the women all a once over, each visibly concerned and intrigued. He slid into his jacket, zipping it up as he looked at Miss Ross. "You’re going to go out there!?" Mrs. A screeched out again in her thick, raspy Brooklyn accent. She had a look on her face that only meant one thing. "Good luck, buddy." "Somebody has to do it." Peters turned to the door, scanning the situation even more carefully than he had done before. "Helen, if you see me turn around wave my arm ( big arm wave) like this, call the Police." "The Police? Do you think-" Mrs. Witkowski cut herself off in mid sentence, looking at the other two women and smirking a bit. She looked back at Mr. Peters, nodding in agreement and running a hand through her hair. "I will, Jack." "If it comes to that, is all I’m saying." John assured the woman, even though he’d settle for much, much less work than that. "Anything I can do?" Miss Ross, the slender twenty-eight years young brunette, asked of her boss. Peters had been staring out into the courtyard, pondering his next move. He heard Lisa Ross’ offer and met her eyes with his. "Back me up." John pushed forward on the glass door, Miss Ross right behind him, both heading toward the bright outdoors. On his way out something caught his eye. "Who are those guys?" --- April 19th, 1996 Bear Creek HS, Alaska 1:23pm Michael Munson was a large young man, built almost like a Black bear. His jaw was strong, his eyes were kind, and most noticeably; his frame was much larger than any high school student in history. He often had to produce birth certificate records for most of his sporting events, to verify his age. He was sitting in the front row of the bleachers. The sun beat down on his face as his hair blew in the cool, calm spring Alaskan breeze. His rich, brown eyes were intently gazing into the distance, and then he glanced down at Jimmy. "It’s ok, pal… you’re safe." Jimmy had been crying, which was why his eyes were bloodshot and glazed over, looking at Mike filled with fear. The left side of his face was bruised and scraped, swollen to the point his left eye was almost engulfed by his eye socket. His lip had been cut and the bridge of his nose gashed open, too. His shirt was soaked in blood, tears, and snot. As Jimmy’s eyes peered into Mike’s, almost as if they were having a conversation in ESP; a shadow fell over them both as Jimmy’s focus moved from Mike to directly behind him. Mike didn’t turn to check it out, however. He just looked forward. "How’s the nose Jimmy?" John Peters was standing next to Mike and Jimmy, blocking out the sun and checking out the welts left on Jimmy’s 16 year old mug. Jimmy touched it, wincing in pain from the sting and shook his head, trying to fight back from crying again. "It hurts. Bad." Jimmy almost choked on the pain, snot, and tears in the back of his throat. He coughed a bit, spitting out a blood rich loogie. "It’ll be sensitive for a little while, probably going to take a day or two to get the swelling down too." John flagged over Miss Ross. "Why don’t you let Miss Ross take you to the nurse? Get an ice pack and she’ll bandage that up for ya, before you head home." Jimmy was smart, nice, and the funniest person to be around. The only question he had was why anyone would do this to him? A question that needed answering. James Clark peered into his best friend’s eyes as he stood up. Mike did not acknowledge the fact Jimmy was looking at him, but nodded as his friend passed by his line of sight. "How a bout you, Big Bear? How are you holdin’ up?" John looked around behind him and then ahead, waiting for a response of some sort from Mike. Peters bent over, taking a seat next to the big kid, resting his elbows on his knees and looking around more. "They were looking for me, you know?" Mike still did not meet eyes with Peters. He was glaring ahead as his mind went awry. "They came here, slashed my tires, and were looking for me in the halls. They couldn’t find me…but they found Jimmy." "Mike, I know what you’re thinkin’ about right now. The first reaction is to get payback, am I right?" Peters folded his hands together, noticing that the idea had played Mike’s head a few times. "Well, I’m not your father but I am going to give you some advice; finding whoever did this to Jimmy and then beating those kids up won’t solve anything. It’ll just cause more trouble for you." Big Bear sat back, letting out a deep breath, and crossed his arms over his chest. "Jimmy’s hurt, yeah. But the only people around here with trouble, Mr. Peters, are those spineless chumps. That’s for damn sure." Peters leaned backward, taken aback by Big Bear’s words. He looked over his shoulder at Helen, Lisa, and Mrs. A standing in the doorway with Jimmy, who was being looked at by the nurse. He glanced back at Mike, still fuming with rage. "Listen to me, Michael…" John tried to paint a picture with his hands as he was talking to Mike. "Sitting here, being angry over all of this is exactly what they want from you. They want to terrorize you, and make you angry, wanting to do something rash and completely uncalled for. These are the losers of the world. The people who don’t even deserve a beating. These are the people who plague this world with their negativity, hate, and cruel intentions." Peters took another breath of hope. "They want you sitting here, feeling pissed off and angry. They feel they can control you that way." The Big Bear from Bear Creek broke his stare, turning his head slowly to meet his lacrosse coach’s. "Well Mr. Peters, I’m not going to just sit here." Peters’ eyes went wide. He stood up, interjecting himself into the path of Michael Munson. They met, eye to eye. Almost, Mike had some height on his own coach. "You’re going to go over there?" Peters was giving Mike a choice it seemed. Kill or be killed. Mike looked past John Peters. As he did so, the scene flipped viewpoints to find out what exactly was behind the camera. "Someone has to." Mike brushed past Mr. Peters, making his way down the bleacher staircase. In the middle of a now ruined and torn apart football field sat two cars and seven assholes. All were wearing their own red varsity jackets with ‘SEWARD’ stitched on the back and ‘SA’ on the left breast. Big Bear stood in front of them, eyeing each on up and down. They all were posing and making facial expressions to show their true douche bag nature. Big Bear was not impressed, not even a smidgen. The viewpoint switched again, back to in front of Mr. Peters. His eyes were watching intently on the spectacle in front of his eyes. His face moved with corresponding smacks and thuds of bodies crashing and smashing, even the sound of someone landing on a car hood. A thunderous crash came from the incident though, causing Peters to spin around and start waving his arm. "Hello, Sheriff Royer? This is Helen Witkowski at Bear Creek High. I think you should come out here." --- May 13th, 2007 Flight #421 Fort Worth to Little Rock 6:31pm "Take that bitcheeeeezzzz!" CRACK ‘Big Bear’ Munson Monsoon shot out of his chair, crashing his forehead straight into the ‘Call Flight Attendant’ button, and the rest of the plastic overhead area on JetBlue 747’s. His knees buckled as he drifted to his right, smashing into the aisle floor. Heading toward him was the flight attendant – and drink cart, connecting the corner of the beverage vessel with the button-imprinted section of his face. "G’damn wheel, be messin’ me up always!" SMASH. SMASH. SMASH. The obviously frustrated, yet young, flight attendant heaved and hoed the cart back and forth, trying to run over whatever was in her drink cart’s way. The woman sitting next to Big Bear, who was a bit freaked out from all of the racket, interjected herself. "Oh my- Maam! There’s-" "Maam!? Don’t you maam me, woman. If you think you can maam me just ‘cause to you, I’m just some peach-cobbler-makin, fish-or-chicken-servin’, coke-or-diet-coke-askin flight attendant – well let me tell you something, hussy-" The woman interrupted; "You’re killing a man with your drink cart." "It’s no biggy," Big Bear slurred through his swollen face. The smashing stopped as Big Bear popped his bruised head up from the ground. "No really, I’m okay." The woman blushed and helped Big Bear to his seat. The stewardess handed him a bunch of those travel bottles of Jack Daniels, Jim Beam, and Bailey’s Irish Cream and a giant bucket of ice. He grinned at her, who rolled her eyes and could be heard saying, "Coke or Diet Coke?" down the aisle. "Does that hurt?" The woman examined his face, pressing at certain points. By now, Big Bear had enough of this pin-the-pain-spot-on-the-Big-Bear nonsense. "how about here?" "What are you a doctor, lady!? I’m not some experiment!" Big Bear lifted his hands to grasp her wrists, but she slapped at his knuckles causing retreat. He looked at her in the ‘owww’ way, she poked at his face. "Actually, I am. Veronica Knowsalot. Nice to meet you…" Big Bear stared at her blankly. She paused. "Nice to meet, you… mister…" "Monsoon. Munson Monsoon. You can call me Big Bear." He grinned as her face went sour. "Well Big Bear, I think you might have a concussion. You might want to go to a hosp-" Big Bear’s hand was shutting her mouth for her, pinching her lips together and making her looked down as his mits cross-eyed. "That’s out of the question, Doc. I’ve got a big work week ahead of me." He thought to himself for a second, visibly. He thought about Tony Gamble. He thought about Chainz, too. Then he thought about Tracy. Mmmm Tracy… "BIG work week." "What do you do?" "Well, nothing yet. But I just started. This week, my first night on the job; I’ve got to prove myself to the world against two mean motherskunkers." "What line of work are you in, Mr. Big Bear?" "I take out the trash." "Excuse me?" "The trash. I take it out." She pinched her nose a bit, trying to interact with the total Neanderthal that Big Bear seemingly was. "I got that part. You're a trash man?" "Alost, baby. Almost. I work in a company full of deadbeats, self-righteous assholes, and people who would rather break your neck than break your fall. Partners turn on partners, friends turn on friends, and worst of all; they treat the fans like crap." "Okay, you’re too big to be a NASCAR driver, so where-" "PRIME." Big Bear watched her sit still. "Professional wrestling?" "Professional wrestling. It’s simple, really. I want to change the world, have a positive impact on people all around me." Dr. Knowsalot nodded as she listened to the Burly Beast from Bear Creek. "Sure, I could have been a cop. I could have even been a doctor, like you." She shook her head no. "the fact is, the real scum is polluting the airwaves that our children are watching, America’s youth. The world’s youthies! And I, for one, don’t want every little, itty-bitty PRIMEate to grow up thinking that FU is cool. And guess what? I get the opportunity my first night on duty, against Tony Gamble." She stared at him a bit; more interested in his version of whatever it is he is talking about. She nodded again, too. "Where is this?" "I get to go to Little Rock, Arkansas’ Alltell Pavilion for my first real demonstration, my first official shift as PRIME’s Big Bear. Not any Big Bear. Not any Bear, either. PRIME’s BIG BEAR." "Are you nervous?" The doctor asked, wiping a few splashes of saliva from her face. "Hah, lady," Big Bear exclaimed, tossing his arms up into the air. "The day I get nervous fighting scum like PRIME has to offer is the day you fail a blood test. This is what I’ve always wanted to do. It’s almost like my destiny to stand up for the little guys who can’t do anything, the kids who are too young to know, and the people who just get nauseous at the though of all the despicable cowards in the world. You’re a doctor, so you know you’re a hero..." Big Bear opened his mouth, but was cut off: "Ladies and gentlemen, we’ll be arriving in the Little Rock area in just a few moments. Please make sure…" As the loudspeaker still cackled and buzzed in the background, Munson glanced at the doctor who was still interested in something about Big Bear’s rant. "Well for 18,000 teeny, tiny PRIMEates, in a town called Little Rock; I’ll be the hero. At ReVolution 127, on Thursday night; I’ll be the hero. For years to come, followed by many more championships down the road; Big Bear will be the proven hero of PRIME." The doctor smiled a bit, feeling a bit inspired and flattered by this outburst from the stranger from Alaska. He leaned back, smiling to himself. He shut his eyes, thinking to himself about his debut at the Alltell Pavilion on Thursday night. This was the moment Mike Munson thought about after tearing apart 7 of the Seward Stallions’ starting defense. After all that violence, mayhem, and brutal rage; he thought about how he could use this as a gift. A gift he needed to hone, to learn. He wants everyone to know about it. He wants to world to change from it. How could Mike Munson change the world? One chump at a time. Perhaps two, on occasion.
