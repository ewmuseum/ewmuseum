Precio De Oportunidad: Uno

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Continuous Precio de Oportunidad - Uno PRECIO DE OPORTUNIDAD - UNO By Danny Ferguson EXT. LOS ANGELES - DAY There's a certain orange glow that Southern California takes on when you see it in the movies. The sun hangs in a certain way, and the clouds keep out of the way, avoiding that purple glow that Florida is better known for. SoCal is orange, and it's orange today, as we pan away from the beach to a beaten down urban street. A few passers-by move along the boardwalk, skating, jogging, whatever it is they do that makes them think they're improving their health. We all die, some people just stay in denial longer. There's one car on this street. There would be more, but I don't have the time or the budget to keep zooming in, so that's hitting the cutting room floor. One car. On another day, the El Camino would have been called gold, but a few years without upkeep on the paint job and the reddish-yellow tint of the entire scene make it more of a dull tan. We continue moving down from the California landscape and into this car. Before we get there, there two voices pierce the ambient noise. VOICE 1: We've been here for three hours. VOICE 2: And? VOICE 1: And we ain't seen a damn thing. VOICE 2: So we wait longer. As we get down to eye level with the car, the second man is finishing his sentence. He sits in the passenger seat, looking out into the ocean. Blonde and close cropped, he wears a blue Cuban shirt. The driver sits as high as his partner, but he fills out more of his seat. He slumps to his right slightly to get his shoulders to fit inside the car. The black t-shirt clings to his chest unhealthily. DRIVER: Killing me. PASSENGER: You need to get used to it. This isn't a job for someone with a short attention span. You were a freakin' SEAL, for Christ'ssake. DRIVER: And now I'm an ex-SEAL. Ex, as in, 'don't wanna fuckin' do it no more.' I don't get my rocks off stayin' still no more. PASSENGER: But you do get paid. DRIVER: OH, is that right? The passenger looks away from the water and makes eye contact. PASSENGER: Money isn't the only form of payment. (Quick, emotionless) Start the car. DRIVER: Oh, we leaving now? PASSENGER: The mark is at 9. He's leaving. Start the car. The big man sits back a little and calmly turns his head to look out his own window. About 50 yards away, a man in a black hooded sweatshirt and cargo shorts looks around uncomfortably before starting off in the opposite direction on foot. Back in the car, the men shuffle in their seats as the engine sputters to life. PASSENGER: Patience is supposed to be a virtue. DRIVER: I've never had a job that involved a lot of virtues. The Camino rolls out in stealthy pursuit. CUT TO: EXT. MEXICO CITY, MEXICO - EVENING Mexico looks very much like Los Angeles as the sun is soaked up into the horizon. An overhead shot of the city shows the streets all but barren. But something is a little different about our location. It's not 2005. This is the Mexico of several years ago. The voice is unlike any mystery voice you've ever heard before. It's not one of our other characters playing Sam Elliot. This is someone new entirely, someone that I can assure you, you will never meet. Thick and grizzled, he doesn't hide his Hispanic accent, despite the Americanized speech. VOICE There have been five men who have worn the El Spiffy mask since it first appeared in Mexico. The origin of the mask remains a mystery to all involved, but its legend has grown with each new host. The shot dips down and begins moving through the city streets as light is drained from Mexico City. The voice continues as the sound of crowd noise begins to build in the background. VOICE The mask has been passed from man to man with no connection between each other, and a with the Spiffy character changing at every step along the way. However, to know...to truly know the history of El Spiffy is to understand that something far greater is at hand. The camera-eye view comes to a stop just outside of a rather large building. 'LUCHAS SABADO' is scrawled on a placard that leans against the wall, along with several names. Midway down, '¡El Spiffy!' stands out in pink paint. CUT TO: INT. ARENA - NIGHT They are packed to the rafters inside the building, but nobody notices, because they're all looking at the ring. There, with a faint set of overhead lights keeping the squared circle aglow, a pair of men dual for the crowd. One, a monster of a man, stomps around the ring before climbing a turnbuckle and shouting 'RUDO!' to jeers and a smattering of garbage from the audience. The other, crouched behind him, gives the universal 'shhh' symbol, holding a single finger to his lips. Well, they're not really his lips. The mask on his face is very familiar to fans of El Spiffy. And by 'very familiar,' I mean it's the same damn one. But you probably guessed that by now. VOICE Like all things that we as a people deem popular, the mask is powered and fueled by a very strong evil. But those who have cheered it, who will it on to victory and spend their very life savings just to be close to it, that Evil is never known. As the large masked man turns back from the crowd, this older El Spiffy is waiting, and stands up straight, sticking his hand out and declaring something intelligible. The big man, bewildered, takes the hand, and El Spiffy shakes it vigorously. He then raises the rudo's hand to the crowd, who boos. Maintaining the foolish smile, Spiffy leads the man to the ropes and holds them open for him. The big man steps out onto the apron, basking in the jeers from the crowd, thinking his opponent has given up. El Spiffy sprints across the ring and hits the ropes. By the time the big man realizes the ruse, it's too late. He turns as Spiffy somersaults over the ropes, catching his head with a swinging hurricanrana that sends both of them crashing to the floor. The crowd jumps to their feet, and the scene is lost in a cacophony of shrill cheers and a mass of waving arms. VOICE The true impact of the mask is not felt the lights have been shut off and the doors to the arena are locked. The toll is taken behind the scenes, where each host has been left a broken shell of how he began. FADE TO: EXT. MEXICO CITY - NIGHT The crowd empties from the small arena, still buzzing from the night's events. Children linger around, waiting for their heroes to emerge and give autographs. Away from the bulk of the crowd, one child stands with his mother, wearing an El Spiffy t-shirt and looking awfully forlorn for someone who just watched his favorite luchador pick up another victory. CUT TO: INT. LOCKER ROOM - NIGHT El Spiffy sits on a rickety bench, head hanging above his knees. The other technicos, for lack of a shower, are collecting their things and preparing for a speedy exit. Unlike the rudos, who are free to de-mask and escape the awaiting crowd, the technicos are simply obligated to remain behind for the fans. Another, unmasked good-guy approaches El Spiffy and stands there, waiting for the man to look up. A few seconds pass before he finally does. TECHNICO You headed out soon? I should note that they're speaking in Spanish, but bothering with the subtitle thing would be a hassle on both of us, so this is a dubbed film. EL SPIFFY Yeah. Soon. TECHNICO Can't disappoint the fans, friend. You know most of them came to see you. EL SPIFFY I know...I know. He stands reluctantly and begins gathering his things, keeping an eye on his peers as he does so. He gives them a quick wave as they gather at the door, as if to say, 'I'll be there in a second.' The technicos, anticipating the dame kind of grandiose entrance the know Spiffy can provide, shuffle out to a faint eruption of applause and cheers from the crowd outside. As they go, Spiffy turns back to his bag and shoves everything he can into it. Without changing from his tights, he throws the bag over his shoulder and heads in the opposite direction, looking for a way out. VOICE The first shared trait of the El Spiffys is a coldness, a complete alienation from friends, family and anyone else who would find their love and care without reciprocation. CUT TO: EXT. MEXICO CITY - NIGHT The crowd begins to trickle away as the wrestlers disappear into the night, tailed by overzealous fans. The same boy still stands there, watching the door. SHOT: The boy's point of view. As he stares at the doorway, a bag flops to the ground just off to the side. Back at the shot of the boy and his mother, they slowly move around the building to see El Spiffy dangling from a window, cursing as he unhooks his tights from a stray nail. Finally, he pulls free and drops to his feet with the kind of agility normally reserved for the ring. He bends to pick up his bag, and the boy steps forward. BOY Daddy? Spiffy pauses in the crouched position, but never turns. It was unintentional freeze, the kind caused when the hair on your body stands at end. The kind you react with when the son you left behind calls out to you. After a moment, he stands back up and continues into the night, never turning to look back. The boy tries to follow him, but his mother holds him back, staring a hole in the back of the ever-popular luchador as he fades into the Mexico night. VOICE The first quality of the El Spiffy host is a coldness. Complete ambivalence and alienation from those who make the mistake of caring for you. Tears well up in the eyes of mother and son as we fade away. FADE TO: EXT. LOS ANGELES - LATER THAT DAY Across the city, Danny Ferguson steps into the sun and squints - an expression that doesn't change much from the usual sneer. He produces a pair of sunglasses from a breast pocket on his sportcoat. A voice calls out from behind him. REGGIE: You know it's awfully damn hot to be wearing a coat. DANNY: And you're awfully damn ignorant to have your priorities all out of whack in this town. REGGIE: Yeah, well, maybe something just doesn't line up between 'my priorities' and 'this town.' DANNY: (without turning) NEWSFLASH! Somebody call Variety! I've got a front page story for them! He smirks playfully as he walks, giving some idle waves to people he recognizes as he and the rest of Team Ferguson walk down the street. They're headed from lunch to a meeting with Danny's agent, Allan North. Danny's suddenly dynamic demeanor is as much for the attention of the ever-present L.A> Crowd as it is to make fun of Reggie Delray, his oft frustrated wrestling manager. DANNY: We'd better make sure that the put a big picture of you with that article, though, since most of the reading audience probably doesn't know who the hell you are. REGGIE: Yeah, well, wait until PRIME heads to Savannah. Then we'll see who gets asked for autographs. DANNY: Probably still me. REGGIE: Probably. But then after they do that, they'll ask for a picture with the great Reggie Delray. Looking around at the crowd uneasily, Reggie finally decides to share. REGGIE: Speaking of fans, we're being followed. Danny stops for a second and turns around. His bodyguards, Jim and Dametreyus, both pause as well. Danny doesn't remove his sunglasses, but looks just over Reggie's shoulder. DANNY: Is she hot? REGGIE: Not a she. Danny tries his best to be covert. He gestures for his manager, Vicki Siguchi (who has been here the entire time, but as usual, is inconsequential) to move towards him so she won't block the street. Since Vick is on the phone, as per usual, she doesn't notice the signal. Danny tries again, to no avail. Finally Jim Pibb grabs Vicki's arm and drags her out of the way. DANNY: Who? The car? In the distance, a worn out El Camino struggles to get into a parking spot. Both the driver and the passenger have their heads turned. REGGIE: Nah, in front of the car. The kid. A young-looking man in a black hoodie rests against a wall, blending in slightly with the locals. DANNY: Eh, no big deal. That's Hollywood. REGGIE: I don't think so. You don't spend a career working in the South and not pick up something about stalking fans. When people follow wrestlers, they want something. For girls, it's dick. For guys...it's pride. DANNY: (calmly) Hmm...ok, I've got a plan. REGGIE: Okay. DANNY: First, you never call me a wrestler again. I'm a fucking actor. I will also accept being called a star. Secondly, I'll let you tell your crazy road stories to Jim and Dam while I'm meeting with Allan, that way you can get it out of your system. Danny turns and continues walking, and the rest of the team follows closely. CUT TO: INT. SHOP - DAY It's still Mexico, but the decor and dress of the characters let us know that it's an older Mexico than we were previously watching. Posters on the wall remind shopgoers of the evil NAFTA agreement. A Menudo song is playing from a tinny tape player. We're not in a time warp, kids - it's the early 90s. A man in his 20s pushes through the handmade clothing in the store, looking for one thing specifically, and not finding it. His build is familiar, but we don't address the issue. SHOPKEEPER (O.S.) You find it? MAN Not yet... He digs through another rack and a box of clothes, throwing them into the air as the shopkeeper, an aging but jovial man, appears. Ducking the first few cloth projectiles, he catches a few more before grabbing the man's shoulder to stop him. MAN I swear it was here. I saw it that day. SHOPKEEPER And why wouldn't it be by the masks? Are you saying I don't keep an organized store? He turns to his side and tosses the stray clothing in his arms across the room, where it lands in a heap. Walking away, he beckons for the man to follow him. MAN That's the thing, though. I KNEW it wasn't there. That's how I noticed it! The shopkeeper waves his arm at a row of luchador masks that enter the frame as they walk over to that section. SHOPKEEPER If it's not here, I can't help you. He pokes at a mannequin head with the beginnings of a multicolored lycra mask hanging from it. The man, frustrated, searches the walls. MAN It's not here! SHOPKEEPER (sighing) And you can't find another one you like? MAN No! (he leans on the counter with the mannequin head and looks up at the keeper imploringly) I start my training tomorrow. I need this mask before I start! I need to find out who I am! The shopkeeper rolls his eyes and drops down to meet the gaze of his customer...er, prospective customer. SHOPKEEPER You kids, you have your priorities out of order. The mask doesn't make the man. It's the man that makes the mask. Create yourself, then you'll have a name. He walks away, and the young man spins around, resting against the counter. Then, his eyes widen, and he disappears offscreen. MAN (O.S.) Here it is! Amidst another mini-storm of apparel, he emerges, holding in his hand the red, yellow and green number that we've come to know as El Spiffy's trademark. SHOPKEEPER (concerned) Where'd you find that? MAN It was right here! It was here the whole time. SHOPKEEPER (grave) No it hasn't, son. I've never seen that mask before. He furrows his brow as the man approaches the counter, ecstatic. MAN Oh, come on! You probably just forgot about it. How much? SHOPKEEPER (still unnerved) Take it. It's yours. The man just smiles and produces a few bills, handing them to the man. MAN You're too generous for your own good, friend. I can't thank you enough for this. SHOPKEEPER (attempting to ease up) We shall see. We shall see. Good luck with your training. Before he can finish, the young man is bolting out the door. SHOPKEEPER Good luck. CUT TO: EXT. HOLLYWOOD - DAY. Team Ferguson is walking down the street, and we see them from the perspective of a car window. Reggie Delray looks uneasily over his shoulder as they keep going. The shot pans out as they continue down the sidewalk. DRIVER: Ok, here's what we're going to do. PASSENGER: Hit me. DRIVER: Later. But right now, you get out and I'll toss you a rope, then we can pull this damn thing in and dock it on the curb. A wide shot shows the El Camino hanging half-in, half-out of its parallel parking spot on the street. PASSENGER: I think that might call attention to us. DRIVER: It's a necessary evil. (looking behind his shoulder and hitting the gas)) Jesus, this thing must be 20 feet long. PASSENGER: Didn't- DRIVER: (cutting him off) I never had to parallel park any of the shit I drove in the service, so fuck you. The Camino lurches backwards and nearly takes the bumper off of the car behind it. The driver thumps the steering wheel. DRIVER: Fuck it, we're going to the garage. Where are our boys? The passenger looks out down the street. Team Ferguson is walking into a glass building, presumably the home of Allan's office. Several yards behind, the boy in the hoodie moves with the pedestrian traffic and glances up at the building. PASSENGER: Quattro just stepped into the office building. Cinco is mulling about outside, trying to blend in. DRIVER: Does he know we're here? PASSENGER: Yeah, he's not a dumb kid. He knows we're around, he just doesn't know where. DRIVER: Well, we'd better get moving, right? PASSENGER: We'd already be on foot if you could bring this fucking boat in to port. DRIVER: You know what? He doesn't get a chance to answer, because a sports car screeches its tires and clams into the front right corner of the Camino as it heaves out into the street. The force of impact causes the other car to spin out into the street and block the other cars, which were thankfully (and smartly) moving much slower than the sports car. Nearly everyone on the street turns to look, and the pedestrians all start to gather on the sidewalk, forming the usual peanut gallery of gawkers. DRIVER: Fuck! PASSENGER: We've got to move! DRIVER: Fuck! PASSENGER: Yeah, we covered that. Get your stuff and let's move. We don't need a positive ID. DRIVER: But what abou- PASSENGER: It's covered. Move! The passenger is already halfway out the door, pulling two things from his pocket. The first is a cell phone, which is's dialing one-handed. The other is a business card. The driver of the sports car opts to avoid any whiplash legislation and stands up out of his car. Before he can say anything, the passenger hands him the card. DRIVER: Call that number. Everything will be taken care of. Show it to the cop, too. The sports car guy looks at the card, confused, then looks back to watch the driver jogging to catch up with his partner. Both men push through the crowd of passers-by, who voice their concern about the potential hit-and-run situation. PASSENGER: (into phone) This is number 52112. I will need a new vehicle in about 15-20 minutes. As soon as possible. The situation is well in hand. 76933 just had an incident. Yeah, I gave them the line. He listens for a little longer and hangs up. His counterpart, 76933, looks annoyed. Both men keep walking towards the glass building across the street. 76933: An incident? 52112: It's for the report. I wasn't ratting on you. 76933: No, I see how it is. The first man stops, keeping the phone clenched in his fist. 76933 stops as well. 76933: I was just kidding, man, I.. (he looks ahead) Where's the kid? 52112: He knows how to pick his spots, apparently. The shot changes to just behind the two men, showing an empty sidewalk in front of them. CUT TO: INT. LOCKER ROOM - NIGHT It's several years after we last visited Mexico, but still before the turn of the millenia. The locker room is far more worn than the last time we saw it, but it's just as loud in the arena. All we can see through the faded curtain is a few glimmers of light and a crowd comparable to the first show. They chant as we fade in. CROWD Spif-fy! Spif-fy! Spif-fy! A crowd of wrestlers in the technico locker room flanks the curtain, waiting for someone to pass through. Finally, a small party gives the fabric the Red Sea treatment. It's the same El Spiffy we've seen in this chapter, recognizable only from build. His mask, face and chest are covered in blood. Some of it has rubbed off onto the pair of referees who are supporting him. He moans in pain as his right leg drags slightly behind. As Spiffy passes completely through the curtain, the crowd stops chanting, but a buzz remains. The wrestlers crowd around, but Spiffy does his best to shove them away, swearing and spitting blood. As the referees try to attend to them, he slams one of them into a locker, forcing the officials to give medical attention to someone else. Letting out a string of profanities, he lies back on the bench and groans again. The technicos disperse, preparing for another autograph session. No one checks with Spiffy, though, as they all know better by now. A healthy referee and one of the wrestlers converse quietly as they depart. REFEREE The ringside doctor said his knee was torn in two. He might not be able to walk right again. WRESTLER Good riddance. As they trickle out, only one of the young ring boys remains, forced to stay behind and clean the locker room when the wrestlers have left for the night. A few of them play around with him, pushing and shoving to laugh at him, as they go. He takes the abuse and stands firm, broom in hand, watching El Spiffy. Across the room, Spiffy tries to move his right leg and lets out an instinctive cry of pain. He falls back onto the bench as tears begin to mix with the blood on his face. Without thinking, he reaches back and begins to untie the mask. VOICE When the host is destroyed, when the mask has used it up and it is no longer of use, then the mask moves on. It wills itself to the next body. The cycle begins again. Sitting up again, and removing the mask in the process, Spiffy holds it in his hand. EL SPIFFY (to no one in particular) Three years. I haven't taken this off in three years. He looks up and sees the ring boy, who quickly looks away, not wanting to be caught seeing El Spiffy unmasked. On any other day, such a crime would have been unredeemable. EL SPIFFY You want to be a star, kid? The boy doesn't speak, and doesn't even react, because how do you react to that question? Instead, before he does a thing, the mask flies across the room and hits him in the chest. Dropping his broom and ignoring the blood, he catches it, and looks back at the man on the bench like he's Mean Joe Greene. MAN That mask is your ticket, if you want it. All you do is put it on, and it will do the rest. Giving an awkward glance, and displaying the same kind of face seen on his son years earlier, the man thinks about saying more...but the pain in his knee returns in full force, doing away with any of those thoughts. Without another word, the man lies back on the bench and thinks about what to do next. Quietly, El Spiffy slips out the door. FADE TO BLACK. TO BE CONTINUED...
