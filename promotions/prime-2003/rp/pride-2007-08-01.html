Pride

1 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/1/07 An early afternoon in Georgia for late July usually ends up being hot and sticky. But there are some ways to beat that. The wind whistled off the Atlantic and over the brown sand, then through the vegatation and up towards the white Victorian house with a wraparound porch. Out on the front, his massive frame draped over the front steps. Titan sat in the sunlight, black shirt and black fatigue pants in the bright sunlight, but not sweating at all. Compared to sitting in the Israeli desert with an M16 on the shoulder, sitting in a hot Georgia afternoon was downright pleasant to the massive man. Open across his lap was a small book. Titan leaned forward, but held the book forward so that he can read without any sweat that hasn't formed yet to drop on the pages. The sun lit the text plainly. Titan read his Bible everyday, just as he had since he was fifteen years old. And for the year, he opted to follow Billy Graham's advice: Read one Psalm today to learn how to relate to God and one Proverb a day to learn to deal with people. But Titan was re-reading a certain section that he had read more than a month ago. Proverbs 16:18 ”Pride goes before destruction, a haughty spirit before the fall.” Titan read it again. Then he read it again. And with a sigh, he took his Bible and set it beside him. His mind raced, repeating that verse over and over again. Most people know only the first part, and they know the King James version, as imperfect as that translation is. “The pride goeth before the fall.” Imperfect? Yes, when you go through two levels of translation instead of just one, as most modern translations are, it is more imperfect. But, in Titan's mind, he had to admit it sounded very poetic. Pride goeth before the fall He thought about it. Why would he be so focused on this term? Oh, he knew very well. He knew what he was doing when he marched out on national television a few weeks back and called Tchu out. Was he making a name for himself? No, his first run had done well enough of that. After all, being a singular man that very nearly won the tag straps by himself was a huge milestone already. He had thought, in his mind, that Tchu was too proud and needed to be taken down a few pegs. The problem was, he couldn't quite get past a nagging thought: was he about about to be taken down a few pegs instead of Tchu? There could be regarded a huge amount of arrogance. Huge in overconfidence of size, overconfidence of ability. With a sigh, he picked up his leather-bound Scripture and headed into the house. He carefully ducked his head to avoid cracking the door frame yet again and walked into the kitchen. He opened his refridgerator and bent forward, almost dropping to a knee. He reached in, taking out a two liter of ginger ale and setting it on the counter gently. He turned and opened up a cupboard and took out a full loaf of bread and let it drop, under its own gravity, to the counter. He reached into the same cupboard as delicately as possible and came out with a knife and a jar of peanut butter. With practiced slowness, he spread the extra crunchy substance on each slice and rebuilt the loaf upward. Once his towering stack of mashed Georgia crop was done, he took the two liter and the stack of sandwich and headed into the living room. Carefully, easily, he slid past the endtable and eased into a specially-made easy chair. It was the only thing in the room that would support his weight for a long period of time. He set down the sandwich stack on that same endtable he had so carefully skirted around and unscrewed the top off his bottle. Pride goeth before the fall Was he prideful? In a way, he was. Of course he was. He was one of the best in the IDF MP. He had done some things in PRIME no single man had ever done. But the giants of the world were not unbeatable. He reached back in his memory to another story he knew well. It was in 1 Samuel, chapter 17. The Israelites stood facing the Philistines when Goliath challenged the Israelite champion to fight him. And who was he defeated by? He was defeated by a child with a sling and five smooth river stones, then killed by his own sword. Is it just the same story transplanted centuries later into a wrestling ring? Is Tchu the proverbial David ready to defeat a giant that has laid out a challenge? If that was the case, he'd made a grave mistake, agreeing with those critics that say that PRIME's Wrecking Ball would be having his way with the Bumbling Giant. Their words, of course. Would the Lord deliver him to Tchu as so many others had fallen to this force? No, he didn't think so. He munched at a sandwich and took a long pull off the two liter. No, he didn't think that was quite it. Really, it was, in the big man's mind, more of the opposite. Tchu had been stalking up and down the lines looking for someone that can put him down hard. Sure, Nova had beaten him, but he hadn't put him down, done so unquestionably. So, in an odd twist, Titan was being the David of the story taking on the diminuitive giant of Tchu. The story had a twist of irony. If he was comparing it correctly. That was the trick of Scripture. Were you reading it correctly, interpreting it right? The big man took a big breath and reached for another sandwich, only to find the stack gone and the two liter empty. He tended to forget that he ate and drank fast when he was thinking. With a sigh, he leaned back in the chair. “Would that be a post-snack nap, I see you preparing for?” Titan's hand dropped and immediately snagged the pistol he kept tucked next to the cushion in the seat. The large, .50 caliber Desert Eagle stood still, pointed at the small man's chest. The man gave Titan a blank look, just like always, and helped himself to a seat on the couch. “You changed the locks. I don't know why.” “Figured it'd give you a little more of a challenge.” “Locks? Please. Besides, it's not as if you aren't ready. Every time I show up, you're ready to put a hole in me with that portable cannon of yours.” “Old habits die hard. What do you want, 21?” The smaller man gave Titan that same blank look again. Betray nothing, he had said, nothing verbally, physically or emotionally. Then, your subject will start to doubt he's even talking to a human. Problem is, that doesn't work so well when both sides knew the trick. Titan put the pistol back and leaned forward in his recliner. “Come on, what is it?” “I thought you might like some company.” “Don't lie to me. You're terrible at it.” “Titan, it's irritating that you're saying things like that.” “That I'm saying it or picking up on it?” “Indeed.” The one word response said volumes out of the man. Profile 21, as Titan had always known him, betrayed nothing if at all possible. The little man was right out of a cheesy spy novel or a James Bond movie. He had been Titan's instructor in interrogation techniques and was a master at gathering information. And breaking and entering. “Why would I need company, 21?” Titan said, dropping his elbows on his knees. The smaller man flashed his eyes towards the Bible lying on the kitchen counter. “You seem to be processing something. Might some outside input be helpful?” “You must be absolutely bored,” Titan responded, sitting back. “Work has been slow, lately,” came the reply. “To answer your question, yes.” Titan sighed. Might as well tell 21, since he was here and had deduced as much. “Proverbs 16:18.” His statement was met with a flat stare. So, the little man didn't know Scripture. It was always nice to see 21 come up short somewhere. “The phrase you'll know it by is 'The pride goeth before the fall.'” The other man nodded slowly. “I'm not sure why that would be bothering you.” “Which one of us is the proud, 21?” The other man altered his expression a hair, barely lifting an eyebrow. “Between myself and Tchu,” Titan went on. “Was I proud for challenging him, or is he prideful? I'm not sure which.” “I think there's a difference between pride and confidence, so the parable in question doesn't really apply to either of you.” “It's a proverb, not a parable.” “Fine,” 21 said, waving a hand dismissively. “Regardless, Tchu knows how good he is. He has a list of proof. You know how good you are. And you don't believe he has what it takes to beat you. I think you saw an opportunity, not any pride going on.” Titan chewed the inside of his lip. Pride or confidence? Which was truth and which was his head being played with? “That means I've got some work to do,” Titan said finally. “Indeed,” 21 replied. “Video study?” “That's the best place to start.”
