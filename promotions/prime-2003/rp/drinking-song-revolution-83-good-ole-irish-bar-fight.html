Drinking Song (ReVolution 83 - Good Ole Irish Bar Fight)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Timeless It was many and many a year ago, In a kingdom atop a hill, That a man was born whom you may know By the name of Angelo Deville; And this man he lived with no other thought Than to seek out his foes and kill. He was a child like no other child, In this kingdom atop the hill; He smiled a smile that wasn’t a smile, This devilish Angelo Deville; He stabbed with a tongue as forked as his road Just for a taste of the thrill. Blood in a flood flowed downward like wine, From this kingdom atop the hill, But stained most of all were the hands of this man, This monstrous Angelo Deville; Wherever his eyes fell death made a shrine, Ordered by sword or by quill. He fought like the Irish that tainted his blood, The Italian side gave him his will, And wherever there reared an advantage to lever, In this kingdom atop the hill, The smart money said that the strings were entwined With the whim of Angelo Deville. And his reign overcame any threatening dissension, In this kingdom atop the hill; In the ring or a bar or a chicken coop, It mattered not to Angelo Deville; Drunkards, faggots, or C-movie stars, He’d bludgeon until they were still. For his skill it was stronger by far than the skill Of those who were older than he--Of any far larger than he-- And neither abdicated kings from federations of old, Nor crownless princes climbing the hill, Can ever dissever the gilt from the throne Of the incomparable Angelo Deville. For the moon never beams without crippling dreams Of the unconscionable Angelo Deville; And the stars never rise without raising their eyes To the unconscionable Angelo Deville; And so, all the while, he lords with a smile, The corpses, unknowing, he has yet to defile, In his kingdom atop the hill, From his throne far above the hill. -- Inspiration from Poe's 'Annabel Lee', perhaps my favourite poem of all time.
