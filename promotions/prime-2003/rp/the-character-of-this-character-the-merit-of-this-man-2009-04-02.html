The Character Of This Character, The Merit Of This Man

2 April 2009

Date: 4/2/09 "Sometimes being a friend means mastering the art of timing. There is a time for silence. A time to let go and allow people to hurl themselves into their own destiny. And a time to prepare to pick up the pieces when it's all over." -Gloria Naylor July 7th, 2007 7:14 P.M. "Whaddya mean ‘not a heel’ anymore?" Over dinner, Danny Ferguson and I were discussing the intricacies of my comeback. Away tending to my business for the last eight months, he’d lured me back in. I couldn’t say no to reforming the A-List, couldn’t say no to a surprise return, couldn’t say no to the itch. "Chan, it’s 2007. While wrestling ain’t exactly high art, it’s evolving. It’s not enough anymore to just have bad guys and good guys. People want shades of gray. Sure, they still want to root for one side over the other, but they don’t need us to be perfect anymore. Hulk Hogan isn’t walking through that door." "I….don’t get it. Was I not good enough during our heel run or something? Listen, I don’t mind going face, just don’t make me do this tweener bullshit. Those guys never sell jack shit for merchandise and they’re kept out of title programs. It’s career suicide, Dan." Stuffing a bit of lobster into his mouth, Ferguson shook his head vehemently. "Alright, let’s slow it down, even though, you might have to turn in your Asian badge for not understanding this. It’s simple. Did you like Babel?" "Fuck no." "What about United 93?" "Nope. And before you give me some patriotic bullshit, it was just too depressing." "Children of Men?" "No, what does this have to do with anything?" "Fine, Ebert, why don’t you tell me a movie you DID enjoy from the last year." "Uhh…Casino Royale?" "Borderline gay." "What?! Bond is like the anti-gay." "Yeah, but when I imagine you watching a Bond movie, you’re just drooling over Daniel Craig. It’s creepy." "Don’t you think it might be creepier that you imagine me drooling over another man?" "Back to my original point. You know why Casino Royale was awesome?" "Yeah, because–" "Wrong. It was awesome because it was real. Bond gets hurt by women, he makes stupid smack-myself-in-the-forehead decisions. He isn’t just a sexual deviant who needs to get his gun off anymore." "Wait…isn’t that the basis of the whole–" "Nope. Not anymore. In 2007, people want real. They don’t want familiar characters in even more familiar scripts. They want to see people. They want quirks and neuroses and fears." It took me a moment to remember what we were talking about. I’d always been under the impression that I was good for this job because of a silver tongue and a slammin’ bod. If you got any farther past those two things, you’d be in danger of seeing all the inner ugliness of Chandler Tsonda. "I love this reality kick that you’re on, bro, but even reality TV can’t handle people’s actual personalities. That’s why it’s all scripted." Danny and I always had a tendency to drift towards the inane, probably because there wasn’t anything real in our lives to talk about. It was either argue about pop culture, yell at Facey for making the limo stink like hydro, or quietly sit with our own colossal insecurities. Is that even a friendship? "People love sports even more now than they did fifty years ago? Why? Because Kobe can get caught with Little Kobe trying to ‘make a little backdoor cut and go to the hole hard’ and we get to watch Mrs. Airhead Bryant rock that apology ring and sit next to him in press conferences, even though we can see in her eyes that she’s one more late-night rendezvous with a team groupie away from going Lorena Bobbit on his ass." Danny had to take a breath after that soliloquy of an answer. "You don’t think maybe TV or the internet just makes it easier to get access to the product? Oh, ya say, just like how it gives wrestling fans the ability to bitch 24/7 anywhere on the globe and know that some other unimportant cyber barnacle will agree?" The smarks had been rough on our first run as the A-List, endlessly bitching about our airtime, about our holding several titles at once, about how it didn’t make sense to have three upper mid-carders in the same stable. I’d never paid a lick of attention to it, but I’d been soured on the whole "information age" aspect of wrestling. "It’s human drama, bitch. People eat it up." "So you’re saying that when you came back from being locked up in China, you just channeled your actual personality on TV, and people bought it?" "By the truckload, Sally." "What if Chandler Tsonda The Real Person isn’t as good a product as Chandler Tsonda The Character? I liked playing an asshole. It felt natural." "That’s because you weren’t acting." "Hey, I can still say no to your stupid ‘come back and beat the hell out of Nova’ plan." "I’m just saying," he replied, wholly unapologetic, "that if you go out there as yourself, it’s liberating. You get to do what you want. If you wanna call Nova an overgrown turd, you can. But you don’t have to sacrifice a good relationship with Lindsay Troy just because he’s a smelly douche." "I thought the A-List didn’t need allies. Three of us could take down anybody. "Chan," he said, with somewhat mocking laughter, "do you really think that three people can take on an entire federation and win?" "Sure, if they had each others’ backs and–" "Either it would tear them apart or they’d all get to share in the joyous experience of losing their minds together." I quietly pondered my next question. This idea, to actually be Chandler Tsonda on TV, it seemed gutsy. It seemed destined to fail, given how well I knew myself. "So if someone beats the living hell out of me when I’m playing myself or being myself or whatever…how am I not supposed to take that as a personal affront?" "Sure, it makes business personal." Weirdly, though, that was the clincher. My competitive streak had always been balanced out by distancing myself from the job, saying that it was about the fame, not the actual win-loss record. But I began to see that truly investing myself into PRIME would force me to confront that competitive fire. I wanted to hear people call me the best, and that would only happen if I gave myself to the company. "Dan, this isn’t one of those decisions you can unmake." "Someday, you’ll thank me for this." /~/~/~/~/ February 13th, 2009 5:51 A.M. Sunlight trickled over my closed eyelids. I felt the warmth of an entirely too early sunrise–where was I? I fumbled for memories. I remembered showing up for ReV 187. Seeing Dawkins. Rayne. Something was wrong. It wasn’t just a fuzzy recollection, it was a full-on frat boy-style blackout, a wormhole through my memory. I opened my eyes…except they didn’t open. "Nurse!" My ears pulled in scattered and distant sounds that I vaguely recognized as voices. Couldn’t recognize them, not in my hazy tunnel of sleep. I knew I wasn’t alone, though. That was helpful Finally pushing open my lead eyelids, I had to squint in the face of dawn’s illumination. Salmon washed against bright red, as the sun slowly awoke. After six years on and off in the wrestling biz, you recognize the feeling of an eye that’s swollen shut–my left one was. The right would open, but only halfway, giving me an irritating half-picture of the scene around me. I had to see the tubes in my arms to believe them, since I sure as a motherfucker didn’t feel them. A hospital bed? I hadn’t spent a night in a bona fide hospital since…Jesus, when was it, 2006? "Nurse, I think he’s wakin’ up! You hear me, bruh?" While it took away nothing from this moment, Bryan Dawkins was the type of dude you half-expected to be sitting there at your hospital bed. "Where…what…uhhh…hey, kid." Seeing his smile extend ear-to-ear like that made me think that something horrible had happened, as if he was all the more thrilled because he hadn’t expected to see me open my eyes so soon. Car crash? No, I was in the arena. Seizure? Didn’t make any sense. Slipped and fell? Not the type of glamorous injury that I was used to. "Mornin’, bruh. And what an early one it is, eh?" He turned his head towards the meticulous rising sun, inching up the horizon with each passing minute. "You been out since Wednesday night, ya know. And havin’ spent nearly twenty-four hours in this damn chair, I can tell ya, bruh, I’m glad you’re awake." "I appreciate the gesture, Dawk, but I don’t need a…uhhh…" The word was gone. A mid-sentence blackout. I looked down at the tubes in my arm. "A babysitter?" "Yeah." "Little fuzzy, aren’tcha, bruh?" "It’s just too goddamn early, that’s all." "No, Chan" he responded, voice no louder in volume but now strong as granite. He wasn’t smiling anymore. "You were just in a coma, bruh. We didn’t know…" Dawkins trailed off, looking back to the sunrise. Why had one of my best friends expected the worst? Doubt now slowly began to churn through my veins. I couldn’t feel anything–had to be painkillers, otherwise my swollen face alone would’ve been enough to have me moaning–except for the nauseous uncertainty rising in my gut. "Kid, I’ve been through worse. I’m a tough cookie." "Bruh, the nurse said you probably wouldn’t ever remember what happened to you on Wednesday night." "That’s probably why she never made it as a real doctor," I shot back, gruffly. There’s a certain type of cranky when you wake up from a nap too soon or get too little sleep. Now imagine that, except your body’s been accustomed to being at rest for the past twenty-four hours. I felt off, and my mood reflected that only too well. "I remember just fine." "Ya do?" "Mind like a steel trap, kid. I remember rolling into ReVolution with you and Ty. We cut a promo so hot Matt Mills got a sunburn. I kicked it backstage, since I had the night off. Ty dipped out for a second to shoot the shit with Phoenix. Meanwhile, I–" "Was biding your time while you frantically tried to remember what actually happened?" The look on Rayne’s face was stern, like a man who’d taken plenty of lumps in the recent past. "He doesn’t remember a goddamn thing. Not about the mystery asshole who fucked ‘im up, not about passing out underneath every piece of furniture in that locker room, not even about what it felt like," Rayne offered, voice as sharp and cold as an icicle. "You’re a lucky gal, Tink. Nobody wants to remember a beating like that." "What…" I opened my mouth before thinking, and every question I had rushed to my head, increasing my nausea. "…did Tyler do?" In the real world, you get to call the cops. Maybe they fuck around and snag themselves some overtime in the process, but if you get beat this badly…they find the motherfucker. I was certain my employer would be ripping the roster apart to get to the bottom of this. Dawkins went to open his mouth, but he turned back to Rayne. Ty folded his arms across his chest, shaking his head slightly. "C’mon, kid," Rayne urged with a hand gesture, "tell Tink. Tell him what the fabulous new boss did." "Chan, you gotta understand that I’ve been here since you have, and Ty wasn’t far behind. We don’t know if she might have–" "Nothing, Chan. The show went off without a hiccup. After her Universal Champion left in a stretcher, unconscious, that skank was more worried about giving Country some petty fine for the truck stunt than she was about you." It hit me hard. I hadn’t busted my ass for many things in life. A couple women, parts of my modeling career, the start-up of Tsuperstar Enterprises, and PRIME. That was it. And now, the company that I’d happily spilt blood for, couldn’t be bothered to do so much as send a suit to check up on me. No, not the company. Her. "Can’t go gettin’ worked up about this, bruh. Nurse said you being able to ever step back into a ring is all mental, and if you try to do any kind of physical activity in the state you’re in…" Dawkins lowered his head, and I felt my stomach curdle as it dropped. "You tell him, Ty." "Nurse said that ol’ fancy doctor said that if you don’t rest up and let this shit fully recover, you might never wrestle again. Said whatever shit’s been wrong with your back–and fuck you, by the way, for being too much of a stubborn jackass to tell us your back was this bad–was made worse by this." "But they’re real hopeful, bruh. Tests this week should come back negative, which means you might be back in the ring for UltraViolence. That’s good news, right?" "I’m coming back for Culture Shock," I announced. It wasn’t a matter of medicine or pain or anything else. It was a matter of getting back in my ring. "You don’t take it slow, you’ll throw your entire career away, even if you can come back that quick. Tink, nobody doubts that, behind your hot pink fingernail polish and you’re my Little Pony t-shirts, you can take punishment. But if you come back from this too early, you will fuck yourself up." "And, bruh, I don’t want Nick havin’ a godfather who’s a gimp at forty. Just be reasonable, Chan." "You two know that if you were in my position, you’d do the exact same thing. You’d kill yourself to get back out there. And then you’d find the fuck who did this. No matter what." Dawkins simultaneously nodded and shrugged, and when Ty didn’t meet my gaze, I knew he agreed. "Somebody took a shot at me, at my career. When I get unhooked from these tubes–and you damn well better believe that’s a "when not if" scenario–I’m gonna raise seven levels of hell until I find who did this." "Can’t tell ya not to, bruh. There’s some bastard out there waiting to get his pineapple knocked straight off." Still not making eye contact with either of us, Rayne spoke up, in that quiet way where it was clear that he’d been thinking, not just spouting off the top of his head. "You’re not gonna make too many more friends shakin’ down the roster to find out who did this." Rayne had a doctorate in revenge, I was sure. As easy as it was for him to make enemies, I expected that he and vengeance were bosom buddies by now. "Doesn’t mean you shouldn’t do it, though. Count me in for a straightjacket and the padded room next to you, Tink." On a normal day, this would’ve been the cue for that grin, but Ty’s mind was elsewhere. "Bruh, you rest up, alright? And don’t go givin’ these nurses too tough a time. I gotta fly home to see Nick." I could barely lift my fist to knock it against Dawkins’s. "Ty," he called, moving towards the door, "you coming?" "Easy on the gas pedal, kid. I’ll be there in a second." As Dawkins exited the room, Rayne took one step towards the bed, and still didn’t seem that close to me. "Getting what’s yours, Tink. There’s a price to that." "A price I’m willing to pay." "And if you find out that you gotta compromise, gotta throw the good guy act in your back pocket, to get the revenge you’re looking for?" "I’ve dealt with the devil before. I know he drives a mean bargain." "All I’m sayin’…" He paused, took a breath. "…s’at the devil never offered a deal that he wouldn’t come back to collect on. Don’t be surprised if you wake up one morning and you’ve lost more than you thought you were putting on the table."
