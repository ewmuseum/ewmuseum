Tattoos & Bad Knees

1 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/1/07 Morning “Jesus Christ!” Showcasing some impressive reflexes, Scott tilted back a bit and jerked his head to the right, letting the hammer soar passed his ear canal and slam into the free-standing shelves on the far side of the garage. Several items crashed to the concrete floor as the middle shelf busted loose, courtesy of a direct hit. “You just threw a hammer at my head!” “I missed.” “Because of my awesome speed and agility.” “I’ve got another hammer, needle-nose pliers, two screwdrivers, and sixteen wrenches in various sizes. Chances are one in twenty hit.” “And your shelves get ruined.” “I can afford more.” Matt wiped a bit of engine oil off his thumb with an old rag, then tossed it into the mess that was his toolbox. “Why the hell did you just chuck a hammer at me?” “Natural reflex any time you ask for a favor.” “That’s not fair, bro. You didn’t even hear what my favor was.” “I’m guessing you want me to lend you money.” “Well… yeah… but its way more noble and stuff than it sounds.” Scotty scratched the back of his head and leaned against the wall. “I mean, it’s for good reason, ya know?” “It’d be a first.” Matt slammed the top drawer to his toolbox closed and folded his arms across his chest, standard procedure for the battle of attrition he was likely about to wage. In all the years he could remember, his little brother had never asked for money unless it was to pay off gambling debts… or to help rack up new ones. “Ok, let’s hear it.” “I need some cash to help with rehab.” “Rehab?” “A good place. That’s why I need the help… it’s a bit pricey. But I want to get my life on track, and it’s pretty clear I’m not going to be able to just turn this shit around on my own. So I’m finally gonna get some help. Professional. I mean… a couple months ago, when I went double or nothing on a big debt by guaranteeing you’d defend your IG championship… that was sorta the peak of the mountain.” “Yeah, I coulda killed you for that. Fifteen thousand dollars? And dragging me and my career in the middle of it… what the hell were you thinking?” “I wasn’t. Like I said… lucked out that you won and I got off the hook, but it was a real wake up call.” “I see.” “And…?” Scott laughed and pushed himself away from the wall. “I mean, call me crazy, but I was kinda expecting more of a reaction than ‘I see.’ Aren’t you gonna tell me how proud you are, how I’m finally standing up and doing the right thing? Or, at the least, grumble and ask how much I want to borrow.” “I was a Psych major in college, Scotty. I know how much a good rehab facility costs.” “Ok. So? Are ya willin’ to help your little brother out?” “Maybe.” Matt kept his arms folded across his chest. He didn’t bother to look at his younger sibling. Instead, he stared down at his reflection in the jet black paint of his 67 Camero. It probably wasn’t the most honorable of notions, but as he looked over his classic car, the thought occurred to him that he could probably afford another one in red, maybe one in yellow too, if he weren’t constantly bailing Scott out. “Maybe, huh? Great.” “Hey, I didn’t say ‘no’. That’s what counts, right?” “It’d be nice for a bit of support, Matt.” “Just hard to get behind it. You’ve had this little epiphany before, Scotty. Several times. Never has amounted to much.” “Never been this serious before, bro. I’ve never looked toward professional help. This time, I meant it. I want it.” “Sure, Scott.” “You remember what Dad used to always harp about? About hangin’ on to the past and not moving forward?” “He always harped on you. I tended to listen the first time.” “Right. Point is, Dad used to always say you can’t live in the past. And when you can figure that out, you can wipe the slate clean and start over, start down a new path in life. Just wipe away the past. ‘Like a snake shedding its skin’, he used to say.” “Ok.” “I’m ready to shed that skin. To start over… just like Pop always ranted about. You remember that, right?” “Yeah.” The speech did sound familiar. “So ask Dad for the money.” * * * Noon “Whoa!” As Matt felt his groin freeze, he jumped up from his seat and slammed his knee into the table, rattling the plates and silverwear across its surface. “Damn it!” “You ok?” “Yeah.” Matt snatched a napkin and attempted to dry off his crotch, then dropped the cloth across the small puddle on the table, pushing ice cubes back into his toppled-over glass. “Just one of those days, I guess. Scotty asked for decent sized chunk of change this morning… for rehab. I kinda blew him off. I mean… I’m gonna help him out, but I don’t like it. Part of me feels bad about giving him the cold shoulder in the first place, but even after deciding to loan him the money, can’t help but wonder if I’m throwing away cash.” “You’re doin’ the right thing. Give him a chance to really get his feet under him. Maybe he really means it this time.” Kevin grabbed a couple more napkins and helped sop up the mess. “Yeah. Like I said… just one of those days.” “Well, unfortunately, it’s not about to get any better.” “What’s that supposed to mean?” “Your knees…” Kevin pointed to the very joints Matt had just smashed into the underside of their table, “… in my professional opinion as your physical therapist… are in bad shape. I think it may be time to think about hanging up the boots for good.” “They’ve just been bugging me lately… nothing to worry about. We can just schedule another surgery.” “Matt, you just had surgery six months ago. It’s only a temporary fix.” “Temporary?” Matt flagged down the waitress and asked for a new glass of water. “There’s such a thing as crummy knees. Call it bad genetics, call it whatever, but coupled with years of abuse…at a certain point, its not about broken bones and torn ligaments. Your knees are just shot. Surgery will only relieve the pain temporarily. And every time we operate, that relief will be less and less, until finally, surgery doesn’t help at all.” “I didn’t hire a physical therapist to point out the fact that I should really be retiring. I have my wife for that. You’re supposed to help prolong my career. Not end it.” “I’m just looking out for your physical well-being. That’s my job. I’m not saying you have to quit now, today, and forfeit your Infinite Gauntlet title. But for God’s sake, Matt, if you don’t stop this garbage, you’re goin’ to spend the rest of your life in pain, limping around like an 80 year old man. Even at half that age.” “You’re right… that didn’t make my day any better.” Matt nodded his head as the waitress swung past and delivered a new glass of water, but kept his lips locked, biting off a ‘thank you’. “Matt, you don’t need to be doin’ this anymore. You’ve got a gorgeous wife, a beautiful girl, and your first son on the way… in what… three months now?” “Yeah.” “All I’m sayin’ is, there’s no need to wreck the rest of your life to get in a few more kicks. Wrap it up, go out on a high note, and then spend the next few decades enjoying your many blessings. Besides, you can’t keep doing this to Mary. August was the second time you’ve retired. Isn’t it about time to make it for real?” “I retired from PRIME. Not from wrestling altogether.” “Typically, Matt, ‘retire’ means to be done with… not to just move on to more of the same.” “I cut back my schedule, Kevin. Significantly. I went from wrestling a couple times a week to a couple times a month. I don’t travel all that much. I don’t take near the physical punishment. Let a guy enjoy his successes, will ya? Nobody’s pushing you out of your chosen career.” “My chosen career doesn’t physically cripple people’s futures.” “That was tactful.” “Sorry.” “I’m not saying I want to do this for the next ten or twelve years,” Matt pinched the end of his straw and stirred the ice in his drink, watching as the cold cubes swirled in circles, clanging against the sides of the glass, “but is it really gonna kill anyone if I keep at it for another ten or twelve months?” “It’s your life, Matt.” Kevin stabbed at his salad with his fork, struggling to latch onto a leaf of lettuce. “I just think your kids might want their dad to be able to chase them around the backyard and give them piggyback rides through the park.” The mood crashed about like Matt’s previous glass of water. It even seemed the restaurant went quite. Matt pushed his plate away from him. “I do love to eat, Kevin,” he shook his head, “but you know how to kill a good lunch.” * * * Evening With zombie eyes, Matt stared at his reflection in the mirror. The black letters of her name bounced back into his gaze. Mary It’d been nearly two years since he’d gotten the tattoo. Tattoos. As he gazed at her name across his throat, it reminded him of the other art that adorned his body. Instinctively, he reached for the back of his neck, running his fingers across the letters ‘FTW’ He could feel them on his fingertips. They burned. He couldn’t quite remember what had driven him to get the tattoos. In thirty years of existence on the earth, he’d never given it a thought. Then one day, he’d walked into Miami Valley Ink and gotten two. He remembered telling the artist he wanted her name across his throat… because he wanted to remember to put her ahead of his career. Now, almost two years and God only knew how many wrestling matches later, he wondered if he’d lost sight of what was literally right in front of his eyes. Kevin had been a little more direct than Matt would have liked at lunch, but that wasn’t to say that his words didn’t hold merit. Every day for the past month or so, his knees had ached. Some days it wasn’t too bad. Just the occasional tingle when the rain came. Other days, every step he took sent a jolt of pain up and down his legs from thigh to ankle. It wasn’t enjoyable. And hearing that surgery wouldn’t help much… “Hey, good lookin.” Mary walked into the bedroom and stepped up behind him, wrapping her arms around his, and kissing him on the shoulder. “Whatcha thinkin?” “Nothing. Was just looking at the artwork.” He pointed to her name. “Forget you had ‘em?” “Something like that.” The smallest of smiles forced through his lips. “Kinda like that one. Not so much a fan of the other.” Mary peered over his shoulder and glanced at the three crude letters on the back of his neck. “Yeah, ‘Fuck the World’ isn’t one of my proudest traits.” “It’s a wrestling gimmick. I just wish you would have left it in the ring.” “I know.” “Now, c’mon,” Mary gave a tug of the arm, “if we don’t hurry, we’re not gonna have time to get dinner before the movie starts.” “Gimme just a minute.” “Ok. I’ll be waiting in the car.” She gave him a kiss on the cheek. “Hurry up.” As Mary left the room, he turned back towards the mirror. He looked exhausted, likely from too many heavy conversations in one day’s time. Still, every word that’d been spoken was firmly in his mind. Not one had slipped, no matter how much he’d brushed them off at the time. And the listening had served him well. Maybe Kevin was right and it was time to hang up the boots for good, before he had to live the next fifty years regretting it. Maybe Scott had the right idea. Maybe their father had known exactly what he was preaching about when he’d talked of letting go of the past and shedding like a snake. Perhaps, it was time to start over, down that new path in life. He heard the car horn honk from the garage. With a confident nod to no one in-particular, Matt once again reached to the back of his neck and rubbed his hand across the three-lettered tattoo, but the ink wouldn’t wipe clean. Some things were under the skin.
