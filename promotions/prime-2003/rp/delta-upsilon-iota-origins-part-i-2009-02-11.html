Delta Upsilon Iota: Origins (Part I)

11 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 11th, 2009 WINSTON-SALEM, NORTH CAROLINA APRIL 13th, 2002 "Mr. Cobb, I’m afraid that I don’t have any good news for you. I’m truly sorry." WINSTON-SALEM, NORTH CAROLINA APRIL 12th, 2002 "Dammit son, you’re really starting to piss me off! You’d better get off the field before I throw you off of it myself! Hey Baby Huey! Get in there and show me something or come Monday your big ass will be back in high school! And I thought I had some football players out here!" Henry Jackson Cobb might only be a seventeen-year old redshirt freshman, but at six and a half feet tall and just a shade over three-hundred pounds, much is already expected of him. And judging from his position coach’s disposition, those who went before him haven’t exactly been delivering. As a cool spring breeze blows fallen leaves from the nearby oaks across the practice field, Cobb jumps from the sideline onto the playing field. Anxious to finally prove himself, he straps his helmet on and readies himself for a man five years his elder. Now lining up across from one of the Demon Deacons’ top rising seniors, a right end expected to anchor next season’s defensive line and possibly even earn All-Atlantic Coastal Conference preseason honors, Wake Forest’s offensive line coach’s afternoon appears to still be several light years away from improvement. The funny thing about appearances, however, is that they are sometimes quite deceiving. Case in point. As the coach’s whistle begins the pass rush drill, the three-year letterman attempts to bulrush the inexperienced Cobb, driving forward in a sudden and violent display of raw athleticism. He goes nowhere. And fast. Using his tremendous reach advantage, Cobb throws repeated blows to the chest, parrying each and every attempt to close the distance between the two warriors. The whistle’s shriek now signifies the end of the battle, as Cobb and his counterpart step back and attempt to gather what little oxygen and composure that this ten seconds or so may afford them. They get eight. Having learned from his recent miscalculation of Cobb’s strength, the veteran now instead elects to test his agility, offering several fakes and jukes on the whistle’s cue before dipping his body and attempting to circumvent Cobb with an expertly choreographed swim move. Although he does indeed get closer to the orange cone that serves as his endgame, he does so horizontally, as Cobb drives into the veteran’s left shoulder and deposits him immediately into the unforgiving turf. As the whistle again sounds and the veteran rises to his feet attempting to dislodge several inches of sod from his facemask in the process, it is now the defensive line coach’s turn to express his…distaste. "You motherfucking pussy! You gonna let this fucking child come out here and whip your ass like this?!? This kid’s seventeen fucking years old and he’s already making you his little bitch! Motherfucker, if you can’t win this shit how the hell are you gonna lead us this fall?!? You better turn this shit around!" A watchful coach’s attention to detail often blinds him from more trivial happenings going on around him. The kickers and punters taking an unscheduled leave from their drill work to chat with the ladies track and field team. A charity softball game on the nearby field seeing its first home run. And subsequently its first celebratory beer bong. But sometimes, this tunnel vision also prevents more meaningful recognition. Like the wad of white training tape that has blown from the athletic trainer’s cart on the surrounding track onto the practice field. And directly behind Hank Cobb. And again the whistle blows. The defensive end again attempts to overwhelm the younger Cobb with raw power, this time hitting him with absolutely everything he can muster. Again deflecting the initial surge, Cobb cannot however prevent his adversary’s wily grabbing of his jersey, which immediately eliminates the precious gap between the two. Now they are one. As both men lean forward into the other, their bodies and wills both exerted to the utmost degree, it is the defender that first adjusts his footwork, lowering his body in order to gain the leverage necessary to drive his taller opposition. Sensing this adjustment, Cobb too steps back in order to correct his own positioning accordingly. But the proper adjustment never comes. For as Hank Cobb steps down with his left foot, he does so directly onto that seemingly trivial little piece of tape. And the cleats never catch. As his opponent senses the give, he presses further forward, forcing Cobb to throw his right leg back in a desperate attempt to stabilize himself. That one leg, propelled downward by the combined force of two three-hundred pounders operating at maximum intensity, drives its cleats deep into the ground. Deep enough, that as Cobb’s leg straightens, the cleats remain unearthed. So deep, in fact, that the cleats hold long after the knee above them gives way. There is breaking. There is snapping. There is tearing. And finally, another whistle. WINSTON-SALEM, NORTH CAROLINA APRIL 21st, 2002 Having been subjected to a weeklong procession of every possible combination of family, teammates, classmates, and hospital staff, Hank Cobb has grown long tired of visitors, of both the official and unofficial variety. As the time nears four in the morning, his eyelids finally begin to lower, having faithfully maintained their longitude during the last three showings of this very same infomercial. Their descent, however, is interrupted by a quiet knock on the hospital room door. "I’m alright ma’am. I think I’m fixin’ to finally get some sleep." "Son, the last guy who called me ma’am is still looking for two or three molars, so you might want to choose your words a little more carefully." "It’s mighty hard to do so when I don’t even know who I’m talkin’ to yet." "Fair enough." As the door swings slowly open, a large figure steps through and removes his cowboy hat. "The name’s Duke Williams. But you can call me hoss."
