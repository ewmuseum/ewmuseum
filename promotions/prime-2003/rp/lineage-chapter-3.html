Date: Various Miami, Florida Two months ago... For the thousandth time, Denise Baxter dug her manicured fingernails into the fabric of her jacket and dragged them down to the hemline. The navy blue threads were beginning to fray one-by-one, each one a tally mark of the increasingly futile minutes she spent waiting for her damned phone to ring. She just hoped the 175-pound unconscious lump on the sofa in the other room stayed that way long enough to ensure he didn't hear the first word of this impending conversation. After all, those were her instructions. Take Tommy Wade in, put him up for the night, send him on his way and, by all means, make sure he doesn't find out who's been pulling his strings. Of course, the way he looked when he'd shown up in the back alley two hours earlier gave her no indication that he'd be waking anytime soon. Hell, total nuclear apocalypse would probably elicit little more than a snore and a dismissive sniffle from him. Having never met the erstwhile Mr. Wade, Denise wasn't sure what kind of creature she'd find crawling through the back streets of South Beach, but she certainly wasn't expecting the vision out of a George A. Romero movie that she laid eyes upon. "Death warmed over" was probably too nice a distinction. His hair was wet and matted, his face unshaven and deathly pale, the bags underneath his eyes so heavy they threatened to pull his entire head down to the ground. His jeans were torn and soaked through, his unzipped hooded sweatshirt held together by masking tape and the fading Celtics logo on the back. It was the kind of appearance that, if she had seen him on the street, would have caused Denise to look for the nearest police officer. But, he came to her, because he was told to. And she took him in, because she was told to. A shower and a hot meal later, and he was fast asleep. The sheer stress of it all probably had a bit to do with that — as did the sleeping pill she'd surreptitiously ground up into the leftover mashed potatoes. She'd been specifically told not to take any chances of Tommy finding anything out. Until now, she'd followed her instructions to the letter. She was owed a big favor after this. Not to mention a new jacket. And I'm free ... freefallin' Damn. Forgot to turn the phone to vibrate. Denise panicked as she grabbed for her phone, hoping she'd pick up in time before Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers provided her guest an unexpected wake-up call. Heart pounding, she answered. "Yeah?" The voice on the other end was distant and soft, but, boy was it familiar. "He there?" "Yeah." "He sleeping?" "Yes, Troy." In a hotel room on the other side of the world, Troy Douglas smiled and sighed with relief. Throw the ridiculous odds and probabilities out the window, his half-assed, hare-brained plan had actually worked. He put his phone back to his ear, noticing the four deep indentations on his forearm. He'd made himself so nervous the last few hours that he'd nearly yanked off his skin with his fingernails. "Thanks, Denise. You did great." Denise scowled, staring daggers at her phone and praying that whatever linking satellites connected this call out in Earth's orbit could somehow transmit this as well. "What choice did I have? My next-door neighbor growing up who might as well be my big brother calls me and says he needs me to pretend to smuggle his deadbeat future brother-in-law out of the country. Why me?" Troy held back a chuckle. He knew something like this was coming sooner or later. All he could be glad for was that there were large continents and multiple gargantuan bodies of water separating him from Denise Baxter, or he'd likely by ducking to avoid whatever clock radio or three-inch heel she'd just chucked at his head. "Because you're an actress, and you're the best girl for the part." With her free hand, Denise angrily grabbed a bottle of perfume off her nightstand and nearly smashed the phone to bits with it. Realizing that would only result in her being out a brand-new Droid, she stopped herself and laid the bottle down, silently mouthing a few choice swear words at the phone. "I'm in musical theater, Troy!" she fired back. "I'm in Miami doing an month-long run of Spamalot as Wench Number Four!" "What, couldn't get Wench Number Three? I know some people who can make that happen." The famous Douglas sarcasm. The goddamn flippant attitude that had given her cause to knock his head off since they were six years old and he was making jokes in the sandbox. For a guy who could be so serious, Troy still had the tact and sense of humor of a junior high schooler sometimes, and something about being around Denise seemed to kick that into overdrive. Best way to deal with it was to cut to the chase. "Screw the bullshit, Troy," she spat. "There's a guy on my couch who looks like he's been stuck in a rusty meat grinder for three weeks, and I want to know why you chose me of all people to do this for you!" "Two reasons," he answered, his words measured and calm. "First, he doesn't have a clue who you are, so you might as well be anybody to him. Second, and most importantly, I trust you." Damn him, Denise thought. Just like his father, Troy had that uncanny ability to turn any situation upside down with the snap of his fingers. She felt her death grip on the phone relax, and her thoughts of bludgeoning Troy into a coma with an inanimate carbon rod for forcing her into this started to dissipate. "Alright," she said. "What do I do next?" "I'll e-mail you everything you need to know in a minute. Just make sure that Tommy doesn't know I'm involved." "Why?" Troy had fought with this answer for a long time, but when he and Sam set out to do this, they knew the exact reason why. "Because in order to run back, he's got to believe he's running from something." "You know, nobody actually talks like that." Troy cracked a grin. As usual, she was right, which was incontrovertible fact number one about their nearly 30-year friendship. "Thanks for doing this, Dee." "What can I say? You're persuasive when you want to be," she quipped. "Besides, you owe me big time now, mister." "Excuse me, but I believe you still owed me?" "From when, exactly?" "Senior year, when I finally got Derek to ask you to the prom and got Eddie Fiero off your back, if I'm not mistaken." He wasn't. Damn him, again. "I think this one's WAY bigger, buddy-boy." "Whatever you say, Mrs. Fiero," Troy said, forcing Denise to concentrate every fiber of her being on not chucking the phone out the window in mock rage. "Just get him on the plane to Saitama. I'll handle things from there." "You'd better." With a *beep*, the conversation was over, and Troy slumped back into his bed. It was 2 a.m. in Miami, 4 p.m. in Saitama. If he knew Denise, she'd already gotten Tommy to trust her to the ends of the Earth, and he'd be on a plane to Japan by the time Troy was on his third episode of Scrubs later tonight. All he could do was wait, and hope that this infernal plan he and his fiance had hatched would somehow turn out right side up. They'd argued for hours into the night when Tommy showed up on their doorstep, begging for a helping hand. Samantha knew, as soon as they suggested going to the authorities, that her twin brother was going to bolt as soon as possible. By now, he wasn't even sure which one of them had thrown the idea out there, but somehow, they came to realize that if Thomas Wade was going to run, they had to figure out a way to control where and to whom he ran to. Troy had the resources and the friends, Sam understood her brother's compulsions and paranoias, and with Troy about to head out of the country with PRIME, they could steer Tommy places where he couldn't get himself into any more trouble. So why, weeks later, finally overseas, after doing everything he could to lead his future brother-in-law to where he needed to be, did Troy constantly wake up at night in a cold sweat? Why did he feel pangs in his stomach as he calculated his next moves? He taken to thinking of little else. He'd posed a million different questions and posited ten million different answers, but invariably, all his myriad different trains of thought brought him back to the same terminal, the same damn answer. He was playing God, and he had no right to do it. There it was, in all it's bloody, ugly glory. He was playing God, pushing fate along at his own pace so that he could play the hero, so that he could finally save someone. Guilt, even seven year old guilt, had that funny habit of popping up in the strangest of situations. Somehow, he'd struck upon the misguided notion that this was his opportunity to make up for letting Lauren and his father die in his goddamn home while he was a continent away. He had to do for Sam what he couldn't do for himself. He had to save somebody, and to do it, he'd play whatever role he had to. God, the Devil, Keyser Soze, the boogeyman, he'd play it to the hilt. Even if it meant that with each secret, cryptic text message, with each piece of subterfuge, it was turning his insides to jelly in sheer disgust at his own gall to play Russian Roulette with a man's life. He had to. He had to. At least, he had to justify it to himself. Troy looked out his hotel room window, squinting as the late afternoon sun glared into his room, reflecting off the stainless steel cityscape around him. He got up, closed the curtains and grabbed his iPod off the desk. Soon enough, Tommy Wade would be on a plane to Japan. Everything else would take care of itself. She's a good girl, loves her mama... ********** West End, London Tuesday afternoon Troy watched Tommy like a hawk these days. He was getting twitchy, and Troy could sense it. It was the way he nervously scratched at his temple, or fidgeted endlessly in a chair, or paced around an empty room like he was trying to carve a trail into the hardwood floors. Troy knew why. This was the end. London was the last stop. After this, Tommy was getting dragged back to the States, back to the cops, or worse, back to the sharks he was running from. For days, Troy had been battling over whether to tell the truth, whether to finally wipe away the CGI and special effects wizardry and show his future brother-in-law the wires Troy had pulled to get him here. Every time, he'd managed to restrain himself. Why? He wasn't sure. Maybe he liked the illusion, maybe he enjoyed the chance to play James Bond and live out some godforsaken adolescent fantasy. Maybe he was guilty for playing puppet master for all this time, or maybe he was scared that as soon as the puppet saw the strings, he'd simply cut them and run. So as Troy vacillated, wobbling on the fence like Humpty Fucking Dumpty, they sat in their hotel room, aimlessly flipping between re-runs of Fawlty Towers and Doctor Who. And every few seconds, Troy saw Tommy's eyes dip frantically towards his right pocket. For a while, he let it go. The nervous tics and twitches that had consumed Tommy Wade were an increasingly part of Troy's life, and while he knew why the nerves were there, Troy hadn't psychoanalyzed each one. And then he knew. The phone. Tommy was looking at the pocket where he kept his phone, subconsciously begging, pleading for another mysterious text message that would get him out of his inevitable trip back into the lion's den. Just like that, he knew what he needed to do. It was as clear as one of those postcard-perfect fall Saturdays when all he cared about was stepping onto the gridiron. Except now, the time to play games was over. As Tommy twitched, Troy reached into the pocket for his second phone, the one with the blocked number, the one that had started this whole damn exercise in the first place. Not even looking, he tapped his thumb over the keys in silence. He didn't need to look anymore. He knew it was right. For a moment, his thumb hovered above the send key, held back almost as if there was some magnetic field trying to stop him. And, for a millisecond, every doubt he'd pondered rushed back into his brain at warp speed, nearly causing him to scream for help. He'd gone up against 350-pound offensive linemen and seven-foot tall professional wrestlers. Pressing a damn button on a cell phone took just as much strength. 3... 2... 1... *BEEP* Tommy's eyes grew wide as he felt the familiar vibration on the outside of his thigh. For a moment, his gaze was so rapturous that, had you just stumbled onto the situation, you might've thought he'd just seen the Virgin Mary appear in a tortilla. Rousting himself from his wonder, Tommy pawed the phone hungrily, not caring if he smudged the screen. He licked his lips, salivating as he finally saw the message on the long-blank screen. TURN AROUND. "Huh?" Tommy turned his head with such force that he cracked his neck. He stared, goggle-eyed at Troy, who was looking at him sheepishly. Tommy's mouth flapped open and close, the saliva building up as his throat grew dry. The silence hung there, hot and thick like a sweltering summer day, until Troy finally diffused the situation. He dug into his jeans pocket, pulled out a cell phone and flipped it into Tommy's lap. "You?" "Me. And Sam." Troy couldn't even look at him as he answered. "All this time? ALL THIS TIME?" Troy nodded. He knew Tommy's next move before he'd even gotten up out of his chair. He wasn't going to do anything to stop it. "You son of a bitch!" The right cross to the jaw stung briefly, just like the many he'd been on the receiving end of before in his life. Troy's head rocked back, but his expression never changed. He just nodded again, watching as Tommy Wade cocked his fist back once again. This time, Troy blocked it and caught it. "Let go of me!" Tommy was wailing, his eyes darting in a thousand different directions at once, sweat leaking off his brow. "Do you know what you DID to me?" Another nod. "You manipulated my life! You sent me around the world like I was some fucking FedEx package for two goddamn months, you bastard! Why?" And now, Troy Douglas had no choice but to answer. "Tommy," he said. "I don't expect you to understand this, and I don't expect you to forgive me, but I need you to listen to me. Now." ********** One hour later... Troy spoke, Tommy listened. A few times, it looked as though he'd interject, or just walk out the door and never come back, but a lifted hand or a pointed glance stopped him each time. At last, the message was clear. "You're not going to like this, you never will, but this was for your own good." Tommy Wade wasn't going to be quiet any longer. "My own good? Bullshit. What do you know about my own good?" "Not me, Tommy. Your sister. Your twin sister," Troy said. The look in Tommy's eyes let Troy know that one sunk in. "She knew you weren't going to just go back to Boston, whether we forced you or not. She knew you were going to run. Jesus Christ, you stole our wedding rings!" No way out of that one, Tommy reckoned. Still, he had to say something. "But why the goddamn wild goose chase? Why the safe houses, the secret text messages? Why not just TELL ME?" "Because you wouldn't have trusted us. And, at that point, Sam and I couldn't be sure that you weren't being watched." "And now?" "That part's taken care of. Sam went to the FBI, they discovered every little detail of Boyle's money laundering operation that was running through your restaurant. As of three weeks ago, you're in the clear. How's that for a deus ex machina?" Tommy wasn't sure whether to hug his soon-to-be brother-in-law or find the nearest sharp object and plunge it into his carotid artery. He did neither, remaining in his almost catatonic state cemented to the chair. "You had no right..." His voice trailed off, like even Tommy didn't know where his thoughts were leading. Troy knew the feeling. "No," Troy said. "We didn't. But, this was still the right thing to do." Troy didn't know if Tommy believed him or not. There would be time for reconciliation later. "Last show of the tour's coming up. When I'm done, we go home, and you go get your life back." Thomas Gabriel Wade nodded. That was all Troy needed to see. Bingo.
