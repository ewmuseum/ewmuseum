There's Always A Way

Date: I sold my car to buy gas June 3rd, 2008 With hand firmly attached to remote, Christian Daniels sat in the living room darkness of his beachfront home and scanned channels at 3 in the morning. Scratching at five day old shadow on his face, Christian slouched down in the comfort of the leather couch and stared at various television images: Infomercials about facial products, a rerun of The Tonight Show, Girls Gone Wild, and more infomercials. Nothing piqued the interest of the former North Carolina mobster because everything lacked an answer to his burning inquiry. For the past two nights, he’s wallowed around with an imagination that won’t settle. This wasn’t a recurrence from months ago, when he had the inkling that international assassins lurked outside the house waiting for a light to turn on. Instead, the reason his imagination wouldn’t quell revolved around longtime friend and protégé, Devin Shakur. Christian usually had a keen sense of deducing when something was awry, but his intuitions were being questioned more and more by the hour. No new information was out, and Devin hadn’t called with any potential roadblocks. Christian wondered if Devin would even know about any quagmires considering he just reached the pinnacle of his profession. He was all too familiar with fame in the family. Practically everybody but Christian was successful at some venture in life, and almost none of them knew how to fully grasp it. Time and time again he would have to use his vast amount of resources and eliminate every problem that his brothers amassed. Now doubt began to mingle. Was there just a little too much cynicism swirling around in his mind? Had he dabbled so long in the underground that false situations were keeping him afloat? All of his questions were answered when he flipped the channel one more time to a North Carolina twenty four hour news outlet. "Good morning, I’m Chase Dolloway." A dashing man on the television said, trying extremely hard to convince people he wasn’t reading off a teleprompter. "We will get to the forecast in a moment after these top news stories. Gas hits a state average of 4.06, what are consumers doing to lower their intake? Raleigh police are on the lookout for a national celebrity wanted for questioning in the murder of Braintrust CEO Mike Grimwood." His ears didn’t hear the rest of the news stories. His mouth hung open like a mailbox with a loose hinge. Unfortunately for the glass table placed near his giant legs, the rest of his body failed to comply. With a violent thrust of the right leg, glass flew in every conceivable direction of the living room. Remnants stuck in the wall, were embedded in the carpet, cuddled in the cushions of the couch, superficially stabbed the skin of Christian, and a few managed to find his long black hair. At least he knew that his cynicism was warranted. Every second floor light turned on in haste. Doors swung open like 5 PM on a Friday at most work place. Confounded voices raised questions and rushed to the staircase prepared with knives and guns at the ready. With quickness like an infantry unit and power of a rhinoceros, the entire Smith family bolted down twelve stairs and turned the corner. Still clouded in darkness, Michael Blaze turned on the nearby kitchen light. Amidst a sea of broken glass Christian sat on the telephone, blood mixing with ink of his tattoos. Before any of the Smith boys could fire a question off, Chase Dolloway enlightened them about the problem. "Raleigh police are seeking out PRIME wrestling superstar Devin Shakur for questioning regarding the murder of Braintrust CEO Mike Grimwood. A spokesperson for the department has issued a statement that Shakur is a person of interest because he has information that could lead to the apprehension of the suspect. Everybody assumed their positions, grabbing a phone or manning a computer. Completely ignoring the shattered table shards all over the floor, the Smith clan once again found themselves at war. ---------------- Raleigh Police Headquarters June 3rd, 9 AM Edward Mendez hated his life. Ever since a bullet tore through both of his legs back in 2004 during a shootout, forcing him to sit behind an uncomfortable desk neck deep in paperwork, he yearned for something better, something that would enrich his life. Well respected by his peers, there was always a sense of inferiority lingering around Edward. Fellow workers treated him with flattering kindness, inquiring how his children and wife were, and encouraged him to keep on trucking, before dropping twenty pages of paperwork atop his desk wide calendar. To keep the paychecks coming and food on the table, Edward always did as he was told, never questioning any of his superiors. They were capable of dictating the rules and giving out the orders. The only thing he could do was sit in a wheelchair and put ice on his right arm should the limb tense up. Today, Edward got up and anticipated that it would be like any other day. Somebody would get him his coffee, extra milk and extra sugar, and three stacks of paperwork would be sitting in front of his family picture. One was labeled ‘Done’, the other ‘In Progress’ and the biggest stack was marked ‘New’. He was right. Sifting through the ‘In Progress’ pile so that the ‘New’ stack could be accommodated, Edward failed to acknowledge the work phone sitting in his small desk drawer. For the last six hours, it beeped seventy two times, once every five minutes. He never took the work phone home because Edward believed in the philosophy that once you were there, work never intercepted any family moments. Picking up his writing on the murder of a gang banger, he heard the chirping of that work phone and begrudgingly wheeled himself back to open the drawer. For all Edward knew, the person on the other line was someone from another department calling in to check up on him and inquiring if he would help them out. With the mahogany drawer opened, Edward retrieved the phone and flipped it open to find a number that made him scratch his head. None of the numbers, sans area code, registered to him and the only way to get this number was by a parting of his own lips. Shrugging, he pressed the talk button and placed the phone up to his ear. "Hola?" "Como estas...amigo?" A guttural voice on the other end of the line responded. The voice sounded like a hissing snake, waiting to clasp onto the next unsuspecting prey and devour him for breakfast. Edward’s eyes almost bulged out of his head. If paperwork wasn’t providing protection for his head, Amelia stationed across from him would have called a medic. Sweat started to form on Edward’s brow, which required him to reach into his pocket and pull out a handkerchief. Choking up, Edward responds, "CD, Christ-" Interrupting quickly, for his own sake, Christian retorted. "It’d be smart if you didn’t say my full name out loud, vato. I’m still hot property around your parts." Tugging at the collar on his white dress shirt and wiping another batch of sweat from his face, Edward shifted his eyes back and forth before dipping his voice down a notch. Christian still had the ability to intimidate from thousands of miles away. "What do you want, papi? I ain’t running with you and that gang no more. I’m working here." "How would you like to make a cool quarter grand without any strings attached?" Christian said, slowly drawing his one time informant in, pretending that everything was cool and calm, when ulterior motives rose higher than Ed’s paperwork. "Bull, Madera, there's always something else with you." Edward quipped, undoing a button on his dress shirt to allow for more ventilation while cradling the phone in his right ear. "I ought to report you to my superiors, esse, get off this line." Christian chuckled on the other end, clearly not rattled by the threat. "Sure, that’ll work. They did a real killer job catching me the first time in 92...And the second time in 96...And the third time in 01...Especially that time in 06. Look, vato, all I’m asking you to do is give me a name. I’m sure somebody like you who gets the gusto of the paperwork wouldn’t have to ask anybody, either." Edward mulled over the prospect in his head. 25,000 was a pretty nice number, and would provide his family some financial protection for a respectable amount of time. He could afford a few luxuries instead of stretching his boss’s patience with the overtime card. On the other hand, Edward didn’t want to fall back into his past. He was once Christian’s primary informant when he got his start in the department back in 1999. Fresh out of college and in dire need of steady monetary, Edward accepted Death’s invitation and fed them information for four solid years before moving up in the ranks. If Edward was ever caught having this conversation, he would find himself in a hellacious fight to garner even one disability check. He’s fairly certain, though, he could keep a secret. "All I gotta do is gives you a name, vato?" Edward said, while grabbing a pin and staring absentmindedly down at a piece of department paper so nobody would suspect anything. With his rugged monotone, Christian replied. "That’s all you have to give me, and the money will be in your account come tomorrow." "25?" "25" Writing something irrelevant underneath the department seal, Edwards gave a nod of the head, "Alright, what you need, esse?" "Devin Shakur, you know who that is?" Christian said "Si" "Who is running his case and has people on his ass?" Edward went to say the name, but hesitated for a second. He knew that by releasing the information, lives would be put in danger. Christian and the Smith boys weren’t exactly known for being the friendliest bunch of people to encounter. He especially knew that by releasing these names, a whole new can of worms would be opened and a rivalry born out of smite and death will once again surface. Unfortunately for those souls, the twenty five grand wielded the names out of him, "Larry and Steven Connors." Edward hung his head and phone up simultaneously. The latter action was probably one of the most beneficial. If he hadn’t, even being on a cellular phone, people would have heard a word uttered by Christian Daniels in its loudest form. "MOTHERFUCKER" --------------------------- June 2nd, 2008 When Larry and Steven Connors were children, it was a foregone conclusion what they would be as adults. Two generations of their family had gone into the police force and were decorated detectives while Larry and Steven were kids. From their third birthday forward, they received toys and gifts revolving around being a detective. Their minds never set on achieving anything else other than continuing on the Connors legacy. Unfortunately for Larry and Steven, they never got to ride alongside or work with any of their relatives. During the month of June 2006, all four members with association to the police force: Grandfather Daniel Wilson, father Mike Connors, and Uncles Marcus Galloway and Lance Robinson, were killed for their desire to chase after one particular group which had been a nuisance for over fifteen years to the family. Christian Daniels knew Daniel Wilson when he was the Hollywood style hotshot detective circa 1992. After finding a longtime friend dead, Daniel decided to take down the Death group on his own by raiding their hideout. Brazen by anyone’s standards, Daniel attempted to accomplish this feat without informing any of his fellow lawmen. He would subsequently get caught by Death, beaten to a pulp, and with his family’s life hanging over his head, forced to owe Death a favor. He paid that favor by helping the Smith clan with crucial information during the heat of the long battle. Daniel’s children subsequently got caught in the web of the Smith boys, leaving Larry and Steven to stare at four different caskets amongst a horde of police officers on June 16th, 2006. From that day forward, they made a vow to one another that anybody involved with the crime would suffer. No stone would be left unturned. Much to their chagrin, the Smith’s weren’t exactly writing their contact information on a postcard and sending it to the Connors twins. The two year anniversary was soon on the horizon and not so much as an iota of new information had surfaced. Somehow, the Connors were able to move on, cherishing the memories of their fallen guardians and doing all that they could to be the best detectives available to the department. Twisting the knob on his deluxe apartment in downtown Raleigh, Steven Connors walked inside and dismissively threw his keys onto the kitchen counter. A step behind was Larry Connors, removing the overcoat from his Tan Suit and placing it on the coat rack immediately on his right. Steven immediately went over to the refrigerator, threw open the door, and grabbed two beers, tossing one to his twin. Popping them simultaneously, the twins clanked bottles before going into their individual bedrooms at different ends of the apartment. Their after work routine was always the same: Take a shower, watch Glenn Beck on MSNBC, eat dinner at Luigi’s Italian Restaurant, come back home and finish up any nagging matters that couldn’t be concluded during the workday. Never once in their two years of work did this routine change and if they had their way, it never would. First on the agenda for Steven and Larry was to take a trip to their offices and drop the day’s excess work off. Two murders and a string of robberies occurring in the span of a single day wasn’t exactly something they could just dump off on Edward Mendez. Not to mention they had a few other cases which needed to be solved. Shuffling into the closet like space, Steven and Larry pulled on overhead strings, bringing light into their workrooms. Both consisted of a desk, mirror, small bookshelves, and an immense amount of newspaper clippings which covered every available crevice of wallpaper or open area in the office. On their ceilings were pictures that the News and Observer took on June 16th, along with interviews from fellow officers regarding the death of the Connors elders. In numerological order by date, from the left wall near the door all the way around to the right, were excerpts from various media sources: magazines, newspapers, periodicals, journals, etc, highlighting the Smith family. Much like a psychopathic stalker, or Michael Scofield from Prison Break, nothing would be left to chance. They thought about this case everyday. If they were to solve this, it would bring about closure to the deaths which had plagued and torn apart the family apart. Masking each brother’s mirror were nine pictures. Eight of those pictures belong to the Smith clan. All gave hearty grins in their poses, which were littered with X’s and slash marks. The ninth picture, freshly placed in the middle of the mirror, and tidier than a suburban family home after spring cleaning, belonged to Devin Shakur. Emanating from the picture were single red marks, linking him to the eight other pictures. ------------- June 3rd, 2008 11:10 AM Two hours elapsed without any headway regarding the Connors Twins. Everybody in the house knew that dealing with them would not be so easy the second time around, considering that every law enforcement agency in North Carolina, and probably the United States, had them as highly wanted fugitives. Scattered around a kitchen table overflowing with documents, Christian Daniels leaned back in his office chair and looked his fellow criminals in the eye. "We’re going back." Nobody in the room needed clarification on what Christian said, and all responded at the same time, "WHAT?" Christian stood up from the table, not bothering to check his damaged right arm which had nine fragments of glass in it before Jason Hardy painstakingly removed them. "Show of hands here, kids. Who thinks Devin did a decent job of handling his business when we were 10,000 miles away. Jason, Michael, and Matthew all raise their hands while Mallory, Brandon and Chris kept their hands on the table. "Keep your hands up. Alright, now how many think he and Rachel can handle the Connors when word gets out that he’s linked to us in a very direct way." The three remaining members with their hands raised each curiously glanced at one another and slowly put their hands down. Christian gave a half frown and nodded his head accordingly. "That’s what I thought, we’re going back." Jason looked up at the mammoth Christian standing in front of him and threw his arms up in air. "Dude, you can’t be serious." Contorting his face into a full scale frown, like he had been insulted, Christian shifted his weight forward and looked down at Jason over the table. "DUDE, I am, and we’re going to do it." "How are we going to get back into the States? You know we’ll be dead before we get 200 miles to any part of any coast." "There’s always a way to get around any barricade." "Even if we get there, what the hell are we going to be able to do?" "There’s always a way" "And what everybody gets away all hunky dory like the last time? No, we were able to skate last time because we had everything figured out well in advance. This is on the fly at best. People have been plotting our return for years now, and you know we’ll get caught." "There’s always a way" Having the most expertise in police planning, Jason stood up and folded his arms defiantly across his chest, "There’s no fucking convincing you is there?" Christian struck an identical pose, adding the feature of his eyebrows descending down right above his eyes. "There’s. Always. A. Way." "Well you figure that way out and let me know, but until then I think you are out of your fucking mind!" Jason said, slamming over a vase on the kitchen table and exiting the living room in a fit of rage. Christian gave Jason’s back a final scold before turning to the rest of his crew, "Anybody else thinks I’m out of my fucking mind?" Nobody moved. "Good, then get Rachel on the horn and let’s hash out a plan."
