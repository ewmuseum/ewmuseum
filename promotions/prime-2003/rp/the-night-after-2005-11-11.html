The Night After

11 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/11/2005 “RAAAH!” came the shout of frustration that bellowed out backstage. Post-show, Revolution 78: the time was about thirty minutes after the show had ended and Scott Matthews was still on the warpath. Security had been working to corral him to one area, managing finally to block him into the catering area. The good news was that he was finally cornered. The bad news was that he was finally cornered. Up until now, they’d been able to give ground to keep him from hurting folks, to give him a path of least resistance. Plus, they were right in catering and there were some hungry wrestlers about. So security had some major incentive. “Mr. Matthews,” one of them said, taking a few steps forward, “I need you to calm down or we’re going to have to take you down.” Scott spun, facing him. “Fine!” he roared. “Take me down!” So the man dove for his legs, trying to immobilize him. Two more moved in to try and slow the monster down. Scott, still nursing the shoulder that he’d slammed into the turnbuckle, wasn’t having any. His left arm swung, taking one man across the chest with a solid THWACK. He was lifted bodily off his feet and almost seemed to float through the air. He landed solidly on one of the catering tables, more gracefully than Jeff Hardy ever managed and sent an array of food flying. He lay there, groaning from both the impact of the big arm and the landing. The other man dove, latching his hands on Scott’s injured shoulder. The Manbeast roared a scream of pain and swung his head. The crown of Scott’s head made impact on the bridge of his nose, causing it to explode with a splash of blood and a yelp of pain. He staggered away, trying to blink out the tears in his eyes as Scott’s good arm reached for the guy at his feet. Scott got ahold of the man’s belt and lifted, pivoting and heaving with all the might in his body. The poor guy sailed right into two of his colleagues, leaving them to all crash to the ground unceremoniously, and of more immediate concern, painfully. “Break ‘em out!” came the shout. There was one tool in the arsenal of PRIME security that hadn’t been used yet. They all lifted a weapon out of the holsters that resided in the small of their backs. Since Rampage had been hired, they had been given out to all the security personnel. They all lifted and aimed and Scott spun like a desperate animal. The first fired with a soft puff of air and a dart fly, taking Scott squarely in the good shoulder. Two more soft puffs and one landed in the right forearm and lower back of the monster. He staggered, his vision starting to swim and his eyes unfocusing. With one last unsteady step, he tried to maintain his balance and went crashing to the unforgiving floor. As he lay there, his perception bled away to black as he saw the feet of the men circling him. * * * The big man lay there, trying to gather his thoughts. His head felt packed full of cotton and his body wasn’t responding terribly well. He lay on his back, a cushion under his head, but his long hair hung in his eyes. Fine, he didn’t care. But he tried to get up, and that’s when he was worried. He couldn’t move his arms or legs. He couldn’t move his whole body. Slowly, he tried to consider what might have happened to him. He might still be under the effects of the tranquilizers that he’d been hit with. That’d explain why he was thinking so slowly, why he couldn’t move. But as he lay there, trying, he could feel straps against his skin. His forehead had pressure on it and he looked up. With a puff, he tried to blow his hair out of his eyes. It didn’t budge. That didn’t make any sense at all. Unless, of course, he was strapped down; that might be might be it. He tried to sit up and felt pressure again, moreso than before. He thought the drugs might be wearing off. But he just lay there. He wasn’t going anywhere anytime soon and there was no one around. He didn’t do it often, but he was forced to reside in his own brain. Not if he could help it. He tried to get the word ‘Hello’ out. Instead, it came out: “Mhmn.” Yup, his body still wasn’t feeling all that hot from drugs that the darts had pumped into his body. So he was stuck laying there. Alone. The thought of that alone was enough to make him start involuntarily sweating. He was alone, strapped to a gurney in a room that he couldn’t see anything of but the ceiling. So instead, he lay there, his mind racing. What had exactly happened? He remembered hitting the turnbuckle shoulder-first after missing the RAGE. He was lying there, thinking about all the stuff he’d done after the match. He had gone on, attacking security, flipping over every table he could get at and generally being an all-around nuisance. But he lay there, thinking in his own mind, about what was really bothering him. He still didn’t know himself. He didn’t know what he was. He almost wanted to cry. It’s why he had lost and half of why he had flipped out after the match. He’d gone to talk to his coach, the man that had overseen what he considered his most shining moments in high school. He’d lead Scott through times he was proud of, and instead of telling Scott what kind of man he was, he painted the same picture that so many wrestling journalists, both real and pathetic, had written over the years. He was a mindless beast, ready to annihilate whatever stood in the path to victory. It was that very picture that scared him so badly. He didn’t want to be a monster like Ivan Stanislav or Meanstreak had been. They didn’t behave like real people; they didn’t feel or love. He desperately wanted to love and be loved. There was what Alyssa had said: he was sweet, loving, caring person. She referred to herself, his mother, his sister and friends. Friends like Gabriel that he’d had in the past and such. But he was still confused: how did those two go together? What was he? He heard a door open and a man in an EMT shirt leaned over. “Mr. Matthews,” he said softly, “the drugs are wearing off and you seem to have calmed down. I’m going to let you out so you can head to your hotel for the night. Are you calmed down?” “Mkay,” he managed to mutter out. “Might wanna get me a ride.” * * * It was the kind of morning that usually followed a night of hard drinking. Scott’s head throbbed, his body hurt and the puncture wounds from the darts were aching terribly. He sat on the bed, pulling on his standard combat boots and pulling the hem of his jeans down over the top. It was colder now, no call to be going around with shorts on, right? There was a knock at the door. His head whipped towards the sound and he immediately regretted it. His head ached again. Slowly, he pushed himself up and made his way towards the door carefully. He lifted the chain latch and twisted the knob, pulling it open. A ray of sunlight stabbed in, making him cringe. The figure framed in the blinding golden aura smiled at him. “Heeeeeeeeeeeeeeeyyy SCOTTY!” Vangellus Oxios. Loud voice. Not the kind of thing that you need to hear when a fly buzzing through the air five feet away would make Scott hold his head in agony. “Awww, dammit, Van…” he shouted. Then he paused. This didn’t add up. Van was just about Scott’s antithesis when it came to life on the road. He flew everywhere, went home no matter the cost… so what in the hell was he doing here? Scott didn’t say anything, but the question hung in his mind. Oxios looked into the eyes (as best he could) of his tag team partner. Scott shielded them from the sun, so a little lean forward gave him better position. And the cleaning lady walking by got a nice view of his ass in those tight jeans that he was wearing. Work that ass, Van! 'Bloodshot eyes. Sensitivity to volume. Allergy to sunlight. Either those tranquilizers were provided courtesy of Jack Daniels or you were out drinking last night.' “I didn’t have any time to get any liquor last night. Lord knows I coulda’ used some,” Scott muttered, trying to push the door shut. Unfortunately, Van was still standing in the doorway and leaning, so the door wasn’t closing and Scott… Scott certainly wasn’t getting any relief from the sunlight. “Van, what are you doing here?” The smaller half of the Tag Team Champions shrugged his shoulders, hands folded over and behind his head to give Matthews a little respite from the light. 'Well, I was making a cake next door and thought I might ask for a cup of sugar. Or I might just have come to check up on you. One of the two.' Scott gave him a wary look. It was… reassuring to know that Van cared, but unsettling. It was out of character for Van to act this way… and yet, it was one thing he could really use. “I appreciate that, Van. I really do.”
