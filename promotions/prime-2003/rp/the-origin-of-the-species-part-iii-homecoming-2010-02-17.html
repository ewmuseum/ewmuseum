The Origin Of The Species: Part III - Homecoming

17 February 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17-02-10 A cloying industrial smog clung to his skin as he surveyed the land over the mount of debris and sod. Through a cold fleeting rain he studied a half acre of ground at the base of the hill bordered by broken mortar and bricks. Silently he lamented the plot, letting the rain soak through his clothes to the skin. Flicking his gaze to the horizon he regarded the sky line; far to the east billowing plumes of smoke reaching up from great chimneys attached to the foundries of faceless corporations. In the west the skyscrapers of the business district peered through the brooding grey clouds, watching over the factories that helped build their empires. A fog descending from the north obscured his view of the freeway, and having already come from the south he didn't care to look back. The sound of stones crunching underfoot broke his concentration after a while, but still he stared longingly at the land below. Squinting through the rain he clenched his teeth together, his jaw muscles pulsing as Ryan approached from his rear, eventually stopping by his side and breathing heavily. “Hey man. What's up? This is some place for a meeting...” he said between gulps of air. “Thanks for coming.” Von Kelsig replied dryly, glancing only once to his left to greet the man who claimed to be his brother with a curt nod. “Aren't you supposed to be halfway across the world right now?” “Supposed to be.” Stealing a few glances at his eyes and trying to work out the giant's state of mind, Ryan folded his arms and hunched his shoulders against the wind, gazing out over the wasteland that had seemed to captivate his brother. “So what the hell you doing back here?” he asked, slicking his hair back out of his eyes as fat drops of rainwater trickled down his face. “Been thinking a lot about life recently,” the giant began, “plus I guess I owe it to you to see this thing through.” “Well...thanks, I guess. But why here?” Ryan asked, hawking up a wad of phlegm in disgust at the thought of the miasma of rain and factory smoke clinging to the back of his throat like an oily film. Von Kelsig took a deep breath and blew it through his mouth, taking one more look over the horizon before kicking at the dirt and pondering over the words he needed. “I remember when this all used to be fields and woodland. The factories were still there but you couldn't see them through the trees. See the patch of land down there? That used to be a house. A mansion, fourteen years ago. I can still remember it like it was yesterday, before it all turned to shit. It was my home. I lived there with my family. Mom, dad, my sisters, my brother. I was the eldest. My father built it from the ground up, took around nine months to finish. I always thought of it as a castle, it even had its own tower in the east wing.” “The house was finished in eighty-seven, and it only lasted for nine years before it became that pile of rubble down there. In fact we're probably standing on the rest of it. Got a lot of fun memories of the place. Twenty three years ago this place would be buzzing with life; music pumping through the air and bodies laughing and partying. Don't gimme that look man, I was an animal at seventeen. Whenever the folks were out on business I'd call up all my friends and round up some girls and just party through the night and past the dawn. Good times. Always covered my tracks afterwards but to be honest I don't think my parents would've ever minded. We were close, for a well-off family.” “Dad was always organizing family events for us, making sure we stayed tight-knit as a family because of his work. He didn't want to become one of those workaholics that pay for a life they're always away from. It was a happy upbringing. I went to college and had my social life and all was well.” “You can imagine with a life like that nobody really pried into each other's lives all that much, we were too busy just living our own lives. So you can imagine the shock when we found out what dad had been up to behind our backs. He had his group of friends he hung out with, like any guy, but what we didn't know was instead of going down the pub or golfing or whatever he was taking trips to Atlanta and Vegas and places like that, putting down more money than sense on throwaway bets.” “Mom had no idea...none of us did. By the time we found out what was happening it was too late. On my twenty-fifth birthday, back in ninety-six, we had a big family gathering in the house to celebrate. Dad hired a band and the whole estate was decked out. All my family and friends were there...it was a great day. We spent the evening in dad's bar after everybody left, just the immediate family. I don't remember exactly when it happened but I recall mom hearing something in one of the upstairs rooms, like something breaking.” “She went off to see if something had fallen over or if someone hadn't left the party yet, and that's when we heard the scream. She came running back downstairs hollering that two of the bedrooms and one of the en-suite bathrooms was on fire. Turned out a gang had shown up, led by this loan shark that dad had gotten in bad with. He owed a lot of money. Mom had to force it out of him but he eventually confessed to blowing all his wages on gambling and then some. He had a real bad addiction, and fucked up getting involved with this shark.” “So anyway, the house is burning from the top down, a real violent fire. Molotov cocktails. This shark is outside with his goons, and when we try to escape they just bombard the house with more incendiaries. Way out here there's no neighbours or anybody close by so we were fucked. I got out...but...well...I was the only one. I fell right into their arms and they beat me half to death. Nobody else made it out.” With a guilty look on his face, Von Kelsig turned and shrugged, averting his gaze every few blinks as Ryan, having forgotten about the rain and the smog, tried to process what he was being told. The look on his face was of disbelief and offence at the story. “That...is complete bullshit. You know that right?” he said eventually, cocking an eyebrow. “Thing is, I know. But it's the story I've told for the last decade. Every company that's hired me has posted it in Hessian's character biography. I poured so much emotion and belief into it, even picked out this location in case people called bullshit on it. It's always looked like this. Hell that hole in the ground used to be a factory that produced bleach or something for a cleaning company. This is the first time in ten years anybody's ever heard the truth of it. There was no fire. No mansion. Shit I never even had sisters. You know that.” Von Kelsig said with a lowly sigh. “Yeah...I do. So why the story? Why the cover-up?” Ryan probed, a hint of umbrage in his tone of voice. “Because it's a lot easier than the truth,” the giant shrugged apologetically, “you think I don't have a good reason for scripting my own life? If I wanted to involve my family in the life I live then believe me, your life would be far different from what it is now. It was easier for me to just smooth over the truth with something I was comfortable with, be it a lie or not.” “You told people your family was wiped out in a fire? That comforts you?” Thrusting his hands into his pockets, Ryan turned away from the giant and scuffed the soles of his feet against the remnants of an iron girder lodged in the dirt. “Like I said, it was easier.” Von Kelsig growled, with a distinct finality in his voice. “What about me? What about mom and dad? And just when did you decide to cave in and face all this? Last time we spoke you were acting like I was making all of this up. Now all of a sudden you're telling me about some secret life you've made up?” Ryan said, almost shouting, his arms out at his sides, “What's the deal man?” The strain was all too apparent in his eyes, Ryan had noticed. A low grumbling emanated from his throat before winding into a groan. Running a hand down his face the giant strained the rainwater from his beard and stared straight into the heavens, letting even more wash down upon him. “You wouldn't understand Ryan. Just...look, lately work has been hell for me. I've been going through some shit and I guess it's just making me re-evaluate the things in life. I told you earlier I owe it to you to make things right so that's what I'm doing.” “First I wanna know why you swept us under the carpet like we never existed.” Ryan asked now in a sombre tone. “Sorry pal but that's my business. You sought me out to bring me home, that's it. When I'm ready to tell you the deal I'll let you know.” Glancing down at the wasteland, Ryan stuck his tongue in his cheek and looked the giant up and down. Furrowing his brow he sank his hands back into his pockets and turned away, heading back down the hill to his car. “Whatever man. There's more important shit to worry about. I'm guessing you got time before you need to fly back out?” “I got a day, day and a half.” Von Kelsig called after Ryan, treading carefully down the hill, “What do you mean there's more important stuff to worry about?” Ahead he could see Ryan shake his head and increase his pace down the hill, his arms at his sides now pumping back and forth as he nimbly navigated the loose soil and rocks before hopping to the ground and skulking towards his car. Von Kelsig thought he heard him mumbling something to himself as he came off the mount. “You say something?” he called. “I-..no. We're going to Sibley street. You know where that is?” Ryan asked after biting his tongue. When Von Kelsig shot him a confused look he sighed and gestured to his Chevy, “just follow me then.” ---- Alone with his thoughts on the drive, Von Kelsig kept his eyes on the Chevy while contemplating his destination. The thought of ever divulging the truth about his past made his stomach turn, but in the last several months as his life seemed to spiral further and further into the abyss he found himself re-assessing his career and the lonely hours spent outside the ring. In one year he'd gone from having it all to losing it again, from titles to glory to Gloria. He had survived a force-fed addiction to heroin, but he had also lost to Troy Douglas. Either he was enjoying a massive high or crushing depression. There was no middle ground. No place where he could just relax and watch the world go by with blissful ignorance to the pains he had endured. A dark secret welled in the pit of his soul; the reason he couldn't tell Ryan the things he wanted to know, the reason why a recollection of a life he had never lived was posted on PRIME's website for the millions of fans to digest. His hands shook as he realized where he was heading. Even as a Toyota Prius sped towards him at a roundabout, whether due to acceleration problems or not, his mind stayed focus on the past where normally the horrible memory of being rammed off the road would have shaken him up worse than he was now with only his memories for company. He recognized the dilapidated state of the neighbourhood as the F-150 growled across the cracked blacktop. The last time he rolled through this neck of the woods was to meet Ryan for the first time, when the clasp was lifted on Pandora's box. Passing the shithole Comet bar and taking a left he found himself twenty years in the past. Nothing had changed. Small warehouses and business units gave what life they could to the barren lots and empty parks where government grants had denied the neighbourhood play parks where youths could binge drink in peace. The chain link fence running the length of the avenue to his right banked right onto Sibley, guiding him to a single broken brick wall with its jagged and crumbling edges. He remembered it well, having spent one summer dedicated to trying to push it over with his bare hands. It was in shipper shape than now, but it amazed him that it had stood so long. Beyond the wall, a line of sickly looking line of alder trees guarded a large redbrick and sandstone town house. In its glory days of the early 1900s it had served as a county court, standing proudly amidst a clutch of private businesses and two-up-two-down apartments. Twenty years ago it still held its vigour, its roof solid against the elements and those same alders bowing pitifully at its front door soaring into the sky and staring out over well-maintained and productive mills. As he parked up behind the Chevy beside the wrought-iron fence he felt his heart sink as low as the roof, which was sagging under the pressure of over a decade against the wind and rain. What windows he could see beyond the alder leaves were dank and covered in mildew. Veins and depressions peppered the brickwork from the sandstone foundation to the oak panelling bordering the top floors and attic. The sun shone brightly but a dark foreboding hung over him as he shot Ryan a smile and stretched the tiredness from his muscles. “Home sweet home.” Ryan remarked, oblivious to the state of the house having spent his whole life with it. “Christ, the old place hasn't changed much...” Von Kelsig answered, knowing fully that it had but refusing to add another dampener to what already promised to be a deadening experience. “Alright man, you ready for this?” Ryan asked as he pushed the front gate open, the rusted iron grating against the pavement. The giant felt his bones grinding with the scrape of the metal but nodded in response and followed Ryan through. As his palm grazed the bollard of the entrance he remembered its touch from years of jetting out into the street to play with his friends, then years later meeting a pretty young thing for a date. Exhaling loudly he stopped short of the front steps and took a look at the brown grass that made up the front lawn, stretched thin across the front of the house. “Ryan...” he muttered. “Yeah?” “Listen, when we go in here...don't tell them who I am. OK? Not at first.” he pleaded, more with his worried eyes than the nervousness in his voice. “Heh, dude they're gonna know who it is as soon as they clap eyes on you. That is if the pirate beard doesn't throw them off.” “Still...just call me Angelus. Alright? I don't want to jump into this at the deep end, take it slow, yeah?” Ryan rested his hand on the door, stained and chipped with flakes of paint and crud. Von Kelsig never once commented on the state of the house. “Whatever man. You're here. That's all that matters. I won't make it any harder for you.” With that he opened the door, a waft of damp and woodsmoke escaping from the darkness within as he skipped inside and called out to his folks. Standing immediately beyond the void, Von Kelsig felt his throat tighten and his skin leak with sweat as he followed Ryan into the house, unaware of what waited for him within...
