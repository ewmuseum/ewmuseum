A look at the past...(Part I)

12 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/12/05 It’s a rather cool September night, and there is a slight mist in the air. Nothing is visible in the darkness of the sky, but there are footsteps in the distance of the night. Boom Boom Boom The thuds get closer and slowly we see the outline of a large man. The man is dressed in shorts and a hooded sweatshirt. He walks down the block with his head held low, and he looks as if he is moving in slow motion with every step seeming to take minutes to complete. The man gets to a gap in between buildings and takes a right turn. He walks down the alleyway, and as he gets to the end a delightful aroma fills the air. The man walks into an unmarked doorway and noise takes the place of silence. The man takes off his hood, and reveals his face to be that of “The Polish Punisher” Tom Walczak. As soon as he enters an older lady comes over to him and gives him a big hug. “Tomek, what took you so long to come back here?” The accent is extremely thick and one could tell that she is a native of Eastern Europe. She has short red hair, and looks as if she is a happy grandmother seeing here grandson for the first time in a long time.“I had business to take care of Janina, but I came here as soon as I could. You know that I love your food more than anything in the world.” The lady blushes and as Walczak takes off his sweatshirt she quickly grabs it away from him, and puts it on a nearby coat hanger. “Come here, I got a seat for you right here. We could talk.” “We do have a lot to catch up on don’t we.” “Sit down, sit down you are probably tired.” The lady shows Walczak over to a nearby table, and pulls over a seat for him. “I’ll go get you some flaki, and then we can talk.” The lady heads back into the kitchen as Walczak makes himself feel more at home. He spreads out a little bit, and looks around for a sign of any familiar faces. I knew that Janina would be happy to see me. It’s just a shame that I haven’t seen her in so long. She was one of the few people that actually wrote to me while I was in jail, and she was the closest thing that I ever had to a mother. Walczak has a blank look appear on his face. I’ll never forget the first time that I met Janina. She was the first person in America to treat me like a human being and I think that’s because she knew the experience I was going through. Walczak begins to think back to that time. It was in December of 1990, and a bit of snow was coming down on the streets of Greenpoint, New York. This was shortly after Walczak had come to the United States, and he wasn’t at all prepared to make it through a cold and bitter winter. He has on the same clothes that he had when he left on that ship nearly two months ago. He also looks much different than the present day Walczak. He has somewhat long hair, and is not as tall or as big as in present time. Sneakers, a long sleeve t-shirt, and a pair of jeans had been enough to keep him warm for now, but the past couple of days have taken their toll on the young man, and although the boy is trying to be strong and hide his pain it is visible that he is hurting. Gdzie ja moge zostacz? Wondering where he could stay the boy looks for an empty stairway or some place that could provide some warmth. He can find no where to sleep so he just continues to walk. I thought that this country would be better than my old one, but it has done nothing for me. I thought that the land of opportunity would at least provide me with a place to sleep, but it has not even given me the simplest of life’s needs. Walking at a faster and somewhat angered pace a young Walczak continues to have no luck in finding a place of rest. His pace continues to quicken and he eventually runs into an alleyway. I just want to get away from this society. People look at me as if I was a freak, and all I want to do is to be one of them. I want to go to school, and learn English so that I too can live the life that these people live. As Walczak reaches the back of the alleyway he realizes that there is no where else to go. He turns and starts to make his way back, but realizes that it is hopeless. Bewildered, he starts to rapidly turn in nervous circles. He pounds his fist into a doorway and a loud thud echoes throughout the alleyway. Walczak crouches to the ground and tears start to stream out of his eyes. CREAK The door next to Walczak opens and a lady comes outside. She seems to be in her late forties early fifties, and she seems to be cautious as she open the door. “What the hell was that?!?!?!” She looks around and sees Walczak sitting on the ground. “Was that you? Did you just knock the shit out of my door?” Walczak is still slow to grasp the English language, so he has little to no clue what the lady is saying to him. “I….No….Speak any English.” The lady looks at him, but not in a weird or demeaning way. She seems to understand how the boy feels.“Czy pan mowi po Polsku?” A look of absolute amazement comes over the face of Walczak and for the first time since he has been in America a smile comes to his face. “Tak.” “Well, than come in and explain to me what a boy your age is doing outside in the cold.” The lady brings him inside. No people are inside but the lights are on, and tables are still set in case someone comes in to eat. This is unbelievable. I cannot believe that I have found someone who just took me in without asking questions. Wait? Maybe I am getting ahead of myself? Maybe she just wants to know why I punched her door, and than she will kick me out again? Whatever, it doesn’t matter I am warm now, and this should get me warm enough in case I have to sleep outside in the cold again tonight. The lady pulls him over to a nearby table and sits him down. “What is your name?” “Tomasz Walczak.” “Hello, Tomek, my name is Janina. Now, will you explain to me why you hit my door?” I knew it she just wanted to know why I hit the door. Oh well, I will try and make the explanation as long as I can so I can stay in here just a little bit longer.“I was outside, and I couldn’t find a place to stay for the night so I just nervously ran down this alleyway. I was pacing up and down and then all the anger just built up inside of me, and I hit whatever was closest. I just so happened that it was your doorway.” “Well, why the hell can’t you find a place to sleep? Did you run away from home or something?” “You could say that.” “What do you mean?” Walczak isn’t sure if he should tell the lady the truth or not. He is worried that if she finds out that he is an illegal alien that she will call the proper authorities and he will be shipped back to Poland to face his punishment, but finally with a deep sigh he begins to tell her his story. “I ran away from an orphanage in Poland a couple of months ago, and I have been trying to make a living for myself here, but I have had no luck.” “Yeah it’s hard to catch a break if you’re not from around here, but I’ll try and help you out for a little.” Walczak does a double take not being sure if he heard what Janina said correctly. “What do you mean that you will help me out?” “You can stay here for a little bit, but you will have to work for it. I’m going to put you to work, and in exchange you will be able to sleep in a cot in the back, and get to eat whatever you want.” “That is amazing. I will do whatever you want.” “You better, or else your ass will be right back on the street.” Walczak smiles and his face begins to turn red. The lady pats him on his head and she begins to get up. “I’m going to go get you some flaki. They should warm you up, and then you can go and rest up.” Just then Janina taps Walczak on his back and he wakes up from his day dream. “What were you thinking about?” “Oh nothing, just my next match.” “Well enough of that let’s talk about what you have been up to.” Walczak and Janina begin to talk, but in the back of Walczak’s mind are still those first days in America…
