What Is It You Want?

8 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 8, 2007 CUE: Mood – Esoteric Manuscripts ---- Easton’s abode was lonely, but he expected it to be lonely. Bare as bleached bones, he had spent a week trying half-heartedly to make it feel like home and it still felt like a prison, throw pillows nonwithstanding. He had gotten himself a Baltimore souvenier keychain like it would help, but one single key swung on it. Easton could extrapolate that into a metaphor for his current situation had he wanted to be bothered. He had spent the time meditating, establishing himself in Baltimore. Finding a dealer; that was relatively easy, but it was a step that needed to be taken. Making himself familiar in the town. None of that was really helping him, though. No matter how much he wanted to get out of this shitty little apartment suite, he always found himself back here. Physically or mentally, he was always drawn back. He sat on his only chair, looking at his only desk, or more specifically, his only phone on his only desk. He had been staring at it for hours, elbows on his thighs, hands hanging. His hair swung lightly in front of his face, straight and clumped in places, but he was used to it. It barely obscured his vision, transparent. All his senses were focused on that little morsel of technology, all screaming at it in unison. Ring. Fucking ring. He hadn’t been in contact with his wife for months. She had to be worrying about him, she had to be. She had to have heard of his arrest and release; both had been all over the news. She had to have been holding out hope, knowing that he couldn’t be the one to actually go so far as to kill someone, especially not a billionaire heiress. He imagined her sitting alone in their home, broken up, cradling herself and waiting for him to return to her. Easton was broken up, too. He didn’t know what had separated them – was it his chasing of the mystic or his schedule or what? – but whatever it was had kept them apart for far too long. He had seen her briefly, intermittently, but there was no true return, no Odysseus reclaiming his kingdom and his love, even if there wasn’t yet a Telemachus. He damn well hoped there weren’t suitors, not yet. He wanted to come back before that happened. He was scared, though. It already happened once, with a man he trusted, and worse, with a man he still trusted. Maybe Easton was gullible, naïve. He preferred to think that his friendship with Justin and the love he shared with Jeanne were stronger than that. It dug at him now, though. Maybe should have gotten madder. Maybe he should have shown Justin his fist. Maybe he— An obnoxious MIDI tune chirped from his phone and his eyes lit up. As he scooped the phone up and flipped it open, pressing it to his ear, a thought occurred to him: how would Jeanne know his phone number, anyway? "Mister Easton Hall?" came a slightly nasal voice that Easton could associate with a stereotypical middle-management wage-whore. "Speaking." "Hi, David Alansson, with PRIME. I just wanted to call and go over a few things with you." "Don’t waste my time," Easton said tersely, hand rubbing at his forehead. Fuck. He had spent so long sitting here and she didn’t even have his number? "Mister Hall, I think you need to hear this. For the sake of your job." "I mean," Easton grunted irritably, "get to it and don’t piss me off with corp-o-shit jargon." "Right." The guy was annoyed. Easton was annoyed. What a happy situation. "Well, Mister Hall, we just want to remind you of your… delicate situation. Don’t step out of line, because we can’t be lenient with you, not as things stand." "Great." "If you want a list of do’s and do-nots and that sort of thing, please don’t hesitate to drop by our offices or give us a ring. You have the PRIME HQ number?" Just like a big company to have an official list of morality. "Sure." Easton wasn’t a terrific liar, but not dragging out or words cut down on detection. This guy missed it. "Good. You’re aware of your booking this week?" "Bastille?" "Yes, sir. We wanted you to drop by a studio and cut us a promo to hype up this match. That’s not a problem?" Now, this of course wasn’t a question of whether it was actually a problem for Easton or not. This guy knew it was a problem. This was a vaguely diguised Do you want to keep your job? that Easton knew the answer to. "Nah. Gimme the longitude and latitude and I’ll be there." "What?" "I’ve got a Helio, it won’t be a problem." "What are you talking about?" Easton sighed. "I’m fucking with ya. Gimme an address so we can end this conversation." There was a pause on the other end of the line. "Certainly." CUE: Sage Francis – The Buzz Kill ---- Easton’s mind had been burning as he headed down to this studio. He had to get to Jeanne, he had to meet with her. He had wanted her to find him when she was ready, but if she didn’t know where he was, how could that happen? She was up in New York, too far for him to go in this short time and come back. He was going in the opposite direction, and then what? Across the world, all in service of the megagiant known as PRIME. It tore him up as he tore open the door, stepping into the well-lit studio. Only a few crew members were present, but it didn't really appear as if they needed too many guys. They were all glassy-eyed and stiff-limbed, the routine having turned them into fleshy robots. The sole animated android came up to Easton cheerily, his pretentious mustache seeming to bounce as he clapped Easton on the shoulder. "Hey, Easton Hall with PRIME, right?" "Yeah, that's me." "Great, awesome. I'm Chance Strumand, director." He held out his hand and Easton gave it a shake, his grip too hard to just be a show of manliness. Chance didn't wince, though; he was used to this kind of treatment. "You know what you're supposed to do, right?" He gestured to the large PRIME banner hung up over the far wall. "Of course." "Good, man, good. I don't really know what these promo things are supposed to look like; PRIME sort of just gave me a call yesterday and I'm not really… um… not really familiar with their product. You know?" Easton knew. The guy didn't look like a wrestling fan at all. "So we're gonna shoot you straight-on, no frills, and you just… you know. Speak your mind." Easton nodded, shrugging off Chance Strumand's shoulder. Chance Strumand's type was not the type that he liked to associate with, but he didn't figure PRIME just had agents in every city. Sometimes, you had to work with what was around. Easton ran his hand through his hair and moved in front of the banner, turning to face the single camera. He didn't try to look tough or sexy or even presentable. His thick, shaggy beard hung down over his chest and his eyes peered out past the hanging draperies of his brown hair, red with neurosis or the lack of sleep. Chance stood behind the camera, counting him down. He glanced up as the fist shook in the air, signaling him on. Easton looked straight into the camera, not speaking for a second. Chance gave him a slightly distressed look, his hand resting on his hip, leaning forward intently. Easton didn't even notice. "What… what is it that you really want from me?" Easton asked. "You drag me out here to talk about what? Some nobody named Bastille? Someone that no one except management has ever heard of? The fuck, do you even care?" He gestured behind the camera. "You pair me up with some know-nothing piece of shit and expect me to what? Come out here and give you a grade A promo? Fuckin' end the Palestine confict with these words?" Easton spat and glanced back at the camera. Chance's eyes seemed like they were going to pop out of his head, but once again, Easton refused to notice him. "Bastille, I'm gonna make you tap out. PRIME, I'm coming for the Universal title, sooner or later. Short, sweet, and fuck you. I don't have time for this." Easton glanced behind the camera. "Is that all?" It was. Chance Strumand wanted Easton Hall the fuck out of his tiny little corner studio. He had half a mind to burn the tape, but the money offered him made the decision for him. All Easton could think about was Jeanne. If only he knew what she was doing, how she was feeling. If she would ever take him back. The slight chill in the air shook him and he decided it was time to find some other shelter, be it a restaurant or his tiny apartment.
