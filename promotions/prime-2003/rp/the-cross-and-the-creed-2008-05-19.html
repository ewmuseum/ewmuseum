The Cross and the Creed

19 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 19, 2008 "... and that concludes tonight’s episode of ‘Who Wants to Flip a Millionaire’." Crucifix watched to see if Winston was making any attempt at getting up. The Englishman seemed content to remain on his back, staring up at the ceiling. The dirty canvas padding on the floor wasn’t very comfortable at the best of times, and being repeatedly thrown down onto it had been rather draining, not to mention painful. So while Winston remained horizontal, his eyes wide and his breath coming in short gasps, Crucifix bowed slightly and walked over to where his water bottle was propped up by the wall. He unscrewed the lid and lifted the bottom of his purple ring mask to quench his thirst. The other twin, Lynette, sat cross-legged on the remaining two clean towels in the Dojo. Ever since Wang disappeared weeks ago, the Dojo had taken a turn for the filthy. She opened her eyes and came face to knee with Crucifix’s purple workout pants. "Could I get one of those towels?" he asked, drinking from the water bottle a second time. Lynette looked up. "It’s not bad enough that you’re abusing my brother in front of me. Now you want to subject my body to the god-knows-what that’s smeared all over this floor?" A knock on the Dojo’s front door, and Crucifix felt a glimmer of hope. "Maybe that’s Wang. He’d lost his keys down a sewer drain and finally managed to fish them out. He’ll get this place clean in no time." "Only if he uses fire," Lynette muttered and closed her eyes again. Winston kept staring at the ceiling. Crucifix opened the front door, and the cool night air rushed in past him. Wang hadn’t found his keys. He hadn’t returned. Two men that Crucifix had never seen before were standing on his stoop. One man, a lean man with a sports coat and the top two buttons of his shirt unbuttoned, appeared to have been the one who had knocked. The man who stood on the steps behind him was the most ridiculously handsome man Crucifix had ever seen, who looked at the masked man with eyes as blue as the ocean. The thin one seemed annoyed not to have Crucifix’s attention. "Are you the one they call David Cross?" Crucifix looked at the smaller man and shrugged. "I guess it depends on who 'they' are. If you mean those two behind me, well they call me 'bastard' and 'the ugly one'. I suppose there are a few people who might call me David Cross." The Handsome One covered his smile with the back of his hand and turned away. The Thin Man didn't appreciate the funny. "We've brought you a message." "Well I don't see a copy of the Watchtower in your hand, so I'm guessing you're not Jehovah's Witnesses." Crucifix asked, "Who is this message coming from?" "Mister Creed would like to see you," said the Thin Man. Crucifix stepped out onto the stoop and closed the doors behind him. "Alex is awake?" The Thin Man shook his head. "Mister Creed, the senior." Surprised was one word that could have been used to describe Crucifix’s reaction. "Why in God's blessed but overused name would the great Kodiak himself, Vic Creed, want to see me after slapping a restraining order on me?" "Kodiak?" said the Thin Man with a wry smile. "Sabretooth's more like it." Crucifix caught the reference. "Oh snap! YOU call him that too?" The tension seemed a bit diffused between the three men, so Crucifix took a chance at getting some more information out of Creed’s henchmen. "What are your names?" he asked. "I'm Dean," the Thin Man said, "and this is Thor." Crucifix looked at the large man standing on the stairs. "Thor?" Dean explained. "It's short for Thorvaldson. Mikkel Thorvaldson." "So Thor works for Sabretooth?" Crucifix asked. "Marvel's going to have to choke a bitch." He looked at the big man, the part-Viking / part-Brad Pitt hybrid. "Where are you from?" Thor spoke with only a slight accent. "I'm from Longyearbyen, Svalbard." "..." "It's on the island of Spitsbergen." "..." "He's from Norway," Dean said finally. Crucifix nodded. "I should have guessed, given the chiselled manly features that just scream Sex Viking." Dean looked at Thor, who appeared a little uncomfortable. Realizing what he’d just said, and how it sounded, Crucifix tried to clarity was the best approach. "No no guys. Totally not gay," he said. "I'm all about the chicks. In fact, I have a beautiful blonde woman inside right now that wants nothing to do with me." "Is she the one that calls you Bastard or Ugly One?" Thor asked. "You know what? It doesn't even matter. Point is, boys, is if Vic wants to tell me something he can come here himself." Dean’s hand slid inside his coat as he took a step toward Crucifix. The stern tone he used told Crucifix that the fun and games were over. "If you want to see Alex again, you'll do as you're told." Dean’s hand never had a change to leave his coat. Crucifix had him by the wrist, and Dean could feel his breath through the mask that was suddenly inches from his face. "Is that threat coming from Vic, Dean? Or you?" The Viking was at Dean’s side, and Crucifix could feel those ocean-blue eyes on him. "Please to trust me," Thor told him. "It will be fine." That seemed to be reassurance enough. After all, would a Norwegian lie? "M'okay." Thor pulled a silver flask with an iron cross etched into it from his own coat, and offered it to Crucifix. "You drink, you sleep. You wake up, you see Alex." Crucifix became very aware of his appearance, wearing a purple karate Gi with his ring mask, and nothing on his feet. For a moment he felt quite vulnerable. Still, he needed to know that his friend was safe. When he noticed that Dean almost appeared to be sizing him up, Crucifix told him, "You touch me Dean, and I'll hurt you so bad they'll have to invent new words to describe the pain." Dean turned around and looked out into the dark street. There were no street lights, and most of the surrounding buildings were either empty or could not afford to pay for the power to run any kind of lights. With a single bulb illuminating the stoop, the Dojo was the shining star in the night. Crucifix watched him pull his hand out of his coat just as he disappeared into the darkness. The hand wasn’t empty. In this neighbourhood, that was probably a good thing. Crucifix unscrewed the top of the flask, lifted his mask, and gulped down the contents. He pursed his lips when he’d finished, the taste of sour apples sent a shiver down his spine. Within seconds, he felt that shiver spread from his spine to his limbs. His arm dropped to his side, his legs quivered, and his head became very heavy. The large Norwegian grabbed him by the shoulders to keep him from tumbling down the stairs onto the sidewalk. Crucifix felt that he should thank him for that, but he struggled for the words. "Thor?" he managed. "Yes?" The world seemed to fall away from him. Or was he falling away from the world? "Doessis mask mak' my butt look faaaaaaaaaa -" Dean pulled the car up to the bottom of the steps. He rolled down the window and looked up at Thor, who’d slung his purple bundle over his shoulder like a sack of potatoes. "He is quite strange," Thor said. "Open the trunk." -==- Crucifix felt the cold, clammy sweat all over his body before he could even open his eyes. He was trembling, and it was hard to breathe. He felt a heavy pressure on his chest, and the side of his face felt cold. That’s when he realized that he a) was lying on his stomach, and b) wasn’t wearing his mask. He thought about his hands, and it took a moment for the sensation to make the trip from his palms to his brain. The trembling or sweat might have had something to do with that. The message that made it to the brain said that he was lying on a smooth, hard surface. A floor. A polished floor, one without the slightest hint of grit. Okay, now for the eyes. What story could they tell? They rolled around in their sockets, picking up details that were sent to the brain and said "it’s dark in here." Since he couldn’t hear any movement nearby, and he didn’t feel like he was in immediate danger, Crucifix decided to simply lie where he was until the trembling subsided. Minutes later, the sweat and shakes had passed. More importantly, his strength had somewhat returned. He pulled himself off the floor and knelt with his hands on his knees, surveying the landscape. Yep, it was a dark room. "Great. I wake up and I'm in the abyss," he muttered. "Not quite," said a familiar voice behind him. The remaining rust shook loose from Crucifix’s brain, and shot adrenaline into his body. He leapt from his knees to his feet and spun around, arms outstretched but slightly bend, palms down, fists clenched. A long desk lamp clicked on, illuminating a small portion of the room. There was a large wooden desk sitting in front of what looked like a wall, but when the wall began to slide into the floor, Crucifix surmised it was actually more like giant blinds. The moon was high in the night sky, and the stars above paled in brilliance to the twinkling lights of the city below. The desk light and ambient light lit up more of the room. It seemed to be empty, save for the desk and the low wall sitting behind it. Crucifix knew it was no wall. It was the Devil. Vic Creed. It had been a number of years since Crucifix had laid eyes on the man. His white hair was still in the "high and tight" position, a militant look that Vic had kept for most of his almost twenty year wrestling career. His eight hundred dollar sunglasses were gone, another common staple to the self-professed "Main Man’s" apparel. He was almost never without them - he’d even worn those sunglasses the night he shot Crucifix in the face. Tonight, Crucifix looked into Vic’s eyes and saw the man who had tried to kill him. KILL HIM BACK his mind screamed, but his body knew better. He had to bide his time, let his strength return. Vic wasn’t the kind of man you attacked at less than one hundred percent. For the moment, Vic was holding all the cards. Crucifix would play the game, for now. "Where am I?" "You're in my office," Creed replied simply. The man’s voice boomed in the empty office, sounding much like the shotgun he’d unloaded in Crucifix’s face. "Your office?" Crucifix looked around the dimly lit room. The only piece of furniture was the desk, although he did notice a set of double doors to his left and a single door to his right. Good to know. As soon as he had the strength, he’d be throwing Vic out of the room... and doors weren’t going to be an issue in that equation. The office looked to be high enough off from the street below, that a dive through the window would kill even the mighty Creed. "Don't you have to actually DO something to justify having an office?" Crucifix asked with his hands on his hips. Creed laced his thick fingers together and rested his huge hands on the desk. He was wearing a black suit of some kind, custom-made to fit his tree trunk arms and a back you could show a movie on. "Little man, I don't need to justify a damn thing that I do. Especially to a nothing like you." "Right." Crucifix looked around again. There were no blinking lights, nothing that might reveal surveillance cameras. Good. No witnesses. He turned his attention back to the monster sitting behind the desk, and tried to use his peripheral vision to see if there was something on the desk he could use to plunge into Vic’s eye, ear, or maybe throat. "You really need to fire your decorator," Crucifix said. "Not a single thing here that screams ‘appease my ego! I used to be something once upon a time!’ Not even a portrait, although I don’t think paint could quite capture the ‘roid rage." Vic pressed his palms together. "I think your face does a better job of that." The sudden introduction of light coming from his left took Crucifix momentarily off-guard. Dean and Thor walk in through the double doors. While Dean continued into the room, Thor closed the doors behind them before joining Dean beside Vic’s desk. He almost seemed happy when he saw Crucifix standing nearby. "You are awake?" he asked. "I've got this taste in my mouth like dirty gym socks, and Dean I'm looking at you." Dean plaintively held his arms out to his sides. "Sir you told us to keep him quiet," he said to the big man behind the desk. "I would have thought rendering him unconscious would have accomplished that," Vic said. "He snores," Thor said. "You snore," he told Crucifix. "Where's my mask?" Thor patted the breast of his coat. "I'm holding it for you. It's okay." Crucifix looked at the three men in the room. "I gotta tell you Thor, things don't look very okay from where I'm standing." "If I wanted to do anything to you, idiot, I would have done it while you were asleep," Vic told him. "You’re just not worth the effort. You’re nothing." Crucifix opened his mouth to reply, but Vic quickly cut him off. "You’re adopted," he explained. "Your family isn’t your own. Your name isn’t your own. You stole your wrestling name from your friend. You stole wrestling moves from Alex. I gave you that look. Do you even know who you really are? Or what you really are?" Crucifix’s mouth hung open for a moment, searching for an answer. He shrugged. "I’m bored, that’s what I am." "Still trying to be funny, when you’re so very not." Vic picked up a pen and dropped it into one of the desk drawers. Crucifix was disappointed to see such an ideal weapon disappear. "That’s right! You have nothing of your own!" Dean chimed in. The Main Man behind the desk looked at his hireling with a very disapproving glance. "Busted, Dean!" Crucifix said and pointed at the thin man. "Sabretooth just gave you the skunk eye." When he noticed that Vic was now looking at him, Crucifix said, "His words Vic, not mine." Dean’s shoulders slumped. "We’ll deal with that issue later," Vic said. "Now I’m sure you’d like to see Alex." Thoughts of revenge disappeared for the moment. "Is he okay?" "If by okay you mean 'comatose', then yes he's still okay," Creed told him. "He's stronger, from what my specialists tell me." Despite his ruined face, it wasn’t difficult for Vic to read Crucifix’s expression. His eyes said it all. "Look, I don’t care if you trust me or not. I just want what’s best for my son." Crucifix nodded. "Riiiight. Like when you tried to gouge his eyes out. Or when you tried to cripple him. Perhaps I should remind you that you’re Vic Creed, resident psycho." "My therapist says I have sociopathic –" "Whatever!" Crucifix pointed at his head with both hands. "Look what you did to me face! You tried to kill me!" Vic looked down at the desk. "The drugs put me in a bad place." He lifted his eyes. "What you see before you, I have regained through hard work. My business. My body. There’s plenty of rage I still need to deal with, and if I don’t want to lose what I have I need to be clean to do it." "Yeah," Crucifix said in disbelief. "I look around this room and I see plenty for you to be angry about." He motioned toward the window. "Nice view, by the way. I’m not in the city anymore, am I?" "You are. You’re just in my city, not yours." Crucifix sighed. "Great." "And as far as what you see..." Vic’s hands disappeared behind the desk. A moment later, he’d navigated his wheelchair around to the front of the desk, only a few feet from where Crucifix stood dumbfounded. Creed looked down at the blanket covering his legs. "Looks can be deceiving." When he’d first seen that the Main Man was at a desk, Crucifix was worried that Lindsay Troy hadn’t destroyed him as much as they’d both thought she had. Seeing him in a wheelchair, a man who went by the wrestling moniker of the Alaskan Juggernaut, well that still seemed like a fitting sentence for his many crimes. No, not fitting. It wasn’t enough for what he’d done, but it would do. "The surgeon told me that if I had the procedure to repair the damage, I still would have had only a five percent chance of regaining the use of my legs. Plus, the day of the surgery fell during a Law & Order marathon. I’m a big fan of Hang ‘em High McCoy." Vic looked up at Crucifix. "Look, I knew a long time ago I was going to Hell. I just never thought I’d do it while I was still alive." "You’re saying you’ve changed?" "I’m saying that I am who I am, without the drugs. I’ve still got a twenty-six inch upper arm, I still use colourful language, and I’m still the last person you ever want to cross." Crucifix was rendered speechless. He was having a hard time wrapping his head around what he was seeing, and hearing. Still, crippled or not, that was Vic Creed sitting there. It wasn’t really a moral issue after that. "It just so happens that your arch-nemeses now are stairs." Creed didn't reply, but the comment clearly hit him hard. "My son is in the room at the end of the hall," Vic said, after a long pause. "Mikkel will show you the way." Thor nodded and walked up to Crucifix. He reached into his jacket and pulled out the purple fabric of Crucifix’s mask. As they pass by Vic, Crucifix quickly snapped a roundhouse kick at Vic’s head. Vic grabbed his shin before the blow could crush his face. He held it there a moment and the two men locked eyes. Thor placed a hand on Crucifix’s shoulder and squeezed, pulling him away from his employer. "I let you catch that," Crucifix said as he pulled his leg back. Vic wiped his hand on his blanket. "I let you think you did." The two men continued their stare. Crucifix looked at him with anger. Vic stared back with disgust. "I think I’ve been fair about all of this," Vic spat. "I’ve had the restraining order lifted. I brought you here, unharmed. I haven’t attacked you. In fact, you attacked a man in a wheelchair. Now I’m going to have Mikkel take you into the other room to see Alex. You’ll see he’s in no danger, and now that he’s no longer being poisoned he’s in fair health. Once you’re satisfied that Alex is safe, Mikkel will pour you another drink. You will drink it, and you will wake up at home, unharmed." "As long as Alex is with you, he’ll never be safe." "The court thought otherwise." "If this is your way of showing you’ve turned over a new leaf, I’m not buying it." "I’m not selling anything. You’re weak. You disgust me. I shot you in the face because you got in my way, and I don’t regret it. I’m doing this to save the treads on my tires." Thor kept his hand on Crucifix’s shoulder and guided him toward the door on the opposite side of the room. Crucifix shrugged the hand away and straightened his Gi. He turned his back on the crippled Kodiak. "You know she’s going to get to you eventually," he shouted over his shoulder as he walked away. Vic’s voice echoed off the walls of his office. "If the bitch parks in my handicapped parking stall, I’ll shove both my legs up her ass. From what I’ve heard, there’s more than enough room in there. Mikkel, show David, or whoever this freak is, to my son’s room. After tonight, I trust I won’t be seeing you again. If I do, I’ll make you wish the shotgun had been in your mouth when I pulled the trigger." Dean stated to follow after Thor but Vic held up a hand. "Dean? A moment please." He stopped in front of his employer. Vic wheeled himself back behind the desk. Dean watched his partner usher Crucifix through the door. Thor didn’t look back when he closed the door behind them. Once they were alone, Vic spoke. "Thank you. About this comic book nonsense." Dean looked as if he wanted to speak, to explain himself, but Creed silenced him with a hard look. "Now you know the type of people that read comic books, don’t you? They live in their parent’s basement, no lives to speak of, and the last time they saw a vagina was when it was pulled over their heads at birth." Dean was apologetic. "Yes sir, I’m sorry sir." "You disrespected me Dean. I can’t have that. I won’t. It’s bad enough that people see me as weak because I’m in a wheelchair. People will either respect me, or fear me." Vic shook his head. "Sabretooth. Do I look like a big cat to you?" "No sir." "Exactly." Vic nodded. "However I do have something in common with the extinct predator." His hand moved under his desk, and the double doors on the far side of the room slowly swung open. The clack-clack of something hard tapping against the tiled floor caught Dean’s attention. He watched as the biggest Rottweiler he’d ever seen trotted into the room. It wasn’t a pure Rotty, that was for sure. There was something mixed in, maybe German Sheppard as well as Horse. It was a big dog. Vic nodded at the beast. "You see... I have fangs." The dog’s head perked up at the name. "Fangs," Vic said and nodded at Dean. "Dinnertime." There was a slight growl as Fangs sprinted across the room, its pads and nails striking the floor made it sound like a train of death clattering down the tracks. Dean turned and bolted for the door on the other side of the room. To his credit, he almost made it before the dog brought him down. A second later, he screamed. A second after that, he was silent. Vic turned off the light and wheeled himself to the open doors, leaving behind the sound of wet, tearing meat. "Don’t worry Dean, I’ll send someone by to mop you up."
