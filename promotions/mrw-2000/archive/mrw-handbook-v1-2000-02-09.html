Midwest Regional Wrestling is a 'regional' promotion, although I'm assuming you've gathered that by the title. MRW is a part of the 'Alliance of Champions', which is an umbrella organization housing a few different promotions. Each of the promotion in the AoC is its own entity, with its own titles and such, but there is one MAJOR title that garners over all the AoC, which is the AoC Alliance World Champion. The MRW will be one of these such promotions.
The MRW is going to be your normal promotion, with standard rules for the most part, unless otherwise specified before.
Yes, the MRW will sometimes dab into the 'extreme', showcasing some of the more brutal matches for those hardcore fans.
Standard matches will be housed under regular wrestling rules, wins by pinfall, submission, or countout. The referee is also under contract to stop a match due to his decision, which most of the time will be disqualifications. Of course, that decision is going to be up to the referee himself. Some might just be a bit more lenient than others.
The MRW will be run out of its headquarters in Chicago, Illinois, home of its owner, '2 Smooth' Setzer Van Strife. The events of this promotion will be STRICTLY out of Wisconsin, Minnesota,
Illinois, Michigan, Indiana, and Ohio. There will be no travelling to other states, unless a large event with the entire
AoC is held outside of this region. This keeps the MRW as its name, a regional promotion. What is our objective here in MRW? Firstoff, it's for the handlers to have a good time. Interact with each other, come up with angles, you know, things like that. It takes a LOT off my back if I have everyone coming up with their own feuds, instead of spending my time trying to figure out who would feud well with whom. I'd also like to have everyone come into
MRW with an open mind, and come to the realization that you will NOT win all the time. Please don't expect to. If you handle a guy that was 123-0 in the XCGWKJAF, it doesn't mean he'll go
123-0 in the MRW. That's how it is. I don't enjoy having someone e-mail me expecting their wrestler to block and counter
EVERY MOVE in the book. It's not possible. Bottom line - If you expect to have an invincible wrestler, don't join the MRW. 2. Who runs MRW? (Fantasy Purposes) The MRW is headed by the CEO, Setzer Van Strife. Although Van Strife is also a wrestler himself, he always wanted to have his own promotion to watch over. When the AoC came to Van Strife with the idea, he seized the opportunity to create a promotion in his hometown, with the surrounding states also having access to see the action. With the midwest being one of the greatest wrestling regions in the world, Van Strife quickly bought into the idea. This didn't come with some financial help, though. Van Strife needed someone to watch over the MRW while he was away wrestling.
That is when he contacted long time friend Jason Wnuk. Van Strife and Wnuk went into a joint venture, and Wnuk is the Chief Operating Officer. He is most likely the man that will be seen both behind the scenes AND in front of the camera, since Van Strife will more than likely be busy handling promotion, as well as his wrestling career. Van Strife has given
Wnuk FULL creative control, so look for Wnuk to turn the MRW into the best region the AoC has to offer. With the combined financial stature of both men, look for the MRW to TRY and keep up with technology, though for now, there's not any kind of "JumboTron", big pyro displays, or anything like that. Keep that in mind when deciding whether or not to join.
3. Who runs MRW? (Real-Life Purposes) The MRW is run by none other than [name withheld]. I'm 20 years old, and have been in e-wrestling since Spring of 1997. I've tried once before to run a fed that really never got off the ground, but
I was still a bit wet behind the ears. I am now confident that I can do a good job with the MRW. I will be doing a bulk of the match writing, booking, and putting together the flash show, which will most likely be nothing more than a flash packet. I will TRY my damndest to get all
MRW-related information out ON TIME... however... don't bite my head off. I know how frustrating it is to wait.
From time to time I WILL be asking, sometimes begging, for people to write matches. Now, in the AoC, matches are normally anywhere from 6k to 12k... not too much considering what some feds do.
Plus, it WILL count towards a push for your wrestler, nothing too huge, but it will be put into consideration. Plus, those that normally get upset about late cards don't go ahead and take initiative to write them themselves. So, if I hear bitching about that, I'll offer you a match to write. Remember, I have a life as well. =) I'm not god.
4. What are the titles in MRW? In addition to the AoC Alliance World Title, the MRW recognizes three titles. One of these titles is for ALL singles wrestlers, one is limited to those wrestlers known as 'juniorweights', and the last is for tag teams. As time goes on, and as the need for another title arises, the MRW may or may not decide to add another title, depending on the need and the handlers' response. Rest assured, ALL handler will have a say when a new title is discussed.
4.1 MRW Midwest Heavyweight Title The MRW Midwest Heavyweight Title is the top prize in the MRW, however, when it comes to AoC events, it is just a minor title, probably more closely to an Intercontinental or United States
Title. The man that holds this title will usually be the one that is the number one contender to the AoC Alliance World
Title when the MRW's turn comes around. The title will be defended every two weeks, unless the holder gets a chance at the
AoC World Title. If the need also arises, and the champion answers a challenge to his title, the MRW Executive
Committee will meet to see if it deems fit. 4.2 MRW Juniorweight Title The MRW Juniorweight Title is reserved for those wrestlers that are 240lbs. and under. This is considered the MRW's secondary title, and if not enough wrestlets fit the demand for this category, the MRW will change this title to something a little more geared towards a secondary title, most likely a television title. But that's just in case. The holder of this title will be the number two contender to the AoC Alliance World
Title, when it comes to be MRW's turn at it. This title will be defended every two weeks, unless the holder is going for the AoC Alliance World Title. And, as with the
Midwest Heavyweight Title, if the champion answers a challenge, the MRW Executive Committee will meet. 4.3 MRW Midwest Tag Team Titles The MRW Midwest Tag Team Titles will be held a little differently than most tag team titles. The idea came from SVS's affiliation with the NLWP, who uses the idea of singles wrestlers forming as tag teams, instead of actual tag teams themselves. The singles wrestlers are responsible to find themselves a partner, or be forced into a random partner situation. These teams will then fight to find out who the best team is.
The MRW Tag Team Titles will be defended every two weeks as well, just like the other two titles. And, as with the other two titles, if the champs answers a challenge, the MRW Executive Committee will meet to discuss if it will be a title match or not.
5. What are the shows? 5.1 Alliance Championship Television (ACTV) This is the AoC's weekly show. 'Alliance of Champions' has a weekly show called ACTV or Alliance Championship Television.
The show is simply two hosts sitting in a TV studio. Picture something like SportsCenter. One of the hosts opens the show and sends it to the first region. Then that region will run its 3 matches (more if they like) and do whatever. In rings, dark matches, whatever. It essentially covers all of the regions in one big show, in which the AoC Alliance World Title will be defended. The MRW's section of the show will more than likely have more then 5 matches.
This show will be broadcast every Tuesday night. 5.2 Midwest Mumblings This will be MRW's roleplay packet. It will come out once a week, normally the day before ACTV. This is an open forum for MRW's wrestlers to voice their opinions about past matches, upcoming matches, or anything else they have in mind.
This show will be broadcast every Monday night. 5.3 Supercards and Pay Per Views. MRW will occasionally run a 'supercard', essentially a 'Clash of the Champions' type thing, every couple months. This will be a card to end/start feuds, and have EVERY champion defend their particular title. The names of these 'supercards'
will be determined at a later date. More than likely, the first card the MRW will hold will be a supercard, with a tournament/battle royal to determine the inaugural champions. We'll then take it from there, with a supercard being held every other month for the most part. It's all up to the handlers.
6. Who are the staff members? 6.1 Announcers 6.1.1 Evan Brown Brown is a 30-ish male, who is keen to the sport of wrestling. He knows what he's talking about when he speaks, and will be the one to call it right down the middle, hardly ever showing leaning one way or the other. You can't get much more professional than Evan Brown. Always quick to give an opinion of a wrestler, either good or bad, Brown studies the background on many wrestlers before even agreeing to calling one of their matches.
6.1.2 Brandon Williams Williams is a broadcast major out of the University of Illinois-Chicago, and is also one of Van Strife's best friends.
Van Strife decided to give him a chance as a favor, to give him the break he can use later down the road. Like any typical college male, he is into alcohol, women, and more alcohol. However, he is a HUGE wrestling fan, and tends to 'mark out'
when certain wrestlers enter, or when certain wrestling moves are executed. He also enjoys cracking on certain people, and is often times known to side with the 'bad guy'.
6.2 Interviewers These are the people you use when you wish to have someone interview your wrestler in a flash.
6.2.1 Joey Masters Masters lives up to his last name. He is calm, cool, and collected, the perfect person to run an interview. His questions are always straight to the point, he never dances around what he really wants to ask. Most wrestlers enjoy his style, while others feel like choking him. But no matter what, Masters always nails the interview.
6.2.2 Isabella Martinez Martinez is a sweet, cute, and VERY flirty 25 year old. She always gets the attention of the guys in the locker room, and can sometimes be seen cuddling up to a wrestler to take away an interview from Masters. She is always paying attention to her looks, and LOVES getting comments about them. However, she'll also get a good interview from a wrestler.
7. How do I succeed in MRW? 7.1 Roleplaying Roleplaying is the backbone of the MRW. It shows you're actually giving an effort to be active, as well as gaining heat and showing what your character is all about. It is the BIGGEST factor in deciding what your wrestler's push will be like, and who wins or loses their match. Please keep the following in mind as your write your roleplay for MRW.
1. SWEARING IN ROLEPLAYS. Bottom line here - if you hear it on television, go right ahead and use it... words like 'ass', 'damn', 'bitch', and things like that are absolutely fine. Anything else isn't cool to use, and you know what I mean. If you absolutely MUST use those words in roleplays or in-rings, please use
[CENSORED] or [BLEEP]. No, not even doing stuff like f**k or anything like that is cool. We know what you're trying to say.
2. ATTACKS IN ROLEPLAYS. Refrain from attacking/beating/killing/maiming other wrestlers, announcers, commentators, refs, executives, or anything else you don't handle yourself. If you would like to do so, PLEASE contact the handler and get permission. Otherwise, I get bombarded with e-mails asking why I let
so-and-so attack them when they didn't know. 3. KAYFABE IN ROLEPLAYS. We are the MRW, not the WWF, WCW, or ECW. Don't mention those real-life promotions, nor any of their wrestlers. Please refrain from naming other promotions in e-wrestling as well. It's not that I don't like giving them the plug, but the focus on your roleplays should be the MRW, not any other fed you might be in. Also, for purposes of this hobby, try not to say 'e-wrestling' or 'e-wrestler'. We try to keep this as real as possible in MRW, so it should actually be considered as a real promotion. Not to sound nerdy, but it looks weird if someone says 'e-wrestling'.
4. FORM OF ROLEPLAYS. Normally, I really don't care how you format your roleplays. I would really like it in a certain fashion, that being the following, however, if you have different ways of writing your roleplays, go right ahead.
[ThA TrU RuFF RydAH stands in the ring, sipping a 40oz.] TTRR: YEEEEEAH BOYEEEEEE. 5. LIMIT TO ROLEPLAYS. In MRW, you get one roleplay per week for that week's Midwest Mumblings. This will count towards your win/loss in your match, or give you heat for another week if not wrestling that week. You may also send in an in-ring or a pre-match interview for each week's ACTV, but those won't count as much towards a win or loss, though it might count if the match gets really close, and I need a deciding factor.
7.2 Strats A wrestler's strategy, or 'strat' for short, helps the match writer out IMMENSELY. We can see what moves you'd like to use, how you'd like to defend an opponent's offensive attack, and even interfere in other wrestler's matches. They hold some merit on booking, as one of the final deciding factors if its close. They also let you have complete control over your character, instead of letting a match writer run him ragged. A simple strat looks like this:
ENTRANCE: How you want your wrestler to enter. OFFENSE/DEFENSE: Moves your wrestler will use on offense or defense. Also, any bumps or spots you'd like to see. WIN/LOSS: What your wrestler will do if he wins or loses. ANYTHING ELSE: Other things you'd like to include... attacks in other matches and the like.
7.3 Angles Angles are the lifeforce of MRW, and of e-wrestling in general. It's what makes you guys have fun, and actually want to send in roleplays and strats. I will try as hard as I can to get someone into an angle, but frankly, I can't be a creative genius for all of you. What I would rather have is for ALL of you to create your OWN angles with others. Get ahold of fellow MRW handlers, ask them if they're interested, and go from there.
And please, if you can, make sure you let me know of your intentions beforehand. I don't want any of these "I was planning on taking a spear and slicing my opponent's arm off to start the angle off". I'll probably laugh before telling you no.
:) 8. Booking Policy The MRW is going to be a booked fed. I will be the judge of who wins and who loses. I will make my decision based on the roleplays, the quality of strats, the angles you're in, as well as the amount of heat your character has. Also, like I said before, not a huge factor but what might be a deciding factor is your availability to write matches. There will be reasons behing why you win or lose, and if you're curious, please ASK and don't BITCH to me. I have an EXTREMELY low tolerance for bitching, so don't do it, because I might just boot you.
I'll give reasons if you ASK. I'll tell you to pretty much go to hell if you decide to bitch at me. That's how it is.
9. Where do I go to help out? Well, here in the MRW, we can always use the help. Whether it's with match writing, coming up with angles, or anything like that, it would be appreciated, and make the MRF run a lot more smoothly. Otherwise, any suggestions, comments, or anything else that you would think would make MRF better is always appreciated. Just drop me a line.
10. Joining - Application Alright, if you've sat through this entire thing, and read it all... and you STILL would like to join, please fill out the application below. Once you've filled that out and sent it to me,
I'll review it, and get back to you. If the need arises, there will be a waiting list, and it'll be used according to longest waiting. The roster WILL freeze at 20, and will not go above that ever, unless the MRW takes over the entire AoC.
