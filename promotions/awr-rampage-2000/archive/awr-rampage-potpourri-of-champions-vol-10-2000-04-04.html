Potpourri of Champions, Vol. 10

4 April 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
----------- Derek Diamond ----------- [The camera opens in a small hotel room in an out of the way Ramada. A red duffel bag hangs from the back of the room’s only chair and a pair of khaki slacks and a blue "button down" shirt are draped over the chair’s arm. The sunlight has just begun to drift in through the windows casting shadows across a sleeping figure. Sprawled out across the queen-sized bed is Derek Diamond. Diamond, dressed in a pair of gray sweatpants and a white "wife beater," tosses restlessly as the light crosses his faces and pierces his slumber. Groggily he stretches and wipes the sleep from his eyes. He yawns and sits up in bed, stretching his arms and rolling his head to either side. He stretches out his legs and pushes against the wall in an attempt to expel the last remaining thoughts of sleep from his body. He looks at the camera and smiles]
[Derek Diamond] Geez, you guys don’t sleep much, huh? Let me grab a shower and I’ll meet ya down stairs in the restaurant, k?
[The camera begins to back out of the room and Diamond climbs out of bed. The camera fades to black, the camera fades in on the modest hotel restaurant. A buffet has been set up on a pair of adjoining card tables and is covered with bacon, watery scrambled eggs, toast, a carafe of milk and a bowl full of single serving boxes of cereal. The room is full of businessmen and truck drivers, all preparing for a day of work or travel. Diamond, in the pair of khaki slacks and blue shirt last seen in his room stands at the buffet, back to the camera, filling his plate. He walks to the table and sits; the camera closes in on his upper chest and head. He pulls a hat from under the table and tugs it down on his head]
[Derek Diamond] Thought I’d let ya see how a real wrestler lives. Nothin’ fancy ‘bout my life. But it’s honest and that’s what I like
‘bout it. I’ve never claimed to be the next big thing like some guys or even the greatest thing since sliced bread but I’d like to think I’ve got a future in this biz. Maybe earn enough to stop driven my broken down Nova [grins] get me somethin’ with more than three horses under the hood, that’d be pretty sweet. Comin’ up pretty soon I’ve got my debut match in AWR and I’m getting’ mighty psyched. Can you imagine walkin’ the same aisle as some of the sport’s legends? [nods and grins] I can and it’s makin’ me all kinds of nervous. Yeah, I’ll admit it, I’m nervous. But who wouldn’t be? Only a liar would tell ya otherwise and
I’ve made a rep outta speakin’ the truth, no matter what. So, I’m nervous. But at the same time I’m confident I’ve got the skills to make it and next week I get the opportunity to show ‘em off. Come
Tuesday
I’m gonna be standin’ toe to toe with Tiger Muni [nods, smiles and his blue eyes seem to glisten] ‘course I outta mention there’s more than one way to skin a cat. I plan on showin’ off at least four of ‘em.
[Diamond takes a bite of the eggs and grimaces. He puts the fork back down on his plate and takes a bite from a slightly burnt piece of toast. He washes that down with a gulp from his glass of orange juice and shakes his head at the thought of having breakfast with the fans of the AWR]
[Derek Diamond] I’ve heard a lot of talk outta the older guys [nods] ‘bout pro-wrestlin’ bein’ ruined by the new guys, ‘cause some of the newguys have a little more glitz and pizzaz then they’re used to. Now,
I’m not sayin’ its bad to be old school but you gotta remember that times change and maybe your time is just runnin’ out. I got the upmost respect for Tony Virtue and Mark McMahon, heck I’m thrilled to be able to compete against guys with track records like theirs. ‘Course that’s contingent on the fact that they realize just ‘cause I’m not [grins]
‘old school’ doesn’t mean I haven’t got talent or respect, for the guys that made this sport. ‘Cause I do, on both counts. I just like the thrill of hearin’ the crowd cheer. It’s my main reason for bein’ in this sport. But you can’t discount my love of competition and the beauty of the physicality of the sport. There’s just somethin’ wonderful ‘bout a perfectly executed moonsault. Know what I mean?
Look I gotta get to New York. It’s time this Diamond glistened in the brightlights of the Big Apple. [smiles]
[Diamond pushes back from the table and tosses his napkin on top of his plate. He gives the camera a point and walks out of the restaurant.
FADE] ----------- Sleepwalker ----------- (The scene opens up in a cemetary. It is nighttime in this New York cemetary and the camera is getting a good shot of all the headstones and mausoleums across this dark and dreary place. In the distance, walking across the grounds, is a lone figure, carrying a lantern on a stick. As the camera closes in on this figure, we can see that it is AWR wrestler,
Sleepwalker. He has his gas mask on, and is dressed in a plain navy blue jacket with black pants and boots. We finally get to a tree where Sleepwalker puts the lantern down and stares at the camera. He backs away from it and leans on the tree where he sinks down into a sitting position.)
SLEEPWALKER: Hope. For a lot of people, it's all they have. They live their whole life without ever having a piece of something great. They live their life in search of relief, in search of happiness, in search of love.
They never find it, but the fact that they're looking gives them hope. They'll never stop looking. Tommy Payne... do you have hope? Are you looking for happiness? Are you looking for love? Are you waiting for a better day? We're scheduled to be in a hardcore rules match. We're scheduled in a match where the referee can't say or do anything to me to pull me off of you. The referee won't be allowed to stop the onslaught that I plan on unleashing onto your body.
Maybe you shouldn't look for happiness or love yet, Tommy. Right now, you should be looking for a coroner.
Let's examine what happened at our last match... you beat me. Plain and simple. I'll accept that. You were the better man that night. I heard three slaps on the mat. I was too weak to kick out. I let you beat me.
I now have to live with that every day OF EVERY HOUR THAT I LIVE! I let you, a weaker vessel, some happy, smiling "Hero", who poses for pictures and portrays himself as the epitome of everything that is good, beat me. I haven't been able to sleep, Tommy. I see you everywhere. I have dreams now where I dismantle and destroy you, just for you to reappear and I have to destroy you all over again. Fine. You were the better man that night,
Tommy...
maybe hope came through for you. In our next match, Tommy... will your hope pull you through? I unleashed pain on you before. I plan on unleashing even more this time.
I was a hard man to put down before. I'll be an even harder man to put down now. Pray that I know when to stop, Tommy. Pray that I'll take some sort of pity on you and I won't go all the way. Hardcore rules. Anarchy. Violence.
I don't have hope anymore Tommy. All I have is pain. I'm the Sleepwalker. Scream, because no one will hear you. (scene fades) ----------- "Disgusting" Richard Dorkus ----------- [The scene opens to a local gym in downtown Chicago. Many huge men and woman roam around the place, but only one HUGE man can be seen sitting in the corner, eating popcorn. That man is none other than
"Disgusting" Richard Dorkus. Dorkus, wearing a white t-shirt which seems to be about ten sizes too small for him and a pair of denim shorts, sits in the corner, alone while enjoying the popcorn which he is stuffing down his throat. He looks up at the camera, with about twenty bits of popcorn in his face, some of it around and stored in his bushy beard. He slowly...
VERY slowly, gets back to his feet and walks over to the camera. Once he gets there, with his mouthful of popcorn, he speaks]
"D" RD: Hayee theyare..... [He bites, crunches, and finally swallows. He then speaks, this time with nothing in his mouth]
"D" RD: Sorry about that, you caught me at a bad time... Well, it was a good time for me, because I was eating, but bad time for you, the viewers... You know what I mean, I hope!?!
[Dorkus then turns his head and faces a guy, probably mid-twenties, very fit, running on the treadmill.
Sweat is pouring off the man's head as he runs. Dorkus turns around shaking his head] "D" RD: How can he put himself through that much pain? Just looking at him wares me out.... And makes me even hungrier!
[Puts his hand in the giant popcorn packet and pulls out several more to place in his mouth. "Yum yum" and
"Mmmm" can be heard coming from Richard's mouth. Instead of watching this horrible site, we pan around the gym. We see several people, some of the treadmill, some on the bicycle and rowing machines, some doing chin-ups and some doing weights. We then pan back to where Richard was standing but has now vanished. The camera turns around and surprisingly, see's him on the treadmill, tapping in some instructions. The camera goes over. The platform then starts to work but VERY slowly. All Dorkus has to do is walk VERY slowly. While he walks, he eats, before turning back to the camera and speaking]
"D" RD: Now this is what my idea of heaven is! .... A nice stroll and a nice packet of popcorn. Still, I would've preferred a big, fat, juicy, greasy...
CHEESBURGER!! [Dorkus goes into a day-dream for a few moments. He then shakes his head out of the daydream and speaks again]
"D" RD: Anyway, enough about my pleasures, I wont waste anymore of your time, or my time, so let's get down to business.... Yes, the business I'm talking about is ... The A...W...R.
"I have been told that the AWR is a small promotion, that is new and trying to progress into a top federation. Well, I agree. If I thought the AWR would stay a 'small' promotion, I wouldn't've joined.
I HATE the word _SMALL_! ... BIGGER IS BETTER!" [Suddenly, the platform starts to move faster, much to Dorkus' dislike. He presses all the buttons to try and slow it down, but instead it gets faster. He looks worried, then shouts]
"D" RD: HELP! .... SOMEBODY HELP ME!!!! [Luckily for Dorkus, a very tall and well built guy comes along and presses the button to stop the machine]
STRONG GUY: Just press this big red button in future. It'll stop the machine, ok? "D" RD: Thanks dude. [The strong looking guy, raises his eyebrows, looks at Dorkus and then walks off. Dorkus steps off the platform and starts to breath heavily. Sweat, already is pouring off his face only after a slight jog for a few seconds]
"D" RD: DAMN! I could've died then which means.... NO MORE FOOD! NOOOOOOOOOO! [Everyone inside the gym, looks around at Dorkus who is going mad at the thought of no more food as the scene fades out]
----------- "Gemini" Todd Phelps ----------- [The scene is in a suburban house. In an easy chair sits Todd Phelps, watching the NCAA tournament on a big screen TV. He clicks the TV on mute and faces the camera.]
TODD: Reality. We need to talk about reality because many in the AWR aren’t facing up to it. First, we have Devon Case. Case, two weeks ago, you lost an important match against McMahon because you can’t face reality. And since you really can’t, then I’ll lay it all out simply for you. The reality of the situation is, Case, that you think you can go around and make just as many enemies as you want to and still think that you can come out on top. Uh uh, not going to happen. Case, making enemies is only going to get you beat down after beat down every week. You cannot go out and lay out every single person. Now, this doesn’t mean that you have to buddy up to everyone.
After all, there are men that I don’t like as well, but I get along with the general population pretty well. And it’s always important to have some support.
[Todd points at the TV.] TODD: Just like the NCAA tournament. The reality is that only one team can win it all. Only one team can come out on top, and so only one man can win it all in the AWR. Right now, it’s McMahon. But one thing the man on top always has is support. Whether it’s from the fans, from friends, or from teammates, there is always that measure of support in a champion. Take this lesson to heart, Case.
[Todd rests back in his chair once again.] TODD: Which brings me to this week, and yet another man who needs to face reality, even worse than Case.
"Overhype" Dr. Barkley needs a serious dose of reality, and I’m just the man to give it to him. He believes that he’s popular. He believes that he’s rich, and worst of all, he truly believes that thing he calls a car is the most prized possession on earth.
Let me lay it out to you Dr. Barkley. You’re not popular. You’re not rich, and that car is nothing more than a piece of trash. Heck, I’m betting my old
Matchbox cars that I’ve had since I was a kid are twice the value of that thing. [Todd sits up and points straight back at the camera.] TODD: And you know what? I doubt that Dr. Barkley takes this lesson seriously now, so I figure that I’ll have to teach it straight to him in the ring this week. One thing about this lesson is that the cold, hard slap of reality is a lot worse than any move that
I could lay on you, but maybe when it’s over, you’ll truly realize the work and the heart that a guy has to have to become a champion.
[Todd leans back once again.] TODD: And if that really does happen, I’ll have succeeded once again. [Scene fades out.] Sent via Deja.com http://www.deja.com/ Before you buy.
