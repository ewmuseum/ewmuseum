AWA Rebuilding, Looking For New Members

6 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
After a brief hiatus, the AWA (http://awastars.netfirms.com) is re-opening its doors and is looking for fresh talent. What Is The AWA? Born in the fall of 1997, the AWA began as a regional federation, then known as Steel City Wrestling. Shortly following it's two year anniversary, the SCW filed bankruptcy due to undisclosed reasons. Two months later, the SCW reopened, now under the name of Pennsylvania State Wrestling. Lasting only a few short months, the PSW fell under financial hardship, opting to sell its name rather than fold entirely. Now under the reigns of former PSW superstar turned promoter Frank Justice, the PSW flourished, soon adding itself to the elite ranks of the World Wrestling Alliance. After the collapse of the WWA in May of 2000 Justice sold his portion of stock in the PSW to former tag team partner Stu Anderson. Under the control of Anderson, the PSW once again gained prominence. But all was for naught, as a black cloud once again fell over the PSW. Unable to procure a stable television deal, Anderson began to seek outside funds to support his failing enterprise. Seeking out his old friend Justice, Anderson devised a stunning, yet not unheard of, plan to gain popularity within the wrestling community at large. Renaming the PSW the American Wrestling Alliance, Anderson divided the stock of the AWA between himself and Justice. Each in firm control of 50% of the company, neither could make a financial decision without the approval of the other. The AWA thrived for two years before a hostile takeover in the form of Justice's daughter, Madison Justice, forced the AWA to close its doors in the fall of 2002. Reconciling with his daughter, Justice and Anderson have set about reopening the AWA. How Does the AWA Work? The AWA is a booked federation. This means that the quality of your roleplays or angle suggestions directly affects your success. Be aware however that it is a sad fact of the wrestling world that occasionally people get jobbed. If it improves an angle you may well find yourself jobbed from time to time; the AWA is all about entertainment and sometimes losing a match adds to that entertainment. Whatever the outcome of the match it will not be discussed. Everybody, including Hogan, Austin, and The Rock loses a match at some time or another and so will your wrestler !!! If I receive an e-mail complaining about the result of the match it will be ignored, if I receive another the handler that sent it will be warned, one more and he/she will be fired. The best way to cope with a loss is to pick yourself up and get on with some decent role-playing. Each handler may take control of ONE singles Wrestler and ONE Tag-Team, but will be expected to fulfill the role-play requirements for each one, i.e.- two per week for the singles wrestler and two for the tag-team. Requests for additional wrestlers will be considered but this will require extra role-plays, simply doing a joint role-play for all of your wrestlers will not be enough. Roleplay FAQ Role-plays are the cornerstone of the AWA. It is down to the discretion of the individual handler as to what goes into their role-plays but the following must be borne in mind when writing your role-plays: Swearing is permitted but only to a degree. Try and think about the sort of thing you usually hear in the WWE & WCW. This rule is fairly relaxed but if any particular handler constantly over uses swearing of an unacceptable nature he/she will be warned once and then fired. Absolutely no personal attacks on any other handlers in the federation. As far as the wrestlers/characters are concerned handlers don't even exist. Do not speak out of character. If_you have something to say from a personal point of view you should do so by means of a private e-mail and not under any circumstances passed off as a role-play. Never include other handlers wrestlers in your role-plays without their permission. If, for example, you role-play and have your wrestler attacking and beating up another handler's wrestler without their permission, you will receive one warning and one warning only. If it happens again then you are gone. The idea behind role-playing is that it is what your guy can do and say, not attacking someone else when they can't defend themselves in another role-play. If_you are unable to role-play for any reason, such as holidays, exams, or illness mail the President and say so. It will be necessary to provide an estimate of how long you will be out of action in order that you can be removed from future cards. If you are a title holder and your expected absence takes you beyond the mandatory defense period [see below] you may, depending on the circumstances be stripped of the belt. AWA Championships There is no limit on how may titles one wrestler may hold ... you get what you earn. Don't expect a champion to have to defend his belt week after week. Title defenses are compulsory in only one of three weeks except the Television title which has to be defended every card. This does not, however, prevent a fighting champion from putting his belt on the line on each and every card if that is what he wants to do. All titles WILL be defended at the joint PPV on the last Sunday of every month. Here is the breakdown of titles in the AWA: AWA Championships World Heavyweight Championship World Tag Team Championship International Championship Television Championship Creative Direction Generally speaking the direction that you want to take your wrestler in is left entirely to you. It is down to you to contact other handlers, arrange feuds, or form alliances. From time to time you may be asked by the owner to begin a certain angle with your wrestlers. He may want you to begin feuding with another handler or simply say that your current gimmick is going nowhere and suggest an alternative. You do not have to comply with this request. It is a request alone and not an order of any kind. The intention behind an angle request is a suggestion only. Refusal will not have an adverse effect on your wrestlers push. Cards The AWA runs two Cards a week: Revolution, appearing on Tuesdays, and the Saturday Night All-stars. Both appear via pay-per-view; a brilliant idea by Anderson to save production costs and avoid jumping through the many hoops presented by the FCC. These are the cards that are generally used to build angles and generate heat between wrestlers. In addition to this there is a joint pay-per-view, Sunday Showdown, scheduled the final Sunday of every month. When it comes to card writing, we here at the AWA take a different approach. From past experience, I learned that many people enjoy full, well written cards, most simply scan through, looking for the bits and pieces that may contain their wrestler or that of a wrestler they maybe currently engaged in an angle with. So to alleviate unwanted strain on our writing staff, the AWA relies on a unique, yet simplistic approach. Instead of boastful cards, chalked full of useless dialogue and move descriptions, we write in a summary style. If you've ever visited a wrestling news site, you'll understand what I mean. For those of you who haven't, let me elaborate. Our cards will focus, not on the match details per say, but rather on the overall flow of the match; noting major spots and the final match outcome. All non-match segments will be presented in a condensed format, however promos written by the handler will be included in their entirety. You'll get the feel of the event without all the extra fluff. This makes the individual roleplay all that more important; requiring the handlers to interact more closely with each other and expand upon angles. Revolution and SNAS will both have 5 matches each. These matches will be given to people who regularly roleplay_and are a first come, first served basis. The angle and roleplay deadlines for the cards are at 6 PM Monday for Revolution and 8pm Friday for SNAS. Sunday Showdown will contain between 6-8 matches depending on the amount of heat going into the card and the demand for matches and all angles and roleplays are due by 12PM Saturday. Sneak attacks, etc ... are permitted, and to make one you must e-mail a request to the Owners no later than 24 hours before the appropriate card. Do not think that every sneak attack will be permitted. If the attack is pointless or if you are seeking to attack someone who is already heavily involved in angle with someone else it will not go ahead. The attack must advance an angle. As far as stables are concerned any angle requests that include your stable mates will require an e-mail from you and a confirmatory e-mail from the other parties concerned. Please remember that the purpose of Stables is to have a colleague watching your back, it is not to have a crowd of wrestlers constantly watching your back and "running security" for you. Angles involving Stables that would effectively make your wrestler impervious to attacks will not be permitted. By all means a Stable can come out and help once an attack has taken place [if you are anticipating one] but they will not be permitted to prevent that attack. In a similar vein the use of outside 'security' such as having your wrestler accompanied to the ring by security guards or anything else is absolutely prohibited. One wrestler must be as susceptible to an attack as another. Joining You join the AWA by e-mailing the AWA Owners (or [detail withheld]) or filling out an application on the AWA Promo-Net (http://awastars.netfirms.com/cgi-bin/Ultimate.cgi). _Once your application has been received and reviewed you will receive an e-mail welcoming you into the AWA and giving you instructions on how to begin your career. It is vital you act on this e-mail or you will not receive anything to do with the AWA. As stated above you are permitted one singles wrestler and one tag-team per handler. To create a tag team you must submit a sheet for each wrestler who forms that tag team. From time to time you may decide that your current gimmick is going nowhere and wish to change your wrestler completely. This is fine, except that you will not be permitted more than four changes a year. If you require more than this then there is obviously a problem with your role-playing and it creates unnecessary work on the site. Final point - Original Wrestlers only are accepted into the AWA. Do not bother submitting an application for Hulk Hogan, Steve Austin or anything that copies existing wrestlers gimmicks, i.e.- 'Stone Cold' John Smith. If you are not able to generate your own ideas and gimmicks the AWA is not the federation for you.
