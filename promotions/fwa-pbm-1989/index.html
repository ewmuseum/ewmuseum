Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Fantasy Wrestling Alliance began as a play-by-mail game in the late 1980s, run entirely on paper and postage; no card, roster or ruleset from that original run survives, only a fan history site's own later word that the game existed and folded sometime in the early 1990s. Its record picks back up only when the same organization resurfaced in 2020, five years before it closed for good.

The revived FWA was not one promotion but an alliance of named regional territories, each keeping its own roster and its own championship history: Midwest All-Star Wrestling, Georgia Elite Wrestling and Mid-South Wrestling in the American heartland; Sin City Wrestling and the All-American Wrestling Federation; Championship Wrestling from Florida, Defiance Championship Wrestling and Northeast Hardcore Wrestling on the East Coast; Motor City Roughneck Wrestling, Mid-Atlantic Championship Wrestling, California Extreme Wrestling and Rocky Mountain Wrestling; Calgary Stampede Wrestling and United Kingdom Chaos Wrestling abroad; Puerto Rico Monsoon Wrestling, Lucha Revolution Wrestling and Neo-Japan Pro Wrestling further afield; and FWA Women's Wrestling running its own women's division alongside them. Thirty-eight wrestlers are recorded holding twenty-one titles across the alliance's own territories.

Between a territory's ordinary championship reigns, the alliance's own commissioner created interim titles to fill the gap, later permanently vacating or retiring each one rather than folding it back into the main lineage — the MASW North American, United States and Deathmatch titles among them, the GEW National, Elite, Canadian Pride and Global Tag Team titles among others, all eventually marked vacated on the alliance's own record. Two of those Georgia Elite Wrestling commissioner titles, the Canadian Pride and USW titles, were built as outright spin-offs of titles from an earlier, unrelated play-by-mail game, the IWF — the alliance's own spreadsheet crowns each with the exact billed name of that older game's own former champion, without saying whether the same performer was continuing a persona decades on or the alliance was simply reusing a name it liked.

The alliance's own history site frames the whole 2020s run as one continuous organization taking up the original 1980s game's own name rather than a new venture borrowing it, and dates its second and final closure to March 2024. What became of any individual territory once the alliance folded is not stated by the record that survives.
