A GeoCities e-fed that promised 'originals only' on its own rules page and then staged Triple H, The Rock, Chris Jericho, Ric Flair, Goldberg and Chyna under their own names anyway, alongside a Detroit heel who wrestled with a cane and a giant billed from "the Depths of Hell." Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The New World Wrestling Alliance opened on GeoCities space by 1998, billing itself — twice, in capital letters — as an "originals only" federation. Its own Stables and Wrestlers rules page refused any real-life wrestler application outright. The record it actually staged tells a different story: its three surviving pay-per-view broadcasts headline Triple H, The Rock, Chris Jericho, Ric Flair, Chyna, "The Franchise" Shane Douglas, Francine, Buff Bagwell and Goldberg, every one of them under their own real name, sharing cards with original creations like a cane-carrying Detroit heel called Scott Credible and a 330-pound giant billed from "the Depths of Hell," Hellwolf.

Its weekly television, Tuesday Night Turmoil and Thursday Night Showdown, survives only as empty template shells — blank card tables and "?????" placeholders where a date and a venue should be. What the record actually holds are three named "Slamfest"-brand pay-per-views: a 30 May 1999 card at the Tokyo Dome where Buff Tortoise won the World Heavyweight Championship from the Rock in a ladder match, an August 1999 "Fall Classic: War Games" headlined by a Triple H–Shane Douglas Hell in a Cell, and a 25 June 2000 Madison Square Garden card that crowned the fed's first-ever Internet Champion — twice, in two different, never-reconciled matches on the same night.

Six of the fed's own seven dedicated title-history pages were never filled in, sitting as blank templates reading simply "Vacant" even where the pay-per-view scripts themselves show a champion clearly defending the belt. Only the Internet Championship's own ledger was ever completed, crediting Scott Credible as the inaugural titleholder. The fed's own second-tier belt carries two names depending on which document is read — "International" on its ledger, "Intercontinental" in its own pay-per-view scripts — and both readings stand, unresolved, exactly as the fed's own hand left them.

The site's own last dated update, 4 July 2000, announces a relaunch: "the NWWA... is now officially open," with rosters "slowly building back up once again." Nothing dated later survives, and the record does not say how, or whether, that relaunch played out.

Storylines

The arcs below are recovered from a full, cold read of all three surviving pay-per-view scripts.

Chris Jericho's self-appointed enforcement Written by AI, 2026

At the 29 August 1999 "Fall Classic: War Games," Chris Jericho declares himself the show's own "enforcer" mid-broadcast, unprompted, and works both ends of the card off it. In the Hell in a Cell opener he slips Triple H a pouch of blinding powder through the cage bars to use against Shane Douglas, accompanied by Francine; Triple H throws it in Douglas's face and pins him with the Pedigree seconds later. In the main event, a Title vs Title match promising the winner the World Heavyweight Championship and the loser the Intercontinental Championship, Jericho enters the ring during a Triple H/Goldberg run-in and cracks the World Title belt over Kirk's own head, then revives the downed referee just long enough to count the Rock's pin on top of him — retaining the World Title for the Rock and, per the match's own stipulation, leaving Kirk still Intercontinental Champion regardless.

Miss X's swerve, and the beatdown that preceded it Written by AI, 2026

At the earlier 30 May 1999 "Slamfest," a backstage vignette finds Buff Tortoise, Dale Hill and Jax stomping the Rock to the floor outside the Horsemen's own locker room before the show goes to the ring. Hours later, in the Intercontinental Championship's own Steel Cage main event, Kirk's valet Miss X arrives in a Horsemen shirt and hands Ric Flair a chair to use on Kirk — then, as Flair crawls for the cage door, strips it off to reveal a DX shirt underneath and pulls Kirk to safety instead, the swerve costing Flair the title. In the same night's ladder match for the World Heavyweight Championship, a third man, Cougar, runs in to double-chokeslam both the Rock and Buff Tortoise before Tortoise climbs back up to pull the belt down first.

Jaymac's turn decides the World Title Written by AI, 2026

Hellwolf defends the World Heavyweight Championship against White Tiger and Joe E. Awesome in a three-way at the 25 June 2000 "Slamfest" with the finish still open when Jaymac runs in during the match's final minutes. He first takes a chair away from Tiger as if to stop him — then turns and levels Awesome with it instead, clearing the pin for Hellwolf to retain.
