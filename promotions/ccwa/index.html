Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Continental Championship Wrestling Alliance ran a roleplay-judged points ladder toward four titles — the World Heavyweight, North American, Television, and Tag Team Championships — from a roster built one signing at a time on its own hosted forum. A hack near the fed's own founding wiped nearly everything it had, including roughly twenty results and, with them, every earlier title reign's own won-date; what survives afterward opens on Stone Fox, the promotion's own Administrator, signing wrestlers and naming a Commissioner and Vice President through December 2004, and closes on a fed still adding names to its own card into March 2005 — Ronnie McNeil defending the World Heavyweight Championship against Lyger, a new Flash Championship tournament underway to replace a retired Television title, and a returning legend, Maverick Reno, promising the promotion a new website of his own design.

Its rules staged competition as literal: a wrestler who wanted the North American Championship needed 25 points, the World Heavyweight Championship needed 40, earned two at a time for a roleplayed loss and four for a win — the ladder, not a booker's word alone, decided who got a shot. Tony Stecchino wrestled his way through CCWA's main events, sat as the board's own Administrator, and in February 2005 handed the presidency to Chris Sanchez and Alex Sarson while writing his own retirement-toned promo, “Legendary Status Is Achieved Not Granted” — a retirement that lasted one PPV, a faked kidnapping confession, and a return to the ring at Sensation. Faith, CEO under a full kayfabe profile of her own — a Blackpool, England native styled “Your Sweeta Senorita” — carried two former championships from earlier promotions into the fed's own front office.

Cards ran at real major arenas across the western United States: the Sensation pay-per-view returned to Key Arena in Seattle after what its own commentary called a four-year absence, a card at the Staples Center opened December 2004's roster, and the fed's own weekly show taped out of Spokane and, by March, Taco Bell Arena in Boise. Sensation itself inaugurated the Television Championship on Chris West and turned over both the World Heavyweight and North American titles — Ronnie McNeil and Danielle Northsky both winning belts the fed's own December hack had erased the history of. How it ended isn't in the surviving record — the last held news dates to 14 March 2005, mid-tournament, with no closing post.

Storylines

The Dream Maker's heist Written by AI, 2026

A cloaked figure interrupted CCWA's own CCWAtron broadcast three times across Sensation (7 Feb 2005) — before, during, and after the night's own opening match — taunting the roster and promising a debut, never named. The distraction cost Vexed his focus in that very match, letting Kris Krimzon steal the pin. After Ronnie McNeil won the World Heavyweight Championship from Johnny Dollar to close the show, the figure was finally revealed: Eddie B., billed by CCWA's own commentary as “4WW's former star” — the same Eddie B. who had already made a name for himself elsewhere as 4WW's own Tempest Champion. He superkicked McNeil, tore the physical championship belt from him, and busted Johnny Dollar's head open with it, closing Sensation on the theft rather than the title change.

Two weeks later, at The Show (22 Feb 2005), Eddie B. cut a promo dismissing McNeil as a “half-assed champion” making a mockery of the belt he'd carried elsewhere, then beat Psyno only by a rope-assisted cheating pin that referee Barry Terinski caught. Confronted afterward by Kris Krimzon, Eddie B. finally confirmed what the CCWAtron interruptions had cost: “I've made a name for you... no one knew who you were before the Dream Maker gave you that win against Vexed.” The two set a Hell in a Cell match for the following week at Wildside — a show the held record never reaches.

That same night, in the main event, McNeil defended the title against Tony Stecchino. McNeil pinned Stecchino clean for what looked like the finish, but Eddie B. yanked referee Barry Terinski's leg out of the ring mid-count, and Stud ruled the match a No Contest. Eddie B. then beat both men down with a chair, coerced the groggy Terinski into an unsanctioned three-count, and grabbed the belt again to declare himself “the next CCWA Champion” — a claim the record never recognizes as a title change. Kris Krimzon chaired him from behind to close the show, took the belt back, and returned it to McNeil. The held record ends before the Wildside rematch, Eddie B.'s own claim on the title still unresolved.
