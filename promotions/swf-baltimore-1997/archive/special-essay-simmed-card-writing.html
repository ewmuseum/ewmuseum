Special Essay: Simmed Card Writing

12 April 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Well I do feel like I have to give my counter opinion on the matter somewhat. I'm sure there will be rebuttals for what I have to say. Hey, there always are. :o) > > I have been in many e-feds in the past year and have seen many cards > posted on the newsgroup so, as a player, I would like to throw out some > general opinions regarding cards from simmed feds. > > First of all, I only play in simmed feds so this discussion has nothing > to do with booked feds and should not be applied to them. > > For those of us who are interested in TNM and WLS feds, many of us have > joined for two reasons: fairness, accuracy and realistic matches. Many > TNM and WLS fedheads try to do their best to meet these requsts when they > write up cards, but many I have seen are often way, way too long and > wordy and often eliminate what many of us see as the most important thing > -- holds executed. Now, as an ex-fedhead who used sims, I think that one reason for cards being "too long", in your words, are that the card writers enjoy writing the cards. Not that really has much to say behind it because we're not to the good part of the essay yet. > > Most simmed feds have the same setup for cards -- a face announcer and a > heel announcer. The cards begin with a description of lots of fireworks > and lots of sparks and explosions. Then the announcers begin rundowns of > the matches, throw in perky little predictions and comments, then proceed > to describe the matches as though you'd see them on TV. > > Thing is, guys, and this is VERY important -- we *cannot see them*! Yeah, you can't see the cards, but the point to e-wrestling is, you're *imagine* that you're at the card. That your a fan viewing it on tv. You read the opening sequences to a card, and get a picture of what it looks like. You read the matches, what the commentators say, the entrances, ect. You picutre those events as well. This just isn't something you sit down and read. There's no getting a cup of coffee, opening it to the a certain section and reading a match like it was newspaper article or anything else literary. Yeah, it is something you read, but the whole point to role-playing is for you to, and I stress this word, *imagine* that it is real. That's why it is called fantasy wrestling right? No > matter how good a writer you are, we can't hear the explosions or see the > fireworks and sparks. Right, you can't see really them, but if you have trouble imagining them. YOu've missed the whole point to e-wrestling, or fantasy wrestling, in general. You picture and imagine what you think it'd look like. THe descriptions, the commentary, and all the other stuff is there to give you some idea. YOu have to do the rest. And as far as the writing of the cards go, all the > banter and cute comments mostly go unread as they are superfluous to the > real action. What is the real action? I could open another fed and for hte cards, just send out the logfile that TNM spits out. That'd give everyone a chance to see how their wrestler did, sure, but I sure as heck know that I'd get bored to tears on that. I enjoy seeing what the commentator's have to say about my wrestlers, be it from how much of a pompous jerk he is, to how much they love him and believe that he is what he calls himself. (using Mr. America there as a general reference) > > As far as the match descriptions go, again, remember -- no matter how > good a writer you think we are -- *we can't see the matches!* When my > wrestler wrestles someone else, unless it's one of those WWF or WCW > leagues, I don't have the slightest idea of what my opponent looks like > unless my wrestler is wrestling one of my other wrestlers. Hence, I > cannot picture the match. From what you've said here. It seems that the whole concept really has escaped you. Take this as a flame, but it's anything but that. I have no idea how some of the opponents of my wrestlers look, but that doesn't mean that I can't sit down and try to imagine the match. You're right, it'd be even easier for me to imagine a match between my two wrestlers. I made them up and I know them inside and out. But how boring is it to see two guys you've made up go at one another, unless for angle purposes. I know I'd get tired of seeing that because it's too easy for me to picture. I'd rather have a little to start with and be able to imagine the whole thing myself. Hence, all quests to allow me to "see" that > match are fruitless. Thus the long, involved descriptions and banters > back and forth are mostly immaterial to what many of us would like to > read. You keep bringing up that you can't see a match, so I'll say what i said before. You've missed the point. The long descriptions and the bantering, for some, give them a basis to *imagine* what the card would be like, to them, if it were real. What the card would be like if he was to sit down and watch it on tv and not read it off a screen. It gives a card "life", if you would say that, but you do have a point that it can be taken too far, and I can be accused of that I'm sure. > > The one thing I like to see from TNM and WLS cards is the holds that were > selected by the sim that were used in the match. What holds did my > wrestler use? What holds were effective? What holds were used against > my wrestler and how effective were they? How did the match flow really > go, according to the sim and not some fake announcer? I can't *see* the > match but I can gauge how it went by what the sim spit out without having > to listen to some make-believe broadcaster go on and on about what he had > for breakfast while making fun of some fake fan in the stands and, by the > time the match is over, give us four holds total from the match. Sure, I > know announcers don't announce every hold on TV, but then again, you can > SEE the holds as they are going on! And if the cards don't tell you every > hold used, you cannot gauge the true ebb and flow of the match. Alright, this makes no real sense to me. You can't see the announcers. You can't envisions the descpritions, but you CAN see the holds? All TNM puts out on a logfile is something like this. They lock up. Mr. X whips Don into the ropes. Mr. X hits Don with an elbow. Mr. X executes a Boston Crab. Don reaches the ropes after 5 seconds. Mr. X gives the sign for the X-Drop. Mr. X executes the X-Drop and goes for the pin. Earl Hebner counts: One, two, three. The winner is Mr. X. Time of match: 0:00:33. Wow! What a card. I can see that but it's kinda hard to actually envision that as a match. That's why the descriptions are used. To put it simply, I'd rather see something like this. [The scene cuts back to the ring.] DT-Up next we have the newcomer Mr. X taking on former WWW World champion Don. Mr. X sure has caused a lot of stir since joining the WWW. He's gotten under a lot of peoples' skin. PVB-Yeah, but he messed with the Underground and he'll pay for that. DT-According to him, he's above the law when it comes to people seeking justice for what he's done. Let's go to Felix Irwin for the introductions. FI-Introducing first! Making his debut in the WWW! From parts unknown and weighing in at 273lbs. Here is the self proclaimed "Only Man that is above the law", Mr. X! ["Zero" by Smashing Pumpkins plays as the fans boo. Mr. X walks out from between the curtains. Mr. X takes his usual crucifix pose as fireworks shoot out behind him forming and "X". The fireworks die down as Mr. X continues to the ring. Mr. X banters with some of the fans and laughs. Mr. X climbs into the ring and gives the finger to the ringside fans.] DT-Mr. X is not the crowd favorite on hand and that's apparent. PVB-Yeah and he's copying Retro by flipping the people at ringside off. Everyone imitates the Underground. FI-His opponent! ["St. Elmo's Fire (Man in Motion)" by John Par plays as the entire arena goes crazy.] DT-Listen to the response that Don has recieved from the fans here tonight! PVB-It makes me wanna wretch. FI-Hailing from San Diego, California and weighing 256lbs. He is accompanied to the ring by his manager Melissa! Here is former WWW World Champion, Don! [Don walks out from between the curtains with Melissa at his side. The lights dim as lights from the rafters flicker to the beat of the music. Melissa leads Don down to the ring as Don slaps the hands of the fans. Don climbs into the ring. Mr. X says a few comments as Don climbs up to the second turnbuckle and raises his arms in the air. The crowd cheers even louder.] DT-Don was a former world champion here in the WWW and is looking to regain his title at the upcoming pay per view when he takes on Retribution for the title again. PVB-Much like the first time he'll lose. DT-Both men appear ready to go in the ring. Let's get to the action. [They lock up. Mr. X whips Don into the ropes. Mr. X hits Don with an elbow. Mr. X executes a Boston Crab.] DT-Mr. X starts this match out strong. Mr. X is new to the WWW, but he's no newcomer to the sport. He was a former world champion and had held other belts in various leagues around the world. PVB-Yeah, but the WWW is better by far. He'll be nothing than a match filler within a month. Mark my words. [Don reaches the ropes after 5 seconds.] DT-Don was able to reach the ropes easily. Mr. X slapped on that Boston Crab too close to the ropes. PVB-Yeah, but Don was never one who possed ring intelligence in the first place. He believes that everyone plays fair. What an idiot! DT-The point of wrestling is to see how well you can do on your own skill Paul. PVB-Sure thing Donnie. That's why everyone and their brother boasts to be so "hardcore" today. [Mr. X gives the sign for the X-Drop.] DT-Mr. X already giving the sign for his Samoan Drop finisher! [Mr. X executes the X-Drop and goes for the pin.] PVB-That was weak. Mr. X will be the laughing stock of the fed after Don kicks out of this. [Earl Hebner counts: One, two, three.] DT-Oh my! Mr. X makes short work of Don tonight! PVB-Wha..? How!? DT-Let's get the offical word. FI-The winner is Mr. X in a time of 0:00:33. ["Zero" by the Smashing Pumpkins begins to play. Mr. X picks up Don and throws him out of the ring. Mr. X gets the microphone from Felix Irwin.] MX-What the heck was that!? A former world champion and I beat him in under a minute! If that's what this fed has to offer, then hand the belt over to me right now Retardo! PVB-He's gonna pay for that one. MX-It'll make your life a whole lot easier! DT-Hold on! Here come the Underground! [Retribution and Mr. America make their way through the crowd. They hop over the guardrail. Mr. America slides into the ring under the bottom rope and nails Mr. X from behind with a lariat. Mr. X crashes to the mat. Mr. America picks him up and nails him with the Made in America.] PVB-Oh yeah! Now he can claim to be from America. DT-Mr. America with his leaping piledriver finisher. PVB-No one does it better! Enter the WWW WOrld champion and Franchise! [Retributions climbs into the ring. Mr. America continues to stomp on Mr. X. Retributoin walks over as Mr. America stops and picks up the mic. Retribution sets Mr. X up for the Pay Back.] PVB-Bay Packs are a bitch! DT-Paul! Watch the language! This is just uncalled for! [Retribution nails Mr. X with the Pay Back.] DT-Retribuiton hits his modified Hotshot finisher. He set him up like a power bomb, but instead tossed him over his head throat first onto the top rope. Mr. America has the microphone. MA-Mr. X huh? Not too long ago you came into this fed shooting off about how you were gonna be the big thing. How you would easily take over this federation. You even went as far as to call yourself the "only man above the law". Well, you may be above the law, but you're not above the Underground! [Mr. America drops the mic.] PVB-YES! Kick him some more! [Retribution Gorilla Presses Mr. X to the outside of the ring. Retribution exits the ring and sets up a table. He sets Mr. X up onto the table as Mr. America climbs to the top turnbuckle. Mr. America sends Mr. X and himself through the table with a flying elbowdrop.] DT-Mr. X just put through a table and finally security is down here! [The WWW security runs down and breaks up the melee. There's a mixed reaction from the fans as Retribution and Mr. America walk back up the aisle.] DT-While the paramedics help Mr. X back to the locker rooms, let's go to Marc Mayhem who's with Tundra for an interview. ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------Now, you take your pick. As you can see, I used all of the TNM moves that were in the match, though that was right off the top of my head. All I did was add in, a lot for that matter, of stuff to help the readers *imagine* the card. Sure, some of this stuff no one else will know about that is outside of this fed, again something off the top of my head. No one outside the fed knows what was said or what happened exactly, but they know that the handler, no one really does handle him, of Mr. X really did some good flashes and caused enough heat for the Underground to come down. Again, those were just examples I used. > > The best cards I have seen written are in the WCA by [name withheld]. He > makes sure to include every hold used while also throwing in a little > creativity to spice things up. The HWL also has some good cards and > recently at least one of their writers has taken to giving a blow by blow > account exactly as it comes off the sim and then throws in a few comments > to spice up what's going on. This was a very positive step in the right > direction. Now, I may be taking this the wrong way, but if you mean blow by blow. This is what i'm thinking. DT-Mr. X and Don circle around...they lock up! Mr. X sends Don into the ropes. Don is a former world champion here in the WWW, but Mr. X is a well seasoned veteran of the sport as well. PVB-Elbow by Mr. X sends Don to the mat....Mr. X follows it up with a Boston crab but he had Don to close to the ropes and the ref is calling for the break. Ect, ect, ect. I'm not a big fan of that, but I have seen some great card writers use it, examples would be Stevie (OWA) and Ian (NWLP). Even then, I'll admit that a blow by blow gives the match a much better feeling of being real when it's used. I just prefer to see the logfile as it comes out because I like to see how my wrestler did move wise as well, but I also want to see every other aspect of the card. I wnat to read what the announcer have to say. I want to read the description of my wrestler's entrance. I wnat to read the crowd interaction, because it helps me to *imagine* what the match would be like if it were real and it also helps me play my characters in a much better since, so they're not bland. > > The key thing simmed cards should focus on is not the announcers or > their personalities, but the match. The worst cards I have ever seen are > the long, wordy and superfluous cards where the announcers banter back > and forth about irrelevancies and go on and on about things that have > nothing to do with the match itself and then give you only a handful of > holds to work with. I can see the point in that, but the point to a wordy card is to give it that 'real' feeling. You don't see announcers shut up for a match to let it happen. To use an example of commentator's of the "real" (ironic isn't it? :) ) wrestling talking aobut something that has nothing ot do with a current match. Awhile back, Benoit and Guerro were taking each other on. Both of those men, in my opinion, are good and it was going to be a good match. Well I get to hear the announcer's talk about Hogan while the match goes on. Hogan wasn't even in the arena at that time. (this was before the nWo stuff) I read on here earlier that a lot of the e-leagues look to "real" wrestling as sort of something to go by and I'll agree with that. A lot of times, you'll see announcers banter back an forth on something that has nothing to do with the match on hand, I know I get tired of hearing nWo this and nWo that (though I do love to hear the wit of Kevin Nash). It gives that card a 'realism' feel to it. This is very frustrating to us players who want to > see the ebb and flow of the match and don't care if the announcer had a > good day or not. > > My suggestion to those who write simmed cards: Let the sim rule. Many of > us want to see everything that the sim said. Banter and creativity really > is secondary. Actually, I'd say the two go hand in hand. Without the commentary I believe that card is no good. >Realism is the number one thing You want realism, but the entire point of e-wrestling is to be able to *imagine* the match, or card for that matter, as "real". Not just something that a logfile says. Oliver put a lot of work into TNM to make it as real as he could, but there are still a few flaws with it. (though I still say TNM is great. *insert more butt kissing to Oliver here*) Like Jay said, no one recovers from a piledriver that quickly and hits his opponent with backdrop driver, only to have his opponent to get up and hit his secondary pin and get the win. That just doesn't make sense, but as a card writer. It's your job to have it make sense. I could easily edit the above, with the right use of certain things like interference, ect., to make the above exchange of moves make sense and to give it "realism" and many of us have joined > simmed feds because we believe sims are the best form of fairness and > match reality that is available in e-wrestling today. Now this gets into an arguement of old. Booked vs. simmed feds. I won't get into that arguement because both have their drawbacks and fortes. Now, you can call a simmed fed the best form of fairness, but all it takes is this. I enter a fed your in and make a TNM moster. I have TNM and I can study it to make a kick ass wrestler. You on the other hand can't and pick the moves you see your guy doing and what you want him to do (all this being hypothetical of course). Well both of our men get signed to face each other. You're the world champion and my guy is the number one contender. You flash your heart out and I don't say a word to you, yet my guy wins because I knew what moves to take and how to place them. I'm sure you'd feel pretty cheated and frustrated that you put forth effort, yet my guy won because I knew how to play the sim. I know that you'd feel cheated because I would as well. Hence, you have to enter some of the rules for booking. In reality, if my guy were to have a world title shot nad never talked he'd job to the world champ. Yet, my guy wins just because I knew how to play a sim. How real does that sound to you as well as how fair does that sound to you? So I urge you > simmed fedheads to not detract one iota from the sim results in your > cards. Only time I've ever done that was when I cut the match because the loser never said a word. I'll push the guys that flash their hearts out against guy who say nothing because the guys who put effort in deserve that. I'd rather see, in my own personal opinion, a guy who's a champ yet says nothing at all lose in a matter of minutes than to let the whole match unfold. Add a little, but not too much because overdoing it makes the > cards wordy and superfluous. That gives it a sense of "realism" to it as well as help the you to *Imagine* the match. We only have so much time and it is highly > doubtful that we are going to read page after page after page of material > that is filled with irrelevant material. > That's why I'll always come back to read a card when I have time, no matter how long I have to wait. I'll skim to a match with my guy and check out the winner, but I always come back because I want to "see" the match with my imagination instead of just reading that because my guy's finisher got countered with a small package he lost. > Make the cards short. Make them brief. Make them a little witty, but > don't try to overdo it. But most of all -- let the sim rule! Actually, let your imagination rule. It makes it all that much more enjoyable. > > Now some people may say this is nothing more than "cut and paste" work. I > say if it lets us see what really happens in a match, call it what you > want. As far as creativity goes, if you leave out information given to > you by the sim in order to be creative, you are doing a disservice to the > people who joined the fed to see what the sim says about the match. > If you leave out stuff from the sim, then yes. You are robbing people of what they want to see, but it's not that hard to be creative and not cut out match parts. > Again, simmed feds -- let the sim rule! Try doing this and this is just a suggestion. Pick a card from a fed that your in and read the card. Let your imagination picture the whole card as if it were real and put yourself into teh shoes of a fan of that federation who's just turned on his television because he's so exicted that it's 8pm and his favorite wrestling show is on on Wed nights. It's a whole different experience. -- [name withheld] [detail withheld]
