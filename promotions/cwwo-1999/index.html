Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Cyber World Wrestling Organization opened with an information page on 3 February 1999, its own founding president promising a federation judged on “quality over quantity” roleplay rather than volume, sent to him directly and forwarded to the whole roster by blind copy so no one could be junk-mailed. Cursing was allowed short of the “F word”; two warnings meant suspension, and two suspensions meant being “fired on the spot.”

One weekly broadcast, Monday Night Madness, carried the promotion, running roughly nine matches in play-by-play, with occasional results-only House Shows counting toward the same rankings and title reigns. Four singles titles anchored the card — the CWWO World, Internet, Hardcore, and Television Titles — alongside World Tag Team Titles held back until enough tag teams existed to justify them, and one pay-per-view a month, each carrying its own name: New Year's Revolution, Clash of the Titans, Mayday '99, Title Wave, Summer's Over, Halloween Horror Night, and Here Comes Santa Claus.

Its 16 March 1999 Monday Night Madness relaunched the show under “a brand new look and a whole new attitude,” broadcast from a sold-out Cincinnati Gardens with commentators Ryan Steen and Frank Fuller at the desk. A Television Title match fell apart when “Mr. N-17” Rob McBride turned up on crutches with a broken leg and traded punches with challenger Justin McKagen before security dragged them apart; the promotion's own president turned it into a triple-threat match on the spot, adding Michael Morrison and Steve Saxson, which McKagen won and kept his title after Saxson was disqualified for a chair shot. Brian Lee Sampson retained the CWWO Internet Title in a cage match against Predator, whose manager, Lastkind, interfered repeatedly; afterward, Saxson and a debuting “Your F'n Studd” Shawn Leary jumped Morrison and declared themselves the promotion's next Tag Team Champions. The broadcast closed with the president announcing that reigning champions Ice Cold Eric Bordan and Viper, if they won their title defense that night, would face new arrivals Sex and Violence and the Ruff Ryders at the promotion's Clash of the Titans pay-per-view.

A 13 May 1999 notice announced the promotion had “reopened from a merge with the short lived TWA” at a new web address, calling itself “back and running with the best, hardest-working staff around.”

No further document survives after that reopening notice.
