WFW Road to Glory: Edmunds (c) vs. Wylde

25 February 2004

PaulNJ21 — 25 February 2004 The RP/Angle deadline is Sunday March 7, 11:59 EST . Send all angles to [detail withheld] This match is for the WFW World Heavyweight Championship. This match will be held live at Disneyland California Adventure. EGarrett — 28 February 2004 AND NOW, A MESSAGE FROM YOUR WFW PRESIDENT... INT. WORKOUT ROOM Alex Wylde is seated on the bench of his workout room in the basement of his Presidential Office. He appears to have just finished a set, as he rests with his head down, sweat dripping from the ends of his hair. He wears a pair of silk basketball shorts, sneakers and a wristwatch. He glances at it as he begins to remove his workout gloves.
WYLDE Well...I guess hundreds of guys in this business might think I'm finally joining the weight-room fraternity. Usually I don't talk to anyone at these times. I'd rather be alone with my thoughts. But this time I'll have to make an exception. Sometimes you just have something to say...and it doesn't matter where you say it.
He tosses the gloves on the ground and runs a hand through his closely-cropped hair. WYLDE Everybody within the sound of my voice was a kid once. Those of you who aren't kids that is. But even so...you see...you all had dreams at one point. Maybe you wanted to hit the homerun in the World Series. Or maybe you wanted to be the Prima Ballerina up on the grandest stage. Everyone had their personal fantasy. The problem is...of course...that life isn't a storybook. And people change as they get older. Reality stays the same whether you accept it or not. We all just have to learn to adapt or spend the rest of our days in the mental hospital. So, the end result is that fantasies tend to remain just that. The dream of hitting the last second shot turns into getting a promotion at the firm. Something far less interesting. But it's still achievement...and it's something you can strive for.
He stands up and heads out the door of the weight room, producing a key and locking the door.
WYLDE The thing is though...even when you work for that promotion...or you want to make employee of the month of that Burger joint. Things don't always inspire you like they did when you were 10-years-old. I guess sometimes the truth can be so cold and bland that it sucks the flavor right out of you. Some people never get it back. But there are always those occassions...those little moments in life when you can catch a glimpse of somebody with that wonder in their eyes. See that little kid again. You ever see somebody win an Academy Award? For the first time? Especially those guys who came up from dirt in acting. I swear that sometimes I didn't understand one bit why anybody gave a damn about that show. After all...your kids can't eat Oscar. And having a gold statue doesn't keep the maggots out of your casket. But that was until I watched the show. I saw the gasps and the silence when those little envelopes were opened. And then they read off somebody's name...and the music plays and they come down to address everyone. The statue has no real meaning...other than recognition. But when you look at that person's face...when you see their eyes when they accomplish that goal... it's one of the only times in your life that you'll really see a person's heart fulfilled. That moment...when everything comes together for a person who's focused their life on achieving a goal...that's the best damn TV you'll ever see. And that's why most people watch that god-forsaken show.
He heads down the hallway, towards the elevator out of the basement. He gets there and hits the "Up" button.
WYLDE ...*most* people...that is. You see, I'm a pretty perceptive person. And while all that congratulations is going on...and that pure moment happens for that winner...you have to look real close...because for a brief second...when they put all those nominees on screen...you can see the faces of the four or five losers. The people who worked just as hard as the winner. The people who were just as good. The people who'd convinced themselves that they wouldn't be nominated so as not to get their hopes up. The people who'd been telling themselves all evening that they probably wouldn't win. But you know in your heart of hearts that when they started reading those names...they got those butterflies in their stomach. It hit them that they could achieve a dream at that very moment...in front of thousands of people. And then...in their little corner of the screen...you can watch them come within inches of the mountaintop...only to watch some idiot Action Star walk off with the trophy for playing a retarded guy in some stupid movie. I'll be damned if their beating hearts don't plop out onto their laps at that moment. THAT moment...is why *I* watch those programs...
The elevator door opens and Alex steps in, the cameraman following him closely behind as he hits the button for the third floor.
WYLDE Because you see...there's nothing I know more in life than the feeling of your heart plopping out of your chest onto your lap. The empty joy of the bridesmaid. Or that Ms. America runner-up with the beautiful fake smile and applause for the woman who just took the one thing in life that she really wanted and tried to get. Especially when the winner is some butt ugly chick with a great personality. It might pain me to admit this...but in more ways than one...that's been my career up until this point. This business is about competition...it's about wrestling matches...selling tickets...and all of those things. I've done everything I can in my career to be the best at both...and I know I'm pretty damned good at both. Hell, you'd probably be hard-pressed to name someone better. And that 10-pound title. It's ultimately meaningless in the grand scheme of things. But somehow...someway...over nine years...the brass ring...the formality...the official crowning of greatness...has yet to fall into my lap. And it doesn't keep the maggots out of your casket. Nor am I going to spew some crap about it being a boyhood dream. I really just wanted to bust some heads when I was ten. But it's a funny thing about goals. The more they elude you...the harder they are to get...the more you start to want them. And over time...when it gets snatched out of your hand over and over...something that you may legitimately not care much about...can turn into an obsession. Some of us may wear it on our sleeve. Others, like me, can hide or control their emotions...
The elevator reaches the third floor and Wylde steps out. The office is empty except for him, and he heads over to his desk, where a duffel bag sits.
WYLDE ...but at this point I'm not afraid to say that the belt. Having that highest crown...has eluded me. It's been snatched from my grasp...and it's created an obsessive knot of bitterness in the pit of my stomach. Now, I want it. I know how good I am. I think everyone else knows also. But now it's time to make it real. I didn't see Scotty Michaels. I didn't see Doc Silver. Nor do I see Sean Edmunds right now. All I see is that big gold target around his waist. And it's about damned time it came home. At Road to Glory...there will be no run-ins. There will be no screwjobs. There will be no draws. The BS ends. The stalling ends. I've got unfinished business to take care of in my career...and when the time comes...Alex Wylde's greatness...will finally become official.
He opens the duffel bag and takes out his street clothes. As he pulls on his shirt and starts to get dressed...the camera pulls back. FADEOUT.

EGarrett — 6 March 2004 AND NOW, A MESSAGE FROM YOUR WFW PRESIDENT... INT. PRESIDENTIAL OFFICE It's after hours. The Office is empty. But the latch turns on the main door and in walks a silhouette. It reaches over and flips on a small number of lights. It's Alex Wylde, wearing jeans and a t-shirt under a leather jacket. He carries with him a duffel bag that's only about half-full. The camera pulls in closer as he walks through the hallway. He glances back at it and speaks.
WYLDE I'm not the one partying at this point. But that's exactly the way I want it. Y'know...they say silence is golden. That's because it's so valuable and hard to find in this world. If a phone-ringer was on right now you'd probably hear it. If a pager or computer was on it'd be calling attention to a message too. That's because when you're an important man...a lot of people want a piece of you. They need your approval for a move...or want advice on something. But what they don't understand...and what Sean Edmunds doesn't understand...is that important people need room to be important to themselves. It's almost as if it's not fair to use your talent solely to further your own goals once in a while...
He heads to his office and pulls out a keyring. Within a second or two he unlocks the door and heads inside...pausing briefly for the camera to follow. On his desk is a stack of mail and a half-dozen post-it notes with Elaine's handwriting on them.
WYLDE But some things aren't fair in life. You learn to appreciate that early in your career when you have no pull and are trying to get somewhere. I'm in the process of teaching that to everyone in World's Finest Wrestling everyday. And it's a cold, hard reality that Sean Edmunds is gonna hit when he's done when that warm fuzzy feeling of his abbreviated title reign ends. He might know it already...and that's why he's partying up a storm for the moment. And telling the world any kind of bunk he can to make it appear as though he's not concerned with this match. The hidden hope being that if he can convince Sherri and the rubes watching...then maybe he can start to believe it himself. There's one problem with that philosophy, though. It only helps if you're capable of defeating your opponent and don't know it. In other cases...not knowing your own limitations can be disastrous. It can make you look stupid. Like this situation. Especially when people notice that you resort to juvenile "psyching-yourself-up" tactics and still fail. A smart man knows when he's overmatched...and readjusts. There's no shame in it. I'd recommend the same to you. Maybe you could hatch some plot with Sherri...find someone to pay off to try and hatch some plan where your fledgling title reign survives this match. It's what I recommend to save your sanity.
He sits down at the desk. Opens and closes the desks...checking for something. He fails to find it.
WYLDE Because I know you, Sean. You take pride in your accomplishments. Sometimes too much. I've always taken some amusement in it, actually. That's why I'm somewhat sad that I have to be handed the task of bursting one of your bubbles at this pivotal point in the history of World's Finest Wrestling. You've been my biggest fan for quite a while. Which would probably explain why you claim to be disappointed in me lately. I've been known to enjoy a good party. A joke at the expense of some of my lesser competition. And it always tends to amuse everyone but my opponent. It may even do so to the point that at various points...people have cheered me despite my utter apathy towards them. But you...like everyone else...are disappointed by my recent change of attitude because you don't understand me...and never really did.
He leans back in his chair...and runs a hand through his cropped hair. WYLDE You see...the reason I partied...the reason I make jokes or make fun of idiots...is the same reason that I've been known to be completely cold to the pain or needs of other people in my pursuit of excellence. Because it serves me. Those jokes I make are funny. In fact...no one finds my occasional antics more amusing than me. And that's the ONLY reason I do it. Because...you see...I can get so bored by the attempts of others at times that I need to find other ways to keep myself interested. For a time that involved utterly irritating them as much as humanly possible. In World's Finest Wrestling...it's involved confusing or screwing with people to make myself laugh. It may have amused others. It definitely amused you. But no one should ever...for a SECOND...think that my aim was to amuse anyone but myself. So what can you learn from this lesson? Because I like you...I won't let this particular revelation fly your head.
The phone rings. He picks up the receiver and hangs it up without so much as a glance toward the phone.
WYLDE From the moment I walked into this business...everything I've done has been in the service of one person...Alex Wylde. I used to pretend to play to crowds...smile and be a cool guy. There was success. But ultimately, people saw through it. I aligned with some like-minded people...guys who believed only in their own thrills above all else. Nihilists. We shared a lack of concern for the world and the people in it. A thousand people could be killed in some stupid bombing halfway across the world. It didn't wash for a second with me or them as long as the glass was full and we had something to cut up. We'll always have things in common. But the crucial point where I differed is that I couldn't take my eyes off the brass ring. Most people fly to see the clouds...others do it to look down on everyone else. You can guess that I'm the latter.
The camera pulls back from the desk...revealing the panoramic view of Jersey City that comes from the window behind him. The sun is setting...and the office is somewhat empty except for his desk.
WYLDE Because...no matter what happened...no matter how great the parties got...I just couldn't keep my sights off that last success. Those titles...the victories...the need to prove that I was the best in the business. I don't know if I wanted a spot in the hall of fame...but I definitely wanted people to know that I belong there. Overall, that goal proved more important than anything else. So I had to give up the fun and get in the ring.
The camera pulls back out. WYLDE Which brings us to World's Finest Wrestling. And me. Your president. In this office...where I always inevitably up. Alone. Plotting in pursuit of my own ultimate triumph. Years of small victories leading up to final battle...and one that I am overqualified to win. Which brings me to you. The accidential quarry who wonders why I don't talk much about you as an opponent. I can answer that for you easily. I don't talk much about you because I don't give a damn who my opponent is at this point. As the best in this league...I'm long overdue to have what you're holding. I know you're plotting. I know you've recruited a valet and probably a few other people to try and show up at ringside. I've advised you to do so. But you could bring a riot squad and it's not going to stop me anymore. You're talking to the man who has his finger on the pulse of every heartbeat in this league. You can't outplot me. You can't outwrestle me...and you don't have a prayer in hell of being more prepared. Face it, Sean...you're the poor bastard who got stuck in the middle of the target when the sniper pulls the trigger. And he's had years to line up. You can deny the situation...or you can accept that your brains are going to be splattered on the wall. It doesn't matter anymore. Just show up.
He gets up from the desk and shoves the chair under. FADEOUT THE PRECEDING MESSAGE HAS BEEN PAID FOR BY YOUTH UNITED FOR ALEX WYLDE AND WORLD'S FINEST WRESTLING.
