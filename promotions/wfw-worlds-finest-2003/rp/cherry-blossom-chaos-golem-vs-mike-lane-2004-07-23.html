Cherry Blossom Chaos: Golem vs. Mike Lane

23 July 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
PaulNJ21 — 23 July 2004 Remember, all RP conducted for Cherry Blossom Chaos should be from various locations in the Washington, DC Area.
The RP/Angle deadline is Friday, August 6th 11:59PM EST. Send all angles to [detail withheld]
Badnews — 31 July 2004 (FADEIN to Mike Lane clad in a pair of ray bans, a collared polo t, a pair of khaki shorts and sneakers standing in front of the Vietnam War Memorial...)
LANE: (staring at the wall reading through the names, shaking his head) All these names. At one time every man on this wall was more than just a name carved in stone. They were once living, breathing, happy go lucky people. Maybe they had a family... maybe they were alone, maybe they went to valiently defend their country, or possibly knowing they were going to die. It's amazing, to myself at least how we memorialize people. Just reading these names I don't know a thing about the type of person (looks at wall) Jason Tosh was, or (scrolls through the wall) Dale Quigley, they gave their lives and all they received was a one inch space on this massive wall.
LANE: Stuff like this gets me thinking, how am I going to be remembered when I'm gone? Mike Lane the wrestlers? The drug addict? Or that rich prick who pissed off just about everyone he came in contact with? The way I'm seeing things now I'm more likely to vanish into obscurity, I'll leave behind a tombstone that goes unkempt that no one visits, and maybe someday somebody will pass by and wonder who was the guy thats buried six feet underneath that momument.
LANE: Basically any way you cut it I have a legacy to leave behind, because I don't want to be that name etched in stone with no story and a past thats soon forgotten (smirks and shakes his head)
LANE: Pretty sad life I lead huh Golem? Yeah I'm not here to go on a giant, depressing rant anymore than I already have. I just wanted to come out and introduce myself because you probably havent heard a great deal about me. I'm Mike Lane, I've been around the block a few times and had my share of ups and downs since showing up back here a couple of months ago. Thats all you really need to know, I've won titles but thats ancient history and nothing you should be bothered with now. You could ask the guys I've wrestled recently about my style, moveset.. but you'd need to hire a search team to find them because they've packed up and moved on.
LANE: So now you know what you need to about Mike Lane, so tell me about yourself Golem, what makes a guy like you tick? And you're going to have to delve into detail because all I know about you is you're one ugly bastard and you got a shot at Wells for the BAD title after this show. I'm usually not so ill prepared, but I guess i'm just not interested enough to try and research your life story so make it easy on me man, ok?
LANE: But when alls said Golem I'm glad its you I'm fighting, I'm glad I'll have an opponent whos probably going to be here next week and not ride off into the horizon because this business isn't for him. I'm sure you're a warrior you and me have been in this industry for years, I know how it operates and I'm assuming you do too.
LANE: That being said you're probably wondering then WHY I'd be so happy about being booked against a guy whos got a title shot right after he gets done with me. (smirks) Because afterall I know how the gears turn. I'm fighting you because the bookers, men who are much smarter than me know you're going to beat me. They don't think I stand a snowballs chance in hell of winning this match, and they needed someone for the number one contender to run over en route to his title match.
LANE: But still Golem, I'm happy its you, I'm psyched for some real competition, not another green rookie. I'll beat on you, then you beat on me some, we'll go through the motions for ten/fifteen minutes and when you beat me I'll try to act suprised.
LANE: I know my role, and what it boils down to is I'm a stepping stone, I'm midcard refuse that gets fed all the rookies to see if they have what it takes, but I'm also the small speedbump for guys such as yourself to ride over on the road to bigger and better things. I don't enjoy my role mind you, but it's one I'm learning to accept by the day, so don't get offended if I sling a few one liners and mamma jokes at you, cuz I'm just letting off some steam.
LANE: But Golem, if the impossible does happen and you slip and break your head open on the way to the ring and I actually win this thing I wouldn't worry you're headed for great things, I'm just going to sit around stagnant until my contract runs out or the prez can find some cheaper help to open curtains for him.
LANE: In the end maybe that is my legacy, maybe when my name gets etched onto some marble and they lower me into the ground it'll say "Mike Lane.. stepping stone for much greater men...." Its not flattering, and not even respectable, but it is something
Vertigo — 2 August 2004 (Cue Up: "The Day the World Went Away" by Nine Inch Nails. Fade into Arlington National Cemetery at dusk, and amid the solemn grave walks the figure of Golem. There is no one very close to him...perhaps because he has green fur, and that is just wierd looking. Anyways, he glances about and then begins to talk.)
Golem: The fallen, eh, Mike Lane? The downtrodden, the refuse...the heroes. I honor you, you gladiator of the evening time, that you should so rightly understand the inner workings of this place. It is not so far from my own mouth that I should try to explain how and why some people serve as little more than my stepping stone. You have captured and embraced these lonley little words and have made them into a legacy. Unfortunately, it is a shared legacy, between you and a great number of people, but well, you can't have a forest with a few sturdy trees, right? The single is just as important as the whole, and don't let anyone tell you otherwise. Truth be told, I do know quite a bit about you, it's my job and you were not a hard man to catch tapes. Like you said, you and I have long since ran parallel in this wrestling world, and now that we intersect, I feel slightly the worse for it. Because, much in the vain of what I carry for Copycat, there is a mild level of respect for someone who has lasted this long and has never went out of their way to irritate Golem. In fact, of what little information you have on me, it actually turns out to be faulty. I don't just have a shot at the BAD world title. If it suits me, I can take my shot at the North American title. Basically, I get to choose between the glamour of the BAD world title and the chance at unhinging my good friend Psycho. Decisions, decisions...but one that must wait for a later day. My business with you, now, is pressing. I would like to take trust in you and believe that you will not enter this contest all upset-minded, but well, that is everyone else and I cannot rightly over-extend myself and look past you. That would be doing a disservice to someone whom I have come to so much care for. I understand that you might be forced to unlease scathing verbal witticisms and that is alright. My fur has grown staunch and days will have gone ill when such things should ever again burrow themselves beneath my skin. I am a paragon of this industry, a staple, a tower than is sheathed in a magical wall of strength. You shall try, I should think, to at least leave some lasting impression on me, so one might see your grave amid other graves and be afraid. Such is not your luck with me, but be glad. An impression in your glaring acceptance of the situation has been made and will not go forgotten. Such a grave marker as one that says "Stepping stone for much greater men" is glorious when the man's tomb is encircled with appreciative greater men. You and I both know that my title shot is secure and I do not truly even have to show up for our match, but that would be cheating you, King of the Valley of the Stepping Stone. Indeed, your epitaph is yours to be had, if all goes according to plan. Hell, I will be the first to push for your title shot after I defeat whichever champion is more fit for my liking. But as you know, this clockwork business doesn't much abide to the whims of fur-covered fiends and their self-righteous speak. So it should come to pass that your hands should rightly scathe me as I always figured they would attempt to. I will feign great damage, friend, as though at your hands, and see what I can do for you to the busy people in their towers of stone and windows. Indeed, such kind words of yours, Mike Lane, He Who Shall See Me Again, have curried my favor. That will not rightly save from might and claw and dangerous things, but you shall have kindness soon enough to repay your kingliness. And that much may very well touch the gavel to the grave.
(Golem walks to the end of the line of graves and an extra tombstone lies on the ground beside the last grave. It reads, "Mike Lane - Defender of Things Others Would Wish Not Defended". Golem stands over it quietly and a smirk curls round his lips. Then, all fades to black.)
Badnews — 3 August 2004 (FADEIN on Mike Lane sitting on a bench in front of a project in a bad section of Washington DC. Off in the distance a bunch of kids are running through sprinklers, cops are having some words with a guy standing on the corner.)
LANE: I gotta say when you look how the other half lives it almost brings a smile to your face. You agree right Golem? These people here are destined to become nothing. They're nobodies, they pay a couple hundred bucks a month to live in this slum, in this (points to building) dump, they wake up every morning and they know its never going to get better than this. They could try to better themselves I suppose. They could work hard 18 hour shifts at the crap factory and still barely earn enough scratch to feed themselves and their eight children. I'm in an area where all hope is lost. Its a far cry from right down the street where bills become laws, multi-millionaire lawmakers decide which mistress they'll visit at whatever luxury apartment they own and what car they're going to take to get there. They have the whole world in their hands, while the people here have empty hands.
LANE: Look, I'm not here to do a commercial for the impovershed. I'm here in this backdrop to illustrate whats going on in my head right now. Because you see Golem you have the world at your fingertips, you are one of the fat cats of the wrestling industry... The BAD title, the world title, take your pick. Chance are you'll probably wind up winning both somewhere down the line. You have a future. What I have is a seemingly endless career ahead of me where I'll be fighting the next rookie to walk into a WFW arena in an opening match. I'll beat the kid mercilessly and wollow around getting the short end of the stick in matches with guys such as yourself. Its a sad, sad situation living in this prison with no bars. But like I told you before I've accepted it, I just figured you needed a glimse into what makes me tick, yanno so when you beat me you can carry with yourself some sense of accomplishment.
(A crying kid, about 6 or 7 years old wearing just a pair of tighty whities runs by Lane crying from the fire hydrant over to his mother)
LANE: (smirks) Look at that, a young kid with a mother who brought him into this world way too soon. She's probably not even old enough to drive but we trust her to bring this kid up the right way. He'll be doing drugs by the time he hits junior high, selling them a few years later, probably dead before hes old enough to legally drink. I'd feel his pain if I thought I had a soul, or any compassion. I'd cry along side his mother if I didn't believe in fate and that this child was just born into the grave anyway. So I'll just shake my head and smirk, because hes a lot like me, born into the world the same way I was born into wrestling and he, much like myself will die before his time even comes.
(FADEOUT)
