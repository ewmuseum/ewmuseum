GARTHIsTheLaw — 6 April 2004 (Cueup: "We've Had Enough" by the Alkaline Trio) (Fade in on the outside of an expensive-looking rental car. It is parked at a parking meter on a city street, surrounded by office buildings. We see the Copycat get out of the car on the other side and walk around the front of the car to the passenger side, which the camera is focused on. He wears jeans, his trademark beret, and a Bloodhound Gang T-shirt. He leans against the car)
Copycat: Jersey City. Ordinarily, I ensure that the settings of my promos reflect a point I'm trying to make. But not today. Today I'm here to make a point of a different kind. Today, I'm not out to make a point about my opponent. I'm here to make a point about the state of affairs in WFW. It's a point that's been a long time coming.
(Copycat starts walking. The camera swings around so it's in front of him as he walks) Copycat: At the Superbowl of Wrestling, I'm scheduled to go one-on-one with Dan Ryan, the number-one man in CSWA, for whatever that's worth. I've been told that this match is some sort of dream match, one people have wanted to see for a long time, but I can't for the life of me figure out why. Sure, he's a good wrestler and interesting on the mic, but I guess I just don't pick up on the similarities everyone else picks up on. Or maybe I'm just focusing on the differences a little too much. Probably because one of those differences is the reason for my little visit this afternoon.
(Copycat stops. The camera turns to show a familiar-looking office building. The camera turns back to show Copycat)
Copycat: If you can't figure out where I am, you're hopeless. Watch out, camera guy. (Copycat walks past the camera and opens one of the glass doors of the building. The camera follows after him. Copycat glances toward the receptionist's desk, but she is talking to someone else at the desk, so Copycat sits down in a nearby chair)
Copycat: A couple of the guys involved in the Superbowl of Wrestling -- Scotty Michaels in particular comes to mind -- have been complaining that Sean Edmunds, Anarky, and I get to have these interpromotional dream matches while they're stuck facing people who are -- God forbid -- actually IN WFW. Fact is, though, they haven't considered that maybe, just maybe, those of us thrust into these interpromotional matches aren't exactly thrilled about the prospect either. You see it as an opportunity to prove my worth to WFW. I see it as a waste of my time.
(Copycat shoots a bored glance toward the receptionist, who is apparently still with someone else as Copycat does not get up)
Copycat: What do I gain from this match? If I win, I have a victory over the champion of a defunct league who I'll probably never encounter again. If I lose, I drop a match to someone everybody probably expected me to lose to anyway. Regardless of the official outcome of that match, I really have no way to win here. I'll go in, I'll fight Dan Ryan, maybe I'll come out on top, maybe I won't. But either way, as soon as the next card rolls around, I'll be back doing what I was doing before...getting denied my opportunities to make an impact.
(Copycat glances more impatiently at the receptionist, but still does not get up) Copycat: Because that's what it's all about in WFW these days. No, Copycat, you can't have a match with someone you care about, you have to have a triple threat match with a couple of guys you couldn't give three-quarters of a sh(BLEEP)t about for a title you don't want. No, Copycat, you can't have a title shot, you have to face Anarky 713 consecutive times. No, Copycat, your team can't have a fighting chance in War Games because by what I'm sure is SHEER AND TOTAL COINCIDENCE, the team member who has more reason to hate you than your opponents turns on you and joins your opponents. No, Copycat, you can't have a meaningful match at Superbowl of Champions, you have to face someone completely irrelevant. And for the last time, no, Copycat, you can't under ANY circumstances face Sean Edmunds or Scotty Michaels, the people you'd actually have some INTEREST in facing. Certainly not. Now go out there and get gang-beaten by L.O.V.E., that's a good boy. Something's seriously lacking in WFW, and I'm here to find out why.
(Copycat glances up at the receptionist, then gets up from his seat and approaches her desk)
Copycat: Hi. Looking for Alex Wylde's office please. (The receptionist does not look up) Receptionist: Mr. Wylde's office is suite- (She looks up and stops mid-sentence, then quickly changes gears) Receptionist: Um...are you a fellow wrestler? Copycat: What makes you think that? Receptionist: Well, I don't know, I just...I mean, you LOOKED... Copycat: Is it the size? Always gives me away. Okay, it's possible that I might be a wrestler.
Receptionist: Well, um...can I ask what you want with Mr. Wylde? Copycat: What's with the change of attitude? Something I should know about here? Receptionist: Um...we try to avoid letting other wrestlers, particularly WFW employees, near Mr. Wylde's office. You understand how some of the people in the business are.
Copycat: Well, I assure you I don't intend to do anything rash. Receptonist: I see...but still, it would be better if you would just tell me what it is you want from Mr. Wylde.
Copycat: I'd rather tell him myself. All you have to do is tell me where he is. Receptionist: As I've said, we try to avoid situations like that. It isn't an official policy, but it's something we choose to do. Now, sir, if you would just tell me what you-
(Copycat leans over the desk and looks the receptionist right in the eyes, interrupting her. She shies back a little)
Copycat: Okay, I'll tell you what I want from Mr. Wylde. Will that make you happy? Receptionist: Sir, please don't do any-(interrupted) Copycat: All I want is a little RESPECT. I am sick and tired of not getting it from Wylde, WFW management, other wrestlers, announcers, and MOST OF ALL at this moment, mousy receptionists who don't know what's good for them.
Receptionist: Sir, please stop, I don't want to have to call-(interrupted) Copycat: Security? Are you going to call security on me? LOOK. I don't have any problem with you, but if you don't start treating me with respect, I am going to DEVELOP a problem. Now, all I did was ask you where Alex Wylde's office is, and you could at least have the common courtesy to tell me.
Receptionist: Sir, I'm afraid I can't-(interrupted) Copycat: Oh, for Christ's sake. (Copycat reaches over the desk and grabs what the receptionist was looking at a moment ago. He reads over the list of office assignments, then drops the paper back on her desk)
Copycat: There. Thank you SO much. (Copycat storms toward the elevator, the camera following. We hear the receptionist in the background)
Receptionist: Security? Yes, there's a man heading up to Mr. Wylde's office who- (The receptionist is cut off by the sound of the elevator door closing. Copycat pushes a button for a floor)
Copycat: It is about time people start showing me some respect around here. I didn't want to have to do that, but she left me with no choice.
(The doors open. Copycat starts to step out, but then stops in the elevator doorway) Copycat: You guys sure move fast. (The camera turns around, showing the two security guards blocking Copycat's path) Guard #1: Sir, please come with us. We've been told you caused a disturbance downstairs. Copycat: A disturbance? The only thing disturbing down there was the lack of respect that receptionist showed me. You really ought to give her a performance evaluation.
Guard #2: A comedian, huh? Come on, pal, we're taking a ride back down the elevator. Copycat: I have business with Alex Wylde. I just want to have a conversation with him, and you have no right to stop me.
Guard #2: Your friend downstairs said you caused a disturbance, which gives us a right to escort you from the premises.
Copycat: That so? You've got a right to escort me from the premises, is it? Well, let me tell you about RIGHTS. I've got the right to get the RESPECT I deserve. I'm one of WFW's top performers both in the ring and on the mic, and I have the right to respect from the two of you. Now, once again, all I ASK is that you let me talk to Alex Wylde.
Guard #2: Not a chance, bub. You're coming with us. (Copycat, an annoyed look on his face, starts walking forward. The guards move to block his path, but he just pushes right through them and continues walking)
Guard #1: SIR! If you do not come outside with us RIGHT NOW we will have to use force! Copycat: Funnily enough, I was about to say the same thing if you don't leave me alone and let me talk to Alex Wylde. And according to the document I saw earlier...
(He stops outside a door) Copycat: ...This office belongs to him. All you gentlemen have to do is let me go about my business and we can avoid an ugly scene.
(Copycat knocks on the door) Guard #2: Please remain in your office, sir! We're taking care of this situation! (Copycat tries the door. It is locked) Guard #1: Sir, this is your final warning. Just come with us and there won't be any trouble.
Copycat: You won't even listen to what I have to say! That makes you no better than Alex Wylde, or the rest of WFW management, or the wrestlers in the WFW locker room! Now, I will HAVE my conversation with President Wylde, I will HAVE the respect I deserve, and you will NOT stop me!
(The guards position themselves to take Copycat down if necessary. He eases up) Copycat: Whoa, fellas...I don't want to cause any trouble here. (The guards back off a little bit, and Copycat takes the opportunity to kick the door with all his might. It holds fast, and the guards grab Copycat's hands and try to restrain him. While they do, he kicks the door again and it falls in. The camera swings around to show the office; inside is a terrified-looking middle-aged man, with a business suit and a balding head. The guards continue their attempt to restrain Copycat. He has an amused look on his face)
Copycat: Hm. You don't look like Alex Wylde. Businessman: N-no! His office is one floor up! Copycat: One floor up? That doesn't make sense. (He ponders that for a second, then throws the guards off) Copycat: Huh. These clowns must've stopped the elevator before it got to the floor I pushed. Go figure. What about you? You know Alex Wylde?
Businessman: N-not well...I just saw him a moment ago, though, he was heading out for a late lunch.
Copycat: He isn't here? What a shame. Looks like I'll be leaving with these fellows. (The guards are back up and approaching Copycat. Guard #2 has a gun in his hand. Copycat raises his hands above his head)
Copycat: Well, aren't you going to escort me out? I'm tired of this place anyway. (Guard #2, with his gun still pointed at Copycat, leads him away. Guard #1 starts to follow, but then turns and spots the camera)
Guard #1: Give me that! (Guard #1 rips the camera away. It goes staticky for a moment, then the image goes out altogether)

GARTHIsTheLaw — 12 April 2004 (Fade in on a stage of moderate size, such as one might find at a high school or community center. After a few seconds, Copycat enters stage right. He wears jeans, his trademark beret, and a WFW Copycat "Smartest Player in the Game" T-shirt. The echoing of his footsteps and the absence of other sound indicates that the camera is the only member of the audience. Copycat stops in the middle of the stage and turns to the camera)

That's what we're supposed to be doing, isn't it? That's why our match is a dream match, right? Clever vs. clever. How very CLEVER on the part of WFW's (through gritted teeth) ESTEEMED President Wylde.
(Copycat clasps his hands behind his back) Copycat: I tried to explain my standpoint without metaphor, Dan, but you just didn't listen. You -- not entirely surprisingly -- turned everything I said into a joke again. Well done. Well done indeed. You made a lot of people laugh, I'm sure. Of course, you'll expect me here to say I wasn't laughing, and I wasn't. That's the idea, yes? Everyone laughs but your opponent. And then that humorless opponent goes into the match with the psychological disadvantage of knowing he's been one-upped time and time again, so he loses, because without that psychological advantage, a man is really nothing in the ring. Nothing at all.

What good is outright comedy without a few jokes not everyone is going to laugh at? Please, Mr. Roboto, save us from our crazy workaday modern society, and take us back to a simpler time!

It doesn't get the audience up and laughing, but it gets the audience THINKING. At the same time, it gets the opponent thinking negative thoughts, causing him doubts, making him second-guess himself to the point where even HE doesn't expect to win. And when your opponent doesn't really expect to win...that's just the best. That's easy money. So the rebuttal part of the promo, that's where it's really at. It's the thrill of the fight, if you will.

After all...they're almost the same as mine.

I knew him, Horatio: a fellow of infinite jest, of most excellent fancy: he hath borne me in his mind a thousand times; and now, how abhorred in my imagination it is! my gorge rims at it. Whether you WANT to respect me matters about as much as the actual outcome of our match, who wins and who loses. What MATTERS is that you WILL respect me by the end of the night, I will see to that. At the Superbowl of Wrestling, I will secure what I desire, what by all rights should be mine already. I WILL get the respect...that I deserve.
