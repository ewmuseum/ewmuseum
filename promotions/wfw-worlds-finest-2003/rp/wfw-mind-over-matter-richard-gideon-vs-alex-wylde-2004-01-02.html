WFW Mind Over Matter: Richard Gideon vs. Alex Wylde

2 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
PaulNJ21 — 2 January 2004 This match is a Number #1 Contender's Match for the WFW World Heavyweight Title. The winner will recieve the first WFW World Heavyweight Title shot after the PPV.
The RP/angle deadline is Tuesday, January 13th 11:59 PM EST. Please send all angles to [detail withheld]
Methos17 — 4 January 2004 (The scene is a common one, the home of Richard Gideon, WFW North American Champion, in the midst of New Year's celebration. However, the sea of faces and smiles of the people present do not contain the young phenom. The camera slowly backs out of the study and heads towards a room not seen by the WFW cameras. In a small bedroom, Richard Gideon is seated in a suit and tie, with bandages on his hands and head. As he straightens his tie in the mirror he sees the camera and starts to talk to the WFW faithful)
Gideon - Well, I didn't figure to be speaking on tonight on all nights, but perhaps a New Year's Eve talk will get my final thoughts of the year that was 2003 out of the way. In my first year here in the WFW, I've gone from a rookie who had no fanfare for his arrival to an actual player here in the league. These past few weeks I've been gutted and bled like I was a hunted animal. Psycho's convinced the WFW BOD to have me in these hardcore abortions of matches, and I've come out the winner on paper. But in reality, I've lost twice when it counts. Psycho, you poor excuse of a man, you wanna fight me, without your toys and without the hardware? I'll be there. I've beaten you in a ring, and I've beaten you and your partner in the barbed wire. I'll do it again. But if you really want me that bad in a ring, then man up and try and beat me in the match and not the post match.
(Gideon touches his bandages tentatively, almost cringing when he does so. As his finishes his tie he continues)
Gideon - But for me to discuss 2004, I must discuss two things - my reign thus far as WFW North American Champion, and my chance to become WFW World Heavyweight Champion. I've won the second-largest prize we have here and I'm not stopping yet. I believe I have the tools with which to acquire the big gold, but I have to go through the President himself to get there. Mr. Wylde, you are the WFW President, which means you aren't a stupid man. You're an accomplished wrestler so that means you're not a bad wrestler. My question, dear sir is that can you reconcile the politician with the fighter? Can you manage both careers?
I won't answer that, because honestly, I don't know. But I do know I'm going to be ready to take on my biggest challenge of my career, and the first of many challenges of 2004. But for now, I enjoy the coming year. Good luck to you sir.....
(fade out as Gideon continues getting ready) EGarrett — 5 January 2004 AND NOW, A MESSAGE FROM YOUR WFW PRESIDENT. INT. OFFICE Alex Wylde is checking out of his Presidential Office for the day. He heads out the front door...taking off his button-up shirt...revealing a plain tight black t-shirt underneath. Most everyone else has left so Alex flicks off the lights and leaves.
WYLDE "Well Richard. I guess by this point you figured out that I pulled some strings and had the stips for our match changed. Hopefully you don't mind...no disrespected intended. I figured you'd enjoy having the opportunity to test yourself. Maybe go for the World Title...see just how deep your potential runs. How far your youthful exhuberance can take you. Hopefully further than mine did.
Of course...no opportunity comes without cost. In this case...your chance coincides with mine. You can answer the questions you have about yourself. Maybe find out you're better than you ever dreamed. Or I can squash you like a bug...then do the same to Shawn Hart or whoever the champion is and prove to the world once again that the rich only get richer.
Obviously one option is more exciting than the other. It may tell a better story...get over an up-and-comer etc. But that's not why I'm doing this. You see I believe that I will and should win this. I don't really think it would do that much damage to your fledgling career...after all at this point you have a frog's chance on a highway of beating me. No one would fault you for the loss. And I...I mean the WFW...can continue to make money off of you as the North American Champion. It's a pretty prestigious position I'll admit.
But in this case Richard...it's more for the greater good than anything else. I'm asking you to accept that this time you're taking one for the team. I don't think you can disagree that there'd be nothing better for this league right now than to have Alex Wylde as the World Champion. It would increase the viewing audience tenfold. Furthermore...as another one of our champions...you would enjoy the peripheral benefits of this increased exposure.
It only starts with money. Hot cars...flashy jewelry. What do you like, kid? Rings? Those new Lebron James sneakers? Maybe a *****in' pair of Oakley sunglasses? All of it can be yours if you just give up and get out of the way without a fight. It would also save me the exertion of having to pin you. And that's not to mention the greatest benefit. Groupies. T&A...hotel four-ways. You do like girls don't you Richard? Hmmm...maybe I shouldn't be so sure. Just trust me...whatever you're into...you will achieve beyond your wildest dreams as soon as you lose to me.
Now granted...if you put up a fight there's a lottery chance of you pulling out a win...in which case you'd have even more prestige. But let's be realistic. You have a lotto ticket on one hand and a sure thing on the other. In this world...you're better off not risking it. So come down...lay down...then give your interview about losing while on your way to the bank. It's a pretty attractive option if I do say so myself. And quite frankly. You Don't Have A Choice. (c)."
FADEOUT. THE PRECEDING MESSAGE HAS BEEN BROUGHT TO YOU BY YOUTH UNITED FOR ALEX WYLDE AND WORLD'S FINEST WRESTLING.
Methos17 — 11 January 2004 (The camera fades in to Richard Gideon, seated in the back of a cab. He seems to be lost in thought, but then begins to talk to the stedi-cam man in the passenger seat in front of him)
Richard Gideon: Well, I'd like to say that I'm not a bit apprehensive about this match. I'd like to say going up against the president of the WFW. I'd like to say these things, but I can''t. See, I've thought long and hard about your offer Mr. Wylde, and I must respectfully decline. You see, I have to earn what I get here. If that means I have to go through the duly-elected potentate of our fair federation, then so be it.
(Gideon pulls out a piece of paper that appears to have been folded up quite neatly in his coat pocket)
Gideon: I wish I could say this is a letter from a fan, saying that I can do it, because he believes in me. I wish I could say this was from a sick family member who's told me to win one for them. But no....it's blank.
(Shows the paper to the camera) Gideon: This paper represents everything I've done in 2004. Nothing. It's a new year, so that means I have to have new accomplishments. I can't live off of the fame of being the North American Champion. I can't live off of the fame of taking Doc Silver to his limit, or surviving in matches I had no honest right to. I am walking into 2004 facing off with you for a shot at the big one. Bob Evans always says, "don't ever make a deal you aren't afraid of giving up." In this case, I'm going into a match I'm not afraid to lose. I have nothing to lose in this really, except a shot at the title. Except a chance to knock myself into the upper-echelon of this league. Except a chance to prove myself in 2004.
(Gideon pauses for a second as the driver slows to a stop outside a restaurant in downtown NY)
Gideon: Honestly, this whole deal is a little strange for me, and hopefully indicative of the change coming my way this year. Last year I faced mainly rivals and rivals-to-be. In a few days, I face off with someone I've never really had any feeling about one way or the other. Sure, I voted for Hart, but that's going with the Devil you know rather than the one you don't. No hard feelings eh?
However, there is someone out there, who wishes strongly that this match end in your favor for the sad reason that he's got a fixation on me. Psycho. I thought I would greatly appreciate any legal manuevering you can pull off to keep the riff raff out of this thing and ensure this is a one on one affair. Then I realized perhaps you wouldn't mind that sort of thing. As a result, I'm going into our match with mindset of a 2 on 1. Do I mind? Yes. Will it matter? Depends on whether or not you will be a man and face me in singles alone.
The Gauntlet's tossed Wylde. I know nothing about you. You know everything the WFW does about me, which is essentially my short career. 'The System' is shutting down for this session...good talking to you.
(Fadeout as Gideon hops out of the cab) EGarrett — 16 January 2004 AND NOW, A MESSAGE FROM YOUR WFW PRESIDENT Alex Wylde is seated in a folding chair front of a computer-generated red, white and blue "WFW President" graphic. He's wearing a black t-shirt under his black and green leather jacket and jeans.
WYLDE "Good afternoon, Richard. I hope this friday finds you in good spirits. Or maybe not...because as you said you are feeling quite "apprehensive" about this match. As well you should be...because what is fear, after all, except the bodies' heightened awareness in the face of inevitable intense physical pain or embarrassment? I would actually say that fits your present situation to a T. Your options include the embarrassment of having to lay down in front of 20,000 people...or the intense physical pain that would result from trying to resist my conquest.
I would definitely be afraid if I were you. But of course...I'm not...because my options are quite different. My choices are the great feeling of triumph and control that comes from forcing a man to embarrass himself in front of 20,000 people...or the sheer sadistic pleasure of beating someone within an inch of their life if you dare resist. So in fact, I'd be lying if I said I've been doing anything except enjoying a cup of coffee or two in preparation for a rousing weekend.
Now...I know you're concerned about Psycho or Alcoba Guerrera or some other fool that you've come across interfering in this match. I've been given a memo with your request that I attempt to legislate this into a one-on-one contest. Unfortunately...if you knew anything about your league government...you'd recall that I have no power over my own matches. But of course...that's what I expected from a Shawn Hart voter...no political knowledge other than what he can read on a t-shirt. Anyway...the booking is up to the committee. And after all...if I could book myself I'd have been World Champion for about three months now.
In fact...I've had to work my way through the rounds here like everyone else. Apparently it's "what's necessary for the integrity of the league." Integrity is over-rated as far as I'm concerned...but that's beside the point. You do get the benefit knowing that I would be champion with or without booking...so maybe it will help your piece of mind. Then you won't have to waste your breath and energy complaining about the booker being champion after you are soundly trounced in this match.
Now...it seems that you've built up your piddling courage to force me to beat you with my bare hands in this match. At least for now. Remember...you do have a couple of days to change your mind and lay down. It would make it easier on both of us. But...if you want to wrestle...the deck seems to be soundly stacked against you. I mean...accidents do happen. And if Psycho or some other derelict peasant should happen to interfere in the match and make me not have to break a sweat...then I'm going to take the opportunity. And said person who interfered might find themselves open to benefit from a cushy job in my new Presidential-slash-Championship regime.
You may think I'm not "fighting you like a man," Richard. But the fact of the matter is...I'm not a man. I'm a politician."
FADEOUT. THE PRECEDING MESSAGE HAS BEEN BROUGHT TO YOU BY YOUTH UNITED FOR ALEX WYLDE AND WORLD'S FINEST WRESTLING.
