New World Madness: El Arco Iris vs. Adam Benjamin

30 September 2003
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
PaulNJ21 — 30 September 2003 Cut your promos here. The RP deadline is Monday October 13th 11:59 PM. Send all angles to [detail withheld]. Diablo — 1 October 2003 (CUE UP: "Na Na Na Re" by Daler Mehndi.) (FADEIN: Rainbow backdrop, El Arco Iris stands in his mask and a tie-dye t-shirt with yellow cuorduroy shorts. He somehow seems even more sprightly than he's been before, obviously elated for his first WFW win.)
El Arco Iris: Finally, things have begun to fall into place for El Arco Iris. El Arco Iris new that with his refocusing of his energies, the new wave of good feelings will be unstoppable. Now, as El Arco Iris has a few loose ends tied up, he must secure the whole tapestry. I return to a particularly bad case in one Señor Adam Benjamin. Señor Benjamin demonstrated perfectly to me that he was in dire need of being released from the yoke of anger and hate. Even though El Arco Iris' initial attempt at trying to get through to Señor Benjamin was a failure, El Arco Iris made a significant amount of progress. He did admit to El Arco Iris that he was, indeed, an upset soul. This makes El Arco Iris very happy, as admitting the problem is the first step to recovery, and means you are willing to help El Arco Iris to help you. Rest assured, El Arco Iris shall be the banister that guides your hand as you ascend the rest of the steps to total bliss! The second time around, El Arco Iris hopes to finish the job he started.
(El Arco Iris changes from his jovial stance, he slumps his shoulders and lets his head roll down a little bit, perhaps expressing sadness or concern.)
El Arco Iris: Señor Benjamin, though, said alot of nasty and hurtful things before. One thing he said is that El Arco Iris is not a winner, and never shall be. El Arco Iris has already proved he can win, and in awesome fashion, so El Arco Iris will not even bother addressing that, but the other thing he said to El Arco Iris was that he, himself, was a wrestler, implying that El Arco Iris, indeed, is not. Alot of El Arco Iris' opponents think that simply because El Arco Iris is a person of constantly amiable humor, and not the hateful sort, that he is not a determined athlete, and does not care about the sport in which he participates. El Arco Iris loves, nay, LIVES to wrestle, and knows that this is what El Arco Iris makes a living doing, wrestling. But simply because I don't think of doing nasty things to people doesn't mean I am not a winner. Because El Arco Iris always is ready to greet his opponent with a hearty handshake and, perhaps, a heartfelt hug does not mean El Arco Iris will not put up the struggle a good opponent expects. El Arco Iris remembers a time, moreover, still longs for the time, when there was one thing every athlete, in EVERY sport shared, and that is what made the competition so enjoyable, and not a frustrating and quite dangerous endeavor to participate in.
(El Arco Iris folds his hands at his chest and raises his head toward the camera.) El Arco Iris: El Arco Iris has prepared another special video treat, for your viewing pleasure, that shall hopefully help you in your quest to rid yourself of these nasty nasty tendencies of yours. It shall illustrate to you one key element that many of today's so-called "sportsmen" rarely exhibit, and that is probably what leads to their abhorrent emotional state. Let us begin!
(El Arco Iris walks over to a really old looking film reel projector, and presses a few buttons and it activates. The camera fades out and then in to the movie. We hear a bunch of projector-type crackling noises and then a faded, 70's educational film-looking screen pops up. It is a black screen, and words show up in rainbow colors.)
(Screen: "El Arco Iris film productions present") ("An El Arco Iris film") El Arco Iris (V/O): "Sportsmanship: The Lost Practice" (Camera starts to play some old educational film-sounding music. We fade to a young streotypical caucasian American boy dressed in a baseball team uniform with the word "Cougars" in cursive across the front of the shirt. He looks directly into the camera.)
El Arco Iris (V/O): Little Johnny plays on the local little league baseball team. Today is the big game, and Johnny wants to play his best, don't you Johnny?
(The little boy nods into the camera silently, responding to the narrator.) And are you ready to excercise good sportsmanship at all times, Johnny? (The little boy again nods, this time a little off-cue) Well then, we best get going, the game starts in 10 minutes! (The little boy nods enthusiastically, runs off shot, but after a short 1-second pause, he's seen walking back into the shot with a cigarette between his fingers--AND THEN WE CUT to Johnny standing on the plate with a bat in his hand and a smile on his face!)
Now Johnny is up at bat! He wants to score a home run for the team, don't you Johnny? (The little kid looks into the camera and nods again) Of course you do. Watch out for that first pitch! (A ball wizzes underneath Johnny's bat and into the catcher's glove. The umpire makes a hand motion as some crudely dubbed in audio shouts "STEEE-RIKE!")
Awww, too bad, Johnny. Looks like you didn't hit the ball. Now, what should Johnny do in a case like this where the outcome is not in his favor, like now? Should he:
(Johnny drops his bat and begins to kick the umpire in the crotch) A) Deliver swift kicks to the umpire's coin purse until he vomits? (Johnny then stops kicking the umpire, whose face is now covered in his own vomit, and looks into the camera with a dissatisfied frown, and then shakes his head from side to side.)
No! Of course not! Repeated kicks in the goonies, although very funny, accomplish nothing. How about...
(Johnny's eyes widen and he looks at the camera with a smile as if he has an idea. he runs off shot, and comes back with a roll of razor wire, and wraps it around the neck of the umpire.)
B) Maybe choking the umpire with concertina wire and resuming said assault on his manbags? (Johnny begins to tug on the razor wire, choking the umpire, and kicking him some more in the crotch.)
...And maybe busting up his nose? (Johnny turns to the camera and smiles in acknowledgement and begins to punch the umpire in the nose, making him bleed)
How about perhaps forcing him to eat his own vomit? (Johnny takes note, and begins shoving the umpire's vomit back into his mouth.) No! That isn't right! (Johnny looks into the camera again, this time a little confused, scratching his head.) Now, Johnny, even though the combination of blood, vomit, and twice-regurgitated vomit spewing from his mouth and nose does make for a very interesting visual effect, this doesn't help us at all, and doesn't change the fact that you screwed up! What he SHOULD HAVE DONE...
(We cut back to how it was right before the pitch was thrown. The pitch is thrown, and it again whizzes under Johnny's bat. The umpire makes some hand motions, and this time the audio says "STEE-RIKE 3! YOU'RE OUT!" Johnny looks upset, but then he calms down.)
Johnny now calmly accepts the official's call, and walks back to the dugout, knowing that it was his fault for missing the ball, and not the umpire's. Thanks to this little concept called sportsmanship, everyone can still have fun playing the game, and everybody's A-OKAY!
(Closeup on the umpire's face, he smiles and gives a thumbs up to the camera. Cut to Little Johnny, who does the same.)
Sportsmanship is the one thing that every athlete needs just a little of. If we all learn to treat each other with respect and dignity, then no matter who wins or loses, everybody leaves that game...with a SMIIIILE on their face!
(The movie fades out, and we fade back to El Arco Iris at the projector as the projector bulb fades out. El Arco Iris stands with a proud smile on his face, and gives a sigh of satisfaction as we FTB)
