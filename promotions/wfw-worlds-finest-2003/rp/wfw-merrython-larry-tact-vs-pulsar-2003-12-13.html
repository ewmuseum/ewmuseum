WFW Merrython: Larry Tact vs. Pulsar

13 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
PaulNJ21 — 13 December 2003 The RP/angle deadline for this match is Wednesday, December 24th 11:59 PM EST. Send all angles to [detail withheld]
Tact — 21 December 2003 Fade in: to a view of a city skyline. The shot is just high enough to peak over the surrounding buildings, while just low enough to still catch sight of store signs and people -- who do not look the size of little bugs -- on the street level. The clear, deep blue sky is already getting dimmer as the sun is setting; the buildings on the verge of consuming its light. For the time being, the resulting effect is a mass of shadows cast onto the streets, casting shade onto glass windows of buildings and the figures of the city's inhabitants. A flock of pidgeons come cutting past the shot, and a couple break off from the pack, landing on the blacony railing of this particular apartment. Suddenly a hand comes into the picture, and moments later a clear glass door comes sliding open; the light still having projected on the glass, making it seem as if it were not there. Nonetheless, a man walks out from the apartment, in front of the shot, and the camera now zooms out some to focus on this person, dressed in khaki pants; black shoes; and a short-sleeved brown shirt that shoes his toned, muscular and tanned arms. The man walks to the opposite side of the railing as the pidgeons, who seem ignorant of his presence as they nip at their feathers. The man places his hands on the smooth black railing, taking in a long breath before speaking.
Man: You know...there's something to be said about freedom.... As his deep voice trails off, he exhales the breath in a transparent cloud of air. Man: And I don't just mean as a country. Obviously, being here in the U.S. is a great privilege. But this isn't a political program, and I wasn't talking about that freedom. I meant freedom from this industry -- this business -- of wrestling. It's got it's perks to be out of the fold. You don't have to worry about any recovery time. No thoughts of how much time you have left to spend in a given place. One can be where they want to be, without taking any bumps or bruises. No constant need to keep thinking up strategies and battleplans for an upcoming match. Just...freedom. Freedom to do something else, something less stressful on the body, and in some cases better for one's wallet.
The man leans his forearms on the railing and takes some time to just watch the sun as it continues its slow descent below this urban land. After a short time, he can be heard laughing quietly.
Man: But when it comes down to it, I'm not so much different than most people who have spoken on being away from wrestling. Perhaps I've found different alternatives to it, but when it comes down to it, I'm not really cut out for that "easy life." It's nice on the side, but it's not what I want to do. So what happens? Just like many others, I get sucked back in. I didn't really expect to be out of the loop for so long as I've been, but when business stalls, or management feels a place has accomplished what their set goals..well...sometimes circumstances leave you with your freedom, unexpectedly. I thought, though, that I'd rest up, take some downtime while I could, and then start over again. Now, after scouting around, making some calls, and getting some returns, I've settled on a choice. And so, Larry Tact is back, once again.
The sun is partially covered by the buidling tops, and Tact turns his head to look at the camera. His olive green eyes have a lively hue to them. His hair comes down, in smooth bleached blonde spikes, to the bottoms of his ears, and he gives a grin before continuing.
Tact: Of course, I'd heard of WFW for a time, and I didn't doubt it's personnel. But there's a time for everything, and my time hadn't come to enter..until now, that is. I could talk about how my opponent's time to speak seemingly hasn't come yet, either...but that would be pointless. While there's a point to speaking before a match, the biggest talk comes in the ring, after each participant has been introduced and that bell rings. When it rings again, no words will change what has been witnessed between those bells. And anyway, too much talk turns it cheap. I'm not here to talk up a storm. I'm here to wrestle. If someone wants to continue shooting their mouth off to me, that's not a problem. I'm not saying I can't fling around my own points. But I'm more concerned with addressing any issues in that squared circle; the place where business can always be settled in.
Tact turns his whole body around, now, and leans back on the railing, his hands still touching it.
Tact: A word of advice, though, Pulsar. Don't expect me to have much need to get back my stride. I may have been out of the fold for a bit, and recuperating, but I had no injury that needed healing. Just the minor marks that come with extended, consistent time in the ring. My time off also doesn't mean that I haven't kept in shape. In short, I won't be slacking in this match...I never do. When I come to wrestle, I make sure I'm ready to go. I expect the same of you, because I don't appreciate my time being wasted. Having said that...I'll be assuming you're ready to go all-out, because I don't expect anything less. And if you want the thought of defeating me to be anything more than just that, a thought...then you'd better be ready. Otherwise, you will...be humbled.
Tact gives a lopsided grin and turns his back to the camera again. The few remaining beams of sunlight cast onto his face.
Tact: It's nothing personal. It's just the price everyone must be prepared to pay when they face me. It's the price anyone pays when their freedom gives way to their desire, and freedom floats away....
Tact continues to stand, his hands resting on the black balcony railing, watching the pidgeons hop off into the air and fly towards the sun, as it disappears behind the city skyline of buildings. Fade out.
stuclif — 23 December 2003 FADE IN: CAMERA ZOOMS IN TO PULSAR SITTING ON THE TOP TURNBUCKLE OF THE NOW FAMILIAR DIMLY LIT RING, LOOKING QUITE RELAXED WITH A COMFORTABLE EXPRESSION ON HIS FACE.
PULSAR: You know what, I couldnt agree more with this Larry Tact........ talk can become cheap thats why I dont do alot of it. Your also spot on about the whole "freedom from the industry" part. Ive tried it all before, but like yourself, Tact, I couldnt stay away. Hell, Ive even travelled half way around the world, left friends and loved ones to be apart of the "business". Its not only the fact that i have to make a living for myself, its the competition, its the intencity, not to mention the brutality.
PULSAR JUMPS OFF THE TURNBUCKLE, LANDS LIGHTLY ON HIS FEET, PACES A BIT BEFORE TURNING BACK TO THE CAMERA.
PULSAR: Just one thing I am curious about, Larry. Hows the mindset? Its your first big showing in a while and as confident as you appear, theres has got to be some nerves, a few butterflies perhaps, you know as well as i do that the fear doesnt go away, but fear and nerves are to different beasts that effect the whole in ring performance differently..... fear can be mastered and used to an advantage, while nerves can cause a wrestler to self-destruct.
CAMERA PANS INTO A CLOSEUP PULSAR: I know your just coming back, and i know you expecting a brutal match with no holding anything back. Your expecting all of Pulsar.....your going to get all of Pulsar. Every time i step into that ring im focused on finding the right time to unleash the Blackhole and dish out as much pain as physically possible and i expect nothing less from you. Trust me, you havent been counted out but i can garantee.....(CAMERA PANS OUT TO SEE PULSARS TWO HANDS SNAP A NECK IN FRONT OF HIM) you will be knocked out!!!
Tact — 24 December 2003 Fade in: to Rockefeller Center. The street lights are on in full force, with children and adults both swarming around on this night. It isn't cold, surprisingly, but instead a cool air flows through the city, allowing the people to pass along through the streets in a less bundled up, and more comfortable, way. Presiding over the center, in all its bright glory, is the famous Christmas tree at Rockefeller Center. It overshadows the people, the stores, and just about everything around. The camera focuses now on one man, Larry Tact, who stands by the ice skating rink at Rockefeller Center. His back is to the rink itself, with his black coat pressed against the railing as it extends down to his ankles. Underneath the coat is a deep red sweater, stonewashed blue jeans, and black boots. Tact looks at the camera with a calm expression and a small smile; though his eyes are telling of his focus on nothing but the camera. Tact puts a hand in one of his coat pockets.
Tact: Not the most focused upon place of New York City, for most of the year. But during Christmas season, Rockefeller becomes a spectacle for all to come see, and experience, for themselves. It's not the flashiest place...but it gets the flashiest tree. It isn't the busiest place...though it has some busy company. It doesn't have many of the most popular of stores...and yet it had its "fifteen minutes" on Felicity. The trick, you see, is not to think you know a place...but to just know it. Otherwise, you may start assuming the wrong things.....
Tact puts his other hand into his other coat pocket, and begins walking alongside the railing above the rink, which lies below the street level, although it is visible from there.
Tact: Pulsar, do you see what I mean? Do you think you do, or are you sure you know? Well I won't mince anymore words. Here's how it is, straight up. You can believe whatever you want about my mental condition at this moment; in other words, my mindset. I haven't had a match, under the lights, in quite a few months; this is true. I also can't deny that there's nothing that can substitute, no amount of ring practice, for the real thing. But don't assume too far, Pulsar, or else you'll get into the wrong frame of mind, yourself. I don't want that to happen, though, and so I'm going to give you just a bit more depth on this.
Tact turns right and continues along the side of the railing. Tact: When Merrython comes around...you can damn well be sure that Larry Tact will be with a perfectly sound mindset. I won't call it the "winning" mindset, because I don't believe there is just one of its kind. People find success in their own ways, by travelling down their own paths. For example, you seem like you may have possible anger issues. I won't look into what you did in your last promo too much, since well, we all know the art of deception and the art of showmanship. But one thing I must say about your actions was this....you certainly spoke something about yourself with not just your words. You also spoke with your actions. However, being that your actions did not speak very loudly, perhaps you'd be better off investing more time in talking? Maybe you just need to talk things out with someone. Otherwise, Pulsar....you may be the one having trouble, dealing more with the kind of nerves you're thinking of, come match time.
Tact chuckles a bit as he hangs another right, going around to the next side of the rink and walking along it. He glances down at the rink, and the camera does as well for a few moments, where we see many people, mostly younger ones, skating around on the large ice rink. Some falter, a few fall, but all regain their composure if it is lost, and resume their skating happily. The camera then resumes its focus on Tact, from a side profile as he continues walking.
Tact: I have taken time off for various reasons, and one of them was to calm myself down again. It was to reevaluate myself, my technique, and my mentality. Not as a result of doubt, or because I didn't think I was performing well. It was simply because I always strive to improve, and there is always something to improve on. But Pulsar, when I hear you and I see you talking about my getting nerves and butterflies, as if it's an oddity, it shows me just how much you still have to learn. Of course I'm going to get a few nerves, and some butterflies. The first time in a new place, especially. On top of that, getting a chance to wrestle right at home, in my home sweet home of New York City...sure I'll feel a bit tingly inside. But it's nothing I haven't felt before, Pulsar; nothing new to me. The nerves I get aren't from fear, they're from excitement. The chance to take on a new challenge, make an impact on another promotion. The nerves serve as thë reminder that answers my question of, "Is it worth it to come back?" The answer to that, which the nerves provide, is simply.....
Tact stops and swerves right again, rounding the corner in a second and leaning down on the railing at which he started, looking at the camera with a grin and a vibrance in his olive green eyes.
Tact: "Yeah, it is worth it." Cuz coming back and wrestling again gives me that spark of life; a spark that nothing else can match. And until that spark dies out, it'll never be a waste to come back. It will never not be worth it. So yeah, I feel those nerves, and I don't expect them to go away. I don't want them to, Pulsar. If I did, then I wouldn't be here.
Tact pauses and smirks as he continues to look directly at the camera, unfaltering. Tact: As for fear..? Fear is no match for me, when I have the mindset that I need to have to get something accomplished. And when I have the buzz of my nerves to go along with that mindset...forget about it. Fear will take a hike in fear of my drive, due to the sheer force that I put into the task at hand. Whatever it may be, if I have the combination in my corner, fear won't stand a chance. And if you aren't careful, Pulsar...then that same force I wield will beat you down and out, too. It's not all-out brutality...it's not intimidation....it's just the sum of three things. My mindset...my will to survive...and my wrestling.
Tact gives a quick wave as he turns and heads down some steps, leading to the ice skating rink. The camera pans up and around to the Rockefeller Center Christmas tree, it's lights shining brightly in the dark blue night sky. Fade out.
