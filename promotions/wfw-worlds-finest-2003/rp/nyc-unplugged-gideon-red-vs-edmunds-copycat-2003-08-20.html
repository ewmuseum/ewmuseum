GARTHIsTheLaw — 21 August 2003 (Cueup: "Bloom" by Sinomatic) (Fade in on a TV screen. An episode of "King of the Hill" is playing. The sound is turned down to the point where the dialogue is unintelligible, and the scene is a fairly generic one, so we can't really tell which episode is being shown. The camera slowly zooms out, and as it does, we begin to see a little more of the surroundings. The setting appears to be Copycat's obscenely large house. As the camera continues to zoom out, we see Copycat sitting in an easy chair that looks to be astonishingly comfortable. He wears jeans, his trademark "THE CAT" beret, and a Tenacious D T-shirt. In his right hand he holds a television remote, and he has a bored expression on his face. Copycat pushes a button on the remote and we are now watching the History Channel)
Copycat: Well now. To say I am faced with a bit of a conundrum would be an understatement.
(Copycat turns to the camera) Copycat: As I'm sure all of my Cat Lovers watching out there are aware, I am one of the many candidates in the running to become the very first president of World's Finest Wrestling. At first, my declaration of candidacy just had to do with one or two of my own goals. Thinking all the other candidates were vastly underqualified, not wanting to have to face such incredibly boring opponents as Ebony Jade and Southern Thunder, et cetera. But lately, I've started to notice some much more important reasons why I should be WFW's first president.
(Copycat glances at the television and changes channels again. We're now watching some hideous MTV reality show. He turns back to the camera)
Copycat: The first of these reasons is the fact that the OH-so-eagerly-anticipated first candidate turned out to be Alex Wylde. ALEX WYLDE for hell's sake. This is a man who DEFINES disappointing. Everything this man DOES is disappointing. When he gets the paper in the morning, the paperboy is disappointed. When he goes out to get the mail, the mailman is disappointed. When he goes down the street to buy some bread from the grocery store, the cashier is disappointed. And the cashier's manager is disappointed. Hell, I'll bet the farmer responsible for the bread feels a sudden twinge of disappointment when Alex pulls it off the shelf. And THIS is the man who's received the most hype in the presidential race? Well...we just can't have THAT.
(Copycat changes the channel again, this time without even looking at the TV. We're watching something low-budget on the Sci-Fi Channel)
Copycat: But that's just a minor reason. The most compelling reason for my candidacy - in MY mind anyway - seems to be this match I've been given for NYC Unplugged. I was a little perturbed at first that the North American Title shot that I obtained by beating Southern Thunder at Spirit of NGEN was suddenly up for grabs again for no apparent reason, but it didn't take me long to realize that my opponents are merely to be used as speed bumps for me. After all, it DID only take me two matches to get a title shot, and I'm all about paying my dues. No, my problem here does not stem from the WFW board of directors' decision to delay my title shot, that's no problem a'tall.
(Copycat changes channels again. We're on Comedy Central now, and it is a fairly recent - and enormously disappointing - episode of Saturday Night Live)
Copycat: My problem, to be honest, has to do with my partner in this match. I'm a pretty easygoing guy, so for the most part, I don't hold grudges. And as such, I don't develop many long-lasting rivalries. Thus, when I DO develop such rivalries, they usually become fairly well-known, due to their relative rarity. It is no secret, therefore, that Sean Edmunds and I have what you might call a (making quote marks with fingers) "professional rivalry." We haven't faced each other in...sheesh, must have been at least four years. But regardless, it's understood by most in the business that Sean Edmunds and I, for lack of a better descriptor, just plain don't like each other.
(Copycat changes channels again and we observe the cartoon train wreck that is Spongebob Squarepants)
Copycat: So why...oh why...oh WHY would we, in our first encounter in four years, be placed on the SAME TEAM in a tag team match? It boggles the mind. Now, I don't quite know where exactly I would direct blame for this, but there must be some blame doled out. There is no excuse for not knowing that Edmunds and I simply will not work well as a team. Surely those responsible for this match would realize this! And when I decided that those responsible for making matches in WFW didn't know what they're doing, it became all too clear to me that I simply MUST be the next WFW president - I will prevent this sort of foolishness from happening.
(He pauses) Copycat: ...That was my thinking. But now we come to my conundrum. Because it occurred to me that maybe there WAS a good reason for teaming me with Sean Edmunds. I've looked over some recent promos from our opponents, Richard Gideon and Felix Red, and I've concluded that said opponents are, from what I've seen, boring as sh(BLEEP)t. Clearly these two are to be used as cannon fodder for Sean and I. Yessir, I'd say that this match is more of an experiment being run on Sean and I. Two WFW newcomers that are pretty big names in the wrestling business, and on top of it all they don't particularly care for each other...what happens if we put them on the same side?
(Copycat grins) Copycat: I'll tell you what happens. You've got a situation where two of your top stars have to THINK a lot more than they might otherwise. Technically, they should be focusing on the match ahead of them, no matter how dull it might be. But the entire time, they're both going to be thinking that it would be MUCH more interesting if they'd stop paying attention to what's right in FRONT of them...
(The camera swings around in front of Copycat so we can see to his left side. Completely obscured from view before, there is another chair. In it sits a woman who, from her looks and lack of clothing aside from a few tactfully placed pieces of string resembling a bathing suit, is probably a stripper of some sort. Copycat briefly glances in the woman's direction, then back at the camera)
Copycat: ...And take a look at what's right beside them. So I suppose I can conclude that whoever is responsible for teaming me with Sean Edmunds might have known what he or she was doing all along. It isn't the best situation...but if it keeps things interesting, I'm all for it. I guess my presidential campaign will have to fall back on the fact that all of my competition for it is painfully underqualified for the job...but I have a sneaking suspicion that that by itself might just be enough. And THAT, my much more thoughtful I'm sure friends...is just all there is to it.
