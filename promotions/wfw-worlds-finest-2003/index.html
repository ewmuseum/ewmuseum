Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World's Finest Wrestling opened on the fwrestling.com hub on 29 January 2003. Its founder, who says he began in the hobby in 1991, started the Prodigy league NGEN and later left it to another president, posted a welcome that set out how the league would work: promos judged on quality and character, a 24-hour stacking rule, a single angle rule (no wrestler-against-management angles; attack "the Board of Directors" instead) and a clean slate, "so the dawn of time begins with the first card." The match rules, posted the same day, banned the nutshot.

The first card, the Curt Hennig Memorial Show from Madison Square Garden on 26 February 2003, began a fourteen-man tournament for the first World Heavyweight Title. Each match had a folder where the members posted their wrestlers' promos, and the founder wrote the broadcast from them, with Brandon Jacobs and Zack Sirus at the table. Almost Live in Trenton cut seven men to three, and on 29 March, at Vox Hiberionacum in the Revere rain, Doc Silver pinned Shawn Hart to become the first champion while Michael Manson, left unpinned, took the first North American Title. Richard Gideon won the Vox Hiberionacum Cup battle royal and pinned Silver in a tag match, Rat Fink stole Manson's belt, and the free pay-per-view Cherry Blossom Chaos on the National Mall in May made Psycho champion. In June Doc Silver escaped a razor-wire cage to win the title back.

What this record holds is 89 threads from January to June 2003: the broadcasts and their results, the lineups and the members' promos, the league's rules and a few wrestler profiles. The tournament's introduction thread, which announced a former CSWA champion's signing, is served on the CSWA wing, and the fed's later years, to 2009, are outside this record.

Affiliations

New Generation Enterprises (NGEN)

Predecessor to WFW (World's Finest, 2003–2009)
Its founder says he started NGEN and left it to another president; the fed claims the NGEN world title's lineage but crowned its own first champions in March 2003.

New Generation Enterprises (NGEN, New Generation, 2010–2011)

Successor to WFW (World's Finest, 2003–2009)
Its founder relaunched World's Finest Wrestling as NGEN on 20 January 2010: the WFW world, tag and North American titles were sealed for new NGEN titles and the BAD title kept its name.
