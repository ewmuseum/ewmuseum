WFW Mind Over Matter: Intro

22 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
PaulNJ21 — 22 January 2004 ::Brandon Jacobs, Vic Waters, and Jonathan Marx are sitting courtside as the camera pans the crowd all sporting their black and orange Princeton Tiger gear::
BRANDON JACOBS: Welcome everyone to Mind Over Matter, we are live from Jadwin Gym before a capacity crowd here on the campus of Princeton University. Joining me today along with my regular partner Vic Waters is Princeton Alum Jonathan Marx. Welcome Jonathan.
JONATHAN MARX: It is my honor to be here. World’s Finest Wrestling embodies everything I love about wrestling and when I heard you were come to my home, I had to be here in person. The Board of Directors very graciously offered me a chance to try my hand at color commentating with Vic so I thought I’d give it a shot.
VIC WATERS: You’re still wet behind the ears Johnny, but don’t worry, I’ll carry you. Poland Spring water?
BRANDON JACOBS: Don’t touch that stuff Jonathan, that Poland Spring water is highly flammable.
JONATHAN MARX: Vic, I’m surprised at you, public drinking on a college campus? You are going to corrupt the morals of all these poor unsuspecting college students.
VIC WATERS: What are you? Straight edge? JONATHAN MARX: No, I’m not a choir boy, but I’ve never drank a drop of alcohol in all of my life besides from a bit of cough syrup when I was sick. Alcohol impairs the mind and I treat my mind like a temple.
VIC WATERS: Sometimes the brain is better numbed. I’m sure Shawn Hart has seen the bottom of the bottle a lot lately after what happened with him at Merrython.
JONATHAN MARX: Shawn Hart is a great wrestler, I took him on in NFW and he even beat me, the NFW North point leader, in a hell of a battle. Sometimes he makes decisions, like going into that match with Michaels at Merryton, without any backup which baffle me. But when Hart brings his A-Game, he is one of the elite of the elite.
BRANDON JACOBS: I thought I would never see anyone get screwed as badly as Michaels and Luster screwed Manson, but they found a way to top themselves and that is damn impressive. I never saw that coming.
JONATHAN MARX: Scotty Michaels isn’t going let that tile go either. He has been fighting his way back up to the top of the mountain for a long time now and now that he is there, he has no intention on handing it to Edmunds.
BRANDON JACOBS: Edmunds has a lot of momentum going. He defeated Michaels, Copycat, and Wylde in the four way match for the title shot. He beat Gideon in his debut match. The only two guys that Edmunds has really struggled with beating is Jared Wells and Copycat.
JONATHAN MARX: Copycat and Jared Wells will never say it. But they are praying that Edmunds wins the World Title because they know that because of their long history together, they both will be in line for a title shot.
BRANDON JACOBS: Copycat who is going for the WFW North American Title at the PPV and Scotty Michaels has shown that title can lead to bigger and better things.
VIC WATERS: Right now, we actually have a WFW North American Champion in Richard Gideon who beat Scotty Michaels before Scotty went on to win the World Title.
JONATHAN MARX: That is eerily similar to Doc Silver and Manson. Both men had a claim that they were the real champion because Doc never pinned Manson. He pinned Shawn Hart. Hopefully Gideon can win his match tonight and move on to get his long awaited shot at the champion.
VIC WATERS: Gideon? What about President Wylde? Now there is a guy who has been wrestling for a decade for a fair shot at the World Heavyweight Title despite being a brilliant talented wrestler inside and out of the ring. But he has been bogged down by politics, given shots at secondary titles and not allowed to go for the big title in a fair fight. Would you be able to do that Marx? Would you be able to put up with that for as long as Wylde has?
JONATHAN MARX: While I am a man of great patience, it is hard not to aggravated by it all. I really try hard not to let things bother me, but I love this sport. I’ve loved it since I was a kid. When you see people repeatedly banging their head on the glass ceiling and getting shabby treatment, it is hard not to frustrated, especially when he is as talented as Wylde is.. Wylde is a much better man than I am. I couldn’t have done it. Both men in the main event truly deserve to face Scotty Michaels.
BRANDON JACOBS: Speaking of Scotty Michaels, fresh off of his World Title victory, he will be in action here tonight as part of a three way dance with the winners being able to name the stipulation of their choice for their match.
VIC WATERS: That is a very dangerous match. Who knows what devious thoughts will come from the minds of everyone involved. Imagine if El Arco Iris won. We could have First Hug, Care Bear on a pole, a Smurf or Surrender …
JONATHAN MARX: Smurf or Surrender? BRANDON JACOBS: El Arco Iris brings joy to millions of children and adults around the world. He is the Walt Disney of professional wrestling yet you feel the need to mock him. El Arco Iris has the heart of a small child.
VIC WATERS: Does he keep it in a draw in his desk? JONATHAN MARX: I can relate to El Arco Iris, we both yearn for better days, for me it is the old school wrestling I grew up on, for El Arco Iris… childhood. In an every increasing messed up world, it is great to have another world to escape to where times were better and bring back happy thoughts. El Arco Iris is a modern day version of Peter Pan. In many ways, El Arco Iris is my favorite wrestler, even more so than Manson.
BRANDON JACOBS: ::gives Marx a look:: Have you been into his Poland Spring water? JONATHAN MARX: *laughs* No, no… It is just that as you get older you have more of an appreciation of times gone past. For instance, you have both been to the new WFW Memorial built to those no longer with us, right?
BRANDON JACOBS: Vic hasn’t yet but I have, it is a tremendous place to go to look back at history. All of the greats are there. There is this feeling as you pass through the veil of remembrance of both happiness and sadness at the same time. That place brings back a lot of memories, even things that I had forgotten about. Everyone should visit there at least once in their lives. I really miss those guys. But you know what, times have to change, wrestlers have to retire and the wheels of time must go on, this is a young man’s sport. I think this current group of talent is every bit as good, if not better than those we paid tribute too and that is not a statement I make lightly.
JONATHAN MARX: You could be right about that. The more I watch WFW, the more I don’t miss the old days as much. It makes me feel good to be a wrestler. Speaking of which, isn’t it about time to begin the show?
BRANDON JACOBS: Good gawd you are right, let’s take a short commercial break and when we comeback, Mike McGee will make his return in a three way match against Good Eddie and Pulsar. STAY TUNED!
