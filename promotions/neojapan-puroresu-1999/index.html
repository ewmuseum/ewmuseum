Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

NeoJapan Puroresu staged itself as a wrestling company owned by a fictional conglomerate, MISHIMA, whose CEO Kazuya Mishima used a November 1999 press conference to announce his own retreat from day-to-day control: “NJPW is a wrestling company. We are not 'sports entertainment.' We have no place for that kind of nonsense.” Control passed to Vice President of Puroresuringu Operations Hiroshi Okasa, while J1 Champion Shoichi Mishima, billed as “Generation 13” of a corporate breeding program the press conference otherwise refused to discuss, defended the promotion's top title as its own showcase attraction.

Its own Player's Guide, revised to Version 3.0 by 15 December 1999, describes an angle-driven, booked promotion modeled on traditional puroresu rather than screwjobs or run-ins, preferring a three-to-one ratio of Japanese to outside wrestlers and a weekly television show, Denkoo Puroresu, backed by four to six supercards a year. It carried three championships: the J1 Rikidozan/NJPW Unified Heavyweight title, worn as two belts at once; a J1 Middleweight title for wrestlers under 219 pounds; and the Asia-Pacific Tag titles.

Its own two-night J1 Real Grade Tag League, taped through 29 December 1999, closed the surviving record with three title changes at once. “Burning Jadoh” Jinsei Asaka and Ryo Inoue, an ad-hoc pairing thrown together for the tournament, beat the corporate-favored Outer Dimensions in the tag league final; Tasogare no Prince won the vacant Middleweight title in a featured undercard match; and, in the main event, a masked wrestler billed as Genji Yamato dethroned Shoichi Mishima for the Unified Heavyweight title itself, closing the broadcast by dousing the fallen champion in fire extinguisher mist as the arena looked on in confusion.

How the promotion carried its new champions forward from that night isn't in the surviving record; the two-night supercard, and the corporate ownership storyline it closed on, is the last surviving document for this promotion.
