A weekly federation of November 2001 promising on-time shows and classic feuds over graphics, which signed ten wrestlers in its first week and posted its second show that month. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Signature Wrestling Federation opened applications for singles and tag team wrestlers on 4 November 2001, its own founding post naming the story over the spectacle as its whole reason to exist: “We are not about great graphics or 'extreme' for the sake of 'extreme'. The SWF is about creative people coming together and creating one hell of a story.” It committed to producing “quality and on time shows every week” and pitched itself equally to veteran handlers with a pet project and newcomers chasing a first title.

A follow-up two days later redirected applicant inquiries to a corrected address and reported “some promising apps” already coming in.

By 9 November the roster stood at ten wrestlers with eight more spots still wanted, the tag team division proving the hardest to fill. That same post named a hope beyond its own roster: “Hopefully we will air on the new TEWF network, so people can get a look at us as well.”

Its second show went out on 26 November 2001, and the post announcing it was satisfied rather than promotional: “Our second show came out pretty good, I thought.”

That 26 November post is the last document the Signature Wrestling Federation's own record carries. Whether it ever aired on the TEWF network it hoped for, how many further shows followed its second, and how or when it ended aren't in the surviving record.
