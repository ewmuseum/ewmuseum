A hypermart-hosted Usenet e-fed that absorbed wrestlers from a folded rival within weeks of its own April 1999 launch, running weekly broadcasts and pay-per-views around a heavyweight and a regional title through an on-screen ownership takeover and a mid-year staff reshuffle. Twenty-five wrestlers survive across two shows and three roleplays. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Great Lakes Online Wrestling opened with a recruitment post on 13 April 1999, promising wrestlers and tag teams a small, fun-first operation rather than a polished one, and its own broadcasts leaned into the coincidence of its acronym from the start — one commentator's shirt in its first weeks read “GLOW — Gorgeous Ladies of Wrestling… err Great Lakes Online Wrestling.”

Its debut card, Genesis '99 (2 May 1999, before 7,500 fans in Erie, Pennsylvania), crowned all four of its championships in one night: Tim Beck as its first Lightheavyweight Champion, King Apples and Lint Ball as its first Tag Team Champions, Victor V. Hill as its first Northeast Champion after pulling the belt down from the rafters on top of a fallen Kris Klepan to win a ladder match, and “Simply the Best” James Pillow as its first Heavyweight Champion, beating Jonathan Hammer out of a steel cage by inches after Hammer's foot caught on the bars mid-escape.

At its second pay-per-view, Redemption I (26 June 1999, Philadelphia), King Apples beat Andrew “Flash” Tucker and then revealed on-air that he, not Tucker's own handler, had been involved with the founder's wife, and that she had secretly sold him the promotion; Apples fired the founder and Tucker on the spot and installed himself as owner, in time also becoming its own reigning World Heavyweight Champion.

After what its own October 1999 recruitment call for the G-Cup admitted had been “some problems over the summer,” it relaunched with a sixteen-man tournament mixing its own roster against outside entrants for a future shot at Apples' title, and brought back a veteran rival from Apples' past, “Desire” Brandon Bloom, to challenge him directly. Billed as “the final GLOW card of this century,” its 31 December 1999 show closed the tournament with Apples retaining the World Heavyweight Championship over Bloom in a fifty-six-minute match refereed by James Pillow, then unveiled the returning Brotherhood faction, led by “Cold and Calculating” Tony Houston.

Its record continues into 2000: by March, Bloom had become Apples' own reluctant Tag Team Championship partner and “The Cobra” Marco Carlson held the Northeast Championship after driving its longtime holder, Victor Hill, into retirement. Its 22 March 2000 broadcast ends on a swerve: the Brotherhood's Eddie Jacobs turned on both Apples and Bloom, hanging each of them in a suspended steel cage above the ring alongside the promotion's original founder, who declared the start of a new “Reign of Terror” under Jacobs.

No further show survives past that 22 March 2000 broadcast.
