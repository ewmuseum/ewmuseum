NSPW: The Change Begins

18 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hey guys ... Just looking around to see if any talent is looking for a home. I'm specifically looking for heels. Oddly enough, the faces are what is in over abundance in the Fed. Crazy. Anyhow, I'm looking for maybe 2 or 3 heels, depends on how the apps come back. If you're interested, the first show is below along with the App. And as you can see, the results are done a bit differently. This was only the first show so there were some rough edges but it is a work in progress. - Mike ------- Live September 9th, 2002 : Bell Time @ 7:30 PM Taped September 7th, 2002 The Change Begins The first ever New South Pro-Wrestling event kicks off with the announcers, Gordon Hayes and Todd Patrick standing in the ring introducing themselves. The fans are pretty excited as they break out into a "NSPDub! NSPDub!" chant. There is maybe 150 fans in attendance. Not exactly the ground breaking fanfare you'd see at most wrestling events. Patrick and Hayes talk for a bit, going back and forth. Surprisingly - neither is showing any real alignment. Their is a lack of face/heel announcing as both seem relatively neutral to things. They hype the opening match, a Tennessee Triangle between three very young and small wrestlers. Both exit the ring as the introductions take place. Ryogo "Rising Dragon defeats "JFL" Ike Noble and "Lost Boy" Jonathan Darling via Dragonsault at 13:45. The match started off with a great deal of chain wrestling involving all three. Ryogo Matsuda had back-to-back-to-back-to-back arm drag takedowns on Noble and Darling. Darling was quickly taken out of the picture after Matsuda botched a Dragon Suplex. The Lost Boy was stretched out but the match continued between Noble and Matsuda anyway. Noble took control for a short while with a series of rest holds that kept the explosive rookie Ryogo grounded. Eventually, Ike Noble lost his once firm grasp on things and paid dearly. Ryogo hit consecutive Belly-to-Back suplexes, eventually hitting a release Suplex and following it up with the Dragonsault - which was just beyond impressive. Post-match, "Sanguine" Sammy Knight was almost barred from the arena. As Knight attempted to enter, Security did not believe he was an actual wrestler but a member of the Treetop Piru's, a sect of the infamous Bloods. After Jerry Quinn rectified the situation, Knight was said to have been "fuckin' pissed" that it happened and swore on his baby boy's life he'd take his frustration and disgust on Brody Serra later in the evening. Father Vengeance beats Dennis Windham via Boston Crab at 13:32 Before anyone could say "go" these two men were going punch-for-punch all around ringside. Windham took an early advantage with a series of lariats on Vengeance. Eventually, Windham brought it into the ring and that was where it all went wrong. Vengeance dodged a boot to the face and connected with a huge snap powerbomb for a two count. As Vengeance took firm control, he executed a beautiful Spinebuster onto Windham that got a very close two count. Vengeance took a few moments to berate Windham, telling him he was sinful and barbaric. Windham caught a second wind, albeit it briefly, hitting a series of dropkicks. It was all for naught, however, as a third dropkick was blocked by Vengeance and turned into a Boston Crab that got the W. "Sanguine" Sammy Knight defeats Brody "Death Wish" Serra via pinfall at 2:50. This match was not long, it was not classic, and it was not very exciting. It started off quick with Knight living up to his word, taking his anger out on Serra. The big man, however, fought back. Serra dominated Knight for a brief period with elbow shots, forearms to the back of the head, and closed fists. As Knight regained control with a low blow, Serra's manager John Morgan Daniels attempted to club Knight with his walking cane - but it backfired and he hit Serra instead. Knight covered and that was all she wrote, surprisingly. An examination post-match showed Serra was wrestling with a level 3 concussion and has been banned from the ring for a minimum of 2 weeks. Before the main event, co-Promoter Roy Watts came out to everyone's surprise. Watts had a mic and began speaking about his intentions in NSPW. As the fans were behind Watts for giving the South a much needed wrestling promotion to call their own, Watts quickly turned on them saying his goal is to, simply, control everything and everyone. Watts has no regard for Jerry Quinn's wishes of keeping the Promoters off of Television. He said Quinn doesn't know how modern wrestling functions because he's stuck in 1986. Watts said he has a plan to become full owner of NSPW and no one will stop him. This prompted, of course, Jerry Quinn to abruptly come out. Quinn was noticeably caught off guard by Watts' sentiments as he tried to squash the problem. Quinn said he doesn't know where this is coming from and that he thought both had the same vision. That vision being running a good, old fashioned wrestling promotion that focused on wrestling as a sport, not a soap opera with a side of wrestling. Watts grew angry and rebutted that it was Quinn's vision, not his. Quinn pleaded with Watts to rethink things but Watts said it was too late, it's already been thought of. Watts told Quinn next week he is going to unveil his plan and there is nothing that can be done. Quinn looked heart broken to say the least as he left the ringside area and Watts joined Todd Patrick and Gordon Hayes to do commentary for the main event. Blackheart defeats Chris Virtue via Below Zero at 23:04. This match was one of the best matches anyone in the arena has seen in quite some time. It was largely a Junior Heavyweight style match, both men with quick attacks and no hesitation to take it to the air. Out of the gates, Chris Virtue surprised nearly everyone but himself as he took control. Virtue took Blackheart down with a Snap Suplex and followed it up with a springboard moonsault for a two count. Virtue continued dominating Blackheart after applying a Sleeper Hold. Blackheart appeared to be fading but somehow managed to grab the nearby ropes. Virtue paid no regard for the break as he began stomping unmercifully on an almost unconscious Blackheart. Blackheart played possum as Virtue loomed over him, sending him out of the ring with a sling shot. Blackheart rested up in the ring as Virtue almost got counted out because he couldn't ignore ringside fans. Back in the ring, Blackheart showed why he is the veteran as he took Virtue to school. An Enziguri stunned Virtue, only to be taken down with a springboard Bulldog for a near fall. Blackheart quickly went to the ropes as he landed a beautiful Corkscrew Anarchy Moonsault. Virtue tried to mount a comeback but it was quickly dodged by Blackheart after a desperation DDT. Blackheart went up top once more and connected with Below Zero, a Somersault Leg Drop, for the pinfall. Post-match, Roy Watts was proven a liar as his plan was not waiting 'till next week. Brody Serra, faking his concussion, hit the ring and stomped a mudhole in Blackheart. Virtue joined in as Dennis Windham joined them as well. As all three took turns absolutely demolishing Blackheart, Iron Fists Steve Watkins decided to actually earn his paycheck and hit the ring. Watkins cleared house, leveling everyone but the Kitchen Sink with vicious right hands. The shots looked as stiff as I'd ever seen. Watkins was quickly joined by Sammy Knight, as both helped Blackheart vend off his attackers. Watts got on the mic and said if Watkins, Knight, and Blackheart want a war ... they will get one. And just like that, a line appeared to be drawn in the NSPW with Dennis Windham, Brody Serra, and Chris Virtue on one side and Steve Watkins, Sammy Knight, and Blackheart on the other. I don't know where this is going but I'm fairly intrigued.
