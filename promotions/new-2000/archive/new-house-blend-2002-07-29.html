N.E.W. House Blend

29 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hey everyone, welcome to the inaugaral edition of National Extreme Wrestling's new house show report, House Blend. I'll be your analyst, Cameron Cavendish. I know some of you will wonder why I'm qualified to report and analyze these cards. SO I'll tell you quickly before we begin. I'm 30 years old and the youngest of four brothers, all of whom became independent wrestlers. There were the twins Boswell and Cosgrove who used to wrestle as The Blue Blood Brothers in the 80's and early 90's and now run their own promotion, Outer Banks Wrestling from the family home in Manteo, North Carolina. My middle brother Choate, the power wrestler in the family, who runs the most popular gym in the Outer Banks after having several vertebrae fused in a mid-ring accident. And then there's me, who like Steve Goodwin later on the card, was supposed to be one of the most exciting young members of a wrestling family in the industry until I tried to leave the friendly confines of the family promotion. Now I work behind the scenes here at NEW doing all I can to see that you the people get the best action possible. And now, onto to my report of the recent house card for the Clearwater Boys and Girls Club held in the gymnasium of Clearwater High School in Clearwater, Florida. I hope you all enjoy it as much as I did. -------------------------------------------------------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------- OPENING MATCH JAMIE UNDERWOOD VS CHRISTIAN CRESCENDO I was worried I was going to miss this first match because the traffic was murder getting into Clearwater. But I got to the high school and found a good backstage perch with a couple minutes to spare. And I was ecstatic to make it on time. I'd been bugging Victor and Christian to put these two together we had hired Underwood. The silver tongued love goddess, Dawn Wells, stepped up to start the show. And immediately things got off on the right foot as Fight For Your Life by Y & T began pounding out of the high school's sound system. Chris may not understand why that sheet music jumpsuit he insists on wearing in the ring needs to go. But you can sure count on him to rock out any card you put him on. And the dude can wrestle too. Having seen him time and again down in Outer Banks Wrestling, I can certainly attest to that. As Crescendo was was in the ring stretching for the match, the sweet 80's metal went away and was replaced by Refuse Amen by Amen. Which I guess is cool if you're 19 and a fresh-faced rookie like young Jamie Underwood. But it certainly wasn't my thing. Not that I had too much time to appreciate the musical sublteties of Underwood's theme music. As with that clean-shaven baby face of his and those dazzling blue eyes, all the female interns were stampeding over me to get a mere glimpse of the confident young Underwood. Note to Vic and Christian: Put these girls in a ring with the opportunity to date Jamie on the line. The way people love catfights, all that clawing and scratching would do wonders for the ratings on Shockwaves. As I got back to my feet and dusted myself off, referee Ben Johnson signalled for the match to begin and the two men began circling each other. Underwood finally decided to attempt the first blow by coming forward and kicking the music buff in the gut. But Chris was alert to that and spun the youngster down with a dragon screw. This irritated Jamie mightily as he slammed the mat with both hands on his way back up to his feet. Getting vertical again, Underwood challenged Crescendo to charge. Which he did. But not in the way young Underwood was hoping. Instead Chris went for the baseball slide, double heel-tripping the rookie as he came out of the young man's legs. Again Underwood was annoyed and pounded the mat. But this time getting up and getting in the face of Ben Johnson. This is where Chris made his mistake. While Underwood jawed with the official, Crescendo tried to reach in after him. But young Underwood showed good peripheral vision and jabbed Chris in the eyes as he did so. The youthful New Yorker brushed aside Ben Johnson and began laying stiff chops into his blinded victim. Using the chops to back Crescendo up into proper position, he leveled the more experienced opponent with a superkick that sent Chris to the outside. But Underwood would give him no time to rest, backing up just enough to get a head of steam and suicide dive in between the ropes and drive Crescendo's back into the railing. Chants of "N-E-W, N-E-W" rang through the gym as he threw Chris back in the ring. Chris staggered to his feet just in time to kiss the mat again curtesy of a springboard bulldog by Underwood. Wasting no time after that, Underwood ran to the nearest corner and hit his Ace Splash, a shooting star press off the top turnbuckle. Immediately, Ben Johnson counted down the 1,2,3. But by the time the three hand hit, I was already in the bathroom. I wasn't about to get more heel marks on my back from all those horny young interns. -------------------------------------------------------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------- MATCH 2 STEVE GOODWIN VS LARRY GAMBLE Emerging from the bathroom, I ran into one of the brightest young rookie talents in the South. A smallish guy just a shade over 200 lbs, a hard but likeable face and one of the best conditioned bodies in the business today. But until I got back to my viewing perch and heard the pane of glass break I had no idea why he was the there. But as the gritty opening riffs of You May Be Right by Billy Joel started echoing through the crowd it became clear. Larry Gamble was getting ready to go out and put on a show. Larry didn't get anywhere near the re-action he would have down in the Outer Banks. But that didn't matter to Larry. It was time go out there arms raised and do whatever it took to please the hardcore NEW audience. But I, and surely Larry as well, was thrown when the delicious Dawn Wells announced there was a last minute change to the contest. Larry's regularly scheduled opponent had not arrived at the arena and a substitution would be made. A substitution that would seem to work against him as Now or Never by Dope came to life in the gym and Steve Goodwin walked down the aisle. Right then I knew Larry was in trouble. He may be a talented young wrestler with great upside. But Goodwin was one of the men Boz and Cozzy had shown me a lot of footage of when they were training me for the ring. So I knew the guy would stretch young Gamble to the limit and probably beyond. The bell rang and Larry wasted no time in trying to get to Goodwin, immediately dropkicking him in the right knee. But Goodwin, being the sharp veteran that he is, got the arm in place and dropped an elbow across Larry's back as he was going down. Still Larry had his point of balance and he was going after it with a series of standing sentons to the back of that right knee. A prime example of why we Cavendishes expect great things out of him. When he doesn't get ahead of himself, Gamble follows basic principles of wrestling in unusual ways. But in this early part of his career that last name is not to be ignored. Larry has the tendency to take unnecessary gambles. Gambles like throwing Steve Goodwin out of the ring after that opening salvo. Larry loves to fly and he hates coming back from a match not having had the chance. And since this was NEW, one of the biggest shows in the industry, Larry decided to break out one of his most daring moves. He sprinted to the far end of the ring and went for a no hands plancha over the top rope. But unfortunately the veteran Goodwin had the presence of mind to catch Larry in mid descent and Hot Shot him on the security railing. After that severe impact, Goodwin wasted no time getting Larry back in the ring, rolling the youngster in the ring at the count of four. Entering the ring himself, he hiptossed Gamble to center ring. but instead of pinning Larry, he decided to show some moves of his own. Jerking Larry up into a series of three shortarm clotheslines and hitting a short bulldog on the fourth pull. It looked like he would put Larry away with a front facing STF but mid-way through the leglock portion he got another idea. He yanked Larry up a fifth time and buried a knee right in the daredevil's gut. Then as his new notion he set Larry up for a belly to back but at mid-move turned him around for a powerbomb that I'm told Goodwin calls his Blue Bomber. Whatever he calls the move, it was effective as he quickly got the one, two, three. Larry, my friend, you better believe when you get back to The Banks Boswell will breaking this one down for ya. You're a good kid but you really should have done better. -------------------------------------------------------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------- MATCH 3 THE ANDRAZOONS VS EL TORPEDO AND MIKE VON SPEEDY The young prodigy Gamble melted back into the dressing rooms and the local music prodigies in the Clearwater High Marching Band blared to life playing the Mexican national anthem. Which heralded the arrival of the next vic. . . uh competitors. To their credit though, Torpedo and Von Speedy did put on a united front coming down the aisle. Torpedo, a Silver King/El Dandy-sized lucha in a gun metal gray bodysuit and black warhead mask was on the left. While Von Speedy, a well built cruiserweight with the face of Kerry Von Erich and the body of Chavo Guerrero Jr, was on the right. And as they walked down the aisle I couldn't help but re-make my long standing wish that Mike would find another field. He's a good guy but he gets so much flak because folks think he's trading on the ghosts of the Von Erichs. But little did I realize things were about to get worse. I'd never heard of the Andrazoons before and when I heard Nancy Boy by Placebo suddenly dribble out the speakers, I was sad that streak was about to end. Glitteris and Succubus came out of the curtain in their own inimitable style and had me yearning to call the Unversity of North Carolina. We need to find out what genius thought it would be a good idea to clone the Exotic Adrian Street. TWICE! He needs to be arrested for crimes against humanity. It felt my eyes would burst looking at these two garish, I guess you'd call them men. Finally the bell gave a merciful peal and the match was on. El Torpedo sprinted after Glitteris such as he can being the grandson of the legendary luchador Molassioso. While the poor Carolina Kid, Mike Von Speedy doubled up in laughter at the sight of these latter day Spiders from Mars. Which proved to be a big mistake by the three time OBW champion. Succubus, not the least bit happy over this mocking, scaled the ropes nearest Von Speedy and guillotine legdropped him across the neck while the more normal clone was still fighting hysterics. Glitteris meahwhile had El Torpedo backed in a corner and was kicking him down for all he was worth. And although I'm told after the fact this isn't Glit's usual fighting style, he was obviously very annoyed by the third generation slug's training film offense. But that's nothing unusual. People usually are. You could say it's a family tradition. While I'd made my check on Glitteris and El Torpedo, Succubus had been suplexing the life out of my brother's pet project, the Carolina Kid. But now he was really going to make Von Speedy pay for his insolence. He whipped the ersatz Von Erich into the 'Zoon corner where he and his strangely compelling lady love Tiffani Twisted tied CK to the tree of woe. Finding himself counting the threads in Succubus boot laces would be the least of Von Speedy's concerns though as NEW's answer to Ziggy Stardust began dropkicking him repeated in the immobilized knees. Once Glitteris had finished mugging El torpedo on the other side of the ring, Succubus untied the Carolina Kid and the two Zoons prepared to train wreck the jobbers at center ring. But Von Speedy's knee buckled from the abuse it had suffered. So the Zoons both came running at El Torpedo and sandwiched him in between twin flying superkicks. Succubus then scraped Von Speedy and hit his spinning spike mare, the Pure Morning to add insult to Von Speedy's injury before collecting the one, two, three. I guess watching these Andrazoons was a better experience than I'd expected. They actually showed some good moves and solid teamwork. But I have no intention of apologizing to them for the David Bowie cracks. They do look like The Spiders From Mars. As for my assessment of Tiffani Twisted, take it for what it's worth. After all, it comes from guy who used to think Dale Bozzio was hot when she sang lead for Missing Persons. -------------------------------------------------------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------- MATCH 4 LARRY GIONET VS ZEKE CALHOUN The show continued as its lightning pace as barely had Von Speedy and Torpedo headed back through the curtain than the plucking banjo string of the classic bluegrass instrumental Foggy Mountain Breakdown by Flatt and Scruggs was heard. And no, for the curious, this reporter has no knowledge either picker being related to former NEW preliminary Dowell Flatt. Not that this was at all germain to the young man coming down the aisle. He was a big ole babyfaced plowboy in coveralls and tan leather work boots. A look that when combined with his short black hair and muttonchops that look like they'd cover half of I-20 made him look like a combination of a Godwin brother and Rico the Underqualified Stylist. As Calhoun shook and shivered his way down the aisle to the music and piled in the ring, the music suddenly took a dramatic turn as Bombshell by Powerman 5000 exploded through the gymnasium. The no-nonsense New England scrapper Gionet scowling and pounding his left hand with a balled fist as he approached in all his black leather glory. A look that despite Gionet's natural intensity had everyone from the local ring girls to our own more qualified stylist, Russell Gay, all atwitter in post-card anticipation. The bell sounded and Calhoun seeing this as his chance to impress went right after Gionet who was still on the apron trying to enter the ring. Hauling Gionet in, the plowboy for Walnut Grove, Mississippi began raining hard forearms on the more experienced Yankee and started to pull off Gionet's leather jacket. However, this proved to be a mistake as Gionet began firing European uppercuts at young Zeke with his free right arm while the Southerner tried to pull the leather jacket off Larry's left arm. Having Calhoun stunning, Gionet pulled the jacket off himself and tossed it to a gawking ring girl before turning back intendiing to tear into the young rookie. But instead being folded like a gas station road map by a Zeke Calhoun clothesline. But that was the last offense Calhoun would get in. Gionet wasted no time getting to his feet and lulling the bigger Zeke into a collar and elbow lockup. But almost before the big ole plowboy could even start thrusting Larry to the mat, the Stoughton, Mass native began laying in heavy kneelifts in rapid, mountain climber like succession. A string of snap suplexes finished with a release German sent the Southerner heels over head into the ropes where Gionet attempted a camel clutch. Zeke's escape by grabbing the ropes didn't slow Gionet's attack though. As soon the plowboy was back in arm's length, Gionet staggered the rangy youngster with another forearm shiver in prelude to one of the best jumping piledrivers I've seen this side of Paul Orndorff. All that was needed then was Gionet's patented swinging fisherman neckbreaker, The Legacy, and the north had once again beaten the south. I'd call it a decent showcase match as Calhoun showed some determination despite being overmatched and Gionet struck together some very solid offensive chains. Besides, the ring girl won't hook me up with her sister after the show if I don't say something nice about Gionet. -------------------------------------------------------------------------- --------------------------------------------------------------------- MAIN EVENT "EXTREME WAR MACHINE" COREY IRONS VS VINCENZO SANDORA After an autographed picture giveaway, the show headed for home. As the lucky winners stowed away their treasures, the strains of O Fortuna by Carl Orff were heard. Out from the curtain as the music kicked up came the trio of Vincenzo Sandora, valet Cynthia Garibaldi and mutual bodyguard Ozzy Bates. And except for a few wolf whistles at the stunning Miss Garibaldi, the crowd went mild. So mild in fact, this reporter even heard rumors of crickets chirping up in the nosebleed seats. But that was no fault of Sandora or his entourage. This is their first appearance in NEW and the pops will come. Sandora started doing some shoulder rolls and conferred with his entourage as the lively keyboards of Danger Zone by Kenny Loggins jaunted through the small high-school gym. The crowd prompted exploded but whether it was a legit pop for Irons or an 80's Appreciation Society had bought up a sizable number of tickets was a great debate backstage. There were more wolf whistles to be had by Exotica as she led her "Extreme War Machine"down to the ring. Upon reaching ringside, Exotica immediately began waving fingers of warning at Ms. Garibaldi. But spotting Bates, Irons leaned over and whispered words of caution to his lady light before breaking the ropes. The bell rang and it was right into a test of strength for the two competitors. But Irons, having about a 20 pound advantage on the reputed mobster Sandora, started to get the better of that exchange. So showing why he's in NEW, Sandora buried a couple shoulders in the EWM's gut and delivered a low blow head butt for bad measure. With the momentum swung his way, Sandora proceeded to demonstrate his technical prowess by hitting a forward rolling neck snap to make Curt Hennig jealous. A few apply and repeat STF's paired with a couple of EWM knee joints sandwiched between the mat and Sandora's right foot were used as prep for the figure four. But Sandora over-estimated the damage he'd done to Irons and the figure four was reversed despite the fact it had been applied at center ring. Sandora pounded the mat in frustration as he broke the hold, unaware that Irons was already piling to his feet for his own attack. The awareness came too late for Sandora in the form of a running spear. Pulling the Italian to his feet, Irons promptly dazed him by stringing together a swinging neckbreaker, a Skull Splitter DDT and an old Mid-South style piledriver. Ozzy Bates tried to assist his employer when Irons went outside for a chair but Corey promptly gave the bodyguard a front row seat the hard way and that was the end of that idea. Exotica brought her man a second chair and Corey was back in by the seven count. Sandora tried to rush Irons as he wedged the chair in the near corner. But Exotica alerted her man to the charge, allowing Irons to sidestep and watch Sandora fly into the chair. A release German suplex later Sandora found himself in the sharpshooter Corey Irons refers to as The Web Of Death and this one was in the history books. Danger Zone piped back to life and the 80's Appreciation Society let loose another loud cheer of musical approval. Then after the ring was cleared, the lights were dimmed and it was time for all of us to go home. Overall a very good card. Solid showings by the NEW newcomers, especially Underwood and Gionet. An impressive albeit bizarre tag team match. And a clinic in the main event. Which garnered a good couple grand for the Boys and Girls Club. Good action and an even better cause. Can't complain about any of that. Next time NEW has a house show in your town I highly recommend you go. I can guarantee you won't be disappointed.
