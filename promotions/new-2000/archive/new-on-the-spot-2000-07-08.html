Higor puts the shot glass that he hold in his hand back down on the bar.]

Higor Rosa: Who would've thought, eh? Who the f[CENSORED]k would have thought that The People's Drunk would ever wrestle again? Nobody believed in my return. Nobody gave me hope. You know what though?
F[CENSORED]K that S[CENSORED]T! Nobody out there ever thought of me returning. As a matter of fact, I bet most of you were happy that I was gone. Was I really paralyzed? Not really. I just tore some muscles and s[CENSORED]t, so I had to rebuild my muscles and s[CENSORED]t at a small pace, which took me about six months. I bet that old fed that thought they had rid me from the world of wrestling is pretty pissed off right now. I'm not under contract with that garbage promotion anyway, so I can't even mention them. I can't even remember the name of the wrestlers that did this to me. I just remember waking up from a coma in the hospital after about two and a half weeks. Only two people visited me in the hospital for the time I spent there without getting out. My old friend outside of this job, Jarred Bombay, and my prodigy, which
I brought into the sport and trained him myself, Shawn Foster. I wanna thank the both of them extra for helping me get back on track and being able to get under contract with N.E.W. and back into the ring. Without you two there to back me up and force me to get back, I probably wouldn't have tried to come back on my own. Anyway, now onto N.E.W. matters.
[Higor turns around and grabs another shot of tequila. He dries that shot down without hesitation. He puts the glass back on the bar. Four shots are left now.]
Higor Rosa: Damn, this s[CENSORED]t's good! Anyway, N.E.W. I'm sure most of you know what you are getting into now that I have returned.
I am going to continue right where I left off last time. Being the most hardcore motherf[CENSORED]r in the business. That's how I left and that is how I shall keep it. Hardcore legends never get rusty, so I am far from rusty. I am a hardcore legend. Unfortunately, I didn't get to prove myself as much when I made my return match last week. I lost to two lightweight nobodies. Bah! That was terrible. Not only that, but
I was the first one to be eliminated. Since when have Parker and Madison been able to beat me? That loss was a joke. This week I get Tyrone
Parker back in the ring for another match. Hey Parker, don't get too excited about pinning me last week. You won't get it done again. I hope they allow me to bend the rules in our match, because I will be bending them to the maximum limit there is when a ref isn't watching.
Hahaha! You're in for a real treat, Parker. You, a small cruiserweight, versus me, a hardcore heavyweight. I have the height and weight advantage on you. That also means I have the strength and power on you too. Only possible thing you have me beat on is speed. Not even how high flying you are, because I guarantee you I can fly better.

Higor Rosa: Tyrone, think about it for a minute. Its true. Its true. N.E.W. Heavyweight Title. I need some sort of warm-up match before going for a major federation championship belt. I know I am the best talent in
N.E.W. right now. Not even anyone in my own can come close to my level.

You don't joke around with the booking of a major superstar like that. Especially when the superstar is a returning hardcore legend like myself. Parker, your time is coming. I shall waste you like its the good ol' days in that minor, Los Angeles Wrestler organization that we used to wrestle for. Oh, wait, I forgot! I was the superstar there. You were just another nobody trying to earn main event status as hard as you could.
See you around, loser. Hahaha. [Higor grabs another shot glass and drinks it down as the screen slowly fades away to black, with two other shots of tequila remaining.]
_______________________________________________________________________ |_______________________________________________________________________| _____ _____ _ _ _ | | | | __| | | | | Jeremy Steele | | | |_ | __|_ | | | |_ |_|___|_| |_____|_| |_____|_| Promoted by: Brandon Bloom _______________________________________________________________________ |_______________________________________________________________________| [Black.] VO: I wanted to come back on my terms. [We hear an uncomfortable sigh.] VO: Looks like I don't have much of a choice. [Fade in: The shot opens to the exterior of a house located in a neighborhood. The setting is a small suburb of Detroit, Michigan, known as River Valley. The house is one story, but wide, with an old fashioned long front porch, screen door, and wooden bench that hangs from metal chains. The place looks fairly plain from the outside, very simple, nothing too extravagant, perhaps even a shade below generic middle class. It is past sundown and the area is dark, for the most part, with only the front lights over the screen door providing any illumination. The camera hears a shuffle and moves accordingly, trying to capture what was previously only a shadow on the far corner of the bench, revealing it to be a man. The figure quietly rises, walking into the light, taking slow steps until he reaches the center of the shot, finally captured in the illumination of the bulbs overhead. He is tall - six five - and well built like an athlete. The man is dressed in a pair of steel toed Doc
Martens, dark blue carpenter jeans which fit loosely, and a button down checkered shirt with the sleeves rolled up. His hair is short but shaggy, parted down the middle, a dirty blond color. His eyes are blue, but surrounded by dark circles, giving him an older look to offset his babyface complexion. He is tan, Caucasian, with a light salt and pepper color of stubble on his face, which makes him appear even older than his thirty years.]
[The man, of course, is not just any man, but four year veteran Jeremy Steele, a figure from the past, a once young, confident blue chipper who is now burnt out and tired, judging from his looks. He takes a moment to rub at his chin, then gives a deep, unsettling sigh before speaking.]
JS: Life's got a funny way of catching up to you. [Steele pauses, running a hand through his hair.] JS: Have you ever noticed the irony of life? I mean, take me for example. Four summers ago, I finally caught a break into this business, and once I got in, I sunk my teeth into it... really got a good grip, you know? I was hungry... for the glory, for the television time, for everything. After about half a year, I turned into this can't miss prospect. I worked my way through every big time promotion of the time... from Portland to Baltimore to
Anaheim to Yakima, every region that was big, I was there. I did pretty well for myself, I'd say.
[He pauses, gesturing with a hand.] JS: Of course, I had to contend with my own personal life. The loss of my brother, a rough knee injury right when I was striking it big, the failure of my first marriage... [sighs] life can be a real bitch, and I found that out first hand in a hurry. You learn not to take things for granted when things like that happen to you. You sit there and think, "Oh, that's the sort of thing that always happens to somebody else." Then, one day you realize you are that somebody... and it sucks. Don't ever let anybody tell you otherwise.
[He stops again, rubbing his forehead.] JS: I guess it was, oh, fall of 1998 when I had finally had enough. I just finished this stint up in Canada, Burlington I believe it was, for this promotion that was pretty hot stuff at the time. People considered it the second coming of the Double Eye, I guess. [shrugs] I guess I'm jaded from my experiences in those big time promotions. Never made it past the mid card, which always gnawed at me. I used to hear all the time about my potential... well, fat lot of good my potential did for me. So I took a leave of absence, put away my tights, and went down to the Bahamas for a little r and r. Best thing that ever could've happened to me. I met Cameron there... she's my current wife. We wed a couple of months after we met. It just clicked, you know?
[Steele pauses, smiling softly, as he remembers.] JS: I thought about coming back... I did, for a while, in the Ohio region
'cause it was close, and I missed the small time feel. Even won myself one more title for the trophy case, but you know, those things are overrated. I left the ring when I found out Cam was pregnant with our first child.
[smiles proudly] November 21st, Joseph Scott Steele was born. I vowed I'd be there for him as he grew up. I hadn't wrestled in months, and I had no intention to do it again... I had my own family, now. The business had aged me, made me lose my wild streak... hell, it made me reevaluate my entire life more than once.
[He stops, biting his lip.] JS: I just couldn't bear the thought of leaving a wife and child behind. I'm thirty years old now. That isn't old by a long shot, but in this business, all I see is guys in their early twenties with their whole lives ahead of them. Men like me... we're few and far in between. A dying breed,
I guess you can say. I didn't care, though... let the industry change, let it move on, I said. I didn't need wrestling.
[Then, he drops his head. A tinge of shame takes over in his voice.] JS: At least, that's what I thought. [Steele stops, holding a hand to his head. He slowly walks forward and sits down on the front steps of the porch leading to a walkway that connects to the sidewalk. As he sits, we get a glimpse of his face. His eyes are reddening. Steele pinches between his nose with his thumb and forefinger, eyes shut tight, then exhales deeply. Then, he lifts his head back up, a helpless look across his face.]
JS: I didn't handle the money too well... made some bad investments... spent more than I ought to have... loaned to people I shouldn't have... and somewhere along the line, it got to the point where I was having trouble putting food on the table. Now, in this modern age, the woman can just as easily be the breadwinner of a family, but Cam's got the baby to look after.
We just can't find anybody to take care of things around the house. She wants to be there, right now, too... this is our first child, she wants to be there for everything, not away in some office while some eleven year old girl cares for her kid and charges six fifty an hour for it. So it's my responsibility... I had to find a way to make more money. I've got a family to support now. But all I've ever known was wrestling. I never had anything but part time jobs before. Wrestling was the only full time job I ever had.
So, there's your irony for you. The main reason I left this sport was because I wanted a family... but now, to support that family, I've got to wrestle again. I never thought it would come to this, but I can't think of any other way to make ends meet. I don't want to be a failure as a husband and a father. I feel like this is my only choice.
[Steele shakes his head, a sad look on his face.] JS: Part me of is almost glad... I never really got to go as far as I wanted in the ring. I was so close to stardom all those times... [shakes head] but
I blew it. I burned out and couldn't handle it. I can't tell you how many times I walked into a promotion, got a big thing going, then turned tail and ran... because I was afraid of success. I was afraid I wouldn't be able to handle it.
So, here I am, under contract for NEW. I came here because I like the feel... I thought it had the best to offer me at this stage of my life. I'm not here to do much of anything... I don't care about feuds or titles or even recognition anymore. I just need the paychecks to make sure I can provide for my wife and son.
Of course, the better I do, the more money I'd make... so maybe that'll be enough to drive me to get back the edge I once had. The competitive fire within me was put out a long time ago... but maybe, deep down, it's still there, waiting to be lit again.
[Slowly, Steele rises, whiping off his hands on his jeans.] JS: Like I said before, life's got a funny way of catching up to you. When
I was a little kid, I used to have daydreams about wrestling in front of huge crowds... I couldn't wait for the day when I could wrestle.
[Steele frowns, shaking his head sadly.] JS: Now I pray for the day when I don't have to anymore. [He runs a hand through his hair once again, letting out a deep sigh. Slowly, he turns, reaches out, and opens the screen door. We can faintly hear a woman's voice and the coos of a baby in the background. Steele steps inside, closing the door behind him. The camera lingers on the front porch for only a moment, but then it fades to black.]
