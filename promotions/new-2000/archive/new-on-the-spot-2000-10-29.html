[Cut to a clip of Ken Curtis blasting Eddy Jacks across the face with a steel chair.] VO : He changed his gimmick. [Segue to a shot of Eddy Jacks, in a blue mask, getting pounded by a young-looking Rob Donovan.] VO : He changed the way he wrestled. [And, from there, to a shot of Bad Eye McBaine stuffing a light bulb into Jacks' mouth.] VO : And still, he lost. Almost every match, almost every night, for thirty years. [Montage of still images from the NEW: Jacks power-bombing Jarrod O'Lachlan into oblivion. Jacks smashing Jeremy Steele in the mouth with a heavily-taped fist. Jacks choking out Tyrone Parker.] VO : And Jacks' moments, here in the NEW, have been _ beautiful _. [Jacks scoring with an uppercut on Damian Rose. Jacks flattening Chris Courtade with a knee to the groin.] VO : An old man is dying. His body is self-destructing. But here, at the very end, he is _ winning _.
[We fade out... And find ourselves standing outside a battered log cabin. If the caption at the bottom of the screen didn't tell us we were in Ottawa,
Ontario, we'd swear we were down in the States, staring at some North
Cackalacky slave dwelling.
We're not. This is Canada. This is Canada before the industrial revolution, before electricity, before civilization.
A dirty shack, on the outskirts of oblivion. This isn't a historical site. We're not here for a history lesson. This is where You Know Who grew up. Yeah, we're talking about Jacks. Eddy Jacks, to be exact. See him? He's over there, standing at the steps. Black jeans, white shirt, old as dirt...you get the drift. There're a few new details, though: the burns on the face, the marks on his neck...testaments, respectively, to recent battles with the Strangler and Chris Courtade.
Get out the tissue paper. Everyone's favorite Crusty Old Bastard is bound to get all misty-eyed.
That's how these 'coming-home' segments always go, y'see: somebody says they're gonna win, and then gets weepy. It's like going on Oprah.
But enough idle chit-chat. Jacks is here, he's not queer, and he's rarin' to speak.] EJ : Fifty-six years ago, right 'ere in this damned spot, Mama
Jacks puked me outta her friggin' womb.
If LOCO'd been here, he mighta gone an' ate me. [Chuckles.] EJ : It was so bad 'ere, growin' up, that I can _ afford _ to laugh. [Pause.] EJ : Everybody else cries an' bitches. Everybody else says, 'I coulda done this, I shoulda done that.' [Jacks walks up the steps, onto the front porch. We're amazed the rotted wood supports his weight. Maybe he's on a diet. Who knows?]
EJ : Which brings me, in a roundabout way, ta Rick Stevens. People like the Strangler, Dallas Reed, an' Chris f'n Courtade... They've been around. They know the _ drill _. [Jacks pats the frame of the cabin.] EJ : Old, stable, sturdy. M' adopted dad built this cabin sixty-seven years ago. Ground underneath, part o' the Canadian shield, 's even older. [The trademark long-a$$ pause. Here's looking at you, UEW.] EJ : Yer barely 20, Stevens. Yer still a kid. Yer head ain't sittin' right on yer shoulders.
In other words, ya got _ problems _. Yer big an' tough, six-feet-seven, cast outta iron, but a 'killin' machine'? [Dubious look.] EJ : That's stretchin' it. [Jacks fiddles with the doorknob.] EJ : An', besides, way I'm feelin' right now, you could be six hundred fifty pounds an' still not make a dent in m' friggin'
hide. I'm on a winnin' streak, kiddo. Went through _ the Strangler _. Went through _ Courtade _. They both hurt me, bad, but I'm still standin'. Still suckin' wind. [Jacks looks around.] EJ : Visitin' yer ol' homestead's supposed t' be therapeutic. Everbody does it. Hardin does it, seemin'ly on a month-ta-month basis. So does
Thunder. Courtade did last week. They go home, apparently ta sort things out, get things together.
I don't need anythin' sorted out. My genitals don't function, my new knees're rigged outta some space-age polymer, an' I'm barely able to see out of this piss hole of a right eye socket.
But more important than all that, I'm alive...an' winnin'. Can't neglect that fact. [Jacks leans against the wall.] EJ : Yer still sortin' things out, Stevens. Mebbe a visit ta _yer_ homestead would do ya some good, help ya figger out the meanin' a'
life. If it works for Hardin an' all them other boys, hell, it oughtta do wonders for you.
But, yer probably listenin' ta this, an' sayin', 'I'm twenny, I'm angry, my 'mones are ragin', just you watch me kick Eddy Jacks'
senile arse.' [Spits.] EJ : Or mebbe ya think, on account a' the fact that Baltimore an' the Strangler an' God knows who else are out ta get me, ya've got a better'n average shot o' takin' me down?
[Jacks shakes his head.] EJ : Not on yer worst day, kid. Not a chance in hell. [Pause.] EJ : Step inta the ring wit' me, Stevens, wit' that sorta philosophy--that the ring is a friggin' mudhole--an' yer in fer a rude awakenin'. Ring ain't a mudhole, boy...it's an operatin'
table. Baltimore, an' the Strangler...they're on the back-burner. It's main event time, yet a-friggin' 'gain.
[Looks away.] EJ : Yer in a main event, Angel face. Enjoy the limelight. Won't last long. [And on that note, we...
