An E-Fed Universe-hosted weekly promotion running two recap shows (Shockwaves, On the Spot) and periodic Slam Dance and Brawl on the Bayou supercards, with house shows in Clearwater and Charleston reported in 2002. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

No founding statement of National Extreme Wrestling survives. Its earliest surviving document — posted to rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy and to its own site at efeduniverse.com — is an On the Spot report dated 1 July 2000, and through the rest of that year it ran as a weekly angle-writing newsletter, On the Spot alongside Saturday Night Shockwaves and Saturday Night Slam Dance, with each wrestler's own segment posted as its own vignette rather than assembled into one narrated card. “The People's Drunk” Higor Rosa opened one such report explaining a storyline return from injury in his own words: he wasn't under contract to the fed that had written him out, couldn't even recall its name, and remembered waking from a coma after two and a half weeks with only two visitors — an old friend from outside the business and his own trainee, Shawn Foster.

After a 30 January 2001 committee notice, no further document survives until July 2002. Its own On the Spot and Sunday Night Shockwaves postings that summer survive only as its president's links to text files hosted off its site, no longer readable; what carries whole from that year is a new recap column, House Blend, fronted by an in-fiction analyst, Cameron Cavendish, one of four wrestling brothers by his own account. His first column recaps a charity card for the Clearwater, Florida Boys and Girls Club at Clearwater High School's gymnasium on 29 July 2002: Jamie Underwood over Christian Crescendo, Steve Goodwin over Larry Gamble, the Andrazoons (Glitteris and Succubus) over El Torpedo and Mike Von Speedy, Larry Gionet over Zeke Calhoun, and “Extreme War Machine” Corey Irons closing the show over Vincenzo Sandora, valeted by Cynthia Garibaldi and bodyguarded by Ozzy Bates.

Cavendish's second column, from the Elks Club in Charleston, South Carolina on 18 August 2002, keeps most of the same roster and adds Ernest “Brick” Wall, Red Carter and Curly Cuse as the Style Council, newcomer “Airwalk” A.J. Summers, and a main event pitting “Sex Pistol” Pete Sheridan and his manager Claire Maynard against Jamie Underwood. Both cards ran under the same promoter, Christian Leadfoot, and referees Ben Johnson and Michael Hunt — the promoter, one referee and one wrestler (Wall) all carried over from its 2000 broadcasts. A roleplay posted the following day has Maynard handcuffing Sheridan to a table leg the night before his own first-round match in a tournament against Vic Ventressa, promising to free him once he learns to cut a promo.

No document survives after the 18 August 2002 House Blend. How, or whether, it closed after that isn't in the surviving record.
