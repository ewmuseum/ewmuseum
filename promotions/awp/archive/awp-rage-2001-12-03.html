AWP RAGE, 3 December 2001

3 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

— Part 1 of 3 —

[As we flick through the channels of our TV screen, all the usual garbage is on. Nothing worth the time or effort to watch. Eventually, the screen explodes to life as we discover the latest installment of AWP. The opening scenes are bringing the screen to life, like no other show has been able to do in quite some time. Power bomb here, Death Valley Driver there, all rounded up with the warm fuzzy sensation we get, mixed with the stomach wrenching feeling we all know too well as we see one of the top stars be nailed with a stiff chair shot, leaving a deep indentation in the steel, as the once pale face becomes alive with the flow of crimson red blood, bright with oxygen suddenly dominate the facial features.) [We find ourselves wanting to see more, and put the remote to one side. As the opening images die down, the roar of the crowd that fills the AWP arena booms through our speakers at home. The screen comes to life as the fans make their presence known, filling the room with such a noise we feel we could be at the arena, watching every punch, every breath taking feat, see every drop of blood hit the mat. Yes, we are hooked. We want to know what this is, who are the ones these fans crave to see.] [Suddenly, the lights drop. The fans let out a quick pop, and even at home we can feel the pressure as we wonder who it is that will appear. Our hearts start to beat faster, and faster still. A loud explosion fills the arenas, and as it does so, it fills our room. We jump slightly, and immediately laugh at how something so simple as a loud bang on a TV could make us react in such a way. But then it hits us. This is what wrestling is all about. This is the element that hooks millions of people around the world to the sport. Immediately, we love it. This is our addiction, the passion we, the viewers at home hold.] [Following the explosion, a blinding light fills the room as a wall of pyros fills the screen. The room becomes illuminated following the short, yet bright and loud explosion. Calm follows the roar of the crowd, and the low sound of an electric guitar comes across the PA.
