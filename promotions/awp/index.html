A fantasy federation opened in October 2000 by an on-screen female president, which ran RAGE, Total Darkness and Breakdown through 2001 under a succession of presidents and returned under the same banner in 2004. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

American Wrestling Promotions opened in October 2000 on the story of its on-screen founder, “Lady B” Brittney Christopher, a trainer's daughter and former manager who at last had “all the power, all the fame, and all the spotlight.” Its debut card, Ready to Rumble, came that November, followed by Sunday Night RAGE broadcasts, Voices of Venom roleplay packets and a midweek rumor column.

Through 2001 its own handbooks told how that power changed hands: Brittney forced out and the champion she managed, Jean Paul Valley, throwing down the American title; a brief presidency for Akira Musashi; and his “friend” Desire, Brittney's cousin, taking over, deposed for a time by her own commissioner, Tommy Grayson, before ruling again. That spring it staged a King of Cruisers tournament pay-per-view, Spring Riot, jointly with Knoxville Championship Wrestling.

After some weeks off the air it came back in November 2001 billing itself “an all new AWP,” adding a Breakdown broadcast beside RAGE, merging three titles into a Triple Crown and adding a Cruiserweight title. Sinister held the American Heavyweight title against his lifelong partner, the Professional, as the Reign of Terror re-formed around him, and in December Chris Del Rio produced the deed to the company and had Desire escorted from the building.

The banner returned in October 2004 with the same founding story, Brittney now 29, and a new roster of eight building toward The Rising at Madison Square Garden: four Fatal Four qualifiers feeding a Tables, Ladders and Chairs final for the vacant American Championship.

Its last surviving word, a December 2004 update, still previews that card as upcoming, promising a “salute to the old AWP”; whether it aired is not recorded.
