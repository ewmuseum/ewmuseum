Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The CyberNetic Wrestling League unveiled itself on 9 May 1998, its own retrospective footage showing company president David Cameron unveiling the league's logo under the tag line “The Future Force in Fantasy Wrestling.” Its first card followed on 31 May, opening a five-belt championship structure — Million Dollar, Intercontinental, Extreme, World Heavyweight and Cruiserweight — across its own Lord of the Ring '98 pay-per-view.

That first card's title matches turned on outside interference more than once: the Million Dollar belt changed to Polar Bear off a mid-move reversal of Axe; the Intercontinental cage match ended when a debuting IWF wrestler, Mi-Lo, blindsided both remaining men with a chair, letting Attrition claim the title; Rally Jackson won the World Heavyweight Championship from Dan Johnson with a Tiger Driver; and “Sexy” Simon Pretswell took the Cruiserweight ladder match after a rival wrestler, Simonneth Murdock, knocked the champion Xavier Hart from the ladder mid-climb.

The same card introduced its own headline angle: president Cameron brought out “Malibu” Mike Donovan, a wrestler billed as a recently retired former champion elsewhere, as a surprise signing. Donovan seized the microphone from Cameron to declare “it's Malibu Time” and, later that night, ran in on the World Heavyweight title match with a chair, laying out both the champion and a challenger before a debuting wrestler, the Beantown Bully, blindsided him with a spinebuster.

By June 1998 the Extreme Championship belonged to BJ Mullinax, whose own in-character “Trash Talk” column that month goaded a rival, Tim Beck, into a parking-lot brawl over the title rather than a sanctioned match. By the league's second pay-per-view, Battlezone '98, the World Heavyweight belt had passed to a wrestler billed as Guru, defending against Anthony Gold in a brawl that ended with both men crashing through a table at ringside; how the title moved from Rally Jackson to Guru between the two shows is not recorded. The same card's street fight between Damon Knight and Mystical was ruled a no contest after the two wrestlers, having wrecked a car and set it ablaze mid-match, were led away by police.

The league's own last surviving document is the first part of a retrospective it titled “The End of an Era,” recapping its full run in the style of a highlight reel; it is undated within its own text but was posted 16 November 1998. It breaks off mid-recap, in the middle of the Battlezone '98 main event, and no further part or later document survives.
