Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

JDMJr's Ultimate Wrestling Federation dates itself, in its own repeated words, to January 1996, though its earliest surviving post is a 9 August 1998 recruitment ad pitched at longevity: it “won't close down,” and offered “bloody dismemberments, weaponry, gang warfare... cash tournaments and championship title trophies,” hosted on a Geocities page.

It ran six championships spread across weight classes plus a stable-versus- stable Gang Warfare Title, alongside its own signature Arena division, fought inside a steel cage rather than a standard ring.

Rage for Order, on 8 January 1999, ran fourteen matches and also carried the promotion's own 1998 Year End Awards. JJ Rock beat Myst, who had taken on the entire stable Legion of Elite alone and was hospitalized afterward; the reigning Heavyweight Champion, Pure Pain, ended Padre/Father Death Muerte's undefeated casket-match record in a non-title bout; and Biohazard, bleeding from self-inflicted cuts, ended the reigning Arena Champion's own undefeated run.

Its follow-up, the Ides of January Supercard, survives thinner: Omega's Glory won by disqualification after Venom's own associate set himself on fire at ringside and attacked with a burning cross, and a falls-count-anywhere brawl ended at the announce table, spilling into the crowd and hospitalizing two young fans.

Blackout, a twenty-two-match card on 3 February 1999, saw Powerhouse retain the Arena Championship; the Superheavyweight Title was declared vacant that same week after the promotion severed ties with its champion's handler over his conduct toward its own president. Five days later it opened its 1999 Battle for the Belt, a five-division elimination tournament — tag teams, lightweights, heavyweights, superheavyweights and the Arena division — with each reigning champion sitting out until the final.

How that tournament finished isn't in the surviving record: roughly half of each division's own bracket remains unresolved, and no champion was crowned in any of the five before the record stops. Its own broadcasts continue without a gap through the rest of 1999, and a 7 December 1999 post announces a relaunch as “UWF 2000” rather than a closure.
