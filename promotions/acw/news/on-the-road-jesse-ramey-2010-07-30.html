ON THE ROAD - JESSE RAMEY.

30 July 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
JENNA: Hello, Jesse! We've just touched down in Oslo, Norway... and with COURAGE 117 on the horizon; I wanted to get your thoughts on several matters. Firstly, what did you think of Finland? I've never been there before, so I found the last week in Helsinki to be awesome. How about you? JESSE: This isn’t my first rodeo on a world tour, Jenna. When you’ve been in the business as long as I have the chances of wrestling a venue for the first time in your career are slim to none. The only advantage that Scandinavia holds over top of our tour of the old USSR is the fact that I can finally get back to what I do best, actually wrestling. Despite the fact that being able to maim people week in and week out was beginning to grow on me. The whole Scorpion Fighting division isn’t necessarily my cup of tea. JENNA: Okay. Going back a bit further, I wanted to ask about FIGHT NIGHT 2010. You placed a respectable fourth, finishing ahead of Max Danger and just behind 'The Alpha Dog' Mark Weiler, in the second Scorpion Fight Night Challenge. How did you find the experience of the two-hour maxi-fight? What was running through your mind during the whole affair? JESSE: Max Danger is nothing more than a footnote in the wrestling history books as far as I’m concerned. Some names will stand the test of time, but that name will only be known as a one hit wonder and will never stand with the legacy that I plan to leave in my wake. And as far as Weiler is concerned the world is better off knowing that he won’t even be a foot note, but a forgotten page in the book completely. As for the Scorpion Fight Night Challenge as a whole, I have to say that the on-goings of the match should have been viewed as a success. What more could the Russian crowd have wanted than to see people being ran over by golf carts, two men falling from thirty feet into a metal stage, and a flaming ring? I’m sure after that show Jeremy Hunt was a very pleased individual. The question is will things ever go back to the way they used to be, Jenna? Or have they merely changed for the better? JENNA: Cool, cool. Any fond memories of the time spent in Russia? Aside from, you know, being beat by Khristain Keller. Although, I suppose that wouldn't qualify as a fond memory... but anyways, yeah. Any fond memories you'd like to share? JESSE: Probably the bloodiest singles match in A-C-W history, and unfortunately I did have to go down in the record books as the loser of that match. But trust me when I say this Jenna, the rivalry between myself and Keller is in no way dead. So, maybe losing to Keller was a fond memory for me Jenna, as it was the completion of my transformation. JENNA: Yikes. Okay, moving on. I spoke to referee Leon Hurst just before COURAGE 116, and he's expressed serious worry about your state of mind nowadays. Seeing how as Hurst was recommended to ACW by you, isn't it nice to see that people are worried by your turn to the dark side? JESSE: The man that gave Leon Hurst his reference into A-C-W is dead and gone, Jenna. Hurst can be worried for me all he likes, but the realization that I’ve come to is that sometimes you have to be a little cold, and dark to get ahead in this world. Does that mean I’m a bad person? It really shouldn’t, considering the fans are still cheering for me. I’ve served my entire career catering to what I thought the fans wanted, but now it’s not about what they want me to do. It’s about what they want to do. You see, Jenna, I’m still giving those people what they want to see. Because deep down inside of every single one of them, they know they wish they could do the things I am doing. To cause bodily harm to someone that they hold a great deal of malice toward, without any kind of repercussion. I allow them to live out their deepest, darkest fantasies. JENNA: Right, then. So, with this 2010 King of Ages Tournament laying ahead for you, how do you fancy your chances? Who do you think will win? If you win, what will you do with your King's Choice? JESSE: My time is coming in All-Star Championship Wrestling, Jenna. The road may be a long winding one to the top, but I have a feeling that by the end of the KING OF AGES 2010 pay-per-view we will all be ushering in a new era for A-C-W. The Dark Ages are coming, Jenna, and when they do believe me the fans will rejoice as their new king stands triumphant before them. JENNA: That's... nice. I guess. So, that's all I have for you. See you at COURAGE 117, Jesse! Feel better!
