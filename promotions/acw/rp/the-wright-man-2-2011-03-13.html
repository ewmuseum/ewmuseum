The Wright Man - 2

13 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13 March 2011 Coming through the curtain after another hard-fought 25-minute brawl in front of a mere 500 people in Oklahoma, Hank Wright stepped through the curtain covered in sweat and gratefully accepted a bottle of water, possibly even more so than the standing ovation his peers bestowed on him: “Awesome job, Hank” and “Fucking fantastic” were just some of the superlatives being tossed around. Weight shook hands with all of them and then asked the group to give his opponent, 25-year-old Todd Grainger, props for his performance. Grainger embraced his much taller, older and wiser opponent and as Wright held him close, he whispered: “Thank you. You were great tonight. You made me look good.” Hank excused himself after another round off ass-kissing and headed into the dressing room where some other wrestlers congratulated him on his performance. He accepted the praise and then sat on a bench in the locker room, dying for a drink and knackered. He’d had the match of the night again but for Hank, who had been the top draw in Mexico as recently as 2009, even that was hollow. He may’ve been getting on but he could still go, that much was obvious, but could he still cut it at the top? He seemed to think so. Wright took his towel and headed for the showers. A young guy, who must’ve been no more than 23, tentatively asked ‘The Tank’: “Are you coming out later Hank? Have a beer or two?” “Not sure yet, buddy. We’ll see.” “Alright man. See you soon anyway. You were frickin’ great tonight.” “Thanks.” Hank thought to himself: “These guys are easily pleased.” SilverHAWK was standing alone: “Damnit. Voicemail.” The ACW legend waited for the annoying lady to tell him to leave a message after the beep. In the meantime, he cleared his breath. He hated talking to these things: “Hey Hank, I don’t know if you remember me but it’s SilverHAWK. We met a LONG time ago. How’s it goin’? Anyway, ACW is lookin’ for new talent. Give Henry Irwonsen a ring. He’s our senior official but also helps with talent relations and recruitment. He’d love to talk to you.” “Hey, is that Henry?” “Yeah. Speaking.” “Henry, it’s Hank Wright.” Irwonsen, who thought it was only a casual conversation moments ago, adopted a serious tone: “Hank, great to hear from you. You got HAWK’s message then?” “We wouldn’t be talking if I didn’t Henry. What’s the situation?” Henry chuckled: “Hank, Jeremy Hunt wants more talent in ACW. We’re going to a place you may know very well in the near future – a little place called Mexico. Heard of it?” It was Hank’s turn to laugh: “Sure have.” “Listen, Hank. We’re bringing in a few guys for the Scorpion Fighting division and we think you’d be ideal for it. You’ve got the experience, size and style to really make an impact. Plus, Mexico knows you. It’ll be the perfect place to start.” Wright was buzzing. Not that he betrayed that feeling to Henry: “It all sounds good Henry but where are you going after Mexico? Back to the States? I’ve got a little boy here in Texas and…” Henry spoke candidly: “I won’t lie to you Hank. We won’t be back in the US for a while yet. After Mexico is okay though. We’ll be in Canada. A lot of guys are happy about that! After Canada, it’s likely we’ll be going to Australia.” Hank’s heart sank for a second. There was a pause at the end of the phone but when Henry asked him if he was still there, Wright piped up and confirmed he could still hear the ex-Spirit titleholder: “Yeah, I’m with you Henry. Can I ask you a question?” “Anything Hank. Any questions you have, fire away.” “Why now? This isn’t your fault Henry, nor HAWK’s, but why didn’t this phone call come in ninety-three, ninety-four at the latest? Why has it taken ACW almost twenty years to get back to me?” “I don’t know. You got injured…” Wright cut him off: “I healed. Pretty good too.” Irwonsen conceded: “I don’t know Hank. I don’t have the answer to your question.” Both men wanted to move the conversation along: “Henry, I’ve just got one more question: I don’t come cheap. If I’m going to travel the world and be away from my boy for months at a time…” “Money’s not an issue Hank. ACW is on fire right now and even in the midcard, you’ll make more than you’ve ever done in your life. That’s what you’ve waited and worked for, right?” Hank nodded, not that Irwonsen knew that: “One more thing: When do you want me to start?” “Take a plane to Mexico City on the eighteenth or nineteenth of January. We’ll send you the ticket.” Hank was amazed: “As easy as that?” “As easy as that unless there’s something else?” The Texan was at a loss, which was unusual for him: “Not that I can think of.” “I must say I’m surprised Hank, given your reputation, but it’s that simple if you want it. We want you on the team.” “I’ve changed Henry.” “I’ve heard Hank. And I believe you. Just show it and everything will take care of itself.” Wright couldn’t help but smile. Everyone else had vacated the locker room at this point and he was completely alone yet he felt so content, much more than he had in a long time. Probably the last time he’d worked in Mexico, professionally-speaking at least. “Henry…I don’t know what to say other than…thank you.” Irwonsen replied: “My pleasure. Welcome to ACW Hank.” Oh, those words sounded good. They usually say the first time is the sweetest but Wright wasn’t sure of that – and he would know. “One last thing Hank…” Well, there had to be, didn’t there? It couldn’t have gone THAT smoothly. “Yes?” “You shouldn’t just thank me. You should give John Stein a call. He completely sold me on you.” Hank couldn’t believe it. Actually, he could but upon hearing the man’s name, tears almost filled his eyes and he let out a laugh that did betray his emotions this time: “Thanks Henry. I will.” Irwonsen hung up and Wright was alone again. He actually did cry as he sat back, only a white towel protecting his modesty: “John, you bastard…” He nodded to himself as tears trickled down his scar-riddled cheeks: “I love you.”
