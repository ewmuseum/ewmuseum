Dear Ailis

22 November 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 22 November 2010 Dear Ailis, The truth is, I felt nothing. We downed shots cross-armed then stumbled to a club with the words Never Sleep Again painted fifteen feet high on the wall outside. There, away from your friends, I told you about how I wanted to be a fighter and you told me you wanted to be a stripper. We straw-bombed alcho-pops at the bar. You said, just joking, I want to be a vet. You told me about how you loved animals because they could never disappoint you. Your face was pink and green in the club lights and I could see blue veins networked like spider-webs beneath your pale skin. You were like a B-movie vampiress, red-haired, with a body like a knife. I told you I thought you were beautiful and you turned away, you couldn't look me in the eye. You told me not to talk stupid. Then you smiled, all coy, and you said, you're not too bad yourself. And I felt nothing. We went to the Disney store one December afternoon and pretended to be shopping for our made-up children. I asked you if we could get Tommy an Iron Man doll, and you said no, because you don't want to raise him to be in awe of violence. You suggested that we buy Cody a stuffed giraffe but I vetoed that. I said that the giraffe is the most homosexual of all animals, and I wanted grandchildren to leave my millions to. You laughed. We agreed on buying them a cat beanie baby in a mug. At the check-out, we talked about how we wanted our boys to grow up to be doctors and open a practice together. The woman behind the counter said it was so nice to see young couples so interested in their children. After we left, I kept the mug and you kept the beanie baby. You later told me that you named him Dean Junior and slept with him under your pillow. And I felt nothing. When we first had sex, the Martini you thought would steady your nerves just made you clumsy. I rolled you onto your back and buried my tongue into your neck. It's snowing, you said, between those small wounded noises that you always made. I pulled your legs over my shoulders, ran my fingers over your nipples. It's like a fairytale, you said. The street light shining through the crack in the curtains cut an amber triangle out of the darkness, and in that light I saw flashes of your body under mine, your hair spread over the pillow, your eyes closed, biting down on your bottom lip. You pulled me in close and planted kisses like butterflies on my face. While you made love, I fucked you. And I felt nothing. We rode on the world's fastest roller-coaster in the pitch black, New Year's fireworks behind us painting the sky with splashes of gold and drizzles of purple. You screamed, beamed, held my hand as tight as you could. Dizzy in each other's arms, I laughed with you as we stepped off the platform. You bought a photograph of the two of us, wind-blasted and grinning, those fireworks exploding in the distance. And I felt nothing. The next time I was at your home, that picture was framed above your bed. I asked you why you put it there, and you said because it made you happy. Because you wanted to be reminded of the fun times we had together. Because you loved me. And I looked into your eyes, gently took your hands in mine, and kissed you on your lips in such a way that your heart stopped, time and space and everything became transparent and fleeting and all that existed in your world, the only thing you had that was solid, that was forever, was me. And with my breath catching in my throat, I said, I love you too. And I felt nothing. I stopped calling you. You tried calling me, but I didn't answer. You sent me text messages, at first concerned, then confused, then threatening. You asked me why I said I loved you. You were so convinced this was for real. You still loved me, wanted me, more than anything else in the world. I was the only man for you. I made you happy, I made you whole. I ignored you. I was fucking an eighteen year old girl in the back of my car when I received the message from your mother. She said you had overdosed on the Zopiclone you'd been prescribed for your insomnia. That you were dead for three minutes. And I felt nothing. Oh, Ailis. Falling in love with me is like falling in love with a television set. I was everything you needed, perfectly tailored to suit your every mood, your every whim. I was fun-loving and comical, dark and mysterious, open and friendly. I was your white knight and your darkest desire, your surrogate father, your pampered baby. A man of a thousand faces, changing the channel to satisfy your cravings. You were so pathetic, so easily led. It was a pleasure to fuck you, a pleasure to fuck you over. I visited you in your sleep, with drips and wires worming in and out of your every orifice, saliva smeared across your grey cheek. Your red hair gone dull, your eyes swollen shut. I watched you for a while, your every painful breath sending tremors through your body. And I felt nothing. Ailis, if I could feel truly sorry, I probably would - if that's any consolation. There is nothing left of me. I tried to fight it for so long but now, I have come to terms with what I am. I do what I must to survive, to satisfy my needs. I am an empty and callow creature, feeding on the weak, getting stronger every day. Ailis, it was nothing personal. Nothing is. By the time you wake from your coma and read this letter, I will have left for America to face my greatest test. You will never see me again. Whether you have the strength to survive this final revelation is no concern of mine. Yours, D.D.
