Outside the Lines -- Three

8 December 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08 December 2010 The collective feeling at ESPN Los Angeles at that moment was one of disbelief. The houselights came up and the darkness was lifted. Standing there, in all his bloated, gaudy glory, was Chester B. Wallace. Much like Oso’s, his story was well known, mostly due to the efforts of ESPN itself. The network had made him infamous over the last year, linking him with every major sports related scandal they could report. The worst thing was that he’d likely had a hand in ninety nine percent of them. Yet, again like The Bear, Wallace had been dealt with swiftly with his punishment becoming as publicized as his misdeeds. He had basically been exiled from all of professional sports and those he wasn’t banned from wouldn’t dare touch him with a ten foot pole. So what was he doing at ESPN? He continued to clap slowly, his voice booming in Oso Bannon’s direction. Thomas Walcott’s face was virtually frozen in shock by now. “Great job, Bear! GREAT!” Bannon scowled. His new found partnership with Chester Wallace made his insides curdle even if it was only business. He hadn’t interacted much with Wallace during his football career. Even he, with all the issues surrounding him, hadn’t been that stupid. The man was a snake, plain and simple. When he spoke again, Oso could’ve sworn he heard hissing in place of words. “That will serve our purpose just fine!” Wallace spoke excitedly before scowling in Walcott’s direction. “Thought this fairy was going to sink us for a second, but you handled it like a pro!” Walcott was too blown away by what he was seeing to fight back. He looked at one of his producers and mouthed: “Us?” “To think I took you for being an awful speaker!” There was no containing Wallace at that point. Oso simply wondered how he’d take him for anything else considering they only conversed when discussing business plans. “Now hear this!” Wallace screamed so loudly that anyone on the lot could hear them. The cameramen scrambled back to their posts. Whatever was happening it needed to documented. “I am pleased to announce that I too am looking to put my past behind me. I’ve been watching Oso here for awhile now and to see what he’s gone through has really put things into perspective for me. If he can rebuild then so can I!” Say what you want about Chester B. Wallace, but there was no denying that he had a way with words. The whole room stood transfixed aside from Oso who had seemed to drift off into his own mind once again. “In the spirit of rebuilding, we have decided to pool our efforts. I’m proud to say that as of this past October I am Oso Bannon’s personal manager. I won’t be taking questions at this time, but can say that we will be respecting, not appealing, our respective bans. You will see Oso again in the VERY near future, but not on the football field. Stay tuned.” It was so silent in that room you could hear a pin drop. Wallace stood looking satisfied. He loved to leave people hanging. He rubbed Oso’s shoulders, raising an eyebrow at Thomas Walcott as he did so as if to say “How’s THAT for a story?” “Come on, Bear.” He said softly. “We’ve got a plane to catch.” And with that the two departed... leaving a brightly lit soundstage full of nothing but people who were in the dark behind them.
