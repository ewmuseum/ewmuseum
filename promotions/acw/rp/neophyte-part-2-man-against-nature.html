Neophyte part 2 - Man against Nature

19 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Neo Xealot Akuma, Djinn, Akira Neophyte part 2 - Man against Nature Location: Los Angeles, CA Date: September 19, 2004 Number (Archive): 16 Number (Wrestler): 2 “You said ‘our family martial art’- what did you mean by that?” “I was referring to the truest form of Akumate. The Art embraced by our lineage. Most of the sons in our mother’s bloodline have been trained to use it but a select few have grasped its most potent form.” “How is it different from regular Karate or any other martial art?” “Well while most martial arts these days simply teach you how to kick ass, they were traditionally attached to contradictory stigma - a creed of mental and spiritual calmness.” He paused to swallow. “Aikido for example is built around one’s finding of one’s centre and the achievement of total calm even in the event of conflict. Honour and mercy are other stigmas of traditional martial arts.” “Did you get this stuff from a textbook?” Kinjite joked. “Hardy har har. You think you’re funny but you’re not!” Kazuya shook his head with a sneering grin and continued, “Akumate is a paradox of most existing martial arts – it embraces chaos and rage and that is why very few in the past have been able to utilise it to its full effect.” “And you are one of them right?” “Correct. I have been told by our grandfather that my particular brand of rage was tailor-made for the style. See Akumate is essentially based upon Shotokan Karate but its true form is the one that teaches you through its practises to savour and eventually use pent-up rage until death.” “So what you’re saying is that the more pain and suffering its masters endure, the more deadly he will become in theory.” “Yes. The old masters of the Konomoto were warlords and warriors but poets and philosophers as well. They scoffed at the idea of martial arts being practises of peace. Our clan was thus the first to practice Akumate. They believed that rage – blind and total – was the greatest source of power for the average human being.” “So we went insane in battle and fucked everybody up.” “Not necessarily Kin. Rage as you know it is an emotion characterised by clenched fists, grinding teeth, the raising of one’s voice and recklessness. Akumate teaches us that Rage has many forms, it can be a kick, a punch, or even the most fluid and beautiful movements. It also teaches us that success is achieved not only through the existence of rage but through complete and utter control over it.” “Sounds too philosophical for my liking.” “You’re right. And there is philosophy behind Akumate” “And that is?” “Chaos is the one constant of nature and by embracing chaos, man becomes the equal of nature. Man must become like the Demon… hence Akumate.” “I don’t believe in that statement at all.” “Most people don’t” “Do you?” “Yes.” “Oh… why do you believe it?” “I believe it because people in general suffer more than they experience pleasure.” “I don’t understand.” “I see nature as a living thing and because it is an entity it must have a natural enemy. I therefore see man as the enemy of nature. You may argue that we are the architects of our own destruction but I think nature provides the foundations of human breakdown. See, Akumate is the one virtuous practise that gives us the means to use suffering to our advantage. We use suffering as a fuel and thusly gain infinite power because…” “…chaos is nature’s only constant…” “Exactly and it is therefore an inexhaustible supply of power for us should we learn to utilise it and deal with nature on its own terms. We Fight the…” “…We fight the World.” “Yes and we use chaos to do it because it is the one power we have that is equal in its potential to that of nature. Akumate therefore gives us the power to be Gods if we want.” “Is that what you want?” “I think every man wants to be a God, which is why so many of us worship and follow unseen deities. It’s like at school when you follow the popular guy. You hope to become him in the end. So yes, I do want to be a God.” “I still don’t understand.” “I’ve explained as best as I can Kin.” “I mean I don’t understand you…” “Why not?” “Because…” …I am afraid of you.
