Idle Chatter

11 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11 March 2011 Stop me if you've heard this one before. There's this wrestler, right? A pretty good one, if you want to believe the smarts who write their dirt sheets and talk on podcasts about professional wrestling. He's kinda popular, but he hasn't reached that level of respect where the people in the back think he's a money man and could hold the big prize. Eventually, he gets put into an ill-advised tag team with a bunch of crazed lunatics flanking them, and it all ends in tears and someone going to jail. Thankfully not this wrestler, although he does get fired because of this association and because he was perceived to have no charisma. He languishes for years in the independent scene and overseas in places like Japan, where he thrives and forms a small, dedicated cult following. Another company takes notice of him, but they decide on a whim that they should give this guy a mask and a new gimmick. And thus, this wrestler, immaculately talented though he may be... was forced to dress and act the part of a blithering idiot by the name of "Baron von Blackberry". Suddenly, this wrestler had a whole new following, those that adored the bizarre and often-times contradictory things that this raving lunatic in a weird, bulbous, blackberry-themed mask would say. My name is Coral Avalon. I am also, quite unfortunately, the guy who plays the part of Baron von Blackberry on television. Truth be told, I wasn't exactly overly enthused to throw on the old labcoat again and act the part. I also hadn't exactly told very many people my little secret that I was, in fact, Blackberry. I mean, who would expect that this boring old stick in the mud, whose entire persona was built on *wrestling* and nothing else, would be capable of such a thing as Blackberry? Even I'd be surprised. Part of the reason why I kept it to myself was simply for the sake of kayfabe. It's a dying art, and in this daring internet age that we live in, there aren't very many people who DIDN'T know that wrestling was all this big sham of a circus that people like me participate in. The biggest reason, however, was a bit of shame. I prided myself as being a wrestling-first kind of guy, and the fact that I was deriving a lot of my more recent success under this ludicrous mask of mine just felt like betraying that mentality. So as best as I could, I tried to hide the fact that I was Blackberry - even from people who would know me. Especially Marcus Dox, I can't freaking imagine the things that man would say if he'd found out that I was running around as a giant fruit and shouting "SCIENCE!" as much as humanly possible. Anyway, my contract with Sin City Championship Wrestling expired a while ago, and I went back to the independents wrestling under my own name for a change. Trading the bright lights of Sin City for some buildings that barely had tall enough ceilings for me to execute my frog splashes might have seemed like a bad trade, but I certainly didn't miss the scumbags that permeated the halls of SCCW. At some point, Simon Knox approached me. Simon is the youngest of the three Knox brothers that wrestled in and around the world. That mongrel idiot most of you know as Steve Knox is largely regarded as the most successful of the three, and that fellow you might have seen around who calls himself "Captain Justice" is the oldest of the three. Simon told me that ACW was hiring again, and that he and Connor were going to sign up, but that they needed someone with known integrity to vouch for them. And, well, I was an ACW alumnus. Actually, I had pretty much had my most famous moment as a professional wrestler up to this point in ACW, when I competed in the single bloodiest match of my career - and possibly in ACW's long history - a match called Gimmick Hell. People still ask me to this day if my little "Spider-man" act, where I pretty much hung unwillingly from the barbed wire cage before I fell into a casket of light tubes hurt. And I would tell them that that one spot nearly ended my career, maybe even my life. That match was so bloody that Annabelle, my wife now, actually got upset to the point that she all but made me promise to never compete in anything like it again. To my credit, I've more or less kept my word. Needless to say, that little abomination of a match, the brainchild of one Lowell Dot Com, hadn't been seen or done ever again. Since I won the match, I lived with the infamy of being the guy who won it. Or, more specifically, the guy who won the match without using a weapon. Simon didn't know I was Blackberry at the time, particularly since I hadn't wrestled under that mask in a while. But he needed someone to vouch for him, for he and his partner, Connor O'Reily, had a bad reputation following some particularly nasty behavior in their last major promotion, PRIME. So, I did something that I might consider to be one of my life's biggest regrets. I agreed. And not only did I agree, but I told him my little secret: I was Baron von Blackberry. He was quite... puzzled. "You?" he asked, with a surprised expression on his face that was quite rare for a man with a poker face as legendary as Simon Knox's, "You were that giant idiot running around Sin City Championship Wrestling ranting constantly about science and robots?" "Yup." I would say non-chalantly. "And why would you keep this from us this long, exactly?" "Because I wanted it secret, Simon. You and Connor have a talent for not knowing when to shut up." Simon didn't say much after that, which was decidedly odd for the guy. Connor O'Reily was a guy I trained in Joey Malone's "Backyard", and he was Simon's tag team partner. He was everything you should never be as a professional wrestler in this day in age: rude, impulsive, terrible with money, and utterly incapable of making capable decisions without others around to do it for him. That's why he needed his very responsible twin sister around most of the time to make sure he didn't screw up horribly. In many ways, I didn't envy Miranda O'Reily, the "public relations lady" and manager of the Princes of New England. Miranda already knew my big secret, as one of her talents was that she was exceptionally skilled at designing costumes for professional wrestlers. All of the ring gear you see the Princes of New England wear? That's her handiwork. So, too, were the tights I wore when I wrestled as the Baron. She even redesigned my mask when it had become damaged after one of Sin City's house shows. I figured if I was going to work with the Princes again, after our last great venture in the reborn "Squared Circle" promotion where I had led them in a particularly insane stable that included myself, the Princes, and a particularly nasty woman in my life by the name of Inoue Doi. We *may* have held every title in the promotion at one point. As for why I decided to put the mask back on? Maybe I just missed being a damn fruit.
