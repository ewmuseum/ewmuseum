The Fall (part two)

18 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 18, 2010 2007, on the road to recovery… “…You’re kidding, right? Dad, you didn’t let her do this, did you?” Billows laid in the hospital bed, cause that’s really all he was capable of at that point in time. What with being crippled and all that. He’d grown tired of the awkward moments spent being given a shave by his father, so he’d grown a bit of a beard. A quick flick of his eyelids and his attention had gone from his mother to his father. Ray Billows frowned, “Ry, I promise you, I just found out about it on the drive over. Do I look like I’m happy about it?” Martha Billows stepped in, “Ryan, I know you two have had your differences in the past, but for better or worse--we’re a family.” “Mom, Jeremy is a lot of things, but he’s no longer any kin of mine.” Billows knew the words stung his mother, but he also knew that there were times you had to be blunt with the woman. She was a stubborn as a mule and as deaf as white Dalmatian when she wanted to be. “Ryan, we all make mistakes. Granted, your brother made some pretty huge mistakes, but he’s still your brother.” Martha turned around, breaking eye contact. Billows hated that, it felt so disrespectful given his current state, “Yeah, well, like I said. He might be your son, but he’s not my brother, and I don’t want to see--” A knock on the door brought a hard to swallow rage to Ryan’s throat, cutting him off. He looked at his father who looked back at him with eyes full of apology. Truth be told, Ray himself wasn’t a huge fan of his older son, but he still held out hope that Jeremy would turn his life around and redeem himself from the black shadow he’d cast over the family’s name. For the unenlightened, when Ryan was 15 years old, his older brother Jeremy was placed in prison for raping a minor. At first it had been considered statutory rape until the minor broke down in tears, crying that she’d told him to stop, crying that she felt so ashamed, that she was sorry, that she wished she could die. The minor’s name was Nancy W., and at the time, she’d been Ryan’s first love. And that’s where the bad blood came from. Some grudges should be dropped, sure, but some deserve to be set in stone. Ryan remembered the constant teasing he’d put up with all through high school, as if he was a sick fuck like his piece of shit older brother. He remembered how his brother’s actions had rendered his high school existence pretty damned pathetic. He remembered his brother when Jeremy had first come home from prison, not a shred of guilt to discern. Finally, Ryan remembered being on a brief two day vacation from wrestling, spending it at home with his family. He’d gone for a jog, and he’d seen his brother’s car parked outside of [detail withheld]. That night had been the last time Ryan saw his brother. He’d held his father’s pistol to Jeremy’s crotch, promising that if he ever suspected his brother of coming in the vicinity of minors again, he’d blow Jeremy’s balls off. But he was pretty sure mom and dad didn’t know about that last part--call it a hunch. Ryan remembered all of these moments in a split-second as he watched the doorknob turn. The room was silent, Ryan placed his eyes on his mother, fixing to tell her that this wasn’t going to go good, and that’s when the door burst open. “BABY BROTHER, WHAT HAVE YOU GONE AND DONE TO YOUSELF?!” Jeremy entered the room with as much gusto as he could muster, which was quite an amount considering he had all the maturity of the girls he longed for. He carried in his hand a stuffed lion wearing a neck brace. Cute. “Mom, dad, great to see you all as always! Ry-ry, look at you, this is horrible. And here I thought wrestling was fake.” Jeremy grinned, knowing full well that if Ryan was mobile, his pride would have pinned Jeremy up against the wall for that comment, “Anyways, look, I brought something to cheer you up.” Jeremy shook the stuffed lion before making it dance up Ryan’s chest. Ryan swallowed hard, trying as hard as he could, for the sake of his mother. But it was no use as Jeremy brought the lion up to Ryan’s face, “Look, I’m a young lion!” “Are you fucking high?” Ryan finally spoke to his brother, the first time in about three months. The first time since he’d held a gun to his crotch. “Ryyyyan…” His mother started in, but Ray Billows gave Martha a stern look that he saved for rare occasions. Not too surprisingly, Martha was generally the root of a fair number of these occasions. Jeremy chucked, a bit uncomfortable, “Well…no…he’s a lion. Like, you know, your little… wrestling… thing. THE YOUNG LION. And he’s got a neck brace…” “I’m 26 years old. Stuffed animals might impress the usual age bracket you surround yourself with, but as far as I’m concerned, you can take that lion and choke on it.” Ryan spat venom at his brother, but remained fairly calm while doing it. It was hard to get too worked up when you couldn’t move anything but your damned eyeballs. Jeremy sighed, “Oh here we go with the dramatics.” Ryan shot right back, “Here we go with the apathy towards your past. ‘Oh, no biggie, I just slept with little girls when I was in my late twenties, big whoop.’” “Oh fucking drop it Ryan, let the past be the past. I did my time for my sins, I paid my penance, I’ve made it up to society.” Jeremy sneered. The bitter blood ran thick in their veins, and Jeremy lowered his eyebrows before stepping closer to Ryan, leaning close, and whispering in his ear. “Or what, Ryan? You gonna blow my cock off? I don’t see that happening right now.” Ryan closed his eyes, sighing, frustrated beyond belief. He took several deep breaths before licking his lips and opening his eyes, staring straight ahead, “Jeremy, I swear to everything holy, if you think that I’m going to be like this forever, you’re wrong. And if you think I’m going to forget your hot shit bravado bullshit you’re pulling right now, you’re wrong. And if--” CRACK Jeremy Billows sent a thundering right across his little, paralyzed brother’s face, splitting his cheek open, “You have no idea how long that’s been coming your way, baby brother!” Ray Billows was immediately on top of Jeremy, wringing his neck as he pulled him violently out of the room. Ryan simply stared straight ahead, blinking. His insides were alight with emotion, a California wildfire of rage. Martha Billows held a hand to her mouth, in shock for mere moments before she began feeling horrible, “Ryan…I…I didn’t…” “I’ll see you tomorrow mom. Have the nurse grab a doctor, I’d like to make sure that didn’t, um, screw anything up.” Ryan swallowed hard. Martha didn’t have it in her to leave him like this, “Honey, I’m sorry. I’m so--” He hadn’t raised his voice to his mother since he was a child, but he did so now, “LEAVE NOW, MOM! GET OUT!” His mother sucked in a sob, clutching her chest as if her heart had just shattered. She obliged though, awkwardly collecting her purse and her jacket before heading to the door. She started to turn around, but decided against it. Ryan was left alone for a few brief moments. Left to stew. Left to wallow. Left to let this moment join the rest of the disdained memories he had of his older brother. Present Day Another day, another grave. The only reason he stopped by this site every now and then was because he knew it was what his mother would have wanted. And for the most part, he would come, and he would contain his emotions. He wouldn’t necessarily pay his respects, but he would be respectful enough not to send his boot through the fucking headstone. Today, however, the memories had washed over him without warning. He stood there clenching his fist, and unclenching it, and repeating the process. It took all he had not to strike out at the marble grave. Instead, he spit on the gravestone, sneering, “Nice catching up, big brother.” Billows turned around, and trudged up the hill to hop on his bike and leave. He’d had his fill of gravesites lately.
