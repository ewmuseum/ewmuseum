Xolotl

13 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13 March 2011 “Xolotl.” “Hello Ometeotl. I trust you had a safe trip and a good meeting with the others?” “It went well, my son. Take a seat.” I walk through the doorway into his office on the tenth floor of La Corporacion de Serpiente y Aquila in Tlalpan, Districto Federal. Amongst the hills and trees of this district stands our building. Its tanned rock façade with a flat base and large tower looks inconspicuous on the outside. It would be trite to say that its appearance is deceiving, but I think it would be apt. Tialpan is the third biggest office of La Corporacion, behind the home office in Sinaloa and in Houston. The office focuses our operations on the eastern part of Mexico and the Caribbean. Ometeotl is the fourteenth board member to hold that position in the near four centuries of the Corporation. It is mainly an ecclesiastic position; he interprets the messages and guides the younger members. He guides and provides a shoulder, but rarely does Ometeotl get involved in actual ‘business.’ That makes this meeting rather odd. One seeks out Ometeotl, not vice versa. His note was worrisome; stating only ‘Xoltotl, come to my office tomorrow. There is much to discuss.’ I play with its wax seal in my pocket, when I walk over and sit down I notice that some of the red wax rubbed off on my thumb. I hope he does not notice it, or the slight tremble in my voice. “I got your note late last night. Forgive me looking disheveled, I was quiet alarmed to hear from you—“ “Not a worry, my son. The meeting went long and there was not time to waste. We had an interesting development involving you.” He seems like he has rehearsed this speech. His hawkish face had deep-set bags under his eyes. Ometeotl removes his silver-rimmed glasses and place them on his coffee table. I unbutton my jacket button and he glimpses my holster and sees my knife and Desert Eagle. I guess this would be the best time to describe my actual job within La Corporacion. I am Xolotl. Named after the Aztec god, the broken dog whom leads the dead towards the underworld. I am the seventh person to take this name, and there will be plenty to hold this position after I depart. I am one of the board’s designated soldiers. The Xolotl name is given to someone who fights with enough savagery to merit being compared to the brother of Quetzalcoatl. And also any man who has the potential to conquer another chosen one. “Have they found the other? I am ready. Leading the shark boats has been tiresome and beneath my prestige.” “I agree Xolotl. This is an interesting opportunity. The western wind will be in Mexico for the next few weeks. His business will be running several shows through the country before they head elsewhere.” “Is it time?” “I don’t think you are ready to face the wind. Nothing against you or your ability. It is just not an…appropriate time for that fight. La Corporacion has a different task for you. One that’ll let you break into his company.” “Ok sir. What’s it.” “There is a man who wrestles for him who leads a group. His name is Gabriel, their group is the Night Life. An assorted group of ‘ravers.’ I really try not to laugh. I can’t put a finger on Onmoetyl’s age, but he older than my father. His researcher describing to him what a ‘rave’ or whom a ‘raver’ was would have been amusing. “They’ve won a contest and come into a significant amount of money. Hundred Thousand Dollars. Some of that has been put toward that as a retainer for our services and product. Since you have had experience with their business and company. The board wants you to be our point man on this. Account executive if you will.” I twitch. If I’m seen back in ACW, my cover will be blown. I probably will not get out intact. My eyes trace the office. The cream and maroon marble floor reflects the glare back up into my eyes. I don’t really like the rod-iron furniture, but it is a Corporacion standard. It prevents people boring holes to place bugs. Last person who did that lost both his family and both his arms to Ometeotl. That was years ago, and I hope he is a more forgiving man given his age. Personally, I doubt it. I feel the first beads of sweat form on my scalp, irritating my freshly shaven head. I run my hand across the stubble and try to choose my next words carefully. The board already made their decision so any trying to fight it was futile. I can only voice my objections and hope they’ll be heard. “Understood Ometeotl. However, I can’t just walk in there. It will end horribly. There will be no strategic advantage against him. Should not someone unbiased or unaffiliated run this? I will have to fight him immediately. I have no cover. I am too much history to get away with it. I could get killed on sight.” “I said that this would be an interesting opportunity, didn’t I? Buy a ski mask. Do not talk; you will have men to represent you and do the work that you find…unbecoming. You have always been a smart man and blessed fighter. You are better than just being muscle. You will get to run this operation and prove yourself to the board.“ He puts on his glasses and stands up. He places his hand on my shoulder. “This gets you back in the door. It will not end the same way. Xolotl, you will infiltrate. Then at the right time, you will unmask. We still need our footholds in All Star, this is the best chance for both La Corporacion and you. The other cartels will not be able to recognize you this way. They won’t know your destiny, your ultimate power. This will give you the opportunity to prove you are the champion of the Dog and you will start to take down their pantheon. Take his soul and grow even stronger. As the books said, you will take the throne from your twin and lead the future generations of La Corporacion.” “Alright.” Omeoeotl sits back down and takes my hands into his. He bows his head and closes his eyes. He clasps my hands and I follow in bowing my head. “Then it is decided. Xolotl, lets close our eyes and pray.”
