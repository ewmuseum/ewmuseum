Almost Famous - Part Three

3 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 03, 2010 The air reeks of sweat and curdling blood. There is barely any light in this tiny room where Oso Bannon is intensely conditioning. Right now it’s crunches. Soon it’ll be pull ups and curls. A room over there’s a worn out boxing ring. Its canvas feels no pain and gives out no forgiveness. At least it hadn’t for the five hours Oso was inside of it today. Outside the sun is burning bright. A Lexus, new, but in need of a wash, pulls up to a building so miniscule that you wouldn’t know it was there unless told. Chester B. Wallace turns the car off and approaches the door, his usual enormous grin and jolly wobble in tact. His plates read California. Looks like he’s found his way home. It’s here that the two environments meet. Wallace opens the door emphatically and greets Oso, who looks as if he’s never seen light in his entire life. Seeing Bannon’s uncomfortable squint, Wallace shuts the door before speaking. “Christ, Bannon. How the hell can you see in here, let alone train?” “My eyes have adjusted and my focus is clear,” states Oso blankly. “Okay” responds Wallace, in a tone suggesting that he thinks Oso may be a little off his rocker, “Where’s Kory?” “In the main room.” Oso spoke flatly once more. To some he may have seemed hypnotized. Wallace searched the room with his hands, almost coming close to running into or stepping on Oso more than once, until he reached a door. Now the lights shined bright and the ceilings grew high. All around people were training. Inside of the beat down ring, two men went at it as if their lives were on the line. Outside of the ring watching intently stood a man who looked in good shape in health, but also completely worn. It was clear that this man had been many places and seen a lot of things. As Chester Wallace approached him, it grew increasingly apparent that some of those things were Wallace’s own acts of bad taste and ill repute. “Thought I told you not to come down here, Chester.” said the man sternly. “I had to come check on my investment, Kory. You know this is my last shot.” Wallace responded. “I know because if it wasn’t,” Kory paused, “I wouldn’t be helping you. I just hope that when I get the kid good to go and out of here that I never have to see you again.” To this Chester Wallace didn’t even feel the need to respond. Instead, he smiled wider and plodded along towards his desired conversational destination. “How is he doing?” Kory raised his eyebrows. “Pretty damn good, actually. He’s learning quick. He wrestled two exhibitions against two other students today. Another month with me and I’d sign him to a developmental deal myself.” Chester B. Wallace could hardly contain himself. It was time to put the wheels in motion. “Kory, if you can handle one last favor, is it possible that you may be able to get me SilverHAWK’s number?”
