The Sins of the Son; Part II

28 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 28, 2010 April, 28th 2010; 7:30PM Ramey Family Cemetery [detail withheld], West Virginia “So, could you please tell me exactly what we’re doing?” The voice of Jesse Ramey resonated from within the cab of a 2010 Chevy Silverado. The scene expanded to see the driver of the vehicle was Jesse’s older brother, Chris, “You’re finally going to find some kind of peace today little brother.” The vehicle veered off of the rough dirt road onto another smaller road that looked barely traveled. An old beaten up sign missing most of its letters could be seen, but what exactly it said couldn’t be made out. “Chris, I really don’t see the point in this.” Jesse’s evident signs of being irritated could be seen as he sighed and began moving around in his seat. “If I had known you were going to be dragging me out here I would have never agreed to go anywhere with you.” Jesse said with an almost malice in his voice, “We could have gone anywhere, seriously. We could have gone to the hospital and spent time with mom. We could have just stayed home and I could have played with the kids again, but no! Instead you have to drag me out here into the middle of nowhere? And for what; just so that you can get some kind of sick enjoyment out of seeing my skin crawl?” Chris chuckled lightly for a moment and then turned to his brother briefly, before turning his attention back to the grassy road. “You can think whatever you want, Jesse. Our father didn’t just pick on you because of your career choices. You may not have seen it because you were too blind to care about anyone except yourself back then, but my life wasn’t easy either.” “There was no pleasing that man,” Chris said very sternly, “if mom were able to tell you right now, she would agree with me. No one and I mean no one, had to put up with more shit from him than she did. You can cry about it all you want, but he’s not here anymore. So, the bitching needs to stop and you need to come to some kind of terms with yourself about this whole thing.” Jesse turned to his brother even more frustrated than before, “That man was totally incapable of showing any kind of love for anything other than himself. Not once in my life did I ever hear the world “I Love You” come from his lips. Even a thank you every now and again for all of the back breaking work we did around the house to help him would have nice! I know you can’t tell me that you don’t feel the same way, because I never heard him say anything of those things to you growing up either.” The truck came to a slow stop, and Chris put the shifter into park, “This is exactly what I’m talking about with you. You’ve got to let go of this pent up frustration man. No, you’re right, and I did resent dad for all of the bullshit that he put both of us through. More of it was you though, you’re my little brother, and I always wanted to look out for you. Even though I put you through so much hell growing up, I’ve always loved you. I could see the way dad treated us killed you more than it did me, and that hurt me more than the way dad treated us.” Chris placed his hand on Jesse’s shoulder, “But here is the thing Jesse; if you don’t let go of all of that resentment then eventually it’s going to kill you. A man isn’t meant to hold that kind of stuff in, and God only knows how all of that stress hasn’t caused you to lose your hair like the rest of us. The only way I could get over it all, was by not even thinking about it like you are right now. He may have put us through hell, and never showed us any kind of affection. But here is the kicker, he did.” Jesse’s eyes widen with even more rage at the words coming from his brother’s mouth, “He did?! How in the hell do you come to that deduction Watson?! In the few days I’ve spent with you and your kids, I’ve seen you come home from work every day! And what is the first thing you do when you get home?! You hug your kids, you tell them you love them, and you sit down to eat dinner with them! How in the hell do you think that man showed us love?!” “Every day it was the same old routine! He would come home and if we hadn’t done enough in the garden, or fed all of the animals we got our asses kicked! He never sat down with us like a family to eat; he would just grab that bottle of Wild Turkey and sit on the fucking couch watching television! And on his days off of work he would work our fucking fingers to the bone until he would get tired and we could try to slack off the least little bit! So, honestly Chris, you tell me how the fuck that hard ass ever showed us that he loved us?!” Chris very calmly opened his door, “Come on,” Chris exited the vehicle and Jesse stubbornly waited several moments before exiting the vehicle as well. “You and I may be totally different people than dad, in our own little ways. Over the years though I’ve realized exactly what he had to go through working in the coal mines. I want to be exactly like him some days. If you had seen the things I’ve seen and had the close calls that I’ve had; you’d want to drink your life away as well.” “It’s like the old saying Jesse, you’ll never know until you walk a mile in the other man’s shoes.” Jesse walked toward his brother placing his hand around his shoulder, “Well, I’ve walked more than a mile in his shoes. He worked in those mines slaving his life away for almost fifty years. Do you really think dad wanted to break his back his entire life? He really didn’t have the choice to do anything else though, mom got pregnant with me in eleventh grade. Dad was lucky he graduated high school, and mom didn’t even have that option.” “He had to make a choice, not for him, but for the family he was going to have.” Chris paused, “The same choice I had to make when I found out Sharon was going to have Christina. There isn’t much around these parts, you either go into the coal mines or you go to college. When you’ve got a family on the way, college isn’t an option because the coal mines are so tempting. It’s good money; and despite the fact that you know you’re going to end up six feet under earlier than you should. That’s enough to make any man drink; and it’s enough for me to realize that he did love us.” “Now, to my biggest point of all,” Chris stopped Jesse right in front of their father’s headstone. “He never gave up; he worked his fingers to the bone. He broke his back, and he died because of all of the coal dust he had inhaled in those fifty years. In that whole time though Jesse, he never complained, but he provided for us every month. We always had a roof over our head and clothes on our back. We may not have always had the things in life that we wanted, but we never did without anything we needed. He may not have told us that he loved us, but he sure as hell did show it just by being there.” Chris paused looking down at the headstone, “He may have been a drunk, just like most of his brothers, but he wasn’t a dead beat. I think I probably would have resented him more if that had been the case, but it is far from the truth. Now, you need to make peace with that, and it’s better to do it now than it really would have been when he was alive. And that’s sad to say, but it was just the nature of the beast. Dad was too stubborn and you’ve got the same traits going for you. The two of you clashed so much that whatever you would have needed to say it wouldn’t have really mattered; because in the end you would have just butted heads.” Jesse’s exterior expression had gone from that of malice and frustration to almost nothing as he continued to listen to his brother. “Right now though Jesse, you need to make some kind of peace, not because I want you to; but because you need to. It’s now or never, and if you can’t come to grips with this, you’ll never be able to come to grips with anything else in your life. Neither of us are getting any younger, and believe me life is so much better knowing that you’ve made peace with this. I beat myself up for years, and when I saw what it was doing to my family I made myself make peace with this.” “It’s just not worth holding a grudge because you think dad didn’t love you,” Chris paused one final time. “When the fact of the matter was that he did. Just not the way we wanted him to, but the love was there. Through all of the bad times, all of the abuse, and all of the drunken nights; that man did care for us. He just showed us the best way he could, by making our lives better than his. By making sure that we had all of the things in life that he didn’t have growing up.” Chris patted Jesse on the back and slowly walked back toward his truck. Jesse stood a tear began to roll down his cheek as he looked on at the beautiful headstone of his father. He then looked up toward the dark sky above, as a small drizzle began to fall down. Then Jesse spoke softly as not to be heard, “I forgive you; and thanks to a son that you obviously had some kind of impact on, I understand. I’m sorry I may not have been the son you wanted me to be, but I am a better man because of what you did in my life.” Jesse looked back down at the headstone, but it wasn’t fully known if the water streaming down his face was from the rain falling or the heavy eyes he was holding. “I have lived my life hoping I would never be anything like you, and in some ways I never want to be. I just wish I could have heard you tell me that you loved me, just one time.” And with that final comment Jesse tucked his hands into his pockets and turned walking toward the truck where his brother was waiting.
