The Sins of the Son; Part I

20 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 20, 2010 The slowly eerie pumping of a breathing machine could be heard in the background, along with a heart monitors repetitive beeping. It was evident at this point in time that we were located in nothing short of a hospital. The view of the lens began to become more clearly and lying alone in this certain hospital room was a solitary elderly woman. No guests in the room, no flowers; just an elderly woman lying alone with only the breathing tube shoved down her throat to keep her breathing. The door to the room slowly crept open and a very beaten and battered looking Jesse Ramey stepped easily through the door. He closed the door partially behind him as he walked toward the hospital bed; he grabbed the hand of the elderly woman and knelt to one knee. His face was almost emotionless, with the exception of a single tear rolling down his cheek. “I’m not even sure what to say at this point in time,” Jesse started speaking very softly, “the nurse at the station told me you wouldn’t be able to understand anything I’m saying right now. So, really what’s the point now? It’s been what? Seventeen years since the last time we spoke? And I’ve ruined any chance of being able to tell you that I love you and know that you actually understand that I mean it when I say it.” Jesse breathed heavily trying to hold back the tears, “I just don’t understand why the choices that I’ve made in my life had to keep me away from you. You were always there for me, and supported every decision that I made. It wasn’t your fault that I never came home. It was his fault; if he could have only understood that no matter what he thought of me I was going to take my own path in life. I wasn’t meant to follow in his footsteps like Chris was; and I’m happy with my life. The lifestyle choices that I’ve made weren’t to hurt any of you or go against the grain, they were my passion. They will always be my passion, and nothing that anyone else tries to tell me will change that.” “For nineteen years,” Jesse sighed once more, “he always resented me because I didn’t want to drop thousands of feet below the surface of the Earth. Come out after a nine hour shift every day looking like I had shoved my face in a pile of coal all day long and then go right back in the next day to do the same thing. I respect him for everything he ever did for this family, but there is nothing in my heart that can ever forgive him for the love he never showed me. You were always there for me; and you supported me when everyone else had turned their back on me. I’m sorry I couldn’t be there for you when you needed me the most. And I know I’ve been selfish because everything that I went through, I know you had to suffer it for a longer time.” It was at this point in time that the nearly shut door began to creak open once again. A heavy sound of feet started to enter the room and quickly stopped in place, “What are you doing here?” Jesse slowly turned from the bed still holding the woman’s hand, “I came to tell our mother good-bye is that okay with you?” “Not really,” the man said as he entered the room more, “you should have been there before any of this happened. You should have been here to tell her anything you’ve got to say before she went on that fucking breathing machine, but where have you been? Off gallivanting around the world, doing your drugs, and hooking up with whatever hooker you can find that’s cheap.” Jesse snarled a bit as he released his mother’s hand and stood back up, “You know Chris, staying here was probably the best decision you could have ever made. The apple doesn’t fall far from the tree, and you’re just like dad. I’m sure if you’d have done anything with your life to make that woman proud, it would have been to have taken something from her. Instead you and everyone else in the family want to call me a letdown, but how can you not look in the mirror every night and know that you are in fact the letdown.” Jesse tried to calm himself as he walked toward the hospital window overlooking the slowly drying river. “I did with my life what I’m passionate about. Is it my fault that none of the rest of you wanted to do the same. You gave up on your dreams a long time ago because you knew that if you worked in the mines you would make good money. You had talent though, and even mom could see that. That’s the reason those paintings and drawings you did while you were still in high school still rest over the fireplace. Didn’t you ever realize that?” “Sometimes Jesse it’s not about pursuing out dreams,” Chris stated as he walked toward their mother’s bed. “Sometimes you have to give up your dreams and do what’s right for your family. Do I regret not continuing my work as an artist? No, and there isn’t a day that goes by that I look back and regret anything. I have a family; and you have two nieces and nephew that you’ve never seen. You think that we have this huge vendetta against you because you didn’t work with all of us. To be quite honest for the longest time I did resent you, but do you have any idea how proud and sad I am to show my kids every week my brother on television?” Chris grabbed hold of his mother’s hand and a tear began to roll down his cheek as well, “I’m proud to tell those kids, Jesse; that you were able to succeed in your dreams. I’m sad because every week when they see you they always ask me when you’re going to come home and I have to lie to them. I have to tell my own children that maybe you’ll come and visit them next week, and it’s always next week. You like to think that by staying away from us that you’re going to be better for it. But in all honesty you’re no better than the rest of us; as much as you’d like to think you are. The only person who has ever had any problems with you is dad; and no that will never change.” “I’m glad you’ve come back, but it’s too little, too late for mom, Jesse.” Chris turned his attention back to their mother, “Dad’s been gone for a long time Jesse, so where have you been? Every day after work I would go and see mom, and I resented you ever single time I would go to see her. And after dad died she became just like my children, wondering when you were going to come home. Every single day Jesse, every single day; she sat in front of the window in the living room. She was waiting on her long lost son to come home so desperately. That’s why I resent you Jesse, because you made our own mother wait heartbroken for seven years on you to come home.” Jesse was visibly shaken, his hands resting on the cooling unit of the room. Still staring out of the window and into nothingness; or for him at the moment it may as well been nothingness as the words of his brother sank into his brain. “You know Chris; for once in my life I’m not going to fight you on this one. But I can’t go back in time and make everything right. If I could step into a time machine and go back to when dad died I would be here by her side every moment of every day. I don’t have that capability though and now I have live with my decisions for the rest of my life. I don’t know why I didn’t come home sooner, and I don’t know why I neglected to come to see mom before she got into a state like this. Maybe I was afraid I wouldn’t be welcome; maybe I was afraid that because you’re so much like dad you would have just ran me off like he used to do.” “As far as I’m concerned,” Chris stated, “you seem to have done well for yourself in life. Your family still needs you, and sometime you need to come and see your family. Just because mom can’t hear you doesn’t mean my kids can’t and they still look up to you even though you’re not around. Don’t make them wait their whole life to see you, don’t make them wait like mom did.” With everything that was coming from his brother’s mouth Jesse continued shaking more and more. He turned to his brother the tears streaming down his face and he sucked it up a little more trying to hide the pain, “I need a smoke.”
