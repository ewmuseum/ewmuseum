R-Complex, Pt. 4: Heat Seeker

11 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11 March 2011 31st December, 2010. The individual locker rooms you see on TV? They're a myth. Everybody uses the same set of locker rooms, the same showers, the same rotten toilets backstage at an ACW event. It's not a bad thing. I've worked shows before where the name guys would have their own locker rooms and it killed the comraderie, the atmosphere. It's just before the End Game 2010 pre-show, and I'm sitting on a steel folding chair across from Rud Albion, who winds white tape around his hands. Around us, the other workers jostle and shove each other and try to laugh off their nerves. Management say this is going to be the biggest event in ACW history, and nobody wants to be the guy that blows his finish, that fluffs his lines. Rud and I might as well not be here, though. We're deep in concentration, in another dimension even, plotting out the blow-off match to our big angle. Trying to visualise it all in our heads. "Do you seriously think you can chop me that hard? Enough to draw blood? Have you done that before?" I ask. "Not in ACW," Rud replies. He must sense my worry, because he quickly adds, "Haven't needed to, in a storyline, up until now. But while I was wrestling back in the UK? Remember Captain Carnation? He was one of the Tommie Knockers. Split open his right bicep after 30 minutes in this First Blood Match we were up to. He hadn't been too keen on the idea of it, until he heard the response from the crowd..." We share a grin, and then Rud, in the way that he always does both in and out of character, adds a bonmot. "Really, the only trick to bleeding the hard way is to hit something hard and to hit it repeatedly. Just as long as you're up for it, I can draw blood." It's moments like these the perverse nature of our sport becomes conscious for me. This is the only profession in the world where you're asked to bleed for your money. The thought passes, though, as soon as it makes itself known. "I'm up for it. The heel has to get his comeuppance, right?" I say. Rud raises his eyebrows knowingly. "One thing I'm worried about, I have to bring it back up again, Rud... the chair shot you mentioned. I'm okay working snug with strikes, but hitting you with that reverse side shot sounds pretty dangerous. What if I knock you out?" "I'll play it smart, drop my chair in time to block it with my hands. No worries, Dean. I mean... we could even play with the timing of it, you want, before we head out?" Rud mimes having a chair in his hands, keeps his eyes locked on mine to measure my expression, to make sure I'm comfortable with what he's saying. He swings his imaginary chair in slow motion. "Chairs into each other, into each other again, you go for my head and I duck, I spike you in the stomach. Then I give you a shot to the back. Raise the chair... block. Block." He shows me how he wants me to block the shot, holding the invisible chair over his head. "You hit me in the knee, and I'll drop the chair and get at least one hand or my forearm up for that reserve side shot... and if you're still worried about it, aim for my shoulder." Rud has back-pedalled. First he said he'd get two hands up to block, and now he's telling me one. I'm concerned that he's going to try to take it hands-free, but assure myself that he's been doing this stuff for a decade. He has much more experience than me working weapons spots, he must know what he's talking about. And we both know how dangerous concussions are. "And the Butterfly Suplex off the apron, you ready for that?" he says. "That'll set-up the third to last mic spot." "I'm ready. I took bumps to the floor plenty in Japan, although the apron wasn't so high." I pause to mull it all over. "So we've got the big no-sell showdown to open, the hangman's noose spot, the chair duel, the apron suplex and the run up to the finish is all meat and potatoes. We're looking good." The excitement bubbles out of me. I try to hold back a smile, but wind up laughing out loud. It must be infectious because Rud starts laughing too, planting a mock punch on my jaw to acknowledge it. "I can't wait to hear those fans explode when I say I respect you," I say. "I've been looking forward to this for a month." "And I've been bloody proud with the work you've put in," Rud responds. "To hear you tell 'em what I already know will be icing on the cake." We share a friendly laugh. I'm still beaming as Rud continues, "It's been an unreal debut run for you. I mean, crikey, you've even got me heated in the back with some of the shit you say. But you've really pulled out all the stops, which is how it should be. I mean, the effects that they've let us get away with, that battery set-up with the smoke out of the ears? Went off brilliantly. I'm really quite proud of what we've accomplished. After helping to give others that last bit of push that they needed, it feels good to be getting my own push back on track." Before my arrival here in ACW, before this angle, Rud hadn't made too many friends in management with his outspoken concern for the direction of his character. Joining the Huntsmen, leaving the Huntsmen, joining the Pack... their decision to bounce him between stables had taken its toll on his credibility. But the last show, when he went out there and took those rubber bullets, the fans were screaming for him like he was the last hope of humankind. "We've really put something together, haven't we?" I say. "Can't believe we got so much leeway from management to write our own gig. You've been great, I can see why so many of the lads back home sing your praises. They always said you were a class act." Rud looks down, accepting my praise with modesty. There's a silence, our enthusiasm giving way to pre-match butterflies and anxieties. The suddenly serious atmosphere inspires me to get something off my chest, something I need to articulate. "I've got to thank you, mate, for volunteering to work with me," I say. "Even if management did love it, I'm still getting heat from some of the lads for that Catholic church skit I did for my debut. Thanks for being there for me, and thanks for being a friend to me when nobody else wanted to touch me with a ten foot pole. I don't know if I could have stuck it out without you." "Been my pleasure," Rud smiles. "For various reasons I've had a few of my own hurdles to get over." He leans toward me and shakes his head subtly, then continues, "It isn't easy being gay in this business, I'll tell you that. So that Catholic church skit, it was almost a selling point. If this all goes to plan tonight? You won't just have to stick it out with me, because ACW will be stuck with the both of us for the long term." I extend my hand to Rud, who takes it in the full force of his grip. "Let's do this," I say. "Let's blow these fucking Italians away." When I go out there tonight and hear the Italian fans booing their hearts out, see their trash flying through the air; when I hear the ground-shaking pop when Rud locks me in that half crab and I scream out "I RESPECT YOU!", I'll remember all over again why I chose to make my career as a heel. *** 2003. With less than forty-eight hours to mull over Scum's words and work out what he meant, I was in a panic. Walter wasn't much help. “What that guy told you sounds like a load of cracked-out jive to me. Forget about it. I'll handle the promo work. I'll be better this time.” After his dreadful performance at the last show, there was no chance in hell I was going to let Walter take that stick. After hours of pacing up and down, cutting promos in front of the mirror and tearing out my beard-hairs with frustration, finally, something clicked and it dawned on me what I had to do. I managed to get my hands on a pair of star-spangled trunks that almost fit me. That Friday night, Walter and I stood in the ring before two hundred and fifty geordies. Walter took the microphone first, telling them in his warbling, nervous voice that we, the All-American Youth, had flown across the pond to show English wrestlers how it was really done. They yawned, they checked their watches, and it looked like it was going to be two nights ago all over again. Then, with Scum's words replaying in my mind over and over, I snatched the microphone from Walter. My stomach churned with dread. Only once or twice had I ever had a live microphone in my hand in front of an audience. “You can't go out there and just PRETEND. You have to BELIEVE.” And suddenly, my nerves were gone. I believed. “Hello, my fellow Englishmen,” I said, not even attempting to conceal my cockney accent. They looked puzzled, so I clarified. “That's right. The All-American Youth has an Englishman in its ranks. 'Why?' I hear you ask. WHY would an Englishman wrap himself up in stars and stripes and stand before you alongside a proud American like my compatriot Walter Lake? “I hear your question and I answer it with one of my own: what has this country ever done for YOU? Because I'll tell you what it's done for ME: NOTHING.” They started to boo. It was working. I was on a roll, I could feel it all spilling out of me. I don't know if I really did mean it. Part of me must have. It was like picking the scab off an old wound and letting it bleed for all to see. “This country has done NOTHING for me but bind my hands with its PHONEY, BORING traditions, pump me full to puking-point with its tedious culture, drag me by my ear through an appalling educational system with no prospect better than being a miserable, grey SLAVE-TO-THE-GRIND at the end of it all. You know what? You can take this rainy little island, you can take all of its dull people, you can take its nostalgic memories of its fallen empire and you can STICK IT ALL WHERE THE SUN DOESN'T SHINE. “In America, I found the freedom I had longed for all of my life. In America, I was allowed to be myself at last, AWAY from all of you BORING SODS. I am PROUD to be part of the 'SPECIAL RELATIONSHIP'. I am PROUD to be an AMERICAN CITIZEN. And let's face it, it won't be long before this crummy little hole is annexed by the greatest superpower on this Earth anyway. You might call me a turncoat, but really... I'm just AHEAD OF THE CURVE.” Walter's jaw was hanging open. The people were on their feet, pointing their fingers, screaming obscenities at me, booing and hissing. The elation I felt was unlike anything I had experienced before in my life, being reviled by so many people at once. It was perverse, but I loved it. We worked almost exactly the same match with the New Bulldogs that we had two nights prior, but this time, the fans waited for the outcome of each exchange with baited breath, exploding with boos or cheers as we or the Bulldogs gained the upper hand. They didn't even notice when I bumped for Hardway's finishing lariat too soon and his arm missed my head by six inches. They still counted the three with the referee and showered him with cheers and applause like he had vanquished the second coming of Hitler. When we got to the back after the match, Harry had changed his tune from the last show completely. “Son, when you went out there and flat-out told them you were English, I won't lie: I was going to fire you the moment you got back here. But that was fucking incredible. Just INCREDIBLE. I'm going to change my plans. On Wednesday, you're going to win the straps in Edinburgh. You'll carry them around for the rest of the tour and drop them back to the Bulldogs at the big show in London next month. We'll build it in local media, maybe even get some guest appearances on local radio...” Harry's spiel was unending. He led me around in circles with his arm slung over my shoulder, blowing smoke up my arse and telling me of his big plans. Dizzied by the overwhelming negativity I'd received from the punters and positivity from the other workers, I didn't even wait until I'd changed out of my gear and cracked open a couple of celebratory cans of lager with Walter in the dressing room. I was on my second can, chatting to Hardway and Walter about my success, when the familiar smell of cigarette smoke and old dirt alerted me to his presence. “I watched you out there tonight.” I turned around. Scum leaned against the wall, cigarette lodged between his teeth, standing on one leg and sloppily lacing up his boots. “You followed my advice.” There was still something about him that unnerved me. I think he knew that he had this effect on people, played with it. Mastered it, even. “Yeah,” I croaked. “Thanks so much, mate. Couldn't have done it without you.” “Done what?” he smirked. Hardway shook his head at the sight of the ambling stick-figure in the black suit as he stretched out his back in a sequence of pops. “Take no notice of that twat,” he said to me under his breath. “He's always been the same.” Scum headed out of the locker-room for his match. Just before reaching the door, he stopped and looked over his shoulder at me with those eyes that looked as old as the Dead Sea Scrolls. Bruised black. “You fancy coming for a drink tonight, Creepy Eyes?” he said. “We've got plans,” Hardway answered for me. “Yeah, I'll come for a drink with you,” I overruled him, shooting Hardway a sideways glance. Scum nodded once and left. Both Walter and Hardway gave me an ear-full about it, but there was no way I was going to turn down the opportunity. More than anyone else I had met, Scum offered me an insight into the side of wrestling the Garrick family had ignored. I had to study and learn from him if I had a hope of progressing in this business. In the end, I learned too much.
