R-Complex, Pt. 2: The All-American Youth

22 February 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 22 February 2011 27th November, 2010. “You should have stayed, brother,” Walter tells me over the phone. He's drunk. He only ever calls me 'brother' when he's drunk. “These ACW guys know how to par-tay!” Lying on the cheap hotel's cheaper mattress, chewing sunflower seeds and staring at the Africa-shaped damp patch on the ceiling, I say, “I'm having a great time right here, Walter.” “It sounds like it,” Walter laughs. “You take this too seriously, brother. The Italian press wouldn't have given a shit about you travelling with the faces. Nobody cares about that stuff any more.” Since Vince McMahon went on TV and exposed the business as a work, heel heat has been hard to come by. Real heel heat, anyway. An old promoter once told me that when he was running a regular show out of Kentucky in the seventies, he had to make the teary-eyed announcement to the fans that his top heel had died in a car crash two nights prior. And it was the biggest, loudest ovation he had ever heard. They legitimately hated that guy. He was a prick and they were glad he was dead. These days, the fans know it's a work. Heels walk out there and get cheered, because the fans appreciate and enjoy their performances. Some guys that work as heels are okay with that. But what really puts arses in those seats is a man that the fans genuinely hate with every fibre of their being, in and out of the ring. They'll pay good money to see that sort of character beaten to within an inch of his life. The only way to achieve that kind of heat is to play the character twenty-four seven, to never let them see your good side, make them believe that you really are an evil son of a bitch. It's not enough to work the marks, you've got to work the smarts, too. The way I see it, being a heel is a lifestyle. A tough, but extremely rewarding one. When I hear those fans burst out in cheers when Rud Albion beats my arse into the ground at the pay-per-view, it'll be worth the long nights spent travelling alone, worth the looks I get when I walk down the street with my evolution tattoos on display, worth the Italian press hounding me. So I tell him, “They don't think they care, Walter. But they do.” Walter sighs. “Look, I'll come meet you at the hotel tomorrow and travel with you for the rest of the Italian stretch. I'll wear the damn pink wig and top hat all day long if you want me to. But after the pay-per-view, I'm gone, brother. It was a temporary deal and ACW creative say they don't have anything else for me once I'm done playing DeLillo. After that, though, you're going to have trouble finding yourself a guy that's willing to be as strict as you.” “I'll cross that bridge when I come to it,” I say. “Enjoy your night, Walter. You deserve a good party. You did really well out there today.” “You can always trust me to deliver, old friend. Catch you later.” After hanging up, I switch on the TV and lie back, listening to some incomprehensible Italian game show. I feel a little less alone. *** 2003. I was watching TV in the Garricks' living room with Charlotte's red hair spread out over my lap when Walter called to say he was in England. “I'm working for this new promotion, Horizon Wrestling, run by this long-time referee called Harry Shank. The guy says he's going to pump some life into British wrestling. They're doing a nationwide tour, hitting all the major cities.” “Sounds good, Walter.” “Yeah, the thing is, YGC was supposed to be here and he never got on his flight. I've tried everyone. Nobody knows where the hell he is.” Young Gun Cooper was another former Garrick student. One of the most naturally gifted young men they had ever trained, according to Brandon. I'd met him once or twice and had been instantly and tremendously jealous of him. “Right, so you want me to ask around, see if I can get a hold of him for you?” “It's too late for that, dude. What I'm asking you... well, Harry has asked me if I know anybody that could fill his spot on short notice. Would you want to...” Before he could even finish his sentence, I said yes. Living with the Garricks was a fulfilling life, and fighting alongside them had done a lot to hone and perfect my skills, but I knew that I needed to experience the business away from their microcosmic world, outside of their mantra of 'only work-rate matters', if I was to grow as a professional wrestler. The next morning, as we sat at the kitchen table while Ray scrambled eggs for us with a cigarette wedged in the corner of his mouth, I delivered the news that I was leaving to the people who had become my adopted family in all but name. Lindsey fawned over me and whined of how she'd miss me, Ray congratulated me with a curt handshake and Lance, although glum that he had lost his principal drinking partner for a while, wished me all the best and pulled me up out of my chair to give me a hug. “You'll do us proud, bud, I know you will.” Brandon, though, who was a wise young man if not an especially optimistic one, looked me dead in the eye and said, “Dean, you're one of the smartest guys I ever met, but be careful. Don't lose your way.” Two days later, I was at the airport with all of my worldly possessions stuffed into a single suitcase, those weathered Union Jack trunks balled up in my coat pocket. Charlotte wrapped her arms around my waist and tip-toed to kiss me on the neck. “Promise you're coming back?” “I promise,” I said, holding her face to my chest. “Then go have your adventure. Just remember, this is where your home is now.” She pressed a polaroid photograph into my palm. It was of the two of us, laughing, hair blown across our faces, Lake Winnipeg stretching to meet the sky behind us. *** On the plane, I had dreamed of standing on the top turnbuckle before of a crowd of hundreds of English wrestling fans, lapping up their welcoming cheers and praise. I'd be their fellow countryman, returned from his lengthy study under the greatest wrestling family in the world, clad in his Union Jack trunks to wave the flag and vanquish evil-doers with his Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu skills. But it turned out I was playing an American heel. “What the fuck is this?” Harry Shank cried when I first introduced myself to him. His hand raked back the wisps of his thinning hair. “Walter, WHAT THE FUCK? I thought your mate was American!” “Does it make a difference? He's one of the best I've been in the ring with,” Walter posited in my defence. He was, and always will be, my own personal hype machine. “He's a great worker, he trained with me at the Compound. He's toured Japan...” “I don't give a fuck if he's toured the fucking City of Atlantis, Walter! I NEED A FUCKING AMERICAN!” Turns out, Harry had the big idea of putting YGC and Walter together as a tag-team. The All-American Youth. He wanted them to face off against his patriotic tag-team champions, The New Bulldogs, Hardway and Fincher, to cash in on the anti-American sentiment generated by the War in Iraq. Despite our protestations, he was determined to go ahead with the idea despite YGC's replacement being an Englishman. “You'd better get practising your bloody Yankee accent, matey, because I'm not changing this program for anyone. And lose the fucking grubby Union Jack trunks!” he had said, pointing a quivering finger at my attire. I asked around in the back until a guy at least twenty pounds lighter than me loaned me a plain black speedo. My balls threatened to fall out of it any second. Walter and I met with the New Bulldogs and discussed the key spots in our match. It all sounded good but I was still thoroughly depressed. “I'm sorry, dude, I didn't know me and Kyle were booked as a tag-team,” Walter apologised, placing a hand on my shoulder as we sat on a bench in the locker room. “It's an experience, right? And these New Bulldogs, they're pretty decent workers. We can put something together.” “I'm a fucking Englishman. I've got a cockney accent and I'm pale as semi-skimmed. And I've got to go out there for my big homecoming match and play a bloody American heel, having never played a heel before in my life. Yes, Walter, this will be an experience. But definitely not a good one.” “Look, just follow my lead. I'll do the talking. We'll be okay.” We went out there to 'Star-Spangled Banner'. Walter bumbled his way through a vaguely pro-American promo to dead silence. All eyes were on me, the obvious Englishman, looking painfully uncomfortable in his undersized trunks. I tried to make the best of a bad situation, punctuating Walter's words with comical sneers and shaking my fist at the bemused grannies in the front row. But, in a word, we bombed. We worked our arses off in the ring against the New Bulldogs but ultimately, nobody cared. There was no story. No heat. As far as bad guys went, we weren't even on their radar. We were jobbers. After the match, Harry, irate already because his main event hadn't turned up yet, ripped us to shreds. “How the hell do you fail to get heat when you're riding the biggest wave of Anti-American sentiment ever seen in this fucking country? Young Gun Cooper would have torn this fucking place down and he's a TEENAGER! You Garrick-trained arseholes are good for one thing, and that's keeping your mouths shut and staring at the fucking lights!” “I've never played a heel before, I'm fucking ENGLISH, how am I supposed to...” I began. “Shut your bloody mouth!” he said, throwing my pay-packet at me. It was so light it floated into my lap. “Look. I'm giving you another chance. We're doing the same match in Newcastle on Friday night, and you better show me something different or you're off the fucking tour.” “Something different? Like what?” “I don't fucking know! That's what I'm paying YOU for, isn't it?” Just then, the door to the locker-room slammed shut. “YOU! WHERE THE FUCK HAVE YOU BEEN? I've been tearing my bloody hair out waiting for you to turn up, the main event starts in five fucking minutes!” All eyes turned towards the figure staggering drunkenly across the locker room. He was wearing a crumpled black suit worn thin here and there, a white shirt flecked with grime. He was deathly pale, black-haired and haggard-looking in the extreme. His sleepless nights manifested in black rings around his eyes. Another man, small in build, thinning hair gelled into depressingly bad liberty spikes, ambled around behind him. He was evidently on drugs of some kind. The suited man leaned against the wall, threw off his shoes and pulled his wrestling boots out of his hold-all. “Blah, blah, blah. I'm here now, so stop your fucking yapping, Harry, or I'll turn around and walk straight back out the fucking door.” “Look, Sebastian, I know you've got your big spot in the US lined up, but you should show some fucking respect to the people that helped to get you there. What... what are you fucking doing?” Sebastian was tipping white powder from a thin aluminium vial into the crook of his thumb. “Heavy night, mate. Didn't get home until... fuck... four o'clock this afternoon. Need a little pick me up,” he said, snorting up the powder in one. “Why, got a problem?” Harry was speechless. “Just... just get out there and work your fucking match.” Sebastian pulled his boots on with a grumble. The entire locker room fell silent, turning away from him and pretending they hadn't noticed his presence. Not one of them wanted to make eye contact with this strange man. In my naivete, however, I kept my eyes locked on him the whole time. He was about to walk past me but stopped, looking me dead in the eye. “What the fuck are you looking at?” he spat. I said nothing, just held my stare. “Are those contact lenses?” he said, curiously. “No.” He held his gaze for a little while longer. “Interesting.”
