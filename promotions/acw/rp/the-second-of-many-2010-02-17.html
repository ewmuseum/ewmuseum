The second of many.

17 February 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 17, 2010 The night was quiet. Wind rifling through the late summer leaves above him was the only break in the silence. He listened and moved on. After a while, he stopped behind a small bush and froze at the sound of approaching footsteps. The darkness of night and the dappled moonlight through the trees allowed him to remain unnoticed. “I swear!” one man laughed. “It was my girlfriend’s sister. What are the chances of that happening?” “With you, I think the chances always improve!” the other said. “I’m just surprised she wasn’t your sister.” The men laughed and continued their walk. Both were carrying small, automatic firearms slung over their shoulders. High boots, fatigues, and the fact that the men spoke in Farsi let him know he was getting close. This was another clue that he was on the correct path. Movement without sound was also one of his many talents. When needed he could move swiftly, blending the sounds of movement with the blowing leaves. After a few more minutes he could see the lights of the camp. Voices floated through the night air, all conversations were held in Farsi. A very strange language to hear deep in the Smoky Mountains. Thin smoke floated toward him and he tasted Eastern White Pine and Mountain Ash from the fire. His first view of the tents, eight in a circle around the fire, confirmed he was in the right place. A twig snapped somewhere behind him and he leaned into the Virginia Pine near him. Again, voices came to him from the darkness of the night... behind him and to his left. “I know I saw something move.” “Sure you did.” Anoki shot back. “And maybe you are growing fearful of field mice.” “It must have been a very big field mouse!” Germaine quipped. Anoki laughed boisterously. “I think you’ve been in this forest too long. Before you know it, you will think an army of bears has us surrounded.” “You are very funny, Anoki, but I know what I saw.” Their conversation ended with laughs from both men. He followed, using their laughs and footsteps as cover. The largest tent was an indication of where he should go. Slowly he moved around the circle in a clockwise flow. He started to move again when he noticed something in the leaves at his feet. He brushed the dry leaves aside and recognised the ranger uniform below them. He had found the missing ranger. Investigating further he saw that the ranger’s throat had been slit. Not a pleasant way to go. He stepped over the body and moved until he was behind the largest of the tents. Through the nylon wall, he could see movement around a light source. He stepped closer. Voices inside the tent suggested a less than friendly discussion was occuring. “I told you; we do not strike until we receive explicit instructions!” “Our leadership is weak. We must act now before we are discovered.” “One more word about weak leadership and I will personally cut your tongue from your mouth while I spit in your eyes! You hear me?” “I apologize, Hayden. I am more than ready to complete our mission. Tell me what you want me to do and I will follow you through the gates of hell.” The name, Hayden, resonated repeatedly in his ears and he knew he had found his target. Soon his mission would be complete. "Leave me now. I have things to do." Kavan nodded, not wanting to cause any further trouble. “Yes, Hayden. Let me know if you need anything." “Kavan, hang on.” Hayden spoke up after a moment's thought. “I know you are a good soldier and I trust you with my life. May Allah be with you.” “And you.” He watched as Kavan left the tent. Soon, very soon, he thought. The light in the tent faded while he watched. Listening, he could hear the sigh of the cot under Heydar’s weight. The call of a night bird pulled his attention to the wooded area around him. The night was clear and a bright moon now hung in the eastern sky. Shadows on the western side, he thought. The mesmerising moonlight danced across the ground as the wind blew through the trees. He turned his attention back to the tent. Quiet snoring told him that Hayden was fast asleep. Silently he moved forward so he could touch the tent. Feeling under the back wall, he discovered the floor was attached to the walls. He would have to go through the wall instead of under it. His knife cleared the boot scabbard and he touched the razor sharp blade to the nylon wall of the tent. The blackened blade would not reflect the moonlight so no caution was needed there. Like a scalpel through soft flesh the knife sliced the nylon without making a sound. In a matter of seconds he was inside the tent and kneeling beside the sleeping Hayden. He brought the knife to Hayden’s ear and cupped his hand over Hayden’s lips. Hayden’s eyes opened and the man in black put pressure on the knife, sending its tip ever so slightly into Hayden’s ear. “Don’t even struggle.” he whispered through clenched teeth, his voice like sandpaper. “You’ve made a mistake by being here, in my country. It’s something you’ll have to pay for. Snow Flake sends his best. I... well. I am simply known as Death.” With one strong thrust he drove the knife through flesh, bone and brain. He then rotated the knife as if he were trying to screw it into the ear. One long convulsion ran through Hayden’s body and he was dead within seconds. The man known as Death pulled the knife from the ear and wiped the blade on Hayden’s uniform. Death pulled a gold ring from the dead man’s hand and pulled the medals from his chest. Silently, he glided through the opening in the back of the tent. From shadow to shadow he moved through the trees and brush, down the mountain. This Snow Flake had paid him a very good wage for Hayden’s death. The ring and medals would serve as proof of death and assure his receipt of the balance owed him. Sunrise would bring prayers. The absence of their leader would alert them. He would be long gone by that time. Death, he thought, has a way of coming and going without being seen. When the shadow known as Death was off the mountain he made a phone call. It was short and to the point. The call was to the home of the highest-ranking Homeland Security officer in Knoxville. Both the phone call and the fact that it was to the home of the officer would attract much attention. Hopefully more than the body of an Osida operative in the Smokey Mountains. Either way, he was sure he would never be connected to the death or the Osida cell. "Death is the ultimate gift." Perhaps it was.
