R-Complex, Pt. 3: The Genius of Scum

22 February 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 22 February 2011 27th November, 2010. Fever-sweat sticks the sheets to my body and the room's stale air is heavy in my lungs. Dreams of my mother crying over a teddy bear savaged by the dog. The dog was shaking it side to side in its maw, its torn stomach spilling its white guts. She watched from between her fingers. Tears, black with mascara, paved dark roads down her cheeks. I can smell her cigarette smoke in the hotel room. In my stupor, I wonder if she has died, and I have experienced an otherworldy visitation. I laugh at my own irrationality. I'm out of Soma. It's been so long since I slept without it that I've forgotten how to. So I pace around the small room, hands on my aching head. Tiring of this very quickly, I decide to look around to see if any previous occupants have left anything interesting. A stash of weed maybe, or at least a book or, Christ, I'll take a magazine. Anything. But all I can find is a copy of the Holy Bible in the top drawer of the dresser. I measure its weight in my hand. It makes me think of him. Makes me think of Scum. *** 2003. “And now, making his way to the ring, hailing from London's dark subconscious, weighing in at two hundred and twenty pounds... SCUM!” All of the boys in the back were fighting to get a peek as Sebastian Christopher, the man known as Scum, walked down to the ring. The fans loathed him unanimously and whole-heartedly. Old folks who had, up until now, watched in silence outside of the occasional polite applause, became frothing berserkers hurling obscenities. Parents screamed for his blood alongside their children. The groups of smarts dotted around the arena in their Max Danger T-shirts began listing his out-of-kayfabe crimes at full volume: “YOU CAN'T WORK!” “WHAT DRUGS ARE YOU ON TODAY, ARSEWIPE?” “YOU'RE FUCKING POISON! POISON!” Scum hadn't even said a word. There was just something about him, an aura that I couldn't fully articulate. It was in his slow, uneven paces towards the ring. In the way his mouth twisted into a subtle half-smile as he looked out into the stands and saw their hatred. In the way he stepped between the ropes and sauntered around, absorbing their abuse, like he lived to be reviled. He had become an object of nameless hatred. He had tapped into the dark side of human nature. The boys hated him, too. “That twat,” Hardway commented to nobody in particular, as he stood next to me watching through the crack in the curtains. “How does he get this fucking heat? He's a worthless druggie and in a few months he's going to be making hundreds of thousands of dollars a year. I just don't get it.” Scum, still wearing the ugly suit he had arrived in, lifted a microphone to his lips. He paused, listening to the fans' raucous boos, his smile getting wider by the second. He lowered the microphone again, laughing, outstretching his arms to welcome their resentment. “YOU DON'T DESERVE TO BE IN A WRESTLING RING!” “WE HATE YOU, SCUM, WE FUCKING HATE YOU!” “I HOPE YOU DIE!” He still hadn't said a word. “I hate the cunt but when I see this, the hair on the back of my neck stands on end. He's fucking magical,” Harry said. “He's a fucking genius. How does he do this? Will somebody tell me how he fucking does this?” Finally, he began to speak. “People of Sheffield. Your Prophet has arrived.” The boos didn't die down for a second. “And what, you may ask, does my clairvoyance confess to me when I look out into the stands and gaze upon you? I'll tell you, Sheffield. “I see a horde of unemployed, unwashed, ill-spoken drains on society, destined to spend their whole lives rotting in council housing estates. I see a generation of children raised by the television in lieu of their absent fathers. I see men growing up to squander their youth drinking on street corners, unskilled, uneducated, without a hope in the world. Pumping impure street drugs into their veins in an attempt to escape their tedious and miserable existence if only for a moment. “I see desperation, depression, poverty and vice. I see thousands of gravestones, each one a testament to an individual wasted life spent in isolation and misery, each one etched with the obituary: Couldn't Be Arsed.” As I watched the fans react to his rasped, choking words with even more bile, I began to feel it myself. In the pit of my stomach. A sort of anxious desire to smash his face in and achieve some kind of catharsis, to disprove his bleak outlook. He was somehow tapping into the subconscious fears and insecurities of the common man, throwing them in their faces, making these abstract concepts into a physical object on which they could vent their hatred... and that object was himself. “You might ask of your Prophet, what does he recommend? What medicine can cure this terrible affliction we suffer from? “My answer... is nothing. “There is nothing that can save you. There will be no revolution. There will be no progress. There will be no redemption. Only emptiness. Loneliness. And, at the very end... nothingness.” And he laughed. No sorrow, no pity, only cold amusement at the plight of thousands, millions of British people. The reaction was visceral, splenic and violent: the couple of hundred fans in attendance were on their feet, screaming, shouting and throwing rubbish. I was in awe. The awe remained with me as the promotion's lacklustre top face, 'TNT' Trent Newton Thomas, charged down to the ring to an ovation that didn't befit his mediocre standing in the eyes of the fans, and Scum took sloppy, business-exposing bumps all over the hall to the derision of the smarts. When TNT dropped Scum with a botched superplex and covered him for the three count, retaining his meaningless Commonwealth Title, the fans celebrated like he had single-handedly saved the world. They hugged, clapped, got out of their seats and stormed the ring to hug him and ask for autographs. On the mat, Scum lay, eyes closed, with a smirk on his face. *** “Oh shit, sorry mate, I didn't know you were in there.” Scum sat on the toilet, trousers around his ankles and the hem of his shirt crumpled on his lap. A cigarette, mostly burned down to ash, drooped from the corner of his mouth. As he spoke, the ash tumbled off the end. “Eh? Oh, it's you. Creepy Eyes. Heh. Fuck it. It's not like you haven't seen a bloke having a shit before, right?” And he let rip. This would be the second time I spoke to Scum. After the show, Walter, the New Bulldogs and I had piled into the local tavern for a few drinks. We'd noticed Sebastian sitting up a far corner with the sickly youth he had brought along to the show, but the Bulldogs warned us off from approaching him. “He's a fucking arsehole,” Hardway had said. “Loose cannon. Can be nice as pie one minute, then drop the nut on you the next, know what I mean? Besides, he looks like he's having fun with his fucking smack-head boyfriend. Leave the cunt to it.” Still, I caught a few sideways glances of him, seemingly talking about something at length and with much animation as the dope beside him nodded empty-headedly. After a few pints, I stopped looking, and I had all but forgotten about him until I barged in on him doing his business in a toilet cubicle. “Sorry,” I reiterated to him, closing the door. I moved into the vacant cubicle beside his. For a while, there was silence, beside the sound of our respective bowel movements. “Harry was giving you a good hiding when I got there today,” he said. “Yeah,” I said, tentatively. It was weird having a conversation on the toilet. “He wanted me to pretend to be American to get the heel heat.” Scum laughed and flushed the chain. I heard him buckling his belt, his footsteps against the tiles, then turning on the tap to wash his hands. It wasn't exactly optimum conditions, but still - I couldn't miss my chance. “Look, you were really incredible out there tonight. You're such a great heel, I just...” Scum jumped up and hung onto the cubicle door, looking at me over the top. I practically jumped out of my skin and froze in place for a moment, feet lifted off the floor, hand over my privates. “And you want advice?” he smirked. “I... uh... yeah,” I stammered. His blackened eyes were wide with cocaine and sweat glazed his forehead. His hair was matted in his face. I was worried. “My advice to you is get out of this fucking stupid business,” he said. He held his piercing gaze for a moment longer before dropping off the cubicle door. The door to the Gents slammed and I was alone. I sighed hopelessly. I still no ideas on what to do Friday night in Newcastle to turn it around, to keep myself and Walter on the tour, and the top heel had just refused to give me a shred of advice. As I flushed the chain, I was considering looking through the Yellow Pages for an acting coach, booking some impromptu lessons to improve my American accent. Maybe picking up some tanning lotion to give me that California glow. Or perhaps just calling Harry, telling him to stick his tour up his arse and getting the first flight back to Winnipeg. As I washed my hands, I glanced up at the mirror and had a heart palpitation for the second time in five minutes. Behind me, standing by the door, Scum leaned against the wall with his arms crossed over his chest, watching me. “If you're not going to quit this retarded business, if you're determined to waste your life rolling around with other semi-naked men, I suppose I could offer you some advice,” he said. “Anything mate. I'd be really grateful,” I replied, enthusiastically. I locked eyes with the dark reflection over my shoulder. His face was consumed by shadows and his eye-sockets appeared like empty black pits. After a moment's thought, he began to speak. “People in this day and age aren't easily fooled. You can't go out there and just PRETEND. You have to BELIEVE. What you tell those people has to be the truth. The worst kind of truth. The kind of truth that makes you sick to your fucking stomach. “Tell them what they already know, but don't want to think about. Take them out of their comfort zone. Hit them with something they can't ignore. “Trust me, they'll hate you for it.” With that, he turned and left.
