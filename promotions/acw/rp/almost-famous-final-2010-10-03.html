Almost Famous - Final

3 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03 October 2010 “Where do we go from here?  The words are coming out all weird.  Where are you now?  When I need you...” - The Bends, Radiohead Oso Bannon was losing control. He was in the best shape of his life, but visions of his time here on Earth flashed before him like he was on his last legs. He wanted to hate himself for being so reliant upon drugs, but so far he seemed to have no problem being clean. Except for a few pain pills here or there. “All those wrestling guys do these,” he thought sometimes to help him sleep. To subside the guilt. Instead he settled on hating himself for things he couldn’t have helped. The horrors he so vividly remembers from his early childhood and the love for his whore mother that still fills his heart have left him with only the desire to be better. Better than the savages in his homeland. Better than his mother. Better than everyone on the field. And now, better than everyone in the ring. He can’t help but wish he was someone else. If he could’ve grown up normally, he wouldn’t be so fucked up now. And wrestling? What an enticing, yet enigmatic mistress! He felt that familiar rush as soon he’d gotten into the ring at Kory Storm’s wrestling school. He knew, deep down within himself, that he could do it, but knows nothing about it. He’s void of all knowledge regarding the sport and the business. He couldn’t tell you Hulk Hogan from Hurricane Helms. Settling into a weight bench, he chuckles. “What the hell am I thinking?” His cellphone rings. His ringtone: “Highway to Hell” by AC/DC. It was the only cassette he owned as a child. He looks down at the caller ID... WALLACE ...And ponders whether or not he should answer. He hated Chester B. Wallace with a passion. He’d met heroin addicts and crackheads with stronger moral fabric. Still, the man was right. “He’s my only shot...” He flips open the phone. “What do you want, Wallace?” Chester Wallace’s voice booms in all its Alabaman glory, exclaiming, “We’re going to Europe! I got you a tryout!” A highway to hell, indeed.
