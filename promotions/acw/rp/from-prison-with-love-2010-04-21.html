From Prison With Love

21 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 21, 2010 "I'm supposed to be getting better, but what is better? Is better not wanting to kill people? I still want to kill people. They tell me it's wrong, is it wrong? Do these people really deserve the air they breathe in every day? I don't think any of us do." -Prison Diary of Sean Williams The walls of a maximum security prison are ment to demoralize, they are ment to be draining on the men they contain. Some men try to turn them into something else, while others embrace the demoralization. They leave the prison saved, they leave the prison better people than when they came in. At least that's the plan, nobody ever really leaves a better person. Some get a new appreciation for their freedom, but they still have the same mental impulses for the crimes they commited. Crimes of passion, of rage, of greed. So why do we release them? Why do we let them walk back into the world? Why would anyone in their right mind, let Sean Williams walk out the doors of a maximum security facility? Well, when your crime is tax evasion, eventually you get to walk out the doors a free man. "If the point of prison is to lock up the bad people, why would they ever let any of us leave? Why not just let us sit in here and steal from, rape, and kill each other? Wouldn't that make the world a better place? If the system worked correctly I wouldn't get to leave. I'd be stuck here forever. Instead, I get to walk free, into the sun, into peoples homes. I get to co-exist with pre-school teachers, and walk this earth with only a small blemish on a computer record. Is the system right? No, we should change it. But the nice people want to believe we can change, we cant, and we wont." -Prison Diary of Sean Williams Sean watched the walls of the prison pass by him, he knew each block. He had walked this same path to the mess hall every day for the last two years. Finally at noon, he would walk into the world a free man. Free to do as he chooses. Free to do what he wants.
