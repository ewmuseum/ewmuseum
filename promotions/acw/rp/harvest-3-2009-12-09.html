Date: December 09, 2009 He bounced off the brittle mat, the loose board crunching beneath him. The ring was moments from giving way. He could hear his own breathing echoing off the rippled metal ceiling. He rolled to his side and noticed an old woman sitting in a simple folding chair alone, her neck cocked sideways and a face indifferent to what she was watching. His eyes darted on an x-axis. The 'crowd' was five people. Fingers slid into his wet hair and tugged his scalp. On his feet, he was slung to the ropes. The long band, frayed to the core, approached. He clutched it and watched his opponent come up empty on a dropkick. The brawler shuffled his feet and lunged at him, but he ducked, and put him in a reverse headlock. * There was something familiar about those eyes that Dan Felton could not put his finger on. He had seen that grimace before on thousands of faces, but there was something familiar about the aggressor. The decaying leather cowboy boots, the worn jeans. The lean, muscular frame covered in a simple v-neck t-shirt. The face--framed by a stern, prominent jaw. The puckered lower-lip, evidence of tobacco use. The hair was longer, and he wore a beard; a long beard that hid a animal's soul underneath. Felton looked down at the faded Xerox that listed the matchups; the bands forming the text broken up and nearly impossible to read. He looked up. It became clear who he was watching. * He thrusted a boot in his opponent's midsection and squeezed the head in between his thighs. He hooked the elbows and lifted, falling back and landing the double-underhook piledriver. 'Voices After Midnight,' Felton gasped. Upon impact, the ring gave way, creating a sloping divot in the corner of the ring. A five year old boy in the first row went into hysterics but was prompty reprimanded as if at church. Cowboy rolled over onto his unconscious opponent. The count was an afterthought. 1 2 3. 'BBBBLOWRHN!' the feedback erupted from the Fischer Price P.A. system. '..AND YOUR WINNER, OLIVRE BRALEY!' One pair of hands clapped, the haunting reverberation muffled by the expansive walls. Olivre Braley climbed through the ropes at an overweight man who seemed to cower as he passed. Braley stopped and frowned. 'I aren't gon' hurt y'.' A white grin emerged from his dry lips. Felton nodded and watched as the Midnight Cowboy departed through the gymnasium concession area and into his dressing room, without a second-glance from those nearby.
