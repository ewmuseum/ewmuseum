Nothing Gets Better..

31 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is The ACW site's own Experience board post (#112) by Max Douglas, dated 31 December 2002, from the fed's first site, acw.ewmania.com (its acw2 edition, 2002–04) as captured in the Wayback on 30 May 2003. The site's own page furniture (the menu and the link back to the character's biography) is not reproduced.
Nothing Gets Better.. Written: 12.31.02 I was awakened by the sound of telephone ringing in my ears. The first thought that came to my mind as I opened my eyes was the aching in my head. The phone rang again as I rubbed my eyes and leaned over to pick it up. 'Hello?' I said in my groggy morning voice, 'This is a wake up call for a Mister Max Douglas.' The man on the other line said in a very courteous voice. 'Thanks.' I replied, still half asleep. I hung up the phone then laid my head back down, and stared up at the ceiling. I glanced over at the clock, it read '8:00'. I sighed and ran my hand across the top of my head as I sat up. I swung my body around so that my feet were now on the ground. I could barely see anything the room was so dark. I had pulled the shades shut tightly last night because I knew the sun had a tendency to shine right into my face in the early morning. I rose from the bed and stretched. My head was throbbing with pain, I tried not to think about it. It had been almost a week since I had the match with Alias but the pain was still there, along with the gash that accompanied it. I slowly walked to the bathroom and switched on the light. I squinted my eyes for a second, trying to adjust from complete darkness to the light of the bathroom. I didn't look into the mirror, because I couldn't. I couldn't look at my own face, it hurt too much to see myself. But still, I began to peel the bandage off my forehead, revealing the wound underneath it. I shot a quick glance into the mirror to see how the healing process was coming. It looked a lot better than it did yesterday. The cut was getting smaller and smaller each day, which meant I didn't have to wear the bandage much longer. I slowly walked to my left and turned on the shower. The water was cold at first, I would have to wait for it to get warm before I could actually step in. That's when I started to think about what I had planned for the day. I was going to see Ben at the psychiatric ward. No doubt it would bring back old memories of the torment that I went through. All the beatings and loneliness that had gone along with the whole place. I was going to tell Ben that I knew what he was going through and that he wasn't alone, and that I could relate. But most importantly, I was going there to show him that I cared. Because sometimes that's all someone needs, to know someone cares. I stuck my hand over the shower head to see how warm it had gotten. It was still a little cold, but not steaming hot either. So I stepped in and just let the water run down my face and back. I thought about the things I'd say when I got to the ward. I wondered if I'd even be allowed to see him. I thought back to the days when I was in there with him. I had no visitors, but saw many other patients with them. I tried to think back about what time the visitors usually showed up. I couldn't really remember. I had tried to block everything from the days I spent in there. All I could really remember were the screams at night and the beatings I was treated to. I didn't wash myself, I had done that the previous night at the hospital I had just been released from. I was just trying to get myself awake. I turned the water so that it was at its coldest point and proceeded to stick my face right under the shower head. I was in a small state of shock from the coldness of it, but I began to get used to it. I turned the running water off and climbed out. I slung a towel around my waist and ran my hand through the brown hair on the top of my head. I sighed as I went back into my small daze of sleepiness. I got dressed and looked over at the clock it flashed '8:15'. Hard to believe fifteen minutes had gone by since I had been woken up by the phone. I slowly put on a black ACW T-shirt and grabbed my car keys and wallet. I yawned as opened the door and made a left towards the elevators. The hall was dimly lit. The walls were covered in bathe and pinkish paint. The long walk down to the elevators was quiet. I passed a few maids pushing cleaning carts around, but didn't make any eye contact. They didn't acknowledge me at all, but I was used to people doing that. I stepped onto the elevator. There was already someone on it when I entered and before I could press the 'L' button for lobby, I noticed it began to flash. The older looking man had pushed it before I had gotten on. He wasn't much. About five foot, ten inches. Still a good amount shorter than me. He was pretty well built though. He was dressed in an extremely nice suit and tie. I figured he was going to a corporate meeting of some sort. The elevator stopped and a short 'ding' rang out from above us and the doors opened. He stepped out infront of me and took a left as I just kept walking forward. The lobby was nothing like the rest of the hotel, it was loud and busy, far from the likes of the thirteenth floor hallway that I had just left. The doors of the lobby were left wide open, letting in the summer breeze. It was a nice breeze, it was refreshing as I walked out the doors and towards my truck. I dug the keys out of my pocket as I made my way over to the red Ford Ranger. I rubbed my eyes, getting the last bit of sleepiness out of me, and opened the car door. It still smelled like a brand new truck, but it was a 98 model. I turned on the ignition and the car lit up. I looked down at the clock, then green numbers read '8:23'. I began to back out of the parking space and turn the radio on. Music had become a big part of my life. Actually, it had always been a way for me to cope with my problems. A lot of the lyrics the musicians wrote, I could relate to. All the bad lyrics. The depressing lyrics. I guess I felt a certain attachment to them. The music was there when nobody else really gave a shit. The song playing right now was 'Creep' by Stone Temple Pilots, it was already half over. As I pulled out of the hotel parking lot, I turned it up loudly. 'Feeling uninspired Think I'll start a fire Everybody run Bobby's got a gun Think you're kinda neat Then she tells me I'm a creep Friends don't mean a thing Guess I'll leave it up to me Take time with a wounded hand Cause it likes to heal Take time with a wounded hand Guess I like to steal Take time with a wounded hand Cause it likes to heal, I like to steal' As the song ended, I lowered the radio and stopped at a red light. I yawned and then sunk into thought once again. I began to think about what it would be like when I saw him. Would he accept me? Would he be happy to see me? Or would he be pissed off that I didn't write back? Nonetheless, I needed to find out. I thought it was my responsibility as an 'older brother' to do this. I didn't feel obligated. I wanted to go. The light turned green and pressed my foot on the gas. I still thought about the days when I was in the ward. I remember that I couldn't sleep in fear that I would be hurt. I would crawl up into a small corner inside my padded cell and bawl. The images of the doctors in white coats began to cross my face. My breathing started to pick up. Tears began to swell up inside of me. I could hear the belts smacking against my back. I could hear my own screams. I turned the radio up as loud as it could go, to try and drown out the sounds of pain that I was hearing. It didn't work. I pulled over to the side of the road and slammed my head against the steering wheel. A slight honk could be heard over the screaming music of Godsmack. The whips and screams grew louder with every strum of the guitar. Every beat of the drum. I could feel the pain inside of me. I could feel the belts crashing down onto my back. I could feel and hear it all, just like I was there. Just like I was back in the ward. Then the song ended. And the screams and pain stopped. I turned the radio off and lifted my head up off the steering wheel. I whipped the tears from my eyes and turned the car off. I laid my head back and looked up. My breathing was beginning to slow down and come back to a normal rate. I put my hands on the top of my head and just sighed. I slowly heard a voice. It was saying 'I hate you' repeatedly. It wouldn't stop. 'I hate you, Aaron.' The words made me cringe. I realized what was happening. The voices were back.
