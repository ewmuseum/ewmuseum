blind Title: In a Moonlit Memory - Five Date Made: 4 . 26 . 04 Location: The Past A summer wind pierced through the sultry air of the inner city. The rhythm of honking cars and strolling people came together in one insignificant tune for the world to hear. Planes reached for the sun, birds breaching through the clouds. Buildings stood tall and silent, guardians of their fair city. Faces roamed through the crowd, ghosts of a dying race. To the masses, it was just another day for business. To the individual, it was just one day closer to that underestimated moment where their lives would end. And to one soul, it was just the day after. Standing under the clear blue sky, he gazed down at the headline of the newspaper in the container standing on the edge of the street. All around him, the swarm of locusts he called the human race flew along, many of them brushing up against his back as if he were a bush pushing through a gate. A tree sticking out of a mock patch of dirt on the edge of a sidewalk. A rack of clothes you’d find outside a Salvation Army store. None of them saw him as a person; he was only a pronoun found in a world outside the realm of their reality. He was nothing to them. It was a mentality he tried to avoid. That was why he carried his camera, why he took his pictures, and why he kept a black shoebox under the floorboards of the apartment he never bothered to use. He knew he would have to return soon though; he had some additions to his collection of distant memories. But for now, he just stared at the newspaper, and thought back to the midnight hour where, for a moment, everything made sense. He could remember the smell of her perfume and the color of her… as turquoise as the sea … tear-flooded eyes. He could still taste the champagne on his lips, swallowed to fulfill a wish that would never come true. And he could still remember, no matter how hard he tried to forget, the last tear that fell from grace. The only thing he couldn’t remember was her name; he wasn’t even sure if he had asked in the first place anyway. It was such a little detail, but it made all of the difference between remembering and forgetting. Murder: Young Woman Thrown From Third Story Window His hand reached down into his pocket slowly and absently, in search for change to unlock the article from the container. Instead, his fingers took hold of a plastic slip of paper, otherwise known as a polaroid picture. There were places that the dead lived on; he was only fully aware of two of them. One stood still in the grip of his hand. The other was too deep inside for him to grasp at the moment. A man, wearing a gray suit with a blue tie, quickly walked up to the container, completely ignoring him as he stood right in front of it, dropped some change in and grabbed hold of the last newspaper. The door slammed shut, leaving the inside barren. By now, the sun was already going down over the horizon. Philadelphia was, for the most part, quieting down once again. After hours of standing in that one spot, he spontaneously turned to his right and began walking along the sidewalk, fading into the swarm he had come to hate. Time to move on, he figured, as the camera wrapped around his arm swung inside his coat and the picture in his pocket crumpled softly in his hand. Where he was really going… I know we’ll meet again. … he could never really know.
