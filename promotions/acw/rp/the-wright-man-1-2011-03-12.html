The Wright Man - 1

12 March 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12 March 2011 In November 2007, MSLL was the number three promotion in Mexico and struggling. It had previously been numero uno, even as recently as 2001, but disastrous financial decisions and a roster-full of overpaid underachievers is never a good recipe for business. Antonio Martinez, one of the main writers there, was about to become the head booker at the beginning of 2008. Martinez had been a limited worker and never a star per se but solidified himself as a dependable midcarder in every single top Mexican territory at some point in a 15-year spell. Knee injuries cut his career short but he wasn't ready to walk away from the business just yet and moved behind the scenes. See, even if Martinez didn't stand out as an act, he always made his opponents look good and thought about the match. In fact, he had a flair for helping others with their characters too and so moving into the office seemed to be a natural progression. Antonio was a man of the people too and probably too nice to be involved in wrestling. However, aged 45, he still had years before retirement and a heck of a challenge in front of him. He had to revive an ailing company, one he spent half of his in-ring career working for and all of his post-wresling career as well. Martinez set about assembling his team two months before he officially took over and after hiring two or three confidants, he contacted John Stein, his American brother from another mother, a decent, upstanding man who loved wrestling with every pang of his being. And the boys adored him for it too. Now, Stein isn't a Mexican surname. Not by any means. But John knew basic Spanish and had worked down south under a mask in the past. Like Antonio, Stein didn't stand out as a wrestler but he had a brilliant brain for the business. They had things in common. What Stein also had was his native language - English. Martinez knew that American wrestlers hadn't really gotten over in his native land in a massive way, not like they had in Japan, but the few who had - well, they spoke for themselves. Both men sensed a surging popularity in things north of the border. Mexicans, the largest Spanish-speaking nation on the face of the earth, were tuning into see mainstream promotions. Antonio also knew that his people were extremely passionate and it wouldn't take much to ignite the crowd, particularly coming from an American. Along with Stein and a loyal hand, he decided that while he wanted to remain true to the smaller workers who shifted the tickets and delivered the excitement, they needed a genuine heavyweight positioned at the top of the card. They needed someone who would start a fight in an empty room and tell you to your face that he had fucked your sister and mother one after the other. Stein searched in the south of the States for 5 weeks. Then, he headed to Dallas, Texas. After watching a tall, blonde veteran's entrance, he knew he'd found his man. The reaction to the bad guy rang in his ears. He smiled as soon as he saw the 6'6 badass walk out and very loudly tell a redneck that he'd fuck both him and his girlfriend in different ways if he dared to throw another bottle of Pepsi at him. Needless to say, the fan didn't need to be told twice. Upon seeing that, Stein pulled out his cell phone and, shaking with excitement, punched in the words: "I've found HIM." Him happened to be a guy called Hank Wright. ----------------------------------------------------------------------- At the end of 2010, Jeremy Hunt, the man in ACW, told Henry Irwonsen that the company needed more talent. Henry, a senior referee who works with the talent week in, week out, was shocked to hear Hunt's words. Jeremy added: "You can never have enough talent Henry." Irwonsen couldn't argue with that point but ACW had a talent pool deeper than the Pacific Ocean. It seemd Hunt wanted to turn ACW into a truly global juggernaut and 2011 would be the year. The promotion was going to continue its worldwide tour and after completing their time in Italy, they were set for a return to North America. Unlike the US, Mexicans still took their wrestling seriously, which would suit the superstars of ACW just fine. They're famed for pushing the envelope. Still, there seems to be room for one or two more.
