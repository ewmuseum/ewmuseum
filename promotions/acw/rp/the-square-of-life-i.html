The Square of Life: I.

11 March 2005

Iceman The Ice Master; Cold Man; Mr. Freeze; Iceman (oh, wait, that's his name LOL); Icey; The Man Who is Cold; The Opposite of Fire; Father Winter; Mother Nature (wait, no); Ice Ice Baby... and so on. The Square of Life: I. Location: Iceman's Locker Room. Date: March 11, 2005 Number (Archive): 28 Number (Wrestler): 1 The scene changed to a solemn locker room where cold air circulated around the shower stall, through to the mirror in front of it, all the way to the entrance of the locker room door. The camera caught Iceman, face down in a newspaper, dripping wet from head to toe as he sat in an ACW bath towel. The look on his face showed he was clearly upset. It was a look of anger and frustration… as his eyes scanned over the back page of the newspaper over and over again. Iceman was reading the Detroit Chronicle, a review of last week’s Courage show. Of course, the main report was on page one… an All Star Championship Wrestling appearance didn’t need to go on the last page. The top story examined what Silver HAWK did to ‘Superstar’ Vince Jacobs… and the match Sars the Clown and Lowell Dot Com displayed. But the back page… the page in which Iceman was reading… was dedicated to the “darker” part of the show. There were two types of hardcore fans that would actually read reports in the wrestling industry. The die-hard fans… and the spectacle fans. The die-hard fan would want to know everything as to what happened that night. From the opening sentence of Silver HAWK, to Andy Sharp chatting with Seymour Almasy backstage, to that awfully cute girl, uh, Hillary Duncan? Man, Iceman wished. If only he wasn’t six-foot-four, had a little more muscle and actually looked pretty cool… then maybe he’d have a chance with a girl like that too. Iceman sighed as his eyes went back to the newspaper. “I don’t like Iceman. I don’t want to hear about Iceman, blah, blah, blah…” The words worked like punches as they flew off the printed-page… and slammed themselves in Iceman’s face… as hard, if not harder, than The Loser had done to Iceman last week. “Sure.” Iceman sighed. He lived in an up-tempo world. There was no time for the “making of a star” anymore. There was no time for a legacy -a legacy in any scenario- to grow from its roots, starting in today’s existence. It was the age of the instant. The produced. It was like a good meal… you could wait to get quality… but why wait when you could, in turn, get a quick substitute that would work just as well? In the age of the fast-food hamburger, which, was generally 100 times worse than what you could get for waiting 100 times longer, the people in today’s society wanted their wrestling just like their food. Give them something, anything, just as long as they got it now, and kept them interested for as long as they needed. Gone were the days a bum off the street, with no wrestling value, and hell, no interest value whatsoever, could take it one step at a time -even if it meant taking five-hundred steps- and turn themselves into a fast-food burger… which had all the ingredients of a sit-down meal. Iceman hung his head. Being the useless sidekick to Blast From the Past for so long had practically ruined him. The critics hadn’t even seen him just yet… but they knew they didn’t like him. “Not without Blast…” Another critic wrote. A frustrated Iceman, not knowing what to do next, just shook in vengeance and prayed he’d be able to silence the critics the next time.
