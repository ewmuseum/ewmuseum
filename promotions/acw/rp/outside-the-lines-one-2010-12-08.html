Outside the Lines -- One

8 December 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08 December 2010 The sun is rising in Los Angeles, but the city is already bustling with activity. The highways which connect it together are beginning their trademark process of becoming unbearably congested. If cars were blood cells, we were oxygen, and LA was our heart the 101 freeway on a weekday would be a disgusting, clogged artery. The heart would explode. So would we. Somewhere in the miles of traffic is Chester Wallace in his rented Lexus. Tapping his fingers against his steering wheel anxiously, he remembers the good old days when Los Angeles was his town and he moved through the air to get around it. His mind flashes to a letter from his business manager he received a few days earlier telling him his private helicopter had just sold for half its value at auction. The letter had stated plainly in conclusion, “Better something than nothing.” For him there had never been a “something” in the equation. It was always all or nothing. Now he is nothing. Just another one of the grunts toiling away in rush hour. An explosion would be welcome. Twelve miles away in actual distance and two hours away in that traffic, Oso Bannon sits upright in front of a mirror falling in and out of sleep. A frumpy woman wearing too much make-up is applying foundation to his face. Production assistants and interns scurry around like chickens with their heads cut off, rushing coffees and scripts to people who don’t even know their names. “Oso Bannon to the set please!” A walkie talkie attached to the frumpy woman screeched loudly with a muffled voice and feedback. “OSO BANNON!” The Bear’s eyes shot open and he woke to the same familiar feeling that he often woke to. The feeling of wishing he was nameless. Unknown as the Starbucks carrying ants scurrying around him are. Wishful thinking. His thoughts seemed to carry him to the brightly lit soundstage he was summoned to. He had no idea how he had arrived there -- no recollection of actually walking from Point A to Point B -- but it was usual for him to have no ideas about much of anything these days. Outside he remained a strong, stern giant. Inside he was a zombie. Another nameless, figuratively faceless intern told Oso to have a seat and the frumpy woman returned, almost too immediately, applying more foundation to his face. The Bear’s eyes shot to a well dressed man with a wide smile. He was walking over to him. Redemption was in the air. The look on Oso Bannon’s face changed dramatically. Blankness replaced with a fierce determination. Chester had been pounding the tremendous importance of this day into his head for days, rehearsing how it might go for hours on end. The time for rehearsal was through, the time for action had arrived. The well dressed men took a seat as a nervous woman came over to attach microphones to his and Oso’s collars. “Okay,” she said quietly, “You’re all set. If you two could please have some casual conversation so that we can get your levels we’ll get this thing taped.” “No problem!” The well dressed man exclaimed jovially, looking at Oso with the wide smile that had yet to waiver. “So... are you ready to show the world the real sides of The Bear?” Oso replied with a stiff “yes”. He wasn’t much for casual conversation. A shout emerged from behind one of the cameras facing Oso and the well dressed man. “Okay, we’re on in 5...4...3...2...” Cue music and more bright lights. Cue close up on the well dressed man. “Hello, I’m Thomas Wallcott with an ESPN Outside the Lines exclusive! In just four years, Oso Bannon managed to shake up the entire world of college football only to be exiled from it during his senior season. Today, we’ll sit down with him and ask him the questions everyone’s been dying to... why and what now?”
