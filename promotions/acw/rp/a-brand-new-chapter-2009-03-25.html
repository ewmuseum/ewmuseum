A Brand New Chapter

25 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 25, 2009 March 16th, 2009 8:00am EST Montreal, Quebec, Canada Montreal-Trudeau Airport --- "Excuse me, sir?" a young female with curly black hair spoke. Rousing from his coma with a jolt, Marcus Dox nearly fell out of the three seats he was occupying. He had stretched himself out to catch a few extra Z's before his flight back to America, his home. He tried blinking his vision back into focus. He hated falling asleep with his contacts in. He kept forgetting he wore them now. It was only recently that his eye-sight began to fail him. He hated admitting he was getting older and such things were bound to happen. "Sigh," said the man known as Max Danger in the professional wrestling world as he pulled his rewetting drops from his pocket and applied a drop to each eye. "Ahem." Oh, right, that lady was still there. Marcus looked at the woman, who to anyone else would have been quite attractive, but he didn't pay it any attention. She could have been a ten-foot tall, eight-hundred pound whale for all he cared. Xander didn't reply instantly, obviously. Instead he took a moment to really stretch and yawn. There was a slight gasp from the lady. Prosthetic arm. "Oops," was Marcus' reply. "Now who did this belong to again?" He began to look around as if trying to find an unfortunate person with one arm walking around. The woman, at this point, was leaving the premises, changing flights, and getting the hell out of dodge. Marcus chuckled then began to dust off and straighten his navy blue suit. There was a beep from inside the suit and from out of it he pulled his trusty iPhone. He didn't refer to his iPhone as merely a "phone". That was insulting. The "i" clearly meant there was more to the device. Marcus liked to think it stood for "intelligence". He also liked to called it the DangerPhone. Yes, he was a dork. Xander checked the text message: Good morning, sweetheart! It was from his wife. He smiled and sent a text back of his own: Good morning, my love. How are my girls? Marcus had some big news when he got home. He just hoped Jessica was OK with it. He'd come to Montreal for a simple wrestling show to say farewell to All-Star Championship wrestling, a place where he'd captured his first ever World Championship and a place he had thought he'd retired over three years ago. That was before he returned to Just Wrestling last year. He was back in the game. And now, well, he had a contract in his hand for the reboot of ACW. It was the last thing he'd expected to happen, but he was so overcome with joy from the event, well, he almost signed right there in front of SilverHAWK and the Roy Boys on the spot. But it was right before he put pen to paper that his wife had sent the text "How's everything going? We miss you!" He handed the pen back to the men who brought the promotion back from the grave and said, "Give me a few days." They agreed. They wanted someone of Max Danger's caliber on their roster. Who wouldn't? Though they were a little more than concerned about his potential commitment problem. Though since he'd started working in Just Wrestling, he'd never missed a date, and seemed to be putting a lot of effort into things, so much so that he ended up winning it's Championship. But this was a guy who was notorious for showing up in whatever city he was working the day of the event and leaving, if not the very same night, but early the next morning. He didn't have friends he hung out with. He kept to himself. Was always on his iPhone (and before that, just his regular old prehistoric cellphone), talking to his wife, or texting her, or looking at pictures, or playing games, or browsing the internet. At the event last night, well, he only begrudgingly hugged Kelly Flawless. He didn't know the man. The closest person he might have had as a friend in the entire promotion was that dang prosthetic arm he carried around as a trophy from Just that he'd stolen off of Brock Shepherd the very night he won their title. If those in charge were worried about it, what do you think was going through the Danger Man's head? Well, in truth, he wasn't concerned about it in the least. Point in fact, he felt more at ease than ever before because he had friends. They just weren't in the locker room. They were in the stands last night. They were standing out in the cool night watching on a big screen. His friends were the fans. Crazy, right? They loathed the King of Submission. They'd rather stab him in the face with a soldering iron than shake his hand. But Max wasn't looking for cheers and adulation. He wanted the people to hate him. To boo him. To despise him. It's the persona he wanted them to know. But everything he did, every bump he took, every lump, every time he was laughed at, made to run with his tail tucked between his legs, he did for those fans. It meant more to him that they wanted to see him lose and be humiliated than it did for them to want to see him win. But the winning? That was nice, too. It made them hate him even more, which made him even happier. Andy Sharp. Seymour Almasy. Alias. They all craved the fan's admiration. They clamored for their cheers. Their pats on the back. Everything they did, their body sacrifices, they did to be loved. To be looked at as heroes. But they would be nothing without someone to overcome. That was Max's thankless job. And he cherished every moment of it. Max could trick other tricksters all day long. But the "good guys", they'd bore everyone to tears against other "good guys". Who would care? It'd be just two guys rolling around in their underpants. But with someone like Danger opposite them? That's exactly how heroes were made. We're doing well. Just waiting for you to get home. :) Marcus checked his iPhone for a new text message. He replied, I'll be there as soon as I can. Also, I have some news...
