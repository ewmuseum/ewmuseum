The Fall (part one)

3 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 03, 2010 2007, 3 weeks after the LoCDown match in LoC He’d been out for awhile now, lucky to be alive they said. His mother and father constantly keeping a vigil over him, he was also lucky to have loved ones. There’d been a few other visitors, mostly his co-workers coming to whisper encouragement in his ear. But, the likelihood of him remembering those visits was as slim as a slice. He was one of the wrestlers you heard about, one who loved the sport so much that he’d put his body on the line every time he stepped into the ring. When it was attempted to put regulations on certain moves, he was the guy that would pay the fine simply for the sake of stealing the fans’ collective breath from them. But, at LoCDown, he’d paid the ultimate fine. People might look back and say, ‘he never should have attempted it.’ Well, maybe, but let’s be serious about who we’re talking about here! Besides that, he’d not only attempted the move multiple times, he’d landed it multiple times when he was training for LoCDown. He’d practiced it time and time again. But then, he’d not taken into account the fact that his palms happened to sweat just a bit more when he was performing in front of 20,000 fans instead of 3 trainers. So yeah, maybe he shouldn’t have attempted it. Ever. But could imagine if he’d gone on to land the Suspended Shooting Star Press? Can you even imagine? Whether or not he would have gone onto win the match (he would have) is beside the point. It would have been the most magnificent move of all time! But, instead, he’d slipped, plummeting down so many feet, only to land, with a snap, back-first on the ropes. Three weeks later, he was still comatose in a hospital bed in the Florida Hospital of Orlando. The prognosis? Broken thoracic vertebrae as well as a nearly lacerated spinal cord. The prognosis? The kid was in bad shape. But he didn’t know that. At least not until now. Billows opened his eyes, blinking furiously, seeing the world for the first time in weeks, but seeing it through kaleidoscope lenses. He continued to blink, calling out in a raspy and thirsty voice, “Hello?” Wherever he was, he was by himself at the moment. It was so bright, and he was so thirsty. Hopefully there was a glass of water next to him. He went to lean over and check. He attempted again to lean over and check. Something was seriously not right here. Billows blinked more, “HELLO?” That’s when he heard the steady beeping of a machine to his left, steady and increasing at the moment. Billows continued to try and shift, but he couldn’t even move his head. A thousand pounds of pressure rested upon his entire body, and yet he couldn’t feel the pressure at all. BEEP BEEP BEEP The room was finally aligning itself, and Billows began to recognize it for what it was. The pale white lights, the cheap television in the corner, the tubes that were within his line of sight. The smell. He’d always imagined that purgatory, if such a thing existed, shared this same smell. The general aura. He was in a hospital. That’s when Billows remembered LoCDown. The fall. “HELLO?! HELP! SOMEBODY!” BEEPBEEPBEEP He finally managed to raise his voice to something more than a strained whisper. That was when his father emerged from his blindside, “Oh my god, Ryan! I’m here. NURSE! NURSEEEE!” As Ray Billows gently placed his hand upon Ryan’s left hand, Ryan’s mother burst into the room, dropping a cup of coffee in her rush over to him. She instinctively reached out for his head until Ray gently stopped her, “Can’t do anything like that, M.” Martha Billows sobbed but quickly grabbed her son’s other hand, and brought her face down to it, “Baby, babybabybaby, you’re awake. Oh thank you God. Thank you…” With that, Martha was reduced to a blubbering puddle of maternal love. Billows was relieved that his parents were there, but no amount of relief was enough given his current state. He took a deep breath and moved his eyes from the left to the right, looking from his father to his mother and back again. “I can tell that you’re holding my hands…why can’t I feel it?” Billows questioned. Ray hung his head, and Martha only sobbed harder. Billows bit his lip in frustration, clenching his jaw, he raised his voice, “TELL ME WHAT HAPPENED!” As a nurse finally emerged into the room with something to calm his nerves, Ryan’s father said all he needed to hear, “You hurt yourself son. Pretty bad. But we’re going to get through this as a family.” Present Day The sun was hidden behind a row of angry black clouds, and the skies promised to rain sooner than later. He needed to get a move on, Billows did. But he felt like he’d only been here for mere moments as the thunderheads rolled in. Things had started to pick up again, and he rarely got time to visit anymore. As he knelt down, the rain broke through, as if the world were conspiring against his visit. “I love you mom, I miss you, and I’m sorry.” The same sentence he always uttered upon leaving the cemetery. He stood up, wiped a few matted down strands of hair out of his eyes, and pulled his hood over his head as he turned around and made his way to his car. He hated coming here. It reminded him of the fall of his career… And the fall of his family.
