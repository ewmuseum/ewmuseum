The Wright Man - 8

7 May 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07 May 2011 Following an easy afternoon and ‘working’ lunch with Henry Irwonsen, Wright took in the sights of the Eternal City – and the monuments weren’t bad either. Sorry, it had to be done. He headed straight to the Colosseum on the metro and marvelled as he came out of the station of the same name – there it was. One of the world’s most famous landmarks, the symbol of Rome, and it made the big man from Forth Worth feel small even if he couldn’t help himself from muttering: “Lazy bastards couldn’t be bothered to finish it.” Thereafter, a smooth stroll saw him take in the Imperial Forum, ‘The Wedding Cake/Typewriter,’ The Pantheon, Spanish Steps (Piazza Di Spagna) and the impressive and imposing Trevi Fountain. He decided to give The Pope and Vatican City a miss, at least for today, and retired to his hotel next to Termini Station until the next day, which would be his first day ‘at work’ for ACW even if it was just for another brief chat with Henry and the production team. He’d meet the writers when they moved onto Mexico. The next day, he met Henry Irwonsen at Stadio Olimpico, which is where End Game would be held. This was the day before the pay-per-view, 30th December 2010. Tomorrow was not only End Game but the end of the year (Capodanno in Italian) and a part of him was gutted he wasn’t in Oklahoma to see his son, whom he’d spent Christmas Day with, but he’d have to get used to it plus he was doing this for him too. Hank was no longer a puppy. He’d wrestled for two decades and while he’d earned decent money, he’d squandered some and had never excelled in a significant American promotion. The nearest he’d come to hitting the jackpot was a 3-year period in Japan where he was regularly in the top 3 foreign workers but he gave it up when on the cusp of stardom because he’d grown tired of Tokyo and yearned for Texas. He shook hands with Henry, who introduced him to a group of ACW production boffins who made the sophisticated and complex process look like a breeze. Wright respected them for that because while he knew they couldn’t get in there and do what he did on a nightly basis, they actually had the ability to make him look better or worse with a mere touch of a button. Irwonsen asked one of the technicians to show Wright what they’d been working on. We see a small, intimate building that’s bouncing when a camera zooms into capture the image of a blonde long-haired, 6’6" stud waggling his fingers, infuriating the audience and telling them to bring it on. The volume is deafening as the fans chant: “GO HOME YANKEE!” repeatedly. A huge grin came over Hank’s face as this tale started. Bill Jameson, a thirty-something only labelled as a Mexican wrestling expert, says “Worker has two meanings in wrestling if you ask me: One is the ability to work a match. One is the ability to work a crowd. Good wrestlers can usually do one or the other. Great wrestlers can do both. Hank Wright was GREAT in Mexico.” Hank is shown in the centre of the ring, holding his hands in front of his face to avoid a shower of coins. At one stage, he picks one up and throws it back into the front row, which receives serious heel heat and you can see the security get pushed back for a split-second as they struggle to contain the irate fans at ringside. Hank covered his mouth, laughing but also a little embarrassed, though he needn’t have been. He’d provided memorable footage in his Mexican adventure and it was getting put to excellent use here. Irwonsen whispered to him: “Bill was right.” Wright didn’t even have time to thank Henry for his kind words as he saw Juan Alvarez, a Mexican magazine writer, uttered these words in Spanish: “Mexico was voted the most dangerous country in the world in twenty-eleven, did you hear that? Well, when Hank Wright was in the ring in two thousand and seven, it would have taken the second spot too. He was THAT hot. Man, they went crazy.” John Stein, a road agent for the MSLL promotion where Wright worked and one if his closest confidants during his time there, claimed: “There’d be nights where Hank couldn’t go on last because they’d be waiting for him outside of the arena so we’d try and put him on in the middle of the show. He still had to leave by the back door. We couldn’t take any chances.” The Tank, a brute of a man standing at 6’6”, was biting his bottom lip, quivering at this point, battling, doing everything in his power not to break down into tears. Stein, who’d also aided Wright in securing a return to ACW, meant an awful lot to the Texan and had stuck up for him so many times in Mexico during political issues with fellow heel, Marcos Marquez. Both men would go to war for the other and John had shown it twice in such a short space of time. Wright loved him for it. “Soppy old bastard,” he proclaimed, wiping his cheek as the production crew chuckled. They knew he was referring to Stein but the irony was he could have been talking about himself. Wright is beating up a wrestler, raining down punches while grabbing his t-shirt with the other, turning him into a bloody mess. Thereafter, Hank takes out a bar of soap from out of his trunks, spits on it and then rubs it into his opponent’s face, smearing his blood in the process too. Stein continued his appraisal: “Hank was perfect for Mexico, where the fans believed everything he did and they rekindled his passion. He loved going out there and being the man they loved to hate. And they did hate him.” Hank shook his head and then his eyes lit up. Antonio Martinez, the booker of MSLL, wore a smile as he spoke about his ex-superstar: “Mexicans are really passionate about their country and sports in general. Hank attacked their nationality every chance he got. He used every stereotype under the sun but you believed he meant it and they bought it. They came to the shows to watch him get lynched. He reenergized our business.” Hank’s on the mike: “We got a thing the States called hygiene. You oughtta try it.” Everyone laughed at that line. Irwonsen added to it: “I LOVE that.” In another video, Hank is depicted telling a Mexican man that he can’t satisfy his woman, who’s next to her husband in a stunning red dress. In front of him, Wright grabs the maiden and plants a figure-four liplock on her. Before she can respond, the fan’s flailing arms start scolding Hank’s shoulders and neck area. In the end, security steps in to restrain the tormented soul while Wright urges his wife to ‘call him.’ Wright rolled his eyes at that and held his hands up: “Sorry, kids.” Humour was soon substituted by raw emotion even if his outburst of ‘the bastard’ made everyone in the truck smirk. Former ACW Scorpion Fighting Champion, Keegan, who worked with Hank south of the border, commented: “When I and Hank got together, we decided to adapt the motto: Work hard, play hard. He lived that too. He worked as hard as anyone else in the company and certainly played harder than them too. He was in his late-thirties, me in my mid-thirties and we put the young lads to shame. Hank was the star attraction in the ring and in the bar. You couldn’t beat him.” The Governor applauded Special K without making a noise and whispered to himself: “God bless ya Keegan.” Hank looks comfortable with a microphone in hand: “Why aren’t Mexicans in hell? They jumped the border.” Wright was proud of himself as rubbish was chucked into the squared circle. Boos echoed around the arena. In 2011, both Hank Wright and ACW head to Mexico. Wrong place, wrong time for Wright to return? Vamos a ver. Wright shook his head in disbelief, his thumb permanently attached to his mouth like a newborn baby, before he started nodding, even more so when Irwonsen asked him what he thought. He then changed stance and put his hands in his pockets: “Gentlemen, thank you so much for making an old dog like me feel like a million buck. That was unbelievable. I don’t know what to say. Thank you so much.” Henry told him it would be his debut promo the next night live on pay-per-view and Hank shook hands with the ACW referee and clutched his arm tight: “Thank you Henry. Seriously. For everything.” Before leaving, Hank bowed down to the crew who’d put the touching tribute together for him and he told them all if returning to Mexico wasn’t enough, the hype video had blown him away. As he wandered outside, he would’ve had a cigarette if he’d had one but he’d stopped that a long time ago, particularly as he got older, neglecting weight-lifting and putting every ounce of energy he had into his cardiovascular training, paying the price to be The Hurricane inside and outside of the ring. Instead… He’d have to make do with a tissue.
