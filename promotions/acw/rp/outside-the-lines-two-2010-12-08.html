Outside the Lines -- Two

8 December 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08 December 2010 “It’s a far out thought to assume that anyone watching right now doesn’t already know your story, Oso, but just in case I’d like to start from the beginning.” Thomas Wallcott’s voice projected confidently. Somehow he managed to still maintain his shit-eating grin. “You were born in Mexico City...” Oso sensed that the interview might already be taking a turn down a route he didn’t want to travel on. If people wanted real dirt about his past it wasn’t hard to find, whether overexaggerated or not, on the internet. He felt the urge to interrupt and did. “...Where I spent close to fifteen years.” His voice was deep and raspy this morning, indicative of a man nicknamed The Bear. “It was and still is a mess down there. I thank all things holy for my adopted father for getting me out of there and giving me a positive focus for my life.” Wallcott’s wide, toothy smile morphed into an opportunistic smirk. “But how positive was it really, assuming you’re talking about football? You’ve gone on record saying that your father, who was your high school coach and also the person who got you your tryout with Ohio State, and his expectations for you were part of the reason you turned to the performance enhancers.” Oso sighed deeply and cleared his throat. He had never liked doing interviews. “Those were the words of an angry, misguided boy who wanted to blame everyone else for his problems. It’s almost been a year now and what you see before you is a man willing to accept and take responsibility for his mistakes. I took the enhancers because I felt like I needed to.” “How about the painkillers? The oxycontin? Or how about the cocaine?” Thomas prodded. “Did you need to take those too?” The question hit Oso Bannon like a ton of bricks. Chester had said this interview was organized to help Oso redeem himself in the public’s eye. At that moment it seemed like nothing more than a stepping stone for Thomas Walcott to make the jump from sports reporting to the anchor desk on the national news. And The Bear thought to itself: I should have stayed in hibernation. The seconds felt like minutes. He knew he couldn’t sit in silence much longer. He’d simply have to regain his composure. “Look, Thomas...” Bannon mused. “I came here today not to hear a laundry list of what I’ve done, but to announce to this country and to anyone watching around the world that I am finally moving past it. Everyone has made mistakes. Some much more terrible than mine...” He thought about his mother running drugs through the streets of Mexico City. He reflected on her deteriorating nostrils and how they felt against his cheek when she hugged him. He remembered the day he realized she was an addict and the day she was shot in the face by her superiors in the cartel, making it absolutely sure that he’d never be able to help her fight off her demons. He recalled the vow he made to himself that he would always fight off his. “...Those people had the good fortune of not being exposed in the public eye. Away from the bloodsucking media, the scouts, and the blogs. I didn’t have that luxury. I fell hard while everyone watched and the only people who gave a damn were the federal agents who needed me stable to testify against my dealers. I paid my penance. All I can do is apologize to anyone I might have let down and hope that they’ll give me another chance.” For the first time in four hours, Thomas Walcott was not smiling, a look of shock at Oso’s blatant display of honesty perched upon his tanned face instead. He had intended to keep badgering The Bear, but could now seem to only muster up one last question. “So where is Oso Bannon going from here?” Bannon chuckled. It was the first time he’d laughed genuinely in months. His mind wandered from countless quarterbacks he’d sacked, to awards he’d won, to needles he’d put in his arm, and finally to bumps he’d taken last October during his training with Kory Storm. He knew exactly where he was headed. “...Back to the top, baby. Back to the top.” A lone, slow applause emerged from the dark behind the cameramen and crew. Fade out.
