All-Star Championship Wrestling And so, we kick off 2005 with the promise of change.

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
All-Star Championship Wrestling And so, we kick off 2005 with the promise of change. Change from the turbulence of 2004. ACW is no stranger to instability, but the amount of tumultuous situations the company had been plunged into last year alone was quite ridiculous. It all started so rosy, too. 2004 was heralded as the year of revival, and for a while there, ACW was firing on all cylinders. And then, slowly, the same old problems crept back in: the revolving door of roster talent, issues with the network, and unforeseen circumstances with the all-around management of the company. The latter two have been very evident, in fact, over the course of the last three months. Think back to the last ACW PPV. Struggling? That's because RELENTESS took place at the tailend of September last year. What is it now? The middle of January, 2005. Four months between PPVs, and with six shows in between? That tells it all, doesn't it? For what it's worth, the highly-paid bigwigs over at the headquarters of *SHOWTIME* are to blame. These are the people that stuck with the raunchy and controversial programming that thReat used to offer. Yet, *SHOWTIME* was ready to bail when the attendances were dipping and ratings for ACW's COURAGE~ were starting to dip. For most of the year, the ratings were quite impressive, despite the (sometimes) shoddy production of the show. But at the first sign of frailty & debility of the ACW product, *SHOWTIME* executives got their collective panties' into a bunch and decided to take action. The result? Only two telecasts of COURAGE~ in October. This was then followed by another two telecasts of COURAGE~ during two months. You read correctly. Oh, sure, we all also got to see ACW's Best of 2004 'review show' and the Awards 2004 'gala event'. As entertaining as those two events were, it just goes to show that the ACW product alone cannot be blamed. And to their credit, the ACW talent and management have been getting back on track after their dip. During their struggles with *SHOWTIME*, ACW resorted to holding several 'house shows' at really small arenas, just for the sake of progressing their product. Guess what? The matches were top-drawer. That's what wrestling is really all about, isn't it? The actual wrestling. And with the first ACW event of 2005 in the books, I can safely say that this company is really back on the road to big things. Okay, there are still some aspects to be worked upon. Given a couple more months to work out all the kinks, though, I'm certain that everybody on the payroll will be putting their best foot forward. You want to know why? Simple. They have the passion. The desire. The hunger. ACW in 2005? Mark my words -- the company is going to reach new heights, and set the standard for everyone else. Forget all the other staleness you're watching elsewhere. ACW is wrestling's salvation.
