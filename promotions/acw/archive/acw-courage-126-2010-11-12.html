A C W THE EMPIRE STRIKES BACK COURAGE 126 ¤ 12 NOVEMBER 2010 ADRIATIC ARENA, PESARO, ITALY INTRODUCTION PRE-SHOW ¦ HOUR ONE ¦ HOUR TWO POST-SHOW Introduction It's safe to say that after the events of IN ACROPOLIS 2010, Greece will never forget ACW... and will probably never welcome ACW back to any of its many islands. On the same note, nobody will also forget the events that transpired on that night: Keith Scott Zimmerman finally defeated Trey Vincent and put to rest the latter's hope of regaining the World Championship. Jade Argent and Khristain Keller? Nobody knows what happened to them. Which means, there's no #1 Contender for the World Title. Yet. Amongst the other highlights? God's Forgotten Son claimed the Spirit Of ACW Championship, Tyson XL nicked the [Black] Scorpion Title, Z bested Rud Albion in an epic encounter, The Night Life dethroned The Entourage to become new Tag Team Champions, Alias beat Jesse Ramey to lay claim to the King's Choice... and hell, so much more. Now, then. The countdown to END GAME 2010 has begun. Just what will unfurl as we approach the climax of ACW's tour across Europe? And when exactly will Jeremy Hunt and Z unleash their dastardly masterplans, both of which have given SilverHAWK nightmares? Let's find out together. Next: PRE-SHOW. ---- IN THE DARK~! IN THE DARK #1: The Payne Gang vs. The SOBs The first of two tag team matches -– ALL BEFORE TONIGHT'S HOTLY ANTICIPATED BROADCAST OF COURAGE 126 WENT LIVE –- was about to begin, and could be considered a grudge match left from IN ACROPOLIS 2010. The Payne Gang and The SOBs both could claim to have had the match won towards the climax of the chaotic battle, but in the end Abilities prevailed; and through shady means, too. That made them golden in the eyes of Jeremy Hunt, by the way. The SOBs and The Payne Gang felt nothing was settled on that cold night in Athens, so here we were. A rematch of sorts, sans the newly-minted cronies of Jeremy Hunt. “Jump” by House of Pain hit the speakers as both Sheamus and Selby O'Brien made their way out. The scuttlebutt around the locker-room was that Sheamus was less than happy with his brother, Selby, for being the one that ate the fall causing his team to lose. Selby was uncharacteristically solemn, feeling less than energetic with Sheamus being pissed at him, but he hoped a victory tonight would turn their luck around. The reception for the brothers had soured as of late. “Deliverance” by Corrosion of Conformity hit the speakers as the fans gave a good ovation to the fighting brothers from Kentucky (USA -- where the chicken is awesome), The Payne Gang. Kassidy's and KC Payne's bond appeared quite stronger than that of their opponents, the two remaining in a good mood despite wanting to give a nice receipt to Abilities. KC and Selby O'Brien would start things off for each team as they shook hands, despite Sheamus' initial protests to sportsmanship. KC and Selby started off the action fast and furious, Selby running circles around the older of the Payne brothers for a few moments. KC put a stop to that shit about two minutes in with a twist-around into a harsh swinging neckbreaker. A tag to Kassidy and the big man went to work, bowling over Selby with a couple of high-impact shoulder blocks. Sheamus bit his lip as he watched Selby get decimated by a couple of quick tags and tandem moves between the Payne Gang, including a sick double three-point tackle and a double Hip Toss that sent Selby nearly flying all the way across the ring. During an exchange where KC went low, Sheamus distracted him. This allowed Selby to take a rather uncharacteristic cheap shot to get the upper hand for the SOBs. With the match finally in their control, the Irish twins showed off some great tandem moves of their own, showing why they were considered two of the All-Star Academy's original group of break-out stars. A catapault into the ropes from Sheamus followed by a slingshot dropkick from Selby put KC through the ropes and to the floor. The double-teams continued for the next several minutes as Selby dazzled KC with a couple of big high-flying moves including a big cross body from the top. Sheamus took the road less traveled, getting cheap shots and chokes with the tag rope when available. Kassidy had seen enough and rallied the fans to give KC the energy to fight back. KC eventually dodge a running splash from Sheamus and tried to make the tag to his younger, tank-like brother. Sheamus stopped him, but found himself getting kicked away. The complexion of the match changed completely when Kassidy sprung into action with an IMPRESSIVE slingshot shoulder block to double over Sheamus. Kassidy Payne continued his onslaught, bumrushing him with clothesilnes, shoulder blocks, then finally a big double choke overhead suplex that got a close fall. Selby tried to stop Kassidy, but he ate a running headbutt to the chest that knocked him clear out of the ring. KC got back into the action and tackled Selby, taking both of them out of the ring, leaving the legal men. Sheamus reversed a big slam from Kassidy with the timely eyepoke. Sheamus then went to the apron and landed a perfect springboard dropkick to the back of the big man's head that got a close fall. Grabbing him by the head, he went for a Tornado DDT off the ropes, but Kassidy's uncanny strength let him hold on, putting Sheamus back to his feet. A kick to the gut lead to the Great Payne (a variatation of the Styles Clash)! A three-count later and The Payne Gang were the winners. Kassidy and KC celebrated like it was 2007 (?) as the fans gave them a nice ovation. They mouthed to the cameras that payback was on their mind and eventually, they'd tussle with Abilities again. Meanwhile, Sheamus and Selby looked dejected with another loss. Sheamus left his brother high and dry, blaming him for not being there to protect him. About a month ago, The SOBs had seemingly turned a corner and were looking to embark on a winning streak. Yet, here they were weeks later; freezing their arses off in Italy, and back at square one. Oh boy. IN THE DARK #2: Big E. Smalls vs. Das Effiziente Reich The second of two important tag team match-ups was about to begin. Mach 2 took a year off from ACW and made his official return at IN ACROPOLIS 2010, defeating Aleczander and spoiling the reality star's ACW debut. But tonight, he and his tag team partner/student, Big E. Stark, were facing a tall (pun intended) order against the team of Ulf German and Der Deutscher Soldat. While the Reich had yet to score many major victories, people noted an improvement in their ring game. The chance to break through was now. Ulf German and DDS got no love from the Italian fanbase as they wore matching vests with a German flag design and even waved their country's flag proudly. Ulf and DDS talked strategery in their bid to win against their opposition. Next, Mach 2 and Big E. Stark, followed by the lovely (DAT AZZ~!) Leticia 'Lettie' Rios, the high-flying trio made their way to the ring. Mach and Big E. planned as well in this battle of power against speed (and a little bit of power Big E. brought). The action started off a little bit slower than the last match with Big E. Stark and Ulf German sizing each other up. Ulf tried to power down Big E., but the rookie showed some impressive skill in being able to toss around Ulf a bit. He dazzled him with a couple of throws, followed by a very EFFECTIVE Dropkick from the three-hundred pounder. Ulf tagged in DDS as Big E. tagged in Mach 2. Mach and Big E. blitzed the big man with a couple different double-team moves, mainly Big E. using his mentor like a projectile before dumping him over the ropes with a clothesline. Eventually, some chicanery (mainly DDS swatting Mach 2 out of the sky) gained the advantage for the Reich. From there, fans got a little bored as the next five minutes were spent exchanging painful-looking restholds and lumbering offense between Ulf and Soldat. Eventually, Mach 2 slipped between the legs of Ulf and caught him with a springboard gamengiri kick to the head. Tag to Big E. Stark! Stark got into the match, actually HITTING a big Cross Body off the top rope onto DDS! After rolling off him, he hit Ulf several times with some alternating jabs, followed by a big Bionic Elbow. Stunned, he was whipped off the ropes and slammed into the canvas with a powerslam for a close two-count. DDS tried to break up the skirmish, but Mach 2 came back into the fray and hit a big flying Pump Dropkick to the head. DDS was rocked, so Big E. Stark shot him to the outside with a clothesline. A powerbomb by Big E. Stark put Ulf back down on the canvas as Mach 2 got tagged. THE SPEED OF SOUND. The same corkscreew shooting star press that had spelled the end for Aleczander also hit tonight. But he wasn't done. He tagged in Big E. Stark. Mach 2 dropkicked DDS in the leg, sending him off the apron. Big E. got some mad pops from the crowd as he smirked... BIG AZZ SPLASH! The Superfly-esque splash lacked the grace and precision that Mach 2 had, but it didn't matter. It was enough for the team to get the three and the important first win as a tag team. As for Ulf and DDS? Another bitter loss to deal with. Things weren't looking good for the Germans. IN THE DARK #3: Orlando Grant vs. 'Zen-Zational' Zhang Tao vs. Antwone MacDaniels A couple of weeks ago at IN ACROPOLIS 2010, Zhang Tao and Antwone MacDaniels had a rather unique opportunity to compete against Carrachio Salfuego and Fight Man for the chance to become the new #1 Contender to the [Black] Scorpion Championship. Shenanigans involving Arkady prevented aforementioned match from even taking place. That didn't stop Zhang Tao and Antwone MacDaniels from demanding for a rematch. This wasn't exactly it, but both men looked thrilled nonetheless to be given the opportunity to compete in front of a capacity crowd. Orlando Grant, on the other hand, didn't look all too pleased. Perhaps it was because of the fact that he didn't feature at all at IN ACROPOLIS 2010, and had to settle for competing in a pre-broadcast match. Still, Orlando kept a tight smile on his face as he easily went to down on Antwone and Zhang Tao. The fans were excited to see Orlando's barrage of unique offense, but eventually Grant's luck ran out; a running turnbuckle splash aimed at a prone Antwone failed (thanks primarily to Zhang Tao pulled Antwone out of dodge), and the Beijing Zen Master very nearly capitalised with a missle dropkick off the top of the turnbuckle! Thankfully for the crowd, the OG of ACW managed to kick out. And what happened to Antwone during the ensuing period of domination from Zhang Tao? Well, he'd decided to ask fans in the front rows if they had any PCP or crack to spare since he was fresh out. Charming. At any rate, Orlando Grant was able to turn back Zhang Tao's offense and dropped him with the Big Shit Poppin', just as Antwone -- empty-handed and sad-faced -- climbed back into the ring. He too got a taste of Orlando's Big Shit Poppin', and the Ziploc Boy proceeded to pin both of his opponents for the win. After the match, Orlando warmly celebrated with the fans, but it was clear that the former United States Champion was feeling rather dejected that he wasn't going to be featured on the live broadcast. Antwone, meanwhile, stumbled out of the ring and once more began bugging the fans seated in the front rows for some crack. Awwwkward. IN THE DARK #4: Rory Hayes vs. MIS vs. Valley Baptiste Ever since their legendary battles in the All-Star Academy in the year, MIS and Valley Baptiste were embraced into ACW officially and big things were expected of them. To say that they've not been able to live up to those expectations would be somewhat accurate. Still, many people in attendance figured that this match presented both men with a very good chance of winning. You know, seeing how the third man in the equation was none other than Rory Hayes. But Hayes was a very different man. He'd won his first singles match of the year just weeks ago at IN ACROPOLIS 2010, exiling 'Vicious' Greg Miles from ACW in the process. Hayes was now also a member of the cabal, which was probably why the likes of Iceman and Malk Al-Haq were at ringside for this match. The Boston Mangler was immediately overwhelmed by the combined efforts of MIS and Valley in the early moments of this Triple Threat Match, but Rory very easily chopped both of his opponents down and introduced them to the harsh reality: he wasn't a pushover any longer. Hayes wasn't the biggest joke in all of ACW any more. And he proved it in sensational fashion, crushing both MIS and Valley like they were nothing. The crowd were stunned. Sure, they'd had that Rory was once upon a time a competent wrestler and an accomplished brawler... but he hadn't been able to showcase those talents in the longest of times. Well, tonight appeared to be the beginning of a fresh start for The Masshole. With Iceman and Malk watching on, Hayes completely laid waste to MIS and Baptiste. He even saw it fit to drop Valley with his Boston Massacre finisher, before proceeding to lay MIS out with his Curbstomp trademark that he hadn't busted out in god-knows-when. An emphatic victory for Rory Hayes, who celebrated smugly. Newfound confidence for a man that was once described as the biggest waste of space on ACW's roster. Those days, it would seem, were firmly done and over with. IN THE DARK #5: Quintana Rogerio Santiago vs. Rokko Strange vs. Koji Takada vs. Temperance Hellas vs. JP Boudreau vs. 'The Natural' Nathan Adams And finally, the last treat for the fans before COURAGE 126 went live came in the form of a barnstormer of a match. Six different individuals with distinct styles, all gunning to see who would be to secure a pinfall or submission decision. With all six of them in the ring at the same time, it was only a matter of time before all hell truly broke loose. Rokko Strange and Temperance Hellas concentrated on bashing the heck out of each other, while QRS and JP Boudreau teamed up to combat Koji Takada. Nathan Adams, dubbed the 'Rory Hayes of the All-Star Academy' found himself left out of the proceedings. Which actually meant he had the opportunity to pick his spot. Unfortunately, Nathan's vision of picking his spot saw him fail at a double clothesline attempt to QRS and JP Boudreau. For his troubles, Nathan got double-suplexed. JP Boudreau was then taken down a notch by Koji Takada, while Temperance Hellas springboard cross-body block missed Rokko Strange completely and saw her collide into Quintana. Who, rather awesomely, rolled through the cross-body block and planted the Greek female warrior with a fallaway slam. With the bridge. For the win. In a blink, the match ended as frenziedly as it had begun, and Quintana Rogerio Santiago celebrated coming out on top in a match where literally anybody could have triumphed. After the match, Koji Takada continued to wail on JP Boudreau, blaming the French-Canadian for not being able to win the match. Boudreau tried to fight back, but 'The Natural' Nathan Adams decided to back Koji up. That was, until Quintana came to JP's rescue and chased off the twosome of Takada and Nathan Adams. QRS and JP Boudreau then bumped fists, much to the delight of the capacity crowd. Next: HOUR ONE. ---- Goodbye Again, The Return to an Uncomfortable Throne IN ACROPOLIS 2010 had been...something. Something awesome. Something wicked. Something of devastation. Something that broke regulation. ...something that would see one and all tune in, tonight, to SlySports. Here we were, being presented with another edition of COURAGE, and these PPV follow-ups were always something to write home about. IN ACROPOLIS had witnessed some vendettas laid to slumber, but there were always those men of pride who refused to leave well enough alone. In this sport, there were those who knew when to hold em' and when to fold em', and there were those that would continue betting with a losing hand simply because they held out hope that whatever they held up their sleeve would award them the last laugh. Every once in awhile, though, ace in the sleeve or not...the betting man is forced to retire his hand. This was one such instance. The crowd held no idea how things would kick off tonight, but they were quickly given their cue when “April Suits” by Taproot kicked up over the speakers. “How could you abandon me, I thought you'd always be, There for me, standing tall, one and one for all, My support modeling, rolling as a team But instead on our own, enemies of old” They hadn't been expecting to see Billows this evening. See, at IN ACROPOLIS, he'd finally succeeded in his goal of getting underneath the skin of one Midnight Cowboy. It had been a chaotic night for the two men, but the Smooth Operator had come out on top, sending Billows plummeting from the top of a semi-truck's trailer to the cold cement below. Billows had left on a stretcher. But there was his music, proving that the constant gnat, the malicious mosquito was still a-buzzing, still there to remind them just how much of a nuisance he could be when he put his mind-- Oh shit. The boos halted all at once, simultaneously. And all negative thoughts that had been given were quickly recanted. Billows was coming out alright... But he wasn't walking out. The Risen Lion, a man who had spent nearly three years in personal therapy of all sorts to rescue himself from the imprisonment that only a quadriplegic knows...a man who had survived where other men would have waved a white flag... That man was making his way out from the back using air. A straw fixed to his mouth, the tube running through God only knows what kind of mechanisms to the structure that Billows sat upon. A wheelchair. An air-powered wheelchair. What had been a sea of booing fans was now a collection of men with jaws agape and gooseflesh rising. Women holding hands to their heart, tears welling in their caring eyes. Children not quite grasping the severity of the situation, but knowing it was bad enough. Billows, with a bandaged head that was obviously the least noticeable injury, took another deep breath and exhaled twice into the tube, signaling it to turn right. He moved a few paces via his exhaled breath, then signaled it to turn facing the crowd. He let the straw fall from his mouth and quickly grimaced, “Cut the music, and...have somebody ready to come stick that thing back in my mouth when I'm finished here.” Billows was solemn, but managed a smile, reflecting on what he'd just said, “That...uh...That's what she said.” The crowd wasn't laughing though, and if Billows could have nodded in understanding, he would have. His music died down, and never had an arena been as silent than it was then. For three years, Billows had been on the outside of the sport, looking in—this was the reason one of his monikers was The Watcher. The fans knew that, and they feared that tonight was him doing all he could to ride off into the sunset with dignity, to be a Watcher once more. “The production boys gave me this fancy headset...I guess Jenna knew her arm would be tired from holding the mic up by the time I was finished here...heh.” Billows realized he wouldn't be able to make light of his current situation, “I...I...really...don't know where to start with this. I'm generally pretty good with my words, at expressing my feelings, but there's a lot in me right now, and I'm fearful that it's just going to kind of come pouring out. And I suppose that's for the best, because tonight, for me, is a night to be honest with you.” Billows could only stare straight ahead, but every fan in attendance realized he was talking to them. “You know, as much as I'd like to stand up right now and race down to that ring, I can't. Even worse, though, I can't turn my head to look as many of you as possible directly in the eye and tell you...it was all bullshit. It was a fun character to play, that bastard Billows that you all grew to hate as much as you'd loved me, and I think I did good being the bad guy...but it was all bullshit. This sport means the world to me, and the career of providing you fans with something entertaining...that career is the love of my life.” Billows inhaled sharply, “Well, it was the love of my life. It's, uh, gonna be awhile before I get to step in a ring again. A long while. ...For...forever if there's no wrestling rings in Heaven,” Billows offered a sheepish smile, “Apparently all the rehabbing I did in the three years I was recovering didn't see a 12 foot drop from the top of trailer coming...or at least not the way I landed it. I landed it wrong, fucking...lost my cool for a split-second... Look, Long story short, some...some shit got jostled. I landed wrong...and...I more or less re-broke my back.” The crowd murmured, their worst fears being confirmed. “The doc's are pretty pissed I'm here tonight, they're cutting me open tomorrow to try and restore some movement in my hands, maybe my arms if I'm lucky...but they didn't sugarcoat anything this time. There is ZERO chance that I will ever walk again.” Billows swallowed hard, “I fought my way out of this jam once before, and it was the hardest thing I've ever done in my life, and that was with very low odds of me ever walking again...but now I've been given no odds...and...” Billows took a deep breath, closing his eyes, fighting back tears. “I'm not out here to focus on that though, I only touched down on that so that you all know what's going on. I'm uh...I'm out here to say goodbye. I feel like this is a chance I never got way back during my first injury. I spent a long time in a coma, lucky for me there was no head trauma involved this time and I could argue my way out of the hospital bed to get here tonight, cause I'll be damned if I have to spend the next 40 or 50 or 60, however many years in this goddamned contraption without the memory of getting to say farewell to the people who were on this rollercoaster with me.” Some fans were openly crying now, not ready for such an emotional beginning to COURAGE, not ready to say goodbye to a man who had literally laid his body on the line for them. “I won some, I lost some. I was good, I was bad. But no matter what, I did everything I could do, every time I was in that ring, to make sure that you all felt like you'd spent your hard-earned money right. I slept well at night because I would see when you all shot out of your chairs in sheer amazement. I was every promoter's dream because they'd see when you came up to me after a show just to tell me, 'Billows, I've never seen anything like that, I can't wait til you all are back in town so I can bring a friend.' And I'll never forget when one of the boys walked up to me in the back and pulled me aside and told me, point blank, 'Thanks kid, my son hasn't watched me in 6 years, and he watched me tonight because he wanted to see his dad face Billows. My son thinks I'm cool again because of you.'” Now Billows was tearing up. “I lived a dream, folks, and you all were such a huge part of it. A thousand 'thank you's' don't even begin to cover how grateful I am. In the grand scheme of things, I might not have changed lives, but you changed mine.” THANK YOU BILL-OWS THANK YOU BILL-OWS THANK YOU BILL-OWS The crowd was letting Billows know, he was more than appreciated. A single tear streaked down Billows cheek as he swallowed hard, smiling, lips quivering under the stress of trying to keep some form of composure, “But sometimes you roll the dice, and the dice don't always come up in your favor. IN ACROPOLIS was one of those times for me, and I'm paying for it pretty big now...” The crowd grew hush as Billows grew silence. He had company. Midnight Cowboy came stepping through the curtain, his music hadn't hit out of respect for the moment. Some of the crowd cheered, but many members of the crowd were more concerned with hearing what Billows had to say, wondering just what Midnight Cowboy was doing, face or not, interrupting him. Midnight Cowboy had a microphone, and he quickly took off his Stetson Hat, holding it to his chest as he knelt down in front of Billows, “Kid, t'last thang I aim t'do is interr'up ya, but uh...I done come out here t'seek some absolution, then Ah'll be on mah way.” Cowboy began, “Ah done had mah'self a career that Ah been nothin' but downright proud of, Ah always done the right thang. In Acrop'lis, I put a big black mark 'cross that career by injurin' y'self. S'a moment that'll always stay with me fr'all t'wrong reasons. Ah--” Billows interjected, “Stop, listen.” Midnight Cowboy, who had been hanging his head in shame now stared up at Billows. Billows continued, “You're a Cowboy man, not a goddamn psychic. You couldn't have seen this coming in a thousand years. Neither could I. Do you think if I thought there was any chance of me returning to one of these stupid fucking wheelchairs that I ever would have climbed up onto that truck? No. I wouldn't of. This, this shit, it's just another one of life's woulda-coulda-shoulda's, and sir, it is no fault of your own. I don't blame you in the least, and as a favor to me, Cowboy, I ask that you stand up and leave with the knowledge that this is not your fault. Okay?” The crowd cheered as Midnight Cowboy took a deep breath. He was a rock, a man who very rarely showed any emotion besides being calm and collected, but even Midnight Cowboy was noticeably on the verge of tears. Midnight Cowboy stood up, nodding, and put his hat back on. He turned and took a step towards the back. “And Cowboy,” Billows' words stopped The Smooth Operator in his tracks, “Just know if I could, I would shake your hand right now.” The crowd roared at that as Midnight Cowboy nodded, tipping his hat at The Risen Lion, shocked at how much better he was feeling. The Smooth Operator turned back around and Billows continued, “But since I can't--” L.O.S.T. KICK!!! W T F ? ? The crowd's jaws dropped as Billows stood over Midnight Cowboy, sneering and breathing heavy. Holy shit Billows deserved an Oscar for his performance, such a performance that even as Billows delivered the boots to Cowboy, the crowd was still too shocked to boo. Billows lifted Midnight Cowboy up to his feet as best as he could, but Midnight Cowboy was currently out for the count. The crowd was back in it now, getting over their initial shock and booing full-force. The Risen Lion propped Cowboy up against a metal column, part of ACW's entrance stage, and Billows turned around, “AND THIS, MY FANS, IS FOR YOU!” Billows ripped the headset off and sprung forward, CLANK L.O.S.T. KICK!!! AGAIN! Midnight Cowboy collapsed to the ground, using all cognitive energy to focus on clutching the back of his skull. In the back of his mind, he was convinced that he was holding his head together—that's how bad he was hurting at that moment. Billows wasn't finished, however, and he turned around, stomping over to the 300 pound Air-Powered Electric Wheelchair. The crowd gasped as he grunted and managed to lift it up over his head. The Watcher turned around and took a few steps toward The Smooth Operator, a sick twisted smile surfaced as he grew ready to bring the contraption crashing down on the skull of Midnight Cowboy, but that smile quickly faded... From the back came a bevy of men, trainers, road agents, and even a few wrestlers who had seen enough. The crowd roared as Alias led the pack, quickly stepping in front of Billows, “COME ON KID! PUT IT DOWN! YOU'VE INJURED HIM, DO YOU WANT TO KILL HIM?!” Billows took a deep breath and raised his eyebrows, making it clear that the thought had crossed his mind. The Risen Lion started to move forward, however, a big man stepped up to the left of Alias, blocked Billows' path. Injured a thousand different ways, his shoulder taped up as well as his abdominal section, Spike Saunders was still a giant intimidating force. And while he didn't have his trademark shades, and he wasn't dressed for action, he was still there. The crowd had read the reports that IN ACROPOLIS would be Spike's last on-screen appearance in ACW, and the fact that he was out here brought forth a notion of how serious this situation was. “I'd listen to Sheffield.” Spike calmly offered. Before Billows could even think, three more wrestlers stepped in on Alias' right. Aria and the black and white Arkadys. The crowd was losing their mind as Aria locked eyes with the man she'd spent time in LoC with, they'd at one point been fast friends, now they did their best to pretend the other didn't exist. With the Arkadys at her back, Aria stepped forward, knowing if anybody could end this calmly, it was her, “You don't need to listen to me because we were at one point good friends, you need to listen to me because good guy or asshole, whichever role your heart is set on playing...you're better than this.” Aria said. Billows was breathing heavy, the wheelchair wasn't getting any lighter. He looked at the five folks in front of him, and he gave out a holler of frustration before throwing the chair down and shouldering past Aria, pushing his way through the concerned parties. As a bevy of ACW personnel looked after Midnight Cowboy, Billows stopped next to The Smooth Operator, putting everybody on their guard once more. “We'll finish this later, Cowboy. Bet on it.” Billows promised. With that, Billows whipped a curtain to the side and stepped backstage. The crowd still couldn't believe what they'd just seen, they couldn't believe the deceit Billows was capable of. He'd sat there, absolutely motionless, he'd played on their emotions so well. He'd even managed to squeeze out a tear himself. And the violence? They couldn't believe the level of violence he'd brought forth either. They'd heard of bitter blood, but Billows had been ready to completely Gallagher Cowboy's melon. Alias gave Saunders a pat on the back, solemn, shaking his head, Sheffield walked over to the crew to check on Cowboy's status. The other four did as well. Suddenly, the crowd was roaring as a hand reached up out of the middle of the trainers and road agents, and clutched onto Alias' arm... And Midnight Cowboy was being helped to his feet, bleeding from the back of the head, he was definitely beat up, but he would be alright. A proud man, he started to try to walk to the back, but stumbled—still a bit woozy. Sheffield and Saunders caught him and it was a sight to see as the massive Spike Saunders bent down, hunched over, in order to aide Alias in offering Cowboy some shoulders to help him to the back. As the rest of the party filed to the back, one thing could be said... The IN ACROPOLIS fallout had begun with a bang. curse Backstage, in a particularly hidden spot, one man reviewed the recap of the proceedings of the last few minutes and grunted loudly to himself. His fists were clenched tightly, and beads of sweat trickled down the side of his face in spite of the supposed top-notch air-conditioning installation. Shaking his head, this man... this, former Champion, turned away from the monitor and looked directly into the camera. His nostrils were flared, his eyes were widened in psuedo-rage. But in an instant, all of his anger evaporated. "I am outraged. However, I am not the least bit surprised." John Sarsgaard uttered somewhat resignedly, despite the pocket of cheers from the crowd. Once it was free from his jaw, Ramey hung his head for a moment, letting out a cough before a sound began to emanate from his lips, “Uhuh huh huh…” “Is he crying?” War questioned, as he looked to his both confused and for a moment, even embarrassed for Jesse Ramey. “Uh huh haha HAHAHA!” The sound eminating from Ramey sent a chill down the spine of WAR, as Hunt looked up to his bodyguard slightly angered and otherwise unimpressed. “I do not believe he is, Mr. War.” Hunt shifted his attention back to Ramey, and tilted his head, “It would be a shame if your final moments were shaded with a loss of sanity.” “I won.” “Pardon?” “I beat Alias.” “I beg to differ, Mr. Ramey.” Hunt's words spat towards Ramey, immediately defensive, “If that was the truth you would not be here, paying the price for your own hubris.” “Alias couldn’t beat me!” Ramey snapped for a moment, before shifting his demeanor into something calmer, and breathed deeply in and then out as he echoed something that Alias would say weeks later as he forfeited his name, “Only Sheffield could, so I won.” “That said…” Hunt's hand removed from the small of his back, the cold tip of something metal forced deep into Jesse's chest. “I am no longer in the market for moral victories.” And then there was silence, but only to those that were close enough to the massive sound, then the echo within two men's ears began to reverberate. For the third man however, a pair of designer dress shoes would seal his fate. Hunt pushed Ramey backwards with a smile on his face, and just before the sun would finally come to a rest the sight of the blood pouring from Ramey's chest could be seen drizzling into the dirt that surrounded him. In this nearly three to four foot, by six foot hole, that had become the final resting place of one Jesse Tyler Ramey. Hunt nodded to WAR and the two casually entered the limo, as if nothing of importance had even happened. The following weeks would be different, there would be no music and no response from one man to another for the things that had happened at In Acropolis. Instead Jeremy Hunt would make sure that his memory would be forgotten, and the wrestlers nor the fans, would be none the wiser. F I N.
