Next: PRE-SHOW. ---- The Father, The Son, and The Unholy Agent The room was dark when he awoke groggily from what felt like days of slumber, the only source of light a faint one coming from an old looking television set that sat worn a couple of yards in front of him. The last thing he truly remembered was the thump the private jet he’d rented back in Los Angeles made when it connected with the runway, confirming that he’d arrived in Mexico City the previous Friday. Or was it Thursday? The actual day and date, just like everything else he’d seen or could recall since, was masked in a thick haze he just couldn’t shake. He wasn’t sure how long he’d been there or who was behind his captivity, but he was certain that it had been a good while and that whoever it was had no desire to provide him with any answers. It was at this point that Chester B. Wallace realized that he might actually be in trouble. Decades of shady business dealings and back-handed tactics had left him virtually friendless in life and his immediate family had long since passed away... who would miss him? He longed for something as simple as the ability to wipe the drug-induced sleep from his eyes, but the restraints that had been tightly wound around his wrists and the uncomfortable, wooden chair in which he sat made it impossible for him to move his arms even the slightest bit. He groaned and growled with frustration, fuming, when suddenly he spotted a familiar face amongst the blurriness that was enveloping his vision. He moved his ear as closely to the television as humanly possible so as to intently listen to what it had to say. He suddenly had a great and desperate interest in what was going on in the outside world. ”One of Mexico City’s sons returns to his true home for the first time in almost a decade when worldwide sports entertainment juggernaut All-Star Championship Wrestling begins its tour of our fine country this evening -- one that includes more than a few stops here in its biggest city. A hero and true inspiration to people here, especially the youth, Oso 'Bear' Bannon was possibly best known for being one of the only premier Latino athletes to ever grace an American football field, but is now embracing his native culture by entering the respected and noble world of professional wrestling in which Mexicans have excelled for a century. His presence here will surely not go unnoticed as he’s scheduled to participate in several community activities while ACW is on its tour and it has a lot of people, like the kids in this after school program, excited to say the least!” The screen flashed to a room full of smiling faces, bright colors adorning every inch of the walls. ”GO BEAR!” The children screamed in unison and then let out their best and loudest versions of a bear’s growl. Tears began to well up in his eyes. He hadn’t left Oso on the best note following his match at the END GAME 2010 pay-per-view, but they’d been in close contact since and he had a feeling that things would be different once they made it to Mexico. It was all the kid could talk about. While he was browsing the internet for the best resorts and restaurants, Oso was making plans to make a difference in his homeland, which he thought would help him towards making a difference in his own life. Ol' Bear had even smiled at him. To say that vibes in the Wallace/Bannon camp were good leading up to this moment would be an understatement. But none of that mattered now that one of the many with a grudge against him had caught up to him. People didn’t get out of situations like this, especially not in Mexico where your captors will bind you completely, but leave your mouth free so as to still be able to hear you scream. And then you go so crazy that you oblige them, the bastards... “LET ME OUT OF HERE, DAMN IT!” he wailed. “PLEASE!!!” Almost on cue, a rickety door that he didn’t even know was behind him swung open. A gruff, smelly man entered the room, breathing heavily, and walked slowly to where Chester was unwillingly sitting. The Southerner felt his presence behind him and the hairs on his neck stood at attention. He began to sweat profusely. Swiftly, the gruff man brought his hand upon Chester’s right shoulder. The (in)famous agent winced, waiting for a pain that never came. “Mr. Wallace...” came a deep, thickly accented voice. “I’ve been waiting for you to wake up.” He heard the scrape of wood against wood as the man dragged a chair across the floor and placed it in front of the old television. Chester mourned the loss of the only light he’d had for as long as he’d been held there as it was overtaken by the man’s massive stature. The man took a seat right across from him, but his face was cast in shadow. “It’s about time we have a little talk, Chester. It’s okay if I call you Chester, right?” “Wh-wh-whatever you’d like.” He stammered whenever he was nervous, which was a rarity until recently. Still, he was glad to finally be getting the chance to possibly get some answers. That is if the man was even his captor. “My name is Pablo Ibarra and my business and my business associates are what keeps this country running. It isn’t always the easiest thing to do and sometimes it can get bloody, but I do a pretty damn good job and I have for many years. I see much of you in me and once you get out of here and can do a little research into who I am and what I’ve done you’ll see that’s a very large compliment.” Chester replied meekly, able to focus on but one section of what Pablo Ibarra had just said to him: “So you aren’t going to kill me?” The man laughed deeply, pulling a small pistol from the waistline of his pants. He reached over and grazed its barrel against Wallace’s face. “No, Chester. I’m not going to kill you. Not yet anyway. In fact, pay the debt you owe me and you can forget that I even exist.” Chester Wallace raised his graying eyebrows. “Debt? What debt? I don’t even know who you are.” The gruff man chortled again, stood, and with a flick of his fingers a flame appeared and illuminated his face, which was all of a sudden inches away from Wallace’s. Beneath the dirt and scruff, it looked oddly familiar to the Alabama-born agent. “You sure about that?” He tried his best to come to a conclusion, but kept drawing a blank. It’s hard to keep track of grudges and debts acquired when that’s what most of your confrontations end up in. The man sighed and returned to his seat, the gun pointed directly at the agent. “Surely you know what a finder's fee is, Chester?” Chester nodded agreeably. Of course he did. Any other time he’d have scoffed at such a ridiculous notion and the conversation would’ve been over, but any other time he’d have had a choice. “Well let’s just say you’ve found a goldmine that I dug many years before you stumbled upon it, friend.” “Nonsense!” the agent scoffed. “I only have one client and he...” Chester B. Wallace gulped, Pablo smirking across from him. He remembered going over all the documentation regarding that one client when he was working out their deal and the consequent deal they signed with a certain major wrestling federation, the documentation which had included his birth name... “Ibarra.” the agent whispered, a light bulb that he wished was real going off in his head. “That’s right, Chester. You’re milking talent straight from my cohones and into your bank account, but all I’m doing is getting jerked off! Fortunately for me, the powers-that-be brought you into my word and gave me the opportunity to collect. So, allow me to further introduce to you how we conduct our business in Mexico.” Ibarra cocked the pistol he was holding, rising again to draw it close to Chester Wallace’s heart. “Three weeks, amigo. Three weeks from this day to pay me one hundred thousand dollars and I think that’s being generous. Do that and you’ll never see me or hear from me again. Don’t and I’ll see to it that your meal ticket expires.” Chester Wallace’s face, which was already pasty by nature, turned a ghostly, sick white. “You’d kill your own son? Your own blood?” “And maybe you too. Who knows?” Pablo Ibarra responded coldly and grimly. “Blood means nothing to me, Chester. It’s just something to be spilled and not cried over, like milk. Only green flows through my veins. I’d have thought you’d have understood better than anyone!” He rose completely to his feet, keeping the gun pointed at Chester all the while. The agent was trembling uncontrollably, finding little relief in the fact that he heard footsteps which he assumed meant Oso Bannon’s father, a man the twenty two year old couldn’t even recollect ever meeting, was walking away from him. His deep voice called from a short distance. “Remember... three weeks. And know that you can’t slither out of this one. I’ve got eyes everywhere. This country, this city... it’s mine.” With a snap of his fingers, two burly and masked men exploded into the room and began to release Chester B. Wallace from the straps that had been keeping him bound. Dragging him to his feet, he collapsed under his own weight. It was going to take awhile before his legs would be of any use. Complaining in Spanish, the two men carried the agent out of the room under Pablo’s watchful eye. Crossing the threshold of the front door, Chester’s eyes seared with pain from a sun that he hadn’t seen since who knew when. It was setting. Once they got acclimated, he could see the shack where he must have been kept moving further and further away from him. Darting them around, he quickly noticed that there was little else around. He knew then that he would have never been found. The reintroduction to light and fresh air was shortlived as they stuffed him into the trunk of a maroon Toyota sedan that dated back a quarter century. He settled back into the darkness quickly, accustomed from days of being alone with nothing but his own thoughts. He knew that this journey would soon end and that he’d be let back into the general population of the free world, but he was anything but free... For over the next three weeks, he would still be bound and should he not meet the ransom set by his captors, his only release could be into the arms of angels. The Toyota wound through a desert road, glow from the city lights ahead of them growing with every inch. As it sped along, it passed a weathered sign. WELCOME TO MEXICO CITY! ENJOY YOUR STAY! For Booster personally, END GAME 2010 a coming of age for him. Mutual respect had been earned. Booster no longer felt small or insecure. He did, however, go back to thinking about his father. 'The One' Jimmy Reid. Booster blinked as he swung open the door to his locker-room and saw his father sitting on a chair, flanked by Der Deutscher Soldat and Bobby Minio. Booster cleared his throat and tentatively placed his sports bag on the floor, by his side. "Ah, hey. Been a while." Booster croaked, a little nervous at Soldat glaring at him.
