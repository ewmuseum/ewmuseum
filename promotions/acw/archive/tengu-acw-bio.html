Tengu Biography Real Name Yamato Katashi Nicknames the Cursed-Spirit, the Hiroshima Hell-Hound, the Bird of Prey Hometown Hiroshima, Japan Birthday Unknown, believed to be around 23 or 24 years of age Place of Birth Believed to be Hiroshima, Japan Height 177 cm (5 ft 9 in) Weight 102.5 kg (225.5 lbs) Blood Type Unknown Eye Color Brown Biography Some say the tengu (天狗) is a cursed human being. Others say a demi-god or demon spirit. Stories of travellers stumbling upon the tengu’s nest to find giant eggs, high in the remote mountains of Japan. Yet they dare not disturb them for fear of the tengu’s wrath. They say the tengu possesses the powers of teleportation, telepathy, premonition and thought projection. That they are able to invade a person’s mind and drive them into and over the brink of insanity. They are also known to shape-shift. Yamato Katashi was a quiet child. As a baby, his parents thought him to be autistic and were quick to abandon him for fear of great shame upon their family. Found by an old man by the name of Nobu Katashi, he was taken under his wing and put to work as soon as he could manage. Memories of Yamato’s youth, whose name was given to him by Nobu, are not pleasant or fond memories. Nor are they bad. They are just memories. A noble and kind man was Nobu, who only knew hard-work. He instilled this ethic within Yamato, whom he thought of as a son. Yamato called him “father” in honour of his rescue and the kindness he had received from Nobu Katashi. Nobu worked for a cleaning company who had a contract with a sporting arena in Hiroshima. Having spent most of his life in school or working with Nobu, Yamato enjoyed being able to see the various sporting events. In particular, the martial arts and wrestling shows which came through Hiroshima. Nobu always feared the worst of Yamato’s nature. Having been brought up with the old man’s kindness he didn’t seem to understand the essence of it. Often of a night, Nobu would find the boy on the roof top, squatting and watching out over the neighbourhood. When he spoke to you it was as if he were reading your mind, as if he already knew what you were going to say. He did fine in school, not great and not badly. His social skills seemed to lack. Children of the neighbourhood began to call him “Tengu Katashi”, after the mythological creature of Japanese folklore. It did not bother Yamato. He embraced the mystery those around him cloaked him with. In his free-time, what limited time there was between his education and work, Yamato studied the arts of judo and wrestling. Large touring promotions from Tokyo would often breeze through, Yamato became ever encapsulated by the performances and the athleticism. He began to concentrate on his wrestling. He went in search of a trainer to assist him with his progression into professional wrestling. Yamato found a small gym nearby his neighbourhood and approached the trainer, Makoto Hiroshi Juro, with an exchange of talents. He would clean the gym an hour before each lesson. Makoto agreed and training began. Nobu grew jealous of the arrangement and the disrespect that Yamato showed him in not expressing an interest in karate, like so many in Nobu Katashi’s family had before. He beat the boy within an inch of his life, forbidding him to participate in wrestling as long as he lived under his roof. Yamato healed, eventually, growing hungrier to wrestle. The hunger grew stronger and stronger. His will overcoming him. He faced Nobu again, telling the man he called “father” he still wished to wrestle. Nobu beat him again. Yamato would not desist in his yearning to become a wrestler. A destiny seemed to be prevalent to him and as each beating passed, Yamato began to understand Nobu’s technique further. His patience, which people mistook for stupidity, was a virtue. Watching and studying, getting inside of Nobu’s mind, Yamato began to understand. On the eighth occasion, when Nobu attempted to beat him, Yamato was able to swat away his attacks by preparing his body for strikes and swatting away others. Tengu was alive! But the enjoyment of seeing the old man grow frustrated seemed to feed another hunger within Yamato, and he persisted in enticing the old man to strike out. To attack. Eventually, the old man gave up his will to persist against the evil which was clearly growing within the boy he’d brought to life. Nobu Katashi took his own life to save his honour of bringing Yamato Katashi into the world. Yamato’s training continued, not only the physical understanding and technique of wrestling, but the mental training. The training only that of a tengu could persist with. Yamato embraced and became the childhood nickname. Eventually, he learnt all he could from Makoto Hiroshi Juro and put his savings together for a flight to America, where Tengu could spread his wings. Tengu appears on the ramp, staring down at the ring. He slowly walks, as if he were stalking the ring, stopping occasionally to snappily turn his head and stare at the fans. He’ll finally get to the ring where he moves toward a corner and climbs up the ring-post ever so slowly, until he gets to the top where he perches, like he’s ready to pounce. He watches out over the crowd, peering out slowly over them all, or directly at his opponent. If his opponent is in the ring he cocks his head around, like a bird of prey investigating it’s next kill. If his opponent is not in the ring before he is, he remains perched until they arrive, watching them inquisitively as they make their way down. Entrance Music “Der Golem” by Fantômas
