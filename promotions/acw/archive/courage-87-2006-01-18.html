With a short hop, Coral had jumped on Hound's knee with his left leg, and the right leg that didn't touch Hound's knee came rocketing towards Hound's face. SMAAAACK! SHINING YAKUZA KICK. That was a new one from the Coral Avalon playbook. It knocked down Hound and potentially might have even knocked him out. However, Coral had been rattled throughout the contest and was finding it difficult to follow up. He managed to crawl on top of Hound's massive frame, hoping to score a win of some type. One. TWO. THR--KICKOUT. It was Coral's first two-count of the match, which gave him a little hope at least. Coral pulled himself back to his feet, before he went to the turnbuckles and climbed up to the top rope. From there, Coral waited for Hound to get up before he threw himself at him with a cross body block. Hound was powerful, but disoriented from the shots to his head, and he was unable to use his strength to catch Coral Avalon in midair. So, Coral took down Hound and got a cover. One. TWO. ...uh, no? Because Hound sat up with Coral still in his arms. Hound got to his feet, Coral still in his arms, before he delivered a rib breaker-styled backbreaker. Hound held on, and then proceeded to do it again. Then, Hound held on again, and proceeded to land a fallaway slam on Coral, pitching Coral all the way back and near the ropes. Hound immediately crawled over and made the cover. One. TWO. In the ropes. Hound, still the aggressor, pulled Coral back to his feet and landed a gutwrench suplex that planted the Kleptomaniac in the center of the ring. However, Hound did not go for the cover. Instead, he called to Hemlocke, "Chair." Hemlocke was quick to answer the call, pulling the timekeeper from his chair and grabbing the steel chair he was sitting in. She folded it up and slid it into the ring. Referee Dick Childs would have done something about this, but the rules of Coral's title clearly stated that weapons were allowed. Hound stalked behind Coral, waiting for him to get up so he could waffle him with the chair. Coral got up even as the fans were warning him of what lurked behind him, and then turned around. Hound took that moment to wind up and swing at Coral. He missed. Coral dodged to a side, diving onto the canvas with a roll and ending up with his back to the ropes. Hound recovered from his swing and took another shot, but Coral again dodged. The chair hit the ropes and bounced back, hitting Hound in the face. Hound was stunned, but maintained the grip on the steel chair... at least until Coral came in with a standing roundhouse kick (a bit out of character for Coral) that knocked the hair from Hound's hands. However, Coral wasn't aiming for Hound himself with the kick, and therefore the chair didn't reflect into Hound's face, but simply fell to the mat to Hound's side. Once Coral's other foot hit the mat again off of the roundhouse kick, Coral hopped with the leg that had always been grounded so he could spin around and catch Hound in the leg with a stiff spinning kick to the leg Coral had previously been working on. Hound staggered, clearly stunned from the blow. Hound clearly had not anticipated Coral being tough like this, nor did he expect what Coral did next. The kick was designed to stun Hound long enough for Coral to get behind him. Coral reached up and grabbed Hound by his chin, before he leapt up and planted both knees in Hound's back and fell backwards. LUNGBLOWER. However, Coral did something that many fans didn't realize he could do. He linked the Lungblower into a submission hold made famous by Samoa Joe, and had it right in the middle of the ring. COQUINA CLUTCH. Coral had Hound in the rear naked choke, and had scissored the big man's body, applying the hold perfectly. Coral held on to the hold as best as he could, but Hound was fighting it. Hound actually managed to drag himself towards the corner, as Coral didn't have the body weight that Samoa Joe had in keeping his opponents trapped in the hold... and Hound eventually got to the ropes. Coral let go of the hold after a four count, and Hound slid to the outside to regroup. However, Coral didn't want Hound to take a breather and came running out after him. TOPE SUICIDA. Hound went down off of the tope, and Coral was finally in the driver's seat for real, for the first time in this contest. He pulled Hound to his feet and managed to roll him back into the ring. From there, Coral slingshot himself over the top rope and came down with a legdrop across Hound's throat before making a cover. One. Two. SHOULDER. Avalon didn't let that bother him. He took the time out to kick the steel chair out of the ring... like hell he'd use it here. He then waited for Hound to get up, before he attempted a roundhouse kick. Hound blocked it, holding on to Coral's leg before he straight-lifted the Kleptomaniac up for a powerbomb. However, Coral wasn't about to die that easilly, and he quickly slipped out of the powerbomb, landing on his feet in front of Hound. Then he got a kick in the gut. Hound whipped Coral into the ropes, before he attempted a big boot. However, Coral performed a baseball slide, and while he was sliding past the slightly off-balance Hound, Coral snatched Hound's leg and tripped him up. He held on to Hound's ankle and managed to lock in... ...the ANKLELOCK. Coral had Kurt Angle's favored submission hold locked in at the center of the ring. Coral put on as much pressure as he could possibly put on in the hold, trying to get Hound to tap out. Hound cringed in the face of the hold, it was being applied on the same leg that Coral had been pounding earlier in the contest and he was feeling the effects. However, Hound was too tough to give up now. He stood up, though hunched over with his hands and one foot on the canvas, before he shot forward. This, in turn, caused Coral to go flying out through the ropes and hit the floor. The fans, who had grown excited as Coral got the upper hand, quickly deflated. Hound got to his feet, a little winded and with a damaged leg, but he soon found the energy to get to the outside. He grabbed Avalon and tossed him back into the ring, before he put Coral in a standing headscissors. POWERBOMB. Hound wasn't done yet. He lifted Coral back up to his shoulders again, before he drove any air Coral had left. POWERBOMB. Quickly, Hound went for a cover, hooking a leg. One. TWO. THRE-SHOULDER. Coral had barely kicked out, yet again, in spite of Hound's three hundred plus pounds on top of him. Hound was bewildered. Hound grabbed Coral by his hair and whipped him into the ropes. He seemed to be going for his version of the Black Hole Slam, which he called the Rottweiler. However, Coral held on to the arm as Hound grabbed hold of him, and shifted his body so that he was on TOP of Hound's arm, rather than under it. End result? Coral landed on Hound's arm and immediately locked in a Crippler Crossface on the Dog of War! Coral pulled back on Hound's head as far back as he could, trying to strain his neck and shoulder with his leverage advantage. Hound fought it, though, not willing to give up quite yet. His career still had future in it and he wasn't gonna end it based off of Coral Avalon's persistance alone. He slowly got to his hands and knees, before he dead-lifted Avalon onto his shoulders. He had broken the Crossface through this method, and now balanced Coral onto his shoulders before falling to a side. DEATH VALLEY DRIVER. Both men were down as the fans deflated. At Dick Childs' count of seven, Hound was up first. He pulled the battered Kleptomaniac up to his feet, pushed him into a corner, and began to pound him in the skull. The bandage on Coral's forehead was forcefully ripped open in this barrage, exposing his head wound from Gimmick Hell. Another punch managed to cause Coral to bleed for his second match in a row. The fans booed Hound while Dick Childs tried to get the match out of the corner. Hound picked up Coral, before running out of the corner and levelling Coral with what could only be described as a running spinebuster. Hound went for the cover, hoping that Coral would learn to stay down. One. TWO. THREE-NO. Coral just barely got a shoulder up off of the canvas before Childs could get the three count down. Hound glared at the official, starting to get frustrated at the fact that he couldn't put the Kleptomaniac away. Hound pulled Coral up to his feet, and whipped him into a corner. Then he brought all three hundred and forty-five pounds into the corner, whereupon he met nothing BUT corner as Coral slipped out through the ropes, ending up on the apron. Coral wearilly held on to the ropes, trying to catch his breath, before he flew off with a springboard dropkick that caught Hound in the back of the head and knocked him down. Coral immediately took advantage by chickenwinging Hound's arms and flipping over. CATTLE MUTILIATION. Coral had Bryan Danielson's submission hold locked up, though his own feet were close to the ropes. Hound had nowhere to go and was having difficulties getting to his feet since Coral wasn't about to let go. Coral had both of Hound's arms bent in unnatural angles, and it looked possible that Hound may end his career right here and now. So, it should be of great fortune... and in fact a boon to Hound, that Hemlocke was there to trip up the Kleptomaniac and break up the hold. The fans EXPLODED in boos, as Hemlocke walked away as if nothing had just happened. Coral glared at Hemlocke on the outside, but decided not to go out after her like a stupid face would do. Instead, he turned Hound over onto his back and attempted what I guess was going to be a Boston Crab. However, Hound's legs were too powerful and he easilly tossed Coral off of him. Hound got up, but Coral met him with a kick in the gut. Then Coral grabbed Hound's arm, which had been weakened a bit from the Mutiliation. Single arm DDT. Upon connecting the move, Coral maneuvered his leg in front of Hound's face, and attempted to lock in his coveted Koji Clutch that had won him several victories in the past. However, Hound grabbed Coral's leg before he could lock it in, and began to stand up. Unfortunately for him, Coral was resourceful enough to hang on to Hound's arm, converting the Koji Clutch into a hanging jujigatame. Hound was in a lot of pain... at least, until he swung Coral into a turnbuckle, bashing his head into it. Coral let go of his hold, and the fans were upset again that Coral couldn't force Hound to tap out. Hound picked up Coral, scooping him up for a running powerslam. However, halfway into the run, Coral slipped out while still holding on to Hound's head... and the fans erupted because it seemed like it was time for the Ratings Spike. However, Hemlocke jumped up onto the apron and distracted Coral... which worked. Coral let go of the inverted facelock and marched over to the green-haired woman. Hound took the opportunity to recover, and when Coral realized what was about to happen, he turned and charged back on Hound. ROTTWEILER. It was over. We were going to have a new Scorpion Fighting Champion. Hound covered, hooking a leg. One. TWO. THREE. SHOULDER. It was close. So very close. But Coral Avalon had kicked out just inches before Dick Childs counted three. Hemlocke was irate on the floor. Hound was perturbed. He actually looked up at Childs and slapped his own hand three times in quick succession, wanting a faster count. Even worse for Childs was the fact that Hemlocke had hopped up onto the apron, yelling at Childs to do his damn job and count the three count like he should've done earlier in the contest. While Childs was dealing with Hemlocke, Hound was pulling Coral to his feet and preparing another powerbomb, while his back was to Hemlocke. However, Coral managed to flip out of the powerbomb for a second time in this contest, before he caught Hound with a dropkick in the face. Earlier, Coral's dropkicks had little effect on hound, but Hound had practically exhausted himself trying to put away Coral. As a result, Hound was sent staggering backwards... ...and into Hemlocke. Hemlocke was knocked off the apron, and the bounce off of the ropes was enough time for Coral to get to his feet and hook up Hound. Flatliner. And you know what Coral usually did after hitting the Flatliner nowadays? That's right. KOJI CLUTCH. Hound was trapped. This was Coral Avalon's finisher, and he had nowhere to go. The difference between this hold and the others Coral had applied on Hound earlier was the simple fact that this was a choke. Which meant that Coral didn't even need to make Hound tap out to win the match. Hound tried desperately to get out of this hold, even as the fans were urging him to tap out. Hound attempted to power lift Coral, but Coral held on and Hound couldn't maintain his strength even to get to his feet. Hound's wrestling style had finally caught up with him. By expending all of his energy early in the contest, he had nothing left to combat a legal chokehold like Coral's Koji Clutch. Coral has bled and Coral had suffered, even in the face of an opponent like Hound just a few days after enduring Gimmick Hell, but he had the upper hand and Hound couldn't hold on. The Dog of War found that his vision was blurring. He didn't know what happened to Hemlocke, and he didn't know what had just happened to her. He did know that it was over for him and his career, as he made the critical choice before he could pass out. Tap. Tap. Tap. The fans roared. Coral released the Clutch and laid beside Hound, exhausted. He had survived yet again, even in the face of a much larger opponent. Dick Childs collected the Scorpion Fighting Title from the timekeeper and laid it on Coral's chest. Coral soon stirred and got to his feet, before he held his title high over his head to the roar of the crowd. Soon, the Kleptomaniac was out of the ring, and he began his slow, but victorious stagger to the back. A fitting beginning to what would surely become a memorable title reign for Coral Avalon. Winner > Coral Avalon via submission; retains the Scorpion Title End. No. NO. NO. It wasn’t supposed to be like this. It wasn’t supposed to be like this at ALL. Hound, still dazed from his match with Coral Avalon, snapped his head up. He was PISSED. He tapped out. That little mongrel had made him submit in front of millions. This wasn’t supposed to happen. Violence Jack. Father Shanahan. He promised that all would be well. He promised Hound the world. And now…his world was gone. His one chance at greatness, eliminated. Hemlocke, on the other hand, was angry as well as Hound knelt in the center of the ring. Grabbing a mic, the Jade Jezebel stood over her charge and sneered. “You…you SICKEN me!” She snapped before slapping Hound across the face, sending out a loud “OOOOOOOOOOOOOH!” through the crowd. “WE WERE SUPPOSED TO RULE! NOW YOU DON’T EVEN HAVE A LEG TO STAND ON, YOU LITTLE BITCH!” Her scathing words pierced Marcus Brown’s black heart. He loved this woman with all his heart, even though she would never feel the same. He did nothing but take the abuse. “You’re not worthy to be at my side. You’re not worthy for the GUTTER!” Marion Sterling paced around the ring, never taking her eyes off her pet. “Be gone with you, Marcus Brown. Forever will your name be associated with failure and blown opportunities.” He sat up. Failure. The one thing he wanted to avoid. There was no escaping it. It was in the stars. He was doomed to be as such. With the fans jeering every last word spewing from her mouth, her vile tongue continued. “I will find somebody new to be at my side. Somebody that won’t be a major screw-up! Somebody that KNOWS how to get the job done.” She would. Marcus knew that. And he’d go down in the annals of ACW history as being another person who’d just come and gone without finding the true path of success for himself. Wait… No. It didn’t have to end like this. He could still be remembered. He didn’t want to do it. Part of him was telling him not to do it. He couldn’t. This would all blow over. Hemlocke turned her back on Hound and sneered over the shoulder. “Be gone from my sight. Your task is done and you are now worthless to me.” Still sore in his arm, Hound finally stood up, towering over The Jade Jezebel. Well, what would they do? His job was already gone and would be in ACW no longer. Sorry… Turning around, Hemlocke cast one last glance at her charge, but would later live to regret such a decision when the fist collided with her skull. A sickening shot to her face sent her to the mat in a heap as the fans gasped in horror of what they had seen. Remaining unconscious on the mat, Hound towered over Hemlocke before he grabbed the mask on his face and peeled it away, once again revealing the cold, lifeless face of Marcus Brown. He didn’t even bother to look at the mask anymore as he tossed it right next to Marion Sterling’s fallen body. Climbing out of the ring, Marcus knocked over the steel steps with brute force before tossing them into the ring with a bit of effort. He was still weary, but not weary enough to do whatever it was he was about to do. Marcus went through the ropes again, glaring down at the woman he allegedly cared for. With a bit of effort, the middle child of the Brown family placed the set of steel steps in the middle of the ring and looked out to a sea of screaming fans, telling him not to do whatever he was thinking of doing. This was madness. He had no job anymore, but he would leave an impression for those that even thought about the name of Marcus Brown. Scooping Hemlocke’s prone form over one of his shoulders, the 345-pound being slowly walked the final steps up the steps before casting one last apathetic look to the fans of ACW. This would be how he’d be remembered. A force to be reckoned with and feared throughout this sport. He’d make people talk for ages about how he would attack that poor woman as he drove her, neck-first onto the steel steps with a Tombstone Piledriver and a sickening collision following that. They’d tell people about that time they went to the show and saw that beast be quickly taken down by ACW’s security staff, most notably the 7-foot-tall head of security called Rex Silver. The man speared him to the ground and restrained him as fans sang praises of his goodbye. And while he was being restrained by those six guards – Rex, included – he took one last walk down the aisle. He would be forever remembered in ACW as a failure…but a failure with nothing to lose anymore. He wanted his publicity. And despite not having a job anymore, he would certainly be talked about for quite a long time.
