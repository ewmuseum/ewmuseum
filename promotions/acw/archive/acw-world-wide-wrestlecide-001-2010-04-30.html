A C W EVERYTHING OR NOTHING WORLD.WIDE.WRESTLECIDE 001 ¤ 30 APRIL 2010 ACW (ALL-STAR) ARENA, MONTREAL, CANADA Opening Once the cue was given, the cameras started rolling and WORLD.WIDE.WRESTLECIDE 001 began beaming to thousands of fans online! The pyrotechnics were of the highest order as close to 10,000 screaming fans welcomed the ACW roster back to its spiritual home after months on the road in Europe. Joe Bishop, the right-hand man to the General Manager, stood in the ring feeling absolutely overwhelmed by the fantastic crowd reception. Bishop expressed sadness that ACW was only back in Montreal for one night only before moving on into unknown territory in the form of Eastern Europe... but nonetheless promised the fans an amazing night of wrestling. WORLD.WIDE.WRESTLECIDE, Bishop explained, was devised as a way to have the burgeoning stars of ACW's All-Star Academy go up against the more established talents on the ACW roster itself. To that effect, Bishop revealed a few matches that would be unfolding throughout the course of the night. Kristinia Kage, who had enjoyed a stint in the Academy's second season while also appearing on ACW broadcasts, would lock horns with two of the Academy's brightest female stars: Haley Mulligan and 'Brash' Betsy Lienhard! Irish Spring, fresh off a gutsy performance at LEGENDS IV, would take on The Kole Brothers from the Academy. 'The Alpha Dog' Mark Weiler would have an opportunity to welcome new Academy signing Quintana Rogerio Santiago, who Bishop declared would be lighting up the scene in no time at all. And finally, Joe announced that by order of SlySports, Abilities from the Academy would cross paths with two legendary ACW figures that would only be revealed when the time was right. And as if that wasn't enough, Joe announced that Orlando Grant would be making his inaugural defense of the United States Championship against a former superstar that agreed to come back for one night only: the fans erupted in a mixture of outright delight and absolute surprise when a spotlight shone on the one and only Jeremy Stevenson in the crowd, who merely smiled and nodded his head. Bishop shrugged his shoulders with a cheeky grin, stating that ACW was always about the blindsides. With that, he thanked the fans for their wonderful support, and signalled for the combatants for the first match of the evening to take their stage. SCORPION FIGHTING RULES Peter Phoenix vs. Fejona Min REFEREE: MARK SHIELDS Peter Phoenix, the Warrior From Down Under that couldn't speak, was making his very first appearance on a non-Academy event and the Montreal fans were completely immense in their support of Phoenix. In contrast, the mere sight of Fejona Min -- who had made her first appearance in ACW close to six years ago and cemented herself in history by capture both the Scorpion Fighting Title and the Television Title -- sparked the fans to have their first big jeer-fest of the evening. Fejona shrugged off the hostile reception and went to work on her opponent. As it were, both Phoenix and Fejona had very similar styles and together carved out a highly entertaining contest. For the first four minutes, neither combatant even reached out for a weapon that would have been legal under the confines of Scorpion Fighting Rules. Phoenix managed to escape an ankle lock attempt from Fejona and pulled off his shuffle sidekick of doom that had seemed to have put Fejona away for good. Alas, the Enchanting Delinquent (as she was often called during her stint in 2004-2005) managed to get her shoulder up. From there, Fejona upped the ante by resorting to tricks like eye gouging and hair pulling. She even retrieved a steel chair, hoping to use it to garner victory. Phoenix stopped any hopes of that by somehow catching Fejona in position for a triangle choke! After nearly 10 minutes of back and forth action, it seemed that Peter Phoenix might have done enough to force Min to tap. As fast as Fejona's body was fading, it couldn't compete with how quickly her mind reacted to probable counters. Punching away at Phoenix's face didn't work, so she had to reach out to the ropes with her legs. Only problem was, her legs were just not long enough. Plan C? A punch to the groin of Peter Phoenix. That got the job done. From there, Fejona got her hands on the steel chair again and connected with a shot to the head, prior to her slapping on her own version of a triangle choke on Phoenix right in the middle of the ring. Peter resisted for as long as he could, but eventually tapped out to hand Fejona Min the victory. WINNER: Fejona Min via SUBMISSION. Backstage with England's Best Jenna McMullen, ACW's lead backstage interviewer, was on hand to have an exclusive interview with the new ACW Tag Team Champions; Rud Albion and Buddy Saxon, the team better known as England's Best. Rud acknowledged the fact that going into LEGENDS IV, he and Buddy were not considered favourites to walk out of Barcelona with the Tag Team Titles but they set out to defy the odds and achieved what many felt was a huge upset. Buddy Saxon chimed in with his own personal thoughts, stating that it was exhilirating to be Rud's tag team partner and that he was supremely excited about the challenges that would definitely arise now that England's Best were the Tag Team Champions. Rud agreed, and revealed to Jenna that he and Saxon had put out an open challenge to the industry for a titles match on COURAGE 111. When asked about the upcoming tour of Eastern Europe, Saxon answered that travel arrangements were going to be rather tricky but he felt the exposure ACW would get would only mean good things. Albion was also positive, and mentioned he was looking forward to the unique environment that the Eastern European fans would definitely serve up. In The Ring with Midnight Cowboy Midnight Cowboy, fresh off the biggest victory of his career at LEGENDS IV, came out with the Spirit Of ACW Title wrapped around his waist and the ACW Television Title on his right shoulder. Soaking up a fabulous response afforded to him by the Montreal fans, Cowboy thanked everybody for all the well-wishes he'd been receiving and expressed how incredible it felt to be the latest Spirit Of ACW, for he had an impressive lineage to live up to. MC took a moment to pay tribute to the man he bested at LEGENDS IV, Trevor Wilson, and stated that Wilson was perhaps the best competitor he'd ever squared off against. At this juncture, Cowboy took the Television Title off of his shoulder and raised it high above his head, bringing about another round of cheers. MC stated that the Television Title also had quite a history, but argued that it was brought back under false pretenses. Cowboy revealed that he had a conversation with SilverHAWK and the two came to a consensus to respectfully return the Television Title to its retired status. While that did garner a mixed reception, Cowboy quickly added that he would do the right thing and defend the title belt in a few moments, to give it a proper and deserving send-off. Cheers of approval rang out, and Midnight Cowboy smiled widely at the sight of his challengers being led out from the back. SIX-MAN ELIMINATION MATCH FOR THE ACW TELEVISION TITLE JP Boudreau vs. Ulf German vs. Carrachio Salfuego vs. Masato Hayajinx vs. Shane Robertson vs. Midnight Cowboy © REFEREE: PAIGE ALLEN Midnight Cowboy got the ball rolling against Ulf German, and right from the start, Cowboy appeared to be a man reinvigorated by his epic victory at LEGENDS IV. Already a hard-hitter to begin with, MC was especially ruthless on this night in Montreal and made extremely short work of Ulf with the Voices After Midnight with barely two minutes on the clock! JP Boudreau entered the fray next, and the crowd were on their feet for the hometown boy. A sparkling exchange between Boudreau and Cowboy ended with the former almost put the Television Champion out of commission with a victory roll. MC was able to squirm out of Boudreau's grasp and tagged in Shane Robertson, who had been growing restless on the outside. Unfortunately for Robertson, he was unable to translate his eagerness into any sort of winning formula; he was unceremoniously taken care of thanks to Boudreau's execution of a sunset flip snap powerbomb! That paved the way for Masato Hayajinx to get involved in the match proceedings, just as Boudreau tagged in Carrachio Salfuego who had been patiently waiting for his turn. Under the watchful eyes of Midnight Cowboy and JP Boudreau, Masato and Carrachio put on a display of fine counter-wrestling with each man trying to find different ways to get the other to stay down long enough. Eventually, Carrachio was able to evade Hayajinx's buzzaw kick and thereafter connected with a high-angle spinebuster that did the trick. The young rookie who had been picked up during ACW's tour of Spain was over the moon, and paid for spending too much time celebrating his elimination of the veteran Hayajinx; JP Boudreau charged into the ring and immediately went for his Quebec Cloverleaf finisher. Tried as he could, Carrachio couldn't reach the ropes and Boudreau racked up his second elimination of the match. That set the stage for a showdown with the Television Champion himself. It was either going to end with Cowboy winning and retiring the Television Title after eight days of ownership or JP Boudreau would go down as the last and shortest Television Champion in ACW history. And after five minutes of all-out action, it was Midnight Cowboy that prevailed after twice fighting out of Boudreau's Quebec Cloverleaf to drill the hometown boy with his Voices After Midnight finisher. The fans may have been rooting for their hometown boy to win, but exploded nonetheless as Midnight Cowboy stood tall at the end of it all. Being the sportsman that he was, MC shook hands with Boudreau who was obviously gutted, but took it all in stride. The two men had words on the way out of the ring, perhaps agreeing amongst themselves that they would cross paths again in the weeks to come. As it was, Midnight Cowboy won and went down in the books as ACW's last Television Champion. For now, at least. WINNER: Midnight Cowboy via SURVIVAL; retains the ACW Television Title and subsequently retires it. Backstage with Simon Blythe With a sneer of total disdain etched on his face, the so-called Agent To The Stars was looking on at the Hall Of Fame plaques that were displayed in the hallways leading up to the exit onto the stage. Indeed, Simon Blythe (Esquire) was in the house, and he quickly went off on a speech revolving around how ACW's view of who deserved to be in the Hall Of Fame was completely wrong. Simon proceeded to promise that the two charges under his wing, Mark Weiler and Jack Harris, would rack up accolades of the highest order in no time at all and show the rest of ACW just what would constitute as being worthy of the Hall Of Fame. Blythe also promised that Weiler would easily win his match later in the evening, lamenting the fact that The Alpha Dog had to be involved in an event that scrawny teenagers with pimples were watching on the Internet; to that effect, Simon warned the likes of SilverHAWK and Joe Bishop that consequences of the highest order would rain down on the pair. SINGLES MATCH Rory Hayes vs. Buddy Saxon REFEREE: TRENT SAVAGE Going into this match, Rory Hayes was obviously hoping for something of an easy night out. Buddy Saxon's recent association with Rud Albion had boosted his confidence tenfold, and the Bournemouth Boy was looking to pick up his first singles victory of his ACW career. Both men got into a slugfest in the initial moments of the match, which was of course more of Rory's cup of tea. Until Buddy Saxon countered a spinebuster attempt into a crushing DDT! The fans figured that was it, but Hayes stayed alive and was immensely angry that the DDT had caused a cut on his forehead. Luckily for Buddy Saxon, he could seemingly do no wrong and basically stayed two steps ahead of his opponent. Hayes was dumbfounded as every opportunity he had to inflict some major damage on Saxon resulted in one-half of England's Best countering and landing a high-impact move of his own. Finally, Hayes decided to do something drastic: he lashed out with a mule kick when Saxon caught him in a rear waistlock that went undetected by referee Trent Savage and followed up with a sickening spinebuster. Buddy somehow got the shoulder up in the nick of time, but that only gave Rory the incentive to line the Bournemouth Boy for his Boston Massacre finisher. Once again, Buddy found a counter and pulled off his Saxon Slam finisher that led to the subsequent three-count! The fans were jubilant and The Bournemouth Boy was over the moon at having captured his first singles victory. WINNER: Buddy Saxon via PINFALL. Backstage with Joe Bishop Joe Bishop had gathered referees Tomaz Heinreich and Paige Allen in his makeshift office for the evening, and explained to them that with the ever-increasing ACW roster, more and more matches were being signed. To help remedy the situation, Bishop and SilverHAWK went ahead and hired a new referee; one that had been recommended by Jesse Ramey. Leon Hurst was introduced, and Bishop asked Heinreich and Allen to show Hurst the ropes. Both agreed, and chimed in with their scepticism of ACW spending the next couple of months in Eastern Europe. Joe Bishop explained, with a straight a face as he could manage, that it'd be a good opportunity to make grounds in a brand new market, and asked Leon Hurst if he had any problems with Eastern Europe. Hurst confirm he didn't, and added that he was simply excited for the opportunity. Bishop was pleased and revealed that Hurst would have his first match to referee later in the evening, which prompted Tomaz Heinreich to immediately offer his services as an aide. Hurst accepted, and together with Paige Allen, the three referees went on their way to get further acquianted with one another. TRIPLE THREAT MATCH Kristinia Kage vs. Haley Mulligan vs. 'Brash' Betsy Lienhard REFEREE: BELINDA DECROIX Each of the three combatants in this match got a positive round of cheers mixed in with cat-calls. And then, hometown girl and Academy referee Belinda Decroix made her way down to the ring with a giant smile on her face. Clearly, her family members were in attendance and Belina relished being home after months with the Academy in the States. With four lovely women in the ring at the same time, it was no surprise that every male fan in the arena was watching on intently. However, the match that unfolded turned out to be even more gripping than anticipated. Right from the start, it appeared as if Haley and Betsy had made a pact to take out Kristinia and she who triumphed at LEGENDS IV found herself being tossed out of the ring at every possible juncture. The strategy for Betsy and Haley was simple; get rid of the veteran and fight each other for the victory. At one stage, Kristinia got fed up of being dumped out of the ring every single time she got back in, she decided to bide her time on the outside. That allowed Betsy and Haley to battle each other for supremacy, and they did exactly that. The moment Haley managed to get Betsy worn down enough to cinch in a front facelock, Kristinia Kage made her move. She ascended to the top of a corner turnbuckle and effortlessly took flight with a missle dropkick to the back of Haley's head! Seconds later, Kristinia took care of Betsy Lienhard courtesy of her Kaged Out finisher... but surprisingly, that didn't wrap up the match! For a rookie, Betsy's ring presence was spectacular and she got a foot on the bottom rope just in the nick of time. Belinda Decroix, the Academy's representative referee and hometown girl, was adamant in her decision to Kristinia. The latter was irritated that she had yet to clinch victory, and would be even more agitated at Haley and Betsy recovering to once again team up on her. Eventually, however, Kristinia's experience came into play and she played her two opponents off of each other. Betsy surprisingly managed to escape the clutches of Haley's attempted sleeper hold and took her out with an impressive sit-out brainbuster. That paved a path for Kristinia to once again rock Betsy's world with a second rendition of Kaged Out, and the undeniable three-count followed to cap a great showcase of women's wrestling. WINNER: Kristinia Kage via PINFALL. TAG TEAM MATCH Irish Spring vs. The Kole Brothers REFEREE: LEON HURST Having impressed ever since hopping aboard ACW during the tour of Spain, Irish Spring were eager to get back on the winning track after having performed admirably at LEGENDS IV. In contrast, The Kole Brothers were hoping to sort themselves out after an indifferent spell in the Academy and work their way up to contention for the Tag Team Titles no doubt. Joseph Kole started things off with Cillian O'Callaghan, with Darina on the outside performing her managerial/cheerleading responsibilities. Joseph and Cillian appeared to be evenly-matched, with both men leaving nothing to chance and for a while forgetting that it was a tag team contest. Once Joseph got the upper-hand, Andrew was tagged in and the Kole Brothers pulled off a nice tandem move; Joseph raised Cillian up for a suplex, giving Andrew a chance to score with a dropkick to the sternum. Andrew tried to build momentum from there, but Cillian wouldn't go down easily and within moments, made the tag to his brother Cavan. Being the freshest man in the contest, Cavan was a house of fire and even took care of Joseph who made an attempt to pull off a sneak attack. That momentary distraction allowed Andrew to connect with a neckbreaker that very nearly resulted in the win. Once again, the brothers from England had the advantage over the brothers from Ireland. That wouldn't last, as Cavan countered a suplex attempt from Andrew into a front-face leg-sweep that left both combatants on the canvas gasping for air. Tags to their respective partners were made at exactly the same time, and the fans were excited at Joseph and Cillian charging at each other with renewed vigour. In an blink of an eye, Cillian ducked a spinning heel kick from Joseph and caught the latter in a roll-up pin, holding on long enough for new ACW referee Leon Hurst to count to three. Just like that, Irish Spring had won and The Kole Brothers had fallen to defeat once more. Darina herself was quite surprised at the abrupt ending but she celebrated with her brothers vigorously regardless. Andrew and Joseph, meanwhile, were relegated to intense bickering over the nature of the defeat. WINNERS: Irish Spring via PINFALL (Cillian O'Callaghan over Joseph Kole). Backstage with Orlando Grant Amanda Dixon, the All-Star Academy's lead backstage interviewer, was standing by with the United States Champion Orlando Grant, for whom the fans in the All-Star Arena went wild. Amanda asked Orlando how it felt to beat Jack Harris for the United States Title just 12 days prior. Orlando instantly responded that it was immense for him to finally beat The Pikey Madman, but knew then that the war wasn't over. Orlando then talked about LEGENDS IV, where he and John Sarsgaard lost out to Harris and Mark Weiler, and stated that he was hell-bent on making sure the lackeys of SlySports Entertainment LLC would get what was coming to them in due time. Orlando mentioned that he had a bone to pick with both Harris and Weiler, but singled out The Alpha Dog for being a massive bully. When asked about his title defense against Jeremy Stevenson later in the show, Orlando replied that it would be his biggest challenge yet and he was ready to lock horns with the man that studied under Trevor Wilson. SINGLES MATCH 'The Alpha Dog' Mark Weiler vs. Quintana Rogerio Santiago REFEREE: TRENT SAVAGE Donning a fancy lucha mask and bouncing around the ring with unbridled enthusiasm, new Academy recruit Quintana Rogerio Santiago showed absolutely no fear at all at the sight of 'The Alpha Dog' Mark Weiler hulking down to the ring with his manager Simon Blythe by his side. Weiler had the clear size advantage over Quintana, but the latter didn't back down and instead demonstrated his intent to win by blizting Weiler with all sorts of fancy attacks in the opening moments of the match! Even as Mark Weiler began to impose his authority with fierce clotheslines and vicious slams, Quintana always had a spring in his step and appeared to be absorbing all the punishment being thrown at him. After managing to slow down Quintana with three consecutive executions of a punishing variation of a rib-breaker, Weiler began to toss his smaller opponent around with a myriad of suplexes all aimed at weakening Quintana's lower back which he seemed to favour. Taking time to sneer at the fans, Mark Weiler ended up missing a corner slingshot splash that would have surely put down the new Academy recruit for good. From there, Quintana once again upped the tempo much to the disdain of Simon Blythe on the outside, and a springboard forearm smash led to a wonderful tilt-a-whirl DDT that very nearly kept Weiler down for the three-count! The fans were beginning to believe that an upset was on the cards. Those hopes were dashed when Quintana came off the top turnbuckle with a moonsault in mind, and got hammered in the ribs thanks to Weiler raising his knees up at the last possible second. It didn't take long for Weiler to recover his bearings, and after close to eight minutes of back-and-forth action, Mark Weiler wrapped things up by planting Quintana with his Put To Sleep finisher. A good show of power and brute force by Mark Weiler, but an equally brave performance by Quintana Rogerio Santiago who earned a nice round of applause after the end of the match. WINNER: 'The Alpha Dog' Mark Weiler via PINFALL. TAG TEAM MATCH The SOBs vs. Pop Culture Phenoms REFEREE: TOMAZ HEINREICH The fans were certainly looking forward to the now-former ACW Tag Team Champions getting it on with their longtime rivals, but nobody quite knew what either half of the Pop Culture Phenoms thought about losing at LEGENDS IV eight days prior to tonight. Leave it to Ed to deliver, albeit in an extremely drunken and depressed fashion. Ed went on and on about how the situation at LEGENDS IV was extremely unfair and that he felt cheated at Robert Downey Jr. not returning his calls. Klein, Ed's tag team partner, tried to be the soothing moderator of reason but ended up being kicked at by Ed. The SOBs didn't really wait for Ed to finish up his speech, storming out from the back to attack their old foes to much delight from the crowd. The battle finally made its way down to the ring, and referee Tomaz Heinreich was glad that he could get on with proceedings as planned. Of course, Ed had different ideas; upon being kicked in the face by Selby O'Brien, Ed started throwing a tantrum and tagged out to Klein. A befuddled Klein was torn between checking up on his partner and carrying on the battle with Selby. Ed crawling his way to the broadcast booth settled matters and Klein went ahead with the contest, matching Selby every step of the way with his heart and his will to win. But as Ed degraded further and further into his alcohol-induced tantrum, Klein found himself being overrun by The SOBs' constant tagging in and out, Sheamus in particular getting a major response from the fans for some reason. He was, after all, only one man. And it didn't take long for The SOBs to lay Klein out with a spike piledriver that garnered the victory. It was fitting in a way; as Klein's head was planted onto the canvas and knocked him out, Ed curled up into a fetal position at the side of the brodcast booth and slipped into a state of drunken slumber. The SOBs celebrated finally securing a victory over their rivals, but they were well aware that the circumstances were more than a little bit odd. WINNERS: The SOBs via PINFALL (Selby O'Brien over Klein). TAG TEAM MATCH Abilities vs. ??? REFEREE: PAIGE ALLEN Abilities were already in the ring alongside referee Paige Allen, and all three individuals were wondering who the other tandem involved in this contest was going to be. Imagine everybody's shock when Alias of all people walked out. Tommy Vale, ACW's foremost ring announcer, revealed that the former ACW World Champion would be tagging with someone he was quite familiar with courtesy of SlySports Entertainment LLC. Alias didn't even flinch, just staring down Abilities in the ring, probably still coming to terms with being dethroned as ACW World Champion. His eyes almost fell out of their sockets when his partner's theme music blared over the speakers; turning around slowly, Alias came face-to-face with the man he tagged with many years ago during ACW's run in the mid-1990s. None other than 'Supestar' Vince Jacobs was his partner. For one night only, it seemed, the team of Vintage Advancement had been reunited. Alias and Vince Jacobs sized up each other, but not out of spite. Jacobs was the first to speak, furious that he'd been ordered to compete on what he felt was a 'filler show' against his wishes but mentioned nothing about having to tag with a man he'd crossed paths with numerous times throughout his career. The hardcore fans in the arena were going absolutely crazy at this turn of developments and in the ring, both members of Abilities rubbed their hands in glee. Surely, Alias and Vince Jacobs couldn't function. They were wrong. Vintage Advancement entered the ring together and opened a can of whoop-ass on William T. Rex and Abraham Swift that sent the crowd into raptures. This left referee Paige Allen with the unenviable task of trying to get the situation under control. She finally did, but only after Jacobs managed to land a low-blow on William T. Rex. Alias admonished his partner for his dirty tactics and took his place in the middle of the ring, eager to work out his frustrations on Abraham Swift. Once the match was under control, it was the precision teamwork of Abilities that was beginning to shine through in the face of Alias attempting to unleash his pure rage at the manner in which he'd lost at LEGENDS IV. Not wanting to lose on the so-called 'filler show', Vince Jacobs discreetly undid the padding of the top of the corner turnbuckle he was situated at, just moments before Alias sent Swift into that same corner face-first! Swift saw the exposed turnbuckle and could do nothing about it, leaving Alias in shock. Vince Jacobs acted innocent and claimed that the turnbuckle came undone on its own, which referee Paige Allen didn't really buy. Amidst the confusion, Jacobs slapped Alias on the chest thereby tagging himself in, and prevented Swift from tagging out to William T. Rex who wanted back into the ring after only spending a combined total of two minutes wrestling in the match thus far. Vince Jacobs was in fine form, finding ways to wear down Abraham Swift without breaking a sweat. Surprisingly, after a swinging neckbreaker, Jacobs allowed the downed swift to recover and tag out to his partner William. If William T. Rex thought Jacobs had gone crazy, he found himself proven wrong in the form of a Superstar Kick expertly pulled off. During the subsequent cover, Jacobs placed his feet on the ropes for added leverage; referee Paige Allen didn't spot it but Alias did. And he proceeded to push Vince's legs off the ropes, allowing William T. Rex to force his shoulder up from the canvas before the decisive third count. Jacobs was extremely irate and began a fresh wave of discussions with his partner in the corner. That allowed William T. Rex to recover and tag in his partner Abraham Swift. With a mighty roar, Swift charged into the ring and toward the bickering pair of legends. And that was all, for Vince Jacobs spun around and connected with another Superstar Kick! Right before Alias made the blind tag. Jacobs went for the cover, but had to be told by referee Paige Allen that Alias was the legal man. Jacobs did not like that one bit and stormed out of the ring, just as Alias made the successful cover on Swift. The fans were thrilled, but Alias didn't seem overly happy that he'd won and Vince Jacobs... well, he'd left the ringside area in a raging fit. Vintage Advancement's reunion might have been a victorious one, but it wasn't a happy one. WINNERS: Vintage Advancement via PINFALL (Alias over Abraham Swift). ACW UNITED STATES TITLE Jeremy Stevenson vs. Orlando Grant © REFEREE: DICK ZHIRKOV Finally, it was time for the main event. Jeremy Stevenson, having been associated with The Minutemen and Trevor Wilson in general in the past, was as pleasantly stunned as anybody when the fans erupted at the sight of him hopping over the security barricade and making his way into the ring. Orlando Grant cautiously strode down to the ring, clearly wary of Jeremy Stevenson being the first challenger to his newly-won United States Title. Dick Zhirkov, the most senior of All-Star Academy referees, made sure to go over the rules for Jeremy Stevenson; the gist of it was that Stevenson only had 10 minutes to secure victory. Once the formalities were done with, Orlando and Jeremy got on with the match, the fans on their feet eagerly anticipating a classic battle. They got what they asked for, with Stevenson looking like a more agile version of his mentor Trevor Wilson. He even displayed the same bloodthirsty instinct that Wilson was most famous for, turning back wave after wave of Orlando's unique offense. A back-body drop counter to a piledriver attempt by the OG of ACW was a precursor to Stevenson locking in an armbar submission, and Orlando -- even if he had unlimited rope-breaks -- was quite a distance away from the safety the ropes provided. It took a mammoth effort for Orlando to eventually reach the ropes, but many figured the damage had been done. A subsequent single-arm DDT from Stevenson appeared to have been enough to herald a new United States Champion, but Grant kicked out. Slowly but surely, Orlando began the difficult task of trying to regain momentum, although he technically only needed to last a while more to force a draw. Neither Orlando nor Stevenson looked like they were going to settle for anything less than a victory; victory that seemed certain when Orlando executed his Big Shit Poppin' finisher out of nowhere. Jeremy spoiled the celebrations as he got the shoulder up right at the death, which left The Quarantine Boy completely bamboozled. An immediate reprisal of his Big Shit Poppin' finisher was expertly countered by Jeremy into a high-angle belly-to-back release suplex and that was followed up by another go-around at an armbar submission by Stevenson! Orlando survived, once again getting to the ropes, and braved through the searing pain to once again attempt his Big Shit Poppin' finisher. Jeremy Stevenson was unable to escape Orlando's clutches, and the three-count with a mere 20 seconds left in the match left the fans cheering the end of a intensely gripping encounter from start to finish. The night's festivities was nearing its conclusion, but the fans were not done expressing their utter joy at a fantastic evening of wrestling entertainment that been served up. In the ring, Orlando Grant and Jeremy Stevenson shared a handshake of mutual respect at the truly classic battle they'd just partaken in. Orlando Grant had retained his United States Title and lived to fight another day, but as he returned his attention to the stage, he found both Malk Al-Haq and Rory Hayes staring him down. Each man having their designs on the title belt that was in Orlando's possession. WORLD.WIDE.WRESTLECIDE 001 had been an incredibly successful experiment, yet it bode difficult days ahead for Orlando Grant; two equally adept challengers that were simply salivating for their eventual opportunity at the ACW United States Championship. WINNER: Orlando Grant via PINFALL; retains the ACW United States Title. F I N
