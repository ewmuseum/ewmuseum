What this is The fed's own staff page from its first site (acw.ewmania.com, the 2002 to 2004 edition), as captured in the Wayback on 21 May 2003: the in-fiction owners of the company and how they came to run it. The site's own menus are not reproduced.
The following people are the ones that work behind the scenes at ACW, some you see on TV, the other you don't. Majority Owner Ethan Winters owns the majority of the stock in ACW, making him the majority owner of the whole company. After inheriting millions from his now deceased father, who made most of his money in the oil industry, Ethan has joined the ranks of a select group of men, which include some old WWF employees, to try and set the wrestling world alight, but his way may infact cause more harm to the company than good, even though his first major action at the company was to seal a TV deal, he also got rid of most of the roster and replaced them with young talent, most of which seems to be lackluster to say the least. The Other Owners In the early 1990s Ethan met Charlie Dunn, a former road agent for the WWF in the 80s. Along with Dunn came Jimmy Boyd, another road agent, who worked with many federations including the big two, the WCW and the WWF and some other Indy federations which he would like to keep unmentioned for legal reasons. Upon meeting them, Ethan, became intrigued with their knowledge of wrestling, the way it works, the way it grabs people and plays with their emotions, when done right, and he became very excited. When he decided that he would help them out with some small ventures, which they where trying to get going, including a wrestling federation, a small time one, at that time. Ethan gave the duo a small amount of cash to get them going, an investment, but after a year or so, he let them go with it, and stayed out of their way as he looked to go into other areas to invest in, including television. In 2001, after forgetting about the money he had loaned to Dunn and Boyd for the ACW, they came back to him and gave him the sum back, stating that it was no longer needed, as their federation, the ACW, had taken off in the states, and was doing rather nicely for both of them. Ethan went to see one of their shows, and was very impressed. If Dunn and Boyd could mix the talent in which they had, and add to it a few big names, they could spear head the attack on the WWE's top dog position.
