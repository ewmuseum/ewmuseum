ARC II - The Road to Legends III 21st May 2009 Recorded LIVE! from Montreal, Quebec, Canada Card subject to change without notice introduction Some people say that good things come to those who wait. And those who have been waiting, knew that they only need to do so for another two weeks before Legends III. But with one Courage left before the first ACW Supershow of the new breed, it surely wasn't going to be a damp squid was it? No chance. Frank Dylan James vs Rory Hayes Alright. All the yackety-yak had come and gone, at least for now. You see, right now it’s time to get this motherfucker started up good and proper, and by “proper” I of course mean “exceedingly violent.” It’s not every night that Frank Dylan James makes his debut in a new region after all, now is it? Now, sure, it wasn’t exactly advertised that the West Virginia Whack Job was going to be in Montreal tonight for Courage, but when I guy like Frank comes walking up to your doorstep looking for a fight, you don’t get in the way, you point him at someone to fight and then duck the hell out of the way. That, in a nutshell, was the how the contract negotiations with SilverHAWK went just about two hours ago. And you can rest assured that Frank had been drinking heavily since about twelve seconds after scrawling his chicken-scratch of a signature on the dotted line to work for All-Star Championship Wrestling. With all of that said, and the fact that Frank had been milling around in and around the All-Star Arena for a couple of hours now, those in attendance weren’t completely shocked when the Dropkick Murphys “For Boston” rang through the public address system and Rory Hayes made his way down toward the ring. Rory had a scowl for the ages plastered across his face, as usual, and the fans and shareholders alike were giving him six shades of shit as he found his way into the ring. Rory was big, and he was bad, but he was neither the biggest nor the baddest, and he was about thirty seconds away from finding that out first hand. The first blistering licks of Ted Nugent’s “Stranglehold” light the crowd up. Just because the Redneck Riot Scene has never worked in Canada before doesn’t mean that these people are completely ignorant to him. After all, Canadians have the internet too, and that means xtremewrestlingtorrents.net, and that means that a fair amount of the spectators in the ACW Arena understood what hearing The Nuge meant for the health of Rory Hayes. While “Stranglehold” continued, everyone in the building, Rory included, had their eyes glued up the ramp. What nobody bothered to take notice of was that Frank, an alcoholic by nature, was not in the backstage area and had zero plans of making any sort of regular ring entrance. Where he had been since about an hour and a half before the show officially got under way was sitting at Scuffy’s bar pounding back beer after beer after watered down, Canadian beer. It took a few moments, but eventually heads began to turn at the uproar coming from the direction of the bar. Frank was hooing and hawing his way through the crowd, knocking chairs every which direction and staring his wild-eyed stare at anyone stupid enough to stand in his way. It didn’t take long for a path to clear and those seated in the sloped area to give The Hillbilly Jesus a very wide berth as he made his way bare-footed through chairs and personal belongings toward the ring. Inside the ring Mark Shields was having a fair amount of difficulty in keeping Rory Hayes between the ropes. Rory, a former deathmatcher himself, knew of Frank’s reputation and that Frank had competed in two deathmatch tournaments in the last year, one in Japan, and one just a week ago in Las Vegas. Some of his old instincts were kicking in, but Shields admonished him to stay inside the ring as Frank terrorized his way down through the arena. Frank finally made it to ringside, sort of, he’d hopped up onto a chair about three rows back of the guardrail as “Stranglehold” faded and began gesticulating for his opponent to join him on the floor. Shields, a bit out of character, tried one last time to stop Rory from leaving the ring. For his troubles he got a sneer from Hayes that said “shut it” very succinctly. Frank got off of his chair, picked it up, and launched it at Rory inside the ring. He missed by nearly two feet, but still managed to enrage The Boston Mangler. Frank, having finally gotten a reaction out of Rory, decided that chair-throwing was indeed the way to go and launched several more into the ring from his spot in the crowd. “GIT OUT HERE AN’ FIGHT YEW HIPPY BAYSTERD!” That was all it took, Rory had decided that this one didn’t need to start inside the ring after all and he quickly jumped between the ropes to the floor. He headed around the ring and hopped the guardrail before Frank could get a clear shot at him, but don’t for one second think that stopped Frank from sending more chairs flying at him. Inside the ring, Mark Shields had gotten over pretending to be a real referee and had a look of petulance on his face that said he wouldn’t be bothering with any more trying to act like an actual official tonight. Rory and Frank met, three rows into the crowd, and the chair throwing was done. Frank unloaded a vicious looking punch directly to the jaw of the Boston Mangler, to which Rory just smiled and returned the favor. Frank’s eyes went wild and he screamed out for anyone to hear: “TIME TO RASSLEFIGHT!” The next thing anyone knew Frank had put Rory hard onto his back with a vicious lariat and followed up with several kicks to the head and chest before standing up and pounding his chest. “HOO-AHH!” The Redneck Riot Scene grabbed another chair and sent it hard down onto the face of Hayes who was trying to cover up. Frank grabbed another chair and tossed it down onto him. And another. And Another. And several others. Once Hayes was sufficiently covered in a pile of chairs The Hillbilly Jesus backed off a bit, giving himself some room before getting up a running head of steam and flipping himself with a sloppy looking senton onto the pile of chairs and Rory Hayes beneath them. Frank howled out in pain as he slid on his ass and a chair a couple of feet away from his downed quarry. Both men were slow to their feet. Inside the ring, not only had Mark Shields not even bothered with ringing the bell, he looked to be texting on his cell phone while the two brawlers pulled themselves to their feet on the floor. Frank was up first, but he was greeted with a hard shoulder tackle from Hayes that folded him in half and gave Rory the half second needed to reach his feet. He straightened Frank up and gave him a vicious looking elbow to the side of the head before grabbing a chair of his own and smashing it down across Frank’s head, busting him open hardway right in the center of his forehead. Frank staggered, shook the cobwebs loose and took the chair straight out of Rory’s hands before smashing himself in the head with it several times and tossing it backward over his head into the sea of people who had tentatively decided to inch closer in to the action. It’s a good thing the chair didn’t hit anyone, the ACW could hardly afford a Negligence lawsuit at this stage in the game. In his outburst, Frank not only scrambled his own brains, but he left himself open for a hard knee to the gut that allowed Hayes to take control of the match. Rory grabbed Frank by his mess of hair and led him further into the crowd, stopping at odd intervals to punch, elbow, or headbutt Frank just for good measure. They had made it up to the top of the sloped seating to where several vendors were set up, hawking t-shirts, DVDs, and everything else that you can imagine would be abhorrently overpriced at a wrestling show. Frank unloaded another huge right hand that stopped Hayes in his tracks before attempting an Irish Whip. Hayes easily reversed it and sent Frank careening toward the merchandise table where vendors were quickly jumping out of the way. Frank managed to put on the breaks, though, and just in time to catch a charging Rory Hayes with a back body drop that sent him crashing through the table. Frank continued with his onslaught, again sending chairs and now pieces of table flying at his downed opponent. Back in the ring, Mark Shields had finished texting whichever skank he could nail for being an ACW referee and had decided to call the match a No-Contest as it had never even came into the ringside area, let alone the ring. An announcement was made but it fell on deaf ears as Frank continued the fight. At this point he was just hammering the downed Hayes with a single chair, smashing it more with each blow. It would have been about here that security flooded onto the scene. The match had been called, chairs had been thrown in and around the crowd, furniture was broken, and Frank was on a warpath. “YEW BOYS WANNA RASSLE TOO?” He launched himself at the mass of security, sending several of them either running in the opposite direction or down on their asses on the floor. It was quite apparent that Frank had no intention of letting up the fight, and the goon squad were ill equipped to handle his particular kind of crazy. He yelled incoherently, calling them all hippies and faggots, and the Montreal crowd actually sort of took a liking to him, cheering on his blood-drenched rage. Paramedics had managed to make their way onto the scene to check on Rory, and were about to be the next victims of this particular rampage when the cavalry arrived in the form of Rex Silver and Buffalo Brian Slater. And then the break, another right hand was launched in VJ’s direction, but given the skill of the man, he caught it as he turned 180 degrees. High-angled Sugiura Slam. But just as Jack was about to execute the move, Tully squirmed free, leaving Father Jack without his bearings. He soon got them back though. Lariat As Tully ripped Violence Jacks head clean off, he quickly covered the wounded leader of the Sect in what was a formality. ONE… TWO… THREE. And that was it. Tully rose to his feet to the adulation of the fans as he surveyed all that was around him. Alias and Spaes, down on one side of the ring. Sharp and Wilson on the other, wounded, but still locked in their gaze. Daniels in a heap on the map. And Almasy… Almasy was now making his way to the top of the ramp as he looked back into the ring at the victor of the evening. Horace climbed the turnbuckle and raised his fist in the air, as he and Almasy shared what seemed to be a fate set for two weeks time. LEGENDS III Where everything will change…once again.
