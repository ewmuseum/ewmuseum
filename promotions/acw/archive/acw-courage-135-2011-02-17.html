A C W BLACKEST NIGHT COURAGE 135 ¤ 17 FEBRUARY 2011 SALON TEOTIHUACAN, ACAPULCO, MEXICO INTRODUCTION PRE-SHOW ¦ HOUR ONE ¦ HOUR TWO Introduction Who has the momentum? Is it the cabal? Once they had held every established title within All-Star Championship Wrestling, but since losing the Tag Team titles on Courage 133, the terrorist cell had been quiet. Had Sheffield's Choice shaken their leader and ACW's World Champion... Z? Or was he, as always formulating a plan that no one could see coming? Is it the Huntsmen? Being forced into an uneasy alliance, once again, with the cabal... the group of zealots, overlords and their soldiers of fortune, had gained some form of momentum. With the breaking of Billows, and the loss of there newly minted Gateway Championship, just weeks after Keller's double-crossing tore away their Spirit... momentum was something Hunt and his Cunts dearly needed. Or is it ACW? Who since entering Mexico had been befallen by the cruel territory of the Mexican badlands, though it has also been empowered by the Lucha Libre spirit. All-Stars had gone missing, Champions had been lost and gained, but spirits had not been broken. ACW could still survive. It had to. Who has the momentum? Maybe it simply isn't a question of doom and gloom, in the days before the DIA DE LOS MUERTOS PPV, but more the potential for celebration on the road to LEGENDS V! What would be wagered, and what would ACW's All-Star's be willing to lose? Next: PRE-SHOW. ---- The Oldest Story There Is This video was dated 17th February, 2011. It was circulated via the ACW online, YouTube, and Dean Denton's personal website. CERESO 2, Gomez Palacio, Durango. The Mexican prison gained infamy in July of last year after it was found that guards were allowing their prisoners to leave the compound at night to carry out gangland executions in Torreón, even going so far as equipping them with weapons and vehicles to better enact their crimes. To say that the so-called Center of Social Rehabilitation was rife with corruption would be an understatement. It housed hundreds of prisoners, their crimes ranging from petty theft to rape and murder. Tattooed thugs, toothless addicts and violent psychopaths all breathing the same stale air, eating the same tasteless slop, sweating in the same furious heat. And amongst these hopeless dregs of society, these drug-pushers and homicidal maniacs, walked a man who was once a shining example of good morals and old-school values. A man who had fought the good fight his whole life. The Bronze Lion, or perhaps the Bronze Bastard... Rud Albion. Imprisoned here until his trial for breaching a temporary restraining order and greivous bodily harm, Rud had spent the past five days mixing with utter vermin, listening to the incomprehensible legalese of his lawyers and being mocked and abused by the racist wardens. When one of the guards informed him he had a visitor, the prospect of a friendly face had given him a small glimmer of hope. Led to the visitation room in handcuffs and shackles like a dangerous criminal, with deep purple rings around his eyes from his many sleepless nights, Rud Albion wondered who his visitor might be. Perhaps his old friend Buddy Saxon, here to offer him support in his time of desperate need? Maybe Aria Murphy had recovered from her injuries enough to fly out here and give him a rousing speech? Hell, even Arkady's miserable gruff mug would have been a sight for sore eyes right about now. But, when the guards led him to his seat, the man on the other side of the plexi-glass was no friend of the Brummie Bulldog's. Rud's whole body froze up, his eyes widened and his teeth bared instinctively. He began to quake with rage. Dean Denton, the man who, with the aid of his nefarious lawyer, landed the Kingdom's Keeper here the first place. Dressed for business in a black suit and green tie, his fingers laced together on the table in front of him, Denton looked relaxed and composed... the complete antithesis of the dishevelled, scruffy prison garb and unkempt stubble of Rud. He flashed Rud a smug smirk as the guard forced him down into his seat. Denton picked up the phone receiver and motioned for Rud to do the same. Albion was sweating, tense, overcome with hatred as he stared hard into the eyes of the man who had transformed him and his friends into a series of sick 'art projects'; the man who had robbed him of his Spirit of ACW title shot; and who had him locked up in this fucking hell-hole with these low-life crooks. Was Denton here to mock him? To laugh at him? To feed his own sick ego? There was only one way to find out. Rud picked up the receiver. “Hello, Rud,” Denton said, without a hint of genuine emotion. Rud remained silent. “How are you finding prison life?” Denton asked. “How the hell did you get in here?” Rud snapped back, ignoring the question. “My man Shooter has his methods, Rud. You know that better than anyone.” Rud growled at the mention of the drug-addled virtuoso lawyer's name. “So what, you've come to have a laugh at my expense? Take the piss out of me? Have a good old gloat? I don't have to listen to it. Spare me the ear-ache and go wank over your reflection instead, Dean.” Denton smiled. “No, Rud. I'm here to talk to you, frankly, about the nature of this... affair... between us. You've lived your life trying to be a hero, trying to do the right thing, aspiring to some sort of higher moral purpose. And where has it got you? Look at yourself. You're an emotional, physical wreck, locked behind bars and facing a trial before one of the most corrupt judiciaries in the world...” “Listen, Denton,” Rud interrupted. “Back in the bad old days, a man like you would be stoned to death by the townsfolk like the bloody snake that you are. You're a cancer. You're...” “A villain, Rud,” Denton said, flatly. “You're the hero, and I'm the villain. It's the oldest story there is, a story that's been told since the dawning of humankind. But, look around you, Rud. There are so few heroes left. You're one of a dying breed, and there's a reason for that. It's because villains... are simply better adapted to survive.” Rud grated his teeth. Again with the survival of the fittest bullshit, the evolutionary spiel that Denton adopted to justify his bleak, vicious worldview. Rud leaned close to the glass, the phone gripped tight in his hand, and stared deep into those reptilian yellow eyes. “Even if the world really is that unjust, even if heroes are so few and far between, I'm never going to stop fighting,” Rud hissed. “I'd rather fight a losing battle knowing in my heart it's the right thing to do. Simply because I came from a bad place in the world, doesn't keep me from believing there's good in all people. I couldn't live with myself any other way.” “That's nothing but a pre-pubescent fantasy, Rud. A pipe dream. I thought you would have figured that by now,” Denton sighed, shaking his head. “Well, you can't fight ANY battles from inside a prison cell. Which is why I'm here, Rud... to offer you a deal. Only I can drop the charges against you. Only I have the power to free you so that you can bandy about your phoney ethics another day.” “Why would you do that?” Rud asked, perplexed. “Out of the goodness of my heart!” Denton laughed, and continued to laugh until his eyes streamed. Rud watched him through narrowed eyes, wondering how this man grew to be so utterly insane. Then, wiping away a tear, Denton added, “No. Because you can give me something I want, of course. The Spirit of ACW Championship.” The pieces of Denton's scheme began to fall into place in Rud's mind. The tournament for the number one contendership, Sharp's Cabalite rules and Denton's DQ loss to the champ last week. When the whole ghastly picture became apparent to him, and he understood what the terms of the deal would be, his shoulders sank and he held his head in his hands. How could he make a decision like this? Further jail time in this Mexican drugland, or the ability to fight, to survive. Though if he was to be released, how far was too far? “Listen, Rud,” Denton prodded, “The difference between a hero and a villain is that a hero is an idealist, always working towards unattainable goals, motivated by irrational beliefs. A villain is simply a realist, who sees the world for what it really is and uses it to his advantage. And THAT is why you're losing this war. Just this once, drop the heroic theatrics. BE REALISTIC, AND TAKE THE DEAL.” “Heroes are potential, the potential for hope, the potential for a bright future,” Rud murmured, his fingers pinching the bridge of his nose, “while villains are nothing but compromise. Comprised ideals, compromised ethics, comprised ideology.” His heart was kicking up a thunderstorm in his chest. He couldn't believe he was going to do this. “Deal,” Rud said, defeatedly. Denton smiled like an alligator. “So... does that make you a villain, Rud?” “Potentially,” he said, and hung up the receiver. IN THE DARK~! Match #1: Remi De Rozario vs. Dolf Biggler Acapulco, Mexico was on their feet as some action started off the night's edition of COURAJE, hailing from the Salon Teotihuacan. The first match was going to start off the night with none other than the Costa Rican sensation called Remi De Rozario. Coming up short to a loss to cabal member Lutawicka several weeks ago, he was back and ready to try his luck against none other than ACW's blonde braggadocio, Dolf Biggler. Rather than shake hands with De Rozario, Biggler blew a snot rocket in his general direction, which wasn't perfect at all. The action went fast and furious as De Rozario kept his opponent grounded for a couple minutes, keeping him trapped with headlocks and various takedowns that had the crowd popping. Eventually, Dolf Biggler came back with a Hot Shot across the top rope, followed by a powerslam for a close two-count. The fans showed no love for Biggler as he hit a couple Crossface Punches for a nearfall. After a chinlock went awry, De Rozario fought back with a couple of hard Flying Forearms, then let him have it with a big Missile Dropkick for a nearfall. Eventually, Dolf fell victim to the Cartago Sling (Elevated Reverse DDT), allowing Remi De Rozario to score a very impressive victory. De Rozario basked in victory. And as for Biggler? Well, this certainly was not perfection. Match #2: Fight Man vs. Thugson The words “technical master-class” and “classically trained wrestling” would not come to mind for the next two combatants. Fans showed their love for Fight Man and jeered the ever-loving crap out of the Huntsman's newest addition, the mentally unbalanced Thugson. Unlike the first match which contained some semi-capable chain wrestling, we were met with fisticuffs that quickly spilled out to the floor. After making Thugson greet the ring steps, Fight Man proclaimed to the masses that he was INDEED, Fight Man, and therefore got a huge pop. A little too much playing to the crowd and Thugson turned the tide, ramming him into a ring post, then the announce table, then suplexing him right onto the ring apron. From there, he spent the next few minutes ramming Fight Man into the turnbuckles repeatedly and wearing him down by shit-kicking him real good. Eventually, Fight Man fought back and took both he and Thugson down with a big Clothesline that knocked them both loopy, Fight Man collapsed from exhaustion. He got back into it with some punching combinations and a thesz press for a near fall. A string of headbutts caught Thugson in the chest before Fight Man set him up for a Piledriver that was reversed into a rather ugly version of an Omori Driver, dumping Fight Man on his head. After babbling some more about Cylons, the Battlestar Galactica-obsessed Thugson dropped from the top turnbuckles with the Ode To Adama flying headbutt. The three-count was academic and the string of victories for Thugson increased by one. Thugson was proving to be quite a commodity in the Huntsmen while Fight Man had tasted defeat in a stiff bout. Match #3: The House vs. Kesavan and Malk Al-Haq The third and final match-up for tonight's proceeding had huge implications for one of the teams. The big monster duo of The House (Derek Huber and Adam Roebuck) took a pair of mics before their scheduled match and announced that this would be the last of their tour dates with ACW for the foreseeable future. Business would be taking them back to the States, but before that, they wanted to go out with a bang and not a whimper, so they made an open challenge for any two men to come out and face the men with a combined weight of 777 pounds. Out came none other than the 410-pound Tattooed Terror of the cabal called Kesavan (the assigned team captain), accompanied by the Babylonian Overlord Malk Al-Haq, who was close to three bills himself. Kesavan had height over the portly Huber and Roebuck, but they still cleaned house awful quick, disposing of Al-Haq by tossing him over the ropes and hitting a sloppy, but effective Double Flapjack on big Kesavan. Being a former ACW Tag Team Champion, Kesavan knew exactly what to do, though, separating Roebuck from team captain Derek Huber. For several minutes, Kesavan and Al-Haq made quick tags, taking down Adam Roebuck for the moment. It wasn't quite the spectacle the fans were hoping for, watching four bigger guys wrestle, but eventually, Big Adam Roebuck got free by dodging a corner splash from Al-Haq and tagging in Huber. And Huber was a house (read: a blimp) of fire as he mowed down Al-Haq repeatedly with a series of scoop slams and elbow drops, punctuated by a big splash for a near fall. All four massive bodies flooding the ring and starting throwing bombs, fighting spilling out to the floor with Al-Haq and Roebuck, leaving Huber and Kesavan as legal men. Roebuck actually lifted him up and tried for his Bet On Red Powerslam, but Kesavan's strength was too much and eventually, he was overpowered and overwhelmed with a ring-shaking Chokeslam that got the cabal members a win and a heart-breaking loss to close out the House's winless tenure in ACW.
