Vayne — Sep 5, 2012 Round 1 entrance music: "Crazy Train" by Ozzy Osbourne Round 2 entrance music: "Crazy Train" by Ozzy Osbourne Round 3 entrance music: "Crazy Train" by Ozzy Osbourne Round 4 entrance music: "Symphony No. 5" by Ludwig von Beethoven Main Fighting Style: Boxing/wrestling (sprawl n brawl, for the colloquialism, though his striking is far more technical than "brawl" would imply) Preferred Manner of Winning: KO (primarily via overhand left or uppercut, his bread and butter) or TKO (via ground and pound from the top). He'll take what he's given though. Fighter's General Octagon Strategy/Attitude: Vayne is a decorated amateur boxer and wrestler. He prides himself on fighting very technically, and believes that he's a better striker than 90% of those he'll face in a cage. His confidence in this area means he wants to keep fights standing most of the time, and prevent things from degenerating into a sloppy brawl. He throws crisp punches with both hands. His leg striking is rudimentary, but he can throw leg kicks and liver kicks to opponents who think he's completely one-dimensional. He wants to land combinations, keep aggressive opponents on their toes, and generally keep his fists in people's faces until he finds the opening he needs to put his opponent away. Most of what Vayne does in the cage depends on his opponent. He's a very intellectual fighter who wants to counter gameplan for his opposition wherever possible, and tailors each plan to his opponent. In that sense, the Grand Prix's a trial by fire for him since he doesn't have the benefit of a usual full fight camp before each opponent; just his first, so he has to prepare as best he can on the fly. If he's against a slower and/or heavier opponent, Vayne will get on his bicycle, throwing punches in bunches (primarily jabs) while trying to avoid heavier retaliatory strikes from his opponents. His left jab will be his best weapon here as he works to tire the opponent and frustrate them. Once his opponent's gas tank is low, he'll move in with a combination. Alternately, if the opponent's throwing knockout shots, he'll look to time one and counter it with a big hook or uppercut, followed up with punches on the ground if necessary. If he has the size advantage on an opponent, he'll use it, hooking up the clinch and trying to get in some dirty boxing along the cage, leaning his weight on them and trying to tire them out. Once he's gotten his opponent tired, he'll step out and try to shut down the show with a flurry of punches. If his opponent has a better gas tank, though, he'll use the dirty boxing as the end rather than the means, trying to combo out of the clinch, knock the opponent down, and rain down death until the stoppage. For the most part, his wrestling is used defensively to keep himself on his feet able to throw his devastating litany of punches. Against fellow strikers, though, he's more likely to use the wrestling offensively, shooting for double leg takedowns that he sets up with combinations. Once on top, he wants to posture up, throw bombs, and end the fight. He'll posture up from full guard, even, if he thinks he can put things away. The mount is obviously preferable, but he'll take what he can get. His "ace in the hole," so to speak, is an arm-triangle choke that he's been drilling extensively in the gym in anticipation of the Warriors Grand Prix. He'll use it if the opportunity arises, but it shouldn't be his primary, secondary, or even tertiary plan. It's for the perfect moment. Fighter's general octagon strategy/attitude: Unlike most that would fear the environment, JAC has found himself to enjoy it. Over the years adapting to a higher threshold for pain than one would expect. He's fought in all manners of cages, be it legal like the Prix will be, or illegal as was the case with nearly all the Underground fights he was a part of. He'll start off strong to size up the opponent, taking heed to what type of style is being thrown against him and how best to react. Johannes is one of Western Europe's top prized fighters, and was coined the Adriatic Gargoyle. Not just because of his chiseled physique but also due to his nature to react much like a statue when the hits start coming in. The one thing JAC refuses inside the caged environment is to 'feel it out'. No love taps here. Out the gate expect nothing but strong rights, explosive lefts, heavy jabs, repeated mauy thai style knee's, and applying the pressure whenever the opportunity presents itself.
