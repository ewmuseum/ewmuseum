A one-man mixed-martial-arts angle fed built around a single-elimination Warrior Grand Prix tournament, judged round by round on the competitors' own roleplays, crowning Viktor Reise its first champion before an attempted 2015 revival stalled without a result. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

BodyCount Fighting announced itself 21 August 2012 with an open-invitational, no-weight-limit tournament, the Warrior Grand Prix, promising the winner the title of “the Best Fighter in the World.” Judges scored each round's fights entirely from a competitor's own submitted roleplay rather than a stated outcome.

The tournament ran through three rounds that September before its finals, 21 October 2012, at a sold-out BodyCount Arena. Viktor Reise, billed as “the behemoth of the tournament” and advanced after one opponent failed a drug test and his replacement couldn't reach Germany in time, knocked out an unheralded finalist, Jakob Alexander, in the second round to become the first Warrior Grand Prix Champion, walking away with ten million dollars and a still-unused future title shot.

A recruitment pitch followed the tournament, but the promotion's own record goes quiet until a second Warrior Grand Prix, announced 12 May 2015, raised the stakes considerably: “Death is real,” its own welcome post warns, with an eliminated competitor risking being “brought back to mess up someone's run for the top.”

That second tournament's own signup thread drew five entrants, including a crossover fighter billed as The Second Coming, before its own organizer scaled the field's minimum down to eight and called it “the LAST Warrior Grand Prix ever.” That minimum was never reached, and no result or further dated activity survives.
