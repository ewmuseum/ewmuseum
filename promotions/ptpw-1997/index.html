Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Prime Time Professional Wrestling announced itself on 13 November 1997 as “The New Generation and The Next Force In Pro Wrestling… Where the ICON's Play,” a roster capped at forty wrestlers with match outcomes set at 75% roleplay, 20% angles and 5% strategy submissions.

It planned three weekly shows: Saturday Night Prime Time, its flagship, five to seven fully written matches hosted by “Cool” Ray Roland, Gary Smith and Jim Williams; Wednesday Night War, an interview-and-updates program with no live matches, hosted by “The Mystery Man”; and a proposed Friday Night Extreme Zone, hosted by “Bubba” Bob Silvers and Dante Jones, which its own founding post admits “may not happen yet.” A quarterly special, Canvas Clash, was pitched as its Clash of the Champions.

Its seven-title slate ran World Heavyweight, International, United States, Television and Toughman championships in singles, plus World and United States tag titles, and its founding post laid out a full year of named pay-per-views from January's New Year's Crash through December's Winter WarZone, twelve months deep before a single card had run.

The same founding thread drew an immediate public dismissal from a rival federation's own operator, quoted in the reply: “TCW is better than PTPW… I give you two months tops.” Its own last trace, five days later, still has it recruiting and planning an eighteen-to-twenty-match pay-per-view for 14 December. No document past that survives, and no closing statement of its own is on record.
