A long-running email federation open to both real and invented wrestlers, its own posts tracing a membership drive from a 2001 relaunch through a claimed five-year history. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Action Wrestling Federation, AWF, dates its own start to February 1997 and calls itself, in one of its later posts, “a premiere top-notch e-federation that lasted for over four years.” By April 2001 it had gone quiet for a year over school work and announced a June reopening, aiming to rebuild toward 40–50 members, put every title up for grabs, and run a blended roster of “Real or Fake wrestlers” across its cruiser and women's divisions.

By July 2001 the mood had turned urgent: the fed was “losing members like flies” to college and other commitments and, in its own words, “at the risk of shutting down in the next few months.” It described its house format — one card a week of 6–15 matches, written “play by play” unless roleplaying ran slow, and a pay-per-view every few months “so feuds can be properly built” — and an ongoing “inVasion angle” against a rival e-federation it said it had “always [been] fighting... for 2 years,” insisting, unprompted, that it was “NOT a WWF rip-off.”

By November 2001 that same invasion angle was “just finishing up” after six months, and the AWF was looking to consolidate into “one big federation again,” with a stated ambition of two shows and sixteen matches a week. It still welcomed newcomers plainly: “You don't have to be the greatest roleplaer... We were all beginners at one point.”

No further document survives after 6 November 2001. Whether the federation's run continued past its own claimed five years, how the invasion angle against its rival resolved, or whether the two-show-a-week plan was ever realized is not something its own record answers.
