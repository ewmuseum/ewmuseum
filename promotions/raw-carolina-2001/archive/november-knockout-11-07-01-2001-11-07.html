RAW Carolina — Card Archive

November Knockout 11.07.01

7 November 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
RAW Carolina rolled in Winston-Salem Wednesday night, where 5000 fans buoyed by Wake Forest’s big win over Virginia on Saturday poured into the LJVM Coliseum Annex to catch November Knockout!!
“ERADICATOR” DIRK ROBERTSON & JAYSON RAYGE vs. “BILLIONAIRE” JOE FORBES & JESSIE MULDOON The newly-formed duo of Blood Brothers Dirk Robertson and young Jayson
Rayge opened the show in a big match against veterans Joe Forbes and
Jessie Muldoon. After a good showing against the World Tag Champs last week at Fright Night, Rayge and Robertson were out to cement their top contendership.
Robertson is, as ever, a double-tough competitor, but the former
Television champion is still not 100% healthy, working through injuries he sustained back in September. His partner Rayge is improving daily, thanks to vigorous workouts at the Suplex Academy, and looks to be a future star in the Carolinas.
As for their opponents, they’re heading in the opposite direction, failing to make the splash they had hoped for on their arrival in RAW Carolina. But despite this, they still managed to give the fans an entertaining match.
Forbes and Muldoon dominated the middle portions of the match, surprisingly choosing to focus on the veteran Robertson rather than his green partner. They had success with this tactic, though, working over Robertson’s still-recovering shoulder. But Robertson was tough, and refused to give in, battling back long enough to tag in young Rayge, who came in a house o’ fire. After clotheslining
Muldoon over the top rope and out of the ring, Rayge turned right around and dropped Forbes with his Infinite Rayge reverse Death Valley
Driver. But Muldoon rolled back in to break up the count, and the melee was on.
Rayge paired off with Muldoon, and the two found themselves brawling on the floor, and Robertson made his way back into the ring with
Forbes. Forbes hooked up Robertson for his Billion Dollar DDT – but Robertson countered with a backdrop, and one Total Eradication later, it was all over.
Winners: Dirk Robertson & Jayson Rayge (Total Eradication) Time: 6:14 (**) STONE vs. PHARAOH HOBBES Stone made his dramatic return to RAW Carolina on last week’s RAW-C TV, and finally made his return to the ring in Winston-Salem in a match with the most veteran of the recent Suplex Academy grads,
Pharaoh Hobbes. Hobbes has had a bit of success lately teaming with
Brimstone, but he was completely overmatched by Stone in this singles encounter.
With his Carolinas popularity at an all-time high, the crowd was firmly behind Stone, popping like crazy for his every move. He opened up on Hobbes early with a series of stiff clotheslines, and then dropped him with a quick powerslam for a two count.
From there, it was more of the same. One belly to back suplex, one piledriver, and one Rolling Stone later, it was all over. Welcome back, Mr. Stone.
Winner: Stone (Rolling Stone) Time: 2:04 (*) “THE WEAPON” FRANK REYNARD vs. ROADKILL RICK Never exactly a happy guy in the first place, Roadkill Rick was in an even worse mood Wednesday night after losing the Piedmont Title to archrival Eddie Cutlass on Halloween. But former shootfighting champion Frank Reynard has never been accused of being Mr.
Congeniality, either – so you know this one was a slobberknocker. RKR started quickly, thumbing an unsuspecting Reynard in the eye before the bell, and then dropping him with a quick piledriver for a near fall in the opening ten seconds(!!). As RKR pulled Reynard up, the Weapon caught him with a throat thrust, leaving the former
Piedmont Champ gasping for air. Back and forth the match went, a stiff affair with neither man giving an inch. Reynard dropped RKR with a nice Sambo Suplex at the seven minute mark, and seemed about to put it away. He went for the spinning heel kick, the first half of his finisher Rush Hour combo
– but RKR ducked down and spun also, dropping Reynard with a leg sweep. Reynard hopped back to his feet, and caught Roadkill with another throat thrust – and was met with a cloud of black mist as a result(!!).
Reynard staggered backwards, and RKR drove him to the mat with a big spear. Acting quickly, he hopped to the second rope, and came off with a moonsault that stunned Reynard just long enough to get the three count.
Winner: Roadkill Rick (2nd Rope Moonsault) Time: 8:02 (** ½) “CANNONBALL” LUKE DOBBS, “GREASED LIGHTNING” BOBBY PARKER, HOUNDDOG MURPHY, & STEVE FURY vs. JUDGMENT DAY On Halloween night, Judgment Day arrived for RAW Carolina. Internationally successful tag-team Hellspawn (Abyss & Hades). The awesome Sinder Blokk. And the man who once held a special place in the fans’ hearts, The Hangman nee Lawbringer, Thomas Adler.
Carolina fans got their first look at the monstrous quartet at N-KO, and it wasn’t pretty. The match, to be fair, was a massacre.
Handsome young Steve Fury started the match for his contigent, and quickly found himself on the wrong end of a double-team thrust kick from Hellspawn, who quickly tagged in Adl – Dammit, Hangman.
This is going to take some getting used to. Hangman followed with a big boot to the face, sending Fury crashing into the corner. Hangman tagged in Blokk, who followed with a bruising avalanche that moved the ring a good four inches, and then grabbed Fury by his hair, tossing him all the way across the ring into his corner, where he tagged in
Parker.
But the lead instructor for the Suplex Academy fared no better, and neither did The Appalachian Assassin. The four minute mark found
Dobbs in the ring with Abyss, who dropped him with a stiff thrust kick, which sent him barreling backwards into Hangman, who planted him with a very appropriate Cannonball toss. Hades then climbed to the top rope and squashed Dobbs with a guillotine leg drop, and Blokk then followed from the opposite corner with a splash from the second rope.
When Murphy correctly pointed out that there were no tags made during all this, he was sent crashing headlong into the safety railing, and
Fury followed closely behind. What followed left even the most bloodthirsty fans a bit squeamish – Hellspawn grabbed Parker, and the brutal duo delivered a Pandemonium double kick, one from the top rope, the other from below, leaving him out like a light. But they weren’t finished. Blokk then delivered with another splash, this time from the top rope, and that was enough for the three count.
Dobbs raced in to save his partner, but was press-slammed over the ropes to the floor on top of Murphy by Hangman – and then Blokk grabbed his sledgehammer. You know the one, the one with the cinder block head. He brought that hammer crashing down across the neck of
Parker – again and again and again and again. Finally, after the fourth blow, a full score of RAW-C officials finally cleared the ring, and Parker was hustled to a waiting ambulance.
Winners: Judgment Day (Sheer Destruction) Time: 5:01 (*) JACK ANDERSON vs. THE GUY DRESSED ALL IN BLACK The Winston-Salem crowd was sure exactly what to expect here – hell, most of ‘em believed Anderson WAS The Guy Dressed All in
Black. Needless to say, it would be interesting. TGDAB was introduced first, and made his way to the ring dressed – you guessed it – all in black. He called for Bobby Lee
Wilcox’ house mike: TGDAB: Anderson, Anderson, wherefore art thou, Anderson? [There is a hushed silence from the crowd, either eagerly awaiting
Jack or trying to remember whether wherefore means 'where' or 'why' - it kinda works both ways here.]
TGDAB: I'm sorry, everybody. It looks like in a lighter moment, your hopes were raised. You were all thinking that you'd see the proof of the falseness of something you couldn't bear to believe.
Instead, your illusions are shattered. You've heard Jack's protests. You've heard his reaction to this match being booked. You've heard how much his 'reputation' means to him. So why doesn't he come out for it?
The answer is simple. and you know it in your hearts. I'm already here. [There is a shocked pop as he goes to take his mask off, but at the last minute stops.] TGDAB: You should look not upon what I have become. I ask only this. If thou shouldst never see my face again, pray for my soul. [Slumping into a corner, TGDAB rests as the somewhat bewildered referee tries to decide what's happening. A buzz starts to fill the crowd, and it turns into cheers as Felicity Vass, Jack's manager, comes out, from the backstage area, out into the spotlight.]
FV: You! I don't know who you are, but I SURE as heck know you're not Jack. TGDAB: That right, honey? FV: Yes! You see, I know Jack, I know everything about him, and there's NOTHING that you can do to prove that you're him. TGDAB: Nothing? Not even tell you I know about those white cotton panties you're wearing? What if I tell the crowd out here just what you fantasize about when we're making love?
[Felicity blushes, and rushes backstage, tears in her eyes.] TGDAB: Is this a match, then? Go on, ref. Bewildered, Slick Wilson starts his count, going very slowly, giving the fans hope that Jack would make his way out to the arena to put an end to this madness. But it didn’t happen, and the count reached ten – TGDAB scored a countout victory.
Winner: The Guy Dressed All in Black (Countout) Time: 0:00 (DUD) TGDAB left the ring, arms held high overhead in triumph, showered in boos from enraged fans. But one fan took it too far, leaping the rail and assaulting him in the aisle, screaming “How do you like that, Jack?” as he delivered two shots to the back of the masked man’s head with a small metal object, before security finally pulled him off, escorting him to the back in handcuffs. TGDAB continued his long walk to the locker room, somewhat dazed.
DIREWOLF vs. VINCENT CONSTANTINE AUGUSTUS The crowd was still murmuring after that bizarre scene, but they quickly changed their tune with the return of one of their favorites, the bizarre—yet-lovable Direwolf, in his return to action against the man who once poured crazy glue all over his thick mane of chest hair, ‘Empire Man’ Vincent Constantine Augustus.
Without his substantial financial backing, Augustus started the match slowly, seemingly distracted by all that has happened to him of late.
But Direwolf, never alone in a crowd full of fans, was white-hot, blistering the Empire Man early and often with some high-powered offense, including a series of two counts with a variety of powerful suplexes.
VCA rallied at the five minute mark, planting Direwolf with a Rising Star Suplex, and then following with a sloppily applied fujiwara armbar that left the Hairiest Man on Earth a bit too close to the ropes, as he reached out and clamped his teeth around the bottom rope, forcing a break in the hold.
Frustrated, Augustus made another uncharacteristic mistake, and Direwolf dropped him with a butterfly suplex. Following up with a quick elbow drop, Direwolf then went for the kill – he picked
VCA up, and dropped him with an Alpha Male chokeslam. As referee ‘Cottonmouth’ Joe Lynch dropped to make the count, a long-haired man in black denim and a ripped black t-shirt entered the ring from the crowd, and blasted Direwolf with an electric guitar, drawing the immediate disqualification. Observant fans recognize him as Axe Abraham, the bass player in VCA’s new band.
Winner: Direwolf (Disqualification) Time: 5:47 (* ½) As the bell rang, Augustus took the guitar from Axe and laid in a few shots of his own, leaving Direwolf on the brink of unconsciousness.
As Abraham held young Barry Owen III at bay, Augustus produced an electric razor, and proceeded to shave ‘VCA ROCKS’ into the thick, [name withheld] chest hair of his fallen opponent.
Vinnie C., you’ve got some ‘splaining to do: "Well, well, well, Direwolf. You came back and you brought your asinine little brat along with you. Hey kid, how does it feel to know that your dad is such a big-time loser? Hmmmm?"
[VCA picks up the guitar again and rams it again and again into Direwolf's ribs.] "Why did you come back, Direwolf? Did you see Scott Studd and think that you wanted that kind of punishement? Is that it? Is it? Hmmm?"
[VCA kneels down on Direwolf's arm, begins slapping him around the face.] "You're an animal, you're a brute. You should be in the zoo or a freak show, not a wrestling ring. You're PATHETIC." [VCA stands up.] "Don't you or your mongoloid kid ever even be near me again. END...OF...STORY." Augustus left the ring with his cronie to a huge chorus of boos, pelted with trash to boot – and we’ve got two matches to go!!
Before the two title matches, the Mini-Tron came to life with a shot of someone lying face down in a back stairwell, somewhere in the bowels of the Annex. He’s bleeding a bit from a gash in the back of his head, the blood mixing with his red hair – hey!!
We’ve found Jack Anderson!! Slowly, sorely, the Crimson Spitfire rolls over. . . . JA: “What happened?!?” “FAST” EDDIE CUTLASS © vs. MARK ADAMS, Piedmont Heavyweight Title Finally, the wait is over – Eddie Cutlass has captured gold in the Carolinas. His reward?!? A first title defense against one of the sickest, most sadistic souls in the sport – and a former friend turned enemy – Mark Adams.
Having wrestled almost exclusively as of late in tag matches, Adams seemed to feel that the longer the match went, the more Cutlass’ stamina advantage would come into play, and thus went for the jugular early in an all-out blitz.
But Cutlass was more than ready to prove that his title win was not a fluke – that he, as much as anyone, DESERVED to be a champion.
To that end, Cutlass sidestepped an Adams clothesline and dropped him with a drop toe hold, and followed with an elbow drop to the back of the head before heading to the top for the first big move of the match, a flying elbow that scored a two count.
Adams continued his vicious assault, stomping, biting, poking his way to every advantage he could. But every time he seemed about to string something together, Cutlass would counter with a hurricanrana, or a flying headscissors, or something equally as exciting.
At the nine minute mark, referee Jackie Boone, Jr. stepped in front of a superkick intended for Cutlass, and went down hard. The crowd groaned audibly as Adams’ World Tag-Team championship partner
Dave Pitrelli made his way to ringside, handing Adams a small bag, presumably full of the mysterious, DREADED white powder. But the groans turned to POPS real quick-like as the thorn in Adams’ side Stone raced to ringside and caught Pitrelli with a running clothesline just as he handed Adams the bag, sending the powder flying everywhere.
Stone pushed Bobby Lee Wilcox out of his chair, using the furniture to crack Pitrelli across the brow, busting him wide open. Cutlass tapped
Adams with a dropkick, sending him out to the floor – and into the waiting arms of Stone. A wild melee ensued, as the crowd exploded as Stone and Adams finally got it on one on one. A back elbow from
Stone busted Adams’ nose open again, sending him staggering backwards – and Cutlass flying out of the ring to the floor with a plancha.
Cutlass rolled Adams back into the ring, where Boone was just beginning to stir, and the match continued. Stone stayed at ringside, as did Pitrelli, keeping a wary eye on one another, and the match stretched past fifteen minutes.
At the sixteen minute mark, Stone couldn’t help himself, and reached in to trip up Adams to the crowd’s delight. Boone came over to admonish Stone, who pled innocence – giving Pitrelli the chance to slide one of his plethora of tag-team title belts (I think it was the RAW Atlantic) into the ring. Adams saw it, and caught
Cutlass with a lowblow, looking to DDT him on the belt. But Cutlass powered out, working into a full-nelson – and dropped Adams face-first onto that metal belt with a Guarantee. The pinfall followed three seconds later.
Winner: Eddie Cutlass (Guarantee on Gold) Time: 17:04 (*** ¾) CASANOVA BEETHOVEN © vs. MARC TOWER, Carolina Heavyweight Title The night had seen a little bit of everything, and now it was time for the main event. Marc Tower had earned the right to be here by pinning
Beethoven in that horrific cage match Halloween eve on RAW-C TV. Beethoven has seemingly ignored that fact in recent interviews, which hasn’t set well with Tower.
Beethoven may possess a slight size and strength advantage over Tower – but no one has an advantage in toughness over Tower. He’s got that market cornered. The most dominant Carolinas Television champion in recent memory opened up with an amazing barrage on the purple-haired ho lover, dropping him with a Steel City Slam just two minutes in, sending him out to the floor to regroup.
The crowd loved every bit of this; with Thomas Adler’s recent ‘transformation’, only Jack Anderson and Eddie Cutlass are cheered as heartily as Marc Tower these days. And he did his best to give them a good show as he could, continuing his assault – until the stronger Beethoven countered a DDT attempt with a Northern
Lights Suplex, nearly scoring the three count. Beethoven is not the champion by accident, though, as few possess as devastating an arsenal as he. With utter scorn for both Tower and the crowd, the arrogant champion dropped Tower with a tombstone piledriver, and followed that right up with a savage Cherry Blossom
Bomb for a two and one-eighth count. He then locked in a Texas Cloverleaf, looking to earn a rare tap-out victory. But Tower was able to make it to the ropes, and as the hold was broken, reached up and somehow twisted Beethoven around and hooked him into his own submission hold, the Pittsburgh Strangler(!!). But they were still too close to the ropes, and Beethoven was able to grab the bottom strand before too much damage was done.
After over 15 minutes of action, Tower could feel the title within his grasp, and went for it. He hoisted Beethoven up into an attempted
Tower of Terror – only to see the savvy champ kick him heel first right in the eye(!!), sending Beethoven crashing down to the relative safety of the mat below.
Tower’s eye swelled almost instantly, surely to look terrible tomorrow, but he didn’t care. Beethoven caught him with a rabbit punch just above the swelling, opening him up but good. Tower went to wipe the blood out of his eye – and was instantly scooped up and slammed with a T-Bone Suplex. This time it was
Beethoven who could feel it, and he did not disappoint – pulling a wounded Tower up onto his shoulder, and delivering the Symphony.
Tower’s head bounced off the mat, and Beethoven made the cover, scoring the three count to win a great, great match.
Winner: Casanova Beethoven (Symphony) Time: 19:48 (****) ---- © RAW Carolina, MMI. http://www.yahoogroups.com/group/RAWCarolina
