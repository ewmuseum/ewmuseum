Super Sunday, Bloody Sunday 02.03.2002

4 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Rams versus Patriots?!? No way!! The REAL mismatch of the day looked to be the Super Bowl versus RAW Carolina: Super Sunday, Bloody
Sunday!! The annual Dorton Arena tradition saw five big matches
– and then the big game – and then even more exciting action!! Oh, yeah – and free beer!! Results:
TERRY JACOBS & JUSTIN HARPER vs. CHRISTIAN DEMONSTONE & JESSIE MULDOON
Well, this one promised to be brutal, given the history between these four men. Jacobs and Harper have developed into quite the tag-team of late, much more than can be said for their opponents. Not only have
Demonstone and Muldoon failed to function well as a team, they haven’t been able to stop fighting each other long enough to try. And that’s exactly what happened in Raleigh. As Jacobs and Harper looked on from the ring, Bobby Lee Wilcox introduced their battling opponents – who came out through the curtain brawling(!!).
Then, out of the crowd, the recently ‘resurrected’ Mayhem joined the fray, and the three big brawlers bludgeoned each other all the way back to the locker room area, before this match could even get started. Rookie official Hank Jackson was left with no choice but to call the match off, and Harper and Jacobs headed back towards the locker room disappointed, as were the raucous Raleigh crowd.
Decision: No Match JACK DOE vs. “SCHOLARLY” SCOTT McBRIDE After a few clashes with fans at house shows last week, rookie Jack
Doe looks to have come a bit. . .unhinged. Proclaiming himself a
‘True American Hero’, he has set his sights on ridding the
Carolinas of Jack Anderson, a tough task indeed – just ask Bull Johnson. But in the interim, Doe looked to win the love of the Carolina fans, who haven’t exactly taken to his. . . .mundane ring style. And if Doe wanted to excite the fans with his in-ring work, having the technically-sound, mat-based Scott McBride as an opponent is exactly
NOT what the doctor ordered.
Even given the fans’ disdain for him, Doe was out to win the fans over, a fact that was apparent early on in his nervous, tentative manner in the ring – allowing McBride to score a couple of two counts with a nice schoolboy rollup followed up by a small package.
But Doe rallied, and took control of the match down on the mat with a strong side headlock. Looking to the crowd for support, Doe was met with that anathemic phrase: ‘Boooooooring’.
Doe didn’t like that one bit, and bounced to his feet to jaw at the front row – before thinking twice of it, and heading back to the mat with a bodyscissors that would make Doug Simpkins proud. When the crowd started their chant again, Doe got back to his feet, staring at them, bewildered – allowing McBride to bulldog him from behind for another two count.
McBride then went for a DDT, but Doe countered with a rather flashy
Northern Lights Suplex, which STILL didn’t elicit much response from the crowd. Frustrated, having had enough, Doe set McBride up and drove him into the mat with a Ticket to Stardom cradle piledriver, good enough to end this match right there.
Winner: Jack Doe (Ticket to Stardom) Time: 4:02 (* ½) MACABRE vs. MAYHEM The bandaged monster Unholy signed on with The Network a few weeks back, and immediately let it be known that he was after the Bicoastal
Title held by Macabre, a holdover championship from the waning days of
RAW Atlantic. Macabre subsequently lost that title to Christian
Demonstone, and Unholy shifted his focus – all the way still wanting a piece of Macabre. And when Unholy was revealed to be former
RAW-A superstar Mayhem – thought to be dead after a tragic car accident – it all made sense. RAW Atlantic was a bad experience for Mayhem, and he was out to make everyone involved pay.
It’s still a bit unclear just what soured Steven Thorne on RAW-A in such a manner, but it’s clear he wanted to maim Macabre.
With little regard for the rules he punished Macabre early on, and with official Hank Jackson – fast gaining a reputation as a man who lets a lot of things go – assigned this contest, it got ugly quick.
Having exposed a turnbuckle early on, Mayhem looked to powerbomb
Macabre right into the bare steel – but the Goth mystery man somehow punched his way out into a sunset flip for a two and thisclose count after four minutes of pounding. Macabre then took to the top rope for a textbook missile dropkick for another close call, and then back to the top with Mayhem’s head in tow for a tornado DDT that got a three count – but Mayhem got a foot on the rope juuuuust before Jackson slapped the mat the third time – and the mat continued.
Back and forth the action went, with Macabre keeping the big man off balance with fast-paced stuff, staying just out of arm’s length, until the eight minute mark. Macabre came off the top with a double axe-handle, and, in an incredible show of strength, Mayhem caught him in mid-air, dropping him back on the top turnbuckle, and following with a Terrorizer two-handed chokebomb for the three count.
Winner: Mayhem (Terrorizer) Time: 8:11 (** ½) After the match, Macabre sat on the mat, brooding over his defeat. . . . Then, a voice over the PA. . . Voice: Hey, Johnny... [Macabre looks around, can't see anything...] Voice: Johnny, you know who it is. Johnny, come on, Johnny, listen to me. [Macabre puts his hands over his ears.] Voice: Johnny, it's time to stop playing now. Time to end this little masquerade of yours. Mom's getting worried.
Aaron's getting worried. [Then from out of the curtain...] Voice: I'm getting worried. [Fans pop big for RAW tag-team legend Xander Alpha. Bigger pop when the fans realize that Macabre is Johnny Alpha, the other half of the incredible tag-team duo who seemed to have met their end swanton bombing off the wing of an aeroplane onto the Merseysiders in RAW some time back.]
XA: Johnny, you're scaring Mom. She doesn't like seeing you like this. And she's upset that you haven't called in so long...not since you've started this whole...Macabre thing.
[Xander gets closer to the ring.] XA: Come on Johnny, time to stop play acting now. Come down here and give your brother a hug. [Macabre looks confused then goes by the ropes...and then slingshots himself over and onto Xander Alpha punching away at him until security break them up. . .]
PAUL SHERIDAN © vs. JACK ANDERSON, Television Title I could recap all that’s gone on between these two, but there’s no need. Few stories have ever captivated the Carolinas the way the mystery of The Guy Dressed All in Black did. And now
– Sheridan. Anderson. Add in a title on the line. . . .Oh, my.
The match started fast, it stayed fast, and it ended fast. Back and forth action, neither man able to gain an advantage for just a fraction of a second. Reversals, counters, near falls – did I say near falls?!? How about seventeen two counts – nine scored by Anderson, eight by Sheridan.
At the seven minute mark, Sheridan caught the Crimson Spitfire with a lowblow, dropped him on the top turnbuckle, and faster than you can say ‘Fireball Special’, put Anderson on the mat with his very own hurricanrana from the top rope. But somehow, SOMEWAY –
ANDERSON KICKED OUT!! (tm, Sean O’Brien.)
At the ten minute mark, Anderson returned the favor. First sidestepping an errant missile dropkick, he planted his rival with his own Fireball Special – but again, the move only got a two count, drawing an audible sigh from the Raleigh crowd, by now on the edge of their seats with every move and countermove.
Anderson’s lovely second Felicity Vass grew concerned after this, and began letting her man know just where the clock stood, fully aware of the fifteen minute time limit for Television Title matches here in the Carolinas. The action heated up, each man making tactical mistakes in an effort to put the other away before the allotted time.
At the twelve minute mark, newcomer Jack Doe wandered out to ringside
– immediately eliciting a BOOOORING chant from some smart aleck fans in the front row. He walked over to Felicity, and – well, we THINK he was hitting on her. Rather poorly, I’ll add. She just laughed in his face, walking around to the other side of the ring.
Meanwhile, back at the ranch –- er, back in the ring, Anderson executed a picture perfect superplex. Sheridan bounced off the mat, his body limp. Anderson turned away from the Television champ – just in time to see Doe blindside Felicity with a steel chair (in the back of the head!!). The comely young beauty went down motionless as
Anderson watched from the ring in horror. Doe looked down at his victim, seemingly proud of himself. Then, as if he just realized just what he’d done, he dropped the chair and took off, racing out of the arena. The bell rang, signaling the time limit draw. Anderson thought about chasing after Doe, but thought better of it, choosing instead to tend to his fallen fiancée.
Decision: Time Limit Draw Time: 15:00 (****) MARC TOWER & STONE vs. PURGATORY ([name withheld] wrote this’n) With The Network's JACKIE BOONE, JR., present at ringside, two of the front-running teams in next month's MARK ADAMS CUP Tag Team Tournament collided as undefeated Windy City RAW Tag Team Champions DEMENTIA and
SYNN squared off against former Carolina Heavyweight Champion MARC
TOWER and his partner, STONE. Though the advantage belonged to
Purgatory in size and experience, it was Tower and Stone who had surprise in their favor as they were accompanied to the ring by
Stone's long-time friend and former tag-team partner, "HURRICANE" KIRK
MACLEAN, and his current partner, STEVIE RAY MURDOCH.
Maclean and Murdoch, RAW's reigning Unified Tag Team Champions, proved more than a match for Boone's attempts at interference, preventing
RAW-C TV's Executive Producer from giving any assistance to Purgatory in this match against Tower and Stone.
With outside interference virtually in check, RAW-C's "heroes" immediately set out to put truth to their words by showing Dementia and Synn the consequences of defying Marc Tower or The Elements. A series of quick tags managed to throw the much more experienced
Purgatory off their game and, after several near falls, Tower nailed
Dementia with a "Tower of Terror" that incapacitated the big man while
Stone hit Synn with not one but TWO "Rolling Stone" running Rikishi drivers for the three count!!
Winners: Marc Tower and Stone (Rolling Stone (x2)) Time: 6:24 (**) Incensed at what he considered a personal defeat, Jackie Boone verbally abused the two members of Purgatory as they made their way back up the ramp, stopping only to grab a microphone at the top of the ramp as he made this statement:
"Boys, The Network has been really good to you two...but time after time after time you've let us down. Now I've done a lot of thinkin' about this decision and I'm pretty sure it's the right one to make...
So YOU'RE FIRED!!" And, with that, RAW-C's Piedmont Champion, DAVE "DREADMIND" PITRELLI, and Boone's personal bodyguard, the former ultimate fighter who calls himself MAYHEM, burst through the curtain with steel chairs in hand and laid out Dementia and Synn while their backs were turned, beating both men down until event security managed to pull the two demented giants off their unconscious victims. As Purgatory was taken away by paramedics, Jackie
Boone could be seen rubbing his hands in anticipation of what could be
The Network's BIGGEST night ever!!
“X-PRESS” DAVID JERICHO vs. CASANOVA BEETHOVEN With just fifteen minutes to go before the kick-off of the big game, it was time for the main event of the first session: David Jericho and Casanova Beethoven would meet one on one to settle their red-hot issue, which had been growing exponentially since Jericho’s surprise return to the Carolinas a few weeks ago.
This contest matched up two former Piedmont Champions, and was as evenly contested as you’d think. Jericho started off fast, scoring a series of two counts as he mixed in a variety of suplexes with some off the top rope moves, including a spectacular Macho Man
Elbow that was thisclose to getting a three. But Beethoven reversed an attempted pumphandle slam with a rake to the eyes and fell on top for two, and followed up with a short legdrop and took control.
Beethoven then dominated the next few grueling minutes, really putting
Jericho through the ringer with a series of moves designed to soften up the ho-lovin’ superstar’s neck for his tombstone finisher, The Symphony. He even went so far as to have one of his bevy of hos distract Slick Wilson – not that that was hard, given Slick’s penchant for the ladies – long enough to drill Jericho in the back of the neck with the ringbell.
But the X-Press battled back, spurred on by a crowd who has really warmed to the former rulebreaker in recent weeks. And when Jericho sidestepped a blind charge into the corner and came back out with a bulldog, everyone in Raleigh felt the momentum shift.
Jericho motioned to the crowd – he was going for the kill. He grabbed Beethoven in a facelock, looking for the X-Press Way – but out of nowhere, the bigger Beethoven somehow twisted him up onto his shoulder and drilled him into the mat with a Symphony. 1-2-3.
Winner: Casanova Beethoven (Symphony) Time: 12:22 (***) With that, it was time for the game. The Carolina fans, always a sucker for the underdog, were loving things early on as the Patriots jumped out to a 7-3 lead when Ty Law returned a Kurt Warner interception. And as halftime rolled around with New England still up, 10-3, the fans began to wonder if ANYTHING were possible tonight.
During a commercial break in the halftime show featuring U2, the
Mini-Tron suddenly switched to static, and then to a shot of RAW-C
President “Showboat” Eddie Cannon standing alongside the
Unified World Tag-Team Champions, Kirk Maclean and Stevie Ray Murdoch: EC: Glad you boys could make it back down here for this one. KM: Wouldn’t miss it for the world, Eddie. EC: You saw what happened in the first half tonight with Muldoon and
Demonstone. SRM: Yup. EC: It’s a shame the fans don’t get to see Jacobs and Harper – I think they’ve got a ton of potential as a tag-team. Championship potential, in fact. KM: Is that right? EC: It is. Whaddya say boys – care to put those Carolina tag straps on the line – since you’re here and all?
SRM: Hey – why don’t we give Jacobs and Harper a shot at the titles – tonight?!? EC: That’s what I just said. KM: We’ll do it!! The fans popped big-time as they realized that while you at home were getting banal halftime analysis ‘highlighted’ by a Terry
Bradshaw – Paul McCartney singalong, they were getting: “HURRICANE” KIRK MACLEAN & STEVIE RAY MURDOCH © vs. TERRY JACOBS & JUSTIN HARPER, Carolina Tag-Team Titles Neither of these teams came to Raleigh expecting to face one another, but at least Jacobs and Harper came expecting to wrestle SOMEBODY
– Maclean and Murdoch were planning to spend the evening as emotional support for Stone. But being the champs means never backing down from a challenge, and they accepted the gauntlet thrown down by
Eddie Cannon with gusto.
Jacobs and Harper seemed determined to make the most of their sudden opportunity, and worked efficiently as a team, cutting the ring in half and making quick tags, focusing most of their early efforts on an as-of-late unfocused Stevie Ray Murdoch. But The Mullet refused to stay down, and it’s been said that no one takes a beating like
Stevie Ray, and he proved them right on this night. Harper looked to go for the kill, lifting Murdoch up into his California Dreaming chokeslam – but Murdoch got loose by kicking his heel right into the rookie’s eye. Harper staggered, clutching his eye, and fell back into his corner to tag in Jacobs.
Murdoch missed the tag and kept after Harper, but was taken down from behind by Jacobs. This brought Maclean back into the ring to a huge pop, and he immediately grabbed Harper and planted him with a tornado DDT, before heading to the corner. Maclean launched off the top, crashing down onto Harper with an Eye of the Storm – just as Jacobs raced across the ring and nearly beheaded Murdoch with a Charlotte Cyclone.
Maclean covered Harper – Jacobs covered Murdoch. Slick Wilson slapped the mat once – twice – three times. The crowd was unsure just what had happened, and both Maclean and Jacobs felt they had earned victory for their teams – but Wilson raised the hand of Jacobs, explaining that he and Murdoch were still the legal men, and we had new Carolina Tag-Team Champions.
Murdoch and Maclean were rightfully upset, but popped the crowd one last time by shaking the hands of the new Carolina champs, raising their hands high in victory.
Winners (and new Carolina Tag-Team Champions): Justin Harper & Terry
Jacobs (Charlotte Cyclone)
Time: 7:21 (** ½) The arena was still buzzing over the title switch as the second half began, and that buzz built into a dull roar by the time the Patriots completed one of the greatest upsets in Super Bowl history when Adam
Vinatieri’s field goal sailed through the uprights as time expired, giving Tom Brady’s Bunch a 20-17 win.
And following all that, it was time for OUR second half. LA ESTOQUE vs. DAMIEN Well, that was the scheduled match. Damien Barnett came out to the ring first, his face a mixture of arrogant confidence and downright disgust that he was wrestling in a promotion so far
‘beneath’ him. But then Bobby Lee Wilcox made a disturbing announcement – neither Damien’s opponent, La
Estoque, or Mike Steele had made it to the arena yet. Combine both men’s issues with The Network with the manner in which they eliminated Steele’s former partner Mike Diamond, and you can see why Mel Kilpatrick was so frantic with worry.
So, as he had before the tag title match, “Showboat” Eddie Cannon arranged for ‘suitable’ opposition: a large, powerful looking man dressed from head to toe in black, including a full mask (OH NO!!! not another one). Damien, expecting a match against a fellow cruiserweight, was clearly taken aback, and the big man pummeled him from the get-go.
The match didn’t last long at all, and a buzz ran through Dorton
Arena; was this the mystery man who earlier in the week had promised a return to RAW Carolina? And if so, who was he? Regardless, he completely destroyed Damien, finishing him off in three minutes with a
Pedigree-type maneuver for a convincing victory.
Winner: Mystery Man (Pedigree) Time: 2:52 (*) FREELOADERS vs. KINGS OF HARDCORE ([name withheld], Master of the RAW Universe should get due credit for this one.) Winners: Kings of Hardcore (Disqualification) Time: 8:23 (**) ROADKILL RICK vs. “ERADICATOR” DIRK ROBERTSON (Double duty for the new daddy, writing this one, too.) The fans were on their feet for this next match as former RAW-C Piedmont Champion ROADKILL RICK made his triumphant supercard return to the Carolinas to square off against The Network's DIRK "THE
ERADICATOR" ROBERTSON in what soon degenerated into a Pier Six Brawl that saw both men hit their opponent with everything but the kitchen sink to secure a win for their respective side in the RAW-C/Network war that concludes with tonight's main event between "Showboat" Eddie

Texan rose to the occasion, reminding the fans that, like him or not, he's still one tough SOB.
After nearly eight minutes of back and forth action, Robertson pulled a new trick out of his back, unveiling a new finisher he calls "The

Managing to kick out just as Referee Kevin Smith's hand was about to hit the mat a third time, it was a revitalized RKR that won the match after foiling a Jackie Boone interference attempt from the outside with a faceful of black mist and hitting Robertson with the
"Texas Speedbump," forcing the former Television Champion to tap out while Boone looked on from the floor.
Winner: Roadkill Rick (Texas Speedbump) Time: 8:23 (***) After the match, as Robertson and Boone exchanged angry words over the failed run-in that cost "The Eradicator" the match, Boone had these words to say.
"Dirk, Dirk, Dirk...it was an accident, man. Don't sweat it. Of course you're fired for screwing up AGAIN but you never really fit in with the rest of us anyhow, now did you?"
And, as Robertson struggled to find the words to answer his now former
"manager," Boone's newest protege, Mayhem, made yet another appearance at rindside, blindsiding "The Eradicator" with a steel pipe and leaving him unconscious in the ring as Jackie Boone looked on and smiled.
“DREADMIND” DAVE PITRELLI © vs. MIKE STEELE, in a Steel Cage for the Piedmont Heavyweight Title Pitrelli hit the cage first, accompanied by his manager Jackie Boone,
Jr. and sidekick Mayhem, ready and able to finally put his issue with
Mike Steele to rest. The crowd was still a bit concerned, though:
Estoque hadn’t made it to the arena for his match, and Steele was with him. Could the new mystery man have been responsible for that, or was it (the more likely scenario) The Network?
Well, whatever it may have been, Steele’s music started up as scheduled – but he didn’t step through the curtain:
Estoque did. Armed with an official-looking type document, he stepped into the cage and handed it to Bobby Lee Wilcox, who informed the crowd that it was the contract for his Piedmont Title shot, earned last week on RAW-C TV. Pitrelli looked across the ring at the undersized luchadore and readily agreed.
Estoque charged across the ring just as the bell rang – and was immediately clotheslined nearly out of his mask by the
6’8”, 330 pound monster Pitrelli. Pitrelli then grabbed the Mexican Sensation and tossed him javelin-like into the side wall of the cage, before picking him back up to grate his face (er, mask)
across the wall, drawing a crimson mask beneath Estoque’s. . .well, crimson mask. Facing another in a series of overmatched opponents, Pitrelli had to be the biggest favorite of the night this side of the Rams. But wait a second. . . .Estoque showed a lot of heart, and continued to kick out of one pinfall attempt out of another, only to get slammed right back to the mat again by a dominating Pitrelli.
Finally, Pitrelli tired of toying with the masked man, and headed to the top, looking to end the match with his Hatred Within top rope spear. But as Pitrelli turned around, Estoque bounced to his feet and crotched Pitrelli on the turnbuckle with a leaping heel trip, and then followed him up to the top to deliver a forward Russian Leg Sweep in a modified. . .Guarantee from the top rope?!?
Pitrelli’s face bounced off the mat, and Estoque rolled him up in a small package – 1-2-3. New Piedmont Champion, as the crowd went nuts!! Mayhem made a move to enter the ring, but he was assaulted by Mike Steele who bounded down the aisle to make the save
– just as Estoque pulled off the mask – to reveal
“FAST” EDDIE CUTLASS!!!! Winner: “Estoque” Eddie Cutlass (Top Rope Guarantee) Time: 5:32 (**) As the cage lifted, Boone and his troops retreated to regroup for the main event, and Vernon St. Jean climbed into the ring to have some words with the once again Piedmont Champion:

Or so he said. Cody came to the ring first, still looking as trim and fit as he did in his days as an active competitor as “Dangerous” Dennis Cody. The former NASN executive brought with him an entourage worthy of a heavyweight prize fight: Jackie Boone, Jr., Dave Pitrelli, and Mayhem all accompanied their boss to the ring.
Eddie Cannon entered the ring next, to a thunderous ovation. The greatest champion in the history of the Carolinas looked poised and ready to defend the promotion he helped to make, and had an entourage of his own: Stone, Marc Tower, Mike Steele, and Eddie Cutlass all showed up to support THEIR boss. Kirk Maclean. Tommy Victory. “Thunderbolt” Jack Sampson. The champions of the Carolinas had returned in an awesome show of support. Cannon seemed touched by the support he received from the crowd, and completely missed Cody sneaking up on him and dropping him with a low blow from behind just as Studd called for the bell. Cody went for a quick cover, and Studd made a two count that was –just- a little fast for many in attendance.
Showing the form that made him a World Champion in the old IWA, Cody worked the Showboat over, targeting the President’s right knee, looking to soften him up for his famous standing figure four finisher.
With every twist and bend of Cannon’s knee, Studd was right there, looking for the submission. (A bit too fervently?!?)
Cody went for a standard figure four at the four minute mark, but
Cannon kicked him into the corner, and when the Dallas native rebounded, the Showboat dropped him with a trademark belly to belly suplex. A big chorus of boos rose out of the crowd after that one, and Cannon had a few words for Studd, as well. But the big man remained nonplussed, and made another two count after Cannon dropped Cody with a big piledriver.
At the seven minute mark, Cannon went for a vertical suplex, but
Cody’s work on his knee earlier in the match paid off when
Cannon’s knee collapsed, bringing Cody crashing down on top of him for a two count. Cody then followed with a short fistdrop, and then motioned for Jackie Boone, Jr. to grab the Carolina Title belt from the timekeeper’s table and toss it to him. Boone did so
– but Studd intercepted(!!). Looking at Cody, then looking at Cannon, Studd seemed unsure of what to do – and then turned and clocked Cody with the belt, laying him out cold(!!!!). Studd tossed the belt back to the floor, and Cannon picked up the limp Cody and dropped him facefirst onto the top turnbuckle – a little piece of Memphis Hell.
Pitrelli and Mayhem went to hit the ring – but were cut off by Stone and Steele(!!). Cannon made the cover – Studd made the count – and Dennis Cody was down for the three, out of RAW
Carolina forever. And there was much rejoicing.
