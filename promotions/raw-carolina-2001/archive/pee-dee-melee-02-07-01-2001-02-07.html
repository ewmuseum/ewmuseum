RAW Carolina — Card Archive

Pee Dee Melee 02/07/01

7 February 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
RAW Carolina returned to the banks of the mighty Pee Dee River to one of its’ regional hotbeds, Florence, South Carolina, for the annual supershow known as the Pee Dee Melee. Nearly 4000 fans packed into the
Florence Civic Center, extremely pumped for a night of hard-hitting action. Results:
“AWESOME” ALEC PRICE & GOLIATH vs. CODE RED The nasty ex-Marines Code Red (Sgt. Dan Lorenzo & The Leatherneck) had injured Jeff Bennett, one half of Coast to Coast, causing them to be stripped of the Carolina Tag-Team Titles. Alec Price, Bennett’s partner, has been on a quest for vengeance over the last few weeks, until he, too, was injured by Code Red at Super Sunday. Wanted both men in the ring at the same time, Price went out and found the biggest, baddest partner he could find: 7 foot Israeli Superstar, Goliath.
Code Red dominated early, as Price and Goliath were teaming together for the first time, and didn’t have nearly the chemistry of their polished rivals. The ad-hoc fan favorites finally found a rhythm, however, and a donnybrook ensued.
The ending came with Code Red attempting to spike powerbomb Price; Goliath made the save, catching his partner and tossing him back towards Code Red. Price somehow reversed himself in mid-air to catch
Leatherneck in an awesome Tornado Bulldog while Goliath clotheslined
Lorenzo out to the floor. Price scored the three count on the giant masked man to gain some measure of revenge.
Winners: Alec Price & Goliath (Tornado Bulldog) Time: 8:38 (** ½) CHUCK vs. MARK GOLDEN Mark Golden entered the ring for the next match as the fans buzzed, awaiting the arrival of one of their own: The mystery fanboy known only as “Chuck”, who would score a RAW Carolina contract if he could win this match.
Chuck entered the ring – not from the locker room, but by hopping over the safety railing from his ringside seat, leaving his Coke and popcorn at the timekeeper’s table – to the strains of Def Leppard’s “Demolition
Man”, wearing a heavy black trench coat that covered up his odd wrestling apparel. His eyes looked huge behind a pair oversized eyeglasses, barely tinted red.
He’s got a mirthful smile, a little devious, a little stupid, but altogether friendly. Clutching a microphone in his hand, he stared across at his opponent and sadly shook his head:
“You were a hero.” His voice was a little shaky, it’s not like he’s used to talking in front of a room full of this many people.
“Yes, at one time... the people here respected you. You could beat anyone! Children at home would pretend to be you and claim your victories as their own. I had this one toy of you, Mark Golden, a little posable thingie with the kung- fu grip. I would let him beat up all my Star Wars toys…”
He actually looked quite fond of this memory, despite his dislike of the man that figure would come to represent.
“But look at you now? LOOK AT YOU! A whiny, self-indulgent loser who can’t even look a child in the eyes. You sold us out! Wah! So tonight… I do what every child who watched you fall from grace wishes they could do…”
Chuck removed the glasses slowly, almost ceremoniously from his face with one hand, curling them away to reveal a pair of deep set, ugly brown eyes.
“Stronger than a decaying corpse in summer!” He flexed. “Fatter than Bill Gate’s Wallet!” Chuck jiggled his gut. “Able to navigate 150 channels of cable television without a single sound. It’s a nerd. . .”
He paused for a moment, wondering if he wrote this script himself. In fact, he was deciding already that it needed a rewrite.
“He’s a pain.” All right. So he wouldn’t do this again. “I am. . .SUPERFAN!” Chuck threw off his jacket quickly, placing his hands on his hips as a bright red cape unrolled down his back. Glancing from side to side, he waited patiently for the laughs he prayed he would get from this. The crowd was silent, unsure what to make of this new breed of superhero.
It began with one fan, and soon filled the arena: The howls of laughter – they loved Chuck.
Golden, however, was a different story. He charged the sub 6 foot, 350- ish pound Chuck, attacking him from behind as he removed his cape to reveal a black RAW Carolina t-shirt, black denim shorts, and sneakers, the right one perpetually untied. Golden pounded the younger man down to the mat, and things looked bleak for Superfan.
Golden paused in his assault to glance over the crowd, as if to say “Where’s your hero now?!?” At the moment, Chuck struck. A low blow to the groin sent Golden crashing to the mat. Chuck followed with a diving headbutt to the same region, and Golden howled in pain.
Then, Chuck bounced off the far ropes, rolled into a forward somersault, back on his feet in front of Golden. He pounded his chest like Tarzan, emitting the same primal scream, then dropped his full weight across Golden’s chest. The fans ate it up, and nearly had a coronary when Chuck scored his first two count.
Feeling himself solidly in control of this match, Chuck slowly climbed to the top rope. As he balanced precariously there, facing the crowd, he spread his cape for dramatic effect, shouting “Up, Up, and Away!!” before launching into an attempted moonsault.
But Chuck slipped before launching, the ropes perhaps giving way when faced with a man of his proportions. Instead of the moonsault, he fell onto Golden back first, in a sort of senton bomb.
The crowd responded by chanting YOU CHUCKED UP!!! YOU CHUCKED UP!!!! And, surprise of surprises, the “senton” was enough for the three count, and the RAW Carolina contract. Chuck leaped about the ring in orgasmic joy as the fans’ roared their approval. Finally, he left the ring and returned to his seat behind the timekeeper, where he signed autographs the rest of the evening.
Winner: Chuck (“Senton Bomb”) Time: 3:28 (**, for the sheer drama) RAW Carolina president “Showboat” Eddie Cannon then hit the ring, dropping a large manila folder with Chuck at ringside. He then took the house mike for an announcement: Tonight’s scheduled first round matches for the Carolina Tag-Team Titles had been postponed indefinitely, and the entire tag-team championship division in RAW
Carolina is going dark for an indeterminate period. Cannon then stuck around ringside for the introductions for the next match, perhaps to get another look at Misty Dawn, who brought down her man John
Stonecutter while winking and blowing a very naughty kiss at Cannon. HOUNDDOG MURPHY vs. JOHN STONECUTTER Stonecutter was set to take on the self-styled Appalachian Assassin, crowd favorite Hounddog Murphy. Murphy had taken some time last week to point out how similar he and Stonecutter were, but the 350 pounder was hearing none of it. Stonecutter thoroughly dominated the slack- jawed yokel, to the point where the fans became waaay more interested in the scantily-clad Misty than in the match itself. Misty, the new apple of President Cannon’s eye, was wearing –
Oh,yeah, where was I?!? Right, the match. Stonecutter tossed Murphy around, turnbuckle to turnbuckle en route to a rather easy victory.
After a nasty powerbomb into a top turnbuckle, Stonecutter hit the
Stonecutter, a top rope swinging Diamond Cutter that many in the arena felt crippled poor Hounddog.
Winner: John Stonecutter (Stonecutter) Time: 3:31 (**) COREY “THE COOKER” COOKSON vs. “THE BOSTON MANGLER” ADONIS MORGAN Legendary Canadian star Corey “The Cooker” Cookson received a surprisingly lukewarm reaction from the Florence faithful as he hit the ring for his match against powerful youngster Adonis Morgan. The fans seemed to be reacting to the sheer bravado and arrogance that the
Cooker has shown in recent interviews. Whatever their reaction, Cookson was still the fan favorite in a match-up with the hated Morgan, managed as usual by the even more hated Napoleon Jones.
Any interference Jones would have normally provided was more than negated by Cookson’s second, former tecno luchador Horatio Hornblower.
Free from distractions from their cornermen, Cookson and Morgan proceeded to put on quite a show – Morgan using his size and power to his advantage, while Cookson countered with his diverse style and vast experience to counter.
Morgan began to target Cookson’s oft-injured neck late in the match, getting several two counts with a series of piledrivers and DDTs.
Cookson rebounded, however, and positioned Morgan for the Cooker Spaniel, his reverse Franchiser. Morgan, sensing the danger, reversed it into an Ace Crusher for another two count. Frustrated, Morgan climbed the ropes into uncharted territory for himself. He missed a flying elbow, allowing Cookson to pop up and successfully hit the
Cooker Spaniel for the pin. After the match, as Cookson celebrated by waving his Canadian flag, Morgan attacked him from behind, laying him out with the flagpole, then breaking it over Hornblower’s head before leaving the ring to a cacophony of boos.
Winner: Corey Cookson (Cooker Spaniel) Time: 9:21 (***) “ALL-AMERICAN” CHAMP DURHAM vs. JOSEPH NIGHTHORSE The next match featured two men often mentioned in the same breath with
Casanova Beethoven and Adonis Morgan as the future of professional wrestling, former NCAA wrestling champion Champ Durham and former tag champion Joseph Nighthorse. Their match was excellent, and served to remind us that while their futures are indeed extremely bright, their present looks pretty good, as well.
Back and forth action in this one, as Durham’s suplex-based offense kept Nighthorse off his feet in the early going. Nighthorse was able to reverse a belly-to-belly attempt, and began to wear down Durham with his new, submission-style, mat-based offense. After ten minutes of such action, Nighthorse rolled through an attempted Victory Roll, deftly applying his anklebreaker submission hold in the process.
Durham held on for nearly two minutes, before finally tapping out. After the match, Nighthorse rolled out of the ring, and walked over to
Chuck, who had spent the match holding up a JUMPIN’ JOEY KNIGHT – HORSE
OF A DIFFERENT COLOR sign. Nighthorse grabbed the microphone from ring announcer Bobby Lee Wilcox, and got right in Chuck’s face:
“You got dreams, do ya, Fat Boy?!? Well, I had dreams once, too. And look where it got me!! Two broken ankles and a shattered career!! So, I’m gonna go ahead and shatter your dreams before it gets too far!! It’s for your own good. The longer you fool yourself, the more it hurts when it’s over!!
So let me just shatter your dreams right now!!” Nighthorse leaned across the rail and sucker-punched Chuck right in the nose, busting him open. Nighthorse stalked back to the locker room as
Chuck bled in his popcorn, stunned.
Winner: Joseph Nighthorse (Anklebreaker) Time: 12:31 (*** ½) CASANOVA BEETHOVEN vs. “TRAINWRECK” CALEB JONES vs. JACOB LEGACY The next match was a triple threat match, as the purple-haired, ‘ho- lovin’ superstar Casanova Beethoven battled the Chicago monster “Trainwreck” Caleb Jones and suplex master Jacob Legacy for a shot at Robbie Diamond’s Piedmont Title.
The three became entwined last week at Super Sunday, when Beethoven cost Jones his shot at the gold by attacking Diamond and later injured
Legacy in a match between the two. Needless to say, the bad blood was running high, and the indifference the crowd showed to all three men did not help matters in the least. They would rather see all three men lose, if that were possible.
A nasty, injury-inducing spill by the 450 pound Jones over the top rope left Beethoven and Legacy alone in the ring, where they picked up last week’s classic battle where it left off. Ten minutes of breathtaking action ended when Jones was finally able to make it back into the ring, where he proceeded to avalanche both men in the corner. He attempted to pin both simultaneously, and got a pair of two counts for his troubles. Jones then bounded off the far ropes, only to be tripped up by Legacy’s bodyguard, the 7 foot Phoenix. As he stumbled forward,
Beethoven took quick advantage by pulling him into an implant DDT for the sudden three count.
Winner: Casanova Beethoven (Implant DDT on Jones) Time: 15:51 (***) “DIRTY” DOUG SIMPKINS vs. RONNY RAMIREZ The Florence crowd was in for a real treat in the next match, as legendary former World Champion “Dirty” Doug Simpkins made a now-rare appearance inside the squared circle, as he took on RAW superstar Ronny
Ramirez. This battle came about as a result of Ramirez’ feud with
Simpkins’ Old School over in RAW, as Ramirez sneak-attacked Simpkins last week at Super Sunday with a chair.
Ramirez was accompanied by his buddy Casanova Beethoven, while Simpkins entered alone. While neither man was exactly a crowd favorite, both men were beneficiaries of small town fans’ tendency to pop for anything remotely related to the “big leagues”. The match itself was a good one, as Simpkins used various “old school” techniques like stalling and eye gouges to slow the tempo, frustrating Ramirez’ attempts to push the tempo. Beethoven interfered quite liberally in the match, breaking
Simpkins’ momentum on every occasion.
The final such occasion saw Simpkins set up Ramirez for the ballyhooed
Death Drop second rope piledriver, only to see Beethoven grab Simpkins’ ankles, tripping him up and sending him crashing to the mat. At that point, “Terrible” Tommy Bedford – not even scheduled on the card – arrived with a steel chair and proceeded to waffle first Beethoven on the apron, and then Ramirez, knocking him off the top rope, and leaving both out cold.
Due to the double interference, the referee disqualified both men as
Simpkins and Bedford proceeded to beat Ramirez down with the very same chair. After busting Ramirez wide open with a concussion-inducing
Death Drop on that chair, Simpkins spat “Now that’s Old School for you, punk” at Ramirez as he and Bedford left the ring.
Decision: Double Disqualification Time: 8:34 (** ½) “LAWBRINGER” THOMAS ADLER vs. THE WILD BUNCH, Gauntlet Match As Thomas Adler made his way to the ring for his match with the Wild
Bunch, ring announcer Bobby Lee Wilcox explained the rules of the gauntlet match: The match would begin with Adler and one member of the
Wild Bunch. The match would remain a one on one encounter until one of two things happen; If Adler pins his opponent, the next man enters the ring. If five minutes elapse, then the next opponent enters, and it becomes a two on one affair. The match continues until either Adler or each member of the Bunch has been defeated.
Isaac Walker was the first member of the Bunch to enter the ring, somewhat surprising strategy given that he is almost exclusively a tag wrestler. He was surprisingly effective against Adler early, working over his neck and lower back. Adler reversed a neckbreaker attempt at the three minute mark and took the advantage. He began working methodically on Isaac’s back, passing up several opportunities to pin the brash young hothead of the Wild Bunch in favor of more punishment, including an awesome backbreaker from the second rope.
Five minutes elapsed, and Wilcox introduced Gideon Walker, Jr., who hit the ring to help his younger brother. As he entered the ring, Adler grabbed both Walkers, knocking their heads together. As Gideon staggered and fell through the ring ropes to the floor, Adler grabbed
Isaac for a tilt-a-whirl backbreaker, and then locked him into a torture rack, getting the near-instant submission.
Adler wasted no time or motion celebrating, going instead right for
Gideon. Adler followed Gideon to the outside, slamming his head repeatedly into the steel steps before delivering a very stiff cradle piledriver to the floor. Adler rolled back into the ring, and the unconscious Gideon was counted out.
The crowd began to buzz anxiously; Adler was halfway home, and Adler had “special plans” for each of the two Wild Bunchers left. Wilcox introduced the Hangman, and the fans instantly recalled that he had been a particular recipient of Adler’s war on crime. As usual when facing Adler, Hangman seemed ill-prepared, out of focus, and in general just lost. Adler pummeled the masked youngster, who offered very little in the way of resistance.
Adler seemed to target the neck of Hangman, but battered him in a very general manner for two and a half minutes of awesome action. In a particularly insulting move, Adler used Hangman’s own “Hangman’s Noose”
DDT on him. While he lay immobile on the mat, Adler soared down onto him with a legdrop from the top rope before sticking his knee in the nape of Hangman’s neck, pulling backwards with all his strength until the masked man tapped out.
Tully Roebuck rushed the ring, attacking Adler a mere split-second after Hangman had submitted. Roebuck hit Adler with a couple of blows to the back, and then tossed him over the top rope to the floor.
Roebuck followed him out, but was met by a waiting Adler with a steel chair. Not even pretending to care for the referee’s admonishment,
Adler cracked that chair across Roebuck’s skull, knocking him out cold and earning a disqualification in the process. Two more shots for good measure, and Adler trudged back to the locker room, a self-satisfied grin on his face. The crowd cheered wildly, but was more than a bit confused; why hadn’t their man finished the job?!? Or had he?!?
They didn’t ponder that question for long, however, as someone leaped from the crowd to attack Adler, smashing a longneck beer bottle over his head. As the man turned around, the fans recognized him as independent wrestling stalwart Terry Brody, last seen in the Carolinas a few years back. Wearing jeans, cowboy boots, and his ever-present bandana, Brody pounded away at Adler’s head with that glass bottle until it shattered, slicing Adler wide open. Brody then took a piece of that bottle and cut his own forehead open, seemingly for the hell of it!!
The fans looked on in shock at the carnage until Goliath and Alec Price arrived to chase off Brody, who escaped back through the crowd, showing the crowd the back of his official Terry Brody t-shirt: HERE COMES THE
GODDAMNED JUICE!! Winners: The Wild Bunch (Disqualification) Time: 13:32 (**) “THUNDERBOLT” JACK SAMPSON vs. JACK HAMMER The crowd had hardly caught their breath when it was time for their new favorite villain: The British monstrosity Jack Hammer had arrived once again, to take on one of their all-time favorites “Thunderbolt” Jack
Sampson in a number one contender match. The winner of this contest would receive a title shot at Winter Storm Warning, RAW Carolina’s next big event, live from picturesque Manteo, North Carolina. Sampson is a former Carolina Heavyweight Champion, and Hammer destroyed the current champion Ace Matthews last week at Super Sunday. The fans expected a hard-hitting, intense match from these two powerhouses, and that’s exactly what they got.
Although many of the experts believed that Hammer’s youth and inexperience would keep him from competiting on the same level as an international legend like Sampson, Hammer quickly proved them wrong.
In fact, he more than held his own, dominating large portions of the action with his powerful arsenal of moves.
The ending came suddenly, as Sampson attempted a running clothesline. Hammer ducked underneath, and when Sampson returned off the far ropes,
Hammer quickly turned him inside out with the awesome Nail Gun, his tilt-o-whirl piledriver. The crowd watched in stunned disbelief as the monster pinned Sampson with one boot across his chest. Jack Hammer is headed to Winter Storm Warning for a shot at the Carolina title.
Winner: Jack Hammer (Nail Gun) Time: 8:42 (***) ACE MATTHEWS © vs. “STELLAR” STEVE KELLER, Carolina Heavyweight Title For nearly a full year, Ace Matthews chased the Carolina Heavyweight
Title around Steve Keller’s waist, unable to wrest it from his grip.
Finally, in January, Matthews was able to recapture the title for a third time – by pinning Jack Sampson in a triangle match. Keller has been quick to point out that Matthews has never, ever beaten him one on one – a fact that had many pegging the challenger as the favorite in this championship tilt.
The match went as all matches between these two go – it was an instant classic. For thirty minutes, the two long-time rivals battled, prompting standing ovations from the crowd in several instances, including a breathtaking sequence that saw eleven two counts in a two minute span.
Keller took firm control at the 35 minute mark, and it seemed that
Matthews just could not get the Keller monkey off his back. When
Keller hit his patented “Stellar Performance” corkscrew suplex, it looked to be over. But somehow, someway, for the first time ever,
Matthews kicked out. Keller was in shock. The crowd was in shock.
Keller never seemed to recover -- but Matthews did, spurred on to new life by the roar of the crowd. He fought back to his feet, and a belly to back suplex and a DDT later, he was perched on the top rope, about to vanquish his personal demon. He hit the Aces High flying elbow, and the pinfall, as they say, was academic. It’ll be the rematch everyone is waiting for at Winter Storm Warning: Ace Matthews and Jack Hammer.
Winner: Ace Matthews (Aces High flying elbow) Time: 39:14 (****) ---------- © RAW Carolina, MMI. Sent via Deja.com http://www.deja.com/
