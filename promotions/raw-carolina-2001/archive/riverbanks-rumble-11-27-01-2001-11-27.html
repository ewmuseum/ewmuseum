Riverbanks Rumble 11.27.01

27 November 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
One of the most unique events of the RAW Carolina calendar is here –
Riverbanks Rumble!! The capital city of Columbia, South Carolina is the site of the Riverbanks Zoo – and the Rumble is held smack dab in the middle of the zoo!! Results:
STEVE FURY & PAUL SHERIDAN vs. PURGATORY The opening bout pitted two recent graduates of the Suplex Academy,
Paul Sheridan and handsome young Steve Fury, against two men who have made a bit of a name for themselves recently in the RAW Empire as renegades: Purgatory. Synn and Dementia have looked impressive thus far, and were out to position themselves as top contenders once the
Unified Tag-Team champions (Kirk Maclean & Stevie Ray Murdoch) return stateside from their tour of Japan.
Fury and Sheridan have teamed together infrequently, but still managed to rally the crowd in the early going with some nice, basic double-team moves that kept their more veteran opponents off-balance – double arm-wringers and the like, mixing in a few `you hold him,
I'll hit him with a double axe-handle' type moves.
Fury launched an errant missile dropkick at the five minute mark, leaving Synn in firm control of things. And that's when Sheridan's unofficial `manager' Shona Maynard wandered out, wearing as little as usual. Sheridan seemed surprised to see her, but she settled herself in his team's corner, rooting Fury on to make the tag.
And make the tag he did, after ducking under a clothesline attempt from the 7 footer Dementia and sending him to the mat with a HIGH leaping bulldog. Sheridan entered the ring via the top rope, looking spectacular in mid-air with a 450 Splash – that found no one home.
Dementia lumbered to his corner and tagged in Synn. Synn bounded off the ropes – only to be tripped up by Shona. But it had little effect – Synn barely stumbled, instead reaching over and grabbing
Maynard by the hair. Fury charged over to make the save, only to run right into a chokeslam from Dementia. Synn clotheslined Sheridan over the top rope, and Dementia got the 1-2-3.
Winners: Purgatory (Chokeslam) Time: 6:37 (**) As Purgatory exited the ring to a nice chorus of boos from the Columbia crowd (not to mention an elephant shriek out of nowhere that gave everyone a nice scare), Network executive Dennis Cody made his way out to the ring with his newest associate, RAW Carolina referee
Jackie Boone, Jr. Boone was set to referee the next match, and Cody stationed himself at ringside, perhaps to provide protection for his new `friend'.
JAYSON RAYGE vs. "DREADMIND" DAVE PITRELLI Exulting in his tag partner Mark Adams' death has perhaps made Dave
Pitrelli the most unpopular man in RAW Carolina – and has undoubtedly made him the sickest SOB here. Young Jayson Rayge looked to put a big notch in his belt at the Zoo – a big, 350 pound notch.
Rayge started out hot as always, scoring several two counts on his monstrous opponent, including a nice springboard bulldog from the second rope for two and a half. Pitrelli seemed to be in serious trouble, at which point Cody leaped to the apron, yelling at Boone.
Little Jackie seemed a bit affronted, and yelled back at his new boss --- completely missing Pitrelli braining Rayge with a pair of brass knuckles.
From that point, it was all Pitrelli. Rayge put up a valiant effort, but that shot from the knucks left him without complete control of his faculties – not a condition you'd want to be in when in the ring with Pitrelli. Rayge managed to mount a brief comeback at the six minute mark, and looked to drop Pitrelli with his Infinite Rayge
Driver. But Pitrelli proved too large to lift into the innovative reverse Death Valley Driver, and he collapsed onto Rayge's neck and shoulders awkwardly, twisting the youngster's neck on the way down in violent fashion. Pitrelli made the cover, and it was all over.
Winner: Dave Pitrelli Time: 7:02 (** ½) As Rayge was helped to the locker room by Blood Brothers Dirk Robertson and Marc Tower, the Mini-Tron flickered to life with the startling image of "Fast" Eddie Cutlass lying amidst a toppled over pile of rubber garbage cans, holding his head in pain.
Rookie Paul Sheridan then past, still upset at himself for his earlier loss to Purgatory. Shocked, he leans over to Cutlass, helping him to a sitting position.
PS: What in the world happened – Oh my God!! EC: What?!? PS: I just saw a masked man headed the other way – down that hall!!! Sheridan pointed in the direction from which he just came. EC (getting visibly angry): Which one?!? PS: It was. . . .I'm not sure. . . . . DIREWOLF vs. VINCENT CONSTANTINE AUGUSTUS Slick Wilson was assigned to officiate this match, so Boone pulled up a folding chair at ringside and sat with Cody watching this match.
Could they be scouting VCA for a spot in The Network?!? It's not like he couldn't use the money. At any rate, VCA arrived, pompous as ever, the crowd heavily behind his opponent in this grudge match, the grotesquely hairy Direwolf.
Showing remarkable constitution, Direwolf's chest hair has regrown at an amazing rate, so much so that the VCA ROCKS shaved into it less than a month ago is now only visible to the most discerning eye. But he had not forgotten – and Augustus was going to have to pay.
As much as VCA trumpets his technical brilliance – and he is VERY good – Direwolf was determined to turn this one into a fight, and the
280 pound blueblood was willing to acquiesce. Short of a couple of rudimentary suplexes – set up of course with hairpulls – not one actual wrestling hold was used in this match, as the two bitter rivals battled throughout the arena. Wilson was more than willing to let most things go, utilizing a liberal ten count when the action spilled outside.
After eleven minutes of wild action – which saw VCA get hiptossed into a nearby rosebush and come up bleeding from the thorns – the two found themselves where they had spent most of the match – out on the floor. Despite a slow count, Wilson found himself shouting `FIVE!!'
when Direwolf bounced VCA's head off the safety railing, and headed back towards the ring. But that's when Dave's Crazylegs bass player
Axe Abraham appeared out of the crowd, whacking Direwolf across the back (AGAIN!!) with his heavy bass, sending Barry's Daddy sprawling.
Augustus recovered, and beat the ten count back into the ring, leaving the fans – and undoubtedly Direwolf's acquaintances in the animal kingdom – very, VERY unhappy.
Winner: Vincent Constantine Augustus (Countout) Time: 11:41 (***) By now desperately craving a win from one of their heroes, the crowd looked upward once more, towards the Mini-Tron, where Paul
Sheridan, "Fast" Eddie Cutlass, and RAW Carolina Head Medical Expert
Bones McGuirk are huddled.
BM: I'm sorry, Eddie. It looks to me like you've got a concussion. You need X-Rays as soon as possible, and you can't get `em here. EC: But my match is coming up!! No way!! BM: Look, things are crazy around here after the whole Adams situation. People are watching us very. . . .closely. We can't afford another high-profile accident. If you're not close to 100 percent, then you're not getting in that ring.
Doctor's orders. The number one contender's match is now a one on one affair – Jack Anderson and Roadkill Rick.
Cutlass stared at McGuirk, fuming. COLLATERAL DAMAGE & ????????? vs. JUDGMENT DAY Well, the worst kept secret in wrestling these days was the identity of Collateral Damage's first partner – Stone. But the second partner has been akin to a state secret, and the crowd was pleasantly surprised with the arrival of RAW Carolina's own legend-in-residence –
"Dirty" Dick Rogers!!!!
Stone and Rogers were formidable partners – but they'd have to be, as
Collateral Damage had laid down the challenge to the ruthless
Judgment Day: Television Champion Hangman; the 500 pound monster
Sinder Blokk; and the devastating tag-team combination of Hellspawn, martial arts masters Abyss and Hades.
It would be impossible to expect official `Cottonmouth' Joe Lynch to keep things as orderly as he normally does in his matches; this time, he didn't even try. Two on two, three on two, four on four – only occasionally was this one contested in a one on one manner.
Collateral Damage got things going fast and furious early, dropping
Synn with a Dead End Drop, with the massive Blokk saving his partner, allowing the match to continue.
Rogers even got into the act, dropping Hangman with his Dirty Drop
2nd rope legdrop and getting the fans up on their feet. But Judgment
Day is a tough group, a veteran group; and after Abyss blew Mike
Diamond across the ring with a savage palm strike, they went to work.
As they did last week, Hellspawn worked over Diamond, looking to keep him in the ring at the expense of his more experienced partners, keeping him on the mat with some nice judo-style throws and good old- fashioned backbreakers. And of course, Damien Scratch would distract
Lynch periodically, allowing his boys to bend the rules, if not out and out break them.
Finally, after Scratch's third such malfeasance, Stone had had enough, charging into the ring with a clothesline that sent Abyss crashing into his manager, knocking him off the apron. Hangman followed suit, charging at Stone with a clothesline – which the squat brawler ducked before dropping the former Carolina champ with a nice belly to back suplex. Blokk was in next, and he was dropped with an un-be-LEE-vable belly to REALLY BIG belly suplex. A kick to the gut and a pumphandle slam stopped Hades, and the crowd responded with a standing O.
After that performance, it was no surprise that Stone ended the match, as well, showing amazing, UNBELIEVABLE, strength in putting the 500 pound Blokk up on his shoulder and slamming him with a
Rolling Stone for the victory. Winner: Collateral Damage, Stone, and Dick Rogers (Rolling Stone) Time: 10:02 (** ¾) JACK ANDERSON vs. ROADKILL RICK The crowd was a bit disappointed not to see their guy Eddie Cutlass in this match, but as it was, a chance to see the Crimson Spitfire beat up on the filthy masked Texan RKR was good enough for them.
With a shot at the Carolina Title at one of the biggest shows of the year on the line, to boot. Who could ask for more?!?
Well, fans of Felicity could – but she was there, too, in all her resplendent glory. That skirt. Those legs. You know the deal by now. Anyway.
These two were scheduled to meet last week in Raleigh, but fate intervened in a most tragic way, and their match was postponed. Each man would like nothing better than to head to Charlotte as the number one contender, and wrestled a tough match accordingly.
Roadkill Rick surprised the fans by wrestling a rather clean match, keeping Anderson off his feet early and often with a variety of nice suplexes. Anderson, in turn, did his best with some high energy moves, including a spectacular plancha off the top rope out to the floor.
The match passed the ten minute mark, with neither man able to gain a firm foothold towards victory. Anderson dropped RKR with an atomic drop, and then followed with a bulldog. The crowd bubbled with anticipation as Anderson plopped Roadkill on the top rope, setting him up for the Fireball Special. But as he did, RKR loaded up the
Black Mist and fired – catching Jackie Boone, Jr. right in the face.
Anderson had RKR well scouted, and when he went to the mist, the
Crimson Spitfire leaped off the top rope with a backflip, leaving
Boone directly in the path of the spray.
Boone went down in the corner, holding his eyes in pain. And then, out of nowhere, Eddie Cutlass arrived, leaping into the ring and dropped RKR with a Guarantee. Cutlass then exited the ring with a "Stay the hell out of my business!!", and the crowd roared –
Cutlass had apparently gained revenge on his attacker. Anderson hit
RKR with a legdrop, rolled on top, and a half-blinded Boone made the count: 1-2-3. Jack Anderson is in the main event at Queen City
Showdown.
Winner: Jack Anderson (With a little help from his friends) Time: 13:21 (***) CASANOVA BEETHOVEN © vs. MARC TOWER, Carolina Heavyweight Title Jackie Boone, Jr. remained in the ring after the last match, `miraculously' healed from RKR's Black Mist, intent on officiating the main event. Dennis Cody stood with arms crossed at ringside, intent on making it happen.
But it didn't. No sooner had RKR made his way to the back, RAW Carolina President "Showboat" Eddie Cannon made his first public appearance in some time, and he had a mike in hand:
"How is everybody tonight? Enjoying the show? "I thought so. But let's make things even MORE enjoyable. Boone – get your ass out of the ring."
The crowd popped big-time for that one, sending Cody into an even bigger rage. "See, I told your daddy a few years back when I bought the promotion that I'd keep you around. I really ought to fire you – but a promise is a promise. So, you can keep your job – for tonight, anyway. But you ain't getting nowhere near my main event.
"You can bitch and moan all you want there, Commander Cody – but this is my show. I call the shots. `Cottonmouth' Joe Lynch is YOUR referee.
"And you can take that to the bank." Boone slowly climbed out of the ring, taking a seat with Cody at ringside to watch the main event unfold. And the fans had hope that finally, Marc Tower would get a fair shake in a shot at the Carolina
Heavyweight Title.
Tower has had quite a few clashes recently with Casanova Beethoven for the big strap, all of them classic. And all of them controversial. Tower started hot, dropping Beethoven inside of two minutes into the match with a Steel City Slam, and although Beethoven survived the Canadian Backbreaker into a sitout facebuster combo by draping his leg across the bottom rope, he had to know this match was going to be different.
In response, Beethoven wrestled one of his best matches here in the
Carolinas, pulling out all the stops in defense of his precious strap, including an awesome spot that saw him destroy the timekeeper's table with Marc Tower's body during a Cherry Blossom
Bomb.
At the ten minute mark, Tower reversed an attempted tilt-o-whirl piledriver with a flying headscissors, sending Beethoven over the top rope to the floor, landing in Cody's lap. Tower then followed with a rare high-flying move, hitting a plancha over the top rope and into
Beethoven, Cody, and Boone, sending all three men flying into the safety railing.
Back in the ring at the fourteen minute mark, things started to get interesting. Did I say interesting? I meant controversial. Tower had Beethoven locked in the Pittsburgh Strangler in the center of the ring, with no place to go. Lynch was right on top of the hold, asking – practically begging – Beethoven to give it up. Suddenly,
Boone bolted from his ringside chair and pulled Lynch out of the ring by his ankle.
Lynch hit the floor and shoved Boone down, missing as Beethoven frantically tapped out in the the ring. The fans were outraged – and even more so after Cody bent the very chair he was sitting in over
Lynch's head. Boone hopped into the ring as Lynch went down, and delivered a not very effective sidekick to Tower's head. But it WAS effective enough to make him break the hold, and he turned his attention to Boone – just long enough for Cody to crack HIM with the chair, as well.
The fans were now incensed – and so was "Showboat" Eddie Cannon, who raced out of the locker room with a chair of his own, taking a swing at Dennis Cody, who bailed out of the ring. But Boone stayed behind – and smiled gleefully as Beethoven and Tower both got to their feet at the same time, and Tower was dropped with a Symphony(!!).
Boone dropped to make the count, and got all the way to two and ninety-nine hundredths – only to be pulled out of the ring by HIS ankle, by the strong arms of `Cottonmouth' Joe Lynch. Lynch wasted no time this time – he dropped Boone with the old finisher from his wrestling days, his Jaws of Defeat DDT, sending the crowd into delirium.
Beethoven watched on from above in the ring in disbelief as Boone went down. The crowd reached a fever pitch as Beethoven turned around – right into the waiting arms of a smiling Marc Tower – and a
Tower of Power spinning orange crush bomb. Lynch dove under the ropes back into the ring and counted.
1. 2. 3(!!!). Marc Tower had become the new Carolina Heavyweight Champion. Winner (and new champion): Marc Tower (Tower of Power) Time: 16:27 (****) A wild celebration ensued, as Tower's Blood Brothers Dirk Robertson and Jayson Rayge made their way out to congratulate their partner – and were blindsided in the aisle by Purgatory(!!). The 7 foot
Dementia dropped Rayge with his Sanitarium – a reverse Razor's Edge – right onto the safety railing(!!). He followed with one for Robertson
(!!).
And as Tower looked on, making his way out of the ring to help his partners, he completely missed "Dreadmind" Dave Pitrelli coming from the other direction and spearing HIM into the safety railing(!!).
Pitrelli then turned and floored Lynch with a big axe kick, before drilling Tower once again, this time with a piledriver on the floor.
The crowd looked on in shock as Dennis Cody returned to ringside, standing over Tower with mike in hand:
"Marc Tower. Welcome to the first day of your very short title reign. The Network is watching you, Tower...you and your Blood Brothers...and we don't like what we see." [...] "Unfortunately for you, when The Network doesn't like something, The
Network...cancels it!!"
