RAW Carolina — Card Archive

Thanksgiving Thunder 11.18.01

18 November 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It’s time once again for that Carolina tradition: Thanksgiving Thunder!! A near capacity crowd filed into the brand new Ace Matthews
Center in Raleigh, North Carolina, for a great show!! Results:
PAUL SHERIDAN vs. BRIMSTONE The first match pitted Suplex Academy honor student Paul Sheridan against a recent graduate of the same institution, the powerful
Brimstone. Sheridan has been increasingly visible in RAW-C of late, and appears on the brink of stardom. He has also been spotted recently in the company of Shona Maynard, the latest in a long line of wrestling chanteuses from THAT family.
Sheridan took the fight to the big masked man early, hitting him with a lot of high-flying stuff, including a pair of missile dropkicks and a nice running hurricanrana, all of which scored near falls. But while they may have stunned Brimstone, they had no lasting effect, as he shrugged them off to drop Sheridan with a nice leaping powerbomb for a two count, and a top rope shouldertackle for another.
The battle raged for seven minutes or so, a classic contrast in styles. At that point, Brimstone dropped Sheridan with ANOTHER leaping powerbomb, and went to make the cover – only to be distracted when Shona made her way down to ringside in a VERY short skirt, drawing a few uncomfortable whistles from the males in the crowd (they should feel dirty; after all, she’s barely legal, if that).
Brimstone raged at referee Slick Wilson to get her out of there, but
Wilson, always with an eye for the ladies, would have none of that, and instead went over to speak with the youngster. As he did, she hopped on the apron. Brimstone went over – and got caught with an unsuspecting hit DI-rectly in the groin. He doubled over, and a completely oblivious Sheridan small packaged him for the win.
Winner: Paul Sheridan (Small Package) Time: 7:21 (** ž) The mini-tron flickered to life before the next match, with some holiday hijinks from the Direwolf clan:

Pilgrim outfit carrying a dish of cranberry sauce to the small table.

[Ned meekishly escorts a reluctant Barry owens III away as Direwolf heads to the ring.] COLLATERAL DAMAGE vs. HELLSPAWN All the Suplex Academy graduates in the Carolinas are more than a bit pissed at Judgment Day these days; after injuring lead instructor
Bobby Parker last week, they’re number one with a bullet on the
SA Most Wanted List. To that end, “Shining” Mike Diamond and “Unbreakable” Mike Steele arrived for their match
Sunday afternoon sans their normal wrestling attire. Instead they sported black jeans and Suplex Academy t-shirts – ready for business.
Hellspawn was eager to get down to business, as well – these two sadistic bastards were looking to deal out some real punishment, feeling that their opponents thus far had been somewhat subpar.
CD started out red hot with the crowd behind them, and dropped the smaller Hades with a Dead End Drop not two minutes into the match.
But instead of making the cover and the possible win, they wanted to keep their promise to Parker, and kept the match going so they could deliver another DED later on for Abyss.
Bad move. Diamond looked to whip Hades into the ropes, who reversed at the last second. Diamond hit the ropes near Hellspawn’s corner, and Abyss dropped him with a wicked heel kick to the back of the neck, sending him staggering into a nifty clothesline onto a bent knee sort of thing from Hades, a kind of modified backbreaker that drew a few aaahs from the Raleigh faithful.
Hades kept the pressure on Diamond after that, with his martial arts prowess very much on display as he snapped Diamond’s head back with several stiff savate kicks, mixing in some nice suplexes to boot.
But Diamond was resilient, and backflipped out of a German Suplex attempt, and tagged in tough Mike Steele. Diamond charged Hades with a clothesline attempt, only to see him duck – right into a float kick from Steele, right in the mush.
Steele showed the crowd just why he’s fast becoming the world’s foremost expert on the powerbomb, dropping Hades with three different variations in the first three minutes alone. But it was a different story when the 400 pound Abyss finally tagged back in, and immediately dropped him with a stiff palm strike to the throat.
As the match neared fifteen minutes, Hades snuck around ringside to
CD’s corner, and pulled Diamond down to the floor by his ankles, and a brawl ensued. Referee Jackie Boone, Jr. made his way over to break this up – so of course Scratch took this opportunity to hand a rattan stick to Abyss, who broke it over Steele’s head.
Abyss thought about making the pin, but instead chose to tag in Hades, who applied a nerve hold – just as the bell rang. Boone declared the match a fifteen minute draw, leaving EVERYONE unhappy.
Decision: Time Limit Draw Time: 15:00 (***) DIREWOLF vs. “DREADMIND” DAVE PITRELLI With his son enjoying an early turkey dinner backstage, the Hairiest
Father on Earth headed to the ring for a big encounter with the awesome, physically imposing Dave Pitrelli. Pitrelli has been great in the early stages of his RAW-C career, last week’s tag title loss notwithstanding. Rather, it just put him in a worse mood, and
Direwolf was to suffer for that.
The two big brawlers started the match by trading punches in the center of the ring, with Pitrelli gaining an advantage given his 75 pound weight advantage, and then dropped Direwolf with a vicious belly to back suplex. Pitrelli followed up with an equally stiff piledriver, and went to work on the popular former Television
Champion. Pitrelli dominated the next few minutes, until an uncharacteristic high-flying move ended in disaster, as an attempted flying headbutt left him bleeding from his nose on the mat, looking not unlike his partner Mark Adams on many an occasion. Direwolf, not fazed by the blood in the least, dusted off the old non-homoerotic Liplock, planting one fully on Pitrelli’s lips, sending him reeling in horror. Suitably distracted, Pitrelli then walked right into a DDT, and a nice fireman’s carry suplex that got a near fall.
After ten minutes of brutal action, Pitrelli found himself back in control, and ascended to the top rope looking to end it with his finisher, The Hatred Within – a top rope spear he bridges into a northern lights style pin. But once again, his trip to the top ended badly, as Direwolf sidestepped and watched as he crashed headlong into the mat. Pitrelli stumbled to his feet – right into an Alpha
Male chokeslam for the one-two-three. Winner: Direwolf (Alpha Male) Time: 10:32 (***) The Mini-Tron then came on once more, getting a big pop when the crowd spied Jack Anderson whistling as he walked down the hallway. Anderson stopped dead in his tracks in front of the camera – and spotted young Paul Sheridan lying dazed on the floor, holding his head.
PS: It’s the Guy in Black!! He just laid me out!! JA: Which way?!? Sheridan pointed down the hall – in the same direction Anderson had just come from. JA: I didn’t see him!! Anderson tore off down the hall, in the same direction. Sheridan stared at the camera in pain, a troubled look on his face.
STEVE FURY & HOUNDDOG MURPHY vs. SINDER BLOKK & HANGMAN The next match was a rematch of sorts from last week’s eight man tag, as Judgment Day’s leader Hangman and his huge partner
Sinder Blokk reteamed to take on handsome young Steve Fury and the
Appalachian Assassin, Hounddog Murphy.
And you thought last week was a mismatch. From start to finish, the JD superheavyweights looked very, very impressive. Once again, The ‘Dog’s mouth got him in a bit of trouble, as he mouthed off to Blokk to start the match, only to be nearly decapitated with a Clothesline From Hell, and then mocked viciously as he got to his feet.
It only got worse when Fury tagged in, as he was nearly broken in half with a Press Slam Backbreaker, and then folded up with a Canadian
Backbreaker. But unwisely, he kicked out, and subjected himself to more.
Hangman tagged in, and it was more of the same. He battered Fury unmercifully before finally dropping him on the top rope, and then planting him with The Noose, his two-handed chokeslam from the top rope. This time, Fury stayed down for three.
But it didn’t stop there – Blokk dropped Murphy with a savage Dominator, and then retrieved his cinder block hammer from the floor. He re-entered the ring, intent on maiming Murphy the same way he took out Bobby Parker. But Collateral Damage had seen enough, and came in with steel chairs to save the day, and send Judgment Day packing.
Winners: Hangman & Sinder Blokk (Noose) Time: 3:21 (*) “ERADICATOR” DIRK ROBERTSON & JAYSON RAYGE vs. PURGATORY Purgatory entered RAW-C with much fanfare, trumpeting their success in
Windy City RAW, demanding a shot at the Carolina Tag Titles. But they’re quickly finding out that RAW Carolina is a whole
‘nother ballgame, especially when they climbed into the ring with the Blood Brother pair of Dirk Robertson and young Jayson Rayge.
Rayge started the match a house o’ fire, his youthful enthusiasm leading him to a quick Infinite Rayge Driver on Synn, which the 400 pound Dementia had to make the save to keep the match going. But
Rayge’s inexperience soon cost him, as he ignored the chance to make a tag, and instead was soon dropped with a tilt-o-whirl backbreaker from Synn.
Rayge managed to tag in his more veteran partner, but Robertson soon found himself on the receiving end of a whipping, as well. Still suffering from his injured shoulder, Robertson was in serious trouble after Synn dropped him with three straight shoulderbreakers, and
Dementia tagged in and hit a devastating jack-knife powerbomb. But this time, Rayge returned the favor and made the save.
At the eight minute mark, Robertson was barely hanging on, surviving on his considerable toughness alone. Dementia picked up by the scruff of his neck, twisting him into an impressive swinging chokeslam. But
Rayge had seen enough – climbing to the top rope, he dropped
Dementia with a missile dropkick, drawing Synn into the ring. A wild four-way melee ensued, and as official ‘Cottonmouth’ Joe
Lynch attempted to force Dementia out of the ring, Rayge climbed back to the top, and landed a springboard moonsault bodyblock onto Synn.
Lynch turned around, and made the three count. Winners: Dirk Robertson & Jayson Rayge (Springboard thing-y) Time: 8:51 (** ž) STONE vs. MARK ADAMS, Loser Leaves RAW (Hell in a Cell) -- Special thanks to [name withheld] for writing this one. In one of the most talked-about matches to ever take place in the
Carolinas, former friends Mark Adams and Jimmy Stone battled for their very careers in a Hell In A Cell match where the loser would have to leave the RAW Empire forever.
From the opening bell, Adams and Stone put aside every last bit of wrestling knowledge they possessed in order to mindlessly beat their opponent into submission. Using his height advantage and longer arms,
Adams took early control of the match with a series of brutal punches to Stone's head that rocked the smaller man enough for "The Iceman" to ram his face into the steel mesh of the cell once, twice, three times before Stone was able to break free and return the favor by dragging
Adams' face along the cage and breaking for the umpteenth time his highly-publicized surgically-rebuilt nose. Just three minutes into the match and both men were wearing the proverbial crimson mask, their blood covering the floor of the ring and soaking their clothes, but
Special Referee Dick Rogers had specific instructions -- there would be no easy end to this match.
After nearly seven minutes of nonstop brutality, the likes of which many Carolina wrestling fans have never seen, Adams switched to a mat-based strategy that distracted Stone long enough for a Nightmare
In New Orleans suplex and the first near fall of the evening. Stone managed to power out but immediately stumbled into a modified version of his own Rolling Stone finisher for another near fall. Having his own finishing move used against him seemed to empower The Heart of
Stone and within minutes, Stone had scored not one but THREE near falls over Adams by using variations of The Iceman's own moves against him as well.
The tide of the match turned several times in the next few minutes, with highlights including Adams hitting Stone with a flying fistdrop from halfway up the cage wall and, just shy of the unlucky thirteen minute mark, Stone whipping Adams into the cage so hard that the former World Tag Team Champion broke through the mesh. Adams, bleeding profusely from being thrown through the cage, began climbing the outside of the cell, hoping to gain some sort of advantage by taking the fight to higher ground.
Stone, of course, immediately followed Adams to the top of the cage where the two former friends continued their battle to the finish. And what a finish it was as, moments later, Stone blocked an attempt by
Adams to suplex him off the cage and scored the three-count with a
T-bone suplex from out of nowhere. Winner: Stone (T-Bone Suplex) Time: 15:23 (****) As the crowd came to its feet in a huge ovation and fireworks began exploding around the arena, however, Mark Adams slowly climbed to his feet, removing a remote control from the pocket of his jeans as he did. Distracted by the roar of the crowd, Stone was caught by surprise as the cage began rising toward the ceiling, apparently at the command of the control in Adams' hand. Turning to see what was causing the cage to rise, Stone moved out of the way just as Adams, in a fit of rage over losing the match and his career, charged him from behind in an attempt to knock him off the cage – and went off the cage himself.
Slowly, as if in slow motion, Adams fell the true thirty feet to the concrete below, landing headfirst with an awful, awful sound – drawing cries of fear from many in attendance. The locker room exploded with medical personnel who rushed to Adams’ side. The crowd watched in absolute silence for what seemed an endless ten minutes that saw Adams loaded onto a stretcher and carried out of the arena motionless – and from the looks on the EMTs faces, in very serious condition.
JACK ANDERSON vs. ROADILL RICK After the end of the last match, no one in the building was in the mood for yet another match – especially not the two competitors in the ring. Anderson and RKR went through a series of crisscrosses, leapfrogs, that sort of thing, before ending up on the floor, throwing barely connecting punches at each other while referee Slick Wilson gave the fastest ten count in history, for the double countout.
Decision: Double Countout Time: 1:42 (DUD) The fans may have felt like booing this effort, but didn’t. And when Bobby Lee Wilcox grabbed the house mike and announced that the rest of the card had been cancelled, no one booed then, either. No one knew exactly what had happened to Mark Adams, but everyone agreed on one thing – it was bad.
