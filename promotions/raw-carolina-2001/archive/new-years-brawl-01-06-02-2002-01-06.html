New Year's Brawl 01-06-02

6 January 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Good ol’ Rocky Top – Whooo!!!!! – Rocky Top Tennessee!! RAW Carolina rolled into Knoxville, Tennessee for the first big show of the new year – New Year’s Brawl!! The
Thompson-Bolling Arena crowd was pumped up for a chance to see the superstars of RAW-C live and in person for the first time – and they delivered!! Results:
TERRY JACOBS vs. JUSTIN HARPER Not so much a feud as a friendly rivalry, the situation around this match began at Season’s Beatings when the rookie Justin Harper take the veteran Terry Jacobs to a time limit draw. Even though the match ended in a handshake, both men were eager for a rematch, and got it here in the opener in Knoxville.
Jacobs has been hospitalized recently after a bout with pneumonia, and was warned by several doctors not to make the trip to Knoxville. But after spending eight long years away from the ring, he wasn’t going to be denied, and came ready to rumble. The match see-sawed back and forth for a good eight minutes, with Harper matching his power move for move with Jacobs’ suplex arsenal befitting a man who also runs his own wrestling school when not competing. The men were once again so evenly matched, it would have surprised no one to see another draw.
But at the eleven minute mark, things took a turn for the worse for
Jacobs. He appeared to be very short of breath, evidently a remainder from his hospital stay. To his credit, the handsome young Harper stayed right on top of Jacobs, pummeling his winded opponent with a nice killer instinct. Jacobs mounted a brief rally, but Harper managed to duck under a clothesline attempt and lift the veteran up into his California Dreaming, a devastating Miracle Ecstasy Bomb, and got the three count for undoubtedly the biggest win of his brief career.
Winner: Justin Harper (California Dreaming) Time: 13:22 (** ½) LA ESTOQUE vs. “SCHOLARLY” SCOTT McBRIDE The talent pipeline RAW Carolina has established to Mexico continues to produce dividends with the arrival of yet another high-flying luchador, the colorful La Estoque. Estoque made a spectacular debut last week as Mike Steele’s partner against Purgatory, but looked this week to test his singles mettle against Suplex Academy graduate
“Scholarly” Scott McBride.
The mat-based McBride tried to keep the match out of the air, but
Estoque was having none of it. He took to the air early and often with a variety of moves many of which have to be seen to be believed
(or described). Using a senton bomb as a setup, Estoque climbed to the top one more time, and came off with a flying, twisting, crashing type move that I won’t even pretend to do justice to with a name or description, and drove McBride right through the mat.
The pinfall was academic. Winner: La Estoque (Yeah, right) Time: 3:34 (*) STONE © vs. CHRISTIAN DEMONSTONE, Television Title After a slow start that seems in no way indicative of his future here in RAW Carolina, arrogant young Canadian Christian Demonstone found himself in possession of his first RAW-C title shot, a chance at the
Television Title currently held by the awesome Stone.
Demonstone seemed to surprise the champion in the early going, showing a bit more life than he has in his previous matches, even scoring a pair of two counts with a powerslam followed by a forearm shiver to the throat(!!). But it takes more than the element of surprise to take out an Element, and Stone is no exception. Within three minutes of the start, he had taken control with a stiff T-Bone Suplex, even more impressive considering that Demonstone is over a foot taller.
Neither man was able to string together more than a few minutes offense, and a nice stiff power-technical match developed. Demonstone showed that he is definitely going to be a force to be reckoned with here in the Carolinas, but he fell just a bit short here in Knoxville.
At the twelve minute mark, Demonstone looked for a Dragonscrew Legwhip – but Stone ducked, rolling through and popping back up with a clothesline. Demonstone was wobbly, and Stone finished him off with a Rolling Stone to retain the title.
Winner: Stone (Rolling Stone) Time: 12:35 (*** ¼) JAYSON RAYGE vs. “SENSATIONAL” SCOTT STUDD Scott Studd has been mightily impressive in his most recent run here in RAW Carolina, and appears on the precipice of the kinds of success that eluded him during his first two runs here. Standing in his path this afternoon in Knoxville was young Jayson Rayge, who has appeared directionless in recent weeks following the implosion of The Blood
Brothers. That said, most in the crowd were expecting a mismatch – and that’s exactly what they got. Accompanied by college buddy turned manager-slash-potential wrestler Jordan Valentine, Studd ran roughshod through the youngster, completely overwhelming him. As he often does, Rayge started fast – but Studd waited patiently for the youngster to make a mistake, getting it when Rayge came off the top with a double axe-handle, only to get caught and dropped with a spinebuster.
From there, it was all Studd, showing off his ever-improving physique by tossing Rayge around the ring with a variety of powerful slams and throws. It became apparent to everyone in attendance that he was toying with Rayge, and could put him away whenever he wished. The crowd, fearing for Rayge’s future, began urging Studd to do just that, and official Hank Jackson joined in. Studd didn’t take kindly to this, and began jawing at Jackson.
But when he turned around, Rayge had found something extra somewhere – and leveled Studd with a clothesline. As the crowd roared, Rayge dropped the big man with a DDT for a two count. As he got to his feet, Studd shoved Rayge – right into Jackson. Jackson hit the mat, and Rayge hit Studd with his Infinite Rayge Driver – but no three counter was available.
The crowd looked towards the locker room, expecting another official – but instead got “The Eradicator” Dirk Robertson. Armed with a steel chair, he took a look at Studd – and instead leveled Rayge with it. Studd slowly got to his feet, and locked an unconscious Rayge into the Studdly Stretch. As Jackson came to, that’s what he saw – and rang the bell, giving the win to
Studd. Robertson seemed pleased with himself – but was it because he had cost his former Blood Brother the match, or because he had given the match to a Network associate?!?
Winner: Scott Studd (Studdly Stretch) Time: 7:21 (**) “X-PRESS” DAVID JERICHO & MACABRE vs. CASANOVA BEETHOVEN & “STELLAR” STEVE KELLER Last week’s scheduled match between Casanova Beethoven and VCA quickly turned into something else – an affair that saw unscheduled appearances from Macabre, David Jericho, AND Steve
Keller!! Prescient bookers saw the opportunity, and this tag match was signed. Keller came to ringside with both the RAW Atlantic
Coastal Title and his behemoth henchman “Trainwreck” Caleb Jones. Jones got involved early on, as Keller was in the ring with Macabre. Macabre was shot off the ropes – to be tripped up by Jones. But Macabre bounced right back up, leaping over the top rope with a moonsault onto Jones, and following up by spraying him right in the face with a cloud of Black Mist (purchased at a bulk discount from
Roadkill Rick?!?). Jones had to be helped to the back by RAW-C medical officials – a distraction seized upon by Beethoven, who caught an unsuspecting Macabre with a Symphony – ON THE
FLOOR(!!). Rolling him back into the ring, Beethoven and Keller went to work on Macabre, dominating the next six minutes of the match. The two former
Carolina champs actually functioned quite well as a team, to the surpise of some, including a nice old-fashioned double slingshot suplex that got a two count.
The tide turned at the eight minute mark, when Macabre ducked under a clothesline from Beethoven – and dropped him with a Danse
Macabre spin kick to the back of his head. Macabre went to make the cover – but thought better of it when he saw Keller charge into the ring – right into another Danse Macabre. Keller was sent staggering, right into official Slick Wilson.
The crowd booed the second ref bump in as many matches, but their mood soon shifted when Jericho launched himself into the ring with a senton onto Beethoven. After spending most of the match jawing at each other from the apron, the two old rivals finally got a piece of one another and brawled back towards the locker room, leaving Macabre alone in the ring with Keller. Keller stumbled to his feet, lurching towards his corner to retrieve his Coastal Title belt – swinging it at
Macabre. But the mysterious Goth ducked the shot and stood back up to deliver another Danse Macabre. This time Wilson was in position, and counted to three.
Winners: David Jericho & Macabre (Danse Macabre) Time: 9:22 (** ¾) “DREADMIND” DAVE PITRELLI © vs. MIKE STEELE, Piedmont Heavyweight Title – Steel Chain Match After a few weeks of escalating hostilities between these two men, there seemed to Eddie Cannon to be no way to settle this issue than with the same steel chain that had played its’ way into so many of Pitrelli’s recent matches. Brutality was expected – and no one was disappointed in that regard.
What surprised many in attendance was the new side they saw of Mike Steele. Intent on teaching Pitrelli a lesson, Steele spent most of the match beating, whipping, and choking Pitrelli – both with the chain and without. It was the kind of beating Pitrelli usually enjoys dishing out – but doesn’t mind receiving.
In fact, it seemed the more Steele beat on the Piedmont champ, the more Pitrelli enjoyed himself – and by the five minute mark, both men had busted each other wide open, and the ring was quickly coated in blood.
Referee Hank Jackson seemed a bit squeamish by the six minute mark, and began looking for any opportunity to stop the match.
Seven minutes in, Steele tossed the 300 pound Pitrelli over the top rope, in effect hanging him with the entire length of chain. But
Pitrelli grabbed the chain in his free hand, and pulled Steele down over the ropes with him – a double hanging. If either man would have let go, than the other man would have fallen to the floor, which would pulled him to safety into the ring.
But neither man would let go, blinded by their hatred for the other. In their intent to destroy each other, they both ended up unconscious, choked out, and Jackson tossed the match out.
Decision: Double Disqualification Time: 8:49 (***) JACK ANDERSON vs. THE GUY DRESSED ALL IN BLACK Well, we’ve been waiting for this one for a long time, haven’t we?!? After months of torture, The Guy Dressed All in
Black finally stepped into the ring for a one on one encounter with the Crimson Spitfire, Jack Anderson. Perhaps it was out of shame for possibly believing that Jack could have been TGDAB; perhaps it was the impossibly short business skirt Felicity was wearing; whatever the case, the fans had never been more behind Anderson than they were this afternoon.
No sooner had both men entered the ring than they quickly exited it – brawling into the crowd. Despite no stipulations to allow for it, referee Slick Wilson wisely allowed the brawl to spill all over the arena, probably the only way this situation will ever come to a head. The action briefly made its’ way back to the ring, just long enough for Anderson to attempt a Fireball Special. But TGDAB held onto the ropes, and Anderson tumbled hard to the mat. TGDAB followed with a nice splash off the top, garnering a two and
six-sevenths count. Back into the crowd they went, and ended up trading blows in front of a popcorn stand -- Don’t they all end up out here?!? Anderson sent TGDAB spilling over the counter with a hard right, and followed after him. The crowd exhaled as the two rivals disappeared from view
– and blew the roof off of the building when Anderson stood up – TGDAB’s mask in hand(!!). But their cheers soon turned sour when TGDAB stood up beside Anderson – wearing a silver mask. (Dammit, the old double mask trick!!) Grabbing a 32 oz. beer, TGDAB splashed it into Anderson’s face – and disappeared into the locker room. When he finally cleared his eyes, Anderson’s rival was gone – and a chance at closure with him.
Decision: No Contest Time: 6:34 (* ½) “HURRICANE” KIRK MACLEAN & STEVIE RAY MURDOCH © vs. PURGATORY, World Tag-Team Titles (Special Thanks to RAW Overlord Supreme [name withheld] for writing this one) Stevie Ray Murdoch and Kirk Maclean may be in the ‘big league’ of RAW itself, but even up there, they’ll have seen few sights as imposing as the 7’4, 400 pound plus Dementia.
When coupled with his partner in Purgatory, Synn, who himself weighs in at 280 pounds, the leather-masked psycho probably leapt up to the top of Kirk and
Stevie’s ‘Not Too Keen On Wrestling Right Now’ list. Or, at least, would’ve done, if they’d HAD such a list. But who are we trying to kid? Kirk would probably kill himself live on air doing a move if he thought the fans wanted it, and Murdoch would probably kill himself live on air doing a move if. . .Hell,
Stevie Ray wouldn’t NEED no damned reason! In fact, with some of the moves we saw going on here, it looked for a while as if one of the aforementioned deaths might come to pass, with all manner of craziness going on. An early advantage gained for the champs after a Full Bottle in Front of Me top rope leg lariat by
Stevie Ray got them 2 ¾ was lost when Murdoch went for a hurricanrana on the giant Dementia, only for Synn to catch him from behind and for
Purgatory to hit an incredible double powerbomb over the top rope and down, down, DOWN into the ringside mat. After that, almost everyone in the building was too shocked to move – except for the
Hurricane, who leapt down to join the party, bringing Synn with him courtesy of a suplex from the apron. Dementia came down to make it a foursome, and the resultant brawl was too wild to even BEGIN to describe – suffice to say that ring steps, tables, and even
Lil’ Johnny’s photograph of Britney Hindley played a part in it. But, all good things must come to an end, and so did this match, eventually finding it’s way back into the ring. Dementia hit a swinging chokeslam on Kirk to make a cover, only for SRM to reverse things with an incredible triple jump moonsault, before superkicking the big man out of the ring. As Synn came in, Maclean hit him with a tornado DDT, before locking on a sharpshooter to try for the submission win. Dementia, however, had other ideas. Tossing Murdoch out of the
way with a gorilla press from the apron onto the guard rail, he grabbed his trusty sledgehammer, strode into the ring, and smashed
Maclean full in the chest, causing the Hurricane to sink to his knees in pain, and giving the champs the match by DQ.
Winners: Kirk Maclean and Stevie Ray Murdoch (Disqualification) Time: 7:53 (** ½) MARC TOWER, “SHOWBOAT” EDDIE CANNON, & “COTTONMOUTH” JOE LYNCH vs. “ERADICATOR” DIRK ROBERTSON, DENNIS CODY, & JACKIE BOONE, JR. Now this match – THIS match has been a long time in the coming. Ever since NASN executive Dennis Cody revealed his plan to take over RAW Carolina with his Network, he and Eddie Cannon have been at each other’s throats – and it’s only natural that this has led to a match, with each man taking their most trusted allies as partners.
The referee?!? None other than legendary competitor “Thunderbolt” Jack Sampson, perhaps the only man available with the presence to control this one.
The match began with Carolina Heavyweight Champion Marc Tower squaring off with old acquaintance Dirk Robertson, and the champ got the better of the early exchanges, nearly scoring a quick after a nice drop toehold – elbow drop combo. But a nice thumb to the eye stopped
Tower’s momentum, and Cody tagged himself in after slapping Robertson’s shoulder. The difference between Cody and most – or should I say ALL – Television executives is his background: Cody is a former pro wrestler, and a successful one at that, capturing all the top titles in the IWA, including their World Title. Still lean and trim in his traditional midnight blue trunks and boots, he urged Tower to tag in
Cannon – and he did. Cannon stepped into the ring, and he, too, was looking not too far removed from his days as an active competitor – and quickly dropped Cody with a go-behind into a belly to back suplex. He bridged into a pin, but Jackie Boone snuck in with a boot to the midsection to break it up – and got a stiff clothesline from a charging Joe
Lynch for his troubles. That’s pretty much how the match proceeded for the first ten minutes; whenever one man seemed to get an advantage, one of his opponents would make the save, forcing him to start all over. Lynch showed flashes of the potential that marked him a future star before the unfortunate Japanese incident that left him with a ruined knee and a ruined career, even managing to drop Robertson with his Jaws of
Defeat DDT for a surprise two count. And Boone surprised no one by not even tagging in, standing on the apron and egging on his teammates.
After twelve minutes of back and forth action, a dazed Robertson made a mistake and tagged in Boone – who found himself staring right into the massive arms of the Pittsburgh Powerhouse. Without even thinking twice, Tower grabbed the ex-ref and dropped him with a stiff
Tower of Power as the crowd roared. Sampson dropped to make the pin, only to be pulled out of the ring by Cody.
Cannon raced around ringside to go after Cody, and Sampson did his best to pull them apart. Back in the ring, Robertson found his trusty steel chair and waylayed Tower with it – before being dropped a second time with a Jaws of Defeat from Lynch. But as Lynch got up, a recovering Boone blinded him with a devastating fireball(!!). Lynch went down in agony, and Boone added a shot from Robertson’s chair for good measure, busting Lynch’s face wide open.
Cody motioned for Sampson to get back in the ring, and he did so – just in time to make a slow, very chagrained three count with Boone on top of Lynch, giving The Network a very tainted victory.
