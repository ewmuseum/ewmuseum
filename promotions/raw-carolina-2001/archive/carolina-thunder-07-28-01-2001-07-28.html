Carolina Thunder 07/28/01

28 July 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Carolina Thunder is here!! From Charlotte, North Carolina's venerable Independence Arena comes a historic night of firsts for the hottest regional promotion in all of professional wrestling, RAW
Carolina!!
For the first time ever, a RAW Carolina supercard was broadcast live on the North American Sports Network, the weekly home for RAW-C TV.
But before the broadcast began, the 8000 fans in attendance were treated to a handful of non-televised contests:
The first dark match saw young Suplex Academy graduates STEVE FURY &
KID DYNAMITE team up to take on the rough and tough veteran WALKER
BROTHERS (Isaac & Gideon, Jr.). Anyone expecting a walkover for the rough and tumble Texas veterans was mistaken – the rookies scored a thrilling upset victory when Dynamite pinned Gideon, Jr. with a spectacular Sky Twister Press that got a rousing ovation from the already rabid crowd.
The strange newcomer ROADKILL RICK made his Charlotte debut in the evening's second contest, taking on a longtime Queen City favorite
HOUNDDOG MURPHY. Longtime observers have often noted that there's no quicker way to being despised by the Carolina fans than defeating the
Appalachian Assassin in Charlotte (see Johnson, Bull and Beethoven,
Casanova), and RKR certainly validated that theory. The grungy masked man was nearly booed out of the building after crunching the `Dog with his Texas Speedbump combo, a powerbomb into a Boston
Crab.
Another newcomer, popular CHRISTOPHER SCARLET arrived in Charlotte for a big showdown with his toughest challenge to date, the strange force known as THE JUDGE. Scarlet impressed the fans by making quick work of the the underachieving agent of justice, knocking him out cold with a Scarletkick for the easy pin.
The final dark match of the evening saw the veteran leader of the
Wild Bunch TULLY ROEBUCK take on powerful youngster ALEX PATTERSON.
Ol' Tully's been on a bit of a losing streak these days, but reversed field Saturday night with a somewhat surprising victory over a disappointed Patterson. After a lackluster effort, Patterson was forced to tap out after being locked in Roebuck's legendary Rio
Grande Backbuster. At exactly 8:30 – OK, 8:37, as NASN's preceeding live spelling bee ran over -- , the arena erupted with cheers as Carolina Thunder went on the air!! RAW-C TV host Tim Dallas sat at ringside, along with a very special partner. . .

Carolina Television Title has become one of the most hotly contested in the whole of the RAW Empire. Several men staked a claim to top contendership, but when the dust settled, two men remained: Direwolf and Marc Tower.

Unfortunately, Tower's intentions were made easier by the fact that once again, Direwolf was unable to find childcare, and was forced to bring Barry to ringside once again. Direwolf displayed a bit of his old bloodthirsty ways in this match- up, even going so far as to bite Tower, deep enough to draw blood!!
There were blatant punches to Tower's "lower groin region", and a series of brutal, consecutive headbutts that drew blood from both men!!
Robertson tried his best to keep the match clean, showcasing his trademark offensive innovations in this, his highest-profile title defense to date. He scored several two counts on Direwolf with his trademark spots including a Chicken Wing Crash and a Last Sunset. As for Tower, The Eradicator was out for blood; the brawling between these two old friends turned bitter enemies rivaled anything Direwolf threw at Tower.
Tower had little time to get much offense in, as he was the chief target of both of his opponents, so when he did, he made it count.
A devastating Tower of Power on Robertson, and a Tower of Terror on
Direwolf each would easily scored the victory in a one on one match, but were broken up under the triple threat rules.
Robertson and Direwolf even managed a double team move, as they hit
Tower with a double slingshot suplex that got a two count; Barry even got involved in that one, tossing his mahogany counting blocks at
Tower as he lay on the mat.
Meanwhile, Barry's antics continued at ringside. Finding the rather attractive ring girl, he handed her a can of those creepy crawly things you can buy in a quarter vending machine. She screamed upon opening it, expecting a Coke; Barry cackled, replying with, "Yeah dat right beesh, scream fo' yo Mahogany Heir Apparent!"
Barry continued, running around the ring, screaming "BEATCHU MY STICK!", hitting on all the young ladies at ringside. "Hello young leddy, want to discover the secret in mah plaid boxas?"
The match see-sawed back and forth and back again for a intense, brutal ten minutes, as the three competitors seemed intent on destroying each other – and possibly winning the title in the process. After nearly decapitating Direwolf with a clothesline, the champ turned to Tower and followed a swift kick to the gut with a textbook cobra clutch suplex. But Tower snuck a shoulder up at two and a half – and as he did so, Barry tossed a pair of scissors at him.
Finally, Tower had had enough of Barry's antics. Tower ducked under the ropes, headed for the child – as the crowd gasped in horror.
Robertson, sensing something horrible was about to happen, turned his attention from Direwolf long enough to grab Tower around the waist, trying to keep him in the ring. The crowd popped big-time at that point, as Hounddog Murphy made his way out to ringside, scooping up
Barry and carting him out of harm's way, back to the locker room.
Robertson let go of Tower, sending him tumbling to the floor – and turned back to the ring, right into an Alpha Male chokeslam from
Direwolf. One three count later, we had a new Television Champion.
Tower, who had given chase to Murphy and Barry up the aisle, stopped when he heard the bell, and turned to smile at Robertson – who certainly did not smile back. Robertson pitched a fit in the ring, banging the mat in frustration and kicking the ropes before making the slow walk of a defeated champion back to the locker room.
