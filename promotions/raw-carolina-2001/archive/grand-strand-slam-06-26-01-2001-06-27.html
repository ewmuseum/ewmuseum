Grand Strand Slam 06.26.01

27 June 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
RAW Carolina’s summer beach tour continued Tuesday night with a show on Myrtle Beach’s Grand Strand, the Grand Strand Slam!!
Results: Before the first match, the traditional ten bell salute was sounded, paying tribute to the loss earlier in the day of RAW Atlantic superstar Steven “Mayhem” Thorne, killed in a tragic five car accident in New York. He will certainly be missed.
“FAST” EDDIE CUTLASS & “ERADICATOR” DIRK ROBERTSON & MYSTERY PARTNER vs. BULL “GATOR” JOHNSON & ALEX PATTERSON & THE JUDGE The opening bout featured six of the top contenders for David Jericho’s Piedmont Title, in a match set up after the wild brawl at the conclusion of last week’s Bull Johnson – Dirk
Robertson match. Cutlass and Robertson made their way to the ring, where their trio of opponents awaited, and the five men turned towards the aisle, looking for the identity of the mystery man. The arena went dark, save a lone spotlight on the aisle, as a tall man in jeans and workboots made his way to the ring, still wearing his mask. He hopped into the ring, grabbed the microphone from Bobby Lee Wilcox, and had this to say:
“Ladies and gents, stop the wondering now. I AM. . .Marc Tower.” With that, Tower unmasked to a mostly lukewarm crowd reaction. Hardcore fans of the independent wrestling scene recognized him as one of the superstars – and head promoter – of XWB in Buffalo,
Dirk Robertson’s old stomping grounds. But most fans didn’t know that – instead, they just saw him as the partner for two of their favorites, and that was all they needed to get behind him.
Robertson seemed visibly shaken at Tower’s arrival, just as he had last week. Clearly, there’s more to this relationship than meets the eye. As such, his innovative moves lacked their usual crispness and focus – he hit Patterson early on with both the
Last Sunset and the Chicken Wing Crash, which only scored two counts. Patterson could sense something was wrong, and began to work over his bitter rival, looking to soften him up for their semifinal date in the
Television Title Tournament (this week on RAW-C TV, Thursday morning,
2 AM, NASN).
Tower tagged himself in, and immediately earned the crowd’s gratitude by going to work on Patterson, looking to take up his old friend’s fight. But he seemed distracted, as well, and his trademark moves like the Tower of Power and Tower of Terror modified powerbombs also only earned two counts. Patterson tagged in Johnson, and he quickly took control of Tower, battering him with his own array of powerbombs. Johnson tagged in the Judge, which set Eddie Cutlass chomping at the bit – and he got his wish, tagging in and hitting the deluded lawman with a series of lightning-quick maneuvers, scoring several two counts.
After ten minutes of solid, back and forth action, Robertson and Tower botched a double clothesline, allowing Patterson to roll to the floor.
After briefly arguing amongst themselves, Robertson and Tower followed him to the floor, where they began a wild brawl.
Cutlass found himself alone in the ring with his two rivals. Johnson charged him, catching him with a vicious clothesline. Johnson scooped him up quickly, going for the powerbomb half of the Gatorsmash
Jambalaya – but Cutlass slipped out the back, sunset flipping Johnson to the mat, much the same way he pinned Patterson last week
– but the Judge made the save.
Jackie Boone, Jr. had lost all control of this one, and seemed on the verge of throwing the match out, but didn’t. Instead, he watched as The Judge pulled Cutlass up by his hair, and with the help of Johnson, delivered a spike Death Sentence, his inverted powerbomb.
Amazingly, Cutlass kicked out, as the crowd rallied behind him. But a second powerbomb, again with the help of Johnson, did Cutlass in, and he fell for the three count as Robertson and Tower brawled with Patterson on the floor.
But that wasn’t enough – they gave Cutlass a third Death Sentence, as the horrified crowd hoped for someone – ANYONE – to save their golden boy. Finally, Robertson and Tower broke away from Patterson long enough to chase the Judge and the Gator away, but the damage was done. Cutlass had to be helped to the locker room, barely able to stand with an apparent concussion.
Winners: Bull Johnson & Alex Patterson & The Judge (Spike Death Sentence) Time: 12:11 (** ½) STEVE FURY vs. STONE A recent graduate of the Suplex Academy, young Steve Fury was jacked sky-high for his supershow debut – too bad it came against the awesome force known as Stone.
Since joining the Empire Club, Stone has seen his career skyrocket
– and his viciousness in the ring taken to new heights. Fury was game, but Stone beat him within an inch of his life.
He hit Fury with headbutts. Belly to back suplexes. Belly to belly suplexes. A devastating gutbuster that may have broken a rib or two.
Fury mounted absolutely no offense as Stone continued to toy with him. Finally, satisfied with the destruction, Stone hit his Rolling Stone
Rikishi driver to score a very easy three count.

DIREWOLF vs. JESSIE MULDOON From the bizarre to the sublime; as Sidious exits, the manbeast Direwolf arrives, this week without his troublesome young son Barry. The two tall, powerful international veterans went at it tooth and nail in this classic power match.
Muldoon dominated the early going, even breaking out a little of his offensive repertoire of his Doomsday persona, clearly catching
Direwolf off guard. He nearly decapitated the chesty-haired superstar with a Love Muscle clothesline, but was only able to score a two count.
The crowd soon rallied to Direwolf’s aid, however, and he soon mounted a comeback against his fellow RAW-C newcomer, reversing a
Groin Stopper inverted atomic drop in mid-air into a sort of modified
DDT. He followed that up with a fireman’s carry suplex, and scored his first two count.
At the eight minute mark, Direwolf reached into his back of tricks for one of his signature finishing moves, The Liplock. Yes, it’s what it sounds, where he grabs his opponent and kisses them, refusing to let go until they submit. In his desperation, though, it seemed
Direwolf had forgotten Muldoon’s sexual preference. He didn’t submit – it didn’t bother him at all, despite
Direwolf’s. . .uhm, unattractiveness. Surprised by the ineffectiveness of his maneuver, Direwolf staggered backwards – but Muldoon caught him around the throat, and drilled him into the mat with the Chicago Connection Chokeslam, and went for the pin – but Direwolf got his foot across the bottom rope to save the match.
Muldoon thought he had the match won, and let referee Slick Wilson know just what he thought, allowing Direwolf enough time to recover.
He reached up, grabbing a handful of Muldoon’s tights in a roll-up – Full Moon over Myrtle Beach!! – and scored the quick three count.
Winner: Direwolf (Roll-Up) Time: 9:02 (** ¼) SIDIOUS THE CLOWN © vs. WILD STYLE, Carolina Tag-Team Title As the challengers awaited in the ring, the suddenly single tag-team champion came to the ring obviously down in the dumps, to the theme song from “Ducktales”. For those of you under – or over – a certain age, Ducktales was. . .never mind. The mini-Tron behind him flashed images of the dearly departed Kewlic in happier times, focusing on his in-ring work the last few weeks.
Sidious wore a t-shirt with Kewlic’s picture on it, rather tame for his standards. But once the bell rang, Sidious was a man possessed, acquitting himself quite nicely in a virtual handicap match with the tag-team specialists in the early going. Sid was angry, and looked to take out his aggression on his opponents, who were clearly taken off guard.
Sidious looked to take the fight to Wild Style anywhere and everywhere in the building – including the ringside area, where he hit
Brody and Hamilton with corkscrew dropkicks off the guard rail and a nice plancha onto Hamilton putting him through Bobby Lee Wilcox’ ringside table.
The young challengers regrouped, however, and took advantage of an overzealous Sidious crashing headlong into a ringpost to seize control of the match. They began to work the deranged clown over with several of their trademark double-team moves, including an awesome spike double underhoook piledriver that has to be seen to be believed.
But the champion wouldn’t stay down. Just when it seemed that the belts were in Wild Style’s grasp, Sidious would mount just enough offense to send them reeling, and allow himself a quick regrouping.
At the five minute mark, the crowd erupted as “Fast” Eddie Cutlass made his way to ringside, his head heavily bandaged, apparently looking to join Sidious once again as his tag-team partner.
He was followed quickly by the RAW Carolina medical team chief of staff Bones McGuirk, moving faster than anyone ever thought possibly for the stodgy old codger. McGuirk grabbed Wilcox’ mike as he got to ringside, and pleaded with Cutlass, noting that not only did
Cutlass have a “big ol’ bump on his noggin”, they might need to get him to the hospital and “open him up, and see what’s going on in there.”
Cutlass refused to heed such sound medical advice, however, and hopped up on the apron, eager to assist his friend. Referee Jackie Boone,
Jr., never the stickler for rules one would think his job would entail, allowed him to do so. Only Sidious seemed to have a problem with it – he had heard the ‘diagnosis’, too, and would not tag Cutlass in, unwilling to expose his friend – and hero – to more harm.
As Cutlass looked on helplessly from the apron, Sidious continued to take a beating, yet would not stay down for the three count. At THAT point, the crowd popped again as Flash made HIS way to ringside, chair in hand, and proceeded to plant himself firmly in the corner of Sid and Eddie, cheering them on and urging the hot Myrtle Beach crowd to do the same.
After Sidious somehow survived a back suplex – neckbreaker combo, Cutlass had seen enough. He charged into the ring and decked
Brody with a clothesline, and then tapped Hamilton with a dropkick as the crowd went nuts-o. Boone didn’t like it, however, and grabbed Cutlass, trying to force him back into his corner. Sidious grabbed Brody and ran him towards the ropes, in an attempted roll-up.
As he did so, Flash hopped up on the apron, chair in hand, and took a swing at Brody – who ducked, causing the chair to crash hard into Sidious’ forehead instead.
He went down like a load of bricks. Brody made the cover – and we have new Carolina tag-team champions.
Postmatch, as Sid lay groggy on the mat, Cutlass ripped into Flash for this helping hand gone awry, verbally berating him. But Flash was the picture of recalcitrance, and he apologized profusely, even cutting
Cutlass off to help Sidious back to his feet. The three men left the ring together, to a huge pop.
Winners (and new champions): Wild Style (mistimed chair to the noggin) Time: 9:02 (**) “ICEMAN” MARK ADAMS & FLASH vs. TOMMY TRENTON & EZ KILLER The crowning of new tag champs left the teams in the next match eager to establish themselves as the new number one contenders, as the red hot duo of Mark Adams and Flash took on the struggling young team of
Tommy Trenton and EZ Killer.
Flash returned to the ring for the match wearing a Myrtle Beach Pelicans t-shirt, looking to get a cheap pop from the fans of the hometown minor league baseball team – and of course it worked.
He and Adams continue to make great strides towards becoming a well-oiled team, making frequent tags, keeping the man in the ring fresh, all the little things that mark tag-team champions.
Trenton and Killer didn’t fare as well, however, as their once white-hot momentum has slowed to a crawl in recent weeks. Flash hit
Killer with a moveset he has dubbed the Flying Kickass, which consisted of Killer being in the Tree of Woe, followed by a rolling clothesline, and then finishing up with the Crazylegs Shuffle – think Road Dogg with better rhythm.
Adams also looked good, eschewing his normal hardcore style for some nice suplexes, including a blizzard suplex and a cradle brainbuster.
But it was Flash who scored the pinfall in the end, hitting Trenton with The Miracle reverse suplex, his devastating finisher.
Winners: Mark Adams & Flash (Miracle) Time: 3:21 (*) EL CID vs. “X-PRESS” DAVID JERICHO ©, Piedmont Heavyweight Title David Jericho defended his Piedmont Title against the Spanish luchador
El Cid in the next contest. As Jericho entered the ring to a hugely negative reaction, we got this:
[The lights go out and a loud thumping sound is heard over the PA system. Suddenly, and in cue with Freddy Mercury's voice, five letters appear on the screen.
F-L-A-S-H The King of the Impossible steps through the curtains to a nice pop from the crowd. Freshly showered, he is wearing long red trunks with black boots and a t-shirt that reads "If I was Krispy, I'd beat
Sidious." Flash carries a steel chair in hand as he makes his way to the ring. He walks around and sets the chair up next to the timekeeper before sitting down.]
Both Jericho and Cid seemed a bit off-put by the arrival of Flash, but he seemed more interested in watching the match than getting physically involved. It quickly became apparent that he was out to cheer Cid on, much the way he did for Sidious earlier in the evening.
Whenever Jericho would take control, Flash would rally Cid by having the crowd chant such witticisms as “Davey Sucks – Jericho
Blows” and things of that sort. When Cid had the advantage, he urged the crowd to cheer louder and louder, trying to get inside
Jericho’s head.
Flash got plenty of opportunity to do both, as the match see-sawed back and forth in the opening stages. The war of words between these two men this week seemed to morph into a battle for respect, as each man seemed intent on forcing the other to submit, in a bit of departure style-wise for each of them. Jericho barely escaped
Cid’s Viper Strike dragon sleeper a couple of times; likewise, Cid was able to free himself from an attempted X-Press Way on three separate occasions. As the match passed the ten minute mark, it continued to be very evenly-contested. Jericho made the first real mistake of the match, missing a missile dropkick attempt, and Cid went to work. A Running of the Bulls spear led to a quick Nightmare dragon suplex, and a
Godsend victory roll almost scored a three count. Cid then went to the top rope to attempt a Sword of Spain shooting star press – only to miss.
But Jericho was hurting – too slow to make the cover in time to get a three count. But Cid was in worse shape – a jumping spin kick nearly scored a three count for the champion, as did a fisherman’s suplex. Jericho then was finally successful in locking Cid in the X-Press Way. For nearly two minutes Cid held on, refusing to give in – and then the bell rang.
Jericho stood, celebrating an apparent submission victory, but referee
Slick Wilson informed him that the fifteen minute time limit had elapsed – and the match was ruled a draw. The fans gave a bittersweet cheer – Cid didn’t tap, but he didn’t win the title, either. Surely, a rematch between these two is not far off.
Decision: Time Limit Draw Time: 15:00 (***) “LAWBRINGER” THOMAS ADLER vs. “BLACKSMITH” JOHN SMYTHE ‘Dirty’ Doug Simpkins returned to the Carolinas last week with a challenge for Adler from his protégé, John Smythe. Whether or not this match was set up in some sort of collusion with RAW-C
President ‘Showboat’ Eddie Cannon remains to be seen – but the fans didn’t care, and were salivating at the prospect of the first match-up between these two bonafide superstars.
Adler started strong, focusing his offense early on Smythe’s right arm, reasoning that his technical advantage would be neutralized if he had a bad wing, especially given Adler’s near hundred pound weight advantage. Adler employed arm bars and the like, and soon had Smythe in some serious pain.
In one spot, Adler took control of a test of strength with an arm wringer, and then gave Smythe a stiff kick right in the armpit. Adler then smoothly drilled Smythe’s arm with knee and elbow blows at the same time, effectively scissoring the arm. Adler then hopped up on the second rope, and then off, landing with a legdrop across
Smythe’s elbow. Smythe, however, was not to be done in easily, and took control of the match after Simpkins tripped up Adler coming off the ropes, causing him to stumble right into a bulldog that nearly scored a three count.
Smythe showed why many consider him one of the finest technical wrestlers in the world, bruising Adler with all manner of suplexes.
The action went back and forth, well past the twelve minute mark. Adler had taken firm control, and had Smythe in the corner, battering him with a series of punches. Referee ‘Cottonmouth’ Joe
Lynch got in between the men, trying to get Adler out of the corner; somehow Smythe managed to knock Adler and Lynch’s heads together, leaving Adler dazed and Lynch out on the mat.
With Lynch stunned, Smythe pulled the larger Adler up in a full-nelson, setting him up for the Falling Anvils dragon suplex. As he did, Simpkins motioned for Smythe to bring Adler over towards the ropes, where Simpkins waited with a pair of brass knuckles. Smythe did so, and Simpkins took a swing at Adler – only to see the
Lawbringer duck out of the full nelson, and connect instead with
Smythe’s nose. Adler dropped down to cover, Lynch came around, and counted to three.
Winner: Thomas Adler (botched Old School chicanery) Time: 14:21 (*** ¼) JACK ANDERSON vs. VINCENT CONSTANTINE AUGUSTUS ©, Carolina Heavyweight
Title After nearly a year of chasing Morten Wyngarde all over the globe looking for a title shot, the Crimson Spitfire Jack Anderson got the next best thing: a shot at Wyngarde’s protégé Vincent C.
Augustus, the Carolina Heavyweight Champion. The match had a special feel to it from the get-go, as each man was accompanied by an entourage worthy of a Mike Tyson fight. VCA was introduced first, accompanied by mentor Morten Wyngarde, bodyguard
Jack Hammer, and stablemate Stone. Anderson came out next to a huge pop, accompanied by his manager Felicity Vass. He then called for
Bobby Lee Wilcox’ mike, and introduced his special cornerman for the evening:
Former RAW superstar – and his big brother –- Thomas Creighton. The crowd popped huge once again for the arrival of a bonafide wrestling legend, and this one had all the makings of a classic.
Anderson was pumped through the roof, and with the crowd behind him from the get-go, took the fight right to Augustus. Anderson kept the champion off his feet with a nice series of legsweeps and arm drags, showing off his technical knowledge.
VCA kept rolling to the floor, conferring with Wyngarde, trying to break Anderson’s rhythm, but nothing was working. It seemed to be Anderson’s night. Anderson slapped VCA in an anklelock, only to see Augustus drag him over to the ropes – giving Hammer a free shot right in Anderson’s jaw.
This touched off a brief wild melee at ringside, as Creighton came over with some hard right hands for Hammer, and a few for Wyngarde and
Stone for good measure, before retreating to his own corner. But the damage had been done back in the ring: VCA was in control.
Augustus began to move systematically through his catalogue of suplexs, bruising Anderson, scoring several near falls. Leg capture suplex, Japanese ocean cycle suplex – VCA showed off nearly as many throws as a Wyngarde match, as his mentor is a renowned suplex master.
At the six minute mark, Augustus tossed Anderson to the ropes – Anderson ducked a clothesline, ran the ropes, and ended up on VCA’s back for a sleeper hold. Augustus flailed wildly, staggering around the ring with the smaller man on his back, as the crowd roared. Like clockwork, Wyngarde leapt to the apron, engaging referee Slick Wilson. Hammer leapt onto the opposite apron, only to be knocked right back down by Creighton. But there was no answer for
Stone, who clipped Anderson right in the nose, sending him crashing back down to the mat.
If you thought the crowd was already loud, you hadn’t heard nothing yet – “Lawbringer” Thomas Adler had had enough, and made his way to ringside, headed right for Stone. Another wild melee erupted, with Creighton and Adler more than holding their own against the Empire Club, much to the crowd’s satisfaction.
But VCA was back in control. He taunted the Myrtle Beach crowd voraciously as he worked over Anderson, mixing in a beautiful inverted facelock brainbuster suplex amongst his many variations of a Fujiwara armbar, softening up Anderson for his Lockdown finisher.
Augustus really went to work on Anderson now, but the redheaded stranger just wouldn’t give in. And when Augustus went for a powerbomb – YOU CAN’T POWERBOMB JACK ANDERSON!!! He reversed it with a textbook hurricanrana, for a two and nine hundred ninety-nine thousand, nine hundred and ninety-nine millionths count
– It was that close. Really. Honest to God.
As Felicity led the crowd in cheers – Those legs, those. . .never mind – Anderson rallied once more, and a beautiful Northern Lights Suplex scored another REALLY CLOSE two count. On and on the battle raged, approaching the twenty minute mark and all-time classic status. The arrival of Adler seemed to curtail most of the outside the ring shenanigans, but the threat of more hung heavy in the salty coastal air.
At the twenty minute mark, Anderson was back in control, and looked to hook VCA into a Falcon Arrow – but Augustus reversed it, sending
Anderson all the way out of the ring, crashing hard through Bobby Lee
Wilcox’ second ringside table of the evening.
And that’s when Hammer and Stone struck, moving over to put the boots to Anderson. Creighton and Adler were not far behind, and the two sets of corner men were soon brawling wildly all over the ringside area and up the aisle towards the locker rooms.
That left Wyngarde alone with Anderson. As he moved in for another cheap shot, he was met with a swift quick to the groin from Felicity, and he doubled over in pain. Anderson got to his feet slowly, and saw in his doubled over old rival an opportunity he couldn’t resist
– and he DDT’d Wyngarde on the floor. The crowd roared, spurring on Anderson. He headed back into the ring a house o’ fire, taking down Augustus with a series of punches and clotheslines that had the champion reeling. After a nice swinging neckbreaker, Anderson’s enthusiasm got the better of him, as he headed for the top rope. But for once, his high-risk maneuver paid off: He crashed into a prone Augustus with a beautiful, if not textbook, Sky Twister Press that nearly blew the roof off of Kimball
Gymnasium.
As the wild brawl continued at ringside, Anderson moved in for the pin, but stopped dead in his tracks by the appearance of a masked man in the aisleway: old RAW UK nemesis – and Wyngarde associate
– Hidden Dragon appeared out of nowhere, and stood staring at Anderson. Anderson looked as though he’d seen a ghost – a fact which Augustus quickly took advantage of, hitting Anderson with a knee to the back, sending Anderson spilling to the floor.
Dragon moved quickly; as Wilson restrained Augustus, the large masked man scooped up Anderson – and delivered a Symphony to the floor?!? He rolled Anderson back in, and VCA rolled him up with the
Lockdown – for the three count. Augustus had escaped title intact once again.
Winner: Vincent C. Augustus (Lockdown) Time: 21:12 (****) Hidden Dragon grabbed a chair, and climbed into the ring, braining
Anderson with it twice before Adler and Creighton could break away from their brawl to get in the ring and chase him away. The entire
Empire Club entourage – Augustus, Wyngarde, Hammer, Stone, and the masked man made their way halfway up the aisle – where they stopped, and Hidden Dragon slowly unmasked – to reveal CASANOVA BEETHOVEN.
As the stunned crowd looked on, the Empire Club called for a house mike, to address the assembled heroes in the ring.

Anderson and El Cid. Tell them Vinny.”

[Crowd booooos.] VCA: “Why don't you bunch of fat, stupid, in-breeding pieces of dog-puke shut up and listen to a man with a bit of class...

How quickly you inbred hicks change your minds. You know I was sitting at home, with my shaven head, watching Raw Carolina fall apart. You see there was only one figurehead, that was Vincent. He saw the drop in class and morals, if you ever had any. He had to take action. He brought in Stone and then me. Imagine if you will, a tornado that blasts through a city –
OOMPH!!!!” The crowd erupted as a figure came flying off the top of the mini-Tron with a picture perfect flying somersault DDT that drove
Beethoven’s head right into the concrete – “Hurricane” Kirk Maclean. Maclean quickly rolled to his feet, bounded to the ring before Beethoven’s stunned fellow Club members even knew what happened, and stood alongside Adler, Anderson, and Creighton to a thunderous reaction.
