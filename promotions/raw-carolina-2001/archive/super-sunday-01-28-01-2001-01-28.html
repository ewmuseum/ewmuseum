RAW Carolina — Card Archive

Super Sunday 01/28/01

28 January 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
RAW Carolina held its’ annual Super Sunday this afternoon in Raleigh, North Carolina, at Reynolds Coliseum on the NC State campus. The event featured twelve exciting wrestling contests sandwiched around big- screen showings of the Ravens -- Giants Super Bowl. Results of this unique card:
HOUNDDOG MURPHY vs. JOSEPH NIGHTHORSE The self-styled Appalachian Assassin Hounddog Murphy took on the returning Joseph Nighthorse in the opening bout. Nighthorse has undergone a severe attitude adjustment since his previous stint in the
Carolinas, when he was known as “Jumpin’” Joey Knight. Having had both ankles broken and his career nearly ended by “Hard” Knox Roberts,
Nighthorse is more than bitter at a sport that would allow such things to happen to a man. That said, Nighthorse proceeded to try his best to send Murphy out of the sport in a similar fashion. The good-natured Murphy came in expecting a clean fight from his old friend Joey, but didn’t get it.
Nighthorse bit, kick, scratched and clawed his way to a dominant victory, ending it when Murphy tapped out to the Anklebreaker.
Winner: Joseph Nighthorse (Anklebreaker) Time: 3:24 (* ½) GLADIATORS vs. NIGHTMARES With the tag-team champions Coast to Coast seemingly vulnerable due to injuries, the Gladiators (Julius & Claudius) feel that now is their time to claim the gold, and looked to make a statement against the young masked Nightmares (Fear & Dread).
And a statement they did make, simply overpowering their fellow youngsters with an awesome array of power maneuvers, finishing the match with a Circus Maximus on Fear for the pin just shy of four minutes.
Winner: Gladiators (Circus Maximus) Time: 3:52 (**) “ALL-AMERICAN” CHAMP DURHAM vs. SGT. DAN LORENZO The next match featured the very first RAW Carolina sponsored graduate of the EWU, former NCAA wrestling champion, Champ Durham, taking on one of the finest cruiserweights in the world and one-half of Code Red,
Sgt. Dan Lorenzo. Before the match began, a brawl nearly started in the crowd as an overweight fan in the front row blocked the view of everyone behind him by holding up his CHUMP DURHAM sign, despite the fact that Super Sunday was not televised. Cooler heads prevailed, and the fan’s sign was confiscated.
The match itself was the first in Durham’s career against a cruiserweight like Lorenzo, and it showed early on as Lorenzo befuddled the mat-based Durham with his high-paced offense. Eventually, Durham capitalized on a missed moonsault and proceeded to suplex Lorenzo out of his boots for several minutes. Lorenzo reversed a Durham suplex to momentarily re-take the advantage, but Durham rolled him up for the pin.
Winner: Champ Durham (Roll Up) Time: 9:01 (***) ROBBIE DIAMOND © vs. “TRAINWRECK” CALEB JONES, Piedmont Heavyweight Title Popular high-flying Piedmont champion Robbie Diamond defended his title against the 451 pound Monster of the Midway “Trainwreck” Caleb Jones in a battle of two of the young up-and-comers in RAW Carolina.
The powerful Trainwreck tossed Diamond about like a rag doll in the early portions of the match, dominating him with his 200 plus pound weight advantage. A spectacular reversal occurred when Jones launched
Diamond javelin-style into the turnbuckles, only to see Diamond brace his arms on the top ropes and catch Jones in an unbelievable rolling head scissors, sending both men crashing to the floor.
As the cobwebs cleared, ho-lovin’ superstar Casanova Beethoven arrived, running to ringside with a chair in hand, blasting Diamond in the head over and over, screaming at him, “Give my my title shot!!” The referee called for the bell, signaling a disqualification as Beethoven battered
Diamond into unconsciousness. Finally, in a final dastardly act, Beethoven delivered a vicious Symphony on Diamond through the timekeeper’s table as the fans showered him with trash. Diamond was stretchered out, suffering from severe head and neck trauma.
Winner: Robbie Diamond (Disqualification) Time: 3:42 (* ½) JOSHUA STRONG vs. “THE BOSTON MANGLER” ADONIS MORGAN, Loser Leaves the Carolinas The war between former teammates Joshua Strong and Adonis Morgan came to a final, tragic end at Super Sunday when the two collided in a Loser
Leaves the Carolinas match. These two powerful young lions, once thought to be the future of tag- team wrestling, put on a solid, bone-crushing display of power wrestling in this match-up. Evenly sized and of even strength, the two battled. . .well, evenly for nearly ten minutes.
At that point, Napoleon Jones, the former manager of this once- promising tag-team wandered out to ringside. Jones had vowed neutrality in this war, but that soon proved to be an empty promise as he immediately tossed Morgan some knuckledusters, distracted the referee, and watch his man down Strong with the foreign object. The pinfall was academic. Joshua Strong is gone from RAW Carolina.
Winner: Adonis Morgan (Brass Knuckles) Time: 10:12 (** ½) Before the next match could get underway, the show was interrupted for the second week in a row by an appearance from noted RAW UK figure Jack
Hammer. Hammer was alone this week, without his employer Morten Wyngarde. After making his way to ringside, Hammer stood in the ring as the crowd booed him lustily. He waited for them to calm down just a bit before addressing them with his East London accent:
"I have some very good news for all you sad, sorry excuses for human beings. Do you know what it is? Well, since you generally don't know your arses from your elbows, I guess I should tell you. I had such fun last week beating down on Hounddog Murphy that I've decided a short flight over to America in a private jet from London each week isn't really that strenuous.
“Therefore, to keep myself in shape and generally make a mockery of everyone in RAW Carolina with my talent, I have signed up to compete in this backwater league. If you thought what I did last time was impressive. . .you've seen nothing yet."
Hammer threw down the microphone contemptuously, and turned to leave the ring. No sooner had he done so, however, when the
Breeders’ “Cannonball” played over the loudspeakers, signaling the arrival of perennial fan favorite “Cannonball” Luke Dobbs. Dobbs ventured to ringside, calling for the mike:
“You big cockney-sucker!! You come down here, thinking you’re better than the rest of us?!? Well, I got two words for ya: You ain’t!!!!”
With that, Dobbs leapt into the ring, and another impromptu match ensued. “CANNONBALL” LUKE DOBBS vs. JACK HAMMER Dobbs entered the ring the proverbial house o’ fire, and staggered the 300 pound Hammer with a series of punches and kicks. Hammer quickly regained his composure, however, and leveled Dobbs with a nasty clothesline. He followed that up with a devastating powerbomb to set up his dreaded Nail Gun: a tilt-o-whirl piledriver. The pinfall was academic. Hammer left the ring to even more heat from the crowd than he entered.
Winner: Jack Hammer (Nail Gun) Time: 1:34 (*) “THE LAWBRINGER” THOMAS ADLER vs. TULLY ROEBUCK Many in the Reynolds Crowd had come specifically for this match: Thomas Adler, fast becoming a folk hero in RAW Carolina for his rampage through the Wild Bunch, was about to square off against the leader of the pack, Tully Roebuck, in the first session’s main event. Roebuck entered the ring first, supremely arrogant, armed only with his ever- present sneer. Adler entered next to a huge ovation; he was armed only with a twinkle in his eye.
Before the match began, Adler grabbed the house mike and asked Roebuck to take a look at the projection screen TVs set up to show the big game. As Roebuck looked on, the TVs showed the Wild Bunch locker room – where Adler is laying into Isaac Walker, Gideon Walker, Jr., and
Hangman with a crowbar. Hangman in particular took some savage blows from the crowbar. After bashing them repeatedly, leaving them motionless, Adler turns to the camera and winks.
As the TVs winked off, Adler attacked Roebuck with savage ferocity. It seemed that something had snapped within Adler, and many in attendance began to fear for Roebuck’s life, despite his past transgressions.
Not exactly concerned for his gang’s welfare, but nonetheless distracted, Roebuck managed some token offense. But that’s all it was –
Token. Adler finished him off handily with the top rope clothesline he calls the Guillotine. The delirious crowd counted to three along with the referee; the match may not have been the classic they were expecting, but Adler had stuck it to the Wild Bunch – again.
Winner: Thomas Adler (Guillotine) Time: 4:46 (* ½) The projection screen televisions were then employed in their intended usage, as the fans were provided with free snacks and beer to watch the first half of the big game, as the Ravens opened up a 10-0 halftime lead.
At halftime, ring announcer Bobby Lee Wilcox entertained the crowd with his delightful Bob Newhart impression, and then introduced RAW
Carolina’s newest signee, the massive John Stonecutter. The 350 pounder stood up and acknowledged the crowd’s polite recognition, before being jerked back into his ringside seat by his flirtatious valet, Misty Dawn.
Wilcox then asked the crowd to please welcome a bonafide wrestling legend in attendance, “Dirty” Doug Simpkins. Most of the crowd was not even aware of Simpkins’ presence, as he was seated in the top row of the arena, said to be his favorite seat for scouting talent. Rumor has him looking to add to his Old School army; is one of RAW Carolina’s own next?!?
At that point, another efed veteran and current RAW superstar Ronny Ramirez appeared in the bleachers beside Simpkins, and broke a wooden chair over his head. He hit the downed Simpkins one more time for good measure, before screaming loud enough for everyone in the arena to hear, “That’s Old Skool for ya!!!” [Ed. – How we know he meant to spell it that way is another matter entirely.]
The crowd buzzed afterwards about the attack, which was apparently in retaliation for Old School shenanigans at Wrestleworld, RAW’s latest pay-per-view offering. The second half began, and the crowd roared back to life as a 17-0 snoozer became interesting again when Ron Dixon ran back a Ravens’ kickoff to cut their lead to 17-7. They went positively apesh#t as Jermaine Lewis returned the favor with a TD return of his own to push it back to 24-7.
[“THREE TOUCHDOWNS IN 36 SECONDS!!!!” POP!!!!!!!!] Soon, the lead was 31-7, and the restless, increasingly drunken fans became bored with the game, and began chanting feverishly for more action. But, true to their word, RAW Carolina waited until the final gun, as the Ravens won 34-7.
The second half of the card began less than five minutes after the game had ended, as Wilcox returned to the ring to introduce:
COREY “THE COOKER” COOKSON vs. MARK GOLDEN The evening’s second session began with the RAW Carolina debut of venerable Canadian superstar Corey “The Cooker” Cookson, as he took on fellow veteran “The California Stud” Mark Golden.
Cookson has upset some of RAW Carolina’s established stars with his contention that RAW Carolina is somehow beneath a man of his stature.
But as the entire locker room looked on just behind the curtain, they soon learned that the somewhat arrogant man in front of the camera stays right there, and the man in the ring is all business.
Cookson put on a clinic in the ring, displaying the trademark mix of lucha, pureoseu, and solid mat work befitting a man with his 21 years of international experience. He ooh-ed the crowd with a corkscrew splash, aah-ed them with a crucifix facebuster, and won their support – not to mention the match – with his oddly-named “Cooker Spaniel”, a reverse Franchiser.
Winner: Corey Cookson (Cooker Spaniel) Time: 3:48 (**) As a battered, weary, and generally pissed-off Mark Golden followed the triumphant Cookson to the locker room, the same overweight fan from the
Durham—Lorenzo match leaned over the rail to get right in his face, waving his GOLDEN IS SILENCED sign right in Golden’s face. Golden, in a snap bit of poor judgment, grabbed the fan by his hair and pulled his considerable girth over the railing. Golden began wailing on the boy with forearm blows, while the crowd looked on in horror.
Amazingly, the fan took the blows, and began to battle back, grabbing a Thunderbolt Jack Sampson foam finger from another ringside fan and battered Golden with it!! The fan tossed a soda in Golden’s face, blinding him, before rolling into the ring and beckoning Golden to follow. Enraged, and needing no prompting, Golden did so, charging right into a decent knife-edge chop. Golden staggered back into the corner before being avalanched.
The Raleigh crowd was absolutely apoplectic at this point. The fan looked around, feeding off the energy of the crowd, before launching his 300+ pound frame into a Bronco Buster on Golden (“Face full’a stuff!! Face full’a stuff!!”)
At this point, security finally hit the ring, and dragged the fan away – and the crowd booed mightily. They have yet to see enough of Joe
Superfan. “AWESOME” ALEC PRICE vs. THE LEATHERNECK The crowd was still buzzing about the “damndest thing they’d ever seen” as the next match began, pitting one half of Coast to Coast, Alec
Price, against the seven-foot masked Leatherneck, one-half of their most bitter rivals, Code Red. Code Red was responsible for injuring
Price’s partner “Unbelievable” Jeff Bennett last week at Throwdown in Tiger Town, and were looking to do the same to Price in Raleigh. The match never even began. As soon as Price reached ringside, both Leatherneck and Sgt. Dan Lorenzo attacked him on the floor. Leatherneck clotheslined Price from behind, moments before Lorenzo clocked him with a chair. They then delivered an absolutely sick spike powerbomb onto the chair before Israeli superstar Goliath arrived to chase them away. Price was able to walk away under his own power, as
Code Red was stopped before putting him on the shelf, as well. Match: No Contest Bobby Lee Wilcox then announced that due to their injuries suffered earlier in the evening at the hands (and crowbar) of “Lawbringer”
Thomas Adler, the Wild Bunch brother duo of Isaac and Gideon Walker, Jr. would not be able to compete in their scheduled match against “El Gaupo” Ricky Gomez & “Mad Dog” Billy Mancini. Despite being robbed of a scheduled match, the crowd popped like crazy. Wilcox stayed in the ring, moving right down the card (or is that up the card?!?) to introduce:
JACOB LEGACY vs. CASANOVA BEETHOVEN After Casanova Beethoven’s devious attack on Robbie Diamond in the evening’s first session, Jacob Legacy found himself in the unfamiliar role of fan favorite in their match. Add the fans’ support to Legacy’s manager Jericho Weaver and massive bodyguard Phoenix, and Beethoven found himself at long odds.
A fine technical match ensued, back and forth solid mat action for 15 minutes. Beethoven began to take control of the match, hitting a series of impressive moves, including a Cherry Blossom Bomb and a beautiful T-Bone Suplex, but was unable to put Legacy away. When
Phoenix hopped up on the apron to help his boss out of a bad spot, the crowd roared, hoping that Beethoven would get what he had coming to him.
But it was not to be. “The Nihilist” Ronny Ramirez made his second unscheduled appearance of the night, as he leaped over the security railing, leapt to the apron, and nailed a near-indescribable tornado leaping tombstone on Phoenix, spilling the big man’s blood all over the floor. As Legacy came out to his aid, Beethoven attacked from behind, knocking Legacy from the ring to the floor.
Ramirez grabbed the referee’s attention as Beethoven ripped up the mats laid over the concrete floor and hit his full-nelson tombstone, The
Symphony, on Legacy, knocking him out cold. Beethoven re-entered the ring, and earned the easy countout win.
Winner: Casanova Beethoven (Countout) Time: 16:24 (***) ACE MATTHEWS © vs. “TERRIBLE” TOMMY BEDFORD, Carolina Heavyweight Title Much has been made over the last week about the fact that for the first time in a long while, RAW Carolina’s hired gun Tommy Bedford would be fighting for no one but himself in this big title match against Ace
Matthews. Matthews entered the ring first, to a huge hero’s welcome. Bedford entered next, to a nearly as huge cascade of boos. Bedford stepped through the ropes, and called for the mike:
“I almost didn’t make it out here tonight. . .like it matters to you. I just had a long talk on the phone with a very persuasive man in London, England. It’s been said that Tommy Bedford’s never seen an offer he could refuse; and with what this guy was waving in my face, they’re right. “So, long story short, I won’t be beating Matthews’ ass tonight for his precious tin strap. I sold my spot in this match to the man that’s gonna do it instead. . . .JACK HAMMER.”
The crowd gasped in horror as the monstrous Hammer made his second appearance of the night, wearing a smug grin on his face. Morten
Wyngarde’s limitless financial reserves had come through again, and we got: ACE MATTHEWS © vs. JACK HAMMER, Carolina Heavyweight Title Matthews appeared to be nonplussed by this shocking turn of events, and immediately threw out the careful gameplan he had laid out for Bedford and went right to the attack on Hammer. The crowd roared mightily as their hero moved right into Hammer with a series of big forearms, and followed that up by knocking him out of the ring with a flying forearm. For the first time in his brief-but-impressive run here in
RAW Carolina, Hammer had shown weakness. The momentum shifted immediately upon Hammer’s re-entry into the ring. Hammer began systematically, methodically destroying the champion with his powerful arsenal, all the while working the neck to set up his devastating Nail Gun. Matthews fought back several times, but each time his attack was beaten back easily. Hammer dominated the champion in a manner that hasn’t been seen in Matthews’ home territory in years.
As he felt the title slipping through his grasp, Matthews mounted a desperation assault, first slipping out the back of an attempted suplex to hit a series of dropkicks and then an inverted DDT to score his first two count of the match. A innovative Rocker Dropper from the second rope got another two count, but that was all as Hammer kicked out again.
Matthews bounded off the ropes looking for added momentum and force, and as he approached the challenger, Hammer caught him in mid-air, twirled him, and hit the Nail Gun. The crowd gasped in shocked horror as Hammer paraded around the ring, pleased with himself, taunting the crowd, before placing the toe of his boot on Matthews’ sternum in an arrogant cover. Too arrogant, it seems, as Matthews was just able to sneak a shoulder up before the three count.
Hammer was incensed; he pulled up the nearly unconscious champion, shot him off the ropes, and delivered another Nail Gun. Then another. And finally a fourth. The crowd was numb at this point. The referee tried to step in and stop the carnage, perhaps to award the title to Hammer – and got a Nail Gun of his own for his trouble. A second official came down – Nail Gun. Finally, “Thunderbolt” Jack Sampson and Goliath hit the ring, and Hammer finally backed down, spitting on the fallen champion as he left. The official decision awarded the match to
Matthews on a disqualification, but he looked anything but a winner out cold in the center of the ring.
Winner: Ace Matthews (Disqualification) Time: 6:46 (** ½) ---- © RAW Carolina, MMI. http://homestead.juno.com/dougsimpkins/RAWCarolina.html
