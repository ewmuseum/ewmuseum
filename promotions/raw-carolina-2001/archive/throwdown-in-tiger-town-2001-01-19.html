RAW Carolina — Card Archive

Throwdown in Tiger Town

19 January 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A RAW Carolina record throng of over 3500 fans showed up at Littlejohn
Coliseum in Clemson, South Carolina, for Friday night’s huge event,
Throwdown in Tiger Town. Results:
ROBBIE DIAMOND © vs. JACOB LEGACY, Piedmont Heavyweight Title The series of matches that Robbie Diamond and Jacob Legacy had for this title in December are absolute classics, a must watch for workrate fans everywhere. Diamond finally realized his year-long goal, and wrested the title from Legacy. This, then, was the official return match.
As always, Legacy was accompanied to ringside by his manager Jericho
Weaver and his massive bodyguard Phoenix. And as always, Diamond was carried to ringside by a very supportive crowd. And when you look at it that way, 3500 to 3 hardly seems far odds.
The match itself, of course, was phenomenal. Every time these two have met, their match has been easily the match of the night, and the
Throwdown was no exception. For twenty minutes, Diamond battled Legacy with his explosive, high-flying offense while Legacy countered with his punishing suplexes. It seemed that the first man to make a major mistake would lose the match.
When Diamond missed his frogsplash moonsault at the 21 minute mark, the crowd buzzed with concern. Legacy reacted quickly, and hit three rolling German Suplexes, and Diamond barely got a shoulder up. Then, as Legacy tried another suplex, Diamond rolled him up with a small package for the pin out of nowhere. Diamond retained on this night, but Legacy will be back.
Winner: Robbie Diamond (Small Package) Time: 22:03 (**** ¼) JOSHUA STRONG vs. “THE BOSTON MANGLER” ADONIS MORGAN The match between Joshua Strong and Adonis Morgan at the Boot Camp
Brawl ended in a wild double disqualification brawl, and RAW Carolina
President “Showboat” Eddie Cannon ordered an immediate rematch, hoping to end this war quickly before one’s career was ended by the other.
Napoleon Jones made his way to ringside as the match began, and proceeded to perch himself in a neutral corner as he has in each of the other meetings between his former charges. Jones has so far refused to take sides in this most un-civil war.
The match was not expected to last long, given the size and strength of both competitors combined with their background in tag-team wrestling.
And it didn’t. A hard-hitting, power-packed match ended just short of the six minute mark when Morgan caught a charging Strong with a sick powerslam for the pin. The bell didn’t stop Morgan’s onslaught, however, as he continued to pound on Strong until officials came out to break it up.
Winner: Adonis Morgan (Powerslam) Time: 5:52 (**) Before the next match began, the crowd began to buzz as they noticed legendary former world champion “Dirty” Doug Simpkins sitting in the upper reaches of the Coliseum, observing all the action. The buzz over
Simpkins’ attendance was soon replaced by a louder one, as RAW UK superstar “Tycoon” Morten Wyngarde surprised everyone by coming out of the locker room to the ring, accompanied by his massive bodyguard Jack
Hammer.
Wyngarde proceeded to run down American wrestling in general and Carolinas wrestling in particular, much to the crowd’s disgust. He then issued an open challenge to anyone in the building, saying that no one in attendance could last three minutes with the physically imposing
Hammer. Several in the crowd looked towards Simpkins – and they missed the arrival of Hounddog Murphy, who came out to defend Carolina’s honor. And so we got:
HOUNDDOG MURPHY vs. JACK HAMMER Hounddog Murphy came out to defend his fans and his region, and succeeded only in proving Wyngarde right, as he was summarily squashed by Hammer. Hammer bullied the self-styled Appalachian Assassin from pillar to post before ending it mercifully with his imposing Nail Gun, a tilt-o-whirl piledriver. The fans littered the ring with trash as
Wyngarde and Hammer made their way to the exits.
Winner: Jack Hammer (Nail Gun) Time: 1:42 (DUD) TOMMY VICTORY vs. CASANOVA BEETHOVEN, Falls Count Anywhere on Campus The war between the ‘ho-lovin’ superstar Casanova Beethoven and veteran
Canadian superstar Tommy Victory has really intensified over the last few weeks, and they looked to settle the score in a match where pinfalls would count anywhere on the campus of Clemson University.
The crowd was a bit concerned that most of the action would take place where they could not see it, but that problem was remedied when RAW
Carolina officials unveiled several projection screen TVs around the arena, and a set of handheld cameramen who promised to follow the action wherever it went.
Technical questions answered, the fans settled in to cheer Victory on in a wild brawl that did indeed spill all over the campus. The match started in the ring, but didn’t stay there very long, as Victory chased
Beethoven into the locker room area. The battle then spilled out into the parking lot, and then into the adjacent football stadium, where
Victory put legendary Coach Frank Howard’s Rock to good use, ramming
Beethoven’s head into it repeatedly for a near fall.
From there, the two heated rivals brawled into the Student Union, and then down Fraternity Row. The wild brawl saw several two counts scored by both men, and a variety of foreign objects used by both. Finally, after nearly fifteen minutes, Beethoven cracked Victory’s skull open with a Symphony on the front steps of the Chi Omega sorority house.
After a winded referee counted to three, a bevy of blonde Southern beauties streamed out of the house to party with the purple-haired superstar. The fans inside, however, were not impressed.
Winner: Casanova Beethoven (Symphony) Time: 14:32 (*** ¼) “LAWBRINGER” THOMAS ADLER & GOLIATH vs. ISAAC WALKER & GIDEON WALKER, JR. The fans back in the arena were getting restless for more live action, and exploded with cheers when Thomas Adler and the 400 pound Israeli superstar Goliath made their way to the ring to do battle with the Wild
Bunch, in the form of the Walker brothers.
The Walker brothers were impressive with their teamwork, and to no one’s surprise were a much more cohesive than their opponents. But what Adler and Goliath lacked in teamwork, they more than made up for in individual displays of awesome power.
After ten minutes of good, solid, relatively clean action, Adler ended a back and forth match when he hit his Guillotine top rope clothesline on Gideon, Jr. for the pin. Hangman and Tully Roebuck then hit the ring as a four on two brawl ensued. Adler and Goliath then quickly turned the tide, clearing the ring of the Wild Bunch, topping it off with a monster double chokeslam on Hangman that sent the crowd into a fever pitch.
Winners: Thomas Adler & Goliath (Guillotine) Time: 10:18 (***) “THUNDERBOLT” JACK SAMPSON vs. “TERRIBLE” TOMMY BEDFORD The match between Jack Sampson and Tommy Bedford was billed as a number one contenders match, with the winner receiving his title shot at RAW
Carolina’s next big event, Super Sunday. Sampson has headlined countless shows the world over in his long career, but this would be a first for Bedford. The match was a good, old-fashioned contrast in styles, as Sampson’s power game matched up with Bedford’s “ring generalship”. Bedford’s bag of tricks is as deep as any in RAW-C, and he used them all in Clemson.
In the end, though, it was an old-fashioned wrestling move that got the surprise victory for Bedford, as he powered out of a piledriver attempt for his patented cradle piledriver and the three count.
Winner: Tommy Bedford (Cradle Piledriver) Time: 11:38 (*** ½) COAST TO COAST © vs. CODE RED, Carolina Tag-Team Titles Coast to Coast (“Unbelievable” Jeff Bennett & “Awesome” Alec Price) were scheduled to defend their tag titles against the monstrous ex-
Marines Code Red (Sgt. Dan Lorenzo & The Leatherneck), but it never happened.
As the champions made their way to the ring, Code Red came out of the stands to attack them savagely. After dropping Price throat-first over the safety railing, Code Red targeted Bennett, and brawled him over to the corner of the arena, where the basketball goals where being stored.
There, they delivered a depraved spike piledriver, driving Bennett with sickening force into the heavy base of the goal; then, in possibly the sickest moment in RAW Carolina history, Leatherneck launched Bennett, javelin-style, into – and through – the thick glass backboard.
Officials finally broke up the carnage, and Bennett was stretchered out a bloody, broken mess, as a horrified Price looked on.
Winners: No Contest ACE MATTHEWS © vs. TULLY ROEBUCK, Carolina Heavyweight Title Ace Matthews, who won the Carolina Title at Boot Camp Brawl, defended it in Clemson against the surly leader of the Wild Bunch, Tully Roebuck.
These two longtime rivals have met many times, and know each other’s styles inside and out. Every contest between them is an even, back and forth affair that swings on one critical mistake, and this one was no exception.
Roebuck locked in his Rio Grande Backbreaker fourteen minutes in, but
Matthews was able to make it to the ropes to break the hold, thanks to the exhortation of the fans. As Matthews pulled himself up on the ring ropes, Roebuck moved in, stomping away; Matthews leaned away from a stomp, grabbed Roebuck’s extended leg, and rolled him up for the pin.
Winner: Ace Matthews (Roll Up) Time: 15:03 (***) VIK VENTRESSA © vs. “STELLAR” STEVE KELLER, RAW World Title For the first time in history, the RAW World Title was defended at a
RAW Carolina evnt, as Vik Ventressa put his title on the line for the first and only time before Wrestleworld against one of the Carolinas’
all-time greats, “Stellar” Steve Keller. Ventressa cut a scathing promo before the match, saying that Keller and all of the Carolinas were “minor league”, and therefore beneath him.
The crowd turned on Ventressa instantly, making Keller the de facto crowd favorite, a very rare role for him.
Ventressa dominated the early portions of the match, and Keller indeed looked outclassed and out of his league. After a series of near falls,
Keller snapped out of his funk, and took the fight to Ventressa. The crowd cheered Keller – despite their every instinct – as he nearly suplexed the champion out of his boots, and got three straight two and a half counts.
Clearly feeling that tonight was his night, Keller took to the top rope – a place as unfamiliar to him as the inside of the crowd’s hearts. His attempted flying elbow was a spectacular failure, and
Ventressa seized the opportunity to lock him in an excruciating STF.
For nearly three minutes, Keller hung on, refusing to give up either on the match, or more importantly, his dream. With all his might, Keller drug Ventressa to the ropes, necessitating a break.
Ventressa was beside himself; he had never expected this to go this long. Keller, who had wrestled Ace Matthews in a 45 minute marathon at
Queen City Showdown, felt fresh as a daisy at the twenty-five minute mark where the match now stood. Buoyed by the champ’s frustration,
Keller launched an all-out assault with everything he had, finishing his flurry with his trademark Stellar Performance corkscrew suplex. As the referee’s hand slapped the mat the third time, Ventressa’s foot lay draped across the bottom rope. And the match continued.
This time, it was Keller who was frustrated. He turned to argue with the official, and when he turned back to Ventressa, he was met with the dreaded Heart Punch and went down like a sack of bricks. Ventressa covered. As the referee’s hand slapped three, Keller’s foot was draped across the bottom rope – but this time no one saw it.
Well, 3500 fans saw it, but the one person in the building who could do anything about it – the referee – did not, and Vik Ventressa escaped the Throwdown in Tiger Town with his World Title.
Winner: Vik Ventressa (Heart Punch) Time: 27:41 (****) ----- © RAW Carolina, MMI. Sent via Deja.com http://www.deja.com/
