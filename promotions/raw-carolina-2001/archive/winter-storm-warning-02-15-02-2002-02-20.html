Winter Storm Warning 02.15.02

20 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Minges Coliseum on the campus of East Carolina University hosted its’ first-ever RAW Carolina supercard Friday night –
Winter Storm Warning!! Results: MACABRE vs. XANDER ALPHA (Written with emotional panache by [name withheld], RAW Dean Emeritus) Everyone was still in shock at seeing that Macabre, the popular but dark competitor in RAW Carolina was actually the once clean cut and pure, light and hearty Johnny Alpha. This was, of course, revealed by
Xander Alpha who confronted him, only to be attacked. The challenge was issued, Xander Alpha against Macabre, and if Xander won, Johnny would have to give up his gimmick that was upsetting Momma
Alpha. If Xander lost, he would leave Johnny alone. The crowd were buzzing with curiosity for this match. Macabre was in first, and then came the silver-spandexed Xander Alpha, with a mic in hand.
"Johnny, I don't know what's going on in your head. But I know what's going on in my head...and my heart. But I can't seem to get through to you Johnny. But there's someone here who I hope can."
[Suddenly Momma Alpha appears from behind the curtain and gives Xander a hug. He passes her the mic.]
"Johnny. I want this to stop. I want you to snap out of this phase you're going through and come back home. Come back to momma. I miss you. Momma loves you. She wants her little Johnny back."
[Macabre stares coldly from the ring. Momma Alpha seems very annoyed.] "I don't want you two to fight. I'm not going to let it. But I swear, I may be old, but if you don't snap out of this now and be a good boy like you used to be, I'm going to go into that ring, put you over my knee and smack you like I've never smacked you before."
[Macabre continues to stare coldly. Momma Alpha throws her mic down and begins to walk up the steps. She steps into the ring, Macabre walks up to her, looking down on her fists clinched...]
[ CROWD POP! ] [Macabre throws his arms around Momma Alpha, hugs her, crying. Xander Alpha rushes in and joins in the embrace. Macabre's tears wash away his deathly white face paint. Johnny Alpha is back.]
Decision: No Match VAN WILD vs. “SCHOLARLY” SCOTT MCBRIDE With scant few dry eyes in the house, the Greenville crowd settled back in for the supercard debut of surfer dude superstar Van Wild, who squared off against young mat specialist Scott McBride. Wild offered up a handshake before the match, which the wrestling
‘purist’ McBride took with great delight – before sucker punching Wild in the jaw with his free hand. On the offensive,
McBride took it down to the mat, where his amateur background allowed him to wrestle circles around Wild – but not come anywhere close to a pinfall.
As for Wild, he took it all in good humor, and never appeared to worry in the slightest that the match was in jeopardy. Ever the student of game film, McBride managed to reverse a Wild Ride (airplane spin into a bulldog) with a crucifix, drawing a two and a half count – and an audible gasp from the crowd. But Wild rebounded with a stiff powerslam, and then headed up to the second turnbuckle, waited for
McBride to stagger to his feet, and came off with a bulldog for the pin. Winner: Van Wild (Second Rope Bulldog) Time: 5:02 (**) The mini-tron flashed to life as Wild made his way back to the locker room, showing the masked HENCHMEN standing in the hallway, confronting
JACKIE BOONE, JR. Thug: C’mon, Jackie – money’s tight!! You don’t have anything?!? JBJ: You boys let me down – I got nothing for you. I got my new team now, and if you think I’d rather have you than Pitrelli and Mayhem, then those masks must be too tight.
Brute: You little – Brute picks Boone up by his collar, slamming him against the wall. But Thug gently taps his partner on the shoulder, who turns to see “Dreadmind” Dave Pitrelli standing behind them, grinning wickedly. Brute places Boone on the floor softly, brushes off his collar, and hightails it down the hall.
“BLADERUNNER” XAVIER WINDWALKER vs. DAMIEN After a triumphant return to the Carolinas last week with a win over Damien Barnett, Xavier Windwalker (nee EZ Killer) readily accepted Damien’s challenge for a rematch, on the grounds that he was prepared to take on a fellow cruiserweight, not a 300 pound bruiser.
After a big win over Dirk Robertson on RAW-C TV, Windwalker looked to have all the momentum in his corner, and battered his smaller opponent from the opening bell much the same way he did in their last meeting.
But Damien knew what kind of fight he was in for this week, and after kicking out of a running powerslam, he reached up and punched the
Bladerunner right in the throat with both fists in a manner that would make his older brother Spikyjim proud, and went to work.
Damien repeatedly took to the air, off the ropes, off the top turnbuckle, through the ropes to the floor – sending his body crashing into Windwalker’s with spectacular variety – none of which could secure the three count, as the bigger, stronger
Bladerunner kicked out of each one. At the six minute mark, Damien came off the top with a clothesline – only to be met with one from Windwalker midair, nearly decapitating him. Windwalker motioned to the crowd – he was going for his Bladecutter pedigree. But
Damien was ready, and delivered a sort of reverse headbutt to Windwalker’s groin, freeing himself and staggering the big man. As Damien regrouped, Windwalker scooped him up into another running powerslam – but when he hit the mat, he rolled Windwalker over with him into a pinning crucifix, and Hank Jackson made the three count, giving the cruiserweight the upset victory.
Winner: Damien (Crucifix) Time: 6:42 (***) HELL ON EARTH vs. MERSEYSIDERS (Come Back to the Five and Dime, Jimmy Dean, and write this match.) This was a debut for Hell On Earth, and many people saw it as the chance for the 'Siders to get back into the swing of things, after having struggled since Power O' One reunited Alan Mangan and Lee
Roberts a few months ago. However, the Rebel and Blacktop clearly had other ideas, and took it to the RAW veterans straight from the get-go, giving fans a close up view of some hardcore power wrestling - and, at one point, a REAL close up view of Alan Mangan, who got backdropped over the top rope by
Rebel before Blacktop hit a flying clothesline on him from the apron. Hell, apparently, aren't at their best once a match has been going a while, but we didn't get to see that particular weakness this time - after just 3 minutes, Blacktop hit Mangan with the devastating jackknife powerbomb he calls the Career Ender. Lee Roberts charged in to make the save, only to be caught himself by Blacktop, and fans were treated to their first sight of the End Of The Road, an incredible move in which Blacktop picked up Lee and got him in position for a inverted crucifix powerbomb, before his partner leapt up and pulled him down with the Confederate Drop kamikaze DDT. Cover was made, and the Siders weren't getting up any time soon.
Hell On Earth make a VERY impressive debut, and word of mouth should ensure that people will be watching out for their first appearance on
RAW Aggression, which is thought to be coming at the first card after Ring Dreams. Siders, on the other hand, are slipping, and with the introduction of Men In Black to an already stoked tag division, will need to watch out that they don't get left behind in the title race.
Winners: Hell on Earth (End of the Road) Time: 3:12 (*) The mini-Tron flashed back to life, and we had followed The Henchmen down the hall after their encounter with Jackie Boone, Jr. They turned a corner, and walked right into Suplex Academy kids Scott
Starring and Othello Washington, reliving their excellent time limit draw at Sunday’s house show. Still pissed off, Thug sucker punched Washington, and Brute followed suit on Starring, as the
Henchmen proceeded to put the boots to the kids. Grabbing a nearby trashcan, they spike piledrover Washington right through it, and then delivered the same to Starring, before walking off disgustedly.
CHRISTIAN DEMONSTONE © vs. JESSIE MULDOON, Coastal Television Title It’s been a busy ten days for Christian Demonstone, first losing the Coastal Title, then unifying said title with the Television Title with a win in a triple threat match over Paul Sheridan and Mayhem
Wednesday night on RAW-C TV. And for his first title defense – originally slated to be for his Bi-Coastal Television Title only
– he takes on a former ally, big Jessie Muldoon. Many expected this to be an evenly-fought contest, given these two powerhouses’ familiarity with one another from their brief run as a tag-team – but that wasn’t the case. Demonstone dominated this match from beginning to end – not that that was a long span. Muldoon was crushed by a Canuck Driver just north of the three minute mark, and Slick Wilson counted the merciful pin.
But Demonstone wasn’t finished. Grabbing Muldoon in a waistlock, he proceeded to launch him across the ring with a German
Suplex – and then another – and then a third. Finally, secure in the belief that he had placed the final nail in Jessie
Muldoon’s coffin, he left the ring to a huge round of boos from the Pirate faithful. Winner: Christian Demonstone (Canuck Driver) Time: 3:05 (*) PURGATORY vs. “DREADMIND” DAVE PITRELLI & MAYHEM ([name withheld] on the keyboards for this one.) The next match of the evening saw former Windy City RAW Tag Team Champions DEMENTIA and SYNN get their shot at revenge against Jackie Boone, Jr., and the now-defunct Network as they squared off against DAVE "DREADMIND" PITRELLI and The Man They Call MAYHEM, The Network's two enforcers who attacked Purgatory from behind at "Super Sunday,
Bloody Sunday," adding injury to insult after they were unceremoniously fired by Boone. From the opening bell, Dementia and Synn dominated Pitrelli and Mayhem, taking advantage of Pitrelli's recent distraction (i.e., the "Little Voice" in the big man's head) and Mayhem's frustration over his recent losing streak here in the Carolinas, as Jackie Boone looked on unconcernedly from the floor.
After several quick tags on the part of the former WC-RAW Champions, Synn was able to score the first near-fall of the night on Mayhem, much to the resurrected Ultimate Fighter's dismay.
From there, a tag out to Pitrelli turned the match back to the favor of Jackie Boone's two enforcers as the former Piedmont Champion manhandled Synn AND his much bigger partner, nearly decapitating
Dementia with a clothesline from...well, that one's been copyrighted so let's just call it a DEVASTATING clothesline. Another tag out to
Mayhem saw the man who makes his official RAW debut later this week at their Ring Dreams pay-per-view go for his "Prayer for Dying"
double-underhook face breaker only to catch a faceful of fireball for the THIRD time from Jackie Boone, Jr., as the referee-turned-manager was trying to hit Dementia. One quick schoolboy rollup later and
Purgatory had gained some measure of revenge against their former Network stablemates, much to the delight of the fans in Greenville. Winners: Purgatory (Typically botched Jackie Boone interference) Time: 7:42 (** ¼) The match may have ended there but the action most certainly did not as Mayhem, now livid over suffering yet another loss at the hands of his manager, slowly got back to his feet and confronted Boone on the floor as the one-time Network executive was making his way back to the dressing room as if nothing had happened. Putting his hands on Boone,
Mayhem lifted the smaller man clear off the floor and, with the encouragement of the near-capacity crowd in attendance, Gorilla pressed him onto the metal guardrail separating the ring from the fans(!!).
Picking his now unconscious manager up off the floor, Mayhem screamed out "You're Fired, Bitch!!"...and gave Boone the "Prayer for Dying" to a surprisingly HUGE ovation. At this point, Dave Pitrelli grabbed
Mayhem to hold him back from inflicting any further damage and found himself taking a ham hock fist square in the face from the enraged giant. Pitrelli and Mayhem slugged it out as RAW-C officials tried to separate them until Mayhem's longtime friend and associate Chris King made an appearance at ringside himself, clipping Pitrelli's legs out from under him and allowing Mayhem to lay HIM out as well before grabbing a microphone and making this statement.
"Dreadmind, you've lost your f<BLEEP>king mind defending this chump and I've had it with you, too. Consider this the severing of our relationship and, if you want to come looking for me, I won't be hard to find, bitch.
I'LL BE IN RAW!!" Mayhem and King then exited the arena to (go figure!!) a HUGE pop as ring attendants and paramedics tended to Boone and Pitrelli at ringside.
ROADKILL RICK vs. CASANOVA BEETHOVEN After splitting a pair of matches with fellow former Carolina champion Marc Tower on the house show circuit last weekend, Casanova Beethoven looked for a win over a former Piedmont Champion to vault himself back into title contention. Roadkill Rick, on the other hand, has yet to find his groove since his recent return to action following his injuries at the hands of The Network; a win here would definitely get him back on the right track.
The match was a wild brawl, with the still unusual phenomenon of the crowd fully behind RKR. All over the ring and down to the ringside area it raged, with Beethoven’s sidekick Sebastian Sin more than willing to get involved – usually with disastrous results, like when Sin pinned Roadkill’s arms behind him for a chair shot, only to see him slip free and catch the chair from Beethoven himself.
After nearly ten minutes of nonstop action, the two men found themselves back on the floor, where RKR sent Beethoven crashing into the safety railing with an Irish Whip. Roadkill fidgeted with his mask, and the crowd buzzed – they knew what was coming –
PURPLE MIST(!!). But Beethoven was ready, grabbing his lackey Sebastian Sin and pulling him into the path of the mist in one fluid motion. Sin went down clutching his eyes, but not before Beethoven used him as a shield one more time to kick RKR in the stomach, doubling him over to deliver a Symphony on the concrete(!!).
Beethoven rolled him back into the ring, and scored the 1-2-3. Winner: Casanova Beethoven (Symphony) Time: 9:56 (***) As the boos cascaded over Beethoven on his way back to the locker room, the Mini-Tron flashed to life one last time for the evening, as we once again followed the continuing misadventures of The Henchmen.
This time, they were standing in front of a soda machine with RAW-C President “Showboat” Eddie Cannon, who drew a big pop from the crowd after Super Sunday. Thug: C’mon, Showboat – Did you know you were my favorite wrestler as a kid? Brute: Mine, too!! C’mon – give us our jobs back! EC: You boys made a big mistake. You lined up on the losing side, and to be honest with you – most of the boys in the locker room aren’t gonna be very forgiving. But – you’re not the first visitors I’ve had tonight. Othello and Scott stopped by earlier.
Brute: Who? EC: The two kids you attacked in the hallway earlier, numbnuts. They pleaded with me to reinstate you so that they could kick your ass. At first, I wouldn’t budge, but they were insistent. So, tell you what: Next week, at Deep Freeze, you two clowns will take on the kids, Othello Washington and Scott Starring. You win the match, you get your jobs back. You don’t – go ahead and pack your bags now.
Thug: Those two? You got a deal, Showboat! “FAST” EDDIE CUTLASS © vs. “ERADICATOR” DIRK ROBERTSON, Piedmont Heavyweight Title The fans were stoked for this one – the return to the ring of one of the most popular stars EVER in the Carolinas, Eddie Cutlass.
Well, he’d been in the ring for the better part of two months now, masquerading as luchadore La Estoque. But after recapturing his
Piedmont Title from Dave Pitrelli at Super Sunday, he unmasked, and the fans rejoiced. His first challenger of his new reign was the double-tough Dirk Robertson, himself a former Television Champion, in a match that promised to be an excellent affair.
Cutlass was on fire in the early going, eager to show his fans that he hadn’t missed a step, and scored several two counts in the first few minutes. He went for a superplex at the four minute mark, but
Robertson blocked that with a headbutt – and then launched himself up and over Cutlass’ shoulders, grabbing his legs on the way down for a Last Sunset sunset flip powerbomb. He looked to have the three count, but referee ‘Cottonmouth’ Joe Lynch saw that he had both feet on the middle turnbuckle for leverage, and stopped the count.
The match see-sawed back and forth for the next several minutes, Cutlass utilizing some of the new lucha moves he had incorporated into his arsenal as La Estoque, while Robertson unveiled a few more innovative moves befitting his days as a backyard wrestler.
The end of the match saw Robertson in control, as he climbed onto the second turnbuckle looking for a tornado DDT – but Cutlass blocked it, grabbed The Eradicator around the shoulders, and dropped him with a Guarantee from the second rope, earning the three count to end a great match.
Winner: Eddie Cutlass (Second Rope Guarantee) Time: 12:22 (*** ½) TERRY JACOBS & JUSTIN HARPER © vs. MIKE STEELE & STONE, Carolina Tag-Team Titles Terry Jacobs and Justin Harper shocked the RAW Empire at Super Sunday by capturing the Carolina Tag-Team Titles from the Unified Champions
Kirk Maclean and Stevie Ray Murdoch in a huge upset; now the new champs look to legitimize their reign, starting with a match against two of the men directly responsible for the demise of The Network,
Mike Steele and Stone. The match was a clean, scientific affair, with all four men firmly in the fans’ good graces. Things looked bad for the champions when
Steele locked his fellow rookie Harper in his Wolfe Bite sleeper, but the California kid stumbled to the ropes with Steele on his back, causing a break in the hold. Not a minute later, he lifted Steele up and back down with his California Dreaming chokeslam, only to have
Stone make the save and two and thisclose. Nonstop action ensued, with all four men managing to hit their finishers on an opponent, only to see his partner make the save.
But the match soon turned the way the challengers wanted to see it, with the experienced Stone in the ring with the rookie Harper, and the man from Boulder began working the youngster over, scoring several two counts in the span of a minute and a half.
Stone flipped Harper up onto his shoulders, and looked to be one Rolling Stone away from capturing the tag titles – but Jacobs had other ideas. He bolted across the ring, catching the diminutive
Stone with a Charlotte Cyclone lariat(!!). Harper fell on top of Stone, and Slick Wilson looked to make the count. Steele came in to make the save, but he, too, was caught with a Cyclone, and it was all over.
Stone got up slow, looking pissed – but reached out and shook Jacobs’ hand, anyway. Harper offered his hand to Steele, who took it as the four men congratulated each other on a great match in a nice moment.
Winners: Terry Jacobs & Justin Harper (Charlotte Cyclone) Time: 7:22 (***) JACK ANDERSON & “X-PRESS” DAVID JERICHO vs. JACK DOE & PAUL SHERIDAN Jack Anderson was a bit wary heading into this match; teaming with a guy he has had issues with in the past to take one his archrival and the man who waylayed his fiancé with a steel chair? Who booked THIS crap, indeed.
Jack Doe, on the other hand, could not have been happier – he’s been clamoring for a match with Anderson since he got to the Carolinas, in order to expose the Crimson Spitfire as the
‘fraud he truly is’. Whatever Doe’s delusions, this one promised to be a dandy. Doe started the match for his team, against the powerful, chiseled David Jericho, who didn’t have a lot to say regarding this contest. Doe tried to get the crowd behind him by clapping his hands,
Rock n’ Roll Express style, but was rewarded with a big chorus of boos – attacking the sexiest woman in RAW-C will do that for your fan appeal.
Despite his still-bland style, Doe was aggressive in this match, what with an arena full of fans to impress. After getting Jericho down on the mat with a snapmare, he really wrenched in that rear chinlock, like he never had before, and Jericho looked to be in a world of hurt.
But Jericho quickly powered out of that, and sent Doe sprawling with a clothesline into his corner, where he tagged in the former Television
Champion Paul Sheridan. Sheridan tried some high-paced stuff, most of which bounced right off the granite Jericho, who finally gave the fans what they wanted, and tagged in Jack Anderson.
The fans have been waiting to see Anderson rip Sheridan apart for quite a while now, ever since the rookie was revealed as the evil mind behind TGDAB gambit. Sheridan tried some chain wrestling early, but
Anderson would have none of it, instead punching the kid right in the jaw, following up with a DDT that brought Doe in to make the save.
This brought Jericho back in for the first of many wild ‘all four men in the ring at once’ sequences this match would see.
As the match passed the ten minute mark, the rookies were actually functioning quite well as a team, tagging in and out with regularity, keeping Jericho off-balance. Doe dropped the X-Press with a hammerlock slam and tagged in Sheridan, who came off the top with a moonsault – thisclose to getting the three count. Sheridan grabbed Hank Jackson around the collar, livid at the two count – allowing Shona Maynard to sneak into the ring and deliver an Exploder
Kick right to the ‘lower midsection’ of an already-winded Jericho, who went down like a lump. Anderson smelled trouble, and as Shona dropped off the apron, her work done, he charged into the ring. But Sheridan met him head-on, looking to herd him out of the ring. As Sheridan took Anderson up and over the top rope with a clothesline, Doe dropped Jericho with a Ticket to
Stardom, and that was all she wrote. Winners: Paul Sheridan & Jack Doe (Ticket to Stardom) Time: 13:34 (***) Sheridan rolled back into the ring, and as Hank Jackson raised the victors’ arms in. . .er, victory – Doe blindsided his partner with an enzugiri(!!), before leaving the ring to a confused buzz from the crowd with a huge smirk on his face.
“SENSATIONAL” SCOTT STUDD © vs. MARC TOWER, Carolina Heavyweight Title In Scott Studd’s eyes, the only way to legitimize his title run was with another win over the former champion, Marc Tower. And this one had to be clean – no Network, no Eddie Cannon, just two top competitors giving their all, let the best man win.
And if you think Marc Tower wouldn’t agree, well, then, you’re crazy. Normally the strongest man in the ring in any match, despite weighing just 245 pounds, Tower knew he couldn’t match strength with the massive Studd. So in a bit of a departure from his normal style, he took a fast-paced approach, and had an unsuspecting champion off-balance in the early going, even scoring a two count with a bulldog from the second rope.
But Tower’s strategy backfired at the four minute mark, when Studd sidestepped an attempted clothesline from the top rope and rode Tower to the mat with a sort-of crossface armbar, and held onto the hold once they hit the mat. Studd kept Tower in the crossface for the next minute and a half, softening up his neck and back for a potential
Studdly Stretch down the line. Studd clubbed away at Tower’s lower back with punishing forearms and side suplexes, really brutalizing the former champion. At the eight minute mark, he locked in the Studdly Stretch, and things looked grim for the Pittsburgh Powerhouse.
But as he had done in their previous match-ups, Tower refused to give in, his face racked with pain as he inched closer and closer towards the ropes. Finally, after four(!!) minutes in the Stretch, he reached the bottom rope, necessitating a break.
But the hold had done some damage – Tower could hardly stand up. Studd pulled him up to his feet, taking him down just as quickly with a stiff clothesline, but only got a two count. Belly to belly suplex
– another two count. Belly to back suplex – but Tower flipped over the top, and dropped Studd with a reverse neckbreaker(!!).
Still limping badly with the injured back, Tower somehow hoisted Studd up into a fireman’s carry, and dropped him with a Pittsburgh
Plant, for a two count of his own. Tower followed up with a high-angle spinebuster for another two, and a flying forearm that would make Tito Naranja proud – but only for a two.
Back and forth the battle raged for the next few minutes, each man delivering one high impact move after another. At the twenty(!!)
minute mark, Studd planted Tower with a DDT, and then dropped him on the top rope. Studd was looking for a superplex; but Tower wasn’t quite ready for that, and shoved Studd hard off the ropes to the mat. Studd got up groggy, and the crowd buzzed as Tower stood on the top rope.
He launched, looking for a double axe-handle, but Studd caught him in a bearhug, and with one quick motion tossed him up into a sort of modified overhead belly-to-belly chin-first into the top turnbuckle
– a sort of modified Studd Gun. Tower went down like a shot, and Studd made the cover for the three count, and – more importantly – a legitimate win over Tower.
Winner: Scott Studd (Studd Gun) Time: 20:31 (****) ---- © RAW Carolina, MMII. http://www.yahoogroups.com/group/RAWCarolina
