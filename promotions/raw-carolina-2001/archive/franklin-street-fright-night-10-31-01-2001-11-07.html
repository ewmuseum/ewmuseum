Franklin Street Fright Night 10.31.01

7 November 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
There's no wilder place in the Carolinas to be for Halloween than the stretch of Chapel Hill known as Franklin Street – and that's exactly where RAW Carolina was, for Franklin Street Fright Night!!!
COLLATERAL DAMAGE vs. PAUL SHERIDAN & HARBINGER Since graduating from the Suplex Academy, young Collateral Damage – Mike Diamond & Mike Steele – have been very impressive, and have positioned themselves as the clear future of the RAW-C tag-team division. In Chapel Hill, they found themselves up against a pair of current SA students, Paul Sheridan and the apocalyptic Harbinger.
This match was the first opportunity for the promising Sheridan to team with Harbinger, and it showed. CD dominated from the opening bell, with Sheridan on the receiving end of Steele's wide variety of powerbombs the majority of the match.
Harbinger fared no better, and the match looked to be over at the five minute mark when CD caught Sheridan with their impressive Dead
End Drop elbow drop backbreaker combination. Diamond went to make the cover – and a VERY pissed off Your Worst Nightmare hit the ring.
Apparently still peeved at CD for their confrontation with Adams in a Greenville hospital recently – or maybe angry over last night's RAW-C TV – or maybe just pissed in general, they hit the ring bearing steel chairs, and one shot to the head of each of Collateral Damage spilled them out of the ring.
A double chair shot sent Harbinger out to the floor, as well, leaving the tag champs alone with Sheridan, and they quickly dropped him with an assisted chokeslam onto a steel chair, which Pitrelli followed up with a legdrop for no reason. Their bloodlust sated, the champions left the ring to a huge chorus of boos. As for Sheridan, he left the ring on a stretcher, victim to two top teams' finishers in a span of two minutes.
Decision: No Contest Time: 5:13 (* ˝) MARC TOWER Š vs. "THE WEAPON" FRANK REYNARD, Carolina Television Title The fans were excited as all get-out at the prospects of the next match-up, pitting two of the absolute toughest in all of RAW Carolina one on one for the Television Title. Marc Tower got a huge pop from the Chapel Hill crowd, well on his way to becoming a top fan favorite in the Carolinas.
The match was a hard-hitting affair, both men fully aware of the ten minute time limit that makes winning the Television Title so tough for challengers. Tower came in still smarting over last night's RAW-
C TV match, and Reynard took full advantage of the gash on his forehead, peppering him early and often with stiff kicks and hard punches to the wound, leaving the champ a bloody mess early on.
Reynard seemed to enjoy the sight of blood on his opponent – but Tower enjoyed his own blood, as well, as the taste of it seemed to wake him up. Tower kicked it into high gear, attacking Reynard with his entire arsenal – Tower of Terror, the Steel City Slam, Tower of
Power – but nothing could get a three count, as the former pitfighter kicked out of each one.
After Reynard kicked out of the Tower of Power, Tower rolled to the floor, frustrated. Reynard slowly got to his feet in the ring, groggy to the point where referee Slick Wilson checked with him to see if he could continue. As Wilson did so, Your Worst Nightmare made their second appearance of the night, dropping Tower across the safety rail with an assisted chokeslam. The crowd booed heavily as
Pitrelli rolled Tower back into the ring and the champs disappeared like thieves in the night.
Reynard covered Tower as he rolled into the ring, oblivious as to what happened. But Wilson had seen what happened, and called for the bell, disqualifying Reynard. Reynard went nuts, grabbing Wilson by the collar, shaking him violently until the official explained just what happened. Reynard was incensed, and took off down the aisle to find the men that may have cost him the television title.
In the ring, Tower lay motionless from all the blood loss. After a minute or two, he got to his feet, only to stumble through the ropes and off the apron down to the floor. Blood Brothers Dirk Robertson and Jayson Rayge made their way out to assist their comrade back to the locker room.
Winner: Marc Tower (Disqualification) Time: 7:32 (**) "BILLIONAIRE" JOE FORBES vs. VINCENT CONSTANTINE AUGUSTUS A war of words sparked this encounter between the newcomer Forbes, bent on making a name for himself, and the former Carolina champion
VCA, eager to keep his spot among the promotion's elite. As for the fans, they couldn't care less about a battle between one arrogant
[name withheld] kid and another – well, another FORMER [name withheld] kid, but close enough, right?!? – but that didn't mean they didn't want to see them kill each other.
Despite their arrogant rulebreaking, both men in this contest possess a world of technical talent, and the early portions of this match were a textbook example of that. Counters, counter counters, and yet more counters were tossed about with abandon, with neither man able to gain a clear advantage.
After four minutes of such action, Augustus became clearly distracted with someone in the front row. VCA rolled to the floor and got in the man's face – DIREWOLF!! Smiling all the while, the Father of the
Year taunted VCA by removing his CHESTHAIR CLUB FOR MEN t-shirt to reveal that his chest hair had regrown fully after the dastardly glue attack perpertrated by VCA some months back.
Shaken, VCA got back into the ring, where the more experienced Forbes took advantage with some quick kneedrops to the back of the head that looked VERY painful. With Direwolf cheering him on, Forbes dropped
VCA with a gutwrench suplex, and then headed for the top rope. But he missed a flying elbow attempt, and Augustus quickly capitalized with a Rising Sun Suplex, nearly taking Forbes out of his boots. He then slapped on the Empire Clutch, to which Forbes eventually tapped out, giving Augustus the victory.
Rolling back to the floor, VCA got right in Direwolf's face, and the two big men jawed back and forth. Having had enough, VCA paintbrushed Direwolf across the face. Direwolf jumped the rail and a pull-apart brawl quickly ensued, bringing out scads of RAW-C officials and Chapel Hill's Finest to break them up.
Winner: Vincent Constantine Augustus (Empire Clutch) Time: 6:35 (**) "LAWBRINGER" THOMAS ADLER vs. HANGMAN You think the crowd was pumped for this one?!? Since arriving in the Carolinas in January, Thomas Adler has had the Wild Bunch in general and the Hangman in particular dead in his sights. And now, ten long months later, he got his one on one match-up with the Hangman.
No one's quite sure just why he's targeted Hangman so, but Adler looked to take out the entire ten months worth of frustration in the opening minute. After knocking the 330 pounder to the mat with every offensive move, Adler would drop to the mat and pummel him with blows to the head – a man possessed.
Adler beat the Hangman down into the corner, and then grabbed him from behind with a chinlock – a rarity in this match. Adler spun, chinlock intact, and threw him by the neck over the top rope. Not wasting a second, he followed up with an impressive slingshot splash to the floor.
Slick Wilson was giving both men a wide berth, giving them a chance to settle this decisively. As he watched, Adler pulled himself off the floor and proceeded to drive Hangman through Bobby Lee Wilcox'
ring announcer's table with frightening precision. Once back in the ring, Adler dropped Hangman with a stiff clothesline – and that's when it all went down. At long last, the mysterious large robed figure whom we last saw at the Adler –
Beethoven title match in Charlotte (think back. . . .THAT'S the one) appeared in the aisle. And he's a big dude. Referee Slick Wilson stared in the aisle at him, unsure of his intentions.
As Wilson watched – Adler reached into his tights for a pair of brass knuckles – and laid the Hangman out cold with them. He made the cover, Wilson turned around – and counted to three.
Winner: Thomas Adler (Brass knuckles) Time: 4:02 (**) Hangman was out cold – and Adler went for the mask. The crowd roared its' approval, as it was high time to finish this once and for all.
With surprisingly little trouble, Adler pulled off the mask, looking at a face a bit TOO familiar to be coincidence.
A confused crowd looked on, as Adler grabbed the house mike: "You see this? This is my boy. Met his mother on the road, when I was young, horny, and stupid. One idiotic fling, a night of wasted energy, and she spent all those years living with the result. It took her more than twenty years to finally get the nerve to tell me.
That was one year ago. "When I came to RAW Carolina, it was to find him. Fine example, isn't he? When I saw what he had become, I swore I'd teach him the respect his mother forgot to put in his head. I... I..."
Suddenly, Adler's mood shifted – uncertain, confused, a bit dizzy. Then – just as suddenly – it shifted again. He wasn't bothered in the least. Calm. Rational.
"Pity the poor fool did not realize how much the son was like the father." Suddenly (again?!?), three more robed figures joined the one already in the aisle. Two were smaller, one in particular, while the third was even larger than the first. The smallest man stayed at ringside while his three companions climbed into the ring with Adler. At this point, no one in Chapel Hill knew WHAT the hell was going on.
Adler was surrounded by the robed men as he ripped off his jeans – tearaways, revealing a pair of dark pants, and very high boots.
Reaching down, he turned the tops of his boots towards the floor, leaving a six inch decorative fringe. Each of his three ringmates reached into their robes, and handed him an object.
The smallest of the three handed him something small, which he took before reaching up towards his face – first one eye, then the other.
The second man handed him a vest, which he slipped on. The third – and largest – handed him a hood, which he slipped over his head.
The crowd buzzed – and as he did so, his three companions dropped their robes. Sinder Blokk. Hellspawn – Abyss and Hades. Out on the floor, the smallest man of the four also drops his robe – Damien
Scratch. And Thomas Adler – was now the Hangman. The original. The crowd sat in stunned silence, until Led Zeppelin's highly appropriate "Gallow's Pole" echoed through the arena. Adl – Hangman – picked up the mike:
"Thomas Adler is no one, nothing. He has never been anything, save a useful, unwitting servant. He had no notion that my coming was the true reason for his meeting with his cretinous child. A year of mortal time, I have bided, lurking, watching, and waited for the moment when I would strike down this interloper.
"Now, I have done so. The punishment has been meted out, and the one, and only Hangman has returned to claim his throne once more. I am not alone, however. Those who have stood with me in the past have returned as well, and now, Judgement Day will rule this place with a mailed fist. Welcome to a new reign of terror, oh ignorant masses."
Oh, my. ROADKILL RICK Š vs. "FAST" EDDIE CUTLASS, Piedmont Heavyweight Title in a Steel Cage / Ladder match The crowd sat in stunned silence – what in the hell was going on with Thomas Adler?!? He was the original Hangman, back in the original RAW?!? But their silence didn't last long once the ring crew headed out to put up the STEEL CAGE.
After the Cutlass – Sidious ladder match at the Charlotte Shootout in September, a contest that is garnering serious write-in consideration as Match of the Year in the ongoing Wrestling Digest RAW Carolina
Awards, fans were drooling at the prospects of a similar match involving Cutlass and his arch-enemy Roadkill Rick – only this time, the ladder – and the ring – would be surrounded by a steel cage.
An interesting dichotomy emerged in the early going of the match; Roadkill Rick was treating it as a standard cage match, using the unforgiving steel as a weapon, ramming, raking, mashing, doing everything he could to gain an advantage in true Roadkill style.
Cutlass, in contrast, chose to forego the cage completely, complementing his basic fast-paced arsenal with a few quick splashes and short elbow drops off the middle of the ladder.
At the five minute mark, Cutlass missed a legdrop onto RKR from the ladder in the first big spot of the match. Grabbing Cutlass by the hair, RKR rammed him headlong into the cage wall – and then inspiration struck. Folding the ladder over Cutlass, in effect sandwiching him with it, Roadkill rammed him into the cage again, ladder first, following with a dropkick for good measure.
The eight minute mark saw Cutlass drop RKR with a crisp Northern Lights Suplex, and then position the ladder to allow himself to climb to the top of the cage, standing on the middle of the crossbar – to launch himself onto RKR with a beautiful senton, drawing the evening's first `Holy Excrement' chant. Dazed himself, Cutlass repositioned the ladder underneath the dangling Piedmont Title and began the long, arduous climb, reaching the top rung – only to see
RKR kick the bottome leg of the ladder out from underneath him, sending him spilling into the ropes.
After several more close calls, Roadkill Rick bounced Cutlass' head off the mat with a piledriver (old school!!) at the eleven minute mark, and went for the title belt. Two rungs up, he changed his mind, and instead headed for the corner. He climbed onto the top rope – and changed his mind again, this time headed for the top of the cage. As the crowd buzzed, RKR turned around, intent on maiming his rival, and arched into a beautiful moonsault – which missed
Cutlass completely as he rolled out of the way. The fifteen minute mark found both men on top of the ladder on either side in the corner, and after a remarkably UN-contrived series of twisty and turny spots, Roadkill managed to bend Cutlass over the top of the ladder with the Boston Crab half of his deadly Texas
Speedbump. But somehow, using the ladder for leverage, Cutlass managed to flip RKR up and over the top of the ladder, sending him headfirst and upside down into the wall of the cage, while Nice Guy
Eddie slid down the ladder to the mat below. Back up they went at the seventeen minute mark, as both men stood on opposite sides of the ladder, trading blows. RKR staggered Cutlass with a left, nearly causing him to lose his balance and let go of the ladder. As Cutlass struggled to hold on, Roadkill fidgeted with his mask, and the crowd groaned/gasped/said DAMMIT – he was setting up the Black Mist. Cutlass saw this, too – and jammed his fingers through the mouth hole in RKR's mask(!!). Cutlass screamed in pain as the Texan bit down on his fingers. As the crowd watched in amazement, Cutlass used his last bit of strength to leap over the top of the ladder, and catching RKR on the way down – deliver an unbelievable Guarantee(!!!).
Roadkill Rick bounced face first off the mat – completely unconscious. Clutching his bleeding fingers to his chest, Cutlass climbed slowly back up the ladder, all the way to the top – and grabbed the Piedmont Title.
The long wait is finally over – "Fast" Eddie Cutlass is a champion in RAW Carolina. Winner (and new Piedmont Champion): Eddie Cutlass Time: 18:34 (**** ˝) MARK ADAMS & "DREADMIND" DAVE PITRELLI Š vs. "ERADICATOR" DIRK ROBERTSON & JAYSON RAYGE, Carolina Tag-Team Titles This one's heated up fast and furious. The war between Your Worst Nightmare and the Blood Brothers shows no sign of letting up, even after that bloodbath two nights ago on RAW-C TV. And after winning the RAW World Tag-Team Titles from Tommy Bedford & Knox Roberts the night before, Adams and Pitrelli were flying high.
Adams started the match working over the right knee of Robertson, looking to give the Eradicator one more malady to hinder his ring development. Starting with a dirty chop block and following up with several drops of his own knee, Adams seemed well on his way to accomplishing that goal.
Robertson managed to tag in young Jayson Rayge, and that's when the tide turned. Still unmistakably green, Rayge was on fire tonight, hitting the champs with anything and everything in his arsenal, even managing to drop Adams with his innovative finisher, a reverse DVD he calls the Infinite Rayge Driver. Only a heavy boot from Pitrelli saved the title change on that one.
Pitrelli was as impressive as ever, working over both of his opponents with his imposing 330 pound frame, nearly breaking the still not 100% Robertson in half with a pair of powerbombs at the six minute mark.
Eight minutes in, Adams dumped Robertson out to the floor and moved quickly to jaw with Jackie Boone, Jr. while Pitrelli loaded up his steel chair. Rayge came over to save his partner, but caught a chair to the face for his troubles. But just as Pitrelli wound up to brain
Robertson, Stone appeared out of nowhere to a huge pop, leaping over the safety railing out of the crowd to snatch the chair away.
At the sight of Stone, Adams flew even more off the handle. Rolling out of the ring, he knocked Stone down with a clothesline. He dug into his tights as Stone got to his feet, and came up with a cloud of
(DREADED) MYSTERIOUS WHITE POWDER right in Stone's face!! Blinded, Stone walked right into a Big Easy suplex from Adams onto the floor. Robertson, meanwhile, rolled back into the ring – just in time to beat the ten count, and earn his team a hollow countout victory.
Winners: Dirk Robertson & Jayson Rayge (Countout) Time: 8:27 (** ž) Robertson then quickly rolled back out to the floor, where he and a recovered Rayge helped drag a stunned Stone to safety, as the tag champs climbed back into the ring to jaw at Boone for counting them out.
Adams and Pitrelli stood in the ring, being pelted with trash from the irate fans, jawing at Stone and the Blood Brothers out in the aisle. Suddenly, an eerie sound filled the arena, children singing. . . .
"Ring around the roses, Pocket full of posies. Hush up, Hush up, You all....fall....down!" An explosion rocked the Mini-Tron, and when the smoke cleared, a pair of shadowy figures stood in the aisle, backlight by a bright spotlight so that they appeared as mere silhouettes. One of them spoke:
"Fright night....Hallowe'en tricks and demonic treats. Adams, Pitrelli .... you seek to unify all the tag titles in the Raw Empire. But you have neglected one. The federation may have closed, but its champions are far from retired.
Undefeated, we stand victorious over all who get in our way. Trick? Or treat? Neither. Truth! You deceive yourselves if you believe yourselves to be the best of the best, without facing the unstoppable force! The time for your redemption is at hand! Sin is in.....and so, we begin!"
A second explosion shook Franklin Street, and when the smoke cleared, the men were gone. CASANOVA BEETHOVEN Š vs. JACK ANDERSON, Carolina Heavyweight Title Completely in the spirit with Halloween, Jack Anderson's manager Felicity Vass, the lovely Felicity Vass, complemented her nice micro- mini business skirt with a pair of little cat ears, which brought more than a pair of little cat calls from the increasingly inebriated clientele. Casanova Beethoven made as grand an entrance as ever, complete with full complement of hos – which he completely ignored, taken as he was with the lovely Felicity (Did I mention her loveliness?!?)
Anderson used his quicks early on in an attempt to frustrate the now two-time (two-time) Carolina champion, ducking and weaving, sticking and moving, before tapping Beethoven with a dropkick to the chin that sent him spilling out to the floor.
Once on the floor, Beethoven got a good look at Felicity – and decided he wanted an even closer one. Moving in on the lovely (did I mention that yet?!?) lass menacingly, Beethoven was cut off by a flying Anderson – who intercepted him with a slingshot plancha out to the floor, which got the crowd on its' feet.
The Cameron Crazies who braved the cool night and the ten mile trip down Highway 15-501 serenaded Jack with an old RAW UK favorite chant of `You Can't Suntan', which he good-naturedly acknowledged by mooning them with his bare white (and I do mean WHITE) ass. Probably not a good move by the Crimson Spitfire – when he dropped his head for the moon, Beethoven dropped HIM with a DDT.
Beethoven then started working over Anderson with some of his powerful suplexes, only to see Jack slip out the top of a vertical suplex, and drop Beethoven with a clothesline. Rolling to a neutral corner, Anderson waited, and when an increasingly irate Beethoven charged, he dropped him with a reverse bulldog for a near fall.
Having fun now, Anderson started the old crisscross, and after a pair of leapfrogs, dropped Beethoven on his face with a drop toe hold.
Beethoven rolled back up to his feet, and made as if to hit the ropes. Taking his cue, Anderson dropped down to the mat – only to see Beethoven reverse field and drop a heavy leg across the back of his head, and follow up with a quick roll-up and a healthy handful of tights for a near fall of his own.
From time to time, Anderson glanced down the aisle, perhaps awaiting the promised arrival of The Guy Dressed in All Black – we know him as
TGDAB, for short. But Beethoven didn't seem to care one whit about that – like a lot of us, he seemed certain that as long as Anderson was in the ring, then he had nothing to fear from TGDAB.
At the fourteen minute mark, a buzz in the back of the crowd got Anderson's attention. He took his eyes off his opponent for a second – long enough for Beethoven to plant him with a lightning quick German Suplex. He barely got a shoulder up, and one last glance into the crowd revealed some of the Cameron Crazies in a brawl with a few of the local Carolina faithful.
But all things considered, Anderson looked to be in good shape to win the singles gold that has eluded him in his various stops across the
RAW Empire. At the nineteen minute mark, he dropped Beethoven with a kick to the head, and one Irish whip later, planted him with a bulldog.
The crowd buzzed, as they knew what was coming next. Anderson pulled Beethoven up, and dropped him with a German Suplex as the buzz got louder. He pulled the champ up again and sent him into the corner with another Irish Whip – but Beethoven had other ideas.
Sticking out an arm as he rushed into the corner, he caught referee `Cottonmouth' Joe Lynch with a clothesline, sending the sturdy official sprawling. Anderson followed him in, dropped him on the top turnbuckle – and hit him with the Fireball Special (also known as a top rope hurricanrana).
But Lynch was still down – and couldn't make the count. Anderson went over, shaking him back to his feet – and Lynch called for the bell. Beethoven had been disqualified for his blatant abuse of an official.
Jack Anderson was the better man on this Halloween night – but Casanova Beethoven was still the champion. Winner: Jack Anderson (Disqualification) Time: 20:11 (*** ˝) --- Š RAW Carolina, MMI. http://www.yahoogroups.com/group/RAWCarolina
