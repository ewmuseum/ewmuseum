Grand Strand Slam 06.12.02

15 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Wednesday night saw Myrtle Beach, South Carolina once again play host for RAW Carolina's annual Causeway Spectacular, The Grand Strand
Slam!! Ten great matches were scheduled for the fans who filed into Kimbel Gymnasium on the campus of Coastal Carolina University. Results: "THE GOLDEN DRAGON" CHANG TAKATA vs. "COLDKNOCK" BROCK NEWTON Interesting sign in the front row for this one: WHAT THE HELL IS A COLDKNOCK?!? Burning questions aside, this match pitted the two newest members of the ever-swelling RAW Carolina talent roster. But other than their brief stints in the Carolinas, the two men have little in common; where Takata is an accomplished international veteran, most recently of Rocky Mountain RAW, Newton is a promising rookie from the mean streets of Atlanta.
And his inexperience showed in the early going, as Takata took him to task with his savvy puro blend of martial arts kicks and stiff suplexes, scoring several two counts. But the 21 year old Newton was nothing if not resilient, eager to prove himself in the biggest match of his brief career, and began firing back with some nice shootfighting-style body shots, and mixed in a Tazzmission type chokehold that nearly saw Takata tap out before his manager Barry
Hart reached in behind referee Hank Jackson's back and rake Newton's eyes to break the hold.
A stiff Doctorbomb at the six minute mark swung the tide back in Takata's favor, but the follow-up diving headbutt tasted nothing but canvas. Sensing an imminent victory, Newton rolled to the outside and returned with a steel chair – which he promptly tossed to the rising Takata, who caught it just in front of his face as Newton superkicked the steel, snapping it back into Takata's face in a move the rookie calls the Koldnok Kick (indeed listed on his official bio as his trademark maneuver). Takata went down hard, and rookie official Hank Jackson called for the bell, disqualifying Newton for the illegal use of the chair.
Winner: Chang Takata (Disqualification) Time: 6:33 (**) Hank Jackson looked to roll out of the ring as the bell rang, but couldn't escape the wrath of an enraged Newton, who dropped the official with a stiff shot from that same steel chair, a move that will certainly put a sizable dent in the youngster's pocket book.
OTHELLO WASHINGTON vs. "THE OUTLAW" MORRIS BASS (Mama played fiddle, Chris Gale sang bass. . .and wrote this match.) The crowd buzzed with anticipation as the next match between Othello Washington and "The Outlaw" Morris Bass approached. The crowd was firmly behind Washington, although they knew the odds of him winning were slim. Just before referee Slick Wilson started the match however, the crowd began to get excited as Terry Jacobs, dressed in street clothes, came to ringside carrying a steel chair. Wilson thought that Jacobs was attempting retaliation on Bass because of the chair shot he gave to his tag team partner Kevin Simmons during their match at Battle at the Beach. Surprisingly, Jacobs just unfolded the chair at ringside and sat down, as though he was scouting Bass as
Wilson signaled for the match to begin. The Outlaw immediately went after Washington, attempting a clothesline but Washington ducked and hit Bass with a low blow, hopefully cutting him down to size with the ref's head turned.
Washington began to deliver some stiff body shots to The Outlaw, then he impressed the crowd by irish whipping Bass into the ropes and tossed him over his head with a monkey toss. But what the rookie didn't know was Bass landed on his feet and brutalized Washington with a Fisherman's Neckbreaker pin attempt that only brought a 2 count. Then Bass picked him up and slung him across the ring with a powerful Belly to Back suplex, all the while he was laughing and calling him 'Simmons' as if he was punishing "The Predator" in the ring. Bass continued the beating as he delivered his reverse Death
Valley Driver which he calls the "Outlaw Driver". Bass then threw Washington in the corner and began choking him with his big boot that almost earned him yet another disqualification.
It seemed that the only thing Bass was concerned about was sending a message to Simmons. Bass picked Washington up while he continued to laugh and started slapping the young rookie around until Othello began to fight back with lefts and rights, startling the veteran.
Othello then hit consecutive swinging neckbreakers on Bass, but he stopped the third attempt by nailing Washington with a vicious big boot.
The Outlaw decided then to put the rookie away by signaling for the Widow Maker. This brought Terry Jacobs up on the ring apron, carrying the steel chair. Jacobs and Bass began exchanging heated words as the crowd chanted for Terry to hit him. But instead, all
Terry did was toss the chair to Bass and began to walk back to the locker room. Bass then proceeded to step forward to pound the rookie on the mat but he was nowhere to be found! Suddenly he looked up and saw Washington flying though the air with his finisher, the Legacy missle dropkick, as it connected with the chair sending the giant to the mat. Slick Wilson immediately counted the 1-2-3 for the upset win!
Winner: Othello Washington (The Legacy) Time: 6:25 (**) After the match, Washington was celebrating the victory as Terry Jacobs walked back up the ramp getting high fives from the crowd. The celebration was short lived however as Bass cracked the chair across the skull of the rookie, knocking him out. Bass then grabbed the mic:
"Hey Jacobs! You think that was a pretty funny move huh? Let me guess...Simmons put you up to it, didn't he? Well guess what? now his blood is on YOUR hands!"
The Outlaw then set Washington on the top turnbuckle pounding his body some more. Then standing on the platform, Jacobs and the crowd looked in horror as Bass delivered a Hangman's Chokeslam on
Washington from the top turnbuckle down onto the steel chair in the middle of the ring! The crowd moaned as Washington's head cracked off the cold steel. Bass then stood over Washington's motionless body and laughed as officials eventually carried Washington out of the ring.
BRUISER FLINN vs. RYAN DELANEY The night's third contest pitted a youngster out to make a name for himself, Ryan Delaney, against a cagey veteran who some feel has re- energized his career here in the Carolinas, Blood Brother mentor
Bruiser Flinn. It's already been announced that Delaney will be part of the four-way melee for the Coastal Title at Carolina Thunder, so there were concerns amongst the crowd that he could be overlooking the veteran brawler Flinn.
Ill-founded, those concerns. Delaney was on top of his game in Myrtle Beach, as he has been since arriving in the Carolinas a short time ago. Showcasing his unique mix of high-flying and battlearts, he had the veteran Flinn on the ropes in the early going of the match, scoring several two counts.
But a man with the experience of Bruiser Flinn wasn't about to go down without a tough fight, and soon turned the tables after powerslamming Delaney right out of an attempted senton in an awesome spot. Bruiser soon took the action out to the floor, where Delaney regained control with a lowblow before crotching Flinn on the ringpost with an atomic drop.
The action raged back and forth for the better part of seven minutes, when Flinn looked for a reverse Russian legsweep – but Delaney blocked it, and somehow twisted it into a full nelson on Flinn, and carried that up and over into a dragon suplex. Flinn crashed hard into the mat, and Delaney followed quickly with a leap to the top rope and another quick trip right back down with a corkscrew moonsault – Chasing the Dragon all the way to the count of three.
Winner: Ryan Delaney (Chase the Dragon) Time: 7:04 (***) THE MISTRESS vs. SIDIOUS THE CLOWN The next match was eagerly anticipated by the `sports entertainment' cluster of the RAW-C audience, if not the armbar marks like your humble narrator. The Mistress hit the ring full of confidence after her win last week over Sidious, equally confident that she could do it again this week. She was accompanied to the ring this week by
Sidious' former familiar, The Dummy (dressed in a Mini-Mistress outfit), and they made their way to ring to the strains of Meredith
Brooks' "Bitch". Sidious also hit the ring confident – that win or lose, HE was gonna have a good time tonight.
The two combatants stood toe to toe in the center of the ring as the match began, staring each other down – until Sidious backhanded the taste right out of The Mistress' mouth, drawing a big OOOOOOH from the partisan crowd. But The Mistress responded with a paintbrush of her own, and then a pinpoint dropkick to the nose that sent Sid crashing over the top rope to the relative safety of the ringside area.
The Mistress dominated much the next portion of the match, getting the crowd on its' feet and keeping Sidious off his with some high- flying moves mixed with the occasional well-timed lowblow, which he undoubtedly deserved. Sid riled the crowd again at the three minute mark when he whipped The Mistress headfirst into the turnbuckles, and then followed in and played the bongos on her. . .uh, posterior.
The Mistress came out rightly incensed, and let loose with a series of hard right hands that staggered The Clown, but a wild haymaker was ducked under by Sid, who came up with a full nelson – and then further disgusted the crowd by thrusting his hips into his `bongo drums', screaming FATHER NELSON, FATHER NELSON (who knew he used to be an altar boy?!? I KID, I KID.).
Mistress countered this one with a savage mule kick that doubled Sid over in agony, and she followed up with a big bulldog that got a two count. She then headed to the top rope and came crashing back down with a flying legdrop for another two count, and the crowd rallied behind her. A second trip to the top proved less successful –
Sidious rolled out of the way of her moonsault attempt, and she crashed hard into the mat. Sidious locked The Mistress in a dragon sleeper, going for the kill – or so we thought until he began shaking her, first to the left, then to the right, staring as her, uh, undulations. Hank Jackson stepped in to stop this tasteless display just as Sid swayed her just a bit harder – sending her crashing into the ref, taking him down.
Evidently pleased with himself for taking out a defenseless official, Sidious taunted the crowd – leaving himself wide open for a lowblow from The Mistress, which drew a BIG pop from the crowd. Feeling the end was near, she hoisted him up, looking for a Dominatrix – but he countered by raking her eyes, and followed up with a Pie In the Eye crucifix. Mistress was pinned flat on the ring – but still no official.
This brought The Dummy into the ring, holding a steel chair, looking for Sidious. Sid released the crucifix, and as The Dummy swung the chair at him, he pulled The Mistress by her hair into the path of the chairshot, which caught her flush between the eyes, rendering her unconscious.
By now, Jackson had started to recover, and Sidious went for the cover. The Dummy stood in the ring. . .well, dumbfounded, apologizing profusely to the unconscious Mistress as Jackson counted the 1-2-3.
Winner: Sidious the Clown (Dummy Luck) Time: 6:02 (**) As the bell rang, Sidious leapt to his feet in victorious exultation, grabbing The Dummy by the wrist to take him back to the locker room.
But The Dummy would have none of it, and wriggled free. Sidious began bullying the helpless imbecile, poking his finger in his chest, backing him into the corner. Meanwhile, The Mistress regained her senses, and got to her feet, steel chair in hand. She circled in behind Sid, looking for payback – but he ducked at the least second, leaving The Dummy to take the blow(!!). Sidious rolled out of the ring, laughing all the way back to the locker room.
"THE CURSE" ESTEBAN CORTEZ © vs. THE OMEGA KID, Coastal Television Title The fans in attendance had wondered all night whether or not Esteban Cortez would be able to make his scheduled title defense against the high-flying Omega Kid; after all, The Curse had been locked up earlier in the week for several assault charges in the greater
Rochester area. But those wonders were all put to rest with the arrival of `Showboat' Eddie Cannon, who informed the fans that he had spoken with the judge and convinced him to allow Cortez to earn `a decent living' on a work release program, and the match would go down as scheduled.
As per the work release stipulations, Cortez was accompanied to the ring by four guards, mirrored sunglasses and all. What we've got here is. . . oh, never mind. Strother Martin aside, it appeared from the early going that the time served had not served Cortez well.
Omega Kid was fresher, faster, and generally better than the champion from the opening bell, and it seemed only a matter of time before he finally captured the title.
After being manhandled in the ring, Cortez rolled out to the floor to gather his thoughts – and an attractive woman in her mid-40s leaned over the railing and slapped the taste out of his mouth, while Omega stood in the ring shouting "THAT'S my Mom!!!"
As Cortez finally located the Kid's family the hard way, the crowd buzzed as Ryan Delaney made his way down the ring – carrying a canoe paddle(!!) – wrapped in razor wire(!!!). His other hand carried a cold, hard steel chair, and it looked as though he meant business.
His face looked a little puffy from the hard battle with Bruiser Flinn (so THAT's how he got the name. . .) earlier in the night, but Delaney was focused – on sitting down in that same cold, hard steel chair, which he unfolded at ringside to watch the goings-ons.
Back in the ring, Omega continued to make short work of Cortez, taking him down with an awesome springboard plancha for a two count, and then a sky twister press that saw Cortez barely able to get a foot across the bottom rope to break the count. The Kid could TASTE the title, and motioned to his legion of fans that he was going for the Omegabreaker – and that's when the prison guards hit the ring, and began beating down Omega(!!). This obvious Cortez setup led `Cottonmouth' Joe Lynch to call for the bell, disqualifying the champion as he joined in the beating to make it five on one.
Winner: Omega Kid (Disqualification) Time: 4:02 (*) Suddenly, the crowd exploded as White Wolf raced down to ringside, `Magic Hammer' in hand, swinging it wildly in an attempt to clear the ring.
It worked. White Wolf grabbed his little buddy, and the two of them made their way up the aisle, where Omega Kid had gathered his wits long enough to call for a mike, DEMANDING that the match be restarted until there was a clear winner!!
The crowd roared at this suggestion; they wanted to see more, too. But not Cortez. Simply shaking his head no, he rolled out of the ring, grabbing his title belt off the timekeeper's table, and headed for the showers.
But not before he bumped into Ryan Delaney. Yeah, he was sitting at ringside through all of this, just watching, waiting. And now he made his move. Raising the paddle, he whacked one of Cortez' guards across the head with it, and the blood flowed immediately. Another guard charged, and tasted the razor wire. And another. And the fourth.
Finally, with no one at ringside but himself and Cortez, Delaney faked a shot with the paddle. Cortez ducked, but Delaney dropped the paddle and kicked him right in the teeth(!!). Cortez staggered backwards, and Delaney moved cat-quick behind him into a full-nelson
(that hold again!) and dragon-suplexed him on the FLOOR(!!). Finally (and I mean FINALLY), a horde of security rushed out to contain the Pittsburgh native. CHRISTIAN DEMONSTONE © vs. THE EQUALIZER, Piedmont Heavyweight Title ([name withheld] does title matches!) In a match that saw the past and future of Canadian wrestling collide, newly crowned Piedmont Heavyweight Champion CHRISTIAN
DEMONSTONE put his title on the line against former four-time SWF World Champion THE EQUALIZER. From the bell, the 6'7" Demonstone took the fight to his smaller, much more experienced opponent, hoping to knock the Hall-of-Famer down a few pegs and set the pace for the match, but The Equalizer proved more than capable of handling himself despite the champion's nine inch size advantage.
Capitalizing on his years of experience wrestling bigger, stronger opponents, The Equalizer utilized a strategy designed to chop
Demonstone down to size, eventually taking the big man off his feet with a flying clothesline from the top rope that left the champion wide open for the cover and a surprising two-and-a-half count.
Thinking the title was ripe for the taking, The Equalizer called for his patented "Face Plant" finisher and pulled Demonstone to his feet, only to have the champion counter the hold at the last possible second with a bearhug slam that brought the near-capacity crowd to its feet.
Sensing the momentum had turned, Demonstone took control of the match and whipped the former SWF Champion into the turnbuckle, following him into the corner with a devastating clothesline before executing a stunning release German suplex that launched The Equalizer clear across the ring!! Pulling the legendary Canadian to his feet, the
Piedmont Champion set his challenger up for the "Canuck Driver" and, three seconds later, it was over -- Christian Demonstone had successfully defended his Piedmont Title against The Equalizer.
Winner: Christian Demonstone (Canuck Driver) Time: 11:10 (***) During the break in the action, the Mini-Tron flashed to life to show Ric Beauty's valet Vicki is walking around backstage looking for Van Wild, obviously upset about the way he was looking at Lola the Waitress. She flings open a door and finds The Omega Kid – still bloody from his earlier battle with Cortez, apologizes, and shuts the door.
She flings open the next door and finds Sidious The Clown, still in the greasepaint (does it EVER come off?!?). She closes the door before he has a chance to invite her in.
The third door's the charm – one-half of the RAW Tag-Team Champions, Van Wild. VICKI: I am so glad I found you. I mean it has been a while since I heard from you. And then I saw you with that waitress and. . .
VAN WILD: Oh, er, hey Vicki, how are you? Sorry I haven't been in touch, you know how busy I've been now that we're the RAW Tag champs. I was meaning to call you.
VICKI: But what about the waitress?? VAN WILD: Who? Ah, Lola...she's just a friend, Vicki. I got her some tickets to a show a while back, that's all. Hey listen, how'd you like to come to the ring with me and Scotty for our title match tonight?
VICKI: Really? VAN WILD: Yeah sure. I gotta go get ready, then I'll see you later, okay? VICKI: Okay. TERRY JACOBS & "PREDATOR" KEVIN SIMMONS © vs. THE BLOOD BROTHERS vs. PURGATORY, Carolina Tag-Team Titles ([name withheld] does BACK TO BACK title matches!!) Following their time limit draw at Battle On The Beach, RAW-Carolina promoter Eddie Cannon rewarded both PURGATORY and THE BLOOD BROTHERS with a shot at the reigning Carolina Tag Team Champions, TERRY JACOBS and "THE PREDATOR" KEVIN SIMMONS -- a decision which set the stage for a Triple Threat match at Grand Strand Slam that would see all three teams fighting tooth and nail to bring home the gold.
From the bell, Simmons and Jacobs utilized a strategy of quick tags and double-team moves designed to work each opponent's particular weakness, a plan of attack that allowed the defending champions to quickly take control of the match. The tide soon turned, however, as
Purgatory and The Blood Brothers unconsciously began working together, keeping the champions separated and softening them up for the final blow.
Eventually, that unintended teamwork between the two teams began to backfire, and, when Marc Tower attempted a cover on Terry Jacobs only to have the pinfall broken by the fast-thinking Dementia, the match quickly erupted into a three-way brawl with Dementia and Tower fighting up against the ropes, Synn and Jacobs brawling on the floor, and Simmons and Robertson slugging it out in the middle of the ring.
As "The Predator" narrowly escaped a "Total Eradication" powerbomb and locked Robertson in his patented "Durham Deathlock," the Mini-
Tron suddenly lit up with a shot of a pissed off "Outlaw" Morris Bass. BASS: "Simmons!! Hey, Simmons!! You think that sending your little buddy down to cost me my match was really smart, don't you? Well, you were wrong...and now someone has to pay for your mistake!!"
The camera zoomed out to show Bass holding Simmons' wife, Lindsay, by her hair. BASS: "And, since it looks to me like you're not man enough to take your own punishment, I figured I'd find someone close to your heart to take it for you!!"
The Outlaw then chokeslammed Lindsay through a table and, as Simmons broke the hold and left the ring to race up toward the dressing room area, Bass began laughing psychotically as the Mini-tron went black.
Now alone against four opponents, Jacobs fought off his challengers to the best of his ability but soon found himself falling victim to a "Chicken Wing Crash" double-arm tornado DDT from Dirk Robertson for the one...two...three.
Winners (and new champions): The Blood Brothers (Chicken Wing Crash) Time: 13:09 (** ½) WHITE WOLF vs. RIC BEAUTY Viking Warrior White Wolf has been as impressive as any newcomer in some time here in the Carolinas, and most experts agree that he is just one big win away from superstardom. And if his opponent at the
Slam, Ric Beauty, couldn't solve some of his serious mental issues but quick, he just might find himself on the wrong end of Wolf's big win.
Early on, it appeared that Beauty's battle with The Beast within was under control, as he took the fight right to The Great Dane with a nice sequence of chain wrestling, suplexes and the like which the beach crowd really seemed to enjoy. But the match turned when Beauty looked to the outside for support – and Vicki wasn't there, only Val – which immediately turned him into the snarling Beast.
The Beast went after Wolf, trying to brawl with him – not a good idea when facing the 270 pound Scandinavian Sensation (man, sometimes these are TOO easy). Powerful, clubbing blows from the meaty hands of The Wolf put Beauty down on the mat – leaving him easy pickings for an iron claw(!!).
But there's force – however mighty – that can't be stopped with a well-placed thumb to the eye, and that's just what Beauty dug out of his bag of tricks at the six minute mark to stumble the Wolf, and followed up quickly with a nice running kneelift that put the big man on his back. Beauty then shocked everyone by drilling Wolf with a piledriver, and the momentum seemed to have shifted firmly to The
Bipolar Brawler (man, I'm on a ROLL). Beauty dropped Wolf with a textbook side suplex, and it looked like he was going to go with his finisher. But he couldn't decide which one, as the two warring factions within his own medulla oblongata
(eat your heart out, Slim Goodbody) did battle once more. The momentary indecision cost him – dearly. He turned around, apparently deciding upon the Beauty Breaker, but Wolf was up on his feet, and drove him THROUGH the mat with an Unfettered Might! super spear. The pinfall followed shortly thereafter, three seconds thereafter to be precise. White Wolf had his big win, and Ric Beauty had a lot of explaining to do to The Beast.
Winner: White Wolf (Unfettered Might) Time: 7:54 (***) "FAST" EDDIE CUTLASS © vs. JUSTIN HARPER, Carolina Heavyweight Title We didn't even have to wait for the bell to start this one – Justin Harper was ready for another shot at singles gold in the Carolinas, attacking the Carolina champion and all-around good guy Eddie Cutlass from behind as he handed his title belt to Bobby Lee Wilcox in the corner. Harper clubbed Nice Guy Eddie down to the mat with punishing forearms, and then went to work with some stomps on the back of
Cutlass' head and neck, and we were off and running. Harper was in firm control in the early portions of the bout. Knowing full well Cutlass' long history of concussions, Harper utilized high-impact moves throughout the match – DDTs, piledrivers, and the like, including a super-stiff Shining Wizard at the 13 minute mark that notched a two and 999999/1000000. It was that close.
Honest. But I get ahead of myself. Harper has been spending his free days of late training at the world- renowned Keening School for Grappling Arts, looking to fill the holes in his ever-improving game, and there's little doubt this rookie will one day be a top star. Displaying an even more vicious style than we've seen before, Harper mixed and matched his extensive martial arts training with some nice mat wrestling, stretching Cutlass six ways from Sunday, all the while focusing on the champion's upper regions, including a brutal spot about four minutes in that saw him lock Cutlass in a Rings of Saturn-type hold, all the while delivering headbutts to the base of the champ's skull.
The aforementioned Shining Wizard came at the aforementioned thirteenth minute, and turned the tide of the match. Cutlass was in firm control, and looking to polish off the kid with a Guarantee – but Harper caught him with a cheap lowblow, and Cutlass doubled over right into that deadly jumping knee strike. Harper measured the fallen champion, and hit him with a standing moonsault that was so,
SO close to a three count. This brought out Sidious the Clown, much to the crowd's chagrin. Coming down armed with his trademark steel chair with the rubber chicken tied to it, he assured the fans in the front row that he was just there to `protect' Cutlass, and indeed it seemed that was his intention as he stood quietly by the timekeeper's table and watched.
Back in the ring, Harper dropped Cutlass with his own Guarantee, but the reverse Russian legsweep only scored another two count. But the insult behind the move seemed to fire up the champion, and he soon found himself punching out of an attempted powerbomb from Harper, landing on top with a sunset flip for a near fall. Cutlass then shoved Harper out of an attempted bulldog, and then dropped the challenger with a spinning heel kick – and then a brainbuster(!!) for another two count.
Cutlass went to work, and caught Harper coming off the ropes with a nice dropkick, and followed up with a nice vertical suplex.
Suddenly, Sidious dropped his chair and vaulted in one fluid motion from the floor to the apron to the top turnbuckle, waiting, waiting – for Harper to get to his feet so he could nail him with a missile dropkick. The crowd booed heavily, more so as Sid laid the boots to
Harper as Cutlass looked on, dumbfounded. Lynch had no recourse but to toss the match out, calling for a disqualification. No surprise then Harper's livid reaction, but the
Myrtle Beach fans were equally as livid – Sidious had done his best to ruin Cutlass' word. Winner: Justin Harper (Disqualification) Time: 17:22 (*** ½) As Harper rolled out of the ring, still a bit groggy but very, VERY pissed at the one man Freakshow. . . .
[Sidious rolls out of the ring, and grabs a mic from Bobby Lee Wilcox. Eddie stands to his feet in disbelief. Sidious leaps back into the ring, and walks over to Eddie, his hand extended forward.
Eddie slaps his hand away in disgust. Sidious lowers his head as he raises his mic to his lips.]
Sid: Eddie, I was just helping you out man. I wanted to keep you on top....keep you in the game brother. Who else is a better champion then you?
[The crowd reacts with mixed results.] Sid: I just wanted to save you........FOR MYSELF!!! [Sidious nails Eddie with a low blow. As Eddie doubles over in pain, Sidious reacts with a quick knee to the forehead. Sidious tosses his mic aside, as he continues his attack on Eddie. He picks up his former
teammate and whips him into the corner. Sidious follows up with a cartwheel into an elbow. Eddie slumps into the corner. A smile comes across Sidious' face, as he raises Eddie to his feet.
Then with his shoulder, Sidious picks up Eddie and places him on the top turnbuckle. Sidious steps down from the ropes and walks to the center of the ring. Then with quick feet he charges at Eddie.
Sidious steps on the second rope beside Eddie, and delivers a kick to his jaw. Eddie rolls off the top turnbuckle and onto the cold floor below. A harsh boo spills out from the crowd over Sidious, standing proudly in the ring as medical help pours out for the fallen champion. . .]
VAN WILD & SCOTT STARRING © vs. "TERRIBLE" TOMMY BEDFORD & "HARD" KNOX ROBERTS, RAW Tag-Team Titles With the crowd still upset at the finish of the previous match, the main event began with NASN President Tim Dallas making a welcome return to the RAW-C broadcast position, joining Diamond and Rogers at ringside for the match, and letting the fans know just how proud he is of the way that the new RAW is shaping up.
And the first televised title defense for the new RAW Tag-Team Champions was certainly shaping up to be a beauty, as the upstart duo of Van Wild and rookie Scott Starring took on two men who need no introduction – to the RAW Tag Titles OR the Carolina fans – Tommy
Bedford and Knox Roberts. From the get-go, it appeared that at least ONE member of the champions had done his homework. Starring is becoming well-known in the locker room for his video preparation, and found no shortage of footage on such a renowned team as Bedford and Roberts. The rookie started the match with Bedford, and the champs soon had the ring cut in half, dazzling the crowd with quick tags and sudden double-teams that scored several two counts, and it looked like the Stone-Faced
Assassin Roberts might not even get into the ring. The highlight of the early going saw Wild catapult Bedford across the ring, right into a spinning heel kick from Starring, which staggered the Nashville toughman back towards Wild, who took him for a sunset flip that would have gotten the three if Roberts hadn't charged in to make the save.
The tide turned in favor of the challengers when Wild made the mistake of trying to slug it out with Bedford, long regarded as one of the toughest brawlers in the sport. Wild paid the price when
Bedford laid him out with one big left hand, again regarded as one of the best lefts in the business. He then tagged in Roberts, who went to work quickly and efficiently on Wild, wearing him down with some painful mat-based submission work.
The match went on for nearly twenty minutes in this fashion, with each of these four fine competitors controlling portions of the match with their own gameplan. Starring used his trademark high-flying hit and run style, Wild high-impact power stuff; Bedford remains a consummate brawler, and Roberts dominated when the match went down on the mat.
Wild found himself in the ring with Bedford, again stupidly trading punches, as the twenty minute mark neared. With Val at ringside cheering him on, Wild ducked under a wild haymaker from the fatigued
Bedford and planted him with a superkick before diving to his corner and tagging in his rookie partner.
The crowd sensed that the end was near, and Starring signaled something to Wild. Wild whipped Bedford into the ropes, and caught him coming off with a thunderous powerslam, in perfect synch with
Starring who bounded off the middle rope with a spectacular follow-up Asai moonsault. Roberts came in to break up the count, but Wild clotheslined him over the top rope, and Slick Wilson counted the 1-2-
3. Winners: Van Wild & Scott Starring (Powerslam – Asai moonsault) Time: 20:02 (*** ¾) But the action didn't stop with the bell. As Bobby Lee Wilcox announced the victors, Val wandered out to ringside and grabbed a cheering Vicki from behind, dragging her towards back towards the locker room by her hair. Van Wild spotted this, and immediately moved to help her – only to be cut off by the late-on-the-scene Ric
Beauty, the orchestrator of this set-up. Beauty went after Wild with his familiar steel chair, battering the tag champ across the back with it. Starring moved to help his partner, but he was caught from behind by Knox Roberts and laid out in the ring.
Beauty continued to pound on Wild, with Vicki screaming for him to stop, as the show went off the air.
