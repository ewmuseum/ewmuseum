Criminal Intent 06.06.01

6 June 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
["Lawbringer" Thomas Adler leans against the wall, next to the locker room door. This is the arena. It's Criminal Intent. It's almost showtime. For once, Adler is right out in the open. His grey duster is open at the front, revealing his wrestling gear. He's ready.]
Time is short, ladies and gentlemen, so I won't waste time getting elaborate. Criminal Intent begins in a few minutes. Time to go to work.
Before I do, let me give a little advice to someone called the Judge. Obviously, you've got a pretty warped notion of justice. You certainly have no idea of how to bring law and order; all I've seen from you is chaos. This isn't a pretty business, but there are limits. RAW Carolina already has law. I brought it here. If you plan to stay, you'd better figure out exactly where you stand. Stay on the path you're walking now, and the law will come to find you first.
I'll also say to this Jesse Muldoon character that he can feel free to send me a contract. Your stomping ground? You're nobody in
Carolina, boy. If you couldn't hack it in the "big time" organization, you have my sympathy, but you're quickly losing it. Nobody likes a smartass, so unless you want the kind of attention you don't need, I politely suggest you take your self-serving crap elsewhere. Consider this being let off easy. I won't be so kind in the ring.
Enough of the new. Back to the old. This is it, Brody. You asked for it, you've got it. One more chance to take revenge on me, for giving you the beating you earned. A last opportunity to protect your kid and his friend, to act the part of father. This time, your career really is on the line. This time, I finish it.
You had your chance to be bad, Brody. I took up your challenge, met you head on, and beat you at what you thought was your own game. You weren't satisfied. You tried to goad me into lowering my guard, but
I was too smart to fall for it. You got angry. You made the mistake of dragging your little boy into this, and he paid for it. He may pay for it still. You just got angrier. So, President Cannon has arranged this little get together, and we're still not sure if he's in on this with you. Guess what, Brody? It doesn't matter.
When Cannon put ink to that contract, he signed your pink slip. That piece of paper spells the end of your years of screwing people. No more skulking around, blindsiding people with beer bottles. It all ends tonight. If Cannon decides he's your buddy, that's fine. If he decides to call the match the way he should, that's even better. It doesn't change anything, either way. I'm walking into Criminal
Intent one of RAW Carolina's elite. You're walking out a bad memory, maybe a lingering aftertaste in the mouth of the fans, but it adds up to the same thing.
Retired. You'll apologize for my being so abrupt, but I like to be alone before my matches. Excuse me.
[Adler ducks into the locker room, and the cameraman walks away.] Cue the pyro!!! RAW Carolina moved up the road to Fayetteville, North Carolina for its' next big show, Criminal Intent! The Crown Auditorium was the site Wednesday night for the big show. Results:
SIDIOUS THE CLOWN Š vs. "DAMN DESPERATE" DICK ROGERS, Carolina Tag- Team Titles The opening match saw Sidious the Clown defend his tag-team titles in a SINGLES match, against Empire Club lackey Dick Rogers. As usual,
Sidious entrance was nothing short of flashy, as he entered the ring to the worst pyro show of all time. As the thousands and thousands of sparklers burned themselves out, Sidious stepped into the aisle to a huge ovation, clad in a gawdawful green and blue tuxedo – complete with pink gloves. One title belt was strapped around his waist, the other held in his hand by Kewlic.
Rogers started off hot early, peppering the bizarre clown with a series of hard right hands, followed up with a nice side suplex.
Sidious rolled to the corner to make the tag, but Kewlic lay limply on the apron, just out of reach had Rogers booted Sidious in the back of the head. Sidious fought back with a pair of dropkicks and a nifty cartwheel kick – and made the tag to Kewlic.
Sidious tossed Kewlic into the ring – right into Rogers' face. Rogers peeled the sock off, slammed it hard into the mat, and began to stomp away. Rogers looked around – seeing Sidious recuperating on the apron – dropped to make the cover. At the two and a half count,
Sidious rushed into the ring, and broke up the pin. Sidious grabbed his partner and tossed him into the corner, where he tagged back in.
Sidious hit the ring, and leveled Rogers with a nice leg lariat. Sidious then followed with the greatest move in sports entertainment - - the US Citizen's Kneedrop. Sidious kicked Rogers' shoulder, and then knocked his own knees together. Sidious reached down, removed his kneepad, and tossed it into the crowd. Sidious bounded off the ropes, stopped above Rogers' head, did a little `shaky knee dance', and dropped a knee right on Rogers' forehead.
From there, Sidious peeled the dazed Rogers off the mat, and put him right back down with the Pie in the Eye reverse crucifix for the pin.
Winner: Sidious the Clown (Pie in the Eye) Time: 3:42 (*) "ERADICATOR" DIRK ROBERTSON vs. "MR. RAW" ALEX PATTERSON Two of the top newcomers in the Carolinas met in the next contest, as the burdgeoning issue between Dirk Robertson and Alex Patterson continued. This issue took a very serious turn at a pair of recent house shows, as these two ended each other's undefeated streaks.
Both of these men have made great strides since arriving in the Carolinas, and whoever emerges victorious in this feud should be in line for a Piedmont title shot down the line.
The match was very even, as Patterson traded suplexes with Robertson's backyard style of innovative maneuvers honed in years of backyard wrestling. Several times, Robertson seemed to have the match in hand, only to see Suzanne Patterson distract referee Slick
Wilson, always one with an eye for the ladies.
The end of the match came when Suzanne hopped onto the apron, hoping to distract Robertson this time. It worked, as Robertson grabbed her by the hair, threatening her with a little man on woman violence.
Patterson moved over and kneed Robertson in the back, and set him up for a RAW Bomb. He hoisted him up – and Robertson reversed it mid- air, bringing Patterson down for a modified DDT and the victory.
Winner: Dirk Robertson (modified DDT) Time: 6:22 (** ź) "FAST" EDDIE CUTLASS vs. EL CID As the match began, Sidious the Clown made his customary Eddie Cutlass match run-in, hitting the ring and dancing in circles around
Cid, before gleefully rolling out to the floor to watch.
What he saw from his ringside seat was an excellent contest, bred in the fact that these two men know each other inside and out. Cid has developed a little bit of a tougher edge since arriving here in RAW-
C, even going so far as to attack Stone with a pair of brass knuckles. But he kept it clean against Cutlass, fast becoming one of the most popular stars in the Carolinas, with his `protector of the weak' mantra.
The match was very high-paced, as the two young cruiserweights went at it tooth and nail, hammer and tongs. Cid dominated the early going, but Cutlass took to the offensive at the five minute mark, going for an early Guarantee only to see Cid fight his way out with a
well-placed elbow to the throat. Cid capitalized with a nice series of chops and leg lariats, following up with a standing moonsault that nearly scored a three count. Cid then scaled the top rope, looking to deliver his
Inquisition missile dropkick – which Cutlass swatted aside. Cutlass took a moment to regain his senses, and lifted Cid into a brainbuster – and as he brought him down to the mat, Cid held on and rolled Cutlass into a cradle, and a nice handful of tights was enough to keep Cutlass down for the three count.
Sidious was enraged – he leapt into the ring and splashed Cid, sitting on his chest and smacking him about the face with a series of `sissy slaps'. Cid powered his way out of this `predicament', and tossed Sidious over the top rope to the floor by his blue hair.
Cutlass rolled out of the ring to check on his friend. Winner: El Cid (Brainbuster reversal) Time: 6:52 (***) Postmatch, as Sidious made his way back to the locker room, Bull "Gator" Johnson stormed into the aisle, hitting Sidious with a brutal clothesline that nearly beheaded the clown. Johnson then went for a big splash – Sidious rolled out of the way, and went on the attack. Sidious wailed away at Johnson with Kewlic on his right hand – to little or no effect. Johnson sneered at the weak offense, and hit Sid with another clothesline. At this point, Eddie Cutlass made his way onto the scene, and Johnson beat a retreat to the locker room.
TOMMY TRENTON & EZ KILLER vs. WILD STYLE The next encounter featured perhaps the two top young tag-teams in the Carolinas, as Tommy Trenton & EZ Killer met Terry Brody's young team Wild Style, Drew Brody & Shannon Hamilton. The winner of this match would surely be in line for a title shot in the near future.
Wild Style started out very hot, hitting their fellow youngsters with a flurry of their innovative double-team offense, including a spike double underhook piledriver on Trenton that Killer was forced to make the save, and the match continued.
Killer and Trenton continue to gel as a team, and this match was the next step in their evolution as a solid contender. They unveiled a few nice double-team moves, including an old-school double slingshot suplex.
The match featured ten minutes of excellent tag action that the crowd enjoyed thoroughly. The ending came when Hamilton scaled the top rope, only to miss his patented corkscrew legdrop. As Brody looked on helplessly, Trenton locked in the Trentonizer, and Hamilton quickly tapped.
Winners: Tommy Trenton & EZ Killer (Trentonizer) Time: 10:22 (** ž) "HURRICANE" KIRK MACLEAN Š vs. "X-PRESS" DAVID JERICHO, Piedmont Heavyweight Title After the controversial finish to their match at Coastal Collision, an immediate rematch was ordered and accepted, as neither Kirk
Maclean or David Jericho was happy with the way things went down in
Charleston. The rematch was even more even-contested than the original, as both men had become familiar with one another's style. Jericho's explosive offense kept Maclean on his heels during the opening segments, and he managed to lock Maclean into his painful X-Press
Way, which Maclean escaped by grabbing the ropes.
Maclean rebounded and went on the offensive, returning to the high- flying style he had abandoned in the last match, and staggered
Jericho with several dynamic maneuvers, including a nifty moonsault headbutt. After twelve minutes of such back and forth action, Maclean sidestepped a missile dropkick, and capitalized quickly with a Gory
Special Suplex.
Maclean headed for the top rope, looking to end the match with his
Eye of the Storm, a somersault senton with a twist. He launched – and connected – a bit too solidly, as his momentum carried him off of
Jericho, who rolled with the move, ending up on top of Maclean with a handful of tights.
Referee Slick Wilson counted to one – two – and Maclean got a shoulder up. Three. Wilson didn't see the shoulder come up, and slapped the mat a third time. He called for the bell, and raised
Jericho's hand in victory.
Maclean leapt to his feet, and as Wilson awarded Jericho the belt,
Maclean began arguing with him, pointing out – correctly – that his shoulder had been up before the three count. Jericho couldn't believe that Maclean would mar his moment – and laid him out with the title belt. Jericho continued to pound Maclean with the belt to huge boos from the partisan Fayetteville crowd – when RAW-C newcomer Flash made his way to ringside. He hit the ring, and Jericho backed off, as the two men stared each other down.
At that point, Maclean's longtime friend – and recent Jericho agitator – and another RAW-C newcomer – "Iceman" Mark Adams hit the ring, headed straight for Flash. Given these men's past histories, the fans rightfully expected them to tear into each other. Adams and
Flash glared at each other intensely, as Maclean looked on, unsure of what to do.
Jericho also looked on, and slowly moved towards the ropes, looking to take this unexpected opportunity to exit the ring – when Adams and
Flash turned and hit the new champion with a double clothesline, which sent Jericho tumbling over the top rope to the floor to a HUGE pop.
Adams and Flash looked at each other, and then at Maclean, and the three men left the ring together to a huge pop from the crowd.
Winner (and new champion): David Jericho (roll-over) Time: 13:21 (*** ˝) JACK ANDERSON vs. BULL "GATOR" JOHNSON After an impressive showing at the weekend house shows, Bull Johnson came into Criminal Intent brimming with confidence, and after destroying Sidious the Clown earlier in the evening, looked to do the same to the Crimson Spitfire Jack Anderson.
As for Anderson, the lack of response from his fellow fan favorites to his recent plea for unity has left his self-confidence a bit shaken, if not stirred. Couple that with the fact that he was climbing into the ring with the biggest monster in RAW-C, and
Anderson was in trouble. He started the match very tentative, more unsure of himself than ever – and where Jack Anderson's concerned, that's saying something. Johnson battered the redhead early and often, as Anderson's tentative hit and run style had little effect.
Johnson took firm control with a savage running avalanche that crushed Anderson in the corner, leaving him gasping for air with possible broken ribs. He followed that up with a monstrous powerbomb, but Anderson somehow managed to sneak a shoulder up at the last second.
Felicity could tell her man was in trouble; she also knew that having the crowd behind him always gives Anderson that something extra, so she began exhorting them to do just that. And when a woman of
Felicity's <ahem> `gifts' wants to get a bunch of guys from Fort
Bragg to do something – they do something.
With the crowd rallying behind him, Anderson waged his comeback. A series of running forearms took Gator off his feet, which Anderson followed with a nice enziguiri the second Johnson got back to his feet. Perhaps Anderson was too pumped up by the crowd, as his next attempted move was a belly to belly suplex – easily reversed by
Johnson, outweighing Anderson by over two hundred pounds and all. But Anderson would not be deterred. Johnson shot him off the ropes, only to see Jack duck under a clothesline and come off the far side ropes to catch Johnson with a dropkick. As the crowd urged him on,
Anderson headed for the top rope. Once he got there, he seemed a bit unsure of what to try, and launched into a Flying Clusterfrick, which
Johnson sidestepped, sending Anderson crashing hard into the mat.
Seemingly inspired, Johnson decided that he, too, would like to try to fly. But the climb to the the top turnbuckle was a slow, arduous one for the 450 pound Gator. As he climbed, Anderson got to his feet, and followed Johnson up to the top turnbuckle, where he began laying into him with a series of punches. With Johnson dazed, Anderson leapt high into the air, looking to catch Johnson with his Fireball Special top rope hurricanrana.
He caught Johnson around the neck – but Johnson was too massive, and
Anderson couldn't get him over as they plummeted towards the mat, and the result was Johnson delivering a powerbomb from the top rope, folding Anderson neatly in half. The three count, as they say, was academic.
But Johnson wasn't through. He continued to pound away at Anderson, for no better reason than because he could. He gave him another powerbomb, and then a brutal Gatorsmash Jambalaya, his powerbomb –
Texas Cloverleaf combo.
As Felicity pleaded with Johnson to break the cloverleaf, the fans pleaded with someone – anyone – to help out their man. And who should answer the call but the strange, hairy, hard on the eyes newcomer Direwolf, who entered the ring a house o' fire, and proceeded to clothesline the big Bull Gator up and over the top rope to clear the ring. Direwolf then helped Anderson up to his feet, to a huge ovation for both men.
Winner: Bull Johnson (Top Rope Powerbomb) Time: 7:12 (***) "LAWBRINGER" THOMAS ADLER vs. TERRY BRODY, Career v. Career It was the rivalry that helped launch RAW Carolina to national prominence. A bitter blood feud, born not over titles or women or money, but simply two men who couldn't stand the mere sight of each other. And now, four months after Brody broke the first beer bottle over Adler's head, they would meet one more time – for the last time.
Loser. Leaves. Town. RAW Carolina president "Showboat" Eddie Cannon ordered this match, and made his first appearance of the night for it: Decked out in the zebra shirt of a RAW-C referee, believing that he is the only official in the Carolinas capable of bringing order to this one.
Alder started out very hot, as is his wont; Brody withstood a barrage of early power wrestling, being tossed around by the larger Adler with reckless abandon, as the crowd ate it up. Adler, not entirely sure where Cannon stood on this issue, elected not to try and cover
Brody until he was sure Brody couldn't get up – no slow count was going to screw him out of his career.
A well-placed thumb to the eye gave Brody a breather, and he followed up with a shot from a chain-wrapped fist that Cannon either didn't see or chose to ignore. Given the high-stakes of this match, either one seems likely. Brody delivered several rabbit punches with that chain, bloodying Adler with it – including a nasty cut on his right ear, which bled profusely the rest of the match.
Brody fought with all he had, and pulled every trick out of his wild man bag. After downing Adler with a nice DDT, Brody climbed on his old, destroyed knees to the top rope – and connected with a rare moonsault. Still, only enough for a two count.
Brody kept the pressure on, punishing Adler. The tide shifted once again when Brody hit the ropes, looking for a running neckbreaker.
Adler sidestepped, and caught Brody in a press slam – Once, twice, and the third time up and over the top rope to the floor. As Brody lay motionless, Adler climbed to the top turnbuckle – and came crashing down with a legdrop across Brody's throat, leaving both men in a pile on the floor.
After Cannon administered the longest ten count in history, presumably because he wanted the match to end cleanly in the ring, both men made their way to their feet. As they climbed onto the apron, they began trading blows. Adler caught Brody with a particularly stiff shot to the mush, and then hooked him under his arm – and delivered a front layout suplex through the timekeeper's table!!
Again, both men were down on the floor. Again, Adler was the first one to his feet. He moved towards Brody – and was met with a broken table leg right in the face!! Brody staggered to his feet, using the table leg the way Ichiro wields a baseball bat – very well (that one's for you, Earl). Again, Cannon let it go, and the fans were thankful – they wanted this one to end clean in the ring, as well.
Brody grabbed Adler by his hair, and rolled the bloody fan favorite into the ring. He went for a pinfall, but could only score a two count. Suddenly, out of nowhere, Adler countered with a swinging DDT that left Brody lying in the center of the ring. Adler pointed to the top rope, and the fans exploded in anticipation of the Guillotine.
Adler climbed, and waited for Brody to get up as the crowd buzzed. Brody did, and Adler launched, catching him with his devastating top rope clothesline. Adler went for the pin – and then thought better of it. Instead, he hooked Brody into a deep camel clutch – in the center of the ring. Nowhere to go. Nowhere to hide.
Adler leaned back on the hold, but Brody wouldn't give. Cannon pleaded with him to submit before Adler broke his neck – but Brody wouldn't give. Two minutes went by. Three minutes went by. Brody's eyes began to glass over. Cannon raised his arm – it fell. He raised Brody's arm a second time – it fell a second time.
Cannon raised it a third time – and it fell a third time. Cannon called for the bell. Adler released the hold. Brody fell to the mat. Unconscious.
The fans erupted in a cacophony of joy, and Adler raised his hand in joyful victory. Terry Brody will never wrestle in a RAW Carolina ring again. Winner: Thomas Adler (Camel Clutch) Time: 22:14 (**** ˝) VINCENT C. AUGUSTUS Š vs. STONE vs. "SENSATIONAL" SCOTT STUDD, Carolina Heavyweight Title No one felt worse about the treatment of Scott Studd in last week's main event than RAW Carolina president "Showboat" Eddie Cannon.
Always a man of action, he felt the only fair thing to do would be to book an immediate rematch, throwing one of Studd's allies into the ring in a three way dance to boot. Studd felt confident with his chances in a the triple threat contest; so much so, in fact, that he vowed to retire from the ring for ninety days should he lose the contest, raising the already astronomical stakes.
Augustus came to ringside with his usual entourage, mentor Morten
Wyngarde and bodyguard Jack Hammer. Studd and Stone each came out alone, but moments before the match was to begin, "Hurricane" Kirk
Maclean made his way out to ringside, and promptly positioned himself in a chair at the wreckage that was the timekeeper's table. Wyngarde petitioned to have him removed, but Maclean gave referee `Cottonmouth' Joe Lynch his word that he would no interfere, and that if he did, he would be escorted to the locker room.
With that settled, the match began. VCA seemed content early on to stand back and watch as Studd and Stone took it to each other, only jumping in when he had an opening for a cheap shot, or to break up a pin attempt. VCA made his first move about four minutes in, just as
Studd clotheslined Stone over the top rope to the floor. VCA charged
Studd, hitting him with a knee in the back that sent him spilling to the floor, as well.
VCA moved immediately to distract Lynch, setting into motion a plan fans have seen before, as Jack Hammer moved over and began pounding on Stone and Studd. That didn't last long, however, as Studd and
Stone banded together, fighting back with dual punches, and then double clotheslining Hammer over the safety railing into the crowd.
Studd and Stone then headed back to the ring, where they showed some nice teamwork, hitting Augustus with a variety of double-team moves, alternating covering him – Augustus always kicking out (barely) at two.
A mistimed double-team spot led to the end of the match, as Stone held VCA up for a Studd superkick – which Augustus ducked out of, and
Studd laid out Stone instead. VCA dropped to the mat, exhausted after the lastest round of double-teams, and Studd seized the opportunity. He locked the champ into his Studdly Stretch, and seemingly locked the title up, as well.
As Morten Wyngarde's prized possession screamed in agony, Stone began to stir on the mat. Augustus would not give in – Stone got up to his feet. Augustus would not give in – Stone moved over behind Studd, and lifted him up and onto the mat with a German Suplex, breaking the hold – and saving the match in the process. Stone followed up quickly with his Rolling Stone, laying Studd out cold.
What happened next shocked everyone in the building – "Hurricane" Kirk Maclean at ringside perhaps most of all. Rather than covering either man and win the title, Stone helped Augustus to his feet – and the two men commenced to lay the boots to Studd. A hush fell over the confused crowd – Just what the sam hill was going on here?!?
And then, as a bewildered Maclean looked on, it became all too apparent – Augustus and Stone shook hands, an event that was followed with yet another Rolling Stone for Studd, and one VCA Lockdown later,
Studd's shoulders were pinned to the mat for the three count.
Winner: Vincent C. Augustus (Lockdown) Time: 9:12 (**) As Augustus and Stone celebrated with Wyngarde in the ring, Maclean snatched the mike away from Bobby Lee Wilcox:
"Hey Champ...congratulations on your "successful" title "defense". Too bad you didn't impress ANYBODY with your performance in that ring tonight." The crowd roared at this. "The only reason you're the Carolina Heavyweight Champion, Augustus, is because you've been damn lucky...but your luck is about to run out. I want that belt, Champ, and I'm challenging YOU to a title match...if you're man enough to meet me in the ring. Get ready,
Champ...because this Hurricane Warning...is for you!!"
Augustus and Stone dove out of the ring, headed for Maclean. But the
Hurricane was too quick, and disappeared into the crowd before they got there, leaving the brand new Empire Club fuming in the aisle.
