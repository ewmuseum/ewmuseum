Nuclear Winter 12.28.01

29 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Say what you will about The Network, but the television deal with NASN continues to pay dividends, as RAW Carolina expands its’ territory once more with Nuclear Winter, coming to you from the campus of East Tennessee State University in Johnson City, Tennessee, the final show of a great 2001!!
TERRY JACOBS v. PAUL SHERIDAN Not many men can take eight years away from the sport, yet climb back in the ring without missing hardly a beat. Then again, not too many men have spent the last eight years running one of the most well-respected wrestling schools in the South like Terry Jacobs has.
Little wonder, then, that his transition back into competition has been a smooth one. After a modest winning streak a few weeks back, rookie Paul Sheridan has taken a step backwards in the win column just as he stepped forward in competition level – not a rare occurrence for one still finding his way. But Jacobs took him as seriously as he does every opponent. Starting the match with a handshake, Jacobs proceeded to pick the rookie apart with some crisp, technically sound offense, like a nice airplane spin backbreaker that got a two count.
A signature Jacobs move, the Southern Sunset springboard reverse DDT got a two and a half count at the three minute mark, but Sheridan bounced right back up with a small package for two, and a pair of dropkicks, and then bounded off the ropes – right into one of the best lariats in the game, the Charlotte Cyclone. The three count was academic.
Winner: Terry Jacobs (Charlotte Cyclone) Time: 4:12 (* ½) CHRISTIAN DEMONSTONE v. “EL TIGRE” ANTONIO MUNOZ, JR. Terry Jacobs was impressive in the opener, but the loud-mouthed, short-tempered Christian Demonstone may have one-upped him in the next contest. This huge 275 pounder looks good with his shirt off – and even better in the ring, completely overmatching lucha
star-in-the-making Antonio Munoz, Jr. Not three seconds in, Demonstone planted Munoz with a T-Bone Suplex, and it was more of the same the rest of the (brief) way. A dragonscrew legwhip, followed by a Fishermanbuster, and Munoz was set up for the Canuck Driver, the Quebecois’ version of a vertaebreaker. Less than two minutes after it started, it was all over.
Winner: Christian Demonstone (Canuck Driver) Time: 1:45 (*) JUSTIN HARPER v. MIGUEL FUJIMOTO Rookie powerhouse Justin Harper turned some heads at Season’s Beatings with a time limit draw against veteran Terry Jacobs, and looked to capitalize on that momentum in Johnson City with a match against another Suplex Academy grad, Miguel Fujimoto. Harper is himself a recent graduate of another renowned training school, the
Knightraven Dojo in his native California.
Fujimoto took to the air quickly, stunning the bigger Harper with a few quick moves off the ropes, and then scored a nearfall with a beautiful quick senton from the second rope (a senton bullet, maybe?!?). But Harper regrouped quickly, showing nice composure for a rookie, and took control for good at the three minute mark with a nice
belly-to-back suplex. Harper then dragged his opponent to the top rope with him for a sort of Super Kneebreaker that drew ooohs from the crowd, and followed up with a standing moonsault. Sensing the end, he wrapped up Fujimoto in the Figure Four he calls the Cedar Rapids Clutch, and Fujimoto quickly submitted. Can a rematch between Harper and Jacobs be too far away?!?
Winner: Justin Harper (Cedar Rapids Clutch) Time: 4:11 (**) STEVE FURY v. “SENSATIONAL” SCOTT STUDD After a brutal beating at the hands of newcomer The Unholy on RAW-C TV last night, handsome young Steve Fury got right back off the mat to serve as the opponent in the supercard return of the Kentucky powerhouse, Scott Studd. Despite Studd’s silence on the issue, the appearance of Jackie Boone, Jr. at several of his matches last week has convinced many fans that he has indeed thrown in with The
Network.
As a result of those suspicions, Fury was a solid crowd favorite here
– but it didn’t really matter. Studd has been in top form since returning, looking to prove to Eddie Cannon and the world that he is worthy of top status. He even appears to be stronger than ever
– if that is even possible.
At any rate, Fury was overmatched here. The youngster got in a few moves, including a nice springboard bulldog for a two count, but that was about all. Studd used a nice variety of stiff suplexes and slams to weaken the kid, before finally dropping him over the top rope with a Studd Gun to secure the three count.
Winner: Scott Studd (Studd Gun) Time: 4:32 (**) STONE © vs. JAYSON RAYGE, Carolina Television Title Since capturing the Television Title from the Hangman at Queen City
Showdown, Stone has been on quite a roll – get it?!? -- , defending the title against any and all comers. Friday night’s comer was young Blood Brother Jayson Rayge, eager to taste his first championship success here in the Carolinas.
As he often does, Rayge started out fast and furious, and had the champ on the ropes in the early going, including a string of four two counts in a minute and a half. But as he often does, Rayge made a rookie mistake, and missed a kneedrop from the top rope (maybe he’s been watching too many Thomas Creighton matches). Stone then went to work, and having weathered the rookie’s storm, the stocky champ unleashed a barrage of suplexes, and mixed in a near-crippling piledriver for a two count at the six minute mark.
But Rayge was as resilient as ever, refusing to stay down for the required three seconds. At the ten minute mark, he mounted a brief flurry, and dropped Stone with his Infinite Rayge Driver, and appeared to have the title won. But they were too close to the ropes; Stone got a foot up, and the match continued. At the thirteen minute mark,
Rayge went for a tombstone – but Stone kicked out of it, reversing it into the set-up for a running Rikishi Driver – and one Rolling Stone later, Stone had successfully defended his title in an outstanding match.
Winner: Stone (Rolling Stone) Time: 13:24 (*** ½) MIKE STEELE & ???????? v. PURGATORY The heinous attempt on Mike Diamond’s life before Christmas left the scheduled Collateral Damage versus Purgatory bout very much in doubt. But Melanie Kirkpatrick vowed to find a replacement partner for her guy, and came through in spades. Fans who caught the RAW-C TV special last night were not surprised to see the same masked luchadore who ended that show come out as Steele’s partner, ready to take on The Network’s bloodthirsty goons Synn and Dementia.
Let’s describe the newcomer, billed simply as La Estoque. White tights and singlet with red trim, a dandy red satin sash and a full red mask with white trim. Hair?!? Pulled through a hole in the back into a topknot ponytail. Fashion report out of the way, we can also tell you that he’s no slouch in the ring, either, showing off a lot of fancy high-flying stuff that we mere gringos have no names for.
Steele and the luchador meshed well together, and gave the Windy City
Warriors all they could handle. They even improvised some double-teams on the spot, including a beautiful rocket launcher
– senton bomb for a two count, and a powerbomb – guillotine legdrop for another. Purgatory was as tough as ever, and with rumors of a RAW World Tag-Team Title shot for the winner of this one, the Forces of Evil were in top form. At the nine minute mark, Synn dropped Steele with his Confession tiger driver, and made the cover.
But rather than make a traditional save, Estoque went for a spectacular one. Coming off the top, he crashed into Synn with a something or other that had to be seen to be described, other than a four alarm WOW. Hank Jackson stopped his count, and moved to get
Estoque out of the ring – and that’s when Dave Pitrelli hit the ring, steel chain wrapped around hand, and clocked Steele with it for the second time in three nights. Jackson turned around to see
Synn back on top – and Pitrelli on the floor – and made the three count, as an on-rushing Estoque was sent out of the ring by a nice float kick from Dementia.
Winners: Pugatory (Network shenanigans) Time: 10:32 (***) CASANOVA BEETHOVEN v. ??????? Former Carolina champ Casanova Beethoven claimed not to have been fooled by scheduled opponent Vincent Constantine Augustus’ claim of retirement, feeling it was but a mind trick by his old ally turned rival. So imagine his surprise when VCA remained true to his word in retirement – and who should step in to fill his boots in this match but former RAW Atlantic superstar Macabre(!!).
Clearly not expecting this, Beethoven was battered early on, as Macabre unloaded, clearing the ho-lover out of the ring not once but twice to regroup – to no avail. Back in the second time,
Macabre dropped him with a Death Valley Driver, and only an alert
Slick Wilson spotting Beethoven’s foot on the bottom rope saved the three count.
Beethoven did manage to drop the smaller masked man with a clothesline four minutes in – but after sending him off the ropes, Macabre ducked under another clothesline and connected with the Danse Macabre, his trademark leaping spinkick to the back of the head. Beethoven went stumbling, over the top rope to the floor. As he looked to clear the cobwebs one more time, a solitary figure leapt from the crowd to attack him – “X-PRESS” DAVID JERICHO!!!!!
Jericho unloaded on his old rival with a brutal assault, as both men traded blows up the aisle towards the locker room. Wilson tossed the match out, declaring a no contest. Intrepid RAW Carolina reporter
Vernon St. Jean climbed into the ring to grab a few words with
Macabre: VSJ: What a debut showing here in the Carolinas for one of my favorite RAW Atlantic stars – MACABRE!! I gotta ask youOOOMPH!!!!!!!!!!!!!
Before Macabre could say his piece, he and Intern Vern were rudely interrupted by another unexpected run-in – RAW Atlantic Coastal
Champion “Stellar” Steve Keller and his hired goon “Trainwreck” Caleb Jones!!! Beating Macabre down, they proceeded to drive him headfirst into the Coastal Title belt with a spike piledriver(!!). Stepping over his fallen rival, Keller grabbed the mike Vern dropped when he ske-daddled:
SK: I told you, Macabre!! You can’t run!! You can’t hide from me!! We’re gonna finish this one way or another!!
And now – Now you’re on MY turf!! And with that, one of the greatest Carolina Heavyweight Champions EVER left the ring. MARC TOWER & JACK ANDERSON v. “DREADMIND” DAVE PITRELLI & “ERADICATOR” DIRK ROBERTSON The end of the main event at Queen City Showdown found opponents Marc
Tower and Jack Anderson joining forces to fight off a Network assault;
naturally, this match was made as a result. With the injury to Eddie Cutlass and Robertson’s defection to The Network, Tower and Anderson find themselves atop the RAW-C fans’ popularity list – especially when they’re across the ring from the hated, demented Piedmont Champion Dave
Pitrelli, once again without his valet Sara. The match started off relatively scientific, with the four men wrestling in various combinations with one another. Pitrelli was the most impressive early on, dropping Anderson with a brainbuster that probably would have gotten the three if not for Tower making the save.
After six minutes or so of such action – well, that’s when the fit hit the shan. Because that’s when Jackie Boone, Jr. wandered down to ringside, followed closely by Dennis Cody, making a rare in-arena appearance.
They appeared at first to be casually observing the action, rooting on their boys – but everyone knew better. Especially referee
‘Cottonmouth’ Joe Lynch, so preoccupied with their arrival that he missed Pitrelli clobbering Tower with a steel chain around his fist, drawing blood.
But when Boone leapt to the apron, steel chair in hand aimed for
Tower’s head, Lynch took action. Grabbing his former officiating comrade by the hair, he pulled him over the ropes and into the ring – and delivered the Jaws of Defeat DDT he made famous back in his days as an active wrestler. His man down, Robertson then dropped Lynch with a clothesline to the back of the head.
By now, the Showboat had seen enough, and Eddie Cannon raced to ringside and got into a melee with Cody, while the four actual in-ring competitors traded blows in the ring. Official Hank Jackson came out to continue the match, and immediately worked to separate Tower and
Pitrelli in one corner, as Anderson climbed the top rope in the other, looking to come off onto a prone Robertson.
Another run-in?!? Sure!! That brought out The Guy Dressed All in
Black, who came out of the crowd and into the ring, stunning Anderson
– still on the top turnbuckle – with a punch, and then flipping him with a quick Fireball Special hurricanrana. A groggy
Robertson rolled on top of Anderson, and Jackson turned just in time to count the three count.
Winners: Dave Pitrelli & Dirk Robertson (Fortuitous Run-In of the
Masked Kind) Time: 8:21 (**) Cannon was enraged. Rolling into the ring, he called for Bobby Lee
Wilcox’ mike: “I’ve had about enough of this!! Next Sunday in Knoxville – New Year’s Brawl!! I’m taking matters into my own hands!! “I’m taking this guy –“ Cannon pointed at Tower. “ – And I’m taking THIS guy.” Cannon pointed at Lynch. “And we’re gonna have a six man tag. You.” Cannon pointed at Robertson. “You.” Cannon pointed at Boone. “And ESPECIALLY YOU.” Cannon pointed at Cody. “And as for you – “ Cannon pointed at Hank Jackson. “I don’t think you can handle this one. So I’m bringing in my special enforcer. The referee for this six man tag
– “THUNDERBOLT” JACK SAMPSON.
