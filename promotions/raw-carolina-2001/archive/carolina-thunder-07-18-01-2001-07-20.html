RAW Carolina — Card Archive

Carolina Thunder 07/18/01

20 July 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
With just one show to go before we reach Carolina Thunder, the intensity level in RAW Carolina has reached new heights. With that in mind, over 5000 fans poured in the Asheville Civic Center Wednesday night for Midsummer Maelstrom. Results:

But, the fans gasp, when they see that he's dragging behind him, being pulled by a noose around her neck, Lilliane Devereaux. A loud wave of "What the hell?" goes through the crowd. Jack gets to the ring, climbs in, but leaves Lilliane on the floor, while he still holds onto the rope.]
JA: Johnson! Get the hell out here, or your bitch dies! [Unbelieving, the crowd doesn't know what to do.] JA: You sent Felicity to the hospital, you mother(BLEEP). I'll send this gal to the morgue, if you don't come out here, and agree to my terms.... Look at her... first, her face will turn red, then blue, then purple, then black....
[Devereaux struggles, grasping at the noose, but seemingly slow, and weak.] JA: Where are you, man with two nicknames.... Bulllll, Gaaaator.... come out and plaaaaaaay.... [Still no sign of Johnson.] JA: Oh... if you wonder how I caught her like this? A bit of chloroform on a rag does wonders... THAT, did you?!?] “HARDCORE SENSATION” MARK ADAMS & FLASH vs. WALKER BROTHERS The crowd settled down after that hot, hot opening for a night of exciting action. But anyone expecting a competitive match in the opening bout was solely disappointed, as Mark Adams and Flash ran roughshod over Isaac and Gideon Walker, steamrolling towards their tag-team title showdown with Wild Style at Carolina Thunder.
The Walkers did manage to control the very early going, but 90% of the match proved to be nothing but a showcase for the immeasurable skill of Adams and Flash. Adams showed a little bit more of his promised return to a more hardcore style, eschewing his recent suplex-based attack in favor of wild brawling. Ordinarily, this would favor the
Wild Bunch, but Adams has proved through the years to be more than proficient in this style, and worked the Walkers over.
The roof nearly exploded when Flash tagged in, as he is fast becoming one of the most popular men in RAW Carolina. He barely gave the fans their money’s worth, before pancaking Gideon Walker, Jr. with a beautiful Miracle reverse suplex, which scored the easy pinfall.
Winners: Mark Adams & Flash (Miracle Suplex) Time: 4:22 (* ˝) DIREWOLF vs. ALEX PATTERSON Two of the top contenders to Dirk Robertson’s Television Title met in the next contest, as the Father of the Year Direwolf took on the powerhouse Alex Patterson. As always, childcare providers were in short supply, and Direwolf was forced to bring young soon Barry to the ring with him. Barry didn’t mind – he spent the whole match hitting on Patterson’s valet and wife, Suzanne – which sat none too well with the former Mr. RAW.
Of course, his son’s presence at ringside was nothing if not a distraction for Direwolf, which Patterson took immediate advantage of, tossing the hairiest man in wrestling all over the ring with his textbook crisp suplexes. Direwolf rolled to the floor to take a breather – and check on Barry – when the roof came off the
ACC as Hounddog Murphy arrived, and assured his new friend that he could take care of Barry. Direwolf assented, Murphy dragged a kicking and screaming Barry back to the locker room, and Cy Sperling’s favorite wrestler entered the ring a different man.
Without the “Barry distraction”, Direwolf went to work, showing the form that has made him an international superstar many times over.
A running piledriver, a brainbuster, a German Suplex – Patterson mounted a late rally, but it wasn’t enough, as an Alpha Male
Chokeslam planted him on the mat for a three count. Winner: Direwolf (Alpha Male Chokeslam) Time: 5:34 (**) MARC TOWER vs. BULL “GATOR” JOHNSON This one got real ugly, real quick. Referee Slick Wilson decided early on to let most things go in this one, or else the fans may not get their money’s worth. Johnson’s a nasty individual to begin with – as is Tower – but toss in Jack
Anderson’s treatment of his manager Lilliane Devereaux earlier in the evening, and we had a recipe for brutality.
Johnson hammered Tower from pillar to post early on, using the ringsteps, the ringposts, a chair here, a table there, and a lesser man would have been broken in half. But Tower battled back, showing remarkable resiliency despite giving up nearly 200 pounds to the Cajun monster. Tower is no stranger to using anything and everything not bolted down at ringside to score a pinfall, and he did so with alarming accuracy.
At the eight minute mark of a wild brawl, Johnson planted Tower with an unbelievable superplex – and that’s when Dirk Robertson arrived. No one in the arena was surprised to see the Television
Champion, as his problems with Tower have been well-documented.
Robertson hopped up on the apron – and was immediately clotheslined down to the floor by Johnson, blind in his rage.
Robertson got back up and came in the ring after Johnson. He charged
Bull – who ducked, sending Robertson crashing headlong into
Tower. Robertson staggered backwards, and Johnson dumped him over the top rope. One awesome wheelbarrow powerbomb later, and Johnson had a brutal victory.
Winner: Bull Johnson (wheelbarrow powerbomb) Time: 8:38 (***) Before the next match, the Mini-Tron came to life with the following: We watch as El Cid steps out of the locker room. His fists are taped in preparation for his upcoming match. He turns and looks at the Coke
Machine. Its’ lovely red glow shines upon his face. A smirk begins to
form, as we can only guess he is mesmerized by this object. Suddenly without warning, El Cid is knocked to the ground.
The camera pans back to see the smiling, demented face of the man, better hung then the average bull, Sidious the Clown. Sidious holds a mighty, rubber chicken, strapped with barbwire to a steel chair. Sidious carefully places the chair aside and reaches into his pants. He pulls out his mighty, brown stained, list and a Playboy magazine. Sidious kneels upon El Cid's back, as he places the Playboy in front of Cid's eyes. Sid: “Look at her...yes she is nice. YOU CAN'T HAVE HER!!!” Sidious rolls up the magazine and smacks El Cid upon the back of the head. Sid: “I hate you Krispy. I seriously do. I know that you were the one that took my duck away. I know that you did the job. Got done with the deed. You are truly evil. What did the fellow
Mexican brethren duck of yours do to you? Huh? WHAT DID HE DO TO
YOU!!!”
Sidious grabs El Cid's head and smashes it against the floor. Sidious gets up off El Cid and walks away. But suddenly he whirls around and bellows.
Sid: “One day you will confuse!!!!” The crowd buzzed in confusion; Cid was the one?!? WILD STYLE Š vs. JESSIE MULDOON & THE JUDGE, Carolina Tag-Team Championship It’s often said that title shots make for strange bedfellows – OK, not really, but I liked the clever turn of phrase. So sue me – but perhaps none were stranger than the duo that teamed to take on the Carolina Tag-Team Champions Wild Style. Jessie Muldoon and The Judge had had their share of differences as of late, but agreed to put them aside to chase championship gold.
Or at least we think that’s what happened, but we’ll never know – The Judge no-showed the match, leaving Muldoon to go it alone against the champions, a tall order in any sense. But Muldoon was game, telling the Asheville crowd that if a clown like Sidious could hold tag-team gold by himself, there’s no reason he couldn’t.
Muldoon seemed to forget that the last time Sidious defended the titles by himself, he was run over by this very same Wild Style combo of Drew Brody and Shannon Hamilton. Terry Brody’s high-flying young charges dizzied Muldoon with their lightning-quick offense, and looked to put him away in short order.
But Muldoon powered out of a crucifix pin attempt by Hamilton into a
Fallaway Slam, and a Manly Obsession brainbuster out of nowhere forced
Brody into the ring to make the save. At that point, as referee
“Cottonmouth” Joe Lynch ushered Brody out of the ring,
Sidious the Clown made his way down to ringside, fresh off his demolition of El Cid. He leapt onto the apron, and as Muldoon lunged towards him, he sprayed him with the flower on the lapel of his jacket
– as the crowd roared with laughter.
Muldoon staggered back, right into a leg lariat from Hamilton. He tagged in Brody, and one Nothing Is As It Seems finisher later – a fireman’s carry neckbreaker combo – Muldoon was pinned.
Ringside fans got a whiff of Sidious as he passed, his flower smelling suspiciously like. . . .urine.
Winners: Wild Style (Nothing Is As It Seems) Time: 5:22 (* ˝) Bobby Lee Wilcox then urged the crowd to welcome RAW Carolina’s two newest superstars, who were seated in the front row.
“Golden Boy” Christopher Scarlet and Roadkill Rick got nice ovations from the crowd, who then turned their attention back to the ring.
SIDIOUS THE CLOWN vs. STONE Showing more fire than he has shown in any previous match here in RAW
Carolina, the normally happy go lucky Sidious the Clown stormed the ring, taking the fight right to his opponent on the evening, Stone.
Stone seemed to be one of the hottest prospects in the sport upon joining the Empire Club, but his star seems to have cooled considerably in the last few weeks. He was, however, more than willing to lay a beating into Sidious, a man he knows very, very well.
Stone’s down and dirty brawling made for a nice contrast with Sidious’ cruiser style. Sidious unloosed a corkscrew plancha for a two count, and trapped Stone in the Walls of Sidio, nearly earning the submission victory before Stone could reach the ropes.
Sidious looked to have the match under control when El Cid arrived in the aisleway, looking to extract revenge from the earlier attack. He charged the ring – only to be intercepted in the aisle by Eddie
Cutlass, who speared Cid to the ground, before brawling with him back to the locker room. Sidious watched with great interest – perhaps Eddie was truly his friend, despite Flash’s accusations.
Stone took advantage of the distraction to lift Sidious for a bodyslam – only to see him reverse it into his Pie In the Eye crucifix pin, for the three count.
Winner: Sidious the Clown (Pie in the Eye) Time: 6:48 (**) Sidious was met in the aisle by an elated Cutlass – but brushed right past his friend. Neither man was sure what to think at this point.
“ERADICATOR” DIRK ROBERTSON vs. EL CID, non-title As this show was not televised, Dirk Robertson’s Television Title couldn’t rightly be on the line in his match with El Cid. Still suffering from his earlier confrontations with Eddie Cutlass and
Sidious, Cid stumbled to the ring, right into the waiting arms of the very dangerous Robertson.
Robertson was still seething over his earlier encounter with Marc
Tower, and looked to take it out on Cid. Showing off the innovative offense that made him first a backyard wrestling legend and then the
Carolina Television Champion, Robertson dominated the early going, including a nice Chicken Wing Crash which he followed with a Death
Valley Driver for a near fall.
But Cid battled back, showing the heart that has made him a champion many times the world over. He scored a couple of two counts of his own, first with a Corona Killer tiger driver and then a Question fisherman’s suplex.
After twelve minutes of excellent action, Robertson lifted Cid into a gutwrench powerbomb – only to see Cid climb out of it mid-move, and twist Robertson in a Godsend victory roll – which scored the sudden three count.
No sooner had the bell rung than Marc Tower hit the ring, putting the boots to Robertson. As Cid rolled out of the ring to safety, the two former friends traded punches in the center of the ring, until a host of officials pulled them apart. Surely this issue will come to a head soon – perhaps at Carolina Thunder.
Winner: El Cid (Godsend) Time: 12:34 (*** ˝) “X-PRESS” DAVID JERICHO Š vs. “FAST” EDDIE CUTLASS, Piedmont Heavyweight Title The next match was for the hotly contested Piedmont Title – If Eddie Cultass could unseat David Jericho, it would mark the third title change in the last six days. The fans’ normal robust reaction for Cutlass was tempered somewhat by the small percentage who actually believe that he had something to do with Kewlic’s demise – the vast majority agree with Eddie – that he was set up. And as for Flash’s accusation that Cutlass was in fact a clone?!? We won’t even dignify that with a response.
But if the accusations had no effect on most of the crowd – they did affect Eddie. His usual crisp ringwork seemed just a bit off, as he was obviously affected by the lack of trust in him. And just a bit off was more than enough for David Jericho, who immediately went to work on Cutlass.
Jericho dominated with surprising ease in the early going, including an awesome spot where he gorilla pressed Cutlass over his head not twice but three times. An attempted side suplex by Jericho led to
Cutlass hitting his Guarantee, but Jericho was able to get his foot on the bottom rope before the reverse Russian Leg Sweep could score the pin.
Cutlass battled back with some nice suplexes of his own, but every attempted comeback was cut short by a dominant Jericho – but he couldn’t put Fast Eddie away. As the crowd rallied behind
Cutlass, the fifteen minute mark approached, and Cutlass scored several a series of two counts with a brainbuster, and then a fallaway slam – but the champion wouldn’t stay down. Jericho reversed a bulldog lariat with a clothesline of his own, and looked to end the match.
The time continued to tick away, and Jericho kept the pressure on, hitting Cutlass with suplex after suplex – but couldn’t put the plucky fan favorite away. Finally, Jericho had had enough
– and motioned to the crowd that it was time for the X-Press
Way. As he did so, a familiar voice wafted through the rafters of the
Civic Center, one we’ve heard increasingly over the last few weeks:
“Oh, Daaaaaaaaavid. . . . .” Jericho was dumbfounded, staring around the arena, looking in desperate vain for the source – and as he did so, the time limit expired, and for the third week in a row, Jericho had hung on to his title with a draw.
Decision: Time Limit Draw Time: 15:00 (***) Prior to the main event, RAW Carolina bossman “Showboat” Eddie Cannon hit the ring, mike in hand, for a few more announcements about Carolina Thunder:
“David Jericho, we’ve seen you escape with that Piedmont Title for the last time!! Two men in the last few weeks have taken you to the time limit – and at Carolina Thunder, they’ll
BOTH get their rematch!! That’s right, Triple Threat – David Jericho versus Eddie Cutlass versus El Cid!! With the Piedmont
Title on the line – First pinfall wins the title.
Now then, against my better judgment, I’ve given in to Jack Anderson’s demands: Normally, I’d wait ‘til he calmed down, and then worked out the details, but desperate times call for desperate measures: Texas Death Match. Jack Anderson. Bull
Johnson. Carolina Thunder.
Enjoy the main event.” “LAWBRINGER” THOMAS ADLER & JACK ANDERSON vs. VINCENT CONSTANTINE AUGUSTUS & CASANOVA BEETHOVEN New Carolina Heavyweight Champion Thomas Adler still does not know who his opponent at Carolina Thunder will be – the Empire Club has yet to announce its’ representative in the title match. But
Adler got a shot at both men tonight, as he teamed with the slightly pissed off Jack Anderson to take on Vincent C. Augustus and Casanova
Beethoven in what promised to be an all-out war.
Adler and Anderson showed much more cohesiveness than their opponents, who still seem to be at odds over just who exactly will get that coveted shot at Thunder. Anderson and the champ showed they meant business early on, as they lifted VCA up into a double suplex, only to drop him over the top rope, sending him crashing down into Morten
Wyngarde.
Another great double-team spot saw Anderson hit Beethoven with a spinning powerbomb, which Adler followed off the second rope with a leg drop onto the ho-loving superstar, bringing in VCA to break up the three count.
Despite their squabbling, the Empire Club was more than formidable, using cheap tactics and Morten Wyngarde’s assistance at ringside to keep things interesting -- and then all hell broke loose.
The Mini-Tron flickered to life, showing images – over and over and over, from all angles – of Felicity falling prey to Bull
Johnson’s Gatorsmash Jambalaya. And standing underneath the Mini, laughing, was Bull “Gator” Johnson. The scene switches to Felicity crying in pain, getting booted by Lilliane
– and that’s when our boy Jack snapped. Anderson charged down the aisle, spearing Johnson into the Mini-tron, nearly knocking it over in the process. From there, they rolled across the floor in a wild brawl. Meanwhile, back at the ranch
– er, back in the ring, a two on one situation quickly developed, as Augustus and Beethoven took advantage and dropped Adler with a double clothesline, and then a double suplex.
Then, out of the crowd, a shadowy figure emerged, brandishing a familiar steel pipe. He leapt into the ring, and bent the pipe over the back of Augustus’ head and the crowd popped big-time:
“Sensational” Scott Studd had returned to the Carolinas!! Studd grabbed Augustus by his beautiful long hair, tossing him over the top rope to the floor, where he quickly followed, the beatdown continuing. A loss to VCA at Criminal Intent has cost Studd a 90 day
‘retirement’ – which meant he shouldn’t even be in the building. As security rushed down, he continued to brawl with Augustus at ringside, smashing his knee between the safety rail and that pipe several times, leaving the former champion hobbled.
Anderson and Bull in the aisle, Studd and Augustus at ringside – that left Adler alone with Beethoven in the ring. A quick thinking
Morten Wyngarde took advantage of the understandably distracted referee “Cottonmouth” Joe Lynch to toss Beethoven a chair, which he cracked over Adler’s skull – and pinned him,
Lynch turning around just in time to count to three. For the second week in a row, Casanova Beethoven had pinned the Carolina Heavyweight
Champion.
