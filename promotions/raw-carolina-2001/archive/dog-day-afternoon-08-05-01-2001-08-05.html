Dog Day Afternoon 08.05.01

5 August 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Post Carolina Thunder, RAW Carolina turned to the beach: Wilmington,
North Carolina, for Dog Day Afternoon!! 7000 fans packed the Trask
Coliseum for the big matinee. Results:
CHRISTOPHER SCARLET vs. ROADKILL RICK Many times, two superstars entering a promotion at the same time on opposite sides of the fans' hearts become interminably linked – see
Murdoch and Smythe in RAW, or even Beethoven and Adler here in RAW-
C. And to many observers, Christopher Scarlet and Roadkill Rick are starting to have that feeling. Both men came into this match undefeated – something had to give.
RKR struck the first blow, attacking Scarlet from behind as the opening bell rang. Scarlet fought back quickly, though, and was soon in control in the match-up, displaying the high impact offense that has made him a quick hit with the RAW-C faithful, including a nice slingshot somersault splash that scored a two count.
Roadkill battled back, however, and took control of the match himself after using a headbutt from his VERY thick head to get out of a superplex attempt, sending Scarlet crashing to the mat. RKR climbed down to the middle rope, and hit the youngster with a moonsault for another two count.
After ten minutes or so of solid action, Roadkill caught an unsuspecting Scarlet with a dropkick to the groin. Referee Slick
Wilson admonished RKR for that bit of bad sportsmanship, which the nasty masked Texan didn't take too kindly to. Roadkill turned and got right in Wilson's face, berating the senior official, threatening physical harm. RKR's temper allowed Scarlet a chance to recover, and as Roadkill turned around – he walked right into a Scarletkick for a sudden three count. The crowd exploded, and salivated at the prospect of a rematch between these two, after such a narrow decision.
Winner: Christopher Scarlet (Scarletkick) Time: 10:25 (***) "FAST" EDDIE CUTLASS vs. "ERADICATOR" DIRK ROBERTSON Perhaps no RAW Carolina superstar has come so close to championship gold only to walk away empty-handed more times than Eddie Cutlass. A victory in his match at Dog Day Afternoon with former Television champion Dirk Robertson would leave either man one step closer to yet another shot.
Although Robertson is popular with the RAW-C fans, the Wilmington crowd was solidly behind Cutlass in this contest, as he has become one of their favorite superstars. The action in the match swung back and forth, with a plethora of two counts, working the crowd into a frenzy.
Cutlass had taken control late in the match, and dropped Robertson on the top rope, looking for a superplex. But the Eradicator was having none of it, and caught Cutlass with a lowblow, sending Nice Guy Eddie crashing to the mat. Cutlass got to his feet, and Robertson launched into his Last Sunset flip into a powerbomb – which scored the three count.
Winner: Dirk Robertson (Last Sunset) Time: 11:34 (*** ¼) IKE SAMPSON vs. JESSIE MULDOON After a disappointing return to the Carolinas at Carolina Thunder,
Ike Sampson looked to rebound in a big way as he took on another international superstar, Jessie Muldoon. Big Ike hit the ring to a huge pop from the Wilmington crowd, eager to have an old favorite back in the fold.
Muldoon may have been the only man in the arena not impressed – and he quickly caught Sampson with a kneelift to the midsection before the opening bell. The two burly 300 pounders traded blows toe to toe in the center of the ring, a stand-off won by Sampson. Muldoon rolled to the floor to recuperate, and the crowd went bananas.
Muldoon took an eight count, and then re-entered the ring, re- focused. The two men traded hold for hold in an excellent power match-up, bruising each other for eight hard-hitting minutes.
Muldoon seemed to have the better go of it, scoring two counts several times, including ones with his Chicago Connection chokeslam and his Fall From Humanity front face DDT. Sampson, not to be outdone, scored a handful of two counts, as well, doing so with a spear and then a sidewalk slam.
The crowd groaned as an errant Sampson clothesline felled referee
Jackie Boone, Jr. at the nine minute mark. As Sampson went to check on Boone, Muldoon caught him from behind with a belly to back suplex. He then went into his tights for a roll of quarters, and clocked Sampson with it.
With Sampson unconscious, Muldoon revived Boone, and motioned for him to count. Before Boone's hand could slap the mat a third time, "Thunderbolt" Jack Sampson raced down and pulled Boone out of the ring by his leg – just as Ike got a shoulder up. Boone was enraged, and immediately called for the bell – disqualifying Ike for the interference.
The crowd was enraged. "Thunderbolt" Jack was enraged. And Ike? Well, he wasn't too happy, either – especially with Jack. He shot his brother a look that could melt concrete as he left the ring – but the decision stood.
Winner: Jessie Muldoon (Disqualification) Time: 10:11 (** ½) EL CID © vs. SIDIOUS THE CLOWN, Piedmont Heavyweight Title Perhaps it's only fitting that El Cid's first defense of his newly won Piedmont Title would be against his longtime rival, Sidious the
Clown. Sidious has recently managed to convince himself that not only is Cid responsible for Kewlic's demise, but that he is also responsible for replacing Eddie Cutlass with a clone (?!?). Needless to say, there's more to this story than meets the eye. In Sidious'
defense, Cid has yet to deny either of these allegations, but that may just be more of the mindgames between these two old familiars.
Justified or no, Sidious' new beliefs have finally pushed him over the edge. He came into this title match with a fury that we have yet to see from him in RAW Carolina. Early on, it became apparent that he was out for one thing: Cid's blood. Biting, scratching, anything it took to make Cid bleed.
Cid was surprised by this onslaught, the last thing he expected for the normally goofy Sidious. Grabbing Cid by his hair, Sidious tossed him over the top rope, and then followed him to the floor to deliver a jawbreaker right into the safety railing. A hot dog vendor trolling for customers in the front row lost at least one sale as
Sidious grabbed a dog from him, took a bite, and then tried to stuff it down Cid's throat.
Cid managed to get the match back in the ring, where he was on more even ground. In lieu of Sidious' new attitude, the Piedmont champ chose to keep it on the mat, wearing the Clown down with a Strange
Times rolling leglock, and his Viper Strike dragon sleeper. At every opportunity, Sidious tried to quicken the pace, cartwheeling around the ring into dropkicks, moonsaults, planchas – you name it, Sidious attempted it on the back end of a cartwheel.
The match ended suddenly, as high-paced matches tend to do. Sidious was on the offensive, and rolled into a cartwheel. When he came upright again, Cid caught him with a Running of the Bulls spear that drove Sid's head into the mat. Cid then quickly followed with a
Sword of Spain shooting star press, and it was all over. Cid had the victory and the title, bur Sidious surely earned another shot at the title before it's all said and done.
Winner: El Cid (Sword of Spain) Time: 11:22 (*** ½) The Mini-Tron comes to life, showing RAW-C Champion "Lawbringer" Thomas Adler stretching in his locker room before his match – to a naturally huge pop. His ankle is taped pretty heavily, and he is apparently waiting to lace up the boots `til the last second.
Suddenly, the door bursts open, and Adler catches a chair shot right to the head from a heavily muscled black man, who turns to the camera – Sebastian Sin had returned to RAW.
And where Sin is, you know who won't be far behind – and then Casanova Beethoven came in, clutching a black baseball bat (Mahogany, anyone?!?), which he slams into Adler's taped ankle. Adler muffles a cry of pain, and a TON of security pour into the locker room, pulling the dastardly duo away:
CB: Now lets see you defend that title with a F*bleep*d ankle. You have sinned!! DIREWOLF vs. VINCENT CONSTANTINE AUGUSTUS The next match was nothing if not a contrast in styles, as the regal, elegant Vincent C. Augustus took on the hairiest man in professional wrestling, Television Champion Direwolf in a non-title match-up. VCA made his way to the ring first, to a huge chorus of boos, accompanied as always by his mentor and benefactor, former RAW UK Heavyweight
Champion Morten Wyngarde. VCA called for Bobby Lee Wilcox' mike:
"Well, well, well. It seems as if Mr. Cannon has decided that the TV champion Direwolf is in need of a lesson from a true champion, a champion that does not need a belt. Well, as that man, I, The
Empire Man, the icon who has individually made RAW Carolina the phenomenon that it is, am more than happy to oblige."
The boos rained down from the fans, as VCA stalked the ring like a caged tiger. "Look at me Direwolf. Look at how I hold myself. Look at the poise, the grace, the presence that I possess, and as a consequence, the respect I command. I may not have leather and gold strapped around my waist, but I am still a champion. Direwolf, you can be plated in gold and you'll never be a champion.
"END - OF - STORY." The Television champion arrived next, to an unbelievable crowd pop, his unlikely popularity augmented by his foulmouthed young son Barry, a quick study in working the crowd. Direwolf had once again retained the services of Barry's most successful babysitter to date, the gorgeous Ned, who accompanied the Pintha family to ringside.
The crowd popped again as "Hardcore Sensation" Mark Adams stopped by, with a personalized replica Carolina Tag-Team Title belt for Barry, and a double bird to flip towards VCA and Wyngarde. Ned enjoyed this scene very much – that Adams is a handsome man.
As the match began, Barry began his usual pattern of making a nuisance of himself, trying desperately to get Ned's attention – who was trying even MORE desperately to get VCA's attention. Whether or not it was genuine attraction or a mere ploy, it worked, as VCA was definitely off his game in the early going, allowing Direwolf to take control.
As Direwolf tossed the smaller Augustus around the ring, Ned watched with great interest. Barry began to get upset, and moved over to
Bobby Lee Wilcox' ringside table, and began pouring water on the various cables nearby in an attempt to electrocute the ring announcer – if that wouldn't get Ned's attention, nothing would. Of course, the cables were encased in rubber, so nothing happened, but
Ned still made his way over to scold Barry.
As punishment, Ned forced Barry to sit in his lap, so he could keep one eye on his young charge and the other on the match at the same time. Direwolf noticed this, and didn't like it one bit. He broke off his beating of Augustus to hit the floor and have words with Ned for getting a bit too `familiar' with Barry. Of course, VCA being the conniving opportunist we all know him to be, caught Direwolf from behind during all this with a running knee to the back, sending the
TV champion crashing into the ringpost. Barry didn't like this one bit, and attempted to defend his father by latching onto VCA's leg and biting(!!).
Of course, VCA didn't like THIS, and pulled the youngster off, and raised his hand as if to strike him. Ned, of course, didn't like
THIS, and slapped Augustus across the face for raising his hand to
Barry. Did Augustus like this? Not on your life – he leveled Ned with a STIFF right hand, drawing the ire of the ladies in the crowd;
Ned is gorgeous, after all. Direwolf recovered long enough to get VCA back in the ring, as Barry helped Ned to his feet. Barry was joined in his efforts by several overzealous female fans who leapt the railing to help Ned back towards the locker room (and perhaps further?!?). Barry didn't like
THIS (enough already!!) at all:
"Hey, leddies, I got's needs to...git back heah fo I beatchu!" He proceeded to throw a temper tantrum at being ignored by the ladies, and lay on the floor kicking and screaming until Hounddog
Murphy once again arrived to take him to the back.
Oh, yeah, there was a match?!? Right. VCA took control with his usual mindgames – playing possum, that sort of thing, all the while working on Direwolf's back. Direwolf, on the other hand, attempted to overpower the former Carolina champion, using his size and strength advantage to the fullest.
At the six minute mark, Direwolf attempted a vertical suplex, only to see VCA block it, and counter with a DDT. Augustus rolled Direwolf onto his stomach, then scaled the top rope to come right back off with a double knee drop right into the small of the TV champ's back.
Despite Augustus' underhanded techniques, Direwolf kept the fight clean, wanting to set a good example for his son, and further his case for Father of the Year. He attempted to wear VCA down, and at the eight minute mark, looked to connect with an Alpha Male chokeslam – only to see VCA counter with a judo chop to the throat, followed up with a kick to the groin.
After a couple of softening up suplexes, VCA whipped Direwolf into the ropes and planted him with a flapjack. He then hooked Direwolf in the Empire Clutch submission hold he unveiled on RAW-C TV this week, and cinched it in in the center of the ring.
Augustus leaned into it, really locking it in – and then the bell rang. Referee `Cottonmouth' Joe Lynch looked confused, until Wilcox informed him that the ten minute time limit had expired – partially due to the early match theatrics.
The bell rang again, but VCA wouldn't release the hold. Lynch pleaded with him, but soon found himself shoved aside by Wyngarde, who entered the ring – with a jar of glue.
Opening the jar, Wyngarde proceeded to pour the glue all over the hairy chest of Direwolf, as the more primordial of the males in attendance squirmed in empathic pain. Finally, their dastardly plan complete, VCA released the hold.
Decision: Time Limit Draw Time: 10:00 (**) As VCA and Wyngarde returned to the locker room, gloating about the glue incident, a black baseball bat flashed out of the shadows, laying both men out. The mini-Tron didn't give a clear look at who was swinging the bat, but it did leave both men unconscious, Augustus bleeding from the scalp.
"THUNDERBOLT" JACK SAMPSON vs. STONE The Wilmington crowd was ambivalent at best towards Stone as he made his way to the ring for his big match-up with RAW-C legend Jack
Sampson. Sure, they had all seen him turn on the Empire Club on RAW-
C TV this week, but he had seemingly turned his back on them earlier in the year; couldn't he just as easily do it again?
But the squat brawler went a long way towards alleviating those concerns by wrestling an outstanding, clean, scientific power brawl with Thunderbolt, and by the end, the crowd was behind BOTH men.
Stone was very impressive, tossing the larger Sampson around with a variety of suplexes and slams. Stone even hit Sampson with his own version of the Thunderclap dragon suplex to the tune of a two count.
At the six minute mark, Ike Sampson made his way back out to the ring, sliding under the bottom rope and bringing the match to a standstill. Before anyone knew what was happening, Ike kicked Stone right in the midsection, and then planted him with a Deep Freeze double underhook DDT.
Referee `Cottonmouth' Joe Lynch had no choice but to call for the bell, disqualifying Jack Sampson. The fans were bewildered. They knew Ike could hold a grudge; was he still mad over his loss to Stone at Carolina Thunder? And then they had their answer. Ike grabbed the house mike as he rolled out of the ring:
"That's one back, Jackie. And I still owe you one." Ike Sampson was paying back his brother for costing him two matches in a row. Winner: Stone (Disqualification) Time: 6:42 (**) JACK "THE ANSWER" ANDERSON vs. CASANOVA BEETHOVEN The next match-up promised to be a barnburner, as RAW Carolina's two recently certified psychos – Jack Anderson and Casanova Beethoven -- squared off. Anderson entered the ring first, to a huge ovation from the Wilmington crowd. It's doubtful Jack has ever looked this bad – still wearing the black Creighton jersey and matching tights. He was unshaven, unkempt; eyes black, sunken in, as if he's gotten no sleep since Carolina Thunder. And in his hands – a black Louisville
Slugger. As Anderson awaited the introduction of Beethoven, a weird red mist filled the arena. Then the Mini-Tron came to life, with Casanova
Beethoven standing over a young woman in a red bikini who looked very much like Felicity Vass:
CB: Welcome everyone and young Jack. Look who is my newest ho. Thanks to Bull Johnson for loaning her to me. So, Jack – or is it Tommy? I have done your woman, yes another ho. Cretin family, I have got the one thing that will get you mad as hell.
"FV": Yeah you see Jack, you weren't going anywhere. There is so far a meal ticket can take you. I am with the number one contender for the Raw Carolina title. It makes sense.
CB: Take it easy, Jack. Oh- look behind you – Anderson did – and turned around right into a double superkick from
Beethoven and Sebastian Sin, who had entered the ring as Cas' message played on the Mini-Tron.
Sin rolled out to ringside as the bell rang, and Beethoven took full use of the advantage gained by the double superkick. Beethoven's target seemed to be the head and back of Anderson, as he put Jack through a series of backbreakers, suplexes, etc. Several attempted covers forcing Anderson to kick out further weakened his back, as well.
Despite assists from the floor from Mr. Sin, Anderson battled back, sore back and all – you don't talk about his family's women and not expect to pay for it. The crowd rallied behind him, but rather than the usual high-flying when they do so, Anderson instead took it to the mat, working over Beethoven's arm with a variety of painful looking submissions.
Sin broke up an attempted Anderson pin by pulling him out of the ring by his ankles – to say Jack was pissed was an understatement.
Anderson shoved Sin down to the floor, and then went to his corner for the Louisville Slugger. Anderson swung the bat – really hard – at Sin's head, but he ducked and Jack bashed the ringpost instead.
By now, Beethoven had joined them on the floor, and rammed Anderson face first into that ringpost.
As referee Slick Wilson admonished Sin, Beethoven took full advantage. After shoving Bobby Lee Wilcox out of the way, he hit
Anderson with a double underhook tornado (!!) powerbomb – through the ring announcers table (!!).
Beethoven rolled Anderson back into the ring – and somehow, someway,
Anderson kicked out. If anything, the trip through the table re- energized him, as Anderson caught Beethoven with a decidedly low blow, and then went right back to work on that arm. But now, it seemed that the intent wasn't to earn a submission, but rather a debilitating injury.
Things weren't looking so good for the ho-lover, so Sebastian Sin sprang into action, leaping to the apron for some words with the official. As Sin distracted Wilson, Beethoven reached into his tights for his infamous pair of brass knuckles – and leveled Anderson with them. Beethoven then hooked Anderson, looking for the Symphony – and that's when Thomas Adler hit the ring. Adler grabbed Sin, tossing him off the apron and into the safety railing. He then climbed into the ring, clotheslining Beethoven down, and saving
Anderson from the Symphony. Wilson called for the bell – yet another disqualification. Adler tossed Beethoven into the corner, and continued to stomp away. After a minute or so of this – and the accompanying crowd wackiness –
Anderson joined in. As Anderson wrapped Beethoven's arm around the ringpost, Adler rolled out to the floor and dug around under the ring, returning with Anderson's bat.
The more squeamish fans turned aside, and the rest watched Adler bash that bat off of Beethoven's already gimpy arm – more than once.
Then, again under Adler's direction, Anderson dragged Beethoven to the center of the ring, standing on his wrist with one foot and his shoulder with the other. Adler climbed to the middle rope, and came off with a big stomp to Beethoven's arm, leaving the purple stubbled superstar in agonizing pain. Adler nodded curtly to Anderson, and grabbed the mike:
"Empire Club, I told you the job wasn't done. Here's a small example of why you shouldn't start things you can't finish. You try to take something of mine, I take something of yours. Next time you cross me, I'm going to pay you back tenfold. Now come and collect this piece of garbage. He's starting to stink up the place."
Adler stepped over Beethoven in an attempt to leave the ring – but then thought better of it, giving Beethoven a vicious kick right in the jaw, before spitting on him and FINALLY leaving – to a standing ovation. Impressed, Anderson followed suit, first with the kick, then the expectoration – and another standing O.
Winner: Casanova Beethoven (Disqualification) Time: 8:58 (***) "HARDCORE SENSATION" MARK ADAMS & FLASH © vs. "X-PRESS" DAVID JERICHO & MARC TOWER, Carolina Tag-Team Titles The new tag champions Mark Adams and Flash faced a dangerous – if not exactly on the same page – pair in former Piedmont Champion David
Jericho and Marc Tower in their first big title defense. Given the individual issues that have surfaced in the past few weeks between
Flash and Jericho, as well as similar issues between Adams and Tower, and this one promised to be explosive.
It became obvious early on where the teamwork advantage lay: Adams and Flash have morphed into remarkably cohesive team as of late, and opened the match with a flurry of frequent tags and double-teams that left their opponents staggered and the crowd on their feet. Not only that, they mixed in a little bit of hardcore brawling, a suplex here and there, and even some submission holds – it was impossible to tell where they were coming from next, and the crowd loved it.
Despite the opening flash (*ahem*) by the champions, Tower and Jericho are no slouches. Individually, they showed some flashes of brilliance, but every pinfall attempt was broken up by the outside man, and every attempt at some in-ring continuity was a failure, as well.
A cool spot saw Flash lock Jericho in his own X-Press Way, and as
Tower came in to make the save, Adams lifted him up and planted him with HIS own Tower of Power. After seven minutes of such action, the crowd was surprised to see Windy City RAW superstar Jamal Byers – an associate of Jericho's – make his way to the ringside area. Jericho stood on the apron and nodded to his friend, who proceeded to trip up
Tower in the ring, causing him to stumble right into a Miracle reverse suplex from Flash – and the pin.
Jericho left the ring, disgusted, with Byers – and Tower was alone in the ring, none too happy over what had just transpired.
Winners: Mark Adams & Flash (Miracle) Time: 7:32 (* ½) "LAWBRINGER" THOMAS ADLER © vs. BULL "GATOR" JOHNSON, Carolina Heavyweight Title The Carolina champion hit the ring, confident that with the injuries sustained by Casanova Beethoven, his title defense would be incident- free. Well, as incident-free as a match with the 450 pound Cajun monster Bull Johnson can be. Adler had remarked earlier in the week that Johnson is the most dangerous man he has faced yet in RAW-C – at least physically. And with a tender ankle going in – his title reign could be shortened considerably.
Despite the ankle, Adler still held a speed advantage over the much larger Gator – it's not often that the Lawbringer is smaller AND quicker than an opponent, and Adler took advantage. Adler employed a bit of a hit and run strategy, forcing Johnson to overreach for him, then slip away, and hit him with drop toe holds and the like. Adler even showed some VERY impressive strength, as he sidestepped a charge by Johnson only to plant him with a powerslam for a two count.
This frustrated Johnson to no end – finally, having had enough, he caught Adler with a bulrush into the corner, and took control.
Johnson ignored Adler's injured ankle, choosing instead to employ his very effective high-impact offense, tossing the 300 pound Adler around in a very unfamiliar fashion, with a variety of innovative powerbomb spots.
Johnson shot Adler off the ropes, attempting a big lariat – but Adler ducked underneath, catching Johnson's arm at the same time to whip him into the corner where he crashed headlong into the turnbuckles.
Adler then drug Johnson out of the corner by his arm and slamming into it with an elbow, driving Bull face first into the mat and hyperextending his elbow all at once – perhaps a signal to Casanova
Beethoven?
Adler continued to work on Bull's arm, figuring – rightly so – that he would have a hard time employing his powerbombs with a bad wing.
At the ten minute mark, Adler charged Johnson, looking for a spear – only to see Bull hang on and DDT Adler across his own knee in a Gator
Breaker. Rather than go for the pinfall, Johnson whipped Adler into the corner, and followed up with another bulrush – which Adler sidestepped. Adler followed up with a quick double axehandle to
Johnson's sore elbow, and then a quick to Johnson's knee that sent him to the mat.
Adler then climbed to the top rope, awaiting Bull's struggle to get back to his feet. And as he did so, Adler launched, catching him with a Guillotine clothesline – for the pinfall.
Winner: Thomas Adler (Guillotine) Time: 11:01 (** ¾) ---- © RAW Carolina, MMI. http://www.rawcarolina.cjb.net
