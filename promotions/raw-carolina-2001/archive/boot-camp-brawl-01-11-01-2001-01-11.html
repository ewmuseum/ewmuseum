Boot Camp Brawl 01/11/01

11 January 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
RAW Carolina held its’ highly anticipated Boot Camp Brawl Thursday night before a raucous crowd at Lejuene Field House on Camp Lejuene in
Jacksonville, North Carolina. Results:
“EL GAUPO” RICKY GOMEZ & “MAD DOG” BILLY MANCINI vs. NIGHTMARES Ricky Gomez and Billy Mancini continued their climb up the tag ranks in the opening bout as they faced the young up-and-coming Nightmares (Fear
& Dread). The crowd was solidly behind Gomez and Mancini for this match, and they dominated nearly from start to finish.
After Mancini managed to plant Dread with the Beware of Mad Dog, Gomez came in and finished him off with a Love Potion Number Nine to the crowd’s delight.
Winners: Ricky Gomez & Billy Mancini (Love Potion #9) Time: 4:12 (* ½) JOSHUA STRONG vs. “THE BOSTON MANGLER” ADONIS MORGAN Their troubled team having finally imploded, Napoleon Jones’ two precocious superstars, Joshua Strong and Adonis Morgan, met in a wild brawl. Jones refused to take sides in the match, choosing to sit in a neutral corner while his charges settled their score.
The ring proved to be too small to handle this brawl, as they again battled all over the Fieldhouse. Into the stands, concession stands, locker rooms -— you name the place, Strong and Morgan took their fight there. In the end, the referee had no choice but to call for the bell and signal a double disqualification.
Decision: Double Disqualification Time: 8:03 (** ½) ROBBIE DIAMOND © vs. “TRAINWRECK” CALEB JONES, Piedmont Heavyweight Title The mega-popular young high-flyer Robbie Diamond won the Piedmont
Heavyweight Title last week with a tough victory over Jacob Legacy, but the new champion got no breaks as he faced the 400 pound monster “Trainwreck” Caleb Jones.
Jones dominated the early portions of the match, tossing Diamond about like a lawn dart, including a positively evil press slam from the ring to the floor. The feisty youngster refused to stay down, however, and soon mounted a comeback.
Diamond battled out of a bearhug with a series of nasty forearm smashes, and then proceeded to keep the much bigger Jones off-balance with a series of hit-and-run moves, several of which employed the top rope. His run of offense ended when Jones caught him coming off the top with an awesome sidewalk slam. He immediately covered with all his weight, and the crowd was sure the match was over. But Diamond somehow kicked out again.
Jones was massively upset, and tossed Diamond over the top rope into the guard rail, followed him outside, and then smashed him with a chair, earning him the disqualification. Jones wasn’t finished; he continued to batter Diamond, squashing him repeatedly into the guard rail with injurious intent until Tommy Victory and Goliath made the save.
Winner: Robbie Diamond (Disqualification) Time: *** (10:49) GLADIATORS vs. ISAAC WALKER & HANGMAN Two tag-teams headed in opposite directions met in the next match as the streaking Gladiators (Julius & Claudius) took on the slumping Wild
Bunch team of Isaac Walker & Hangman. It would be a match with two distinct sub-matches within: One when Walker was in the ring, and one with Hangman.
The Gladiators opened up the match a house of fire with their trademark power offense, clearing the ring of the Wild Bunch, delighting the crowd. When Walker re-entered the ring, he immediately took control with a series of crisp suplexes. He tagged in Hangman, and control shifted back to the Gladiators instantaneously.
The Wild Bunch in general and Hangman in particular have been haunted by the mysterious “Lawbringer” Thomas Adler. Each time Walker tagged in Hangman, he seemed very distracted, constantly taking quick looks at the locker room, seemingly waiting for Adler to appear. The Gladiators took advantage of his distraction, pounding him mercilessly. Hangman finally tagged Walker, who tried desperately to even the score. He again took control of the match, before tagging Hangman once again.
A visibly distracted Hangman stumbled into the ring, and was easy prey for a Circus Maximus and the pin. After the bell, a very irate Walker verbally abused Hangman, before leaving him alone in the ring. The ghost of Thomas Adler had cost the Wild Bunch another match, this time without even setting foot in the arena.
Winners: Gladiators (Circus Maximus) Time: 8:38 (** ½) TOMMY VICTORY vs. CASANOVA BEETHOVEN Tommy Victory and Casanova Beethoven didn’t even wait for the ring introductions to end before going after one another. The war between these two has really intensified in the last week, and these match was but another battle in what promises to be a lengthy war.
Beethoven controlled the early going with his power arsenal, including an awesome Cherry Blossom Bomb. He went for the Symphony early on, but
Victory countered with a nice jawbreaker and took control.
A back and forth match continued with both men very evenly matched. Victory’s one advantage, the support of the crowd, was more than countered by Beethoven’s illegal tactics. The end of the match came with both men brawling wildly at ringside, with the official on the outside trying in vain to separate them. Beethoven pulled the ref into a charging Victory, dazing them both. Beethoven acted quickly, and delivered a spine-jarring Symphony to Victory on a steel chair before rolling back into the ring. The ref awoke in time to count to ten, giving Beethoven a countout win.
Winner: Casanova Beethoven (Countout) Time: 11:53 (***) COAST TO COAST © vs. JACOB LEGACY & “TERRIBLE” TOMMY BEDFORD, Carolina Tag-Team Titles “Awesome” Alec Price & “Unbelievable” Jeff Bennett, known collectively as Coast to Coast, defended their Carolina tag-team titles against the unlikely duo of former Piedmont Champion Jacob Legacy & RAW-C’s hired gun “Terrible” Tommy Bedford. Jericho Weaver, Legacy’s manager, had signed an open contract to make his man a champion again. He then went out and got the best partner money could buy: Bedford.
The match was a disaster from the beginning: the Champs’ polished double-team offense completely dominated the challengers, whose unfamiliarity with each other certainly showed. Every time Legacy got anything going with his suplex-based offense, the champs would pull off a double-team move that turned the tide. Every time Bedford brawled his way to an advantage, another double-team.
After ten minutes of fast action, Bedford left the ring, disgusted. A bewildered Legacy was left alone in the ring, unaware that Price was about to hook him in a Tornado Bulldog for the pin.
Winner: Coast to Coast (Tornado Bulldog) Time: 10:41 (***) “LAWBRINGER” THOMAS ADLER vs. TULLY ROEBUCK Thomas Adler was less than pleased with the sneak attack perpetrated on him by the Wild Bunch Tuesday night in Wilmington, and he intended to make someone pay; namely, Tully Roebuck.
Adler launched into Roebuck with awesome intensity; wasting no time with actual wrestling holds, Adler battered Roebuck with punches and kicks in an all-out brawl. Roebuck was shell-shocked, unable to fight back. And that’s when Adler turned it up a notch. He hit Roebuck with a neckbreaker. A piledriver. A reverse neckbreaker. And then an awesome powerbomb that folded Roebuck in half so that his own knees bloodied his nose.
At this point, the rest of the Wild Bunch stormed out to ringside. Adler was prepared for this, too. He picked up Roebuck and tossed him over the top rope javelin-style, sending him crashing into his cronies. The 330 pound Adler followed Roebuck over the top rope with a death-defying plancha, taking out all four men all over again. He grabbed the Walkers by the hair and cracked their heads together.
Hangman was gorilla-pressed onto the ringsteps. Adler then picked up
Roebuck again and tossed him back into the ring.
As Roebuck struggled to his feet, Adler perched on the top rope, waiting to deliver his Guillotine. As he waited, the Walkers struck, shoving him off the top rope to the floor. The ref called for the bell and the disqualification. Adler hit the floor hard, and the Wild Bunch pounced. They stomped away on Adler; Isaac Walker picked up the ringsteps and slammed them on Adler’s head, opening up a nasty cut across his forehead. The brutal beating continued until Goliath arrived and chased the Wild Bunch away.
Winner: Thomas Adler (Disqualification) Time: 2:04 (* ½) ACE MATTHEWS vs. “THUNDERBOLT” JACK SAMPSON vs. “STELLAR” STEVE KELLER
©, Triangle Match for Carolina Heavyweight Title
When Carolina Heavyweight Champion “Stellar” Steve Keller laid out both
Ace Matthews and “Thunderbolt” Jack Sampson at the end of their match last week, he thought he was killing two contending birds with one stone. He certainly had no idea that it would lead to a defense of his
title: against both men in the same match. Sampson entered the ring to an incredible response; the one Matthews received was but a bit louder. Keller entered, arrogant as ever, to predictably huge negative heat. The match was an instant classic.
Matthews and Sampson continued their hostilities from Mountain Mayhem, and both men hate Keller with a passion.
The action was fast and furious; Matthews and Sampson started the match with a series of double-team moves on Keller, but soon turned their attention to each other. Keller sat in the corner and watched the two fan favorites pound each other, and snuck in to cover Matthews for a two count after Sampson hit him with a running powerslam.
The match continued with all three men dominating equal spells of the contest: Matthews with high-flying, Sampson with power, and Keller with matwork, cunning, and guile.
At the eighteen minute mark, Matthews lay in the corner, stunned by a
Keller corkscrew suplex. Sampson and Keller locked up in the center of the ring, and Sampson used a waistlock to get Keller in his finishing
Full-Nelson suplex, the Thunderclap. The fans counted aloud to three, but Matthews broke up the count before the referee could. Incensed,
Sampson grabbed Keller by the hair and tossed him over the top rope.
At that very moment, Matthews grabbed Sampson from behind in a roll- up. The ref counted. . .to three. Ace Matthews had finally recaptured the Carolina Heavyweight Title.
Keller sat on the floor outside the ring in stunned disbelief as Matthews celebrated with the fans in the ring. He had lost his title to Matthews without losing the match to him. The man who could not pin him had won his title – without pinning him.
