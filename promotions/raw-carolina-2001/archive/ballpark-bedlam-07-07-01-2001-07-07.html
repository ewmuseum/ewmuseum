RAW Carolina — Card Archive

Ballpark Bedlam 07/07/01

7 July 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
RAW Carolina headed for one of the most gorgeous ballparks in all of minor league baseball, the Durham Balls Athletic Park, for Ballpark
Bedlam Saturday night. Results:
Before the first match got underway, the Piedmont Champion “X-Press” David Jericho made his way down to ringside to a huge chorus of boos. Carrying a small desk and folding chair, Jericho proceeded to set up shop beside the timekeeper’s table, producing a notebook and pen, as well. Intrepid RAW-C roving reporter
Vernon St. Jean came over, looking to find out “just what the hell Jericho thought he was doing”:
DJ: “Simple, Vern. I’m taking notes on all the matches with all these great Piedmont Title contenders, to help me with my match later on tonight.”
To say that the crowd felt Jericho was disingenuous would be an understatement. “ICEMAN” MARK ADAMS vs. STONE The opening contest promised to be a good one, as old familiars squared off as Mark Adams met the Empire Club’s
‘muscle’, Stone. Adams was accompanied by his regular tag-team partner, Flash, who promised referee Slick Wilson he wouldn’t get involved in the match, and was only there to cheer his partner on. The fans were nervous, given the additional ringside presence of David Jericho – but it was all for naught. Jericho proved to be an even worse student than fans would have thought
– dozing off at his desk less than five minutes into the match!! All week long, Adams had been promising a return to his hardcore ways in this match, saying he could care less about winning or losing, as long as he beat Stone within an inch of his life. But that couldn’t be further from the truth.
Adams took a completely opposite tact, throwing every suplex in the book at Stone, looking to keep him off balance. Adams’ focal point was the legs of Stone, as he used the suplexes to get Stone on his back, and then came back with figure fours, deathlocks, boston crabs and the like, looking to take out the squat superstar’s vertical power base.
Despite a reputation – well-deserved, I might add – as a prolific brawler, Stone is no stranger to mat wrestling, and countered
Adams several times with suplexes of his own. The match waged back and forth for some six minutes, neither man able to gain a firm advantage.
At that point, Stone reversed a suplex, sending Adams crashing down with a nice German variation. Moving quickly, Stone scooped Adams up, and hit him with a quick Rolling Stone, and it looked to be over.
However, before Stone could make the pin, the mini-tron flickered to life, as Flash cackled with glee at ringside:
At long last, the promised SWF footage rolled, consisting of only one spot -- Stone's partner, Cameron Steele, is shown climbing to the top turnbuckle. Stone whips his opponent into the ropes which knocks
Steele off balance and causes him to crotch himself on the top turnbuckle. Meanwhile Stone is rolled up and pinned by his opponent.
The mini-tron flickered back to black, and Stone was furious in the ring. He stared at Flash out on the floor, pointing, using some very choice blue language to threaten Bodzilla – when out of nowhere,
Adams grabbed Stone, schoolboying him with a healthy handful of tights for the three count. The huge pop from the crowd startled Jericho, who awoke from his ringside slumber long enough to feign jotting down some notes, looking suitably impressed.
Winner: Mark Adams (Schoolboy) Time: 7:12 (** ½) Eagle-eyed fans who follow international wrestling noticed hot young superstar Johnny Troisi sitting in the front row, clad in a curious
BAW t-shirt. Ring announcer Bobby Lee Wilcox took special care, though, to point out the young men sitting beside him, Suplex Academy students Steve Fury, Brimstone, and Isaiah Walker, who all got a polite round of applause.
“LAWBRINGER” THOMAS ADLER vs. CASANOVA BEETHOVEN The first bout in RAW-C President “Showboat” Eddie Cannon’s protracted title shot solution saw two RAW Carolina mainstays, Thomas Adler and Casanova Beethoven, face off. These two have been mainstays in the promotion since the switchover to RAW back in November, so it is surprising that this is the first time they have met in any sort of meaningful one on one contest.
Regardless of the outcome of this match, Adler was all set to receive a title shot in the main event – but that didn’t mean he was taking it easy here. In fact, he looked to make an example out of
Beethoven. Adler has always been one for right over wrong, and he takes a dim view over the way Beethoven deals with the fairer sex, and looked to make Beethoven pay for it.
The early going of the match saw Adler in firm control, keeping the contest down on the mat, wearing Beethoven down with some grueling mat work, using his size and strength advantage to grind Beethoven down with rear chinlocks, arm bars, and the like.
Just as the crowd started to get restless – this isn’t the match they’d paid to see, and you know Jericho nodded off again
– Adler turned it up a notch. He elbowed Beethoven in the back of the neck (yee-owch!!), then grabbed him in a good ol-fashioned claw hold. For those of you younger than a certain age, a claw hold is. .
.never mind. Anyway, he lifted Beethoven up off the mat, charged the corner, dropping to his knees as he drove the back of the ho-loving superstar’s head right into the turnbuckle.
After enduring some serious punishment early on, Beethoven utilized a standing switch to reverse an Adler suplex into a belly to back of his own, and went to work on the veteran fan favorite. Beethoven showed little ring rust after his self-imposed month-long exile; in fact, his moves seemed crisper than ever, scoring several two counts, including a nice t-bone suplex and a devastating cherry blossom bomb.
The match teetered back and forth, drawing the crowd into this confrontation between one of their favorites and one of their
“unfavorites”. No one was surprised when, at the ten minute mark, Empire Club head Morten Wyngarde made his way to ringside with bodyguard Jack Hammer. As Adler and Beethoven traded blows in the center of the ring, Hammer leapt to the apron. As referee Jackie
Boone, Jr., moved over to get him down, Adler seized the opportunity to catch Beethoven with a low blow – and then press slamming him out of the ring onto Wyngarde on the floor.
He then moved over to Hammer, and a nice clothesline sent him spilling to the floor, as well. Beethoven crawled back into the ring gingerly, and was met by a kneelift from Adler, who quickly scooped Beethoven up into a brainbuster – only to see Wyngarde trip him up from the floor, sending Beethoven crashing down on top of him – for a veeeeeery long two count.
Both men got to their feet, and Adler was met with an eye gauge – and then, quick as lightning, Beethoven lifted him and sent him crashing back down to the mat with a Symphony, his full nelson into a tombstone piledriver. With Wyngarde once again grabbing
Adler’s leg for good measure, Beethoven covered – and scored the three count. Winner: Casanova Beethoven (Symphony) Time: 15:12 (*** ½) Postmatch, the entire Empire Club joined a gloating Beethoven in the ring, and proceeded to deliver a five on one beatdown, with VCA the most aggressive, looking to soften Adler even more before the main event. Augustus waffled Adler with a chair a few times, but then the calvary arrived – Jack Anderson. “Thunderbolt” Jack
Sampson. Kirk Maclean. Mark Adams. Eddie Cutlass. The fan favorites cleared the ring to a big ovation, and Anderson seemed particularly pleased. Perhaps his call to action a few weeks ago had not gone unnoticed by RAW-C’s fan favorites after all?!?
VINCENT CONSTANTINE AUGUSTUS © vs. “HURRICANE” KIRK MACLEAN, Carolina Heavyweight Title Still buzzing after the unexpected ending to the previous match, the crowd settled back in for a match they had long anticipated: Kirk
Maclean’s first – and seemingly only – shot at
Vincent C. Augustus’ Carolina Heavyweight Title. The match began with a standard collar and elbow tie-up, at which point VCA simply rolled out of the ring, grabbed his title belt off
Bobby Lee Wilcox’ table, and headed down the aisle, back towards the locker room. The crowd booed heavily – would this vile bastard rob them of another title match? Official
“Cottonmouth” Joe Lynch, sighed resignedly, no choice but to lay the ten count on the champ, over Maclean’s protestations.
Even a drowsy David Jericho looked on with interest. At that point,
RAW Carolina President “Showboat” Eddie Cannon made his first appearance of the night, microphone in hand:
”Listen up, champ. This is how it’s gonna go: You’re going to wrestle tonight. I’m not going to threaten you with a fine. I’m not going to threaten you with a suspension. You don’t get back in that ring – I’ll beat you within an inch of your life, and then I’ll roll you back in that ring. And if you – and your little lackies in the
Empire Club – don’t like it – well, you can take it up with them – “ With that, Cannon was joined in the aisle by Jack Anderson. “Iceman” Mark Adams. Flash. “Fast” Eddie Cutlass. “Thunderbolt” Jack Sampson. And “Lawbringer” Thomas Adler. “This match is gonna happen. And it’s gonna happen tonight.” The crowd roared as Augustus beat a retreat back to the ring, and
Cannon’s troops assumed their positions in the aisle. As Cannon exited, he noticed Troisi in the front row – and seemed none too happy about it. The Empire Club did make their way out to ringside, as well – Wyngarde, Hammer, Beethoven, Stone – and the
Mexican standoff outside the ring only added to the drama inside of it. As if it needed it. The match itself was a classic. Maclean controlled the early going with his high-impact, high-flying offense, scoring several two counts, including a two and nine-tenths after a textbook reverse Gory Special suplex.
We’ve all seen, however, that no one in the Carolinas knows as many suplexes as VCA, a fact he reinforced tonight in Durham. He used all the old favorites, and even broke out a few new ones, including a stiff straightjacket suplex that nearly ended the contest.
Throughout the match, Augustus went to his last trump card, continually putting his hands on Lynch, shoving him, grabbing his shirt – desperately trying to earn a disqualification. But
Lynch was wise to his act, and wouldn’t comply. VCA went so far as to punch Lynch, who rolled with the blow, then simply stood back up and punched Augustus right in the mouth, drawing a huge cheer from the crowd, which got even louder when Maclean hurricanraned the stunned champion for a near fall.
Back and forth the action went; as Maclean neared victory, Augustus would employ a patented cheapshot to turn the tide back his way
– thumb to the eye, punch to the throat, that sort of thing. And just as it seemed he would put Maclean away, the crowd would rally behind Hurricane, and he’d stage yet another comeback.
Finally, after nearly twenty-five(!!) minutes of action, Augustus went for his Lockdown la majistral cradle – only to see Maclean roll through into a small package, and one handful of tights later, a three count, and a new Carolina Heavyweight Champion. The crowd erupted as
Maclean held the title high overhead in celebration. All of his allies at ringside spilled into the ring to join the celebration
– even Adler, who offered a simple smile, and a “See ya in a bit.”
Winner (and new champion): Kirk Maclean (Small Package) Time: 24:41 (****) MARC TOWER vs. ALEX PATTERSON The arrival of his old fried Marc Tower has certainly thrown Dirk
Robertson’s life for a loop; Tower’s interference in the
Television Title tournament final didn’t sit well with his old friend, either – nor did it sit well with Alex Patterson, one of the men who was cost the title by Tower.
As David Jericho continued to catch his forty winks, Tower squared off with Patterson in a match-up of two skilled ring technicians.
Although neither man is overly large, both possess deceptive power which they show off in their impressive arrays of power moves.
Patterson came out both guns blazing in the early going, looking to pay Tower back for costing him the TV Title. As his wife Suzanne looked on at ringside, Patterson worked over Tower’s lower back, setting him up for the devastating RAW Bomb. Tower battled back, and began his own assault on Patterson’s lower back, setting up the
Tower of Power. The arrival of Dirk Robertson at ringside gave the fans a definite rooting interest, as they didn’t particularly care for either competitor in the actual match. Robertson just stood stoically at ringside, not wanting to get involved on behalf of either man.
Robertson’s arrival did seem to light a fire under Tower, who fought with an increased fervor once his old friend was sighted. He powered out of a Patterson boston crab, and a reversed suplex later, caught Patterson with a stiff, stiff Tower of Power, which scored the pin. Tower quickly exited the ring, and began jawing with Robertson in the aisle, until the Television champion turned and walked away.
Winner: Marc Tower (Tower of Power) Time: 4:42 (**) Before the next match began, Flash made his way back out to ringside, putting his finger to his lips, urging the crowd to stay quiet as he approached a sleeping David Jericho – and kicked his chair out from under him. As the crowd roared – and Jericho fumed –
Flash rolled him into the ring and delivered a quick Miracle reverse suplex. El Cid then dashed out of the locker room, already in his custom-made referee shirt, and cut off the attack, forcing Flash out of the ring.
As Jericho cleared the cobwebs, he rolled out of the ring – only to be stopped in his tracks by a (familiar?!?) voice:
“Oh, Davvvvvvvvvvvvvvid. . . . . . . .” Seemingly upset by this, Jericho stormed off to the locker room. As he made his way down the aisle, the mini-tron flickered back to life, showing Flash once again, this time talking with Sidious the Clown:
FLASH: “I’m tellin’ ya, Sid, I talked with our old pal “Deadhead” Jerry Weir last week, and he told me that he saw Jessie Muldoon with Kewlic the night he disappeared.”
SIDIOUS: Oh, yeah? FLASH: Yeah. The mini-tron faded to cotton candy. SIDIOUS THE CLOWN vs. JESSIE MULDOON The next match featured two of the more ‘unique’ stars in RAW Carolina, as “The Only Male That Counts” Jessie Muldoon squared off against the bizarre, inimitable Sidious the Clown. The ubiquitous – tonight, at least – Flash accompanied Sidious to the ring. Before the match started, he huddled with Sid in the corner, exhorting him to do something. Finally Sid came out of the corner, reached into his tights – and produced The List. He screamed at Muldoon “Where were you?” Muldoon, of course, denied any involvement in Kewlic’s demise, prompting Flash to yell at Sidious, “Well if he won’t give you an honest answer. . .beat it out of ‘em!”
Sidious attempted to do just that, charging the 300 pounder – only to charge right into a kick to the ‘lower groin’, followed by a quick DDT.
Muldoon followed that up with a quick Love Muscle clothesline, and almost had a sudden three count. Sidious tried to mount some offense, but was cut off at every pass by an inspired Muldoon, who seemed to have a counter for everything.
Muldoon began to wear Sidious down, utilizing a nice arm drag from the top rope – a throwback to his Doomsday days – and a sleeper hold that very nearly sent Sidious under. He then took a page from Chris Benoit’s book, hitting the smaller clown with three consecutive German Suplexes, the final with a bridged pin attempt for another near fall.
Sidious battled back, ducking under an attempted Love Muscle, and bouncing back with a nice leg lariat. He then went into his cartwheeling offense – here a cartwheel into an elbow smash, there a cartwheel into a body splash. But Muldoon was more than ready for this, too – Sidious went for an Asai Moonsault, only to be deflected by Muldoon into referee Jackie Boone, Jr.
As Boone lay helpless, Muldoon rolled outside the ring, hit a surprised Flash with a DDT, and returned with a steel chair, which he proceeded to piledrive Sidious on top of it. From there, the match was over for all intents and purposes, but Muldoon continued to batter
Sidious once Boone awoke, including a nice Tornado DDT.
The end of the match saw Muldoon shoot Sidious into the ropes, and as he rebounded, blast him right in the greasepaint with a roll of coins, leveling Sid. Muldoon then pulled him and planted him one more time with a Cradle Suplex, and with a heaping handful of tights for a completely necessary good measure, scored the pin.
Flash charged the ring, helpfully pointing out the dimes littered all over the canvas to Boone, but the decision stood. Flash turned towards Muldoon – and caught a roll of quarters (y’know, cause he’s bigger) for his troubles. Muldoon then pulled up
Sidious and delivered a stiff powerbomb. A second followed, and then a third, before the Chicago monster finally left the ring to a chorus of boos.
Winner: Jessie Muldoon (Coin Operated Cradle Suplex) Time: 6:11 (**) The mini-tron then flickered to life once again, showing Thomas Adler lacing up his boots in the locker room, preparing for his title shot in the main event. Suddenly, from nowhere, the deposed champion VCA attacked, blasting Adler with his Billion Dollar Knuckles, getting in a few shots before Jack Anderson and Jack Sampson chased him away.
DIREWOLF vs. BULL “GATOR” JOHNSON The bizarre man-beast Direwolf has been on quite a roll since entering
RAW Carolina, and has earned his first title shot later this week on
RAW-C TV. In fact, the only blemish on his record thus far came at the hands of the Cajun crusher Bull Johnson, so it was with this in mind that he came into Ballpark Bedlam looking to even the score.
Johnson was accompanied, as always, by the cycloptic Amazon Lilliane
Devereaux, uniquely ravishing as ever. Direwolf was accompanied, as always, by his foul-mouthed son Barry, who, as ever, brought his mouth that only a sailor’s mother could love. Barry spent most of the match hitting on Lilliane in typical fashion, tossing his mahogany blocks bee-atch at her when she spurned him.
As always, the distraction of his son at ringside cost Direwolf considerable pain, as he continually looked to his son only to get clobbered by Johnson. The big Gator battered Direwolf severely, utilizing his innovative powerbomb variations, scoring several two counts.
Direwolf managed to dish out his share of punishment, as well, mixing in some wicked headbutts with an incredible German Suplex on the 450 pounder that nearly killed both men.
After eight or so minutes of hard-hitting action, Direwolf attempted a foolhardy piledriver on Johnson – only to see the big Bull collapse on top of him, earning a two count. Johnson pulled Direwolf up and attempted a short arm clothesline – only to see Direwolf duck, and go for his Alpha Male Chokeslam. Direwolf got Johnson a full two inches off the mat before his arm gave out, and Johnson then delivered that clothesline he was looking for earlier.
Johnson then headed for the second rope, looking for a big splash to end it – but he missed, as Direwolf rolled out of the way. The president of the chest-hair club for men then made his way to the second rope, and connected with a flying headbutt, which scored the three count to end an exciting match.
Winner: Direwolf (Flying Headbutt) Time: 9:02 (** ½) Before the Piedmont Title match got underway, Eddie Cannon returned to ringside with a bevy of the DPD’s finest, and walked over to where Johnny Troisi sat. A heated argument began, and Cannon shoved
Troisi. Troisi responded by sucker punching Cannon, and was quickly grabbed by the security and escorted out of the stadium. The crowd was extremely confused; what the hell was that all about?!?

Flash: "Durham, North Cackalacky..." [Pause for the cheers.] Flash: "Never fear, the next Piedmont Champion is here!" [The King of the Impossible grins as he stares forward at a seemingly annoyed David Jericho. The crowd is behind Flash all the way.]

As Jericho continued to work over Flash, Mark Adams wandered out to ringside, wielding a baseball bat, eager to ward off any unwanted interference. Jericho pounded away at Flash with a couple of nice suplexes, and very early on locked Flash in the X-Press Way, looking for the submission victory. Cid got down in position, raising

“break his damn leg”. Finally, Mark Adams had seen enough, and reached in the ring, pulling Cid out. Jericho floated on top of Flash to make the cover – And Cid counted. One. . . . .Two. . . . . . . . . . . . . . .but he couldn’t quite bring himself to count to three – and then the bell rang. Everyone in the ring looked around in confusion
– to find “Showboat” Eddie Cannon standing in the aisle with a microphone: “This whole farce has been a debacle from the start! If you think I’d let any decision in this match stand, you must be smoking some stronger stuff than I am. So let’s call the whole thing off. This match is over – Get the hell outta my ring!!”
Official duties officially relieved, Cid opened up on Jericho, hitting him with a dropkick right in the mouth, and then climbing the top rope to deliver an awe-inspiring Sword of Spain shooting star press. With that, he gave Jericho a kick in the head for good measure, and left the ring to a mixed reaction.
Decision: No Contest Time: 6:23 (*) “FAST” EDDIE CUTLASS vs. THE JUDGE After taking last weekend’s house shows off as he recovered from a recent series of concussions, Eddie Cutlass returned to action to take on one of the men responsible for his most recent injury, The
Judge.
These two men are no strangers to each other, having met many times before in other promotions before heading to RAW Carolina, and showed it by constantly countering and even counter-countering each other’s arsenal, providing for a very evenly contested match-up.
Having The Bailiff on the outside is always an advantage for the demented Judge, and this match was no exception. Several times he was able to steal the advantage from Cutlass as the Bailiff leapt to the apron to occupy official Jackie Boone, Jr., allowing the Judge to use some decidedly illegal tactics to take control.
After eight minutes of back and forth action, the crowd gasped in horror as The Judge lifted Cutlass for a powerbomb – just the kind of move that could concuss his brain once more, which they knew could be the last. But their fears were unfounded, as Cutlass was able to maneuver out of the attempt by flipping up and over the Judge, rolling him into a sunset flip that scored a quick three. Fast Eddie had struck again.
Winner: Eddie Cutlass (sunset flip) Time: 8:09 (** ¼) Before the final two title matches of the evening, intrepid reporter
Vernon St. Jean climbed into the ring, microphone in hand, and introduced his special guest for the evening: a top contender for the
Carolina title, the Crimson Spitfire, Jack Anderson!!
The crowd popped big-time for one of their absolute favorites, who made his way to the ring with a goofy grin on his face – only to be ambushed in the aisle by the Empire Club!! With Morten Wyngarde and Jack Hammer leading the way, they savagely beat Anderson in the aisle, stomping away. Vincent C. Augustus, still outraged at losing the title earlier in the evening, joined them, bouncing
Anderson’s face off the safety railing repeatedly. Someone was going to pay for his title loss – he didn’t care who.
Finally, help arrived in the form of a chair-wielding Thomas Adler and
“Thunderbolt” Jack Sampson, who put an end to the dastardly attack by chasing the Empire Club into the crowd, and presumably out of the building.
WILD STYLE © vs. TOMMY TRENTON & EZ KILLER, Carolina Tag-Team Titles Since establishing themselves as top contenders to the tag titles, young rookies Tommy Trenton and EZ Killer have fallen on hard times, with inconsistent ring work and poor communication leading to several crushing losses. Perhaps inexplicably, the championship committee awarded them one last title shot, against the new champions Wild Style
(Drew Brody & Shannon Hamilton).
The champs dominated the early going with their signature high-flying double-teams, including a beautiful double springboard DDT that got the crowd on its’ feet, and a backbreaker – somersault legdrop combo that did likewise. A missed Firebird Splash by
Hamilton, though, turned the tide, allowing Trenton to tag in Killer. The big man made an immediate impact on the match, hitting Hamilton with a float kick that nearly decapitated him. He followed that up with his Death Blow modified DDT, which very nearly scored his team the titles, only to see Brody break up the pin.
But eventually, the champions’ crisp teamwork combined with the challengers’ youthful mistakes was too much to overcome.
Trenton misfired on a suplex, allowing Brody to land on his feet and quickly counter with the Evolution, his version of the Vertaebreaker.
Killer came in to make the save, but Brody rolled out of the way, and
Killer legdropped Trenton instead. Brody dropkicked Killer out of the ring, and Hamilton came off the top with a Given to Fly corkscrew leg drop that put Trenton away for the three count.
As the champs left the ring, Killer re-entered, only to be shoved down to the mat by Trenton. He got up, shoved Trenton, and the brawl was on. A host of officials came out to break up the skirmish, but it appears we’ve seen the last of Tommy Trenton and EZ Killer as a team.
Winners: Wild Style (Given To Fly) Time: 6:34 (**) “HURRICANE” KIRK MACLEAN © vs. “LAWBRINGER” THOMAS ADLER, Carolina Heavyweight Title After a long, wild night of action, the crowd settled in for one last contest: Two of their all-time favorites squaring off for the richest prize in the gam – er, Carolinas. The crowd seemed pretty evenly split as far as who they were rooting for, but firmly united in their desire to see another classic match – one that would both begin and end with a handshake.
It started off to their pleasure, as Adler offered his hand to Maclean after the opening bell, and the new champion took it. But the friendliness ended there, as Adler immediately went after
Maclean’s knees, knowing that Maclean’s biggest advantage was his high-flying, which he could counteract severely by damaging a knee.
To that end, Adler started the match with a bang. As Maclean charged him off the ropes, Adler catches him with a stiff kick to the kneecap, both damaging Maclean’s leg and sending him spilling to the floor. Adler then grabbed the top rope, slingshotting himself out to the floor with an elbow drop across Maclean’s knee.
With Adler’s gameplan limiting his high-flying, Maclean battled back with a bit of mat wrestling, surprisingly successful considering he was giving up over a hundred pounds to Adler. He used arm drags and leg throws – with his good leg – to keep Adler off-balance, and mixed in a nice flying somersault DDT to good effect.
The match was shaping up nicely, every bit the classic everyone hoped it would be. Adler continued to work Maclean’s knee, and the champ worked hard on the mat, keeping Adler on his back, and his knee out of harm’s way.
After about fifteen minutes of action, Adler seemed more fatigued than
Maclean, his bruising, high-impact offense seemingly having taken more out of him than Maclean’s matgame had. An uncharacteristic mistake by Adler allowed Maclean to hit him with an impossible wheelbarrow suplex – and head to the top rope.
Maclean was poised on the top, looking to deliver a high-impact death knell on the Lawbringer. But Adler got to his feet – cat-like in his quickness – and before Maclean knew what was going on,
Adler was on the top turnbuckle with him. Adler grabbed that injured knee, and tossed Maclean to the mat with a dragon screw legwhip that looked very, very painful.
Maclean writhed on the mat in pain – even more so when Adler stepped in with a single leg Boston Crab, looking for the submission.
Adler leaned in, really putting the pressure on Maclean’s knee – and he finally tapped. Whether it was from the pain, or concern for the long run – it’d be tough to start his RAW career off with a ripped up knee – we’ll never know, but
Adler earned the submission from Maclean. As the bell rung, Adler released the hold immediately, helping Maclean to his feet – and the two men shook hands again. Maclean took the title belt from referee “Cottonmouth” Joe Lynch and handed it to Adler, raising his arm in victory for all to see.
