Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

OWE opened on 20 October 2002 out of a wrestling school and office complex it billed in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, under an in-fiction owner who had called in an old friend to run the wrestling side. Its own handbook promised an international federation, coming to a different city, sometimes a different country, every week for its Sunday Night Impact broadcast, plus a Friday-night talk show and a pay-per-view every other month.

The debut card filled the First Union Center and closed on a three-way main event that set the promotion's tone: an interfering handler, a stolen pipe shot and a beatdown that introduced two more arrivals before the credits rolled. Week to week, OWE kept adding faces this way, a debut interrupting the show before it, a rivalry announced live rather than on paper — a feud between two of its newer wrestlers was still raw enough in its third week that the promotion's own commissioner had to settle it by booking a pay-per-view match on the spot.

Its first Television Championship went to the last man standing in an eleven-wrestler battle royal at its second broadcast, in Boston. Its World Heavyweight title sat vacant until the promotion's own first pay-per-view, Liberty Or Death, where three qualifying matches — one of them decided by a fast count from a referee its own commissioner had planted — fed a ladder match that the promotion's newest headline act came out on top of.

A voice threatened that new champion from off camera on his way out the door one week earlier, promising an old score from four years back. Whether it was ever paid is not in this record.

Storylines

The vacant titles Written by AI, 2026

OWE opened with two championships on its own books and neither one held. The Television title went first, decided at the promotion's second broadcast in an eleven-wrestler battle royal that came down to Sabbath and Jesse Madrid; Sabbath won it with a side kick. He held it twenty-eight days, losing it in his first defense, at the promotion's own first pay-per-view, to Vincenzo Sandora's Fahgettaboutit DDT.

The World Heavyweight title took longer. Three matches at that same pay-per-view fed a ladder match for it: Johnny Glamour outlasted Jeff Keenan in a Hell in a Cell bout whose first pinfall was overturned for happening outside the cell; Setzer Van Strife beat Brian Floyd in a Dueling Canes match on a fast count from a referee the promotion's own commissioner, Calvin Peete, had personally installed; and Brian J. Blottie survived a brutal chair-assisted rematch with Doyle Woodall, a feud that had already cost both men their own OWE debuts three weeks earlier. In the ladder match itself, Van Strife pulled down the belt over Glamour and Blottie to become the promotion's first World Heavyweight Champion.

The Woodall/Blottie feud Written by AI, 2026

Doyle Woodall and Brian J. Blottie cost each other their own debut matches in the promotion's first two weeks — Woodall's arrival distracted Blottie out of the three-way main event on opening night, and Blottie returned the favor against Keenan the next week. Commissioner Calvin Peete booked the two men against each other at the first pay-per-view after their own broadcast match ended in a double count-out and a brawl through the crowd.

The stalker Written by AI, 2026

After beating Anthony Virtue in his OWE return, Setzer Van Strife was addressed over the arena's own PA system by an unidentified voice, promising a visit from someone Van Strife believed he had "done away with" four years earlier. The thread is not resolved in the promotion's own surviving broadcasts.
