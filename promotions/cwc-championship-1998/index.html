Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Championship Wrestling Coalition introduced itself on 30 March 1998 with a staged press conference: president Jean Watts, flanked by a silent four-man board, promised “the greatest E-federation ever, bar none” on a roster of exactly three wrestlers — The Backbreaker, “Naturally Gifted” Peter Hand and Zero “El Niño” Gonzalez — while two out-of-work wrestlers, Matt Randall and “Sweet Daddy” Al Diamond, crashed the podium looking for a broadcasting job. Watts announced weekly Sunday cards starting 3 May, a first pay-per-view planned for early June, and three titles to come: a World belt, a second singles title still unnamed, and a tag title.

Its weekly Sunday Night Madhouse carried a World Heavyweight Championship tournament from its own first edition, with a masked wrestler, Leviathan, and Paul Kingstead both advancing through early rounds alongside Havoc and Crimestarter Jim.

Neither the World Heavyweight nor the newly announced International People's Championship tournament survives to its own finish: the fed's own First Night pay-per-view, where both were scheduled to conclude, does not survive in the record. Leviathan is already champion by the next surviving broadcast, and Jose Garces is billed champion from early June, with no match for either title preserved.

Its only surviving pay-per-view, Prime Time in North America, ran 21 July 1998 and crowned two more champions on-page: Paul Kingstead won a four-way elimination match over Frank Claiborne, Lord Keeblor and Nekrataal to become the first North American Heavyweight Champion, and the L.A. Wrecking Crew beat the Hardcore Icons for the vacant World Tag Team Championship.

No closure notice of its own survives. A rival promotion's own broadcast that August reported that the Coalition's general manager had shut it down, and that a wave of its wrestlers, the L.A. Wrecking Crew among them, had joined that promotion's roster afterward.
