Acknowledgements 1. What is IICW? 1.1 Kayfabe IICW stands for International Internet Championship Wrestling, a wrestling league based out of Portland, Oregon. The IICW prides itself on providing family entertainment. Emphasis is on clean wrestling, as
Weekend War focuses not on insane gimmick matches, but showcasing the true talents of the wrestlers involved. IICW is not hardcore, is not extreme, is not any of those other catch phrases. It's simply a place where wrestlers wrestle.
1.2 Non-kayfabe version IICW is a fantasy wrestling league, one of many on the internet. It began in October of 1998, when [name withheld] decided to open a fed where old school and fun had meaning.
The goal of IICW can be summed up quite easily in one word: fun. Many of us devote endless hours to this game, which is why it simply has to be fun. If you don't enjoy writing flashes, reading cards, or whatever, please don't do it.
We try to provide handlers full control over their characters, allowing them to guide them wherever. To do this, however, you'll need to provide us the flashes, the angles and to some extent, the strats. We try and use all of them, but keep in mind we're dealing with a large group of ideas. Don't get bent out of shape if something doesn't go your way once and a while.
2. Who runs IICW? Only one man runs this whole promotion. [name withheld] handles the main running of the league, those include booking duties, writting, webmaster, etc.. (and it ain't easy!)
3. What shows does IICW run? 3.1 Weekend War When: Weekends Hosted by: Tim Cross and Jerry Leelan Where: IICW Coliseum in Portland, Oregon What: Weekend War is IICW's big show of the week. It consists of five (usually) live matches, written in long summary format with the main event match (usually) written in play-by-play format.
Interviews to further angles also appear here. In short, this is _the_ show in IICW. Matches, interviews, angles, everything goes down on MWeekend War. 3.2 Title Talk When: Every two days or so Hosted by: nobody Where: N/A What: Flash packets where the wrestlers talk the talk. These are usualy made for character developement and trash talking.
3.3 IICW Update When: Whenever needed Hosted by: Nobody Where: N/A What: Not a show at all, simply an update on where things stand. Upcoming cards or any information are sent out by the prez. 3.4 Pay Per View When: Various times in the year Hosted by: Tim Cross and Jerry Leelan Where: various locations What: Pay Per Views are IICW's biggest shows. All the feuds die down, new feuds light up, surprises show up, everything happens here in an action packed time. Currently, there are four per year:
IICW New Year's Bash (NYB) - (Royal Rumble type, held in January) IICW Wrestlefest (WF) - (The big daddy, held in April) IICW Summer Splash (SS) - (Lethal Lottery type, held in July) IICW Gold Rush (GR) - (A normal PPV card) 4. Who are those guys in the shows? 4.1 Tim Cross IICW's face broadcaster, Tim Cross always sticks to providing the action. Tim always makes sure the shows stay on the right path, be it pleasing everybody, not offending anybody, or whatever.
4.2 Jerry "The Feen" Leelan IICW's heel broadcaster, Leelan always tries to get the snide remarks in. A former wrestler turned broadcaster due to injury,
The Feen may recall the glory days, or put down those who weren't like him as a wrestler. Basically, typical heel action. He's quick to jump on a bandwagon, and even quicker to jump off.
4.3 Mike Whalden A traditional wrestling fan, wanting to preserve the integrity of the sport, Whalden really drills the on goings of the league when given a chance. He's unbiased, which is why he often receives interview duties.
4.4 David Pelter David is IICW's ring announcer. Tries to be like Michael Buffer, but fails in his attempts. He's been around the wrestling world for a while and knows most of the wrestler names.. unlike Buffer ;)
5. What titles can I shoot for? 5.1 IICW World Heavyweight Title Basically, the big one. This is the top title in IICW, with the most prestige. It's not defended very often, to keep the title shots at highest standard. Every pay per view has a defense, as well as usually once a month.
5.2 IICW Intercontinental Title IICW's number two title. It's defended more often, probably every other week or so, which makes some believe this is the real title of the number one wrestler in the league.
5.3 IICW World Cruiserweight Title This title is for the IICW wrestlers that weight in at 235 lbs or lighter. The title is defended every other week or so. The Cruiserweights are a special case in the rankings. Whenever a cruiserweight (235 lbs or less) takes on another lightweight, the outcome is based in the lightweight rankings. If a cruiserweight takes on a heavyweight then the outcome is based in the heavyweight rankings. Therefore, cruiserweights could be a contender in both divisions. Basically, what they do in each division is totally independent of the other.
5.3 IICW World Tag Team Titles (not active yet) Same as 5.1 but for tag teams. Defended more often, about twice a month. 6. How are matches decided? IICW is a booked league, meaning that all matches are decided by a human brain. Basically, what you do each week in IICW results in the booking decision for that week. The more you do, the better chance you have to win.
Good flashes are an obvious must. This is the only visible thing to the entire league, and probably bears the most weight of the decision for that week. However, although you may have the better roleplay, you won't win every match.
Angles could be the most important, and may override all other booking decisions. If the bookers have something in mind down the road, or there's an angle which can be furthered through the result of one match, it may go that way.
Always remember that losing doesn't kill you. Also to note, upon entering the league, everybody loses a match or two. We need to find out if you're in it for the long haul, not splitting after one bad thing happens. This way, every wrestler goes through an initiation period, learning the ropes in the league. Slowly, they'll challenge the "old guard" and become one with the league.
7. What can I do to win? 7.1 Flashes Flashes are where you get to speak for your wrestler. They're what the league sees, and how people will react. This pretty much where things happen in how the character grows.
When flashing, use common sense. Don't use excessive profanity, or if you do, [bleep] it out. Don't use other wrestlers without permission. Don't use racial remarks, or anything else which may offend. Use your best judgement when sending things in.
There are only two spot for flashes, with one available for in-ring interviews on request. "Title Talk" is the main show, where everybody talks about their upcoming match on "Weekend War".
Feel free to use the IICW fictional staff in your interviews, provided you keep them in character. If you'd like to use somebody to interview your wrestler for a flash, Mike Whalden is always the best choice, as he'll stick to asking questions.
IICW formats all it's shows the same way, trying to keep the flashes in a recognizable format. This is:
[Setting, descriptions, actions here.] MW: blah blah blah. Any descriptions or actions go into square brackets. To start a wrestler's speech, use the initials of their name, followed by one space, and talk away.
Although we'd like you to help us out and use this format, it's not compulsory. As long as we can read it, it's fine.
7.2 Strats I'll be the first to tell you that I'm not a huge fan for certain strats. Giving me a move-per-move detail for your wrestler is
_NOT_ a good thing in IICW. However, if you want to do something special during the match, like use a weapon or make your manager distract your opponent, then that's perfectly fine to submit.
I try and follow all strategies, but it may not be possible all the time based on somebody else's strategy. Keep that in mind.
Again, use your best judgement when strategizing. Obviously you can't strat to beat the other wrestler up with a baseball bat.
Non-match strategies are covered in the next section on angles. 7.3 Angles Possibly the most important aspect of IICW, creating angles allows you to truly guide your wrestler to greatness.
IICW always tries to accomodate its handlers by using their angles, keeping it as a handler participation league.
Angles may include post match attacks on another wrestler, turning on another wrestler, doing something during the show, or things like that. Anything which you'd like your wrestler to do on
Weekend War, but doesn't involve wrestling is classified as an angle. But, angles also includes the long term planning between two wrestlers. More often then not, these involve two or more handlers working on a storyline between their two wrestlers. And, more often then not, these are the most interesting angles, as it has double the brain power working on it. We encourage these types of angles, with proper notice.
Angles allow you to control your wrestler. Make use of them. 7.4 Miscellaneous We've always got things behind the scenes for people to do. Matches, proof reading, keeping us from killing ourselves, whatever. If you're really bored and want something to do, we'll find you something.
8. What are the deadlines? Sometimes, you can get away with sending things after the deadline. We're pretty flexible. But, when we can't wait any longer, or have to work on something with the deadline in mind, they are followed.
This is the warning, so don't complain if you send something post-deadline and it's not used. 8.1 Weekend War Strats: Monday In-rings (on request): Wednesday night, 9:00pm eastern Angle requests: Monday night, 7:00pm eastern 8.2 Title Talk Flashes: Any night, any time before 9:00pm eastern 9.
