A three-times-presided Usenet booking promotion whose 1997 war with the WWO -- fought on the record from both sides -- ended in the federation's own booked absorption, decided by a single match's own pre-signed stipulation. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

WIF opened with its first recruitment post on 23 May 1996: “THE WORLD INTERNET FEDERATION HAS ITS FIRST EVER COMPETITOR!” — Enigma, out of Bermuda — over an entirely from-scratch character-creation form, run first by e-mail and planned for a web page by the end of that August.

Its own presidency changed hands twice within its first year, each time announced in its own recruitment posts, and its name was briefly disputed by a second operator who opened a rival WIF the same summer: “There is already a WIF and I run it... Could you please change the name to avoid confusion before its too late.” By that October a third president had settled in, endorsed in the newsgroup by one of its own wrestlers, Dark Ninja: “It has to be one of the best leagues around, and has been in existence for several months.”

By mid-1997 it carried four championships: Lancaster Bomber had just taken the World Heavyweight Title from Barbwire in what its own card report called “a classic encounter”; Roundhouse held the Internet Championship; Tony “Falcon” Silver held the Hardcore Championship, defending it against the dethroned Barbwire in a no-disqualification match whose loser had to say “I respect you” to the winner; and Search & Destroy, billing themselves four-time champions, held the Tag Team Titles.

Its record ends at Stranglehold, 19 October 1997: a joint broadcast with a rival promotion, the WWO, staging a thirty-man, two-team battle royal inside a steel cage, with, in its own broadcast's words, “every man... something riding on the outcome.” How that war finished isn't in the surviving record; its own account of it ends mid-match.
