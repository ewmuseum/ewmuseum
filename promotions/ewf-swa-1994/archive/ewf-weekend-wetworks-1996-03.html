====="THE LEAPING LEPRECHAUN" PADDY O'HARA vs. "COPPER" MIKE DULL=====
Paddy O'Hara made quite a return against "Copper" Mike Dull. O'Hara opened up with a flurry of dropkicks that dazed Dull. Armdrag Fujiwara combinations worked the big arms of Dull to reduce his strength, and various take-downs kept Dull on his back. O'Hara took Dull to suplex school with snap, vertical, belly-to-belly and belly-to-back variations. Dull managed to mount a small comeback with an eye rake, punch combo and bodyslam, but after a missed elbow drop, the only thing he could do was what he had been doing the rest of the match... roaring incoherently as he was pummeled by the quick Irishman. "The Shilelagh" top-rope cross-body block ended the match rather gracefully.

====="THE FALLEN ANGEL" SHINJI YAMATO vs. EL HAM-ON-RYE=====
The fans rejoiced at being able to see a decent high-impact, Japanese-style wrestling match. El Ham-On-Rye was known in both the Mexican leagues and the Japanese federations as an accomplished high flyer. Shinji Yamato was a little less well known in circles outside of Japan. Yamato prayed to his warrior spirits before removing his white robe, which had, in black, the Japanese characters for "Angel" across the back. El Ham-On-Rye waited for Yamato to signal he was ready before locking up with the Japan ese athlete. El Ham-On-Rye was able to get a decent offensive going in the beginning, hitting with armscissors take-downs, Japanese armdrags, and martial arts maneuvers, but Yamato came back with a string of armdrags followed by two quick backflipping dropkicks that stunned his opponent. Yamato hit a series of chops followed by a lightning fast martial arts combo in the corner... and then took El Ham-On-Rye down with a second-rope Frankensteiner. A handspring elbow smash followed by a powerdrive elbow d rop almost won the match for Yamato. El Ham-On-Rye managed to duck a flying clothesline and come back with a monkey flip, then a victory roll for a two-count. He hit a powerful roundhouse kick to the face that knocked Yamato to his knees, then trapped Yamato in a bow-and-arrow. After Yamato broke the hold, he found himself in another painful lock in the Nakano deathlock. Two minutes in that took a lot out of the "Fallen Angel," but he was able to block a flying side kick and strike back with a vicious chop to the throat. After a series of rapid-fire dropkicks, Yamato went to the top to finish El Ham-On-Rye off. Unluckily for him, El Ham-On-Rye was not quite ready to quit and dropkicked Yamato out of his moonsault. A belly-to-belly suplex right onto the neck almost pinned Yamato, and the ensuing Northern Lights press had the same effect. El Ham-On-Rye's one mistake was in taking too long to get to the top for a high risk maneuver. Yamato dropkicked his legs out so he crotched himself, then went up a nd snap suplexed him off the turnbuckle. A few seconds later, Yamato was coming off the top for his "Falling Angel" swan dive and three seconds after that, his arm was raised in victory by the referee.
(+1 Shinji Yamato for jobber match)
