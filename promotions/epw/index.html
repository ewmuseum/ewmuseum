A recruiting federation that never posted a card, known only through its own dramatized advert and two handbook editions naming a growing and changing writing staff. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Extreme Pro Wrestling introduced itself on 2 October 1997 with a dramatized video-script advertisement: its own president, cradling a gold title belt on camera, daring the audience to sign up for a coming title tournament — “Do you want to be remembered as a _LEGOND_?” — before the federation had even opened.

The same day it issued a handbook laying out a booked, roleplay-judged promotion running two weekly cards: Sunday Night Showcase, its main event slot, carrying four to seven matches and most of its title defenses, and Wednesday Warzone, a smaller card of two or three matches used to push angles and give rookies stage time. Its three championships — a World Heavyweight Title, a Northeastern Title, and a single set of World Tag Team Titles — each carried a monthly defense requirement, with a stripped title going to a match between the promotion's own number-one contender and a challenger the president picked personally.

On 4 November 1997 its president posted seeking more writers, describing the promotion as having run “6 succsessful cards in a row” and closing in on its own title tournament. No card script of the promotion's own survives to confirm that count.

A revised handbook edition followed on 10 November 1997, adding a rule barring real wrestlers outright and recording a larger writing staff than the October edition had listed. No further document survives; whether the promised title tournament was ever held, or how the promotion ended, isn't in the surviving record.
