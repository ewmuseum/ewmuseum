Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

This wing portrays World Championship Wrestling, the real wrestling company. World Championship Wrestling here is a fan federation's fiction that keeps the company going after its real 2001 close, not a statement of fact about the company or the people it names. Its own masthead reads WORLD CHAMPIONSHIP WRESTLING, its columns cover Monday Night Nitro, and it bills real wrestlers under their own names; its belts are its own rows, never the real ones. It is a different wing from WCW (World Class, 2002), a federation of the rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy newsgroup that shares only the initials.

What survives is a page headed WCW Title Histories, three columns and a results page. The title history lists ten WCW belts, 253 entries in all, from Vampiro's first World title to Mark Jindrak's win at Spring Stampede on 11 May 2008; its dates run from September 2001 to May 2008 and many entries carry none, and its footnotes record turns of its own storylines, among them Sting unifying the WCW and WWF world titles and Edge unifying the WCW and WWE titles. The columns are Scott Hudson's Hudson Ramblings, which ranks Mark Jindrak first after Spring Stampede; Jim Ross's weekly column, which names Robert Roode its Superstar of the Week and welcomes Joe Hennig and Val Venis as new champions; and Adventures in Larryland, which previews the Greed pay-per-view. The results page gives the Spring Stampede card of 11 May 2008, ending with Jindrak winning a three-way match for the World title.

Its lineage runs on into TNA. The news of the TNA fan federation on 8 June 2008 has Panda Energy buy WCW and merge it into TNA, taking the Big Gold title and the contracts of its stars, and the World Champion at the end of this title history, Mark Jindrak, is the champion that news has slated to jump. The two are separate wings joined as predecessor and successor on that news. This federation's page was found in the TNA federation's own member directory; its own subdomains are gone, and the one that carried its columns also carried the roster of a different, Japan-themed federation.
