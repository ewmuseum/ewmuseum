Introduction to the MCWF

25 May 1999
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Introduction to the MCWF By [name withheld], President Welcome to the MCWF! OK, sure, just because you’re reading this doesn’t mean that you’re necessarily a member of the MCWF automatically. But hopefully it means that you’re at least curious, and that’s a beginning. I will attempt to explain my vision for the MCWF, what you can expect from me as president, and what I expect from you should you decide to join.
To start with the basics, the MCWF is a booked fantasy wrestling federation. If you already know what all those terms mean, feel free to skip the rest of this paragraph. For those of you still reading, a fantasy wrestling federation is a kind of interactive fiction in which we all together strive to create something which allows us to feel a little bit of what it might be like to actually participate in the world of pro wrestling. Except for the addiction to pain medication. Your job will be to create a wrestler (or wrestlers), give him (or them) a personality and background, and then interact with other members to create matches, write out interviews, and if you stick around long enough, you might even get into a hot feud! The term booked means that like real wrestling federations, the outcomes of all matches are determined by human judgment (usually mine). The other popular option is simmed federations, in which some type of computer simulates an actual match and pseudo-randomly determines the winner. If that sounds appealing to you, then you’re in the wrong place.
For those of you who are here because you skipped past the previous paragraph, you missed the part where I revealed the URL of my collection of nude photos of Britney Spears. Ha! Just kidding. As if I’d share that with you people.
Anyway, here’s the nitty gritty of how I hope things will work here in the
MCWF. Keep in mind that none of this is set in stone. I reserve the right to ignore any of the following if I think the situation warrants it. And if, over time, I find that the system isn’t working as I envisioned it, I reserve the right to permanently alter it. And anyone who bitches too loudly about it will have none of my sympathy. I will make every attempt to be a benevolent dictator, but let none of you forget that I am a dictator, and you will be well served to treat me as such. Except that I’ll have none of that "Et tu, Brute?" shit.
So, to begin with, the MCWF’s main weekly show is sent out to you every
Tuesday. It is called Tuesday Nitrous. It will be jam packed with mainly wrestling matches, written out in what is known as the Action Block System.
I could explain it here, but it’d be a lot faster (and more amusing for you) for you to just read a Nitrous or two. If you’ve got a few hours to kill, go to the website and read them all. It’s at
www.mv.com/ipusers/aurora/ if you’ re interested. I’ll wait for you to come back. Who writes Nitrous, you ask? Yours truly will probably write two or three of the matches per week, and the rest will be written by MCWF members, on a volunteer basis. Now, when I say volunteer basis, I mean that no one will ever be forced to write a match on the same week he’s got five papers due and a final exam coming up. But on the other hand, I expect each MCWF member to contribute whatever he can. If that’s one match per week, great. One every two or three weeks is fine. But I’d hope that you could find the time for at least one match per month. In the end it benefits you. The more matches people write, the more matches I can book every week. In an ideal world, everyone will get a match every week, and that can only happen with great participation.
And don’t worry if you’ve never written matches before. It’s not that hard, really. I’ll always give you a basic idea of how the match will flow, how it will end. If you don’t think you have the ability to write some sort of semi-decent match, you probably don’t have the writing skills to succeed in the MCWF anyway.
So, Nitrous is over and your wrestler either won or lost. At the end of the show, Dean and Rexx, the commentary team, will announce the lineup for next week’s show. Matches get signed in a number of different ways, and as I said before the number of matches I sign is limited by the number of volunteer match writers I get, so if you want to write for Nitrous, you need to let me know before Tuesday, and preferably as much in advance as possible.
Likewise, if you’ve said you’ll write a match but can’t, let me know as soon as possible so that I can make other arrangements. You can obviously request a match with a particular wrestler, either by challenging him directly, or by arranging things through me. Sometimes a particular match will be an obvious singing based on the results of that night’s Nitrous, and sometimes
I’ll just go on my own and book a match for two wrestlers with nothing else to do.
So, what do you do next, now that you’ve got a match signed? Two things, really. One, you need to come up with some sort of strategy package for the match. The strategy package, in a nutshell, is your best chance to convince me that your wrestler should win the match. Your attempt at this should take two parts. One, show me that you’ve given some thought to the actual wrestling strategy that your wrestler will use. Know your opponent’s strengths and weaknesses, and let me know how your wrestler plans to avoid and exploit those. Even better would be for you to suggest actual "spots" in the match that you’d like to see happen. How you’re going to counter one of his favorite moves, or a really sneaky way that you’re going to use a foreign object. That makes it easier for the match writer, and will probably result in a more interesting match to read. The second part of your strategy package is just as important, and it has to do with angle development. You need to convince me from an outside point of view, why it makes sense for your character to win this match. For example, assume that you win the match and let me know how you think you will work that into whatever angle you’re working for your wrestler. If you’re not working some sort of angle for your wrestler, think of one, pronto, because if you don’t then there’s a good chance you’re going to job to someone who has one. This will in all likelihood be easier if you were the one who requested the match, because you obviously had some sort of plan in mind when you asked for it.
The important thing is to be creative. Make your strategy package as compelling as possible. Try to outthink your counterpart. If you anticipate what he’s trying to do, and think of a clever way to counter it, you’ve got a good shot at succeeding. Similarly, if you can think of a truly brilliant ending to the match, or some type of extra-curricular that would be really memorable, you’re going to make it awfully hard for me to award the match to your opponent.
So after I receive the strategy packages from both combatants, I will look them over, and make a decision as to who wins the match. Don’t get too upset over losing, and don’t get too excited over winning. It’s not like this is really a true test of skill or anything, it’s just for fun. I expect that everybody in the MCWF will have great writing skills, so it’s not as if I’m saying you suck if you lose. It’s just that for whatever reason I thought it made more sense for your opponent to win. And from my own personal point of view, I’ve always enjoyed playing my characters after a loss. How a character deals with adversity is usually more interesting than how he deals with success. That’s why most heroic figures face a lot of adversity. And that’s why Barry Horowitz is the greatest hero in wrestling history.
There is one other way for you to define your character, and it’s probably the one most familiar to those of you who have been around doing this sort of thing for a while. It’s called flashing, and what it consists of is you writing out some sort if interview for your wrestler.
The MCWF has a weekly flash show, called Showcase. There will usually be one or two matches on this show, but its primary purpose is to give each of you an opportunity to let your wrestler or wrestlers speak directly to us. Sort of like when a real life wrestler cuts an interview to hype an upcoming match. Now, in most e-wrestling leagues, the flash is the primary way that match outcomes are determined. However, in the MCWF, the strategy package will be taken much more into account, although consistently great flashes will not go unnoticed either. And, to be honest, if you’ve got the creative skills to come up with great flashes, you’re probably good enough to think of good strategy packages as well.
The reason that I place more importance on strategy than flashes is simple. I really don’t want you to spend that much time on writing flashes. I’ve read some awfully elaborate flashes in my time, and every time I read one I can’t help thinking about midgets and exploding boats. I’d much rather you write up a brief interview with your wrestler in a locker room after his match on Tuesday ranting about his defeat or gloating over his victory, and making some sort of statement to his next opponent. Don’t feel the need to make it too long or too complex. If you’ve got the time to write a huge 10K flash, then forget it. Do a 1K flash and volunteer to write a match that week instead. I’ll be much more impressed and grateful.
Your other option for Showcase is to write up a squash match for your wrestler, against one of the many MCWF jobber characters I made up. You can find a list of them somewhere on the web site under "Roster". Your wrestler might want to send a message to his opponent with a particularly brutal performance, or it might just be that you want to demonstrate to the writers exactly how your wrestler behaves in the ring. It’s also a good way to get practice writing matches if you’ve never done it before. If it’s a not-so-good first effort, nobody is going to be pissed off that you ruined their match. Most of the time, you’ll probably do a flash, but this other option is available as a change of pace for you.
Also, you are obviously expected to produce only one flash per week, since we only have one flash show. If you have the time to do a flash every day, forget it. Volunteer to write a match instead. The only occasion I would allow two flashes in one week is if one of them is an in-ring interview for
Nitrous. And I will only allow that under special conditions. If you have an in-ring interview, it needs to serve some purpose crucial to the angle you’ re in, which can’t be done in any other way. You’ll need to get my approval a week in advance, since no wrestler will wrestle on a Nitrous on which he has an in-ring. Simply put, doing an interview on Nitrous should be a pretty big deal.
That’s about it. I could go on and on, but I fear if this gets too long, nobody will read the whole thing. If you think you’re interested in joining the MCWF, let me know. There’s no silly application process or anything. I don’t have a waiting list at the moment, so if you’re willing to give the
MCWF a try, I’m happy to have you. If you’ve got any questions about the
MCWF, feel free to send me an e-mail.
If you’ve gotten this far, I really should thank you for reading the whole thing.
