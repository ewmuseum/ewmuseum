A December 1997 fed set up by two former operators of the CWF/WCWF chain, deliberately choosing a new name over reusing WCWF. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

MCWF announced itself in December 1997 as a deliberate departure from its own two founders' earlier promotion. One of them wrote in the founding post that he and his co-founder “have decided to join forces and start up a brand new federation from the ground up” after running the CWF and its outgrowth, the WCWF, together from 1993 to 1995. The new name was a deliberate choice: another e-fed was still using the initials WCWF, and MCWF's own recruitment post said so plainly rather than compete for the letters.

Its founding broadcast, Tuesday Night Undercooked, aired 3 December 1997 numbered as its 300th episode rather than a first — continuing, in its own account, “almost six years” of a Tuesday show under a new banner. It opened on the MCWF World Championship, held by Steve Coley, backed by his uncle, the promotion's own owner, and by a stable of allies — Octane, Slash Maraud, the “spiritual advisor” Styx, and the manager-turned-wrestler Porno Inferno — known collectively as the Klubb.

The same broadcast staged the start of a feud: former three-time champion Rexx Ryder, reduced to a commentary role, snapped on air and attacked Porno Inferno, then challenged Coley directly to a singles match at the promotion's next major card, Wrestlefest VI, with the stipulation that Coley step down from ownership's shadow if he lost. The same card introduced two new tag teams making their MCWF debut, the prison-themed Chain Gang and the “Parts Unknown” duo Davey and Goliath.

A handbook issued 25 May 1999 by MCWF's own president — the other of its two co-founders — describes the promotion still running on a booked “Action Block System” of written strategy packages rather than a simulator, a weekly card called Tuesday Nitrous, and a flash show, Showcase, for in-character interviews and squash matches against the roster's own jobbers.

No closing statement of MCWF's own survives past that 1999 handbook. Whether the Coley/Ryder feud that opened the promotion, or Wrestlefest VI itself, was ever staged as planned isn't in the surviving record.
