A federation across Binghamton, Utica and Albany, NY whose Ring Wars and Power Hour shows carried a Hardcore Championship through a 1998 relaunch, then returned in 2001 under new ownership to crown a Heavyweight champion through an eight-man tournament and a new Television champion on its Ring Wars broadcasts. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Mohawk Valley Wrestling ran out of Binghamton, New York, opening under the name Ring Wars in late 1997. After a gap of several months it relaunched under a new name, Power Hour, with a “Yankee Pride” pilot episode broadcast live from the Binghamton War Memorial on 21 May 1998 — a broadcast whose own highlight reel cut back to footage from Blizzard Blast '97, tying the new show to the promotion's own earlier run.

That pilot built its main event around a Hardcore Championship decided by a Capture the Flag Hardcore War, fought across the whole arena and won by whoever climbed a suspended pole to retrieve the title. The reigning champion, “Silencer” Chris Magnum, entered vowing to beat every man in the match — the Professor, Aaron Anderson, Shane Slaughter, Solitude, Alain Fiscus, Shawn Walsh, Scott Bradley and Kid Vicious among them — to keep the belt.

By December 1998 the promotion announced a run of free-agent signings: “Bad Boy” Mark Bagwell, billed as a former EWA World Champion, and a mob-styled stable, the GF Family, led by manager GoodFella and introduced to the assembled media at a press conference alongside his nephew “Sir” Polo Stetson and “Desire” Brandon Bloom.

A promo dated 6 December 1998 stages an apocalyptic, religiously framed confrontation, the character Stigmata delivering judgment to a wrestler named Thomas, part of the same run of storyline output closing out the promotion's year.

A further update dated January 1999 shows the promotion still running a weekly Ring Wars card and defending four championships. No closing notice of any kind survives past that update.
