Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

NEWA opened its recruitment drive on 12 February 1997 under the slogan “A new standard in wrestling,” promising a Royal Rumble at its debut show, Mat Wars, to crown its first World Heavyweight Champion. Within two weeks it claimed about forty wrestlers signed and was still recruiting more. Its 28 February Mat Wars broadcast opened on a controversial finish from the previous show still unresolved — had Psycho Stud's feet touched down first against Colossus? — and built to a rematch after Psycho Stud was written off the show entirely: masked men ambushed him with baseball bats outside his own limousine on a tape played live to the crowd.

A wrestler using the ring name the Acer joined NEWA early and, by his own fed's later retrospective, lost his NEWA debut to Jules Winnfield in thirty seconds before beating him months later in a loser-leaves match that ended Winnfield's career outright. NEWA's own retrospective, aired that December, calls it a hollow win: “Not much to boast about though. The fed closed down the next week.” The same broadcast handed out a one-off Acer Excellence Award to three people, honouring NEWA's own founding and the stable that grew out of it, the Old World Order.

Before that ending, NEWA had already lost pieces of its own staff: its Vice President and two more of its own people left in June 1997 to found a separate promotion. A third party elsewhere on the newsgroup called NEWA “now-almost defunct” that August, which the fed's own record neither confirms nor answers directly.

Exactly when NEWA closed isn't fixed any more precisely than that: its own record keeps running through a March newsletter and a June recruitment call after its debut, and its own December retrospective narrates the Winnfield match and the closing that followed it as already past history by the time that show aired.
