A newsgroup federation that opened a sixteen-man tournament for its own vacant Unified World Championship, then closed less than three months later with the title never awarded and its own first pay-per-view never run. Thirty-one wrestlers, two tag units and five shows survive from its January 1999 handbook onward. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The World-Wide Wrestling Association named itself in full throughout its own Player's Handbook, posted 8 January 1999, three titles announced at once: a Unified World Championship, an American Championship and a World Tag Team Championship. Its first broadcast, Clear the Lane, followed nine days later out of the Richmond Coliseum, and its main event did double duty as an origin story: King Agony beat Danny Flannagan with a Crucifix Powerbomb after both men traded chair shots, and the promotion's own on-screen co-presidents, feuding brothers Lance and Dave Younger, used the moment to announce a sixteen-man tournament for the still-vacant Unified World title.

That tournament ran the length of the promotion's surviving record, and the Younger brothers ran it as its own storyline: overruling each other's decisions on air, tearing up time limits and disqualification rules mid-match, and turning the bracket itself into as much of a draw as any of the wrestling. “Boardwalk” Timothy Jensen won early rounds with his Dreambuster; “The Enigma” Joe Hutchings advanced on a Shooting Star Press he called the Extinction Level Event, then later a Michinoku Driver into a dragon sleeper that put an opponent on a stretcher; “The Genius” Alfred Scarsone submitted two different men with his figure-four headlock, the Braincell Killer, the second time only after “Superstar” Ken Warren stripped his opponent of a chair to even the odds.

A tag team feud grew up alongside the tournament and outlasted every round of it: Ghetto Thunderstorm, formed on air when Devon Henderson ran in to save “Lightning Feet” Deon Langston from an ambush, fought For Unlawful Carnal Knowledge from their first meeting through the promotion's own last surviving card, where Henderson closed it out by pinning Alex Thomas with his Nodowa Bomb, The Thunderbolt, only for a four-man brawl to break out once the bell rang.

The tournament itself never finished. Ghost-Faced Killer, a recurring masked antagonist, attacked a referee and both competitors in one quarterfinal to force a double pin the promotion ruled a shared win, then wrestled an entire later match disguised as a second Ghost-Faced Killer before unmasking near its end. On the promotion's own last surviving broadcast, 1 March 1999, Timothy Jensen became the first man to climb free of a three-way cage, eliminating Ian Walsh and King Agony and advancing to face whoever won the still-unresolved Hutchings–Warren/Scarsone semifinal. The same broadcast closed on the Younger brothers' own feud at its sharpest: Lance, found handcuffed to a pipe in his own office, freed himself, and had his brother Dave arrested on live television in return.

No further broadcast survives. The Unified World Championship was never awarded, its bracket stuck at the final four; the American and World Tag Team titles announced in the founding handbook were never contested at all. A third party posted, three weeks after that last broadcast, that the promotion had closed without running its planned pay-per-view — a claim disputed in the same conversation and never confirmed in the promotion's own words.
