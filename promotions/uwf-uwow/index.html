Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The United Wrestling Federation is the founding member of the UWOW alliance — the fed that ran alone under the UWOW banner from a 2001 newsletter through a September 2006 relaunch "under a new vision," before the January 2008 launch of the XWF and ICWC alongside it turned UWOW into a three-fed interfed. Every named competitor across its surviving record is a real professional wrestler, cast by the site's own hand into fictional title chases: Abyss's eight-man tournament win over Kurt Angle opened the 2006 restart, and the World Heavyweight Championship changed hands seven more times on the surviving ledger — through Angle, Brock Lesnar, Sting, Christian Cage (inside an elimination chamber over five other men), Angle again, and Ric Flair — before owner Matthew Hopkins stripped Flair over a dispute involving Vince McMahon and the Stamford Outsiders, and the tournament called to replace him never reached a final after a riot closed the arena.

Three of the restart's own titles left the fed entirely rather than simply changing hands: the Intercontinental Championship went to ICWC with Ultimate Warrior, the Television Championship went to XWF with Ravishing Rick Rude (renamed there the North American Heavyweight Championship), and the United States Tag Team Championship went to XWF with Demolition (renamed there the World Tag Team Championship) — the surviving record's own clearest evidence that UWOW's three feds shared one continuous roster rather than three sealed rosters under a common banner. The fed's own ledger says a fourth title, the Canadian Heavyweight Championship, went to XWF with Lance Storm as well, though no surviving XWF title is credited with continuing that lineage.

The surviving capture holds the fed's own current-era title-history ledgers in full, an earlier (2001-2002) title-description page from before the restart, and nine served show scripts running from Halloween Havoc 2007 through the fed's own last captured broadcast, Onslaught (March 2009) — a crossover card staging the United Wrestling Federation itself as a third rival institution alongside the real-world WWE and TNA brand names, never once presenting itself as either. A dedicated roster page, if the fed ever ran one, was not part of this capture; seven further dated 2006-2007 shows named in the fed's own event listings and ledger citations survive only as citations, not served text.

Storylines

The nine served shows this wing holds (Halloween Havoc 2007 through Onslaught 2009) run as one continuous serial rather than nine unrelated cards, and two arcs carry across nearly all of them: owner Matthew Hopkins' running war with Ric Flair over the World Heavyweight Championship, and the three-way collapse of the Rhodes family. Both are recapped below, show by served show.

Flair, Hopkins, and the Dynasty Written by AI, 2026

Ric Flair beat Kurt Angle for the World Heavyweight Championship at Bad Blood (March 2008) on a fluke finish — a substitute referee, tripped by Christian Cage's own ringside interference, controversially ruled Angle submitted. Owner Matthew Hopkins spent the next several shows trying to take the title back by other means: booking Flair into a six-person Golden Opportunity ladder match he still somehow won at Onslaught (Feb 2008), then a four-on-one Dynasty beatdown at Bound For Glory, then a Thundercage title defense against Abyss at the (uncaptured) 2008 Ultimate Supercard that the fed's own ledger says Flair survived. The Horsemen themselves fractured over the feud — Arn Anderson, Tully Blanchard and Barry Windham took a Hopkins payoff to leave Flair for dead at Onslaught, Anderson turned back on his own at Bound For Glory (DDT-ing Hopkins in the ring), and Flair and Anderson reunited for the Bound For Glory main event.

The alliance turned inside out at School of Hard Knocks (Sept 2008), when Hopkins — served with a summons from Vince McMahon — let Flair talk him into publicly defying the McMahons rather than settling, only for the Undertaker to debut standing over a beaten Hopkins with Flair nowhere to be found. By November to Remember, Flair was booked against his own new allies, teamed with Abyss against Undertaker and Christian Cage, and sabotaged Cage backstage before Triple H and Shawn Michaels ran in with a sledgehammer, bloodying the Undertaker and confirming Flair's own new alliance with WWE. Hopkins stripped Flair of the title outright at the top of Starrcade and ran a sixteen-man tournament to replace him; Flair fought into that tournament's own second round before the Starrcade broadcast itself was cut short by a riot and a police shutdown, following a Stephanie McMahon kidnapping angle and Vince McMahon's own on-screen arrival — the served card ends before a new champion is ever crowned on the page. The fed's own title-history ledger separately resolves the tournament at a later show this wing's own capture never held, "UWF Brand New Fear, Jan '09" — a Triple Threat crediting Randy Orton the win over JBL and Flair, corroborated by the ledger alone.

The Rhodes family war Written by AI, 2026

Dustin Rhodes opened Halloween Havoc 2007 by refusing to wrestle his younger brother Cody Rhodes at all, installing Barry Windham as a replacement opponent, only for their father Dusty Rhodes to break up the beating — and for Dustin to reveal the whole rescue as a set-up, siding openly with Ric Flair and the Horsemen. Dustin spent 2008 escalating: a chair-and-pipe beatdown of both his father and brother at Bad Blood that ended with a spiked piledriver delivered alongside Chyna, who became his regular partner in crime as part of Hopkins' own Corporate Dynasty. Dusty and Cody answered back where they could — Dusty locked the Figure-4 on his own son to win the Brand New Fear WarGames match for Sting's Army over the Horsemen — but the feud was never resolved on the page: the last served card to feature Dustin Rhodes, November to Remember, still has him allied with the Dynasty against his own family's side.

Affiliations

United World Of Wrestling (UWOW)

Member of UWF (2001)
UWF is UWOW's own founding member fed — the fed the alliance's own 2001 newsletter names outright, and the fed whose September 2006 relaunch "under a new vision" the current-era title-history ledgers begin from.

International Championship Wrestling Council (ICWC)

Shared continuity with UWF (2001)
The UWF Intercontinental Championship migrated to ICWC with its holder, Ultimate Warrior, continuing there as the ICWC Intercontinental Championship — a title lineage physically crossing from one member fed to the other, not merely a shared banner.

X-Treme Wrestling Federation (XWF, 2008)

Shared continuity with UWF (2001)
Two UWF titles migrated to XWF with their holders: the Television Championship, brought by Ravishing Rick Rude and renamed the North American Heavyweight Championship, and the United States Tag Team Championship, brought by Demolition and renamed the World Tag Team Championship. A third, the Canadian Heavyweight Championship, is said by UWF’s own title history to have moved to XWF with Lance Storm as well, but no surviving XWF title is credited with continuing that lineage.
