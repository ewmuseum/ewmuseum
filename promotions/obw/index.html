A three-brand e-fed built around its own Vixens division, run at onlinebrutalwrestling.com and documented on a wiki thrown open to its own roster. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Online Brutal Wrestling ran at onlinebrutalwrestling.com, and said so on the front page of its own wiki: “OBWrestling.wikia.com is for all the info about www.onlinebrutalwrestling.com! The best efed there is.” The wiki opened in July 2012 and was thrown open to the roster — “Anyone on OBW can edit and add pages growing the wiki” — with a hand-picked board named on the same page to keep it in order.

The federation ran three brands, Execution, Storm and Vixens, and its own roster table gives the board a fourth column beside them. The Vixens division carried four championships of its own, and it is the half of the federation the wiki documented most completely: the Vixen's World Championship ledger runs ten reigns from Cream Stylez's inaugural win on the 1 February 2012 debut of Wednesday Night Catfight, and the Television title's runs six reigns across five wrestlers from Kitten's win at Big Shot four weeks later.

The main roster's belts are listed rather than chronicled — an OBW Championship and a World Heavyweight Championship at the top, with middleweight, anarchy, ice, hardcore, triple threat, British, allstar, intercontinental and two sets of tag team titles beneath them, four of those already marked retired the last time the list was touched.

Around the wrestling it ran television of its own. OBW Big Brother was a house game hosted by Daffney, its cast drawn from the roster and its evictions posted as they happened. Laced Up was a talk-show parody the Lace Twins made to mock the wrestlers they disliked; it “recieved very good reviews but never returned for a second episode.” Misbehavior, the Daffney Awards and Daffney Tells It ran beside them. The federation's own Hall of Fame table dates its inductions from 2006 to 2012.

It closed twice, and its own record dates both. Ray's page carries the first: he and the Execution general manager “decided to close OBW in the spring of 2009 due to financial problems,” and he made his last old-OBW appearance at a goodbye show, before reopening the federation himself with a redesigned site and a new pair of general managers. Snickers' bio card carries the second, in passing: OBW ran “till the company closed on December 2, 2014.” The wiki outlived the federation by more than four years — its last edit of any kind is 4 February 2019 — and onlinebrutalwrestling.com no longer resolves.

Storylines

The arcs below are recovered from a full read of all 43 held documents — the federation's own three title ledgers, its champions list, its faction and show pages, and every surviving wrestler bio.

The Vixen's World Championship's own decade-long cast of claimants Written by AI, 2026

The belt is born already a unification: Cream Stylez beats Jade Mystique on the debut of Wednesday Night Catfight to merge the old Divas and Execution Women's titles into one, and Jade — who has already lost a Women's Championship battle royal to Snickers on Execution's own third episode — spends the belt's whole life chasing it back. She takes it from Cream in a three-way at Heart of Wrestling, loses it to Snickers in a mixed tag Snickers wins alongside on-screen boyfriend Chad Bowens, and watches it pass through Cassie James (the belt's own youngest champion, and the same week OBW Galaxy learns she once worked as former GM Madman's own prostitute at eighteen), Kitten, and the Lace Twins before Ayden Starr wins it in her own debut match. Snickers reclaims it a second time from Ayden and Velvet under extreme rules at Big Shot 2013, only for Ayden to take it back a month later at Heart of Wrestling in a falls-count-anywhere match against Snickers, Myroslava Ivanova, Angelic Slade and Layla Lace — still running, and by that July already the longest Vixen's World reign on the ledger, when the record stops.

Maneaters, Vixens 2.0, and the stables that ran the Vixens locker room Written by AI, 2026

Named the federation's own 2012 Stable of the Year, Maneaters — Snickers, Jade Mystique, Leda, Myroslava Ivanova and Dannity Khane — held the Vixen's Television and International titles at once and the Tag Team belt for 115 days, Snickers standing as the group's own recognized champion. Its rise runs alongside a rival uprising: Djinn, freed from the interpreter she'd used since a career-ending neck injury elsewhere, forms Vixens 2.0 with Velvet, Norelle Stevens, Amy Rose and Pamela Cyr to attack the division's own "has-beens." Ava Nichole books the group into a 5-on-5 elimination tag at Violent Voting 2012 with every Vixens 2.0 member's own OBW career on the line; Djinn eliminates both Ava and Kaystar (the second with her own Hades Lock, after Ava had already caught her with the Ice Cold, the finisher of Djinn's own new on-screen partner Ice) before a teammate's stray kick costs Djinn the match to Kitten. Vixens 2.0 wins anyway, and Djinn — handed the Storm general managership in the storyline's own wake — has carried a standing grudge against Ava ever since.

Chad Bowens's three stables, and the betrayal that ended the first two Written by AI, 2026

Bowens debuts on Storm 28 under GCW transplant Manny Fresh's own stable Genesis, winning his first tag titles with fellow member Roshandala Babcock, before turning on Fresh at Big Shot 2012 to align with Jackal and Rapp3r as G2 — Fresh gets his win back at that year's Heart of Wrestling in a match the record calls a show-stealer. After losing the OBW Championship to a cashed-in Ice, Bowens leaves the company outright, returning masked in 2013 as a "henchman" for the stable the Prophecy, secretly working Punisher from the inside — isolating Punisher's own friends and, by the record's own account, kissing Punisher's own wife — before turning face on Punisher's rival M-Star at Heart of Wrestling 2013. A third stable, G3, follows a first OBW World Heavyweight Championship win over MVP Lux — a win set up when four other wrestlers jumped Lux beforehand — before an injury forces Bowens to vacate the belt he'd just won.

Jade Mystique's cancelled wedding, and the marriages that followed it Written by AI, 2026

At the height of her second Divas Championship reign, Jade Mystique accepts on-screen boyfriend DJ Ando's proposal and launches a two-episode reality show, "Jade Ties The Knot," to cover the run-up. On the wedding day itself Snickers interrupts to air footage of Ando's own affair with her; Jade calls her "a jealous bitch" on the way out of the ring before the tape rolls anyway. The rivalry runs into Alcatraz Aggression, where Jade and Ando lose an inter-gender double-championship tag to Snickers and Chad Bowens after Jade — distracted by Ando — costs her own team the match and her title with it. Two real marriages follow the cancelled one: Ayden Starr marries Christian Malice on 20 January 2013, twelve days after their first on-screen kiss ended her engagement to Rapp3r, and the marriage itself becomes a match stipulation that July, ending when Christian loses to Punisher at Alcatraz Aggression; and Snickers marries James Parker on 11 January 2013, divorcing him three months later.
