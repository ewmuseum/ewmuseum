Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Internet Wrestling League was founded 1 December 2000 as part of the Geeksoft.Net network of federations, and its own record credits it as the longest-running promotion inside that network before Geeksoft closed its doors to every federation it hosted in early 2002, leaving the IWL without a home and on hiatus.

Its own chairman brought it back on 28 January 2004 with a press conference announcing the end of that hiatus: “The Internet Wrestling League was one point in time one of the better promotions out there. Now at a time where many new and coming promotions are closing within a month of opening, I think it would be a perfect opportunity for the IWL and other organizations with similar backgrounds to return.” The relaunch planned two events for early February — a returning IWL OverDrive at Madison Square Garden on 5 February, downscaled from the arena's full capacity, followed four days later by IWL Monday Night — and a fresh push to sign free-agent wrestlers to contracts.

By September 2004 the relaunched IWL was running King of the CyberRing, billed as an annual tradition even in its own return season: a thirty-two-man single-elimination tournament with random seeding and no consolation match. Its own chairman announced two changes to the format on PTC Radio that year: the winner would take the IWL Internet Heavyweight Championship, vacant since the reigns of the Punisher, Axel Young and, most recently, Machine Knight K', rather than only a title shot; and for the first time the tournament opened beyond its own roster, pairing an IWL Bracket of contracted superstars against a new World Bracket open to any wrestler willing to register, the two brackets' winners meeting at a pay-per-view final for the King of the CyberRing crown itself.

No further document survives past that September 2004 announcement; how the tournament played out, and how long the relaunched Internet Wrestling League lasted afterward, isn't in the surviving record.
