Tort Enterprises rises in Wall Street news April 14, 2001 BOSTON — Tort Enterprises, an entertainment company with businesses ranging from sports to broadcasting, on Thursday posted a 16 percent jump in first-quarter profits on record revenue driven by its recent sport entertainment operations. The quarter was carried by gains in Tort's recently aquired wrestling segments, which offset weaker reality programming and product spin off results that suffered from a slowing U.S. economy. Tort Enterprises said earnings from ongoing operations totaled $1.017 billion, or 18 cents per share, for the quarter compared with $.592 billion, or 12 cents per share, in the same quarter last year. Including 2001 accounting changes, the company posted a profit of 6 cents per share. The results met Wall Street targets. Analysts on average had pegged Tort Enterprises at earnings of 17 cents per share for the period, with estimates ranging from 16 to 18 cents per share, according to tracking firm Thomson Financial/First Call. Revenues rose to a record $18.49 billion in the quarter from $15.996 billion a year ago. ``The record results for the first quarter once again demonstrate the ability of Tort Enterprises' diverse mix of leading global businesses to deliver earnings growth, increased margins and strong cash generation despite a challenging economic climate,'' said Gerald Peterson, chairman and chief executive. Industrial operations generated an 11 percent increase in first-quarter revenues, boosted by the power systems, medical systems and personal hand held computers. Those long-cycle businesses saw operating profits jump 36 percent while short-cycle businesses, more affected by economic conditions, reported a 5 percent decrease in operating profit. The power systems group saw revenues jump 33 percent while aircraft engines posted a 12 percent increase in revenues. Those gains helped offset a 5 percent decline in appliance revenues and a 3 percent decrease in revenues from the WLC cable broadcasting operation. Shares of Tort Enterprises, a component of the 30-stock Dow Jones industrial average, gained ground after the announcement, rising 159 cents, or 2.76 percent, to $44.86 in early trade. The stock has edged lower since hitting a 52-week high of $60.50 in late August. Still, it has outperformed the Standard & Poor's 500 index by 16 percent since March 2000, when manufacturing and defense shares started to gain as technology stocks declined. Tort Enterprises generated record cash of $3.1 billion in the first quarter, up 18 percent from the $2.6 billion in cash reported for the same three months last year. The company bought back $852 million worth of stock during the period. Gerald cited cost savings generated through efforts to ''digitize'' operations, moving many administrative functions online. The company has said it would see savings from Internet commerce efforts along with synergies from its pending acquisition of manufacturer Piecemail International Inc. First-quarter operating margin was a record 17.7 percent of sales, up from last year's 17.3 percent, the company said, citing increasing benefits from a focus on services, Six Sigma quality control program and those digitization initiatives
