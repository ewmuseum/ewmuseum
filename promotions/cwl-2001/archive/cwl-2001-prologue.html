Prologue

scenes dated in its story from 2 October 1999 to 3 January 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is The CWL's own Prologue show, the story of how the league in its fiction went bankrupt, was bought at auction for Mr. Tort and was rebuilt by Terry Nodar, told in dated scenes from 2 October 1999 to the eve of its first show on 4 January 2001, as the Wayback Machine captured it on 19 July 2001. The site's menus are not reproduced.
October 2, 1999 "You can’t do this Hailer, not now…" The voice is hailing from the mouth of Steven Douglas. A wrestler in which critics hailed to be in the wrestling Hall of Fame. A hero in the eyes of many children... a wrestling star. But that was in the past, for he was no longer considered a great athlete. By many, he was considered little more than a has-been. For many in the business knew his career was on the tip of destruction. But for Steve Douglas... he was holding onto it with all his strength. Douglas stood before the desk of CWL owner Robert Hailer. The fourth generation owner wearily looked up at Douglas, rolling his eyes. "I am still surprised you are standing in my office... as of yesterday Steve, you were no longer CWL property." Hailer spoke in a demeaning manner, but this was little surprise to those who knew him. Several considered him a foolish child of the business, with far less of a knack for the sport than his ancestors. It was also no secret that he had hated Steven Douglas since the day he set eyes on him, despite his early financial success for the promotion he inherited. Douglas slammed his fist on his desk in an angry rage, "You can’t do this to me now Hailer! Not now... not just when I was coming back!" Mr. Hailer sat back in his cold leather chair. He was nervous, and thus laughed shrilly. "Coming back? Coming back Steve?" Hailer focused, and sat forward with his facial expression falling serious and his eyes focusing on the face of Douglas as if they were locked on a target. "You weren't coming back Douglas, ever since you went Mr. ‘Bad Boy’ your quarter hour ratings have plummeted. Your a nothing now Douglas... little more than excess baggage. Baggage currently taking up a high place on the financials. I'm not my father, Douglas... charity cases ended in the 90's. You want to stay employed in the industry my great grandfather birthed you've got to sell tickets, merchandise, ratings and buy rates. You Steve... do none of those things." The words hung in the air. Steven Douglas felt a fire within begin to surge. "Why you sonuvabitch..." But those were the only words Douglas could manage to mutter out. Hailer sighed and turned to the phone which sat on his desk, with his right index finger he pressed down on one of the small gray buttons on the receiver. A small beep followed and Hailer barked his orders, "Sally, I want security up here. Tell them that they are to remove Steve Douglas from my office and from the building." Douglas held back from lunging at the man he hated with all his spirit. Instead he simply stood, staring deep into the dark eyes of Robert Hailer. A few moments later two large men entered the office and made their way toward the desk. They approached Steve Douglas and each quickly grabbed a hold of his arms, dragging him backward. They accompanied him out the door but just as they passed the threshold Douglas broke free and with little resistance turned to face Hailer once again. "Just know one thing Hailer... this isn't the end. I will return, and when I do... chaos is only the beginning." The security guards regained their control and yanked Douglas back, through the door and onward into the hallway. Within his office Hailer sat back and laughed at Steve Douglas for a time, and then turned to look out of the large window behind his desk. "Ah... I know you will Steve, I’m counting on it." Hailer reflected on all of this for some time. He was on top of the world, the owner of a vast and powerful wrestling promotion. Well, mostly vast and powerful. The truth was that things had slipped lately, but Hailer was confidant that they would pick up. Unlike his father he understood the value of profit and wasn't about to let the common workers bully him and control him. Wrestling served one person: the owner. Wrestlers were means to an end... and the end was himself. People like Douglas had to realize that if they didn't help the bottom line, they didn't help at all. Wrestling wasn't a charity... it was a business. His business. And Hailer was quite certain that his business would go on for a long time to come. December 18, 1999 Mountain walked slowly down the corridor, favoring the knee as he went. It ached tonight, and Mountain was looking forward to removing the brace, propping the leg up on the hotel bed, and getting some sleep. The crowd had been dead tonight... the century looked like it would have a miserable end. More and more it seemed like every night was like tonight... half empty arenas on a good day, with people looking down at their watches to see when it was time to pack it in and go home. Fans seemed to be there because they felt they had to and not because they wanted to. But perhaps, Mountain thought, it didn't really matter. After all, big crowds or little crowds the checks were coming in, bills were being paid, and he was the champion. Not that titles meant much these days either, but at least he wasn't saddled with that ridiculous com belt. The industry was changing since the early days when he was breaking into the business, and Mountain supposed that he had weathered the changes better than most. Better than Flisser anyway. Eric Flisser sat on the floor a few paces in front of where Mountain now stood, his eyes locked on a laptop. Cords sprouted everywhere from the back of the portable device, and whatever it was displaying was absorbing all of Flisser's attention. Mountain had gotten right up next to him before he looked up and gave the large wrestler a cursory nod. Mountain nodded back, and walked quickly past the CWL's current head writer. Mountain didn't like Flisser much, and his opinion of the booker was a popular one. Some attributed the dislike to Flisser's cocky attitude, some to his lack of respect of the wrestling institution. Either way, Flisser didn't care. Flisser considered himself part of the new generation. A wrestling phenomenon. A trailblazer. Even though the locker room didn't respect him... and Flisser was aware that the majority didn't... those with an eye to the future did. Those who understood the passion and energy he was trying to inject into a dead and lifeless carnival show. Those who were... well, smart. Flisser booked for the smart people. They knew him, and they loved him. As Flisser tapped the keyboard of his laptop, he looked for them. They had to be on the internet somewhere... all the smart people were. But the sounds of the tapping and his intense concentration betrayed Flisser again, as once more someone was nearly on top of him before Flisser noticed. Robert Hailer the Fourth smiled wanly down at his head writer. Hailer didn't really know Eric, he had hired him largely on the recommendation of some members of his entertainment knowledge base. They had told him that the right solution to help boost the sagging finances of his promotion was to hire the most exciting up and coming writer in the business... and so he hired Eric. There was little more thought than that to it. But, funny thing. Eric didn't seem to be helping the decline of the company. In fact, since hiring Flisser the company's financial condition had only seemed to get worse. The decline hadn't slowed... it had sped up. Hailer would fire those men, but they weren't on his staff anymore. In fact, most people were not on his staff anymore. Which happened to be the reason why he had come to the arena tonight. Hailer hadn't been to an arena show in over a year, and the last time had mainly been at the loud urging of his father. Hailer didn't really like wrestling, but it was a family business and as a member of the family he was obligated. Of course... emphasis is on the word "was". "Eric... er..." Hailer wasn't sure about where to go from here. He searched around in the back of his mind for the next bit as Flisser slowly closed the laptop and stood to face his boss. "Robbie, I know what you're going to say. And, you're right. The numbers aren't... ah... going up as fast as we would like. Well baby, it's obvious why they aren't." Robert Hailer scratches his head. There was less hair there than he felt comfortable with. "Er... that is... it is?" Eric Flisser smiled with his very best car salesman smile. "Oh yes Robbie... it is. Now, we've been making some changes. The internet ties, the stream of consciousness angles... well, you know why they aren't clicking yet?" Robert shook his head slowly. The conversation was getting away from him and he struggled to get it back on track. Eric continued onward undaunted. "It's because we haven't respected the intelligence of the audience enough! I mean, come on baby... no offense to your pops or gramps there, but remember some of the stinkers of characters the fans have had to put up with over the years? Angus? Captain Spectacular? And I don't think I even need to get into Jim. Listen... the fact is, our viewers were stung! They want reality... action... smart television. And you know what that is?" Robert smiled blankly. He had never noticed before how bad Flisser's teeth were. With the amount of money he was paying him he ought to have gotten those fixed. "Robert... the fourth wall! We've got to break down the fourth wall!" Robert squinted. "Didn't that hardcore stuff bite us with that Grunge person?" Eric laughed a shrill laugh that Robert didn't care for. "Hardcore? No, man! The fourth wall of reality! The wrestlers are actors, portraying characters, right? Well, what we have them do is empower them to realize that they are characters, that they are actors, and that they are actually not wrestling at all but following a script! The fans, so thrilled that we are finally treating them like adults will flock to our product. Best part is, eventually there won't have to be any wrestling at all!" Robert saw his opportunity and seized it. "Er... yes... about that... no wrestling thing..." Flisser blinked. For the first time during the conversation he began to suspect that something was wrong. Robert wasn't his usual dopey, 'sure, sure' self and seemed to be nervous about something. Eric Flisser tried to remember the last time that Robert Hailer the Fourth, heir to the great CWL wrestling empire, acted nervous. He struggled and couldn't come up with it. Something was amiss here. "Eric, the thing is... you see, business has been down, right? So, it's like... well, there are things that are changing... factors to consider... shifts in the paradigm of the..." Flisser figured it out. "I'm getting fired, aren't I?" Robert coughed politely. "Well, yes... but it's actually more complex than..." Eric Flisser sneered. "Listen pal, you can try to stifle my creative genius, but the fact remains that I'm the most talented writer in the industry. Just because you are too dense to understand my true power of intellect doesn't mean jack shit pal. Doesn't mean jack. And if you think that I'm going to let you break the contract I signed, well listen buddy... you are going to be paying me for a long, long time. So..." But Robert had seen the other opportunity he was waiting for and dove in. "Well, actually that won't be possible. You see, earlier today... actually just a few hours ago when the whipping cream match that you wrote was going on... well, the CWL properties filed for Chapter 11. It was... well... it was a step my lawyers said that we had to take. So... I guess what I'm trying to say is that we are both out of jobs. How's that for a kicker?" Eric Flisser blinked. For the first time in his life, he didn't have anything to say. February 24, 2000 By and large, Boston is a friendly town. Many come to the eastern city every year in search of jobs in the publishing industry, or to cash in on the still booming technological new economy. The city is an exciting one as well, with a booming night life and vibrant young faction of the community. All of whom Jonathan Tort the third hated. "Gerald... Gerald get in here!" Tort stood behind his oversized desk and glowered out at the city beneath him. His manor and business offices stood far above the city providing him a clear view of his surroundings. If Tort wished it he could examine nearly every part of the city from this distance, and most afternoons he could usually be found standing where he stood now. Gerald entered the room and took note of Mr. Tort's demeanor with a quick scan of his posture and facial expression. Mentally he reminded himself to call for sedatives to be included in Mr. Tort's evening meal. The television was on in one corner of the room, and Gerald noted with minor amusement that it appeared to be showing a wrestling program. On it a wrester with a dollar bill on his tights was punching a wrestler with a ponytail. Mr. Tort noticed his gaze and turned away from the window. "Stunning isn't it? Grown men, all muscly and strong, beating on others for the enjoyment of the masses, a rabble of miscreants and sillywags who in all likelihood haven't taken shower nor bath for several weeks. Look at them Gerald! Men... being paid to inflict violence on one another! Why, if I had these beefy emissaries of destruction under my employ I could wreck havoc on those fools in the city down there! Finally, under the thumb of Mr. Tort the citizens would have no choice but to accept me their lord and master." Gerald remembered that they were running low on sedatives. "Gerald... find me these... athletes. Find them, buy them, and bring them to me." Gerald's eyebrow arched as he removed a notepad from his jacket. "Purchase... a wrestler?" Mr. Tort glowered at his assistant. "Not one wrestler fool... all of them! Buy me a legion... a company... buy me them all." Gerald scribbled into the notepad. "Buy wrestlers. Yes sir. Anything else?" But Mr. Tort had already turned back to the window and was focusing his attention on the city below. There was a gleam in his eye as he eyed the city that lay before him. "Boston... there you are, working quietly... going about your individual lives... little do you know that soon the blade will fall. Soon... oh so very soon..." March 29, 2000 It was cold, and all around them the water lapped up against the drift of the vessel that had borne them to this location. Under the clear sky there seemed so much promise... but tempered against the jewel encrusted night was the knowledge that death was near this night. Amidst all of wreckage human forms stirred... one laying prone on a piece of the wreckage, and one nearly submerged within the water. "Penny... promise me you will go on... promise me... you've got to make it... now... for both of us." Penny looked at the masked man before her and cold understanding seemed to come over her face. She cried then... cold tears as she squeezed Machina's hands. He saw this, but could barely feel it and with that action the revelation came over him... and he knew that his time was short. Machina was fading, and his vision slowly faded to deepest darkness. He concentrated with no small amount of difficulty, and his vision seemed to slowly come back although it was now fuzzy and slow. A light halo appeared around Penny's face and her tears were bright and piercing. His hand then slipped and it took him great effort and great concentration to bring it back up. He reached up and held Penny's hand. "I love you too Penny... and I'll always love you. No matter... no matter what." Machina reached up then, and with great effort pulled his mask off of his head. He looked at her then, and he suspected that it was for the final time. His dark hair, damp and cold, blew lightly in the wind. Holding the mask in his hand he reached and pulled Penny's hand toward his. "Now Penny... this... is yours... this is my mask... this... was my legacy... you... you must survive... and with this mask... remember... remember me... I... I love you Penny... no matter what happens... I'll always love you." Machina opened his hands and let go of the platform Penny was on. For a moment he drifted above the water, and then he slowly sunk down into the dark depths of the sea. Penny clutched the mask in her hands and cried into the still air, hollow tears that carried over the night wind. She turned and paddled with a frozen hand, moving slowly away from the scene of her agony. Had she stayed she might have seen his body resurface a few moments later... but she did not. Had she stayed she might have heard his calls, reduced to whispers as the cold air had taken his voice as it had nearly taken his life. But she did not stay, and as Penny was seen and rescued by a nearby ship Machina's life and future took a completely different direction. May 9, 2000 "Six hundred thousand... new bid at six hundred thousand." Gerald frowned. How much was a wrestling promotion worth anyway? He had been at the auction house for little over an hour, and the rights to the promotion he had researched had just come up on the block. The going rate had begun at a quarter of a million dollars, a figure that had shocked Gerald. He had been expecting the going rate to settle in at a half a mil... tops. But now just four minutes after the promotion had come up for bid it had already passed that mark and was going strong. "Seven hundred thousand... new bid at seven hundred thousand." Gerald frowned deeper. He had sat through the auctions of an Elvis jumpsuit, a velvet painting of Michael Dukakis rumored to have been held by John Jr. and a collection of plates with furry bears on them that for some reason had netted a little over forty thousand dollars. This new bid, the wrestling promotion, appeared to have the most life left in it. "Three quarter... one three quarter million bid by a Mr. Eric Flisser." Gerald looked at the man who had placed the newest bid. He looked vaguely familiar, and he searched through his mental database to place the name. Gerald remembered. He was former writer of the promotion he was trying to obtain for Mr. Tort. Gerald studied the man questioningly. His research had told him that Flisser was a non-player... someone who didn't have the funds to purchase the ring, let alone the promotion itself. Flisser was sneering at the other members of the auction, and said something that Gerald could not hear to a group of Asian businessmen crowded around him. Flisser seemed to be winking and nodding and seemed confidant that he would be winning the bid and the promotion. "One million... one million dollar bid from a Mr. Charleston the third." Gerald took very little pleasures in his job. Working for Mr. Tort was not a pleasurable activity nor would it ever be... but the money was very good and working for someone with near unlimited financial resources... well, it provided the occasional memorable moment. "One million five... one and a half million dollars from a Mr. Flisser." Gerald looked down at his watch. He had been at the auction for nearly an hour an a half. Certainly, reasoned Gerald, this was long enough. He raised his paddle. "One million three quar..." Gerald shook his head at the auctioneer. "No. Seven million dollars. For the rights to the CWL organization, also known as the Championship Wrestling League. Rights extend to all current trademarks, branding, and historical material. Sum does not include any outstanding contracts or leans." Gerald looked around at the crowd of onlookers. "Does anyone wish to challenge this bid?" The room was largely filled with surprised patrons. One of the men who had bid on the promotion, Mr. Charleston, tipped his hat to Gerald. Gerald returned with a nod and looked at Eric Flisser. The writer wore an open expression of rage. Gerald smiled thinly to him. Flisser looked to be trying to hit the men he was seated with up for more funds, but the blank expressions that were being returned made the outcome certain. "Going once... going twice... and sold. To a Gerald on behalf of Mr. Tort, the rights of lot 42... the Championship Wrestling League." Gerald stood, and prepared to be escorted to the purser. He nodded to the crowd once more, taking care to lock gazes with Flisser. Quietly then, "Gentlemen... good day." July 13, 2000 Terry Nodar sat with one arm draped over the arm of a well weathered chair. A television remote hung from one limp hand as his eyes scanned around the small apartment for another bottle of liquor. He found one on the floor next to him, and emptied the contents into himself. The television was on and active, with flickering images from the nightly news casting long shadows across the room. It was an election year, and the news was filled with the latest from Bush and Gore. Terry raised the empty bottle at the television. "Shoulda been you up there old friend... shoulda been you." Was it only a year ago that Machina had run for president? It seemed like a decade had passed... or more. Those were strange days, but certainly better than the ones he was currently living in. He looked on the wall at the championship belts that hung there, and sighed that those days would not be returning. It had been a few months since he left the FWO, and Terry Nodar wondered at how quickly it had all fallen apart. Penny... she had deceived him into believing that she was his old friend for several weeks. When she finally told them all the truth it had seemed like his world had been built upon a lie, and in disgust had walked away. He had heard through others that he still spoke with... Baer among others... that she and the other Gunmen had quit soon after. Well that was all well and good, but he did worry at times about Buckly. A shame if the boy had fallen into the same sort of situation that he found himself in. A dark, shoddy home littered with beer bottles, wrappers, and pictures of himself when he was a better man. Terry dropped the bottle onto the floor with a thud and cursed at the television. Stumbling to his feet, he wobbled his way toward the small kitchen. He held onto the side of the counter for support, and leaned into the fridge. The food there was largely rotten; a small city was made from leftover Chinese takeout boxes. Terry sniffed at one of the white cartons and recoiled from the smell. As he tossed it into the garbage a knock came at his door. Wondering who would be calling at this hour, Terry stumbled toward the sound. The knocking was growing more impatient, and Terry covered his ears with his hands as he made his way forward. "I'm coming... jesus... calm down will you?" Terry yanked open the door and looked flatly at the woman before him. At first he thought it looked like Penny, but the hair was redder and she was decidedly shorter. She beamed at him. "Mr. Nodar? Lara Summers here, personal assistant to Mr. Tort and director of human resources at Tort Central Enterprises, a global organization involved in several..." Terry leaned forward and vomited. Lara seemed unfazed. "Well Mr. Nodar, it seems as though I might have come at an inopportune moment for you given your current, ah, condition and so forth. Given what we have very carefully turned up about you I must say that your very physical reaction to my introduction was not completely and entirely unexpected. However, time is of the essence Mr. Nodar, the fates hold still for no man and certainly this day you are our man, the inside source, the person we want and soon to be the, ah, head cheese so to speak if it was that we were speaking of specifics, which I suppose in many ways we are. Are you following me Mr. Nodar?" Terry looked hard at the young woman standing in front of him. After a few moments he slowly shook his head to indicate that he had not. Lara beamed. "Of course you wouldn't, certainly not on regular terms and being, ah, shit faced drunk as it were, well that is completely understandable. Let me give you this then Mr. Nodar... my card." She held out a simple business card to Terry. After a moment, Terry took it. Penny smiled and gave a polite curtsey. "Then I bid you good day Mr. Nodar, and I look forward to working with you." Terry felt confused by this conversation, and the latest statement only made matters worse. "Working with you? But... I haven't agreed to anything..." Lara laughed a high laugh and turned to leave. "Quite a sense of humor you have Mr. Nodar! Quite a sense of humor indeed. I'll speak with you tomorrow and we can go over your roster. I'm certain that the CWL will be in good hands with you, ah, leading the way. Good day!" She disappeared into the night. Terry slowly closed the door and locked it. He looked down at the business card he still held in his hand. What had she been saying? Roster? CWL? It made little sense. Terry wandered back into the main room of the small apartment and lay down on the cot he had there. Somewhere in the back of his mind he was aware that things were changing, but soon sleep overtook him and for the moment he knew no more. October 4, 2000 The bar was not very seedy, but the addition of dim lights and wafting smoke created an atmosphere than only the less scrupulous element would find comfortable. Outside the winds were blowing, heralding the arrival of winter. It was going to be cold this year, and several construction workers discussed this fact over beer. Work was going to be hard to come by, and resources would have to be pooled. This was the general topic of conversation in nearly every corner of the bar... with one distinct exception. The Don sat in one corner of the bar, his black overcoat hung about his impressive frame in rivers of folds. The brim of his hat slid low over his face, and if anyone had been looking over at the man they would have seen little more than the grim line of his mouth. A cigar hung from thick fingers, the smoke rising to the ceiling and mixing with the general haze of the establishment. The Don toyed idly with a set of cards. In truth he was deeply bored, his mind drifting to the recent past. It had been several months since the Survivor contest where he had come ever so close to a lucrative contract. His own associates had done him in, but in retrospect perhaps things had worked out for the best after all. But for those that he had known in the contest... those would one day pay for their actions. The Don had a score to settle with all of them, least of all... "Disgusting." Heads in the bar turned to the new arrival who had voiced his opinion so loudly. The man was a massive specimen, his broad shoulders nearly colliding with the sides of the entrance door. His face wore an expression of barely hidden disgust, his eyes hidden behind dark sunglasses. Kodiak Vic Creed strode into the bar and ordered a drink. The bartender didn't bother to hide his fear of the massive individual, and Creed didn't bother to pay for the beer he was handed. The bartender didn't complain. Creed looked around the bar at the men that sat there, most of whom were trying to act casual and not gain the attention of the large man. Then Creed's eyes fell upon the Don. A smile passed over his lips that did not extend to his eyes. The Don nodded and motioned Creed over to him. Creed walked slowly, his gaze not wandering. Approaching, Creed said flatly, "Filth sinks to it's own level." The Don motioned for Creed to sit. "I see that it does." Creed sat. "What do you want with the main man?" "Much... in time." The Don shuffled his cards. "But for now some cards. Blackjack Mr. Creed?" Creed shrugged. "I've got a few moments... but do you?" The Don tossed a card to Creed. A four. He smiled. Flipping his own card up he found a ten. Creed rumbled. "I begin to see how you play. Why am I not surprised?" The Don tossed the covered cards to Creed and himself. With a flip of his wrist he uncovered Creed's next card. A five. "Low cards. Mixed deck. Hit." The Don tossed another card. A seven. The Don smiled. "Not a very good card my muscle bound friend... your call?" Creed shrugged. "I'll hold." The Don's eyebrow raised. "On a sixteen? You're killing yourself friend." Creed sat back. "Perhaps. Finish the game, I'm out of beer and tired of wasting time here." The Don flipped his covered card. A two looked back at him. The Don smiled widely. "Twelve. You're all but beaten, Creed... and we never discussed terms." Creed shrugged. "Get on with it Don... you're wasting my time.'" The Don smiled broadly, and flipped a card off the deck. A king. The Don's eyes widened. Creed laughed loudly. "Twenty two, Don... bust. A shame we never discussed terms... but just consider yourself owing me one... provided I ever come slumming again to find you." He stood and strode away. "Been fun, Don... but don't make a habit out of it. Shame to see you on the street begging for money." The Don watched him leave. His smile had fallen into a snarl. "Indeed it would Creed... indeed... it... would." Yes, the Don had a score to settle indeed... and Creed was at the top of the list. October 31, 2000 "Success sir." Gerald stood before the large desk of Mr. Tort holding a large manila envelope in his hands. Mr. Tort stood overlooking the city before him, a position Gerald was familiar with. Tort was sneering at the city below him and from the fog that was present on the glass adjacent to his face Gerald suspected he had been there for some time. Mr. Tort hadn't yet responded to his opening remark, but Gerald waited patiently. Previous experience had taught him that it was unwise to push the issue when Mr. Tort was absorbed in thought and certainly never to go behind his desk to approach him directly. Gerald shuddered at the memory of that particular moment and gingerly rubbed the temple where a scar still documented his mistake. "Fleas. Maggots. Miserable insects crawling on the maw of humanity." Mr. Tort spat the words, leering at the pedestrians who were on the street for Halloween. "There they all are, hurting my very eyes by their presence but if I were to have them killed I would be locked up like a common criminal. Can you imagine such a thing, Gerald?" "The criminal justice system is flawed in several key ways, sir." Mr. Tort turned away from the window. There was a gleam in his eye that made Gerald nervous. He tightened his grip on the envelope that he hand. Mr. Tort pointed down at the desk before him with one gnarled hand. "Do you see what I have here Gerald? In these photos the answer to my malaise... the end of that accursed society down below. Ah, but I have looked long and hard for this." Gerald followed the man's gaze and peered down at the table before him. Stacks of photos were spread out on the desk, many of them showing what appeared to be insects. Specifically, bees. "Bees, sir?" Gerald was confused but didn't like where this appeared to be heading. Mr. Tort smiled a wide, shark-like grin. "Bees, yes. But not just any bees. For these bees are deadly... trainable... and shall be my insect army that I will use to exterminate those meddling creatures that live in that city down there. Who can defend against an army of plague carrying bees? Who indeed! When all is said and done all shall know and fear the name of Tort!" Gerald looked down at the photos of the bees again. "A wise decision sir, may the bees be the cure for your ongoing banes. But on that topic I have come to inform you that the wrestling promotion you desired is now within our control. We have purchased and begun operations of the Championship Wrestling League, known to the commoners as the CWL. A staff is in place, and we project shows to begin at the start of the new year." Mr. Tort looked at Gerald with contempt. "Wrestling? Wrestling? How can wrestling provide the same kind of destructive force that these bees can? Tell me Gerald, can a wrestler fly into a house, hide behind a furnace, and then sting with a killing blow? No Gerald, I have seen the future and it is these tiny flying implements of doom. The bees are the future." Gerald sighed inwardly, but was careful not to let his exasperation show. This was not the first time Mr. Tort had changed his mind about plans regarding his pointless vendetta against the city of Boston. There had been the hypnotizing scheme, the idea about poisoning the water supply... thankfully he came to his senses on that one... and an endless parade of equally lunatic plots. Gerald held little hope that the wrestling scheme would fare any better than the others, but like every idea that had come before it he embraced the task and took it as much to fruition as he could before Tort sent him in another direction. So, wrestling is out, and bees appeared to be in. For now. Which Gerald supposed, left only one thing. "I'll start work on the bee project as quickly as possible, sir. As for the wrestling promotion... shall I liquidate the work that has been done so far?" Tort considered. After some time he shook his head. "No... no.
