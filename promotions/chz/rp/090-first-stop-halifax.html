First Stop: Halifax
Written: 28th July 2002

Jim Surge looks down at his scuffed brown leather boots and kicks the seat ahead of him. An elderly man with a wrinkled nose whips his head around and annoyedly yells: “do you mind?” to which the Arizona cowboy sighs and nods his apology. The members of Redemption Song are checking out the close quarters on the VIA Rail Canada train from Halifax, Nova Scotia to Quebec City, Quebec. At each city, Redemption Song are scheduled to appear with their new valet Misty to test market her to the Canadian audience.

“You’d think we’d get a private car for fuck’s sake,” Jim mutters nastily aloud as he purposely kicks the seat in front of him again, this time with added strength. A small “oof” is heard from the elderly man in the seat ahead of Surge, this time there is no talkback from the man as if realizing he is in a no-win situation against the huge tag-team wrestling prospect. Surge sighs deeply, realizing the irony of the situation, or perhaps the coincidence of it all (he could never get that straight) as he cannot believe Redemption Song’s current prospect of a prolonged stay in CHZ despite killer credentials.

Ben Nighthorse Campbell is not in such a mood. In fact, the Apache Ghostrider is in full spirits as he looks across the aisle at Jim, with Misty resting her delicate, sleeping face against the cowboy’s shoulder. Ben smiles widely and shakes his head in disbelief as he notes his half-brother’s “vibe.”

“Hey, we’re paying for this ourselves out of the Watkins Group budget,” Campbell begins in a highly animated tone which does not at all suggest a hint of Native American heritage, “which, by the way, we’ve been abusing an awful lot lately.”

Surge makes a strange face with his mouth puckering up and starts to shake his head from side to side in a “no” motion.

“Have you given any thought about turning control of the Watkins Group over to Tom Core? That way he could actually be our manager you know? Your own uncle, uh, our own uncle trusted this man.”

Jim raises his right hand up to his face to feel the stubble that has grown in since his appearance on OSW’s Slam! last night. Surge has been burning the candle from both ends starting last night, going to an after Slam! party until the wee hours of the morning, then driving back to New York City from Hershey Park, Pennsylvania and then an express flight up to Nova Scotia to begin the train tour!! Jim’s face is that of someone who is close to making up his mind, but is trying to examine every possible outcome so as to make the right decision.

“I’m not ready to do that yet,” Jim says in a slow western drawl, his head pointed back down at his boots.

Just then, the door to the car opens and Tom Core walks in dressed sharply as always in a gray suit with black wrap-around sunglasses.

“Gentlemen,” Core begins in his high-pitched, whiny voice, “they are ready for you. Wake this chick up, eh?”

“What are you Canadian now?” Surge asks Core, gently shaking Misty awake as he does.

“What’s that supposed to mean?” Core questions, his face becoming more animated as if he would defend himself from the much larger cowboy.

“Nothing, let it drift,” Campbell intercedes, reverting back to his calming monotone. Core and Surge look at each other and nod in agreement as Misty finally comes to.

“What’s going on?” the beautiful raven-haired young woman whispers up to Surge.

“Time to wake up Mist,” Jim answers. “We have to flaunt you to the Halifax crowd to see what they think.”

“This is really degrading you know,” Misty says as she stands up in front of her seat and slowly arches her back to stretch. Jim stops everything he’s doing to watch her perfect body move. After shaking his head clear, the cowboy grabs Misty by the arm and walks out to the train’s side door. At the side door, Core has set up a small podium from which hang alternating red, white, & blue American banners and red & white Canadian banners. Core and Ben Campbell are already standing at the door and nod when Surge and Misty make it to the door to face the crowd. An assembled crowd of about 250 cheer and whistle at the sight of Misty. After a few moments though, Surge starts giving the finger to the crowd and the cheers turn to hisses and boos.

“You friggin’ IDIOTS! PREPARE FOR YOUR REDEMPTION!!!” Surge screams into the microphone attached to the top of the podium. The crowd assembled to gawk at Misty start to boo and yell cusses back at Surge and the podium. Ben Campbell then helps keep the heat going by starting to give the finger to the crowd.

“Uh Jim,” Core tries to whisper up to the cowboy, wiping off some sweat which continues to form on his forehead and upper lip, “this is supposed to be playing nice to show off some Misty T&A!!”

“Shut up,” Jim says softly to Core, “SHUT UP!” he screams into the microphone intended for the rest of the crowd. “CHZ? CHZ?? This CHZ thing wants us to face two greasy Mexicans in our debut match!! Us, Redemption Song, against two greasy Mexicans.”

Ben Campbell has a deep expression of hatred wash across his face like high tide. His large, red forehead pulses at the temples as he makes his way to the podium. Even though much smaller than his white-man half-brother, Campbell’s aura seems to shove Surge out of the way allowing Campbell to stand front and center at the podium. Holding a pointed index finger out to the crowd, he begins in his mesmerizing, ancient monotone:

“People, people,” Campbell moves his index finger in a wave motion which includes all of the local Halifax audience of 250, “all of you people. You shall be redeemed. We fight for you and you’re too stupid to realize it. And then, you expect us to perform for you against Mexicans?! MEXICANS!! Mexicans, who offered rewards for Apache and Navaho scalps. Mexicans, who helped the American army in several widespread murder massacres of Native Americans. Mexicans, who never even learn English and then complain when people don’t understand their Spanish, even though they’re in the United States or Canada!!”

“BEN!” Tom Core whispers in a loud whisper so as to get the Apache’s attention. He didn’t want Ben to continue, for fear of getting into racist territory. “End this.”

Ben doesn’t look at Core but hears his manager and does as the lawyer wishes.

“People of Halifax, this is the first stop of our 7-city Canadian tour. If we do decide to sign with CHZ, rest assured that we will not stop until those maple leaf championship belts are around our waist. And we will accomplish this feat by any means necessary!!!”

Misty walks up to Ben while playing with her black hair and puts her arms over his shoulder, allowing the Canadian audience to gawk at her tight, explosive body. Jaws drop as the foursome make it back to their seats aboard the VIA Rail Canada train.

“I have to put up with this all the way to Quebec City?” the elderly man with the crooked nose annoyedly asks as the Watkins Group members walks past him to their seats.

“Shut up tough guy,” Surge yells at the old man, getting up in his face.

“Oh, you’re really tough,” the old-timer replies, “picking on an old man.”

“Shut up,” Surge says.

“Should I prepare for my redemption?” the old man jibes again as Surge takes his seat next to Misty.

“Shut up old man before I hide your dentures,” Surge says.

The old man laughs as the train pulls out of Halifax Station.
