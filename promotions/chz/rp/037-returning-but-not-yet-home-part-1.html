Returning, but not yet home, Part #1
Written: 24/6/02

(OOC-What I say about the old CHZ staff in this RP is not true, just making a good story)

A small white Lear-jet touches down at St. Andrews airport on the outskirts of the suburbs of Winnipeg. The plane’s wheels skid on the tarmac, leaving marks of burnt rubber as the pilot brings the plane to a halt and guides it to the waiting set of stairs. The doors are pushed open as the stairs are pushed into place and out steps CHZ superstar Steve Brent, a CHZ sports bag slung over his shoulder, his trademark black trench-coat flapping around him in the breeze, Steve steps down the stars, with a feeling of deja-vu, the last time he was in Canada, in Toronto, at the Toronto City Airport, stepping off a plane with a CHZ sports bag slung over his shoulder, of course back then it was a different CHZ, run by some fools who ran the company into the ground before Steve had even had his first match at NBK Pride.

Petapaw, the old owner, was trying to bring his own company down with the NBK, and he succeeded, but it was also the former vice-president’s lack of booking sense that ran the company into the ground. Steve had made a verbal contract with the former vice-president, before getting a sweeter deal with the now-defunct WWA, allowing him a World Title shot, Steve went to WWA, but after winning the title the fed closed, leaving Steve as a free agent, and he chose to go to CHZ. He was spoken of as a big signing, the WWA champion coming exclusively to CHZ, but out of bad blood, because of the last deal that fell through, the former VP booked Steve in the opening match, calling it a ‘rookie’s match’ when Steve was anything but a rookie.

But the show fell through, Steve showed up a the arena, along with a few other CHZ stars, to find nothing but an empty arena, thousands of fans outside, on the verge of a riot, but no a single thing set up on the inside, it was clear that the pervious owners had run with the money, but they left the blame on the shoulders of the superstars, so much blame that few ever made it anywhere in the wrestling world, luckily one of those few was Steve Brent.

Steve walks down the remaining stairs and towards the terminal, he steps through the revolving door only to see a face he wished he never would see again.

“Steve, Steve, remember me? Your old manager, Joe Fergusson?” Steve walks on as if ignoring his former manager.

“Steve! Steve?” Joe grabs Steve’s arm, “Don’t you remember me? Your old manager, the one who got you the contract with the old CHZ?”

Steve stares a Joe coldly, “The manager who almost locked me into a deal with CHZ, when I got a better offer from the WWA?”

Joe seems taken aback, but tries to make his old client change his mind, “And the same manager who got you a new contract with CHZ when the WWA fell through.”

Steve stares a Joe for a second, “The deal that left me high and dry, and the manager who did nothing but soak up what was left of my money and get me nowhere after the old CHZ fell on its ass, don’t try and make me have fond memories Joe, because there aren’t any. I’ve made my own way back here, and that’s how it’s gonna stay, me, on my own, making my own deals, and staying away from some leech.” Steve pushes past Joe, but Joe follows him, bringing up moments from the past.

“Don’t you remember how the old CHZ paid your way so you could visit parts of the world you had never visited before? It was my influence that got you there”

“The world, don’t you mean Canada? There’s nothing to see but leagues of snow, ice, and a couple of bears.” Steve replies, barely paying attention to Joe.

“Then why are you back here? There must’ve been something good in Canada to make you come back, what was it?” Joe pleads with Steve.

“The money,” Steve looks around at some of the people at the airport, “And the blondes.”

“See, remember all those press conferences I got you, I bet you met some top chicks there didn’t you Steve?” Joe asks, sounding victorious.

“Don’t patronise me, other people are a waste of time.” Steve tries to put as many people between Joe and himself, but the short, balding manager manages to keep up with the well-built athlete.”

“Don’t you remember the first time you came to Canada Steve? You almost caused a riot at the Toronto Airport, that must’ve been fun.”

Steve looks down menacingly at Joe for a second, “I’ll be the one starting the riot on you in a second if you don’t (expletive) off!”

“Alright, alright, alright already, I’m just trying to meet up with an old friend, that’s all.” Joe backs off.

“Joe, you always used to say that I tried to screw with people that had more power than me, and you were right,” Steve leans down, face to face with Joe, “So can you imagine what I do to people with less power than me?” Steve spits on Joe, who backs away in fright. Steve spins on his heel and follows the signs that lead to the exit of the airport. Pushing past people on his way, not caring who he pushes, Steve makes his way to the exit and hails a cab.

“Where to?” The cabbie asks, “The hotel closest to the Winnipeg arena, I don’t care about the quality, just take me there.

As the cab pulls away from the airport Steve has another ominous feeling of Deja-vu and looks out the window, with only one though passing through his mind, they said he’d come home, but was this really his home? He’d never been to Winnipeg, he was born and grew up in Chicago, Illinois, could an American ever feel at home in Canada?
