To Reminisce
Written: 23/7/02

He sat in the room, alone, with only a golden trimmed dagger to keep him company. The room itself almost complimented the feel of it all, seeming completely empty itself, though inside it was lively. It was packed with three rows of computers, each monitoring something else with different images and texts flashing across the screens. Some of the computers contained bright green 3D maps of the world where others listed names of men and women who had been dealt with by The Disciples and their current status.

'The Disciples' Matt said to himself. The young wrestler was born and raised in Vancouver, comfortably outside the city, though these days he blended in a little too well. His nose length hair was dyed a dark red and hung in tangled strands in front of his face. His features were dark, hiding a rather vibrant and humane personality, and his eyes were modified so that they were silver in color, a symbol of his status among The Disciples. As for his clothing... Matt found the days of his casual wear over, and sported a long, black leather cloak that concealed the weapons and tools of his profession, and had a silver cross embedded on the back. Other than that, he more mostly black and various signs of his religion. Perhaps most obvious about the man who looked nothing short of a deranged rock star was the golden plated bible that was chained to his belt with a bladed cross barely visible in its texture, another symbol of his status.

Matt shook his head a little as he thought about The Disciples. The elite religious organization had caused their share of problems for him, yet, they were the reason he was who he now was. They handled all the things the churches couldn't, and they kept the lid on it all. From exorcisms to divine messages, The Disciples kept an eye on the world and made sure no one saw what they did. Matt had dealt with his fair share of exorcisms and hauntings back in Europe, but of course, North America was that much worse. The churches refused to believe in possessions in modern times and when it bit them in the ass, it all fell into Matt's lap.

His latest case resulted in what seemed to be his failure and the death of a young woman who had been through possessions for countless years. She was Matt's age, and her name was Cynthia Smith. Cute, charming, nice... and now, dead. After he had completed the exorcism, or so he thought, she was found the next day in her room, hanging by her neck in her closet. Since then, Matt had thought of nothing else, and it was slowly eating away at him like a virus in his head.

The door creaked open and an older man casually walked in. He looked grumpy, with dark creases around his face and a graying hair that was receding. He was wearing a suit much like Matt's, though he was shorter and built nothing like a wrestler. The old man smirked when he noticed that Matt had stuck the blade of his dagger into the oak table he sat at and was flicking the side of the blade, spinning it a few times while he stared off into the distance.

'I hope you know that table never did nuthin' to you kid' the old man barked as he sat across from Matt. 'That and yer' payin' for it!'

Matt looked up and gave the old man a cold glare. 'There's a phone right over there. Call someone who cares' he muttered as he returned his gaze to the spinning knife.

'Kid, I'm your supervisor. You don't talk to me like that.'

'I just did, X...'

X glared at Matt for a moment, looking like he was about to explode; instead, he grabbed Matt's wrist as the wrestler went to spin the dagger again and frowned. 'Look, I know this is gettin' to ya' kid, but you gotta' let it go! In this line o' work, you win some, and ya' lose the rest. You gotta' accept the good with the bad, ya' know?'

Matt shook his head a little and let the dagger drop. 'X... man, you don't understand. I failed man, me all alone, and it caused someone to kill herself. I mean, do you know what that does to my head? All that faith in God, all that praying and all that faith that I'm supposed to get from being a damn good wrestler... for what? To fail?'

'C'mon Matt. I don't pretend to know what the man upstairs be thinkin', but I know that he's always got somethin' cookin' in his holy noggin. What happened, happened for a reason, and just 'cuz you don't know about it yet don't mean jack! Give it some time... it'll all become clear.'

Matt pulled his arm away from X and rubbed his head. 'Man, this is just messing with my head more. Just give me a few days you oldie, I'll be good as new eh?'

X blinked and gave Matt a smack upside the head. 'Oldie!? Boy, when I was your age I...'

Matt grinned as X realized he was sounding as old as Matt claimed he was. Matt leaned forward as his grin faded and the talk became serious again.

'Hey X?' he asked?

'Yeah?'

'Do you think a dream can mean anything? I mean, scientifically speaking, they mean nada, but...'

X raised a brow. 'You joined a religion to learn science? What, and did ya' join wrestling ta' learn ballet? Spit it out kid.'

'A'ight, it goes like this. I was sleeping...'

X rolled his eyes. 'I woulda' never guessed.'

Matt smirked a little at the comment. 'Hush old man. Anyways, I started to dream, and she was there. You know, Cynthia, but she was surrounded by a light and she looked just like an angel. I don't remember much about it, but she kept telling me that it wasn't my fault. Now, isn't that something she'd say to me since I blame myself for failing?'

'Maybe,' X chimed in, 'but maybe she's right. You did yer' best, you have it yer' all and in the end, maybe it wasn't enough. I dunno' how God sorts out the naughty from the nice, but if she was surrounded by a light and in yer' dream, she didn't go to Hell, and that's somethin' big on yer' agenda kid. If she don't blame you, then why blame yourself?'

Matt tapped the table in thought and shrugged. 'Yeah, I suppose you're onto something, but hearing she died hit pretty hard, ya' know? Then there was the deal with The Confessors and that was like Hell itself.'

The confessors were a branch off The Disciples that dealt with the members of the group itself when they broke their own rules. Matt was absent from the ring for a week because he was being interrogated by The Confessors, who were ruthless and known to use tools of torture that hadn't been changed since medieval times. Matt escaped that fate and was released without knowing the cause... not that he complained.

X gave Matt a hard slap on the shoulder. 'Ah well, cheer up kid! You've already been promoted from the PWA and now you're in a real federation. Better yet, yer' in a Canadian federation. There's no place like home right?'

Matt grinned. 'You know it old man. My eh saying self is back and that's an upper in a wave of downers man.'

X grinned as well, but the look faded as he pulled out a folder and slipped it over to Matt and paused. 'You know what this is? It's a Class 3 this time.'

Matt eyed the plain folder with a glare for a moment before slowly opening it. Staring back at him was a picture of a woman with dark hair, model-like features and deep blue eyes. According to the report on the next page, she was a Disciple Class 3, which meant she was seeing and experiencing signs from God. Matt knew how rare this case was, and only five other people had ever been a 3 in recorded history. All of them had been women, two of them whose names were locked up in the Disciple archive, and the first recorded ever being the famed Joan of Arc.

'She's been seeing and experiencing things,' X explained. 'She says that she's been hearing the whispers of the saints in her ears, and one of our investigators got from her, completely accurate descriptions of the saints. She has also been experiencing things if you would turn the page' X said as he motioned to the folder.

'Whoa,' Matt exclaimed as he came across a picture of the woman's back that had been cut and broken into a detailed picture of a war between Hell and Heaven.

'Yes, and that took over a month to form, inch by painful inch. Even now, the doctors she has seen cannot explain it nor can they explain why the wound refuses to heal. She has lost no blood at all from the wounds and any attempts to remove the depiction from her back has failed. Rather miserably if I might add.'

Matt looked up from the picture and spoke in a lower voice as if someone was listening. 'Any ideas?'

'Turn the page ya' dolt!' X exclaimed in a voice that seemed to engulf Matt's. The wrestler did so and began to read over the report.

'Visions of the future, visions of the biblical past, talking with the saints, experiencing the sufferings of Christ... ouch. Is there anything this woman isn't going through?'

'Hell,' X bluntly stated, 'though I'm sure that'll change now that yer' on the case right?'

Matt stifled a snicker and finished flipping through the pages. 'Okay, I don't see any problems with this one. Do demons, no spirits, no pain and suffering for me... sounds good. This should help me get over what happened with Cynthia I need it man.'

X slowly reached into his coat pocked and lowered his head to the table for a few moments in thought, and seemed to decide against what he was going to do, and placed his hands back on the table. Matt noticed this and gave his superior a quizzical look as the long pause stretched its length to the max.

'What's in there X?' Matt quietly asked. X just shrugged and didn't answer. With that, Matt, to the disbelief of X, lunged forward and pulled en envelope out of X's pocked. The old man tried to grab for the envelope, but Matt quickly darted out of the way, jumped over the table, tore open the package and unfolded a letter. After whizzing through the short letter, Matt's expression became angered from interested as he dropped the letter on the table and eyed X like a hawk.

'So that's why they released me?' he asked under gritted teeth.

'Yes...' X mumbled.

'You gotta' be kidding me man! It's the church, they can't be doing that!'

'I know... but they didn't want their failure emerging. I got it from the higher ups back in Europe this morning. You're not supposed to see it.'

Matt slammed his fist against the table that knocked the dagger off as it clanged against the cold floor. Matt didn't take his eyes off the letter as he calmly put his shades on and cracked his neck.

'Matt,' X called, 'what are you thinking of doing?'

Matt sighed a harsh sigh and whispered in an equally as harsh tone. 'You've read it, I know you have. I do everything I can to reach out to Cynthia, and even through all the bad experiences and the haunting memories, the one thing that makes it all worth while is knowing I saved her. Then it feels like I was hit with a truck for the next week because I hear she killed herself because I didn't get rid of her demons!' Matt paused and picked up the letter, spitting on the wording and tossing it on the floor. 'I find out the church makes her death look like a suicide to cover their own tracks, making my efforts in vein, and taking a life because they didn't help her before I did!' he exclaimed in a louder voice.

'I know,' X whispered back, 'but you can't just go out and change the church! It doesn't work like that kid.'

Matt lowered his head and chuckled coldly. 'You know, whether anyone sees it this way or not, we work for God. What the church did was no act of God man!'

'Let The Disciples handle it kid. They'll take dare of it their way.'

Matt tilted his head a little and gave X a cold grin. 'Haven't you heard, I am a Disciple?'
