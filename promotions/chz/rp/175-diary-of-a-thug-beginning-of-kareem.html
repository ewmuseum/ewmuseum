Diary of a Thug: Beginning of Kareem
Written: June 2nd, 1980

Voice:

'The Beginning....'


June 2nd, 1980

'Change The Channel.'

'Okay, Daddy.' A 4 year old african-american boy crawls across the open room to a small television set on a wodden box along a crusty wall. The person on the television screen is unreconizable and by the looks of it, the television is barly working. The Young boy turns the rotted old knob of the panal on the tv. 'What do you want to watch, Dada?'

'Ah- ****. Put the news on.' A older african-american man commanded to the toddler. He sat on an old, ripped lazy-boy as he eagrly awaited to see what was going on in the world. The little 5 year old returns to his position he was laying in on a gaudy red rug.

A young white woman with brown hair appears on the screen. She wore a purple suit with a white under-shirt. 'And in other news today' The woman says with a hint of fakeness. 'A drive-by shooting occurred at the local middle school, Franklin Junior High. Where 1 child was killed and 2 were injured.'

'Man...Kid's these days.' The man speaks with a groan in his voice. 'I'll tell you, Kareem, these kids out on the street will kill you *** just for a hit of weed. They're cold blooded hoodlums Kareem.' He shook his head as he got up off the recliner and with a stretch, walked into an open hallway, with yellow walls and old, turned up wood floors. 'You can change the channel if ya want, Kareem.'

The small boy smiled and crawled back over to the tv and turned the knob on the panal until he found what he wanted to watch. Boxing. He crawled back over to the rug and laid his head on his hand which was laying on the floor. He gleams into the screen. His dark-brown eyes looking at the two men on the television, studying their moves.

After a couple minutes of watching the television, a 5'5' dark skinned african-american boy roamed through the small hallway with broken, wood floors. He was wearing ragady cloths. Blue, beat-up jeans, with a blue and white striped shirt. He had brown eyes and looked very athletic. With his muscular body he aggresivly moved through the room, to the door, to the front yard. He was looking at the small boy on the floor the whole time he was walking. He finally was able to blurt out, 'Hey, Kareem. Want to come play a lil' ball?' He asked with attitude.

'No I don't want to, i'm watching tv.'Kareem said.

'Come on. You gotta be good at something in Brooklyn!' The boy snapped at him. 'Come on, it'll be fun.' he perswaded.

'I'm watching tv! I don't want to play stupid basketball with you, Jamal!' Kareem exclaimed.

Jamal had enough, Kareem was going to play basketball rather he like it or not. He walked behind Kory, and furiously grabbed his arm and pulled him up from the ground. 'You're *** is going to play no matter what you say!' Jamal snapped again.

'No I don't wanna.' Kareem cried, trying to hide the pain from Jamal's harsh grip. 'No, No, No!'

Jamal pulled him to the door until he was dragging him to the small drive-way with a basketball goal on the garage roof. The grass around the driveway was light-brown, it was old and dead. A small fence was all the that seperated the house from the dirty road.

'Aiight, i'm going to teach you how to play basketball. Aiight. Now this is how you...' Minutes turn into hours as the sun went down over the horizon. Suddenly, the white women walked towards Kareem and Jamal.

'Kory, Jamal!' The woman says. 'Supper time!' The two boys run into the house and race to a old, barly standing table inside a kitchen. Four chairs are seen at each side of the table. Kory's father is seen at the top of the table, the two sons on the sides facing each other, and the mother walks through the small hallway and into the kitchen, to the other side of the table across from the father.

'Let's say Grace.' The man gently announces. Everyone adjusts themselfs so they are able to hold hands with one another. 'Dear, Lord.' The father starts. 'Thank you for all the food we have....'

Voice:

'We were poor, I didn't know it because I was to young, but we were poor. Life was good though, we had a house, we had food on the table every night, and I had a father, a mother, and a brother. Yeah, life was good....
