A slow recovery
Written: 5th August 2002

Back up on his feet for the first time since Saturday is Jim Surge, the Arizona cowboy and one half of the cowboy and Indian professional wrestling tag-team of Redemption Song. Surge has gone back to the Steel Bell Ranch in his hometown of Tombstone, Arizona to recuperate and ponder his next big decision.

At some point in every wrestler’s early career they have a tough choice to make, and Jim Surge now has to make that big decision. An official statement was due to CHZ officials early on Tuesday morning as to whether or not they were going to compete on this coming Wednesday Wild, the historic restart of Canuck Heat Zone under new owner El Diablo. But the usually confident rancher was now growing more and more frustrated at his inability to arrive at a decision.

Jim’s half-brother and tag partner Ben Nighthorse Campbell has been able to get up and move around since early Monday morning, already engaging in light stretching to try and get his body back into as near top shape as he can manage.

Surge, on the other hand, was a different story.

Jim felt as if he had fallen through the very edges of outer space itself into the silent black hole of feeling utterly alone. It is Jim’s natural response to compensate for this uncomfortable emotion by brooding and becoming self-absorbed. It sort of helps to “lick the wounds,” so to speak. During this time Jim also explores every cell of his memory, attempting to learn from whatever mistake he had was made that caused his fall. Within him, a deeply seeded hatred has just sprung its first roots, and it drinks deeply from the wicked black emotions and thoughts flashing through the cowboy’s mind at about 100 miles per hour.

Surge is forced to close his eyes, which now feel as if they weigh more than 100 pounds. Slow motion and strobe lights.... slow motion and strobe lights.... he softly repeats the words to himself under his breath as his eyes start to roll back up into his head. Jim’s legs buckle and he immediately begins to collapse but Ben’s lightning-fast reflexes somehow allow him to make the save and help his half-brother over to the red leather couch on the Steel Bell Ranch’s front porch. Ben calls for Misty who appears instantly with some water seemingly without even being asked, a slight scent of gasoline on Misty’s clothing.

Ben and Misty splash some of the water onto Jim’s face and the rest they attempt to pour down the cowboy’s throat.

“Misty, don’t pour so fast,” Ben says with a genuinely concerned expression crossing his face. “We don’t even know if he’s awake yet he might choke.”

Misty, with her eyes wild and teeth gnashing against each other in increasingly more maddening fashion, pours the water more quickly into Jim’s mouth causing the cowboy to start to gurgle and spew out a mouthful of water. An appalled Ben recoils just for a second before he cocks his fist and pulverizes Misty’s right wrist.

“What the hell is wrong with you, woman?” Ben shouts in his commanding, ancient voice, his face ablaze with anger. “You trying to kill him?”

Misty falls backward in pain and hisses back at the Apache Ghostrider with such venom that Medusa herself would actually mark out. After a second, though, Misty drops her head into her hands and starts to rub her forehead as if suffering from a great headache.

Ben moves into Jim and straightens up his half-brother on the couch.

“Short breaths now,” the Native American says in a calming monotone, “deep breaths later.”

Surge closes his eyes, more confused than ever. He can’t help but think back to just 2 months ago when he hated Ben’s guts for being a racist bastard against white people. And now, here he was taking comfort from the man. It just was a little too much for Jim at that moment, he now wanted to be alone. Jim raised his arm and motioned to Ben that he was okay. Ben scanned the room quickly for Misty and noticed the raven-tressed beauty sliding her way back over near the couch and near Jim.

Ben catches Surge’s eye, glances quickly over at Misty and then back at Jim. Jim nods his understanding of the warning and Ben Nighthorse Campbell gets up and walks back inside the ranch.
