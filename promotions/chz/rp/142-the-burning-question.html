The Burning Question

The Burning Question
Written: 08.13.02

Blackness.

After a few moments, somebody walks out of the bushes with a toolbox, and pops the hood open. We can see it’s a man, who looks to be in his fifties, and wears a running suit. He has a large scar on his right cheek, and his right index finger is cut short, only reaching to the middle knuckle. He takes a few tools out, and leans into the engine compartment. He is bent over for as few minutes, until he finally stands up and walks around to the driver’s side door. He opens it slowly and quietly, and leans down towards the pedals. It is unknown as to what he’s doing, but it cannot be good. After another minute or so, he stands up, closes the door, and then closes the hood. He puts his tools back into the tool box, and then walks across the street. He approaches a white Ford Taurus and climbs in. He immediately starts the engine and races off down the street.

Blackness.

Paul Harrington stands, staring into space. He is in his private elevator, holding his briefcase. He is wearing a black suit, with a red CHZ tie on. A small portion of his cranium is covered with a bandage from his attack on “Wild”. Suddenly, a voice cuts in.

“Paul! You’re spacing again!” Victoria yells.

Suddenly Paul snaps out of it, and sees his secretary sitting at her desk, holding a book in her hands, and laughing at Paul.

“Another ‘vision’?” she asks.

“Oh yeah. Weird one.” Paul says, stepping off of the elevator.

“Well, spill the beans.” Victoria says setting her book face down.

Paul walks over, and sets his briefcase on the desk, and also sitting on the desk himself.

“A man…someone who seemed really familiar did something under the hood of the family’s car.” Paul explains.

“You mean the family who reminds you of your family?” Victoria ponders.

“Yeah. Then he took his tool box and sped away. It was scary. Like he tampered with something on the car.” Paul says.

“Maybe you should be keeping track of these visions. Maybe they are trying to tell you something.” Victoria says.

“You think so?” Paul asks in disbelief.

“Oh yeah. I’ve heard of it happening before.” Victoria says, trying to convince her boss.

“Well, maybe I’ll do that.” Paul says, standing up, and snatching his briefcase up from the desk.

Paul walks towards his office and opens the door. The office is dark, and quiet. It is a very different place compared to what it usually is. Paul tosses his briefcase to the side of the room, where it lands on the couch and pops open. Along with a bunch of papers, a tape falls out. Paul takes off his suit jacket, and drops it on the couch as he reaches for the tape. On the front of it, it is labeled, “Wed. Wild – 08.07.02 – 0-1”. Paul sighs, and walks to the television set that hangs in the corner. He turns the power to the TV and VCR on by pressing a button on each device. He inserts the tape, and presses the play button. The video plays, showing his match with Tyler Van Serio. Paul walks over to his desk, and steps to the left side of it. He turns a small lamp on, and then turns, ready to sit in a big, leather Laz-E-Boy recliner. He sits, and puts the foot rest up. He then begins watching the tape with deep thought. A few minutes pass, then the eerie silence is broken by the sound of Paul’s voice.

“One match, one loss. But it’s fine. I’ve got my shot at the number one contendership to the National Title next week. I have my chance to get revenge on Stimpo for bashing me over the head with a lead pipe.” Paul says.

He reaches up, and begins rubbing his head. He then reaches over to his desk, and takes a bottle of Tylenol. He unscrews the cap and shakes two pills out into his hand. He pops them into his mouth, and swallows, as he puts the cap back on the bottle.

“I have the opportunity of a lifetime to gain number one contendership next Wednesday. All I have to do is get my head in the game. Wednesday, all four of those guys are going to learn that when I get pissed off, I get even.” Paul says, staring at the TV.

Tyler Van Serio runs into a kick to the gut from Paul on the TV, and gets lifted up for the Eternal Suffering. Suddenly, Tyler slides down Paul’s back, and rolls him up getting the three-count. Paul turns his head away from the TV at this sight, and sighs.

“I can’t allow any more stupid mistakes. I let my head drift in that match. I thought I had it won, so when I got him setup for it, I let my head drift and saw myself pinning him. Then, before I knew it, he was pinning me.” Paul says, not believing his actions from the week before.

Paul reaches over to his desk, and picks up the VCR remote. He points it towards the VCR, and presses a button, stopping the tape. Paul tosses the remote back onto the desk, and puts the foot rest down. He rises out of the chair, and walks to the VCR. Paul presses a button, and the tape pops out. He takes it from the player, and walks over to a bookcase. He sets the tape on end, farthest to the left, right up against the side of the case. He turns, and walks to the wall of windows on the back of his office. Curtains cover the window, blocking the view to the busy Las Vegas street below. Paul presses a button located on the wall and the curtains part in the middle, as they open. Paul peers down to the street, and watches the people scurry across the busy avenue, trying not to get killed.

“It’s funny how people struggle to cross this road, trying not to get hit by a car. That’s how it is in the business. You try to cross paths with someone, and not be on the losing end. That’s the way it goes in this business. You win some, you lose some. That’s just the way it is.” Paul says, still staring out the window.

He turns, and walks back to his desk. He reaches his destination, and looks down at the top of the desk. Sitting in the middle of the desk, is a picture of Stimpo. Paul reaches into his pocket, and comes back out with a small object. He takes the picture of Stimpo, and holds it by the upper left hand corner with his left index finger and thumb.

“The only thing Stimpo doesn’t realize is the fact that this time, I win, he loses. End of story. This is my time. Another thing he is going to find out the hard way, is that when you mess with a pissed off business man, you will get burned.” Paul says, showing anger on his face.

With those words, Paul flicks the object in his hand, which we now see is a lighter, and positions the flame underneath the bottom right hand corner of the picture, igniting it. It burns for a few seconds in his hand, until he drops it into the garbage can by his desk and walks over to the couch by the office door. He picks up his coat, and walks out the door as the shot settles on the garbage can. His voice can be heard as the flame continues to burn.

“Victoria, take care of the fire in my garbage can, will ya?” Paul says with no worries in his voice.

Blackness.
