Written: 15/7/02

Davis Fury RP #4: New Opportunities…

Davis pressed the ‘stop’ button on his video recorder remote control, he then places the remote on the arm of the chair beside him. He warily rubs his eyes and lets out a little yawn. He had spent the majority of the morning watching Theo Knox and Luke Black tapes, he was studying their fighting style and their weaknesses. In his opinion this was the strength of his game…he always studied tapes on his opponents…he always went into a match prepared that way if he lost, he knew that it wasn’t because he hadn’t underestimated his opponent and that he had gave 100%.

He was kinda glad that the Wild Wednesday card was put back a week…it had given him even more time to prepare for his upcoming match…it had also gave the opportunity to visit his wife’s grave. When the actual funeral took place, he couldn’t bear to sit through the entire ceremony. He refused to be subjugated to a million questions and words of sympathy, which to him were empty. He was also ashamed to face the crowd. He knew that he was the one responsible for her death, so how could he sit there through the service? The answer was he couldn’t…instead he watched the ceremony take place from a him overlooking the cemetery…standing beside a massive oak tree that somehow managed to bring him comfort. He knew that their friends and their family would scorn him for this…but he knew that she would understand. She always did understand him.

When he went to visit the grave earlier in the day it was quiet…not very many people in the cemetery at all…when he got there he found an assortment of flowers, some were dying and some were new. Davis knelt down at the grave and placed the yellow flowers that he bought for her at the side of the headstone, before removing some of the dead ones. Whilst he was removing the flowers he read the remorseful cards, which went with the flowers. An auntie here, an uncle there, until he came to some flowers with red and white petals. They were from SGT Buckweiser. On the card was a message for both his wife and for him. It had Buckweiser’s phone number on it and he requested a meeting. Davis took the card from the flowers and delicately placed it in his top pocket.

Davis, now in the luxury of his own home, extracted the card from his top pocket and looked at it one more time. Should he call him and open up old wounds, he really didn’t want to open? The answer was easy. Yes, of course he should. What was more important the revenge of his wife’s death or the uncomfortable feeling in his old friends presence? There was no choice to make. He picked the receiver of his phone up off the hook and placed it to his ear, simultaneously dialling the numbers from the card.

Sgt Buckweiser: Yeah!

Davis: It’s…umm…It’s…err…

Sgt Buckweiser: Yes I know Davis! I‘m very glad you rang me…

Davis: I’m just sorry it’s been so long.

Sgt Buckweiser: Don’t worry about it! I take it you got the note from the flowers I sent…

Davis: Yeah I did.

Sgt Buckweiser: You know [Pausing] we missed you at the funeral…

Davis: I just couldn’t face everyone, you know?

Sgt Buckweiser: Why Davis? We’re you’re friends!

Davis: Because people would attempt to console me…badly and each and every time someone would try to console me, I’d remember the real reason she died.

Sgt Buckweiser: What does it have to do with you Davis?

Davis: It was my fault that she died! I am the one responsible for her death!

Sgt Buckweiser: I don’t wanna hear you talkin’ like that Davis! It was Tolkien Anderson who killed her, not you!

Davis: You know full well he did it because I killed his son in a drugs raid eighteen months back…

Sgt Buckweiser: Nevertheless it wasn’t your fault! You didn’t pull the trigger! It was just one of those things…

Davis: Maybe…Maybe not nevertheless that bastard will pay for what he did!

Sgt Buckweiser: I heard that you were going on some fool hardy attempt to gain some level of revenge…listen, Davis, this guy is bad news…

Davis: Don’t you think I know that?

Sgt Buckweiser: Yeah I know. What I’m tryin’ to say is that, maybe you should let the cops handle it…

Davis: Hell no, Buckweiser! I’m goin’ after this sum of a bitch myself!

Sgt Buckweiser: I thought you’d say that! Having any luck tracking him down?

Davis: How did you know he’d left town?

Sgt Buckweiser: I have a couple of surveillance reports on my desk…

Davis: Can you give me any details on his whereabouts?

Sgt Buckweiser: Well lets just say the abandoned warehouse on 34th Street in New Jersey looks like a good place to go this year…although the crime rate has risen over the past two months!

Davis: Thank You Buckweiser, I’ll never forget it!

Sgt Buckweiser: It’s okay! Remember though, if you go there it will be very dangerous!

Davis: Don’t worry Buckweiser, I’ve been to New Jersey before…they love me over there!

Davis places the receiver back down onto the hook and smiles to himself. He had done it! He had found him! All that was left to do now was to go and take his revenge, but the question was, ‘Will I feel better, once Tolkien is dead? He knew the answer instantaneously.

Davis: Hell Yeah!

************

Sgt Buckweiser places his receiver back on the hook, looks around his empty smoky office, and then lifts the receiver again. He sits back in his chair, placing his feet up onto his desk. He then pops a cigar into his mouth a lights it…just before the person on the other end of the phone picks up.

Buckweiser: Yes, hello Mr. Anderson.

[Pause]

Buckweiser: Davis Fury has been taken care of!

[Pause]

Buckweiser: Yes, I directed him to that warehouse of yours on 34th Street, New Jersey.

[Pause]

Buckweiser: No Idea when sir, but I would think that it will be as soon as possible.

[Pause]

Buckweiser: Your operative in the CHZ may think he will fight this Wednesday at the Wild Card, but if I know Davis Fury he’ll get to the arena VIA New Jersey.

[Pause]

Buckweiser: Yeah, he is a stubborn bastard isn’t he? [Laughing]

[Pause]

Buckweiser: Thank you Mr. Anderson, call me again if you ever need my services!

[Pause]

Buckweiser breaks into a laugh as he places the receiver back down onto the hook and joyfully continues to take big puffs out of his cigar.
