Divine Vengeance?
Written: 7/25/02

The church was almost like a stereotype taken from a movie and projected into reality. It rested 25 miles off the eastern edge of Vancouver, and contained everything from the oak benches to the stained glass windows. The windows had the sun shining through them, illuminating the floor of the grand church where followers were knelt with their bibles in hand, praying. Beyond the marble floor and the altar with candles was a confessional in the corner, also made of fine oak, and it was the point of interest for Matt Blitz.

Matt could feel the eyes on him as his black boots clicked against the floor while he walked. He was the only one in the church wearing dark shades, used to conceal his silver eyes... and more or less the only man with nose length red hair that hung in tangled strands in front of his dark-featured face. His black leather cloak swayed at his knees as he walked, and his golden plated bible clanked against his thigh with each step. He looked like some deranged rock singer, but his goal this day was not to blend in, it was to be a Disciple.

Matt gripped the crumpled paper in his pocket as he slowly opened the door to the confessional and stepped in. The paper had numerous codes and numbers written that couldn't be understood by anyone who wasn't a part of the religious organization known as The Disciples. In basic terms, it meant 'deal with it', and it was music to Matt's ears.

Blitz looked around the dark box and took note of how stuffy it was. He could hear some breathing coming from the other side, and he tensed up a little, tapping his finger against his knee in an aggravated manner. Sitting on the other end was Bishop Paul Verrinas, the man who had gained Matt's hatred.

A few weeks before, Matt was dealing with an exorcism that was the hardest in his time as a field man for The Disciples. He had gone through absolute hell, even considered leaving the group because of the experience... but knowing that he had helped Cynthia Smith, the possessed girl, made it all worth it. Then the news came that she killed himself, and he was interrogated as someone who had caused another's death because of failing his job, and he could still feel the wound that had caused. When released for no known reason, Matt grew suspicious and found out that she was actually killed by the church so they could cover up their own failure. Of course, they made it look like a suicide, and everyone took the fall but them. It made Matt sick.

Matt took a breath to relieve some of the adrenaline coursing through his veins, but the breath was shakey at best. 'Forgive me father, for I have sinned.'

'How have you faltered as a lamb of God my son?' a calm voice on the other side asked.

'I know of a sin committed, a grave sin, yet I let it slip through my fingers like sand.'

There was a brief pause. 'What sin do you know of?'

'A commandment father, has been broken. Thou shalt not kill, the same that I know has been broken. I fear that I cannot let it pass by, and I must do something about it father.'

There was a longer pause this time on the other end as Matt's heartbeat began to inch up towards his throat. 'You are a good man my son. I bid you to speak with the authorities, and let his punishment be dealt correctly in life, for he shall be punished more correctly so in death.'

Matt shook his head, even though no one would have seen him do so. 'Bishop, I can't do that. I fear that God wishes to pass his judgment through me, and I cannot deny The Father.'

There was a sigh from the bishop on the other end as he spoke in a lower tone. 'My son, we all wish to use the power of judgment, yet how can we? None of us can perfectly judge, for none of us knows all. Only he who knows all may judge, and that is the almighty himself. One cannot hope to understand the will of God, hence, you cannot truly wish to judge in his name when you know not his will.'

Matt clenched his fist and rested his cheek against the wall. 'What gives you the right to judge?' he asked in a cold whisper.

'Pardon?' the bishop asked, bewildered by the question. As he answered, Matt noted the sounds of footsteps leaving the church, one by one marching out.

'What gives you the right to judge?' Matt repeated with a colder tone this time.

'I do not believe I have the right to judge, my son,' the bishop replied, drawing a cold grin to Matt's face. Matt could tell the bishop was struggling to keep his calm voice in tact.

'Then why do you choose who deserves life and death bishop?' Matt asked in a taunting tone, placing his hand over something in his cloak. 'What gives you the right to choose someone's fate?'

'Only God can choose one's fate!' the bishop snapped back at Matt, growing aggravated at the persisting questions that had nothing to do with confessing.

Matt cracked his neck as the sounds of exiting footsteps stopped. 'Was it God who decided Cynthia Smith's fate was to die Verrinas? Was it God who decided she should be found in her closet with a rope around her neck?'

The bishop paused. 'I don't know who that is sir,' he muttered in an unconvincing tone.

'Tell me bishop, what gives you that right? When she began to speak in different voices, what gave you the right to judge her as not being possessed? When she was influencing the minds of others, what gave you the right to reject the pleas of help from her family? When Cynthia Smith was possessed by countless demons for three years, bishop, tell me... what gave you the right to turn your back on her?'

'I know now what you speak of sir!' the bishop stuttered back, not completely bewildered. 'You should leave this confessional at once!'

Matt let a cold laugh escape his lips, but he didn't miss a single step. 'When you finally decided to help her, you sent in a priest who ended up killing himself over the ordeal. Do your followers know this bishop? When you finally came to The Disciples for help, we helped her, we saved her! What gave you the right to take that away from us... from me! By God almighty, what gave you the right to take from her the freedom she waited three years for while you turned your back on her!?'

There was another pause from the bishop who could only respond in a shocked whisper now. 'The Disciples?'

'My name is Matt Blitz, Bishop Paul Verrinas, and I saved that girl's life. I gave her the freedom you denied her, and you went beyond your ignorance to deny her of her freedom once more just to cover your failures up,' Matt hissed as he reached inside his leather cloak. 'As I said, what right did you have?'

'What right? Mr. Blitz, Disciple... if I didn't do what had to be done, none would follow the church! They would see our failures and turn their backs on us! It would have caused chaos in the churches and our followers would have fallen from faith! What I did... I did for God,' the bishop frantically tried to explain, stuttering each word. Matt could almost feel the man's heart choking him as he spoke, and Matt relished the moment.

'I see,' Matt muttered as he trailed off, 'and I understand. The possessed girl is dead and no one would believe her frantic parents. The Disciples couldn't say a word, because let's face it, we don't exist. It's almost perfect.'

'Yes!' the bishop exclaimed. 'You see now, this was in the best interests of everyone. No one has any knowledge of The Disciples, the church is free of the burdens and the followers still remain strong!'

Matt's hand shook inside of his cloak as he gritted his teeth and tried to glare a hole right through the thin wall between him and the bishop. 'Was... tell me Verrinas, was it God who decided that the price to be paid was worth the life of a girl who only wanted to be free!?' he snapped as he pulled a golden lined dagger out of his coat and lightly pressed the tip next to his head. 'I doubt you heard, but there is no one in this church anymore. You see, The Disciples are powerful people bishop, very powerful, and what you did has pissed them right off. You murdered an innocent girl to cover up your own screw ups, and that doesn't go well with us. As far as anyone knows, there is a fire in your quarters, caused by a fallen candle that ignited some cloth. Unfortunately, the bishop burned to death in those fires, but don't worry Verrinas,' Matt coldly muttered with his head still pressed against the wall, 'because the fires you'll feel afterwards will be a lot worse than what you would have felt here. I doubt you'll even remember what's going to happen to you in the last moments of your life'

Matt heard the door on the other side swing open, which he expected, and hence darted out of his booth as well. Sure enough, their scrambling feet echoed in the empty church and smoke was coming from the bishop's quarters. The bishop himself was scrambling for an escape, but in his blind fear, he ran into a hard spine-buster from Matt, instantly knocking the wind out of him and sending the bishop sprawling on the marble floor.

'I bet that felt pretty good bishop. I'll tell you what... I have to kill you in say... five minutes? If you can give me a good wrestling match in that time, I'll let you go free,' Matt explained, offering his hand to the bishop. Verrinas got to his feet, staggered a second and charged at Matt with a spear. Matt game up with a rising knee and caught the bishop head on in the face, taking him to the floor again.

Matt knelt down and removed his shades, casually looking over the bishop. 'Four minutes.'

Verrinas got up slowly and tried to hit Matt with a right hand. Matt telegraphed it and drove the bishop down on the marble floor with a hard power-slam. Before the bishop could even feel the wind leave his battered body, Matt yanked him up by his hair and hit a snap suplex.

'Three minutes.'

The bishop barely got to his feet, and instantly, he pulled out a dagger from his robes. It surprised Matt a little, but the bishop was too battered to be effective with his lunge, so Matt sidestepped, grabbed his wrist and hit a reverse DDT, nearly knocking Verrinas out cold.

'Two minutes,' Matt exclaimed as he surveyed his handiwork.'Does wrestling feel fake to you my friend? I figure we'll go by wrestling time instead, so instead of five minutes... we'll just go by how long it took me to kick your ass and leave it at that, okay?'

Matt pulled up the barely conscious bishop, who was bleeding from the back of his head and locked him in his Inevitable Judgment, a modifed sleeper hold. In a matter of seconds, Verrinas was out cold, but Matt held it on for another minute, depriving the brain of all its blood. With that, he picked the unconscious bishop up and rested him on the alter.

Matt was walking towards the door when he looked back at the figure that had caused him so much grief in the past few weeks, still feeling every ounce of hatred for the bishop as he did the day he found out the bishop was the cause of it all. 'Burn in hell,' he muttered as the flames from the quarters began to seep into the main area.

Matt replaced his shades and stepped outside where two men in suits and another man dressed like him was waiting. The two men in suits were Confessors, a section of The Disciples that dealt with interrogation and torture. The other man was a filed man, just like Matt.

'Has it been taken care of?' one of the suited men asked. Matt nodded once in reply.

'So, Blitz, ya' taking that case or what?' the field man asked, slapping Matt on the shoulder. It amazed him how different the field men of The Disciples and The Confessors were, socially speaking.

'Yeah,' Matt said, looking back at the church. 'Just had to tie up some loose ends is all. I'll be on it by tomorrow.'
