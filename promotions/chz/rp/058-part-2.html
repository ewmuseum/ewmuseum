part 2
Written:

“Okay you two, rise and shine,” says the high-pitched and boy-like voice of Redemption Song’s lawyer Tom Core. Core, impeccibly dressed in a dark blue suit with a red tie has just walked into Surge’s Watkins Group office to find the half-brothers unconscious on the floor, and Jim’s glass desk completely destroyed. Jim’s computer, however, remains on and looks as though it was placed upright after the attack. The rest of the office is vacant, aside from the videophone which is obliterated in the corner furthest from the door.

Jim Surge and Ben Nighthorse Campbell struggle to sit up but are forced to close their eyes from extreme diziness. A dried trail of blood runs from Ben’s temple down to his cheek and some of his shoulder-length black hair is caked together with blood. Surge doesn’t seem to have been cut, but Core’s words come to him as if they were in slow motion. Surge makes a dazed faced as he strains to hear every word and then comprehend the words as a sentence.

“I don’t know what happened,” Ben states in a slow monotone. “We were jumped.”

“Really,” Core sarcastically says while rolling his eyes. “As if every security alarm in this place didn’t go off. Welcome to corporate America boys. If you think wrestling is bad, you just have no idea.”

“What are you talking about Core?” Surge finally utters, rubbing the top of his head with both of his tanned hands.

“We were raided,” Core says matter-of-factly, crossing his arms over his chest as he looks at the still-seated tag-team. “They wanted the computer files for the Watkins Group. They were able to empty some of our smaller bank accounts but luckily we were able to save most of the assests.”

“Thank goodness,” Surge replies, “Two weeks on the job and I nearly destroy the whole company.”

“Well, its not all good news,” Core says with a sigh, removing his eyeglasses for a second to rub his eyes. “They got a lot of information about who we do business with. This still might blow up in our faces.”

“Bastards,” Ben says as he stands up for the first time in what seems like days. The Apache Ghostrider’s head is throbbing so much it causes Ben to bend sharply at the knees and crouch down with his head down to ease some of the pressure. “Greed, greed, greed, this is why I hate the white man.”

Surge looks up at his half-brother with a hateful eye of his own, taking personal exception to Ben’s comments this time.

“Excuse me?” Jim says as he too struggles to his feet.

“I’m not taking it back Jim,” Ben says as he returns to a fully standing position, as if to back up any aggression shown by the cowboy.

“Settle down you infants,” Core says in an attempt to diffuse the brewing situation. “There’s more news, wrestling news, Misty’s not going to be there for your match with Total Violation. She says she’s not ready to see you yet Jim.”

“I guess fucking your cousin really messed with her mind, eh cowboy?” Campbell says bitterly, knowing his words would leave a mark.

“Fuck you,” Jim replies as he finally stops rubbing his head.

“Last thing,” Core says in a final attempt to get the tag partners back onto the same page, “one of the competition have been whining and talking trash about you guys in their promos. American Ronin.”

“The frosted-haired gay guys?!” Surge exclaims in disbelief.

“I didn’t know they were gay,” Campbell says with a bit of a smile. “But I guess the frosted hair is a bit of a giveaway.”

“And the whining,” Surge emphatically says, “if they’re not center of attention.”

“Total Violation is going to destroy you two unless you get focused,” Core warns, his little boy voice sounding alarmed.

Surge and Campbell look at each other and start to laugh, knowing that they’ve got something up their sleeve for the twisted tag-team of Scourge and Violator. Both men kind of nod to each other in recognition that it feels much better to be laughing on the same page rather than at each others throats. Ben senses that Jim wants to shake hands but the Native American merely raises an open palm and nods deeply.

“Guys, one more thing,” Core says as he adjusts his glasses. “You might want to think about naming a proxy Chairman for the Watkins Group, maybe someone with a little more, eh, experience?”

“Experience?” Surge says, throughly surprised at Core’s suggestion. “Don’t you remember I’ve been helping run my family’s cattle ranch for just about my entire life?”

“Jim,” Tom says with a bit of a city-slicker snicker, “cattle and the entertainment business aren’t exactly the same sort of thing. Anyway, your the boss and it’s my job to give you counsel. That was my advice. As long as you agree to at least think about it and then I can prepare a statement for the board of directors and the stockholders.”

Jim nods his head slowly in agreement, seeing the logic of Core’s point.

“I’ll think about it,” he says in a thick western drawl. “I’ll think about it.”
