Starting a New Journey
Written: 7/20/02

Stimpo getting in touch with his roots...flashback style:

Lying on a park bench, I rested from the long day's work in handling the Mafia and retrieving my mask. It was quite a busy Friday that day at the park, many people gathered together dancing, singing and the occasional picnic. Where the benches lay in the center of the park, I lay there beneath tree branches shading me from the bright sun. The throngs of citizens all stay in the sun either bathing, or simply roasting on their barbecues. Music was playing loudly from every angle and all seemed to be the same kind of music, Mexican. CD players blast out songs from such artists as Carlos Sal Gado or Jaime Antonetty at great capacity that it seems that the bench I was laying on was experiencing it's own private quake.

I attempted to keep my eyes shut and ears closed but the soothing sounds of the Mexican pop tunes dive into my head. Uncontrollably, I rose from the bench with my body almost twitching while my eyes instantly close. When I was fully stern, I began to break out in a Mexican type dance and with that, drawing attention. With the music starting to slow to a stop, I follow suit by settling down to a mere bounce on the balls of my feet. I then opened my eyes and saw that an entire crowd was staring at me laughing historically at my Mexican 'dancing.' In seek of new confidence from the rude crowd, I reached my hand into my coat pocket and there I felt it, my mask. With the music still in my ear, the mask's touch seemed to be slightly different this time. A perplexed look came upon my face as my mind started to feel strange as if a flashback was starting. My body became relaxed as I stood there with eyes closed now, and a mind full of lost memories.

-----Flashback-----

The year is 1986, I was a little, active boy at the age of 8. A lonely street in Mexico where hopscotch boards were made of chalk visible on the ground was where I was at the time. Minding my own business, I was just playing hopscotch when slowly but surely, a crowd was gathering around what was a past condemned building. Being the mischievous boy I was, I knew it was my duty to inspect what was going on. With my sombrero upon my head, I headed straight to the crowd

I pushed my way through the people crowding around the building wondering what could possibly be going on. To my astonishment, I found myself staring at a huge banner across the top entrance of the new building and that message read:

GRAND OPENING!

Based on observations, something had just opened up in the little town in Mexico, and I was soon bound to find out. Making my way to the door, I was met by my mother, Robin who was an intelligent teacher for Mexico. Robin was about 5’6’’ and had golden blonde hair. Slightly tan, she fit in nicely with the crowd in Mexico even though she was originally from the states.

“What’s going on mother?” I questioned her.

“Didn’t you hear Brendon? McDonalds is becoming an international restraunt. They’re now opening their newest branch of restraunts here in Mexico!” She informed me.

My face lit up with excitement as I said, “That’s great mother, but what’s a McDonalds?”

My mother looked down to me with a smile as she told me, “It’s a restaurant, but in this one the kids can get food and toys.”

The moment I heard toys, I darted into the restraunt in search of new found enjoyment. To my surprise, there were no toys, only tables and chairs and a counter with a man standing behind it wearing a ‘McDonalds’ shirt.

“Where are they mother? I demanded to know where these promised toys were.

“Well we have to buy something to eat first, would you like a happy meal?” Mother said entering the store.

“As long as there are toys in it.” I replied.

The two of us walked up to the counter and were the first in the town to order from the new restraunt, McDonalds. Following my mother’s advice, I ordered a happy meal going along with the wanted toy. After ordering, the meal immediately came after. I opened my happy meal and sure enough, there was a toy.

A mask, a multi-colored mask with spaces for only the eyes. With extreme happiness in my eyes, I threw the bag of food behind me and into the trash and left only the mask in my hands. I lifted the mask up and started to slowly put it on my face. When the skin on my face and the interior of the mask connected, it’s almost as if my fate was sealed there and then. Confidence surged through my insides as I came out of the McDonalds wearing the mask and throwing off the bulky sombrero.

Time past as my mother and I drove home to our Mexican home in the center of the town. With three windows on the top and two on the bottom of the house and the door in the bottom center, the house was the average, every day house. Hearing the noises from novelty horns playing ‘la cucaracha’ as they zip by and also the occasional federalé cars chasing speeders or perhaps a criminal.

Walking into my past home, I automatically went straight up the stairs and into my room for some privacy. When all alone, I took the mask again and placed it under the floor boards under my bed so nobody could ever take it from me. With satisfaction on my face, I crossed my arms and went back outside to play some more hopscotch.

------End Flashback------

Back in the reality, 2002 where I was finally back up from the flashback and everyone who was watching me were back to their original business. I felt my head for some kind of fever, but none was found. I went back and sat back down on the shaded bench and began to reflect a few things about the flashback.

“McDonalds? Happy meal? Sombrero? Hopscotch? Was this the way I used to be?”

I asked myself entirely confused. Then a light went off in my head, the mask. I pulled the mask I had from my coat and placed it in front of me. The mask I held now was a totally different mask than from my flashback. The one I carried now was an imposter! The real one was in my homeland, where my roots were.

“The real mask is back home, where I belong too!”

I exclaimed standing up from the bench in a determined way as I continued on.

“I belong in my homeland, a small town in Mexico. The true Stimpo is only found in Mexico!”

Another thought had just crossed my mind as I said this, how.

“But how should to get there?”

I planned on just taking the regular old taxicab but then I thought, “That wouldn’t be the true Mexican, Stimpo way. I quickly ran over past the street, past the bus station and into a local barn. Stepping in, I saw what I needed for the journey ahead,

“A JACKASS!”

I screamed in excitement at the site of my new travel ways.

I snuck in quietly and leaned toward the donkey.

(Whispering) “I’ll be back later tonight for you chico. Tonight, we go to Mexico!” I encouraged the donkey.

I quickly crept out of the barn without anyone noticing. I walked casually back to the street and whistled loudly for a taxicab. One of the yellow and checkered black cars showed up and I automatically got inside and instructed the driver to head over to the apartment complex on East Avenue, where my current home was.

That night…

Upon arrival, I stepped up the stairs up to my apartment and stayed there for a few hours until it was nightfall. I came out of my apartment and ran all the way back to the barn where my jackass was. I went as silent as a mouse and took the donkey from the barn and brought him out onto the street. The entire city seemed silent that night.

“Just you and me now jackass.” I said patting the donkey’s neck.

I climbed up on the donkey’s saddle and clicked my feet against it's hips. Like a chain reaction, the donkey started to trot along the side of the road.

“First stop, supplies.” I told the donkey leaning toward it’s right ear.

Pitch black night and the only company being a jackass. My journey for my homeland and the true mask was nearly ready.

I was right, I needed one more thing for the journey, supplies. Being in Rochester, the only grocery store worthy of carrying the supplies needed to go to Mexico was the self acclaimed superstore, Wegmans. From the farm, I traveled with my jackass and went down Titus avenue along with all of the American cars.

**Many, many hours later...**

Trotting my donkey through the parking lot of Wegmans, I was dodging cars left and right until I could finally find a parking place next to a pole. I then got off the donkey and tied a rope to it's neck, attaching it to the pole. I patted it's neck a few times before bidding it farewell for a few minutes.

'You stay right here, I'll be back with supplies, and a backpack so you can carry them for me, alright?' I told the donkey.

The donkey shrugged as I smiled back at him. I patted it's neck once more before actually leaving him and venturing off into the enormous store. Trying to keep my secret immigration to myself, I hid my face from the public and the security cameras by putting my hand over my face. I thought to myself of what I needed, and it was quite a bit. Using my keen knowledge of savings, I decided to head on over to subscribing area for a Shopper's Club card. I tried to hide my face until I finally reached the counter to get the card. An extremely sexy lady stood behind the terminal with a smile on her face and her teeth shining in the light.

'May I help you?' She asked me with her bright smile. I slowly removed my hand from my face and put my hands on the counter and looked to the girl.

'Sure, I'd like to save some pesos by getting myself a Shopper's Club card.' I ordered the girl.

A confused look came upon her face as she said back, 'Excuse me, did you say pesos?'

Alarmed at someone uncovering my plans, I quickly changed the subject. 'Do you have any cards?'

'Yes, just fill out this form with your name, social security number, address and phone number.' The woman instructed me grabbing a form and a temporary card along with it.

I looked at the form and the first obstacle was the name, without giving away that I was shopping here for the Mexican trip, how could I keep my name secret? I looked around the walls of Wegmans and saw my salvation. A small, rectangle poster was hanging up behind the counter, the poster was an advertisement for the Primetime Wrestling Academy. On it were pictures of the current students there, one picture was a picture of a long time friend, Jman. It gave me the idea of simply using his name instead of mine.

Frantically I said, 'My name is Jeff, Jeff DeJohn.'

The lady looked at me as if I were crazy. 'Alright, just put it on the form.'

I nodded back to her as I put my name down on the form. I filled out the rest of the information easily and handed it back to her taking the temporary cards instantly. I turned away as if I were home free then I heard her voice again.

'Wait!' She screamed as I came to an immediate halt. Thoughts went threw my mind of being in a jail cell with a strong man named Bubba, beating me everyday. Sweating profusely, I slowly turned around to face the lady. She was holding a round metal object. My eyes widened as I went psycho.

'I'm too young to go to jail! You can't prove anything!' I cried to her.

Once again, a confused look was displayed on her face as she said back to me, 'You just forgot your free key chain. Everyone who gets a Shopper's Club card gets a free key chain.'

A huge sigh of relief exited me as I went back and grabbed the free key chain. 'Thanks.' I replied to her as I placed the key chain into my pocket and was off to shop for my great journey.

'What to get, what to get.' I contemplated to myself.

I went down the lines with a metal cart and started shoving such things as bread, ice cream, tacos, salsa, nachos and even the newest and least expensive type of donkey food.

'Ass of Jack? What kind of donkey would eat this? Oh well, mine will.' I said to myself looking to the cart. 'This should suffice.' I said satisfied.

I pushed the cart back up the aisle and went straight up to the first chashier I saw, which was empty. I loaded my things on the conveyer belt quickly and then I saw something I would never forget. A sombrero was floating from aisle to aisle as I watched it with my jaw dropped. I put my things there and looked to the cashier who was checking the items already.

'I'll be right back.' I told the cashier.

She shrugged as I immediately ran from aisle to aisle following the floating sombrero. At long last, it settled and fell into my arms after chasing it for a whole minute. Inside the sombrero was a small piece of paper that read in clear blue letters:

For Stimpo's eyes only...

I looked around as I slowly opened the letter. I read the letter to myself, it read as follows:

Stimpo...your journey begins now. It may be tough, but you must find your roots, you must find your true self. Your real mask will be found, meet me at the country line in three days at noon. Be there Stimpo, your past depends on it. Take this sombrero so I know who you are when you arrive.

Good luck and God's speed to you.

-Anonymous

I folded the letter back up and placed the sombrero on my head.

'My past depends on this journey? I haven't even told anyone, how could someone know?' I asked myself. I shrugged as I took the letter and the sombrero back to my cashier who was already for me with the bags in the cart.

'Just swipe your Shopper's Club card, Mr. Jeff DeJohn.' The chashier said with a smile.

I did as she told me. With a crooked smile, I slid the temporary Shopper's Club card through the slot causing me to save a bundle. I paid the amount for the journey's supplies and carted them out back to the parking lot. I reached my donkey and to my surprise, another letter was placed on the saddle of it, along with two bagpacks already on the sides of the jackass.

'What now?' I asked myself as I grabbed the new letter which read:

Three days...do not forget, and feed this donkey, it's been special to me.

'He knows this jackass? Great.' I folded the letter back up and placed in my newly acquired sombrero. I then filled the two bags on the donkey with the supplies. I untied the rope to free the donkey and patted it's neck a few times to ease it's pain from being stuck there. I once again mounted the donkey and guided him out of the parking lot. I leaned in to the donkey's left ear and began to speak to him.

'Just you and me now jackass, just you and me all the way to Mexico!' I encouraged him as we trotted down the side of the road on our way to my roots, and the truth behind the mask. Full with supplies, the illegal immigration to Mexico for me had begun.
