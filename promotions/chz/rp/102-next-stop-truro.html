Next stop: Truro!
Written: 29th July 2002

“Next stop, Truro,” says an extremely overweight train conductor in a panting, husky voice as he walks down the aisleway of Redemption Song’s semi-private VIA Rail Canada train car. “Truro, next stop.”

Jim Surge’s head jerks to the side and a confused expression crosses his deeply tanned face.

“Wait, wait, wait,” the cowboy says in a hushed but animated tone, “what the fuck did he just say?”

The old man with the wrinkled nose sitting in front of Jim turns his ugly face back around and yells toothlessly at the Arizona native in a shaky voice:

“Watch your language boy!”

Jim grunts and smacks the back of the old man’s chair as he gets up from his seat and moves across the aisle past Misty, who is jostled awake by the commotion anyway. Jim makes his way over to the other side of the aisle where Redemption Song’s lawyer and manager Tom Core is resting next to Ben Nighthorse Campbell. Both Tom and Ben are awake - Tom dressed perfectly as usual in a navy blue suit with a gray tie and black sunglasses, Ben dressed like Jim in blue jeans and a black-on-gray Redemption Song t-shirt. Tom is reading today’s edition of The Wall Street Journal and lowers it when the annoyed cowboy approaches.

“Yes?” Tom asks in a semi-mocking, high-pitched voice which betrays the grey streaks throughout his helmet of hair.

“What the fuck is Truro?” Jim says, his green eyes emphatically expressing his concern, “I thought we were going to Quebec City?”

“We are going to Quebec City,” Core replies as he takes out a wet-nap and wipes the newsprint from his fingers, “we have 5 other appearances to make first.”

“You mean, we’re not going to see Edmonton, Toronto, Montreal, Vancouver? The big Canadian cities that actually have a wrestling market??” Ben chimes in, starting to realize what his half-brother was getting at.

Jim notices the conductor struggle with his girth to exit the car and move on into the next one. Jim thinks that the conductor has a belly only slightly smaller than Godzilla’s belly. For the cowboy, the exit of the conductor is the green light to get loud:

“Core, you booked us on a LOCAL TRAIN??” Jim yells in a wild western drawl, totally flabbergasted that his lawyer and manager didn’t let him know exactly what was going on. “For Christ’s sake the name of the tag-team that YOU WERE IN was called Success EXPRESS!!! God damn it man!! We have to appear in front of suburb trash and talk about wrestling? The only heat we’re going to get up here is the fucking heat we’re going to have to generate to keep our own hands warm.”

“Jesus, Jim, Canada’s not exactly the Arctic Circle you know,” Ben says in his standard mock-monotone.

Tom Core smiles nervously up at the cowboy, obviously feeling the “annoyance’ vibe that his tag-team prospect was trying to get across. He attempted to do what he always does in situations such as this: remain cool. Core noticed the old man with the wrinkled nose turn back around with a worried expression. Core’s eyes met the old man’s and Core tried to assuage the man’s fears with a smile. It worked, the old man smiled and walked down next to Jim. Misty pulled herself into view by peeking her head across the aisle near Jim’s leg.

“Watch your mouth son. Quit taking the Lord’s name in vain like that,” the old-timer says.

“Yeah, I noticed a little group of altar boys in CHZ already - Children of God and Matt Blitz,” Surge says, busting straight into his in-ring act and grabbing the old man by the throat as if he was going to lift him into a chokeslam, “and I tell you what gramps, since they’re already so used to being on their knees they’ll have no problem being my bitches all around the GOD DAMN CHZ RING ANY TIME WE WANT! ARE YOU BITCHES READY FOR YOUR REDEMPTION?!?!”

The old man gasps and Jim releases the man’s throat. Tom Core’s sunglasses nearly fall from his head as he jumps up to assist the old man.

“Real nice you moron!” Core yells at Jim, only the high-pitched tone makes it more comical than intimidating. “How many times do I have to tell you about leaving us open to lawsuits? You’ll never get picked up by a major fed if you cause too much unnecessary trouble.”

Something truthful in Core’s words makes Jim nod his agreement. Ben moves in next to his half-brother and puts a red-skinned arm up over the cowboy’s shoulder in an ultimate yet unknowing symbol of American unity. To them, it is still just another moment in the growing relationship of two men just learning to accept the other as a person, never mind a brother. So far, the relationship has grown very tightly. Surge can sense that Ben wants him to calm down so that the conductor doesn’t return. He takes a deep breath and is able to comply just as the train pulls in for a stop.

Once again, the side car doors open and this time a small red, black and white podium is set up just outside the opening. A crowd of about 7 people gather at the Truro.

Jim looks back toward Core who has made the wrinkle-nosed man comfortable again back in his train seat with Misty holding a cold washcloth to the old man’s forehead. Core races back over to Jim and Ben and says in his boy-like voice:

“Listen boys, everybody has to start somewhere. Let’s make a local scene out here and move on. A rolling stone gathers no moss and all that crap.”

“In front of 7 people?” Ben says, shaking his head that even the added attraction of having a live Native-American doesn’t seem to be drawing out here in “Truro.”

Tom sighs and moves out to the podium. The 7 people there feel to stupid to boo so instead they try to look as if they are too good to listen to what Core has to say.

“Erm, yes,” Core says into an absolutely unnecessary microphone. “Redemption Song will not be able to make this appearance, they have the flu.”

“Wait, both of them?” a young blonde haired man in the crowd of 7 yells, “what are they homos or something?”

The rest of the 6 members of the crowd laugh.

“Uh, no,” Core says, his voice shaky and uncomfortable in front of this small, bizarre group. “They are half-brothers and as far as I know they do not want to have sexual relations with each other. Yes, they are tag partners and good friends but that is it.”

The audience members look at each other and nod that they accept the answer for now unless evidence proves otherwise. Core waves his hand and the train starts to pull away, leaving the podium, in the dust. The podium, adorned in the colors of CHZ but noticeably lacking any official CHZ logo has done nothing to help solve the mystery as to whether Redemption Song will sign CHZ contracts and honor their first booking on August 7th Wednesday Wild.

“Next stop, Moncton. Moncton, next stop,” the conductor yells as the train pulls away from Truro Station.

“Great,” Jim mutters as Ben smiles. “Just fucking great.”

“Hey, watch your language,” the old man says as he turns his head back around. “That’s the last warnin’ I’m going to give you.”

Jim looks back over at Ben who shrugs his shoulders and laughs as he slumps back down in his seat in an attempt to fall asleep.
