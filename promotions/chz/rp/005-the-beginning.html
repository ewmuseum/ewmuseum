The Beginning....
Written: 2/6/02

One Year Ago…

Davis stood in front of Sgt Buckweiser’s desks, wearing full police uniform, waiting for the Sgt to turn his attention from the paper work he was compiling, to the problem at hand. Davis was quickly beginning to lose his temper with good Sgt, he hated standing in front of his desk, like a naughty school boy waiting for punishment from the headmaster. Finally the Sgt puts his signature on the last of the paperwork and then slumps the whole lot in the bow handily marked ‘out’. He then presses the button at the end of his biro, to withdraw the nib, and places the pen back into his breast pocket. The Sgt then slowly looks up into Davis’ eyes, looking accusative, but remaining silent.

“Do you know why you have been brought before me today?” Sgt Buckweiser questioned, complementing his accusative look.

Davis Fury’s expression becomes blank. Could he know? No, it wasn’t possible, Buckweiser could know. Or could he? Was this just his paranoia playing tricks upon him? Or did Buckweiser know about his drinking problem. He felt his face go red and a strange sensation fill his body, the familiar feeling of panic attacks. No! He had to remain calm, otherwise the Sgt Buckweiser would realise that something was indeed wrong. If he didn’t know already.

“Know I haven’t got a clue why you called me here, Sgt.” Davis replied, hoping his voice didn’t give his anxiety away.

The Sgt simply nods thoughtfully. “I see.” He begins, rising from his chair. The Sgt then strolls around the side of his desk, and sits on the corner, directly facing Davis. “How long have you been on the New York state police force?” The Sgt questions with a smug smile etched upon his face.

Davis pauses for a second, obviously calculating the answer to the question posed by the Sgt. “About eight years.” He states confidently.

The Sgt nods again. “And what did you do before joining the police force?” The Sgt questioned further.

Davis didn’t like this at all. The Sgt had never been this interested in his past before. What exactly was he trying to get at? Davis did not have a good feeling about this whatsoever. “I was at a police training college in Calgary.” Davis replied.

The Sgt nodded again. “So your Canadian?” He continues to question.

Davis simply nods in reply, and places the question number three in his ‘Ten most Dumb questions ever asked” list.

“How come you moved from Calgary, to New York? He began. “…If you don’t mind me asking that is.” The Sgt inquired.

Davis was now sure that something was wrong. The Sgt already had the majority of this data from his file, which he noticed, open on the corner of the Sgt’s desk. This wasn’t good. Davis was now almost certain that Buckweiser knew about his problem. But who had told him? Nobody knew except Marie, his wife.

“No I don’t mind. I fell in love with woman, Marie, who was on holiday in Calgary. So I moved here with her and we got married, soon after.” Davis answered, honestly.

Again the Sgt nodded in understanding, pissing Davis off more than a little. “Do you have a problem?” Sgt Buckweiser asked bluntly.

Davis was taken back by this question. It confirmed his, already nagging suspicions, Buckweiser knew that he was an alcoholic. Davis knew that if he was ever found guilty of this he would be kicked out of the force, without a second thought. But, he reasoned all Buckweiser had were suspicions, nothing more nothing less. He couldn’t be fired on suspicions, he quickly surmised. The best course of action was indeed, to continue to lie.

“…um…no. What makes you say that?” Davis replies, innocently.

Sgt Buckweiser smiles, then lowers his head a little. “ I had hoped that you would be a man and admit that you had a problem…” Buckweiser begins, rising from the table. “…But I guess that was a little too much to ask.” The Sgt states solemnly.

Davis shakes his head in disbelief. “…I…I have no idea what your talking about!” Davis lies.

The Sgt shakes his head in disappointment. “I’m very disappointed with you Davis. Why don’t you just admit and be a man? I can practically smell the ale on you!” Sgt Buckweiser states his voice raising a few decibels mid-sentence.

Davis is physically taken back by the statement, forcing him back into a chair, positioned opposite the Sgt’s desk. He rests his right elbow on the arm of the chair and strokes his chin thoughtfully. He had suspected the Sgt’s knowledge of his problem when he was asked to enter the office, but hearing the conformation of this was simply shocking.

“…I…I see.” Davis begins. “…Wha…what happens now?” he inquires.

“You know the penalty, for being under the influence of mind altering drugs, while on duty! Instant dismissal.” The Sgt instantly replies.

Davis nods his head solemnly. “Yeah, I knew that.” He quietly states rising from the chair and heading towards the Sgt’s door.

“Where are you going?” The Sgt asks.

“For a drink!” Davis states simply, turning the doorknob.

The Sgt quickly walks over and closes the door in Davis’ face. “Wait there a minute! I’m not going to just stand by and let you go and drink yourself tom death.” The Sgt declares.

Davis closes his eyes and lowers his head. “All I have is my wife and this job! And you’re taking one of them away from me! You no longer have any control over me Buckweiser, so just let me be!” Davis replies, trying hard no to raise his voice.

“I know, but you’re my friend and I don’t want to let you leave here without giving you a little direction in life.” The Sgt explains, calmly.

Davis shakes his head once again in disbelief. The man fires him then doesn’t want to let him go without any direction in his life! This was priceless. “Direction? You’ve just fired me! I’d thought you’d remember because you were there when you said it!” Davis declares, failing to control the volume of his voice.

Buckweiser picks a card up from the desk and simply hands it to Davis. Davis takes the card from Buckweiser and reads. When finished he looks up at Buckweiser. “Jerry Buckweiser, wrestling promoter! Are you taking the piss?” Davis questions bluntly.

Bucikweiser sighs. “My brother owns a wrestling promotion. I could ask him to train you up!” The Sgt declares.

Davis’ face becomes blank. “Become a wrestler? Me?” Davis questions, more to himself than to Buckweiser.

“You’ve got the size and the stamina, you could make it big. Plus you’re my friend and I can’t just let you walk out of here with nothing!” Buckweiser pauses for a second You need to deal with your problem first though, agreed?” Buckweiser declares.

A thin smile develops upon Davis’ face as he places the card carefully into his pocket, and heads out towards the door. “Thanks.” Davis replies, without facing him.

As Buckweiser watches as Davis Fury walks away from his office and out of his life, he notes that Davis did not agree to give up his problem. This worried the Sgt greatly, but it wasn’t his problem anymore and besides only Davis could make the decision and him alone. Buckweiser rises from the desk once again and closes the door to his office.

Present Day…

Davis sat on his white leather sofa, waiting for the last of the alcohol to leave his body. He had been sat there for the past couple of hours, moving now and again to throw up. While getting himself clean of the poison from his body, he had been going over his life so far and analysing the beginning of this whole mess he found himself in. He had figured that the whole catalyst for this whole disaster was indeed alcohol and his addiction to the substance. The chain of events that had ravaged his life only began with his dismissal from the NYPD and in a sense ended with it.

Davis retrieved the blood stained note from the coffee table and read it once more, tears developing in his eyes.

Dear Mr. Fury,

By the time you read this letter I’m sure you’ve found the body. Let me be the first to give you my deepest condolences. When we came to your house we were looking for you, but instead we found your wife. Of course, we tortured her at first to find out your whereabouts, but it seemed she did not know. Then I realised that if I killed your wife, irony would emerge and I so love irony. I believe you know the reason for my attack, so I’m going to let you stew over it and make you regret your actions!

Sincerely Tolkien Anderson…

Davis placed the letter back onto the coffee table, and looked at the still blood stained floor. Earlier he had come back from a wrestling tour of Japan to find her lying dead upon the floor. Immediately he phoned for the ambulance and the police, but his wife had been dead for hours. Then he found the note, and knew instantly who sent it. Tolkien Anderson was a crime boss, who controlled the majority of the East Side of New York. Davis had crossed paths with him a few times and as a resultant, the crime lord wanted his blood.

He found the letter a few minutes before the police arrived and knew instantly what this was all about, so he kept the note to himself. There was nothing the police could do about him anyhow, Tolkien owned most of them. In retrospect, he was directly responsible for the death of his wife. If he were at home instead of touring Japan, he would be the one lying in the morgue now. As soon as he read the note he knew what he had to do, knowing that it wasn’t going to be easy and it would undoubtedly get him killed.

He had decided that he would hunt Tolkien down and provide his own kind of law and order and when it was all done he would have vengeance for his deceased wife. That was for damn sure!
