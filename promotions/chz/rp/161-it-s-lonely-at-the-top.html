It's lonely at the top
Written: 24th August 2002

“I can’t believe that Misty ran off with those friggin’ jokers,” Jim Surge says, running his hands through his short brown hair more to scratch his own scalp than to do anything else. The Arizona cowboy has been stung by the apparent defection of his long-time valet to the masked tag-team known as the Mystery Assailants at the last Wednesday Wild.

Ben Nighthorse Campbell looks back at his half-brother with disgust. The Apache Ghostrider has no sympathy for his redemption Song tag-team partner, especially after Surge has virtually ignored Misty since her return to the Watkins Group about a month ago.

“Well what the hell did you expect?” Ben says in a titanically sarcastic manner. “You think you can just flaunt your relationships with other women in front of someone who thought you wanted to get back together with?”

Jim Surge stands up from the black leather couch in Redemption Song’s brand new living and training quarters at Montreal’s infamous Rue de Mal #13 and slowly walks over to his partner. Campbell, seated in a neo-futuristic black metal rocking chair, notices Surge’s freshly pressed khaki pants and new white button-down shirt as the Tombstone native gets his face right up into his. Surge’s intense green eyes peer straight at Ben’s jet-black hair and worn leather headband from which hang 6 black feathers and 6 white.

“Now you listen to me,” Surge says, his face turning red with anger and he presses his right index finger against Ben’s chest. “I am the reason that this team is where it is - not you. Not the man who cost us our only PWA loss to that overrated, one-phrase fuck Nerezza. Not the man who cost us our loss in OSW to that other overrated piece of human garbage Graphic Violence of NC-17. You’re the reason we’re still stuck here in CHZ and not playing with the big boys.”

Campbell stand up and thrusts Surge’s arm off of his chest with utter disgust.

“Get your damn hands off of me,” Campbell barks, a whole lifetime of resentment against white men telling him what to do and how to act erupting to his normally calm surface. “You’re the reason that Misty left us you self-centered asshole.”

Ben turns his back on his half brother and walks over near the mudpit in the downstairs living room where the CHZ Heel’s Ball was such a success less than a week ago. Ben crouches down near the mud and takes a small bit of mud and rubs it between his fingers as he closes his eyes - remembering a mud-covered Misty wrestling hard against Angel Texan Summers to no avail as the Texan was able to beat her.

“If you weren’t running your gigolo ass all over the friggin place,” Ben says with his eyes still closed, “Misty would still be here with us.”

Jim moves over from the chair where Ben had been sitting and makes his way slowly over to the living room bar and pops open a bottle of mineral water.

“Screw you you damn redneck,” Surge says in absolutely misdirected anger. Surge again runs his fingers through his own hair and lets out a deep sigh at the same time, apparently realizing that jumping on his half-brother is not easing the pain that in lingering from somewhere else within himself.

Ben, always the more perceptive one of the two, still cannot help but be human and he keeps his mouth shut in tempered anger. Almost as if by some sort of divine intervention, Tom Core comes running down the stairs into the living room area, completely unaware of what has transpired.

“The beginning of a great dynasty here folks!” Core yelps in his strange, high pitched voice. Redemption Song’s lawyer and manager, dressed in another finely tailored suit and tie getup, has a genuinely exited expression on his 38-year old face that looks much older due to the grey streaks in his hair. “That was a great title defense against Gold Rush on the last Wild. Looks like you boys ended another tag-team’s career.”

Jim immediately gets an “oh brother” look on his face and tries to look over at Ben, but Ben remains crouched down near the mudpit, ignoring his partner and Core.

“You know,” Core continues, still oblivious to the scene he has interrupted, “for a lot of people, the final goal is winning the championship. They have nothing after that - nothing. It’s like Ric Flair used to say - when you’re the top dog, everyone is gunning for you. And the second you start to rest on your laurels is when it all falls apart.”

“Except if you’ve never accomplished anything,” Jim says with scorn in his voice, obviously sending a veiled barb back at his half-brother. Campbell gets up from his crouch and shakes his head negatively. Core finally picks up on the brewing bad vibes between the CHZ Tag-Team Champions and steps in.

“Listen you two,” Core says with authority, his voice even assuming the depth of a very serious and successful man. “You two are the real deal. Yes, you are now here in CHZ - but you’re undefeated. Why are you dwelling on losses from ages ago when you’ve been rolling lately. Didn’t you just hear what I said? Unless you two stay hungry - and more importantly - unless you stay ON THE SAME PAGE - you better kiss any chances of going up to the major federations goodbye.”

Jim and Ben immediately look at each other. Ben’s stoic and far-away expression cuts right through Surge and the cowboy quickly looks away with slight pangs of guilt rising up into his throat. Surge’s thick neck expands slightly as he takes a big gulp and a deep breath at the same time - obviously attempting to calm himself.

“We have a lot to work on,” Core says in summation. “You know exactly what we have to do. Yet another early-card match for men of extremely high caliber. Are you happy with that?”

“Hey, we’ve destroyed the entire tag division - including ending two careers and the Mystery Cowards are next,” Surge says, his pride again flowing to the forefront of the tone in his voice.

Ben again closes his eyes and shakes his head, knowing that his partner’s answer was far away from being on the same page with him.

“God Surge,” Core says in a pleading, concerned fashion, “you have to get your head back on straight. Whether or not you are the reason that Redemption Song is where you are now is neither here nor there. YOU might be the one to lose it all. Castles made of sand, Mr. Surge, fade into the sea, eventually.”

Jim Surge manages a half-hearted smile, more for the Jimi Hendrix reference than for any counsel that Core has given.

“And what, praytell, do you have up your sleeve to get Misty back?” Surge asks, surprising Campbell in the fact that his tone sounds genuinely concerned.

“Misty?!” Core says, the high-pitch returning to his voice as if he had now regained control of the situation now that all of the information was out in the open. “Is that what this is all about?”

“Well it sure as shit isn’t the competition,” Campbell says, surprised that it took the lawyer this long to see what was happening within his own camp.

“Listen you,” Core says seriously, pointing at the Apache, “don’t underestimate the competition, especially these Mystery Assailants. They might not only have Misty.”

“WHAT?!” Jim and Ben both say in unison, their eyebrows raised.

“A scouting report is missing,” Core says solemnly. “We’re going to have to change everything up.”

“In 4 days??? We’re going to change our whole style in 4 days??”

“Not for Wild by dear boys,” Core says with a nasty smirk. “It is time for the real mind games to begin.”
