The time is now...
Written: 29th August 2002


[i]*The scene slows fades in to reveal Matt Hunt looking off camera, who has a thoughtful, yet disgruntled expression. As the shot pans back a few inches, Hunt is actually staring into a mirror and is inches away from his own reflection. There is complete silence, with the exception of Hunt inhaling and exhaling deeply to give the impression that something is on his mind. However, a slow confident scowl appears and within seconds, Hunt starts to talk*[/i]

The time is now…

[i]*He moves his head back, so that he is now face on with the mirror and repeats himself with a sterner, clearer tone*[/i]

The time is now to take this opportunity by the scruff of the neck!

[i]*More silence is followed, in fact Hunt doesn’t even move – not even a blink*[/i]

But there’s a burning question that digs away at me…deep, deep inside. ‘Have I got what it takes?’ Course, you hear that everyday. Any up and comer green rookie will always belt out that same question, pleading for his peers and for the management to provide the answer.

But my question is different.

I don’t care about the popularity. I don’t care about the fame. And yes, I don’t care about the money. Because those three things are the first that enter a rookies head.

“Will everybody like me?” “Will I be famous?” “Will I get rich?”

For me, this business is what it’s purely all about. I am a traditionalist, which means that the only thing I care about is this business and more importantly, my in-ring ability.

Do I believe deep down that I can take the one chance that comes my way?

Do I believe that I can take the opportunity and have my own sordid way with it?

Do I believe that I can push myself harder and harder and harder, only to be recognized as the absolute best?

[i]*This slow methodical speech pattern is uncharacteristic. At that point, the camera pans back even more to reveal that he is leaning on his forearms, bent over. Moments later he stands upright and turns to face the camera. The room is dark and the mirror and sideboard are the only objects in the area. Hunt begins to talk*[/i]

I do believe that either I’m fooling myself or I do have the ability to take opportunities and to push myself.

We’ll soon find out, eh?

What Jake Schultz said the other day hit the nail on the head. If I am to cheat and sneak around while the referee’s back is turned, I get success. However, if I set out to get the job done on my own, I fail…miserably.

I see the pattern, Jake.

I also understand the pattern too, which forces me to contemplate hard about whether I rate my own chances. Whether my own ability is indeed…enough

Do I really need outside interference to win a match?

Do I rely too much on sneaky incidents for a match to swing in my favour?

Well based on what’s transpired in that last few weeks, all signs would point to yes.

Is this trend going to continue? Will the pattern repeat itself?

All these questions are filling my head with a pessimistic attitude towards a big occasion.

If I were to stand victorious after some sneaky assistance, I would stand a giant with some gold in my hand. Oh, but I cheated my way to the top – but who’s going to remember that?

[i]*Hunt pauses for a spilt second before continuing*
[/i]
However, if I were to go the honourable road but fail in my quest…I would be the defeated. The man who people who pat on his back and tell him “better luck, next time!”

Do I want either of these things to occur?

Do I want to win the prize by cheating, conning the ref and ripping-off my rival?

Do I want to lose the contest admirably and be forever known to myself as “The Nearly Man” or “The Choker” or “The Man Who Dropped The Ball?”

No, I don’t. I don’t want either

For once, I will not use outside interference to my advantage. Course, cheating does get you the upper hand. But not in the contest of this magnitude, not with so much at stake, not with so much involved that could either guide career to the next step or destroy it in an instant.

This pattern that Jake mentioned, isn’t going to continue because I won’t let it. I won’t let this big opportunity pass me by, just because I wanted to take the short route.

I will create a new pattern that will simply state that…well, you’ll soon find out

[i]*Hunt pauses and has a sinister expression as he continues*[/i]

I suppose that my in-ring all-round ability will be futile in a contest of this kind. “Barbed Wire Table Match”, it’s like it sends a shiver down my spine. It’s an anything goes contest, with each individual wanting to inflict a lifetime supply of pain and suffering on the person that stands across the way, the person who stands IN their way.

All along, I’ve tried to convince myself that I don’t need “foreign objects” to assist my cheating cause when those same items are as legal as an armbar in the contest that comes before me.

Well, allow me to fetch my toys and we’ll get going

I could preach all day about how I’m going to use that or how I going to do this, in order to get my name mentioned as the victor…

But what would be the point? Is there so much trash-talk that just goes unnoticed?

Trash talking is a method that has always attracted me as there’s nothing more genuine then just getting to the point. No beating around the bush, no bull…just a straight-forward speech on how I’m going to give the stupid camache some serious slap!

But maybe trash talking has had its day. Maybe people only listen to this style because they anticipate some personal remarks or some humorous statement or a comment that will just drive it’s self straight into the heart, forcing the opponent to let his guard slip.

Do I come across as one of those individuals?

I guess I do, because I am one of those people that slots into the “take no and talk no BS!” category.

But is it all…boring? Have we had enough of straight forward talk that takes up so much time?

Speech itself could come across as tedious. You hear the same irritating tone of voice week in and week out, that it just drives you completely insane. The same topic of conversation, week in and week out. Make all stop, I think to myself

What if you could do your talking, purely based on your wrestling ability? Yeah, take out the entertainment factor and just get down to doing what you love and do best…competing.

But that would label YOU and not the speech or form of topic as boring. Surely a man can’t convey his expression, just by beating on his opponent and with no conversation whatsoever. That would be crazy!!

Or would it?

[i]*A slight smirk appears on Hunt’s face*[/i]

But for now, I prepare for the contest that awaits my presence

My peers known as the Flying Vergomovs will not be in attendance in Montreal, so the angle of outside interference will not be a factor at all.

No need for those anxious glances over your shoulder now, Jake

Those three little fighters are recovering from an automobile accident, which they unfortunately sustained a little over a week ago. My thoughts and prayers are with them as I go into battle

So I guess it’s down to me, will I take the proverbial bull by the horns and make it my hat?

Or will I squander this opening and thus knock my own self-belief?

Something for myself – and you Jake – to think about before the big rendezvous.

[i]*Hunt stares into the lens as the camera slowly fades out*[/i]
