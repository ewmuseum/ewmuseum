True Colors
Written: 07.20.02

Blackness.

Few lights are still on inside Paul Harrington’s office. The clock hanging on the wall says “9:30”, and Paul is well aware of the time. He is busy organizing things, and putting them away. He is very eager to leave his office for some reason tonight. He finally places all the files into the filing cabinet, and locks it up. He walks to his desk, and picks up an appointment book. He begins flipping through it, and stops on a couple pages.

“So, I’ve got my meeting with Michael Pearson tomorrow morning.” He says to himself.

He sets his briefcase on his desk, and opens it. He places the book inside, and closes the top. He locks it, and picks it back up. He then reaches over, and shuts the lamp at his desk off. He then walks to the door, and turns the handle to walk out. He flicks the final light switch to the off position, completely darkening the room. He walks out, and shuts the door.

Blackness.

A woman walks down a sidewalk, quietly, not disturbing anyone. She looks to be in her mid twenties. She caries a purse in her left hand, and a cell phone in her right. She has the cell phone up to her ear, talking into it. She continues walking when suddenly a man rushes by her, snatching her purse from her. He runs past her, and around a corner.

“Hey stop that man! He stole my purse!” The woman yells.

A few onlookers join the woman, and together they run around the corner, and see the thief running down the sidewalk.

Blackness.

Paul pushes the front door of the TAN Office building open, and then looks back towards the desk.

“See you guys tomorrow morning.” He says to the ladies at the front desk.

“Same to you Mr. Harrington.” One says back with a smile. Paul walks out the door, and takes the two or three hundred foot walk to his car, seemingly happy. He pulls out his keys, and presses a button, unlocking the doors to his Honda Civic. He opens his door, and sets his briefcase on the passenger seat. He climbs in, and shuts the door. He takes his key, and puts it into the ignition rather quickly, obviously in a hurry. He puts his seat belt on, and shifts into reverse, and begins to back out of the parking stall. He turns, begins to leave the parking lot.

Blackness.

The thief looks behind him and sees the crowd slowing, so he starts to slow down. Suddenly, a Silver 2002 Honda Civic pulls out of a parking lot, right in front of the thief. The crook looks in front of him and sees the car, but it’s too late. He hits, and flies into the air, landing on the other side of the car. He lays for a few moments, as the driver of the car steps out. It’s Paul. He walks over to ask the man if he’s okay, but the man shoves Paul away. Paul persists though, and helps the crook to his feet. The crook rears back, and punches Paul in the face. The thief then takes off again, as the crowd begins yelling.

“Hey, stop him! He stole my purse!!!” The woman yells.

Paul finally catches on, and begins to chase the other man. After about a minute and a half, Paul catches up, and leaps. He grabs the thief’s feet, and trips him up. The crook does a front flip, and lands hard on the concrete. He struggles to his feet, with Paul trying to keep him down. They both stand, and the crook takes a swing at Paul. Paul blocks the punch, and nails a strong right hand uppercut. The crook stumbles backwards, and falls into a pile of garbage bags. Paul then takes the purse from the crook, and begins to re adjust his suit. Just then, a cop car pulls up, and the police officer jumps out of his car, and tackles Paul. He begins handcuffing him ,thinking he is the crook, when the crowd arrives.

“No officer!” The woman yells.

“What?” The officer questions.

“He’s not the thief, the one in the garbage is. That man stopped the guy who stole my purse.” She explains.

“You’re positive?” The cop asks.

“One hundred percent, sir.” She says proudly.

“Okay then, you, sir, are free to go.” The police officer says, unlocking the handcuffs.

The cop then picks the other man up, and places his hands behind his back. The officer then cuffs the crook, and places him in the back of the squad car. Paul walks over to the woman, and hands the purse to her.

“Ma’am, I believe you lost this.” Paul says.

“Thank you very much.” She says, a smile coming across her face. “How can I ever repay you?” She wonders.

“No payment needed, just watch a program on TAN, or call your cable company to receive it.” Paul responds.

“All right, I will. Thank you again.” She says.

“No problem Ma’am.” Paul says, smiling. He turns and walks back to his car, still straightening his suit. He opens his door, and climbs in. He starts the car, and pulls out, fastening his seat belt on the way out.

Blackness.
