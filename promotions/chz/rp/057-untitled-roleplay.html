Untitled roleplay

21 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is A roleplay posted to the CHZ’s 2002 roleplay board, from the operator’s own site backup (board post #57).
Written:

Death.

It’s not exactly something we in the western hemisphere deal with very well unless we happen to fall into the elite group of professionals who face it on a daily basis. Those doctors and nurses, counselors, morticians and cemetery caretakers, they encounter death so frequently that they are numbed by it. Loss of life doesn’t phase them and they cannot allow it to phase them because their professional integrity would be instantly compromised.

This is not to say that these sorts of people are unfeeling robots, ushering in the next sickie while the deceased’s bed is still warm. Rather, their moments of grief are more brief, more composed, less emotionally charged and with a certain sense of understanding of the sad yet inevitable consequence of life.

And then there are the rest of us, numbed because we encounter death so infrequently.

Jim Surge still finds himself attempting to avoid that very hole as he sits hunched over his sleek glass and platinum desk in the Watkins Group’s downtown New York City headquarters. The Watkins Group, now headed by Surge, is the small yet very rich branch of Watkins Entertainment, Jim’s uncle’s former company. Every once and a while the powerful and compact 6’2” 277-pound cowboy gets up from his black leather chair and wrings his hands out over his head. Uncharacteristically attired in olive dress slacks, brown loafers and a gray polo shirt, Surge paces his office and emits deep sigh after deep sigh, waiting for his Redemption Song tag-team partner Ben Nighthorse Campbell to arrive.

Surge walks back over to his desk and dramatically pulls out his chair as if he was going to just snap it up into the air and brain someone. But there was no one there to hit, no one to unleash his frustrations upon. Instead of a violent outburst, he gently puts the chair back down onto its legs and slides it under his desk and instead puts his head as far back as it can possibly go. The muscles on his tanned neck become hyper-defined and a pulsing vein or two are noticed only when he runs his hands up his neck and cheeks to cover his eyes and then finally resting atop his shortly cut brownish-blonde hair.

Jim Surge, however, isn’t numb when he thinks about the death of Mandy Watkins, the beautiful former Miss Arizona whom had been his lover but had met her fate in a limousine crash on her way to the airport to do some advance scouting for Redemption Song. To make matters worse, at the reading of Mandy’s will it was revealed that Mandy and Jim were cousins. Jim vomited for hours after the will reading and had gag reflex for days after whenever he thought back to being intimate with Mandy.

Its been nearly a month now, but he can recall the stinging memory of when he first heard the news on TV. For Jim, it is a bittersweet memory, not emptiness, confusion and despair. He intended to dump Mandy and walk out on her multi-international entertainment company owned by her father Marty, Jim’s afore mentioned uncle, the very day that they both died.

The twisted irony of death for those shocked and numbed by it’s ever-looming reality is that frequently people attempt to numb themselves further to “get their mind off of things.” This method is the method of the weak, the cowards, those afraid to face themselves in the mirror and perhaps admit that they are nothing more than one speck out of hundreds of billions of specks; nothing more than dust in the winds of time.

Jim Surge is far from numb. He doesn’t allow himself the luxury of being so self-absorbed that the death of someone close to him would interfere with his LIFE. He doesn’t allow himself to be a coward, not even in his empty office where he could do so in private. Instead, he feels the pain, he fights back tears when it rages and he smirks arrogantly when he is stronger than it. He gives silent vigil to all those living whom have recently lost loved ones. The Tombstone, Arizona native allows the pain to flow through him until he nearly feels faint with piercing headaches, knowing that he will be able to call upon this anger, this fury, as long as he lets it run its course. Otherwise, as with most people, it would eat him up and control his very existence, exploding when IT wanted to.

Knock! Knock! Knock!

“Come in,” Surge says in a calm, western drawl that jostles him out of his introspective daydream. Jim takes a deep breath and opens his eyes to see Ben Nighthorse Campbell enter the room.

“Jesus man, what did you do to this place?” Ben remarks as he takes in Jim’s office. The office was almost completely empty, every piece of furniture and artwork had been removed except for Jim’s glass desk, his black leather chair, a black 10-inch screen videophone and his computer situated in the center of the tan-walled room.

“I needed some space to concentrate,” Jim answers calmly, knowing that his perceptive Native-American half-brother could sense that some serious mental energy had been expended within the confines of his sparse office. “I’ve come up with the perfect strategy for the giant retard and the dog fucker.” Jim motions for Ben to walk over to his desk to check out what he’s got up on his computer screen and the Apache Ghostrider obliges.

“You’re probably the strangest cowboy I know,” Ben says with a smile in his signature deep monotone.

“Yeah, fuck you. And you better hold your own against these jokers because half-brother or not, I need a partner that can wrestle and not one that’s here to offer me a handkerchief.”

“Damn,” Ben replies, readjusting his mindset to match Surge’s intensity, “understood. Did you call Misty?”

“Yes,” Jim answers as a whole new swell of emotion pulses through his soul. Misty is another ex-girlfriend of Jim’s (this one is not a cousin) and a former valet during the 7 long years that the cowboy made his reputation as one of the fiercest and intense wrestlers in the southwestern and western United States independent circuits. Jim had contacted Misty about the prospects of her coming to help Redemption Song in their upcoming OSW MET encounter with the twisted tag-team Total Violation. Misty was actually with Jim when Jim made his stunning debut in PWA, crushing a mega-jobber named Keith Gray with a front facelock, but he dumped her for Mandy shortly after his eye-catching performance.

“She said that she would think about it,” Jim says, finally looking up to see his half brother and noticing Ben also “in cognito” in a pair of blue slacks, a white, yellow and blue plaid shirt, his jet black hair pulled back into a neat ponytail and no leather headband or feathers. Ben, usually barefoot, was also wearing a pair of brown loafers.

Campbell walks away from Jim’s desk and paces the four corners of the room, allowing his mind to reach out and sense the not-so-subtle vibrations and chaotic energy that is New York City. Ben can’t help but flashback to quite similar-looking rooms at the Watchung, New Jersey Reservation where he was raised. A shiver runs through the American Indian’s body as he remembers bleak-looking examination rooms where white medical doctors would line up the Native American children and administer disease vaccinations and blood, eye, hearing, blood pressure and scoliosis tests.

It was a chilling experience back then for the young boy, a feeling... of being violated. A feeling caused by the white man doctors in a white man’s world. At the time, Ben swore that when he matured he would do everything in his power to help redeem himself and his people. To help Native Americans step off of the Reservations and go to a doctor of their own choosing. And when he learned that many Native American “owned” casinos (supposedly pumping much-needed wealth into the American Indian community for education and housing) have to pay off like green grocers every month to the white man for “protection” against “fires, explosions and other natural disasters,” his resolve grew.

Now, if Redemption Song could go on to win MET and secure an OSW contract, or rise to stardom in CHZ to gain promotion to a major federation, Ben could help to bring about changes within his own community, leading through his example of hard work and paying his dues. An OSW house show was up next!! And Redemption Song was going to be on the card!!! Both Campbell and Surge wanted to make sure that opportunity need only knock once.

BEEEEP!!! BEEEEP!!!

Jim’s videophone lights up and then a loud, semi-muffled and thoroughly amplified voice flows through the phone’s intercom.

“Mr. Surge,” the feminine voice begins as Ben snaps out of his daze and walks back over to Jim’s desk to find the cowboy now seated, “she’s here.”

“She’s here?” Jim answers, thoroughly shocked.

“Yes sir, she requests that you come downstairs to meet her as soon as possible.”

Jim turns off the videophone.

“‘Mister Surge,’” Ben says, a bit surprised that his half-brother would make someone use such a formal title to address him. “You are one messed up cowboy.”

“Yeah, I’m starting to get into this whole Watkins Group thing,” Jim says as he opens the door.

CRACK!!!

Jim is completely caught off guard and knocked the hell out with a CINDER BLOCK as two huge men dressed in all black with black ski masks rush into the office to corner Ben Campbell.

Campbell deftly leaps up onto the top of Jim’s desk to try and see if he can counter, but it proves to be an unwise maneuver as one of the intruders simply overturns the glass desk, shattering it and sending shards into the Apache’s right arm and leg. The pain doesn’t last long, however, because the cinder block is used again and Ben is sent spiraling into the dark vastness of unconsciousness.

Violated again. Crushed again. More vows. More redemption. A reckoning of mind, body and spirit awaits. Its the only possible thing that awaits. Redemption, or death.

The song continues... echoing through the throbbing yet sleeping heads of both partners. And when they awake, God help whomever is in their way.

Redemption Song has arrived.
