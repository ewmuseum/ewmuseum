PWI Interview
Written: 07/23/2002


*The scene fades into an average sized room. In front of us is a large table, placed in the center of the room and covers up most of the space. There are eight chairs that are evenly distributed around the black mahogany table, with eight small glasses turned upside down in front of them. A large glass jug of water is situated in the center, an even reach for those from the eight places, along with a medium-sized bouquet of flowers that brighten up the room. A small door is behind the table on the far wall, with two artistic framed pictures on either side at head’s height. To our left is a large window, which takes up the whole wall. There are four panels, which break the light and allow open space that enters the room. There’s also two sets of black blinds hang down above the window – both pulled down and both open enough to let a little touch of light in. To our right is a small drinks trolley, containing mostly alcoholic beverages with all the trimmings – glasses, bottles, ice, etc, etc. Behind that is a tall black lamp, which sprays the light diagonally into the ceiling – lighting up the whole room.

Two representatives of the American wrestling magazine – “Pro Wrestling Illustrated”, fill two of the eight seats at the table. Senior Editor Harry Burkett and Contributing Writer Liz Hunter are those sat opposite each other at the table, waiting for Hunt to arrive. Burkett is typically dressed like a middle-aged journalist – drab brown suit (that has probably seen it’s best years) with a white shirt and loosely fastened tie. He has short gray hair, that has a right-sided partion and he’s also wearing reading glasses that hang off the edge of his nose. Hunter is a dark blonde, with her curly hair tied up into a bun. She has taken her dark blue jacket off and placed it her chair, wearing a cream blouse and seemingly a dark blue skirt. Hunter talks in her husky female tone as Burkett goes over his notes*

Hunter: “Do you think this is a good idea?”

Burkett: “Er…yeah, yeah I do. To get a better understanding of this new CHZ, I think we need to talk to those involved – starting with the roster. Why, don’t you?”

Hunter: “Yes – because of what you’ve just said. No – because this Hunt character seems too much. He blasted former CHZ management on public radio, leading to that small-time station to cut the interview short because of his comments. I’ll be damned if we only get a few lines that are worth printing. Plus, the guy was brought up in a circus?! His nickname includes the word ‘freak’…

*Burkett interrupts the frenzied female with an assuring tone*

Burkett: “It’ll be fine. I’m sure that Dan wouldn’t have secured this interview – if he thought it was gonna be a waste of time!”

Hunter: “Plus, he’s British…and I’ve heard him talk. He talks funny!”

Burkett: “Yeah, I have to admit – I had to listen carefully when listening to his radio interview!.”

Hunter: “See, what is it about the damn British? They can’t seem to keep their mouths shut and when they do decide to yak, they talk funny!

Burkett: “Well, how about you just save all this enthusiasm for when he arrives.”

Hunter: “Okay, sorry Harry – I’ve got so many different things to discuss and I’m kinda anxious!!”

Burkett: “Anxious??”

Hunter: “Yeah, a little concerned about what this Matt Hunt has got to say. It’ll be fine though!”

*Hunter turns her small glass upside and reaches for the large jug of water and pours a small amount. She then replaces the jug to the center of the table and takes a sip of the water. Meanwhile, Burkett glances at his watch and exhales deeply – showing a sign of impatience*

Hunter: “I wish these people kept to their agreement. 12:00 means 12:00, not 12:30!!”

Burkett: “Calm down Liz, he’ll be here in a sec…”

*At that moment, the door bursts open scaring Burkett witless. Hunter nearly chokes on her water, after witnessing Burkett’s startled expression. It’s Matt Hunt who walks in, dressed casually in a dark green pair of shorts and a white polo shirt. He has a black baseball cap on, which draws attention to his long thick sideburns. He pauses with a quizzical expression and glances at both reporters as Hunter continues to cough her guts up. Burkett stands extending his hand as Hunt talks in his infamous East London dialect*

Hunt: “What’s going on?”

*Hunter clears her throat as Burkett composes himself, while shaking Matt’s hand*

Burkett: “Er…nothing. Matt, I’m Harry Burkett – the Senior Editor of the PWI and this is Liz Hunter, the Contributing Writer.”

*Hunter nods her head as Burkett reaches over and pats her on the back*

Hunt: “You alright, love?”

*Liz looks up with a puzzled expression as Hunt takes a seat and gets comfortable. He sits so Burkett is to his right and Hunter is to his left*

Hunter: “Er…fine, thanks!”

Burkett: “Er…Matt, thanks for this opportunity. We know that you’ve got a tough schedule and that you’ve a busy week ahead of you.

Hunt: “Yeah well, that’s what this business is all about, innit?. But enough of the small talk…let’s get on with this shindig shall we?”

Burkett: “Firstly, you have a variety of names. What should we call you?”

Hunter: “Yeah…should we call you…Mr. Hunt? Mr. Circus Freak? Mr. Circus? Mr. Freak? Mr…”

*Matt interrupts as he scowls*

Hunt: “Hold it! What the bloody hell are you two on about? Look, just call me…either Matt, Matt Hunt, The Circus Freak…er…Mr. Hunt…erm…yeah, yeah – call me either of those.”

Hunter: “Okay, The Circus Freak, we have to…”

*Hunt interrupts again, seemingly frustrated*

Hunt: “Hold it!!. Call me Matt for the rest of this interview. Got it?”

Hunter: “Um…yeah, no problem”

Hunt: “Pukka, now…”

*The crafty cockney motions with his hand, for both the journalists to get with the questions*

Burkett: “Well, we have to congratulate you on your ‘promotion’ to the CHZ from the academy. You…”

*Hunt interrupts while leaning back in the chair and lifting his feet, so they rest on the edge of the table*

Hunt: “Yes well, couldn’t start this thing off without the “well bloody done, old chap” and all that. I must admit that I never thought I’d get out of the PWA – it was a long and hard road, but I’ve finally made it and I’m on my way to the top!”

*There’s a silence as Hunt gazes up to the ceiling with his hands behind his head, sighing in arrogance. Burkett and Hunter both scowl at the daydreaming Hunt with confused and bewildered expressions*

Hunter: “Er…did you even have a match in the PWA?”

Hunt: “Eh?”

*That question breaks Hunt’s stare and he pulls his hands back out from behind his head and takes his feet off the table with an embarrassed appearance. He can’t seem to find the right words, but finally gets there*

Hunt: “Well…erm…I…er…well, not exactly no. But I did work hard…and…erm…and I did push myself for a considerable amount of time!”

Burkett: “Weren’t you there for only a week or something stupid like that?”

*Hunt gives Burkett a blank stare and then rotates his eyes around to give the impression that he’s lost for words…again*

Hunt: “That…er…um…yeah, that just proves that the academy bosses KNEW how much work I’d put in and…erm…save…the trouble? Yeah, save the…erm…trouble of…”

*His voice trails off as Hunter pipes in*

Hunter: “I was informed that the new CHZ is basically being hand-picked, purely based on the fact that the company needs a roster. As far as I’m aware, your ‘promotion’ wasn’t based on your ‘hard work’.”

*Hunt sighs and glances back to Hunter who waits for a response*

Hunt: “Ye…yes, you could say that. But at least I was in the first group to be picked, eh? Eh? Yeah, you didn’t spot that did you? Yeah…how about that then?”

*A gloating Hunt folds his arms*

Burkett: “But that still doesn’t…”

*Hunt is obviously aggravated by the questioning and aggressively bangs both his fists down on the thick table, interrupting Burkett*

Hunt: “LOOK!! Like The Incredible Hulk Peter Banner played by Bill Bixby once said “don’t you make me angry, don’t you bloody dare…you wouldn’t like it when I’m angry!”

*There’s a pause as Hunt glances at both interviewer with some annoyance*

Burkett: “Hulk didn’t say “don’t you bloody dare” did he?”

Hunter: “Not that I’m aware of. Hey, you’re not gonna turn green on us are ya?”

*Hunt rests his elbows on the tip of the table and buries his head in his hands in impatience, while the two reporters continue their discussion. Once again, they are startled and interrupted by a peeved Hunt*

Hunt: “SHUT UP!!!”

*Silence. Burkett looks down at his notes, as does his female colleague*

Hunt: “That’s better, now lets get on with this…”

*After another pause, Hunter looks up and reluctantly speaks*

Hunter: “Okay…erm…you mentioned last week about your background and past. After so many years with a cover-up story, why would you suddenly reveal your real past?”

*Hunt pauses before answering*

Hunt: “Simple. I’m embarrassed to admit it now, but I was actually ashamed of my circus upbringing. When I was growing up, I wanted to be in with the crowd you know? In Walford – part of East London - if you had a different background, then you would be up to your neck in it, I’m telling ya. If you were seen as different to everybody, that gave you an unwanted bull’s-eye on your chest, mate. Plus, if my background was to become common knowledge – I would have been singled out and I’m pretty sure that my life would be a complete misery. To say that the school playground bullies intimidated me would be an understatement. So, to blend into the surroundings – I simply said that my Dad owned a chain of market stalls and my Mum worked in a back-street café. Nothing fancy, nothing to draw attention. But I realized after all these years, that family is the most important thing is your life – beats money, beats work…heh, even beats a bit of old rumpy pumpy in the sack!! Even if my parents decided to drop a clanger and leave me at the circus, I’m proud to say that those guys at the circus are the closest thing that I have got to a family. So instead of pushing them out of my life, I decided to let them back in and show them my appreciation of the work and effort they put into bringing up to be the man, you see here today! Now, I know for a fact that you two pen pushers didn’t come here for a cry story – so we’ll move off that topic.”

Burkett: “Sure. What about the wrestling aspect of your upbringing? You said that you wanted to be a wrestler from an early age.”

Hunt: “Too right I did!! Of course, I was the only one that wanted to be a wrestler in my school – all the other kids thought that wrestling was big hairy poofs in spandex tights, touching each other here, there and everywhere! However, I saw past that stereotypical view of wrestling and was drawn in by the entertainment factor. As I was surrounded by men and women who entertained people all day everyday for the most of their lives, I caught the bug and wanted to follow in their footsteps. I caught the buzz from the crowd, the anticipation and excitement of the people. So, I received advice and help from those closest and toned up to become a professional wrestler. However, once I got to compete in small independent companies – the people didn’t seem to like me or my style of entertainment. Don’t bother me like, cos I’ll still entertain the people – however, it won’t the typical bollocks you see nowadays. Who wants to hear some geezer spouting off about how much he ‘digs the fans, man’? Nah, bollocks to that – I’ll display my special talent to the public, just to let them know that I’m not an ordinary entertainer.”

Hunter: “So you’re saying that you’ve got an unique style of entertaining?”

Hunt: “Got it in one, missus. Like I said, I’m not gonna follow the herd when I can walk my own road.”

Burkett: “Let’s touch back to when you worked on these wrestling styles…”

Hunt: “Yeah, I didn’t want to be no ordinary wrestler – I wanted to be extraordinary, I wanted to be an individual. So, I had the assistance of those closest to me and they were happy to work on me. I’m not going to tell you everything I know – but I picked up something from everybody…strongmen, acrobats, clowns, the ringmaster, the lion tamers…you name them, I’ve gotten something from all of them. Now, I’m not gonna promise that my wrestling style is gonna be the new wrestling phenomenon or that it will blow you all away – I’m just simply gonna say that my style is different and I’ll reach the top in the most unfashionable way possible, just you see!”

Burkett: “I’m confused about your…erm…nickname “The Circus Freak” When I first heard that name, I envisioned that you would be…different…you know, looks wise. That you would have some distinguishing features to set you apart from the crowd – that you’d be about 7 foot tall and…”

Hunt (interrupts): “Ain’t that bloody typical? Just because you heard the word ‘freak’ you assumed the worst, eh? What is it about you bloody Yanks? I know that the term ‘freak’ is used on a regular basis these days with your bloody teenagers – but where I come from, it’s a word that means “you’re different” simple as. It doesn’t necessarily mean that you’ve got a deformed face nor does it mean that you’re got a third arm. So with my unusual upbringing being out in the open and showing my circus relatives that I’m proud about my history, I decided on the name “The Circus Freak”. I cannot lift an object that is five hundred times my weight, nor can I eat metal – which are things that you’d believe a freak does. I’m an unusual person and I will display that in the coming weeks and If I don’t say so myself, I’m not a bad looking bloke…”

Hunter: “That remains to be seen, of course. Now I’m probably asking or trouble here – but it appears that you were in fact a part of the CHZ a while back.”

*Hunt responds with a scowl*

Hunt: “Why would you be asking for trouble?”

*Hunter turns away and itches her neck uncomfortably. Burkett steps in*

Burkett: “Oh nothing, Matt – she meant nothing.”

Hunt: “No, no – that’s bollocks. What did you mean? BLOODY TELL ME! What are you trying to say?”

*Hunter glances back at the annoyed “circus freak” and composes herself before answering*

Hunter: “Well, when you were in that radio interview yesterday – you mentioned the CHZ and it’s former management with some comments that forced the radio station to cut short the interview. They were concerned that the former management would file for lawsuit against the station, seeing they are responsible for their guests and their comments*

*Hunt pauses while frowning and glancing at Burkett who nods his head, agreeing with what Hunter had just said*

Hunt: “They cut the interview short? Those bastards!!”

*Both Hunter and Burkett groan – the female tilting her head back and the bespectacled reporter rubbing his temples*

Hunt: “Bollocks about the lawsuit garbage, it’s because of my accent – isn’t it? Jesus bloody Christ! These damn Mountain Monkeys can talk – “what’s that aboot?” what’s that about mate? It’s about shoving your teeth down your bleeding throat my son! Eh? Eh? Eh? Eh? Eh? Is everything these damn fools say a bloody question?…”

*Hunter and Burkett start to pack all their paperwork into their bags, which were lying next to their feet*

Hunt: “As far as that sodding Ratspaw goes, he was a little shit for the way he treated me. If I ever see that little twat on the street, I’d kick his bloody teeth in. He wants ‘his baby’ back? Well, let’s see if he can…”

*Hunt notices that the two journalists are now stood up and putting their jackets on. He also stands with a puzzled expression*

Hunt: “What are you doing?”

*No response as Hunter sees the opportunity and darts for the door, she fumbles the door handle before pulling it down and disappearing. Hunt turns around to see Hunter speed around the corner, giving Burkett the chance to spin around Hunt and make the exit himself. Hunt glances around in disbelief and he leaves the room muttering to himself before he slams the door behind him*

Hunt (muttering under his breath): “Fucking Yanks!”

*The room is empty and silent and after a few moments, the picture fades to black*
