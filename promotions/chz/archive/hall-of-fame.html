Hall of Fame

March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The CHZ has a long a storied past. Many champions have come and gone, some leaving a lasting impression, some just leaving. The following names have left an impact much to great to be ignored. All members of the CHZ Hall of Fame must be officially retired for 5 months, and recieve a 3/4 vote from the CHZ staff. Millenium Inducted On: 1.1.00 The very 1st CHZ Heavyweight Champion. Although his reign was brief, he beat CHZ LEGENDS (Petapaw, Fringe, and Rickey Jackson) in order to win it. Millenium may be long forgotten on the wrestling circuit, but he will forever have a lasting impression on the CHZ as the 1st elected Hall of Fame Member. Kryptosonic Inducted On: 2.3.00 Kryptonic Sonic reigned as Intercontinental Champion for his entire stint in the SWF/CHZ. He retired as the only wrestler in the history of the CHZ to remain undefeated. Sex and Violence (Dirk Diggler and The Crow) Inducted On: 3.14.00 Five time SWF/CHZ Tag Team Champions...need you say more? Sex and Violence made the foundation of how mediocre single wrestlers could come together and form an excellent tag team. A coehsive unit in the ring...many CHZ Tag Teams felt the wrath of Digglers power and The Crow's speed on a weekly basis. No Tag Team since has come close to Sex and Violence's reputation in the CHZ. That is why they are the first Tag Team inducted into the CHZ Hall of Fame Jaguar-F Inducted On: 3.14.00 Jaguar-F won the Intercontinental Title by defeating JT Fringe. Jag headed both the New York Order, and the Outlawz Revolution. He retired after being defeated by Staz in a Loser Leaves Town match. Rufus Blaq Inducted On: 11.26.00 The leader of the infamous BlaQ Inc., Rufus locked horns with the mighty Thor and his Outlawz on many occaisions. He is the first two time SWF/CHZ World Champion.
