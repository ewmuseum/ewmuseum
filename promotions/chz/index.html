Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Canuck Heat Zone billed itself, on its own masthead, as “The New CHZ – Canuck Heat Zone” — a Toronto-based original-character promotion founded in 1999, held on Primetime Central’s April 2002 channel map as Channel 7, the “Great Northwest Network.” Its own Hall of Fame dates six championships back to 1999, one further title (the North American) to 2001, and a sixth (Television) to 2002; its own Rules page describes a competitive-roleplay promotion built around a submitted “strat” — a written plan of mindset, strategy, moves and spots — that its own writers weighted heavily against the roleplays themselves: “A strat is the BEST way to win a match.”

Its own record's central story was never a wrestling angle at all: it was who owned the promotion. Petapaw founded the CHZ in 1999 and lost it eight months before its own surviving record picks up; Cellrock took it over and was, in turn, revealed by the fed’s own news desk to have been secretly undermined by Petapaw’s own return — a car bombed, a gym torched, a cage sabotaged, staged as a season-long conspiracy angle the promotion’s own “NBK” stable carried out on his behalf. By the 22 March 2002 Wild show, Petapaw was teaming with NBK’s own ‘The Immortal One’ Keyser Soze against World Champion Matt “The Franchise” Helms and Chazzy 2 Cool, with Cellrock himself billed as special guest referee; by NBK Pride, the promotion’s own card put the two founders in the ring together, directly, “For Full CHZ Ownership.”

Two fully-scripted shows survive end to end. On 12 December 2001, Extreme Chancelz retained the Hardcore Championship against Violent Dave in a bathroom-and-loading-dock brawl, and Chazzy 2 Cool won the Intercontinental Championship from Merlin. On 22 March 2002, from the Air Canada Centre, eight further matches ran across a full card — Rage tapped out Television Champion Chris Mathews to win the title clean, Wild Dog won a hardcore triple threat, and Staz — a former World Champion who had once left the country with the belt — tapped out to Kaanen Thorne in a match billed, elsewhere on the same card, as one that would have cost him his job had he lost. The promotion’s next pay-per-view, NBK Pride, survives as a finished card only — seven title matches and the founders’ own ownership bout — with no results page in the held record and no later capture confirming it ever aired.

How the CHZ ended isn’t in its own surviving record. Its own pages survive for barely three months, from 22 December 2001 to 29 March 2002, across the two addresses it kept in that time. The federation itself lasted longer: seventeen months after the last of those pages, a rival’s newsdesk — 4WW’s, on 3 August 2003 — still printed a CHZ show and its score on a shared ratings board. Its hosting account at chz.ewinsight.com had been suspended by January 2008. No later word of the CHZ’s own survives — not a closing notice, not another card — and its record stops with the ownership match still booked and unanswered.

Storylines

The arcs below are recovered from a full, cold read of all sixteen held documents.

The ownership war and the NBK reveal Written by AI, 2026

Steven Petapaw, the CHZ's own 1999 founder, is gone from the promotion eight months before the held record opens — jumping, per an O'Reilly Factor interview the fed's own news desk stages, to a California-based federation called the STOW. Cellrock runs the CHZ in his absence and returns to active competition himself, undefeated 15–0 by his own account. Petapaw resurfaces at ringside in February 2002, and the fed's own news desk spends weeks laying out a sabotage campaign against World Champion Matt “The Franchise” Helms — his car bombed, his gym torched, a cage collapsing under him at Caged Fury — before Petapaw declares himself majority owner at the fed's own February pay-per-view and reveals, on the same night, that he is behind it all as the head of a new stable, the NBK. The fed's own April pay-per-view is renamed NBK Pride in the stable's own honor, and its own card ends with the two founders billed against each other directly — “Steven Petapaw VS Cellrock, For Full CHZ Ownership” — a match with no result surviving anywhere in this collection.

The NBK's in-ring campaign Written by AI, 2026

Beyond the boardroom, the NBK's own on-air members chip away at the CHZ roster directly. Knyghtmare injures The Count; Rage, a former ally of Don “The Game” Rossi and, on the show's own staging, of the reformed Outlawz, opens the 22 March 2002 broadcast by chairing Rossi from behind and dropping a torn Outlawz shirt across his face — then beats Chris Mathews clean for the Television Championship in the same show's own main card, tapping him out with an ankle lock. Wild Dog and Knyghtmare both wrestle billed “from the NBK” that same night, and the broadcast's own main event — Petapaw and ‘The Immortal One’ Keyser Soze against Helms and Chazzy 2 Cool, with Cellrock himself as special guest referee — ends with Wild Dog and Knyghtmare running in to help their own stablemates, only for Commissioner Chris Pedi Jr. to run down and count Helms' own pin on Soze himself. On the other side of the roster, Lucifer and Diablo — both billed, per the fed's own news archive, as members of the stable Disciples of Darkness — feud directly with Rossi, their own former stablemate, across the same broadcast; Diablo wins their North American Title strap match by disqualification after Lucifer's own valet Shiver interferes, and Rossi retains.
