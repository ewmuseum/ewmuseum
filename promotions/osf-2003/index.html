The Fighting Division of Old School Entertainment in 2003: a Chicago octagon federation run by the DaVatta Incorporated mob, with an OSF World title carved from OSW's International belt, a weekly Assault and a B3 pay-per-view. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Old School Fighting took shape in March 2003 as the second division of Old School Entertainment. Its own front page, captured on 1 April, says the Fighting Division “got under way” two weeks after its formation: a Chicago owner brought some of the henchmen, a commissioner was named, a play-by-play man and a head referee were announced, and a tag team arrived with its manager. On a Sunday Night Slam the owner of OSW made the International Championship the OSF World Title and named the 500-pound sumo who held it the first champion.

The division worked from a Chicago octagon. Its weekly Assault carried a best-of-seven between an Atlanta boxer and the vanishing ninjas of the Clan, the champion’s defence against one of the Blue Dragons on 4 June, and a feud between the two Kitagami brothers that ended in a hospital brawl in July. Two rival sets of one fighter’s followers fought in the street, a riot a Chicago police lieutenant described in OSF’s news of 16 July.

In October 2003 a new president reviewed each Assault and the B3 pay-per-view in a weekly column, fined fighters, sentenced two of them to community service, and suspended the commercials because they were earning nothing, noting that the advertisers were “all closed.” By then the title had passed to a new champion, who defended it in the B3 main event.

The last dated document is the president’s review of 14 October 2003, and the site was still serving a roster page when it was captured in January 2004. How Old School Fighting ended is not in the surviving record.
