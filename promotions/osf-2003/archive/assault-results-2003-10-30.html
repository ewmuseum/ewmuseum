Live from October 30, 2003 Free Press The federally appointed temporary president of Old School Fighting had always believed he had an ability to see through most people relatively easily. All he had to do was take a single look into this man’s eyes to know, before he even spoke, that he would be an all around, comprehensive, ongoing irritant. An unavoidable pest. He was a reporter who knew how to act like one, an old fashioned print journalist, the great hood ornament of the eWNews dirtsheet empire. He was also the last OSF contract ever signed by the DaVatta family. The F.B.I. had mandated that all employees without direct ties to the DaVattas would be retained until either the expiration of their agreement with the previous regime or a breach of the contract which would result with the termination of employment. This was fine for most of the company. Most of the employees were decent, hardworking men and women who wanted only to play their part in Old School Fighting to the best of their abilities. Mick Palmer was not one of those people. No, no this man was a parasite. If he could have, our temporary president would have rid himself of the future nuisance at that very instant. Unfortunately, things could not be done that way. While he always favored the most direct course of action, he knew that Mick Palmer could upset the delicate balance he would attempt to create in the “new” OSF. Mick Palmer had all the confidence of a small town sports hero who never left home, disguising it with an ultra-thin layer of false, friendly modesty that only made him stand out even more as an arrogant son of a bitch. Sought after by the DaVattas for his complete lack of journalistic integrity in his somewhat infamous work for eWNews, Mick Palmer was hired at a ridiculously high price to serve as a roaming reporter of sorts. The temp-prez knew that in all likelihood he had been hired to monitor roster activity and keep his criminal employers informed, but this could not be proved. He was, contractually, just a backstage reporter. A backstage reporter who also happened to be a key journalist for the most widely-read wrestling and fighting publication in the industry. Earlier in the week, the reporter requested an interview. He turned this request down, as he turned down the four that followed afterwards. That did not, however, stop Palmer from requesting a “review” of his contract with the president. Only for legal purposes, of course, he just wanted to make sure his agreement with the previous regime was still in order. It was complete bullshit, but again, there was nothing he could do to stop the man from coming in. “You’re a hard man to reach, Mr. A-“ he paused, giving a badboy smirk at his new employer from across the desk. “Excuse me, Mr. President.” “If you plan on wasting my time, Mr. Palmer, I suggest you reconsider whatever you think you might accomplish. As I’ve already told you, I will not be interviewed until I go public with my identity.” “That’s fine, that’s fine, I just-“ “I’ll also have to ask you to turn off that tape recorder in the inner pocket of your jacket.” Our president was pleased by the brief faltering of that smirk. He actually thought it would go unnoticed. It was good to be catching him off guard. Showing he had the ability to surprise this leech was important. “Oh this old thing,” he said through a sheepish grin as he reached into his jacket and turned off the recorder with an audible click. “Sorry, force of habit. As I was saying…I was just hoping to get a few words from you on what you’ve got in sto-” It stopped here. Time to assert control. “I know what you’re looking for, Mr. Palmer,” he said coldly “and I’m telling you one last time that I will not let you interview me. But I will tell you this. In the new Old School Fighting, there will not be room for parasites like the DaVattas, people who think they can exploit this company and suffer no consequences. My powers might be limited by the F.B.I., but I have been granted the authority to eliminate any perceivable threat to the development of OSF by any reasonable means. This means that any attempt to do to this company what the DaVattas did will result in the full termination of the offender’s contract.” As he spoke, our president watched with fascination as Palmer seemed not to just listen, but scan his words, store them away for future reference. He nodded, keeping his poker face. “Well I’ll certainly be on lookout for anyone who fits the description. You know, back when I worked with Semore Smoke in NTICW, they trusted me enough to make me a commissioner. You remember Semore Smoke, right? Highly underrated. People like me and him never get enough credit for out work…” He did this all the time. Mick Palmer was Mick Palmer’s #1 fan. All you had to do was read one of his articles to see it. He didn’t have time for this. “I’m sure you’re a very connected man, Mick.” He interrupted, and Mick seemed genuinely surprised, as if he actually had been off in his own world. “Tell your friends not to call us. We’ll call them.” “If you insist, but just know I’ve got a lot to offer besides my talents as a journalist. So how about next week’s Assault? I noticed on my way in that we’ve started booking shows again.” The president allowed himself a small, almost indistinguishable smile. A little press for the program was perfectly fine, and Mick seemed all too willing to get on his good side – which obviously meant he had an agenda of his own, but he’d deal with that when he uncovered whatever Palmer was planning. For now, free press could be used. “Next week, on the November 20th edition of Assault, Old School Fighting officially goes back into business. It will be the last event we have at the OSE Arena for quite a while, because we’re hitting the road again. You must have missed that memo. I can also tell you the World Championship is going to mean something again, because the job of the Champion will become much more difficult. I’ll explain a little more thoroughly next when I go public.” Mick was scribbling vigorously into the pad, a broad grin on his face, and why not? It was a quote from the still-unknown president, and it had his name under the title. The scab had talked about eliminating threats. That was good for him. Mr. President would soon be finding out, though, that he wasn’t the only one trying to get competition out of the picture. If left alone, the scab would be bad for business. Men like him didn’t sell papers. But Mick Palmer knew what did. Mick Palmer had a plan to get rid of the scab before the scab could get rid of him, a plan that would make the name of Mick Palmer a legend. Mick Palmer knew just what to do, and there was nothing the scab could do to stop him. Why let a scab heal when you can pick it off? DaVatta Incorporated was officially in the red, and that was fine. Just fine. Quietly he sat in his office, the lights dimmed and his feet up. In his mouth a cigar was purged on his lips and he was decked out in a stunning white suit. Odd relaxation habits, but Hancock didn’t get out very much. He was relaxed, though, as the fire crackled over in the fire place and his hands were behind his head. His former employers were gone, and wasn’t it fine? This was the life for him. He could’ve fallen asleep like this. He WOULD have, if the phone hadn’t begun ring. Of all the times for the phone to ring, this one wasn’t one that Jake wanted. It didn’t matter to him though, as he leaned forward in his chair and quickly picked it up. “Hello?” He asked, not caring if he sounded agitated. Suddenly the look of anger that was just painted on his face melted away. He actually seemed to be happy, but then again he was just a loose cannon waiting for someone to light the fuse. So, knowing how he felt was a mystery to everyone - including him. “Yes sir I’ve been very well these past two weeks.” A smile, he hadn’t smiled in a long time and now he was almost grinning from ear to ear, as if his face was about to split in two. “No, of course I’m ready to get back into the swing of things,” his voice boomed with excitement. “With the deal we cut…everything is fine.” Jake leaned back in his chair again and placed his feet back up on their perch. This had been the call he was waiting for and now everything seemed to be falling into place. He took a few puffs of his cigar as the voice on the other end talked for a while, only loud enough for Hancock to hear. “It’s been a joy working for you up until this point sir,” he replied. “I’m just glad to have been able to help you as much as I have. You’ve been very generous in your compensation, I would like to add.” He just smiled; it was all Jake knew how to do in a situation such as this one. He was honestly happy for the first time since coming to OSF. “No, thanks aren’t needed,” he responded. “It’s me who should be thanking you. Everything was your idea…you just let me come along for the ride and I’m grateful for that. Thank you, Mr. Anderson.”
