What this is The OSF site's own move-set page for the character, as it stood in the Wayback capture.
Wrestling Style: 'Adaptive' Martial Arts Set Up Move: N/A (Shawn uses several moves as 'finishers' and therefore doesn't have one setup) Finisher: Kobukson - primary finisher (with opponent lying facedown on the mat, Shawn drops his elbows on the opponent's shoulders and fires a seriers of knees into the opponent's skull) Other Moves: Shawn's arsenal is designed to be adaptive to his opponent and the circumstances he fights in. There are a few nuances that he does not change. He fights with his body squared to face his opponent so that he can use either arm or either leg, yet in doing so, he keeps his dominant side (his right) forward for quick attacks with his hands or feet. Shawn has long disregarded the idea of fighting honorably. 'Honorable' fighting, which precludes biting, eye attacks, and many other tactics means that you limit yourself in terms of offensive and defensive capability. Combat is the kind of experience in which you need every asset at your disposal, and every fight is combat. Finishers Shawn does not like the idea of boxing himself in with only one particular way to come out victorious. In fact, he would prefer to win in an unexpected manner. However, knowing himself, he knows there are a few options that he favors which have definite potential for ending a match. Kobukson (primary finish) -Named after the first ironclad warship, this move demonstrates devastating potential. With opponent lying facedown on the mat, Shawn drops his elbows on the opponent's shoulders and fires a seriers of knees into the opponent's skull. Shawn favors this as his primary finisher because he can very easily apply it to an opponent of any size. Panther's Jaws -Shawn will hook his opponent's head with his right arm, similar to a DDT position. With his free arm, he will reach under the opponent's right arm and lock his hands together, then lift, forcing the right forearm across the opponent's throat. Leg scissors are sometimes used in order to create more pull against the neck. Commonly known as the guillotine choke. Morning Calm -Shawn uses few suplexes, but he does recognize their damaging ability. The Morning Calm is his version of his most damaging suplex, a german suplex variant that he performs by snapping his body to forcefully throw his opponent headfirst on the mat. Warrior's Blade -using two of the most powerful moves in his arsenal together gave rise of the Warrior's Blade. Step one is a sidekick, be it standing or turning, into the opponent's stomach to double him or her over. Shawn will either spin or simply pivot and slam his elbow into the side of the opponent's skull, usually dropping them like a ton of bricks. Broken Resolve -Perhaps one of his cruelest submissions, Shawn begins with a standard armbar, but instead loops his arms around the opponent's in a figure four motion into a top wristlock. The leg scissors provide not only leverage, but pressure on the shoulder which the wristlock attempts to seperate the elbow. Shawn has also performed a hangning version of the move in extreme circumstances, much like a hanging figure four. Warrior's Song -While many prefer the anklelock for quick application, Shawn chooses to use the anklelock due to its ease of application. Shawn's ability to reach an anklelock from several, often mind-boggling, positions gives him the ability to slow press for a submission or quickly twist his arms for an easy break of the joint. Shawn's preferred method of application has the opponent facedown with the leg being held clasped in a leg scissor while Shawn twists at the joint. Other variations include dropping and sweeping to grab the leg or creating a tackle that puts the man over his back and into his clutches. POSSIBLE ADDITION: The Claw: Shawn has come to utilize his hands more and more as effective weapons. Shawn's left hand shoots out, hooking the back of the opponent's neck. He pulls upward and forward with his hand at the base of the neck, causing the jaw to turn upward as the opponent stumbles forward. Shawn's right hand shoots upward in an uppercut, catching the point of the jaw. Tactics: Shawn uses a vareity of tactics in combat, making moves to further the goal of victory not by means of one closed route, but through several tactics, all of which are destructive. -Defanging the Snake: As the Indonesians call it, Defanging the Snake is a favorite tactic of Shawn's. It involves feeling out the opponent's offense, allowing himself to defend against early attacks. After getting a feel for his opponent, Shawn's defense turns destructive by blocking in a fashion not meant to simply deflect the attack, but disable the weapon. Blocking a punch with an elbow, throwing a hook punch at the wrist of a favored hand or slamming a knee into an outstretched leg are all examples of this tactic. -Dissection: Whether or not the this tactic coincides with Defanging the Snake is more that of convinience. Dissection is a methodical tactic that involves finding a weak point and surgically dismantling that weak point with tactics such as chain holds and combining strikes. For example, chaining a wristlock into a hammerlock, then slamming elbows into the shoulder before rolling through to an armbar would be an example concerning dissecting the arm of an opponent. -Opportunistic: This is a tactic that seems fairly obvious idea. For Shawn, targets of opportunity are worth more than gold. By trying to not only expose weak points to attack, but also manuever to the point where these points are open in abundence (such as behind the attacker). Whether an elbow to the head while holding the guard position or sidestepping behind an attempted tackle, this is an option that Shawn always attempts to employ. Outer range of combat: Hand strikes: Shawn throws every punch or hand strike with the twisting of his body in order to biomechanically add incredible amounts of power in each strike. front jab (Shawn's favored hand strike, due to its power from being thrown with his dominant hand and the speed for the same reason as well as travelling a shorter distance.) left cross (a followup strike for his jab that throws his body behind the attack) hook punches (simply swinging punches, but thrown to move short distances for quick hits and meant to hit the sides of a target) uppercuts (standard uppercuts, using with either fist) finger jab (a stiff fingered jab meant for speed and accuracy rather than power, striking usually either at the eyes, the solar plexus or the neck) palm strikes (shots with an open hand used to strike against hard parts of the body such as the skull in order to damage without injuring himself) hammer fist (in the place of the stereotypical role of the chop, Shawn has replaced the old strike with a more damaging strike, hitting with the bottom of a closed fist in order to maximize effectiveness) Kicks: Shawn's body is built in such a way that kicks are the advantage of his frame. For Shawn, keeping a person at a kick's length is one tactic that he finds favorable. Sidekick: your standard sidekick isn't what you'll meet from Shawn Stewart. His training over the years has cultivated a straight, thrusting sidekick rarely matched in the world of martial arts. Roundhouse: a long, swinging kick that has been developed as a combination of the Tae Kwon Do and Muay Thai version of the kick. Meant to be a powerful kick, it is a crushing blow meant to make its way past the target rather than simply to it. In some cases, quick roundhouse kicks are meant to hit the thighs of a competitor, causing them to cramp. Front kick: the front snap kick of Shawn's arsenal is a textbook Tae Kwon Do kick, snapping from a lifted knee to strike a target with the snap of the entire leg. This kick, while immensely quick, is not very powerful, meant more to be a fast, stinging attack at range rather than a heavy damage attack. That isn't to say that given the correct target it won't be, yet in standard circumstances, this is not the case. Hook kick: whether done in a spinning variation or simply a forward version, this is pretty much the reverse of a roundhouse kick, slamming the heel and bottom of the foot through the target, be it striking the side of the head or hooking the knee. Inner range of combat - The proximity of combat that comes past the outer ring of combat is now in a more dangerous realm. There is no room to get away without going through the outer ring or into a clinch, both of which are dangerous against a rounded fighter. Several powerful blows can be landed in this range. Elbow strikes, with the biomechanics of a twisting body, the power of a swinging elbow can triple the power delivered by a hook punch. This attack requires close combat range, which can end a fight in one crushing blow. Quick punches and strikes, such as hooks or uppercuts, can deliver staggering blows at such close range, since, if there is an opening to deliver them, there is no other defense due to the tangled nature of inner range combat. Kicks are very limited, yet stomps and sweeps are not only readily availible, but effective. Another weapon that becomes realistic at this range is the headbutt, delivered with the crown of the skull, usually to the forehead, the bridge of the nose or the sinus area of the opponent. The clinch, a range where both men are clasped to one another in attempts to either create a takedown or an attempt to close the gap against a primarily outer ring fighter and neutrilize those weapons. Similar to inner range combat, punches are useful only if the distance required to make enough energy to make them effective is a hooking motion. In this range, the power of the kneelift becomes a reality. By either hammer at the thighs or body, or perhaps forcing the opponent's head down to position, the destructive power of the hips and quadriceps can hammer at the opponent's body. This position also gives an opportunity for use of chokes. Shawn does not use joint locks in this position due to a lack of reliable leverage, so he moves to use chokes, which need only the power of the limbs in question to choke and cut the blood supply to the brain. Grappling, which is what can be referred to as mat wrestling, is a whole new range of combat. On the ground, position is everything. Shawn nkows this and takes steps, such as never turning his back on his opponent, always attempting to hold some form of control by holding a limb, assuming the guard or moving to the offensive mount. Armbars, kneebars, wristlocks, and chokes play a prominent role at this level. Defanging the Snake Prolonged fights are almost aways to Shawn's advantage. Incredible cardiovascular conditioning allows him to stay sharp through long bouts. In this way, he can wait until his opponent is sloppy enough to make mistakes, or his techniques (like these following) can take effect and severly hamper the opponent. These fit easily with one of Shawn's favorite tactics, Defanging the Snake or Destruction, as described above. Cut the Base: Shawn relies on a reach advantage, even when his stature doesn't technically give him one. By dropping low at range, Shawn fires either a hard sidekick to the kneecap or a swinging roundhouse to the side of the knee to literally cut the base out of his opponent. Breaker: Shawn's feet are surprisingly quick. If the opportunity presents itself, Shawn will shift his stance and fire a hard back hook kick low enough to cut into the back of the opponent's knee in order to break the support of the opponent. Sledge: If battling a big opponent or perhaps a slower one, he'll take advantage of the extra space and move in for the clinch. For the Sledge, Shawn ducks low and slams his knee into the ribcage of his opponent and follows the blow by swinging his bodyweight into a rear elbow that slams the other side of the man's ribcage. Precision: Precision is one of those techniques used when trying to slow disable an opponent, or 'defang the snake' as he likes to say. If the opponent begins to throw punches, Shawn's hand will meet it, grabbing at the wrist and pushing it aside to open up the body. From here, Shawn's opposite hand will ridge into the second knuckles of each finger (think a half-fist for a sharper ridge) and drive it into the opponent's bicep, causing cramping or possible tearing of the muscle. Hailstorm: More of an event than one move, Shawn will move quickly, getting close to the opponent and into the inner range of combat. His hands move quickly, striking in two ways: One, slamming palm after palm against the areas above and below the eyes of his opponent. The second is for the curled fingers of these palms to slash, cutting at the same tender tissue. This combination is meant to create distracting pain to the opponent and obstruct his field of vision by either causing blood to flow too freely around the eyes or causing the tissue to swell. Takedowns: Shawn relies heavily on his ground game when working with someone a good deal faster or larger than he. By grounding an oppnent, he can make use of his flexibility and agility. He has a handful of takedowns that will put a man on the mat and give him the advantage. -Front facelock: a common hold in the world of grapplers is the front facelock. In fact, Shawn's guillotine choke is a variation on this hold. By hooking his right arm around the opponent's neck, he tucks the man's head against his side and uses his left arm to lock the hold. By viciously swinging and pivoting his body, Shawn can wrench at the neck or possibly slip his left hand underneath the opponent's right arm for the Panther's Jaws or swing hard enough to put the man onto the ground. -Armbar takedown: Provided an extended arm or possibly even extending it himself, Shawn can take advantage of the opponent and straighten the arm out, locking the elbow straight under his armpit and loop his forearm under the other man's forearm or wrist. Using the force of leverage to hyperextend the elbow, Shawn can force the other man to the mat. -Single leg takedown: a risk that Shawn takes in shooting for the legs is only hooking one leg. By hooking behind the knee and forcing his shoulder against the opponent's body to put the man off-balance or into the chain-link of the cage. By hooking one, it allows Shawn to get to his feet while still controlling that leg or move into a dominant position. -Double leg takedown: in more efficient attacks, Shawn's hands hook behind the opponent's knees and using the force of his shoulders, puts the man flat on this back. This also allows Shawn to take a dominant mat position, however, he cannot rise to his feet in order to pursue a more damaging vantage. -Fireman's carry: the classical fireman's carry of greco-roman wrestling is still of use on the mat. By dragging the man across his shoulders and onto his back, Shawn attains a powerful position as well as a stunned opponent. -Slam: Shawn has the ability to hook a man front a double leg takedown or a fireman's carry position and if the man is light enough, stand and viciously slam the man to the mat with both the opponent's and his weight. -Suplex: By allowing his arms around a standing opponent, Shawn can, in some facsimile, swing and suplex the man to his back and on the mat. -Spear: Desperate times call for desperate measures and in some cases, Shawn must charge and dive, hitting his opponent with incredible force.
