Fighter Spotlight: Shawn Stewart

12 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is The OSF site's own news post (#16), dated 12 May 2003, filed under Boon Crocket, from the site as it stood in the Wayback capture.
Boon: Hi, everyone! OSF Reporter Boon Crockett here with the first in our new series of OSF Spotlights! Yes, with an ever-growing roster and its own show about to debut, Old School Fighting truly is taking off and proving the critics and doubters wrong! With this series, we hope to give you a more in-depth look at the personalities of OSF. My subject today is arguably the most promising rookie in all of Old School Entertainment, Shawn Stewart! Shawn, welcome. Stewart: Thank you, Boon, it's a pleasure. Boon: From the moment you signed with OSF, you've made it a point to defend fighting against its critics. Recently, you've spoken out against the pending anti-fighting legislation. What is it about this sport that means so much to you? Stewart: Unarmed combat is the essence of hostile human interaction and even some non-hostile cases. I assume you've seen brothers wrestle. To discredit fighting and try to eliminate it puts people at risk. If all they are taught are archaic martial arts, what chance does a soldier have in battle, a cop on the street or a person defending him or herself. Fighting, such as OSF, allows a litmus test of combat against the very best. You learn what's superfluous, you learn what's useful, you learn what works for different people and then you can pass it on to your students and friends. Fighting, though people deny it, is a way of keeping people safe, not breeding hostility. Sure, children fight, but they have always done so. If you want to be concerned about children, focus on guns or weapons, not schoolyard fighting. Boon: Wow, that's some answer. You've obviously given fighting a lot of thought over the years. How did you get your start in this sport? Stewart: That came from my father's job with Hyundai. When he got a job as a consultant, little did we know that at one point we would have to move to Korea. While we were there, my parents encourage me to read and when I found a book about the devoted warriors of the Sillian kingdom on the Korean peninsula, I was hooked. I began my training in Korea in Tae Kwon Do, then back in the States under a different instructor. I've tried to branch out to learn new styles ever since and have always enjoyed ways to test myself. The most effective means of testing myself is, of course, fighting. Boon: Well, you've certainly found one man that's willing to fight you, even if it's not always inside the octagon--Brock Newbludd. Thoughts on The Innovator? Specifically, any reaction to the incident in the parking lot Sunday in Boston? Stewart: *sighing* Yes, that involved a few mistakes on my part, namely the aggravation of an old knee injury. As for Brock himself, here's what it all boils down to. Brock underestimated me and now, after he's pissed away his chance, wants to do it again. I don't see why he deserves it. We can both move on. That's apparently not the way Brock does things. What happened in the parking lot amounted to a violent hissy fit on his part. I lost my temper and I'm not in top shape anymore because of it. Boon: Are you going to miss any time due to the knee injury? Stewart: Well, in the past I've iced the knee down and worked it carefully until I knew what kind of shape I was in. Now, since everyone knows I'm hurt, it depends on management. What they think of my appraisal of the situation is what I'll go by. Boon: Let's say Newbludd keeps pushing the issue. Would you still refuse to grant him a rematch? Stewart: Of course I would. He hasn't done anything to deserve a rematch. As for the fiasco in the parking lot, that simply proves my point. The whole affair just shows that he is a person no one would want to work with, and besides, why should I grant him a fight? Because we traded shots in the parking lot? I've already proved myself. Boon: It seems I'm detecting a little hostility and perhaps... forgive me if I misinterpret... fear of Newbludd? Stewart: Fear? Apprehension, of course, and in my current state, I'm honestly afraid to hop in the cage with anyone at the moment. I'd like to walk with my children. Forgive me if I'm still a bit shaken up by having my knee wrenched and being suplexed through the back window of my car. Brock is good, there is no doubt, but I've proven myself. Why do I need to do more? Because he's being responsible this time? Hey, he can use that. Boon: I see. Well, we're about out of time, so I'd just like to close by discussing your future goals. You're currently undefeated in OSF. How long do you think you can continue that streak before the bubble bursts? Stewart: To be honest, you never know. You can always miss that punch that hits your jaw. The opponent's hands could be around your throat before you can blink. I'll work my hardest to continue the record, but any competent fighter is capable of beating any other competent fighter on any given night. Boon: Do you think, right now, you deserve a shot at Tempest and the OSF Title? And if not, then who does deserve a shot? Stewart: Not in the condition I'm in, no. However, after some time I think I'd be ready. As for deserving, I don't know. I suppose I could qualify as such. I'm listed as the number two contender. However, that's not to say Blitz, Akage or even Brock could be in line for a chance. But I'd be careful: Tempest is one of the nastiest fighters I've ever seen. Boon: Any final words for the OSF fans? Stewart: Keep your eyes on the screen. In the Octagon, you never know who will win. Boon: Shawn Stewart, thanks for the time. Stewart: Thank you for this opportunity, Boon. Boon: Look for another Fighter Spotlight later this week!
