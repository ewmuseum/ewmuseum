What this is A roster card of the OSF site (it lists the character under Division: Fighting), as it stood in the Wayback capture.
Division: Fighting Status: Active Real Name: Oliver Kinzler Birthdate: August 10, 1969 Hometown: Leipzig, Germany Height: 6'1 Weight: 242 lbs. Nicknames: 'The Suave Sadist', 'Lord Krauser', 'The German Shooter', 'The Mad Commandant' Allignment: Face Entrance Music: Edited version of the score of the Italian opera, “Massa Di Requiem Per Shuggay”, as performed by Krauser’s personal hired orchestra (seated on an upper balcony of the arena) he has imported with him from Germany. See History section for further detail on this theme. For reference purposes, let it be noted that the ensemble consists of all standard sections of a small orchestra, plus Krauser’s own addition of two electric guitar players and a bassist. In affairs involving the Red Army, Krauser conforms to using the Soviet National Anthem. When possible, however, he has his own orchestra play a rendition of it. Information Combat Style: Technical/submission with a heavy focus on suplexes and groundwork, and when called for the German uses a martial-arts derived striking attack style that is both precise and efficient. He will manipulate the minds of his adversaries in hopes of angering them to result in errors, doing so through various taunts and ridiculing tactics intended on causing them to ‘lose their cool’, and then capitalizing on their faults. Rarely does an opponent emerge from a match with Krauser without nursing an aggravated injury of some sort gained in the encounter, as he enjoys focusing on particular areas of the body to hinder his adversary’s offense and defense. The German’s favorite underlying tactic is tiring out an opponent to set up for either his primary finishing hold, the Morbid Symphony, or one of the many others he has utilized to finish matches, like the array of quick-hitting armbar and kneebar submission variations he has at his disposal. Krauser takes great pride in his wrestling style, as with it he can effectively deal with any opponent of any size and any discipline and never be at a disadvantage. Massive opponents have been left nearly crippled after having their legs blasted with Krauser’s brutal style of offense, cruiserweights grounded and driven endlessly to the mat after being intercepted in the Commandant’s broad arsenal of suplexes and takedowns, and brawlers’ wild and ultimately predictable style essentially neutralized by the calculating German machine. Regardless of size, the nature of the Morbid Symphony gives it the capacity to take down any man and that feature in itself makes Krauser a danger from opening bell to ending bell. Set Up Move: N/A (The Morbid Symphony is rarely set-up by any particular techniques; The New Reich is often foreshadowed by Krauser more heavily working on the neck of an opponent) Finisher: Morbid Symphony & The New Reich ( 1) Morbid Symphony - Standing behind the intended victim, Krauser locks on a half nelson with one arm and with the other reaches around the victim’s chin and squeezes across their throat in an effort to choke them into unconsciousness. He usually applies a scissorhold with the legs and pulls them to the ground while applying even more pressure. This maneuver is also known as the Kataha-jime in martial arts. 2) The New Reich - Krauser applies a front face lock on the victim and throws their near arm across his shoulders. He then grabs the victim's near leg and hooks it, lifting the victim so they are upside down and into a position similar to a Mexican stretch/muscle buster across his back. He then shifts the opponent so they are in front of his body and sits down, dropping the victim straight down so they land on their head and neck. Krauser debuted this move in the European independents as a Super version (off the top rope), and has also performed a running version on smaller opponents for added impact.)
