OSF — In-card segment

Walking

4 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is An in-card segment from the OSF site's own archive, dated 4 June 2003, for the show Assault, from the site as it stood in the Wayback capture.
Shawn takes a tentative sip on his water bottle, limping on his bad knee. Shawn: Kenyatta. What's up? Jackson gulps down his cup of regular strength Sanka coffee and pours himself another cup. Kenyatta: Got me some ninja to squash. Kenyatta uses his left hand, as the right is sore up from repeatedly hitting the cage in his past two fights. Shawn motions to Kenyatta's hand. Shawn: You sure they're not winning a war of attrition? With a 'Hmph', Jackson shakes his head at Shawn Stewart's question. His face shows a clear grimacing look. Kenyatta: They ain't winnin' nothin'. I'm ahead 2-0, it's gonna be 3-0 after tonight, and it'll be 4 straight in two weeks. And even though I'll have kicked their asses and won the Best of Seven, I'm still gonna beat the hell out of three more JUST BECAUSE I CAN. Shawn takes another sip consideringly. Jackson sees Stewart's facial expression. Shawn: Listen. These are not people to take so lightly. I'd suggest you watch yourself Kenyatta: What? You don't think I'll win? Mr. 'I'm a master of a bunch of martial arts so I know everything?' Ain't takin' nothin' lightly. Kenyatta points to himself with the thumb on his right hand. Kenyatta: I'm a fighter. I'm a confident fighter. For me, I'm a fighter first. But to everyone else, I'm just another black man with a gripe and a liking for beatin' up white boys. I can't have a little faith in myself? I can't think I'll win? You don't expect or have anyone else tellin' you 'Don't take people lightly' when you think you'll win. Second I get self-confident there's a mob of people tellin' me to stay fuggin humble. Jackson forms two fists, holding them out touched at the wrists, nonthreateningly. Shawn shakes his head. Kenyatta: When I start swinging these around, I win. It's not a matter of being big-headed. It's a matter of fact. Shawn: There's a difference between self-confidence and not seeing your opponent's abilities. Jackson shakes his head. Kenyatta: I see a bunch of short Asians who messed with the wrong Negro. I think you're the one who needs your eyes checked. Shawn smirks at him. Shawn: You'd be surprised. Kenyatta: I ain't got time for some Neo-reject who thinks he's a secret agent and can keep track of everybody at all times of the day. If you ain't got nothin' better to do than pretend you're in the government YOU TOOK THE WRONG FRICKIN JOB. Shawn very nearly laughs out loud. Shawn: Tell me, Kenyatta, at the end of the night, how you're feeling. Shawn takes another drink of water and gestures to a door. Shawn: Your abode, I believe? Kenyatta: Yeah. I'll tell you right now. I feel like I haven't lost yet. How does that feel? Shawn smirks again, unconsciously stroking his goatee. Shawn: It's fleeting, kid. Real fleeting. Kenyatta: Kid? *Looks around* Oh, it's one of those power-things. Whatever. Shawn: Have a good evenin', kid. Kenyatta: Later, old man. Kenyatta Ahmed Jackson parts with his fellow fighter. Kenyatta: I'll show him. Opening the door, a ninja casually walks out. Kenyatta: WHOA! What the--? Hey, what were you doin' in there? The ninja, who is much shorter and thinner than Kenyatta, just walks along his way. He seems absent-minded, and doesn't even hold his head straight. He looks all over the place and has a walk resembling a stumbling gait. Kenyatta just scratches his head. Kenyatta: What the hell was that? Shaking his head, Kenyatta looks back into the locker room. On the floor is a small gray box attached to a metal cylinder, covered in an array of wires. There is a red LCD display on the front with a time of 00:06 on it. Kenyatta: Aw, sh*t! Closing the door, Kenyatta takes cover a few feet away. Six seconds later, there is an explosion. BAPF! The door remains intact. Wondering what just happened, Kenyatta creeps up to the door and slowly opens it. As it opens, a mountain of foam bubble-solution pours out of the room. Kenyatta: Aw, dammit! Kenyatta's locker room soaked in foam bubbles, the solution reaching to the ceiling, he is unable to go inside.
