Prologue .04: Into The Fire

15 July 2003

by Arson Zanders

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is The OSF site's own roleplay post (the site calls them Promos) (#87) by Arson Zanders, dated 15 July 2003, written from NextGen Offices, from the site as it stood in the Wayback capture.
April 19, 1998 And I'd give up forever to touch you 'Cause I know that you feel me somehow You're the closest to heaven that I'll ever be And I don't want to go home right now And all I can taste is this moment And all I can breathe is your life 'Cause sooner or later it's over I just don't want to miss you tonight And I don't want the world to see me 'Cause I don't think that they'd understand When everything's made to be broken I just want you to know who I am The radio played on with the Goo Goo Doll’s hit, “Iris,” as I sat in the lowly living room. It was dimly lit, smelling of urine, with an overhead fan that made more noise than the most annoying vacuum cleaner. A trashy looking young woman sat at a table near the doorway to what I assumed was the bedrooms. She looked fairly busy, filing her nails and remembering to chew her bubble gum, and her skirt looked like it needed to be a few inches longer in order not to break any city laws. “So, how long have you worked here?” I asked her, trying to start up a conversation. Truth be told, I had been sitting on the same damn couch for almost fifteen minutes in utter silence. I was ready to talk to a chimpanzee if necessary. She barely looked up at me, completely into her own nails. I guess vanity has no end with some people. “Oh, I’ve been for a couple of weeks now. My brother lets me work here so I don’t just sit at home.” “Oh, that’s nice,” I replied, thinking that this chick was trailer trash from the get-go. “And your brother is?” “Her brother is the owner,” A voice from inside of the bedrooms said. A large man walked out, fitted with an 1850’s beard and mustache and rippling muscles to boot. I stood up as he entered the room, nodding at him. “Good to finally meet you.” He shook my hand intensely, trying to make me buckle with his grip. I didn’t humor him. “You didn’t give in,” He remarked, looking impressed. “I like you already. Come into my office.” He led me into one of the bedrooms, which had all of its curtains pulled closed and only a candle at the desk as a source of light. I found a chair carefully and sat down. “My name’s Jason Alexander,” He said, pulling a few papers out of his desk. “I’ve been running NextGen Fighting for nearly three months now.” He made a sarcastic face. “I apologize for my sister, Nance. She’s a bit of an airhead, and slutty to boot.” I tried to be as nice as I could. “Oh really? I didn’t notice.” “Sure you did,” He replied. “Everyone does. So what brings you to my office today?” “I want to join your organization.” Alexander chuckled, sitting back in his chair and propping his feet up on his desk. “And why would you want to do that? A lot of young men like yourself get hurt fighting.” “I’m not as young and innocent as you think I am,” I replied, looking him dead in his eyes. “I can see that,” He remarked. “I have to tell you that NextGen just started up a few months ago. We’ve only had four events so far, although each have been hotly contested. In fact, six men have had their careers ended here. I don’t know how much longer I can keep the shop going.” I leaned forward. “So you need fresh bodies for the war. I’m your man.” “It’s not that simple.” He grabbed a few papers and threw them in my direction. “Those are medical release forms and your contract. They basically say that you release NextGen of all liability in the case of serious injury and that you understand these terms while signing.” He sat there, waiting for me to turn him down. I grabbed a pen and signed my name at the bottom of both forms. As I looked up, he just nodded at me and extended his hand. “Welcome to NextGen Fighting. I didn’t catch your name…” “Zanders…” I said with a sinister smile. “Arson Zanders, at your service.”
