Re-Emerging...

25 March 2003

by John Crichton

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is The OSF site's own roleplay post (the site calls them Promos) (#2) by John Crichton, dated 25 March 2003, written from Unknown, from the site as it stood in the Wayback capture.
“Look not mournfully into the past. It comes not back again. Wisely improve the present. It is thine. Go forth to meet the shadowy future, without fear.” - Henry Wadsworth Longfellow (1807 - 1882) He had to leave the past behind him, but this was not an easy task to accomplish. Especially when others wouldn’t not let him forget. They would never forget. After all he had been the bane of their existence for many months. Granted, it wasn’t technically him who performed the brutal tasks that he would be forever remembered for, but that didn’t mean he could forgive…or forget. For a while now, the name John Crichton had been had been cursed and dragged through the mud. And rightly so. The acts he carried out were no better than those of Al Capone or any other Mafia boss, but that was when he was ’possessed’. But why would anyone believe this? They only had his word for it. Yes. It seemed like a bad science fiction storyline, but it had really happened to him. Now, he stayed his rat-ridden apartment, not going out unless it was absolutely necessary. His face hadn’t been shown on the news or in the papers that much, so only 1 in 10 people ever recognised his real identity. The only thing John Crichton could accredit the US army for. They needed to keep his identity secret in order to keep their own asses out of the crap. It was especially prudent at present, especially with all of the anti-war trouble George Bush had to deal with. However, he figured that somewhere along the line he and the government would meet up again, somewhere along the line. It was inevitable. Almost his lot in life. To be hunted. Although now, it would do the government no good to capture him. His powers were now lost to him. After he was dispossessed by the evil entity he helped as many people as he could with power, often receiving no acknowledgement mush less reward. Still he persisted. He had to. He owed it to the public. It was his fault he had been possessed. It was his fault so many innocents had died. His fault. After a few months he could feel his strength slowly diminishing. He was losing it a little by the day. Yet still he was there to help people. Foiling the odd robbery here, saving the odd distressed child there. Upon reflection it was very dangerous. Especially with his powers fluctuating. He had to stop helping people about a week back. When he nearly killed himself and the person he was trying to help. Soon after the incident his powers left him completely. He was now an ‘average’ human being. However, he had suspected that the day would have come anyway, even before his powers were beginning to fail. The experiment the government performed upon him over a year ago, was only supposed to be temporary. In fact the scientists at the time couldn’t believe that he had the powers at all. Despite his power he could not save the woman he loved. He could not save Aeryn Sun. Perhaps it was better this way. To be ‘average’. It would make his life relatively normal again. He would be ‘different’ no longer. Perhaps he may even be accepted by the public now. He doubted it. After all the demon wore his face when it went on the rampage. That would be hard to forgive and forget. It would be even harder for Crichton to explain. He was a little worried what the aftermath would be as a result of this. No longer having his powers, made him decidedly vulnerable. Even more so, when his past was considered in the same equation. Still, he refused to spend his entire life indoors, sat on his ass in his apartment waiting for his number to come up. That is why he got back into the sport he loved. That his why he became a wrestler again. Inevitably it would cause friction and there was more of a chance he would be spotted in public again, but anything was better than staring at those four walls day in, day out.
