No Love Lost Part Three: Making the Right Choice (Ascension RP#3)

15 June 2003

by John Crichton

Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is The OSF site's own roleplay post (the site calls them Promos) (#48) by John Crichton, dated 15 June 2003, written from Chicago, Illinois., from the site as it stood in the Wayback capture.
The noise of the helicopter drowned out the sound of the general street noise below and helped Crichton escape thinking about his current situation. Essentially, he had made a deal with the devil and was now waiting for the devil to stab him in the back. He didn’t trust Fleming in the slightest, so any deal made with the man had to be taken with a sea-load of salt. Crichton had already decided to betray him first. It was the only way he and Aeryn would benefit from the situation. Even if Fleming did deliver his part of the deal, Crichton didn’t want anything to do with it. A clone of Aeryn for him to play with? How could Fleming even consider that Crichton would upend the bargain? Simple. Fleming had the power. Fleming had placed a tracking device deep within Aeryn’s body and therefore would have very little trouble tracking them down if this went sour. Still, Fleming was being over confident, which could lead to him making a mistake and allowing them to capitalise on the situation. At the moment, however, Fleming had the option to be over-confident and cocky, because he had all of the power and was orchestrating the situation like a puppeteer. The over-powering noise of the helicopter was over-ridden by the commotion elsewhere in the cabin. Crichton didn’t so much hear the shouts and screams of the guards, but sensed aggravated movement, and consequently drew his attention to the beautiful city below and to the two guards at the other side of the cabin. The tracking monitor was bleeping a monotonous tone and flashing red. It had locked onto Aeryn. “Crichton!” Fleming shouted, above the noise of the helicopter. “Do you know what this means?” Crichton nodded. “Of course I do Fleming. I’m not an idiot.” “She’s close,” Fleming replied, apparently regardless of Crichton’s previous comment. “So be prepared to turn on your charms.” “Okay.” Fleming grabbed Crichton’s forearm sternly and glared directly into his eyes. “Don’t fuck with me on this one, because if you do, this time you will die!” Crichton didn’t like Fleming at all, but what if he did go along with the deal? He would get Aeryn back. He would be free from the army. He would receive a normal life. The pain would be over. The need to look over his shoulder at every turn would be over. He never said he didn’t like the pain. There was no way Crichton could follow through with the agreed deal, he wouldn’t be able to live with himself if he did and he wouldn’t be able to look any version of Aeryn in the eyes again. How could he betray the woman he loved? The deal betrayed every moral he stood for; so why was he even thinking about it? Crichton felt his gut turn inside out and the helicopter descended to the streets below. The helicopter landed on top of a high building, just the one of many in a row, ranging from different heights and various widths apart from each other. As soon as the Helicopter landed, he jumped out of the cabin and then ran out of the reach of the helicopter propellers and waited to be briefed by Fleming. Crichton watched as Fleming and two guards quickly jumped out the cabin themselves and made their way over to Crichton’s position, ducking slightly and fighting the wind resistance. As soon as they were safely out of the way the helicopter soundly took off again and made a quick getaway. Fleming held the small, hand-held, tracking monitor out in front of Crichton. “According to this,” Fleming said, as he checked the data and keyed in some simple commands. “Aeryn is a couple of buildings over. Go get her Crichton.” Crichton simply nodded in reply and moved closer to the edge of the building. Fleming placed a firm hand on his shoulder, and Crichton looked at it, then at Fleming. “Remember Crichton; don’t fuck with me on this one. Remember what is at stake here!” Fleming added. “Don’t worry,” Crichton replied, removing the tracking device Fleming had just craftily placed on his shoulder. “I’ll remember.” With that, Crichton ran, jumped over the 3 meter gap between buildings and carried on toward his intended goal. As Fleming watched him go, he bent down and picked up the discarded tracking device Crichton had discovered and then smiled. One of the guards glared at him uncomfortably. “How are we going to track him now?” Fleming glared at him, his smile still widely apparent. “Don’t worry yourself about that Rogers.” He replied, heading over to the fire escape stairs. “You wait here just in case he does go along with the deal. I’m going to get some coffee.” * * * * * He was beginning to get tired. Despite being fit, leaping from building to building was hard work and if Aeryn was on the move, he couldn’t see how he would be able to catch up with her. Crichton ran past a small building-like entrance to the building below and that’s when he found her. He ran past, hardly noticing her, and he only did so when she called out. “Halt!” She shouted, “What do you want?” When Crichton heard her, he had already past her and was running up to jump across to the next building. However, when he heard the voice, he tried his best to stop, but in the end he could help himself from falling over the edge of the building. He managed grab a hold of the edge of the roof, but still it was a decidedly precarious situation to be in. After a couple of seconds or so, Aeryn appeared, her head pooping out over the edge. A brief glint of recognition rippled across her face, but then instantly changed back to an unnecessary stern look. “Who are you and want do you want?” she demanded. Crichton couldn’t believe his ears. He instantly forgot about his position and worried about Aeryn. Didn’t she recognise him? Or was she joking? The latter didn’t see plausible at all and the first suggested that she’d fallen into her brain washed personality. “Don’t you recognise me?” He replied, hoping to catch some sign of recognition. “John Crichton?” Another glint of recognition crossed her face. “Crichton?” He nodded. “Yeah.” “Shit!” she simply exclaimed, dragging Crichton up from the ledge of the building. For a second he simply stood there, trying to get over yet another near-death experience. He’d had many in his long life time, but he hadn’t one for a while and it was almost ironic that it was caused by the woman he loved. “Crichton…” Aeryn said, as she raised her pistol and aimed it at him. “What do you want?” Crichton looked at her, almost bemused, it appeared that Aeryn was being over ridden by her pre-set programming and that she was fighting it. Crichton held his arms out at either side of him, palms open, to try and put her at ease. “Aeryn it’s me, John Crichton, you know me. You know who I am, so can you please just put down the gun?” Crichton said, in an extremely calm tone. “John Crichton? Doesn’t ring any bells! I’m here to assassinate someone, so why don’t you just tell me who you’re working for?” Aeryn replied. “You know me!” Crichton pleaded. “Your father, Hank Sun, he trained me up to be a wrestler when I was down on my look. I met you when you were a waitress at a diner just outside Chicago. I saved you from a drunken customer,” Crichton paused. “You served as my valet, when I worked for the PCW. I watched you die at the hands of Fleming, when you came to rescue me from the army.” The same glint of recognition washed over her and this time she lowered the gun. Her eyes started to swell up with little droplets of water and she put her left hand over her mouth in an attempt to mask that she had begun to cry. Crichton walked over to her and embraced her, resting her hand upon his shoulder. She cried. “Aeryn, I’m here because the army sent me, they want me to hand you over to them.” Crichton said. Aeryn looked at him, a little bemused. “What?” “Making a deal with them was the only way I could get close to you! I don’t intend to follow through with it!” “How did you find me anyway?” “They planted a tracking device on you - turn around.” Aeryn turned and Crichton spotted the device instantly. The greyish, circular patch, about 3 centimetres in diameter had been placed at the base of her neck. Gently, he peeled it away and threw it onto the floor. “We need to talk.” he said, “But we need to move before they find us here.” “Okay” Crichton grabbed her hand and they made their escape. * * * * * The diner was nice on the outside, but a little shitty on the inside. The coffee was bad and his mood was equally as bad. His mobile phone rang. Bad news undoubtedly. Fleming extracted his mobile phone, pressed the ‘receive’ button, then placed the small device up against his ear. “Sir, the tracking device on the target went dead. How do you want to proceed?” the voice on the other end quickly fired. Fleming paused for a second, quickly thinking of a strategy. “This was expected. Get the helicopter back to the building and switch to the secondary target.” “Yes sir.” the voice replied. The phone went dead and Fleming quickly, extracted some money and placed it on the counter as he left. A smiled developed upon his face. “Crichton I knew you were going to fuck me over on this one!” he muttered to himself, as he left the establishment. * * * * * Crichton and Aeryn had put enough distance between themselves and the army, but still stuck to the roof tops. It was easier this way. Less people would see where they were going and it reduced the risk of Fleming ever catching up with them. “So, who is this Fleming then?” Aeryn asked, as they sat behind a small slop on top of the building. “Fleming is the bane of my existence. He is the one who killed -” Crichton stopped himself. He didn’t want to reveal that this Aeryn was a clone of the original. “He pissed me off severely.” “He killed the other Aeryn didn’t he?” She said, bravely. Crichton looked at his feet. “Yeah. She died trying to save me. It was Fleming who killed her.” “I remember being trapped inside the tank. Tubes coming out of every orifice, pumping sedatives and vitamins into my body.” Aeryn explained. “Sounds like cloning.” Crichton replied. “I know I’m not the real Aeryn - “ she started, “But my feelings remain the same, I…” The sound of the bullet cut through the air and more importantly the bullet cut through the back of Aeryn’s skull, killing her instantly. Crichton watched in horror, as he lifeless body fell on top of him and Aeryn’s blood surrounded him. “Noooooooo,” he bellowed, pushing her dead body away. Then he saw the helicopter. How could he have not heard it? How could he have not seen it? How did they find him? The Helicopter drew closer and Crichton could see Fleming sat in the cabin, a sniper rifle in hand. The helicopter still could not be heard. Fleming extracted something from his bag and threw it to Crichton. Then the Helicopter simply left. A tear strolled down Crichton’s cheek as the small hand-held monitor flickered into life and Fleming’s face appeared on the screen. “Crichton…tsk…tsk…you should never back out of the deal, you hurt my feelings!” the computerised Fleming said. “As you can probably tell, we were only interested in killing the target, she was too much of a threat, evidence of our botched experiments. That was your penance Crichton, if you’d of handed her over I would have done it behind closed doors. Now, because you backed out of your side of the deal, I’m going to do the same. No Aeryn play toy for you my boy.” Fleming paused. “And finally, you’re probably wondering, exactly how we found you. Well that’s simple. I placed a tracking device on you. Ain’t that sweet? You led us right to her! Now I know your probably thinking ‘I saw you do it and I took it off ’ back on that roof top there, but quite frankly, that was the one we WANTED you to see! Remember, in the helicopter on the way here, I grabbed you by the arm. That was the real tracking device. Well, be seeing ya!” Crichton threw the monitor on the floor. He fell to his knees and he cried. He was responsible for the death of the woman he loved - again. He had been manipulated by the army - again. He swore revenge - again.
