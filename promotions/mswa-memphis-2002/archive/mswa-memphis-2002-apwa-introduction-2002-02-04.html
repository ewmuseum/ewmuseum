The APWA

4 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
theapwa.tripod.com INTRODUCTION My name is Joe B. and I have been involved in the hobby of ewrestling for about three years now. My roots in the hobby go even farther then that, to almost 15 years ago when I used to play in the old IWA, play by mail fed that you could find inside wrestling magazines.
As far as ewrestling goes, I was extremely lucky in starting out, because the first fed I was ever accepted in was also the greatest fed I was ever in. It was called the NAWA, and run by the best President and Vice President you could wish to have. Unfortunatley the burden of handling a roster of almost 30 wrestlers, holding two cards a week (never more then half a day late) and a pay per view a month, on top of juggling a family and a career and a life proved to be too much. And after 16 months the North American Wrestling Alliance closed it's doors.
A few months after the NAWA closed, the WWA opened it's doors. Another great fed, with excellent guys that was run by and filled with former handlers from the NAWA. Unfortunatley after 6 months the same fate befell it. The NAWA and
WWA were both a place were people who enjoyed creative writing and wrestling were able to bring both of those into one great hobby.
About eight of the former NAWA/WWA handlers surfaced in the various fed's of the National Wrestling Council, for the next few months. Despite having an undefeated record during my tenure there, I did not have any fun, because the dominant rp style in the NWC was so much different then what I had grown accostomed to in the NAWA/WWA. It was never about the Win's and the Losses to me, it was about developing a great ewrestling character and telling a good story.
So that being said, I announce the creation of the APWA APWA Mission Statement If you have participated in ewrestling for any length of time , then you know how hard it is to find a good fed that actually stays open more then a month. A lot of people in this hobby, dive right into running a fed and feel like they need to make it the best , most professional federation on the internet. This usually causes a huge constraint on time and clashes with life in the real world..
Many of us in the hobby, forget that ewrestling is supposed to be about having fun and meeting and interacting with other people, who have the same enjoyment in wrestling and writing as you do.
Hopefully the APWA will be about nothing but having fun. And not about making our hobby seem more of a sure, then a fun distraction.
The APWA is not going to be one of those feds that expects more from you then you can and/or are willing to give. We are a casual fed , which means your participation is based around your time schedule. As long as you remain active in some way , we will continue to include you in everyday events.
This fed is catered around those who feel ewrestling is meant to be a haven for fun , not total and complete seriousness.
How the "APWA" will operate The APWA will be for the most part a roleplay board fed e-fed. Because having a board, makes it easier for everyone to have access to everyones rp's and not causing clutter and confusion through mass emailings.
The "APWA" is a booked fed. The outcomes of matches will be determined by angles/storylines with roleplays playing a smaller role. Roleplays will serve more as a way to set up storylines and angles and develop characters. That does not mean that roleplays are irrelevent, that just means that one roleplay a week or 5 roleplays a week, does not matter. Quality over Quanity. It is a term that all efeds use, but va=ery few actually mean. The APWA does mean that.
The "APWA" will have an open door policy to everyone involved. All handlers will have alot creative control over your characther, and the storylines and angles they are involved in. Your suggestions will be taken in and used to the fullest possible extent. You must take into consideration however , that the other person(s) involved in your feud may have ideas as well and even outsiders wanting to get involved , but rest assure we will do everything we can to listen and use your suggestions and requests.
We will feature one weekly card. These cards will be either full commentary OR in detailed summary. This will depend on my time limitations. I will make no promises.
RULES INTRODUCTION Welcome to the APWA! The APWA is a scripted, or booked e-fed. That means that
Angles are planned out. That does not mean however that roleplays are not important. People will get pushed on the basis of their roleplays, who roleplays the best will generally be in the top storylines and be farther up on the cards.
WRESTLER CREATION You can have one wrestler and one wrestler only, upon entering the APWA. An exception would be made in the case of a tag team, however a legitimate team that tags more than they do singles matches. Veterans that we trust can have more than wrestler, but only if they and we are sure they can handle it.
Original wrestlers only. There are no exceptions. Only original wrestlers. No real wrestlers and no cheap rip-offs of wrestlers. There will be no exceptions.
Hopefully this point has been made clear enough. Creation rules are on the application page. The sample roleplay is what we look at first and if it isn’t up to APWA standards, you will be denied. You will receive an answer as soon as possible, if you were accepted. You may or may not receive notice if you were denied. No matter what DO NOT POST A
ROLEPLAY UNTIL YOU HAVE BEEN EMAILED! ROLEPLAYING All Roleplays are to be posted in the Roleplay Board, which can be found in the
Memphis Wrestling Network section, on the Television page.
We expect one roleplay from you each week in order to be thought of as active. There is no limit to the amount you can roleplay, but remember that Quantity very rarely equals QUALITY. Also if you will not be able to roleplay for a period extending past 2 weeks, please contact us, so we can make arrangements and not job out your wrestler.
Any profanity that occurs “on camera” must be must be censored. The exception is words like damn and ass (along with its variations, like jackass, asshole, and etc..). These are fine and need not be censored, but it shouldn’t be every other word. The APWA is family entertainment suitable for
ALL AGES. Any profanity that occurs “off camera” in a roleplay, is all right as it is not televised.
“The Camera Rule” - Just because a roleplay is posted on Memphis Wrestling
Network board (roleplay board) does not mean that it is on camera and available for all to see. Sometimes a roleplay, just does not make sense as a televised promo, and can actually serve to hurt a storyline or an angle. We here at the
APWA understand that some wrestling handlers like to post roleplays that are not necessarily “on camera,” and fully understand and respect that. Here in the APWA we look for good quality writers, who care about working together with their fellow handlers to create a good story.
Also it would be wise to use a spell check before you post, because constant grammatical errors will be frowned upon.
Do not use insider terms like “jobber”, “shoot”, “angle”, etc.. A wrestler should never talk about winners being known beforehand, he must always act like he is really fighting someone. The APWA harkens back to the era before
Vince McMahon admitted that his WWF is Sports Entertainment, so for all intent and purposes, the APWA is REAL! Anything that says otherwise should be subtle.
NEVER mention stuff like “roleplays”, “handlers”, or other terms of that nature. Never call wrestlers by their handler’s real name.
Do not use members of the front office or other handler’s wrestlers, managers, valets, and characters without permission.
IF YOU ARE NOT READY TO READ THE ROLEPLAY BOARD AND READ THE RP’s OF EVERY
OTHER WRESTLER IN THE APWA, EVEN AND ESPECIALLY IF THEY ARE NOT INVOLVED WITH
YOUR WRESTLER, YOU ARE PROBABLY NOT READY TO BE A MEMBER OF THE APWA. Being aware of what is going on around you, and making sure you SELL every aspect of the APWA is very important.
STRATS AND INSTRUCTIONS For each match, you are expected to send in strats and/or instructions. These strats will encompass how your wrestler will wrestle the match, counter moves, prepare for sneak attacks, etc. Good strats make for better matches, the better the match, and the better the card. It is quite simple, the more effort you put into it the more you will get out of it. Length is not the important factor in a strat, it is the content that counts.
MATCHES AND CHALLENGES When a challenge is made, it is given a deadline date of two weeks from when it was issued. If there is no response, the match is automatically made. If it was an open challenge, the bookers will try and give the challenger a match soon.
However if Wrestler A challenges wrestler B and Wrestler B is currently in a long term angle with wrestler C, the match will be postponed indefinitely.
Avoid challenging for gimmick matches. Most APWA matches are normal ones, we only book gimmick ones with good reason. Usually because of an angle or a feud.
If you plan on doing a run-in during a card, you most notify the staff at least
3 days before the card is to be posted. Sneak attacks must include a valid reason and may be denied at the discretion of the staff. We cannot promise we will use all run-ins, since there may be more than one angle involved in a match and only so much can happen.
Expect if you cost someone a match, for them to be allowed to attack you during your next match in response. This is called the Equal Payback Rule.
The invisible hand in booking is your PUSH. Push consists of ranking, cooperation, and participation. Wrestlers who are ranked higher then their opponents will usually have a better chance of winning. Wrestlers who work well with others and are willing to put others over will have better chances of winning. Wrestlers who add things to the league (ideas, extras) will also receive a better PUSH. PUSH can mean everything to your chances of winning.
Everyone will be treated fairly with their push, if you work hard and accept things, you will be pushed in accordance to what you have given. Its important to understand that you cannot win them all (no matter if you deserved it that week) because sometimes we have plans that expand more then one week.
APWA will sign any matches that we feel should take place and will do so for the most part. However, we also want the wrestlers themselves to get to pick who they would like to wrestle. Therefore, if you want to request a match, simply e-mail me your request and we’ll see what we can do. If you are involved with a feud with someone then expect to be facing them more often than just regular people. This is wrestling people, and wrestling requires continuity.
CARDS Memphis Championship Wrestling is live wrestling show shown on local television in the Memphis, TN area. It usually always originates from the APWA arena in downtown Memphis and usually takes place on Sunday Nights. It can be pre-emted for a variety of reasons however and take place on a different night. You can usually expect at least one if not more title matches every week on this show.
The APWA may have Pay Per View Events, but not monthly like the WWF. If we do have a Pay Per View event it will more then likely be kept to no more then about 4 a year, to have extended angles.
Once in a very great while the APWA may host a free TV Super card (such as the old WCW Clash of the Champions), where up to 9 matches can take place in two and a half hours.
The PPV's and possible free TV super cards are, the end all cards. Feuds get settled, champions are made, and you can never predict what will happen. While some gimmicks might change the number of matches, most Super Cards have about
5-8 matches on them. RANKINGS AND CHAMPIONSHIPS Rankings will be based on various different things, mostly on the basis of role-playing and win/loss. All titles can only change hands with a pinfall or submission unless otherwise noted. You cannot hand a title to someone else, if you do so, said title becomes vacant. Champions may grant shots to anyone (Even non-contenders), but the APWA Front Office may force him to fight top contenders he may be avoiding.
The Memphis Heavyweight Championship is the top belt in the APWA. The holder is almost always the best wrestler in the federation. Headliners & the top five
Superstars are all contenders for this title. Must be defended at least once every month and at every Super Card.
The Television Championship is the second Tier belt in the APWA. The holder is almost always in the Superstar ranks (there are always exceptions). Headliners,
Superstars and top five Midcarders are all contenders for this title. Must be defended every month and at every Super Card. This belt WILL be defended weekly and will be at every pay-per-view.
The Tag-Team Championship is the main prize for all tag teams. It takes a great duo to win this gold. Must be defended once a month and at all Super Cards.
SUSPENSION/EXPULSION/JOBBING APWA reserves the right to suspend or fire anyone at any given time for violating any of the rules, stated above. Also, if you have a problem with how we use your character, come to us in a friendly manner about it. Don’t bitch about it, or you will job, plain and simple.
GENERAL RULES The APWA is a booked federation. Winners are decided by quality of roleplays, quality of angles/storylines and effort. A handler who roleplays even when he doesn't have a booked match coming up, is more likely to succeed than someone who only roleplays when he has a match booked for that week's show.
The APWA is a more old school federation. We try our best to mix decent storylines with great wrestling. We do have extreme matches, but we prefer they only be used for serious feuds and at Pay Per View Events. The rules are still enforced by our officials. A low blow seen by the ref is usually a DQ. You have to the count of ten to get back in the ring and most matches have time limits.
The APWA goes for realism, but keeps it family orientated. No guns or knives. There is no rape, racism, or murder. Everyone lives to wrestle the next day. The worst word you might hear on APWA television is bitch. Homosexuality may be hinted at, but very subtle. Women will not be taking off their tops. Don't like this, tough! On the rare occasions that we hold a PPV event, is when the APWA gets a little racier and the matches can get a little bloodier.
Act your alignment. Heels do not suck up to the fans, faces do not randomly attack people. A face cares about the fans, while a heel hates them. A tweener usually does not care either way what the fans think of him. FACES vs. FACES and HEELS vs. HEELS is very, very frowned upon, unless certain situations (i.e.
Hogan vs. Warrior Wrestlemania 6) All deadlines are Eastern Time zone, unless otherwise noted. All wrestlers upon contract acceptance become the intellectual property of All
Pro Wrestling. Whereas if your character leaves the federation, we observe the right to retain the character to be used to finish an angle and or a farewell match, if you will. In addition to this rule, there is something called the
Showtime clause: Essentially, If you leave, these things happen, and good luck.
But if you insist on making a pissy rant prior to your leaving, your character will be seized and paraded around like a complete moron.
---------- APWA: The Beginning Storyline outline for the opening of the All Pro Wrestling Alliance. PART I: The Purchase In September of 2001, Jack Lynch lost his job. At that point he was the commissioner of the multi-national Word Wrestling Alliance. The WWA was a global wrestling conglomerate, that united independent federations from across the globe, to create one large super promotion. Allied with the WWA were,
Germany’s GWF, Japan’s EAWC and RSW, Mexico’s ALW, Canada’s MLWA, and the GWI and ECCW based out of Chicago and Boston respectively.
After a successful 7 month run, the Phoenix, AZ based WWA began to suffer some stumbling blocks. The Keen Enterprises Board of Directors, (which was the parent company of the WWA) leveled a vote to rid its network of all wrestling related programming. Left without television, the WWA was forced to cease operations. Which left Jack Lynch, once again without a job.
He returned home to Memphis, TN to think about his options in life. He had enough money, to live out the rest of his years comfortably in retirement, but the call of the sport he loved was too great.
While he was working for the WWA, he was in negotiations to purchase the video tape archives of the SMWA, the fed which he had been affiliated with as a wrestler and booker for over 25 years. The SMWA archives were at that time owned by Jerry Barnette, another retired wrestler, who had changed the name of the SMWA to the UWA and had unsuccessfully attempted to take the promotion to larger heights outside of just the Memphis territory.
In 2002, Jack Lynch finalized his purchase of the intellectual rights to the
SMWA and the rights to promote in Tennessee. He immediately began contacting friends in the wrestling business and in other areas of business, to begin to pool investors and other sources of help, to start a new wrestling promotion known as the APWA.
Part II: The first few weeks. For angle and storyline purposes, the APWA will start out as a small, independent fed based out of Memphis, Tennessee. There will be no house shows, and all cards will originate from the APWA Arena in downtown Memphis TN. The
APWA Arena is a converted theater, which holds about 1,200 people.
At first there will be no television. The only Television will be the Memphis
Championship Wrestling cards, which will appear on local Television in Memphis.
Which means that all roleplays will take place off camera, unless it is an interview with a wrestling talk show, not affiliated with the APWA, or something to that effect.
After a few weeks, and a few cards under our belts. Once the roster has gelled and everything is rolling smoothly, it will be announced that the APWA has struck a deal with the local cable company to air around the clock wrestling programming on a cable access channel.
The channel will be called the Memphis Wrestling Network and will air the MCW show, old Memphis wrestling footage, wrestling interview and news show’s as well as “promos” by current APWA wrestlers.
Part III: The future The plan is that after a few months hopefully things will be rolling smoothly and one of two things would happen.
The APWA would apply to become a member of an inter-fed type organization such as The National Wrestling Council (NWC) or the Council of Affiliated Leagues
(theCAL), or the NeWA.
The APWA will change from a small independent regional promotion and become a
National promotion, having shows all around the United States and Canada. In which case the storyline would be that the APWA found some investors with the capitol to fund them and a National TV deal. Including a weekly syndicated show called “Coast to Coast” (the roleplay board), which will be used to show “televised promos” such as in-ring interviews from a house show or other forms of on camera promo’s.
