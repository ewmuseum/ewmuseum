Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

A founding announcement on 28 June 1997 introduced the Virtual Wrestling Association, “VWA for short,” calling for a Vice-President, writers, wrestlers, fans and a web page builder. A full information package five days later filled in the shape: two weekly shows, the six-match, Nitro-style Tuesday Night Terror and the hardcore, street-fight Hardcore Saturday Night, plus a monthly pay-per-view running from a “New Year's Bash” through a “Total War” to a “Hardcore Hell.” It decided results on the “WLS 5.3” simulator weighed against each wrestler's own submitted strategy, sanctioned four titles — the World Heavyweight, North American, Cruiserweight and Hardcore Tag Team Championships — and cast a full broadcast staff of commentators, a ring announcer, interviewers, officials and referees.

On its surviving 4 October 1997 broadcast, three of its four titles changed hands: Alex Extreme beat Predator with a moonsault into a legdrop for the World Heavyweight Championship; Predator, the same night, won the North American Championship from Roger Rampage by rollup; and Quezzy reversed a top-rope move into a rollup to take the Cruiserweight Championship from Dirty Doug Foreyes.

The following month, at its Hardcore Hell pay-per-view, its own president presented the World Heavyweight Championship to Nick Danger. By its next surviving broadcast, 13 December 1997, King Suicide held the North American Championship and Storm Inc. held the World Tag Team Championship, with no document showing how either title changed hands; that broadcast also announced a Christmas Clash pay-per-view never otherwise attested.

It kept operating into 1998 under the same president: a January information package and, by August, a recruitment post billing it as “the new fed on the block,” planning a fresh supercard with “ALL belts up for grabs” under a new pair of show names, Masters of Mayhem and Friday Night Fights. No closing notice survives.
