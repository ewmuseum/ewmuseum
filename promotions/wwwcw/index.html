A self-hosted original-character federation whose 8 November 1997 PPV triple bill narrates its own history on air, including a documented shutdown-and-restart after an earlier Royal Rumble. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World Wide Web Championship Wrestling opened its doors to outside members on 18 June 1996, growing past an AOL-only roster with a plain pitch: “If you join the WWWCW, this would truly be a World Wide Web federation.” Members could carry up to five wrestlers, singles or in tag teams, run on the WLS 5.1 and TNM 6.13 simulators.

It was still running well into 1997, listed in a community fed directory that September and recruiting that October across three of its own hosted addresses. On 8 November 1997 it staged a same-night triple pay-per-view, its announcers opening the first of the three, “On Your Screen, The Beginning,” by correcting themselves live: not the promotion's first pay-per-view but its second — the first had been a Royal Rumble, after which “the WWWCW shutdown” before this restart.

That first broadcast closed its own Intercontinental and United States Heavyweight tournament with Fredo beating War Dog for the Intercontinental belt; the ring announcer awarded War Dog the United States title anyway, on the same night, for having reached the final at all.

The second, Hog Wild 97, ran a fuller card: the Twin Sisters (Crazy Phill and Lunatic Larry) kept their Tag-Team belts by disqualification when the Dogs of Destruction interfered against The Graveyard, and the broadcast reported live that The Graveyard and a third wrestler, Sam Switchblade, had quit the promotion; Iceman took the Intercontinental title from Shooter with Mr. Moneybags's help as the Wolf Pack and the Foundation brawled at ringside; War Dog won a newly introduced Extreme Heavyweight Championship in a five-man Chicken Coop weapons match; and Godfather kept his World Heavyweight belt against Masked Judge only by double disqualification when that same stable war spilled into their match.

The third, Alliance Armageddon, settled the Wolf Pack and the Foundation's own feud in a five-match stable war for a new set of Alliance Championship belts — decided when a masked interloper turned on the Wolf Pack and pulled off his mask to reveal Foundation member Will Womack.

That triple bill is the last night the surviving record shows. How the promotion carried on from there, and how it ended, isn't in the surviving record.
