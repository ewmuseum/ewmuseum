Special Essay: Speaking in favor of Play-By-Mail leagues (i)

Mar 18, 1997
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I would just like to point out some things about play-by-mail leagues that people have not spoken about yet. I am no fan of the IWA, but others are doing a much better job than them and I don't think the whole industry should be painted with their brush. And in regards to PBM vs. E-mail: 1.) COST: First of all, play-by-mail is actually *cheaper* than e-mail leagues. Why? Nothing comes free, and e-mail wrestling isn't free. You have to pay your ISP. They aren't giving this service to you for nothing. Thus, to play e-mail wrestling, you've got to pay $30 every month or $20 every month or whatever. True, with the ISP you get internet access to other things, but suppose you don't want the other things anyway and just want to be involved in wrestling? If you look at just PBM wrestling vs. E-Mail wrestling, PBM is cheaper. You can get by on PBM wrestling with just $5 to $10 a month while you must plunk down around $20, if not more just to have access to e-mail. And we all know about the problems with AOL! Now, you could get juno (free e-mail) but then you don't get internet access and you have to wait a long, long time to get through the ads. Isn't your time worth some money too? So in reality, PBM is the better deal. 2.) EVERY MONTH IS BRAND NEW: In most e-mail sim leagues you choose attributes for your wrestler and that's it. You have no more control other than writing flashes. In PBM wrestling, you can change your strategy each month, You can bounce back from a very bad month to a very good month if you choose the right strategy. Each month is a brand new day, unlike in e-mail wrestling where you are frozen with the same wrestler with the same attributes forever. Now I know that booked leagues have strategy, but again I don't even see booked feds as true e-feds because they are based on the amount you write, not on the value of holds you choose. PBM and sim leagues are a better comparison because both are free from personal bias from one person to another and don't depend on how much you write or don't write. But again, in PBM you can change your strategy every month. 3.) EXPERIENCE CAN COUNT: You can truly dominate PBM if you stay around for awhile. Experience counts in PBM. That's not really the case for e-mail. Sure, in some feds you do get a few bonus points for experience, but that's also a weakness because who says you necessarily get better with experience? In PBM, you can get better with experience after you've been there for four years. Then again, you can be there for four years and still be Barry Horowitz. 4.) MORE THOUGHT: PBM is more of a chess game where thought and strategy go into it as opposed to e-mail, where you write a flash and that's it. Again, booked feds will claim they have strategies, but they are not judged by computers. They are judged by humans, who are open to being prejudiced toward people for various reasons. And many judge on "quality." What's quality to one is juvenile to another. PBM is not as quick as e-mail, of course, but that's good because it gives you some time to think and prepare rather than just shoot off a flash and be done with it. 5.) MORE WRESTLER CONTROL: In PBM, you have much more control of your wrestler. If you want, you can have him wrestle 30, 50 or even 100 matches in one month against whomever you want him to wrestle. This isn't true for e-mail. Sometimes you have to wait months to get a shot at a champ depending on how the schedule is. In PBM, you are guaranteed to get the match you want and you can have as many matches as you want. 6.) MORE HANDLERS: In PBM, you are in a situation with 200-300, if not more, other managers/handlers. In e-mail feds, you usually have between 15-25, if that, because the fedhead doesn't have time to do matches for 200-300 people. Some e-feds have tried to use more writers, but many of them poop out at the last minute since they have no financial stake in the matter. Other things come first for e-fed writers and fedheads such as school, family, work, etc. Thus they don't have time to serve large amounts of people and often cancel cards or back out at the last minute. In PBM feds, you have people who are paid to give their full attention to the leagues and give them their undivided attention and their full time. This isn't true for e-mail feds, which are done as a hobby. 7.) MORE STABILITY: In my experience with e-feds, for every one that survives you have 20 that don't last more than a month due to the time it takes to run them properly. And plus you have a lot of 12-year-olds who run these feds who just aren't mature enough to handle them and fold them up after you've invested a lot of time in them. This is not true for PBM feds, which are run by people who understand the importance of stability and service to the customer. 8.) MORE DETAIL: PBM is much, much more detailed than e-mail feds. E-mail feds give you a card and not a whole lot more, again because the fedheads don't have time to go into depth. PBM doesn't give descriptions of each match because in PBM many times there are more than 2,000 matches a month that get played due to the fact that people many times ask for lots of matches. Instead, PBM not only lists results, but gives reviews of the action, complete standings, descriptions of the wrestlers involved, a summary of who is feuding with who, call-in radio talk shows, fan letters, tongue-in-cheek monthly awards, Most beloved/Most despised lists, etc. The whole shtick is much more detailed and a lot more attention is given to the business than in e-mail simply because people who are doing it as a hobby don't have the time to invest like PBM people do. 9.) MORE ORGANIZED: It takes no time to download a PBM newsletter. A well-done newsletter gives you everything at one time and anytime you want to review it you can just pick it up and read it and know where everything is, as opposed to e-mail where if you want something from several months ago you have to weed through tons of e-mails -- which takes time. Or else you have to organize everything yourself -- which also takes time. 10.) LARGER TALENT POOL: PBM has access to a lot more people than e-mail does. Though the internet is growing, there are millions of people who don't get it and never will. Thus the PBM potential pool of talent and handlers is 100 percent of the wrestling fans while e-mail wrestling is limited to just those on the internet, which right now is only 10% to 15% of the population at the most. I enjoy e-mail wrestling -- though it took me going through about 50 leagues to find two decent ones. Literally. It gets old when your teenaged fedhead sends you copies of his report card and tells you how he wants to kill his parents for restricting his internet time. Even in the best e-mail leagues, the fedhead can only do so much because it's a hobby for him. And if he/she gets too bogged down, the next day you may find yourself out a league and out a lot of time invested. You may not have put a lot of money into it except for the wad of bucks you give your ISP for access each month so you can play e-mail wrestling. However, again, your time is worth something, isn't it? That's why you feel ticked off when an e-mail fed folds -- because you've invested a lot of time in it. In PBM, once a fed gets going, it's gonna be around awhile because people's livelihoods depend on it. You don't have to worry about waking up one day and seeing that all the time you've invested in a league has been wasted. An analogy before I close. There's a difference between cooking a hamburger at home and going out to Burger King. Heck, why go to Burger King when you can slap a burger on the grill yourself? After all, it is "free", isn't it? Well, it may be a little less costly and it may not be. But Burger King is a multi-million dollar business that's stayed around because it does things for its customer that the customer can't do himself. Same as PBM vs. e-mail. PBM has stayed around for years because it can do things e-mail fedheads can never do. E-mail wrestling is fine, but there are lots of things PBM can do that e-mail will never be able to do.
