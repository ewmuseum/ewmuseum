Special Essay: Speaking in favor of Play-By-Mail leagues (vii)

Mar 27, 1997
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Me: >> Janis: > >> All of the e-feds that I have seen allow players to make changes to >> their characters (though not every week, since that's a lot of >> paperwork for the fedhead to sort through), so this is not >> a fair criticism of e-mail wrestling. > Oh, yes it is. I don't know of any who let you change your attributes > every month. And if they do, they are the exception rather than the > rule. When I ran the WeWF I let players change their characters' attributes on a semi-regular basis, and the same when I ran ESW. I have seen the same in every Summit fed. >> If you want strategies, take a look at the Blitzmatch dice rules system >> (used by VCW and perhaps others), which requires both participants >> to submit a strategy for each match. > First of all, Blitzmatch is the exception rather than the rule. Most > e-feds are either booked or simmed. Besides, dice games is not what is > being discussed here. Dice games don't count as simulators? (OK, not if they're played over IRC or something where the players can call one move at a time, but dice leagues resolve matches via the fedhead sitting down at a table and rolling lots of dice, i.e. simulation). Maybe these are the exceptions, but if strategy is what you want in a fantasy wrestling fed, you *can* find it on the net. If the ability to change your character from turn to turn is what you want you *can* have it. Just as you have chosen your favorite PBM over the IWA, you can choose a simmed e-fed over a booked e-fed, or a strategy-based simulator e-fed over an attributes-only simulator e-fed. >> "More matches" does not mean "more control". > I beg to differ. You can select exactly how many matches you want your > wrestler to wrestle in PBM. Granted. But as I said, that's not "more control". A real pro-wrestler makes thousands of decisions every day: what to eat for breakfast, what type of workout program to use, whether to use steroids or not, what color boots to wear, which new moves to learn, etc. "Control" refers to how many of those decisions the fed lets you, the player, make for your characters, and in what detail. Here's an example of what I mean: suppose you are running a heel character and want to interfere in someone else's match. In some games (like the IWA), you can't do it (no control). In some you can do it, but only in a generic way (limited control). And in some you can control the details of the interference. This is certainly the case in those booked e-feds that you love so much :-) and in others as well. >> I don't know about >> the PBMs that you're in, but in the IWA the only moves that a player >> can make are "change stats", "sign match" and "submit flash", > Changing your strategy is a pretty major move. And you get to do that > every month. Most e-feds don't allow that option. I'm not arguing "most". If you want to compare the one best PBM to the worst e-feds, go ahead, but it's a pointless comparison. Instead, compare the best PBM to the best several e-feds, apples to apples. >> However, there's no reason why an e-fed can't have that many >> players, as long as the fedhead automates some of the more >> mundane features. > But this is the exception rather than the rule in e-feds. We aren't > discussing pie in the sky kind of stuff, what can be done in e-feds but > isn't in general. We are looking at what is generally done by e-feds and > comparing that to PBM. When you say "PBM" you seem to be referring to one (one that you've named, maybe two or three) successful PBMs rather than all PBMs (you've pointedly excluded the IWA from the debate). So you're comparing e-feds in general to the single best PBM, which doesn't seem fair. Why not compare the best e-feds to the best PBMs? If you want to argue "I know of one particular PBM which is better than TNM feds run by 12-year-olds or feds which never get off the ground", I won't disagree with you. But I don't think it's a fair comparison. >> You have mentioned a few things which e-feds are not doing, >> but there's no reason why they couldn't be done. > Like I said, I'm speaking in general about e-feds. But as far as saying > that e-feds could've, should've or would've done this or that, it doesn't > really matter if e-feds aren't doing it in general, now, does it? Suppose you provide fedheads with a convincing argument on why they should let players change attributes on a regular basis. Bang, problem solved. Suppose you convince fedheads to frame flashes in a TV-style format. Bang. Suppose you convince a fedhead to allow lots of players and lots of matches, and the fedhead sets up a few computer programs to handle it. Bang again. No one is a player in "e-feds in general", we're all players in *particular* e-feds. If you want to only join e-feds which meet certain criteria, go right ahead. If you think there are improvements to be made to the feds that you're in, go ahead and suggest them. The best thing to do would be to start your own fed, designed the way you want. If it's good, people will copy it. If you only consider what "e-feds in general" are doing *right now* you overlook the vast potential for change in e-wrestling. E-wrestling today is different from the way it was just a few years ago, and in a few years will probably be different from how it is now. If you have an idea for a niche you think should exist in e-wrestling, lobby for its creation, and do what you can to make it happen. Not everybody has the money needed to start a PBM, but there are lots of people capable of starting decent e-feds and who are interested in creating feds that people want to play in.
