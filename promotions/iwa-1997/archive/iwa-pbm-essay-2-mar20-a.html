Special Essay: Speaking in favor of Play-By-Mail leagues (ii)

Mar 20, 1997
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
After being in the PBM game the IWA for about 3 years, I felt I could write a decent addendum to Janis' message: > 1.) COST: First of all, play-by-mail is actually *cheaper* than e-mail > leagues. Why? Nothing comes free, and e-mail wrestling isn't free. You > have to pay your ISP. They aren't giving this service to you for nothing. > Thus, to play e-mail wrestling, you've got to pay $30 every month or $20 > every month or whatever. True, with the ISP you get internet access to > other things, but suppose you don't want the other things anyway and just > want to be involved in wrestling? If you look at just PBM wrestling vs. > E-Mail wrestling, PBM is cheaper. You can get by on PBM wrestling with > just $5 to $10 a month while you must plunk down around $20, if not more 1. If you're paying $20 a month for your ISP **JUST** so you can be in an e-fed, I think you're negelecting the great stuff you can find on the net :) Also, think about it this way: Most PBM cost at least $1 per match, and sometimes offer bonus specials of so&so many matches for $11 or $12 bucks, and that's per cycle. I don't know about other wrestling PBMs, but the IWA has 4 cycles "per month" (on average), so you could be looking at spending around $50 bucks per month for JUST the PBM. That's 30 bucks more than getting the ISP just for email. On the other hand you could just send in 1 match for the whole month, but most people in PBM's that I know (and I know a lot of them) have a lot more than one guy and generally average about $20 or more per month. And as far as Juno goes, the ads only show up while you're waiting for the system to connect and while the messages transfer. It's a pretty darn good deal for free e-mail. > > 2.) EVERY MONTH IS BRAND NEW: In most e-mail sim leagues you choose > attributes for your wrestler and that's it. You have no more control > other than writing flashes. In PBM wrestling, you can change your > strategy each month, You can bounce back from a very bad month to a very > good month if you choose the right strategy. Each month is a brand new > day, unlike in e-mail wrestling where you are frozen with the same > wrestler with the same attributes forever. Now I know that booked > leagues have strategy, but again I don't even see booked feds as true > e-feds because they are based on the amount you write, not on the value > of holds you choose. PBM and sim leagues are a better comparison because > both are free from personal bias from one person to another and don't > depend on how much you write or don't write. But again, in PBM you can > change your strategy every month. Well, yes, you CAN change your strategy in a PBM each month, though if you're in the IWA you run a great chance of them screwing it up (I've had the problem only once, so I can't really complain, but I have several friends who have been screwed out of world title because of the imcompetence of the IWA staff). The other problem with the IWA (and I don't know how it works for other PMB feds) is that usually one or two feds have consistent holds for a month, while the others get totally messed around with, leaving anyone in those feds out of the world title picture. Also, consider what's more realistic: In an e-mail fed (booked or simmed) your wrestler has a definite style, just like in real wrestling. In a PBM game you're trying to guess what holds will be worth the most points, so you may originally have brought in a wrestler like Yokozuna to a PBM game, but if you are sure that a Frankensteiner is going to be worth the most points in a given cycle, you're going to pick it. > 3.) EXPERIENCE CAN COUNT: You can truly dominate PBM if you stay around > for awhile. Experience counts in PBM. That's not really the case for > e-mail. Sure, in some feds you do get a few bonus points for experience, > but that's also a weakness because who says you necessarily get better > with experience? In PBM, you can get better with experience after you've > been there for four years. Then again, you can be there for four years > and still be Barry Horowitz. Experience counts only so far as you it gives you a longer time to get used to the ebb and flow of the points system. I was in the IWA for 3 years, and while I had a lot of titles and stuff, I never thought of my WRESTLERS as getting better, just me getting better at spotting the hold trends. E-feds (particularly simmed feds in this instance) are actually FAIRER because any wrestler coming in has as much a chance of doing well as the next person. This reflects more that their WRESTLERS is of a top notch, potential superstar quality than it does that the wrestlers handler knows the system better than a newcomer. It's like Rocky Maivea (or whatever his last name is) in the WWF: he's a newcomer, but he's immediately the Intercontinental champion. The reason I said a simmed fed is "fairer" in this regard is that a 10 or 11 year old efed handler who may not be a great roleplayer can still do well in a simmed fed. Likewise, a 10 or 12 year old may not be able to keep track of the hold values for a PBM and spot the trends as well as someone in high school or college (or even a college grad like me :)). > 4.) MORE THOUGHT: PBM is more of a chess game where thought and strategy > go into it as opposed to e-mail, where you write a flash and that's it. > Again, booked feds will claim they have strategies, but they are not > judged by computers. They are judged by humans, who are open to being > prejudiced toward people for various reasons. And many judge on > "quality." What's quality to one is juvenile to another. PBM is not as > quick as e-mail, of course, but that's good because it gives you some > time to think and prepare rather than just shoot off a flash and be done > with it. The same thing Janis says is good about PBM is what I consider it's glaring weakness: Time. I don't want to wait half a month to get the results of my matches, and I don't like the "trash talk" being a month behind, including sneak attacks. E-mail feds allow for managers to talk frequently via email or IRC to work out alliances, attacks, and angles that can be worked into a solid ongoing storyline that is near impossible to match with PBM's. > 5.) MORE WRESTLER CONTROL: In PBM, you have much more control of your > wrestler. If you want, you can have him wrestle 30, 50 or even 100 > matches in one month against whomever you want him to wrestle. This isn't > true for e-mail. Sometimes you have to wait months to get a shot at a > champ depending on how the schedule is. In PBM, you are guaranteed to get > the match you want and you can have as many matches as you want. You're not necessarily guaranteed the match you want in a PBM. I don't know about other games, but in the IWA you have to either already be holding a regional title or be ranked in the top 5 to get a shot at the League title. yes, you can get automatic shots at regional titles and tag titles, but you're looking at around 11 to 13 titles, which, in my opinion, makes the titles worth little. An efed is usually run much closer to a "real" league, where there are a limited number of titles an the top contenders are the ones who get the shots. And while you can get 50 or 100 matches in a PBM game, you're spending around 50 to 100 bucks to do it, and is it really realistic that you're wrestlers has the stamina to wrestle that much in a single month? > 6.) MORE HANDLERS: In PBM, you are in a situation with 200-300, if not > more, other managers/handlers. In e-mail feds, you usually have between > 15-25, if that, because the fedhead doesn't have time to do matches for > 200-300 people. Unless Janis' PBM fed differs ALOT from the IWA, those 200-300 managers aren't all in the same league. In the IWA leagues I've been in, you average probably 30 managers per league, sometimes less depending on how active the fed is. So really, the number of managers is about the same in a PBM league (as opposed to the overall game) and an e-fed, and since there are a HUGE number of e-feds as compared to a relative handful of PBM's, you have a much larger handler group in the e-feds. > > 7.) MORE STABILITY: In my experience with e-feds, for every one that > survives you have 20 that don't last more than a month due to the time it > takes to run them properly. And plus you have a lot of 12-year-olds who > run these feds who just aren't mature enough to handle them and fold them > up after you've invested a lot of time in them. This is not true for PBM > feds, which are run by people who understand the importance of stability > and service to the customer. Well, I can't argue with this point. On the other hand, you're not being charged to pay for additional card writers, match processors, etc. > 8.) MORE DETAIL: PBM is much, much more detailed than e-mail feds. E-mail > feds give you a card and not a whole lot more, again because the fedheads > don't have time to go into depth. PBM doesn't give descriptions of each > match because in PBM many times there are more than 2,000 matches a month > that get played due to the fact that people many times ask for lots of > matches. Instead, PBM not only lists results, but gives reviews of the > action, complete standings, descriptions of the wrestlers involved, a > summary of who is feuding with who, call-in radio talk shows, fan > letters, tongue-in-cheek monthly awards, Most beloved/Most despised > lists, etc. The whole shtick is much more detailed and a lot more > attention is given to the business than in e-mail simply because people > who are doing it as a hobby don't have the time to invest like PBM people > do. I don't know which feds (both PBM and E-fed) Janis is in, but I disagree with this point. All the e-feds I've been in (and i've been in a number of them), as well as the one I run (the WIWA), have always given either play-by-play of the matches, or commentary about each match afterwards. Most leagues have guestbooks to use as interview forums or flash links, as well as rankings for both singles and tag, a lot of them now have hotlines or some type, as well as a lot of the other features that Janis talks about. Take a look at leagues like the IIWF that post their cards on this newsgroup and you'll see what I mean. > 9.) MORE ORGANIZED: It takes no time to download a PBM newsletter. A > well-done newsletter gives you everything at one time and anytime you > want to review it you can just pick it up and read it and know where > everything is, as opposed to e-mail where if you want something from > several months ago you have to weed through tons of e-mails -- which > takes time. Or else you have to organize everything yourself -- which > also takes time. I've never had to wait longer than, oh, about 15 seconds to download a card from any of my feds, and I'll take that over waiting 2-3 weeks for a bulletin to arrive. As far as organizing my email to keep track of stuff, I have one folder for each e-fed I'm in, as well as a folder for the league I run. I've never had trouble keeping up on an angle with that, and with the leagues I'm in that have guestbook inteveriew forums, all the interviews are archived, so it's not much trouble at all. Much simpler than having 2 years worth of bulletins from a PBM in a 3 ring binder and having to go through each one to find a certain interview or match result. > 10.) LARGER TALENT POOL: PBM has access to a lot more people than e-mail > does. Though the internet is growing, there are millions of people who > don't get it and never will. Thus the PBM potential pool of talent and > handlers is 100 percent of the wrestling fans while e-mail wrestling is > limited to just those on the internet, which right now is only 10% to 15% > of the population at the most. This, I believe, is almost completely wrong. PBM has a much SMALLER talent pool than e-mail does. Just take a look at the number of leagues on the net (take a look at just the ones that have been around for over a year if you want) and you will have a greater number of handlers than any PBM game does. Why? Because PBM games only have a very very finite number of ways to reach potential customers, mostly wrestling magazines, and you can count on your fingers and toes the number of those (mostly they are the ones put out by Bill Apter). The Apter mags usually just have advertising from the IWA (when they do advertise). On the net you can do a websearch for "e-mail wrestling", find a few feds, then follow links all over the place to hundreds (literally) of feds. Also, while not everyone has a computer, consider that most colleges and now many high schools etc have free internet access for students. Also, the net reaches around the world, so while there may be thousands of wrestling fans in Germany for example, they arent' going to spend money on the IWA or whatever, because they'd never recieve the bulletins, etc. on time. On the net, though, they get the cards at about the same time as anyone else (depending on their email server). E-wrestling has a worldwide talent base.. PBM's are pretty much restricted to where they can get bulletins to in time. > An analogy before I close. There's a difference between cooking a > hamburger at home and going out to Burger King. Heck, why go to Burger > King when you can slap a burger on the grill yourself? After all, it is > "free", isn't it? Well, it may be a little less costly and it may not be. > But Burger King is a multi-million dollar business that's stayed around > because it does things for its customer that the customer can't do > himself. Same as PBM vs. e-mail. PBM has stayed around for years because > it can do things e-mail fedheads can never do. E-mail wrestling is fine, > but there are lots of things PBM can do that e-mail will never be able to > do. Actually, it's a good analogy, but let me give you another way of looking at it: Cooking the burger at home costs a bit more, and takes more time (like a PBM game). Burger King is so successful because it's convenient (they're all over the place) and it gives the customer what they want. An E-fed is much more convenient than a PBM game (just turn on the computer and check your e-mail. No costs involved, no long delays, no lost match sheets, no transposing numbers for holds, etc). PBM games will always have a future, but more and more I believe E-feds will be growing stronger as they offer faster service, and, since they are run by fans and not businessmen, e-feds offer a much greater degree of empathy from fed heads than a PBM ever will be able to do. After all, fed heads on the net aren't in it for the money, they're in it because they love the sport.
