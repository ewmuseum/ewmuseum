Special Essay: Speaking in favor of Play-By-Mail leagues (iv)

Mar 24, 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[name withheld] < [detail withheld] > wrote: > I would just like to point out some things about play-by-mail leagues > that people have not spoken about yet. I am no fan of the IWA, but others > are doing a much better job than them and I don't think the whole > industry should be painted with their brush. Fair enough. Perhaps you could say which PBM's you like, in case others are interested. Skipping over the parts which other people have already replied to... > 2.) EVERY MONTH IS BRAND NEW: In most e-mail sim leagues you choose > attributes for your wrestler and that's it. You have no more control > other than writing flashes. In PBM wrestling, you can change your > strategy each month... Each month is a brand new > day, unlike in e-mail wrestling where you are frozen with the same > wrestler with the same attributes forever. All of the e-feds that I have seen allow players to make changes to their characters (though not every week, since that's a lot of paperwork for the fedhead to sort through), so this is not a fair criticism of e-mail wrestling. I haven't seen TNM, though, so maybe that's what you're referring to. If you want strategies, take a look at the Blitzmatch dice rules system (used by VCW and perhaps others), which requires both participants to submit a strategy for each match. > 3.) EXPERIENCE CAN COUNT: You can truly dominate PBM if you stay around > for awhile. Experience counts in PBM. That's not really the case for > e-mail. Sure, in some feds you do get a few bonus points for experience, > but that's also a weakness because who says you necessarily get better > with experience? I'm not sure I understand. Are you saying that *characters* should necessarily get better with experience? If so, I don't see what you have against a "bonus points" system. If you're saying that you as a player will be more successful with experience (for instance, by learning what types of strategies to have your character use against different types of wrestlers), then any game which uses strategies or allows you to modify the character from week-to-week (or month-to-month) will do. > 4.) MORE THOUGHT: PBM is more of a chess game where thought and strategy > go into it as opposed to e-mail, where you write a flash and that's it. Janis, you say you've tried out a lot of e-wrestling feds, but it sounds they were a very similar group of feds. Play the Blitzmatch rules if you want strategy, or the AWI rules (I couldn't find them on the net just now, but I think they involve match-by-match strategies). > 5.) MORE WRESTLER CONTROL: In PBM, you have much more control of your > wrestler. If you want, you can have him wrestle 30, 50 or even 100 > matches in one month against whomever you want him to wrestle. This isn't > true for e-mail. Sometimes you have to wait months to get a shot at a > champ depending on how the schedule is. In PBM, you are guaranteed to get > the match you want and you can have as many matches as you want. "More matches" does not mean "more control". I don't know about the PBMs that you're in, but in the IWA the only moves that a player can make are "change stats", "sign match" and "submit flash", and that's all the control that the player has. Most e-mail feds let you make more complicated moves, like walking out on a tag team partner in the middle of a match, taking an intentional DQ or countout to avoid losing by pinfall, stealing a title belt, or wrestling under a mask with the risk of being unmasked. > 6.) MORE HANDLERS: In PBM, you are in a situation with 200-300, if not > more, other managers/handlers. In e-mail feds, you usually have between > 15-25, if that, because the fedhead doesn't have time to do matches for > 200-300 people. I don't see having 200-300 players as a plus. However, there's no reason why an e-fed can't have that many players, as long as the fedhead automates some of the more mundane features. I don't know whether you've seen the wargame Galaxy, but it uses an automated turn checker to process each player's move and send back a message confirming receipt and describing any errors. Someone could set up such a thing for an e-fed: each player sends a list of matches to the turn reader, which checks them and concatenates them all to a file. Each week, the fedhead submits that file (of possibly hundreds of matches) to a program which figures winners and losers and puts together some kind of summary, and maybe even e-mails the summary to the players automatically. That would take minimal involvment from the fedhead, basically maintenance work to make sure that the programs are running smoothly. > 8.) MORE DETAIL: PBM is much, much more detailed than e-mail feds. E-mail > feds give you a card and not a whole lot more, again because the fedheads > don't have time to go into depth. I'm a little confused about this. Almost every fed that I've been in gave some kind of description of each match, while you say... > PBM doesn't give descriptions of each match because in PBM many times > there are more than 2,000 matches a month... So what do you mean by "a card and not a whole lot more"? From what you say below, it sounds like you want general stuff and *not* descriptions of each match. > Instead, PBM not only lists results, but gives reviews of the > action, complete standings, descriptions of the wrestlers involved, a > summary of who is feuding with who, call-in radio talk shows, fan > letters, tongue-in-cheek monthly awards, Most beloved/Most despised > lists, etc. The whole shtick is much more detailed and a lot more > attention is given to the business than in e-mail simply because people > who are doing it as a hobby don't have the time to invest like PBM people > do. You forgot to mention that e-mail runs much faster than PBM (simply because e-mail propogates in seconds or minutes, while letters take two or three days each way), once per week or so rather than once per month. So the e-fed writers have a lot less time to work with than do PBM'ers. Certainly someone could put together a once-per-month e-fed, and put comparable effort into it. > 9.) MORE ORGANIZED: It takes no time to download a PBM newsletter. A > well-done newsletter gives you everything at one time and anytime you > want to review it you can just pick it up and read it and know where > everything is, as opposed to e-mail where if you want something from > several months ago you have to weed through tons of e-mails -- which > takes time. Or else you have to organize everything yourself -- which > also takes time. This doesn't sound like a complaint about e-mail feds per se, just against e-mail feds that send out lots of separate mails in-between cards. It sounds like you would rather receive one e-mail each week, with the card and flashes all in the same file, and I'm sure that's how some feds do it (and it's how I would do it, if I were running a fed). Janis, your first paragraph advised people not to paint all PBM feds with the same brush as the IWA, and then you went on to lump all e-feds into the same category as cut-and-paste TNM jobs by 12-year-olds. You have mentioned a few things which e-feds are not doing, but there's no reason why they couldn't be done. It sounds like your ideal e-fed would look something like this: - lots of players (hundreds) - a rules system involving match-by-match strategies (like Blitzmatch) - automation, to allow lots of matches and frequent changes to characters - monthly cards, allowing the writers time to put special things together Heck, if you know C programming you could set this up yourself.
