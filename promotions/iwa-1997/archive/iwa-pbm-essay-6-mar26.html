Special Essay: Speaking in favor of Play-By-Mail leagues (vi)

Mar 26, 1997
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
> >All of the e-feds that I have seen allow players to make changes to >their characters (though not every week, since that's a lot of >paperwork for the fedhead to sort through), so this is not >a fair criticism of e-mail wrestling. Oh, yes it is. I don't know of any who let you change your attributes every month. And if they do, they are the exception rather than the rule. > >If you want strategies, take a look at the Blitzmatch dice rules system >(used by VCW and perhaps others), which requires both participants >to submit a strategy for each match. First of all, Blitzmatch is the exception rather than the rule. Most e-feds are either booked or simmed. Besides, dice games is not what is being discussed here. > > >I'm not sure I understand. Are you saying that *characters* should >necessarily get better with experience? No, in PBM, you get better with experience. Thus, since you're the manager and since you get better, your instructions to them (ie, your strategy) get better and they will improve. > If so, I don't see what >you have against a "bonus points" system. Because this is an automatic increase due to experience. However, experience doesn't necessarily mean someone will get better. Like I said, in PBM, a wrestler can get better with experience or they can end up like Barry Horowitz. You have the opportunity to use experience to better your wrestler, but it doesn't automatically come. >"More matches" does not mean "more control". I beg to differ. You can select exactly how many matches you want your wrestler to wrestle in PBM. You can't do this in e-wrestling. If you want 100 matches this month in any e-fed, I doubt you'll get them. I don't know about >the PBMs that you're in, but in the IWA the only moves that a player >can make are "change stats", "sign match" and "submit flash", Changing your strategy is a pretty major move. And you get to do that every month. Most e-feds don't allow that option. And signing matches is a big deal because you get as many as you want -- not true for e-feds if you want a whole bunch. And as far as "flashes" go, PBM does them a lot better than the e-feds I've been in by organizing them into one neat television-type format like you see in WCW and WWF -- including sneak attacks mixed in with the interviews. This may be done in some e-feds, but not in general. >and that's all the control that the player has. Uh, that's quite a bit. More than e-mail gives you. >Most e-mail feds let you make more complicated moves, like >walking out on a tag team partner in the middle of a match, >taking an intentional DQ or countout to avoid losing by pinfall, I see this as a weakness. The ability to keep a belt via DQ is not fair to other players. I'm glad PBM doesn't do this! > >I don't see having 200-300 players as a plus. Your universe of competitors is bigger. But if you want a small gathering, PBM offers this as well. >However, there's no reason why an e-fed can't have that many >players, as long as the fedhead automates some of the more >mundane features. But this is the exception rather than the rule in e-feds. We aren't discussing pie in the sky kind of stuff, what can be done in e-feds but isn't in general. We are looking at what is generally done by e-feds and comparing that to PBM. >So what do you mean by "a card and not a whole lot more"? >From what you say below, it sounds like you want general stuff >and *not* descriptions of each match. Descriptions of matches are fine, but stuff like reviews of the action, complete standings, descriptions of the wrestlers involved, a summary of who is feuding with who, call-in radio talk shows, fan letters, tongue-in-cheek monthly awards, Most beloved/Most despised lists, etc. adds a whole lot more. >You forgot to mention that e-mail runs much faster than PBM (simply >because e-mail propogates in seconds or minutes, while letters take >two or three days each way), once per week or so rather than once >per month. Depends on what e-fed you're in. I have been in some that do one card a month or one card every two weeks -- about the same as PBM. >Janis, your first paragraph advised people not to paint all >PBM feds with the same brush as the IWA, and then you went on to >lump all e-feds into the same category as cut-and-paste TNM jobs >by 12-year-olds. No, that's not what I'm doing. I have had contacts with about 50 e-feds. About 45 of them failed to even start up. But I saw their rules and I read each new fed that is advertised on this newsgroup and very little is different from the others. So I think it's fair to make some general statements about e-feds as you do in your essays each week. And I will say that I have run into a lot of 12-year-old fedheads in e-mail and absolutely none in PBM. I'm not saying that all e-feds are run by 12-year-olds, but I am saying you don't have to worry about that kind of thing in PBM. > >You have mentioned a few things which e-feds are not doing, >but there's no reason why they couldn't be done. Like I said, I'm speaking in general about e-feds. But as far as saying that e-feds could've, should've or would've done this or that, it doesn't really matter if e-feds aren't doing it in general, now, does it?
