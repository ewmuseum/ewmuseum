Special Essay: Speaking in favor of Play-By-Mail leagues (v)

Mar 24, 1997
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[detail withheld] wrote: >After being in the PBM game the IWA for about 3 years, I felt I could >write a decent addendum to Janis' message: Unfortunately, you're experience in this particular fed was probbly a bad one. I am not defending the IWA for, as I said, I think they are awful. But I do believe that there are some very good things in play-by-mail wrestling that wrestling fans should look at twice, including e-mail people. I don't know about other wrestling >PBMs, but the IWA has 4 cycles "per month" (on average), so you could be >looking at spending around $50 bucks per month for JUST the PBM. I am somewhat familiar with the IWA, and for the record this isn't how they operate. Like most PBM leagues, they put out each of their leagues once a month and they have several leagues. The more leagues you play in, the more money you MAY spend, but you don't have to spend any more money if you want to be in more than one league. If you want to spend $10 a month, you can either spend it on one league or split it up between many different leagues. Sure, you COULD spend $1 million a year on PBM, but the fact is that it's entirely up to you what to spend. And if you only want to wrestle, $5 to $10 a month is a lot better way to go than to hire an ISP just to e-wrestle. On the other hand you >could just send in 1 match for the whole month, but most people in PBM's >that I know (and I know a lot of them) have a lot more than one guy and >generally average about $20 or more per month. This is not true. There are lots of people I know in PBMs who have several guys and still play just $10 a month. They may wrestle one "squad" one month and the other the next or else just split their funds up between their wrestlers. Either way, there are people with substantial stables who still average $5 to $10 a month. It's all up to the player. And as far as Juno goes, >the ads only show up while you're waiting for the system to connect and >while the messages transfer. It's a pretty darn good deal for free >e-mail. Yes, and from what I understand it could take a long, long time for those ads to download. Plus, you don't get the Internet for free with Juno. In fact, you don't get the Internet at all with Juno. >Well, yes, you CAN change your strategy in a PBM each month, though if >you're in the IWA you run a great chance of them screwing it up (I've >had the problem only once, so I can't really complain, but I have >several friends who have been screwed out of world title because of the >imcompetence of the IWA staff). Again, I agree that the IWA sucks. But I've been involved in the PWA for more than five years now and they have NEVER screwed up a strategy. The other problem with the IWA (and I >don't know how it works for other PMB feds) is that usually one or two >feds have consistent holds for a month, while the others get totally >messed around with, leaving anyone in those feds out of the world title >picture. Again, like I said before, I'm not defending the IWA here because it is sub-par. But other PBMs do these things much better. Also, consider what's more realistic: In an e-mail fed >(booked or simmed) your wrestler has a definite style, just like in real >wrestling. In a PBM game you're trying to guess what holds will be >worth the most points, so you may originally have brought in a wrestler >like Yokozuna to a PBM game, but if you are sure that a Frankensteiner >is going to be worth the most points in a given cycle, you're going to >pick it. I know of some players who have particular wrestlers for each style and choose their holds in that manner. And they are very successful. Let's remember, in e-mail you don't even get a chance to choose your holds. >Experience counts only so far as you it gives you a longer time to get >used to the ebb and flow of the points system. Not true. Many hold values in PBM are based on their effectiveness in actual pro wrestling. I was in the IWA for 3 >years, Well, like I said, I'm sorry for you. and while I had a lot of titles and stuff, I never thought of my >WRESTLERS as getting better, just me getting better at spotting the hold >trends. Well, you are the representative of your wrestlers and are their manager and instruct them on what holds to use. Both e-mail and PBM wrestlers are make believe, so you don't really have a wrestler in either anyway. What you do have in PBM is the ability to tell your wrestler what holds to use and as you get better at choosing holds, your wrestler also improves. E-feds (particularly simmed feds in this instance) are actually >FAIRER because any wrestler coming in has as much a chance of doing well >as the next person. But it is a bit unrealistic. A new wrestler coming in must learn the ropes in the fed before he moves up into championship status and that takes time. For a new wrestler to come in and get a title shot after one or two matches (which happens often in e-mail wrestling) is unrealistic and is unfair to the veterans who have toiled long and hard for those title shots. In PBM, you gradually get better as you become more familiar with the fed and soon work your way to not only title shots but winning those titles as well. This is much fairer and more realistic. This reflects more that their WRESTLERS is of a top >notch, potential superstar quality than it does that the wrestlers >handler knows the system better than a newcomer. No, it doesn't. It's all up to the handler on what attributes you choose. It's like Rocky Maivea >(or whatever his last name is) in the WWF: he's a newcomer, but he's >immediately the Intercontinental champion. Wrong. He was there for several months. He just didn't walk in and become the champ. He's been there for awhile. >Likewise, a 10 or 12 year old may not be able to keep track of the hold >values for a PBM and spot the trends as well as someone in high school >or college (or even a college grad like me :)). Wrong again! The younger handlers are just as smart as the older ones in this kind of game, some even smarter. > >> >The same thing Janis says is good about PBM is what I consider it's >glaring weakness: Time. I don't want to wait half a month to get the >results of my matches, and I don't like the "trash talk" being a month >behind, including sneak attacks. Try another PBM game. You can spend as much time doing your strategies as you want or as little time, depending on the route you go, and still have success. For example, in the PWA, the tag team setup is much more complicted than the IWA as in the PWA wrestlers tag in and out and go from strategy to strategy. To figure out the ins and outs of this fully, it would take some time. But if you don't want to spend the time, you can just choose your moves for the singles wrestlers which is more straightforward and easier -- and quicker -- to do. E-mail feds allow for managers to talk >frequently via email or IRC to work out alliances, attacks, and angles >that can be worked into a solid ongoing storyline that is near >impossible to match with PBM's. This is not true. Letters go back and forth all over the nation in regards to sneak attacks and alliances in PBM and the smart managers know how to cope with the delays -- which give you more time to maneuver. >You're not necessarily guaranteed the match you want in a PBM. Yes you do! You're paying for it, you get it! Maybe the IWA doesn't do it that way, but the PBM games I am involved in do! An efed is usually run much >closer to a "real" league, where there are a limited number of titles an >the top contenders are the ones who get the shots. I don't know about the IWA, but good PBMs do it this way. And while you can >get 50 or 100 matches in a PBM game, you're spending around 50 to 100 >bucks to do it, and is it really realistic that you're wrestlers has the >stamina to wrestle that much in a single month? If you want to wrestle 50 or 100 matches in a month, shouldn't you be allowed to? Heck, the WWF wrestlers sometimes wrestle two or three matches a day! > > >Unless Janis' PBM fed differs ALOT from the IWA, I am. Believe me, I am. those 200-300 managers >aren't all in the same league. They are all in the same MAIL league. . So really, the number of managers is about the same >in a PBM league (as opposed to the overall game) and an e-fed, and since >there are a HUGE number of e-feds as compared to a relative handful of >PBM's, you have a much larger handler group in the e-feds. This is incorrect. The universe of PBM leagues are much bigger because all 200 or 300 managers are interwoven in the same system and are connected in a way where they can compete against each other. If you are in an e-mail league, chances are your universe ends in that one particular e-mail fed because it doesn't have connections with the other 100 or 200 or 500 other e-mail feds out there. Most e-feds are very independent. >Well, I can't argue with this point. On the other hand, you're not >being charged to pay for additional card writers, match processors, etc. I guess if you want some things you just have to pay a few bucks for them. And I mean a FEW bucks. > >I don't know which feds (both PBM and E-fed) Janis is in, but I disagree >with this point. All the e-feds I've been in (and i've been in a number >of them), as well as the one I run (the WIWA), have always given either >play-by-play of the matches, or commentary about each match afterwards. > Most leagues have guestbooks to use as interview forums or flash links, >as well as rankings for both singles and tag, a lot of them now have >hotlines or some type, as well as a lot of the other features that Janis >talks about. Take a look at leagues like the IIWF that post their cards >on this newsgroup and you'll see what I mean. No e-fed has what a complete PBM does. I'm talking about bringing the whole thing together. Feuds, complete standings, a review of the ENTIRE month's action, radio call-in shows, letters, awards each month -- I know the IWA doesn't do this, but I know that the PWA does. Again, I'm really sorry about your poor experience in the IWA and I commisserate with you in that. But I don't think you're fully informed on what the rest of the PBM circuit is doing. > >I've never had to wait longer than, oh, about 15 seconds to download a >card from any of my feds, and I'll take that over waiting 2-3 weeks for >a bulletin to arrive. How about downloading a full month of your cards? And keeping track of them in an organized manner so you can see when one of your opponents beat another opponent, how they beat them, etc.? Match apples and apples here. Month vs. month, a PBM newsletter does all the organizing for you. As far as organizing my email to keep track of >stuff, I have one folder for each e-fed I'm in, as well as a folder for >the league I run. I've never had trouble keeping up on an angle with >that, and with the leagues I'm in that have guestbook inteveriew forums, >all the interviews are archived, so it's not much trouble at all. You may have some angles tracked, but not all. You may have some wrestlers tracked, but could you tell me the height, weight, where they are from, their hair color, their manager's name, and what ring music they come down to at the same time? In PBM (yes, I know, not the IWA) it's all their on one page. You just look at it and in the time it takes you to look that up not only does the person with the newsletter know the information in question but is working on his strategy for next month to boot! >> 10.) LARGER TALENT POOL: PBM has access to a lot more people than e-mail >> does. Though the internet is growing, there are millions of people who >> don't get it and never will. Thus the PBM potential pool of talent and >> handlers is 100 percent of the wrestling fans while e-mail wrestling is >> limited to just those on the internet, which right now is only 10% to 15% >> of the population at the most. > >This, I believe, is almost completely wrong. PBM has a much SMALLER >talent pool than e-mail does. Just take a look at the number of leagues >on the net (take a look at just the ones that have been around for over >a year if you want) and you will have a greater number of handlers than >any PBM game does. PBM has access to every house in the entire world because mail is delivered to every house in the world. E-mail feds only have access to those who have the internet, which is only about 10 percent of the population at the most. Why? Because PBM games only have a very very finite >number of ways to reach potential customers, mostly wrestling magazines, >and you can count on your fingers and toes the number of those (mostly >they are the ones put out by Bill Apter). PBMs advertise a lot more places than that. The PWA has advertised on wrestling talk shows, other newsletters and mags as well. And most PBMs don't go with Apter anymore because he's way too expensive. On the net you >can do a websearch for "e-mail wrestling", find a few feds, then follow >links all over the place to hundreds (literally) of feds. First of all, more than likely you will not be hitting the usual wrestling fan with websearches because the average wrestling fan doesn't have e-mail. He is much more likely to go to a mall and pick up a magazine and buy that then he is going to a website because he is unlikely to have a computer in the first place. That's why the PBM universe is so much bigger than the relatively limited e-mail scale. Also, while >not everyone has a computer, consider that most colleges and now many >high schools etc have free internet access for students. First of all, it's not free. That's being paid for by someone. Secondly, you don't have unlimited use of that computer either. Also, the net >reaches around the world, so while there may be thousands of wrestling >fans in Germany for example, they arent' going to spend money on the IWA >or whatever, because they'd never recieve the bulletins, etc. on time. Wrong again. PBMs have customers all over, including England and the Bahamas and France and Germany and anywhere that wrestling is popular. And they have no problems in getting bulletins on time unless the post offices screw up or go on strike. And don't tell me ISPs never mess up. I'm still waiting for an important business letter someone e-mailed to me four days ago. >On the net, though, they get the cards at about the same time as anyone >else (depending on their email server). Yes, depending on their e-mail server, if it works, when it works, etc. Incidentally, I had to give up an e-mail fed I was running because my ISP was so poor and I know that nearly every e-mail fed I've been in the fedhead has had problems with e-mail and has had to change servers. E-wrestling has a worldwide >talent base.. PBM's are pretty much restricted to where they can get >bulletins to in time. People ALWAYS get their bulletins on time, no matter where they live. > >Actually, it's a good analogy, but let me give you another way of >looking at it: Cooking the burger at home costs a bit more, and takes >more time (like a PBM game). No, cooking the burger at home is cheaper. All you pay for is the meat, not the labor. An E-fed is much more convenient than a PBM game (just turn >on the computer and check your e-mail. Um, just pick up your newsletter and read it. No costs involved, no long >delays, no lost match sheets, no transposing numbers for holds, etc). The PWA never loses match sheets or transposes hold numbers. So I don't know what you're talking about. There are costs involved in e-mail wrestling, as previously noted, and a far as long delays in e-mail, well, I've seen some e-mail feds that have some hellishly long delays that make PBM waits seem like minutes. >PBM games will always have a future, but more and more I believe E-feds >will be growing stronger as they offer faster service, E-mail feds are only as dependable as their ISP, and many ISPs are just not ever going to be reliable. and, since they >are run by fans and not businessmen, e-feds offer a much greater degree >of empathy from fed heads than a PBM ever will be able to do. Do you actually think PBMs are run by corporate people with suits and ties who don't give a hoot about you? If you do, then you really do need to learn more about PBMs. After >all, fed heads on the net aren't in it for the money, they're in it >because they love the sport. If people who run PBMs are in it just for the money, they don't last long. Be it wrestling or sci-fi or other PBM games, you aren't going to get rich doing that for a living. That is for certain! If you don't love the sport, you certainly won't be running a PBM for long. Let me suggest one PBM to you: Phantasy Wrestling Alliance Box 542227 Houston, Texas 77254-2227 I would suggest you just send in $5 a month or so and see if what I say is true. Give it a few months, and if you don't like it, quit. That simple. Maybe your view of PBM will improve if you try out a good one.
