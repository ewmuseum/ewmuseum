A Toronto-set Usenet e-fed with a fifteen-year offline-then-online history, revived a third time in late 1999 under its founding operator after a year and a half of dormancy. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Northern Lights Wrestling Promotions ran offline out of the Toronto area from April 1994, moved online in December 1996, and went dark again in February 1998 — only to come back a third time that September, eighteen months later, under the same founding operator who had run it from the start.

Its biggest crossover event, the Longest Road, filled Toronto's SkyDome in May 1997 with a twenty-man Royal Rumble drawing wrestlers from three cooperating federations to decide its own vacant World Heavyweight title. Kid Ego, of the stable calling itself the Coalition, entered the match first and, with his stablemates running interference to keep him in, was still standing when everyone else had been thrown out — the last man eliminated being pulled to the floor by a rival's own foot on the bottom rope, unseen by the officials.

Its September 1999 return stated its own goal in plain terms: cards across the hobby had grown “so huge that they are almost unreadable,” and NLWP meant to bring back a card “you could read... from end to end” and recapture the “live” feeling of a real broadcast. It ran a biweekly schedule built around a flagship show, plus a same-week reaction segment and a full preview show ahead of each card.

No closure statement of its own survives; its last surviving document is that September 1999 relaunch notice, still describing a promotion just getting back on its feet.
