Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

NWC: Japan opened in 1999 as a new territory of the National Wrestling Council, launched after the alliance's own feasibility commission judged a Japanese expansion viable. Its own rulebook, posted 29 August 1999, installed the newly-returned Jim Gamias as its on-air president, named Steve Higgins and Mr. Shirai as its regular broadcast team, and built the whole promotion around one championship, the All Asian Championship, and a touring exchange that rotated wrestlers in from the council's other regions to compete for a prize it called the Chi Medal.

It debuted on 25 September 1999 at the Sapporo Nakajima Sports Center, billing the card as both its own hello and the venue's own farewell: “Konnichi-wa To Eiketsu” — “Hello And Farewell.” Its rulebook ruled out both an American-style tag-team scene and pay-per-views outright, betting the promotion's drama on weekly angle work instead, across a roster its own rules capped between fifteen and twenty wrestlers plus a rotating slate of touring names.

Its own record turns its biggest spotlight on a single returning star: Shiryo no Yoshida, entering behind a masked woman billed as Xieng Chi Yoshida and carrying a championship belt from elsewhere in his own past with the letters “R-I-P” spray-painted over it, used a Super Terrific Happy Hour broadcast to renounce his own history at other promotions and warn, over a rising chorus of boos, that the young promotion was already doomed to the same fate.

The All Asian Championship itself was built out as a twenty-man Super Rumble seeding an eight-man tournament and a planned best-of-five final series. The surviving broadcast of its first round, staged at Nagoya Aichi Prefectural Gym on 17 October 1999, came down to Koichi Fukada and T-Rex Reich trading near-falls before Fukada won it with a clothesline that carried them both over the top rope, Reich landing shoulders-first on the floor.

How the tournament resolved from there isn't in the region's surviving record: its own broadcast closes on the promise of a second round from Osaka, but no further card survives to say who reached the final or wore the All Asian Championship first.
