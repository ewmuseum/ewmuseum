A simulator-booked Invision board that ran Monday Night Sacrafice, Thursday Night Burnout and a Saturday pay-per-view from 2005 to 2009, with a CEO whose office was a forum the roster could walk into in character. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Extreme Wrestling Association opened on an Invision board on 27 October 2005, with a welcome thread and a request for graphic designers posted twenty-one minutes apart. It ran three weekly brands — EWA Monday Night Sacrafice, EWA Thursday Night Burnout and an EWA Saturday PPV — and by 2009 had added a Wednesday Night Mayhem board that never carried a topic. The fed disagreed with itself about two of its own names: the board spells the Monday brand “Sacrafice” and the show titles spell it “Sacrifice”, and the commentary calls the company the “World Extreme Wrestling Association” once against “Extreme Wrestling Association” twice. All four readings stand as written.

Its shows were made by a simulator. Each match opens with Ms. Jackie reading a billing block — weight, hometown, three exclamation marks — and then runs as bracketed move-by-move narration with Jeff Brown, Chris Jackson and David Summers talking over it, and Brodie Shores, Matt Hockens or Jim Angler counting the falls. Wrestlers were billed out of Houston, Osaka, Whitehorse and Moncton, and one of them out of Moscow and North Vancouver in the same sentence.

The storylines happened in the offices. The Offices was a board with a room for each authority figure — a Chairman of the Board, a general manager for each weekly show, and a CEO billed The One and Only — and the roster wrote its way into them, in the two threads below.

Six championships are named in the surviving cards — World, Television, Lightweight, World Tag Team, North American and Hardcore — and the five shows that survive, all staged between 3 April and 29 May 2008, carry four title changes between them: the Model taking the World Championship off Commander D inside a Hell in a Cell, X-Rated winning tag belts the Chairman of the Board had stripped from champions who would not defend them together, and the Extreme Menace and Red Steel winning the Lightweight and Hardcore titles on the same pay-per-view.

The last post the board records is dated 12 April 2009. What survives is the board: five of its shows, three of its roleplays, its announcements and its off-topic threads, and its own index counting eighty show topics, twenty-six pay-per-view topics and a hundred-and-two-topic roleplay zone that the record does not otherwise hold. The host’s own notice, in 2013, records what became of the rest — the servers behind inactive boards had been shut down, and migration requests could no longer be processed.

Storylines

The Ladder Match Written by AI, 2026

On 7 April 2008 The One and Only pinned a sheet to his own office door in fiction — a ladder match at Royal Demolitions, a World title shot to each of the first six names to sign, the champion’s own slot pre-printed at the top — and the roster wrote its way past it over the next two weeks, in the roleplay the board holds under the same name. Andy and the Model found it first and settled who’d sign by a thumb-wrestling match; the Model won, signed all five open spaces at once out of spite, and got slapped by the CEO and a fresh sheet pinned in its place, one name to a line, which the Model then signed properly. Red Steel passed on his own spot to chase the Hardcore title instead, and Phoenix took it in his place. War Master signed next, then Dark Storm — superkicked by King the moment he turned the corner from the CEO’s door, and signing the sheet anyway, on the floor, moments later — then King himself, and finally “The Real Deal” Jimmy Steele, filling the sheet’s own six lines. A board post ten days later, the-return-of, confirmed the match had made the card. It ran as a 5-man ladder-match battle royal at Royal Demolitions on 10 May — War Master and Jimmy Steele eliminated by Phoenix, Phoenix eliminated by King, King eliminated by the Model — and the Model kept the belt. Dark Storm, who signed, is not among the five who wrestled; the record does not say why.

Dark Storm’s disappearance Written by AI, 2026

The same Royal Demolitions build staged a second thread the surviving record never closes. An interviewer caught Dark Storm backstage and asked outright whether he’d made up his mind about OXR’s offer; he got as far as “er...” before two masked men jumped him, dragged him to a car and drove off with him — on the same card, elsewhere, where he was signing the ladder-match sheet and getting superkicked for it. What OXR’s offer was, and what became of the men who took him, is not in the surviving record. Dark Storm wrestles again, unremarked, in the 29 May Burnout’s own seven-man battle royal three weeks later — the thread simply never comes back up.
