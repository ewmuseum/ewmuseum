Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Ultimate Wrestling Alliance ran on the Prodigy online service from 1992 to 1997, and it built itself as an indy promotion in a hobby full of world-touring ones. Its homage was to Philadelphia wrestling, and Philadelphia is where it staged itself: a card datelined Philadelphia, PA, a show out of the CoreStates Center “right off of Broad Street in south Philly,” and a commissioner announcing a new home base at the Palestra.

One night of it survives. On 24 February 1997 the fed ran a Random Rumble — thirty entrants announced, two-minute intervals, elimination by pinfall, submission or over the top rope — to fill a vacant World Championship, with the last two men standing set to meet in a ladder match afterwards. It did not go that way. Tables came out from under the ring all night, a wrestler who was not signed to the promotion ran in twice to a chant of “SHOW TROY THE MONEY,” and the finish arrived with four men going over the top rope on one crossbody block. A replay showed all four touching the floor at the same instant, and Commissioner Sullivan came out and turned the ladder match into a four-way.

That card is the promotion’s own second act, and it says so out loud: the opening voice-over announces that the UWA “has RETURNED to glory,” and a colour commentator back at the desk after a long absence puts it plainer — “A lot of things have changed since two years ago.”

How the promotion ended is told twice, and the two accounts are different stories. In the fiction, it collapsed financially after Anne Mystic’s kidnapping attempt on its first owner, Avery Everette, and the revamp that followed did not last long. Out of the fiction, the account is locker-room bickering and political jockeying, and a founder who stopped being interested in running it and closed up shop. Both accounts come from the same retrospective, written years later by people who had been in the room.

What came through the UWA stayed in the hobby. Tom Adler, Mike Randalls, Craig Miles, Troy Windham, Avery Prosser, Michael Manson, Shane Southern and Anarky all wrestled here, and Hornet appeared once. Three years after the doors closed, people out of the same room opened New Frontier Wrestling, and it is that fed’s own news archive where the Random Rumble card survived — incomplete. The copy ends mid-sentence during the ladder match’s entrances, so who left Philadelphia with the World Championship is not in the record.

Affiliations

New Frontier Wrestling (NFW)

Shared continuity with UWA (1992–97)
New Frontier Wrestling was announced 1 June 2000 and built out of the Ultimate Wrestling Alliance’s own community, three years after the UWA closed on the Prodigy service — “a host of fW.com regulars, UWA veterans and other random applicants quickly joined.” The same page names UWA regulars who continued straight onto NFW’s roster: Shane Southern, Michael Manson, Wildstar, Alex Wylde and Bloodhunt. Recorded as shared-continuity rather than successor-of: NFW’s own About page frames the promotion as something new built from the UWA’s people, not the UWA continuing under a different name — community continuity, not institutional succession.

CSWA

Member of UWA (1992–97) (attested Jul 1997)
UWA is one of fifteen leagues named in the 9 July 1997 register of Prodigy Wrestling BB federations — the register itself is hosted on the CSWA's own Prodigy member directory (pages.prodigy.com/cswa/fwlgin.htm), i.e. FW Central, the CSWA's own community hub. Not a formal superleague membership like the Enterprise Superleague's own six founding feds; recorded as fellow-member-of-the-same-hub instead.

ChampionShip Wrestling Alliance (CSWA)

Affiliate of UWA (1992–97) (1997)
The CSWA ran the Prodigy Wrestling BB’s own FW Central pages out of its own web space, and its League Info register of 9 July 1997 lists the UWA among the board’s leagues. CSWA’s own Unified title history separately bills Tom Adler as an AAWC/UWA star in its 1993 IRONMAN field.

PRODIGY's Wrestling Bulletin Board (WRESTLING BB)

Community — UWA (1992–97) was present here (1992–1997)
The board's own League Info register of 9 July 1997 lists the UWA among the leagues operating on PRODIGY's Wrestling BB, typed Generated / Roleplay, with a president and an empty news slot; the wing's own placard already records the fed as running on the service's WRESTLING BB under the topic FANTASY-PRODIGY, 1992–1997.

All Action Wrestling Conference (AAWC, All Action, 1993)

Crossover booking with UWA (1992–97) (1996–1997)
Xavier Virile, Nathan Storm, Mike Chain and Jimmy Ray Vaughn are all billed on both held Prodigy-era cards, sixteen months apart — the AAWC's own Oktoberfest '96 (25 October 1996) and the UWA's own Random Rumble (24 February 1997). The UWA card also refers to “<> X members,” a group the AAWC card names as the “<> X-Change.”
