Superstars like Paul Kiljoy. Matthew Reason, and Mack Ensasserol have been emerged from the midcard ranks to become champions. Legends like Gunnar Gaines, Trey Porter, and Caleb Temple have breathed new life into the federation. Some of today's best and brightest, such as Steve Spector and Tripp Shade, have made their way into the I-Slash's ranks. Each and every week, fans tune in, with the firm belief that on any given Graphic Nature, anything is possible. Against the backdrop of all this change, one of the few constants in the IWF/WOW is the Trifecta. They were kicking ass three years ago. They're still kicking ass. The mega-team of "Epic" Eric Travers, "Suicidal" Nate McMannis, and "The Big M" Maverick continue to be THE dominant force in the I-Slash. Simply put, very few superstars can say that they have produced at such a consistent quality as these three gentlemen. Eric Travers, once dubbed "Wrestling's best kept secret" has firmly established himself as one of the greatest to ever step into an I-Slash ring, along with the likes of DaMann and Stanton. Nate McMannis continues to entertain and always finds himself involved in the best storylines on Nature. His performances this year include not one but TWO potential MOTY candidates, a sickening PPV war with Paul Kiljoy and a brutal battle with Matthew Reason on free tv. Maverick continues his slow but steady rise to the top. The former IC Champion is teaming with McMannis these days, forming one of the most dangerous combinations in the league. However, with the I-Slash only recently establishing itslf at the very top of the industry, most readers are not familiar with the long and rich history of the Trifecta. For example, Nate McMannis and Maverick weren't always Trifecta members! Interested? Read on... RUMBINGS -------- The roots of the Trifecta can be traced as far back as Christmas Madness 2000, the December PPV of the IWF/WOW. The card featured, among others, a young Eric Travers, who by then had already established himself as a main event hand, going up against Hightower, then the Intercontinental Champion, in a Best-of-7 World Championship match. The pair put on an exhilirating show the weeks leading up to the supercard, and Travers was leading 3 to 0 as Christmas Madness arrived. However, something unexpected occurred. In one night, Hightower somehow mustered the will and fortitude to defeat Travers in 4 STRAIGHT FALLS to claim the IWF/WOW World Title. Naturally, Travers was outraged. He pointed out that he lost his first fall via countout, and felt screwed by upper management. An announcement was made during the PPV that [name withheld] was making his return to the IWF/WOW, and was taking over the ownership duties from [name withheld]. The wheels began to turn in Travers' mind. He came to the conclusion that [name withheld] had screwed him over so that Hightower would have to vacate his IC title and so, give [name withheld] the reason to hold a tournament to boost ratings for the then-ailing company. At the first Mayhem after the supercard, things came to a head. Eric came out during [name withheld]'s return speech and delivered some choice words of his own, accusing the new owner of screwing him over and partisan politics. Travers demanded a title rematch and was quoted as saying that he "never asked to be a martyr for th IWF/WOW." This incited the ire of former IWF/WOW superstar Elijah Flynt, who called out Travers, calling him "useless" and claiming that Travers didn't deserve the title, and didn't deserve a rematch. Sensing a potential ratings booster, [name withheld] quickly signed Travers-Flynt to a match, with Travers' title rematch on the line. The match was easily one of the most competitive and greatest ever on Mayhem. Travers and Flynt battled back and forth, each receiving his share of crowd cheers and boos, to a thirty minute draw. The fans went home happy, and Travers and Flytn developed a solid, mutual respect from the encounter. For the next few weeks, the two would continue to show their rivalry in front of the cameras, while secretly plotting to take the World Title from Hightower. As a result of their draw, a three-way match for the title was signed for Mayhem, involving Travers, Flynt, and Hightower. The match itself turned into an all-out brawl, all hell broke lose, and went to a DQ finish, with yet ANOTHER superstar, Jeremy Thranton, figuring into the title chase. All this set the stage for an epic four-way title match for Wrestlefest 2001. Travers versus Hightower versus Elijah Flynt versus Jeremy Thranton, the four biggest names in the IWF/WOW at the time. Naturally, [name withheld] was worried that the match would once again degenerate into a big brawl, and Elijah convinced him to sign a special referee for the match... IWF/WOW Hall of Famer The Prophet, who had televised problems with Hightower, Thranton, AND Travers. At Wrestlefest, Prophet's true loyalties came to the surface. It was revealed that he had been in cahoots with Travers and Flynt the whole time. The guest "referee" assaulted Thranton, while Flynt and Travers double-teamed Hightower. Towards the end of the match, only Flynt and Travers remained. The two remaining contestants wrestled for the title, and eventually, Travers rolled up Flynt for the win. The cameras went off with all three men raising each other's hands in the air. On the very next Mayhem, Eric Travers, Elijah Flynt, and the Prophet would dub themselves "The Trifecta," and the IWF/WOW would never be the same. EVOLUTION --------- The original Trifecta was drew in the ratings from the get-go. The entire angle was well-executed, and a lot of fans were swerved by the sequence of events. However, the best days of the Trifecta, as well as its worst, we're still to come. Although an instant success, the greatness of the first incarnation of the Trifecta would forever remain only as possibility, as the group immediately hit a snag. Elijah Flynt, a main event player at the time, would leave the federation shortly after the group was formed, due to personal reasons. After he left, Travers and Prophet stayed together and were locked in a feud with Jeremy Thranton. A handicap match was signed for End of the World PPV between the team of The Prophet and Eric Travers against Jeremy Thranton. Travers, then the IWF/WO World Champion, did not agree with the feud, as he felt that it made him look like a weak champion. Furthermore, the match was more of a blowoff to the Prophet-Thranton feud, and Travers' role in the angle was minimal. Unfortunately, at the time, there just weren't any wrestlers for Eric to face. However, the two made the best of it they could. At End of the World, the May 2001 I-Slash PPV, Travers and Prophet revealed the newest member of the Trifecta-- none other than I-Slash Hall of Famer The Idol! While the appearance of Idol renewed interest in the fledgling group, its effects were short-term. Eric Travers had already come into his own by this time, cementing his position as the top heel in the company and the leader of the Trifecta. However, the Prophet's illustrious ring career was winding down at this point, and the Idol never got it all abck together after his shocking return: his matches weren't as exciting as his past performances, and his alcoholism problems were well-depicted on and off-camera. The second incarnation of the Trifecta would last only three short months, and would not be involved in anything worth noting, other than a feud with future member (and then IWF/WOW Commisioner) Suicidal Tendency, better known today as Nate McMannis. Travers dropped the World title to Shane St. Clair at Egos and Icons 2001, and was suspended indefinitely shortly thereafter. When he returned in August, he was booked as a face and the Trifecta was officially dead. The next version of the Trifecta was almost formed upon Travers' return, as he and McMannis had been in contact with each other while Eric was suspended. This would not be the case, as it was McMannis who then took some time off, and the plan was nixed. However, the seeds had been planted for the Trifecta that most of us are familiar with. PERFECTA -------- On the April 19th, 2002 edition of Mayhem (one day before my birthday! - Ed.), Nate McMannis and Eric Travers finally agreed to a temporary alliance. McMannis, still the commisioner at this point, was locked ina feud with Justin Arcola, while Travers was facing the unpredictable Razor Ron Jeremy. To bolster their chances, the two decided to reform the Trifecta, which of course meant they needed a third member, and their choice would surprise many. Their choice was none other than "Angel of Death" Wade Thompson, the first IWF/WOW Champion and then the acting Vice President of the promotion. Although he is Eric Traver's mentor and brother-in-law, Thompson was already in semi-retirement at this point, and his decision to join the Trifecta raised more than a few eyebrows. The group suddenly found themselves controlling a surprising amount of power in the IWF/WOW, with the comissioner, the vice president, and a former world champion compromising its ranks. The group made its intentions clear: they wanted to wrest control from [name withheld] and take over the IWF/WOW. The latest incarnation of the stable found immediate success. Travers and Thompson wrested the IWF/WOW tag team championship a month after the Trifecta was reformed, and the group was figured prominently in the main event of Egos and Icons 2002, which was the first ever WarGames match, which involved all the top names in the promotion at the time. Egos and Icons would prove to be a milestone event in rifecta history, as events happened that would change the direction of the group. Maverick, who had won the Intercontinental Championship earlier that night, turned out to be the mystery partner of the Trifecta in that match, and Travers, McMannis, and Co. would roll through their opposition for the win. However, more surprises lay in store. On the July 18th 2002 episode of Mayhem, Travers and McMannis officially welcomed Maverick into their group. Thompson, having his wrestling "itch" scratched, would return to working behind the scenes, and The Big M was picked to replace him, forming the current version of the stable. The episode would see McMannis vacate his commisionership to pursue the World Title, and the Trifecta close the night by annhilating Paul Kiljoy, then the North American Champion. The Trifecta dominated the I-Slash during this period, and went into Summer Explosion 2002 with all kinds of momentum. That night in August may well be considered the greatest night in Trifecta history, as all three members came up as winners. Travers defeated Sammy Griffin, Maverick bested Kiljoy to unify the IC and Na titles, and McMannis reached the pinnalce of the company by beating Razor Ron Jeremy for the World Title. Since then, the group has had its ups and downs. The arrival of Rick Baptist and the EWA in the I-Slash seemed to dim the lights of the Trifecta, although they still figured prominently in the events going around the company. Nate McMannis would have a stellar feud with Paul Kiljoy that would put both men on the map. Maverick experienced some weight problems that hampered his some of his performances. Eric Travers would win the EWA title from the upstart Mack Ensasserol, and defend it against some of the best in the game. Tension between all three members towards each other could sometimes be seen. As of now, the future of the Trifecta is unclear. Rumours are flying about that the demise of the current Trifecta might be imminent. However, if ANYTHING can be learned from its history, it is the fact that just when you think the Trifecta is dead... it always comes back, in some form or another. Eric Travers, the only wrestler who has been in all forms of the group, spoke with us about his thoughts on the Trifecta. "The Trifecta has been such a big part of my last two years in the I Slash, from Prophet and Flynt to Idol, to McMannis and Thompson, and then Maverick... The Trifecta has been a dominant force throughout it's history. It's no wonder... The one constant has been me!" The three time IWF/WOW Champion continues. "But that said, I can seriously point to one thing that symbolizes how dominant this group has been for the last year. At least one of us has been a World Champion on every single day since Summer Explosion 2002. Nate held the I Slash title right up to PCW's Ring in the New, and the night he lost the I Slash Title, I grabbed the EWA Title and have had it ever since. If that's not a sign of dominance, I don't know what is."
