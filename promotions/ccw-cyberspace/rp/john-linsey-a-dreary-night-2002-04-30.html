John Linsey - A dreary night.

30 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is A Match RP in which John Linsey, framed by a poem and a long descriptive passage, cuts a promo on his three opponents from a playground.
Legend Board | Back to Roleplays Main John Linsey Entitled: A dreary night. Written in Tuesday, Apr 30, 2002, 18:08:33 Location: Sarrowe Park. This is a: Match RP the flame will perish into the fate of glory's hands. Beware of what’s to come. That was the only message I got from it. I wonder if I may, why it is the only message. I wonder what is to become and if I want to change the fates. If I only can. No, I can, I just don’t know if I want to. Curiosity plays it’s part, yes, but what’s more to the question is why I am holding back from the future. And why it’s coming straight at me. Then again, I never believed in fate. I never believed in time. I always thought rules were only to constrict. Only for a line for the ordinary people to follow because they were afraid. They were afraid of what was to come. I wonder if I am becoming them. What the future holds, only I can change. When I change it, though, there’s no turning back. There’s nothing left after I change it but the future beyond. I can only change the fates if I allow myself. What is holding me back? Why does the truth come as no surprise to me? It’s as if I knew that it was coming. It’s almost like I was the one who was supposed to tell these truth. Only if the world would believe. Only if I would stop thinking that it’s all a fairy tale and that I’m thinking everything up. And as the wind hit his face with the force of a locomotive on an endless flight through the graveyard tracks of a dying world, the trees by the side of the tracks scorched and charred by a forest fire, the worries of the world of a childhood filled with the screaming of a marriage that should never have been went through his mind like that very train through a dark dilapidating tunnel of painful memories and horrid pictures of bland broken windows and church bells ringing in a tremendous chorus of sound and bellowing screams of dying rabbits being crushed under car wheels; his mind was in an uproar of confusion, like the thoughts of any child going through the turmoil of change, the rapid destruction of his parents marriage, the only true things in his life he found to be completely false, at a total fault, and he was frightened by it. The fright of a cat in front of a moving van at a thousand miles an hour, and when the reflexes of the cat have failed it, it is flattened into a tortilla shaped patty with limbs; the horrific pop as the spine shatters like a window being hit with a baseball in a downtown residential as the children run from the old hag that owns the apartment. The screams of the same sort echoed in his mind as his feet graced the puddle after stepping off of the concrete sidewalk. Water splashed up from his foot and hit his pant legs darkening the hue. The damp cold rain water caused his calf to shiver as he ran through the drizzle. Sarrowe Park lay only one half of a mile away from his house as he trudged, half running, and half tripping under the weight of depression that had taken his mind away from him. His breathing was heavy and sounded like a steam locomotive’s boiler while pulling three hundred cars behind it. His chest rose and fell, his breath billowing out of his mouth in a cloud of mist that resembled that of the steam of an unattended kettle on the stove top, whistling and screaming from the heat. His black sweatshirt was all too large and cloaked him in what looked almost like a trench-coat, but of a different, softer material. And although it was to big, it did keep him quite warm. The cold air filled with raindrops bit at his ears like the dew on a flower after the dark night has passed. His black hair was drenched and clung to his face which he had already tried vainly to brush out of his eyes. The cold was tangent; you could feel it like it had hands grazing up and down your back, like ticklish feathers floating around you in a swirling current. It hit his eyes and they began to water, or at least that was what he would blame the tears on. The tears brought all too much pain and so he began to run as fast as he could towards his place of rest, his shelter, his home. He turned a corner, the sign read DARWIN AVE. His feet hit the pavement, black asphalt, with increasing intensity as the hope for the soft grass of Sarrowe Park entered his mind, the driving force of his quickened pace. He was clamoring around in his mind for anything to get away from the things in his world that only brought a sense of being less then everyone else. His mind controlled him, as he let it swiftly, with effortless strides; wisp him off into another world as the park came into sight. His heart raced, his head swelled, the beating in his chest grew heavy and loud as his feet neared the edge of the sidewalk and the luscious green that awaited them. He thought he would stop breathing before he would have the pleasure of the grass, the soft pillow of November leaves, placid mulberry trees, grainy wet sand, squeaking rusting swings. At last his foot gained what it had been searching for, the grass fell away under the impact of his foot, it hit the ground like a ball of led falling onto a satin mattress. The dew jumped into the air at his legs and he almost collapsed onto the ground as his thoughts melted into a drizzle of evaporating dreams. The world had left him, he was alone, and nothing could touch him as the mulberry trees creaked and swayed in the background. He let out a long sigh, breathed in deep once more and then finally, as if his legs had become liquid, collapsed under the almost deathly limp weight of his tired legs, a tree surronding his body. His legs practically folded as his body fell to the ground in an exhausting thump. The wet ground hit his palms and a leaf stuck to his right hand. His head was spinning like he had been twirling around in circles like little kids do to make themselves dizzy. His head just spun around in a washing machine motion as the mulberry trees above him floated by like an ancient form of animation through shadows. The mulberry trees looked like natives dancing around a huge fire with him as the center piece of their madness. The green giant pedestals reached for the sky like any gray metal sky scraper in New York. His hair was matted and wet with rain as it crossed his face and the back of his head hit the moist soggy ground. The wind picked up and the mulberry trees began to look like an army of towers forming a giant platoon of soldiers holding deformed dark brown wet wooden pools with green metallic tips. They swayed in the breeze. A voice ringed out, belonging to the man.. 'Group Therapy accomplished nothing of me. Some men, some women, ..they experienced glory and triumph. Me? Defeat and loss is what I gained from the occurance. But the generous CCW has offered me once more another try at grappling its weight. I have another chance of proving myself to the backstage offices of the arena..that the Eagle is to be taken seriously.' His eyes peered through the sheets of rain at his favorite piece of the entire park. His legs began to move before he knew they were even upright. His feet beat against the soft grass and then stepped off of the upper level ground down into the sand pit where the lovely toys sat waiting to be used. And as his feet hit the soft, damp grains of sand the sun broke through the clouds as if God had impaled the gray above John with a heavenly sword. The world instantly became bright and majestic with the light slanting through the clouds, as if the earth had been a tomb that had not seen the light of day since the beginning of time. The clouds parted and the luminescent glow began to intensify more and more until the entire ground had been painted with a golden glow. His feet fell unto the sand and his entire body began to finally rest. 'I endore three men tommorow night. Tristan Matthew, Zane Blackbeard, and Roni Savar. I do hope these young atheletes know what they are in for tommorow night. A nice beat down, complimentary of me. That's quite a deal, if I say so my self now.' The Big Blue Elephant was in front of him now, his feet ready to climb the metal steps leading to the elephant’s long trunk that he would slide down in a matter of minutes. His foot lifted from the ground and made it’s way onto the first of the six stairs, each one brought about its own glee and happiness that filled his head with thoughts of the distant slide that seemed all too far out of reach, his heart quickened as his foot hit the second step, the third, half way. His head began to seem as if it would explode if he did not jump over the last three and slip his way down the stretching sheet metal trunk of the elephant slide. His hands grasped onto the poles on either side of the steps and he pulled himself to the top of the slide skipping up past the last three metal graded stairs. 'Their journey into the CCW will shortly end, just like this slide before me. They are just merely opponents in my path to greatness. Opponents who will be taken off the victim list easily.' 'Now, this Tristan Matthew. The self-proclaimed young flame of the CCW. Tristan, that flame is slowly dying down..your career ends soon. The CCW is surely glad you are not the best to offer, for it seems they do not want to promote a young unintelligent male with the mind of a white rapper.' 'Blackbeard. It awfully comes to view that this man has an image of an overrated imbecile. Sorry to say, Zane..it's true, though. Your battle of triumph will end.' 'And Savar. This man looks like no competition, no threat to me. However, I may be wrong. I've always been the polite man not to jump to conclusions..but we'll see if my predictions come to life tommorow night.' He sat on the top of the slide for what seemed like forever, though it could have only been a few seconds before his hair was thrown into the air and the wind brushed his face as the slide took him away. The sky blurred in front of his eyes as he slid his way down the raindrop covered sheet metal for a trunk. The sky then closed and the mulberry trees flapped like rolling flags in the wind as John's feet collided with the sandy floor of Sarrowe Park. View Biography
