Something Had To Be Done

22 May 2002

What this is A prose roleplay in which Jason McAllister and a friend finish a roofing job while discussing an upcoming match.
Legend Board | Back to Roleplays Main Jason McAllister Entitled: Something had to be done... Written in Wednesday, May 22, 2002, 02:35:56 Location: Rural Iowa This is a: Match RP **Clack-Clack Clack-Clack, Clack-Clack Clack-Clack** A syncopated rhythm echoed out across the countryside, brash and purcussive against the more subtle tones of the wind through the trees and water in the creek down the way. Almost like a machine it was, so out of place with the nature around it. **Clack-Clack Clack-Clack, Clack-Clack Clack-Clack** With a keener ear for detail, two distinctly independent and individual beats were being played out. Each in time and keying off the other. One clear and bright with shimmering intonations that rang like a bell. The other sharp and hard, it's voicing the audible equivolent of the chemical trigger which forces a body to flinch. The work made the day pass quicker, and that was good thing these days. Kelly had headed south again to stay with Savanda for a few days before heading up north to look in on Becky. Not that Kelly heading out was a bad thing, in fact it was Jason's idea, but the quiet gnawed at him. Something had to be done. So he kept busy, little projects like countouring and terracing the planter boxes in the front yard, honing the cylinders on the 350 out of that old Camaro that Maxx Kodiak had blown up in early 2000, and others. But those tasks had come and gone with little relief to the sense that something had to be done. So he'd taken up the biggest task he had supplies for. Roofing the nursery addition he'd been working on. Still, working on a hot roof for hours by yourself is an easy way to drive yourself batty, so he'd called in some backup for company. Jack 'Crash' Williams was about as good of a friend you could find. No sooner than he'd hung up the phone was he rolling up the driveway in that Red Dodge truck that made Jason grimace. Not that it being a 'Dodge' or 'Red' was bad, so much as the big Iowa State flag that sailed from the CB antenna. Both men had started at the work rather silently after a quick rundown of what the general plan was for the overlaps. Time for work wasn't time for talk, at least not in these parts. To Jason, the silence was a relief. It gave him time to think about the upcoming events. But the silence was coming to an end as the last course had been laid and the caps on the gable were all that remained to be done. The last of the nails would be driven, and without the work the subject would come up. Jason knew it. Jack knew it. It was only a matter of time. Jack refrained as long as he could, waiting till after the scraps had been picked up and the tools cleaned away. He even made it as long as it took to dump three spoonfuls of sugar into his iced tea. Jack: So he's back huh? That was good ol' Crash for you. Why bother beating around the bush, when you could aim for the bullseye? The question made Jason grind his teeth in disdain, but at least it was out there. Jason took a long sip on his iced tea before responding, probably more out of need to calm and sort his thoughts than out of thirst. Jason: Yeah, Lucifer must've got tired of the competition, so he's back. Jack: You'd have thought fatherhood would have mellowed him a bit. Looks like he's back to his old stunts again. Got one over on SuMa Monday. SuMa. The name reverberated in Jason's mind for a for a moment. Jason'd never been one for spitting on the floor, especially his own, but it was apparently warrented in this case. Jason: That guy's a damn fraud and he deserved it. At least Paul doesn't hide what he is. That Aussie's due for a reckoning, and considering the way the brackets are set up I'll be glad to give it to him. But in the broader scope, he's small time, inept, stuck on his legacy. Screw legacies, there's work to finish. Jack: I hear that. I just don't like the idea of you going in against Arovnik alone, which you basically are. It'd be different if Phil hadn't been hamstringed. Jason: Would it? Phil came back to deal with Crocodile Dundee, no one else. Kensu's all wrapped around the axle about Paul's kid, but I still don't know if I'd trust him. He's too damn emotional for his own good half the time. Jack: John? Jason: Haven't spoke to him in weeks. Jack: Billy? Jason: Working fulltime out in Denver running the strength program for the Broncos. Jack: Gobby? Jason: Haven't heard word from him since he got back. Jack: Damn. Jason: So what're you doing Thursday? A palpable silence fills the room. Jack stared at the ice in his glass as he recalled the warning the doctors gave him. 'One solid impact' That's the way spinal injuries are. Still.... Jason: No... I was joking. Don't you even fucking think about it. This is my fight, and I'll fight it. Alone if need be. You've got a family to think about. Jack's eyes didn't leave the ice in his glass. He just stared at the intricate structures that are naturally etched into them, realizing how fragile they are. Jack: So do you Jason, so do you... The simple statement dropped it's weight on Jason in a way that only Atlas could understand. All the things that he feared for his friend now applied to him. The risks, did they outweigh the rewards anymore? Does pride overrule responsiblity? How far is too far, how much is too much? Jason: Do you still watch Lockdown? Jack: Every Thursday.. Every Thursday, how many? How many people would see? Enough. Enough to know that eventually his son or daughter would run into one of them. And for that reason, lord knows there were a thousand more; but for that reason Jason looked straight across the table at his friend, looking him dead in the eyes. There was no way on earth that Jason's child would ever hear of the day their father backed away from a challenge, or yielded to a convention that it was right to give up. Jason: Make sure your kids watch Thursday too. Slowly, ever so slowly, a knowing grin crossed the face of Jack Williams. View Biography
