Written in Sunday, Apr 28, 2002, 17:17:36 Location: The McAllister Farm, rural Iowa This is a: Interview Dawn, the quiet hours when the earth begins anew. The wind rustles the leaves on the trees and swirls the long-grass as the first rays of sun peek up from the eastern horizon, sweeping away yesterday’s tedium while making room for today’s promise. Everyday a new beginning comes with sun to greet the world. Everyday it comes. How often do you take advantage of the opportunity a new day affords you? As the first chords of the sun break across the sky, Jason McAllister makes his way out across the gravel driveway outside his home to the corral opposite the house, a mug of coffee in his hand. He sips at the coffee and watches the sun some up, just as he's done nearly everyday for the last 10 years. Just a behavior pattern engrained in him from his youth. Back then, his father would douse him with a cup of ice water if he wasn't up and doing chores by the time the sun came up. Now it's just his way of greeting the world everyday. Dawn, with all it's promise, never catches Jason by surprise, especially these days. Ever since Kelly moved in, things have been a little different. The small things in life seem to mean just a little more. Jason's eyes shift to the 'addition' to the house he'd been working on. The walls are framed and the rafters set, but little more would be done till Monday. The ladder had been safely hidden to keep Kelly from randomly hammering nails into the 2x4's, as she was known to do from time to time. In the pasture behind the barn, a small herd of black Angus grazed it's way across the hill. In a flash, Jason realized how much he was going to miss this little slice of heaven over the next couple of days. He had just about everything a man could want, there was little left to prove to anyone. But therein lay the catch. The difference between little and nothing left could just about fill the Grand Canyon. God, how long had it gone on? The total disrespect of CCW by the supposed 'majors'. First it had been PCW, and more recently OSW, that had deluded themselves into thinking they were the end all, be all in the world of professional wrestling. That would end soon. In the minds of some, it already had. Jesse Ramey knew what was coming, Moose was cagey enough not to stir up the hornet's nest anymore than it was. Even Doug Troy had forgone the usual bravado. Out of the 8 men in the match, only one was about to discover the bitter taste of shoe leather. Poor Clay Vendetta. All this time, and he was hot on the trail of the wrong guys. He didn't have a clue, and probably still won't well into the midst of his first CCW match when he'd be forced to work more technical than an armbar. That's where the differences lay between OSW and CCW. The in-ring philosophy. OSW relied on it's marketing savvy and risque content to sell tickets. CCW sold tickets on the fact that no where on earth could you find a more technically demanding, physical, or dangerous brand of wrestling. CCW prided itself on pushing the envelope farther and faster than anyone else. Sometimes the risks outweighed the rewards though. Last monday, Benny Blades had lost his footing on a scaffold. At least he'd been stabilized. Still, with a subdural hematoma, he wasn't out of the woods yet. Regardless of whom officially was taking credit for this invasion, this match on sunday would be dedicated to Benny, even if only in Jason's mind. Before Jason's thoughts were able to drift too far, the totally out of place sound of light jazz sounded from the house. Snapped back to the present, Jason finished the last of his coffee with a large gulp and turned back toward the house. The screen door opened with a creak, as Jason stepped into the back washroom. A pair of dingy, oil stained coveralls hung from the rack on the wall, a constant reminder that there was still work to be done. Stepping lightly into the kitchen, or at least as lightly as you can in a pair of heavy soled cowboy boots, Jason detected the scent of herbal tea. Kelly had taken to drinking it in the mornings, as it helped with her stomach. As Jason peered out into the living room, he saw her. She stood there in her flannel nightshirt, back to him, looking out the bay window on the wildflowers that grew in the planter in the front yard. For a moment, Jason entertained the thought of sneaking up on her. It was quickly dismissed. She knew he was there. He could tell by the way she brushed her hair back behind her ear, exposing that spot on her neck just below and behind her ear. The temptaion was too much. Jason strode across the kitchen to the living room and wrapped his arms around her. His lips brushing against her neck, his hands naturally found their way to her belly. To her perfectly round belly that had been growing steadily these past 4 months. Sure, it was fuel to a never ending stream of insults from Sebastian Toomes, but mostly that was out of jealousy. In his life, Toomes could only dream of something so pure and perfect. Jason: So how are we this morning? Kelly: Good. Still a little sleepy though. Jason: Nothing a nap won't fix. How's the morning sickness? Kelly: It's getting better. The tea helps. Jason: Good. I'd hate to have to leave while you're not feeling well. The akwardness that filled the silence that followed was palpable. But Kelly knew full well the road that lie ahead. The quiet weekends that would come. Still, she wouldn't have it any other way. She herself had been a 7 time CCW Women's Champion. The stresses and strain of the wrestling world were nothing new to her, and she realized the importance of the days to come. She sighed, then reached back and rubbed the short stubble that covered Jason's head. Kelly: I'll be fine. I'm a big girl, I can take care of myself. You go do what you have to. Jason squeezed her tight again, before letting her go and taking a peek at his watch. 6:10 AM.. Jason: Four hours till my flight. I better start packing. Kelly: Already done. She waved toward the front door, where a black duffel bag lie, CCW logo emblazoned across the side. A grin crossed Jason's face as he shook his head in mock disbelief, Jason: Do you want to go wrestle the match for me too? Kelly: You know I would. Junior and I'd give 'em the ol' sumo bump. Kelly bumped him playfully with her belly, and Jason sold the nudge like he'd been hit by a car. Kelly giggled as Jason playfully staggered backward across the kitchen tile. Jason: Remind me to talk to you first if I ever have to deal with Kimbusa. Kelly: Who's that? Jason: The OSW guy with boobs bigger than yours. Kelly: Well, does he wear a dress? Jason: Nope, that's Ramey's department. They shared a chuckle at the thought of the angle that haunts Jesse Ramey where ever he goes What the hell was he thinking? Guy in a dress? Yeah, that'll get over. On the counter in the kitchen, an alarm clock goes off. 6:15 AM. Jason looks at it for a moment, then looks back to Kelly. Her eyes sparkle in the morning light as she looks expectantly at him. Kelly: Time to go? Jason: 'Fraid so. Walk me out? Kelly: Certainly. Jason makes a grandiose display of extending his hand to Kelly, who makes an even more dramatic event ot the 8 ft walk to the front door. Once at the threshold, Jason spins lightly and wraps his arms around her once more. Kelly: Come back to me.. Jason: You know I will. He kissed her gently, aware of the noticable tension of the moment. This is they world they live in, these are the risks they take. Nothing will ever change that, but the focus is so much sharper with the increased importance of the match, and the fresh reminder of what can happen when something goes wrong. Even as he let her go and carried his bag to the truck, he wanted to tell her, to reassure her, to promise her that everything would be fine. But he couldn't with 100% certainty, and she knew fully well that he couldn't, and he wasn't about to start lying to her now. As Jason opened the door to the Chevy, he looked to her one last time. Her beaming smile and her perfect eyes and the statuesque way she carried their child. And he couldn't help but smile when she pumped her fist in the air, like a semi-driver pulling the cord for the air horn. It was the long standing signal for his finisher, and her way of saying 'Don't worry about me, just kick their asses.'. Jason climbed into the truck and started the engine, the V-8 rumbling in a low, feral growl. He looked in the rearview mirror one more time before putting the truck into gear. And as paradise shrunk out of view behind him, he made up his mind. There would be no fun and games this weekend, no half assed antics There was too much at stake, both personally and professionally. He was going to walk into that alley, and tear down any man that stood before him. It didn't matter if it was one or a hundred men, the decision was made. No one on this planet was going to stand between him and what he was fighting for. The right to go back to that house and that woman with his head held high. So for the next three days, no one is safe. No one..... View Biography
