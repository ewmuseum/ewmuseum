A real-wrestler-portrayal e-fed that phased itself into original characters over the fall of 1997, running a flagship PPV, a house show, and a self-branded fWo parody stable while fighting a worked interfed war with the IWL. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Pro-Continental Championship Wrestling's own served record opens mid-run, on 16 October 1997 at CaliQuake '97 in the Great Western Forum, staging a roster thick with real-wrestler portrayals: Sid Vicious, Stone Cold Steve Austin, the Undertaker, Sandman, Chris Benoit and dozens more crossing paths in a montage set to Dr. Dre's “California Love.” The fed was demonstrably older than that surviving corpus: the dethroned World Heavyweight Champion tells the crowd at CaliQuake he was “one of the few legitimate stars that was here when PCCW was first started, in fact, I was the ONLY star” — a first World Title tournament the surviving record doesn't reach back to. Shane Douglas walked out of CaliQuake the new champion, in an upset over a Triple Threat that also included Chris Candido and Bam Bam Bigelow.

An administrative announcement that fall confirmed a deliberate turn: “ALL ECW STARS HAVE BEEN TAKEN OUT OF PCCW AFTER CALIQUAKE. Other real wrestlers will be phased out shortly.” By its Jersey Jams house show that November, and its Sunday Night broadcast that December, the fed was staging a worked interfed war with a rival promotion, the IWL: Rob Van Dam crashed the Sunday Night broadcast in a PCCW shirt to recruit on the IWL's behalf, only for the fed's own top star to catch him mid-air with a Death Drop and order him out of the arena.

That same December card crowned a new World Television Champion out of the fed's own parody of the nWo, the fWo: Paul-Dog took the title from Ajax Shiva after outside interference, dedicating the win, in his own promo, to a beloved jobber character rather than to any of wrestling's real names. That 7 December 1997 card is the last document held; the fed was still running when its surviving record stops, and a separate thread-index capture attests further activity into 1998 and 2000 beyond what survives here.
