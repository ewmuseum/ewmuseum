Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Wrestling 316 returned for what it called a second year on 17 February 1999, reminding readers it had billed itself, a year before, as “the most ELECTRIFYING fed on the net.” Its relaunch bulletin is a full rulebook rather than a pitch: roleplayers owed at least forty-five lines per roleplay, three times a week; a Friday main card plus two house shows ran every week, with one pay-per-view a month; results for the main card were written “move by move, word by word,” while house shows got brief recaps; and judging went to effort over length, so that (its own example) three short forty-five-line roleplays would beat one two-hundred-line roleplay from a less committed opponent.

Every wrestler had to be invented rather than portrayed as a real performer — “Wrestling 316 permits fake wrestlers only, so you need to create your own character” — built around a strong gimmick the rules encouraged pushing hard, whether a take on a real wrestler's style or something wholly new. Members without enough of a push to hold their own on the main card wrestled house shows against staff-run non-player characters instead, and a wrestler could be sent on a world tour, facing more of those non-player characters abroad, with a good showing earning a return push toward a title shot. The federation claimed a substantial outside audience for the main card itself, citing figures approaching fifty thousand daily page views on its own site.

No roster, card, or title result survives from either Wrestling 316's first run or its 1999 relaunch. How long the promotion lasted after reopening, and how or when it finally closed, are both outside the surviving record.
