Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Phantasy Wrestling Alliance introduced itself in a recruitment notice of 17 February 1996 as a play-by-mail wrestling league already “in business now for five years,” with “hundreds of managers” spread across ten men's promotions, two women's and two mixed men's-and-women's promotions. Each promotion crowned six of its own titles, including a tag team and a six-person championship, on top of an alliance-wide singles, tag and six-person champion and three men's and two women's pay-per-view titles contested at every televised-format event. It staged its cards as a broadcast — good-guy and bad-guy announcers reading out contestant-submitted comments and sneak attacks — and ran the business behind it through monthly newsletters, priced at roughly a dollar a match, carrying a full roster with brief wrestler write-ups, singles/tag/six-man standings, a “Matchwatch” column tracking every running feud, a “Revue” recapping the month's in-ring action in depth, a radio call-in show and a rumor mill.

It ran its recruitment through the mail rather than the newsgroups it advertised on: anyone interested was asked to write, or e-mail their own mailing address, to Tri-J Communications' post-office box in Houston, Texas, for a free manual and sample newsletter. A year later, in March 1997, it was named elsewhere as a well-run postal alternative to the growing crop of e-mail federations — the league still apparently running by that recommendation's own account.

Nothing in the surviving record says how or when the Phantasy Wrestling Alliance closed, or who held any of its own titles. It ran its own league entirely by mail, under its own full name, at a time when at least one other federation of the era was billed only by the same bare initials.
