Contact info ============== | The Front Office | ============== -=-=-=-=-= I. What is the SVW?=-=-=-=-=- Silicon Valley Wrestling (SVW) is a local wrestling promotion based in San
Jose, CA and surrounding Greater San Francisco Bay Area. This fed is an e-mail based, original wrestler federation ONLY. No real wrestlers or rip-offs of any kind (i.e. Ice Cold Steve Nash, Bonebreaker Kid, Big Daddy
Cruel, etc.)
It is a promotion that will focus on a mix of roleplaying, strategy and angle development, but most importantly, fun for all those involved.
-=-=-=-=-= II. Who runs the SVW (OOC)=-=-=-=-=- President: [name withheld]. Background: I've been in e-wrestling a little over two years and have run to previous real wrestler federations, which have been very successful in the past. I exclusively compete in e-mail original wrestler feds and the time has come for me to run one. My goal is for every one involved to have fun and feel like this fed is worth their time and mine.
Vice President: [name withheld]. Background: I have been e-wrestling for just over 2 years using the same character pretty much the whole time. I currently handle Tracker in UEW and
AWMC. I've ran my own federation in the past. I am here to help out Brian with a few different aspects of the SVW, including but not limited to booking, match-writing and talent scouting.
-=-=-=-=-=-=-=III. Who runs the SVW (IC) Staff =-=-=-=-=-=-=- The In-Character Staff for the SVW is as follows -SVW President: Michael Ellis. Ellis is a former high-ranking executive who made some smart business decisions in the stock market and retired early. Only 38 years old, Ellis loved pro wrestling as a child and continued to follow it. He started the
SVW to bring the experience to the Silicon Valley on a regular basis. He is a shrewd businessman who doesn't stand for being bullied around. He doesn't show his emotions often, but you can still sense how he's feeling.
-SVW Vice President: Don Hutchison. Don was and still is the CEO of a high-profile California ad agency. He is a long time time business associate of President Ellis and was brought in as
V-P of the SVW for his exceptional marketing talent and PR ability. He never backs down from his decisions and is known for being a bit of a hot head. He will not think twice about slapping a fine on a wrestler if they get out of line.
-Play-by-Play Announcer: Richard Lancaster. Lancaster has been announcing for over 25 years and has extensive experience in England and Japan. He's well versed in a variety of wrestling styles.
Lancaster has seen it all and hates to see people cheated. He's a stickler for staying on task and calling the match.
-Color Commentator: Johnny Houston. Houston was once regarded as one of the best cruiserweights in the business until injuries forced him to retire in late 1999. A two-time MAWF
Cruiserweight Champion, he knows the sport inside and out and has no problems expressing how he feels. A heel by nature, he doesn't see anything wrong with "doing what it takes to win. WHOOOOOOOOOOO-EEEEEEEEEEEEE."
-Interviewer: Alyssa Lockhart. On the outside, Lockhart looks just like another pretty face, but she's been known to take care of herself and ask the tough questions when need be. She is always walking around backstage looking for anything that might be interesting.
-Interviewer: Jason Joseph. Called J.J. for short, Joseph is a man that lacks the confidence to stand up to some of the bigger names. He'll start off tough and back off if threatened. He is easily manipulated through fear and stays out of trouble whenever possible
Other Staff Members Ring Announcer: Chad Davis Head Referee: Ed Norton Referee: Dave Matlack Referee: Todd Stephenson -=-=-=-=-=-= IV. How is the SVW run?=-=-=-=-=-=- The SVW is run by allowing the handlers to be judged on several different platforms in addition to roleplaying, which will continue to be the biggest factor in match. The breakdown for how much things count in a match is as follows:
Roleplays: 60%- (for more info, see RP criteria) Strategies: 20%- (for more info, see strategy criteria) Angles: 10%- (for more info, see angle criteria) Heat: 10%- Heat is basically a factor of how much you "work" in the fed. If you put out quality RPs, you send in strats on a regular basis and contribute to angles, you get Heat. Consistency is part of Heat as well.
If you do one quality RP every 3 weeks, you won't get as much Heat as someone who does 2 quality RP a week. Heat will be used in very close matches.
================ | The Locker Room | ================ -=-=-=-=-=-= I. Roster/Wrestler Limitations=-=-=-=-=-=- As mentioned before, this is an original wrestler federation. Each wrestler must be under 7' 4" and 500 pounds. Realism is important; 7-foot luchadores are not realistic.
The singles roster will be limited to 30, but the tag division will not a have cap unless the division gets crowded.
Managers/Valets must also be original and limited to 1 per wrestler or tag team. -=-=-=-=-=-= II. Stables=-=-=-=-=-=- Stables are allowed and may be no more than 5 members to prevent an unbalance of talent in one group.
Stables must be a minimum of 3 members and have at least two different handlers involved. -=-=-=-=-=-= III. Important Deadlines=-=-=-=-=-=- Roleplaying in the SVW is done through e-mail. All RPs are sent to the Prez at [detail withheld] . RPs are sent in packets 3 times a week, Monday, Wednesday and Friday. RPs for each are due for each packet at the following times: Monday: Monday Night at 11:59pm Eastern Time Wednesday: Wednesday Night at 11:59pm Eastern Time Friday: Saturday Morning at 2:59am Eastern Time Strategy and Angle deadline: Saturday Morning at 2:59am Eastern Time. -=-=-=-=-=-= IV. RP Packets and Hosted Shows=-=-=-=-=-=- I will send out the packet shortly after each deadline has passed no matter how many RPs have been sent in (unless it is zero which I hope is never the case). 1 RP per wrestler per packet is the limit unless you have discussed it with me and your opponent ahead of time.
In addition to the Friday packet, there will also be a weekly SVW show called "Tech Talk" where the week's best RPs will be highlighted and matches previewed. If your RP makes this show, it is a cut above the rest. Any comments or predictions are purely for entertainment and are not meant to be taken seriously. Remember the word "fun."
-=-=-=-=-=-= V. General Rules=-=-=-=-=-=-=- Each handler is allowed 2 singles wrestlers or 1 singles and 1 Tag Team. It is hard to concentrate on more than 2-3 characters and do well with them.
If you want to bring in more than the allowed, come talk to me and we'll discuss it. Wrestlers will not attack other wrestlers or SVW staff in roleplays. You may not use others wrestlers in an RP without the handler's permission and I must be notified. You may use members of the SVW staff in an RP, but you must use them in an "acceptable" way, which means you can't beat them up, have them act out of character, or in the case of the front office, have them show favoritism toward wrestlers. Clear any questions about this with me and I may make exception if the reason is valid enough.
Sexual references that call a wrestler "gay", "fag", "queer" or referencing homosexual activity (i.e. fudgepacking). Blatant terms such as these will result in major penalization, loss of the match and/or suspension.
Kayfabe: Kayfabe is also called a "shoot" where real life is brought into the roleplay. Do not use a handler's name in an RP under any circumstances.
If you want to "shoot" about things that have happened in a wrestler's past
(i.e. in other feds) and it is pertinent to the roleplay or angle, I will allow it. Do not say, "I was ABC World Champion, therefore I will beat you." Your wrestler may have accomplished a lot, but this a fed that doesn't take past accomplishments into consideration.
You will be treated as well or as poorly as you treat others. If you show me and your other fed members respect and courtesy, it will be returned to you. Vulgar outbursts over silly incidents are a fast ticket out of the fed.
If you have a conflict with me or another fed member, I suggest you handle it on a one-on-one basis. However, should you feel the need to voice your complaints to the whole fed, use the OOC board. I've seen many feds crumble over OOC wars and granted they are fun to watch and be in (I know from experience), it really doesn't do any good in the long run. Therefore, please direct any complaints about matches, angles, etc. to me, the VP and/or the parties involved. It will be handled much better than airing dirty laundry on the OOC board.
Swearing is OK to the extent that it isn't predominant throughout your RP. Don't bother using * to censor out words, we know what you are saying. ============= | The Network | ============= -=-=-=-=-=-= I. RP Criteria=-=-=-=-=-=-=- Since judging roleplays is a very subjective process, here are the main qualities that RPs are judged on so you know exactly how RPs are evaluated
Setting: Is there a setting? Does the setting make sense? Is it creative?
Does it have a purpose? Does the character interact with the setting? How well? Content: Basically what you say and how you say it. Is it relevant to the match? Does it make sense? Long and boring RPs are discouraged. I don't want to read 150-200 line RPs and I also don't want to waste time on 20 liners. Find a happy medium when it comes to length and quality.
Character consistency: Does the character stay in a consistent tone of voice throughout the RP or make unpredictable switches (if your character is based on that, then switching it ok.). If there is a crowd around, does the reaction make sense or do they cheer a heel and boo a face?
Flow: How well does the RP flow? Does it jump from subject to subject or smoothly make the change. Does it begin and end in a logical manner?
Spelling and Grammar: I hate to read RPs that are poorly spelled and formatted. Take the time to at least run a spell checker before sending the
RP in. The more effort you show, the better the RP will be.
-=-=-=-=-=-=-=- II. Strategy Criteria=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=- Strategies are used to make sure that your character wrestles and acts the way you intended it. It also gives you more control over what happens in a match. Strategies in the SVW are not to be 9 page novels; you decide how long they need to be. They can contain specific moves and spots, but are not necessary. Here are the typical things included in a strategy.
-Generally describe how your wrestler will wrestle during the match. Will he be tentative, aggressive, not give a damn, etc. Include different attitudes he may have during different stages of the match.
-Spots: Spots are specific moves or combinations of moves that you want used to put over a specific aspect of your character, you can write simply what you want to see happen but there is a better chance of your spot getting used if you write in full play by play using the announcers and brackets
-How do you see your wrestler winning the match? Does he cheat? Interference from his manager? A face should win clean. Heels usually cheat. -What will your wrestler do if he loses the match? -=-=-=-=-=-=-= III. Angle Criteria=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=- Angles are a key part of e-wrestling and part of the fun. A good angle will benefit both participants no matter who comes out on top. Angles usually will drag out over time so if you attack someone on a card, don't expect a barbwire cage match with them the next week. Let things build. When sending in an angle, keep the following in mind:
-Why is the wrestler doing this? What is the motive? Random attacks with no basis usually will not go through. -Where do you see this angle going? Will it lead to a match? Will it move your wrestler up the rankings? Outline your plans. -Don't send in angles for people that are already involved in a major angle unless you have a way of getting in the on-going angle. The best way to do this is to talk to me and the handlers involved ahead of time, and then formulate a plan.
For on-card interviews, please use one of the designated SVW interviewers or just have the wrestler take the mic himself. Don't make up people for on-cards and angles. Also, keep in mind the reaction you want your character to get. If he is a heel, the crowd should not cheer for him, vice versa for a face.
============ | The Spotlight | ============ -=-=-=-=-=-= I. Cards=-=-=-=-=-=- The SVW will have one weekly card per week called Saturday Night Superstars. The card will be limited to 8 matches. A second show may be added should the roster grow to that point.
The show will be broadcast on local TV Station KICU 36 at 10 pm on Saturday
Night. There will be a Supercard every 2 months. There won't be any Pay-Per-Views because this is a local promotion. The Supercards will be broadcast in prime time on a big network station. Supercards will be limited to 10 matches.
-=-=-=-=-=-=II. Titles=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=- There will be 4 Titles in the SVW. They are: -SVW Heavyweight Title -SVW Peninsula Title -SVW Cruiserweight Champion (wrestlers under 235) -SVW Tag Team Title An additional title may be added if the circumstances are right. An individual wrestler may not hold more than one singles title unless it is part of an angle and cleared by me. An individual wrestler may hold a singles title and the tag titles at the same time.
-=-=-=-=-=-=III. Rankings/Title Shots=-=-=-=-=-=- Rankings will be computed on a weekly basis. They will be based on 3 things. -Win/Loss Record -Quality of Opposition -RP Quality and Activity Title Shots will be granted on a rankings basis or if an angle justifies it. That means a lower ranked person may get a title shot before the #1 contender if there is a superseding angle that justifies it.
