Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Global Wrestling Affiliation opened for business in March 2000, calling itself “a brand new e-fed” on a newsgroup already crowded with promotions insisting they were something harder or more extreme. It offered the opposite pitch: no gimmick beyond wrestling itself, a graduated ladder from jobber to opener to midcarder to superstar, and a promise that standing was earned by roleplaying rather than handed out. Its own recruitment run could not settle on its own name, calling itself an Association in its earliest posts, an Affiliation through the bulk of its spring drive, and an Alliance once its pay-per-views began — three spellings for one set of initials, all served here exactly as its own posters wrote them.

Its own president advertised himself, more than once, as an actual professional wrestler moonlighting as a newsgroup promoter, working alongside a second writer to script two cards a week and a monthly pay-per-view. A five-belt lineup shrank to four within its first month, and a sixteen-man tournament for its own World Title was still being organized as late as April.

Four surviving broadcasts carry the promotion’s only preserved storylines: a Thunder Down Under tournament final in May, a Hostile Takeover co-promoted with a stable called the Cash Money Brothers in September, an August Revenge card built around a Hell in a Cell match, and a November Exodus card that turned the promotion’s own front office into the main event, wagering an interim president’s job against a hostile takeover bid on a coin flip. Both invented wrestlers and real ones borrowed for the occasion shared these cards without distinction.

No closing notice survives; the last held broadcast still promises a fight for control of the promotion rather than an ending. It shares its own initials with an older AOL-hosted league and a Usenet relaunch three years earlier, but nothing in its own record — no shared roster, no shared title, no shared operator — connects the three.
