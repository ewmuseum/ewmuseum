A New York-billed fantasy federation that ran from January 2003, closed that August, and reopened in September 2004 with a weekly Sunday show, Aggression, and monthly pay-per-views, ending on an Ultimate Chaos in Detroit on 3 April 2007 that its own commentary named the company's last. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Chaotic Wrestling Federation's own About page dates its start to January 2003, when three members of the group Chaos A.D. set up a company of their own after another federation barred them. One of them, Dazz, became General Manager of its weekly show, Monday Night Havok, under the company's own President, and the first months brought a Television, a United States, a World Heavyweight, a Tag Team, a Cruiserweight and a Hardcore title. By its own account the company closed in August 2003 over money.

A businessman bought the name and, exactly a year later, reopened it: the first Aggression went out on Sunday, 12 September 2004, with an International title decided in a battle royal and a new Chaos title that could change hands anywhere. It changed owners twice in the first months of 2005, its Women's title history opens in March 2005, and six Hall of Fame pages record its inductees' careers, from Christian Cage's three World titles to Dark Rayne's undefeated run as International and Tag Team champion.

Two of its broadcasts survive as scripts. Genocide, on 22 October 2006, brings Violent A back after almost three and a half years to referee a World title match that Priest keeps over X-Factor, sees Christian Connolly win the International title from Fuel in a sixty-minute Iron Man match, and has Syd Ashley take the Women's title from Faith in a gauntlet. Ultimate Chaos, on 3 April 2007 at the Ford Field Center in Detroit, draws Dazz and Violent A as Chaos A.D. with Talent Inc, sends Jake Fury into retirement with a Barbwire Massacre win, and has Priest keep the World title against John Thomas.

That night's commentary calls it the last event the company will host: the Chairman had sold his majority shares the month before to a man who meant to rename it Motor City Wrestling and move it to Detroit. The site's later pages, from 2007 to 2010, are roleplays for that successor and for other federations, and none of them is part of this run. How the company's own pages ended is not in the surviving record.

Affiliations

Motor City Wrestling (MCW, Motor City, 2007)

Successor to CWF (Chaotic, 2003)
Sold and merged into a new company: the predecessor's last broadcast, on 3 April 2007, has its buyer announce the name Motor City Wrestling and a move to Detroit, and Dazz's roleplays say Faith, Priest and he came over when the buyer took on their contracts. The belts do not carry over: MCW's own champions pages show World, TV, Tag Team and Brutality belts of its own, the past World champions a 2008 roleplay lists (Jacob Laymon, Rayne Young, Annie Alvarez, Alcohol Paul and Whitecloud) include none of the predecessor's last champions, and Dazz calls Priest the predecessor's final World Heavyweight champion.
