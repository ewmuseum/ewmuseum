Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Great White North Wrestling billed itself, on its own talent roster of 2 March 1999, as “HardKore Canada,” running a deep stable of thirty-four singles wrestlers — tracked active, retired, or on hiatus, each with a running win-loss-draw record — alongside five regular tag teams and a further list of singles wrestlers paired as temporary teams when the card called for it.

Three days later, it announced King of Death '99, a sixteen-man interfederation deathmatch tournament open to wrestlers from any promotion, set for 25 April 1999 at the Air Canada Centre. It entered four of its own wrestlers, including its reigning Heritage Champion, “Tigerheart” Rally Jackson, and put its own Tag Team Championship on the line the same night in a “Deadzone Match” — two men fighting inside a roofed barbed-wire cage while their partners fought on a scaffold built over it, a fall won by pinfall or submission inside the cage, or by throwing an opponent off the scaffold and through the roof below. Its reigning Tag Team Champions, Boltz and Jackson, were left to await challengers still to be named.

Whether the tournament or that title defense went ahead isn't in the surviving record; no further document survives past that 5 March 1999 announcement.
