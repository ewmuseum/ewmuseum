Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Violence Reigns Wrestling set out its own origin in a handbook dated 14 July 1999: a real-estate investor character, Cody Walters, a fan of Japanese garbage-style deathmatch wrestling, takes out a $15.5 million construction loan from the Union Planters Bank to build a wrestling venue on land near Winchester and Germantown Road, in Memphis, Tennessee. The facility, under construction since November 1998, was called “the Junk Yard,” and Walters is billed throughout as VRW's promoter and founder.

VRW toured the Mid-South — Tennessee, Arkansas, Mississippi, Georgia, and Alabama, with Florida floated as a possibility — staging its cards in night clubs, bars, and once in a used car dealership's parking lot. Its fictional staff named a commissioner, Jimmy Lee Duncan, a head commentator, J.T. Riley, a heel commentator, Kenny Myers, and a ring announcer, Jason Irving, airing twice a week on a fictional network as a preview show, “Did You Hear the News?,” and a main card, “A Night at the Club.”

Four championships anchored the promotion at launch — the VRW Heavyweight, Violent, and Featherweight titles, with a Tag Team Championship held on hiatus — each to be decided by its own tournament culminating at VRW's first Super-Card, “Bar Room Brawl 99,” booked for the Hydrogen night club in Fort Lauderdale, Florida. A follow-up update of 26 July 1999 confirms the promotion's first card for 1 August 1999 at the Underground Circus in Little Rock, Arkansas, and lays out an August tour running through Memphis, Nashville, and Jackson before the Fort Lauderdale pay-per-view.

No results document from any of those shows survives, and no closure notice survives either. Whether VRW's own August tour, or its first Super-Card, was ever held isn't known.
