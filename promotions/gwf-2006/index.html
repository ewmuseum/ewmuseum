A forum-run fed that closed once, came home for a brief 2009 reunion tour, and staged its own true farewell four years later as "20113 — the Final Encounter." Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

GWF ran on an InvisionFree message board, founded in 2006 by its own site admin, SheltonSplash, its own membership doubling as its own roster the way this era's forum feds routinely worked: an account was a wrestler, a bio card was a sign-up, and a results thread was a show. Its own founder later put it plainly, in the closing show's own comment thread: GWF began life "made from the WWE Gamefaqs board" as "a WWE spin off" in name only — every title it ever crowned, from the GWF Championship on down, is its own invention. Its own weekly programming, Friday Night Brawl and Sunday Night Sacrifice, ran alongside a small paper trail of community fixtures — a Poll of the Week, a Hall of Shame induction thread crediting Wolfenstein's Spirit Squad as GWF's own earliest stable, a Match of the Night board, and a Power 10 rankings column revived by one of its own members in April 2009. Sunday Night Sacrifice's own 27 April 2008 card survives whole, headlined by a ten-man War Games between Cataclysm's own allies and the Numbered Toast/Neo-Evolution alliance that beat them.

Its own record shows it closing at least once before the surviving corpus picks back up: a 2009 news column, "GWF Insider: Reunion Edition," opens flatly — "After the GWF Arena closed down and the fans, wrestlers" scattered — ahead of a brief reunion tour that March, an eight-man tournament crowning Star-Man the new United States Champion on Friday Night Brawl. Three superstar cards survive from the board's own life: Chris O'Malley, billed "GWF's Golden Standard" and a multi-time champion whose feud with Mark Madison the fed's own account calls a Feud of the Year; The Reprobate, an old-school heel out of New York whose own bio card dates an eight-month GWF Interbrand Championship reign; and The Soul of Philly, a March 2009 sign-up out of Philadelphia with no reign yet on the record.

The board's own true end came four years later: on 20 January 2013, SheltonSplash staged "20113" — billed in its own text as "the Final Encounter" and "the final day of GWF" — a full closing show with a complete roster call and match write-ups, thanking the community for "the support and dedication" it had given the fed "over the years." Every reigning title changed hands or ended a lineage that same night: Chris O'Malley pinned Robert Garland to become GWF's final Champion, Chris Williams outlasted a five-way to become its final Intercontinental Champion, and Michael Smart outlasted every other reigning titleholder in the building to become its only Champion of Champions.

SheltonSplash's unfinished revenge Written by AI, 2026

SheltonSplash's own promo "The Living Fairytale" (5 April 2009) recaps, in flashback, a Champion of Champions elimination in which he put out Damian DeNiro — then a chair shot to a freshly injured knee, delivered by Damian after the bell, that the promo frames as the wound Shelton never forgave. The same promo recovers two further defeats from the same stretch of GWF's own history: losing the GWF Championship to Don Cicinni, who needed three Omerta superkicks to keep him down, and losing the GWF Tag Team Championship, partnered with Kevin McAuliffe, to Damian and XO Malone's own Movement. None of the three carries a recoverable date — the promo itself is the only surviving record of all three finishes.

The grudge is still open four years later. The closing show, "20113" (20 January 2013), opens on Shelton begging Damian, absent, to show up for the match he says he needs to "finally exact revenge" — and Damian never does. Denied the one match he wanted, Shelton instead crowns himself Intercontinental Champion without a match, is overruled, and is eliminated first from the five-way that follows; later that same night he fights Ken Davidson to a double-knockout draw, both men falling off a ladder through some production crates. GWF closes for good hours later with Shelton's own revenge still unclaimed.

The United States Championship: Star-Man and The Reprobate Written by AI, 2026

Star-Man entered the 2009 reunion tour's own eight-man tournament as, by the broadcast's own account, the field's least experienced man in the building. On 27 March 2009 he beat a former World Champion, a former IX Champion and a former US Champion in one night, closing the tournament final against The Reprobate — whose own manager, Christina Hernandez, low-blowed the referee and handed Rep two extra weapon shots before Star-Man still finished him with the Super Punch to become United States Champion.

The rematch waited nearly four years. At 20113 (20 January 2013), the two fought again, this time under hardcore rules with barbed wire, steel chairs and brass knuckles in play — and Star-Man retained, never having lost the belt he'd won as an underdog in 2009, closing GWF's own history with the United States Championship exactly where it had been all along.
