Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Fantasy Wrestling Federation opened in October 1995 and built its identity around “The Franchise” Andru Edwards, champion by that December and, on his own rival Scorpio's later account, the man every challenger measured himself against for years after. By its December 1997 bulletin the roster had grown into a six-title promotion — World Heavyweight, Intercontinental, World Tag Team, Extreme, Cruiserweight, and a Unified Television Championship shared with a sister league — running its own annual Slammy Awards alongside the cards themselves.

Its two-year-anniversary pay-per-view, WinteRumble III, aired 28 December 1997 out of Madison Square Garden; only five parts of the results survive. Shane Diamond outlasted a crowded battle royal to win the vacant Cruiserweight Championship, and Mike Marconi upset “Devastating” Daniel Lewis for the Unified Television Championship even as “Sensational” Frankie Starz held on to the FWF's own separate Television belt the same night. Starz then beat Andru Edwards for the number-one contendership after Scorpio — back from suspension and recruiting his own following — distracted Edwards at the finish, and the Heartless Lovers reclaimed the World Tag Team Championship from the Perfectly Perfect Duo. The main event, a steel-cage World Heavyweight Championship match between Submarine and Hades, is not among the surviving parts.

“FWF King of the Squared Circle,” 5 August 1998, ran a full single-elimination tournament to a final between “Pretty Boy” Andrew and Westside Walavan. The same card opened with Destroyer dethroning Hades for the World Heavyweight Championship, then staged a High Noon Match unifying that title with the Television, Extreme, and a sister league's own World Championship: War Child, already the Extreme Champion, hung Destroyer from a noose atop the cage before knocking Alan Reed out cold to leave the ring wearing all four belts.

A new operator relaunched the promotion as “Fantasy Wrestling Federation '99” on 11 March 1999, saying the previous president had “started screwing people out of matches they earned and also started giving himself titles that he didn't deserve,” and rebuilt it around a Heavyweight World and United States title, a Hardcore division, and a Cruiserweight division, running Monday Night Madness and Thursday Night Throwdown cards toward a twelve-event pay-per-view calendar. The relaunch handbook barred copying a wrestler “from a real one” outright, yet its own 4 September 1999 Saturday Sacrifice card billed matches between well-known contemporary professional wrestlers under their own names — a departure from its stated policy the surviving record does not explain.

No document past that September 1999 card survives, and how or when the Fantasy Wrestling Federation's own run finally ended is not in the surviving record.
