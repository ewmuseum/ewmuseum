Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Fantasy Wrestling Association was a booked promotion running from early 1996, staging its flagship show, Sunday Night Showdown, out of arenas billed across North America — the Pacific Coliseum in Vancouver, the Matadome among them — under Commissioner Eagle and called by announcers Dickie Drake and “Fabulous” Felix Fantasia. Its card ran on five championships: the World Heavyweight, North American, United States, Cruiserweight, and World Tag Team titles.

Its own World Champion, the Maltese Falcon, blew the promotion apart in March 1996: he turned up unannounced on a rival Summit Wrestling Alliance federation's broadcast, called the FWA “nothing but a second rate organization where the unemployable can find a paycheck just by hitting someone with a chair,” threw the World Title belt to the ground, and hurled it into the crowd. Commissioner Eagle answered on air, declaring the Falcon still under contract and ordering him back to defend the title on Thunder regardless, and warning the rival promotion it had “picked on the wrong one” and would “fight back hard” — a warning the rival fed's own on-air voice returned in kind, promising the rest of its own roster would follow the Falcon out the door.

By January 1997 the World Title had passed to Steve Gunn, profiled that month in the fed's own newsletter, Hardcore Times, as part of a stable called the Too Hot Crew; the same issue ran an interview with US Champion Malice, who described himself as a loner who wrestled “because it is a chance to cause human suffering” and wanted no part of any stable, including a parody NWO-styled faction active in the promotion at the time. That January it also ran its own Invitational tag tournament, drawing entries from more than twenty other leagues, and hosted the wider AOL e-wrestling community's first annual awards show.

Gunn's own reign ended within weeks: its recapped pay-per-view main event, a three-fall Streetfight/Texas Death/I Quit match refereed by a guest Sandman, went to King 3933540$ Perfect. By its last surviving February 1997 holdings, “Ass Kicker” Adam Anderson held the North American title, “Lionheart” Adam Lash the Cruiserweight title, and the Masters of Destruction the Tag Team titles, with Malice still holding the US title after turning back a bloody barbed-wire defense that ended, unresolved, with a mystery attacker cutting his throat open on the top rope.

Its last dated appearance in this record comes secondhand: a July 1997 broadcast of Mid-West Championship Wrestling, its own operator's second promotion, features one of its own former World Champions turned color commentator and its own Cruiserweight Champion, Adam Lash, wrestling there under the same nickname he'd used at home.

No further document survives past that crossover. A rival fed would later report, in 1998, simply that the promotion “closed down a bit back”; how its own title picture was settled after February 1997, or how it actually ended, is not in the surviving record.
