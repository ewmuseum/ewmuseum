Southern Championship Wrestling — the CWC era

Southern Justice: the closing ceremony

4 March 1998
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A SPECIAL ANNOUNCEMENT from SCW Commissioner Kay and GRW Commissioner "Bulldog" Bob Lane closes out the "Southern Justice" pay-per-view. Kay tells the crowd: "the Continental Wrestling Council began with Southern Championship Wrestling. Since its inception in August of 1996, we have strived to give you the best in professional wrestling... Tonight, with the closing of Southern Championship Wrestling and the beginning of Mid-South Wrestling, we start yet, on another journey." Kay and Lane then induct six wrestlers who defined the promotion's run for a closing honor: The Funklord (in from the International Wrestling Association at SCW's founding, a Tennessee Title holder), "Shogun" Taiki (arrived 1997, also a Tennessee and Southern States Heavyweight Titleholder, a member of the Hot House), Blackline (a former Southern States Heavyweight Champion from Phoenix, Arizona), Godzilla Hadake (a former SCW Television Champion, a founding member of a stable called "the Graveyard of Hopes"), "The Lone Wolf" Brody Thunder (a two-time Southern States Heavyweight Champion), and The Hangman (co-founder of the Graveyard of Hopes, a former Television and Southern States Champion). The ceremony is interrupted when the Hot House (Tully Mantel, Dutch McNeely, Karl Flood and "The Bad Dog" Scott Haggarty, with manager Lady Red) storms out; Haggarty takes the mic to pay tribute to the honorees on the Hot House's own behalf rather than to protest, closing on: "Maybe one day... you'll find yer way back... to Southern Championship Wrestling."
