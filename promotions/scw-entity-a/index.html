A Southeastern regional promotion running a subsidiary Florida branch show under its own masthead, holding ten title lineages — National, Southern States, Tennessee, Television and the Florida branch's own belts — across 23 recorded reigns, five shows and 58 wrestlers from its 1996–1998 run. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Southern Championship Wrestling dates its own founding to around August 1996, by its president's own account — “we have been around for 14 months now”, written that October 1997 while asking the newsgroup for a dedicated vice president to help brainstorm angles, send out character sheets and check daily flashes. It grew a subsidiary branch show, Coastal Wrestling from Florida, whose first surviving card (9 August 1997) filled a vacant SCW Florida Heavyweight Title between newcomers Billy Joe Pherris and Adrian “Tiger” Lee and saw Matt Moyer and Rex Stone retain the SCW Florida Tag-Team Titles over the Warlords.

By early 1998 the promotion had folded into a larger structure, the Continental Wrestling Council, running Southern Championship Wrestling alongside a sister region, Georgia Regional Wrestling, under commissioners Kay and a Georgia counterpart nicknamed “Bulldog.” That arrangement closed on its own terms on 4 March 1998, at a pay-per-view called Southern Justice: Commissioner Kay told the crowd “the Continental Wrestling Council began with Southern Championship Wrestling... Tonight, with the closing of Southern Championship Wrestling and the beginning of Mid-South Wrestling, we start yet, on another journey,” and inducted six wrestlers into a closing honor for defining the promotion's run: The Funklord, “Shogun” Taiki, Blackline, Godzilla Hadake, “The Lone Wolf” Brody Thunder and The Hangman.

The Southern Championship Wrestling name itself returned that August, relaunched across three regions — Carolina, Georgia and Florida — run, in its own recruiting copy, “like the old NWA of the '80s in that it is one promotion that is made up of separate feds all over the South.”

Its 1999 run carried on that structure with a genuine title picture: a Tennessee Title contested across “Roadkill” Len Griffin's feud with the Carolina Crusher, a Southern States Heavyweight lineage, and a National Championship that changed hands to Chris Spencer with the Spencer-Plex the week before its flagship pay-per-view, “One,” on 22 May 1999.

That pay-per-view closed on a masked-man reveal: Retribution, allied through the night with National Championship mainstay John Chapman, unmasked himself in the ring as “Lone Wolf” Brody Thunder — a wrestler the broadcast noted had been banned from the promotion — declaring himself back to clean house and offering Chapman his loyalty against a faction he called “a buncha no-talent thugs.” The same show crowned Mikal Khastanovich and Marc LeRoux as new Television Tag Champions, gave Dan Starr the Brass Knux Title, handed Thunderstorm the vacant Southern States Tag belts, and made Dick LeBarron the new Television Champion.

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. Its last surviving document is that same pay-per-view; no closure notice or later card survives.

Affiliations

The Continental Wrestling Council (CWC, 1997)

Operated by SCW (Aug 1996) (January-March 1998 (this promotion's own account); the alliance's own later record (May-June 1998) shows different administration) — Primary-source self-narration ties the alliance's own founding to this promotion; whether the same alliance continued unbroken under new management, or restarted under the same name, is not resolved by either promotion's own held record.
This promotion's own commissioner tells the crowd, closing a March 1998 pay-per-view, that the alliance began with this promotion at its own August 1996 founding; a January 1998 broadcast already names the alliance's own Georgia region as this promotion's 'sister region.' The alliance's later national newsletters (May-June 1998) are signed by a different regional administrator and describe a wider seven-region structure than this promotion's own January-March 1998 account; no single document bridges the two periods, and central administration of the alliance appears to have passed elsewhere by that point.

Shared ownership

Georgia Regional Wrestling (GRW)
This federation's own served pages repeatedly describe themselves as run 'from the SCW's Georgia Region,' and a 31 May 1998 title defense carries the cross-tag '[CWC:GRW]' for a shared Southern States Championship; Southern Championship Wrestling's own broadcasts independently name a Georgia region as its own territory.
