Team NOP (Jobbers) ---------------------------------- 1. The Purpose of NOP ---------------------------------- The purpose around the start of this federation was to stray away from the 'American' or Western style federations that keep popping up in the masses. These types of feds also like to base their entire plot around a soap opera/novel type epic drama that engages their audience.
Well in Japan, it's much different. The crowd doesn't like run-ins, doesn't like these cheesy angles that don't interest them. They care about the action inside the ring. The wrestling. And this is where NOP comes into play.
NOP is going to be run like a real lucharesu/puroresu wrestling organization that might be found in Japan, for instance, Toryumon or
Michinoku-Pro. That's what this is aiming to be like. Small crowds but great wrestling. The wrestling here in NOP will be action packed to say the least, and it's hopeful it can display itself as close to a real wrestling company might stage itself over in Japan. Enjoy.

Japan. Only a year ago, a piledriver ended his career, and he has now since taken on the responsibility of being head booker as well as
NOP's official trainer. A very serious man, but he knows his stuff. In real life, these people don't exist, as you should know, so the real life consists of two guys.

Codes and Regulations -------------------------------------- 1) NOP is a lucharesu federation, meaning it isn't heavy on 'sports entertainment' angles, matches will have stories, psychology and the like. You won't see flaming tables matches in NOP, nor will you see many singles matches, only singles matches will happen when the feud deserves it or between two top guys.
2) NOP will usually have a trios match and a series of tag matches on one show, with singles matches spread around. Tours will be the main focus, with repeated matches on some house shows and regular shows,
'big' cards, aka, Supercards will only happen once to twice a year, and will only feature main attraction, heavily anticipated matches.
3) No bitching to us, please, if you lose, don't bitch. This is e-wrestling, it's supposed to be fun, develop your character through his in-ring work, if you want him to be a heel, be a heel in the ring, if you want him to be a face, be a face in the ring.
4) Roleplaying should be done in a simple manner, in Japan, you don't see guys cutting 20 minute promos about being screwed over or pie, or anything of that sort, get to the point and talk about your match, how your character feels and how he will react, nothing more, nothing less. We don't want 20k dribbles in NOP.
5) Be friendly to your other handlers, have respect and you will have respect shown to you. You never know who your character may get involved in and with the multiple tag matches on cards, your character will probably face or be alongside every other character in the federation in a long tenure, so, no one is better than anyone here.
We're all here to have fun, have a good atmosphere and get along. 6) Last but not least, NOP will enforce a rule that only 4-5 Gaijins, meaning foreign workers, be allowed on one tour at a time. After that certain tour, 2 more Gaijin could be picked up, and the other 2 dropped, or the same two could continue on, or any other combination you might come up with.
---------------- 4. Touring ---------------- This is the bulk of how NOP will work and run. Tours are the biggest part of the federation and this is where the matches and shows take place. A Tour will consist of a set booked amount of shows within a certain time frame, for around 3-4 weeks. There will be a few house shows, a few TV Tapings, and at the end, the bigger, Tour Ender. These make up a tour. For example, in October, there might be two or three shows in the first week, two or three in the next, two in the next, and another plus then end of the Tour in the next week. These will be
pre-booked with dates already set. --------------------- 5. NOP Titles --------------------- The NOP possesses three titles. All three are very well respected, but I'll list them as how they are thought of inside the NOP. NOP Pacific Heavyweight Championship NOP Junior Heavyweight Championship NOP Trios Championships There they are. The best, and most respected characters will receive these titles. They represent the best that NOP has to offer at each level. The belts are usually defended on Tour Enders, but occasionally, depending upon if a challenge has been issued, or an angle is in place, they may be defended on any given show.
----------------------------------- 6. Schedule and Shows ----------------------------------- NOP will consist of House Shows, TV Tapings which are called and a Supercard and/or Tour Ender show. House Shows are usually 4-5 summarized matches, consisting of trios and tag matches that pit the singles characters together against other singles competitors teams together, as is the case with most Puro bookings.
TV Tapings (NOP Puroresu Weekly): These are the same except a few singles matches that usually have to do with a feud between the two, will air here, along with the trios and tag matches.
Tour Enders/Supercards will contain more singles matches and usually will end a feud between two singles competitors or trios groups. This is the big show where everything falls together and where the crowd is a big draw.
----------------------------------------- 7. Roleplaying and Stratting ----------------------------------------- As stated before in the rules, we don't expect monster roleplays. We expect writing that advances your character and pleases you the writer. Please don't write monster sequences at a pizza parlor or something that wouldn't look right. Serious stuff is good, and comedy can be used for effect too.
Roleplays will be due 3 days before a scheduled TV Taping. Strats, which should list spots you'd like to use and an overall wrestler feeling toward the match, along with anything else you can think of, should be sent in BEFORE the first House Show. I can't stress this enough. It will effect how you do on the House Shows, as well as the TV Taping. They don't need to be huge, but they are something that can help you greatly.
--------------------- 8. Characters ---------------------- NOP prides itself in having creative characters, so to hell with your boring or generic creations! We want the creative juices to flow and shine so show us what you got. Natives of Mexico, South America, and
Asia....Only 4-5 Americans will be on the roster at one time, both divisions. We'd also like to stress that an interesting story is what will make your character here in NOP. That's what this fed will be about, story telling.
Use move sets related to your characters wrestling style. Also, don't give us 30 moves that are finishers either. Start simple and work your way up to bigger moves. Make it realistic, psychology laced, based heavily on whatever style they choose. That way, there won't be any confusion as to what moves your wrestler can/can't pull off in their match.
------------------------ 9. Tournaments ------------------------ From time to time, NOP will hold tournaments for titles, title shots, trophies, jackets, whatever might symbolize a victory. They will be set up in a point system, just as you might see be fought in Japan.
They last for several weeks, and the contestants all fight amongst each other during that time. The top point getters square off for the prize. Most will be round robin singles Tournaments, but Tag elements will also be thrown in.

NOP Announcing Team ----------------------------------------- Yuji Yamada: Has been on the announcing scene for several years now, and has made his way over to the NOP to try and revive his career. Has a small fan following, and has been known to mark out for the faces in a loud fashion. He is in his 50's as well, and knows his wrestling by the book.
Kendo Ijin: Former wrestler and father of perennial NOP jobber, Zenkai Ijin. Kendo will voice his opinions sometimes, but mostly just roots for the heels. He is in his late 50s and is still in mildly good shape.
----------------------------------- 11. NOP Referee Team ----------------------------------- Matsunuga Murakami: Mr. Murakami has traveled the Japanese Wrestling scene for as long as anyone can remember. A respected referee, as is known to be fair, and to call the match right down the middle. He is in his mid-40's, yet still moves like he is a youngster.
Keijo Moyahshi: Mr. Moyahshi is an older Japanese man, former wrestler in the 60s and 70s, strictly enforces the rules and counts at a slower pace than most.
Akira Maieda: Akira is the youngest of the three NOP referees in his late-20's and he is known for not seeing everything that happens inside the ring, yet calls a very fast paced match.
Tomas Salazar: One of two Hispanic referees in NOP; he is the face referee for the trios matches, only refereeing the trios matches, and nothing else. At 31, he's young and crisp with his counts, easily keeping up with the fast paced action.
Ernesto Vega: The other Hispanic referee, he is the heel in the NOP trio matches. He is known to possess an attitude in the ring and out, and his out of control ego sometimes can interfere in how he might call a match. At age 25, he is in his officiating prime.
