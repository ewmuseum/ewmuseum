A lucharesu-styled Japanese-tour promotion whose president announced three signings and staged three house shows across a single October 2001 tour before pausing and briefly reopening in December. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Nagoya Organization of Puroresu announced itself on 16 October 2001 as a lucharesu-styled promotion deliberately built against the American style — seeking wrestlers of Hispanic, European, and Asian heritage while limiting how many “gaijin” workers could appear on any one tour. Two days later it named its first three signings: “Brattitude” Michelle Bailey and “Sweet Dreams” Shane Destiny, both trained by “the Supreme Being” Jeremy Rhodes, and Angel Suicida; announced its first tour, the Crystal Gamma Tour, opening at the Monbetsu City Sports Center in Hokkaido at five American dollars a ticket; and reported a television deal with Nagoya Action TV to air its shows at midnight. A further post the same day added the tag team Tech-Force (Sho Shoji and “Rising Star” Fuyuki Hamada Jr.), Goshikku Mitsukai, and the heavyweight “Blinding Storm” Tetsumi Nanami to its roster.

Its first house show, staged 21 October 2001 in Monbetsu before roughly 125 fans with NOP's own president, billed as Kobe Koresawa, greeting the crowd, ran three tag and trios matches ahead of a main event that ended in a draw between the team of Bailey and Destiny and the pairing of Kourui Saionji and Murmur: The Fallen. A comedy trio playing on pop-culture names — Britney Spears' Boyfriend, Ricky Martin Mask, and Christina Aguilera's Boyfriend — won its debut over the trio New Mexicana Millenium.

A second house show two nights later at the Yakayama Prefectural Gym drew a bigger crowd, New Mexicana Millenium's Gordo Vaca turning back the comedy trio and Angel Suicida pinning Sho Shoji in a tag bout alongside Goshikku Mitsukai. A third show the following night saw the debuting duo of Ryunosuke Uraki and Ryoseika defeat Suicida and Mitsukai, and NOP closed the show by announcing its first television taping for 27 October 2001 at the Tokyo Differ Ariake.

No document survives of that taping, or of any show after it. On 2 December 2001 NOP's own owner posted that it had been “awhile” since anything NOP-related had gone out, invited its earlier roster back, and planned to reopen around the end of December or start of January — this time run through a Yahoo Group or plain e-mail rather than a website, under a front office that had grown to two co-presidents.

A further F.A.Q., posted 3 March 2002, describes NOP being brought back a third time, “revamped,” under a single president again, still built around the same staged front office — Kobe Koresawa as owner, Keiji Kurasaki as booker and trainer — and the same tour-based, three-title structure as its first F.A.Q. A newsletter dated 13 March 2002, Puroresu Press Edition #1, ran promotional vignettes reintroducing “Blinding Storm” Tetsumi Nanami as a returning name and welcoming new signings billed as Genji Gempachi and HERO Ishikawa.

That newsletter is the last document NOP's own record carries. Whether its March 2002 relaunch ever staged a fourth show, whether either of the reopenings it announced for itself in December and January came and went without a card, and how or when the Nagoya Organization of Puroresu finally ended aren't in the surviving record.
