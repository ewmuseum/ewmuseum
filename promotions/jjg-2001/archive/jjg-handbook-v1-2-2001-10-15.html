X: Joining. ============================================== II: Introduction. ============================================== A: In character. In January of the year 2000, Ron Strickland, media mogul and all around good guy was on top of the world. His business ventures were doing well, and, best of all, he was in control of one of the top Wrestling organizations in the world. Atlantic Coast Wrestling Alliance. ACWA was already playing large venues, and touring extensively, and within the year would become an intercontinentally-exposed organization, newly renamed the
"American Continental Wrestling Association". Unfortunately, that never came to be... Paul Jackson, expert Face Commentator and disgruntled employee had a legitimate beef with the ACWA. Filing a lawsuit that almost crippled
Strickland's finances, Jackson had every intention of bankrupting Strickland. The ACWA, as part of the settlement, was destroyed as an intellectual property.
The lawsuit took a year, and halted the ACWA's production. Ron lost millions paying off contracts to loyal workers who, having been celebrities, now had to slink off into obscurity. Some never finding the spotlight again. All of this occurred because the ACWA's Heavyweight Champion, Steve
Starr, struck Paul Jackson. Some even theorized that the pair was in cahoots, working to take Strickland for all he's worth. It can't be proven, but it would make sense, considering the sudden change in Jackson's onscreen persona, and the diabolic nature of Steve Starr both inside and outside of the ring.
Flash forward another 9 months. In the boardrooms with the suits, Strickland has gone the extra step to create a corporate entity, and an onscreen presence that will garner a viewership the ACWA never experienced.
Having recovered financially from his legal troubles, Strickland put forward a first-year budget in the low-mid 8 digits. A sizable portion of his fortune, and an investment that everybody told him was a very stupid mistake, but "It's my mistake to make, and I'll make it if I so please."
said the boss. And so he has. Through links to other wrestling organizations, usage of his own television networks, and an intelligent staff, Strickland may just have found his niche in the wrestling business. October of 2001 may just be the birth of a new era. The Era of Jersey Jack Grappling.
B: Out of character. Simply put, JJG is the sequel to the ACWA, a regional professional wrestling league on it's way to Nation and Worldwide prominence. My own need to work from the other side, as a booker, has lead me to create the kind of league that I, as a handler, would like to belong to.
Unfortunately, I'm stuck as just a booker. Dammit. Regardless, the JJG is a nice, fun, politics-free environment with an emphasis on quick, punchy matches (no 100K monstrosities here) that people actually _read_ instead of skimming. Additionally, with up to 18 matches a week, most everyone should get booked in a match once a week. Finally, with a flash cap of 10K, everyone should be able to say everything they want to, but without trailing on in a fashion that keeps their material from getting read. Extra long flashes can be fun, but let's face it, how can you emphasize quality over quantity if there's no limit to the quantity? It'll have an effect, trust me. So 10K = plenty.
============================================== III: The roles played, and who plays them (staff). ============================================== A: Suits. 1: Ron Strickland. Billionaire. He owns a mansion and a yacht. Well, not quite a Billionaire, but Jersey Jack Wrestling's owner is a corporate
CEO, owns stock in Fortune 500 companies, and owns several cable networks. His shares in Digital Satellite Service and pay per view companies give him a prime position as a broadcaster, and allow him to push his own brand of wrestling onto the public consciousness. He is, indeed, quite the bigwig.
Out of respect for his programming, and his fans' intelligence, Strickland rarely, if ever, appears on his own shows. Either that, or he's too busy in his money bin, counting the cash coin by precious coin.
Ron is a well-built man in his early fifties. He wears expensive suits, looks rich, is rich, doesn't have much hair, and what he does have is white.
2: "The Matchmaker" Robert Kyle. Formerly "Brickyard" Bob Kyle, Robert Kyle has been repackaged in a new, hip, cool way. As his new moniker implies, Kyle is the JJG's head booker. He decides who faces who, who does what, etc. Packing a bad attitude that's been handed to him by the owner himself, Robert is more than ready to lay down the law at any time. He has two primary concerns in JJG, and they're both entertaining the fans. He does all he can to make the wrestlers hate one another. Conflict is entertaining, y'know.
Robert is a skinny fellow in his early thirties, with a mustache and chinbeard. A shillmaster purist with a flair for promotion, Bob wears nothing that doesn't have a JJG logo on it. That means his suits, ties, hats, shoes, everything has a logo on it. It's kinda creepy, really. The most noticable accessory he wears however are his TWENTY BODYGUARDS! Highly aware of how vulnerable it is being an onscreen personality in a wrestling league, Kyle hauls his crew of crack bodyguards (who all dress like Secret
Servicemen) out with him whenever he attends a show. They protect him with their lives. B: Announce crew. 1: Jack Britain. Britain (his real name) is a native of Detroit, Michigan, and has paid his dues all over the country for over thirty years. Working as a journeyman wrestler in as many as 50 promotions throughout his career. Now, a veteran trainer of novice wrestlers, Britain has been turning fans into professionals for ten years. He co-promoted the Hellfire
Bar and Grill's Ultimate Grappling Excitement in Ohio alongside Barney Johnson in the year 2000. Now, in JJG, he functions as the chief trainer in the back, holding daily classes in the JJG arena when the venue isn't in use. Additionally, Jack, the workhorse that he is, functions as JJG's face announcer, and what an announcer he is. Forceful, outspoken, strong, this fireball of a man knows virtually every move in the book, calls the action with confidence, and critiques the action on a regular basis.
In his mid-50's, Britain has maintained his impressive build in the fifteen years since his retirement from active competition. Weighing 250, with only the slightest hint of a paunch, he still _looks_ like he could take a man out. Dressing casually at ringside, you'd hardly know the man was at work.
2: Jenna McDougal. A crazy girl. A CRAZY CRAZY GIRL! Jenna's claim to fame in the ACWA was pinning a casket which supposedly contained the corpse of the undefeated Jerko the Clown. She was an ACWA referee at the time, and counted the pin herself. One could argue also that she's famous, in New
Jersey, at least, for dating reknown pervert Preston Mayfield in 1999... At age 25, she's still in the flush of her youth, and she's enjoying every second of it. In this case, unfortunately, that means she gets to be the heel announcer in JJG. Shrill, annoying, but an excellent speaker, her youthful exuberance (and insanity) plays the expert foil to Jack Britain's age and experience. Dressing scantily, and making no bones about which man she likes in a match and which one she doesn't like, she's crazy. Did I mention she's crazy?
Jenna is a big-eyed, leggy strawberry blonde of about 5'6" and 120 pounds, complete with large breast implants to make her look, more or less, like a cartoon character. At ringside, she dresses either as a club chick (short skirts, low-cut blouse) or a beauty queen (long dress, lots of jewelry).
And high heels. She just _has_ to wear the high heels. 3: Richard Jameson. The host of "Aftershocks", and a workrate freak. Rich holds no loyalties to either the faces or the heels, but rather pulls for any man who can work the ring better than his opponent. 30 years old, and with a lifelong obsession with wrestling, his intensity onscreen is rather impressive. He also doubles as JJG's highly impartial backstage interviewer. He stands, unshakable, unintimidatable, in the face of many a wrestler, and asks the tough questions. Never a bad interview with this man.
Jameson has slightly graying hair, blue eyes, and is basically a pretty normal guy. His odd ability to hype the lowcarders, and men who can't get onto Big Bang (or rather, haven't yet) is suprising, and he does his job with surprising energy.
C: Referees. 1: Tariq Abdul Aziz. A native Iranian and quite possibly the most aggressive referee in the history of wrestling. Known to get in the face of most any wrestler for even minor infractions, between two wrestlers who are at one another's throats, and even attempt an attack on a wrestler that gets physical with him. This, of course, leads to another beating. That's okay, though, Tariq's just the type of ref the JJG needs. A crazy one.
At only 5'9" and 160 pounds, this guy really needs to calm down before he gets hurt. His constant scowl and confrontational posture almost screams
"hit me" to the heel wrestlers. He enforces the rules to the letter, and refuses to let even the smallest infraction pass without screaming at the infractor. He is the strictest referee in the bunch.
2: Freddie Jacobs. Formerly a long-haired, teenage referee who made frequent mistakes, Freddie's learned from his mistakes, and worked enough indy shows in the last 3 years to become a very competent official.
Additionally, he's bulked up to become something of a powerhouse. If he could just learn how to fight, he might make a good wrestler.
The most imposing physical presence in the ring among the referees, Freddie's 5'10", and a very solid 200 pounds. His hair's brown, short, and spiked up with entirely too much gel. Sideburns finish out the look, and he looks every inch the hipster. The ladies love this 21 year old heartthrob, even though he does look like a member of N'Sync. Freddie misses very little in the way of cheating, admonishes, but is slow to disqualify, preferring to let the action rage on and entertain the crowd. He's pretty well balanced as a referee.
3: Minnie McDougal. YIKES~! JENNA HAS A SISTER! That's right, Jenna McDougal's little sister, the redheaded wildchild, Minnie, has gotten into the wrestling business via her Sister's place in the company. The only female referee on the staff, Minnie's taken Jenna's place as the inring,
striped-shirt eye candy of Atlantic City. Minnie's 5'8" and only 110 pounds. That is to say, she's really thin. All freckles and red curls, she's ultra-cute, has a supermodel's build (as opposed to Jenna's pornstar build), and enforces the rules only insomuch as she understands them. Note: She doesn't really understand them all that well.
4: James Randal Brannon. From Blackpool, England, the JJG's senior official is a 50-year-old, 30-year-veteran of the ring. Always a referee, he knows every rule, he knows how to move around the ring to evade the wrestlers, he knows when to call it down the middle, and he knows when to let it go. He is, for all intents and purposes, the perfect referee. He's personally seen to it that Freddie Jacobs has recieved proper training.
That's why the former teenage moron has metamorphosed into a competent official. Only 5'5" and 140 pounds, Brannon's by no means a physical presence in the ring. However, he is very thin, and surprisingly quick for his age, and so can avoid harm from wrestlers that way. James calls it like he sees it, and is nearly flawless in his judgement. He didn't just read every rule in the book, he probably wrote a few of them.
D: Miscellaneous personnel. 1: JERSEY JACK! Wearing a New Jersey Jersey Jack Hockey Jersey (that's a lot of Jersey) over a rubberized suit, the guy in this getup is really, really overheated. The suit itself is designed to make it's wearer look huge and muscular, in a cartoony way. Jack, the JJG's beloved mascot, is just some random dude in a suit, with a massive, oversized cartoon head that looks like it's meant to be menacing, but comes off as comical. JJ is pure comedy, and shows that the JJG can make fun of itself. Mostly he's used to work the crowd when there's no action in the ring. He hangs out at the concession stands and jokes with fans. So on and so forth.
Again, the guy in the suit is random. It could be a wrestler, it could be a referee, and it could be that annoying guy at the show that thinks he's important because he gets to work security. Who knows!? Most likely, it's a different guy each week. The voice, however, is always the same.
Incoherent, and piped in over the PA system, Jersey Jack's voice is heard to often say such wonderful things as "Gimmahjobbo, Mamaho.
ChickieseggahumpaWHOO~!" So, yeah, he's pretty insane. 2: Bobby Black: This man is a voice over veteran, having spent a whole career doing announcements for films like "Teenage Sexpot Slashers 1-19", and "Return of The Killer Bees", Black will do just about anything so long as it keeps him working. When the indy women's jello wrestling scene suddenly died out, Black found himself applying at JJG.
Only 45 years old, Black looks 70. No joke. Being a raging drunk does that to you. His announcements of the wrestlers are mostly ad-libbed, and make for decent hype, but it's so overblown that they tend to come off as
B-Movie material, which makes sense, since Bobby's spent most of his career doing voices for horrible B-Horror-movies.
============================================== IV: Titles. Where would we be without them? ============================================== A: Jersey Jack Big Badass Strap. Primary title. Made entirely of hinged steel, no leather. Weighs about 50 pounds (no exaggeration). Looks a lot like a quality wristwatch.
B: Jersey Jack Big Beermug of Doom!: Secondary title. A large, steel beer mug, mounted on a mohogany base, with a brass plaque that bears the current titleholder's name. Periodically, tournaments for this title will be fought, allowing whoever is the current champion at the time to reassert his dominance, at the expense of his championship advantage. Additionally, should he win, he becomes the X (X being however many times he's won it, duh) time champion~!
C: Jersey Jack Badass Tagstraps: Like the Big Badass Strap, only two of them, and they say "Tag Strap" instead of "Big Badass Strap".
============================================== V: Wrestling events, and how they're handled. ============================================== A. Weekly broadcasts. All 3 will have between 4 and 6 matches any given week. 1: Big Bang. The flagship program for JJG, broadcast on RTN, which is the equivalent of TNN in it's programming lineup. It's a "live" format program that tours New Jersey, Pennsylvania, and New York. Additionally, with JJG's budget, it can make short tours cross-country and into Canada.
Big Bang is 90 minutes, with fully written results, and matches that range in length from 5K-25K (for really big main events). Flashes are, for the most part, limited to backstagers, inrings, and commercials.
2: Aftershocks. The secondary program for JJG, broadcast on SSN, a sports network with many an offbeat program. "Highlight Reel" format program that takes place in the JJG arena in Atlantic City, New Jersey.
Aftershocks is 60 minutes, with cut-spots (ABA, voiceovers, some commentary, and cut to later in the match) matches that range in length from 2-10K.
Additionally, this is where most of the league's weekly flashes can be found. 3: House Shows. Not a television program, but rather, an untelevised weekly event that brings extra revenue in for JJG, and allows new workers, and those who are still training, to get ring time. The card itself should be 10-20K total, and all action is in summary format, written as if it were an "internet report" by a fan. No flashes, but "summarized promos" are allowed. If you don't know what that is, ask...
B. The actual events (live). Production values in JJG aren't as great as some. There is no bigscreen, there are no fireworks (except on supercards), and the venues are smaller in general. For Big Bang, your average crowd will be somewhere in the neighborhood of 1000-5000, depending on the venue. Aftershocks will have appx: 1000, since the JJG arena is just a small warehouse, converted into a wrestling school and arena. The house shows, not being on TV, and not being advertised heavily, will have considerably less, as the place won't exactly be packed.
C. Supercards. Supercards for JJG occur once every three months, the January supercard being a Pay Per View. All results of these cards are in "live" format, with matches ranging from 10-30K. Non-PPV supercards take the place of that week's Big Bang.
D. Pay Per View. Renting out the largest venue that their money can buy, the JJG will pack the house full, one night out of the year, and hold the biggest card you'd ever hope to see. PPV's _do_not_ take the place of Big Bang, meaning that one week in JJG _can_ have 4 cards. However ... the next week probably won't have any material, allowing for the bookers to take a yearly vacation.
:) E. Venues. 1: The JJG arena. Seating up to 1000 people, the JJG arena is Jersey Jack Grappling's base of operations. Robert Kyle runs all the business affairs from an office in the back, the wrestlers are trained here by Jack
Britain when the venue's not in use, and it's also the site for all the house shows, and JJG's secondary program, Aftershocks.
2: Touring venues. JJG's flagship program, Big Bang, tours small venues from Pennsylvania to upstate New York, pestering these locales on a regular basis. Additionally, the yearly Pay Per View events will take place in a large venue, somewhere in the Continental United States. Some shows in
Canada are possible, and the rare show will go further south than PA, but that's about it, for now.
3: The A.C. Convention Center. The largest venue ever visited by the ACWA is now the venue where most of the JJG's supercards will be held. Four supercards will be held each year, and out of those, one will be a Pay Per
View. The PPV will go to some large venue that is _not_ the ACCC. Ain't that great? F. Updates. An update will be sent with each card the JJG puts out, telling handlers when to have their material in, and what the lineup for the next cards will be.
============================================== VI: Your job once accepted into the JJG. ============================================== A: Flashing. With three cards a week, one might expect JJG to be workrate-heavy, but this couldn't be further from the truth. A 10K flash-cap is set to keep anybody from dominating the cards with his/her material. That's 10K split up among all your flashes. This can be bent, of course, but work with us. Don't just send in 25K worth of material and expect it to run. It won't.
"Big Bang", being mostly wrestling, will have no more than 1/3 promos, and these are limited to inrings, backstagers, and the like (as with most rules, this one can be bent. Ask in advance.) Also, ask in advance whether you can do an inring or backstager at all. Airtime on the flagship program is limited, y'know. Sometimes, you just gotta get in line.
"Aftershocks" is the primary vehicle for flashes. Anything that can go on in the world of wrestling, can go on here. That means production videos and the like, inrings, backstagers, and commercials.
What's more, a reference to "summarized promos" was made earlier on in the handbook. Summarized promos are just that, summarized. If you want your character to do an inring or whatever on a house show (non-broadcast wrestling event), just type up a paragraph or two giving the gist of what your wrestler says and does, and we'll run it. Now, unless your match is against a jobber, this won't keep you from losing, so feel free to write more material on the other programs.
B: Stratting: A set form is provided below that shows what the bookers (that's us) want out of a strat. It's preferable to us for you to use the form, and write a comprehensive, but not excessively long strategy. Again, if you can't do it in 10K or less, IMO, you can't do it.
Jersey. Jack. Grappling. ====================================================== Strategy for <Insert wrestler name here> ====================================================== Match of the night: How it should be wrestled: Entrance: Offense: Defense: Spots (fully written if possible): Finish - win: Finish - lose: Postmatch actions: Other activities for the night: ============================================== VII: Rules and regulations. ============================================== 1: Cursing. Being on basic cable, some things are okay to say in JJG. Hell, damn, ass, stuff like that, is fine. Anything you can't say on USA, TNN, etc, will get *BLEEP*ed. If you don't *BLEEP* it, we will. Force us to edit your material enough, you'll be depushed. Have a nice day! :)
2: OOC Bull*BLEEP*. Do not, and I repeat, do _not_ bring OOC issues into JJG. That means no shooting on handlers you don't like. No bickering and expecting bookers to settle your score. Make the game less fun for the rest of the roster, and you're gone. Period.
3: Kayfabe. Kayfabe is enforced like Murphy's Law in JJG. Basically, if what you say or do breaks character in any fashion that suggests real life, meaning real life of the handler (see rule 2) you will be depushed or worse.
Or, if you what you say or do makes an unsubtle reference to real life wrestling, you will be depushed or worse. Thank you very much.
