Now the newswire account of a fictional press conference. -- [name withheld]. E-Wrestling's most polarized non-entity. "* TheHolyEyebrow raises. * TheSacredSpleen vents. * EnlightenedNipple tweaks. * BlessedToeNail claws. [TheHolyEyebrow] Excellent. Now if only Mat would join us as Jesusbeard, we could be complete~!" --- Newswire: Atlantic City, New Jersey. September 21, 2001. Former owner of the Atlantic Coast Wrestling Alliance, Ron Strickland, held a press conference today, speaking out for the first time on the closing of the organization.
"It was a mess, to say the least" commented Strickland, speaking plainly, "A mess that had to be dealt with. As many of you in the know have already been told, Paul Jackson, the ACWA's most reliable commentator, had filed a lawsuit against the ACWA, the Strickland Sports Network, and myself."
Strickland went on to say that Jackson, who was asking for a sum in excess of $100,000,000 for emotional distress, medical bills, and punitive damages after being assaulted on ACWA television, had been paid off.
"...He didn't get what he wanted, which, basically, was my head on a platter. He wanted to shut down the SSN, RTN, take my shares in Pay Per
View companies, take my shares in Digital Satellite Services, and set them aflames. It almost happened, but we finally made him an offer he couldn't refuse."
Jackson was paid one tenth of what he was asking, which, according to
Strickland, "...is more than he deserves, quite frankly, considering that he was struck once, and only once, by the ACWA champion at the time, Steve
Starr."
What's more, Jackson sought ownership of the ACWA, as an intellectual property. Instead, for his role in forming the New Jersey promotion from the beginning, he was awarded higher than normal royalties for the sale of
ACWA "Best Of" videos and DVD's, as well as other vintage merchandise.
"He's set for life, and I do wish Paul the best. Just so long as I don't have to do business with him ever again."
For most of the media in attendance, everything that Strickland said was a surprise. Even moreso was what he went on to say after closing his speech on the ACWA.
"Now, onto more pressing matters. The ACWA, as it stands, is a ghost. A money making machine that has run it's course, and is getting a much-deserved, permanent rest. So, coming this fall, I am pleased to announce a new brand name on Sports Entertainment...
Jersey Jack Grappling." Going on to detail his plan, Strickland was making a break from the more traditional style of grappling that he embraced in his version of the
ACWA. Now, he was going for a wilder brand of violent entertainment, marked by an aggressive style that he felt would make a strong impact on the wrestling world. He emphasized steel over gold, style over substance (a very real break from his previous stance), and ended his speech, which was relatively short, with the following.
"You think you know wrestling? You don't know Jack!" Strickland then left the stage, amidst much amazement. Press releases were passed out, and members of JJG administration solicited interviews from the various magazines.
