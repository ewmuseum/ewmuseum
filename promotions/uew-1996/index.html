A president-run e-wrestling federation whose own June 1996 introduction to the newsgroup claimed seven months of prior, off-list history, attested here through two full card scripts and a self-reported win-loss records list rather than any founding document of its own. Thirty-seven shows are recorded through its own community poll tag continuing to 2001. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Ultimate E-Wrestling introduced itself to the newsgroup on 17 June 1996, already claiming seven months of prior operation — “booked… since DECEMBER,” running two cards a week. Its own recruitment described a strict roleplay league affiliated with the yet-to-launch Maximum Wrestling Circuit, itself affiliated with Dawg Pound Wrestling: “everyone knows the quality product they put out.”

Its earliest surviving broadcast, Ultimate Saturday/Ultimate Sunday (17 June 1996), opened on a highlight package built around its own champions — the UEW belt held by Octane, “Spartan” Troy Walters' American Title defense, the tag team Guts & Glory's title win — and called by announcers Rock Smith and Tommy Thompson over a card of debuts and squash matches.

The promotion ran supercards into the following years, including an annual Ides of March event and a Fourth of July Bash, but its own 15 March 1997 broadcast, “Rebirth of the UEW,” announced a reorganization after what it called “a rough period trying to find someone to take the reins… permanently,” installing a new executive board and promising “a new chapter to the UEW legacy.”

By Holiday Horror 3, staged from a sold-out Tokyo Dome around New Year's 1999, the federation was still running its Ultimate Championship as its top prize: “Top Dog” Rick Styles, a four-time champion and leader of the stable the New Alliance, defended against “Ladykiller” Chris Tyler, while the tag division turned on the champions, The Explosion, defending against the Perfectly Perfect Duo and the Partners in Crime.

The Ides of March '99 broadcast, its own latest surviving card, opened from Mexico City with “the Screaming Drillbit” Jason Keening taking up a $500,000 challenge from Freddie Scuttle to pin Gambit's shoulders for a clean three count.

By that December, replying to a fan asking to join, its own reply said only that the promotion was “undergoing reconstructive surgery” and pointed to a new web address. No closure statement of its own ever appears in the surviving record.
