NWN Rules (v. 1.0)

14 September 1996
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
---------------------------------- NorthEastern Wrestling Network Rules (v. 1.0) By: Duane Davis ---------------------------------- Thanks to [name withheld] for letting me use some of the rules from his fed VWA. Welcome to the NorthEastern Wrestling Network!!! Hey there! Welcome to an e-fed that I think will make it's name in the annodes of the e-wrestling world! I hope to keep this fed small until it's more established. RULES ON CREATING WRESTLERS: (READ CAREFULLY) You have two options for number of wrestlers you can own. You can only have a single wrestler or a tag team, not both. If you want to create a tag team, you can only create two wrestlers but they will only wrestle as a tag team and not single except if the situation calls for it. If you decide to create a single wrestler, he may only wrestler as a single wrestler and nothing else unless a situation calls for it. ================================================================ CHARACTER CREATION: Ok, this is where the creative juices start flowing! This is the basic stuff you have to send me when you create your wrestler: NAME: HEIGHT: WEIGHT: ALIGNMENT: HOMETOWN: ATTIRE: ENTRANCE MUSIC: MANAGER: (if there is one) TACTICS: STYLE: FINISHER (and description if necessary): ADVANCED WRESTLER CONFIGURATION: GIMMICK/BACKGROUND: I'm hoping that you can get by without me explaining name/height/etc., so I'll talk about the things that need more explaining. -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- ALIGNMENT: You have three choices here: face, heel, or neutral. For those of you that don't understand these concepts: FACE -- Short for Babyface (good guy). Examples are Bret Hart or Sting. HEEL -- The bad guy/cheating wrestlers. Examples are Ric Flair or Brian Pillman. ---------------------------- MANAGER: This is the person that your wrestler may depend on for either moral support, or for smacking the opponent upside the head with a chair if it helps you to win. When you send me your wrestler's description, send me a brief description of the manager..keeping two things in mind: 1. Does he interfere? 2. Does he have a foreign object, and if so, does he use it? I will allow you to use a real manager, *if* you can prove to me that you know the guy well enough for a convincing interview. For example, if you want Paul E. Dangerously to manage your guy..you better show that you could "talk" like him (to a point..I'm not expecting mind-readers here)! ------------------------- STYLE: Pick the type of wrestling style your wrestler has: Amateur Wrestler Top notch Brawler Stiff Martial Artist/Grappler Basic High Flyer Basic Brawler Luchador Submission Wrestler High Flyer/Good Technician U.S. Standard Brawler Technician/Brawling Daredevil Flyer -------------------------- FINISHER: Ahh..the thing that all us e-wrestlers want to be famous for: the trademark finishing maneuver. This is the thing that will almost guarantee a victory once executed. All you have to give me is the name of the finisher and a brief description of what the maneuver is like, or closely resembles (Examples: the Perfect-plex is considered a fisherman suplex, and the Sharpshooter is a scorpion deathlock). When you create your finisher, also include the move that your wrestler performs just before he goes for the patented hold. An example would be Lex Luger nailing a powerslam before putting his opponent in the Torture Rack. If your wrestler has a move that could be considered a "secondary" finisher, include that as well. -------------------------- ADVANCED WRESTLER CONFIGURATION: This is one of the neater things in TNM 6.2...the ability to put some personality into your wrestler for the simulator. Here are the things you can include if you want to: * Choose one of the following: - Does he do a move after a cross-corner whip? If you answer yes, include the move that your wrestler performs (example: Stinger splash) - Will he do a move coming out of the corner? This is something that the wrestler would do after he got sent into the corner, but doesn't sell it. (example: a leaping clothesline) - Will he perform a counter-move in the corner? This is what your wrestler would do after he stunned his charging opponent with, say, a kneelift. (example: bulldog) * Will he sit up a la the Undertaker? * Is he cocky? * Will he give the sign for his finisher? * Will he bleed/juice? * Will he work the crowd? If the answer is yes, then include a short sentence that explains what he does to get the crowd going. Example: "Rocko Rock waves his hands in the air." * Does your wrestler have a move that is exclusively his? This is a something that your wrestler is notorious for... a move or action that is not used a lot by other wrestlers. Examples include Arn Anderson's armbreaker, or the Great Muta's mist in the face. - Might this move lead to a DQ? - Will it be a setup for his finisher? * Will he do his finisher outside the ring? * Is he hardcore? * Will he do moves through tables? * Will he do his finisher (if possible) through a table? * Will he beg? * Will he walk out of a match? * Will he no-sell moves now and again? -------------------------- GIMMICK/BACKGROUND: Time to really start being creative here! This is how your wrestler will act in the ring or at interviews. What you have to send me is a brief description of how your wrestler acts: Is he arrogant? Is he patriotic to his homeland? Is he a ruthless SOB? You get the point. Tips on how to do a good gimmick: (Here's some more stuff from Teeters) * READ THIS ONE!!! * 1.) Before entering a league, it's a good idea to ask for a few cards to see what else is out there. It gives you a feel of what type of people are out there and will prevent two people from running characters with the exact same names or gimmicks. Do we really need 5 Undertaker clones running around? 2.) Before you pick a gimmick, here's a few things to consider : - Can I effectively run this character? Is this the kind of person I would be interested in writing for. For example, I once came up with a Russian Olympian type character. I had moves and an outfit and everything... then I had to write interviews and came to the realization... I couldn't come up with a believable stuff for this guy to say because I didn't know Russian and couldn't fake an accent. - Is this character believable? I personally like characters that are realistic. A guy who magically disappears or other such crap can really ruin the game. A human element helps certain characters. People have a tendency to go overboard when pushing a character (usually applies to heels) and then you get McMahon syndrome. Like a guy who was supposedly a drug dealer like character or a nuclear mutant suddenly becomes a face. Yeah, he was eating children yesterday, but now he's a fine upstanding individual! - Be careful with comedy gimmicks. They can be good and bad. Some guys can write comedy, others can't. Also, what you might think is funny, others might consider really lame. And in case you're wondering, I hated Doink (as a heel and face.) As a wise man once said "Dying is easy, comedy is hard." - Keep in mind, not all faces are friends and the same holds true for heels. Some of the biggest (and hottest) feuds in e-wrestling have been between guys of the same alignment. -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- So, this is what a sample sheet would look like: NAME: Derek Holmes HEIGHT: 5'11" WEIGHT: 237 lbs. ALIGNMENT: Face STYLE: Technician HOMETOWN: Albany, NY ATTIRE: Black trunk-tights (like Mr. Perfect) with red along the leg-trim. ENTRANCE MUSIC: "Don't Tread on Me" -- Metallica MANAGER: Francine, Paul E. Dangerously STYLE: Technical/Brawling BACKGROUND/GIMMICK: Derek is as skilled with his mouth as he is with his wrestling. He has a biting sarcasm, which is apparent in his interviews and flashes. He is confident in his abilities, but does not let that get the best of him. He is a very skilled technician..among the best in the league. He will study his opponents and will try to find the counters to moves, and any general flaws in the opponent's game. He is very patriotic to his country (he come down the aisle in a denim jacket with the American flag on the back). He is also outgoing with the fans and will spent time signing autographs and chatting with folks after the events. ================================================================ TAG TEAMS: Well, the creation of the tag team members is simple enough..just follow the same sheet for a singles wrestler. Add on the sheet that he is part of a tag team, and mention his partner's name. Each of your members will get one finisher, and you will also have a team finisher. Example: Road Warrior Hawk's finisher is the top-rope clothesline, and Road Warrior Animal's is a powerslam, but the Road Warriors' combined finisher is the Doomsday Device. Get the idea? I'm still deciding this..but sometime in the future, I *may* allow creating tag teams that include a real and a character-created wrestler. If I allow this, then the rules for using a real partner are the same as having a real manager: You better know the guy well enough to do a convincing interview with him. If the tag team is temporary (say a one-shot on a show or PPV), then the rules do not apply, but I'll allow this *only* if it is a result of something happening on a prior show. Example: Bret Hart was wrestling Yokozuna..when Owen Hart ran in and attacked his brother. Yoko and Owen proceeded to pummel Bret until Derek Holmes came in and made the save. This prompted a tag match for next week between the four guys. ================================================================ INTERVIEWS: - This is the fun part of the whole thing, in my opinion..having your wrestler speak and act! This is where you show how nasty an SOB you are, or what you plan on doing to your opposition. Here are some tips on how to write a good interview (from Teeters): - Try to keep them coherent. Spell check them if possible. - Say what you want to say, but you don't have to write an epic. A lot of the best interviews I've seen have been relatively short (one to two screens long). Who wants to read through 80 pages of ranting? - Try to stick to your alliance. Faces in general shouldn't be egomaniacs and should generally try to be nice guys or at least likable. - Try to avoid "cheap heat". Cheap heat can be something that's in bad taste. Try to avoid things that are racist or in bad taste (that beating up women). There are more effective ways to get characters over. This is something which is partially interviews, partially GM responsibility. One of the things I tend to hate is when players give their characters mega pushes as a face or a heel. If everyone in the game wasn't so lazy :-), I would like to see a somewhat frequent poll of the players to see who they think is good at getting their character over or not. There are several levels of characters : Over Heel : Ric Flair, Vader. The marks hate them, but they've got a style to them that they get some cheers. Not Over Heel : The Honky Tonk Man. This character sucks. Everyone hates them. Open chants of "YOU SUCK" occur when character is around. Over Face : The guy is over. Popular as can be. Guys like Sting, Bret Hart, and Shawn Michaels. Not Over Face : pre-turn Bob Backlund. Yeah, yeah, yeah. Shut up. You've got the charisma of a rock. >From there, the GM can gauge how over the character is. That way you don't have character who's interviews really suck or doesn't act like a face getting huge crowd pops. There are three classifications of an interview: 1) The "Flash" This is usually a one-sided affair. This places the wrestlers in a setting of their choosing, either the NWN Soundstage, the gym, the back alleys of Chicago, the secret hideaway of a criminal organization, you name it. You basically comment on whatever match/feud/"angle" you're involved in at the time, threaten your opponent, etc...the more imaginative, the better. 2) The Interview A third party is usually involved in the interview process, usually a random announcer from the home federation. This is placed in a question and answer format, where the interviewer asks the characters specific questions to be answered. This is usually the easiest kind of interview to script. 3) The Biased Interview (or Piper's Pit wannabes) From time to time a character will run a Piper's Pit style of interview segment, where they can praise other characters of their alignment and make fun of characters of the opposite alignment. These are VERY good to generate heat with, and are often the site of pre-match jumps from other wrestlers. Usually the person handling the weekly interview segment will dictate who is to be interviewed here. ================================================================ GAMEPLANS: Well, it's time to rumble! But what are you going to do? Are you going to attack the guy before the match begins? Are you going destroy the guy if you lose? That's where the gameplan comes in. Since the program I have is a booked fed (in that i just set up the matches..the rest is up to the computer), all I need from you is what you plan on doing before/after the match, and maybe some things you might do during the match. Squashes: (from Teeters) A squash match is a way for you to introduce the other players to what your character can do in the ring. Anyone who takes the time to do this will receive one rankings point for your character. RULES: 1) Squash Matches must follow the format outlined below. 2) Squash minimum - 5 lines; Squash maximum - 15 lines 3) Only one squash for your character every three weeks. SAMPLE SQUASH MATCH: Derek Machismo vs. Harry Janowitz (jobber) Derek strode out to "Invincible" by Pat Benatar, and that was an accurate summation of his dismantling of the hapless Janowitz. A clinic of power moves, ranging from atomic drops to chokeslams, battered Janowitz from one end of the ring to the other. After about five minutes of what was little more than a workout to Derek, he finished him off with his trademark finisher, the Powerdriver, for an easy three and the duke. How to write a Gameplan: Gameplans should be as innovative, as interesting and as effective as the situation warrants, but it should be REALISTIC. Any gameplans that say your 215 pound jr.-heavy will press slam the 568 pound Mabel or any that involves being raised from the dead will be tossed into the trash, and I will decide the strategy *for* you! So what makes a good gameplan? 1) Be as direct with what you want done as possible. The clearer the instruction, the better chance a fedhead will execute it to your satisfaction 2) Be tricky and creative if the situation warrants it. As fedhead, I am pretty liberal, and will allow a lot of things. I do have the right to veto a gameplan if I feel it is unrealistic or will hurt the natural flow of the game. One other thing....by sending me a gameplan, your chances of winning the scheduled match will undoubtedly increase. Or....I can put it another way: if you *don't* send me a gameplan for your scheduled match, you will lose, plain and simple. Sorry, but that's the way it goes. You must send gameplans to myself, with a CC: to the other fed-heads (the addresses will be given to you later), in order to make sure that your gameplan was recieved by at least one of us...otherwise, it might get lost, and your character might be jobbed by accident. ================================================================ PUSHES: A "push", if you are unfamiliar with the term, is the increase in popularity (heel or face) of a wrestler. When a wrestler is being pushed, the number of matches he is in, *and* the number of wins for those matches will increase. In the...ahem....real world of professional wrestling, the head honchos like Vince or Uncle Eric, as well as bookers like Bill Watts or Kevin Sullivan decide who deserves a push and who doesn't...depending on the storyline or the popularity of the wrestler (although with the latter, they sure as hell tend to waste some good talent by not pushing them...but hey, that's my opinion :) Now, in the NWN...all rookies will start with a push rating of around 70 (out of 110). Now, here's where you come in. If you want your wrestler to get a better push and have a better chance at grabbing some gold, then here's all you have to do: ROLE PLAY!!! If you send me strats, flashes, interviews, stuff for the Newsletter, *whatever*....that will definitely look good in the eyes of the fed-heads. If you don't put any effort into your character, he will remain at that level, and remain nothing but mid-card material and superstar fodder, until you either put effort into the character, or he falls victim to the inactivity rule (see INACTIVITY RULE). ================================================================ TITLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES: There are three titles in the NorthEastern Wrestling Network: NWN World Title: This is the big one! The title that *everyone* will be gunning for! If you manage to win it, congratulations....you just became a marked man! NWN World Television Title: Similar to..well..you know what it is. NWN World Tag Titles: Just like the World Title, except for Tag Teams. And, here are the responsibilities for the titles: WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT/WORLD TAG TEAM TITLES: 1. Unless situations make it different, there will be a World title defense at *ALL* Pay Per Views. The champion or champions do not have to defend the title on weekly cards, unless the champion himself wants to. 2. The wrestler deemed the number one contender *one week* after the end of a previous PPV will get an automatic title shot at the next Pay Per View. If a wrestler succeeds the previous #1 contender, he will have to wait until the next pay per view. 3. *If* the champion declares that he is signing an open contract with *anyone*, then the points system does not apply. The challenger will come on a first come first serve basis. Limit: 5 challengers before the PPV. At the PPV though, only the #1 contender can go for the title. 4. If a champion decides to defend the title on a weekly card, he will be allowed to decide the challenger, regardless of his ranking. WORLD TELEVISION TITLE: 1. There will be a title defense at any *televised* events (which includes weekly cards and PPVs). 2. The challenger for the Television title at the next PPV will be the loser of the match between the #2 and #3 ranked contenders. 3. Title defenses on weekly cards will be once every two weeks, and any challengers to the title are on a first come-first served basis. 4. If the champion is determined to be the #1 contender for the World title, he may challenge the World Champion at the next PPV, but only if he chooses to do so. You *have* to defend the title(s) whenever it is scheduled for you to do so.. They will be defended on the NWN shows and Pay-per-views. WEEKLY SHOW(Dont have a name for it yet; will most likely take place on Saturday): This is the NWN's equivalent to Monday Night Raw/Monday Nitro on those *other* feds. There will be Piper's Pit shows, PPV update reports, and any other interesting things I happen to think of. The NWN TV title *must* defended on this show, and/or the World Title(s) may be defended on the show at the champion's discresion or if situations require it. SPECIAL CARDS: This is like WCW's Clash of the Champions. Basically like Saturday's, except just name/name matches. The TV title may be defended at the champ's discresion or if situations allow it. The World Title(s) *must* be defended at least once per two clashes. PAY-PER-VIEWS This is where the big feuds started on Weekly Cards or the Special Cards will end. Most specialty matches will be allowed on these. All titles *must* be defended (except the TV..unless at champ's discresion or if situations allow it) on *all* PPVs! ================================================================ RANKINGS: I will try to post the rankings of the top five wrestlers for each division. It will consist of the win-loss-tie record for each guy, and a point system for help in rankings. The point system goes like this (relative to your current ranking): +20 if you win the NWN World Title +20 if you win the NWN Tag Titles +10 if you win the NWN World Television Title -10 if you lose the NWN World Title(s) - 5 if you lose the NWN Tag Titles - 3 if you lose the NWN World Television Title I determine World Title Contenders. Challenges for the TV Title is on a first come, first serve basis. ================================================================ INACTIVITY RULE: Now this has been a problem I've noticed in many feds the past few months.I see all these great wrestlers....and only a few put any effort into their character (as in sending in strategies, flashes, etc.) In my eyes, it shows that you don't really want to participate or you dont care. Here's what's gonna happen if I don't hear from you: WEEK 1: You get on the Week 1 Hot Seat List in the weekly newsletter. WEEK 2: Week 2 Hot Seat WEEK 3: Your wrestler will be given notice. WEEK 4: You're out of the fed. Now I feel that this is pretty fair, not only for myself and the other fedheads, but for the other participants in the NWN. It's no fun if the fed is made up of characters with no...well...*character* in them. Plus by removing the dead baggage, I can bring in new talent for everyone to fight. Now I will accept some reasonable excuses for not participating in the NWN. As I can attest, schoolwork (for those of you in college/high school) takes up a lot of one's time, and it does take precedence over an e-wrestling fed. I've also been told by one person that he can't participate for a while cuz his computer fried on him. These are reasonable excuses in my mind..and I'm sure that there are plenty of others. Just let me *know* what the hell is going on!! If you do, I will put your wrestler on reserve or something, meaning that he will still be a part of the NWN, but won't do any wrestling until you get back. One last thing. *PLEASE*...if you're not going to participate in the NWN....LET ME KNOW!! It saves *me* and the other fed-heads a lot of trouble sifting through all the dead weight to find the people that *do* want to participate in the fed. ================================================================ I cannot stress enough that this fed is only as good as you want it to be. We will have some great role-players in this fed, and they will bust their asses to help make this one of the best feds out there. You can help this, should you choose to join the fed. If you want to join the NWN...you have to be willing to role play. This is not the type of fed where we just take the characters, run the sims, and just post the results. That, IMO, is not as much fun as reading the wrestlers rant about their opponents, erading the announcers as they provide blow-by-blow and color commentary, and so on. So, if you aren't much of a role-player, then this isn't the fed for you. However, if you are the kick-ass and take no name guy that puts as much into his wrestler as he can, then this is the place for you! IMPORTANT: Once again, I would just like to remind you to ROLEPLAY! If you don't plan on doing so, do not fill out the creation sheet and send it to me! I don't want any deadbeats in this league. This league is for serious gamers as well as for beginners. I want to make this league fun and exciting and you guys are the ones to make it happen. Thanks a lot, and I hope to see you in the squared circle! NWN Head Booker/President Duane Davis [detail withheld]
