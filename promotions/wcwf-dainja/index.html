Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

WCWF opened on the newsgroup on 27 July 1996 and, within a year, its own president, going by the handle Dainja, described its method plainly: it “takes the elements of simming and tosses them in a blender with elements from booking.” It ran seven championships, including one named for Dainja himself, the X-treme Dainja Title, and toured a national arena circuit — Jack Murphy Stadium in San Diego, the CoreStates Center in Philadelphia, the Denver Coliseum among them — with pay-per-views sold by e-mail subscription.

Its roster mixed real-wrestler portrayals — the Undertaker, the Giant, Sabu, Vader, Bret Hart, Chris Jericho, Taz — with original characters like Sgt. Andrew Scott, Colonel Savard, Shaolin Dynamite and the Crow, run alongside a numbered internal newsletter, the Flash Flood, that reached past its twenty-fourth issue inside its first year.

At Warzone III, live from Jack Murphy Stadium, World Heavyweight Champion Sgt. Andrew Scott retained the title against Colonel Savard in a U.S. Marine Death Match, the arena exits ringed with barbed fence and, in the broadcast's own words, “anything goes.” Scott held the belt through the rest of the record; by September 1997 the Giant had it instead, though how the title changed hands between them isn't in the surviving record.

At Kaged II that August, the Undertaker regained the Intercontinental Championship from the Crow in a Final Vengeance casket match, Sabu lost the Cruiserweight Championship to Shaolin Dynamite, and the Blues Brothers retained the American Tag Team Championship in a Four-Way Dance over the Hart-Breakers, the Mega-Powers and the Extreme Team.

Its last surviving broadcast, a Sunday Night Superstars recap taped 14 September 1997 in Denver, still billed itself as “the number one rated syndicated program in the world.” No closure notice survives; the fed was still listed as active in a community federation directory that same month.
