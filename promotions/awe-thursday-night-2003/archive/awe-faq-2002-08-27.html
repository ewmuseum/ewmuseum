The Promotion is primarily run from the AWE Arena, which acts as both venue and head office. An AWE website is currently in the works, and will be provided by Downpour Productions. It will be run completely through e-mail. ************************************************************************************ a) Who runs AWE? AWE, Absolute Wrestling Entertainment, is operated by President and Owner, David Silverberg. Silverberg is a longtime wrestling personality, often associated with other small independent federations. He has experience as a booker, a talent coordinator, and several other aspects of the industry, but his dream was always to own his own promotion. With AWE, he has accomplished that dream. Out of Character, [name withheld] has been a writer and handler in many feds, his most notable work can be found in the NEFW (North Eastern Federation of Wrestling). He has always thought about opening an e-fed of his own, but never had the proper time or aid, though now he has both. b) AWE Mission Statement The guidelines of Absolute Wrestling Entertainment are very simple. It was created to attract the greatest talent around in hopes of putting on a program that would appeal to everyone for athleticism and true competition. Primarily, Silverberg hoped to create a federation where the talent would have fun doing their work and be able to work together to create a fan-friendly environment that everyone could enjoy. Overall, the satisfaction of the fans comes first. AWE is around in hopes of gaining handlers and writers eager to contribute and willing to compete with others in the great game of e-wrestling. AWE wants to put out good, solid programs that are delivered on time and with strong writing, and develop a sense of community and friendship within the handlers. The best way to describe AWE's plan, would be that it is to leave you in AWE. c) AWE Personalities The employees of AWE are few, as the promotion is still very small. Listed here, they are: Ring Announcer: Gregory "Doc" Hanson "Doc" Hanson is an experienced announcer who worked for many Boxing Events in the Nevada area. AWE is very lucky to have him aboard, as he is also experienced in dealing with various talent. Senior Referee: William Yeath William Yeath is a 37-year-old former indy wrestler once known as "The Comet" Billy Yeath. Once age and injury caught up to him, he retired to a quiet life in Nevada, but quickly leaped on the offer to get back into the industry when approached by David Silverberg. He referees the most important matches for AWE. Other Referees: Taka Torohito Taka Torohito was a referee for a Japanese federation until financial burdens caused the fed to close. His ring experience and fair judgment made picking him up an easy call for Silverberg, who also hopes to use Taka in the future to negotiate talent relations with Japanese promotions. Evan Patterson Evan Patterson is a very young referee, whose size and lack of athletic ability kept him from becoming a wrestler. At times, it seems he is bitter towards those who actually are wrestlers, but Silverberg has faith in his ability to be fair. It remains to be seen if that faith is well-placed. Victoria Stevens A former boxing round-number girl, Victoria Stevens came to AWE alongside family friend "Doc" Hanson, and learned the referee business. She is very dedicated to her work, and though Taka Torohito has precedent, she might be second in line for the position of Senior Official should William Yeath retire. Alan Pryor Alan Pryor is a longtime friend of David Silverberg who often accompanied him as Silverberg worked for various promotions. When AWE was started, Silverberg immediately hired Pryor, who had some refereeing experience. Announcers: Play-by-Play: Lester Holland The perfect straight man, Lester Holland is a weedy, nervous little man with poor fashion taste and a sense of humor that is almost never seen. He calls matches as he sees them, and abhors any kind of cheating and is quick to condemn those who do. He is often the target of jokes from other personalities, but he usually doesn't understand them. Color Commentary: "The Encyclopedia" Jerry Brooks Jerry Brooks is a middle-aged former wrestler who has become disgruntled with the industry in general. He and Silverberg were friends years ago, and so he was persuaded to become Color Commentary man due to his experience and name value. Brooks, however, is almost the exact opposite of Lester Holland, prefering to take favorites in a match and call things in their favor. Brooks is called "The Encyclopedia" for his expansive knowledge of wrestling techniques and holds. Backstage Interviewers: Raylene O'Patrick A local weathergirl, Raylene doubles as a backstage interviewer whenever her job doesn't take her away. A perky young girl who sometimes thinks up good questions, but mostly just asks for opinions. Steven Jeffries Steven Jeffries is the main interviewer for AWE, and a good one at that. He tries to challenge the talent to explain their actions or plan out their next moves, and isn't above criticizing them. He sometimes gets in trouble because of his mouth, but he sees himself as a true journalist and refuses to back down. Website Design: Downpour Productions Writers: [detail withheld] , [detail withheld] [detail withheld] , Pak [detail withheld] d) History of AWE Absolute Wrestling Entertainment was the brainchild of David Silverberg, who hoped to create a promotion that would rise from grassroots to become the next dominant national federation. He never had the time nor resources to achieve this goal, however, and contented himself by working for various independent federations instead. But in the year 2000, Silverberg's Uncle died, and left him with a vast inheritance. The exact number is unknown, but within weeks of obtaining his newfound wealth, Silverberg began carrying out his dream. Finding a location in Eureka, Nevada, Silverberg contracted the construction of the 2,800-seat AWE Arena. Completed at the end of 2001, it is small, but very useful. While apparently having his funds tied up in acquiring talent, personalities, and a ring crew, as well as arena staff, Silverberg allowed the arena to be used for small concerts and sports exhibitions. Now, with things up and running, Silverberg only looks to have talented wrestlers sign on the dotted line in order to begin living his dream. ************************************************************************************ [2] AWE Events While not televised yet, AWE will still heavily advertise shows in hopes of attracting enough interest that they might one day become syndicated in local markets. ************************************************************************************ a) Weekly Shows AWE Thursday Night Impact Impact is the weekly show of the AWE, in which as much talent as possible will be featured. The show is one and a half hours long, and will usually feature up to six matches with time for backstage interviews set aside if necessary. AWE Talkin' Smack As there is no television time available for AWE as of yet, Silverberg allows wrestlers to use the camera equipment daily in order to film their own interviews, which are compiled and sent out to the other talent at the end of the day. You never know what's going to be said when AWE Talent starts Talkin' Smack. As long as there are Roleplays to be sent out, Talkin' Smack will be seen every night. b) Pay-Per-View AWE is nowhere near having the ability to put on Pay-Per-View shows, but it is a long-term goal of the promotion. c) Special Tournaments The plan of AWE is to host a series of tournaments for wrestlers the world over in order to generate initial interest. The specifics of these plans have not yet been revealed. ************************************************************************************ [3] On With the Show Now that AWE is ready to begin, it's important to note how the actual card will be planned and play out. ************************************************************************************ a) How AWE is Run All AWE Cards are booked by the President. Match outcomes are also decided upon by the President's judgment. The President judges all matches based on both the frequency of roleplaying by the characters involved and the quality of those roleplays. Quality especially is taken into consideration. b) Handler Influence Handlers are, of course, the lifeblood of AWE. Their input is greatly appreciated, and they are free to send in strategies that will also weigh in the judging of their match if sent in. Handlers can cooperate on their matches and plan them out themselves if they so wish. Any suggestions, ideas, or comments from Handlers on the running of AWE and its shows will be recieved and considered by the President. c) Angles/Storylines The driving factor behind AWE will be the interaction between characters and what comes of them. The hope is that angles will begin and from them will branch larger, more encompassing storylines that will attract attention and increase interest and enjoyment for all involved. While the President will plan some of these out himself, the hope is that Handlers will also think up angles amongst themselves and present them to the President for approval. Handlers are encouraged to work together in order to further their angles, and are free to turn to the President for suggestions, but they must have any and all angles approved before carrying them out. Also, if one wrestler wishes to carry out an angle and does not have the cooperation of the Handler he intends to have involved, that angle CANNOT be carried out. d) AWE Titles At the moment, the only Titles in Absolute Wrestling Entertainment are the AWE Title, held by the federation Champion, and the AWE Tag Team Titles. As AWE grows, more titles will be activated, and the status of the current titles may increase (i.e; the AWE Title may become the AWE Nevada Title, the AWE Western Title, the AWE American Title, and so on.) ************************************************************************************ [4] Rules Every good system is based on rules, and AWE is no different. Please read these rules carefully before deciding whether or not to join AWE. ************************************************************************************ a) The AWE Commandments. I) Have Fun. The basic rule of any e-fed is to enjoy yourself. Above all, remember that it's just a game, and that every game has winners and losers. The beauty of e-wrestling is that everyone has a chance to be both at some point or another, and depending how good you are, can have fun with either one. So above all, remember to just sit back and let the good times roll, and AWE will be good for everyone. II) Cooperate with Others AWE is not a place to let egos play. I don't care who you may have been in another federation or how many years you've put into e-wrestling, or how much better than someone else you believe you are. The main point of AWE is to establish ties between e-wrestlers, not start rivals with people when you should be concentrating on their characters instead. While we understand that sometimes handlers get personally offended at things that are said and "shoot," as it were, on one another, it is not a preferable III) Role-Playing You are required to send in at least two Roleplays per week in AWE. Failure to meet this number will result in a warning, two weeks in removal from the cards. If a handler goes a month without meeting the two Roleplay per week limit (barring sending an explanation to the President) their contract will be terminated. Also, there is a limit on vulgarity in flashing. It is perfectly acceptable to "bleep" words out, as is often done in e-wrestling, but as far as what should and should not be censored, just think of what we are allowed to hear on network television. Anything stronger than the word "bitch" should be censored. If you don't see it on NYPD: Blue, it isn't allowed here. A Roleplay should get your point across, even if it's just to be funny or make your character seem mean or vile. I'm looking for quality, things with substance. Scene setting, some action perhaps, something that stands out. All Roleplays are to be sent directly to [detail withheld] . At the end of every evening, all Roleplays sent in will be mailed out in one Flash packet called "[AWE] Talkin' Smack." As long as they are delivered to [detail withheld] before midnight, you will see them the following day. Roleplays are sent to the AWE Roster through a Yahoo! Group which you will be invited to join upon applying. IV) Decisions Ultimately, President David Silverberg has the final say on anything in Absolute Wrestling Entertainment. He will try to be fair and even-handed, but he will put the good of the collective fed over the good of a single individual. If you are upset with a decision he makes, let him know, and together something can be worked out. ************************************************************************************ [5] Joining Up To join AWE, you must have an original character that falls within the limits of being naturally less than 601 pounds and shorter than 8'1''. Also, any characters that have abilities that are grossly inproportional to their size (i.e; Andre the Giant-type characters that do handspring moonsaults, Billy Kidman-type characters that gorilla press people) will be rejected. Be original. AWE is still a young fed, without the budget for big special effects. There is a tron for entrance videos.) Alignment: (Pick One) Principled A principled character lives by a strict code of ethics. There is a definate right and wrong in the world for this type of character, and they attempt to right those wrongs. There are certain lines that cannot be crossed though, and these sorts of wrestlers will do all that they can within the rules to bring wrong do'ers to justice. A principled character is the "ultimate" face. Scrupulous People of this nature know right and wrong, and believe that those in the wrong should be brought to justice... but if the rules are preventing this, they will bend the rules so that justice can be served. The adage of fight fire with fire can be brought to light with this sort of character. Unprincipled The unprincipled wrestler understands that there are rights and wrongs in the wrestling world. However, as long as they can stretch a "few" of those rules, and don't get caught, they're still okay. As long as the damage done isn't devastating, and the repercussions don't have a long-term negative effect. An unprincipled character will be a nearly "facish" character, as he knows the rules, and will tend to abide within them, with only the occasional 'line crossing'. Taoist Whatever is fun or enjoyable is what is right. A person of this sort seeks out what they like and strives for that above other things. It doesn't matter where something came from or how they got it, as long as they can have fun using it. If someone doesn't understand the humour of a situation, they're missing out on the purpose of the whole thing. Insults and "fun scenes" are more important to a Taoist than an assault or sneak attack on an opponent. Anarchist To the anarchist style of wrestler, the social norms are simply roadblocks to allowing someone to be their best. The anarchist wrestler may or may not be a "me, me, me" type of person, as they may or may not care about their own best interests, as long as they're allowed to push the boundaries of standards and practices. Self-Serving Much like an unprincipled person, only one who works within a system, and as long as the system benefits the selfish wrestler. These people are usually quite aware that their actions can cause misery, but it doesn't bother them. The rules apply only when they are are beneficial. When the laws in place begin to become a burden, they are discarded. The only thing that matters is how things can best serve the person, not the other way around. Aberrant Honor among theives. People of this nature have a code of ethics that governs their lives. They abide by this code above what the accepted social norms are. They will not break their code, and have honor. That's not to say they won't cheat and use shortcuts. On the contrary, the nature of the sport clearly allows it, and they know it. Miscreant A person of this sort is someone who causes grief for kicks. Someone who seems to enjoy making life miserable for other people. They do have some lines that they won't cross... but not many. Most "heelish" e-wrestlers would probably fall in this category if they are true heels. Diabolic Those few that fall into this catagory are evil, plain and simple. There is no level to which these people will go to achieve their goal. Backstabbing, trickery, or whatever else is necessary to get what they want are tasks done without guilt. And it may not be for any natural goal.
