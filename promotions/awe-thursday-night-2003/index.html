Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Absolute Wrestling Entertainment announced itself on 24 April 2002 as a promotion with no television, no website and one building: a 2,800-seat arena in Eureka, Nevada that doubled as its head office, paid for in its own telling by an inheritance. Everything else ran by e-mail. Its rules asked for original characters inside plain height and weight limits, two roleplays a week, and language no stronger than network television allowed.

The Grand Debut Exhibition was taped on 7 May 2002 and circulated on videocassette because nothing else was available. Its president opened the tape from behind a desk; three matches followed in front of a few hundred people, called by a straight-man play-by-play voice and a partisan former wrestler. A man wrestled the opener, took a wad of cash from an identical stranger and left the ring to him. A drunk in a sponsor's shirt beat a nine-foot giant dressed as a Japanese island. A crooner announced his retirement from singing in order to come and wrestle.

A tournament for the promotion's own championship began on 18 May 2002 with eight wrestlers, and two of its first-round bouts survive. The giant went out to the man who had won the debut main event; the crooner put out the silent monster who had lost it. No further round was ever written.

The promotion went quiet that same month when its president lost his computer. On 23 August 2002 he came back and called nine of his wrestlers by name, promising a new writing team and a rebuilt website, and reissued the rules four days later with a rerun of the debut tape attached.

The next surviving broadcast is from June 2003, and by then the arena has a full card, a champion and a monster stalking the locker rooms. Two episodes of Thursday Night Impact survive from that year, the second describing the promotion as the Nevada region of a wider alliance. A poster asking what had become of it in August 2003 got only confirmation that it was gone.
