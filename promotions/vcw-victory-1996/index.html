Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

VCW opened its recruitment on 2 September 1996 with an announcement from its self-described president, Marcus Prime: “I would like to invite you all to join the VCW, better known as Victory Championship Wrestling.” A rival operator answered the same thread the same day to point out that an older, unrelated fed, Virtual Championship Wrestling, had been running under the identical bare acronym for a year and a half already; Marcus Prime's own reply the next morning was to the point: “Sorry, should have pointed that out :)”

Its plan leaned on scale and a hybrid system: six writers on staff, one weekly card of 11 to 13 matches, an interview show ahead of every card carrying handlers' own flashes and lineups, and a match-resolution method mixing booking, dice and simulation together with an in-fiction money economy. Titles were to be decided by turnout — a Heavyweight belt guaranteed, an Online Title, World Tag Team Titles, and a Light-Heavyweight or international belt only if enough wrestlers of that kind signed up.

Its character-creation sheet, posted four days later, asked for a 125-point attribute build (no attribute over 50, a primary skill of at least 25) alongside the usual wrestler profile, and pointed hopefuls at a handbook hosted on its own webpage. Whether the promised first card, planned for roughly two weeks out, ever ran isn't in the surviving record — no card, roster or further post survives beyond this character sheet.
