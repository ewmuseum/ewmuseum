GXW — General Diss

GXW: How It's Done

31 January 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
WEAVER — 31 January 2002 Greetings amigos... It is I, [name withheld], handler of everyones favourite whackout, Gemini. Something has bothered me for a while about FW in general, and I'm writing this little article as an attempt to prevent some of it. The main problem that I see with FW in general is this, everyone seems to measure their amount of success based on their wins vs losses. then they lose three in a row or aren't a world champion a month after they join up and they just stop playing.
That's not the point folks. I don't even worry about Gemini's wins and losses any more... (Which should be blatantly obvious by my last match with zero.) I strictly worry about how 'Over' my two faced boy is.
Over means this. It means that when you sign for a match with somebody, they should be practically dying to get in the ring with you. It means that when someone goes to read the card, they should be just dying to get to your match to see how it turns out. Your success as a FW wrestler is not based on wins or losses or title reigns... it's based on the quality of your matches.
Let me give you a very famous example that is based in real life. Mick Foley vs Undertaker in Hell in the cell. Who won that match? Taker. Who rode it to fame and fortune? Foley. That one match gave Foley an aura where you never wanted to miss a single one of his matches because you just never knew what the hell he was gonna do next.
I aim for that every time I get into a match. Do I really screw up sometimes? Hell yeah. But I try. I try every time out to try and do something memorable. And I think I've succeeded.
Now, as for the reason that I'm writing this article... I have noticed lately a large number of new characters starting to appear in GXW. I see guys that have been here for a while really start to develop, and It bums me to think that they may not be here much longer.
So with that... allow me to give my 'Rules for Fantasy Wrestling'. 1)Sooner or later, everyone loses a close one. It's true. It happens. Once in a while you bust your ass for a match, write some truly amazing stuff, and the cards don't fall your way. It happens. It will happen again. This is not a negative. Trust me. Look at the Rock. How often does he lose matches? All the time. Is he still over? Hell yes. Take your loss and use it as a springboard for a storyline. Focus on something that happened during the match and expound on that. Chris Lehew and eric Davis just pounded the living crap out of each other on the last card, I wish I'd been involved in that because there are about fifteen different spins that I could put on the way that match was written.
2) Just because you're not on the card, that doesn't mean you're not on the card. This is unbelieveably the biggest major sin in every Fed I've been in. People that aren't in on matches don't do anything for the card. They don't ask, they don't get creative and they don't appear in any matches or any backstage vignettes.
That's dumb guys. If you have an angle, you should try and work it on every card. Jump the guy you're feuding with backstage. Cut an interview boasting about how good you are and then have them display it on the Extreme screen. Do something. Because if you don't do something, they forget about you. And if they forget about you... well... that ain't good.
3)Sooner or later, your ideas *WILL* be shot down. It'll happen. You'll spend six and a half hours writing something and it gets shot down. It sucks, it pisses you off and it will happen more than once. Two things to remember here... it's not personal and you are actually the one at fault.
The people running the fed have to balance multiple storylines from every single character, and you have to realize that sometimes stuff that gets written contradicts other stuff that is written. The way to prevent this is to talk with the people running the show. Talk to the guy running your fed, BEFORE you spend a whack of time writing.
Sometimes it's a simple as this... Bob: Hey, do you mind if I kidnap Buttplex's valet on the next card? Pres: Actually, Buttplex's valet is having a sex cahnge next card... so wait til the card after.
See? Isn't that simple? So there it is guys... a simple little list of things you can do to make your FW experience better.
Anybody have any response to this? Comments, critiques, flames?

a longtime handler — 3 February 2002 Absolutely agreed. I'm of the belief that, when it comes to being a heel, you definitely want to build him up to look strong, but when it comes time for him to take a fall, then it happens.
And as far as a babyface taking a loss, it's just a matter of taking that character and finding ways for him to stay in the thick of things and go with logical character development. Case in point: Marcus Johnson just lost the Unified World title. But rather than him demanding a rematch, I'm now having him take the stance that he wants to prove himself on his own merits before getting back into the hunt. That's a logical step to take, rather than just sending him after the belt again.
In several cases, not just here, but elsewhere, I've asked to take a loss to allow somebody to go over and get the rub, or as the chance to advance a storyline. Sometimes it's good to take a loss so that your character doesn't get the upper hand too much, and balance things out a bit until the payoff match arrives.
It's all about building a character, folks. People who work to develop good characters are going to have more fun than those who just want to win. Never forget that.
