A forum-hosted federation on the fwrestling.com hub that ran under the name Global Xtreme Wrestling for most of its life, renamed Global Wrestling Evolution in its final months, and closed in October 2004 without completing the change its own staff had begun to announce. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Global Xtreme Wrestling ran on the fwrestling.com forum under more names than most e-feds live long enough to need. By its own staff's telling it began as NthWA, became Global Xtreme Wrestling (GXW) after a merger with a fed called EWI, and took a final name, Global Wrestling Evolution, in its last year — a history its own co-owner, Erik Zieba, joked he could barely keep straight when he returned to the fed in April 2004, rattling off three earlier names in a row before landing on the one that stuck: “it is GREAT to be back in MW ... um EW ... no it's Nth ... wait GX ...” — “Different name ... same attitude!”

Under Zieba and co-owner Chad Dupree it ran a full card of championships across its documented run — a Unified World Heavyweight title, a Continental title, a Television title, an X-treme title, a Cruiserweight title, a World Tag Team title, a Women's title and, briefly in its first months, a second, short-lived Television Tag Team title — contested on weekly Onslaught cards and quarterly pay-per-views like Global Warfare and Battleground Britain.

Its own X-treme title went from something its own staff worried was “dead weight” in late 2003 to a title built back up by outright chaos: at Global Warfare, champion Clapper defended it by sabotaging a ladder match and running down two challengers with a car. Its Cruiserweight division, meanwhile, crowned a new champion that November through a bracket unlike any of its other titles' own — three-way qualifiers, then a round where the survivors paired up as tag teams, before the last two met alone for the belt.

The fed nearly folded in October 2003, when its own booker asked outright, “Is GXW dead? Have we run our course?” — and its own roster answered back within a day, one handler declaring himself “GXW until it's dragged kicking and screaming into obscurity.” It kept running another eight months under that resolve, through its own final rename that April.

It closed for real on 20 June 2004, its own staff writing plainly: “GXW/GWE has officially closed it's doors as of today.” Two months later, on 27 August 2004, co-owner Chad Dupree died. Erik Zieba announced one further card in his memory that September, The Final Chapter, open to any wrestler who had ever passed through the promotion's roster and billed as the last show it would ever hold.

No result from that memorial card survives; whether The Final Chapter ever actually aired isn't in the surviving record.
