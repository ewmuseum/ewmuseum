A forum-hosted federation on the FW Central hub whose founding wrestlers fought through a Battle Bowl tournament to crown its first World Heavyweight Champion, later merging with a second promotion, WFW, into a co-branded “WFW:NE” before returning to the same forum host in 2015. Four title lineages, 19 shows and 105 wrestlers survive. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

New ERA of Wrestling opened its doors on the FWCentral forum host on 4 January 2004, with its own founding post promising a roster capped tight and vetted hard: applicants would be “scrutinizing... to the toughest degree,” and a handler could carry only one singles wrestler at a time, though tag teams were welcomed nearly on sight, with six slots open. Cards ran a ten-day cycle — a week to roleplay, results within three days — for roughly three shows a month and a pay-per-view every two, built around three titles from the start: a World Title, a Television Title and Tag Team titles.

Its own World Heavyweight Championship was decided at the federation's founding show in a Battle Royal fourway, and Larry Tact is the only name the surviving record's own retrospective coverage credits with the win. The Phantom Republican succeeded him by January 2006, a change independently corroborated months later when another federation's own posting named him champion and Larry Tact his predecessor in passing. By July 2009 the title had folded into a wider merger: New ERA joined forces with a promotion called World's Finest Wrestling to run as the co-branded WFW:NE, and Shawn Hart is billed champion of a unified title on the merger era's own cards, resolving what the surviving record shows only as a co-championship split with a second titleholder. When WFW:NE came apart, New ERA said as much in its own words, applying in January 2010 to join the Championship Wrestling Council “to help the league bounce back from the disintegration of the WFW:NE.”

In January 2011 New ERA rebuilt itself around a season structure: six to eight shows a season, run from the Agganis Arena at Boston University, results told in an “extreme short-form” style modeled on old newsstand magazine recaps, with each season's finale alone written long-form and staged at the DCU Center in Worcester. The reigning World Heavyweight Champion became the promotion's first New ERA Champion under the new name, and its Television and PRODIGY Classic Xtreme titles were retired outright, their own champions folded into a system of bankable championship shots earned by winning three matches running or being named a season's Most Valuable Wrestler. The belt changed hands three times across 2011 alone — The First, then Mr. Entertainment, then Fanatic, then The First again by August — each change inferred from the next card's own billing rather than a stated result.

New ERA then went dark in the surviving record for more than three years, running instead on a self-hosted site it had switched to for full control of its own forums. It announced its return to FWCentral in March 2015, framing the move as coming home: “When New ERA started way back in 2004, our forums were hosted on FWCentral... I think it is only right that New ERA be a part of that.” Its own last surviving card, BANNED in the US II on 6 April 2015, opened a new Season 3 with an enclosed steel cage match for the New ERA Championship, The First defending the title he had carried since 2011 against a challenger named Suicide. The result isn't in the surviving record.
