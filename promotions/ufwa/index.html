Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The UFWA opened 8 August 1997 on a single operator's rules-and-application post, capped from the start to its first sixteen singles wrestlers and eight tag teams and based, in its own words, in North America. Its own president had stepped down as vice-president of an earlier promotion, the TWA, to start it.

It ran one card a week, on Wednesday nights, called by face commentator Jerry Nabisco and heel commentator Berry Hawler, with Andy Lime announcing and William Bishop interviewing. It planned a UFWA Title, a Television Title, and a Tag Team Title, with a Lightweight title held in reserve if enough lighter wrestlers signed, results decided by roleplay and strategy, and a hard cap of two roleplays a week per wrestler.

No closure document of its own survives. But months later, on 21 November 1997, a newsgroup poster opened an unrelated debate about simulated versus booked federations by calling a “moment of silence” for the UFWA, praising its writer and its roleplayers as never boring; a follow-up six days later, from the same poster, recalled rising from a struggling two-to-three-kilobyte roleplayer to the promotion's own top-ranked wrestler by the time it closed.
