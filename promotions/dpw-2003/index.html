A revived Usenet e-mail e-fed running under an alcohol-and-party theme from its December 2003 reopening, naming a Hall of Fame roster from an earlier 1990s run the RSPWF Top 200 independently attests. Seven title lineages, 21 shows and 97 wrestlers survive. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Dublin Pub Wrestling's own earlier run is attested only from the outside at first: the newsgroup's own 1997 and 1998 Top 200 polls tagged several of its wrestlers, and a Hall of Fame the promotion later named for itself — Trillionaire Thad Thorton, Stan “The Man” Riley, Polish Devil, “Godly” Ken Davison, Psycho, Malibu Mike Donovan, Badass John, and Miss Toy — ran through a title history that survives directly from April 1999: a Heavyweight Championship that changed hands twice that month alone, once when a challenger wrestling masked pulled off an upset and once when his own scheduled co-defender was attacked backstage, an Intercontinental title defended in a falls-count-anywhere match the champion's own pet donkey helped him win, and a U.S. title retained against a debuting challenger left burned in the face by an exploding gift box.

“As of December 7th 2003 I have opened the doors back up,” its own operator wrote, explaining the promotion had closed roughly two years earlier when working two jobs left him no time for it. He reopened it deliberately old-school: no reporting other members for what their characters said in a roleplay (“this fed is not for the weak hearted”), wrestlers allowed to write into each other's own roleplays freely, and a mix of original characters and wrestlers built on real performers both accepted.

An eight-man tournament at that reopening crowned “The Maniacle Mercenary” Mitch Armour as Heavyweight Champion, and a twenty-man Royal Rumble that February crowned Rufus “The Amish Giant” Yoder as the first Intercontinental Champion, eliminating the rest of the field alone after two rival stables, the Prodigy and the Pack, wore each other down first. Armour defended the Heavyweight title almost every week through the spring of 2004 before the same Prodigy stable turned on him from the inside: his own title match that April was decided when his manager, revealed as a Prodigy plant, blindsided him at ringside, handing the belt to “The German War Machine” Mathias Mathews and declaring on camera, “Prodigal Son Inc now runs the DPW.”

A second eight-man tournament that December crowned “The Anti-Hero” Ice Kold champion, and a new Women's Championship, created that winter, went outright to its first challenger, Buffy Summers, on the same card a rival wrestler DDT'd her through her own newly-won belt in protest. The promotion's own record closes on 25 January 2005 with Armour reclaiming the Heavyweight title from Ice Kold in a Buried Alive match fought through a broken window and a six-foot grave, burying both Ice Kold and his own valet under the dirt — only for a masked attacker to chair Armour from behind the moment the bell rang, before the broadcast cut away.

Two further cards, “Winner Drinks All” and “Bloody Mary,” were previewed afterward, the latter dated 8 February 2005, but no results for either were ever posted to the newsgroup. Whatever became of Armour's reign, or of the wrestler who attacked him, isn't in the surviving record.
