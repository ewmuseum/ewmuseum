Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Online Wrestling Alliance launched its InterNet Wrestling Pro division on 2 December 1995, a new branch of an OWA already running its own championship elsewhere online, held at the time by Amazon Woman. The debut broadcast ran an eight-man tournament to crown a first InterNet Champion, Ricky Blood taking the final, and closed by booking him against Amazon Woman for the promotion's top title.

Amazon Woman retained that title against Blood in a bloody main event at WinterTime Massacre later that month, while a stable calling itself The Family — Blood, Pretty Boy Monty, Devastator, Rocket, Phantom, and Big Dawg Rich — declared its intent to sweep every championship the promotion carried.

OWA Championship Wrestling ran as a regular weekly broadcast through 1996, its title picture in constant motion: the OWA Championship passed from Amazon Woman through Pretty Boy Monty, Traci, Anthony Hazard, Rachel Ryan, and Stacks Coltrain before returning to Traci, while the OWA Television Championship changed hands just as often among Amazon Woman, Mariko, Masked Mayhem, Johnny Fate, and Asian Invasion. The year's own supercards carried it along — Endurance's Evil Challenge, Crazy Eights, Hardcore Heaven, and Halloween Horror: The Wheel of Torture's Revenge among them.

The year closed with The Final Battle on 31 December 1996: a retirement match between Amazon Woman and Freddy Fever that ran two hours, thirty-two minutes, and seventeen seconds, ending when Fever sealed her inside a casket and pushed it out of the arena. Called at the time as the longest and most brutal match the promotion had ever staged, it closed Amazon Woman's own OWA career; the two wrestlers embraced afterward before a full house.

A seven-night New Year's Grand Prix opened 1997 alongside the ordinary weekly card. The promotion's own Crime Spree pay-per-view, broadcast in four parts on 2 May 1997, closed with Traci retaining the OWA Championship in a Triangle Ladder match against Anthony Hazard and Pretty Boy Monty, the OWA Commissioner personally stripping the belt from Hazard after he refused to surrender it and suspending him for missing an earlier match on the same card.

Crime Spree is the last OWA-branded document in the surviving record; a following pay-per-view it announced for that June is never attested. A community-compiled federation directory posted that September lists the promotion's own web address already renamed to Universal Cruiser Pro — no document of the promotion's own records when or why the change was made.
