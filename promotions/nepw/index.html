Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

New England Professional Wrestling survives in a single document: a founding handbook, self-dated 29 June 1997, announcing that the fed was “almost ready to go.” It covered all of New England — Massachusetts, Connecticut, Maine, New Hampshire and Vermont — and modelled itself, in its own words, on “old NWA stuff more than anything you see today,” deliberately breaking from what it called wrestling's “lame aspects.” A three-roleplay-a-week limit was written into the rules on purpose, so that “more time can be spent on rp's” rather than handlers racing to post the most.

Its own handbook staffed the promotion before a single match had been announced: a play-by-play announcer, two colour commentators for its Monday Night Assault, a separate pair hosting the Friday news show NEPW Explosion, a head referee, and a ring announcer. Three titles were named — the New England Championship, the Massachusetts State Championship, and a Tag Team Championship — none carrying “World” status, by the handbook's own choice, since NEPW called itself only a smaller regional promotion. Four annual supercards were planned, the most elaborate a February tournament called Mystery Date and an August cage structure three stories tall called the Tower of Terror, where the winner had to reach the ground before the other competitors to claim the match and a guaranteed shot at the New England Championship.

No card, result, or roster beyond the handbook's own creation sheet survives to say whether any of it was ever run.
