Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

American Wrestling Enterprise announced itself on 14 August 1997, promising to debut “nothing short of spectacular” on 1 September, and open only to fantasy wrestlers — “no WWF/WCW/ECW etc. contracted wresters need apply.” Its own founder wrestled under his operator persona, Creed.

It ran two studio-framed shows a week, the flagship AWEsome Monday Night alongside Wednesday Wrap-Up and Weekend Wake-Up, built around a recurring commentary team and cold-open sketches, and seven titles: a World Heavyweight Championship already around Vincenzo Galliano's waist by the time the surviving record opens, with Creed himself among the wrestlers chasing it.

A naming mix-up briefly spilled onto the newsgroup that September, when an unrelated promotion's own president publicly disavowed “a charlatan and pretender” also using the name Creed. AWE's own Creed replied that the two of them had already sorted it out directly: “that's like some guy named Bob calling himself the 'real' Bob and accusing everyone else named Bob of being an imposter.”

By November the promotion had drawn corporate attention, staged as a hostile takeover: the in-fiction conglomerate MegaCorp announced it had bought AWE outright and would merge it with a rival promotion, All Pro Wrestling, at a New Year's Eve pay-per-view, War of the Worlds, with “the victorious league taking over the loser.”

Its last surviving broadcast, Weekend Wake-Up on 20 December 1997, still ran ordinary business — eight matches, Creed himself winning his own by disqualification — while previewing the special to come: a full unification card pitting AWE's champions against APW's own across the World, World Tag Team, and three lightweight-class titles at once.

The pay-per-view itself, and which promotion actually survived it, isn't in the surviving record.
