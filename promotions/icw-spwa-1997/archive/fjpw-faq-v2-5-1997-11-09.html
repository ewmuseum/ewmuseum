Changed by [name withheld]. ) - A wrestler is allowed as many roleplays a week as they feel they need to do. They do not have to be done in one day, but they must be in by 4:00 PM on the day before the card to count. All could be done and sent on your last day. This will definitely put an emphasis on quality. - Wrestlers may also do one character development roleplay (Sorta like a look into their history) that will NOT affect their matches in any way. Therefore, they don't count :) Still, they are a good way to show past actions and give everyone a better picture of why your character is the way he is. - Also, you may do one In Ring interview. These are like the interviews where your wrestler comes down on the card, into the ring (hence the name in ring), and does an interview. If you do not want to do an in ring, you can do a short rp that will be put in a card for pre-match comments. Usually do not affect a match unless my disgression sees differently. - Lastly, angles and strats will now partially affect the outcome of the match. In an extremely close match, I will go to the angles to see if something would make my decision better, especially if it saves me from declaring a draw. (TRY to follow these guidelines when sending stuff in! It's NOT the end of the world if you DON'T but it IS nice if you DO! :-) WHAT TO SEND? v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v - TO GET MATCHES: Challenge, Challenge, Challenge! - TO WIN: Roleplay, Roleplay, and (did I mention?) Roleplay! - ALSO IT HELPS TO: In Ring (Showdown) interviews! Just leave space for the announcers to ask questions (or even write them yourself). You fill in the answers or whatever and we'll do the rest! - AND IT NEVER HURTS TO: Send in a STRAT! That's short for STRATEGY. A strategy would include how you want your guy to go about wrestling the match, or any sneak attacks/double crosses that you want to see happen. This is not required every time ... just when you want something special to happen. It REALLY helps improve the angles and plotlines. We can't always follow your suggestion to a "T," but we will try. Please LABEL it as a "Roleplay," a "Strat," or whatever it is in the subject line, followed by your character's name. Keeps us organized! :-) Also a "[FJPW]" never hurts. FJPW Wrestlers: This short information guide should help you immensly on your league participation. Things such as Strats, In Ring Interviews and Roleplays will impact your success rate in FJPW by 100%! So you'd better stay on task and start that roleplaying, folks! Here it is... v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^ v^v^v^v^ A. STRATS! v^v^v^v^v^ v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^ What is a STRAT? Well, guys, if you don't know already... STRAT is short for Strategy. For any match that you compete in here in the FJPW YOU are entitled to 1 match strategy. This STRAT will tell us how to play your character in the matchup, and it will help your chances of winning. For instance... [Bob is set to wrestle Joe on the 23rd of January. He knows that Joe has out roleplayed him and he has very little chance of winning the matchup. In order to help him in his matchup, Bob decides to write a strat. His strat is very simple and it consists of attempting to go at Joe's knees. Here is his STRAT for you to use as an example.] Dear [name withheld] I know Joe is going to kick my ass on the 23rd, but I will come at his knees with a variety of manovers consisting of Figure Four Leglocks, Grapevines, Clips, and more. At one point in the matchup, my guy Bob will attempt to take him outside of the ring for some action and attack him with a chair. That's my STRAT, sir. Thank you! From, Bob Members: Sending in STRATS is a great and easy way to add fun to the matchup and you can get a chance to see how your strategy matches up against your opponents. In Ring Interviews! v^v^v^v^v^ v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^ What Is An In-Ring or On-Card Interview? Well, guys, have you ever seen a WWF or WCW contest where at a certain point the broadcaster says, "Let's go to pre-recorded comments from ____." ? If you haven't, you mustn't be a very avid wrestling fan! That is what an On-Card interview is. Sometimes your wrestler will want to make last minute comments about a matchup and I'll publish the on the card. Or, sometimes your guy will want to grab the mike and rant about his problems in the ring. Will It Help My Roleplaying Standing? Of course it will! In Ring or On Card Interviews are treated at the same level as roleplays, and sometimes higher level. Each card we put out should have at least _TWO_In ring or On Card interviews. If you'd like to write one up, send it to [detail withheld] ,Thanks, and get writing! ^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^ ^v^v^v^v^ C. Roleplaying v^v^v^v^v^v^ ^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^ Welcome to the FJPW Roleplaying Tips sheet. If you are a newbie to the e-wrestling world, this should give you a jump start on how to roleplay and should help you along your journey to being a FJPW Champion! Read this closely and carefully and make sure to drop [detail withheld] telling him how you liked the sheet. Well, anyways, thanks and lets get right into the tips, shall we? ---InDeX--- + Content + Length + Detail +-+-+-+-+-+ Content +-+-+-+-+-+-+ + What Is Content? + Content is not the length the roleplay but the message that the roleplay gets across. Content is one of the three most important parts of a roleplay. A roleplay without content is just jabber and jargon thrown into a file. You must always have some sort of content in your roleplays. + Set A Goal + When you begin a roleplay, set a goal for the content you want to include in your roleplay. Tell yourself what, in a brief rundown, that you want to tell everybody in this roleplay. If you want to tell the whole FJPW that you are here to stay, then remember that fact while you are writing your roleplay. Never write a roleplay with out any topics to write about. + Does My Roleplay Have Good Content? + If you want to know if your roleplay has any content, then read it over and ask yourself what the message you got acrossed was. If you can't answer that question then the chances are, you have no body and content to your roleplay and you need to start over and try again. + More Content The Better + The more topics you have to discuss, the better. You can never have enough content. If you have enough topics to write 5 pages or so, then so be it and go through with it. Content is good people! + Elaborate on Your Topic + If your topic is "I want to challenge Squirt to a match" then ask yourself all the possible questions to stimulate ideas! Ask yourself why you want to wrestle Squirt. Ask yourself how good of a chance you have at defeating Squirt. Ask yourself what type of match you want to challenge Squirt to. Ask youself when you want to wrestle Squirt! The possibilities are endless, people! Cover every area you can! +-+-+-+-+-+-+- Length -+-+-+-+-+-+-+ + Length Is Important + One of the most important parts of your roleplay is its length. You're roleplay MUST be at least be 30 lines (3k) to get anything acrossed in our opinion's. Although it is hard to find the happy-medium between too long and too short, you'll get there if you follow these steps! + Not Too Long + Don't wear your topic down so much that nobody is going to be interested in reading it anymore. Cover what you need but don't exasperate the subject. How do you feel when you watch a 20 minute Hacksaw Duggan matchup featuring a chinlock and a clothesline? That's how people feel reading a 20 k roleplay. Once you stop having fun writing your roleplay it's probably finished. + Not Too Short + You must'nt send in a roleplay that is 3 lines long. In FJPW, everytime you send in a roleplay that is 10 or less lines, that's an automatic loss. Make your roleplay to the point but long enough so it'll take a few minutes to read and leave the reader with a good feeling. Touch base with the subject quite a bit. +-+-+-+-+-+-+- Detail -+-+-+-+-+-+-+ + Add A Beginning and Conclusion + At the beginning of your roleplay, you must _ALWAYS_ add a beginning. A beginning tells who, what, where, when and why of the roleplay. A beginning is also put inside action bars which are () or [] or :: :: or {} In a beginning, you should describe what the character is wearing, what his mood is like, where his is, what he's doing there, ect. ex. [The screen fades in to Squirt. He's sitting in a dark living room rocking back and forth on an easychair. He's wearing his street clothes which consist of a pair of faded denimn jeans, a black tank top, and a nike headband. His black hair is slicked back and his eyes are squinted and feirce. The only illumination in the room is cast by a fireplace with the embers slowly dying out. The cameraman gives him a close up as signals to come over to him.] + Add a Conclusion + The conclusion is more of the same except it would say things like, Squirt is fading out, ect. Be carefull not to make these too drab. It can get tricky to think up the setting some times. <Hint: The setting could have something to do with the content.> + Watch The Spelling + Go through the document numerous times to make sure you did'nt make any foolish spelling mistakes, make sure you add two spaces after a period, you inserted commas where needed, you did'nt have any fragments or run-ons. Don't make yourself look foolish by saying stuff like this: ex. (Wrtler): "im cmin to getg you cuz of what you ded to me that tyme when u did dat thing.." Well, I hope this helped you out with your roleplaying. ===---===---===---===---===---===---===---===---===---===---=== [[- FJPW TELECASTS -]] Frontier Japan Pro Wrestling has ONE weekly card a week which is FJPW RAGE!!! .
