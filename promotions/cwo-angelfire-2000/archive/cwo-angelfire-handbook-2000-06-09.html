Well, behind the Computer, the Championship Wrestling Organization is ran by me, [name withheld], [name withheld], and [name withheld].
But, in the CWO itself, Andy Sand, with the help of Jimmy Rhodes, and Johnny Holiday run things. b. Owner's Background Now, in E-Wrestling, as you all know, and I know very well, age does not matter. Take it from me, I'm fifteen years old, and have been in
E-Wrestling since I was twelve. I've been turned down by a couple of presidents who didn't know what fantasy wrestling was all about if it slapped them in the forehead. But, I've also been accepted into a lot of feds as well, and as a matter of fact, I've been on top of e-feds, as the guy who runs things. The Main Eventer, the real deal, the guy that runs the show, the Top Dog, I've been there, and I've done that, and I own the tee shirt. I have handled various characters throughout my e-wrestling career, but, the guy who has brought five championship belts, is none other than "The Hardcore Icon" or "Pure Chaos" [name withheld]
Franklin. By the time you read this, I'm sure I probably have held, or have more belts, but, this is where I stand on the fifth of May. So, anyway, I'm a short ninth grader, who likes to impress the girls, play baseball, basketball, football, all that good stuff, and oh yes, wrestling. I'm very dedicated to E-Wrestling, and this fed won't be any different. I've ran feds for two years now, off and on. Now - to my partner, Andy Sand.
Andy is a former commentator for the UEW. He has a lot of money, and he is easy to get along with. The Championship Wrestling Organization is being run by Andy, who has absolutely no experience what so ever in running a Wrestling Organization. But, with his creative mind, great personality, and astounding financial status, Andy is sure to hit the jackpot with the CWO!
c. What is the CWO? The CWO is the Championship Wrestling Organization, based out of Orlando, Florida, Andy Sand's Hometown. But, [name withheld] has never visited Orlando. :-) d. How is the CWO ran? The CWO is run under a "Closed E-fedding System." What this means is RolePlays are sent out to the President, and the President sends them out to the fed, in packets. He also uses the "Blind Carbon Copy (BCC)"
mailing option, so others in the fed can not see the mailing list, and can't use it for advertising purposes.
The CWO is divided into four different divisions. The "Heavyweight Division," the "Hardcore Division," the "Cruiserweight Division," and last but certainly not least, the "Tag Team Division."
I will be getting into the specifics of how the CWO is ran, if you keep reading, and please do!
e. Rules Well, there are a lot of Rules in the CWO, but, these are the simple rules. The basics, the easy stuff.
1. If you have the urge to cuss, then, you may use these words, as much as you want, without censoring them in an RP. The A Word The B(itch) Word The D Word The H Word The SOB words That's about all. But, any other word usage should be censored, and you may not use more than five censors per roleplay or in-ring interview.
2. Do not use inappropriate Material (in RolePlays, Angles, and Inrings) for the CWO. What that means is you can't have racial slurs, you can't have nudity, and you can't have any Out of Character remarks towards someone else, which is known as a "shoot" and will get you out of the CWO on a free ticket!
3. If you lose, then don't come to me, or any of the Division Presidents, griping about how you lost. If you lose, then come to me or your division president, and ask us how you lost, and why, and we'll be more than happy to explain. Remember, if you aren't rude, we won't be.
4. You may not use other handler's characters in your RPs. If you do, and you send it to me, without their permission - deleted. 5. You may handle no more than TWO (2) wrestlers in the CWO. If you do try and get a new account, and pull the blanket over my eyes, I'll get you! 6. If A wrestler is booked to be on a card (a match or in-ring) then you may attack him at anytime. If a wrestler is not booked on a card (in any way) then you can not attack him without the Handler's permission. Now, I don't want you to come to me after reading this rule, and tell me to change, because it's stupid. I'll immediately reply back with a "Maybe it's not the rule that's stupid..." and you get the hint for the rest.
7. You have been blessed with Common Sense. Use it. I could list about fifty more rules, but, that'd be a waste of time for both you and I. So, please use your best judgement. If you have any questions pertaining to the rules, or have a question about one that isn't there, let me know. ------------------------------------------------------------------------ 2. Events a. ------------------------------------------------------------------------ 3. Angles and Strategies a. What are Angles? Well, in some feds, Angles are different than what I think of them. In this fed, Angles are attacks, or plans you'd like to do to another wrestler. For example, when Wrestler A is Wrestling Wrestler B - and your Wrestler, Wrestler C in this case runs out and attacks Wrestler A, that to me, is an angle. So, that's what I think of it as, just to let you know.
b. What are Strategies? Strategies to me, are the plans behind the match. For Example, if you're in a match against Wrestler B, and you have some plans or what not for the match, then, you write them and send them in. Strategies go through all aspects of the match, what your character is going to be like, is he going to be focused, or out of control? Is your character going to have a fast or slow paced match. It's a description from start to finish as to what you'd like your character to do.
c. How often should I do angles? As often as you'd like. But, keep in mind that you can write some checks that you may not be able to cash. By attacking too many people, and getting too many enemies, you're liable to get frustrated. Believe me, I know!
d. How often should I do strategies? Strategies should be done for every match that you have, it helps towards winning. e. What is "heat?" Well, "Heat" is a little hard to explain, but, I'll try my best. If you're a heel character, and you want the crowd to hate you, what do you do? You draw "heat" or in other words, you MAKE them hate you. By what you say, and what you do, you should be getting acknowledged.
Now, although this isn't a "real crowd" or anything, "heat" is a big factor. Also, if you are the good guy, you can also get "heat." But, that's a whole different ballgame. The "heat" a bad guy gets is because the crowd hates him, and he does a good job at making them hate him.
The "heat" for a good guy is because of how he makes the crowd hate the bad guy and love the good guy! Understand?
f. How do I go about getting "heat?" Well, interviews, and angles. That is the man thing you do to go about getting "heat." By attacking People, and making the crowd happy or mad at your character from what he says is a good way to do it. Also, in- ring interviews are great ways to draw some attention.
------------------------------------------------------------------------ 4. Division Information a. Divisions of the CWO There are four divisions, the Heavyweight Division, the Cruiserweight Division, the Hardcore Division and the Tag Team Division. We are divided into Divisions, because it's easier to go about things in that matter. If you have a Hardcore Title, and a Wrestler who doesn't even like being a Hardcore Champion as your champion, because he can out
Roleplay the other guy, that's not going to be fun for anyone. So, we're divided into divisions to make things a little more fun and interesting.
b. Can I fight out of my own division? Yes, you may fight outside of your own division, but most of the time, you will be kept in matches within your division. It keeps the work- load down a little, and helps you from getting confused. So, it's all going to benefit you.
------------------------------------------------------------------------ 5. Rankings Information a. How Matches are judged Matches are judged on three things. Now, normally you'd get percentages for this, but, I'm not real good with Math, so, I'm going to go from least to greatest.
Roleplays - How good in Quality your RolePlays are, NOT quantity. Remember, one RolePlay can beat two, if good enough. Strategies - Your match Gameplan. Heat - Which means, how much your character is known in the fed, according to his alignment (face or heel).
b. Who judges Matches That depends on what division your character is in. If I don't have a seperate President for a Seperate Division, then, I'll judge your match. But, if you have a Division President in the Cruiserweight
Division, he judges everything from RolePlays, to Strategies. c. Winning Matches If you read everything above in this section, and the last one, you should have a basic idea on how to win matches. Matches aren't easy to win at times, and don't expect to win all the time. But, if you don't win, I don't want to see any complaints in my e-mail when I wake up, and check it.
d. Point System Well, this Point System is starting to get used on the Internet, more and more for e-wrestling. I've adopted this point system from the UEW
(Ultimate E-Wrestling) league. And, this is how it works. First off, all new wrestlers start off with Zero (0) points. Second, all Singles matches won by pin or submission are worth two (2) added points. Any Gimmick match you win, depending on certain circumstances, are also worth two (2) added points.
If you win by Count-Out or Disqualification, One (1) point is added to your record. Draws are worth Zero (0) points. Losses by pinfall, submission, forfeit, and no-shows are two (2) taken away from your Ranking.
Losses by Count-Out or Disqualification is one (1) point taken away from your Ranking. Any matches with more than four (4) participants (with the exception of Tag Teams) don't count against your ranking. But, a Fatal Four Way, and all Three Way Dances do.
------------------------------------------------------------------------ 6. Titles a. General Idea behind Titles Titles to some are the key to the Fed. By holding a title, you claim your spot at the top of your division, and you get more "heat" for your character, and become known a little more.
b. Global Heavyweight Championship Title This is the big one. The World Heavyweight Championship Title must be defended within Thirty (30) days. But, we don't let the Champion have his Thirty (30) days. He has to work just as hard as everyone else, so, therefore, he'll be defending the title just as much. This Title can only be won if your wrestler is in the Heavyweight Division.
c. National Championship Title The National Championship Title is the only title that can be won and defended outside of one's division. But, it's most likely that this title will primarily be in the Heavyweight Division rather than mixing it with the other divisions. This title will be defended a lot more often, and you may see more title changes with this one.
c. Cruiserweight Championship Title The Cruiserweight Championship Title is the jewel of the Cruiserweight Division, and can only be defended within that division. d. Hardcore Championship Title The Hardcore Championship Title is for the Hardcore Fanatics in the Hardcore Division. It can only be defended within that division. e. World Tag Team Championship Titles These are what Tag Teams drool over, and fight over too. These belts can only be defended when Tag Teams are present. Notice, I didn't say that a Tag Team from the Tag Team Division could only win these titles. Keep that in mind.
e. Winning Titles In order to win titles, you have to act like a champion. If you don't participate enough to be a Champion, and take part in the CWO, then either you won't get your shot, or you'll be stripped of the title if you already have it. We wouldn't just strip it from you without saying anything, oh no, we'd make you fight a grueling match for it and of course we'd know the outcome.

Hardcore Division President: "The Mastermind" Johnny Holiday (Neutral) Height: 6'11" Weight: 275 Pounds Hair: Black Hair Eyes: Blue Johnny Holiday was born in Hollywood California on June 27, 1971. Not much is known about his parents or his childhood. We do know that he was born into a wealthy family and that Johnny has never had to worry about money. Johnny has been a fan of wrestling since he was about three years old and he watched as Ric Flair winning one of his first NWA World titles. Although Johnny never got involved in sport during his school years, he always kept in shape by working out at one of the local gyms in
Hollywood. After going to school for a degree in business, Johnny decided to stick with his first love, weights, and open the first "Holiday's
Gym" in Hollywood California. Johnny's business took off and over the next several years, Johnny would go on to open four more gyms in the L.A.
area. Although he spent most of his time running his gyms, Johnny always made time for wrestling. Going to see a live event was considered "time off" for Johnny. In 1996, Johnny went to Atlanta to open yet another gym.
While in Atlanta, Johnny met and befriended a wrestler by the name of Vendetta. Vendetta, impressed with what he saw, told Johnny that he would be great in the wrestling business, with the right training. Johnny gave what Vendetta had said some thought, and then decided that he needed to bring some fun into his life, so he called Vendetta and asked him if he was ready to start training him. In mid February, 1996 Johnny Holiday began what he now calls the "roughest days of my life". Vendetta trained a young Johnny Holiday for 9 months. During this period, Johnny was put through some of the most severe training any man has ever had to endure.
Johnny counts that he broke a total of six fingers, one arm, three toes, his nose, and several ribs during his training. Johnny never minded the pain, as he knew that once it was over, he would be ready to take on the world. After his training with Vendetta, Johnny took about two weeks off before he made his pro debut. After about two months in the sport,
Johnny went to the BLWF where he went against his style, and won the BLWF IronMan championship. In less than a month after winning this title, Johnny decided it was time to move on, and he challenged for the BLWF World title. Johnny went down in history as the first and only man to win BLWF World title while still holding the BLWF IronMan title. Three months after winning the BLWF World title, Johnny entered into a tournament for the aWa World title, a title that was being defended in the BLWF. Johnny went through the tournament and into the finals where he battled close friend, The Black Widow, and won the aWa World title, once again going down in history as being the only man to ever hold the BLWF and aWa
World titles at the same time. It wasn't long before the BLWF decided to join up with the aWa and become one. Johnny gave up the aWa World title and took a few months off.
Upon his return to wrestling, the aWa had become the PGW. Johnny decided not to wrestle, but to try his hand as a manager, thus started the "House
Of Holiday". As a manager, Johnny took his "House Of Holiday" to the top of the PGW. At one point Johnny teamed with Rick Rude and Kid Superstar to win the PGW Stables Championship. It wasn't long before he found himself drawn into the ring and in a war with the PGW owner and best friend to the man that trained Johnny. That man was Iron Spike. Spike and Johnny had many wars, and always pushed each other beyond the limit, giving Johnny a new outlook on the "Hardcore" side of the sport. Johnny carries a scar around on the right side of his face from his war with Iron Spike. During this time period Johnny Holiday was managed by a man by the name of Rick
Stewart. Stewart was the main reason Holiday was involved in this fued with Iron Spike. The fued hit it's high point when Stewart signed to meet
Spike in a match of his own, in which there would be no rules. Rick Stewart passed away instantly when he was sent flying off a building and to the ground below. Johnny never really showed much emotion about losing his manager and his fued with Spiek would soon come to an end with and epic battle that saw both men give every bit of energy they had. Johnny learned a lot during his battles with Spike, some say this is where he really learned how to play the mind games the way that he does. After spending about a month in the hospital from his last battle with Iron
Spike, Johnny came back to the PGW/aWa, this time as the owner of the company. Johnny ran the aWa for several months befroe he decided it was time to step back in the ring and do what he does best, break bones and show people that he is the true "Master". At this point, Johnny again went back to manage and lead Kid Superstar to the aWa World title. It wasn't long before Kid Superstar decided he didn't need Johnny Holiday anymore, and went out on his own. Some say this was brought about because the aWa forced Holiday and Kid to do battle for the title. In this classic match, Johnny said he would not even try to win because he wanted to see Kid Superstar make it to the top, and stay there. This quote came from Johnny before the match, "I will not wrestle Dustin tonight.... he is not only my friend, but a man I manage and I will not let the World title come between us." It wasn't long before Kid turned on Johnny and an all out war began. No clear winner ever became of this fued as both men were drawn in other directions.
Johnny went from the aWa to the GCWA. In the GCWA Johnny became the first GCWA Florida Champion before family problems pulled him from the ring one more time, this time for almost one full year. When Johnny returned, he found the GCWA was about to close the doors and so Johnny went on to the GCWO. In the GCWO, Johnny was in deep conflict with the front office, and after watching them use politics as a way of running things, Johnny decided it was time to step back and regain his focus while he searched for the right place to display his skills. Just as the
GCWO offered Johnny title shots, he turned them down and left the sport. Johnny Holiday is now running more than 20 "Holiday's Gym's" around the United States and he has just signed a contract with the NeWF.
