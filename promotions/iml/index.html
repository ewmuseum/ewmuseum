Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

IML never had its own site survive — everything known about it survives secondhand, through the SST RAGE PAGE, a fan page one of its own tag teams kept running long after the fed itself had gone. Its operator's own words, on the page's front door: "I am no longer in the IWA. After the IML closed, it just got boring." What he left behind before pivoting the same page to Madden NFL content is IML's entire surviving record.

That record names an unusually sprawling title structure for a fed this thinly attested — a World-style Champion and TV Champion, a US Champion and National Champion, tag and TV tag belts, a Six Man division with its own TV counterpart, and ten separate geographic singles titles (Atlantic, Central, Dixie, Eastern, Mountain, Northern, Pacific, Prairie, Southern, Western) all current as of one dated snapshot, March 1999. Three wrestlers get a fuller record: the Savage brothers, billed "HotStuff" Craig Savage Jr. and "Hollywood" Christopher Savage, who teamed as SST behind a finisher called the GhettoBlaster, and their partner T.A.F.K.A.C., who also cut the fed's own in-character promo column. Between them they held four of the eighteen belts the snapshot names, and their own bio cards claim a longer history behind several others — a three-time Prairie run for Craig, a three-time Atlantic run for Christopher, a five-time Dixie run for T.A.F.K.A.C., three-time Tag Team, TV Tag Team and Six Man runs for the trio together, and a TV Six Man count the three wrestlers' own bio cards can't agree on — none of it dated or ordered against the snapshot or against each other. Nothing survives to say who won any of them, or what became of the other holders once the page itself went quiet.

Storylines

No story survives to recount Written by AI, 2026

The corpus holds no result, no card, and no match of any kind — only a snapshot of who held what, and each of the three wrestlers' own bio cards. The one narrative-shaped document, T.A.F.K.A.C.'s own promo column, is a single undated prophecy about "three brothers" becoming one and a "murder of Crows" reeking havoc, naming no opponent, no date, and no resolution; the same column's own rhetorical question about the so-called Rough Riders' roster goes unanswered. Neither amounts to a crawled arc a Storylines page could honestly narrate without inventing the connective tissue the record doesn't support.
