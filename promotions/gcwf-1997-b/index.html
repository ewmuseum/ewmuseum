Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Global Championship Wrestling Federation opened between two already-running promotions rather than from nothing: its own founding announcement, posted 22 June 1997, is signed by the owner of the ECWF, explaining that running a federation alone had become a struggle and that he had gone to a friend running the Global Wrestling Association to merge the two into one. It billed itself as a booked league built on roleplay quality over quantity — “Your better off sending 2 good LOng hard worked RP's then sending four crappy Rp's a week” — run out of an arena of the same name in Hartford, Connecticut.

It ran two shows a week from that Hartford arena — a Nitro-styled Monday Extravaganza and a Saturday Night Special billed as “PURELY HARDCORE” — plus a monthly pay-per-view, and staged four championships: a World Heavyweight title, a United States Heavyweight title, a Television title and a World Tag Team title. Its first pay-per-view, announced for the Fleet Center in Boston on 27 July 1997, put the still-separate Global Wrestling Association and ECWF world titles on the line against each other to crown its own first champion: ECWF World Heavyweight Champion “Kold Blood” Kevin Kage against Global Wrestling Association World Heavyweight Champion “Mr. Hall of Fame” Tommy Van Dam.

A promo for a follow-up pay-per-view, WrestleStock '97, staged an even bigger claim: a card run at once from the Fleet Center and from Caesar's Palace in Las Vegas, topped by a rematch between Kevin Kage and Tommy Van Dam for that same championship and an eight-man tournament to crown a new Death Match Champion.

No results document for either pay-per-view survives. The promotion's own last dated attestation is a reader's reply to the Saturday Night Special announcement, posted 29 June 1997, which drifts into an unrelated Sting flame exchange rather than adding anything about the fed itself; how the Boston or WrestleStock cards played out, and how the promotion itself ended, isn't in the surviving record.
