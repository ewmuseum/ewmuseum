A real-wrestler-portrayal tournament federation founded in July 1995 whose membership closed at capacity within three weeks of its own founding ad; it ran weekly brackets and a full pay-per-view through a winter season that reached into 1996. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Ultimate Fantasy Wrestling Federation opened its recruitment 12 July 1995, built entirely on real wrestlers: fans picked four singles wrestlers and a tag team apiece, warned to send only the best — “no stupid one's like Bushwhackers or Repo Man” — with the promotion itself set to start on 5 August, once its own operator returned from vacation.

Membership closed within three weeks, and a 31 July post revealed the completed roster in full, face and heel assignments included, and announced the promotion's own opening title tournaments: sixteen-man brackets for the United States and Intercontinental titles, an eight-team tag tournament, and a qualifying run toward the World title — guaranteeing, in its own words, that no signed singles wrestler would be left out.

By its own pay-per-view Rage of the Cage, broadcast 26 November 1995 from “The Shark Tank” in San Jose, the promotion had crowned real stakes around those belts: Arn Anderson dethroned Paul Orndorff for the United States Championship, ending a three-month reign in what its own announce team called the first title change in the league's history; Owen Hart won the inaugural Junior Heavyweight Championship over Eddy Guerrero; Tommy Dreamer retained the Intercontinental Championship over Terry Funk; and Bret Hart outlasted forty wrestlers across two rings in a battle royal to earn a World Championship shot against Big Van Vader.

Weekly cards continued into 1996 under recurring names like Saturday Night Slam Masters and Wednesday Night Ringside; a 27 January 1996 broadcast described the promotion as running for “over 5 months.” No closure notice survives; its own latest surviving document is dated 21 February 1996.
