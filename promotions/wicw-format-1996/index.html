A September 1996 e-fed whose sole surviving document lays out four singles and two tag titles across a three-cards-a-week schedule plus a monthly pay-per-view, one of two wholly unconnected federations to launch under the identical bare acronym WICW the same week. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World Internet Championship Wrestling introduced itself on 2 September 1996 with a single founding-and-rules post: “I am forming a new e-Wrestling league called World Internet Championship Wrestling, and I need new wrestlers!” That document is the whole of its surviving record — a structure laid out before a single match was ever run.

The plan was ambitious: four singles titles (World Heavyweight, Internet, United States and Cruiserweight) and two tag titles (World Tag Team and World 6-Man Tag Team), defended across three cards a week — Monday Madness!, Wednesday Wars! and Friday Free-For-All! — plus one pay-per-view a month, always the first Sunday. Its own main-event rule tied the cards together: Friday's ten-man battle royal winner challenged for the Internet Title every Monday, and the winner of Wednesday's caged “War of the Week” earned a United States Title shot that Friday. The first pay-per-view, planned for 6 October 1996, was billed to crown almost every title but the Internet belt, headlined by a full Cruiserweight Title tournament.

It asked new wrestlers for a full profile — height, weight, alignment, gimmick, wrestling style, up to ten moves and a finisher with its own setup described — and promised its first card that Friday, “provided we have enough wrestlers,” opening on a battle royal among everyone signed up so far. Whether that card, or any of the titles it planned, ever came to exist isn't in the surviving record — no further document survives beyond this one founding post.
