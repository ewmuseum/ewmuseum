Summit Recruitment: Sim v. Booked?

30 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Let me start out by apologizing for assisting in the glut of "JOIN <enter fed name> NOW!" posts, but I'd like for everyone, even those who have no intention of joining a new fed, to read this, look at the rules and give me feedback, negative or positive. Although the debate finally settled on "To Each His Own", numerous times there was the old "Simmed v. Booked" debate, with both sides arguing their sides. The sides were as follows: Booked: "Why should my detailed, story-driven RP/flash/promo mean ABSOLUTELY NOTHING, and my character wind up losing because some arbitrary program decided so? Quality roleplays and good story creations should dictate the winner of a match." Simmed: "Why should a GM, who has his own ideas on what is a "better" RP/flash/promo, get to decide that my promo is worse than another persons? Quality is subjective, and there should be some neutral means of determining a winner in an e-wrestling match." I'm not arguing for either side, but the people in the Summit (we've been at this since 1991) has decided that really neither side is right or wrong. YES, promos, quality promos are important, and a player's time in developing a good, believable, charismatic character is crucial to a good federation. Without good characters, you cannot have a good federation, no matter how talented the writers are. We also agree that there has to be a neutral decision-making force determing winners and losers. Here is where I believe my federation can strike a common ground, and allow fans of booked and simmed feds to play with equal enjoyment. Premier Wrestling utilizes an actual role-playing system, based on a number of different sources, to determine a winner. After all, e-wrestling is just another form of a role-playing game, like Champions, Shadowrun and Dungeons and Dragons, it should have a system that mirrors a classic RPG. With the system Premier uses, a players gets the following: 1) An opportunity to fully flesh out a character with detailed character creation rules. Make your "submission expert" really MEAN something. 2) An opportunity to have your flashes HAVE A FULL IMPACT on the outcome of your match. Unlike computer simulations, you have FULL control over what your wrestler does, allowing the booked fan to still feel his effort on his promo mean something, AND be able to detail intricate strategies on how your character can win. I would like anyone who is interested, even if they are not interested in joining Premier, to look at the rules and give me an honest evalution on them. We're looking for 4-6 good players to bring some new blood into a group that's been playing for over 10 years. Please, drop me a line if you are interested in playing in Premier, or looking at the system. [detail withheld] Thanks!
