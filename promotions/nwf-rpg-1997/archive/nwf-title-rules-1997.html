NWF: Title Rules

19 August 1997

Reply to: [detail withheld] (``The_Expert``_) R 37 08/19 04:59 WWIVnet ->8213 R 36 08/19 01:56 WWIVnet 8320->8304 R 34 08/19 01:28 WWIVNet ->8320 NWF Titles There are 4 titles in the NWF: The NWF title: This title is defended at least once a month. Always defended at PPV unless there is a specific setup for the PPV. In order to get a shot at the NWF Title you must be ranked in the top 5, (not including the NWF TV Title holder). If the Champion wrestles against someone not ranked in the top five, then it is a non title matchup, unless mandated by the Promoter. However, a win against the Champion will put you into contention. The Champion may choose to defend the title more often than once a month if he likes, or the Promoter may choose to put the title on the line if he sees fit. The NWF TV Title: In the tradition of the Former NWA TV Title, this title is defended at ALL TV Tapings. You must be ranked in the top 10 in order to get a shot at the TV Title. The TV Champion rarely gets NWF Title shots. However, once in a while the Promotor may allow the NWF TV Title holder to get a title shot against the NWF Champion. In that case, the NWF TV Title would most likely not be on the line. However, the Promotor may make the match Title VS Title. In this case, the TV Title would be vacant if a pinfall or submission/tapout occurs in the match. NWF Tag Team Titles: This title has the same rules as the NWF title. This title is defended at least once a month. Always defended at PPV unless there is a specific setup for the PPV. In order to get a shot at the NWF Title you must be ranked in the top 5. If the Champions wrestle against a team not ranked in the top five, then it is a non title matchup, unless mandated by the promoter. However, a win against the champion will put you into contention. The Champion may choose to defend the title more often than once a month if he likes, or the promoter may choose to put the title on the line if he sees fit. NWF International Champion: This title is recognized by some people as the "real world's title." The same rules apply to this belt as the NWF title. This belt was designed to create a feud between the NWF Champion, and the International Champion. Then we can find out who the real NWF champion is. (I.E. WCW International World Title, Ric Flair's "Real World Champion" ploy when he first entered the WWF.) All NWF International World Title matches will be best out of three falls. (This was the practice in the world championship heydays of wrestlers like Lou Thesz and Buddy Rogers.) NOTE: Everytime a title is lost, the former champion(s) will be the first to receive a shot at the new champion, after all previous committments have been reached. Keep in mind that the title could change hands before the rematch has occurred. This is one of the instances where the Promoter can order the champion to put the title on the line.
