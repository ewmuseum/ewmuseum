Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The North American Wrestling Federation opened self-dated 7 August 1997, on the word of a president who introduced himself by his own wrestling history rather than his fed's: he had co-run a prior federation until it closed, then run a second of his own from June 1996 until he closed that one too, on the Tuesday just two days before NAWF's own information sheet went out. He wanted, in his own words, something that was “not a so-called 'loop' fed” and promised “no bias in my decisions.”

Its own plan named a full broadcast staff — two commentators split along face and heel lines, a ring announcer, two interviewers, and a head of officials — before a single wrestler had been signed. It set one card a week on Monday nights, capped recruitment at sixteen singles wrestlers and eight tag teams, and laid out four titles in order of importance: the NAWF Title (never a “World” title by its own choice of name), a TV Title, a Tag Team Title, and a possible Light Heavyweight Title depending on how many smaller wrestlers signed. Rankings were to weigh roleplay, angles and win-loss record together, with main events counting for more than undercard bouts.

Its own record consists of two documents six days apart, the second identical to the first apart from an opening plea — “I *REALLY* need good RPers, as well as tag teams” — noting only one tag team signed so far. No card, roster, or result survives to say whether NAWF's own plan for Monday nights was ever kept.
