Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

New Age American Wrestling billed itself as opened in May 1999 by a wealthy Chicago businessman, John Martin, staging him in its own handbook as having built his fortune from a small inheritance and put it toward “the best action, guarenteed” on his own television channel. Its own three weekly programs — Sunday Sizzler, the flagship; Madness, the midweek highlight show; and NAAW: Uncovered, a preview show — ran toward a World Heavyweight, an American, a Lightheavyweight and a Tag Team title.

By its twelfth update, dated 9 August 1999, its own newsdesk was carrying three live storylines at once: Heavyweight Champion “Firepower” Andy Thomas lost by countout after a mystery man dragged him under the ring mid-match, with no sign of him afterward; President Martin unveiled a new Heritage Title on air only for challenger Chris Gotham to seize it and hit Martin over the head with the belt; and the Lightheavyweight title changed hands mid-brawl when Tommy Brooks ran through the crowd and stole it from around the waist of Ryan Walters, who had just attacked the reigning champion.

The same update names forty-plus wrestlers on its own active roster and records a run of fresh signings — “Legacy” Arthur Gybertson, Brian Storm, Levi Ash, Kenneth Morlock, Mitch Gatlin, “Highlight” Dylan Ryder — while warning established names to perform or be cut.

The same note carries a warning about the promotion's own near future: its own president wrote that a return to school would soon force Madness to be cancelled from time to time and its roleplay shows to run as a plain flash packet, with further hiatuses expected in the months ahead.

How the vanished Heavyweight Champion, the stolen Heritage title, or the missing Lightheavyweight belt were resolved isn't in the surviving record; the last surviving update ends promising updates on all three the following week, with no further document surviving past it.
