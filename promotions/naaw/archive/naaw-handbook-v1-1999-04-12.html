What is the NAAW? -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= NAAW stands for New Age American Wrestling, a fantasy federation based in Chicago, Illionois, where it's headquarters and arena are located. Opened in May, '99 by wealthy buisnessman John Martin, the NAAW is becoming recognized as one of the most sucessful promotions around today. With two live shows a week, on Martin's very own TV channel, the NAAW brings you the best action, guarenteed!
-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= 2. Who runs the NAAW? -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= New Age American Wrestling is run by [name withheld], [name withheld] . As it is [name withheld]'s creation, he gets the final say in all matters, but will often seek advice from a panel of experienced EWrestlers. Martin himself is an experienced EWrestler, and knows what the handlers in the NAAW are looking for.
Always looking for that "extra something" to make the federation more intresting and fun, Martin always tries to put the handlers need before his. However, the NAAW greatly benefits from the handlers who help write matches, develop angles and send in top quality roleplays.
-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= 3. What shows does the NAAW hold? -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= 3.1 - SUNDAY SIZZLER WHEN: SUNDAY, 7-9 p.m EST WHERE: NAAW Arena in Chicago, Illinois HOSTED BY: Craig Brown and Stevie Crawford WHAT: The NAAW's main show of the week, featuring the biggest names, the best matches, the most intresting interviews and the cleverest commentators you can find on a Sunday night. Featuring 6-8 LIVE matches and in-ring interviews, this is what the NAAW centers around.
3.2 - MADNESS WHEN: WEDNESDAY, 7-8 p.m EST WHERE: Various Arenas from throughout America HOSTED BY: Paul Harvey and Fiona Forrest WHAT: The second show of the week, hosted every week. Featuring 1-2 live matches, highlights of 6-8 other matches and comments from all off the NAAW's greatest stars.
3.3 - NAAW: Uncovered WHEN: FRIDAY, 7-8 p.m EST WHERE: NAAW Headquarters in Edmonton, Canada HOSTED BY: Austin McCann and Alan Lawrence WHAT: No matches, and a bi-weekly event, NAAW: Uncovered provides previews of up-coming Sizzler, Pay-Per-View and Madness matches.
Comments will be provided from the majority of NAAW superstars. -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= 4. Who hosts the shows? -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= 4.1 - CRAIG BROWN The senior member of the NAAW's announcing team, Brown signed with the NAAW when it was just beginning. He used to work in the Canadian Wrestling
Organization, but was very impressive working with them, enough so to persuade John Martin to bring him to the NAAW. Brown is a quick-thinking, sensible human-being, and knows just about everything there is to know about the sport.
Not afriad to stand up for his views, Brown is often the joke of his colleagues. In his mid-forties, Brown hosts Sizzler every Sunday night in a calm, proffessional manner. His quick-wit and great knowledge of the sport greatly improves Sizzler, and Brown is known by many as the voice of the NAAW.
4.2 - STEVIE CRAWFORD A veteran, and former proffessional wrestler, Stevie Crawford can always conjur up some nasty words for everybody involved with the NAAW. Hailing from Chicago,
Crawford was raised on the streets, and learned to trust nor respect anyone. Thirty-six years old, Crawford's career was cut short in May, 1990 due to a bizzare back injury. To this day, he is still battling to claim insurance for the injury. The master of one-line insults, Crawford often fails to live up to his insults and threats. Co-host with Craig Brown to Sunday Sizzler, Crawford likes few people, but he a very interesting addition to the broadcast team.
4.3 - PAUL HARVEY Brought to the New Age American Wrestling in January '99 by John Martin, after spending two and a half years working for a large British federation that
Martin owned stakes in, Harvey is another over-critical broadcaster who will never back down from insulting NAAW superstars. Harvey is a tough-talking, quick to the point commentator, with a fine wrestling knowledge. Not known for being popular with fellow workers, Harvey knows his stuff, and can be seen on
NAAW: Uncovered each week. 4.4 - ALAN LAWRENCE Not known for his jokes or as being the most sensible for that matter, Alan Lawrence is often an element on fun from fellow NAAW workers. The host of NAAW: Uncovered, Lawrence often needs reminding about what exactly it is that he's supposed to be doing, and isn't "all there in the head" a lot of the time.
Despite his momentary blunders, Lawrence has a fine wrestling knowledge, and is often the source of valuable information. But, it is for his periodic slips on live television that Lawrence will be remembered, and he often has trouble keeping his fellow broadcasters under control...
4.5 - AUSTIN MCCANN The host of NAAW Madness, Austin McCann came to the NAAW with little knowledge of wrestling, having previously commentated on the NFL season. But, McCann is picking things up quickly, and often gives an intresting in-sight into NAAW matters each and every week. A man who certainly knows what he's doing, McCann is justifying John Martin's high ratings off him, as he keeps things running smoothly and consistently every week. A canny sort of person, McCann is a firm believer of getting his point across as quickly and efficently as possible.
4.6 - FIONA FORREST Cited by many as being a key reason in the extraordinarily high ratings each week for Madness, the beautiful Fiona Forrest is a faviourite in the eyes of many men, young and old alike. The tall, dark-haired gem is a joy to watch for all male fans, who tend to agree with *all* of her opinions, contrary to that of her commentating partners, who are often close to exasparation with the
British beauty. Indeed, it was rumoured Madness co-host Austin McCann asked for Forrest to be fired after a show. John Martin, a "ladies man" himself, took one look at Forrest -- one look at the ratings -- and promptly upped her salary.
4.7 - REFEREES Robert Anelka: The NAAW's Head Referee. About as competent as any other wrestling official, the Frenchman is as strict as they come and will take back-chat from nobody!
Paul Jack: Another of the top NAAW officiating staff, Paul Jack has a reputation for being lenient on the superstars who consistently "bend the rules". Always up for hardcore action, Paul Jack is one of the more popular zebra-striped men with the NAAW's most dangers superstars.
Stephen Mallan: Yet to be seen largely by the federation, Stephen Mallan is a tempremental referee, who cannot be consistently rated as a mean or lean referee.
Owen Coyle: Physically the largest of the NAAW's wrestler, Coyle has something of an attitude:  taking no nonsense from the wrestlers, and is quick to fight back if they lay their hands on him.
Kevin James: Possibly, the world's smallest referee, Scottish referring legend Kevin James makes up for his small frame with his enthusiastic refereeing style in and around the ring. A consistent referee, James is often favoured for
Lightheavyweight matches. Eion Jess: Not used frequently on NAAW television, Jess is not rated as being the meanest referee, and superstars often take advantage of this.
4.8 - OTHER IMPORTANT FIGURES Colin Miller: Possibly the world's faviourite ring announcer, Colin Miller has become a firm favourite with fans the world. Never one to make a mistake, Miller has an exciting style, and is popular with everyone involved with the NAAW.
Chris Hillcoat: The NAAW's vice president/commisioner, Hillcoat is often seen on Sizzler on a Sunday night. Often there to spoil sneak attacks, sanction matches and ruin the fun for all wrestlers, the former proffesional wrestler is not a faviourite of the fans nor of the wrestlers.
Buff "Tough" Stevens: Head of Security in the NAAW Arena. Very often the busiest man in the arena, given the number of brawls, sneak attacks and riots he and his team have to break up at every NAAW event.
The J.O.B Squad: Such names as Gary Teale, the Legend, "Flop" Ted Smith, "Grumbler" Alan Stubbs, the Flying Imbecile's, Nash Robinson and the fan faviourite, Mexican star El
Greco were united by the vice president Chris Hillocat to become accessories the NAAW Security staff at UCWPevents. When things get out of hand, this mini-army, complete with their exclusive "We are THE jobbers" t-shirts are brought down to help sort things out.
Stan "The Man" Collymore: The world's oldest living wrestler from the "good old days", often seen around key events. 4.9 - THE PRESIDENT John Martin: John Martin built his fortune wisely, starting out with only a small amount of cash left to him by his deseased parents. Doing everything very much on his own, and always his way, Martin's shrewd dealings provided him with the cash necessary to create the NAAW. He will never back-down from anyone, and is one of the most confident men around. Always keen to have a female valet by his side, Martin is in good shape, and always on top of the situation.

What titles will I be competing for? -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= 5.1 NAAW World Heavyweight Title Basically, this is what your aiming for. Whoever holds this title basically is seen as the NAAW's number one wrestler. Everyone in NAAW aims for this belt. Defended at every supercard and once every three to four weeks.
5.2 American Title The secondary title and once again, everyone in the federation is eligible to compete for it. It is competed for once per week. 5.3 Lightheavyweight Title For superstars weighing 237 lbs. or under. It is competed for at PPV's and every two to three weeks.
5.4 Tag Team Titles Unsurprisingly, this is for tag teams. Again, it is competed for at PPV's and every two to three weeks.

Pay-Per-Views -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= WHEN: No set Dates HOSTS: Craig Brown, Stevie Crawford and Austin McCann WHERE: Fleet Center, Chicago, Illinois DETAILS: Pay Per Views are NAAW's biggest shows. All the feuds die down, new feuds light up, surprises show up, this is what the NAAW is all about. Currently, there are no confirmed Pay-Per-Views.
-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= 7. How are Matches decided -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= NAAW is a booked league, meaning that all matches are decided by a committee of bookers. Basically, what you do each week in NAAW results in the booking decision for that week. The more you do, the better chance you have to win.
Good flashes are an obvious must. This is the only visible thing to the entire league, and probably bears the most weight of the decision for that week. However, although you may have the better roleplay, you won't win every match. You are allowed a roleplay on
NAAW Madness, NAAW: Uncovered, and pending my approval, a further roleplay on Sizzler itself.
Strategies also come into play. Although one of the least important of the four factors, they could be a tie breaker of some sort. And, you can probably get your character over, or do spots you want to see by sending these in.
Participation is a factor. Angles could be the most important, and may override all other booking decisions. If the bookers have something in mind down the road, or there's an angle which can be furthered through the result of one match, it may go that way.
Always remember that losing doesn't kill you. Also to note, upon entering the league, everybody loses a match or two. We need to find out if you're in it for the long haul, not splitting after one bad thing happens. This way, every wrestler goes through an initiation period, learning the ropes in the league. Slowly, they'll challenge the "old guard" and become one with the league.
To sum up though, participation is a large factor. If someone (to make things easier, lets say James Sandison) has been working hard and well every week, and he comes up against Carnage, who has done nothing, he will win the match, regardless of Carnage's work-rate. This is to ensure a fair push for everyone, but it will require patience and effort from handlers, so be warned.
-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= 8. What can I do to win? -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= 8.1 Flashes Flashes are where you get to speak for your wrestler. They're what the league sees, and how people will react. This pretty much where things happen in how the character grows.
When flashing, use common sense. Don't use excessive profanity, or if you do, [bleep] it out. Don't use other wrestlers without permission. Don't use racial remarks, or anything else which may offend. Use your best judgement when sending things in.
There are only one spot for flashes, one spot for backstage comments, and one in-ring interview on request. NAAW: Uncovered is the main show, where everybody talks about their upcoming match on Sizzler.
The backstage comments are limited to just a few words with a backstage interview. The in-ring interviews are limited to those who the president feels are best suited.
Feel free to use the NAAW fictional staff in your interviews, provided you keep them in character.
NAAW formats all it's shows the same way, trying to keep the flashes in a recognizable format. This is:
[Setting, descriptions, actions here.] JS: "Talk Here." Any descriptions or actions go into square brackets, while any spoken word go in quotation marks. To start a wrestler's speech, use the initials of their name, followed by two spaces, a dash, and two more spaces.
Although we'd like you to help us out and use this format, it's not compulsory. As long as we can read it, it's fine.
8.2 Strats Strats help you guide your wrestler in the actual wrestling ring. You can submit specific moves, sequences, targets, or anything else he might do during the match.
We try and follow all strategies, but it may not be possible all the time based on somebody else's strategy. Keep that in mind.
Again, use your best judgement when strategizing. Obviously you can't strat to beat the other wrestler up with a baseball bat.
Non-match strategies are covered in the next section on angles. 8.3 Angles Possibly the most important aspect of NAAW, creating angles allows you to truly guide your wrestler to greatness.
NAAW always tries to accomodate its handlers by using their angles, keeping it as a handler participation league.
Angles may include post match attacks on another wrestler, turning on another wrestler, doing something during the show, or things like that. Anything which you'd like your wrestler to do on the
Sizzler, but doesn't involve wrestling is classified as an angle. But, angles also includes the long term planning between two wrestlers. More often then not, these involve two or more handlers working on a storyline between their two wrestlers. And, more often then not, these are the most interesting angles, as it has double the brain power working on it. We encourage these types of angles, with proper notice.
Angles allow you to control your wrestler. Make use of them. -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= 9.
