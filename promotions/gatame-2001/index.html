Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

GATAME staged itself as a shoot-fighting company founded jointly by a retired Japanese fighting legend, Kentaro Asashi, and an American businessman, Blake Johnson, who pooled Johnson's financing and Asashi's expertise to build, in the company's own words, “a highly competitive atmosphere in which the world's best true fighters can compete.” Its F.A.Q. of 17 June 2001 declared the promotion open to every discipline — karate, kickboxing, jujitsu, sambo, boxing, wrestling — and closed politics out of it entirely: “GATAME exists as a pure, unfettered battleground for the ‘true fighter.’” Matches ran under real mixed-martial-arts rules: fifteen-minute rounds decided by submission, knockout, or a panel of judges, open-weight until the roster grew large enough for classes.

Its debut card, So It Begins, was booked for the Kirashiki Sanyo Heights Gym in Okayama, Japan on 20 July 2001. The lineup grew across three news updates in June: Joe Jackson against Nobuto Karai, Blake Stevenson against “Black Death” Chon Go, and Olympic taekwondo gold medalist Dave Hopkins against Judoka Masa Ajiima were joined by a late addition, Brazilian jujitsu champion Vitor Renato Gallo against Dutch kickboxer Butan Volk. The main event changed hands once: Igor Mikhail, a Russian sambo veteran, withdrew from his bout against Ricardo Powell over a prior commitment, and Powell's opponent became the “feared fighter” Namor Richallo instead.

Four news posts across ten days in June 2001 are everything that survives of GATAME — its F.A.Q., its opening announcement, and two updates building toward So It Begins. Whether the show itself went ahead on 20 July, and what became of the company afterward, isn't in the surviving record.
