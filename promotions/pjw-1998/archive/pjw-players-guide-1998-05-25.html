"Pro Japan Wrestling Players Guide"

25 May 1998

Pro Japan Wrestling Players Guide 1 - What is Pro Japan Wrestling? Pro Japan Wrestling is an Electronic Wrestling Federation designed to capture the flavor of junior Heavyweight wrestling from Japan. While in the US, Junior heavyweights tend to be used as secondary, mid card wrestlers, in Japan, Junior Heavyweight frequently headline major shows, and in some promotions, such as Michinku Pro Wrestling, the entire talent roster is comprised of wrestlers under 235 pounds. Japanese Junior Heavyweight wrestling, features athletic, competitive match-ups, featuring a variety of ring styles. PJW attempts to capture this exciting and competitive style of wrestling in its approach to booked e-wrestling. if your one of those handlers who expects to come in and become a champion immediately after joining, then PJW isn't the fed for you. Wrestlers in PJW must slowly work there way up the ladder. While in most feds the planning is short term, in PJW its long term. If I haven't stepped on your toes yet, then this may just be the fed for you. 2 - Success Through Dedication Success Through Dedication is the motto of PJW. In other words the more you put into the fed and your character, the more you'll get out of it. If your looking to obtain victory in PJW you must be willing to regularly roleplay, communicate with other handlers, and send in strategies for your matches in order to win. The more thought that goes into the roleplays, strats, and angles you devople/send-in, the more time the match writers and staff will take to make sure that the aspects you want to get across are protrayed in matches. 3 - Above and Beyond Our approach to PJW is that we are looking to go above and beyond the standards set by others. PJW isn't a fed striving to be like another fed, its an e-fed striving to set new standards for e-feds. Put simply, above and beyond is the idea that your wrestler is attempting to what others have never done, in an effort to stand out from the rest, and make it to the top. 4 - Application Analysis When filling out your application to join PJW, please take your time and be as descriptive as possible. Your application is the main piece of information used when writing matches involving your character. Virtually every aspect of the match is taken from your application or strat. Therefore it is VERY important that you take your time when filling out the application. Take special note of each section of the application, to ensure that the match writers are able to fully understand your wrestlers personality and ring style. 5 - Getting Matches The President of PJW is responsible for making the matches on PJWs weekly television shows and occasional tours. Frequently, two handlers challenge each other to a match. If both handlers are in favor of the match, then it will generally be made, and take place as soon as possible. Other times, wrestlers will be paired together due to ranking in the fed, past conflicts (i.e. a time limit draw the previous week, wrestler A pinning wrestler B in a 6 Man Tag Match), and various other factors. 6 - How matches are Determined As touched upon earlier, PJW is a booked e-fed. Winners are choose by the president and his consultants based upon quality of roleplays, strategies, and angles. Often this process is EXTREMELY Difficult. But you'll never hear the staff complaining about having to make difficult decisons, since that is an indication that the league is very competitive. Theres almost a 100% chance that at sometime during your PJW experince, you'll disagree with a decison in the outcome of a match. Reguardless, we hope that no matter what the outcome of your matches, you'll still enjoy PJW. Some other factors which weigh on the booking of a match are injuries and ranking. These will be discussed futher in later sections. 7 - Rules & 6 Man Tag Matches In addition to the normal rules of wrestling promotions around the world, PJW features several modifications. PJW encourages clean, decisive finishes, so countouts can only take place when one man is on the outside of the ring. When both men leave the ring, a countout cannot occur. An important part of the atmosphere of PJW is 6 Man tag matches. In PJW these matches are used to setup fueds, further angles, and add depth to storylines. In 6 Man tag matches several rules you may not be familar with apply: -One member of each team is legal in the ring at one time, however, double and triple teaming is allowed as long as the proper tags are made. The DQ count on tag exchanges is 10, but DQs are rare for infractions of this rule. As with other rules, the strictness of the rules depends on the referee of the match. -Tags between team members aren't necessary if a wrestler leaves the ring for any reason. When this happens either of the other two team members can step into the ring. Aside from in-ring match rules, there are also basic rules of conduct. During match run-ins will only be allowed on rare occasions. PJW wants to provide clean matches with clear winners. If you want to attack another wrestler to start a fued or get a match, its perfectly fine. Pre-match attacks and post-match attacks are allowed and serve the same purpose as a run-in during a match only they don't ruin a good match. 8 - Titles The following championships are up for grabs in Pro Japan Wrestling: 1. The World Junior Heavyweight Championship - The top prize in PJW, and we would argue the PREMIERE Junior Heavyweight, or Cruiserweight championship in all of e-wrestling. The World title is an honor reserved for teh few, the proud, and elite of Pro Japan Wrestling. The title must be defended at least once every month. This title defense will be scheduled by PJW. Additional title defenses can be scheduled by the champion through the PJW President. In the event that a champion cannot (due to injury), or will not, defend the title, the champion will be stripped of the title. 2. The World 6 Man Tag Team Championship - 6 Man tag matches are among the most interesting, and exciting matches found in PJW. In additiion to the use of 6 Man Tag Matches to setup agnles, and fueds, there are also 6 Mn Tag titles up for grabs in PJW. The titles must defended at least 2 times per month,. Failure to do so will result in the champions being stripped of the titles. 3. The International Junior Heavyweight Championship - The secondary singles title in PJW. as in real wrestling, in PJW wrestlers are expected to climb the ladder of success, the INternational title is a step on many wrestlers path to the World Title. The title must be defended at least 2 times each month. Failure to do so will result in the champion being stripped of the title. 9 - Rankings & Title Shots Title shots in PJW are based upon rankings. Rankings are based upon a variety of things including: Winn/Loss record, push (determined by Roleplays & strats), and current angles. After each weeks Sunday Night card, a PJW update, including singles and tag team rankings will be released. Title shots will be based upon these rankings, however, don't forget that title shots can also be granted by the champion to any wrestler. 10 - Cards PJW generally presents at least one card per week. This card is an e-wrestling show, titled "Warrior Spirts". The show features interview segments along with 3-6 matches complete with commentary. In addition to "Warrior Spirts", periodically throughout the year PJw will present house shows as part of 2-3 week tours. cards will take place in venues across Japan including: Yokohama Bunka Gym, Osaka Buen Arena, Nagoya Aiichi Gymnasium, Tokyo Budokan Hall, Kumamoto City Gymnasium, Tokyo Korkuen Hall, Kanagawa-Odawara Arena, Fukui City Gymnasium, Tokyo Sumo Hall, Kawasaki Gym, Tokyo Ota Ward Gym, Tokyo Komazawa Olympic Park Gym, Sapporo Nakajima Sports Complex. Once every 1-3 monthes, PJW will present a supercard from Osaka Dome, Kawasaki Stadium, or the Tokyo Dome. While all these shows are of great importance, Tokyo Dome shows are the most important cards held in PJW. Tokyo Dome shows are reseved for major events. Generally all major super cards will be headlined by a World Title defense, but a World Title defenseALWAYS occurs on the Tokyo Dome shows. The defense will also most likely be one that is very antisicpated. 11 - Roleplaying Once or twice per week PJW will release a roleplay packet to members of the league mailing list. This packet will contain roleplays for wrestlers. Roleplays should be quality flashes and not just simple one paragraph statements consisting of 5 lines of dialogue. All talent patisipating on upcoming cards are expected to roleplay. Roleplays are judged on quality and not quanity. Therefore take your time and produce at least one quality roleplay each week. Theres no need to rush out two roleplays are ramble on without purpose just to put out a long roleplay. If you have been scheduled for a match and are unable to roleplay due to time constraints, please inform the President so that any needed adjustments or changes can be made. In some cases handlers will be asked to submit interview segments to air on "Warrior Spirts". Handlers may also request interview segments on cards by contacting the president. These interviews may be set "in-ring" or "recorded" in another setting. There are several rules for roleplaying that should be observed: 1)Stay in character, no straight out shooting. If you want to make some masked shoot comments and they aren't to vulgar, they'll be allowed, but otherwise, shooting or breaking kayfabe is a no,no. Some handlers that do it call it getting heat. Well I'm not sure thats what I'd call it when you insult handlers and tlak about there Rping abilities, if it is heat, its cheap heat, and its not allowed in PJW. 2)No extensive references to wrestlers from the world of real wrestling. This is e-wrestling, and if you buy into the storyline, WWF, WCW, NJPW, AJPW, M-Pro, etc. don't exist. I'll allow passing comments, but once again, its preferable that they be masked comments. Once again, this is yet another tactic that I consider to be "cheap heat". 3)Under no circumstances will comments about other hanlders be allowed. He you want to take shots at the characters in the fed thats fine, but nothing that could be taken as a shot at the handler of that character. PJW is my hobby. I want my hobby to be fun for all involved. That can't happen if handlers are constantly at odds with one another. Other handlers are your friends. I guarentee you'll get alot further in PJW by cooperating with other handlers, as opposed to fueding with them. 12 - Staff of PJW PJW has assembled a fine collection of officals, annoucers, and referees. Hadi Moditian - The acting President of Pro Japan Wrestling. Moditian is 41 years old, and a was highly respected, vetran wrestling annoucer before purchasing PJW from its founder, Yoshi Tashiryu. After 21 years of broadcasting, Moditian still has an urge to do commentary, and occasionally does so on major shows, but otherwise attempts to stay out of the lime light. Moditian, however, is not as passive when it comes to those who disrpect himself, or his staff. While he tries to weild his power behind the scenes, hes not afraid to lay toe law down in front of everyone. Christopher Reece - The "Voice of the PJW." Reece (28 years old) was Moditian's announcing understudy for 5 years before stepping up and taking over behind the mic when Moditian purchased the company. Reece followed Japanese wrestling for many years in America via Japanese video stores. During his college days his took a forgien study course in japanese and got the chance to live in Japan for one year. During that year he met and became friends with Moditian, who took him under his wing, after reconizing his true understanding of the sport. Reece is also very important to PJW, since he also serves as an international talent scout. He calls it down the middle generally, but when push comes to shove, he favors the faces. Ricki Togo - "The Revoluntionary Force of Junior Heavyweight Wrestling." Ricki Togo was one of the top stars of Junior Heavyweight wrestling durings its birth, and evolution into the current style. Moditian and Togo became friends while working together in Ultimate Japan Pro Wrestling. Due to his tremendous knowledge or the sport, and the insight he adds to matches, he was hired by Moditian as a color commentator. While his job is to add insight to the match, hes often tempted to step on Reece's toes and call the match, if he gets into it. Kyoko Kosugi - Kyoko is a 26 year old woman, who before becoming PJW magazine editor, hotline host, and in ring interviewer, was a journalist for the Tokyo Sun Magazine. Shes very good looking and she often uses her ASSets to get the dirt in the lockerroom. Shes rumored to be romantically involved with PJW Superstar Kashan Akuma, so never expect to hear anything but praise for her man. 13- Injuries Injuries play a major part in real wrestling promotions and they are no different here in PJW. Injuries are determined by the president, based on the brutality of matches. For instance if your wrestler is powerbombed through a table, don't expect him to be injury free for the next week or so. When a wrestler is injured it will be posted in the weekly update under a section titled injuries. The section will include wrestler injured, the type of injury, and an overall health ratings from 0-100 %. This is an aspect of the game that more of a learn as you go type of deal. 14 - Application & the Devoplement of a new star Well now that you've read the guide, and your ready to fill out the form, there are few more things you need to know. Bringing a new character into PJW is different from amny other feds. In order to ensure quality, instead of quanity, PJW normally maintains only 18-22 wrestlers on its roster at any given time. When you send your application to the President, you'll recieve a reply that indicated you are either accepted, or not accepted. If your accepted, normally you won't begin in PJW competition at that time. Instead you'll join the PJW "Dream Factory." The Dream Factory is PJWs training school. Ok, basically its our waiting list, but it is a little different, and you have to admit it sounds a whole lot better :-) Members of the Dream Factory are the next in line to move up and take over rost spots as they become available. Also Dream Factory members will, form time to time, recieve, "try-out" matches, in which they'll roleplay and send in strats for. Terrific preformances in tryouts can result in advancement into the league. Also, I'm hopeful that at least one a month a Dream Factory card can be produced featuring all the members of the factory. I'm also open to the idea of someone taking the Dream factory over and running it as a sperate, farm system type league to PJW, with regular cards.
