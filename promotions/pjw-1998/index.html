Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Pro Japan Wrestling announced its own arrival with a single line: “On March 15, at the Tokyo Dome, Pro Japan Wrestling will make its debut.” It built itself specifically around the junior heavyweight style, its own players guide arguing that where American feds buried lighter wrestlers on the undercard, “in Japan, Junior Heavyweight frequently headline major shows” — and adopting “Success Through Dedication” as its motto, with three championships (World Junior Heavyweight, International Junior Heavyweight, and a World 6-Man Tag title) built around long-term climbs rather than quick pushes.

Its January 1999 supercard, Senshi no Tamashii, carried a title feud into its most vicious register: challenger Ace Kilcannon opened one broadcast holding up a mask torn from a beaten Rupan Loco as a trophy, promising the reigning champion, “Dean,” the same treatment. Ed Dean held onto the All-Asian championship a card later by refusing to release a submission hold on Jet Jaguar II even after the referee ordered him to, drawing a disqualification that let him keep the title on a technicality; a returning Tiger Kikuchi, coming out of retirement, called him out for a title match on the promotion's next card, Zenmen Sensou.

The promotion closed at some point after that, and its own self-billed promoter, Hadi Moditian, spent the gap, in his own words, trying “several other aspects of pro wrestling, from managing to promoting the strong style” elsewhere.

He reopened the office on 25 April 1999 with a return card, Phoenix, broadcast live from Tokyo's Korkuen Hall on TV-Tokyo's Samurai TV: “Experience Excellence... AGAIN!” A four-team tournament for the All-Asian 6-Man Tag titles opened the new run, with Zokugun Sangai and the trio of Shoji Suzuki, Toru Nakano and Mascara del Muerte both advancing out of the first round.

No closing notice of that second run survives; what became of Pro Japan Wrestling after its own return card isn't known from what remains.
