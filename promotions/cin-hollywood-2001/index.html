Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

CIN opened in the late spring of 2001 as a Hollywood-based booked league that explained its three letters as short for cinema rather than as initials. Its fiction began with a death: Jefferson Lyons, owner of a make-believe predecessor called Cin-City Wrestling, was found murdered, and his will left the company to its last champion, Miles Gloriosus, who handed its running to a struggling Italian film director, Dean Cicerone, and went looking for the killers.

It introduced itself through a founding FAQ that named a full front office, five referees and an announce team before a single wrestler had signed, then kept its roster informed through Sneak Preview newsletters of news, rumours and rankings and Director's Cut packets gathering its wrestlers' roleplays. Its matches were booked, not simulated, and weighed roleplays, on-card segments, angles and a required ring entrance.

Its opening event, Summer Blockbuster 01': “Premiere,” was staged as a charity show at the Los Angeles Theater Center on 30 June 2001: a sixteen-man, one-night tournament for the vacant Heavyweight Title ending in a ladder match. Mac Rollins, a former Chicago policeman carrying the guilt of a suicide he had failed to stop, won it over The Boy Hedges, and Juan Vasquez and Tripp Shade attacked both finalists once the belt was won.

The card itself was published on the promotion's website rather than to the newsgroup and does not survive. What does is the build around it and the fallout: a fifteen-man battle royal for the Golden State Heritage Title set for X-Rated at the Santa Monica City College gymnasium on 13 July 2001, a cloaked figure striking the president with a package as body parts turned up around the building, and the Lyons family's fight in court to take the company back. The last surviving document is the roleplay packet for that card, posted the day after its billed date; how the promotion ended isn't in the surviving record.
