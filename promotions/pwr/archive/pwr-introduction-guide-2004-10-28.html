Testing the Waters for a Tag Division

Oct 28, 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Soooooo... as all feds do once they get a few shows under their belt, Pro Wrestling Revolution is looking at adding a title. Obviously, the second most used title would be a tag team title. I have reluctance to do a tag division because a] people hate writing tag matches, b] people think tag titles are worthless, c] tag divisions are hard to keep strong, d]... well, hell... there's a lot more. But I'm considering it anyways. With one rule. No "regular" tag teams. I don't want teams that will only wrestle tag matches. If you're going to have a tag team, it needs to consist of guys who can wrestle singles matches too [see: The Briscoes or X-Foundation or Super Dragon/Excalibur or the Pitbulls... come on, guys... feel the indy love]. And... I'd really, really, really prefer to see two handler teams for the most part just to keep things lively. So, if this sounds like something you might be interested in participating in... I'll paste the PWR Info Guide at the bottom of this post. If it sounds interesting to you, shoot me an e-mail and we'll chat! Side note... even if you don't want to be in PWR full time and you think it'd be cool to bring back your old team or put together a "super team" for one tournament, drop me a note about that too. I'm open to suggestions. [detail withheld] is still the addy of choice... and my spam filter is very sensitive so make sure you write PWR in the subject header so I catch it. DAYS, people! ********** Pro Wrestling Revolution - An Introduction Guide v 1.0 February 5, 2004 Pro Wrestling Revolution's Mission Statement ************************************** Pro Wrestling Revolution will be exactly what the name states. Our goal is to do away with the mediocrity that plagues professional wrestling. We will not rely on legends of the past who are far past their prime. We will not cling to the dying trend of "extreme" wrestling. And we will not attempt to hold onto fading glory by bringing back a historic promotion for "one more run". PWR will be a wrestling revolution. A promotion based on building new stars and celebrating new rivalries while still paying respect to those that came before us. It will be a blend of styles from Europe, Asia, and North American. The worlds of puroresu and lucha libre... of old school and new breed... of tradition and innovation will collide in spectacular fashion. From the ashes of the once-great promotion before it, the Revolution shall rise. A new beginning to an era where most assuredly anything can... and will... happen. -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- A Statement Out Of Character ************************* My name is [name withheld] and I ran the best fed you'll ever read which is a lot like running the best fed now except there was competition [Copyright [name withheld]] Basically, I ran a great fed for a long, long time. I've run some characters with less than impressive results. Now you know all you need to know about me. The reason you're all still reading this is to find out a little bit about what I plan to do and why I plan to do it. With the close of the EMWC and the subsuquent failure of a final show for it, I put myself out of e-wrestling completely. I had no desire to do anything e-w related... least of all run a fed. As of late, I've re-emerged from my hiatus and took on handling and match writing duties in a few feds. The itch is back, my friends, and I believe it's time to use a fed idea that I've wanted to do for several years. A lot of people will wonder why I don't just reopen EMWC if I want to do this again and it's really a valid question. There's a few reasons why actually... but here's the main two. One, I want to do something different. Everyone and their mother is reopening their old fed... some of which I'm very enthused about... but it's not 1999 as much as it seems like it and everyone opening their old fed isn't the answer. And two, that's just too easy. It would be easy to throw the EMWC name back up in the air and bask in all the accolades from the past. But that's not what I want to do. I always said the fun part of the EMWC was getting to the top... the hard part was staying there. After nearly ten years, it's damn hard to keep yourself going when you're dealing with the same ideas, same people, same characters, etc. There was no room for reinvention so things got stale. I want to do something new... different... fun. Something where I don't have to worry about "tarnishing" the E's reputation and history. Something where I can experiment and try different ideas out. Something a little bit different than anyone else has done. Most of all, I want the challenge of starting from scratch with brand new characters, brand new announcers, brand new everything... something completely unknown to e-w... and make that brand new fed something that a bunch of people enjoy. This isn't about pressure or expectations or... whatever else. This is about having fun, doing something different, and seeing if lightning can strike twice. And with that... I give you the prototypical FAQ you've all been waiting for. -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Pro Wrestling Revolution FAQ 1. What is Pro Wrestling Revolution? The History ========= As 2004 began, Todd Michaelson found himself in an unfamiliar position... he was unemployed. After nearly ten years of continuous employ with the Los Angeles based Empire Wrestling Council (EMWC), Michaelson was out of wrestling completely. Faced with that future, he has made plans to re-enter the business he has spent his entire adult life in. In late 2003, with big dreams, he first looked to Japan, his home away from home, in hopes that he could start up a small promotion there. Memories of epic promotions like G-Pro danced in his mind, causing him to focus on bringing forth a company that centered on athleticism and skill rather than the soap opera stories and cartoonish gimmicks that plagued wrestling in the United States. Dreams of J*STAR finally coming to pass were on his mind but ultimately he determined startup costs would be too high and returned to the States without a new venture. Michaelson returned from Japan very depressed. He was in a deep state of sadness, to the point where even his new wife was unable to shake him out of it. With some help from his closest friends, she was finally able to get Todd back into action with the opening of the M-Dojo training school where he could pass on his knowledge to a new generation of grapplers. Reinvigorated by his students' enthusiasm but frustrated by his inability to find work for them, Todd begin to toy with the idea of opening a small promotion in his home region, Southern California. The Concept ========== Michaelson realized that for a SoCal based indy promotion to work, he needed to think and stay small. He developed a business plan to use a small core of local workers along with his students to hopefully draw a small but loyal fanbase. He believed that the key to staying afloat with a small live draw would be the sale of video tapes and DVDs nationwide. To accomplish that, Todd needed two things: 1] A positive buzz based on the ringwork of the workers; 2] A little help from his friends - namely those in the industry with the name value to move some merchandise. The Goals ======== As Michaelson and his students got together one week to watch tapes of the major promotions, they were sickened by what they saw. Promotions not fit to shine the boots of the truly historic companies that came before them were being hailed as the top dogs in the industry. The match quality was abysmal, workrate was down across the board. It made Todd disgusted to see his former colleagues sully themselves in promotions not worthy of their talent. When he announced his plans to start a promotion to his students, Todd promised it would be different than the rest, it would be better. He promised an evolution of the sport to the next level. He promised a revolution, the toppling of the tired, cliched formulas that the sport has clung to for decades. And with that... Pro Wrestling Revolution was born. 2. Who runs this thing? Reality: Me. [name withheld]. The guy I talked about above. In the e-world: Todd Michaelson, former EMWC World Champion, former color commentator, former multiple time Announcer of the Year. He's the boss in this show, baby. 3. So, Pro Wreslting Revolution is an "indy"? What does that mean to me as a handler?         In e-wrestling, there's always been indy feds. Those that ran in tiny arenas yet still had huge pyro displays and the global fed's World champion headlining their shows. It's always bugged me and it's the antithesis of what I want PWR to be.         PWR is an indy... and I want it to be an indy in every way. Here's some of the things you need to know about the "indyness" of PWR.         --- We're looking for new names, not old favs. While from time to time I expect to have some of your old favorites on the PWR shows, stacking the roster is not the goal and that's not the intention. I plan on having a roster of mostly unknown characters, brand new characters preferably.         --- The fed is based in Southern California. This means that your character stands a better chance of being accepted if he's local. California, Arizona, Nevada, Mexico, whatever. People from out of the area means being flown in by PWR which brings us to...         --- Fly-ins. They're not unheard of on the indy scene, they won't be unheard of in PWR. Every show will have a couple characters flown in that will spike the crowd/sales. Bigger shows will have more.         --- Once a wrestler makes the "big time", he leaves the indies. While I'd appreciate it if PWR is the one and only home for your character [especially those on the "core roster"], I understand the desire to want to handle in more than one place. Here's my one limitation: If you have a character in other indies, that's fine. But if your PWR character is suddenly on the roster of a fed that runs nationwide in stadiums, he's out of PWR. No exceptions.         --- In character, this promotion was started as a way to get Todd's students some work. It's created to support M-Dojo. Thus, every six months [or so] I'll be creating a handful of M-Dojo graduates ready for the next step. They'll be offered up to the PWR handlers [or handlers wanting to join] to take over handling duties on them. This'll probably also require "trimming" the roster every six months or so to keep it at a manageable level. If you take a M-Dojo student to handle, I only have one rule. If you quit PWR before the next crop of students enters the promotion, you forfeit all your rights to the character and he stays in PWR with another handler. [Yes, I know I have no way to enforce this but I'll hope that by joining the fed you agree to this]. 4. How will winners/losers be decided in PWR?         In a switch from the strongly angle-based booking of my former fed, PWR will focus on roleplays. There'll still be angles in PWR and they will still be important. But when it comes time to picking a winner or loser, they'll take second place in making that decision. 5. What shows will PWR have?         At the outset, PWR will have one "arena" show per month that will be named something different each time. The future may see more shows added as PWR grows and this concept evolves, but for now, one show of matches.         The lineup for the next month's show will come out a day or two after a show is released. Roleplays will be put out once a week [sometimes in packet form, sometimes in "show" form] under the guise of an "internet broadcast" that PWR puts out.         The lack of shows means that getting your angles over in more creative ways will be key. There won't be the chance for trading sneak attacks and the usual forms of building heat in e-w. You'll need to think on a different level to get your feuds over in PWR. 6. What titles will PWR have?         To start with, we will only have the PWR Title [name subject to change]. There will be no heavyweight/cruiserweight split for different titles. All weights will compete in the same division. If the interest is strong in the future, more titles [tag, trios, etc.] could be added. 7. Who are the faces in PWR?         Well, Todd Michaelson's the owner. The rest are still to be determined as this is... like I said... a work in progress. 8. Great! How do I apply?
