Castor Strife: "I'm coming to raze the village."

23 August 2013
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
In this NFW.com exclusive, Devin Millwood sits down with former NFW World Heavyweight Champion Castor Strife to talk about Stryfe, JJ, the World Title, and more! Mystique. Controversy. Starpower. Deception. Blood. These five words best summarize the theatre of absurdity, the Big Top atmosphere that NFW’s Castor Strife has fostered and immersed himself in since the day his career started. But over the last year, the proverbial dial has been turned way up - from winning the 2012 ULTRATITLE trophy in a field of 120 competitors, to losing the NFW World Heavyweight Championship by falling into a pit of broken glass (“Fall From Grace”), to co-promoting his own main event match where he emerged victorious against Dan Ryan before a record crowd in Jerry World, Dallas, and engaging in protracted contract talks with NFW before coming away with a record contract. That is to say nothing of his backstage feud with JJ Deville, and the fact that a mysterious doppelganger calling himself “Stryfe” has arrived on the scene with a possible connection to Alex Austin, the protégé who Strife himself trained. Despite his stranglehold on the rumors and headlines of the day, the former NFW World Champion has remained relatively silent in the press. My own relationship with Strife stretches back to the early days of NFW 1.0, when he was a regular interview of mine as correspondent for LegDrop.com. Recently I caught up with Strife backstage at a UWA show in Philly, where he acquiesced to granting me this exclusive NFW.com interview on every subject, from JJ, the mysterious “Stryfe,” Alex Austin, and current events in the New Frontier. DM: Hey, did Avery [Prosser] put you up to wearing that? CS: This? [tugs on the bottom of his UWA “Seven Deadly Sins” t-shirt] No, I proudly own it. He wanted to audition me as the eighth Deadly Sin, but unfortunately God only made seven. [smiles] DM: There was strong talk about you leaving NFW as recently as last month, due to a breakdown in contract negotiations. Obviously we have seen you show up in other places, including non-NFW ESEN programming, but how close were you to leaving NFW altogether? CS: That is irrelevant, and I never comment publicly about matters best left private. In the end, they showed me the respect I felt I earned, and moving forward NFW will continue to be my primary focus. DM: Does respect equal money? I don’t have figures, but was told you have a record contract for a professional wrestler. CS: Again, I won’t discuss a private matter, but as far as respect and money goes...I think there is something to that. But it’s not the purchasing power of the dollar that gives one respect. Value translates into respect, and money translates to value. The dollar figure they place on me represents my value, and I make no qualms about the fact that I am the most valuable name in wrestling today. Independent research can back that up all day, every day. That’s why, ultimately, Eddie signed on the dotted line. DM: Is [President of ESEN] Quentin Sullivan your agent? CS: Quentin is a friend. [smiles] DM: Let’s shift gears and talk about Stryfe with a ‘Y’. How did this all come about, and are you any closer to discovering his identity? CS: You talk as if this is Hound of the fucking Baskervilles, and I’m on the case. I am zero percent concerned with fake me. I feel like the Freeway Rick of NFW. DM: The fake rapper version of Rick Ross? I think he got sued and goes by Rick Ro-Zay now. CS: Oh, I know the real Rick Ross, we did a radio show together once and he filled me in. I have my own fake Rick Ross. That’s all Stryfe is - a Chinese handbag that’s caught the eye of a clueless tourist. Don’t be fooled, don’t be intrigued. I have double-checked and triple-checked all possible sources, and I can verify that this man, whoever he is, is nobody. What I’m interested in knowing, is who is behind the distraction? DM: You seemed to indicate Alex Austin in your last television appearance. CS: Alex is involved somehow, and he is going to come clean at Reloaded 18. That’s all I am going to say about that. DM: Can we look forward to a Strife versus Stryfe match? CS: No, and why would you want to watch me fight a certifiable nobody? I just snapped back JJ Deville’s neck like a fucking Pez dispenser. Talk about that, the fact that I’m on the verge of annihilating him and The Hellfire Club. DM: What’s your source of hatred for JJ? This has been building for quite some time, hasn’t it? CS: I don’t hate JJ, I just want him gone. He’s bad for business. He eats up considerable time on each show with petulant bullshit that never goes anywhere, never really benefitting himself so much as driving down the value of the product. If you want to be the star, if you want to be the “face,” then act like it. Get in the ring with the man you built your name off of, and prove that you are what you claim to be. DM: Who is that? Troy Windham? CS: Hell no. He built his name off of me! You think anybody gave a shit about JJ Deville before Supercrash, when he pinned me in All The President’s Men? It’s not a fact I’m proud of, but it happened. I extinguished Troy Windham, damn near broke his neck, and JJ caught me by surprise from behind to win the match. Do I think it was representative of his talent level compared to my own? Not at all. But that didn’t stop him from taking full credit for it. DM: So JJ isn’t on your level? CS: What has he done to prove it? He had the opportunity to face me, and declined. JJ Deville versus Castor Strife for the World Title was the biggest match in NFW history, potentially, and he flushed it all away to play Sharks and Jets with his loser friends. Anybody who turns down the opportunity to vie for the big title because he is more comfortable playing on a smaller level, will never, ever have my respect. It is the ultimate mark of a coward. With the exception of maybe Dorchester, that is what the Hellfire Club consists of. Now, you want to play that game when nobody is watching? Fine. But once you start demanding, and receiving, significant television time, that’s when you have a problem with me. DM: What is your ultimate assessment of JJ, then? CS: Talented guy who is scared of a real challenge, because he’s content to let people wonder about how good he might be instead of stepping up to give them a final answer. I think if he ever wrestled me, he would be shocked as to how much more work he needs to put in before reaching the highest level. Nobody outworks me, except maybe Impulse, who works harder but not smarter than me. DM: Speaking of Impulse, he is the current World Champion. CS: Correct. DM: How does Castor Strife fit into the NFW World Title scene in 2013 and beyond? CS: Right at the center, because I cast a large shadow over anybody who claims to be the very best. DM: So you will be chasing the belt for a second time? CS: Why not? I’m the number one wrestler in the world. Are they content holding a belt while being only number two? DM: Impulse doesn’t agree with that. He just characterized his upcoming title match with Jack Harmen and Jack Bryant as a contest between the three best pure wrestlers in the world. CS: Right! And he called me the best “entertainer”. That gave me a nice laugh. DM: How do you respond to that? CS: As I recall, Impulse had a match with me where we were both under threat of expulsion if the result wasn’t clean. That’s when I beat him, and that made me the best “entertainer”. Over a year later, he throws me off a bridge, and that makes him the best “pure wrestler” on the planet. Jack Bryant loses to the TV champion, and that makes him maybe the second best wrestler on the planet. Jack Harmen loses clean to Impulse’s 40-year old mentor at the Ultratitle, which I won clean, and that makes him maybe the third best wrestler. Clearly, Impulse is using some kind of advanced metric to figure all this out. DM: I take it you vehemently disagree with his assessment? CS: All I will say is that Impulse is an extremely hard worker who nobody will ever confuse for a rocket scientist. DM: What are your thoughts on Impulse versus Jack Bryant versus Jack Harmen? CS: I think it is number two versus number three versus number four. I think throwing me off a scaffold made Impulse the rightful champion. I think the rightful champion is also, rightfully, the second best wrestler in the world. DM: Didn’t people say that about you when Joe The Plumber was stripped of the title? CS: Yes, and they were right...for a little while. I tried on multiple occasions to drag Joe back into the ring. Hell, the ESEN superfight in Jerry World idea was originally going to be Joe versus me. Then I made the mistake of convincing Joe to join Ultratitle. He told me, “See you in the finals.” I held up my end of the bargain, but unfortunately, Joe lost to a school janitor in the first round. Now he’s worth zero. But truthfully, Joe’s claim to being the best was in doubt long before that, because he gave up the title and did not commit to keeping himself relevant. On the other hand, I won the biggest tournament in history while Impulse and Jack Bryant opted to stay home. I put to rest all questions about Dan Ryan a few months later. Nobody has defeated me in singles competition on ESEN programming in close to ten years. A lot of things can make you the champion, but only one thing makes you the very best. Ninety percent of the time, the champion is the best, but not all the time. DM: What is your prediction for that match? CS: I expect Impulse to win. I expect to face Impulse and win back my title next year. Clean. [Smiles] DM: Is there any worry in your mind that this Stryfe with ‘Y’ situation could impact your title chase? CS: Why do you insist on talking about this man? He is nothing to me. He is no threat to what I’ve built - to what I continue to build. The Castor Strife Empire suffered a minor setback on a tiny island made of broken glass. I have lost no ground, and am coming back for my title with twice the number of ships, cannons, guns, and mustard gas. Impulse, Harmen, JB, they can fight all they want over which one of them is Chieftain, but I’m coming to raze the village. Period. No impostor in a gas mask can stop me. JJ can’t stop me. Alex Austin had better get back on the right side and quick, because Devin... DM: Yeah? CS: There is nobody in this industry who can dream of stopping me. Nobody and nothing. Copyright 2013 NFW
