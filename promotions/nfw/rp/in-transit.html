In Transit

1 Mar 2012
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I drug my sorry ass across the tarmac twenty minutes later. Angus flanked me to the right, strolling along like a jackass in the wheelchair that he’d taken up ever since Jack Harmen kicked his sh*t in with a baseball bat last month, and Kelly took up the left, her best angry-face on because she was mad about my little bender. That whole character research line wasn’t working on her. My, my, were we an odd looking trio. I sat my bag in Angus’s lap as we strolled up to our private jet, courtesy of one Mr. Castor V. Strife, esquire, and we all stopped in our tracks and stared at the gaudy thing for a good half minute. “Are you f*ckin’ kidding me?” The Bombardiar Learjet 85 that we’d come face to face with definately belonged to the World Champion. The sides were covered in murals, the one facing me was Castor at the center of The Last Supper, and the nose of the jet was stylized into the shape of a gasmask. Seriously. The sheer ridiculousness of it all blunted out the obvious aerodynamic and logistical questions and the three of us just stood there, well, Angus sat, but I don’t think any of us had yet to really understand the extent of the peculiarity of Castor Strife, but this was a really big, blazing red flag. “I’m not getting on that thing,” I said. Angus agreed. “Me the f*ck either.” Kelly’s eyes widened in over-worked rage, then narrowed in that way that men who had been under the thumb of a woman for long enough knew meant that they were about to get flayed alive if they didn’t get their sh*t together. Deflated, I dropped all pretense of having any say so in the matter and boarded the plane. Time lapse... We’d been in the air less than twenty minutes and I’d already skimmed the footage from BRAWL in Detroit on the fifty-five inch flat-screen that had been installed in the stead of two of the front seats on the jet. Castor really did have the best of everything. Me, I’d never really been one to fly from show to show, I preferred the road honestly, but one uses what one has at his disposal when the opportunity presented itself. “Hrmph.” I sank into the leather seat. “Absolutely nothing that pertains to me.” “Yeah, and nothing pertaining to the HEINOUS attack that Fly Higher perpetrated on me with a bat at SuperCrash!” Angus wasn’t going to let this one go. He’s somehow got it set in his mind that he could take Harmen in a fair fight. “Nevermind that,” Kelly chimed in. “You really need to be aware of what’s going on around you if you think you’re gonna run through this place and take the belt from Castor.” My eyes rolled. “Whatever.” … They went from rolling to crossed as Kelly slapped the sh*t out of me. “Don’t you roll your f*cking eyes at me, Eric. I’ve worked too long and too hard to let you get lazy just because you’ve gotten over on everybody you’ve come across so far!” She wasn’t screaming, but kind of whispering in that way that makes your short-hairs twist. “Now I don’t give a sh*t how you’ve got to do it, but you’ve got until we step off of this jet in Buffalo to get your act together and get back on the offensive. Strife might be down, but he’s sure as sh*t not out, and he’s not gonna stay down for long!” “Fine.” I sighed. “What would you have me do?” “How about get back to wrestling on every show you can get booked on, continue beating everyone that the NFW throws in your way, and maybe even get back to pushing buttons and pissing people off!” She nodded. “Wait a minute...” Something was fishy. “Are you cutting a promo on me?” She nodded toward the front of the cabin, above the HDTV, to the mini-cam that I hadn’t noticed before. “Gotta get your face back on TV any way I can.” I nodded. “Besides, Castor already had the equipment built into the jet because he’s some kind of psychotic about his filming, figured we’d get some use out of it. Hell, that whole mansion and the entire studio complex is wired up, if I wanted to I could show you every little thing you’ve done over the past month...” I could feel my cheeks reddening. “How about maybe let’s not do that.” I changed the subject back to wrestling as fast as I could. “I can just go ahead and cut a promo and you can get it edited and on the air? From the friggin’ stratosphere? “Yup.” I cocked my head, color me impressed. Well, it had been several weeks, and never let it be said that Eric Dane isn’t a man who lives to be in front of the camera. I poured myself a drink from a small refrigerator and contemplated for a few moments just exactly what I had to say to the New Frontier.
