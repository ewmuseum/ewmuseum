White Noise

28 Feb 2012
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
White noise. It seared my brain, stabbing like microscopic rays of white hot laser lights. I had no concept of time or location, only the raging, throbbing, explosion in my head. With every bit of effort I could muster I pried open my eyes only to see more white noise. More white hot lasers. “F*ckin’ Christ, Eric, it’s been more than a month...” Her voice was like an anchor, tethering me to reality. Little by little things came back to me. My first face-to-face with Castor Strife had left me more than a little salty. Here I’d thought that he might just be the only man in the New Frontier who truly gets it, and then it turned out he was just another scheister, playing the long con. Steamrolling Alex and Lana into coughing up Castor Strife Productions was a means to an end. This old boys club of a New Frontier wasn’t about to let me, some outsider with a rap-sheet as long as his resume and an ego with the gravity to steal heat from the likes of Strife, Dan Ryan, and D. Ed Mayfield himself just waltz in and start getting decent matches. Oh no, Eric Dane was going to be making his bones against the likes of Barry Lawler and P. King Duk and Shawn Jessica Hart. Oh, wait, that last one supposedly is actually a big deal. The point is that making it to the top of the perennial warzone that is the NFW was always going to take more than a clean bill of health and a string of meaningless wins. That’s what guys like Impulse was never going to get about the wrestling business. It would take a tactical assault on all fronts of the Frontier to even get noticed. It would take declaring War on every entity. Ask Nova. Ask Troy Windham. Ask Calvin Carlton. Ask Castor V. Strife. These are men who understand, though maybe not to a thorough enough extent for my tastes. That’s probably why each of them in turn were bested, and only with a little careful planning and a lot of precise execution. The New Frontier was mine to plunder. “Seriously. How long are we gonna sit out here in Hollywood and rot under Castor’s roof?” I snapped back to the here and now at the sharpness in Kelly’s voice. She’d put up with a lot in these last ten years, and I could always tell when she was getting to her limit. I forced myself up to meet her accusing gaze. “What day is it?” She sneered. “It doesn’t matter. We didn’t make Detroit.” Detroit? What was in Detroit? I gaped at her like a scolded child. “BRAWL, stupid, we missed BRAWL!” “Big deal,” I chided. “Nothing important ever happens at BRAWL.” I sat up, lit a cigarette, resigned that my sabbatical had been a bit overdone, and knowing that I had no business missing shows, televised or not, important or not. I scrambled around Castor’s master bedroom, looking for anything resembling my own clothes and finding a bunch of theater quality bullsh*t and another bunch of leather and lace bullsh*t, none of which I was going to wear anywhere ever. “Where’s my clothes?” “Which set?” She rolled her eyes. “The set you brought when you came out here after SuperCrash, the set I brought when you sent me home a week later, or the set that Angus brought two weeks later when he was finally cleared to fly?” I stopped in my tracks, blinked at her... “Jesus, Eric, just... Jesus.” She nodded at the suitcase that was already packed for me. “Take something out of there, we’ve got a flight in twenty minutes.” “Where to?” “Buffalo.” “Why?” “BRAWL!” Yeah, her limit was passed. Probably had been for a while now. It was time I came back to planet Earth. A man can immerse himself in his enemies’ habits and haunts for only so long before it was time to test what he’d learned in battle. Castor Strife wasn’t prepared for me. But then, I wasn’t yet ready for Castor Strife. The board had been set, and the pieces were in play, but it would take a bit more posturing and politicking to set things on the right course. Greasing the wheels of the Mayfield Machine would open up a lot of doors, but there was yet work to be done. I grabbed Kelly by the arm, turned her around. “Thanks for taking care of me.” I winked. Her nose furrowed. “It’s my job.” I pulled her close. “That’s not what I meant, and you know it.” She stopped me from kissing her. “Don’t even think about kissing me with that skank-breath. Just because I let you f*ck Castor’s little starlets doesn’t mean that I’m gonna let you touch me with their stink still in the air.” She turned and walked toward the exit to this uber-luxurious suite that I had no business crashing in. A slender finger poked in the direction of the master bathroom. “Shower. Five Minutes. We’re leaving in ten.” That woman... she had a way about her that I couldn’t resist. So a shower, then Buffalo. From there, the Frontier is my oyster.
