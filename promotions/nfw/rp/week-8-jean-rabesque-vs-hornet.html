WEEK 8: Jean Rabesque vs. Hornet

17 Dec 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(The screen comes in on Jean Rabesque, as he sits in a chair, not really sure where, not that it really matters, but the camera is slightly above his head so that he is consistently looking up at it throughout, noticeable on his head is a bandage from where he was clocked in the head with a baseball bat in Week 7, Rabesque doesn’t display this proudly by any means, but he also does nothing to prove it’s not there) Rabesque: “Another week down, another ten points, although a double dip would have been really nice. But no, like I said coming in, the whole BYOB thing really ain’t my thing, and I refuse to bow to the honchos who are trying to get me to wrestle a certain style. So I walked into that match completely unarmed, and it cost me some points. Hell, no sweat off my back, I’m still leading this entire damn company in points, and that shouldn’t change this week, nor it will change after this little WarGames extravaganza coming up. “People have been wondering aloud to me if I should be ashamed of getting the points I received last week out of forfeit. Now, I would have liked the match to take place, and I always enjoy being able to put on a show for the fans. But let’s be real here, since I came into the NFW for this new little experiment, I have been open and honest with the fact that I am here with one major intention, and that is to win the Ultratitle. To do that, I need to get as damn many points as possible, and put myself in the best situation possible going into the playoffs. 10 points is 10 points. I don’t care if Garvin doesn’t want to show up, or if someone else wants to lay their body in the middle of the ring and let me pin them. It’s not about honor right now. It’s about picking up those wins. Now, I’m not going to cheat to do it, because I believe that I simply do not think that I have to. So if I don’t have to wrestle another match for the rest of the season and that gets me into the playoffs, then so be it. I’ll take it any way I can get it. “So, this week, we have Hornet, a man who’s skull I get the pleasure of beating in twice now. I mean, come on Hornet, the CSWA match is an inevitability, isn’t it? And do you really think that this is going to be any different? And what would make you think that? “I know you’ve heard it before Hornet, but it still bears repeating, you are a shell of what you once were. You were the man everybody feared, no one in their right mind wanted to step into the ring with Hornet. Hell, I ran in circles that didn’t even come close to where you wrestled, and I still knew your name, and I knew that if I ever crossed over that if I ever wanted to make it, Hornet would be the man that I would have to come face to face with. You would be the standard that I would have to meet. “But now, you’re just one of the pack. Hell, you might not even be that. And then just to throw salt on the wound of anyone who wants to remember Hornet as a legend, we get a nice image of you looking at pictures, dreaming of...... her. How quaint, Hornet? What in the hell is going on? Is that what you’ve been relegated to? We go weeks without hearing from you, and then the first glimpse we get is that? I would be embarrassed if I was you Hornet, absolutely ashamed to tarnish my legacy the way that you are right now. “But at the same time, I still realize that there may be a little bit left in the tank. I realize that you may not be completely done, and in some weird kind of way that I can’t completely explain, I feel that that makes you quite dangerous. I’ll be ready for you Hornet. I’m not worried, quite confident in fact. But I’ll be on the lookout for the cagy veteran that may just try to prove me wrong. “You can try all you want Hornet, but it’s not going to work. That’s just that way that it’s going to be. “No false gimmicks, no false hype, I am Jean Rabesque.” (Fade out) [the thread's next post — 22 Dec 2003] (Hornet slams the closet door as the camera pulls back to reveal an empty lockerroom. He sits down in a nearby chair and starts taping his wrists. An empty lockerroom, in a presumably empty arena. There's a large Band-Aid on his forehead, just below the hairline.) "It's not about honor right now." That pretty much sums up this entire tournament, doesn't it? For all the talk of a 'code,' people get suspended who haven't done anything but defend themselves, and then the league sanctions weapons. I guess you fit right in, Jean, weapons or not. What, you didn't want to give the results of our CSWA match away, Jean? I'm sure Merritt appreciates the silence, but I don't mind. I'm perfectly willing to admit you got the better of me in Chicago, whether or not you were behind what happened. I've never said you weren't a great wrestler, Jean. I've never tried to portray you as not good enough just because it took you so long to hit the majors. I've never bought into the idea that it was your attitude that kept you down rather than your talent. But speaking of being 'real,' Jean. If I'm a "shell" of what I "once was," then what does that make the rest of you. If this "shell" can be tied with Shane Southern for points, and breathing down your neck, then what does that say about I was in my prime. That's the catch-22 for you and everybody else who wants to believe I'm over the hill, isn't it, Jean? If you say I'm nothing compared to what I was, then I must've been something pretty amazing, huh? And if I wasn't, if I was nothing then, then how can be a 'shell' of nothing now? (Hornet grins.) I've never made a living off of what or who I used to be, Jean. If I've brought up my past elsewhere, it's been to demand the respect that I deserve as a man who made it possible for the rest of you to make decent salaries and not have to be on the road seven nights a week. But don't mistake that for living off a legacy, Jean. I'm here because I have to be. But that doesn't mean that I don't appreciate the talent here or the challenge it represents. Unlike Melton, I have an appreciation of the ULTRATITLE, and whether I respect the man who controls it now or not, you can be damned sure that it's something that I <i>want</i>. But don't ever make the mistake of thinking I'm one of the <i>pack</i>, Jean. Much like you, I've lived outside the pack for most of my career. But unlike you, I didn't make it my calling card, I didn't pretend that it was some sort of 'calling' or predestination. In one breath you talk about my 'name,' and my legacy, and my 'standard,' and then you have the nerve to talk about how tarnished it is. Forget the 'cagy veteran,' Jean. Forget the man everybody feared, or the standard everyone was held to. Forget it all for just three seconds, Jean. Because when you do, the second you think you have the advantage over the washed up old veteran, is the moment that you'll find your head smacking the mat. And then the only question is whether your shoulders will stay down for a three-count, or whether you'll tap to the Scorpion. Congratulations, Jean. You've done something that few have been able to do in a long time. You've made me angry. So you keep remembering the man, the standard, you once knew, Jean. In just a few days, I'm going to show you the legend. (Fadeout)
