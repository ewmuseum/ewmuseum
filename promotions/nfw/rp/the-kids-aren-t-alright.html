Part 1: Home “Where to?” asked the cabdriver, as I leaned to the window. I gave him the address, tossed my bag in the back seat, and sat down next to it. “You’re that girl, aren’t you,” he said, as we started our journey, “I watch New Frontier Wrestling, you’re the girl that got hurt?” I smiled, kind of half – heartedly. Yeah, I said, I’m that girl that got hurt. “Well, you look a lot better. I hope they hang the bastard from his thumbs.” Thanks, I said, me too. The conversation ceased as I looked out the window and felt my breath quicken. This was my first trip here since I got hurt, and I didn’t know how things would change. Self – delusion, I realized. Things hadn’t changed, I had changed. Oddly enough, I had Bobby Jack Windham to thank for it. The windy, wintry streets passed by in a blur as the taxi drove though the city enroute to my destination. There were young people all around, on their way to whatever fun was about to happen. The guys were strutting like they were all that and two bags of chips, and the girls looked cold in their too – short, too – thin outfits. Dressing in next to nothing never had any appeal for me. I’m no prude, but if my assets are hanging two inches below my hemline I’d better be in my bedroom with one specific person. Anyways. Other than Castor Strife taking a swing at me to make himself look like a big tough swinging badass when he was challenging RK for the NFW TV Title, and other than Jason Reeves having nothing better to do than attack everyone who happened to be near the ring in EPW that night, I’ve been an innocent bystander at ringside. I stay out of the way, talk to some of the fans at ringside, and pound my hand on the mat when I need to spur RK on. Miss Ivy always said that you don’t have to worry about getting in trouble at ringside unless you make trouble for yourself. Bobby Jack Windham taught me that that’s absolutely not true. Tonight isn’t about Miss Ivy, though, and it’s not about Bobby Jack Windham. “Twenty two dollars and five cents,” said the taxi driver. I handed him a twenty and a five and told him to keep the change, as I collected my bag and left the car. Tonight, I reminded myself, as I stared at the building, is about me.
