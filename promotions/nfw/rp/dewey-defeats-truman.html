Dewey Defeats Truman

22 Jul 2012
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Hold up, I need to see this again." (FADEIN... on the last few moments of the Main Event of Reloaded 12. Specifically, when Castor Strife grabbed Eric Dane off the turnbuckle, planted him with the Director's cut, and pinned him in the middle of the ring for the three. "That was beautiful. One more time." Rewind. Replay. Applause. "Almost done." CUTTO: Static, followed by Eric Dane, from the past few weeks. DANE: I will expect your thanks, your whole-hearted appreciation of the lesson that I’m about to give you on what it means to be the World Heavyweight Champion. Static. Jump cut. DANE: When this is all over and the talking heads are flapping about Reloaded I’ll be in your spot as the Champion of the New Frontier, too. More static. Another jump cut. DANE: You bring the belt, Castor, I’ll bring the keys. We can leave it all in the ring and then I can get on with the business of my legacy and you can go back to squabbling with Impulse over who’s the second-best wrestler in the world. More static, and one more shot of the finish of the World Title match. As soon as the three count is made and the bell rings, more static, one last jump cut, to the Marathon Man himself, y'r humble narrator and tour guide, Impulse. My apartment. One of our cats is sitting on the arm of the chair next to me. And...) "Tell us a story, Uncle Eric?" "Tell us all about the World Titles you've won, and the hall of fames you're a part of, and how you're the greatest wrestler that's ever lived." I do so love a good tall tale. "Tell us about how the NFW World Title is ripe for the taking, and how you've maneuvered yourself into the perfect position to knock Castor down. Tell us how you're the smartest guy in the company for screwing the Windhams as well as Dynasty into giving you a lotta money for a bounty, here and there, and how it proved you were the only one with his eyes open." I also enjoy the occasional comedy. "Or I can tell you a story, Dane. A true take of whiskey and mystics and men, starring your favorite of the boring wrestlers, the Marathon Man himself, Randall Knox. And I'll tell you a story of the wrestler, right here in the New Frontier, who called himself a rebel. Defiant, even. He bucked the system, he took shortcuts where he thought they were warranted, and he repeatedly declared himself the best wrestler in the New Frontier." "When it came time for the defining moment of his NFW career, after all the bragging and all the puffing himself up... he crashed and burned." You're so vain, I bet you think this tale is about you. "Eric Dane? Of course not. I was talking about SARS." "Or Dorchester Stratton." "Or Michael Manson or Troy Windham, during the Season 2 era of the New Frontier." "The point, Eric, is that you're nothing special, you're a dime - a - dozen annoyance that every wrestling promotion has an overabundance of. The irony is that you - and every other supposed rebel that's failed to make an impact here - have called me 'boring' because I defined my boundaries and live inside them. Because I don't believe in shortcuts and I don't believe in taking a victory I haven't earned." "In other words, I'm boring because I'm not like everyone else." Dead - eye stare. "I don't mean to pick on you, Eric, especially when you're at your lowest point." Yeah, actually I do. "I'm doing it because you were the loudest for the longest about how terrible you thought I was and how much better at this you were than the rest of the New Frontier. About how I was a failure for being unable to beat Castor Strife." "Well, welcome to that particular club, Eric." "But I don't know that you belong there." "You see, when I lost to Castor in the New Frontier World Championship finals, it was in a match where I looked him in the eye and came at him with my best. We matched our strengths and weaknesses against each other for nearly forty minutes, with no interference and no pre-match psychological mind games. He came up with the win, clean and legit, and I walked away disappointed but proud of the work I put in, knowing that I'd have another chance in the future at some point." "When you lose a match based on wrestling ability, there are adjustments that can be made to make things happen differently." "You, on the other hand..." Here we go. "You conned yourself into a title shot. You stole Castor's house, his film company, his money, and his every possession except for the NFW Title belt. You showed up for your match with three - three - lackeys in tow who did everything in their power to interfere on your behalf." "You leapfrogged every legitimate contender in the New Frontier and patted yourself on the back for your ingenuity, and Castor Strife pinned you to the mat like the bum'a the month." Slow clap. "Where do you go from here, Eric? You've played your hand. You've shown your ass. You had every possible advantage going into your title match, and you still failed to deliver the goods." "The only way you could've given yourself more of an advantage would've been if you shot Castor in the leg before the match started, and fortunately for him, this isn't the FWO." "Normally, this is where the generic Eric Dane type wrestlers would tell the failed challenger to leave and never come back, since he's failed at the top of the game." "I'm not going to do that, Dane. Stick around." "You might learn something." "Lesson number one: when you put your entire reputation on the outcome of one match, you'd better make sure you can actually win that match." "Lesson number two: I don't know what mediocre promotion you've wrestled in before, but this is the New Frontier. This is where the best come to wrestle." "And if you can't step up your game... then you really don't belong here." Lean forward. "But I'm honestly interested in seeing where you go next. I hear Bret Kelly is looking for a tag team partner." FADE [the thread's next post — 22 Jul 2012] (CUT TO: JJ DeVille, in a big, thick leather chair in his Arlington, Virginia condominium. He has a gigantic remote control in his hand and a smirk on his face. He hits a button on the remote. The voice of Impulse is heard on the surround-sound.) IMPULSE: "Lesson number one: when you put your entire reputation on the outcome of one match, you'd better make sure you can actually win that match." (CUT TO: JJ DeVille, smirking. He hits the button again.) IMPULSE: "Lesson number one: when you put your entire reputation on the outcome of one match, you'd better make sure you can actually win that match." (CUT TO: JJ DeVille, laughing now. He hits the button again.) IMPULSE: "Lesson number one: when you put your entire reputation on the outcome of one match, you'd better make sure you can actually win that match." (CUT TO: JJ DeVille, crying laughing and slapping his knee. He hits the button again.) IMPULSE: "Lesson number one: when you put your entire reputation on the outcome of one match, you'd better make sure you can actually win that match." (CUT TO: JJ DeVille, now on the ground, pounding the floor with his hand in hysterics. FTB) [the thread's next post — 22 Jul 2012] JJ, one would think you'd have something better to do after, what was it, defeating and humiliating me, than to name drop me or Rose every single time you open your mouth. I'm past it, why aren't you? Eric, of course you do. You're predictable. [the thread's next post — 22 Jul 2012] (JJ is laughing, watching the television. He puts down the remote and spins his chair towards the camera.) JJ: "It's simple, Knox. I'm going to remind you until the day you die that I beat you in the middle of a ring after months and months of your boasts about how I couldn't hang with you. And I'm going to remind all the people home until the day that I die that you are professional wrestling's biggest hypocrite. You put your reputation on the line against me. You lost. I won. Ergo... your reputation is SULLIED. Before you go impugning the character of a man of the utmost personal integrity in Eric Dane... judge yourself. Also, I thought you weren't ever going to mention my name again? Isn't that what you told the world? I'm not quite sure because, as Eric Dane said, you bore the world to tears. But if you did... well... just more false promises from the NFW's biggest over-promoted con man." [the thread's next post — 23 Jul 2012] ROOK: "So!" FADE IN: ROOK BLACK icing his knee. He is in his ring gear, but the glossy pants on his right leg have been roughly cut with scissors. ROOK: "I'm sure we all have choices tonight that we're rethinking, wishing we had zigged instead of zagged." ROOK (looking down at his knee): "For me, I should not have eaten the damn cupcake. But it's hard to say `no' to Rose. For the record, I'm not necessarily obsessed with organic food components so much as I've attempted to eliminate refined grains from the diet, but I ate the cupcake anyway, and I regret it." ROOK: "Congratulations, Castor, I am sure you can sleep a little better knowing that your legacy is improved by your victory tonight. Or maybe you'll sleep better just by sleeping in your own bed instead of on a bus or a hotel bed stabbing you in the kidneys with broken springs. Or perhaps you've just ordered a new bed delivered since it's not that hard to imagine Eric having permanently ruined your own for sleeping. Anyway, I didn't really start this recording to address you, but I felt you needed acknowledging and did, in fact, deserve recognition for an incredible performance." ROOK: "I feel Legion also deserves some recognition. I don't imagine it'll surprise anybody that I was not prepared for his sudden burst of aggression and offense, what with the replay showing my eyes bugging out of my head as he went from zero to sixty faster than I could blink. That was not the match I had planned for, nor the match that I wanted, but as has been demonstrated previously, I am a proponent of doing whatever is necessary to survive. It's fair to say that more than once tonight he almost had me. Unfortunately, almost isn't quite good enough." ROOK (adjusting the cold pack): "I have Legion's teeth marks on my knee to show for it. In addition to the prize of course, the Triple Crown Championship." ROOK: "Now, with that said, we can get to the fun part." ROOK: "Eric Dane, you do not ****ing belong here." (ROOK sneers.) ROOK: "I'm sure you don't need an explanation. You made your best play, and you gambled everything on your win, and you lost." ROOK: "What's important is that your loss really illuminates a lot of the nuances of our little competitive sporting event we have going on here. When you gamble everything, you are left with nothing. A different type of challenger for the world title has a potential for a rematch perhaps. A different type of challenger has the fact of his rise to the position of challenger having been founded upon the merits of his rise to that position, and when they lose against the champion, he still has not lost everything. He's still got the momentum of that rise, he's got his reputation, his legacy even. He'd have what he'd learned in that loss in order to make another, stronger, smarter, attempt. For some, losses motivate them to win next time. For others, losses catalyze a change in thinking, and bring about a brand new reinvented competitor." ROOK: "Who knew that those sorts of things were important? Well, I hope a great number of people look at you, at the steps you took to achieve your goals, and realize that those things matter. That the Eric Dane shortcut through NFW will remain an anomaly, a road map to ruin, to career self destruction, that no one will ever consider emulating. That this is what happens what all the necessary components of a rise to the top are stolen and not earned. That this is what happens when you claim to be the smartest guy in the building, when in reality you're just the one with the least amount of patience." ROOK: "So, pack up your ****, and don't let the door hit you in the ass on the way out." ROOK: "I'm sure it's what you'd planned on doing anyway." [the thread's next post — 23 Jul 2012] You seem to be far too obsessed with a wrestler that you claim to be above, always answering with everything I say whether I bring you up or not. Following every aspect of my life, looking for the slightest bit of discrepancy to poke me with a stick and say 'LOOK! He's a HYPOCRITE!' You mention my name every time you talk, which shows you're still defining yourself opposite to me, which is fairly pathetic for a man who claims to be in charge. You talk directly to me, I'm going to answer. You want to call that hypocritical? Go for it. Nobody cares, JJ. The fans don't care, they've always supported me. The other wrestlers don't care, I keep to myself in the back and have good matches with everybody - that's what the boys care about. Eric Dane might care, but he's spent the last few weeks proving to the world that he's an all-bark-no-bite b!tch, so it's not like he matters in the slightest. Regardless, you're Eddie's problem now. Like you should've been from the start: Eddie and the World Champion. He'll beat you back, JJ, and you'll be gone, and there won't be a single sign that you've ever been here. You were going to break me, you were going to destroy the NFW, you were going to lord over all our lives. I'm not broken. The New Frontier is stronger than ever. The Windham Clan has failed to take the World Title, and the only one of its members who had the ability to beat Castor has abandoned you. You're a broken finger, JJ. Annoying while it's there, and immediately forgotten once it's gone. And no, you still can't have a rematch with me. Keep talking, though, it amuses the masses. Rose has hope that you'll eventually man up and move on, me, I don't think you can leave well enough alone.
