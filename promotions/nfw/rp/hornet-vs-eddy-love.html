Hornet vs. Eddy Love

11 Feb 2004

STIPULATION: Falls Count Anywhere [the thread's next post — 12 Feb 2004] (NFW Saturday Morning recap promoting the upcoming Crash TV and showing replays from matches from house shows across the land. Cue Up: “All You Need is Love” as the lights go out in a house show in small town North Carolina. A spot light back lights the Silhouette of Eddy Love accompanied by Calvin Carleton making their way to the ring. Carleton holds the ropes as Eddy Crawls into the ring, the back light goes off, a single bright loud explosion and the house lights are back on. Standing in the Ring is Love wearing a full length dark mink coat synched at the waiste barely showing the tie and white shirt beneath. Love’s hair is blown and sprayed to unequal perfection, larger and longer than ever before. Love already has microphone in hand.) I am (pause, crooked smile) Hurricane Eddy Love. The man with the (Love spins slowly around with arms spread wide in an airplane pose) forty fyyyyyve thousand dollar mink coat, ( Love slides up his right sleeve to check the time) thirty thousand dollar Rolex, (slightly nod head, smiles) and the million dollar smile. Born to a poverty stricken dirt farmer in South Carolina, but through diligence and perseverance He’s been around the world and dined with Kings and Queens after having to sleep in the barn and eat pork-n-beans. I am Eddy Love….. A symbol for hard working America to hold up to their children and say, “If you work hard, you can be anything, just like Eddy Love”. A model of fair play and humility. Men want to be me, the ladies just hope to see me. I am The Blue Ridge Bomber. A wrestler unequalled in stamina, skill and know how…… a wrestler who’s quantity of titles eclipses his number of losses. A tactician, a grappler, a bona fide ring surgeon and a man who has never met his equal in the ring or out. I am the Southern Dandy. A illustration of what is right about the fine people of the sun belt region. The stereotype Southerner, polite, quick witted, hard working and prepared to smack you in the mouth if you say otherwise. …….. Eddy Love , the hard working Southerner’s champion. (Love takes a stroll as the fans pop with mixed reviews.) So I make my way to the (pause) ENN EFFF DUBYA at Mr. Carleton’s request to do that little thing that Hurricane Eddy does and in my very first match they feed me one of my oldest nemesis, oldest in more ways than one, mind you. Now there is no denying the illustrious career of that Damn Hornet……. He’s done more in this sport than most anyone could even remember…… title after title…… main event after main event…… multi million dollar contract after multi million dollar contract. BUT something changed six years ago, didn’t it counselor. For the last six years That Damn Hornet must has stood cold in my shadow. Not a single ray of sun has hit his face. No longer the Greatest American Hero but simply another stepping stone for Eddy Love…. A foil for all those Eddy Love Lovers to celebrate his defeat……. Over and over. I cannot deny that my first win over That Damn Hornet was what gave Hurricane Eddy Love credibility with the wrestling experts, but beating up the fast talking cheap shot lawyer from Greensboro feels better and better every time I do it. When you no sold me and headed for the airport, Counselor, I thought maybe you’d wised up and my chances to exchange blood and sweat with a living legend had come to an end, but now I know….. Sometime at Crash …. When we’ve gone after each other with every ounce of what makes a man go, you’re gonna feel you’ve got more to give, more in your tank than Eddy Love and you set me up for whatever move it is Hornet Splash, Big Head, series of Greensboro suplexes….. Whatever your pleasure, then it’s a trademark Eddy Love counter, a high Hurricane Piledriver, a fast ride with blinking lights and sirens then 12 weeks of spinal rehab for you, Counselor. I’ve owned you Boy, and 5th verse same as the first……. Eddy Love is just too damn good for that Damn Hornet. I see my man Joey Melton has Jean Rabiscuit, in probably the biggest mismatch of the night short of my match. There’s no doubt in my mind that Joey Melton could beat ole J.R. on his worst day…… no doubt in my mind that Joey Melton will not only win this match, but go on to win the South’s regular season title, no doubt that he is probably the greatest wrestler in allllllll the world, no doubt a man that Calvin and myself hand picked as the premiere wrestler to not only take out the South but to destroy whatever Ham-n-egger goes on to represent the North in the quest for the Ultra-Tiltle (Calvin is bouncing up and down smiling and shadow boxing) ……… BUT while I do not doubt any of those things, Joey, I also don’t doubt that even my best friend has a limit on the number of dumb hick jokes he tells about the people of God’s country, and more importantly about myself …….before Eddy Love busts him right in his big mouth. (Calvin’s mouth drops, he begins screaming cut the mic, and trying to reason with Love who shakes him off almost without notice.) So I agree with what that little cum dumpster you run around with said, Joey, if you got something’ to say, come on out and say it. Then we have Chris Shepherd’s Jesus Freak Show. A man who leads with words and sets no example. A man who is by definition the worst kind of hypocrite. And if he comes out and starts those false prophecies and makes one little reference to Eddy Love and how I lead my life I’ll come out there and whip him and his seven foot puppet……. And if Shane Southern staggers in my way….. Well let’s just say Shane stay outta my business and I won’t be havin’ to shut yours down. So, Eddy Love is in…… Craig Miles is out …… Angry Negros have taken over the North, you wanted them free let them date you daughters…… and The Joey Melton Ultra Title tour is in full swing. It’s got drama, beauty, strongmen, con men, freak shows and side shows…… But I’ve been to every Freak Show, every side show and every county fair ground around the globe….. AND WHEN HURRICANE EDDY GOES TO THE FAIR HE ALWAYS COMES HOME WITH THE TEDDY BEAR. (Back to O’Conner) [the thread's next post — 12 Feb 2004] (Hornet's walking out the stage door of an arena. He's fresh from the shower, water droplets still shaking from his head as his breath clouds in the cold winter night.) I am (pause, no smile) Hornet. The man with more money than he can ever spend and the million dollar back. Born to parents I'll never tell you about in Greensboro, in a non-descript house eating non-descript meals. I am Hornet. Formerly the "Greatest American Hero," or the Franchise, or "The One and Only," or the "Businessman." Take your pick. A good guy for ten years. A bad guy for five. And a top draw for 15. I am history. In spite of those who would like to forget, I'm the four-time CSWA World Champion. The man who went from league to league and ring to ring to create the Unified Championship. The man who wrestled from Japan to Greensboro in the CSWA or the UWA or the JWA or the WWR or the AAWC or the USWA or whatever other initials you want to throw out there. I am an addict. A man who allowed weakness and circumstance to lead down a path I never thought I'd go. But you know about that, don't you, Eddy? Weakness and circumstance? Like our dear, departed friend Craig, you know about hiding behind other men and women, right? Doing the bidding of others? Having your strings pulled one way than the other? I mean, first there was Ray S. Cornette. Then there was Sweet Melissa. And now the ultimate Mama's boy, Calvin Carlton. You know, Eddy, I think, in honor of your return, that at CRASH, we ought to party like it's 1999. You remember 1999, don't you? You entered the year as the champ, only for the "Legend Killer" to run into that buzzsaw known as Mark Vizzack. And we remember what happened to Vizzack, don't we? Welcome back, Eddy. I'll be sure to give your love to Sammy. I hope you'll give mine to Cal. [the thread's next post — 17 Feb 2004] (Three 58 inch plasma TV’s cover a wall all showing Hornet’s words. The screens go black and pan back to Eddy lounging in a LOVE seat. Eddy has on a pair of oversized Royal Blue silk boxer shorts and an unbuttoned silk robe. His long blonde hair as usual sprayed to perfection flowing just below shoulder length. He leans slightly forward) Now Counselor, don’t get me wrong. I am aware that you were making an attempt to mock me when you come out here with your little "I am that Damn Hornet" bit. The funny thing is I thought that the kind words that Hurricane Eddy shared with the fans about you and your career shed a brighter light on your heritage than when you get on TV with that $6 Sportsclip haircut, looking pathetic and well (pauses) a little scared. Don’t forget, Counselor, if you keep your receipt you can go back in any time this month for that free neck and ear trim. I had talked about your titles, your money, and your legacy….. So all you really brought to the table where I was concerned, was you, Greatest American Hero, you the Franchise, you The Businessman….. did make me, the Lake Hartwell Heartthrob, wonder just how many silly nicknames a grown man can bestow upon himself. We all know what you bring to the table Counselor…. 10 years of Good Guy…… 5 years of bad guy….. 9 years of titles ……. And 6 years of trying to escape the huge shadow you helped to create , the prototype scientific consummate professional wrestler, in the person of the Southern Dandy, Hurricane Eddy Love. Your legacy is certainly stronger than your revisionist version of history, however Counselor. Eddy Love hiding behind someone. (snickers) Come on, Counselor, you’ve been there nearly every step of the way and who have you seen the Blue Ridge Bomber hide behind????? Ray Cornette????? A man who was allowed to attach his name to mine only briefly and only at the behest of the board of directors from your fabled stomping grounds in hopes of salvaging their slumping famed Corporation. What you fail to mention, Counselor, is that after Chad Merrit and Steve whatever his name is, begged me to breathe fresh air into their Corporation Cash Cow not only did Ray Cornette never accompany yours truly to the ring, but I myself with Kevin Powers ran Cornette and the entire Corporation out of *my* sport allowing only Joey Melton to ever return. If that’s your definition of hiding behind someone, then you’ll soon catch me hiding behind Chris Shepherd, The Deacon and maybe even you, Counselor. As for Melissa, I did hide my most interesting 12 inches behind her, in her and around her……but I found no shame in that. And for Calvin Carleton, well, I’m a company man, Counselor, always have been, always will be….. The Powers of Love, Playboys Inc., The Super Sports Network all thrived with me as member. No one sold harder for CSWA while you were always on the cell phone threatening to walk because Chad Merrit didn’t bring you milk with your Twinkies ….. I can lead, I can follow but I sure as hell don’t get out of the way. You wanna call me hiding behind Carleton…… that’s fine, just don’t make Momma Carleton unhappy, cause that’s when Eddy Love gives up the follower’s role and takes the lead. It’s my game counselor, you’ve known it since you squared off with Hurricane Eddy at Fish Fund. Your accomplishments are vast, your legacy huge, your self worth even bigger, but, my man, your value to me has been simply that of an overpaid whipping boy. It brings one of my favorite stories to mind. …… things were going really well in the CSWA, That Damn Hornet was bringing in the fans and to show their appreciation management gave him some top quality cloth and told him to go to the local tailor and get him a nice blazer sewn. The Tailor, however said there was not nearly enough material and encouraged (sarcastically) The Franchise to try another tailor, and so he did. In fact he tried nearly every tailor in every town throughout his travels. Then Eddy Love came onto the wresting circuit and one day That Damn Hornet happened into downtown Clemson, South Carolina, where the water taste like wine. The Greatest American Hero took his fine cloth and asked the tailor if he could make him a blazer. To the Businessman’s surprise the tailor said sure, and he had enough left over for a pair of pants and maybe a tie. When the Greensboro Lawyer asked how that could be when no one else could even make the blazer, the tailor replied “You’re not half the bad-ass you were since Eddy Love came around” I am Hurricane Eddy Love, no man can stop him, no woman wants to. So on to NFW where you watch as Hurricane Eddy provides inspiration to the helpless and downtrodden through his feats of unequalled skill, stamina and intellect. Don’t feel like the Greatest American Hero is completely without rousing capabilities, however. Thought you all deserved to see it. [the thread's next post — 17 Feb 2004] Life’s curveballs aren’t always easy to catch…. Even when you’re the one throwing the ball. Even when you know exactly where you want to place it, know the path you want it to take, it doesn’t mean that’s where it ends up. I never expected to be a 36 year-old wrestler instead of lawyer. Hell, I never expected to be wrestling in league run by Craig Miles. I certainly never thought I’d be a ‘twelve-stepper.’ But curveballs can be nasty. Step One: We admit we are powerless over our dependencies and that our lives have become unmanageable. But remember, I’m the one throwing the ball. So why not throw a change-up or a slider? Why not take control of things, or listen to the catcher? Wait… I’m the catcher too. I know, doesn’t make much sense, but who else is there? It’s too easy to get used to being in control, to being alone. It’s too easy to get comfortable with one or two pitches and forget that there’s more you know, that there’s more to learn. It’s too easy to do what comes easy. And it’s hard to remember that you were once someone else. Or that you can be something else. We use dependencies to avoid our pain. Concentrating on my physical pain was a way to ignore the emotional. A way to ignore the pain I’d inflicted on others, and on myself. Soma and Vicodin dulled the pain, and dulled the senses just enough to be able to forget. As long as I was dealing with the phantom pains in my knee, the memory of pain, then I didn’t have to deal with anything else. We live in a fantasy world. That one hit home. I’ve worked in a ‘fantasy’ business for fifteen years. I’ve made a living under a pseudonym and with actions that I’d never (I hope) do outside an arena. But there’s been too much reality there too. Over the years I’ve seen guys in the back who are so much different on-camera than off. And I guess there’s something to be said for that. If anything, I’ve been too much myself, worn my heart on my sleeve too much. When I tried to separate my ‘personal’ life from ten years of ‘wrestling’ life, it created a dichotomy, a division, that was never there before. When I walked into the warehouse that became the Merritt Auditorium, it was as a guy who was excited about an opportunity, ready to show what he could do. Years later, I had set a standard, and was a guy who wanted to keep raising the bar. And now, where am I? What do I want? To have a private life? To win the ULTRATITLE? To be a CSWA World Champion again? To prove that I can still set the standard? Our denial kept us from seeing how powerless and unmanageable our lives had become. There’s no one to blame but myself. Every choice I’ve made, from branding Mark Vizzack to no-showing BATTLE of the BELTS and a chance at the CSWA World Title, has been my choice. Even the NFW. Merritt may have ‘forced’ me into it, but I always had the choice to walk… and it’s not like I’ve been shy about using it in the past. Success is a funny thing. The more money you have, the more people you have to answer to, to be responsible for, to pay salaries to. The more fame you have, the more beholden you are to every media outlet or sponsor or autograph-seeker. I’ve cried enough “Poor me’s” over the past few years to last a lifetime, but the truth is the truth sometimes. Maybe it takes a stronger will than mine to manage it. Surrender is the answer. I’ve read stories about NFL coaches who had concerns about football players that had gone through substance abuse programs. They felt like when they came back that they weren’t as aggressive, that they took losses too easily, they were too willing to take losing as part of God’s larger plan. Surrender isn’t something I know much about. It’s not something I do well. I’ve been in enough War Games in my years to know the rules: submission or surrender. I’ve never done either inside a double cage. I haven’t done much of it outside the ring either. I’ve always wanted to be in control. I still want to be in control. But it’s just like success, the more I try to control what happens to me, the harder it gets. The tires spin and more and more mud flies, but the car just sinks deeper. So I’m still figuring out what ‘surrender’ means? Does it mean giving up entirely? That would certainly be easier. It would be easier just to walk away, to leave on my own terms, knowing that I’ll be called names like ‘coward’ and ‘quitter.’ And maybe it would be healthier in the long run… to search for a sense of peace, a sense of purpose, completely outside a wrestling ring or an arena. Step Two: We came to believe that God could restore us to sanity. Like I said, I’ve made my own choices over the years. Sometimes I’ve asked for advice, many times I haven’t. From the time that a wall fell on top of me in 1998, I knew what fear was. It wasn’t an issue inside the ring, but it was a constant one outside. Fear does crazy things to you, literally. It leads to constant questioning and self-doubt, then to paranoia and worse. It lets you rationalize reasons for violence or intrigue, to create seemingly valid responses for sinking a branding iron into a man’s flesh or creating intrigue between friends and loved ones. Sanity is a luxury to sane, a dream to the insane. Insanity comes in many forms… it’s not necessarily schizophrenia or talking to chairs. It’s not always snapping at the sight of blood or attacking the innocent. It doesn’t have to be wandering down alleyways or babbling about “awakenings.” Sometimes it’s just about being lost. About forgetting not only who you are or what path you thought you were on, but how you relate to the world. Or maybe it’s about focusing so much on yourself that you forget who God is. In either case, it’s about being in a place that you don’t recognize, about knowing that you’re just a step away from a place you once knew, but finding out it’s changed, that it’ll never be the same again. The insanity is that we continue the same behavior and expect different results. For now, I live life on the edge of a knife. One missed step, one forgetful moment, and who I am, who I want to be changes. It’s not something that I’ve fought for six years or sixteen, but my whole life. There are times that I want to hurt someone. There are times that the ring is the place I can take out all the aggression, all the misgivings, all the confusion, take it all and boil it down into one simple thing. Ask WASP. Ask Avery Lincoln. Their last moments in the ring were intended to be. Not by them, but by me. Because for whatever reason, I allowed myself to forget. I allowed myself to let anger and aggression take over from reason and sympathy. They weren’t mistakes. I let myself go, and I was able to rein myself back in. I don’t know if that’s true anymore. Whether it’s a chemical change in the brain or a lesser will, walking that knife edge is more important then ever… because if I let go, there’s no guarantee that I can come back, or that I’ll want to. “Or even better, go home on your plane and show the world what you really are... nothing more than a coward who isn't man enough to get the job done. Go ahead. Think you can do this by yourself. Think you can win it all without compromising at least a tiny bit or your morals. Guess what? You're wrong if you think that.” It would be so easy to take Carlton and hammer him against the ring post. To take the smarmy Mama’s boy and drop his face down on the concrete. It’d certainly get the crowd going. But what if he’s right? I’ve spent the last six years pushing away anyone that meant anything to me. Ivy. Eli. Sunshine and Vizzack. Even Randalls and Teri, in some sick, twisted way. (Mark doesn’t count, he’s certifiable.) And now, here in the God-forsaken NFW, the person I’m closest to is Joey? The only thing we have in common is that we ran the same circuit in the old days. And even then he couldn’t be bothered to acknowledge any of us, not until he figured out we could help make him some money. And to be honest, everyone knows that Joey’s a few cards short of a full deck. In the end, it’s not about the legacy of the ULTRATITLE, or even the legacy of my career. In some ways, that was written ten years ago, in others, history will warp and change it so that it’s not recognizable. When it boils down, it’s about deciding who I want to be again, about how to find some measure of happiness. If that means walking away, then for once in my life, I’m alright with that. If it means pulling out a branding iron and altering Eddy Love’s forehead, then we’ll find out if I can live with that too. Fear is spawned from uncertainty. But in acknowledging that I don’t know what to expect, that I can’t guarantee what will happen, and most importantly, that I can’t control it, I’ve found something I didn’t expect. I’m not afraid anymore. Not afraid of what I might do or what someone else might think. Not afraid of being too violent or making wrong choices. Not afraid of leaving wrestling behind or facing it head-on. Not afraid of being alone. ”Perfect love drives out fear, because fear has to do with punishment.” I certainly can’t love perfectly, but I think I know someone who can. And I’ve already punished myself enough for a lifetime. (As Hornet finishes writing in a small leatherbound journal, he reaches over and picks up a cell phone on the mahogany desk. As he searches through the phone’s directory for the number he wants to call, he finds it’s nice to be away from the cameras for a few days, to be able to sit back and focus. The decision he has to make doesn’t chafe like it did before. And that’s what he wants to tell someone. For once it’s not about getting answers, it’s about asking the right questions.)
