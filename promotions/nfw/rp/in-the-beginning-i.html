In The Beginning.. I

16 Feb 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The cold, damp road hadn't gotten any rest all morning as cars whizzed by, slicing through the pouring rain drops in downtown Boston. Shawn Douglas sat on the passenger side of a black Ford Expedition overlooking the city's large landscape, thinking through the consequences that would follow his arrival at the NFW headquarters. The driver sat silently, eyes glued to the street, one hand on the steering wheel and the other fidgeting boringly with his cell phone. "Are you sure this is the right choice?" SD peered over towards his trainer who hadn't even budged. Shawn was having doubts about signing with New Frontier Wrestling since the first day it was proposed to him, but Slade Wilson had insisted. The renowned head of the Wilson Academy of Wrestling wasn't a newcomer to training stars, and Douglas knew it. In his coaching tenure, Slade had garnered the nickname "The Trainer of Champions" from many of the important figures in the wrestling industry. Names such as "Kodiak" Vic Creed and Mitch Wilson were uttered when asked who he had trained and it wasn't hard to see both sat atop of his achievements list as both became multiple times champions, most noticeably, fWo World Heavyweight Champions. "Yes." He finally answered collectedly. The back-story between these two was fairly simple. Slade Wilson had organized a tryout between 8 wrestling hopefuls from the independent scene to find the next prodigy for his school. A long day of sweat, blood and tears followed as one by one, competitors dropped out and quit, leaving only two men standing at the end of the session. The first was Randall Powers. A beast of a man who was often compared to the Alaskan Juggernaut, KVC. He had gone through the entire work out without even so much breaking a sweat. Opponent after opponent, he knocked them down and beat them out of the ring all the while maintaining a cocky attitude, smiling even. The other was Shawn Douglas. The 25 year old from Los Angeles dripped in a pool of his own sweat, barely breathing, as Wilson and his associates stood before them to deliver his judgment. It was a given, SD thought, that Slade would pick Powers. He thought about Vic Creed, about how much the man had done with so little coaching. Wilson trained stars, not mid carders, not upper carders, but main eventers and the man who stood next to him, hands on his hips with a big smirk plastered across his face had all the credentials to be one. But it was still Shawn Douglas who came out victorious. The shock was collective to all but Slade explained his choice wisely. He wanted heart and determination, he wanted someone who gave it his all, someone who wanted it all. That's where he drew the line between Powers and Douglas. Now, nearly 2 years later, both men sat side by side driving along Cambridge street to their meet. "Listen, you need a place where you can test your skills against seasoned veterans and the NFW is filled with them. This is what you need, trust me Shawn. But now tell me, do you really want to shine in this industry, kid?" Shawn nodded sharply. "Then do what I say and you'll get there." SD sighed under his breath as the SUV pulled into the NFW headquarters' parking lot.
