Founded in June 2000 and rebuilt three years later around a trophy borrowed from CSWA folklore, New Frontier Wrestling ran e-wrestling as a fantasy-sports league — a draft lottery, conferences, a points table, playoffs and a Wrestlebowl — for fourteen years across three websites. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

New Frontier Wrestling was announced on 1 June 2000 and debuted at the Vault in Baltimore that July, out of a Yahoo Groups message board. Its own history reaches back further than that: it names the UWA, an indy-styled federation that ran on the Prodigy online service from 1992 to 1997, as the community it was rebuilt out of, and its news archive republished two Prodigy-era cards whole — a 1997 UWA show and a 1996 AAWC show — as its own prehistory. The first two years were CRASH TV out of Baltimore and then the Tsongas Arena in Lowell, Massachusetts, with a World Championship, a Mid-Atlantic Championship and a tag division, until a federal investigation into a masked wrestler with mafia ties took the fed off the air for six months.

What came back was a different shape. In June 2003 the fed reopened as a fantasy-sports league: a televised draft lottery, two conferences, a sixteen-week regular season scored on a published points table — ten points for a win by any route, five for a time-limit draw or a double pin, nothing for a loss — wild cards, single-elimination playoffs, and a Wrestlebowl at the end of it. The prize was the ULTRATITLE, an eight-tier trophy donated to NFW out of CSWA folklore and contested across a whole season rather than defended match to match. Michael Manson beat Shane Southern for the first one at Wrestlebowl I in January 2005; Nova beat Yori Yakamo Jr. for the second at Wrestlebowl II in May 2007.

The seasons redrew the map twice. Season One split North and South; Season Two split East and West, the East staged out of Tokyo and roleplay-based, the West staged across the American southwest and written show-first, each with its own weekly programme — Crash TV against Midnight Madness. From 2007 the conferences went and the touring did not: Brawl shows ran out of Liverpool, Glasgow, the Royal Albert Hall, Madison Square Garden, Monte Carlo, Sydney, Perth, Berlin, Helsinki and Laos, with Supercrash and Reloaded as the cards that mattered. A National Championship and a Television Championship came back in 2008; the tag titles were renamed the Everette Memorial Tag Team Championships after Avery Everette, the figure the fed's own history names as the first owner of the UWA.

NFW's own record shows a promotion that cared about writing and said so. Every show closes on a Credits block naming the writer of each individual segment, and a single card routinely ran to a dozen hands and a quarter of a million characters — Wrestlebowl II's second night is the longest document it left. The roleplay boards lived on fwrestling.com for the whole of the fed's life, on all three of the websites it kept.

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. Its last captured results document is Reloaded 15 from Amsterdam, 23 February 2013; the newsdesk kept posting lineups and press conferences through the summer of 2014, for Memphis and Dallas and Houston shows whose scripts were never captured, and stops on 11 August 2014. The site itself was still being crawled into February 2015 with nothing newer behind it.

Storylines

NFW said out loud, more than once, what it thought it was making. Its Hall of Fame inducted six men between 2007 and 2012 and wrote a dossier for each; its championship history transcribes six lineages and notes, in its own words, where two of them were merged out of existence; and at Reloaded 14 it handed out its own All-Pro Awards — Rookie of the Year, Tag Team of the Year, Wrestler of the Year — in the last winter it ran a full card. The arcs below are led by those three documents and recapped from the shows they ran across.

The Vault, the Rumble, and a champion buried in dirt Written by AI, 2026

NFW opened on 4 July 2000 at the Vault in Baltimore with President Quentin Sullivan and Commissioner Craig Miles introducing a promotion with, they said, no politics in it, and spent the rest of the night proving otherwise. Roger Powell, unhappy with his contract, was made to wrestle three matches in one evening as punishment for trying to leave, and vandalised the Commissioner's car in the parking lot afterwards. Wildstar was told his "Five-Star" nickname was on the line in the main event, lost it to Aidan Campbell when Alex Wylde ran in with a chair, and left the first show NFW ever ran with a name that now belonged to somebody else.

The World Championship was decided in October in a thirty-man Random Rumble that Bloodhunt entered inside a flag-draped casket, carried to the ring by two men in a JFK-assassination parody with Ms. America playing the widow. He eliminated Lincoln McKinley the moment he got out of it. Torch threw two fireballs during the match and burned the wrong man with the second. The Rumble produced two finalists rather than a winner, and Bloodhunt beat Ricky Zane across a street fight, a tables match and a Buried Alive fall to become the first NFW World Heavyweight Champion — a title decided, in the end, by a shovel.

The tag titles followed at Christmas, where the Elite Guard were fired out of the round robin they were meant to be in and the Showstoppers won the vacant belts in the three-way that replaced them. By the spring, Bloodhunt was staging a sniper shooting from the catwalk to announce that he was declaring war on the locker room, and Armando Montezuma had imported a referee of his own, Ricardo Esteban Fernandez, to work a whole tournament with a visible lean.

Banned 4 Life, and the fed that got itself thrown out of Baltimore Written by AI, 2026

NFW's first pay-per-view, Banned 4 Life, ran twelve matches and gave the promotion its shape: a Mid-Atlantic Championship tournament final won by Alex Wylde after Nakita Dubov speared his opponent, Hellmachine taking the tag titles inside a steel cage on a reviewed simultaneous pinfall, and Ricky Zane winning the World Title after a commissioner voided a screwjob. Raul and Cojones Mercado kidnapped Rick Ryconik's daughter Vivian from ringside during her father's semi-final, an abduction the fed kept returning to for the next three years.

The autumn turned on a disputed finish. Crash TV 7, which opened with Craig Miles addressing the September attacks and explaining the three-month gap in the schedule, ended with Rick Ryconik beating Ricky Zane for the World Championship and losing it the same night to Montezuma, on a count made by an unlicensed second referee while the first lay unconscious. The broadcast disputed the finish live and never resolved it; commissioner Mike Manson simply vacated the belt three weeks later and put it in a four-corners match at Christmas, which Shane Southern won.

Manson then burned the Mid-Atlantic Championship on air and replaced it with a Television Championship, decided in a single night at Impact TV 4 — a sixteen-man, four-round tournament ending in a thirty-foot barbed-wire cage and ladder match that Tempesta del Fuerte won after beating four men in one evening. A month earlier, at Crash TV 9, a guest referee had ruled that Steel Viper and Tempesta had pinned each other at the same moment and awarded the fall to both — the fed's own answer to a finish it did not want to call a draw.

Futureshock closed the era with an FBI manhunt running underneath the card: agents searching the Tsongas Arena for a masked man wanted in a crime-family investigation, Craig Miles caught sheltering him, and Miles arrested on camera in the epilogue with NFW's Boston offices shut down under federal investigation. Bloodhunt took the World Title from Shane Southern two falls to one. Rick Ryconik wrestled what he announced as the last match of his career and won it. The promotion went off the air for the better part of a year, and its own archive would later note the hiatus in a single line.

The lottery, the conferences, and the men behind the masks Written by AI, 2026

NFW came back in July 2003 as a league. The Ultratitle Lottery Draft split twenty-four signed wrestlers into two conferences — NFW North under Martena "Doc" Martens, NFW South under Calvin Carlton — handed every draftee an engraved ring, and set them chasing a trophy across a season scored on a published points table. Shawn Matthews chair-shotted Shane Southern the moment Southern was picked. Michael Manson went for DC Stratton with a fork and was removed from the ballroom by security.

The season's real story was a takeover. Avery Prosser assembled the Seven Deadly Sins across All-Star Week, and on Legends Night they dragged the North's own commissioner into the ring and made her sign the conference away, using an unconscious Calvin Carlton's back as a writing surface. The next night's War Games ended with the cage rigged to explode and DC Stratton revealed as the man who had rigged it. The masked Highwayman who kept turning up in car parks and locker rooms all season was part of the same design, and so was a facepainted rumble entrant calling himself WASP who eliminated Hornet from the South Rumble and then ranted backstage about heritage.

All three came off at Crash TV 26, on the same night the promotion's ownership was being argued over at a board table. El Toro, the masked ally who had been quietly helping Shane Southern since the Wild Card round, was caged, beaten and unmasked as Craig Miles — NFW's own founder, who had been written out of his company a year earlier. The Highwayman unmasked as Troy Windham and cut a promo declaring himself the greatest wrestler alive. WASP unmasked as Eddie Mayfield and spent several minutes laying out a years-long conspiracy against Miles that reached back before NFW existed, including releasing Miles's institutionalised father-in-law to attack him.

The season ended at Wrestlebowl I, where Michael Manson beat Shane Southern two falls to one across a plain fall, a ladder match and a thirty-minute Ironman to hold the World Championship and win the first Ultratitle. Southern shook his hand, took the trophy, superkicked him from behind and triple-powerbombed him through it. The trophy did not survive the show.

Tokyo, the Mikoto regime, and the year Doc Silver would not lose Written by AI, 2026

Season Two redrew the map East and West, and sold a quarter of the promotion to a Japanese holding company whose Grand Elder, Susano-O-No-Mikoto, ran the Eastern Conference from a skybox. He humiliated his own protege Kazuo Shizaki on camera for losing, ceremonially burned photographs of eight Western wrestlers while planning to have his own men destroy them, and bribed Maggot to cripple Troy Windham. Doc Silver took the World Championship from Michael Manson on the season's first Crash TV and held it for the better part of a year behind an escalating wall of interference — Ryoko at ringside, Dan Dority and Panda Mask in the aisle, a steel cage collapsing on a challenger at Christmas.

Underneath it ran the strangest domestic storyline the fed ever staged. Cameron Cruise and Beau Michaels-Cruise were billed as estranged husbands; Beau claimed a pregnancy, lost it on air, and disrupted a World Title match by throwing something into the ring. In November a creature called Kooter Michaels-Cruise rampaged backstage claiming to be their son, and Felix Red announced he had built it himself. On the same show, Beau won the right to name a man-servant from the Eastern roster and named Mikoto, who was carried out of his own arena by Beau's escort.

The regime came apart in April. Crash TV 35 gave Mikoto a long on-air apology renouncing his own hardline control and handing the conference to his granddaughter — and then Panda Mask stunnered him and unmasked as Michael Manson, and Mikoto went to hospital. Felix Red beat Doc Silver for the World Championship in the main event. Two days later at Wrestlestock I, Eli Flair made Troy Windham submit in what the fed staged as Windham's retirement match, and Mike Randalls was drugged by a ringside doctor, went berserk through the building, broke the doctor's neck and was arrested.

The season's two conference finals ran the same night in March 2007, and the Western one went seventy minutes — a double count-out fall each, then a barbed-wire cage decider Nova won by holding the larger half of a torn envelope. The special referee branded him with a hot iron and shaved his head afterwards. At Wrestlebowl II Kin Hiroshi took the World Championship from Felix Red in a Last Man Standing match, and Craig Miles and Eddie Mayfield wrestled an I Quit match for nearly two hours that ended with neither man quitting: they reconciled on camera, revealed themselves as the two masked men from an earlier brawl, and reformed a tag team they had broken up years before. The second night retired Eli Flair after thirteen years, sent Michael Manson off on horseback after the promotion's first Vineyard Match, and gave Nova the second Ultratitle at the end of a hundred-and-two-minute match with ten stipulations spun off a wheel.

Version 3.0, and the belt the president would not hand over Written by AI, 2026

Crash TV 40 abandoned the seasonal format outright, and Craig Miles built a National Championship to fill the space with a double-elimination tournament. Teresa Q won it at Supercrash I, becoming the first National Champion and holding the belt undefeated into the summer. The same card gave Dan Ryan the World Championship over a Felix Red whom the new president had forced to swallow his own narcotics backstage before the bell.

That president was Eddie Mayfield, installed at Crash TV 42, and his tenure is the spine of the next four years: a Wellness Plan announced with an on-camera drug test, an enforcer in Cojones Mercado, and a habit of refusing to let champions hold their own belts. When Joe the Plumber beat Nova for the World Championship at Supercrash II, Mayfield had the beaten Nova chaired, declared him fired, and would not hand the new champion the physical title.

Legion and the woman he resurrected as Luci4 ran the fed's horror register through the same years — a barbed-wire noose for the National Champion, a mock memorial for a wrestler who was not dead, and a rivalry with Rook Black conducted almost entirely in threats. The comedy register ran alongside it and was just as committed: a Koopa-on-a-Pole cage match whose own card admits nobody has heard of the stipulation, a Hardkorr Gravy Match, and a six-man tag that ended in two simultaneous pinfalls the referee could not separate.

Crash TV 50 is the fiftieth episode and the largest single document the fed left: a War Games, a Bunkhouse Stampede won by Captain Justice, WildStar losing his career to his own former protege in front of his wife and sons, and a hundred-and-one-minute five-way Pentagram main event fought through the crowd, a production truck and the presidential skybox, which Joe the Plumber survived with President Mayfield forced to count the fall himself after both referees were knocked out. Nine months later, on Reloaded 02, Dorchester Stratton threw lye in that champion's eyes on a talk-show set and blinded him, and Mayfield rewarded the man who did it with the next title shot.

The Windham Clan, the Triple Crown, and the last tour Written by AI, 2026

Troy Windham came back in September 2010 disguised as a fan, attacked Cameron Cruise before their scheduled match, then went through the commentary team, the ring announcer, a security guard and a cameraman. At Reloaded 05 Mayfield signed him and put a hundred thousand dollars on his head in the same segment, and Windham answered by revealing the Windham Clan and having them flatten the assembled roster and the president's own podium. At Supercrash III the Clan beat a Team NFW anchored by Mayfield himself in a five-on-five elimination match, and took half the promotion.

The wrestler who benefited most from the chaos had been in NFW since 2001 without winning anything. Castor Strife beat Impulse in a thirty-seven-minute final at Reloaded 09 for the vacant World Championship — his first title in a ten-year career there — and held it for eighteen months while Eric Dane methodically took his house, his production company and his possessions off him through a contract clause he had not read. At Supercrash IV Mayfield merged the Pure, National and Elite Championships into a Triple Crown, and at Reloaded 11 Rook Black won it — the moment NFW's own championship ledger marks by noting that two of its belts stopped existing.

Reloaded 13 ended the Windham story. Troy Windham had already told JJ Deville, in private, that he had spent five years having him blackballed specifically to build him — and that he had always intended Deville to take his place. In the retirement match against Mike Randalls, Deville chair-shotted both men and then stacked Randalls on top of Windham so that Windham took the fall, and retired. Deville, Dorchester Stratton and Blaine Hollywood ran in to beat both men afterwards, and left as the Hellfire Club.

The last full year was a world tour. NFW ran Sydney twice, Berlin, Helsinki, Oslo and Vientiane, opened its Grand Prix to wrestlers from outside the promotion entirely, and staged a scavenger hunt across a Laotian city as a tournament round. Impulse took the World Championship off Castor Strife in a scaffold match at Reloaded 14, on the same night the fed gave Castor Strife its own Wrestler of the Year award. At Reloaded 15, Jack Harmen won a sixteen-man Grand Prix Royale and a shot at that championship, and Dorchester Stratton beat Jack Bryant in the fifth match of a best-of-five for the Television Championship after Bryant's own cornerwoman threw in the towel. That is the last card the fed's archive holds. NFW went on posting news into August 2014, and the shot Jack Harmen won was never wrestled in any document that survives.

CSWA

Affiliate of NFW (2000)
Two facts: a federation affiliate listing, and a genuine property relationship — NFW's own copyright line states "The Ultratitle is property under a leasing agreement with the CSWA," and NFW's own About page says the Ultratitle trophy was donated to NFW out of CSWA folklore.

World eWrestling Alliance (fWo)

Affiliate of NFW (2000)
Listed as a federation affiliate on NFW’s own affiliates page (“FEDERATION AFFILIATES … fWo”) and among the friends on its fwrestling.com-era page (“FWO (fans wrestling organization)”), and the reason NFW ran on a nfw.fwowrestling.com subdomain around 2005–06 at all — a hosting relationship as well as an affiliate one.

Affiliate of NFW (2000)
A hosting relationship rather than a formal affiliate listing — fwrestling.com hosted NFW’s own roleplay boards for the whole of its life and was its middle-era web host. fwrestling.com is a hosting platform, not a promotion in its own right.

Ultimate Wrestling Alliance (UWA, 1992–97)

Shared continuity with NFW (2000)
New Frontier Wrestling was announced 1 June 2000 and built out of the Ultimate Wrestling Alliance’s own community, three years after the UWA closed on the Prodigy service — “a host of fW.com regulars, UWA veterans and other random applicants quickly joined.” The same page names UWA regulars who continued straight onto NFW’s roster: Shane Southern, Michael Manson, Wildstar, Alex Wylde and Bloodhunt. Recorded as shared-continuity rather than successor-of: NFW’s own About page frames the promotion as something new built from the UWA’s people, not the UWA continuing under a different name — community continuity, not institutional succession.

ChampionShip Wrestling Alliance (CSWA)

Affiliate of NFW (2000)
NFW lists the CSWA as a federation affiliate on its own affiliates page and names it in its own retrospective History alongside the UWA and the fWo as a fellow PRODIGY league; NFW's own copyright line states that "The Ultratitle is property under a leasing agreement with the CSWA", the trophy the CSWA's own founding memoir records being created in its first years as the "Ultimate Title".
