NFW Crash TV 20 1 Apr 2004 North Charleston Colesium Full Circle A drop falls on the windshield as he pulls into the parking lot. Campbell was never the kind of guy to forget where he came from. He was also a creature of habit. No matter how many times he’d gone to an arena, no matter where the arena was, there were two things that always remained the same. First, he’d get there earlier than anyone else on most occasions. When he had a history with a company the guys in production usually had a standing bet going based on who would get to the game first. Second, he’d pick a parking spot he liked, and he always got it. Was this obsessive? Perhaps it was. Most who got to know him eventually chalked it up to work ethic. Campbell was a throwback in trendy clothes. He never had a problem getting a job in the wrestling business once people knew him. It was pretty simple to figure out what they were getting. He was reliable, and predictable. Traits that are hard to find in a business originally founded via traveling carnival. Unfortunately, sometimes those traits work against you. As he pulled into the parking lot he noticed that the production crew simply was no where to be found. A smile parted his lips as tonight the drinks were on someone else. A quick look at the dashboard clock confirmed that he was indeed two and one half hours early for commissary. Early, even by his standards. The Z4 revved into the general pubic parking area. Campbell liked being able to walk unbeknownst among the fans after a show and the only real way to do that is to park with the fans. You’d be amazed the things you can find out about your performance just by listening to the people who paid to be there. It’s honest, it’s sometimes brutally honest. But on one of those good nights, one fan can change your entire career with perspective that they didn’t know they gave you. Again, honesty sometimes works against you. Seconds pass, and the final turn to space 114 section B was in sight. He liked this spot. It had a great view of the water park down the hill. His thoughts lingered for a second, remembering the last time he was here, in this car, a night so long ago, with her. He turned into section B, and standing in space 114, was Vivian Ryconik. The car slowed, she stepped back, and the chapter which ended quite a while before seemed opened, the place mark removed. Campbell rolled into space 114, the steam from his engine block cascading up the frame of Ryconik. Apparently the rain didn’t bother her much at all. Damn, she was beautiful. He thought he’d be more bothered or upset than he was upon first sight of Vivian. After all, he hadn’t heard from her since the accident. One day he was preparing to propose marriage, the next she was gone. There were no explanations, no parting sorrows. She was a wrestler’s daughter. He was a cripple. That was all different now. His hip, his scars they healed. Physically he was the same. The question for him was whether or not he was ready for confronting those he left behind. Of all the battles and all of the potential match-ups, the one meeting he was most afraid of was the one about to happen and the thing that bothered him most wasn’t the person in front of him. It was the person that wasn’t with them. The palm-sized box on the dash was grasped within his gloved hand and put cautiously into the front pocket of his fanny pack. Turning off the headlamps of the vehicle, Vivian stood against the grey backdrop of the garage wearing an outfit he’d seen once before, grey knee boots, grey mini, and top coat. She knew how to push his buttons. Aidan almost missed the "Party’s Over" t-shirt under the top coat. With a moment of preparation he exhaled, released the interior trunk latch and stepped out of the vehicle. If this moment was ever going to be over, he needed to take the first awkward step. Moving towards the back of the vehicle he spoke.. "Hello Vivian, you look remarkable this afternoon." He thought for a moment that his voice may have cracked.. but she seemed to have issues of her own. "Aidan.." Campbell continued his motion around the car, grabbing the Louisville Slugger from the passenger side of the vehicle he stood by the open trunk gathering and organizing his attire and belongings before the walk to the locker area. Vivian moved to stand next to him at the back of the vehicle. "Aidan, I really, I had all these things I wanted to say to you over the past few weeks since you’ve come back.. now that you’re standing here I.." "You don’t need to say any of them Viv." "What do you mean by that?" Ryconik seemed surprised by the abrupt statement. It wasn’t something she expected coming from Campbell, not tonight. "What I mean Viv is that this is a work night. I’ve come to the arena to work. This isn’t the time for a rousing revival of the Campbell family frolics. You know me well enough to find me here, you know me well enough to know that I’m not going to let anything get in the way of business until after the match tonight." Vivian was more than shocked, she was beside herself. She looked good, damn good, just the way she knew he wanted her. He didn’t look different, in fact the time apart made him look even better than she remembered. "So how do I get to talk to you if we don’t speak at an arena hon?" "How about you call me when you’re not living with a guy I have to kick the ass of on a regular basis. That would help." That would have been funny had it not been something she dreaded would be said in a conversation. What she didn’t know was how much it hurt him to be such a hard ass in the first place. All he wanted was to start turning back the clock right now, with one second, one embrace.. But did she want that? Part of her did, but more importantly, did the guy burning into the garage at 60 miles per hour want that for her? Campbell grabbed his duffle bag and slung the baseball bat over his left shoulder. The trademarked motorcycle leather sat comfortably under his arm. Moments passed and a rental car screeched to a halt in parking spot 115. Someone was in his spot.. the spot she had made him park in every time they came here since they were together. 115 would have to do for now. It was who was in 114 that mattered. Too much was starting to come together in his mind. He didn’t like it. He didn’t like the games, and like definitely wasn’t the word to use concerning the man he saw standing next to her. What he didn’t expect was how long it would take or what form it would take. Vivian looked uncomfortable. She looked uncomfortable and beside herself. "I needed to know Shane, and I couldn’t ask you to come here with me." Campbell stopped. This was ripping him up inside. He had a job to do tonight. He had to beat Shane Southern. More importantly, to do that he couldn’t let his feelings for Vivian get inside his head, not now. He waited, knowing full well that Southern wasn’t going to be able to let it go. "Damn it Viv, give me a straight answer.. why’d you take off to meet this guy?" Campbell wouldn’t be able to walk away from this. In fact, he wasn’t going to be strong enough to put this out of his own mind. If he was going to be handicapped tonight, he had to make damn sure Southern was. He needed to get into Shane’s head and it had to be good. In his own thoughts he apologized to Vivian, turned slowly and clutched the small box in his jacket pocket. "Because she loves you Shane and it’s pretty obvious that she doesn’t want to hurt you." Campbell tossed the box at Southern who damn near dropped it due to being somewhat distracted. "I’m going to get some gym time in before our match hot shot." Campbell turned to walk away but not without one last statement as Southern opened the box to reveal a 3 carat rock that probably blinded the poor guy that put it in its setting. "The ball’s in your court kid.. you use it or I will." "God", is all that can be heard from a breathless Ryconik. We fade to black as Campbell heads for the entrance to the arena. He shakes his head as a tear comes to his left eye. He didn’t like what he just did, and he may have lost the only woman he ever really cared for.
