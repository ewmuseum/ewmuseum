Week 6: Too Much Shock in the System? Posted by Natrone Rice on 13 Jan 2006 It started with uncomfortable racial tensions on the night before Christmas, where then it continued with Beau Michaels and Yori Yakamo having some moments of their own on Crashmas. By the time New Year's rolled around, Joey Melton's face got planted on Troy Windham's briefs and internet blogs, media members as well as conservative groups have calling out NFW on its growing "shock value" ever since. Families First activist Gary Pedersen didn't know where to start, "First...I almost can see Shaniqua Carlton's bosom flopping out of her...outfit. Then, there's dildos and latent homoreroticism on Christmas...and they top it off by shoving a man's face in another man's crotch. How the FCC can allow stuff like this on television is beyond me. What if some child sees this?" Some wrestling fans haven't had many more encouraging remarks, some describing the Melton moment as "disgusting," while others are using this, Troy Windham, Beau Michaels and Yori Yakamo Jr. as a prime example of NFW's shift towards "shock value" entertainment. IF you think it stops there, the UWA show broadcast brought hundreds of phone calls and complaints from both African-Americans as well as Caucasians in reference to the pier-six brawl between the Original Showstoppers and Dirty Rotten Scoundrels, where explicit language and racial slurs were broadcast live. Many groups decried Saafir Muhammed and Avery Prosser's usage of the "N" word as a horrible example to young children. When reached for comment, President of New Frontier Wrestling Quentin Sullivan couldn't be reached for comment. Naturally, the founder of both NFW, ESEN and key cog in the UWA machine, Craig Miles did respond to questions over the telphone. On Pedersen he remarked, "I've got a young kid. I don't let him watch wrestling right now. There's options on the internet, on your satellite box, on your cable box to block that sort of thing. If some kid finds his way to watching this at Midnight, or seeing porn which is just as likely -- then the parent hasn't taken the right precautions in preventing that, if its an issue for them." On the racial undertones on the UWA show, "You're seeing something that's heavily overseen by someone that's black, proud, angry and from the streets. That is America whether you choose to look it in the eye or not." On Joey Melton and Troy Windham, Miles remarked, "At least he wasn't the hooker in Season 1." It should be noted that Miles and Melton square off this upcoming Week 7 and Miles didn't want to ruin any of the 'material' he was coming up with due to what he called "downright embarrassing" for a man so worried about how he looks. As this reporter sees it, shock and awe has always been apart of New Frontier Wrestling, but at the end of the day, its never taken the focus off what all pundits would agree is some of the best wrestling ever seen. While its frustrating to not just have it all without shock and awe, one might have to wonder if it weren't for all the 'shock,' would the wrestlers work as hard to overcome that in the ring?
