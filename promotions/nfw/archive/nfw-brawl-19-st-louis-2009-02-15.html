NFW Brawl 19: St. Louis 15 Feb 2009 KILL THEM. KILL THEM NOW. There are moments in life, you only dream about. For many NFW fans, for the majority of the new locker room and regime of NFW under Eddie Mayfield…for once they were unified. LEGION AND VON DOOM MUST DIE! 8 teams entered into a tournament, the winner of the tournament would face the team of Legion/von Doom. If Legion or von Doom get pinned, they’re OUT of NFW. The winning team would also earn a title shot at the EMT Champions. But more importantly…LEGION AND VON DOOM MUST DIE! So, without further adieu, we go to the first round. Chris Chambers and The Cosmo Kid vs. Dos Equis and... Chris Chambers & The Cosmo Kid vs. Dos Equis & … When we last left Dos Equis out in promoting NFW via television spots world, he was snorting Coke Zero and promising to find a mythical, epic luchador tag team partner. Luckily for him, his first round opponents would be the formerly mentally challenged Chris Chambers and his new partner, The Cosmo Kid. Retrained by Steve Knox and possibly in a Clockwork Orange setting for Chambers personally, the duo came out to the ring first and looked ready to rumble. Out next came the newly entered into NFW luchador, Dos Equis. Strangely, he came out alone…and slapping ten’s and high-five’s with the air next to him. Suffice to say, the Coke Zero had reduced his brain to zero calories. The referee checked with Equis to see where his partner was, but was answered in the only way Equis could. "He’s standing right next to me, moron!" Well, never anger a ref in St. Louis ‘cause apparently he’ll willingly place you in a handicap match. The match actually went decently with Equis starting off against Chambers and both wrestlers actually providing a good show. Apparently, Chambers has come a long way under Knox’s tutelage and Equis is way more gifted physically than mentally at the moment. Armdrags, dropkicks, hurricanrana’s oh my! Equis managed to get the advantage during the latter, but became increasingly frustrated with the ref not recognizing any of his tags to his partner. Each time he stepped onto the apron, a ten count would begin and either Chambers or Cosmo would kick him in the head or drag him back in. To put it bluntly, his hot tags were on ice. The end of the match came as Equis got the advantage on Cosmo, put him on his shoulders and tagged is partner…waiting for him to come off the top rope. Unfortunately, the waiting period cost him as Cosmo Kid caught him in a forward rolling cradle for the 3 count, Equis was seen arguing with his partner over what happened, while Chambers and Cosmo Kid celebrated in the ring. Illumination of a Hillbilly Culture Now were back in the ring with Teresa Quaranta, who's having a relatively light moment with the crowd. The lights are dimmed, a giant projector has been wired up, and it's almost time for the feature presentation. The crowd pops as she does the intro, obviously relishing the portion of tonight's festivities that would see her enjoying Happy Atken Fun Time. She cringes at the applause, but gets on with the festivities, kicking off the NFW Last Few Months In Review - We see moments from across the New Frontier since eh... Octoberish, set to O Fortuna and featuring an oddly disproportionate amount of Ms. Quaranta's finest moments. Before the video can complete itself, the house lights come up, and a stupid greased up hillbilly appears on the ramp, rambling about vagina juice and why he is the rightful NFW Champion. This lasts for about 3 minutes, but before TQ can remind the stupid greased up hillbilly that he is, indeed, a stupid greased up hillbilly, familiar music hits and a midget with goofy shades, blue pants, and a megaphone enters from the other ramp to tell the crowd that while the stupid greased up hillbilly is awesome in every way shape and form and he loves him very very much in a heterosexual way, he is the rightful NFW Champion for some reason or other. The stupid greased up hillbilly is not willing to take this guff, tensions escalate, and a fight breaks out. As the greased up hillbilly and the megaphoned mental midget go at each other, Teresa rolls out of the ring and watches from a bemused distance. She eventually backs into the crowd, scooping a hand into a spectator's popcorn and throwning it into the fracas. Before she can go much further, someone breaks out from the crowd and jumps her, and we have to assume that SOMEBODY in St. Louis is VERY SERIOUS about ending the littering that goes on at wrestling events. There's a rush of flying limbs, a crowd of people running away from (and towards) the carnage. By the time security gets on the scene and pulls the former National Champion away from the carnage, the original assailant is nowhere to be found. The fun mood of the segment's been ruined, Teresa runs her hand through her hair nervously, snarls, and storms outta there. Damn party crashers. Brock Alyas and KMC vs. Storm Brock Alyas came out first for his team - alone - and didn't wait for Kooter. He threw himself at Tornado and hammered him with forearms to the face and shoulders, and tossed him over the top like a rag doll just in time for Hurricane to attack him from behind. He knocked Brock into the corner and whipped him across the ring, just about the time that Kooter made it to the ring. When Hurricane moved in to follow up, Brock caught him on the side of the face with an elbow and quickly hit the EXPLODJAH PLEX for an easy three. Brock then left the ring without acknowledging Kooter's presence. Of course, it took another five minutes for officials to clear Kooter from the ring, explaining to him that the match was over and no, he doesn't get to beat down either opponent anymore. WINNERS: Brock Alyas and Kooter Michaels-Cruise Rook Black and Impulse vs. The SST The SST started things off with their manager, the Brotha from Anotha Motha, Leroy Brown, getting on the microphone and declaring that they were a well oiled machine, and that it takes more than just two random mooks coming to the ring together to overthrow the Swat Team. In case you're curious, the fans didn't really believe him. Impulse walked out first, but took a step back and deferred to Rook Black as the National Champion to walk to the ring first. From there, you'd never know Rook and Impulse had never tagged before. The match started off with Rook against IROC, and IROC never had a chance. Rook dismantled him with takedown after takedown and didn't let up for a second. The only opening IROC had was luckily landing on his feet after a backdrop attempt, and he fell toward his partner for the tag. Cousin Vinny didn't seem too pleased to be in the ring with the National Champion, so he tried the reverse psychology gig by talking trash at Impulse. It worked. Impulse asked for the tag and got it. Cousin Vinny mocked Impulse's smaller frame and mask for a few seconds, but when he turned to IROC to point it out and he turned back around, he ate a superkick from the Grand Prix finalist. Neither member of the Swat Team was able to come back from that. The match was an exercise at that point, ending when Impulse locked an ankle submission on IROC, causing the big man to tap out almost immediately.
