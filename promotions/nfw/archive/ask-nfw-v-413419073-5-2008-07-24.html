As you recall, the championship was brought back during Season 1 during the Eastern Conference inception. For Season 1, I think it was just a tool on [name withheld]' end to develop a storyline with Manson and Not Marx...as well as a cool side attraction to Manson/Southern.
For Season 2, the title was basically brought back once again on [name withheld]' Eastern End to placate and entice a certain roster member that I don't really need to name here. From there, [name withheld] and I agreed that after it left that person that the title would become akin to an M.V.P. type of award to the Eastern Conference.
In terms of its devalument/friction -- I actually think neither really happened. At the end of Season 1, you had Manson/Southern who were irrevocably tied to the title's humble beginnings. Once Felix Red took over the title in Season 2, I think it escaped some of the 'godbooking' attached to it and became something that helped define the finale of Kin/Felix's war throughout the whole season. I did choose to keep the Western Conference out of the title picture as much as possible.
Aside from Barry and Dove, I don't think anyone else involved with the title at the time would've trusted my decision-making process and I didn't want to deal with the IM headaches preluding a result if certain West individuals were the challengers.
In the end, I think it was more of [name withheld]' baby than mine during the Seasons. Felix lost to Yori, who lost to Nova...which put the title in its place in the end. I saw it as something that could help build storylines in a structure that was becoming increasingly difficult to develop week to week in the fed. And while I felt the first Season 2 reign went longer than it should of in hindsight, I do believe Kin/Felix's war over the title made it something 'special' and 'storied' enough to carry on past the season structure to where we are now.

Off to Valinor — 24 July 2008 The story between Miles and Mayfield as you know was one of the main factors that got me to join NFW for this season... how did the idea come about and if you could do a What If to it what would be done e.g. adding people to the story etc?
As with all Miles/Mayfield things, something happened in a JK/JN IM one day. This is how everything wacky happens for the record. :cool:
I think midway through Season 1, we were noticing the lack of storylines infused to the 'season structure'. So, as usual with JN, he started UWA speculation. Then, it went to a UWA invasion...but I felt that long-term a UWA invasion was a rehash, so I pitched Mayfield being behind everything.
So, that pretty much covers the Season 1 storyline, which used the UWA invasion as a cover for the eventual Season 2 Mayfield/Miles set-up.
As far as Season 2 goes, shortly after Wyoming...and the positive response to everything in general thus far. (Feud of the Year, etc.) And then with the way the fWo storylines were working...I had the idea of "What if it was all a ruse by Miles/Mayfield on the industry as a whole?" I figured if we got that to work, no heels in our 'history' could match the scope of what would be pulled off. JN stamped it immediately and we were on our way. So, if you backtrack...Miles/Deacon went according to plan in the fWo without me knowing Mayfield/Miles would hug it out at the end.
I think the characters that were pulled in throughout Season 2 were more than enough. We had Adler, Troy Windham, Joey Melton, KVC, Randalls, Eli...I'm pretty sure the ONLY old-school guy who didn't show up to the party was Hornet. And Hornet was around in Season 1.
I would've liked JN to have been more active during the middle of the season, and the real Miles/Mayfield II to have actually happened. At the same time, Craig Miles has never won a World Championship 'cause that never happened...and that's kind of cool to me as well.
On a sidenote, with Miles/Mayfield claiming they destroyed the fWo/CSWA. This was only done because both feds had gone on hiatus and it was a nice loose end we were able to tie up for our own benefits. If both feds were active...well, we wouldn't have had that opportunity to cherry pick 'em. hehe

Off to Valinor — 24 July 2008 Well, I do have one question that does stick out, but it's less about NFW's history and more about the behind-the-scenes.
How much of NFW is actually planned and how much of it is just happy accident? -- Mike 33.4% planned, 66.6% happy accident. I think what happens is that there's a loose framework going into each show from my mind...with help from JN, it gets ordered somewhat more and we have an idea of what we want for certain angles, matches, etc.
Then, everyone RP's which initially stretches the framework. Then I read the RP and have my own reactions to it, which stretches it a tad further.
Then, I get stoned and write the shows...and that's probably when the whole thing snaps and hits us all in the face. :cool:

Off to Valinor — 24 July 2008 Do you think that the idea to scrap the Ultratitle in NFW has revitalised the fed along with the new amount of people coming in? Would nova if he wanted be able to put the UT on the line?
1) Its made things alot SIMPLER. I can't stress enough how hard it was to keep the season structure going, when I'd end up having to replace over 50% of my roster by the time we finished. On the flip side, there was a large contingent of top-notch handlers that didn't know how to handle in the season structure as well. There's more than a few people that joined at the beginning of Season 2 (Devin, Pants) that flaked out for whatever reason and are now our most entrenched veteran handlers. The concept was one-of-a-kind and loads of fun, but if I were forced to do a Season 3 vs. Closing NFW, I would've closed NFW due to the tedious management Season 3 would have involved.
I think the House Shows have revitalized the fed moreso than scrapping the Ultratitle, although we'd never have the house shows without scrapping the Ultratitle...so I guess...yeah, scrapping it has improved handler activity probably by 300-400%. There's way more feedback, way more interaction and just way more freedom for everyone to do what they want.
2) Nova defending the Ultratitle would be akin to the New York Giants defending the Super Bowl or Spain defending the Euro Soccer Championship. In other words, Nova can say its on the line...but I wouldn't recognize it in any 'official' capacity.

Off to Valinor — 24 July 2008 Fine choices (helps I've been reading the archives recently), Armando coming back would not be a bad idea... one more honest to God or whatever deity worshipped by anyone... question: Thoughts on the Asylum crossover last season?
Joe and I chatted ALOT during that summer. He mentioned that he really wanted to get the tA Immortal Title back in tA. I told him I could make it happen and I'd contact Ford. After discussing things w/ Ford and Joe, I got the idea regarding the match that happened within the Western Conference playoffs.
To be honest, there wasn't too much 'thought' put into the whole affair...it was more of "what can we do that'll be completely insane, but still work on a 'wrestling' show." I think I definitely surprised everyone w/ McCabe's actions in a good way.
All in all, it was alot of fun...and I could've seen more crossover storylines, but tA got alot darker in content/attitude from that point forward....while NFW was trying to push itself a little closer towards being about wrestling. It'd be very easy to work together, but I think it'd help NFW's story alot more than tA's at this point.
NFW would have tA on its show and people marking somewhat, while tA would lose alot of its sinister credibility its been growing since the crossover.
GreggG — 25 July 2008 Do you think that the idea to scrap the Ultratitle in NFW has revitalised the fed along with the new amount of people coming in? Would nova if he wanted be able to put the UT on the line?
The whole "season" system was actually my brainchild. A lot of Japanese leagues use that system, and Eric Bischoff actually propsed it for WCW at some point in time, if the rumor mill's correct.
I really thought Season 1 got off to an awesome start. The draft was excellent. I also liked the ROH style we were going for -- there was an "honor" code where opponents had to shake hands or risk being deducted points, etc. But that became a problem behind-the-scenes with more than just a few of the members who didn't like the honor code, didn't buy into the season standings structure. And another problem is with a system like that, EVERY character has to lose at some point, and that really got to a few people in the league at that time.
The seasons structure was fun. But it got really difficult to keep up a format like that w/ standings while you deal with guys having to leave, newcomers wanting to come in, egos, etc.
I was also a problem since I came in w/ Troy Windham, had huge plans, and then as I usually tend to do flaked out because of real world stuff. And when you have a major character and a lot of stuff is built around that character, that becomes difficult.
So, I think the season structure involving the Ultratitle was a grand failed experiment on my end. Katz did a great job massaging it to something that worked better than what we were doing, even if it took blowing up a cage to do so.
