Biron — 22 January 2009 Just wanted to say 'Hey' and I'm looking forward to writing with the rest of ya. I wouldn't mind a little feedback on The Bull's speech either, never really written an over-the-top mobster type.
Thanks, Biron CaryTurkington — 22 January 2009 It's an intro, for sure. Though all we know is that he's italian, apparently sells blow or skag, and hates "th" sounds. Beyond that, the character is extremely shallow at this point. What is going to discern this dude from any other mafia stereotype? I mean, ****, there's already a Sammy "The Bull" Gravano, so you're behind in the nickname factor.
That's not to say the character is loss. That's about 500 words of promo you got there, which isn't nearly enough to judge a character. If I were you, I'd try to make some sort of story arc from this, either through developing the character in prose or attacking other Larrys. It's tough to get a hold of the character at this point.
This is nitpicky, but with the accent, I don't see how "monts" works with the rest of it. Like that, I pronounce it as "want", which makes the dude sound foreign. And then, later on, you ruin all that by saying "worth" . Maybe stay away from "th" words as much as possible?
Overall, I can't say good or bad--it's just extremely incomplete. Remember, this is a min-RP session, so keep on posting until they tell you "no" or "get the **** out of this league".
Biron — 22 January 2009 Oh there's a story to come, simply an introduction. I definitely bit the bullet on "The Bull" nickname apparently, probably something I should have checked out. I still have Vic the Brick though, :cool:.
Monts - months, though looking back it shoulda been munts. Like I said, I'm working out the kinks but I just wanted to get an intro piece up for him. I appreciate your feedback, I'll certainly have to go over it again more than twice to iron out mistakes. Thanks.
CaryTurkington — 22 January 2009 I was thinking "munts" too. One thing I'd suggest checking for accent work is looking at the linguistic codas that are good and bad in the language. Here's one I just found (see the second page):
http://courses.umass.edu/kbj/ling201/content/coda_constraints_essay.pdf It might be tough to decipher, but if you can figure out what those sounds are between the [ ] you can see which are natural in english and which aren't.
Keep on working on those kinks.
