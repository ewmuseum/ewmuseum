Strife, Dane, to Negotiate Title Match ON AIR in Tampa! Posted by Devin Millwood on 6 May 2012 Following some heavy internet rumors, we can now confirm that NFW World Heavyweight Champion Castor Strife will negotiate a title match contract with top contender "The Only Star" Eric Dane. The negotiations will take place at the former home studio of Strife, now owned by Eric Dane, located in Hollywood, California, and will be televised in an on-air segment at BRAWL: Tampa next week. Sources indicate that this had been in development since the night of Reloaded 11, at the insistence of NFW President Eddie Mayfield. President Mayfield let both men know that a resolution needed to be in order, and that if "key stipulations" needed to be ironed out, it would be done on-camera, at an upcoming show. The scheduled "negotiation segment" was made official two hours ago. What the match stipulations will be are anyone's guess, but it's strongly expected that Strife will insist on getting his company back in some form. The champion lost his production company, Castor V. Strife Productions, in a stipulation match between Dane and former associate Alex Austin that took place in January at SUPERCRASH IV. The contract, which Strife unknowingly rubber stamped when Austin and Lana Dremire slipped it into his paper work, stated that if Dane beat Austin in a Submission Rules match, he would gain ownership of Castor Strife Productions. Since Dane's victory and subsequent company takeover, Strife has been featured on a first-of-it's-kind ESEN docu-series, "Beyond Castor Strife", which follows the champion as he travels the country defending his title, living paycheck to paycheck. Dane, on the other hand, has flaunted Strife's studio wealth by remodeling his house and selling his valuables on the streets of Compton (a taped segment that aired at Reloaded 11). As of last year, Castor Strife Productions was rumored to be worth millions of dollars. The company is known for producing violent pornographic films (or: "Transgressive Euro-Extremity" indie genre), as well as half the episodes of NFW East: Ultratitle Season 1, which many found to be more offensive than the films.
