NFW news: Q&A w/Brock Alyas

28 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Q&A w/Brock Alyas Posted by Devin Millwood on 28 Oct 2010 Devin Millwood sits down with "The Ninth Mile" Brock Alyas for a Q&A. Find out Brock's thoughts on his upcoming opponent, High Flyer, the state of NFW under President Eddie Mayfield, and much more! (FADEIN: DEVIN MILLWOOD stands in a dimly lit studio wearing a tight-fitting black NFW polo with red-lettering) MILLWOOD: Perhaps no NFW athlete today has experienced the highs and lows of Brock Alyas. Coming off a red-hot rookie campaign in 2008, Brock’s career took a turn for the worse when substance addiction caught up to him. Now on the comeback trail after a stint in rehab, I sat down with Brock this week to find out where he’s at, and where’s headed. (CUTTO: Millwood sitting across from BROCK ALYAS in the same studio, with a backlit ‘NFW’ sign on the wall behind them) MILLWOOD: Alright, Devin Millwood for NFW.e-wrestling.org. I’m here with the (jokingly) "crack smoking, head-stomping, ninth mile" Brock Alyas. You started off HOT in NFW. Unbeaten in your first eight matches, 2008’s Rookie of the Year. Then you go through a rough stretch, and your career is threatened after NFW institutes a zero tolerance drug policy. Now you’re clean, you’re back in action. Tell us where you are in your career right now, where you see yourself. ALYAS: Right now, I see myself in recovery. I see this as a point in my life that will prove to the world the type of athlete I am and to my company what type of asset I am. I won’t beat around the bush, you’ve all seen the shape I’m in. For once, I am considered the underdog and I feel at ease with that role; and I know for a fact I’m going to thrive at it. MILLWOOD: First match back, you took on Sean Stevens despite being significantly weaker after rehab. No easy feat, but you did well. Now you’re signed on to face High Flyer at Reloaded 3, another big name. Do you feel like you weren’t challenging yourself enough? Is this part of the "new Brock" stepping up to the plate to make a serious run? ALYAS: It’s a new day and yes there is a new Brock - apart from the lousy bum you’d seen in 2009, same old monster but now with my 100% attention. A new Brock, but one that will remind fans of an old Brock. A well conditioned freak of nature athlete that has the heart of a lion. There’s a euphoria that runs through my veins when I’m in that ring, adding to our company’s injured reserve. A high no crack cocaine in the country could give me. Let me put it this way: there’s a new and improved Brock people will quickly find out about. MILLWOOD: Let’s talk about your match at Reloaded. You and High Flyer, "Reel to Reel" Strap Match, a stipulation bout set up by Castor Strife to determine the number one competitor to the Television Title. What are your feelings on that? Do you feel Blanche Barnett being the guest referee will help, hinder you, or is it of no consequence? A lot of guys wouldn’t be thrilled taking a match set up by the current champion. ALYAS: About the Reel to Reel Strap Match - I’ve heard Flyer’s name, I already know he’ll underestimate me and I have a feeling my followers will be only to pleased to see a much improved Brock since the (laughingly) Stevens match. I realized I put my soul on the line when I agreed to play pawn in that freakshow Strife’s little game. I don’t care who the guest referee is, I intend to break that dusty artifact in two. I never go into a match planning to let any filthy sideshow carnie decide the outcome. I know Strife will pull some strings, but he’s in for a war if he thinks I’ll ever let him have the upper hand. MILLWOOD: Crash 47, Chicago, Illinois. Eddie Mayfield is inaugurated as NFW President. Two years later- is NFW better off now than it was two years ago? I know you’ve had your dealings with him. ALYAS: It’s definitely taken a different direction. I’ve come to realize it’s get on board, or get the fuck off the boat, NOW -- so I’ve made the necessary changes in my life to adapt and not limit my opportunities here. All said, I’m gonna play ball. MILLWOOD: How do you see the landscape right now? JTP’s still the champion, but Sean Stevens calls himself the PURE Heavyweight Champion. ALYAS: (quickly interrupting) I didn’t know that Heavyweight championships could simply be made over night and… I always thought championships had to be WON. Although everybody’s convinced that I couldn’t even test negative to compete in a match against Triple X, even if I WON that PAPER BELT -- I’d burn it into ashes and piss on the remainders. MILLWOOD: Dan Ryan seems hell bent on a showdown with both men. Who, in your mind, is the top dog in NFW right now, who do you see contending in the future, and do you see yourself making a run eventually? ALYAS: The balance of power around here has certainly tightened up. JTP is a marathon man who I don’t think has it in him to lose a match. You can pound his head into the ground all night, that googly-eyed prick will still come back and whoop your ass. He’s going to be tough to beat only because nobody’s yet found a way to. Dan Ryan’s a legend in my book. I couldn’t even talk down about him for a minute, the career he’s had and the way he’s handled himself throughout it; I have nothing bad for you little marks to gossip about there. And, of course I see ol’ Brock in the center stage. I’m like that brilliant Heisman winning quarterback, whose since lost his way being caught up in the publicity and pressure that’s going to rewrite his career and elevate it to a level nobody even saw possible. MILLWOOD: Cool man, thanks for your time. Devin Millwood for NFW.e-wrestling.org. Be sure to check in with us, ‘cause we’ve got more interviews, rumors, media, and all kinds of good stuff. (FADEOUT)
