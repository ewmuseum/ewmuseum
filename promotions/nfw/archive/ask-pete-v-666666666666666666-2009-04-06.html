peen. My goal with Impulse was to see if I could run a character completely anonymously and be successful at it. That was literally the only thing that I had in mind when I started talking to Katz about it. I knew I wanted to run Randall Knox b/c he's the only idea I've ever come up with besides Flair that lasted past two weeks. (Dez is a separate entity and does not count)
After that it was coming up with a reason for him to wear a mask - because if Ivy found out he was wrestling without her knowledge he was convinced she would kick his ass.
Everything else about him was an on - the - fly decision. The non-cheating thing, the non-punching thing, even the RP style, was all just a natural evolution.
So he didn't start off as an idealistic character, his 'presence' just flowed that way and now it's really the only thing that makes sense.
Also, peen. Off to Valinor — 6 April 2009 To touch on the 'idealism' aspect of the character. I think that also evolved in my head as a way to build him up as a strong face for NFW, which is hard to do considering today's FW/eW environment. Most faces still want to act like jackasses and smack their enemies in the head with a chair for no reason.
I remember that being the case in my head for the house show match, in which he demanded he get disqualified instead of getting his first victory via someone else's interference.
From that point on, Pete formulated the Impulse Rules of Engagement...and I've regretted my actions ever since. ;)
All kidding aside, Pete ran very far w/ that ball...which in turn, made me excited to book him in specific feuds/matches.

suddenimpact — 7 April 2009 Any advice on writing a female character? (Since, obviously, Ivy's the best female character ever, and it's something I haven't really been able to do.)
I can't tell if you're serious or not. ;-) Honestly, just don't be stupid. Ivy's had her best growth from 2001 to now and I really just looked at all of my friends who are girls and, knowing there's no such thing as a typical woman.
Her temper, her drinking, her stubbornness, her diet, her contradictory relationship as it relates to Triple X and everyone else in her life, are all based on someone I know in real life.
Other than that, drawing a clear distinction between Poison Ivy and Ivy McGinnis helped. You mentioned 'female character' - but what really helped me with Ivy was getting inside the actual woman's head, not the character she plays on TV.

3) Where's Manson vs. Hornet? I'm still waiting on it... :cool: 1) There's been a few. From Ivy's standpoint - Wrestlebowl I, because of everything that it encompassed and drew together. To her it seemed like everything that had been going on with the Last Gunslingers, as you called them, came full circle from her debut in 1995, and everyone involved was finally going to settle up. Where it ended was totally different, but it didn't take away from the moment.
OOC, I loved the lines that referenced how far Ivy had come with Eli, Randalls, Miles, and Adler in the room together.
ELI: "I'm only here because Ivy trusts you." RANDALLS: "Fair enough, you're only here because we trust Ivy." The UWA Pirate TV show. In my opinion, taking the wrestlers, the fans, and the atmosphere into consideration, Eli vs. Mayfield (complete with Ivy's return!) was the single best written WRESTLING match of all of Season 2. Ivy's return just capped it all off for me, and it really had that Philly taste to it.
For Eli... there's three. WrestleStock, with the 'final' match against Troy for obvious reasons, because that was in character the entire reason Eli came back to NFW when he did. OOC I had expected to wage the Ultratitle the entire season and cap the entire thing off with a match with Troy at Wrestlebowl 2 for Eli's "last" match. But I think it worked out better for Eli's entire story arc that he vanquished his nemesis midseason, but then he realizd he still had a chance at doing the one thing he never did: win an Ultratitle.
The Demolition Derby Second Chances card itself - Eli had been the babyface since 2004 (most recently) and had taken a handful of hits for it through the RPing, so when he made it clear that he was absolutely not going to end up in the dirt arena with all of that insanity, I was glad to see him hook Nova's tights and get the pin by any means necessary. I also liked the mixed reaction that the fans gave him for that, it was pretty much exactly what I always envisioned for him: Sure, he's a good guy, he's got fans and he's decent enough, but he is absolutely not a hero and absolutely not opposed to taking shortcuts.
And the West finals. The entire buildup with the RPs was a blast, writing some of the show itself was a lot of fun, and the match itself, whooo boy. It was probably the absolute best I could've hoped for for Eli's exit from actual COMPETITION - for everyone who said (in character, of course) that he was too old, he went through hell and nearly came out on top.
2) I actually covered this with Wrestlebowl 2 night 2. Ivy realized she should've been incredibly pissed about it, but having the kid really mellowed her out.
From her time with Miles in the FWO, as well as their dealings in NFW she knew that he always had a plan, so while she wished she had been involved in the whole thing, she wasn't surprised. Plus, since it was Wrestlebowl, she realized she would probably never see him again and to hold a grudge was just pointless. Never would've thought that two years later they'd both be in the FWO again.
Of course if she wants to see him now she'll have to go to a hospital. What was he thinking? (Sidenote: we really need to write that.)
