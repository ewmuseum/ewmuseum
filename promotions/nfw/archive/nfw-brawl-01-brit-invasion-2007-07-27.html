NFW Brawl 01: BRIT INVASION, 27 July 2007

NFW Brawl 01: BRIT INVASION 27 Jul 2007 Liverpool Olympia, Liverpool (seats 3,750) Introduction Hello all, this is Richard Reese reporting for the NFW Brawl over the world wide web. I was the man who interviewed Kin Hiroshi to start the second night of Wrestlebowl 2, and it seems that while our production teams are getting settled in the United Kingdom, I have the pleasure of opening your eyes to a Brand New World. I know a lot of you have questions, and with all the quasi-secrecy being purported by the fed once again. . . I’m hoping I give you some answers. A lot’s gone down over the weekend and I’m more than excited to see where the future of NFW is headed. That’s first and foremost. We were all a little shocked over the announcement of opening in Britain, but everything that’s transpired over the last seventy-two hours has made me see what a great vision this company has at times. For a lot of these guys and myself, it was our first trip to Europe under any wrestling promotion’s banner, we’ve had great hospitality and the boys have been turning this country upside-down. At the same time, we’re in freaking Liverpool, not even London! Of course, Craig Miles has been vocal backstage about this federation returning to its roots to show its glorious power. I think that was on the 7th bottle of Jameson, haha. The last two nights packed The Olympia, but we weren’t in front of more than a few thousand fans. . . . a few thousand hardcore hooligan drunkards and hobos! Holy crap, has the crowd been insanely loud! Some of these newbies didn’t exactly sit too well with everyone, but when the house came down. . . they ate it up like fish and chips. So, what about the matches, Smitty? Yeah, Yeah, Yeah. . . enough proverbs. Friday night started with a no frills, no pyro, just blaring rock music bombardment as the pitch black lights fired on to Jack Breaker holding a giant fish in the air like He Man’s "Sword of Power." Dusk vs. Jack Breaker Dusk shocked fans already waiting in the ring for Jack Breaker. The match started when Breaker tried to use his speed to take down Dusk but Dusk threw down some lefts and rights to keep him grounded. Dusk kept his advantage by throwing Jack into the turnbuckle and slammed him with kicks and punches to wear him down before throwing him out of the corner and nailed him with a 3/4th Bulldog. Jack tried to mount a comeback, sliding out of the ring, but Dusk stayed on him to avoid letting him getting a break. First he threw him into the guard rail, then he threw him into the apron, but when he tried to throw him into the steel ring steps, Breaker reversed it and Dusk took the dive. Jack tried to slide back in for the ten count win but Dusk slid in just at 9. Breaker charged at Dusk but got caught with the Lights Out Superkick Dusk used. After that it was 1…2…3 and Dusk is the winner. Breaker looked good for being away from the ring so long, while Dusk definitely showed that he could be one of the early risers amongst the rookies in camp, so to speak. Dusk no doubt had an interest in the next match between Clifford Hugo, a greenhorn and Jacob McKail, another PrimeTime Central circuit veteran like Dusk. Clifford Hugo vs. Jacob McKail "X-Ray" by the Maccabees played for Clifford Hugo, marking his entrance into New Frontier Wrestling. Making his entrance into NFW as well was Jacob McKail, who entered as "All My Life" by the Foo Fighters played. The two technical wrestlers went right at it, exchanging reversals and counters against each other. The two followed it up by going back and forth with pin attempts that got the crowd worked up. Jacob got back to his feet after Hugo went for a Back Slide on him and got off the first real offensive move by catching Hugo with a Diving Kneedrop Bulldog he called Fearless Facebuster. McKail tried for a pin but only got two. McKail picked him up only to be hit with T-Bone Suplex to which he only got a two count as well. Both men got up and charged only to take a tumbled over the top rope to the outside. As the referee counted, neither man seemed to notice as they trained punches back and forth. Before either could get back into the ring the referee reached the ten count. Their first match in NFW ended without a winner. One thing to note early is that NFW is placing fifteen minute time limits on the matches, so far, perhaps a trend for a growing roster. Anyway, so as the crew was cleaning the ring, David Tui stomped out to the ring with a microphone in hand. Tui’s been plastering himself in every dirt sheet, mag and some of my colleagues are joking that he’s leaving challenges on bathroom stalls. Whatever the case may be, Tui’s definitely putting his face out there, demanding that anyone face him in the ring. So, Tui’s in the ring yammering away, when all of a sudden the TransSiberian Orchestra starts blasting and MANPOWER stalks out of the entrance curtains, all 7 feet 400 pounds of muscle. . . and orange paint. Manpower vs. David Tui Tui’s eyes bulged as wide as saucers watching this man step over the top rope, but the Fijian Phenom apparently has some coconuts ‘cause he didn’t run away from one of the largest men on NFW’s roster. Instead, he quickly realized that one of the biggest advantages of wrestling Manpower without Calvin Carlton managing him. . . is that he has no brain whatsoever. Lord help us with that tag partner he’s apparently training with. You know who I’m talking about, I dare not type the words for thunder will strike me. Anyway, Manpower takes over the beginning of the match, hip tossing Tui across the whole ring, big boot, clubbing forearms. . . the big man goes for a bear hug, but Tui shows the genetic anomaly of all islander wrestlers – headbutts that kill giants. Manpower bounces off the ropes and Tui shows some significant power with a Belly to Belly Suplex! Tui gets up slowly after that, possibly straining his back, but he shows that’s not the case by sprinboarding into a moonsault for a two-count! Manpower gets locked into a dragon sleeper and the dolt can’t figure out where the ropes are for a full two minutes. . . the fact his neck is too large for Tui to choke out, keeping him awake. Tui finally lets go the big guy is on dreamstreet and takes a cracking side kick to the head, Tui sprinboards again . . . FOR FIJI ASAI MOONSAULT! GOOD LUCK, GOOD NIGHT AND SEE YOU IN THE MORNING. Tui takes it home for his first NFW victory, but charging down the aisle comes Powermaster! "WE’RE MARKING OUT!" (repeat 50x) and you’re in Liverpool. CLOTHESLINE! CLOTHESLINE! DIVING SHOULDER! David Tui’s career is just about over before it’s done as Powermaster lifts him up in the Press. . . and Felix Red charges down the aisle out of nowhere, springboards off the ropes with a British Shield with a picture of Guy Fawkes in a Dominatrix Outfit – VAN DAMINATOR TO POWERMASTER’S FACE! Pure pandemonium turns to complete confusion as Red surveys the wreckage, lights a cigarette and shrugs before walking back to the locker room, not even acknowledging the crowd or staying around for Tui to see his saviour. And if you think that was weird. . . Honey I Fucked Your Mom vs. Joe the Plumber/Mystery Partner The debut of Yori Yakamo Junior and Mittens T. Cat as a tag team across the ring from Joe The Plumber (television title plate not on display this evening). . . and his plunger, who he named as his tag team partner. The Television Champion cut off his opponent’s discotheque music and salute to Catwoman (don’t ask) too berate the two "f***ts" (gay reference) for liking to give themselves the four-fingered lube job that he’s figured out from looking at the blood, when cleaning their locker room toilets. After some thousand fans vomited as Joe licked the plunger and attacked, Mittens T. Cat shocked everyone by no-selling the plunger shot to the head, poop dripping off his six inch pointy right ear, intelligently screaming, "I’VE GOT A FUCKING CATSUIT ON, I’M YOUR KITTY KRYPTONITE KRACKAH THAT KNOWS ALLLLLLLLLLLL ABOUT THE LITTER BOX. . . OF YOUR MOMZ!" Ensue: Slapping fest. Again, Joe lost the battle due to Mittens’ intelligent design, now fending for himself as he took several Kitty Chops of doom before getting flipped over into a Tree of Woe and kicked repeatedly in the head by Mittens. Tag. Yori. SHHHHHHHHHHHROYGROINEN! (Yakamo Style Groin Attack) (Ow.) Yeah, so Joe the Plumber’s getting worked over by Lindsay Lohan in the women’s wing of Attica. Yori kicks the Jesus into him and then back out of him, tagging to Mittens where the stereo drop toe-hold Joe across the middle ropes -> Mittens back handspring Bronco Buster -> YORI 9-7-6! HOLY . . . MOTHER TERESA! Hello? Maybe you shouldn’t choose an inanimate object as your tag partner!? Our Television Champion isn’t going to make it to the next Crash TV as he’s taking a beating from two men that were just crowned by Don King as the Ultimate Carnivores of White Vagina for their ring introductions. Five minutes of unbelievably disconcerting teamwork that’s electric is displayed by H.I.F.M. . . And that’s when the man in the sailor suit ran out, and that’s when everyone lost their 4-1 odds immediate bet that Beau Michaels just arrived to England upon hearing The Spice Girls were back. No, this was some hobo in a kid’s sailor suit that ran into the ring with half of an oar, swinging for the fences on Mittens and Yori. . . ring the bell. . . oh wait, down goes Joe too! The man goes crazy, fans screaming in fear that the kid-suit is about to split in half blinding them. Oh god, he’s got the mic. Its official, I’m hoping this is a Joey Melton spoof. Nope, anyone can get a job here. . . . he’s apparently called The Sailor Man, and he’s the former tag team partner, while also mortal enemy of Joe The Plumber. Through some twisting tails of the high seas and city sewage pipes of the world, their fates are intertwined enough that the Sailor Man has somehow gotten Joe the Plumber to sign the Television Title defense on the first Crash TV against. . . CAMERON CRUISE. Apparently, Cruise was listed as the top contender since he was runner-up to the current co-champions (has anyone seen Shawn Hart? Any hobos out there?) and then Mittens screams bloody murder and tries to attack the Sailor Man, screaming it’s a kitty conspiracy that reeks of pussyhatin’ by homo masturbatin’ fools. . . that doesn’t go over well, Joe’s swinging the plunger. . . I can’t believe I just quoted that. Security rushes out, crowd’s confused to all holy hell and they can’t blame the outrageous amount of alcohol, which makes things even more confusing. Billy Lovemuscle/Maggot vs. Felix Red/Teresa Q Naturally, Teresa Q screaming "Hail Discordia!" and snapping digital pictures of the crowd fit right into the proceedings of the initial wacky, wild and weird ride that the rowdy crowd was certainly ready to watch go crazy. At 170 pounds, there’s more than a few insiders wondering how far she can make it in this federation, but Yori Yakamo Junior certainly isn’t even a cruiserweight. Q. isn’t well known by NFW fans, but she definitely brought some thunder in this match with her kicking ability. Ironically, teamed with former NFW World Champion Felix Red, the fans got to hear a legion of snare drum kicks in this match as they faced off against Billy Lovemuscle, a former CWL namesake and Maggot, the man who recently challenged Felix Red to a grudge match. Two newcomers, two feuding NFW veterans and thrown all together, it brought out a wild 18 minute affair that saw speed and agility running into a wall of power and brawling. The match started with Maggot requesting Felix, getting him and the fans getting a treat early on as Felix quickly raked the eyes, sent Maggot packing against the ropes and delivered a spinning heel kick. Next thing anyone knew, Red makes the tag and Q. Springboards into a Hurricanrana for a quick pin count. Maggot’s probably drunk by the way he’s walking and walks right into a double Superkick from Q. and Red. Red gets tagged back in, works over Maggot but gets countered into a flapjack. Tag to Lovemuscle, who comes in on fire with a series of clotheslines. Control shifts for a little bit, I dug the moment where Lovemuscle and Maggot alternated bionic elbow smashes and headbutts respectively, Red staggers out into a Spinebuster, only getting Lovemuscle a two. Tag in to Maggot who hits the Swandive Headbutt for two. Maggot goes for a piledriver, but Red flips him over and somersault rolls for the tag. Q slingshots in with a corkscrew dropkick, then hits a flipping legdrop getting a pop. Q goes for the pin, but Maggot vaults her off. All of a sudden, the crowd starts screaming. KIN HIROSHI. Looking more paranoid than a mob snitch in Little Italy, NYC, Hiroshi’s sneaking down to ringside cradling his World Title and watching Felix intensely as he’s trying to recover in his corner. Ref collides into Q after Maggot ducks a cross body block. The ref stumbles up, Felix pulls down the ropes and the ref flips over. Obviously, 4-way brawl happens immediately. Lovemuscle nailing fists as Q tries to kick him away, Maggot and Felix in the middle of a slugfest, center of the ring. Maggot charges Felix, but gets flipped over the top rope. Felix stumbles around, his back towards the World Champion, who quickly tries to seize the opportunity and climbs in the ring. Q. is in trouble on the ropes as Lovemuscle whips her across the ring and nails a powerslam. Felix starts hearing the crowd yelling, turns around and sees Hiroshi charging at him, and he quickly bounces off the ropes. Hiroshi swings the belt, but misses Felix . . . Felix bounds off and catches a back elbow from Kin. Kin lines him up, swings the belt – FELIX DUCKS! . . . and Billy Lovemuscle literally gets his first taste of the NFW World Championship. . . . probably not how he wanted to either. Felix slides out of the ring, Kin watching in shock until Maggot pulls him out of the ring and tries to start wailing on him. Teresa collapses on Lovemuscle as a second referee slides in and makes the 3 count! Kin takes off through the crowd, Felix walks to the back before the announcer even says he’s part of the team he’s won as Maggot chases Teresa from the ring, swinging a bottle of liquor given to him by a fan. Lovemuscle wakes up to Maggot offering him the bottle. . . and shockingly, we’ve got another drinker in NFW. The crowd drinks along and Lovemuscle looks disgusted with the loss, but he’s got a big match the next night that could turn around his fortunes. Cameron Cruise vs. Rocko Daymon The night finished off with a match-up involving two wrestlers that are no strangers to each other. Each accompanied by their wives, Rocko Daymon and Cameron Cruise met in the middle of the ring, not an hour removed from Cruise getting the news that he would be on Crash TV challenging for the Television Championship. Daymon, a veteran from ESEN covered feds such as EPW and A1E, made his debut in New Frontier Wrestling. Cruise and Daymon definitely displayed similar ring instincts and style – technicians with a flair for some high-flying. Moreover, fans were treated to a scientific-based match that was a far-cry from some of the night’s proceedings. Daymon had the advantage early, attacking Cruise right as the bell started. Daymon started blasting away rights and lefts, staggering Cruise into the corner where he worked him over with a series of kneelifts and shoulderblocks. Daymon took Cruise down with a hiptoss, worked him over in a chinlock, fought of Cruise getting to his feet by grabbing his hair and slamming him backwards into the mat. Cruise tried to retaliate, but Daymon caught him flush on the face with a vicious running forearm that knocked him through the ropes and out of the ring. Daymon slid out, slammed Cameron’s head into various metallic objects and rolled him back in getting a one-count on a pin attempt. Daymon dropped Cruise with a double arm DDT, getting a near pinfall and whistles from the crowd. Daymon sensing he had full control, whipped Cruise off the ropes and went for a Tilt-A-Whirl, but Cruise reversed that into a rolling headscissors takedown. A series of dropkicks and armdrags took the bullrushing Daymon off his feet before being sent down like a sack of bricks after a ringcracking S.T.O. that got the crowd off its feet. Cruise stepped to the apron taking off for a Springboard Air Cameron Clothesline, got a quick two count and nothing else. On their way up, Daymon caught a sneaky pete elbow to the gut, doubling over Cruise and set up for a running bulldog. Daymon tried to maintain control with a gutwrench suplex, barely getting two from Bruce Phillips calling his first match since the Ultratitle Championship match between Nova and YYJ. Crowd loved the Spinebuster countered into a Lou Thesz press by Cruise, but Daymon rolls out and turns the situation into a vicious Release German Suplex that compacts Cruise like a car in a junkyard. Daymon gets the near pinfall count, starts getting angry with the ref and then his wife for getting angry at him. Cruise with a flash rollup, but Daymon kicks out and we go through a series of a small packages and inside cradles from both wrestlers, but no pins. Daymon goes for a clothesline, but Cruise ducks under for the Cruicifix Cradle and a near pin. As both roll to their feet, Cruise hits the Implant DDT! Cruise rolls him over, but Caitlyn gets Rocko’s foot on the rope. Cruise takes it to the center, Fisherman’s Buster! No dice on the three-count, so Cruise decides to up the ante and attempt the Superplex. Rocko fights him off and turns the tables with a Super Gourdbuster! Cruise has to be dead in the water, but somehow he kicks out and Daymon just about loses it. Caitlyn rolls in the ring, slaps him. He slaps her back! Cruise with a victory roll (which sends Caitlyn flying out) and Rocko kicks out. Both men get up, Cruise with a clothesline that takes them both over the top rope. Phillips starts the twenty count, as Caitlyn attempts to kick Rocko in the ribs to help him up. He ends up clipping out her legs, while Cruise stands up against the ringpost. . . when the crowd starts screaming ‘cause Joe the Plumber is back! Not that security, Mercedes Cruise or even the referee can see this because they’re all trying to break up the Daymon from ripping themselves apart. Meanwhile, Joe the Plumber is carrying the ceramic top to the back of a toilet. Before anyone can say "SHAZZBOT!" CRACK! Joe breaks the damn thing in half over Cameron Cruise’s head, screaming about him trying to steal the poop of Germany to take over the world. Once the Daymons are broken apart, Rocko staggers over to an unconscious Cruise none the wiser to Joe the Plumber hopping back over the guardrail, trying to squeeze the breasts of a rather obese woman in attendance. Cruise is out cold, Rocko rolls him in and climbs up top. . . FROG SPLASH. Uno, Dos, . . . Tres. Its over, Daymon wins his debut match against Cameron Cruise, but not without interference from Joe the Plumber. Check back in twenty-four hours, as we give you the recap for the second show here at the Olympia, where Nova and Dan Ryan would stand across the ring from each other in a tag match, while Craig Miles and Frankie Fargo brought the redcoats a little taste of confederate red wrasslin’.
