NFW news: Reloaded 9 Championship Preview

25 July 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Reloaded 9 Championship Preview Posted by Devin Millwood on 25 Jul 2011 Kerry O'Connor and Devin Millwood preview the upcoming championship showdown between Impulse and Castor V. Strife! (FADEIN: KERRY O’CONNOR and DEVIN MILLWOOD sit in studio chairs in front of empty seats at the RBC Center as ring-crew work to set up down below. O’Connor dons a gunpowder grey suit and light blue tie, while Millwood is in a black button down shirt) O’CONNOR: Welcome everyone to the ESEN Reloaded 9 Championship Preview. I’m Kerry O’Connor, with me is NFW.com web editor and senior analyst Devin Millwood. In just a few days, this arena will transform into an 18,000 seat wrestling venue where a sold out crowd will witness the crowning of a new champion. For a third time, Impulse will take on Castor V. Strife, and THIS TIME it’s for the title of titles. MILLWOOD: That’s right, NFW World Title finals, and nothing could be bigger. Just from a word of mouth perspective, this match is SO big at a time when the company was really in need of a marquee title bout. Definitely there have been big things going on, but at the end of the day, let’s be honest, this business is about championship matches. NFW hasn’t had a World Champion in months, and hasn’t had a big time World Championship match since Crash 50. That’s two years, man. And now we’re getting one of the most anticipated title matches in history. O’CONNOR: Why is that? What makes this match so different from all the others? Certainly NFW has had a plethora of important title fights. MILLWOOD: Yeah, I mean, it doesn’t get more legendary than the Pentagram battle at Crash 50, right? Joe vs. Nova was huge, Manson/Southern was maybe even the biggest, the fatal fourway at Crash 8, I could go on. But what makes this one so special? Easy: the fans are going to see something that doesn’t happen often, if ever. The undisputed top contenders will face off, and a new champion is guaranteed to be made. Not only that, but neither of these guys has been NFW World Champion before. So it’s very exciting, and people know that whatever happens, the outcome will really set the tone for this new era in NFW; this fourth era. O’CONNOR: One thing that hasn’t been said, in addition to this being for all the marbles, is that the two competitors will be settling a score on a LONG running feud. Either way you slice it, this is a match with a ton of BAD BLOOD behind it. MILLWOOD: Without question. They were supposed to settle it at Crash 50; nothing got settled. Castor pressed for a rematch, which was supposed to be IT, last chance, but the way that ended left a bad taste in a lot of mouths, mostly Impulse’s. But I think President Mayfield did the right thing by stipulating that this match be clean at all costs. There’s going to be a distinct winner, and a distinct loser. O’CONNOR: Now, these guys match up pretty evenly. Impulse has the slick grappling, the quickness, and impeccable submission ability. Castor Strife is a lot more devastating in his execution, and has the pedigree of a physically gifted, well-rounded athlete. So what’s the x-factor here? Does it come down to experience? I look at this match myself and think, well, Castor has been here before. This is Impulse’s first World Title main event, but Castor’s had that opportunity and came up short long ago. He’s dealt with these emotions already. Not to mention he has been in there with some of the all-time greats. It seems like the pressure is more on Impulse to get it done. MILLWOOD: I…totally disagree. No, I think, just the opposite, the pressure is on Strife to deliver. Remember, he’s been around. He’s had injuries, had a lot of time off in the past, but the man turns 31 in October. He’s sort of a "young veteran." But this could very well be his last shot. It’s his last mountain, and if he’s not leaving the ring with the belt to embark on a legacy run, I’m not sure where that places him. Remember, there are a lot of other guys vying for this thing when the dust settles. On the other hand, Impulse is young, just like Castor was when he had his first shot. Whereas Castor is at his absolute best right now, I don’t think Impulse is anywhere near his peak. They say an athlete peaks at 30-35. That’s kind of a scary thought, right? That Impulse, as good as he is, could actually get BETTER. Think about how good Castor is at 30 compared to when he had some of those big matches as a young man. Hell, I’d argue that even he could keep getting better. But Impulse, yeah, he just needs to go for it. If he starts putting pressure on himself, the veteran is going to use it against him. O’CONNOR: There is a lot riding on this for both men. I’ve noticed myself, a different kind of intensity about them lately. MILLWOOD: I’ll say this, from being around them and everything, they’re both in extremely sour moods. Just that "Fuck you, I didn’t come this far to lose" type attitude. Impulse has been far less gracious about giving interviews, and Strife has basically been off-limits. I heard a report from sources close to the President that, apparently, Strife missed a public event at the Virgin megastore in Manhattan to promote the match. And, if you know anything about Castor Strife, you know how strange that is because the guy is a promotional fiend. Odd as he is, that dude is a moneymaker and never misses the chance to hype himself. Turned out to be something where his training schedule interfered with NFW’s, and everything was worked out, but it goes to show the level of intensity for both men. O’CONNOR: I know that Impulse’s camp had some issues with the public schedule making it difficult to train as well, but as you said, both he and Castor are not looking to give an inch. MILLWOOD: Yeah, at the end of the day Eddie doesn’t wanna hear that shit from them, and they get that. Castor paid a small fine from what I hear, but it’s all been ironed out. O’CONNOR: We know of the obvious strength advantage Castor has over Impulse, and Impulse’s superior submission technique. Strife claims he is going to grapple with Impulse. How do you see this match playing out in terms of strategy? MILLWOOD: Alex Austin claims 100% that is the strategy, but I think he’s fucking nuts if he straight up wrestles Impulse, and ultimately he is smart enough not to. And that’s not a knock on Strife. He may even be one of the best grapplers on the roster, but no one is in Impulse’s league right now in terms of that. Strife has to use his strength, his dynamism, his ability to improvise, and if he does that he can absolutely win this thing. For Impulse, he can’t look to do too much too soon. He needs to take Strife into the late innings, test his mettle, and engage in a grappling match if and when Castor is tired. Sudden Impact is a match-ender, I don’t think Castor recovers from it, but if you don’t think Castor has trained 5 ways from Sunday on how to counter it, you’re sorely mistaken. Doesn’t mean it can’t or won’t happen, but I think Impulse knows better than to rely on a one-trick pony. That said, I mean, Castor is wise to train submissions with Alex Austin, and I know Impulse added a strength training regimen as he’s definitely put on some solid muscle. So who knows? That’s the thing with these guys. Some athletes, you tell them they can’t do something, and they go out of their way to prove you wrong. I just happen to think intelligence will prevail. O’CONNOR: OK, so for the eventual winner, what happens next? What does the winner have to look forward to in terms of competition? MILLWOOD: Seriously? Have you looked at the roster? I say to the winner, congratulations and GOOD LUCK TO YOU, YOUNG MAN. (O’Connor laughs) Jesus fu – I mean, just looking at the top of the heap, there’s Castor and Impulse, Dan Ryan, Dorchester, Nova, JTP, AND ONE OF THOSE GUYS ISN’T IN THE TOP 5. You figure it out. I think that’s fucking MIND BOGGLING. I took some flack on the forums for saying this, but to me, bar none, this is the most ELITE and talent heavy roster in the history of wrestling. Not NFW, in WRESTLING. And I’ll argue to the death on that. So the champion, right away, knows he will be challenged like no one has been challenged before. It took a lot to get to this point, for both men. You say Impulse is lacking in championship experience, but he went in there and BEAT Dan Ryan, whose gold collection put King Midas on food stamps. (O’Connor laughs) I’m serious man, the kid’s been virtually unbeatable for a long time. And Castor is coming off a huge, HUGE pinfall over Troy Windham. Do you realize that he blocked the Slacknife and almost broke Troy’s neck with the counter Director’s Cut? So he pinned him, then he gets into an absolute dogfight with Dorchester, who is the one guy you don’t wanna meet in a dogfight, and he beats him too. No question about it, Impulse and Castor Strife are 1a and 1b in no particular order. Now Eddie’s gonna kill me for saying this, but I’m saying it anyway. I commend the President on allowing these guys the flexibility in their contracts to wrestle internationally when NFW went through a time of transition post-Crash 50. What that did was allow two of his top stars to keep the iron hot and develop themselves in a no-pressure situation, and if you followed what either man did on the international scale, they basically curb-stomped the globe. Impulse went to Asia and tore through the so-called hard ass Puroresu like a hot knife, and Castor Napoleon’d the fuck out of Europe. So while it hurt losing JTP for the time we did, we now know beyond a shadow of a doubt, with what they did here in the biggest tournament since Ultratitle, and what they did on a world scale, Castor and Impulse will decide LEGITIMATELY who is the champion. O’CONNOR: Who do you like as a top contender? MILLWOOD: Dan Ryan and Dorchester meet to decide who gets the next shot, and there aren’t two men in the world who need to avoid a loss like them. This isn’t just about a shot at the title, but it’s about respect. Nobody hates losing more than Dan Ryan – not even up for debate. And Dorchester’s pride is riding on this match after coming up short against Strife. Whoever wins that is gonna be going HARD at that belt. But aside from them, you know, you have JTP who’s back now and looking to get his title back, but we haven’t seen him in a ring since hiatus. We really don’t know how his injuries have affected him. The guy I’M watching right now, is Nova. He really took it to Rook Black at Darlington, walked out of the fourway with a victory, and that was only his first match back. Impressive, because I absolutely consider Rook Black in the top 7 or 8 in the world. Mark my words, Nova is going to have his shot real soon, and when the time comes, the champion will have his greatest challenge to date. I would still love to see Nova fight Joe first, because as great as he is, he’s still 0-2 against the Plumber. There’s also this guy, don’t know if you heard of him, multiple time World Champion, taller than a skyscraper, goes by the name of DEACON. Yeah, THAT GUY’S on the roster. So is Jack Harmen, aka High Flyer, aka the legend who seems not to be slowed by age. And of course, everyone will be looking to the winner of JJ DeVille vs. Jack Bryant, a major TV title affair in a division that seems to produce superstars by the year. O’CONNOR: The Windham Clan will also prove to be a thorn in the side of the eventual champion, especially if Dan Ryan establishes himself as top contender. So let’s talk about legacy. Obviously winning at Reloaded, capturing the title, will be a significant benchmark in the career of either Impulse or Castor. But they say the champion isn’t champion until he’s DEFENDED his belt. Talk about legacy, and how all the top contenders, not just Castor and Impulse, seem to be chasing it. MILLWOOD: Outside of the top two who are trying to cement their legacy, you have a crop of challengers who are all very accomplished, and all looking to expand their legacy. Dan Ryan’s the former champ, but he never got a full run and has never beaten Nova. Nova was Ultratitle Champion, World Champion, but was dethroned and has to date never beaten Joe. Joe up and left during what was considered to be the greatest title run in history, and has faced intense criticism ever since. Dorchester wants to show he hasn’t just ridden on the coattails of his associates past and present. So everyone has something to prove, and everyone has a goal line. O’CONNOR: You might say that the one standing when the dust settles is the GREATEST of the new, young greats MILLWOOD: You might say that. Who knows what will happen at the end of the day? All I know is, if either Impulse or Castor is able to take this title and RUN with it, taking on all challenges, they have the potential to do something, establish a legacy, that few in wrestling have ever done. This business is constantly evolving. You watch some of the old classics on ESEN, and you see just how much it’s all changed. It used to be that everyone had their style, their specialty, it was about one style, and nobody deviated from that. Eli Flair had the brawling, hardcore style, GUNS was about sheer power, Tom Adler was the catch-as-catch-can WRESTLER, Melton and Doc Silver had that raw endurance, and probably the most dynamic of the bunch was Hornet. Then this guy named Mike Randalls comes out of nowhere, and he was doing shit nobody had ever seen before. He could wrestle, he could brawl, he could strangle you, and he had cardio for days. People watched him and said, "Wow, I need to train like THAT GUY trains". Except you had to get a passport and jump a jet to Cuzco, Peru, take some peyote and train at the summit of fucking Machu Picchu or whatever the hell that guy was doing. Anyway, after he came out, you saw more and more WELL ROUNDED athletes, and I think today’s crop is really the culmination of that legacy, that evolution, and there hasn’t been a better, more EXCITING time to be a fan. O’CONNOR: Well, that does it for the Reloaded 9 Championship Preview here on ESEN. Any final predictions for the match? MILLWOOD: Aside from "it’s going to be good"? No! O’CONNOR: For Devin Millwood, I’m Kerry O’Connor, and we’ll see you at the show! (FADEOUT)
