NFW news: ESEN Officials Narrow WB2 Locations

17 February 2006

ESEN Officials Narrow WB2 Locations Posted by Natrone Rice on 17 Feb 2006 Extreme Sports Entertainment Network officials have confirmed that they've narrowed down the list to five different locations across the world for the season 2 finale pay-per-view, Wrestlebowl 2. The network's owner Craig Miles, NFW President Quentin Sullivan, NFW East Grand Elder Susano-O-Mikoto and UWA President Anne Mystic have all agreed on the finalists per a closed conference held earlier this week as Week 7 production was finalized. Fiona Love, spokeswoman for the Western Conference spoke to the press briefly this morning confirming the conference as well as the fact that while five locations have been chosen, not all cities have their venue situations finalized. Love explained that while many factors went into the decision, the finalists all had very common themes amongst themselves. First and foremost, accessibility for the large fanbase of NFW and the UWA was of importance. On the heels of the Wrestlestock tour, some groups of remote Montana and Wyoming locations were putting packages together to try and convince ESEN that Wrestlebowl could be a much larger version. "Miles didn't want to beat a dead horse into the ground and also didn't feel that the companies involved could make enough money with so much overhead invested in building an on-site PPV wrestling experience," Love explained. "With the finalists in place, we've got cities well-accustomed to this sort of atmosphere, as well as venue operators that have dealt with sold-out capacities." Love noted that in Japan, The Osaka Dome was being considered, as well as Wembley Stadium in the heart of London, England. The three other finalists were a Canadian Venture Capitalist group in talks with Toronto and VAncouver, as well as the domestic choices of Detroit and Dallas. Insider sources say that while all involved are toying with the idea of a possible 60,000-80,000 capacity dome, it won't come as a surprise if they veer away from that direction due to the other idea of the season-ending PPV being two nights, instead of one -- which would be much harder to sell out both nights. "We expect to have our decision finalized by the end of week 8," Love noted while adding cryptically, "as well as any other schedule changes we feel will help everyone involved in the long run."
