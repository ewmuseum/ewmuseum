Sh*t, this is probably going to be taken the wrong way. What I'm trying to say is: sillyness isn't exclusive to the comedic characters, because just as many "serious" characters are guilty of being over the top. It's just a matter of writing angles and RP that fits into your character's persona and does the job of developing them further. But if you're sitting there thinking, "how much stupid over the top wacky bullsh*t can I cram into 3 pages?" then you've screwed the pooch, as they say.
Recently, I wrote an RP where Layne Winters does a spot while handcuffed sitting against a tree, after the police got involved with a violent domestic dispute between him and his girlfriend/valet. All I do is describe her with a black eye, have her decline to press charges, and then I have Layne talking to an interviewer saying "Yeah, that's right I hit women, I'm a piece of sh*t, so what? This is my life." The whole thing was written just to accentuate Layne's persona as the "underbelly of the underbelly of pro wrestling." He's the guy who uses steroids, can't handle his finances, doesn't treat his woman right all the time, but lives to find glory in the ring. Basically, he's The Wrestler (except I wrote the character months before the movie came out, swear to God! lol).
Did I go over the top? No, I think I did just enough to make my point. Now...had I wrote out 8,000 words worth of him throwing bottles at his girlfriend, beating the sh*t out of her, running from the police, getting beaten down by them, her screaming and saying "take that a-hole to jail! Look what he did to me!" and then him passing out p*ssing himself, it would have killed what I was trying to accomplish. Sure, it sounds entertaining, but it does nothing for character development. Plus, what do you write after that? How do you top that? You gonna have him kill somebody? Fake a suicide? Where do you possibly go from there? Should I start novelizing his broken romance with her? F*ck no. If you want that, go read a fantasy or romance novel written by a professional, not half-baked RP written by my amateur ass.
Ring psychology can't exist in FW since there's not really any match going on, and especially since most people skip to the end anyway. But you can accomplish a measure of psychology between opponents through RPing, and going beyond the boundaries of your character kills all that.
Honestly, I haven't been around NFW in such a long time, so I wouldn't be able to comment on whether or not this fed has been guilty of going overboard all the time. Sh*t, I've been guilty of it myself. I know there have been times in the past where maybe things did get a bit too silly, though. Part of NFW's appeal is the crazyness, and I love it as much as the next person, but sometimes you can say more with subtlety, that's all. Hope this didn't come off as too elitist. I'm really just expressing my opinion on character boundaries. Stay within your character, whether you write comedic or dramatic RP.

GreggG — 25 March 2009 Billy, I disagree about ring psychology not existing in NFW. I haven't written too many matches lately. But when I do, I always look for a way -- and JN's going to make fun of me for saying this, since it's his favorite cliche -- to tell a story in the ring.
I like to figure out how to get a match to its start and to its end while having each character's traits and narrative come into play. And one of the best ways to do so is through matches.
One of the best matches I ever wrote was Eddie Mayfield vs. Evan Aho in the CSWA. Aho was a Benoit-type serious wrestler who kind of lucked into the title because of people having to leave, etc. He needed to look strong to get him over as a credible champion. And Mayfield was in the Professionals and I wanted him to look like a gigantic star who would do anything to do what he wanted. My angle to do this was to have Mayfield do something conniving at first to get the upper hand -- he jumped Aho before the match began, and Aho busted his shoulder. And then his meant that Aho had to fight through the pain. And when Aho had the upper hand, Mayfield used every trick in the book to escape defeat.
I kind of have a formula to my match writing style. I don't know if anyone else uses it, but I've learned this after doing this for a really long time. I never just sit down and write a match. What I do, I figure out how the match is going to end. And then I work backwards from there -- how do you set up the ending of the match, how do you get to that point, how do you get there, etc. Then I start from the top and actually OUTLINE how the match is going to go from there.
I might put too much thought into this. Alan Aidan — 31 March 2009 Hey Katz - My minor take on this thread is that serious storylines need to be offset by comedic storylines or (insert adjective here) storylines as part of a whole. I think there's a disconnect sometimes (but not all the time) between handlers that tell a story of their characters and how that story fits into the greater whole of a wrestling card or a wrestling storyline arc.
The question of "can or should NFW do serious storylines?" is really answered by whatever you say when you ask the corollary questions "Does NFW have comedy storylines?" or "Does NFW have enough going on that one or two serious storylines won't totally **** up the feel of the fed you've got going on right now?" If the answer is yes, then it makes no sense not to have them as it stands out as a gap.
Now the next question is do you have the flexibility as a fed both in available writing styles and talent on the roster to do a serious story because the thing that makes a serious story great isn't the "seriousness". It's the character interaction and little bits of human factors humor and other emotions that slowly build together to make a serious storyline memorable.
If you have the following: 1. One or more guys with a good idea that can pitch the idea in 30 seconds. 2. One or more guys that can write well and work well together. 3. Trust in each other so one of those guys can step up and make sure everything makes sense writing wise.
4. Staff involvement so things don't get stupid quickly. ..you can have serious storylines. Comedy and other types are easier, because things can get stupid and that's kind of the point.
