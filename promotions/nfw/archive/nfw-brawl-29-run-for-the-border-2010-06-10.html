BRAWL REPORT Submitted by: Simon Rycrest Via: DROIDARMY As the single fall match was announced, a curious din broke through the crowd as the first "competitor" – of all things, a steel ladder – was carried to the ring by two members of the ring crew. The ladder was allegedly imported all the way from Japan, and was announced as a legitimate former Dramatic Dream Team Ironman Heavymetalweight Champion. This was greeted with barely any response from the crowd, given how such a distinction is equally shared by a monkey, a dog, a blow-up doll, a baseball bat and some 500 more random persons and objects more or less associated with the obscure Japanese federation. The arrival of "the Chrono.naut" Erik Black came with its regular pomp and swagger, accompanied by blaring stoner metal being pumped through the PA. A smattering of indy fans in the crowd cheered him on, though the bulk of the loyal New Frontier fans – memories still fresh of Black’s odd one-man exhibition back in Philadelphia a little over a month ago – sat transfixed in wonder of what the eccentric high-flying stoner had in store for them. After much cajoling and parading down the rampway in his bathrobe and a set of aviators, Black drew a hit of epic proportions from his stainless-steel custom-made bong, and hopped into the ring ready for action, staring across the ring at his "opponent" with eager determination and valor. The referee made his final checks and gave the signal to the timekeeper. Three rings of the bell later, and the struggle between man and ladder began. Black’s offense began with an obligatory knife-edge chop, which appeared to do nothing in denting the stainless-steel surface of his inanimate opponent, but instead left him howling in pain as he clutched the striking arm. Undeterred, Black countered with a series of slams and suplexes, throwing the ladder forcibly from one end of the ring to the other. Black dominated most of the match from this point on, landing a series of high-flying and springboard dropkicks and clotheslines after standing the ladder up, sending it toppling over to the mat again. Though he was the driving offensive force in the match, it would be incorrect to assume that it was a completely one-sided battle. At one point, following a clumsy Irish whip to the corner, the ladder deftly avoided a follow-up Stinger splash attempted by the Chrono.naut when gravity caused it to topple over prematurely. Additionally, a running Senton splash performed by Black on the grounded ladder appeared to be an act of self-inflicted damage. Still, Black’s persistence in conquering the steel ladder held fast. Following an impressive rolling fireman’s carry slam, the dank-smoking daredevil applied an innovated and relatively unknown submission hold, bending the ladder at its midpoint using his knee as the crux. Despite his frequent demands for the ladder to "tap out", his opponents sheer lack of any vocal structure proved it a feeble effort. At this time, the referee seemed rather unsure of how to continue the match, and frequently checked his watch to keep track of how much time he was wasting officiating this bizarre spectacle. With the ladder "incapacitated" following his submission attempt, the Chrono.naut capped things off by scaling to the top rope and lunging off – passing through the thin veils of time, space, and understanding in the process, to which we can all be sure – and landed hand on the ladder with his penchant "420 Splash". After several moments of lying on the mat clutching his ribs in pain, Black mustered the strength to cover the ladder, hooking a rung, and scoring the three count. RESULT: "Chrono.naut" Erik Black wins via pinfall Phone Call w/ Pauly D!
