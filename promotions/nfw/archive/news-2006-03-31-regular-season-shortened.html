NFW news: Regular Season Shortened

31 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Regular Season Shortened Posted by Natrone Rice on 31 Mar 2006 In a stunning move, possibly capitalizing on the reviews/storylines emanation from Week 8's action thus far, ESEN Officials announced that New Frontier Wrestling's regular season schedule would be shortened from twelve weeks to ten weeks. Reporters caught up to Craig Miles in the city of Brotherly Love leading up to his grudge match against JW Collins for the upcoming UWA Pirate TV show. Miles confirmed that Sullivan and himself had taken a look at the schedule as well as the current roster situation for the last several weeks. "We're finally coming to the point of realization that with the injuries and turnover from our events," Miles elucidated from his hotel bar, "...that to have 85% of the conferences stabilized like this, we're gambling on the competitive aspects of the playoffs if we string these guys out too long. If we'd gone 12 weeks, we had 4 more shows per conference in the regular season, along with 4-5 playoff shows. We're cutting the fat and getting to the filet of the situation." Miles, owner and founder of ESEN TV denied any finanical problems. "No...if anything, we crossed the black after Week Five. You're not going to please everybody, but it seems each conference is catering to so many genre of fans that all the arrows on all the figures keep pointing up. The actual fear is that by going to 12 weeks, we'd hit that levelling off/cooling period that the league saw last year between weeks 12 and 16. Having that experience in the back of our heads contributed moreso to the decision than any finanicial risks involved." Miles didn't mince words much later discussing his cameo venture's business with his station, "Hell I gotta hand it to that b*tch, Mystic...this UWA shindig hasn't hurt my wallet in the least either. Two Spectrum shows sold-out, an no NAACP picketers on our doorsteps either."
