NFW Brawl 27: 2010 Opening Night in Philadelphia 27 Apr 2010 Wachovia Spectrum, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania (seats 18,136) Welcome to the Show! Maybe you’re listening on the radio, maybe you’re watching RICE work the mic in a small trailer studio. Two unseen figures work the boards, while RICE watches a couple of monitors capturing the night’s live action and other sports networks. A MacBook takes up most of his desk area…except for four bobbleheads. The OSS vs. The Showstoppers, which wouldn’t be hard to guess if you knew RICE. RICE; "We’re gonna be taking calls for the next hour, we’ve got Kerry O’Connor still in the Corestates Spectrum on FRONTIERtron Square with some ESEN EXCLUSIVE HIGHLIGHTS. This led to a succession of moves from Castor that SHOULD’VE put Saxon away, except the Bournemouth native refused to lose. (CUTTO: CASTOR delivering a succession of VICIOUS knees to SAXON’s midsection in the corner of the ring; Castor holding Saxon up with one arm, letting the blood rush to the brain for a while before dropping him down hard in a brainbuster suplex. TWO COUNT ONLY! Next, Castor hits a fisherman’s suplex, but Saxon kicks out again) O’CONNOR: No matter what he did, Castor just COULD NOT put Buddy Saxon away! Saxon found himself going to the top rope once again, but was suddenly distracted by, well…THIS… (CUTTO: RENO STONE calling attention to himself throwing darts at the bare chest of THE HUMAN TRASHCAN, a noted masochist with an ungodly high tolerance for pain) O’CONNOR: This gave Castor ample time to regain his senses and charge the turnbuckle. Saxon would land on his groin, with Castor locking his head for a HARD superplex. (CUTTO: After the superplex, CASTOR starts slamming his hands on the mat wildly and crawling up to SAXON’s face, yelling…) CASTOR: STUPID! STUPID! I TOLD YOU NOT TO COME HERE! O’CONNOR: Evidently Castor was not too appreciative of his opponent showing up tonight. Seconds later, he picked Buddy Saxon off the mat and hit him with a DIRECTOR’S CUT snap-swinging neckbreaker…WITH FORCE! Castor hooked the leg back, taking no chances here, and while the Philly crowd was yelling in an attempt to will Saxon back to life…it would not be. Castor would get the three and the victory, setting him back on the winning path and a possible TV Title rematch with Impulse! "Chrono.naut" Erik Black vs. Kato the Invisible Ninja BRAWL REVIEW: "Chrono.naut" Erik Black vs. Kato the Invisible Ninja Submitted by: MUTA Via: DROIDARMY The dark match in Philadelphia featured a whopping five-minute "special exhibition" between two unsigned talents. Renown indie wrestler Erik Black – making himself known in New Frontier as "The Chrono.naut" – went up against an opponent only known as "Kato the Invisible Ninja." Black came to the ring first, adorned in what appeared to be a bathrobe and a set of aviator sunglasses, "grooving" rather than walking to the sounds of chugging stoner metal pumping through the PA. He topped off the grand entrance by taking a hit off a bong he happened to bring with him, seemingly in defiance of New Frontier’s new substance abuse policy. The Philadelphia crowd didn’t quite know what to make of the odd and clearly unconventional wrestler, but pockets of fans that recognized them showed their support. His opponent "Kato" came to the ring next, and, true to his title of "Invisible Ninja," could not be seen by anybody in the arena with the exception of "the Chrono.naut." This was made obvious right off the bat as the spotlight seemed to follow absolutely nothing as an invisible force made its way down the ramp and slid into the ring. The five minutes of action could only be described as a see-saw struggle featuring only man. Once the bell rang, Black snapped forward, wrapping himself up in a collar-and-elbow tie-up, and put himself on the receiving end of some arm drags. He countered by shadow-boxing his "opponent" with a series of martial arts strikes, capped off with a sick-looking spinning heel kick. Dropping to all fours, he made it clear that he was making a cover, but fell over right after the two-count as "Kato" kicked out. Black followed by quickly kicking the air in front of him to put his "opponent" back to the mat and hit the mat with a series of running attacks, including a forward-flipping Senton Splash that rocked the ring. After the fourth or fifth series, Black took hard enough of a bump to leave him briefly motionless on the mat... or it could be assumed that "Kato" simply rolled out of the way. The "Invisible Ninja" began working the head of "The Chrono.naut" into what appeared to be a headlock, leaving the audience confused as Black seemingly sat in the ring for several long moments on the verge of passing out. Getting the fans to rally behind him, Black "forced" himself to his feet, broke free, and ran off the ropes to hit the unseen force with a diving forearm that drew a confused pop from the fans, as they couldn’t tell if was actually hitting anything. Black kept the momentum rolling with an Enziguri and a Rolling Fireman’s Carry Slam, done well enough to make anybody believe there actually was an invisible opponent in that ring. This merited only another two-count. Seeing that time was quickly expiring, "the Chrono.naut" decided to go all out for his grand finale, slipping under the ropes to the outside and setting up a table, subsequently setting "Kato" over it. Egged on by the fans and whatever was in his system at the time, Erik Black slipped back into the ring, scaled the turnbuckle, gave the crowd a brief op with a pose, and went careening off with a beautiful 450 degree splash, making a violent and destructive landing through the seemingly empty folding table. The crowd – though unsure of what the hell he was doing – popped at his daring stunt. It wasn’t clear as to whether or not he hit his target or if he just threw himself off the top rope through a table to get a rise out of the fans, but the move left Erik Black unmoving for the remainder of the referee’s ten count. Because it wasn’t clear as to whether or not "Kato" had made it back into the ring by that point, the referee – who was clearly having enough dealing with one guy exchanging moves with himself – rang the bell and called it a night. 'XXX' Sean Stevens vs. Bloodhunt (FADEIN: O’CONNOR holding his earpiece and nodding, still standing in front of the FRONTIERtron which starts replaying the STEVENS/BLOODHUNT introductions behind him and into the highlight reel.) O’CONNOR: "Well Natrone, the main event here in Philly began with Bloodhunt making his way to the ring, flanked by his wife Emily, who held a black velvet bag close to her chest. The crowd erupted in noise as "Triple X" Sean Stevens headed towards the ring for his first singles match in NFW in quite some time." "An early feeling out period turned poorly for Bloodhunt as Stevens was able to repeatedly beat him to the punch and sent the former two time NFW World Champion crashing to the mat with arm drags, hip tosses and a series of dropkicks, Bloodhunt bailed out to the floor looking to regroup." "Bloodhunt returned to the ring and began brawling with Stevens, trying to rough up "Triple X" and his tactics were successful for a while until Stevens countered a vertical suplex and landed it himself, Stevens then landed a flurry of offensive moves that sent Bloodhunt bailing from the ring again." "Bloodhunt returned to the ring and secured a side headlock and tried to keep Stevens on the mat. Stevens fought through the hold and went for a back suplex, but Bloodhunt shifted his weight and landed on top of Stevens, gaining a near pin and knocking the wind out of "Triple X"" "Stevens was rocked by Bloodhunt’s offense to the head and neck, being worn down as Bloodhunt looked to set up the Headshot. However when it came time for the Headshot to be landed, Stevens avoided it and landed a neckbreaker to turn the tide." "Both men got up and Stevens held the advantage, rocking Bloodhunt for a series of two counts including hitting a Frog Splash that looked like it should end it, but Bloodhunt just barely managed to kick out, Bloodhunt in desperation tried to go for the Headshot repeatedly, but every time was denied, before finally being nailed with an X-Factor by Stevens that led to the pin and the three count, giving "Triple X" a win in our main event." "But things didn’t end there, as Stevens started to make his way from the ring, Emily hopped up on the apron and told Stevens to wait and to stay in the ring. Stevens looked interested in what she had to say, and didn’t mind putting his hand on her hip as she talked to him." "Bloodhunt got to his feet, and grabbed a mic, he said that he had something for Stevens, but that it wasn’t his wife…With that Emily went outside the ring and retrieved the bag she’d walked to the ring with…Bloodhunt explained that this match was fought with Olympic style drug testing, that the result was indisputable, and that Sean Stevens had proven himself as the better man."
