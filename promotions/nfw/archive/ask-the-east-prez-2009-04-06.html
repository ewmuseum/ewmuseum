Yeah. I co-sign on EPW. LQJT86C — 9 April 2009 I'm not sure if this question is too specific, but what's your general opinion on the cardwriting process and format style?
Right now, the consensus on FWC seems to be that everyone pitching in for a match works best, especially in the interest of getting the damn thing out on time. In the past, and you know this better than anyone else, one or two guys would write an entire screenplay's worth of an epic megacard which, in addition to people's busy real-life workloads, could take a few months. When such cards were late, it was easy for everyone to direct their frustration because a single person was usually in charge of the operation. At least now, the responsibility is evenly split between several members or more. Finally, I remember a time when many leagues wrote cards that were semi-short form. Maybe one or two guys would write it, but it was "lite." Angles and segments would be given their full due, but matches would be severely cut in length so as to make life easy for the cardwriter. Cards could get out in a more timely fashion, and you could also avoid a possible lack of stylistic contuinity between many different cardwriters.
Anyway, I'm just rehashing sh*t you already know. So I'm just gonna break down what I think are the pros and cons of all three styles, and maybe you could just give us your thoughts on the whole process. (You've probably addressed all this stuff in threads from years past, but I figured I'd get it into your Q&A thread which seems to be turning into the Official JN Book of Knowledge).
Long-form (one or two writers): Pros- one long megacard that retains full continuity. Writers are not dependent on a plethora of others who might or might not hold up the process. Cons- I don't care if you're the greatest writer on the face of the earth or a sloppy writer, sooner or later you're gonna run into severe lateness problems writing cards this way. Also places great amount of stress on a single person, usually. High burn out ratio doing things this way.

Tag Team Wrestling IS wrestling. There's so many facets of the 'sport' that are intertwined in tagteams. It's a unique opportunity to tell more stories, and there's a different kind of heat that comes to the wildness of 4 guys in the ring doing stereo dropkicks that you can't get with a 1/1 match. Every league should have a tagteam division, and I can tell the health of a league by how strong it's tagteams are. It's hard to do now because tags are pretty dead in FWres now, but It needs to resurface.
