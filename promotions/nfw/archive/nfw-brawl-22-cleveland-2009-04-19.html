NFW Brawl 22: Cleveland, 19 April 2009

19 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
NFW Brawl 22: Cleveland 19 Apr 2009 Quicken Loans Arena, Cleveland, Ohio (seats 21,200) HELLO CLEVELAND! You’re expecting some drug-addled, metaphorical allegory of how our lives on the road equate to something magical and inspiring. Maybe a tale of how some hooker blew my nuts off and now I’ve got some special comb. And that’s why you’re unemployed and eating food stamps instead of actually using them. I’m in Eddie Mayfield’s Internship Program, I’m Stephen Fleir and what you think I should be doing is beneath us all, so just stop it. Speaking of beneath us all, I’m typing in the city of Cleveland. Sirius Radio has come alive with M. Wayne Grossard and Sean Toombs talking over Ben Harper’s Glory and Consequence. We’ve got more than a few rowdy fans surrounding Calamity that should be arrested…well, Calamity should probably be arrested as well. I’ve been contemplating a fake phone call to local authorities since he tried to make me drink his homemade apple cider. It was way too warm. Normal Human Beings vs. Cuatro-X Anyway, we’ve got music. We’ve got Biff Busey and I’ve got my barf bag ready as the Normal Human Beings are on the way to the ring. They’re ranting, they’re raving and I’m feeling like a carnivorous earwig is tearing the flesh off my brain stem. I think they challenged any tag team to a match. Hey, that’s Dos Equis’ music! Hey, that’s Cuatro Equis’ logo on the SMOKEscreen, but I don’t get it…since Dos Equis is the only guy walking out to the ring. I’d be pissed he’s double fisting Corona, but in this fed…as long as that’s all he’s double fisting, I’m ok with it. So, without further adieu we’ve got a tag team beatdown of a fat Mexican and ironically, I’m not watching COPS. Not that I’d watch Fox Television, but that’s besides the point. Equis tries to fight hard, but being an overweight, out of shape luchadore doesn’t bode well against young, hungry, living in the shadow of their fake family name freaks. After Equis hits an early run of chest-high dropkicks, King Parsons Buttbusters and a couple of beer-ranas…Jericho connects with a chopblock and Coppola follows up with a rolling knee drop. The pain continues for a while, but thank god for the fans dousing Dos in beer to magically give him second winds after getting tossesd out of the ring. Coppola is setting in a Cobra Clutch, when I thought Cleveland’s economy finally hit where I thought it was and Gund Arena’s lights go out. There’s screams, I’m nearly pissing over the likely crowd riot…and really beating myself up for wearing the good Rolex tonight. Calamity says he may jerkoff, Toombs takes a hit to repress that statement. Grossard mentions he’s hearing some metal, cracking sounds. Lights come on. Ok. FUTUREQuis is apparently REAL. That’s right. He wasn’t a drunken hallucination. A half-foot, 30 pound difference of a man that certainly makes me wonder what’s in 2019’s hot, apple cider. Oh yeah, Coppola is bleeding heavily and wearing a chair necklace. There’s a busted table at ringside, that Jericho’s been left in a wood angel position on top of. Dos Equis collapses on Coppola and due to the ref being severely frightened by FUTUREQuis’ threats of giving him an atomic intestinal wedgie…makes the 3 count. FUTUREQuis carries out DOS in a fireman’s and as he’s on the ramp, right near the SMOKEScreen…unceremoniously dumps shim through some lighting equipment. These two are gonna be great friends, I can tell. Captain Conga vs. Teresa Quaranta With the night off on such a great foot, I could only expect the next wrestler to come out next be called Captain Conga. Shaka Zulu’s rolling over in his grave, while Chaka Khan is eating another Devil Dog. Of course, after making such shady comparison, I just read a bio sheet that stated this guy is from Spanish Harlem. I’m being baited to make a joke that will get me assassinated by the FCC at this point. Meanwhile, we’ve got Teresa Quaranta’s music. The former National Champion unbelievably being a slight voice of reason in an unreasonable world seemed to ignore the theatrics, pyrotechnics and the outstretched fans of the audience. She knew the precarious position of being put into a match against a debuting rookie. The bell sounded and it was Conga flying out of the gates with an array of armdrags and hiptosses as Quaranta was caught off guard by his speed and efficiency. Apparently, the name made her think he was a jobber as well. Before TQ could gain her grounding it was was Conga landing an Overhead Belly to Belly Release that sent her skidding across the map. TQ stood up in the corner, dodged out of the way of an incoming Avalance Clothesline and then hit a sidewinding Enziguiri to reverse the momentum of the match. TQ followed it up with a vicious DDT and then a somersault legdrop off the turnbuckles. A couple of near pinfalls later and TQ went for a suplex only to get Super Gourdbustered. Conga followed up with a Tornado DDT and Gutwrench Powerbomb that got a couple of close pinfalls, but it became very apparent that this wasn’t a normal woman in the ring with him. Of course, he played up to the crowd just a little bit too much and missed a Springboard Moonsault. As Conga tried to make his way to his feet, he was met with a little something called the Process of Illumination. The Cross-Armed Diamond Cutter ended the match and Conga’s official debut. RookPulse vs. FelixKooter With a somewhat normal night for us progressing, we got a matchup of RookPulse taking on the tandem duo of Felix Red and Kooter Michaels-Cruise. So, once again NFW stops the progression of what it can be and instead goes back to what it is. With Rook and Felix on tap for a National Championship match at Crash 48, this match could serve as a springboard for someone to ‘mistakenly’ cause their opponent to clip and fall on a large piece of broken glass. Well, c’mon… What Would Eddie Do? Felix and Impulse started off exchanging forearm smashes and kicks, but Felix won out due to Felix Kicks generally winning out against anyone. However, he missed a dropkick and got caught on the jaw by one of Impulse’s which sent him out of the ring. Impulse went for a plancha, Felix dodged out of the way and Impulse held onto the ropes to land feet-first on the apron. Randall Knox flew off and connected with a reverse cross-body on Felix to cheers, but took a vicious headbutt from Kooter as he was on the wrong side of town outside the ring. Felix knocked Impulse around the ring with a few more vicious kicks and nailed a Sitout Facebuster to crowd cheers. Maybe he should’ve tagged in Kooter, maybe he shouldn’t have. It resulted in the crowd clearly on his side, but it also resulted with Kooter being completely outsmarted by Knox which led to a tag to the current National Champion. Rook connected with some lightning quick forearm smashes, a spinning Roaring Elbow and a Discus Clothesline that finally sent the former radioactive fetus down to the mat. Rook capitalized with a swinging neckbreaker and tagged in Randall. Rook lifted up Kooter in a bear hug, Impulses springboarded with a spinning leg clothesline that got a near pinfall. Another quick tag and double-team move brought a double suplex and Rook smacking Kooter’s skull in the head with an elbow. After a few more near falls, Kooter was able to break free with a double clothesline and make the tag to Felix who vaulted into the ring with a hurricanrana on Rook Black. Black got up and took a boot to the gut, followed by a DDT. Felix then hit a blissfully looking Springboard Moonsault Legdrop (you read that right) and nearly put away the man he was challenging for the National Championship. Felix quickly scaled the turnbuckles again and went for a Moonsault Bodypress as Black got up, but lady’s luck has a way of granting at least one citizen of Cleveland a little luck. Black caught Felix, but the momentum sent him backpedaling where Impulse was able to tag him as he leaned against the ropes. Felix hit a reverse hurricanrana on Black, but never saw Impulse catch him from behind with a quick Back Suplex that had his upper spine folding like an accordion. Not one to let a two-on-one situation go lightly unless it involves a straw and big ice cream sundae…Kooter barreled into the ring with his atypical war-cry. Impulse ducked out of the way of a clothesline attempt, but Kooter caught Black right in the kisser with a big boot as he charged the ring. Randall Knox knew what he had to do and fired the Sudden Impact Superkick, but Kooter having felt the brunt of the attack once before…quickly brought up his arms and crossed them in front of his face to deflect the impact. This led to the slight opening of Felix hooking in a Victory Roll on Impulse as he staggered back, but he was only get a two count. As both stood up, Kooter was on his own impulsive freight train speed and charged in. Impulse ducked out of the high impact spinning back elbow smash from Kooter, but Felix did not and fell through the ropes nearly knocked out cold. Kooter yelled in panic, turned around and lowered his head just in time to avoid another Sudden Impact Superkick… …too bad he found himself clutched around the throat by Rook Black’s hand. Impulse flew off the ropes and hit a spinning legsweep clothesline to the lower back of Kooter, while the National Champ drove him down with the Gauntlet Throwdown! A three count later and you could tell post-match that Felix AND Kooter, especially Kooter was upset with each other after arguing. The fans were nervous and that made me feel GIDDY. Brock Alyas vs. High Flyer After a few minutes of promotions from Sirius and the New Frontier, we were back while a man characterized as a Lunatic walked to the ring talking to a mask he was holding in his hand. The fans booed, Grossard hoped for a stiffer drug-testing policy against guys that Eddie Mayfield would no doubt manipulate into heelish activities, so I thought of contacting a Voodoo Priestess to make a doll of Grossard. Then, I remembered that Eddie would frown upon me for contacting Anne Mystic. So yeah, I just introduced High Flyer. If you’re not paying attention, it’s not my fault or maybe your better judgment. An argument for the ages I could debate, but now the music in the arena was seriously distracting me in a bad way. ‘Cause as usual, I hated Brock Alyas’ music. As Grossard obviously pointed out, we had two men not above breaking the rules, but that wrestled in completely different styles. One didn’t think and just hit like a brickhouse, while one tended to plot and mastermind with the brain of a brick house. Oh, who am I kidding? Alyas has the brain of a brick house too. The bell woke me up from my Alyas music induced post-seizure and apparently a match had started. As Flyer talked to the Fakepulse mask he had placed on top of a ringpost, Alyas crushed him from behind with a rushing double axehandle. Alyas wheeled around Flyer and started gutting him with a series of rapid fire shoulderblocks that had Flyer lifting into the air on each impact. Irish Whip, Turnbuckle Impalement and that was followed by Alyas skyrocketing Flyer up and over with a back body drop. One bullrush clothesline later and Flyer was lucky to have fallen outside the ring. That was until Alyas followed him out and introduced him to various pieces of ringside furniture and fan barricades. Alyas rolled Flyer back into the ring and continued the assault with massive kicks and stomps. Another Irish Whip set up Flyer for a ring-shaking Spinebuster and near pinfall. Alyas snorted and sent a snot-rocket whizzing by the ref's face and then dropped Flyer with a devastating Uranage for another near pin. Alyas yelled at the ref some more, picked up Flyer and placed him on the turnbuckles. As Alyas set up for a Superplex, Flyer took the Fakepulse mask and put it on the Detroit Phenom's face backwards producing my first genuine laugh of the evening! Alyas freaked out and lost control, letting Flyer land on top of him with a modified bodypress and that almost ended the match. Alyas (mask still on) rushed to his feet trying to tear off the mask, but found himself the perfect prey to a Springboard Enziguiri which put the Ninth Mile resident flat on his face. Of course, this led to Flyer freaking out about the Fakepulse mask staring back up at him and he started to viciously stomp the mask aka the back of Alyas' head. Rolling Kneedrop and a Flyer strut followed as Alyas started rolling around, bellowing out at his opponent and the likelihood he was going to rip out his spleen and fill his liver with piss. I think that's when Calamity made some off-color remark regarding Chicken Noodle Soup. Flyer ripped off the mask himself, wrapped it around his fist and started savagely dropping right handed fistbombs to Alyas' skull which stopped once the ref pulled off the mask. Flyer seemingly returned to normalcy and hooked in a La Magistral Cradle with a tug of the tights, but couldn't keep Alyas down for the count. Flyer brought Alyas back up and dropped him with a swinging neckbreaker, then headed up top and came crashing down with a crushing elbow drop. Almost getting the 3 count, it seemed Flyer as feeling a little desperate and he started amping himself up with the Fakepulse mask. He tied it around his boot and attempted a Superkick (oh, the irony!) but Alyas ducked and hooked a Head and Arm Suplex that compacted the Lunatic in half. Both men were down, but Alyas got up and took control with a huge clothesline and diving headbutt. Alyas started ssmelling the blood and reeled in Flyer and delivered a vicious snap powerbomb that nearly ended the match, but Flyer’s Fakepulse masked boot managed to get on the ropes. Alyas argued with the ref, Flyer attempted a Victory Roll, but instead took an Elevator Drop. …and that’s when Cameron Cruise’s music blared! Alyas immediately became wide-eyed considering what had happened at the last house show. Awaiting his bitter archenemy and opponent at Crash 48, Alyas never saw Flyer get up…and crush him with the Locomotive Yakuza Kick in the back. Alyas crumpled to the mat and found himself hooked in the Peaceful Slumber Elevated Boston Crab, while Cruise’s music cut. Flyer held the locked in submission for as long as he could… …and that’s when Cameron Cruise ran down to the ring wearing a Coal Miner’s Glove! Alyas was about to reach the ropes, but Cruise waited for the precise moment where the ref was shielded from view to crack Alyas in the jaw with the loaded glove. Flyer was able to drag Alyas back to the middle of the ring, where the ref raised his arm three times and watched it drop three times. As Flyer’s music played, Cruise took off the glove and turned it inside out…revealing steel-plating. One thing is for certain…when Cruise and Alyas finish this war off at Crash 48, it won’t be pretty…and 50% of our outside interference may be cut. Joe the Plumber vs. Legion So, you’d think that’d be the end. You’d think I’d escape this misery. And that’s when Legion would get a spotlight, while sitting in the upper deck. Chaos, the eventual downfall of NFW, the fight against the oppressor known as Eddie Mayfield. Let’s just say that Legion isn’t exactly happy with the latest environmental change in NFW and said that it was now his aim to destroy everything and everyone that represented it. …and that’s when the dogs started barking. That’s when Ol’ Dirty Bastard started soiling my ears. The World Champion, Joe the Plumber walked out and said that while he may not have the championship belt yet, he wasn’t going to let anyone badmouth his peoples. UGH. Sorry, I puked in my mouth. So, he said he was putting his title on the line, right here and right now against Legion if he had the balls to make his way down to the ring. Well, I think we know where this one goes. Legion started shoving the crowd aside and took a few minutes to get to ringside, where he charged the waiting JTP in the ring. Right hand, right hand, right hand, right hand, right hand, right hand, right hand, right hand. Let’s just say that these two dudes punched each other in the face more than the crowd expected for the first literal two minutes of the match…to the point that the fans were actually in a little shock they’d do that to each other for so long. Finally, Legion raked the eyes and whacked Joe in the face with a forearm uppercut. JTP staggered back, Legion sent him packing into the turnbuckles and nailed a running kneesmash to the gut. Legion reeled JTP back out and hooked an Implant DDT, which produced a 2.9998372 count from the referee and just about 20,000 fans pissing their pants in near-shock. Legion brought JTP back up, sent him packing into the turnbuckles for a second time and charged in with a clothesline…and this time, nobody was home. The crowd exploded as Joe started a round of quick-fire jabs before doing the Brooklyn Shuffle and walloping Legion with a roundhouse right that sent him to the mat. Clothesline, Clothesline, Clothesline…Legion finally stayed down like a normal person, but that left him prey to a faceful of greasy elbow as Joe crashed on top of him. The Bousse decided to really make everyone wretch by rubbing his armpit in Legion’s face. Legion scampered up in a severe state of disarray and found himself wrapped up by Joe and dropped in a modified standing spike spinebuster. JTP then mounted the challenger and started pounding his skull with more rights. Joe ripped up Legion and planted him back into the mat with a Gourdbuster than hit the world’s ugliest running senton across the challenger’s back. Joe than brought Legion up and sent him off the ropes, nailed a boot to the gut and set up for a Powerbomb…only Legion started whacking him in the skull with fists, forearm and elbow smashes. Joe tumbled backwards, Legion landing on top and nearly getting a pin. Both got up and Legion struck first with a kick to the gut and then hit a Reverse Neckbreaker to turn back the tides in his favor. Legion sat up Joe and started barraging his face and back with nasty sidekicks. Frighteningly, Joe tried to sit up and take as many as possible…his final collapse convinced Legion to go for the pin, but only a near pinfall. Legion brought Joe back up, hit a Triple Rolling Suplex with a rollover pin and once again only got a two count. Time to up the ante, so to speak…so Legion headed up top and hit a spinning back elbow as Joe charged him. Legion went back up top and connected with a big splash. The crowd was on edge, seriously nervous over the prospect of Legion winning the World Title. No dice as Joe was able to kickout just in the nick of time. My mind raced towards why Eddie Mayfield hadn’t intervened yet, then I realized this was a lose-lose situation any which way at the moment and hopefully they’d kill each other. Legion reeled in JTP into a standing headscissors and hit a Piledriver. Another 2.99 count and Legion freaked out, started yelling at the ref and the fans. Legion took Joe to the turnbuckles and placed him on top, no doubt setting up for his Super Brainbuster… To the crowd exaltation, Joe started fighting back immediately and knocked Legion from the top rope and hit the world’s greasiest Cross Body Block off the top. As Legion suffocated under the stain-covered chest under JTP, he just managed to kick out from a pinfall attempt. Joe brought Legion back up and slammed him to the mat, climbed up top and somersaulted off…NOBODY HOME. Now, the crowd was freaking out. Legion quickly got up and set up for the Six Million Dollar Drive to Hell Curbstomp, but JTP broke free just at the last second and rolled out of the way. Joe stood up in a stagger and Legion went for a Lou Thesz Press, but Joe caught him and dropped an Inverted Atomic Drop. Legion stood bowlegged, JTP nailed a kick to the gut and FLUSH! Shit River Plunge. Turn out the lights, the party’s over. JTP gets the 3 count and barely survives the surprisingly close challenge from Legion. And now, we wait for Crash 48…as I watch toilet streamers float in the air. ...kill me now.
