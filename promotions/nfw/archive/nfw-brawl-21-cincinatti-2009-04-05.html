NFW Brawl 21: Cincinatti, 5 April 2009

5 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
NFW Brawl 21: Cincinatti 5 Apr 2009 U.S. Bank Arena, Cincinnati, Ohio (seats 17,000) Intro Sirius channel 420, Ben Harper singing about his "Will to Live," and we’re launched into NFW BRAWL. I’m Stephen Fleir, one of the commemorative interns in the new NFW internship program now commissioned by President Mayfield. No drugs, no alcohol, just straight-up wrestling. For those that care, I’m Magna Cum Laude at the Charlotte School of Heelology. Heading up the show announcing duties, we’ve got M. Wayne Grossard and Sean Toombs, while Calamity once again takes over the crowd interactions and 2-minute trans about the state of society. For some reason, I don’t think he’s lasting too long in this role with the way he talks. He’s filthy and insubordinate, so certainly not a Mayfield Man. As Grossard gives a brief rundown and comments on the economic instability of Cincinatti, we’ve got music and one portly luchadore coming down to the ring. Dos Equis vs. Cameron Cruise Dos came to the ring with his new partner... himself. Futurequis. Whatever. Apparently in the future, people have different body types and voices. Regardless, Futurequis got on the microphone and told the crowd that he remembered this match and that Cameron Cruise was going to lose. Cruise disagreed, and, the first thing he did when he came out was grab Futurequis by the back of the neck and toss him over the top. Dos Equis used the moment's distraction to start his attack, with a jump and knee to the back that sent Cruise into the corner. Dos held onto the advantage for most of the early goings, using his speed to keep Cruise off balance and gradually wearing the Crippler down. Cruise would not be denied, however, as he managed to use his ring awareness to hook a foot over the ropes every time Dos tried for a pin; though he was still able to kick out at two. That experience would seem to have been the difference, as Cruise stepped into Dos Equis' Guinness Punch attempt, caught the Luchador of Luchadors by the waist, and dropped him with a modified spinebuster. It seemed to be all over from that point as Cruise dismantled the Luchador of Luchadors with a power assault that did not let up. Until Brock Alyas rushed out of the crowd when Cruise was leaned against the ropes, waiting for Dos to get back to his feet following a powerslam. Brock cracked a chair over the back of Cameron's head and made his escape the same way. The referee's back was toward him, as he was trying to see if Dos was going to quit. He didn't. But he did have enough left in him to crawl over and make the three count. After Dos and Futurequis left the ring, and Cruise was back on his feet, the SmokeScreen played the chairshot, and Cruise's attention went to the direction Brock left the ringside area, and he immediately hit the crowd, looking for his nemesis. High Flyer vs. Steve Knox Maybe one day we’ll clear the crazy out of NFW, I see the desire in President Mayfield’s eyes. A land where brass knuckles are used for pinfalls and not promos with hookers. A world where salt to the eyes isn’t a reference to Felix Red making it rain with cocaine. One day we may reach this Heel Nation of Hope. In the meantime, we deal with men like Steve Knox and High Flyer. The former National Champion entered the ring first, which was probably a good thing in this case. High Flyer walked out with a microphone in one hand and a chair in the other, and declared that this match was now a Triangle Match for the NFW Television Championship. That got a good number of cheers from the crowd, unfortunately, they quickly turned to boos when he turned his chair over and showed the Fakepulse mask duct taped to the back. Apparently, "Chairpulse" was on his side, as he immediately started to threaten Knox with it as soon as he got into the ring. The referee warned him to put the chair down, so Flyer set it up in the corner and took a seat. Knox was apparently not in the mood for this, so, instead of waiting for the referee to remove the chair, he simply walked up and grabbed a handful of High Flyer's hair, and, over the referee's objections, flipped him off the chair into the middle of the ring. It was back - and - forth for several exciting minutes at that point, and the fans were on their feet for the duration. Until Knox reversed an Irish Whip into a backdrop, and followed up with a clothesline to the back of the head that sent Flyer over the top rope. When he reached over to try and pull the Lunatic back into the ring, Flyer busted him over the head with his decorated chair, immediately drawing the bell and a DQ victory for Steve Knox. After the bell, High Flyer argued with the referee, citing that 'Impulse' had attacked Knox, not him. To illustrate the point, he bashed himself in the head with the chair. Twice. Three times. Three more referees hit ringside when Flyer tried to get back into the ring, and he reluctantly walked back up the aisle. Before he left the view of the fans, however, he pulled the mask off the chair and stared at it for a few seconds. And he laughed. Quite madly. Joe the Plumber vs. Normal Human Beings Next up, Cojones Mercado was out to talk for the administration regarding their recent problems with the temporarily positioned World Champion, Joe the Plumber. In short, he was sent out to explain that if Joe doesn’t relinquish his claim to the throne…he no get up.s Dogs barking. Some snaggle-toothed crazyman rapping. Our NFW World Champion, ladies and gentlemen…the skidmark on the Eddie Mayfield Era. Toilet streamers rained down the ceiling and UGHHHHHHHHH. Yeah, just ugh, it’s Joe. He’s got the mic, he’s threatening Cojones and demands that he be delivered his championship belt or he’s not letting anyone come down to ringside. Thank goodness for Eddie Mayfield’s T-1 Connection to the FRONTIERtron. Mayfield is in the Presidential Suite of the Riverfront Hotel. Since being told that this was such a "Shit River" town, he figured to many of Plumber’s relatives would be present and he didn’t feel like breathing the air would be smart either. I wholeheartedly agree. Plumber challenges him to be a man and come down for the UGHN or something, I really don’t understand the guy. Mayfield quotes the air as he tells him he can defend his "championship" tonight in a handicap match. Not just in the sense of a 2-on-1 situation, but he’ll be facing equally retarded individuals as himself. The tag team of Normal Human Beings. The fans rained boos down on Shawn Coppola, David Jericho, and their manager, Michael Heenan from nearly the moment the trio entered the arena. Apparently, their attitudes to date had not endeared the Normal Human Beings to the NFW fans. Also, none of them seemed to care. It was like night and day when Joe the Plumber entered the arena: the fans were on their feet for the unlikely NFW World Heavyweight Champion. He carried his plunger over his shoulder and pulled a covered red wagon behind him. Once in the ring, the BOUSE~!! of the NFW slammed the plunger head into the mat, standing it up straight, and gestured to both Coppola and Jericho. The fans roared as the NHB got very red in the face and shouted several layers of obscenity at the World Champion. Apparently, he was asking them which wanted to sit on the plunger first. Our esteemed official removed the plunger from the ring and called for the bell. It was simple - a handicap tag team match with the World Championship on the line, and Joe had nobody to back him up. He started off against Coppola, who the fans almost immediately began chanting "ACTOR-FAG!" against, broken up only by the "JAY-TEE-PEE!" chant that seemed to have settled in the upper level. They went back and forth for a few minutes, with neither man able to get the upper hand. Coppola seemed to have the edge in official training, but Joe's experience and instinct more than made up for it. Frustrated, Coppola tagged out to Jericho, who fared little better, though the two - on - one advantage all but assured that if they could keep the pressure on, Joe would most likely run out of steam first. Unfortunately the match turned on them after approximately fifteen minutes. David Jericho missed a Lionsault when Ol' Joe rolled out of the way at the last second, and he immediately scooped him for the Shit River Plunge! Michael Heenan was on the spot, however, and pulled Joe out of the ring just as the three was about to hit. Joe didn't take too kindly to this and attacked Heenan, all the while Coppola was helping his partner up. Quickly and efficiently, JTP dragged Heenan to the covered red wagon, which he pulled the sheet from to reveal - much to everyone else's disgust - a beheaded toilet bowl with a familiar odor eminating from the bowels. No pun intended. One extra - fudgey swirley later, and Heenan was no longer a factor. Joe climbed back into the ring with his hands covered with his day job. The referee didn't have a chance to make any kind of ruling on the legality, since the NHB immediately rushed the Champion. He downed them with a hard double clothesline that caught Jericho across the face. Nothing like a chinstain to wake a person up. He slid out of the ring quickly and noisily while Ol' Joe hooked both his hands inside Coppola's mouth. Between the retching of the wrestlers and the fans, the referee was able to record a submission victory, and Ol' Joe had another one under his belt…and I was ready to puke after just watching on the monitors backstage. Impulse vs. Legion So, while the crew was cleaning up in their radioactive material prevention suits, Legion decided to join Calamity in the crowd scene. Feeding the former half of Hellmachine a fifth of Jack was the easy part to distract him, getting the crowd in Cincinatti to start throwing their chairs at each other…not so easy. Legion then got upset and accused them all of falling to the mind control of the new regime that he will tear apart from the very stitched seams it inherited. I quickly checked off Legion as the next wrestler to be "randomly" drug tested. Legion kept yelling about the Revolution, but wouldn’t you know it – we had a champion walk out and say that the Revolution is over, but the Evolution has just begun. Cheers rocked the auditorium as the Television Champion, Impulse stood on the entrance ramp with a mic. He said that Legion may be a man completely interested in senseless violence and anarchy, but that wouldn’t matter in the ring against a wrestler like himself. Legion asked him if that was a challenge, snake’s smile included. Randall Knox smiled back, saying that if he wanted it to be one, then maybe he should stop hanging out in the beer garden. And with that in the tradition of all NFW Brawls, which is an unfortunate side-effect of Craig Miles alcohol-fueled ideas…well, we’ve got a Television Title match. Impulse got in the ring, ready to roll as Legion started shoving aside the crowd to get to ringside. Legion slid in and charged right into a hiptoss, then scurried up into a dropkick on the jaw which sent him flying into the corner. Knox continued the assault with some knife edge chops, but took a vicious headbutt from the We are Many anarchist. Impulse staggered around and got whiplashed from a running clothesline. Legion stayed on the offensive with a brigade of stomps, elbow and kneedrops. Legion tried to hit a piledriver, but ended up getting flipped over by Impulse onto his back. Impulse then connected with a double stomp and running Senton Splash. The crowd seemed to get behind the TV Champ, especially since he was climbing the top rope to probably try something suicidal. Of course, Legion expected such tomfoolery and dodged out of the way of a Frog Splash attempt. The miss took the wind out of the TV Champ and the crowd as Impulses was prey to a dastardly double legdrop to the groin and then a series of gutbusters from Legion. The Devil’s Reject peppered the champion with a series of Muay Thai Kicks in the corner and took him up top for what looked to be a Super Brainbuster, but Impulse was able to knock him off. Knox went for a Sunset Flip off the top, but Legion moved out of the way again. Legion then attempted a Powerbomb, but somehow Impulse was able to counter it with a Hurricanrana. Both men got to their feet, but it was Knox’s foot connecting with Legion’s chin on a Sudden Impact Superkick that spelled O-V-E-R. See you in Cleveland, if you most of you haven’t been arrested or put in rehab.
