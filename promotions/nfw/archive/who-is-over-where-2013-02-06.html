who is over where?

6 February 2013
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Rook Black — 6 February 2013 I was thinking about this a bit ago. In our mythos, who gets the big pops in a region? Bryant would likely own the south, being both a good ole boy and espousing certain redneck values.
Impulse has ny. I'd go as far as to perhaps suggest that the enlightened liberal elements of each coast and midwest also appeal.
I was amused to imagine that Bryan's heavy accent would mean that the folks in Australia would not actually be able to understand him when he's talking. But the good ole boy, roughhousing redneck, would seem to fit in pretty good.
I like to imagine Rook would always get some love in Southeast Asia for beating that evil North Korean General bloody in Tokyo Disneyland way back when. But in reality, I'm sure he's only really over in the Austin City Limits, where the Texan intellectuals congregate.
but this is all speculation really, as the genuine canonical mythos of what's over in NFW must originate from our world building consensus. Contribute your vision!
Biron — 6 February 2013 Very cool idea. Yeah, I'd imagine that JB is the big draw in the South. I really liked your idea for Aussies not being able to understand JB and was going to do bit at a restaurant or something where there was major communication issues. But, as you said, I think JB's roughouse, blue collar image puts him over with the Australians.
Agree w/ Impulse in NY, for sure. Not sure about the rest of the East Coast. Would Castor be a bigger hit in California (LA-Hollywood)? Billy's already said that Castor spent a lot of time in Berlin so you could assume he's really over in Germany and maybe more of Europe.
Akita is probably over as hell in Japan ... or that's my guess. He's pretty accomplished in prominent US promotions so that would be a point of pride.
More comments to come as they pop into my melon. LQJT86C — 6 February 2013 I have Castor being over in Europe and the East Coast, specifically MA, CT, PA, NY, and NJ. Reasoning: he started with NFW during the Lowell days, so the old school hardcore fans are probably really loyal to him for that alone (along with Dorchester, Wildstar, OSS, and whoever else still around from those days). As far as NY/NJ/PA, well, it's a hardcore wrestling market, and Castor has deep connections with some of the biggest name guys who built themselves in leagues based there (Alex Wylde, Mike Manson, bunch of other guys people probably forgot about). I also have some backstory that gives him more roots in NJ and NY, and you'll probably understand more when I finally post that damn article this weekend.
Impulse is huge in NY (ask Pete!), and when NY-based NLW was around, they featured him as the main attraction for that reason.
I see Nova and Harmen being huge on the West Coast (really everywhere; where haven't they wrestled?) for obvious reasons.
Jack Bryant is BIG in the South! I think... JJ is also big in the South stemming from his CSWA days, and his constant shilling of NC. Again, all my imagination.
brusch — 6 February 2013 Agree with all the above, plus: Ocho may get pops in Seattle since he's wrestled a lot there (and still does), and possibly Chicago since that's his hometown...not sure where else.
Teddy Alexander's got Pittsburgh and Philadelphia on lockdown, I'd think. User Poets — 6 February 2013 I think Bryant would be over huge everywhere - he'd be Superman in the South since he's from there, but the take-no-shit Southern wrestler is just plain popular.
Nova and Harmen - everywhere. They've been everywhere, they have their rep. Same with Rook.
I can see people cheering JJ in Texas and Greensboro, but only a small minority - he's been an overall ass for his entire career and has never given the fans a reason to cheer.
Castor - Huge in Northern, non - Bible Belt cities as well as Europe. Impulse - NY Hometown boy and proud of it, but he doesn't have the "I'm from New York which makes me better than you" attitude that Eli Flair had so I think he'd still get his props in places like Boston and Philly and Atlanta which traditionally booed Eli. But in New York City, he's Ric Flair in Charlotte or Lawler in Memphis.
Biron — 6 February 2013 Malik is probably over in the SEC-states, also. Agree on Rook, Nova, and Harmen. Been in most of the big feds and done it all.
