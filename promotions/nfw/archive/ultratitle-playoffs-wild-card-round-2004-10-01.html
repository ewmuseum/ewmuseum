Ultratitle Playoffs WILD CARD ROUND 1 Oct 2004 North Charleston, SC The Story Thus Far... The day NFW grabbed the bull by its horns. INTO THE FIRE (I) "You know what I don’t get?" Shawn Douglas reclined his seat inside the Jeep Wrangler with a pensive, yet quizzical look in his eyes. The empty highway in South Carolina generated an enormous rush of air, with it a sense of freedom Douglas missed dearly over his few months in New Frontier Wrestling. The identity of the man driving the vehicle was obscured by the hood of his sweatshirt, Douglas knew enough about this man that the freedom he felt, would most likely come at a dangerous price. The man had been loved and hated by many friends and foes, even Douglas had personal issues to grapple with concerning the identity of the driver, and what it would mean to his own family to fight by his side. Douglas watched the trees blur by, much like the blindsiding he received upon entrance to NFW. Douglas snapped out of the daydreaming, continuing his question. "Why did the people higher than Prosser wait until now?" "It’s as simple as life and death," the driver responded curtly. "How?" "Avery Everette is dead," there was tension in the voice, but no emotion detected. "Avery Everette?" "The original owner of the UWA," the man turned his face towards Douglas. Sunglasses along with the hood made the rustic face unrecognizable. He continued with a sigh, "I guess we’ve got some time before the show if you want to be bored with rambling facts, hearsay and opinions about a story involving deception, inheritances and attempted murders." …it was silent for a moment, Douglas soaking in the words, trying to detect a sense of sarcasm with a punchline that wasn’t coming… "That’s your warning kid, so take it or leave it." "Any and all answers would be appreciated before I feel justified in joining you, considering what you knew about my Uncle." Douglas said matter-of-factly, a wound within he’d have to rectify and heal on his own time to proceed with this quest. "Fair enough, kid." The driver pulled out a stick of gum and put it in his mouth. "Well, it all started back in early 1993, the UWA that is. Much like the league you’re in, surprisingly enough it ended up being defined through two different runs – entitled 1.0 and 2.0." "Yeah, I never understood that," Douglas replied with a smirk. "Its just like Prosser, man. Everyone wants their era labeled, so they can defend it to the masses. Avery Everette founded the UWA, built it from scratch and fought for it with his blood, sweat and tears. Everette was the one that gave Prosser his first break. Not only him, but Tom Adler, Scott Waters, Stumanchoo, Flic Rair, names you’ve probably never heard of like JW Collins." "Adler…I know that name," Douglas nodded. "That’s because he’s still out there…doing whatever it is that he does…anyway, in ’95 something horrible happened that changed everything. Everette got kidnapped and was held hostage in a farmhouse, probably somewhere in Quaker Country." "Quaker Country?" Douglas asked with an arched eyebrow "UWA was based out of Philadelphia, I imagine that’s where the closest farms are located. I’m surprised you don’t remember it…you were a kid, but hell even CNN covered it." "Can’t say that was a channel I watched back then," Douglas said back with a smile. "Whatever. Adler is important because at the time he was part of the <> Corporation," the man made the hand signal of the well-respected alliance. "When the farmhouse blew up, everybody blamed Adler and his cronies." Douglas’ eyes widened, "It blew up?" "Sure did. Nobody knew what happened, most presumed that Everette died that day. The day he was kidnapped, ironically, Anne Mystic was given control of the company." "Mystic? Never heard of her…" "Well, remember that puzzle piece. Mystic became Interim Commissioner during that period, one notable piece of information lost on my peers is the fact that she was the person who brought the Armed Forces into the UWA." "Who?" "Battleship. Submarine. GUNS. There’s good reason that Anne Mystic has been underground five years, but that’s not even the best one. Everette was presumed to be dead, Mystic and Adler’s relationship was flowering if you will…but secretly, Anne would point the authorities into investigating the <> Exchange as the culprits. Of course, imagine her surprise when Everette returned…next thing you knew, Mystic disappeared to South America – only to land in prison." "Wow." "Yeah, crazy shit. Everette came back rail-thin and addicted to cigarettes. By the look in his eye, you knew the whole experience took the life out of him. For the business, for anything. What got everyone’s goat was that when he came back, he came with a second-in-command: Quentin Sullivan." Douglas nodded, "That name I definitely know all too well." "Mystic took it the worst. For two years, she fought to sink her claws into the operation and Everette. Everybody just figured when Avery went, Anne was next in line. Everette comes back, doesn’t help out Anne in South America. Plus, Sullivan came from the indies and just totally rubbed the veterans the wrong way with his whole ‘Attitude in 90’s’ scheme." Douglas laughed, "He hasn’t lost his touch, I see." "Yeah, well it caused a whole mess backstage. Everette became more of a recluse and just disappeared, Sullivan had no idea what he was up against. Adler used a little known title shot of his out of nowhere on the big PPV, won the World Title and things literally went up in a backdraft when Mystic came out from the back, CELEBRATING with him. Apparently, they were an item – Adler had broken her out. Sullivan closed the place down, fired every wrestler and retooled the federation signing only young talent before reopening in ’97." "Every wrestler?" "Every single one of them. Except one, actually. Craig Miles. Mystic was rehired as a heel announcer, Adler wasn’t let back in. At some point, Adler found out what Anne had tried to play on him and their relationship soured. Even more importantly, this is where Avery Prosser’s anger stems from, since he wasn’t given a job either. Of course, the curse continued – the roster once again went into a revolt over booking decisions, the UWA folded for good in ’99 and Mystic wasn’t ever heard from again." "There’s one thing I still don’t get…why wait until now?" "I wasn’t finished, kid. This story still has a lot of gaps, I’ve yet to fill you in on…and listen, this isn’t just about booking decisions, lost jobs and wages. New Frontier Wrestling has quadrupled in value in the past five years. It’s only growing. We’re talking about individuals whose mental well-being has all but deteriorated in their self-believed, rightful claims that all this money, power and fame should be theirs. We’re talking about a woman who until recently, I hadn’t realized would do ANYTHING to get it back." "Just for once, can’t you make this easy?" Douglas said laughing. "Kid, nothing about this makes sense. Not unless you know how important Avery Everette was to New Frontier Wrestling." Live Intro "ESEN Sports – Its in the ring." INTO THE FIRE (IV) Every happy ending that Hollywood ever bestowed on the public usually would stick to the tried and true moral that your hopes and dreams could always be achieved through hard work. In reality, most of us never get to realize the Hollywood dream. Those eternal hopes and escapist dreams give way to the consciousness of reality and survival within the American society. While the realization disheartens us, through other mediums we still strive to see forms of our hopes and dreams come true. Shane Southern knew this all too well all of his life. With a knee that might as well be superglued and duct taped, he still clung to the one hope and dream he knew that he couldn’t let go. Lately, he’d watch it fade away further out of his reach to a point where certain realizations creeped into his conscience. Not only did Shane Southern want himself to be the best, but Shane Southern’s shoulders also held the hopes and dreams of millions of wrestling fans that would never have the chance of being the best; only to try and feel that moment of victory vicariously through the competitor they connected with most. The character of Shane Southern represents so much to the everyday American, what they see on television and the phenomenon its created during his life would probably not be repeated. But America doesn’t know the real Shane Southern, not that they were once far off from the truth – but when the real Shane Southern, the man that’s trained his whole life to succeed at this level leaves the camera lights...he still has to live with his hopes and dreams...as well as so many others. The problem was that Shane Southern didn’t dream, anymore. Nor did he hope, for in his mind it simply wasn’t enough. The change within Southern would be hard for some to accept, or maybe he did still hope that somebody understood. His locker room door creaked open, making his eyes dart to the doorway where Shawn Douglas walked in, followed by the man that called himself El Toro. "Now ain’t ta' time, gentlemen," Southern said with his Louisiana twang. "We need to talk," the succinct statement came from El Toro who sat on a bench directly across from Shane, their eyes locking for a moment. "I ain’t in the mood ta’ talk, sorry." Southern looked away and started packing up his gear, making the hint that now should be the time for his visitors to leave. Meanwhile, El Toro decided now would be a good time to remove his mask. Southern caught the process out of the corner of his eye and sighed in frustration, knowing that if he looked he’d know something he’d probably regret. Then again, after tonight, what could Southern possibly regret further? ...So, he looked... His jaw moved slightly to the right as he looked into the eyes of the man sitting across from him. A number of emotions ranged through Southern’s body that would drive most normal men mad, thankfully the last year for Shane had numbed him quite sufficiently for these momentsYet, Shane Southern was also completely befuddled by the moment as well... Shaking his head, "What tha’ hell is goin’ on?" "I thought you weren’t in the mood to talk." "I changed mah’ mind. Why are y’all here?" "Because...you’re the only guy I can trust." Hopes and Dreams, they sure are a motherfucker.
