NFW Reloaded 07 26 Feb 2011 Rogers Arena, Vancouver, British Columbia (seats 20,000) HWC (c) vs. Brock Alyas and Phil Atken DARK MATCH The match started unassumingly enough. You'd think a couple guys like Atken and Brock would at least put up a fight, but pretty much no. The champs had control right from the opening bell, countering every attempt at offense and quick-tagging often. Blaine Hollywood hit a spinebuster off an Irish Whip and tagged Malik in and he took his turn pummeling Alyas before finally just planting him in the center of the ring with a DDT. After teasing that he'd allow him to make a tag, he pulled him back over and tagged Hollywood in. Blaine Hollywood climbed back onto the second turnbuckle and dropped a knee across the forehead of Brock Alyas before pulling him up and tagging Malik Anderson in one more time. With a smirk, he entered the ring, picked Alyas up and propped him up on his shoulder, dropping straight down with him into a Tiger Driver. Sensing the end, he tagged Blaine Hollywood and put Alyas into position for the Doomsday Backcracker. Hitting the big move, it was academic and the champions retained with an easy three count. The second forearm missed my back, and the assailant nearly lost his balance as his arm to the elbow sailed past my body. But if I was going to take shots from the Windhams, I'd prefer to deserve 'em. Jack Bryant vs. JJ Deville JJ DeVille entered the arena first, flanked by Bobby Jack Windham and August De La Rossi. Immediately, the trio was showered with boos, popcorn, beer, and probably at least one cup of piss. This *is* NFW after all, and we have standards. The fans kept up the boos, until Jack Bryant stormed down the entranceway, not even waiting for his music. Bobby Jack lunged at the Birmingham Stallion, but JB hooked him around the waist, picked him up, spun him around, and drove him into the mat with a hard spinebuster. If Bobby Jack was his opponent, it was probably time to get the three count. That really set the pace of the match. JJ and JB tore into each other for sixteen straight minutes, with neither man taking a backstep or having the slightest bit of hesitation in his face. Only two things affected the natural course of this match: the first was at the fifteen minute mark when Bandit emerged from the backstage, evidently recovered from the rumored incident in the locker room earlier tonight. He took position next to De la Rossi and Bobby Jack, just watching. The second thing was more atmospheric: Jack Bryant is bigger and stronger than JJ DeVille. JJ put up a good fight, and - admittedly – a mostly clean fight. JJ's big mistake was trying to go toe to toe and blow for blow with Jack Bryant, and JB simply wore him down. JJ fought bravely, and as the flesh (not the spirit) finally fell, there was a distinct cheer for the efforts of the Original King of Cool.
