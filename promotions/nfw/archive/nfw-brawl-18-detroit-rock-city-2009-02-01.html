NFW Brawl 18: Detroit Rock City, 1 February 2009

1 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
NFW Brawl 18: Detroit Rock City 1 Feb 2009 Palace of Auburn Hills, Auburn Hills, Michigan (seats 22,076) Previously...ON NFW! The horrible, squeak of jail bars opening at Scotland Correctional was the worst possible sound you could hear, for an inmate – besides the mysterious toilet flush in the middle of the night. That shit will get you killed. All inmates on Range E were forced to be woken up at approximately 5:05AM on Tuesday January 27th 2008 by that very sound. And you can imagine what the guys doing 25 to life had to say, let it be clear – some rated R comments. After filing some paperwork and bullshit returning the inmate with the belongings he had on him prior to his arrest – a Verizon Wireless cellphone, half a package of Marlboro 72s, a Zippo lighter with a pair of breasts imprinted on them, a wallet containing $63 as well as a belt and shoelaces detached from their proper dwelling. …he was once again, a free man. Wasting no time, the former Inmate immediately lit a cigarette upon stepping through the main gates and onto McGirts Bridge Rd. in Laurinburg, NC. After a couple deep inhales of his favorite medicine, he looked around trying to figure just where the fuck he was. After putting out the cigarette via tossing it still lit onto the street, a car pulled up and lowered its window. With a deep breath, the Inmate creaked out a smile as he recognized the man looking back at him. The man in the car had his own cigarette lit and smiled. "Get in the car, Brock…you’ve got a job to do." WELCOME TO THE SHOW! After a 3-month hiatus with a retooled administration in charge, the Midwest clamored into the Motor City to see the first glimpse of the Eddie Mayfield era. Well, the pageantry was apparently left for the upcoming CRASH 47 show that will come to Chicago in late winter. Posters, banners and other graphic art were emblazoned with Eddie’s grill in Obama colors, but other than that…you wouldn’t have known the difference tonight. Of course, that’s the normal fare for NFW house shows as they just bring the TRON and a few fireworks…but we all know they save the big moments for live television. Sirus Channel 4020 tapped the NFW Brawl introduction from Calamity and Sean Toombs of Hellmachine, who announced that they the new administration was still looking for a new play-by-play man due to Wildstar’s shocking comeback at SUPERCrash 2. New Dawn Rising as tag champs precluded the five-star legend from his announcing duties, so we were left with the raucous legends of NFW early years tag division. The fireworks exploded, the music blared and we were off on the speedway. Mike McGee vs. 'Impulse' The purveyor ANTI-Management came out with a picketing sign with Mayfield’s grill within a No Smoking sign. McGee grabbed the mic and started going off on Mayfield when Impulse’s music hit and the fans roared. Little did they know that ‘Impulse,’ was coming through the crowd and he slid in the ring delivering a low bow to McGree from behind. The crowd booed as ‘Impulse’ choke, gouged and stomped McGee across the ring. Finally, there was something REALLY to cheer about as the REAL IMPULSE strutted out from the entrance and made his way down to ringside. He watched as Fake Impulse started getting distracted, which allowed for a McGee roll-up and a near pinfall. The action continued with McGee using an array of amateur takedowns and a fallaway slam to nearly get the finish. ‘Impulse’ countered with an Inverted Atomic Drop and another low blow, rolled out of the ring and grabbed a chair. Impulse ran around and prevented him from getting in the ring, by playing tug-of-war with the chair until McGee connected with a sliding dropkick that sent ‘Impulse’ into the barricade. McGee rolled him in the ring and hit the UNION BUSTER! (Go 2 Sleep) A three count later and you could see Impulse smiling underneath his mask as he walked back to the locker room. Unfortunately, ANOTHER ‘Impulse’ popped out of the entrance and CRUSHED his face with a shovel! Security rushed him, but ‘Impulse’ took off into the crowd leaving them all bewildered and angry for his actions. As for McGee, he celebrated in the ring and got the microphone to run down Mayfield some more…only to have his microphone ‘mysteriously’ turned off. Winner: Mike McGee Dos Equis vs. La Estrella 100% It's pretty funny that a wrestler named Dos Equis came to the ring just as we ordered a round of them. He took on La Estrella, and Esty remained overmatched from bell to bell. That's not true. He kicked Dos Equis once, I think. Other than that, Beers dominated. He figured out pretty quickly that he was going to win this match by a landslide, so he spent most of it showing off. The fans tried to act annoyed with his 'Top That!' mentality, but couldn't help but pop for the big moves. What can I say, the guy can wrestle. Dos Equis finished La Estrella off with a lethal combination of a double stomp to the belly, slingshotted from outside the ring, and a double underhook brainbuster. Apparently he calls these the Bulimia Nervosa Bomb and Hangover, respectively. Most of the people here will know all about a hangover tomorrow, but La Estrella felt the effects immediately and was an easy target for a three. Winner: Dos Equis Vic Tarano vs. P. King Duk This wasn't a match; it was a massacre. Vic Tarano didn't even wait for the bell before he clobbered Duk with a forearm to the face that sent him into the corner. Sad to say, P. King Duk hitting the corner was the closest thing to an offensive move we'd see out of him all match. Tarano dismantled him from pillar to post and planted him in the middle of the ring with the Bull Rush and an academic three count. Afterwards, he spiked the pron Duk again. The referee threatened him with a reversal/disqualification, and even though he argued a bit, he left the ring without any more damage. Personally, I'd like to see this guy take on Brock Alyas if he ever gets out of jail. All wagers in, which would explode first? Winner: Vic Tarano Phil Atken vs. Kooter Michaels-Cruise This was the match that half the audience was saving their drugs for. Kooter came to ringside alone, though shouting about monsters in his underwear or something of the sort the entire way. Phil was accompanied by his parents, Dirk Dickwood and Helga... Dickwood, I guess? Well, they didn't really accompany him, they trailed about twenty feet behind since he kind of sprinted to the ring. Think about that for a second. He sprinted to the ring to take on Kooter. This wasn't really a match. Atken hit KMC with everything he could think of, but the big purple toddler didn't look like it affected him at all. He got in his shots where he could, and when he was able to grab hold of Atken he hammered him to the mat. Atken countered this by managing to roll out of the ring after every shot, which eventually got the crowd to chant "He's a Coward!" in unison. Which infuriated the Dickwoods, of course. Dirk asked the crowd at one point if they wanted to get in the ring with Kooter. Of course, he asked the drunkest guy he could find, who didn't even know where Kooter was. Go figure. Atken finally got the advantage when he slid outside the ring the last time and Kooter reached for him. Helga picked Dirk up and lawn darted him towards Kooter's mask, dropping all his weight across his neck on the middle rope, It was enough to get him to drop Atken, who scrambled away and reentered the ring on another side. It was also enough for the referee to admonish Dickwood for his interference. Dirk moved away from Kooter enough for Helga to waffle him on top of the head with a steel chair, knocking him back into a rollup pin for Atken. A handful of tights (eeeew) and a three count later, and Atken was out of there, victory in hand. Winner: Phil Atken Cary Turkington vs. Legion vs. The Cosmo Kid Up next was a triple threat match with three very different characters. Cary Turkington was an untested newcomer to the New Frontier, Legion was the anarchist trying to seed the feelings of discontent and dissatisfaction against the new regime and the Cosmo Kid, a newly introduced ally to Steve Knox. What was evident early was Turkington couldn’t wrestle, Cosmo Kid could fly and Legion was out for blood. Turkington and Legion brawled a lot, allowing Cosmo Kid to fly off the ropes and connect with such moves as a split-legged dropkick and flying double bodypress that nearly pinned both competitors. Legion was able to land a curbstomp on Turkington after bashing his head into the turnbuckles ten straight times, but Cosmo broke it up with Cosmic Jam Legdrop off the top. Cosmo went for the pin, but Legion kicked out. Cosmo went back up and for a Moonsault, but Legion got up the knees. As both staggered up, Turkington tossed Legion out of the ring and was able to land a flush right hand on the Kid’s jaw. Whatever’s packed in Cary’s hand, put the Kid down…with a little tug of the tights for the 3 count. Winner: Cary Turkington Rook Black vs. High Flyer High Flyer hasn't had quite the same level of success in NFW to date as he's had everywhere else he's gone. No doubt he was looking to change that tonight with a National Title victory over new champion Rook Black. Unfortunately, nobody told Rook this plan, because he started things off quick and heavy, keeping the challenger off balance with a concentrated attack on Flyer's head and chest. He probably thought if Flyer couldn't breathe and couldn't see straight, he couldn't beat him. If he wanted Flyer to not be able to breathe or see straight, he could've just bought him a third row ticket. Plenty of us are still getting our wares inside. Flyer turned things around when he reversed a suplex attempt into a small package. It sounds small, but that little bit of a break allowed him to slide outside and take a breather. And, of course, when Rook went out to get him, Flyer was ready and dropped him with a shoulder to the gut and a DDT on the floor! It wasn't to be for the Neighborhood Lunatic, however. After a back and forth brawl on the outside that Flyer ended up in control of, he sent the Champ back into the ring and set him up for the Locomotive, only to see Doc Curiosity coming down the aisle. Curiosity was running down with the Curious Hammer and Flyer looked confused on whether to leave the ring…or go for the finish. Unfortunately, he made the decision too late in going for the Yakuza Kick allowing Rook to duck it, spin once, and land a hand on the back of his neck. And not just any hand… The Gauntlet Backhand. The ref made the three count, Curiosity hit the ring…and CRUSHED Black with the Curious Hammer! The crowd went bonkers as he started bashing him over the head, but he got pulled off by Flyer who hooked him for a DDT stopping the assault. Meanwhile, The Life of the Mind collected his title and left the ring without a word, holding his head…leaving Flyer and Curiosity in a staredown, both frustrated with each other…as always. Winner: Rook Black Impulse/Steve Knox vs. Teresa Quaranta/Felix Red The next match certainly sounded interesting from the onset and didn’t let down. Four of 2008’s top NFW singles wrestlers all found themselves in the ring for some tag-team action. Two former National Champions and the two Grand Prix runner-ups. Red and Quaranta were out first to a mixed reaction, while Knox and Impulse came out to a full house pop. Impulse and Quaranta started off the match electrifying the crowd with their dynamic agility. They both traded armdrags, hiptosses before hitting a stalemate dropkick to a crowd applause. Both kipped up and tagged their partners in and Steve Knox awesomely charged into a spinning leg lariat from Felix. Red tried to keep him down with nasty eye rake and stomps, but missed a Senton and then walked into a Roaring Elbow from the former National Champion. Knox hit a Suplex, rolled Felix back to a standing base for a Gourdbuster and nailed it. Unfortunately, he didn’t see Teresa tag Felix on the way down…and that opened Knox up to a Springboard Bulldog. Quaranta caught Knox on the way up with a thrust kick the stomach and then a Snap DDT to the mat. TQ went for an Irish Whip, but Knox put on the brakes and pulled her into a short-arm Sleeper. Quaranta tried to counter with a jawbreaker, but Knox pulled out and Impulse tagged in. Impulse caught TQ on the button with a springboard dropkick and went for a Springboard Moonsault, but Quaranta put up the knees. Teresa tagged out to Felix, who quickly rushed the ring and connected with a swinging neckbreaker to the doubled over Impulse. Felix then hit a tri-force of a leaping fistdrop, snap elbow drop and jumping legdrop. He went for the pin, but the Marathon Man was able to kick out. Felix followed that up with a Snap Suplex and Rolling Senton and only managed another two count. Felix next went for a DDT, but got flipped over by Impulse who tagged in Knox. As Felix rose up, he was greeted with an overhead release belly to belly suplex that sent him skidding across the mat. Felix was able to duck a Gold Rush Lariat, but his Superkick was ducked under and Knox hit a Full Nelson Facebuster. Knox went for the pin, but Quaranta broke it up. That brought in Impulse and we had a fullscale donnybrook going in the ring, which the ref had a serious time trying to keep under control. Good thing Cojones Mercado and his Insurgents stormed ringside! Ok, maybe that isn’t such a good thing… Especially for Impulse – THWACK! …or Felix – THWACK! For obvious reasons, they were the immediate targets of chairshots, which laid both of them out. As Steve Knox tried to sort out what was going on…Quaranta caught him with a low blow from behind and spinning heel kicked him out of the ring. TQ ascended to the top and nailed a Shooting Star Press on Impulse, while Cojones demanded the referee make a quick 3 count. The crowd booed, Quaranta looked uneasy at the surrounding situation while trash hit the ring. Mercado toothed a smile at Impulse and Red before escorting the Insurgents away from ringside. Winner: Teresa Quaranta/Felix Red Joe the Plumber vs. Cameron Cruise In the main event, Cameron Cruise’s dreams were answered. Ok, not really…but after months upon months of demanding a World Title shot, let’s just say that suspiciously he was finally granted the opportunity after dealing with more than a few run-in’s over the past few weeks. Eddie Mayfield said over a recorded video on the STARtron that tonight, Joe the Plumber and Cameron Cruise would be thanked for all their hard work. Cruise came out first, Plumber out second for his first ever World Heavyweight Championship title defense…sans the actual title belt that is apparently still in the new regime’s possession. As JTP played to the crowd, Cruise started off fast and aggressive – taking him off the middle ropes with a modified neckbreaker, which signaled the start of the match. Cruise landed a barrage of stomps, kicks and an elbow drop trying to keep JTP down in the early going. Cruise then hit a clothesline, which JTP shook off with a resounding yell before connecting with one of his own that snapped Cruise to the mat. Plumber quickly mounted Cruise, who looked like he was smelling the taint of a Yeti as Plumber started crushing him with left and right hands. Cruise kicked his legs up and wrapped Joe into a sunset flip position, which for the two count certainly wasn’t worth the whiff of Plumber’s backside. Both men got up, JTP ducked a Superkick from Cruise and took him down with a leaping bulldog to the mat. Cruise took a legdrop to the back of the head and then found himself in the clutches of JTP, a reverse chinlock wrenched in. The Greasy Goblin took a jawbreaker counter, Cruise hustled up the ropes and went for a Cross Body Press, but got countered with a powerslam. JTP went for another cover, but Cruise kicked out at two. Joe hooked in the Tiger Driver formation, but Cruise powered out with a backbody drop. JTP rushed up and Cruise kicked him in the gut and went for the Shipwreck Cradle Piledriver, but JTP countered by kicking him in the head twice. The impact knocked Cruise on his back, JTP on top and after a two count, Cruise bridged out . Cruise turned it over and landed a double arm suplex to break the champ’s momentum. The crowd at this point was fully behind the champion, which chagrined Cruise as he started choking the champ with his boot to a chorus of boos. Cruise hit a Stun Gun and followed that up with a nasty kneedrop across the champ’s throat before hooking in a Slingshot that sent JTP flying into the corner and teetering around…until a well-placed Superkick seemingly knocked him out. JTP somehow kicked out at two, Cruise got up in complete shock and started arguing with the ref. At this point, the crowd got loud which is never a good thing for the men in the ring. A man in an orange prison jumpsuit started running down the aisleway. Hold on, that’s no ordinary escaped convict…that’s… BROCK ALYAS! The hometown crowd exploded in delight as Alyas got to ringside and started slapping his hand on the mat and chanting "JOE! JOE! JOE!" Naturally, all of the Detroit fans started following suit and Cameron Cruise found himself in a dire situation. One of his biggest enemies on the outside of the ring, a World Championship possibly slipping away in his mind… Cruise lifted up Joe and hit a Triple Rolling Suplex combination, went for the pin…but JTP kicked out at two. Cruise ascended the top turnbuckles as JTP rolled around on the mat, trying to will himself up to his feet…Alyas hopped onto the apron and got in a shouting match with Cruise for a brief moment before the Crippler took to the air... TOP ROPE SPLASH! NO! JTP GETS UP THE KNEES! The precious couple of seconds Cruise spent distracted had possibly cost him… JTP got up wearily and reeled Cruise in… SHIT RIVER PLUNGE! UNO…DOS…TRESSSSSSSSS! Ok, so it completely cost Cruise…but that was just the beginning as Alyas rushed the ring and started celebrating with Joe. And by celebrating, I mean that he pretended for sixty seconds to raise the champ’s hand in the air…and then reel him into a Fireman’s Carry, followed by a running DVD! JTP twitched on the mat, while Cruise staggered up… NINTH MILE EXPLODAHHHHHHHHHHHHH! The Exploder Suplex left Cruise and JTP on the mat, down and out, side by side as Alyas exited the ring and raised his arms victoriously in the air. Detroit fans stood up in a mixed reaction, some chanting for Brock…others chanting for JTP. Nonetheless, Cameron Cruise had no fans, no friends…but all the reason in the world to exact his revenge on Alyas the next time they crossed paths.
