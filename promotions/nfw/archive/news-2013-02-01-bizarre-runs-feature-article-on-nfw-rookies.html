BIZARRE Runs Feature Article on NFW Rookies! Posted by Devin Millwood on 1 Feb 2013 BIZARRE UK writer and wrestling journalist William K. Spades penned the magazine's March issue cover story on a select group of NFW rookies training at our Brooklyn facility! Featuring: Leyenda De Ocho, Lucky Seven, Orange Dragon II, Lane Cash, Alex Austin, and Kid Chameleon! Note: This article was written prior to the events of Brawl: Berlin. Below him, The Beautiful Blondes are each hugging one of his boots, NAKED, and looking at the camera seductively from side-eyes using their elbows to hide them titties] Cloak and Dagger: The Secret Roots of NFW’s Rookie Class By William K. Spades Bizarre UK Contributing Writer Daylight cuts off as we shoot into the Holland tunnel. My ears pop, and are unpopped when I dry swallow. Penn Station, the final stop of a three-hour Amtrak express train from D.C. to New York. Up the escalator, through the corridor of fast food and magazines, and about a hundred or so steps between people traffic, rolling suitcases and over a puddle of homeless man piss that shocks my nostrils with it’s stench, I find the ‘C’ line which will take me downtown into Brooklyn. The spirit of Christmas is all around, in the lights and wreaths that decorate the path before the turnstiles, and the steel drums that serenade with holiday hymns. It’s a thin coating that disintegrates quickly when you’re standing in the subway with the locals (who at once recognize how fucking lost you are) – not tourists like me, but black faces, brown faces, yellow faces, and pale white skin that my imagination tells me originated somewhere in Eastern Europe. Forgive me, I don’t recognize this place; I’m from Orlando, from Los Angeles, from D.C., and thought I knew this city from television. Now I smell and taste that urban fume. It smells like bricks, garbage, blood, worn leather, and corroded change. I don’t like it, but it intrigues me. I travel deeper, past the beggars who lug their children from cart to cart with their hands out, and the bankers, lawyers, and government employees who speed home in thousand dollar suits. These are the people who came to the epicenter of a modern day Babylon to find treasure or hell, and might have settled for the in-between. Now I’m out of the heart, and into a main artery: Fort Greene, Brooklyn. Nearly overwhelmed by the culture and the madness creeping beneath its surface, I arrive at destination to meet a would-be ringleader of the concrete carnival, a barker in the flesh: RA Palazzo. *** Palazzo, 38, is a road agent for New Frontier Wrestling (NFW) who spends most of his time travelling between New York and Philadelphia, where the organization’s UWA developmental territory is based. His eye for talent was first put to use behind a camera 15 years ago as the video production manager for the once-popular tri-state independent wrestling promotion, the National Talented Wrestling Association (NTWA). Soon, his duties expanded to include talent agent and booker, and he would go on to discover some of the most recognizable names in wrestling history. That list of names includes Problem Child (better known today as Peter Windham), MW Grossard, August De La Rossi, Wink Manson, Jimmy Mylde, and New York indie legends Great White Void and Se7en. He’s also widely credited with resurrecting the careers of Stan Vick, Ares, and Jean Rabesque. When NTWA folded in 1999, he focused on a new project: managing the career of a 19 year-old prodigy named Castor Strife. Today, Strife is New Frontier Wrestling’s champion, the reigning Ultratitle winner, and arguably the most recognized name in wrestling. Another modern day NFW star, Bobby Jack Windham, came up through Palazzo’s Next Level Wrestling (NLW), an organization that set the New York indie scene on fire before coming to an untimely end in December 2010, when wrestler Zesty Mordant collapsed and died backstage. Two months later the company was sold to NFW, but kickstarted the careers of Windham, Vic Gravender, ‘Electric’ Eddie Patton, Magnus Destructo, and ‘Experience’ Alex Austin, all of whom found homes in successful organizations including NFW. "My job is to work with Avery Prosser [UWA Owner] to find and develop the next Troy Windham, Castor Strife, Mike Randalls, Shane Southern…that sort of thing. Guys who can sell a fuckin’ t-shirt." Palazzo says with a side grin. We walk a block through his scenic Brooklyn neighborhood of Boerum Hill, where the trees are bare both on the sidewalk and in the park across the way, before we find his 2010 3-series BMW. "In every city, county, town, there’s some kid with all the talent in the world, and an idiot who is mismanaging his career, or mishandling him," he says as we start the drive to a training center in Bushwick. "My job is to find the kid, remove the idiot, and get him to the right people. Easiest job in the industry if you’re one of three people. RA Palazzo is one - I can find stars in my sleep, just give me a budget and a contract to throw at him. The others are Avery and Sully [former UWA and NFW Owner Quentin Sullivan]. Everybody else stepped in rainbow colored dogshit. They got lucky." Indeed, UWA and Palazzo have a working relationship that stretches back to the late 90’s, when UWA was independently owned by Quentin Sullivan and was the biggest organization on the East Coast. The much smaller NTWA would send over talent like Se7en, Wink, and Problem Child, in return for struggling veterans who could spotlight and re-establish themselves, like JT Tyler and Ares. "UWA had the money to keep the stars they built, and our owner at the time didn’t. But Sully was always fair; he made sure my boss and I were taken care of. It was sort of the same thing when I sold NLW. Eddie Mayfield found a place for some people, and the rest went to Philly where they got to work with Avery," said Palazzo. We arrive at a dingy but durable training center called The Belly of the Beast (BOTB for short). BOTB features a full-size ring, long bags, a weight and machine area, and a grappling mat. Talent who come here get to train with seasoned pros like Impulse, Malik Anderson, and retired vets like Sean Toombs and Armando Montezuma. Palazzo has also brought in boxing, Judo, and Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu instructors to offer talent a more well-rounded program. "This is where our industry is headed. Guys are expected to be multi-dimensional, so we pay to give them a taste of everything. Gone are the days when you could be a bodybuilder with three moves, or a monkey-boy in a mask with 15 variations of a split-legged moonsault. The game is evolving, and it’s happening right here," he said. Palazzo remarks on the athletes in present company with the pride of a mentor, sounding less like an agent as he slips into promoter mode. He calls over the athletes and instructs two of them to give me a grappling demonstration. In the ring are a masked man (not your average monkey-boy, as Palazzo points out) with a white circled M on the front, and a rather intense looking chap with a Caesar cut and black form-fitted grappling apparel. As they square off, Palazzo turns to me. "OK so the guy in the mask is Leyenda De Ocho, and he’s matched with Alex Austin. Leyenda’s been a stud down in Mexico for a bit, super quick and smart for a young guy. Avery had him on the radar and the company signed him last month," he says as Leyenda backflips over Austin and runs him into the ropes. Austin, who fans have nicknamed ‘Kid A’, has a grappling pedigree that is nearly unmatched. "Alex came up with me in NLW, and I sent him to train with Castor Strife for a while. He was living out of his car, and sitting in his trunk were boxes of submission grappling trophies…I’m talking world-class competition wins. He wasn’t making shit for money, but we fixed that. We’ve got him training here three days a week so he becomes more ring-wise. But if he gets a hold of you, you’re done. Pro-wrestling hasn’t had a submission grappler of his caliber since…probably ever. When he really learns the ring, he is going to be fucking scary." "Easy, TAKE IT EASY! It’s a fucking five-minute exhibition, keep it at 60%. No strikes, no injuries," Palazzo yells at the eager-to-impress athletes. Kid Chameleon, a young guy in a leather jacket and sunglasses, with a late 80’s indie swag about him offers me a Monster energy drink before snapping it open for himself. He asks for Palazzo’s permission to pose for our photographer with a lit cigarette in his mouth. Palazzo reluctantly obliges. When the exhibition ends, I’m crowded by Leyenda, Austin, Kid Chameleon, Orange Dragon II, Teddy Alexander, and Lane Cash. We wait on one more, but after an hour we decide to go ahead with photos. That’s when an entrance is made, and the mood changes. "You’re fired, get the fuck out," Palazzo says to the tardy athlete, a female wrestler called Lucky Seven. Seven is moderately tall with an athlete’s build, feminine to a high degree but possessing long limbs and slender muscle bodies that allow her to dominate most of her gender. She’s no Amazon, but her deltoids are noticeably three-dimensional. Seven reminds me of a Barbie doll that was cut in half and glued to the other half of She-Ra’s tatted up twin. Her demeanor is nothing short of careless, reckless, remorseless, and Palazzo isn’t having any of it. He engages her again. "I’m serious, you’re no longer welcome in this gym." "You can’t fire me, idiot," she replies. "Yeah? Watch me make a phone call." "I told you I had somewhere to be tonight. Your memory sucks, apparently." "Hey, these people want to put you on the cover of their fucking magazine! You wanna make eighty bucks a weekend for the rest of your life? Be a fucking professional." "Shutup," she says, waving him off dismissively while she strips down on the spot to reveal her ring attire beneath. If this exchange reads like a brother-sister spat, that’s because it is. Seven, i.e. Laurie Palazzo, is R.A.’s much younger sibling. The 24 year old New Jersey native is the reigning women’s champion in Prosser’s UWA, a division that was largely stagnant and much ignored until she debuted in early 2011. After winning the inaugural SLICE-USA Grand Prix, she was awarded the belt that was shortly renamed the UWA Women’s World Championship. "All the talent in the world," is how Palazzo describes her. "Anybody who saw her match with Shinobu Aoki last year knows how good she is. The problem is, I set her up in FIRE ONE Pro in Jersey, she does well, then gets herself fired. Somehow Avery earns her respect and gets the most out of her in UWA, so then we send her into DEFIANCE, she makes enemies with everyone in the locker room, and Eric Dane fires her. Long story short, managing her career has been a pain in my ass." This account of Seven’s early career shares a familiarity with Castor Strife, and I mention this to Palazzo who quickly corrects me. "I hear you, but there’s a big difference. Castor always had the respect of his peers, even during the dark days when he was dealing with a severe substance abuse problem. It was a matter of getting him clean and focused. Laurie doesn’t do drugs or drink alcohol. This is her sober. And she isn’t a student of wrestling – she’s a world-class athlete, just like Alex Austin, who made the transition and things came easy to her. I want to see both her and Alex immerse themselves in the business like Castor did, learn the history, struggle, and scratch their way to the top." *** [Column inset: Top row features a headshot of LUCKY SEVEN, head leaned forward and eyes seductively staring ahead. Next to it is the following information] Name: Laurie Palazzo aka LUCKY SEVEN Nickname: The Black Rainbow Height/Weight: 5’8/135 Music: Death Valley ’69 – Sonic Youth Favorite Weapon: Watching her kick a long bag is a thing of beauty. Her flexibility and the raw power in her low, mid, and roundhouse leg strikes would make JCVD blush. Bet You Didn’t Know: Laurie was a decorated gymnast all through her youth, competing nationally in the balance beam and freestyle events before retiring at the ripe old age of 18. She enrolled at Princeton University, but dropped out after two semesters to tour as a performer with Cirque Du Soleil for a year and a half. That’s when she decided to pursue a career in pro-wrestling. Whew! Q&A So...what was the deal with Princeton? (Laughs) That’s the first thing you ask me? Very nice. OK, so I got in as a legacy student because of my mom. My grades were very good in high school – I did the home school thing between competitions after like, 9th grade. But I just wasn’t into the whole campus vibe, and the students there are such pampered little dipshits. Long story short, they suspended me for violating dorm policy, and I said ‘fuck it’ and left. At that point I had the tryout offer for Cirque, so why not take a shot? How is it working so closely with your brother? Which one? [I answer "RA"] Uh, I really don’t work with him that much. I mean I do, but I try to be around other people who don’t...fucking...yell at me constantly. (Laughs) He’s alright, but my preference is to be around Castor and Alex [Austin]. RA’s such a control freak, I’m so thankful he’s not actually in charge of anything. Where did you learn to kick so hard? Classes! I’ve been taking Muay Thai for a few years now, and I was just a natural. Gymnastics makes you a really well-rounded athlete, so that had something to do with it. I train mostly with guys, a few girls, and they all have big time experience. K-1, Glory, all that. Castor pays for it, which is good because I make fucking no money. Show me some love, Eddie Mayfield. [Column inset: Middle row features a headshot of LEYENDA DE OCHO in his mask, holding up and pointing to a gray Paper Boy NES cartridge at the bottom of the frame] Name: LEYENDA DE OCHO Nickname: The Cartridge Cruiser Height/Weight: 5’8/188 Music: Spontaneous Devotion – Random Favorite Weapon: If speed kills, then Ocho is a solitary death squad. As RA stressed to us, Ocho’s threat is not only his speed and risk-taking, but the fact that he combines this nicely with a technical prowess that is off the charts. Bet You Didn’t Know: Leyenda De Ocho grew up in Rogers Park, located in an area of Northern Chicago where good mean tread carefully. This video game and comic book obsessed self-described "nerd" trained with some legendary folks in Chicago, the city famous for producing NFW Hall of Famer Michael Manson among others, but south of the border is where he really cut his teeth. After going undefeated in AAA and impressing in the Ultratitle tournament stateside, NFW jumped on the blue-chipper and signed him to a contract. Here’s another fun fact: Ocho refuses to touch any game system that came out after 1999. Radical! Q&A What’s your favorite video game? That's tough, picking my favorite game would be like picking my favorite child. With most of them, it really just depends on my mood, but I have to say that the Zelda series has always resonated with me a great deal. I'm a sucker for that style of hero. You’re a young guy, just starting out in the business. What are your career goals? It's hard to think about years and years from now, but two goals stick out for the here and now: the Intergalactic Championship, and the NFW Triple Crown Championship. They are substantial honors on their own, yes, but even more than that, they are emblems of something greater. They represent the two great passions that drive my life, my focus, that resonate in my heart mind and body...I must win them. [Column inset: Bottom row features a headshot of LANE CASH striking a V pose beneath his chin like 007] Name: LANE CASH Nickname: King Cash Height/Weight: 6’1/225 Music: Life in the Fast Lane – The Eagles Favorite Weapon: We’d like to say it’s his pure athleticism, his ring smarts, or his wicked ‘Cash On Delivery’ superkick, but let’s be honest: the fact that he’s got two Beautiful Blondes with him ready to bare their assets at a moment’s notice to distract the enemy is as good a weapon as anyone’s gonna get. Bet You Didn’t Know: Lane is a third generation wrestler who enjoys threesomes, designer sunglasses, and going out on the town with...you guessed it, self-proclaimed NFW Commissioner Peter Windham! Rumor has it the two are planning to enter the tag ranks. The planets have truly aligned. Q&A There’s a rumor going around that The Beautiful Blondes are actually mother and daughter. True or False? (Wide grin) Oh, that’s true! What’s it like growing up as a third-generation wrestler? Do you feel pressure to live up to the Cash Wrestling Family’s legacy? Man, it was great. My pops and Uncle brought me to all their shows. I basically grew up backstage. As far as livin’ up to a legacy, I retired my old man. There’s no pressure, bro. I’m already better than any of them ever were. I’m putting the Cash brand back on the map. Taking it to new heights. Some people think there is an ulterior motive behind your alliance with Peter Windham. What’s the real scoop? Peter’s my boy. We’re chasin’ gold. So him possibly being Commissioner has nothing to do with it? Nah, nothin’ like that. BZ
