NFW Brawl 03: BRIT INVASION 5 Aug 2007 SECC Arena, Glasgow (seats 10,000) Introduction I might be known as ‘Big in Japan,’ but after all the drunken kilt flashing in Glasgow this weekend, I can tell everyone on the world wide inter-web that Sean Toombs is ‘Big in Scotland,’ as well. However, Sean Toombs knows his role and will only say that he’ll never look at Kooter Cruise the same again, or that giant one-eyed apple that made a whole front-row of dirty Scottish men faint. . . . so, without further adieu. . . Welcome dudes and dudettes to the third recap from the United Kingdom, as New Frontier Wrestling’s underground tour of the British Isles continued at the newly renovated SECC Arena, where nearly 10,000 Scots packed in for 140 minutes of wrestling moments that could only happen one day at a time in the freakflag nation of the New Frontier. Much like the aforementioned Kooter Cruise reciprocal flashing to his own freakflag to drunken Scots, this night featured some of the weirdest, bumpiest and bloodiest matches any wrestling fan could every dream of witnessing. Things started out strangely enough when Felix Red made his way to the ring wearing a Captain America hockey Jersey, a red, white and blue striped kilt, and a Scotland Yard whistle hanging around his neck. I suppose this was Felix’s way of dressing up as a referee in these confines, or maybe he got dressed up after 32 gravity bingers while watching Rocky III. Cameron Cruise vs. Joe the Plumber and the Sailor Man Something should be noted about these NFW shows – once again, the genius marketing and sales department have struck gold, or maybe I should say Black Label Gold. Ever drink Carling’s Black Label at the price of 2 pounds for 2 pints? No? I don’t think these Scots ever had the chance either, but if Angelina Joliee were offering her p*ssy for one dollar a lick on a Times Square street corner, I don’t think you’d see that be bought up faster. Needless to say, people were drunker than a skunk and rowdier than Roddy Piper on 3 snorted eight-balls of cocaine and a pint of ephedrine. Of course, 2 pounds for 2 pints leads to drunk Scottish men having no problem in some full disclosure that wasn't necessary. 'We Are Family' by Sister Sledge blazed out of the speakers as the Original Cameron Cruise Project made their way to the ring, possibly to answer the flush tolling in Joe the Plumber and Sailor Man's eyes after the last house show in Liverpool. Cameron didn't look exactly pleased to be teaming with 7 foot behemoth, and I'd say the moment of the night was watching his reaction to a man flashing Kooter from the front row, then Kooter returning the favor. Three men vomited, one claimed he was blind and another 16 fainted at what I can only describe as a 30 pound flesh mallet. Since I can't repress the memory, I'll just let that one settle in for a moment. . . . And now onto the match, out next was Joe and The Sailor Man...and well, they brawled up the aisle...with each other...a flaming plunger and oar being wielded. Just another normal start to an NFW event, eh? So, officials had to separate those two (already bloodied), where they finally got in the ring to settle the score against the Cruises. Well, apparently, Kooter and Cameron Cruise are a more stable, sound tag team than their opponents and I believe Lucifer is shivering his frozen teeth right now. Cameron started off against Sailor Man, who was still angered about Cameron's TV Title snub (even though he's not a title holder). An immediate brawl broke out, while Joe screamed for his partner to tag him in. Sailor Man turned around to wail his own partner, but Cruise snuck in a rollup for a near pinfall, before locking in several other quick pins (small package, Oklahoma Roll) that didn't get the job done. Joe was able to tag in Sailor Man, leapt into the ring and started kicking Cameron like a rented mule, but Sailor Man stopped him unaware a tag had been made. Mainly because Felix wasn't even paying attention to the match, but trying to chat up one of the ringcard girls outside the ring. Cameron made the hot tag to Kooter as Joe and Sailor Man started giving each other headbutts. Not effecting either man, they attempted a double clothesline on Kooter, which had no effect. Kooter grabbed both men by the throats and delivered a double chokeslam that got the crowd off their feet. Both freaks on the mat, doing the electric eel while Kooter splashed them both simultaneously. Double kickout on the pin, Felix lazily counting. Kooter gets upset, Felix slaps him. Cameron quickly tags Kooter, having to bear hug him from behind to try and stop him from going after Felix. Cameron says he's only going to count to five once, or Kooter doesn't get any Mountain Dew at dinner... and that stops the purple masked gargantuan. Felix slaps him again as Cameron scales the ropes, Kooter starts whining, but doesn't retaliate. Split legged dropkick off the top by Cruise on the opponents, goes for the pin...Felix doesn't count. Cameron shoves Felix, who mock falls through the ropes and pretends to be unconscious outside the ring. CRACK! Plunger across the back from Joe, Sailor grabs and hits the front backbreaker, leaving Cruise on his knee...Joe starts pulling down his trunks, about to give Cameron the stink nugget shake, but Kooter runs in with a clothesline from behind. Sailor Man catches Kooter with a low blow as Joe falls out of the ring, trying to pry his pants over his fat ass. Scots throw beer all over him...Cameron looks mortified, but says Kooter's getting extra Mountain Dew. Felix is reading "The Watchmen" with a flashlight, but stops to see Kooter lifting Sailor Man into the air with a double-clutch neckhang into a Chokebomb. Kooter covers, Felix snaps his fingers 3 times and rings the bell. Not bad, not great. . . NFW might be on the verge of a cranial eclipse with the tag division it's building. Honey I Fucked Your Mom vs. The Suicide Kings Speaking of the said tag division. In a complete extreme trash match that started from the first strum of Daft Punk, the Suicide Kings tried to exact revenge on Yori, Mittens, Don King and Chuck Woolery. Forget about what happened in the match, let's list the amount of weapons used in the five minutes it took for the match to complete itself. Handcuffs, leather whips, electric noded clitoral stimuli shockers, trash cans, washboards, irons, drumsticks, martini glasses, singapore canes, lawn rake, barbed wired tennis balls, chain-linked leather chaps, and in the grand file: A double-shot of 13" Plymouth Cock YYJ Special Edition and El Kabong; bloodied, but victorious...Yori and Mittens collapse on Stratton for the pin. Rocko Daymon vs. David Tui vs. Jacob McKail Three NFW newbs stepped through the ropes for the next match, which was under triple threat extreme rules, no DQ's and no countouts -- something I heard was instituded when Daymon and McKail showed up late...which wasn't the best sign of the night. I'll be honest with you folks, this one was pretty f'n sloppy for the first ten minutes of the match. I don't want to speak to anyone's professionalism here because only the best join NFW, but McKail looked like he woke up two hours after travelling back in time for a bender with Ozzy Ozbourne in 1979. Rocko Daymon was significantly jetlagged since his first match with Cameron Cruise last week...I can't see how anyone can keep up a schedule like this guy and expect to put in 100% every match. So as the Daymons and McKail as a whole seemed to be missing a beat, David Tui couldn't take advantage. Why? Well, he might be learning about booking three matches in the span of six days. Some spots were missed, the pace was erratic at times and the Scots seriously let these guys have it for the first ten minutes...and it wasn't until the action spilled out of the ring, with beer getting thrown on them that things started to pick up a little. I didn't know if it was the best solutions to their proverbial hangover, but the three started delivering some stiff chairshots and then all hell broke loose. At one point, Caitlyn jumped onto Rocko's back, trying to get him back to the ring. Tui hits a spread-eagled modified bronco-buster on them, sprawling them everywhere and turning around just in time for McKail to launch off the apron with a picturesque hurricanrana. It didn't matter, just as the action got hot, the bell sounded. The fans started booing again, just after getting behind these guys. Their fifteen minutes were up, all three leaving with a draw for their troubles. Hunchblack vs. Rook Black Next up was a match that more than a few of these locals were actually excited to see from a wrestling standpoint as the upcoming cult hero, Hunchblack, took on worldwide superstar Rook Black... or the man that we all thought we knew as Rook Black because anything and everything we've come to expect from him as a wrestler seemed to be slightly different. I wouldn't say the man was emotionless the whole match 'cause the way it ended definitely doesn't speak to that statement, but I will say he was cold to the crowd, and highly concerned with the efficiency of his wrestling. The match started off with Hunchblack being the aggressor, Black basically gauging what his opponent had to offer. For a rotund fellow, Hunchblack's balance and rhythm in the ring is striking. Off the lock-up, he quickly caught Rook with a hiptoss and a clothesline as Rook rolled to his feet. A quick legdrop and some elbow strikes on the mat kept the Texan dazed as the Harlem native attempted the impossible, trying to get 10,000 drunk Scots to clap rhythmically in unison as he did the Robot dance, before tomahawk chopping Rook across the scalp. Rook fell into the corner and got a running hump sandwich (back avalanche) in the corner, some of the nearby crowd groaning as Hunchblack climbed the turnbuckles and literally rubbed his face in it. Not such a great strategy from the youngster, as the tactician master quickly dropped him out of the corner with an atomic drop, followed by a running Texas bulldog. Quickly, Rook tried to grind away the rookie with a side headlock that he managed to hold through a couple of back suplexes. Rook swerved around into a dragon sleeper that was broken up with a modified Samoan Drop from Hunchblack after exerting energy to stand. Hunchblack wildly swings, finds himself in a Cobra Clutch Sleeper, the crowd now booing as Rook Black's ring persona. . . well, its not very exciting to watch a man fight a rest hold for two minutes, only to get put in a similar one for the next 3. Hunchblack eventually fights out and rocks the house with a tornado punch, crushing elbow drop and a little TASTE THE HUMP~! action (Running Senton, I'd so barf). Hunchblack locks in the full nelson, perhaps setting up the finisher on his rag sheet, but Rook slams his head backwards bloodying his nose. Staggering around, Hunchblack falls prey to a Gauntlet Throwdown, but curiously...Rook doesn't break the chokehold. The ref almost counts to three, but sees Rook locking in the choke and then starts counting him out... And Rook doesn't break the hold, the bell rings...Rook looks around quizically and looks on in shock as a nearly unconscious Hunchblack gets his hand raised with a disqualification victory. What's even crazier is that Rook calmly engages the referee in a discussion regarding the decision, looking amused and surprised at WHY he lost. Is everyone completely bonked out on drugs? My god. Sean Stevens/The Southern Baptist Coalition vs. Kin Hiroshi/The New VD This one should've been called: How to get 10,000 Scottish freaks pissed off at you by Kin Hiroshi. The focus should've been on the debut of two more tag teams in NFW, but Hiroshi's theatrics overshadowed everything in the match. 'XXX' Sean Stevens teamed with the Southern Baptist Coalition (Abraham & Jacobs), who were managed by Jimmy Davis. Amusingly, Davis chided Stevens for the whole match for working in the pornographic industry and promised to bathe him in holy water after the match, if he'd be obliged. Abraham and Jacobs were two southern burlies in orange overalls that used alot of ground strikes, but Abraham defnitely wowed the crowd with an early Moonsault bodypress...very impressive for a 270 pounder. Hiroshi and Stevens are no strangers to each other, predating their NFW careers, they both competed in the EWI/GXW leagues run by Erik Zieba. Meanwhile, 'The New VD,' (what's with all these Originals and New's running around?) comprised of Johnny Styles and Johnny Rage (No Johnny 5 sightings) are an up-and-coming tag team that alot of fans get behind on the indy circuits. Of course with Hiroshi's general ornery nature about getting in the ring, we had a dichotomous pairing of a face teamed with two heels, and a new heel teamed with two faces. Hiroshi refused to start against Stevens, so Stevens bowed out in respect to letting the tag-teams having a go. The match see-sawed back and forth, Hiroshi avoiding all tags and getting called more than a few names by Stevens. Then, Hiroshi would threaten to tag in, Stevens would tag in...Hiroshi's bluff called, Stevens tags out. The fans started booing as Kin's sudden spectacle of himself would break up the flow of the match. Eventually, the heelish tactics of the Coalition started paying off as Abraham pulled the ropes down on Stylez as he tried to bounce off them, instead toppling over and cracking across the SECC Arena pavement. The Baptists furiously worked him over with a blitz of individual moves, but you didn't see too many combo manuevers out of the duo. Stylez was able to avoid a Suicide Senton, making the hot tag to... Hiroshi. Who after a good three seconds of shock and appallment, hopped off the apron and walked up the entrance ramp towards backstage. I don't think I've ever seen a Scottish crowed angrier since they introduce the leg razor and toothpaste to the women around here. Stylez and Rage literally tried to drag Hiroshi back to the ring, but he was a goner. Sean Stevens grabbed the house mic and even insulted his manhood, but Hiroshi was shown on the scoreboard getting into a limo and riding off. And thus the show ended, the crowd more than a little rowdy at the result, but something tells me that the unannounced lineup might get a shake-up in order to please the crowd after one lackluster draw, one reversed decision and one World Champion walking out in fear of getting shown up in the ring. If it weren't for the tag division, this night might've looked alot worse. Not a bad show, but NFW needs to put out something great to make up for the end of tonight.
