NFW Midnight Madness 1 18 Jun 2005 Oakland, California Introduction The Story Begins... "There is many a young man here today who looks on war as all glory, but boys, it is all Hell." --William Sherman Angels Fell From The Sky Like Bombs many moons ago... Looking for a savior? Try God. It seems He works wonders for a lot of people. He gives them all a chance to start over. Remember all those drugs you took? Yeah, your nasal passages aren't thanking you anymore. How about the vein in your right arm? You the one, it's right where your elbow bends in. Seems like that purple and red bruise has been a fixture there for longer than you can remember. Oh, and your frontal lobe? Left is right, right is left. Irony is funny, hypocrisy is always okay and what's wrong is right? How'd that brick wall feel when you finally hit it? They say that God is willing to look past all of your sins (all seven of them if you've made it that far), and grant you forgiveness. Forgiveness! You could be knocking on Lucifier's door and one day have that epiphany and find yourself sitting in a confessional on the other side of Father Rapingminor and decide that Catholocism is the way to go. The good Father will listen to your sins and allow you to repent them. He'll allow you to come under God's ever watchful eye and you'll be pure again. You could even be a virgin again, regardless of how many people you've fucked in the past. Now you're one of God's children. * * * "Born again?" "Yes," the Father said. "It seems like you've been through a lot over the years. He could help you find the path." "The path to what?" I replied. "Divinity? Becoming whole? Nirvana?" The Father cleared his throat from the other side of confessional. "What ever that path is; He can help you find it." "If God has always been there, where was he when I was a child?" I proposed. "Where was he when my father was beating me? When he killed my mother? When he killed myself? When I tried to kill myself? If your God is so great, why the hell didn't he preform any miracles? Why couldn't I have a normal life?" "He was there watching," The Father confirmed strongly. "He was watching over you. Some of His children have to go through the hardships before they can finally become happy and free." I nearly laughed, but held my composure. "Happy and free!? I've been suffering through the same shit for twenty-nine years. Not once has your God came to me in a vision; not once has He offered me salvation from my hardships. How do you explain that?" "Well, you're here now, aren't you?" "I'm here of my own volition," I snapped back. "What's your name, my son?" The Father asked. "Donaven," I said. "Donaven Winters." "Well Donaven, maybe it is God's own Will that you are here." "Doubtful." This is when I wanted to stand up and leave the confessional. Though many thoughts ran through my mind. Maybe the father was right. Maybe God was sending me a sign to come to this church, this confessional. On the other hand, maybe I was tired of searching. I needed a place to belong. It had been nearly a year since I'd seen a single friendly face. Ben closed the diner shortly after I quit my job. Craig did everything in his power to make sure I couldn't find a job anywhere else. I took what I had saved up and moved far away. The last thing I did before leaving for good was apologizing to Eli for everything. Everything I did to Angel and to his daughter. My mind had sobered up from everything I had been put through. However, others weren't so trusting in me. I could blame a lot of it on Craig. He spread the lies, the inaccuracies. "I'm sorry Father, but this isn't for me," I stood up from the hard bench. I could tell he was resigned to defeat from the other side. "I understand. Take care, my son, and remember God will be with you." I nodded my head and stepped out of the confessional. Standing in the back of the church I look toward the front. The altar standing in front of row after row of pews. The stained glass windows were beautiful. The giant burgundy curtains that hung at the front of the church, were gorgeous. Everything in the church, right down to the marble statues of Jesus and Mary were perfect. So innocent. However, I knew the fallacies of the Catholic Church (and probably every other church out there), these statues, drapes, the tapestry, stained glass windows were all an ersatz cover up to what the church was really about: money. That's all anyone cares about these days. The Catholic Church will hide behind money, shelling it out to thirty-something year old men who claimed that their Fathers touched them inappropriately while they attended Sunday School. Tell me Johnny, did the Father's erect penis bring you closer to God? Or was it the millions of dollars that you took from the church in court? Of course it was the money. I'd consider performing fellatio on a priest if I thought it'd make me a millionaire. Money heals all wounds and all scars. Though staring ahead at the altar and all the lies it stood for, there was one thing neither God nor God Money could heal. Broken trust. Craig Miles broke my trust. He took advantage of me, knowing that my mental state could easily be manipulated into believing anything he wanted. It worked beautifully until he decided that I wasn't worth it anymore. Beating me into submission seemed to turn a light on inside my head. Unconscious, one would think that was the final light. Or perhaps God calling out to them. In a sense, this was an epiphany. I had been manipulated for far too long. Craig got what he wanted out of me and it no doubt made him a richer man. Good for him, I'm glad. Retaliation had never been a thought, though. There's no point in retaliation. Only causes more pain in the end. I was Craig's blind follower and he was the one who played God and pulled the wool over my malleable mind. * * * What are the odds that our Hero would be able to find God in Las Vegas? The one place where there were more opportunities to sin than any other city on the face of the planet. Gambling, prostitution and drugs were spearheads of the Las Vegas community and here he was looking for a savior. The irony is almost laughable. However, it'd be easier to laugh if he was hopelessly searching for the savior who would stumble onto him at the airport. * * * The cancellation notice finally appeared on the screen I was staring at for what seemed like hours. The flight to Detroit had been cancelled due to inclement weather in the Motor City. Snow coming off of Lake Superior resulting in two-to-three feet. No way any plane was going to land there within the next day or two. If it weren't for my iPod I'd go insane with everyone rushing around for their flights. Yelling at their children, yelling at their loved ones and yelling at the people working for the airline. Controlled chaos at it's best. Just when the music was starting to get good and I was tuning everything out, I was interrupted. "Excuse me," a man's voice said from behind me. "You're Donaven Winters, right?" I nodded my head. "Yeah, how do you know me?" "I've seen you before. I've watched you for a number of years. And I think I can help you." I turned around and looked at the man. "Help me with what?" "Miles," he replied coldly. It had been a long time since I had heard anyone aside from my mind speak his name. "Craig Miles?" "Yes," he said. "Look, we should go somewhere a little less hectic to talk." I stood up, but then my guard came back. "Wait, before we go anywhere, who are you?" The man extended his hand as a sign of friendship. "The name's Mike Randalls. Nice to meet you." Little did I know, that this man was going to be my savior. Can You Hear It Knocking? FADEIN: OAKLAND ARENA, 3:45 PM – day of the show.
