A New Zealand-run promotion of the rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy newsgroup, opening in 2000, closing in May 2002 after the pay-per-views Glory and Caged Rage 2, and reopening in 2004 with a fortnightly show called Impact. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

River City Wrestling was run out of New Zealand for a newsgroup on the other side of the world, and it began, by its own writers' account, with a roster nobody would have written home about. Its owner took handlers no other promotion wanted, gave them pushes they had not earned yet, and let them find out what they could do. Several of them stayed with it until the day it shut.

It rebuilt Alex Extreme and Vanguard, and grew Raya, Luke Kinsey, Juan Vasquez, Vlad, the Leading Men and the American Idols from nothing. Team Canada came in; Chris Courtade, Devon Case and Rosco Riggins arrived later, when it could attract them. Chris Hopper and Gorilla ran their beatdowns, the El Thugs worked the undercard, and a War Games and a tribute match for a fallen Japanese wrestler sat among the matches its handlers remembered longest.

Its cards went out as text files on its own website rather than to the newsgroup, three hours at a time. Glory, billed as its first pay-per-view, was released on 12 January 2002 and drew a week of argument about match length, because two of its bouts ran to nearly ten thousand words each and readers had to decide whether to read them or skim. A Japanese brand debuted in February. Caged Rage 2 followed on 24 March.

It closed on 6 May 2002, and the thread that followed reads like a wake. Its vice-president wrote that he was numb for twenty minutes. A former champion wrote that its owner had written his character better than he did. A rival promoter who had fallen out with its owner wrote that he would not pretend it had not mattered. Two months after it was gone, the newsgroup's mid-year poll still placed it third among every federation running.

It came back at the beginning of 2004 with a fortnightly show called Impact and a third Caged Rage, and by that December it was recruiting again, billing its ring as one that had hosted legends. What survives here is the 2002 run: two pay-per-views known of but not held, a roster named only in the words of the people who wrestled for it, and its own goodbye.
