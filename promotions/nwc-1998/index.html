Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The National Wrestling Council modelled itself on the old National Wrestling Alliance's territory system: a coalition of independent regional federations, each running its own shows, all cooperating under one shared World Championship and a shared World Tag Team Championship. Its earliest surviving document, a role-playing recruitment tour dated 9 December 1997, walks a prospective wrestler through its member offices in turn — the Big Apple Wrestling Federation, Motor City Wrestling, Lone Star Wrestling, the Empire State Wrestling Association, and Canadian Championship Wrestling — each promoter pitching a different appeal, from Motor City's nostalgia for “the golden age of wrestling” to Empire State's insistence that its wrestlers “wrestle grueling, technical matches.” The Council's own executive director closes the tour by telling the recruit that which region he joins is “a formality” — the point was joining the Council itself.

By February 1998 the coalition had grown to five member regions and reworked one of them outright: a new Virginia region absorbed nearly the entire talent pool of an earlier Canadian member. That month's own newsletter previewed a cross-promotional NWC World Title match pitting a Motor City fan favorite against the reigning champion from the new Virginia territory, and looked ahead to the Council's own first full interpromotional supercard, Across Enemy Lines. The Council's own hundred-wrestler retrospective, published a year later, credits that card's own World Title challenge — a Lone Star wrestler against the reigning champion — as one of the most famous title matches in its history.

Motor City Wrestling, the Council's own founding member, closed after a run of fourteen “Coboslam” supercards, its commissioner announcing the final one in the weeks before it aired in March 1999; its wrestlers moved on into the Council's other member regions. The Council itself kept growing past that loss: by June 1999 it counted six member federations and launched its own news service, staffed by correspondents from every region, with a standing forum hosted by its own kayfabe head of council. That June 1999 announcement is the last document in its record.
