An Atlanta-based 1998 regional promotion recreating a late-1970s/mid-1980s southern style as the National Wrestling Council's southernmost region, one of at least four unrelated 1998 promotions sharing the bare acronym GCW. Its own August 1999 broadcast, the promotion's only served show, structures the GCW Glory Championship changing hands. Five wrestlers are recorded. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

This Georgia Championship Wrestling opened on 5 August 1998, its own president introducing it as a “regional” promotion out of Atlanta “attempting to re-create the atmosphere of the late 70's through the mid-80's in pro wrestling” as a booked fed built on roleplaying and angle development. It went on to run as the southernmost region of the National Wrestling Council.

Its only surviving broadcast, Rise to Glory, aired a year later on 2 August 1999 out of Statesboro. The Axeman won the Southern Heritage Championship on the card, and Steve Gaines took the GCW Glory Championship from Hussar in a finish the promotion's own commentary left openly disputed — no replay could confirm whether Gaines had kicked out before referee Shawn Kowalski counted three — amid a stable called the Real Uppermost Echelon setting fire to a French flag at ringside.

No closure notice of its own survives; that same broadcast shows the promotion still running an active weekly slate and an ongoing tournament.

Arizona State Wrestling (ASW, Arizona, 1999)

Predecessor to Georgia Championship Wrestling (1998)
This promotion's own founder, in its own 23 August 1999 founding notice, self-identifies as the founder of "Georgia Championship Wrestling [ASW]" -- the exact full name of the held wing gcw-georgia-1998 (the National Wrestling Council's own southernmost region, Aug-Oct 1998) -- describing this new promotion as a return seeking to recapture that earlier federation's own success. The same person stands self-disclosed by name on both promotions' own pages, corroborating the self-statement with a second, operator-identity axis.
