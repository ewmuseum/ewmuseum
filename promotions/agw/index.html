A long-running freeservers.com e-fed whose surviving title history runs, reign by reign, from a simulator-scored October 1997 founding to a January 2000 hiatus, with roster and academy material carrying its record into the late 2000s. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

AGW's own title-history ledger opens all seven of its founding championships on 7 October 1997 and carries them, reign by reign, to a January 2000 hiatus — the ledger's own last word, not a claim the promotion closed there. It names eleven titles across that span: the World, World Tag Team, Intercontinental, TV, Cruiserweight, Super Heavyweight, and 6 Man Tag Titles from the founding roster, the Anything Goes Title from February 1998, the U.S. Tag Team Titles discontinued and replaced by the Anything Goes Tag Team Titles in March 1999, and a Women's Title whose earliest reign the fed's own ledger never dated at all. One title change on that ledger — Atlas winning the TV Title — lands on a night this wing also holds the results card for, 15 September 1998, the earliest surviving AGW show. A further two belts, a Woman's World Title and a Woman's Tag Team Title, surface only in a much later roster bio card, with no reign document of their own surviving; whether either is the earlier Women's Title under a new name isn't something the record says either way.

The house style shifted with the era. AGW's earliest surviving card, Anything Goes Tuesday (15 September 1998), is a simulator-scored results sheet — exact match times, star ratings, nothing more. An announced Winter Storm card from February 2005 survives with no results at all, just a promised lineup. By the mid-2000s the fed's roster and academy material describes full angled pay-per-views (Ultimate Survivor, Winter Storm, Big Brawl) with a running commentary team — none of those broadcasts themselves survive in this holding. The fed ran a Wrestling Academy out of a Washington D.C. suburb from the summer of 1999, founded and run by Speed Racer with Overkill and Rhapsody training its students — the same school still running, per its own account, "while the AGW has come, gone and returned again." That hiatus shows up on the title ledger itself: the World Title sat vacant for two months in late 1997 after Kid Nitro died in a Bridge match wrestled for a different fed entirely, and the promotion's own later record picks back up with Nelson's debut at Ultimate Survivor 2002.

Its roster cards read like the era's own catalog of borrowed and original names side by side. A handful carry more than a shared name: the fed's own held documents stage a "Rocky Maivia," then a "The Rock," as an actual AGW titleholder in 1998 and 1999, and Hope's own bio card independently narrates the same real wrestler's career arc and her own move to AGW to follow him — the one portrayal this wing's held corpus actually corroborates twice over. Everything else billed under a famous name — a "Shawn Michaels," a "Bill Goldberg," a "Triple H" among them — carries nothing beyond the name itself and reads as the era's own habit of borrowing marquee names for a booking sheet, not a claim about who was really in the ring. The originals carry their own record just as fully: Nelson, a Triple Crown Tag Champion who debuted by eliminating three opponents single-handedly, and Jessica Dawn, billed on her own bio card as a multi-time champion years after the surviving title ledger stops. How the fed's run actually ended isn't in the surviving record — its last dated content is a 2011 roster bio card, well past its last known show cards, and the corpus this wing draws from holds hundreds more show cards this pass did not serve.
