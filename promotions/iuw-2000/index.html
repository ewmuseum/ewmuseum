Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

IUW introduced itself with a Frequently Asked Questions document on 18 October 2000, pitching a small, deliberately capped promotion — never more than twenty-four wrestlers — aimed at players “that have lives outside of the computer,” judged on a handler's best two roleplays a week rather than length or volume.

Its debut card, International Incident, aired five days later on a Monday, opening with its on-screen president and commissioner, Gabriel Storm, introducing the promotion to a full arena and handing off to his broadcast team, play-by-play man Elliott Davis and color commentator Jordan Turner, whose running disagreement over whether wrestling needed “sportsmanship” or violence carried the night.

The card itself put over “The Goldenboy” Christopher Scarlet, a technical wrestler who dominated Roy Anderson with a finisher he called the Golden Gunn, and The Judge, whose Judgement Day finisher put away Alex Collins after a Boston Crab into a Spinebuster. A backstage brawl between two other wrestlers left Hardcoremaster's scheduled opponent unable to compete, handing him a win by forfeit; “The Val” Orange Valkyrie needed only his Val-Driver to finish Gavin Foster; and the night closed with Triple X defeating Jim Gibson with a finisher the card's own announcers argued over the name of.

The FAQ named three championships waiting to be contested — an International Heavyweight title, a Junior Heavyweight title, and International Tag Team titles — with an eight-man tournament to crown the first Heavyweight champion promised for the following week's show.

No further document survives past that debut broadcast. Whether the promised tournament, or any of IUW's three championships, was ever decided is not recorded.
