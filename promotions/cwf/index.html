A 1998 federation that died twice and came back a third time in 2003, opening its last season as a re-union show with the fed most of its roster had come from. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Corporate Wrestling Federation started in 1998 with three people, and its own History page says the roster grew as e-feds got popular. By the end of 1999 it had been voted efed of the year and ranked fifty-fourth on a list of the best of all time, and it had taken in the roster of a fed called the EPWA when that one folded. Then the money ran out and it closed.

It came back in late 2000, when its own people met up in a parking lot and talked. The XWF had just opened, and by the fed’s own account all of that fed’s guys found time to compete in the CWF too; the storylines grew and it was back at the top. In 2001 it lost money again, two of the men running it stopped getting along, and that was the end of the second run.

The third began in January 2003, and it is the only one the record actually holds. The re-union show it opened with put both feds’ old announce team behind the desk to call it, in front of a sold-out Allstate Arena. SeVeN followed on the first of February at the Fleet Center; Redz()ne-2 was carded for the Kemper Arena, and the event calendar ran on through Madison Square Garden with every date marked sold out.

How it ended isn’t in the surviving record. On 7 April 2003 its own front page announced that the owner was about to wrestle his last e-fed match, that management applications were open, and that anyone who could not write two thousand words had no business there. Eight days later the last thing the federation ever posted said the applications were coming in and to act soon. Nothing follows it.

Storylines

Two shows and one uncollected card carry the whole of the wing's third run. The arcs below are what the full crawl surfaced from the shows themselves, not a checklist pursued on its own.

The Kid Gangster swerve Written by AI, 2026

History's own page names Kid Gangster the original CWF's own co-owner, gone since a falling-out with owner Hellfire X — and pointedly not invited to the January 2003 reunion over their still-"on going" feud. The show plays on it twice: a costumed impostor crashes the Commissioner reveal and is chokeslammed for the trouble, then the real Kid Gangster returns for real a week later at SeVeN, unmasking after the CWF World Championship match to attack new champion "The Natural" Mike Diamond and reveal a signed contract giving him a title match of his own design against Hellfire X. The Redz()ne-2 card bills him instead tagging with Hardcore Z against Hellfire X and Diamond — whether the promised singles match was ever wrestled is not attested anywhere in the surviving record.

Two champions crowned at SeVeN, neither confirmed twice Written by AI, 2026

The restarted fed's first two titles were both decided on the same card. Mr. X won the CWF Anarchy Championship in a three-way against Starter and Hardcore Z, Starter written out mid-match by an in-story lightning strike; Diamond outlasted Paco for the CWF World Championship with a compromised special referee counting the fall. The Redz()ne-2 card bills Mr. X defending the Anarchy belt against Sean Manning in a Dumpster Match, and carries two quarter-finals of an otherwise unattested Cold Championship tournament (Chain against Starter, Paco against Ice) — but that show's own results were never captured, so all three threads end the same way: mid-defense, in the surviving record.

Affiliations

Primetime Central (PTC)

Listed on Primetime Central’s own April 2003 affiliate wall, among eleven member feds sharing that same address.

EPWA

Acquired by CWF (1998–2003) (c. 1999)
CWF recruited its roster out of the EPWA when that fed closed down — CWF’s own History page: “Then one day, a fed called the EPWA closed down. Still with the CWF, Starter was the comissioner of that fed. He recruited all the guys from it and most of them became CWF Legends.”

XWF

Crossover booking with CWF (1998–2003) (c. 2000–2003)
CWF’s own History page names eight wrestlers who crossed over from this XWF around 2000–2001: X, Lil’ X, Ice, Andrew Wright, Mick, Antoine, Lord of Darkness, and Beast. None of the eight appears on the roster of the other, better-documented XWF held separately, so the two are recorded as separate promotions that happen to share initials.
