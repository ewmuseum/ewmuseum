Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Empire State Wrestling Association opened around 14 November 1997, billing itself “1/2 Simulator 1/2 Booking, 100% Fun” and fronted entirely by an in-fiction commissioner persona, Capone. A December recruitment promo has Capone lay out his philosophy at length from behind a bar in his own fictional office, contrasting a childhood memory of Madison Square Garden with what he calls modern “sports entertainment”: “That's why I created the ESWA. Not for the money, not for the fame, but for the wrestling... If you leave this table without a contract signed, I could care less. The ESWA is gonna go on whether you're a part of it, or not.”

Its first flash pack, published the same week the promotion opened, introduced the roster that would headline its debut card: Eddie Extreme, flown in from Charlotte for the occasion; the masked Doomsday, promising to finish every opponent with the Doomsday Device on his way to the vacant Empire State Heavyweight Championship; The Dean, a gym-dwelling brawler threatening rivals with “the Failing Grade”; the tag team the European Union, Pierre and Demitri, declaring themselves “the greatest cousins in the history of the sport”; and El Fuego, a masked Lucha Libre import billed as a former MCF World and Lightweight Champion.

That roster headlined ESWA 100% Pure!, the promotion's first card, at Edgemont High School's gymnasium. Its opening match ended when Sam Hansen reversed a late pin attempt to beat the Dial 0 Kid, then tied a bull rope around the loser's neck and dropped him to the concrete outside the ring — a finish the broadcast's own commentary flagged as something “Commissioner Capone can not be happy about.”

ESWA billed itself as the first running territory of the National Wrestling Council, a regional alliance modeled, in the council's own words, on the old National Wrestling Alliance's touring territory system. A promotional tour of the council's headquarters, published that December, sent a prospective recruit through its several member offices before ending, as its own centerpiece, back in Capone's bar for the same recruitment speech word for word.

No title match, and no further card past the first part of ESWA 100% Pure!, survives in the record; the Empire State Heavyweight Championship stands vacant where the surviving documents leave it. How the promotion's own story continued, or how it ended, isn't in the surviving record.
