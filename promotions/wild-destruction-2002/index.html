Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Wrestling's International League of Destruction opened in the last week of September 2002, when a handler who called himself Jake announced a return to fedheading after a short break. Ten days later its first newsletter went out: eighteen wrestlers split into three divisions, a five-card schedule running into November, and a promise that every one of them would matter.

Its second card, Fusion 1.2, crowned all three of its championships in a single night at the New Jersey Arena. Eric Scott outlasted a three-way to become the first Rising Star Champion; Neige Thirteen pinned a valet's own prized challenger to take the Wildside title; and Spikyjim, of the villainous tag team the Suicide Kings, turned on nobody and pinned Eddie Drysdale clean to become the first WILD World Champion, closing the show with his tag partner Travis Lagrange beating down the loser until the newly crowned Rising Star Champion ran in to make the save.

The promotion built its fiction around two rival stables, the Suicide Kings and the Empire Club, and around a valet, Claire Maynard, who opened a public hundred-thousand-dollar bet that nobody could pin or submit her own top star before the promotion's first pay-per-view. By its fifth card, in Montreal, the Empire Club had reached the final of its own tag-team tournament and the Rising Star title had survived its first defence.

The promotion pointed itself toward that first pay-per-view all autumn: WildWorld 1, Born Wild, Die Wilder, billed for the end of its first cycle in December 2002. Its own last held cards look forward to a second cycle beginning that month, previewing a tag-team title match and a rematch between two of its own founding rivals.
