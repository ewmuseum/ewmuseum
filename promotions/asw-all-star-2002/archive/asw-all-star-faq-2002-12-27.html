First card to be announced in early January! ===== > > ALL-STAR WRESTLING (ASW) > > FAQ 1.0 > > JANUARY 1, 2003 > > > > OVERVIEW > > > > All-Star Wrestling, "The Next Big Thing In Internet Wrestling," is a brand > new wrestling promotion devoted to providing its audience with > top-of-the-line wrestling action from some of the hottest up-and-coming > talents in the business. Based in the mile-high city of Denver, Colorado, > and booked into some of the most prominent mid-sized venues in North > America, ASW will hold one televised card every ten days, a pay-per-view > supercard after every fourth card (approximately once every two months), and > an international tour of Mexico, the U.K., or Canada every summer. > > > > IN-CHARACTER HISTORY > > > > All-Star Wrestling, as stated above, is a start-up promotion founded in > early 2003 by an international investment group looking to enter the very > competitive "sports entertainment" industry. Led by investment banker and > financier Nicholas "Nick" MacArthur, this investment group has hired some of > the sharpest minds in the business to run their promotion for them and make > ASW "The Next Big Thing In Internet Wrestling." > > > > OUT-OF-CHARACTER HISTORY > > > > After years of writing for, handling in, and/or running such feds as the > Supreme Wrestling Federation (SWF), the High Impact Wrestling Federation > (HIWF), the Vegas Wrestling Federation (VWF), and RAW, veteran e-w writers > and fedheads John, Jared, Rich, and Frank decided to start with a clean > slate, forming All-Star Wrestling to fill the void left by the closing of > their old e-feds. > > > > CONTACT DETAILS > > > > John - [detail withheld] > > Jared - [detail withheld] > > Rich - [detail withheld] > > Frank - [detail withheld] > > > > CARD AND PPV SCHEDULE > > > > The plan is for ASW to run on a "one card every ten days" schedule, with > four cards per cycle and a pay-per-view supercard approximately once every > two months. Regular cards will have seven or eight matches, with the first > three being "dark" (summarized) matches and the remainder being full > play-by-play, while the as-yet-unnamed pay-per-views will be anywhere > between eight and ten matches, all play-by-play, for your viewing enjoyment. > Weekly cards, pay-per-views, and wrestler promos will be posted to > [detail withheld] while strats and on-cards should be sent to > [detail withheld] and O.O.C. discussion will take place at > [detail withheld] . > > > > ASW SUPERSTARS > > > > ASW Superstars is All-Star Wrestling's anchor show and will come out once > every ten days. Cards will be posted to the allstarpromos group, a roleplay > and strat deadline will be announced, and all roleplays up to the deadline > will be accepted for the show. The promo group will be a semi-open flash > group with the day's roleplays being posted in mini-packets every morning > and evening rather than coming out haphazardly all day long (this is better > than a closed-flash (packet) system because it allows a handler to respond > to challenges, etc., in between cards but has an advantage over open flash > in that you'll only have to worry about your mailbox being flooded twice a > day). Matches for Superstars, as noted above, will be a mix of summary and > play-by-play, with the summary matches being prelim matches or low-level > feuds and the play-by-play (PBP) being midcard and main event level. > Summaries will be two or three paragraphs in length while PBP matches will > run approximately 750-1000 words to keep everyone's attention for the entire > card. > > > > ASW CHAMPIONSHIPS > > > > 1. WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT CHAMPIONSHIP - The top title in ASW. The wrestler who > holds this title will have displayed excellence in roleplaying, strategies, > and on-card performance. Must be defended at least twice per cycle. > > > > 2. UNITED STATES CHAMPIONSHIP - The secondary title in ASW whose owner is > usually considered the number one contender to the World Heavyweight > Championship. A tournament for the United States Title will not be held > until the roster is full enough to support it and this title will be the > only one in ASW that must be defended on every card. > > > > 3. CRUISERWEIGHT CHAMPIONSHIP - ASW's singles title for wrestlers weighing > 230 pounds or less. Though ASW does not limit its cruiserweights to the > cruiserweight division this is the only title with a weight restriction. > Must be defended at least twice per cycle. > > > > 4. WORLD TAG-TEAM CHAMPIONSHIP - Title for the best team of two wrestlers in > ASW. Must be defended at least twice per cycle. > > > > There is now, nor will there ever be, a hardcore division in ASW due to the > insurance liability of retaining medical staff for injuries suffered in > these violent matches. Special hardcore stipulations may be requested and > authorized to develop certain storylines or settle feuds but are not, for > the most part, a part of ASW's day-to-day operations. > > > > PROMOS AND STRATS > > > > The most important aspect of a federation like All-Star Wrestling is the > participation of you, the handler. By writing creative promos and strats to > get your character over as a good guy (face) or bad guy (heel), you become > your character...and the more you get into your character, the better he or > she becomes in the world of e-wrestling. ASW has just a few basic rules for > writing promos and strats and, by following them, you'll not only make the > experience more fun for others, you'll make it more fun for yourself as > well. Again, ASW is a semi-open flash system so there's really no limit to > how many promos you write for a particular show. All we ask is that your > promos get your character across realistically, keeps the reader's interest, > and avoid racist or sexist comments. Swearing is tolerated in promos but > remember this: if they bleep it on TV, you need to bleep it in your email. > As for your strats, they don't need to be very detailed but every bit helps > so try to cover the basics (history behind the match, motivation, any > highpoints you want to use, etc.). If you want to write a copy-and-paste > strat that gets used word for word, feel free to...but remember we reserve > the right to edit content to fit existing storylines, etc. > > > > ANGLES, STORYLINES, AND FEUDS > > > > Whether you call it an angle, a storyline, or a feud, just remember that > they're the most important part of the game. Without enough interesting > storylines to keep you as readers and handlers entertained, ASW wouldn't > survive the first cycle. Angle development is as much a part of your job in > ASW as it is ours as fedheads. It's always great to see angles develop as > handlers work together but remember that the four ASW fedheads are here to > help you so feel free to use us as resources when you hit a brick wall. As > far as what goes into developing an angle, the sky's the limit because, even > if you choose to recycle something you saw on TV or in another fed, there's > always the chance of real life (okay, e-life) interfering and setting you on > a completely different path. > > > > IN-CHARACTER STAFF > > > > 1. NICHOLAS "NICK" MacARTHUR - The forty-something year old face of ASW's > "Executive Board." Call him the president, the commissioner, or the boss. > He's the man running the show and, with the backing he's got behind him, > he's not afraid to put his foot down. > > > > 2. MIKE DIAMOND - The twenty-something year old fan-favorite play-by-play > announcer. A former MLW and RAW-Carolina Tag Team Champion who retired from > the business in December 2002 and signed aboard as ASW's first full-time > staffer. > > > > 3. "TERRIBLE" TOMMY BURKHART - The forty-something year old color > commentator who couldn't make it as a wrestler so chose instead to become a > wrestling instructor, opening his own school in ASW's hometown of Denver, > CO. But, like the saying goes, "Those who can't, teach." And, trust me, > Burkhart can't teach...which is why he became a commentator when his school > went bankrupt in 2002. > > > > 4. GUNNAR GOETZ - ASW's backstage interviewer. Another forty-something year > old who worked his way up through the business by following one strict rule: > "Whatever Gunnar wants, Gunner Goetz." > > > > 5. RENEE ANDERSON - Daughter of the legendary Richard Anderson, > twenty-something year old Renee has stepped into her father's shoes to > become ASW's primary ring announcer (and singer of the National Anthem). Eat > your heart out Lillian Garcia!! > > > > 6. KEITH RICHARDS - Not the Rolling Stones' guitarist, this Richards, at age > fifty-four, is the elder statesman of ASW, serving as an instructor at the > ASW Academy and as Senior Troubleshooting Referee for main event matches. > > > > 7. JOHNNY OLSEN - Referee, age 44. Former student of Senior Referee Keith > Richards who plays it by the book. > > > > 8. RUSSELL McBRIDE - Referee, age 24. Calls it down the middle. > > > > 9. SCOTT WARREN - Referee, age 32. Former rulebreaking wrestler who tends to > turn a blind eye to a fellow rulebreaker. > > > > BOOKING DECISIONS AND POLICIES > > > > All booking decisions in All-Star Wrestling are made by the real-life owners > (John, Jared, Rich, and Frank). Rather than voting as a committee to > determine a winner or winners, the four owners will alternate writing for > and booking each of ASW's four divisions (World, U.S., Cruiserweight, and > Tag Team) for an entire cycle and each owner will have the final say for his > division while in control. While you may not agree with every decision we > make, please try to understand we may have reasons for doing something and, > if you come to us calmly and rationally, we'll be happy to explain our > reasons if we're able to (i.e., without revealing an upcoming storyline, > etc.) All booking decisions will be made on the basis of a wrestler's > promos, strats for the match (to a lesser degree but still important), and > angle development. In other words, while you might think your promo quality > was better than your opponent's for a given match, that opponent may have an > angle in development that merited his winning the match and we'll be glad to > discuss this with you at any time. Remember we're all doing this for fun, > not just you as the handler but us as the owners, so going off half-cocked > if you lose a match doesn't just ruin it for you, it ruins it for us too. > > > > MATCH WRITING AND PARTICIPATION > > > > As with any fed, help with match writing is always appreciated and, the more > you are willing to participate, the more enjoyable this experience will be > for all of us. With four owners writing for each card, we hope to have at > least one or two volunteers to write matches but understand this: writing > for a card you're on does NOT guarantee you'll be winning your match on that > card.
