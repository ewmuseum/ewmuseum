A federation that ran its own first-ever pay-per-view, kept a rivalry with HCW alive across the newsgroup, and completed its life cycle in under a year by merging into a new superfederation called ONE. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The World Online Wrestling Association opened on 22 April 1997 as a one-person, roleplay-judged startup, its founder citing experience running “feds such as the UWA, WOW, and CWA” and naming three championships — the WOWA World Heavyweight, Intercontinental, and World Tag Team titles — before it had a single member. It planned one weekly card, Saturday Night Super Fights, until its roster grew large enough to support more.

A homepage followed a week later, and that spring's launch tournament, Sudden Death '97, put twelve men through a single-elimination bracket to crown its first World and Intercontinental champions alongside a tag-team battle royal. Over the summer it added Grapplefest, Warzone, Summersplash, and Tuesday Night HellHouse to a growing card schedule, and brought outside writers on to help produce roughly five matches a week.

That August, one of its own co-presidents (who also served as interim president of a sister promotion, HCW) publicly counseled handlers to keep their in-fiction personas separate from themselves after a public thread about being confused with one's own character — “do Not base the character totally on yourself.”

Its title picture turned on a screwjob that December. After a wrestler called LOCO stole the WOWA World Title outright the week before, a match staged on 15 December 1997 to crown a new champion turned into a double-identity angle: the champion's challenger, “The Confessor” Rey Jeran, was ambushed mid-match by a second man disguised as his opponent, Vile Vince Viper, who turned out to be LOCO himself. LOCO left with the WOWA World Heavyweight Championship while the real Viper brawled with a returning “Malibu” Mike Madison at ringside.

The same week, HCW folded into WOWA to form a combined superfederation: a 16 December report named WOWA's own founder the incoming president of the new group and said WOWA “will close its doors for good” once the merger finalized after a scheduled cross-promotion show against the NWF. By 23 December the new promotion had a name, ONE, a fifty-wrestler, sixteen-team roster split into Hardcore and Regular divisions, its own weekly schedule, and a kickoff supercard planned for January 1998.

No document survives narrating what became of ONE. WOWA itself resurfaced on 14 June 1999 after what it called “a long haitus,” announcing a comeback show, Lethal Lottery '99, for the following month. No further document survives after that announcement.
