NRG — Show Recap

NRG TV @ Hammerstein Ballroom

31 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
NEW REVOLUTION GRAPPLING Hammerstein Ballroom, New York City --- An NRG "Best Of" package set to Soilwork's "Light the Torch" started the show. Clips included "Hotshot" Stevie Scott throwing fire into the face of Marcus Lord, with a hyperactive Corey Atari going apeshit in the background. Corey Atari nailing his signature superkick, "The Pong Kick," on the chin of Marcus Lord. The Anti-Gravity Kid doing a head-first dive from the top rope to the outside, flipping just in time to topple a dazed and confused Mr. Bubble. "Head Trauma" Inoue nearly tearing the head off of Retribution with a running lariat, absolutely levelling the big man. He is immediately followed by tag team partner "Executioner" Eddie Mills, nailing his own lariat from hell on Christopher Cannon, literally flipping him in the process. Ketsueki Ikeda pushing a ladder over with Christopher Cannon standing atop it, reaching in vain for the NRG World Heavyweight Title as he slowly falls and then flies high above the top rope and into the steel guardrail at ringside. The camera zooms in on an unconscious Cannon, his face nothing but a mask of crimson. The camera cuts to Ikeda returning to his feet and mustering up the strength to set up the ladder, climb it, and successfully retrieve the NRG World Heavyweight Title. "Head Trauma" Inoue and "Executioner" Eddie Mills holding the NRG World Tag Team Titles high over head. Standing next to them and looking evil as fuck is Ketsueki Ikeda, holding up the NRG World Heavyweight Title. A neon green New Revolution Grappling image, blood dripping down the letters. And finally we arrive at the Hammerstein Ballroom, not quite full but damn near... with those there anxiously awaiting the latest offering from New Revolution Grappling. Joey Stevens and Will Borders were introduced - Stevens still wearing a black business suit with the "NRG" patch above the breast pocket. Will "The Thrill" Borders hasn't changed a bit either - annoying hawaiian-print button-down, wifebeater, khaki shorts and Jesus sandals... fake Rolex blingin'. After a lot of obligatory shilling by Stevens about the latest incarnation of NRG, and a lot of bad jokes from Borders, we get down to ringside for the night's first scheduled match. --- x. THE BONECRUSHERS (E & D) d. GIRLZ ON FILM (DR. DETROIT & CHUCK E. BAUERSOX) after an assisted sommersault guillotine legdrop in 9'32". Comments: Girlz On Film were introduced first, prancing to the ring to the sounds of Wesley Willis' "Girls On Film," much to the chagrin of the NYC fans. Detroit, a seasoned veteran independent wrestler, taunted the crowd as expected - specifically picking on a rather large woman in the front row. Bauersox instigated the ordeal, as expected, all the while wearing a bright red derby hat? The Bonecrushers hit the scene all business, "Them Bones" by Alice in Chains blaring over the ballroom's PA system. The crowd went nuts, giving the 'Crushers a surprising reaction. The twin brothers wasted no time hitting the ring, but were quickly attacked by the dastardly Girlz On Film duo before the bell. Detroit and Bauersox seemed a competent team during the early minutes of the match, both men working Bonecrusher E over pretty well with quick tags and a variety of double team maneuvers - double dropkick, double snap suplex, and as much cheating as they could squeeze in while the referee's back was turned. The tides soon turned however. A botched top rope body splash by Detroit was anticipated by a seemingly dazed Bonecrusher E, leading to a simply devastating full press slam to the mat, followed by a hot tag. The Bonecrushers went on to make quick work of Detroit, with Bauersox being disposed of quickly. An incredibly vicious running lariat by Bonecrusher D led to Bonecrusher E hitting the top for a sommersault guillotine legdrop, with the expected assistance of his twin brother on the "sommersault" portion of the move. The cover, the count, and it was over with. Crowd was into this one, especially the big woman in the front row going apeshit over the Bonecrushers due to Detroit's harassment earlier in the match. Bonecrushers celebrated with said woman post-match. --- x. TEAM RUMBLE FISH (CRIMSON DAISUKE, ROACH HANAMIZU & TORO ZOMBIE) d. THE ANTI-GRAVITY KID, MR. BUBBLE & TOSHIHIRO DANZAI two falls to one in 18'44". Comments: "Rumble Fish" by Sevendust led out Team Rumble Fish, with the epitome of punk Roach Hanamizu leading the pack and making some incredibly rude gestures to the camera as he walked down the aisle. Daisuke, the heavyweight of the bunch, looked better than he has in years, you can tell he's been hitting the gym. Toro Zombie was seemingly obsessed with the humping of things? No telling. System of a Down's "Sugar" brought out the eccentric Anti-Gravity Kid, the equally weird Mr. Bubble and puroresu strong man Toshihiro Danzai. AGK was everywhere, slapping hands with the fans, easily the most recognizable of the tecnico trio. Mr. Bubble slapped a few hands as well. Danzai was all business, focused on the ring. Crimson Daisuke started things out for the Rumble Fish trio. AGK started things out for his team. AGK came out strong, rocking the much larger Daisuke with a flurry of martial arts-style kicks, specifically targetting the legs. Things changed quickly when a spinning leg lariat attempt by AGK was thwarted and turned into a vicious one-handed thrust/slam to the mat, followed by a ground-and- pound style barrage of fists from Daisuke. This led towards a near five-minute display of pure domination by Daisuke... punishing the lightweight AGK with a series of running lariats. At one point, AGK ducked another lariat attempt, rebounding off the ropes and attempting a cross body attack... only to be caught and in turn spun around and dropped head-first with a nice variation of the DDT by Daisuke. Daisuke drug AGK to the corner and tagged out to Hanamizu, who quickly went to work ruthlessly stomping away on the Kid. Hanamizu broke out some kicks of his own, damn near knocking AGK smooth out with a running Yakuza kick of sorts, followed by a dash of running senton just for dickish rudo seasoning. After another minute or so of beatdown by Hanamizu, a whip to the ropes and attempted back body drop by Roach was quickly turned to a kick to the chops and a snap DDT by AGK. The Kid even hit the mat after executing the move, exhausted and pulling the DDT out of seemingly nowhere. Both men struggled to reach their feet, AGK barely getting to his first. Hanamizu sprinted across the mat, jumped in the air and attempted a quick hurricanrana, only to be caught in mid-move atop AGK's shoulders. The Anti-Gravity Kid proceeded to run across the ring in this position, and absolutely _drove_ Hanamizu to the mat _hard_ with a running sitdown powerbomb. Daisuke attempted to interrupt the pinfall to no avail. First fall is awarded to AGK's team. x. FIRST FALL: THE ANTI-GRAVITY KID pinned ROACH HANAMIZU with a running sitdown powerbomb in 8'21". Daisuke still managed to flatten AGK's nose with a kick to the face, giving the advantage to Hanamizu as he returned to his feet. Both men went all out for around five minutes, putting on one of the finest displays of light heavyweight wrestling the US has seen in quite some time (although we do send a shout out to the Project X Wrestling Super Middleweight division! Hi guys!) Hanamizu seemingly had things under control after a failed back body drop from AGK was turned into a quite sick looking version of the guillotine face crusher by Hanamizu. However, instead of following it up with a cover... Hanamizu climbed the turnbuckles, attempting to add insult to injury with a high-risk move. A picture-perfect shooting star press attempt went bad when AGK suddenly rolled towards the corner, causing Hanamizu to land in a really sick position... hitting the mat chin/chest first, and just absolutely knocking the breath out of him. Crowd loved this. It was then a fight to see which man would return to his feet first and make the tag, Hanamizu inching towards the outstretched hand of long-time Rumble Fish partner Crimson Daisuke, AGK doing likewise with Mr. Bubble. Crowd pop huged when AGK made the diving tag to Mr. Bubble. Bubble springboards into the ring just as Hanamizu tags Daisuke back in. Absolutely craziness ensued, everyone disregarding the "tag" rule briefly... Bubble attempting to kick the shit out of Daisuke, only to have one of his feet caught and floored to the mat with a lariat, Toro Zombie attempting to cross-body Toshihiro Danzai only to be caught and pressed over the top rope and to the floor (legalizing the "no tag" thing a little bit. Go lucha go!) Daisuke took control of things briefly, but Bubble proved to be a somewhat formidable opponent for the heavyweight, reversing a lot of Daisuke's takedown attempts. Some nice chain wrestling spots, leading to a nice German suplex with a bridge. Daisuke barely kicked out. Bubble hit the ropes for momentum, attempting to knock Daisuke to his feet again with what looked to be a lariat. We never found out though, as Daisuke broke out a very fucked up looking uranage out of seemingly nowhere, his arm remaining over Bubble's chest for the quick three count. x. SECOND FALL: CRIMSON DAISUKE pinned MR. BUBBLE after a uranage in 15'04". Pandemonium from here on out, as an enraged Toshihiro Danzai and AGK hit the ring, as did Hanamizu and Toro Zombie... action was everywhere, Danzai again knocking Zombie to the outside, and AGK leveling Hanamizu with a leg lariat. Danzai went to work on Hanamizu, while AGK hit a beautiful tope con hilo suicida onto Zombie. After disposing of Bubble, Daisuke picked a new foe in Toshihiro Danzai, and it was fucking on. The two puroresu heavyweights duked it out, elbow strikes a'plenty. Daisuke would attempt to tear the skin from Danzai's chest with a knife-edge chop and Danzai would respond with his elbow in Daisuke's mouth. All out head dropping action from here on out, with Danzai nailing a release German for a nearfall. Daisuke with a Tiger Suplex of his own for another near fall. Meanwhile, AGK and Mr. Bubble were attempting suicide with various head-first dives from the top onto Hanamizu and Toro Zombie. Crowd didn't know what to cheer the most - the junior heavyweight display outside the ring, or the strong style action in the ring. Final minutes of the match saw Daisuke get the best of Danzai with an awesome version of the Death Valley Driver. Soon thereafter AGK was poised on the top rope, signalling for a shooting star press to a down-and-out Roach Hanamizu on the outside. Daisuke caught the young Englishman, grabbing him and just pounding him to the mat with a crucifix powerbomb, folding his legs over his head for the cover. Mr. Bubble tried for the save, but to no avail. x. THIRD FALL: CRIMSON DAISUKE pinned THE ANTI-GRAVITY KID with a crucifix powerbomb in 18'44". Team Rumble Fish attempted an in-ring celebration, but Danzai would have no part of it... clearing house quickly. Daisuke gave the tecnico trio the bird from outside the ring, Danzai yelling in Japanese at Daisuke. Hatred! Danzai tried speaking some English but it didn't come out too good, the only thing decipherable was "Next week!" --- x. ADAM "LIGHTNING" HANSEN d. GORY del OLMO and BINKS BRADLEY via pinfall after a Thunderbomb on Bradley in 16'28". Comments: Joey Stevens reminded the fans at home that this match was for the number one contendership to the NRG World Heavyweight Title. The crowd seemed pretty hyped up for this one. The arena went dim as "Needles and Pins" by the Deftones hit the sound system. Binks Bradley emerged from behind the curtain to a cool strobe light and smoke effect. Bradley was introduced at 6'3" and a hearty 280 lbs. He paid no attention to the crowd, all business. Mean looking motherfucker, for real. In Flames' "Man Made God" brought out the equally massive Gory del Olmo, announced at 6'4" and 260 lbs. The former tough man competitor came to the ring with a black towel over his head, black tights, black kickpads, black boots, and black padded gloves. Mmm, blackness. Again, no talk, strictly business. That all changed soon however, as "Brother" by Dark New Day brought Adam Hansen, to probably the biggest heel reaction of the night. The crowd in NYC hated this guy with a passion... and he absolutely basked in that fact, playing it for all it was worth. Hansen relentlessly taunted the fans before entering the ring. Bradley wasted no time, immediately attacking Hansen before the bell, pummeling him to the mat with a stiff tackle, immediately unleashing a flurry of closed fists to Hansen's head. This didn't last long however, as del Olmo showed no love by kicking Bradley square in the face, sending the message that this match definitely wasn't going to be pretty. As Hansen ducked out of the ring, del Olmo continued his assault on Bradley, stomping away at the Kill Devil Hills, North Carolina native. A whip across the ring into the turnbuckles followed by an incredibly stiff avalanche-style lariat to the corner, knocking the wind out of Bradley and causing him to drop to the mat in a sitting position. Not good, as del Olmo displayed another of his stiff kicks... right to the unprotected face of Bradley again. And again. And again. And as he turned around to gain momentum for... err-... no telling really, as he turned around just in time for the brunt of Adam Hansen's surprise superkick, nailing the hispanic monster square in the chin. Match went back and forth like this for quite some time - Hansen doing some serious damage on Bradley, only to be broken up by a "Holy shit! That _HAD_ to hurt!" uraken from Del Olmo. Del Olmo would then rock Hansen's world with a plethora of kicks and elbow strikes, setting up for a big move only to have Binks Bradley gore the living shit out of him. The end sequence saw the action spill outside the ring, Bradley punishing Del Olmo as if he'd killed his dog or something. Hansen livened things up with a suicide dive over the top onto both men. Bradley took the brunt of the dive's impact, with Del Olmo returning to his feet first... only to receive a wicked looking chair shot from Hansen. Hansen wasted no time throwing Bradley and the chair into the ring... nailing the Hansen family signature, the Thunderbomb (double underhook piledriver), right on top of the chair. The cover and this one was over with. Post-match, fans began throwing debris at Hansen as he posed victoriously over the fallen Bradley. Hansen made sure to remind the fans that he was now the number one contender, gesturing for a title belt around his waist. --- x. MAIN EVENT - LADDER MATCH: KETSUEKI IKEDA(c) d. CHRISTOPHER CANNON to retain the NRG WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT TITLE in 18'58". Comments: Back from break, the camera zoomed in on the NRG World Heavyweight Championship, already hanging high above the ring. Stevens and Borders hyped the match, as clips were shown of the first encounter between these two in 2000, when Ikeda first won the belt. Megadeth's "Sweating Bullets" brought out NRG veteran Christopher Cannon, attired in sleeveless work overalls to display his sleeved out arms - spiderwebs on the elbows for that extra penitentiary flavor. Cannon was well received, although he ignored the crowd mostly - just walking to the ring with one fist in the air. "Dirthouse" by Static X sent the crowd into an absolute fucking frenzy, as Ketsueki Ikeda broke out from behind the curtain toting an orange utility ladder. Ikeda wore jeans, cowboy boots?, and a t-shirt that proclaimed "DANGERSOME FIGHTER" and an old "WRESTLE OR DIE!" bandana around his head. Stevens detailed Ikeda's storied history, including his claim of being undefeated in the United States. Match was what it was expected to be, an all out brawl with a lot of scary looking spots involving the ladder, including a scary looking release German suplex, only Ikeda kinda grabbed him from around the side instead of the back and sent Cannon's neck crashing into the ladder in an awkward position. The two brawled in the crowd, even fighting their way up the stairs to the infamous balconies of the Hammerstein Ballroom. No big spot off the balcony as expected, but it was teased heavily. Instead, Cannon forced fans out of their chairs... powerbombing Ikeda onto about five chairs. Pretty crucial. Other big spots included a replay of their match in 2000, with Cannon being shoved from the top of the ladder, over the top rope, and to the floor. Unlike last time though, Ikeda didn't attempt to retrieve the belt... he set the ladder back up and did a breath- taking senton bomb from the top of the ladder onto Cannon at ringside. Definitely a Kodak moment, as flashbulbs went off everywhere. Ikeda sealed the deal with what looked to originally be a vertical suplex that just went all kinds of wrong, as he released Cannon in what looked to be an Orange Crush... only Cannon landed exactly head first, neck again snapping awkwardly, falling limp right on impact. Ikeda set up the ladder and retrieved the NRG World Heavyweight Title much to the delight of the fans, who went apeshit. Camera closed in on Ikeda standing atop the ladder with the title held high above his head. --- A clip of the night's events ended the show - the Bonecrushers victory via the top rope guillotine legdrop, the Anti-Gravity Kid's running sitdown powerbomb, Crimson Daisuke absolutely ruining Toshihiro Danzai's vertebrae with a Death Valley Driver, Gory del Olmo's vicious kicks to the face of Binks Bradley, Adam Hansen's picture perfect superkick on Del Olmo, Ketsueki Ikeda shoving Christopher Cannon off the ladder, and then the not-so Orange Crush from Ikeda onto Cannon. Ikeda holding the NRG World Title on top of the ladder. And finally, Adam Hansen signalling for the belt around his waist. Ikeda/Hansen, next week!
