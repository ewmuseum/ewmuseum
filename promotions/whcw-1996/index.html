A hardcore-leaning newsgroup wrestling promotion whose surviving pay-per-view broadcast staged a construction-site brawl, a handcuffed scaffold match, and title changes across its cruiserweight, tag team and vacant championships. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World Hardcore Championship Wrestling ran on TNM v6.2 PLUS, staged as three cards a week plus a near-monthly pay-per-view; its own operator, in a June 1997 recruitment post, dated its founding to “almost a year” earlier, in 1996.

Its own top stable, the Lords of the Ring — Adonis Mercury, Thunderstorm, Doomsday and Bane — had by early 1998 fallen out with its on-air president, Ross, over what Mercury called a “contract changeover scam” meant to push them out; it cost the five-time former World Champion his title outright, with no successor named on the record. Mercury, in the meantime, had gone on to win a rival promotion's own Unified Title that same year, beating Steve Austin and the Giant in turn, while the Lords sat out its own ring.

Its Rumble in the Bronx 2 pay-per-view, broadcast from Madison Square Garden on 25 January 1998, was built around a four-on-four, no-rules brawl at a real construction site between Doomsday's team and Colossus's own Strike Force — and it spiraled into a 31-wrestler riot, with wrestlers from a rival promotion, SGWF, invading the site as well. A special post-match report ran through the toll: Spinwheel taken out with a dislocated or fractured vertebra after a second-story Frankensteiner, Slaughter poured over with wet cement and hospitalized for oxygen deprivation, Nevermind rushed into surgery for a suspected ruptured spleen, and Misfit's leg broken outright. Worst of all was Punishment, struck in the back of the head with his own sledgehammer by Doomsday after interfering in a match he wasn't part of; a company spokesman confirmed he remained unconscious and unresponsive, and that the promotion would press no charges against Doomsday for a “sanctioned wrestling match.”

Elsewhere on the same show, Chris Tyler won the North American Championship in a “Lover's Leap Match” fought handcuffed atop a fifteen-foot scaffold, finishing Kensai Akira with a plancha off Akira's own dislocated shoulders; a five-man steel cage elimination match settled the vacant Extreme Championship; Speed Racer retained the Cruiserweight Championship with a top-rope Flatliner; and the reigning tag champions, the Sociopaths, were beaten down backstage by Mercury and Thunderstorm before a masked team revealed afterward as the returning Ultranationalists took their titles in the ring.

Its recruitment posts describe an open-fed structure built on handler applications and a running “creation sheet,” and by mid-1997 it had begun building a booked women's division alongside its regular, simulator-run card.

No closure statement survives; the promotion's own last document on record is a 17 February 1998 call for more handlers for that women's division.
