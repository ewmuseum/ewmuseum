ASE's Wild 1/17 (Debut Show)

January 25, 2001, 12:00:00 AM
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[Wednesday. 8:55 PM.] [You've spent the last 10 minutes surfing the 300+ channels that your local cable provider has bludgeoned you with, vainly attempting to find the one that carries the WB, 'cause let's face it, having never watched any of the other crap on that network you have no clue what station number it is.
Finally you stumble upon a picture with a small, transparent "WB" logo in the lower right-hand corner, only to find the annoying kids from Dawson's
Creek staring back at you. Being a wrestling fan you nearly vomit on site.
Thankfully, being a wrestling fan years of hard drinking have helped you to develop a cast iron stomach and you resist the urge to hurl just long enough for the credits on the sappy, teen melodrama to begin rolling. You breathe a sigh of relief.]
[The screen splits in two, the credits to DC (for those hardcore fans of which we pray you're not one) shrinking down into a small window in the upper left-hand corner. The right side of the picture is suddenly filled with a dizzying montage of muscular men doing violent things. The promo guy then attempts to earn his paycheck.]
"Coming up next on the WB, catch all the hard hitting, chest pounding excitement of the newest bullies on the television block as American Sports
Entertainment presents ASE's Wild. Join all your favorite ASE Superstars as they battle for supremacy both in and out of the ring. It's wrestling like you've never seen it before and it's only right here on the WB. Next!"
[The closing shots of Dawson's Creek resume to full-screen size as the music fades and the last few credits scroll by. Then comes a brief ad hyping whatever lame mid-90's sitcom is currently being syndicated by your local WB affiliate. Dawson's Creek, Wrestling, then Family Matters... and they wonder why their ratings suck? Anyway, the ad over and done with, our picture quickly turns a solid red. A giant black ace of spades playing card spins end-over-end from off left, landing face up in the middle of the screen. Below it, words fade up.]
The following is a presentation of American Sports Entertainment. [And then it all fades to black...]
