Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Professional Wrestling Circuit introduced itself on 11 August 1997 as “a free Wrestling fantasy league… just getting started,” run out of a personal AOL members page rather than a Usenet card series. Handlers created their own wrestlers, up to three each, competing for three championships at the outset (a fourth followed later); matches were simulated for their results, with roleplay optional and used only to raise a wrestler's own attributes — a distinction its own adverts drew deliberately against “email feds.”

Its cards ran live rather than posted: an AOL chat room and an ICQ channel carried two weekly broadcasts, timed at 6:00 Eastern, “just 2 hrs before nitro and raw.” Its first, a Monday Night Main Event on 18 August 1997, is recapped in its own words as going “smooth as could have been possible,” its then-undefeated champion wrestling twice in one night and coming out of a cage match “seriously injured.” It later split into north and south branches, built to stage interleague matches at its own pay-per-view specials.

Its own fullest surviving record belongs to one wrestler, Habakuk Duncan: a 49-12 mark, five reigns with its Heavyweight championship, plus a Prince of Power title and a Red Belt Tag Team title, wrestling out of a stable called the Angels of Death. By late October his own handler was offering him to other leagues, noting that those championship reigns “all came before I took over… I Am no longer in it” — one honest marker of how many hands the promotion had already passed through by then.

It declared itself relaunched twice that autumn, “back and ready to roll” on 1 September and “back for good” on 18 October, by then running two weekly cards and a Saturday tournament for wrestlers without a title. Its own last dated post, ten days later, is a repurposed chain letter carrying no news of its operations. No closing statement of its own survives past it.
