What is IWWC? ================================================== = 1.1 - Mission Statement To become recognized as one of the most enjoyable and fun e-wrestling federations on the internet by encouraging full participation by our handlers, producing top-notch events through superior match-writing and detailed angle development, providing creative control to our handlers, and encouraging open communication both vertically and horizontally.
In other words, we want to the IWWC to be FOR THE HANDLERS. 1.2 - History The Internet World Wrestling Council (IWWC) was founded in September 1995, by [name withheld]. We got off to a slow start, but before long people were beginning to hear about the fed, and more and more talented players began signing on, and things really started developing. At the time of it's abrupt and unexpected closure in May of 1996, a number of future superstars called IWWC home--talent such as The Down Boys, former IWWC World Tag Team Champions and purveyors of the funniest angle in the fed's history, Christopher
Stonebreaker, Rob "Blackline" James, "The Natural" Adam Rogers, and everyone's favorite 60 year old underdog, Dowell Flatt, who still seeks his first professional win.
As mentioned, the fed closed abruptly in May 1996, due to someone who ran the fed losing internet access without warning. But fond memories from some of our original handlers ([name withheld] and
[name withheld] in particular) planted the seed of thought in [name withheld]'s mind, a seed that said 'restart that bad boy'. And thus, the IWWC is reborn in April 2000.
As indicated in the name, the IWWC is a world-wide organization. During our first run, we kept the cards mostly within the continental United States, but that will indeed change as we embark upon our second life. We also actively seek talent on a world-wide scope.
Our corporate headquarters are located in Memphis, TN, right across from historic Beale Street.
The IWWC is your basic wrestling federation. By that, I mean we're not one with 'extreme' or 'hardcore' overtones, although it's not uncommon for matches to get out of hand, and for referees to turn the other way. Actually, the IWWC brass has recognized the explosion (pardon the pun) of the extreme style, and particularly the revenue it could create, and added an 'Extreme' division in April 2000.
But for the most part, matches usually stay within the realm of TV-14 content. Along the lines of extreme comes the issue of censorship. While the IWWC doesn't generally censor every four-letter word that gets voiced, we do put reasonable limits on language and content on shows and in vignettes. Generally, if you can't say it on network TV without making the censor guy push the little red button (usually too late), it will be censored in IWWC. You can say it, we'll just put a
[BLEEP!] in it's place. So, basically, it gets said without saying it. Generally, we're open to accepting just about any sort of wrestler. However, names are pretty important to us. It is possible that you apply and get an email asking you to make a change to your wrestler's name. Again, we try to be lenient in this regard, and give you as much control as possible. We're just quirky that way. :)
================================================== = 2. Who runs IWWC? ================================================== = 2.1 - In Real Life That would be [name withheld], the original founder of the IWWC, and only head honcho the fed has ever known.
In the past, I pretty much did it all, from webmastering to booking to card-writing... but that got old in a hurry. This time around, I'll still have an active role in most everything that goes on, but will definitely be employing assistance from our beloved handlers.
If you're interested in my personal background, let's leave it at this. I'm way too old to be doing this, but it's so much fun I just can't stop myself :) 2.2 - Storyline The 'new' President of the IWWC is Mason Cooper, taking over for former President John Armstrong. Little is known about Cooper's background, and he had no known association with the IWWC during it's first run.
Cooper is in his mid-30's, and he has the look of your typical executive--three-piece suit, expensive shoes, business-like demeanor, the whole bit. He's somewhat of a control freak, and has been known to alienate employees and talent alike to accomplish what he thinks is best.
================================================== = 3. Who are the staff members? ================================================== = 3.1 - Announcers/Commentators 3.1.1 - William Wright: William was the lead man of the original IWWC broadcast team, and he's back in that role this time around. He's pretty much a straight shooter, calling it by the book, a believer in fair play. He's in his late 30's, a bit on the heavy side, and quite excitable at times. If he seems a bit upset with his position at times, it may be because he is--he's fresh off a run as the commissioner of the now-defunct ACCW.
3.1.2 - Dr. Bobby Bush: The good doctor is the other half of the original broadcast crew, and he's the Bobby Heenan to Wright's
Gorilla Monsoon. A gray-haired man in his early-60's, Dr. Bush loves controversy and even more, to be the instigator of it. Thus, he's
pro-anyone who breaks the rules. 3.1.3 - Christian Dangerously: Another IWWC original, Dangerously hosts 'The Danger Zone', the IWWC's weekly (maybe) recap show. Roleplays are generally shown on this show, along with recaps, news, updates, etc., etc. It's basically a roleplay show and then some.
3.2 - Interviewers 3.2.1 - Brian Nicklaus: Another original IWWCer, Brian returns to the position he had upon closure in '96. In his mid-20's,
Nicklaus is young, ambitious, and after a better position within the company. He's been known to get a bit pushy in attempts to get interviews, and that has resulted in a few unforeseen 'vacations', if you smell what I'm cookin'. Still, he's about as persistent as they get; and if anyone will get a comment from a wrestler, he will.
3.2.2 - Eddie Eaton: Hired on with IWWC on a reference from Wright, Eaton last worked with ACCW as an interviewer and studio show host. If you looked the word 'deadpan' up in Webster's, you'd see a picture of Eddie. He's in his late 20's, just married, and is pretty much the utility player of the broadcasting team.
3.3 - Officials 3.3.1 - Michael Wilson: He was the head official before, and he's back to enforce the long arm of the law once more. You will not find a more strict referee in the game. He's been known to DQ guys for looking at him funny. Not exactly a big guy at 6', 180, he's surprisingly able to hold his ground against irate wrestlers. He's not afraid to stand toe-to-toe with them, but usually ends up on his back anyway.
3.3.2 - Sam Espizito: Yet another member of the original IWWC referee crew, Sam is the polar opposite of Wilson. He pretty much lets anything go, and is the referee of choice when feuds get heated and need to be settled once and for all. Sam usually raises the ire of Wright, thanks to his 'rules? What rules?' approach. Unlike
Wilson, Sam _is_ a big guy, at 5'10" and 235, and has been known on occasion to floor a wrestler or two.
3.3.3 - Johnny Michaels: He's young, he's fresh, he's easy pickings for the talent. Eager to break into the business, Michaels took a position as an official instead of, *ahem*, a 'resident worker'.
But his youth and size make him vulnerable to general abuse by the wrestlers, and therefore he can be easily influenced, if it means he won't get pummeled.
3.3.4 - 'Hardcore' Tyrone Moore: As his name indicates, he calls (well, just counts, really) the Extreme division matches. A former wrestler himself, Moore is a large black nam (6'5", 290) who is basically waiting until his knee rehabs. When there's a problem backstage, Moore is usually the first one to get the call to help fix it.
3.4 - Executive Team and Other Guys 3.4.1 - Mason Cooper: The President, and final voice in the IWWC. 3.4.2 - Brent Scott: Vice President of Business. While Cooper handles things from an administrative standpoint, Scott handles the day-to-day operations of the fed, especially the monetary issues. You won't see him on camera, but he signs the paychecks.
3.4.3 - Vacant: Vice President of Personnel. Cooper's still looking to hire a front-line man to handle issues with the talent and the staff.
3.4.4 - Dave Stewart: Ring Announcer. A former DJ for an all-disco station in Memphis, Dave hopped aboard the IWWC train because you can only play so much "Stayin' Alive". He is a Michael
Buffer wannabe, and it shows in his attempts to annouce wrestlers. 3.4.5 - Randy Barber: Chief of Security. Randy's another big guy (6'2", 275), and he needs to be to enforce security around the place. He wrestled in the IWWC way back when, although he refuses to identify who he was back then. And probably, rightly so.
================================================== = 4. What kind of schedule do you run? ================================================== = 4.1 - Usual Schedule In a word, flexible. I won't lie; we don't always run cards out on a weekly basis. I'd rather see quality than quantity, and avoid quick burnout, so we'll usually play the schedule by ear. That doesn't mean you'll see one card per month; it does mean you usually won't see one every week. The schedule will change pretty much all the time, and you will always be updated and notified of deadlines, etc.
4.2 - IWWC Explosion The IWWC's flagship show. Lineups will generally be announced well before the event, but don't be surprised to see special added matches, because that's just how it goes. It usually has 7-8 matches, sometimes more, rarely less. This is where you'll see the big angles progress, and big matches go down. It is a full written card, no summarization, and intended for your reading pleasure.
4.3 - IWWC Shakedown Our secondary show. Shakedown is a combination of wrestler vignettes and lower-level matches. Ideally, our newer and developing talent will compete on this show, building themselves up to hit the big time. Matches will usually be summarized. Shakedown is hosted by Eddie Eaton and Brian Nicklaus.
4.4 - Pay Per Views/Supercards We will generally hold four pay-per-views per year. As of right now, the names of these events have not been determined. Of course, suggestions are always welcome, even after we have named them all. On occasion, we also may hold 'supercards', which would basically be an extended Explosion. Each title will not necessarily be defended on a PPV, although that will usually be the case.
4.5 - The Danger Zone The Danger Zone is like a combined roleplay show and update, all rolled into one. Hosted by Christian Dangerously, it features vignettes (i.e., roleplays), news, updates, the works.
================================================== = 5. What titles do you sanction? ================================================== = 5.1 - World Title: Obviously, we've got to have a big dog, and this is it. The most coveted prize in the IWWC. The money-maker. The spotlight-drawer. This title must be defended at least once per month, and on every pay-per-view, unless Cooper decides otherwise.
Life's hard at the top. 5.2 - American Title: The number two strap to hold in the IWWC. The holder of this title is considered the number one contender to the
World Title, although usually doesn't get World Title shots since he's too busy defending the North American Title. Like the World Title, it must be defended once per month and on pay-per-views, unless
Cooper makes a special exception. 5.3 - World Television Title: The holder of this gold belt is the best wrestler on TV. Well, not really, but it sounds good. Actually, the
TV Champion gets the toughest workout of all, because he's required to defend at least twice per month and on (you guessed it)
pay-per-views. Lucky for him, all defenses have a 10 minute time limit, so he has ample opportunity to weasel his way into a time limit draw.
5.4 - Extreme Title: Taking advantage of the Explosion of the extreme style worldwide, IWWC established an 'Extreme' division; and this is it's ultimate prize. The holder of this title can lay the claim to being the toughest guy around, but there will be plenty of other tough guys with chairs, barbed wire, and so forth, to take that claim and shove it sideways up... you get the idea. It's defended whenever Cooper says it is.
5.5 - Light Heavyweight Title: Limited to wrestlers 220 pounds and under. Probably the most exciting division in the IWWC, the Light
Heavyweight Title is known to change hands often. It is to be defended at least once a month, and usually more often that that.
5.6 - World Tag Team Titles: The only prize for tag teams in the IWWC, which means a lot of competition for one goal. These belts are defended under the same rules as the World Title. Wrestlers are allowed to hold this title and a singles title simultaneously.
================================================== = 6. What are the requirements for the fed? ================================================== = Ah, yes, this is where the rubber meets the road. There are just a few basic areas that you'll need to know about if you handle in
IWWC. 6.1 - Roleplaying Yes, we are a booked fed, so roleplaying is very important here. Unlike most feds, though, we don't require you to throw out 2-plus roleplays per week. As we all know, that can get hard, it can get old, and it can drive you to retirement. Generally, I'd like to see one roleplay per week from everyone, if they have a match or not. I see roleplaying as a great way to develop your character, to get him over, to show us what he's really about.
Swearing was covered already, but as a reminder--if you don't hear it on TV, it will be censored. Of course, I'd appreciate it if you'd censor it out yourselves, which would save me loads of time. And also, don't go overboard with it. I don't want to read something that sounds like an excerpt from a Robert DeNiro flick. :)
Also, I'm not overly picky on formats. Just make it readable and it will be fine. And by readable, that means good spelling, grammer, and punctuation. And I'm just sure I have goofed up on all three counts somewhere in this FAQ. :)
6.2 - Strats Strats are not required, but much appreciated. You'll have a lot of flexibility with your strats; there is not set format you have to use, no issues you have to address. Be forewarned, strats may have some weight in the booking decision. So if in doubt, strat!
6.3 - Participation If you don't remember anything else from this FAQ (which you probably won't), remember this--participation is KEY in the IWWC.
Hey, that rhymed! :) Seriously, there's nothing that is more frustrating to a fed-head than to have someone blow the doors down, get ready for a big push and involvement in a key feud, and then disappear for no reason. Please, if you join, be ready to participate. And by that I mean - 1)
roleplaying consistently, 2) getting involved in angle development, 3) offering ideas and suggestions for the improvement of the fed, 4) helping out with anything that needs it. I do understand that many of you may not have the time to do things like write matches, which is OK. But if you do have the time, your help will be GREATLY appreciated. However, there is no reason a handler can't get involved in #1 and #2 above. In order to suceed in
IWWC, you'll have to do those two things. ================================================== = 7. What is your booking system? ================================================== = We're a booked fed, closed flash system. The point of IWWC is for us (meaning myself and the handlers) to develop solid angles that are creative, innovative, and enjoyable. In achieving this, it may mean that the best roleplay doesn't always win. But, please bear in mind, I will be as fair as humanly possible in booking decisions.
There is no "magic formula" for booking. It is a combination of roleplays, strats, participation, angle development, and heat. As in any booked fed, winners and losers are based on subjective decisions made by whoever the booker may be. And in most booked feds, someone will not be happy if they lose a match they think they should win. If you ever, ever, eeeeevvvvveeeerrrr.. feel that way, let me know, but DO NOT cry or complain. I will explain my decision, and that is the end of it. You can and will be kicked out of the league for excessive whining... to my face or behind my back, because I will find out.
