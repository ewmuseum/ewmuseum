Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Pro Wrestling Entertainment ran out of a subdomain and wrote its own name as P\/\/E. Its rulebook opens by saying what it isn't: “The PWE is not like the WWE, we didnt want to copy the WWE name so we made our own name called the PWE, which stands for Pro Wrestling Entertainment.” Original wrestlers only, one character to a handler, and an attack fed capped at two attacks a card — “That means ‘NO KILLING’, seriously Injury etc...etc...”

January 2003 was a relaunch rather than a beginning: its own news calls New Years Resolution “the first Ever Pay Per View in the return,” and the Shockwave after it “the first ever, since the PWE is back.” The pay-per-view ran from Madison Square Gardens on 25 January and crowned three championships in a night. Scott Curtis came down a ladder with the Undisputed Championship; the fed's own president Adam Van Dam took the Entertainment Championship in a Tables, Ladders and Chairs match against a man he then fired on the microphone while he was still lying in the ring; Andre' Sanders took the Hardcore Championship. The tag team belt was hung on the Champions page with VACANT written where the champions' names go, and it was still there in July.

Everything it did was priced. The point system paid +7 for the Undisputed Title and +3 for roleplay of the week, and charged −4 for losing either. A Damage Factor System ran from one to ten, and a wrestler graded nine or ten who chose to wrestle anyway lost automatically. The results themselves read as long move-by-move sequences with two commentators dropped in over the top — and one week the president wrote them himself and said so: “There is no commintation in the match parts..sorry but I didnt want to waste anymore time.”

It was a Primetime Central fed, and it announced its own promotion inside the alliance from the middle of the ring: “We use to be a Public Access Federation in Primetime Central but as of last Monday Night we were moved up to Cable Access Fed.” In April 2003 it sent Scott Curtis and Adam Brown to the alliance's own New Era supercard; both were pinned moments after entering the Global Title match.

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. Shockwave had moved from Thursday to Saturday by April, the fed was running a tournament for shots at the Undisputed Championship, and its own promo board had reached twenty-one numbered roleplays. The last of them is dated 18 April 2003 and filed twice, as #20 and again as #21; the Shockwave they were written for was never captured. The Stables and Tag Teams pages were still empty tables when the archive last looked in July.

Storylines

The arcs below are recovered from a full read of all fifteen held documents and all three surviving roleplays.

New Years Resolution's three champions and the week after Written by AI, 2026

PWE crowns three of its own four championships in one night at Madison Square Gardens. Scott Curtis climbs down a four-man ladder match with the Undisputed Championship after Adam Van Dam, the fed's own president, knocks Micheal Flash through a table and then helps Curtis up the ramp with the belt. Van Dam wins the Entertainment Championship himself in a Tables, Ladders and Chairs match against Carl, who had jumped him backstage before the bell, and fires Carl on the microphone while he is still lying in the ring. Andre' Sanders beats Justice Paine for the Hardcore Championship in a match that ends with a folding chair closed around Paine's knee. Five days later on Thursday Night Shockwave, Lynx, the man Curtis beat for the belt, ambushes him backstage and puts his head through a vending machine, then asks for an inferno match at the next pay-per-view; the surviving record never answers him. The same Shockwave closes with Van Dam losing to Lynx by disqualification after punching out the referee, and with a debuting Johnny Webb, fresh off beating Jimmy Lopez in his own first match, running in to hit Van Dam with his own finisher and stare down Lynx — telling the champion and the president in the same breath that he is coming for both of them.

Vikki Addison's tournament run and a Shockwave that never aired Written by AI, 2026

By April 2003 PWE is running what its own advertising calls the undisputed tourney. Vikki Addison, billing herself the Dark Angel of PWE, beats UnKnown Dragon and earns a Hell in a Cell match against Scott Curtis for the Undisputed Championship on the Shockwave of 19 April 2003, writing the same roleplay for it twice, filed by the fed's own promo board as both #20 and #21. The same card is billed for a three-way between The Russian Hammer, Ultimo Guerrera and Keshia Williams, arriving in PWE billed its hottest new addition; Williams spends her own roleplay of 16 April picking apart the Russian Hammer's three billed names and his claimed in-fiction real name before promising to beat him regardless. The show itself, already postponed once from 12 April, was never captured — Shockwave had moved from Thursday to Saturday by then, and nothing in the surviving record says whether the Hell in a Cell, the three-way, or the tournament they both belonged to were ever settled.
