AWC — Card

Fresh!, 17 May 2005

17 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Tonight’s feed from the Olympic Arena begins with a close-up shot of a sign reading the name of the venue. We now zoom out to see a man enter the building. He has long blonde hair and is wearing a navy blue tank top. In his right hand is a championship belt that certainly does not belong to AWC – the camera zooms in to reveal that it reads “IwE Championship”. The unknown wrestler is definitely looking worried as he walks over towards the reception desk Receptionist: Samuel Livingston? You’re on in fifteen minutes. Livingston nods, but he looks nervous and is sweating as he is seen walking along the corridor. He is muttering to himself. Samuel Livingston: C’mon Sam, you’re a sure thing. All you got to do is go out there and do what you do every week. Win. Livingston closes his eyes. Samuel Livingston: Please Sam, do it for yourself, you said so yourself, you want recognition. Livingston opens his eyes and rolls his open hand into a fist. Samuel Livingston: Wait, what am I saying? Of course I am going to win. I can do this. I know I can! Samuel smirks as the feed switches to our introduction. The screen goes to black then fizzles to life suddenly, the letters “AWC” flashing across in a brilliant white light. A moment of silence dominates, then, backed by “Way Away” by Yellowcard, images of AWC’s superstars appear, one after the other. The guitar plays softly first, then goes straight into a strong, powerful riff. I think I’m breaking out I’m gonna leave you now There’s nothing for me here It’s all the same Transatlantic Champion Alexander Strider proudly holds his newly won championship up for the masses to see at Zero To Hero, as he is being escorted out of the building by police officers. The shot zooms in on Strider’s wicked smile, as the images start to fizzle away. A pulsating white light continually lights up the screen, as shots of many AWC superstars in action are shown. And even though I know That everything might go Go downhill from here I’m not afraid A quick collection of images flash across the screen as the song moves into its chorus. Hate sending Kris Krimzon flying through a row of empty chairs; James Brunt opening his suit to reveal the Frontier, Relentless and Livewire title belts; Ralph Richards emerging from Maddy Estelle’s chest. These images collide suddenly, and the song bursts into its lively chorus, as the AWC logo whizzes across the screen. Way away, away from here I’ll be Away, away, away, so you can see How it feels to be alone and not believe Feels to be alone and not believe Anything The pyros explode into the sky, and AWC Fresh! is on the air. The thousands packed into the Olympic Arena scream in unison as “Way Away” plays with a fury. The camera twirls around the ring and focuses in on the AWC logo in the centre of the ring. We pan over to ringside, while the fans continue cheering, to ring announcers Truth Waters and George Cassidy. George Cassidy: Welcome to Fresh! We’re coming live from the packed Olympic Arena in Atlanta, Georgia, and you know what Truth, I think Harber could have sold out an arena twice this size tonight! Truth Waters: Damn straight Cassidy! AWC has hit the big time for sure. Every one of the six thousand tickets available for tonight has been sold. We switch between several camera shots as the announcers speak: of a fan-created sign that reads “FREDROCK~! > MADDY”; of a group of boisterous youths jumping up and down near the front; of the long queue to the hot dog stand. George Cassidy: That’s right. Well, it’s not really surprising, because tonight we will see the first ever defence of Alexander Strider’s Transatlantic title! He goes one-on-one in the ring with Pierce Lavelle. Truth Waters: After losing a marathon of a match to Alex Strider at Zero To Hero, Lavelle will be looking to redeem himself. George Cassidy: We also have a number of debutants on show tonight in the first two matches, and the veteran Pact will be in action. There is plenty to look forward to on Fresh!, and debutant Crimson O’Malec is already in the ring for our opening match... James Brunt: The following is a singles match. Introducing first, weighing in at 165 pounds... CRIMSON O’MALEC! The short and extremely light (for a wrestler) O’Malec shakes himself into action in the ring. Truth Waters: I do worry, you know. He’s tiny, and he doesn’t look up for this. Let’s hope he doesn’t pussy out. You know you're not a strong man... And you're just about to cry... Hang on... Haaaaang on... It's alllllllright. It's all...right "Carnival" by Our Lady Peace echoes throughout the arena as Samuel Livingston comes out through the curtain, with a look of extreme confidence written all over his face. He walks down the ramp with a smile on his face. James Brunt: And his opponent, from Miami, Florida, weighing in at 201 pounds... SAMUEL LIVINGSTON! Livingston receives a small appreciative pop as he walks down to the ring. He climbs underneath the bottom rope, raising his arms up in the air and smirking. Truth Waters: Both wrestlers in the ring now… George Cassidy: This match is really going to be allowed to go on? Crimson O’Malec is so small! It’s like a grown man against a middle-schooler out there! Truth Waters: It certainly appears that way. Why do we have two Krimzon/Crimsons now, anyway? George Cassidy: Search me. It’s... er... a common name? Samuel Livingston smiles while laughing, pointing a firm finger at Crimson O’Malec, asking the referee to verify that this is, indeed, his opponent. Referee Aaron Davies nods, and waves his hand, bouncing around the ring, announcing for the bell. Truth Waters: Livingston shakes his head. I think he might be taking the cruiserweight extremely lightly. George Cassidy: Who cares? He looks like he weighs a buck and a quarter. Who booked this? P.T. Barnum? Livingston walks over to O’Malec, and tries motions toward the back, telling the fiery redhead to just leave before he gets himself hurt. O’Malec responds by chopping Livingston across the chest hard. Livingston reacts surprised, the crowd playing on the cop with a loud “Whoooo!” Truth Waters: O’Malec is clearly ready. Livingston still wants to give his opponent a chance to beat a hasty retreat. Livingston, angered from the chop, points to the back, giving one more warning. Crimson does a standing foot stomp, kicking hard down onto Livingston’s booted foot. Livingston grabs his injured foot, and bounces in the ring. Truth Waters: You’re in there to wrestle. Livingston gave O’Malec enough chances to back down. He’s going to want to end this before he gets played for a fool. Crimson O’Malec bounces off the opposite ropes, boomeranging back with a low drop kick to the leg Livingston was supporting himself with. Livingston goes down in a heap. O’Malec makes a quick cursory cover… ONE! And Livingston BENCH PRESSES O’Malec cleanly off of him, sending him flying backwards. George Cassidy: He’s got no weight, what’s he gonna do? He’s going to have to figure out a better way to pin his opponent than a standard cover. Like maybe using sandbags or something. Samuel Livingston stands up, and as Crimson O’Malec rises, clubs him in the chest with a forearm. Crimson sprawls backwards, and gets entangled in the ropes. Freeing himself, he is pulled quickly in a tie-up with Sam Livingston… OVERHEAD VERTICAL SUPLEX! Truth Waters: Textbook vertical suplex there. When you don’t have much muscle mass, that can be a punishing move. Fortunately for O’Malec, he appears to do the requisite weight training. Despite his height disadvantage, O’Malec isn’t to be completely counted out of this contest. George Cassidy: That move would have a killed a normal twelve-year-old, but Crimson O’Malec still appears to be breathing. Yeah, you’re right. He is a little big for a teenager. He must have spent a lot of time picking potatoes out of the ground by hand. You get a strong back from that. Truth Waters: He’s not twelve. Livingston makes a cover on O’Malec, seeking to end the match before injuring the much smaller man. ONE! Fiery kick out, and O’Malec is visibly getting more and more pumped up from all the action. George Cassidy: What? Truth Waters: He isn’t twelve years old, Cassidy. O’Malec gets up fast, and bounces off the ropes again, catching Livingston as he runs back with a running enzugiri back-brain kick. Livingston goes down to the mat, and Crimson employs a follow-up spinning leg drop across Samuel Livingston’s neck. George Cassidy: Since when did AWC include midget wrestling then? Truth Waters: He’s not a midget, either, Cassidy! Damn it, O’Malec happens to be the son of a wrestler. He’s trying to get his act together, and he’s out here trying to put on a match. What can’t you understand about that? Have you even noticed the momentum shift in the ring right now? George Cassidy: This reminds me of that saying: life’s short and hard, like a bodybuilding elf. Truth Waters: I never heard that before, and I seriously doubt anyone else has either. George Cassidy: You can’t tell me that doesn’t make sense though. I mean, look at O’Malec. He’s short, and hard. Life’s short and hard. Truth Waters: I get it, I get it! Crimson O’Malec putting on a show of offense here, and the crowd beginning to get behind this contest right now. Livingston kicks out as O’Malec was beginning to pull himself over for a quick cover. Samuel pulls himself up, chopping hard across Crimson’s chest. Irish whip to follow up, and Crimson O’Malec can’t stop the momentum as he rebounds into Livingston… where Livingston deftly flips Crimson O’Malec up and around with a nasty tilt-a-whirl backbreaker. Truth Waters: Tilt-a-whirl backbreaker there, and O’Malec is doubled over on the mat in pain. George Cassidy: He probably broke his spine. His body might not be able to handle all this pressure, you know, not even having gone through a single growth spurt yet. Truth Waters: You’re grating on my nerves here. Crimson O’Malec is a grown man, already. George Cassidy: Sure. Livingston pulls O’Malec to his feet, only to send him back down with a dropkick. Livingston rolls behind the dazed O’Malec, applying a sleeper hold. Referee Davies checks him, and he is holding on, staying lucid out of sheer tenacity. George Cassidy: He’s like a little Jack Russell terrier, isn’t he? Truth Waters: Samuel Livingston is really giving hell to the Irishman. Livingston is quite a skilled wrestler, and though young, is showing off his experience by taking a break from tossing O’Malec around and putting more wear on his opponent. Livingston pulls back hard on O’Malec, keeping him tightly under wraps. O’Malec slowly begins to lose consciousness, though fighting with all his might to keep losing it at bay. Slowly, he pulls himself toward the ropes, fighting a war with Livingston over every inch. Truth Waters: He’s getting closer to breaking the hold now. If he can break out he still might have a chance to put Livingston away tonight. George Cassidy: He’s got about as much chance at beating Samuel Livingston as you do of becoming a golf pro. Livingston can’t hang on to the hold any longer, as O’Malec finally reaches the ropes with the edges of his fingers. Davies makes Livingston release the hold. O’Malec takes the opportunity to headbutt Livingston in the gut. A standing crucifix takedown, and Livingston is laid up on the middle of the ring, holding his neck. Truth Waters: Solid take-down there by the man known as “The Poet”. What are you talking about, my golf game? I happen to be an excellent golfer. Why, just last week I golfed a sixty-five! O’Malec bounds up a near turnbuckle. Posing at the top, O’Malec leaps off, aiming a top rope overhead leg drop right at Livingston’s neck… but finds nobody home! Livingston deftly rolls out of the way, leaving Crimson with nothing to break his fall against. Truth Waters: Astonishing! Crimson O’Malec misses a top-rope leg drop, and the results are simply devastating! George Cassidy: That’s a great score, for a whole round. Where did you golf a sixty-five? The front nine, or the back nine? Samuel Livingston picks up Crimson O’Malec, kicking him in the gut. As O’Malec doubles over, Livingston reverses into a rear waist lock. Truth Waters: … Livingston calls out to the crowd, who has been eating up the display of sportsmanship from these two wrestlers, signaling for his finisher. Truth Waters: The front. I didn’t play the last nine holes. George Cassidy: HA! I knew it! Livingston heaves O’Malec overhead with a German suplex. As if the impact to his head and shoulders wasn’t enough, O’Malec converts the pinning hold into a submission, rolling O’Malec over and applying a step-over toehold submission. George Cassidy: Wow! What a painful hold that is. Truth Waters: I don’t know if O’Malec will be able to hold out long now. George Cassidy: Why not? You thought he had a good shot just a moment ago. Truth Waters: Livingston has been wrestling a great match, it was up in the air until a moment ago. I stand by what I said, Cassidy. O’Malec grimaces in pain, but he doesn’t have anything left in the tank and within just a few seconds he is tapping out. George Cassidy: And it’s over! The midget twelve-year-old taps out to the move known as Living Long. James Brunt: The winner... SAMUEL LIVINGSTON! Truth Waters: O'Malec just didn’t have what it takes to compete with Samuel Livingston, who looks like a promising newbie. George Cassidy: Who does he look like? Truth Waters: No-one... George Cassidy: You said he looked like a promising newbie. Truth Waters: Yes... George Cassidy: Which one? Silas Grief? Eddie Sanders? I don’t think he looks like any of the new guys. Truth Waters: Oh, go away. Alexander Strider is in his locker room, busy stretching out his hamstrings, dressed in his black ring tights and full regalia. Strider is coolly working through his routine when there is a knock on the door. Chris Ashton: Knock, knock, any champions home? Christopher Ashton walks in without waiting to hear “come in”. Strider stops stretching and holds out a defensive hand, grimacing, his eyes narrowing on the man standing before him. Alex Strider: You hold it right there. I want to be fresh for the main event, but I am not above warming up by stomping a hole in you, if that’s what you came here for. Chris Ashton: Be cool, Strider, cool. I just came down because after Zero To Hero I didn’t get to say congratulations. So, congratulations! Ashton extends his hand, looking for a congratulatory handshake. Strider sniffs, glancing down at the hand. Alex Strider: Let’s not waste my time, o.k.? Let’s “pretend” I’m a busy man. What do you really want? Chris Ashton: Strider, I’m hurt that you would think that I’d be here out of anything but brotherly concern and camaraderie and the possible need for you to do me a favor later on. But I digress. While I was browsing in the local newspaper, the Atlanta Journal-Constitution, I noticed something interesting. Alex Strider: I wouldn’t wipe my ass with that newspaper. You’ve got thirty seconds. Make them count. Chris Ashton: I felt the same way – about wiping my ass... yeah – especially after I read this article. And I quote: Chris-Ash clears his throat; Strider glances at the wall clock, as he is indeed timing Ashton. Chris Ashton: “I cannot believe that Alex Strider is allowed to parade around wearing the Transatlantic championship belt. It’s a disgrace not only to Atlantic Wrestling Club, but all of wrestling in general.” Ashton stops reading and looks up to see if he has garnered Strider’s attention. “The Mongoose” is still watching the clock, seemingly unconcerned with the news. Alex Strider: Fifteen seconds. Chris Ashton: “If it were up to me, he'd be thrown out of the fed immediately before he tarnishes the name of AWC permanently. Pearl should have stripped Strider of that belt once he got wind of Strider killing…” Strider’s attention is turned on Ashton, who grins demonically while reading, as if taking pleasure in the article. Chris Ashton: “…that innocent young woman.” Strider grabs the paper, but Ashton refuses to let go. The two play tug of war for a little bit. Alex Strider: What in the name of hell? Let me see that, give that to me. Chris Ashton: Hey, sure thing, champ. I’m just doing my best to be neighborly. Boy, hehe, I know I really wouldn’t want to be caught dead – haha – opening that can of worms. Ashton tosses the paper across. Strider scans it. Alex Strider: Pierce Lavelle wrote that? That’s just great. I’ve got a rookie tossing my name around like it doesn’t mean anything. Chris Ashton: And worst of all, it’s not his business who you kill, is it? Strider steps inside Ashton’s ‘bubble of personal space’, gritting his teeth; Chris-Ash’s grin fades a little as he locks eyes with Strider. Alex Strider: You watch your mouth, Ashton. I may be a lot of things, but I’m no murderer. I don’t usually have this thin skin, but I shouldn’t even be fighting this kid. Chris Ashton: Hey now, don’t kill... the messenger. Ashton’s grin resurfaces. Strider eyes Ashton suspiciously. Alex Strider: Oh, and one more thing. If I find out that he didn’t really say this, I’m coming to find you, Chris. So don’t go thinking that I’m going to work out all my aggression on Pierce Lavelle in that ring. Right about now I’ve got plenty to be angry about. Chris Ashton: Hey, would I lie to you? Chris-Ash leaves Strider, chuckling. The camera pans up to the face of Transatlantic champion, Alexander Strider; the scene end with him clutching the newspaper, seething. The feed zooms away from the ring... away from the Olympic Arena... away from Atlanta... away from the United States of America... away from the world entirely. Into outer space we fly, and a rapid swivel leaves us advancing quickly on a huge ball of fire that comes to fill the screen. ”The sun does not shine for a few trees and flowers, but for the wide world's joy.” – Henry Ward Beecher “If I had to choose a religion, the sun as the universal giver of life would be my god.” – Napoleon Bonaparte “One day, for each of us, the sun will go down for the last, last time.” – James A. Baldwin “There falls no shadow where there shines no sun.” – Hilaire Belloc “If you want a place in the sun, you've got to put up with a few blisters.” – Abigail Van Buren Oblivious to our spectatorship, the Sun continues its storm of silence: hissing, spitting, roaring inside as its never-ending dignity prevails. We return to the backstage area where we are greeted by the young superstar, Pierce Lavelle, who is only dressed in a white towel. Whilst he is standing in his boxers, Sarah Kennedy enters and gets a little bit of a shock. She continues to stand in the same spot and glance at the muscular body of Lavelle, who, obviously embarrassed by the situation, pulls up his wrestling trunks and straps on a pair of white wrist bands. The fans in the arena are coming to life and some begin to chant Lavelle’s name, something he is not used to hearing. Sarah Kennedy: I’m sorry for, er, interrupting... Pierce Lavelle: Erm, yeah it's fine. I wasn't doing anything, I swear! Sarah Kennedy: (smiling) That’s OK; I’m sure you weren’t. Pierce Lavelle: That’s gross, I’d never... The interviewer raises an eyebrow. Sarah Kennedy: OK. So... are you big for toni – I mean, are you ready for tonight's match? Sarah Kennedy's face becomes just a little flushed. The fans in the background are laughing. Pierce Lavelle: (grinning) Am I big about tonight's match? Well Sarah I'm not big about tonight's match, more looking forward to my second chance. The odds are stacked against me though, as I have to face a big opponent. Kennedy frowns. Sarah Kennedy: But Pierce, Alex Strider and yourself are about the same height... Lavelle laughs. Pierce Lavelle: I mean in that Strider is a big shot, Transatlantic champion, you know what I mean? I’m still inexperienced at this level, but I will give it my best shot. The interviewer nods. Sarah Kennedy: I have come with a message from Pearl. You have been chosen to take photos with some of the fans that are here at the moment, backstage. Sarah Kennedy hands Lavelle a note; she is quickly out of the room still embarrassed by her word fumbling and embarrassing inference. Lavelle smiles to himself and straps on his boots, keeping a cocky grin on his face. Pierce Lavelle: Am I big...? “Will You Smile Again?” by …And You Will Know Us By The Trail Of The Dead begins to play. The house lights dim, and the crowd begins to boo outright. A video package displaying Alex Strider’s most recent high spots in the Atlantic Wrestling Club begins to play, showing his technical and submission-based in-ring prowess. Alexander Strider enters as a shadow, backlit by flaming red lights. Holding a microphone in one hand and the Transatlantic championship belt in the other, Strider walks down to the ring, sneering at the crowd who jeers continuously at him. Strider raises his Transatlantic Championship belt high in the air, deaf to the piercing roar of negativity surrounding him. Walking toward the ring, Strider stops and looks at a fan who defiantly holds up a sign at him. The sign reads: ALEX STRIDER = KILLER. Strider grabs the sign and rips it in half, tossing the pieces aside. Strider defends himself, holding the Transatlantic championship up and pointing at it. The fans let loose as they decry the name of the champion. Visibly angry, Strider picks up the pace and heads into the ring, gritting his teeth the whole way. George Cassidy: It really seems to bother Strider that the crowd doesn’t like him. Alex Strider: Ohhh boy. I guess no matter what you try to say, or what you try to do to convince people of the truth… people are ultimately going to say and do what they want. Truth Waters: Now, what’s he talking about here? Is he talking about Lavelle? George Cassidy: Shush up, and we’ll find out. Alex Strider: I really can’t believe it. Here I stand, AWC’s FIRST Transatlantic champion, and my very first trip to Atlanta, and what kind of a welcome do I get? I get you backwoods rednecks calling me a KILLER! This is unacceptable! George Cassidy: This guy’s getting a bum rap. It is unacceptable. Truth Waters: Hey now! We don’t know the facts on his personal dealings either way. The fact is, Strider’s a P.O.S. who EARNED the Atlantic Wrestling Club’s fans hatred with his OWN actions. Alex Strider: First things first: you are all very, very wrong, and you’re behaving very, very cruelly towards me! I deserve respect! I should be cheered! I am not like those losers in the back! I went from ZERO TO HERO in one night! I am the Transatlantic champion! That is something that you can’t take away from me with your jealous boos, or your poorly spelled handwritten signs! The crowd begins to jeer even louder, not taking Strider’s berating lightly. Strider pauses for a second, and someone throws a 36 ounce coke in the ring, ice and soda-pop spilling across the mat. Alex Strider: This is exactly the kind of behavior I expect from Georgians. Say what you want, do what you will. I am not a killer, and I will be vindicated. You people are booing me, and what? Later on tonight, when I STAR in the main-event match, you’ll what, cheer Pierce Lavelle? The crowd pops at the mention of the young up-and-coming AWC star. Strider stops, placing his tongue in his cheek as he shakes his head in disgust. Truth Waters: After Lavelle’s big win in last week’s main event, you’re damn right people will cheer for him. Alex Strider: Typical! You condemn me, and falsely count me as a murderer, while you LOVE Lavelle for no reason! And the IRONY of it all is THREE WEEKS ago, when I became champion of the entire Transatlantic region, all Lavelle managed to do was injure another man through sheer recklessness, yet you cast a blind eye to that! Now, if he hurt Sektor on purpose, that might even be something to respect. But come on, it’s not like he was even showing off. It was an accident! Where, I ask you… is the justice? The crowd continues to admonish Strider with howls and jeering. Truth Waters: What is he talking about? This is professional wrestling, sometimes people get hurt. If you’re too scared, stay outta the ring! George Cassidy: You don’t think he’s got a valid point there? Strider hasn’t injured anyone yet. Lavelle’s come a lot closer to killing anybody. Truth Waters: You both are really reaching for it there. Alex Strider: I can tell you where there surely isn’t any justice… in the media! Your Pierce Lavelle wasn’t man enough to ask me about why I was escorted by police after Zero To Hero, but that sure didn’t stop him from going to the local papers here and running his mouth. And I quote: “Alexander Strider shouldn’t be wrestling for AWC, and like all killers, should be locked in jail…” The crowd cheers suddenly, and Strider is forced to stop reading, unsettled at his unpopularity. Alex Strider: “I, Pierce Lavelle, am going to personally make sure Alex Strider’s time in AWC is a short, painful one. I may be young, but I’m just reckless enough to end his career early. In the name of all that’s good, I will put a stop to Strider’s reign as Transatlantic champion.” The crowd comes back with an even bigger pop this time. Strider steps back, eyes wide with shock. Alex Strider: I knew the people of Atlanta were uneducated, but I never took you for bloodthirsty savages! This is my career we’re talking about! This is why I demand… I demand “Pearl” Harber to stop this match before it starts. I cannot risk my promising future on – The massive crowd inside the Olympic Arena boos again, disapproving of Strider’s clear attempt at weaselling out of a match with Lavelle. The drum roll begins, as the lights around the arena synchronizes with the beat. “Give In” by The Bravery begins to play, as Lavelle stands on the top ramp. Truth Waters: Ohh, here’s Lavelle! The crowd pops, and Lavelle cuts Strider off, speaking into his own microphone as he walks down to the ring. Pierce Lavelle: Okay, quiet! I have come out here, Strider, because you’ve falsely accused me of something I haven't done. See Strider, I know what this is about. I have a feeling that you’ve been tricked. I don’t care one way or the other about you. The only thing that matters to me is that I’ve got a title shot tonight. I’ve earned it, Strider; no matter how much you deny it, you can’t stop it. The crowd begins to chant Lavelle's name, as Strider seems more infuriated by the minute. His jaws tighten as the arena comes to life with chants for the young superstar. Pierce Lavelle: I don't mean to embarrass you tonight in front of all your admiring fans... but it’s not my policy to speak to the media about other people’s problems. I think maybe it was in Ashton’s interest to plant a note and dupe you. But hey… if it fires you up so much, maybe we can just go at it right now? Alex Strider: Don’t push me, Lavelle. I don’t think you deserve a title shot, and as soon as I can get Pearl out here I’m sure he’ll agree that you’re too careless for me to wrestle and risk injury against. Pierce Lavelle: So you won the Zero To Hero tournament, that doesn't make you a 'hero' or somebody respectable enough for a champion, it just makes you a winner. The fans see something they like and stick to it. Should I ask one of the fine ladies backstage to bring you a packet of tissues? Strider act like a man. You are the Transatlantic champion, but you've got to earn the respect of the people, you can’t demand it! Truth Waters: A strong point there made by Pierce Lavelle. I admire his courage to come out in front of all these fans, and to speak against Strider. This superstar after all is new to this business. He’s not a pussy like others. George Cassidy: You’ve got to be kidding me! What if he does hurt somebody else? It’s not like we have an endless supply of wrestlers he can break waiting for their shots in the back! Truth Waters: I’ve said it before: if you aren’t tough enough to be wrestling, you shouldn’t be out here. Strider’s just acting the fool, he’s not worried about getting injured, he’s worried about losing his title! Alex Strider: Well, Pierce, I think it’s just great you decided to come out here, on my time and tell me what you think. It doesn’t matter that you didn’t talk to the reporter. The damage is done. And if what you really want is to throw down right now, then I’d hate to leave you disappointed… Strider throws down his belt and his microphone, raising his fists. Lavelle does the same, and they both step in towards each other as the crowd pops madly. George Cassidy: Here it goes! Strider’s going to unleash hell! "Bohemian Like You" by The Dandy Warhols begins to play, and the fans know what comes next. Strider and Lavelle stop, Strider raising his hands up, and pointing back towards the entrance. David “Pearl” Harber comes out, smiling widely. Noticing that Strider and Lavelle aren’t going to start a fight, he starts shaking fans’ hands. After a moment, he begins to speak. Pearl: Now, hold on just a minute you two. The people came here tonight to see a legitimate main event match; let’s not ruin it with a brawl. George Cassidy: Aww, come on! What’s it matter if they duke it out now or later? Pearl: Secondly, as far as you not having to defend your title tonight, Strider… Nice try, but not good enough. The fans cheer at the news of the main event continuing. Strider leans on the ropes in frustration, voicing his opposition. Pearl: You aren’t in any more danger wrestling Lavelle than anyone else. If you want to be sure you don’t want to get injured, then you should have found a different line of work. The match goes on as planned tonight. I wish the best of luck to you both, and we’ll see you again in the main event. Bowing slightly, Pearl backs out, waving to the fans in the arena. Pierce Lavelle smiles at Strider, and leaves the champion alone in the ring. Truth Waters: Now maybe Lavelle can go backstage and redeem himself with the ladies. George Cassidy: Redeem himself? Truth Waters: Yes – that little incident earlier with Sarah Kennedy. George Cassidy: Oh yeah, I almost forgot about that. Well this main event has become somewhat heated by tonight's debate between our champion and his opponent. Truth Waters: Undoubtedly, tonight’s Fresh! has been quite a spectacle so far. Perhaps Lavelle is getting desperate and having to imitate his opponent. Michael Ryan begins to count Strider out, masking his obvious glee under a thoroughly professional face. ONE! TWO! THREE! FOUR! Truth Waters: Strider seemed to land badly... Pierce Lavelle now helps himself up and takes the opportunity for a rest as the count continues. FIVE! SIX! SEVEN! EIGHT! Lavelle slides out of the ring, resetting the count, and grabs Strider by the legs. George Cassidy: Lavelle finally woke up to the fact that a count-out would let Strider keep his championship belt. Truth Waters: I hadn’t even thought of that. What a pussy thing to do. Pierce Lavelle falls back, executing the catapult to send Strider full speed into the steel ringpost! ONE! George Cassidy: Dear Lord! Now that’s not fair! Truth Waters: Strider face-first into the ringpost! And now he is bleeding... what’s he doing here?! TWO! Blood gushes out from Strider's head as he staggers forward and collapses on the announcers’ table. Lavelle makes his way over to the champion and proceeds to hammer away at his opponent. THREE! George Cassidy: Lavelle’s taking full advantage of Strider being dazed. He should let the chamoion recover. It’s the least “The Mongoose” deserves, for all he does for AWC, but no... Truth Waters: Oh, you really do sound tired. George Cassidy: Well who can blame me? Pierre is tired! Truth Waters: Who’s Pierre? George Cassidy: Strangely enough, I don’t know. Lavelle now gets up on the table. Truth Waters: Oh, no. I don’t like what’s coming... FOUR! Truth Waters and George Cassidy back off as Lavelle lifts Strider, using every last ounce of strength, and places him on his shoulders in a sitting position. George Cassidy: It’s gonna be a POWERBOMB! He stalls for a little bit to get a good grip then delivers a devastating powerbomb right through the table! Truth Waters: THROUGH OUR TABLE! What in the hell?! Immense power channelled by Pierce Lavelle! George Cassidy: Strider is... dead! Truth Waters: I’m so tempted to say ‘Good riddance’, but I’m afraid I’m too shocked at what I see here! The wreckage from our table is strewn all over the place! George Cassidy: This is going to be one big job for the ring crew... The fans are chanting "Holy shit!" as both competitors are out cold. Michael Ryan leaps out of the ring to check on their condition, abandoning the count. Truth Waters: You know, we may have to call this a no contest... George Cassidy: WAIT! What the hell is this?! A familiar face has leapt out from the crowd... a familiar red face... Truth Waters: HATE! It’s Hate! George Cassidy: What is Hate doing out here? Hate, brushing off the protests of Senior Referee Michael Ryan, pulls Pierce Lavelle roughly up and shoves him with all his might into the apron. Lavelle’s spine crashes against the side of the ring and he goes down face first. Hate now pulls the Transatlantic champion out of the wreckage and delivers the Eleventh Commandment right back into it again! Truth Waters: Hate is just out here to cause havoc! He gave Strider the Eleventh Commandment into the wreckage of our announce table and has left Lavelle prone! George Cassidy: Doesn’t he know when it’s not his turn? Paying no heed to the fans booing him or the commentators criticising him, Hate stomps away at Alex Strider. Pierce Lavelle stands unsteadily and takes a step forwards, but before he can get any further Hate launches himself at him with a jumping spear crashing straight into the side of the ring again! Truth Waters: Leaping spear by Hate and Lavelle’s back must really be in pain now! Crashing into the side of the ring for the second time in quick succession. George Cassidy: Really, what is this guy doing?! Hate now heads back over to the announcers and drags Strider up. A powerful elbow across his bleeding face leaves Strider reeling, and Hate follows him over towards the ring before grabbing his head and propelling it powerfully into the ringpost! Strider goes down on his back. Truth Waters: Someone stop this! Hate, without missing a beat, leaps up onto the apron and then climbs the turnbuckle. Savouring the moment as he looks out at the crowd, he slowly turns around to face the two announcers. Looking right at Truth and George, he sprouts a sadistic grin and leaps off, driving his body weight into Alex Strider’s chest! George Cassidy: DEATH FROM ABOVE! Truth Waters: From a good twelve feet up! George Cassidy: Every bone in Strider’s body was at risk there! Someone put a halt to this! Hate is a maniac! Belatedly, Taz Yorke and Butch Radder rush out from backstage, but they cannot prevent Hate from unleashing yet more carnage: his devastating White Man’s Burden finisher is the next punishment for Pierce Lavelle! Truth Waters: Oh, this needs to stop! White Man’s Burden, and Lavelle is BROKEN! Yorke and Radder now muscles their way over to Hate, but a swiping elbow across the face fells Taz Yorke in one, and Hate now launches Butch Radder overhead with a spectacularly brutal release belly-to-belly suplex! George Cassidy: Butch Radder is no lightweight, and Hate just tossed him overhead like a gum wrapper! Truth Waters: Hate’s not exactly a heavyweight himself. He possesses superhuman power in those muscles... and how easily power can turn to evil... Taz Yorke, abandoning all sense of decorum, blindsides Hate with a punch, and the temporary lull is enough to allow Yorke to follow up with a knee to the stomach. Strider, Lavelle and Radder are all down. Truth Waters: Enough is enough... Truth Waters throws down his headset and moves over to help, throwing a hard punch into Hate’s ribs. Hate struggles, trying to push away the bodyguard and the tough former boxer, but Taz Yorke suddenly reaches into his pocket and reaches out a long, gleaming metal hook. George Cassidy: What in God’s name is that?! Hate goes strangely flaccid in their arms. He has stopped struggling, fearful for his life. George Cassidy: Surely Taz Yorke was not planning to use that hook implement? Jesus. The people we employ in AWC... Michael Ryan now comes over too, to attempt to escort Hate away from the ring. Hate manages to burst away from them for a second, but it seems his work is done: all he does is bend down to first Lavelle and then Strider and issue a wide grin to each of them. Then, he walks quickly down the ramp to backstage. George Cassidy: I’m speechless... Cassidy tosses Waters’ headset to him as the medical team rushes over to help the three downed men. Truth Waters: That was just... out of control. George Cassidy: Hate is a monster. Truth Waters: Not just that... I mean, Strider before the match, too... the things people can get away with here... we need some structure... some order... some kind of power... otherwise it won’t just be Hate, won’t just be Strider... everyone will feel free to do what they like and we’re going to see some danger... George Cassidy: Well that was nothing short of dangerous as it was. Hate showed scant regard for human life there. And then Taz Yorke with that hook – Truth Waters: Oh, that is no big deal. Yorke carries that everywhere. He treats it more like a pet than a weapon – no, really! I guess Hate thought the guy was actually going to kill him... George Cassidy: You know, I dread to think what his mother was like. The confusion shrouding Fresh! is broken as the clear sounds of “Bohemian Like You” by the Dandy Warhols rock the Olympic Arena, and fans jump to their feet one and all to herald the arrival of the Entertainment Manager, David “Pearl” Harber. He emerges from backstage, but rather than exchanging words with the fans and signing some autographs or slapping some hands, Pearl is not his usual jovial self. Seeing the ringside destruction, he sighs and hastens his pace, taking the microphone almost with reluctance. Pearl: I’m not happy. Not happy at all. What the hell is this? First, Alex Strider won’t even let Pierce Lavelle into the ring for the match, and then he does his absolute best to retire him from the game. And then Hate interferes where he’s not wanted and ruins any hope we had of salvaging a highly-publicised main event. So you two won’t be gracing the main event again for a while. Strider... next week you take on Samuel Livingston. It’s his big chance after a win tonight to see if he can hack it against the champion. Then we’ll see if Hate can actually exercise some self-restraint in his match against Ralph Richards. George Cassidy: Oh, what a blow – it’s mid-card relegation for Hate and Strider. As if that’s gonna be effective. Strider especially knows that he won’t be down there for long – he’s the champion, for crying out loud! Truth Waters: At least Pearl is doing something. Pearl: So, without further ado, I have an announcement to make. James Brunt steps into the ring and takes off his top hat, preparing it by tipping a bag full of small pieces of paper into the upturned hat. Pearl: This week’s Fresh!man is... PACT! The big screen crackles with static, and the words "INCOMING TRANSMISSION: PLEASE STAND BY" scroll across it as red lights fill the gorilla position. A blinding flashbang of pyro, accompanied by the opening strains of “The Dehumanizing Process” by Chimaira, signals the arrival of Pact. He strides down to the ring, a beaming smile across his face. George Cassidy: Pact?! What did he do? Truth Waters: Uh, he beat Troublesome... Pearl: Congratulations Pact. A great victory over Taylor Troublesome. Just step into the ring, please... James Brunt: Pact will now draw a number from the hat! If it is a 1, he will be entered into the Livewire championship match! If it is a 2, he will be entered into the Frontier championship match! If it is a 3, he will receive a shot at the Transatlantic championship! George Cassidy: Seriously, Pact is the Fresh!man? Truth Waters: Oh, just shut up now. Pact climbs into the ring and eagerly plunges his hand into Brunt’s top hat. He draws out a number and hands it to the ring announcer. James Brunt: Ladies and gentlemen, Pact has drawn the number 2! Pact will be entered into the three-way match for the Frontier championship! Pact nods, relatively happy with this result. Pearl: To all the fans out there, I apologise for tonight’s goings-on. The level of violence and blood on display in our main event was excessive, and rest assured that I will have words with the offenders. “Bohemian Like You” plays again, and the ring is cleared. Truth Waters: Pearl really doesn’t seem happy... George Cassidy: You’re right there. I didn’t think it was that bad... you’re always going to get a few bloody matches... Truth Waters: I guess he just wasn’t expecting that from a match with no special stipulations. George Cassidy: Well, that certainly isn’t the end of that! Lavelle will not be satisfied with a no contest ruling. Hate seems hell-bent on destroying all those who get in his way... Truth Waters: And Alexander Strider is doing his utmost to piss us all off. More of the same in seven days!
