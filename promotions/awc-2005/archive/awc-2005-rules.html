AWC Rules

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Matches The outcome of every match is decided by staff judges based on the quality of writing put out that week in each competitor’s roleplay. However, AWC does appreciate that sometimes, in order to progress a story, certain matches may need to be angled, and we have no problem with doing this as long as a member of staff receives written requests from ALL competitors in the match for this to happen. Fresh! For Fresh!, every match carries the stipulation of one roleplay per competitor, except for all team matches, for which the limits are usually one roleplay per team but can be negotiated up to one roleplay per competitor in each team – this is advisable in the event of a match between teams comprised of more than one handler. Each Fresh! show card (held every Tuesday) normally consists of between four and six matches. Fresh!burst, the shortened version of Fresh! held on Sundays before pay-per-view events, features between two and four matches. Pay-per-view events For pay-per-view events, every match carries the stipulation of one roleplay per competitor, except for all team matches, for which the limits are usually one roleplay per team but can be negotiated up to one roleplay per competitor in each team – this is advisable in the event of a match between teams comprised of more than one handler. Each pay-per-view show card (held on Fridays) normally consists of between six and ten matches. In the week after a pay-per-view event, there is no Fresh!. Formatting Action takes place in present-tense prose, with no brackets or other formatting. Commentary and other speech is formatted in this way: -The speaker’s full name appears in bold, with a colon (also emboldened) and a single space separating it from the speech -The speech appears as normal text; i.e. not formatted -There are no inverted commas or any other formatting around speech A line gap must always be left between blocks of action and lines of commentary. Note that we use a single stroke of the space-bar, not a double space, after a full stop (period). Falls and count-outs are formatted in this way: -ONE! TWO! THREE! (in italics; usually with line gaps between the counts) is how a successful pin is counted -ONE! TWO! THREE! FOUR! FIVE! SIX! SEVEN! EIGHT! NINE! TEN! (in italics) shows a successful count-out Note that we make no distinction between counts towards pinfalls and count-outs; these must be made clear in the prose. The ring announcer does not make the infamous call "The following match is for one fall!"; instead the announcement goes: James Brunt: The following is a singles match. Obviously, that varies depending on the match stipulation. This is how the ring announcer calls a wrestler’s arrival at ringside: James Brunt: Introducing first, from Houston, Texas, weighing in at 264 pounds... the AWC Livewire champion... “ROCK HOT” SHAWN VICTORIA! James Brunt: And his opponent, from Miami, Florida, weighing in at 275 pounds... “THE STONE” WAYNE JACKSON! On conclusion of a match: James Brunt: The winner... THE PHANTOM HOOKER! A guideline for when to use the ring announcer: whenever a wrestler’s music plays, there should be an introduction from Brunt. This includes when a wrestler comes out to cut a promo in the ring. Any song lyrics used in ring entrances should be italicised and left-aligned. The two commentators for events on the West side of the Atlantic (i.e. US, Canada, Mexico, the Caribbean) are Truth Waters and George Cassidy. The three commentators for events on the East side of the Atlantic (i.e. Europe) are Dave Kern, Jeff Marx and Steven Smith. The original East Atlantic commentary team was DOOOOOBLIN DAVE~!, Hans Heinemann and Miguel Rodriguez; these lost their positions to the old PCW team of Kern, Marx and Smith in late 2005. More information is available on the Staff page. Every week after the card goes up an assignments thread will be posted on the forums. Handlers are requested to reply to this stating which, if any, matches they will be writing. Although match writing is not compulsory, it is much appreciated by AWC as a whole, and as a general rule we would expect every handler to write a minimum of one match a month. Fresh! matches are due in by 8pm GMT on the day of the show; pay-per-view matches should be sent to us by 11:59pm GMT on the day of the show. Roleplays Notoriously difficult to classify, roleplays are extended pieces of writing, usually of between 1000 and 5000 words (but no minimum or maximum is enforced), in which the writer develops the story of his chosen character. There are many different styles of roleplaying: a few years ago the fashion was to write in script style, in the present tense and in the third person; now, many writers choose to use the past tense and write in the style of a first-person novel. Some writers vary from roleplay to roleplay; others even use varying styles within an individual roleplay! It’s really up to you. You do NOT have to mention your match in your roleplays. Reference to wrestling is not necessary to further your character, and therefore we will not penalise you for absence of it. In the same vein, therefore, we do not request that you censor profanities. We do not request that you keep bad language, sex or violence to a minimum. Write what you need to write about in order to develop your story – AWC will not restrict you in any way. You won’t restrict me? Well how come I can only post one roleplay per week? That assumption is incorrect. Every member of AWC can post as many roleplays as they like, whenever they like – however, only one roleplay counts for each match. This MUST be nominated by filling in the appropriate field in the submission script. What if I don’t have a match? Then you don’t have to roleplay – but, of course, you are welcome to do so. AWC is not one of those disciplinarian feds that insist on handlers burning themselves out; we are not going to punish you in any way for not roleplaying in a week when you are not booked. We would prefer you to relax, take a couple of days off, work on some segments, maybe plan out the way your stories (both on-show stories and roleplay-based stories) are going to go for the next few weeks. Also, volunteering to write a match for Fresh! in your week off is of far more use to AWC than you churning out a roleplay simply because you feel you should. The roleplay deadline for Fresh! is Tuesday 6pm GMT (i.e. when Pierre H. gets home from school) – that’s usually equivalent to 1pm EST, but might be dependent on whether the clock changes in your country. Note that GMT is constant all year round – while Britain does move forward one hour in summer, it simply moves on to BST, which is one hour ahead of GMT. The deadline for Fresh!burst is Sunday 6pm GMT (i.e. when Pierre H. gets home from work); the deadline for pay-per-view events is Friday 6pm GMT. How will my roleplay be judged? At least one member of staff will be assigned to judge your match. For all title matches and main events, all members of staff will judge your match. Each judge will inevitably have his or her own preferences, but generally we will be looking for strong character development intertwined with an interesting story - and make sure your SPAG is good! Can I use other AWC characters in my roleplays? The NPCs – non-played characters, i.e. the commentators – are free for your use, but please do not take this as an invitation to do straight question-and-answer roleplays with an AWC interviewer. If you want your character interviewed, you should usually do it in an on-card segment. Staff characters, such as Pearl, may NOT be used without staff permission. That includes ‘voice on the other end of the phone’ situations. Other handlers’ characters may NOT be used without the permission of the handler. You do not need to obtain the permission of staff to use other handlers’ characters, however, as it creates unnecessary difficulties. Roleplays must be posted using your backstage account. If, for any reason, there are difficulties with backstage, post your roleplay on the backup roleplay board on the forums, and it can be added to your archive later when the problem has been dealt with. Segments We would usually expect every handler to contribute to at least one segment for each show. Segments are the primary arena in which you develop your storylines, and so are crucial to success in AWC. Even if you’re the best roleplayer in history, if you never write any segments you’re not going to receive a huge push because by default you won’t be involved in any angles. And remember – angles are usually the stimulus for the best and most exciting roleplays. E-wrestling is a lot more fun when you’re working with other writers, so we recommend you get involved in as many storylines as you can without completely confusing yourself and your readers. It’s no crime to be in the midst of several consecutive feuds – in fact, it’s far more interesting. Can I use other AWC characters in my segments? The NPCs are free for your use, as are staff characters, but be aware that we may make modifications to your usage of these characters. If you want to use another handler's character in a segment, you should collaborate directly with the handler. The deadline for submitting segments for Fresh! is Tuesday 10pm GMT. For Fresh!burst it is Sunday 10pm GMT and for pay-per-view events it is Friday 11:59pm GMT. Ins and Outs If you want to join AWC as a handler, you should fill in the Application form (click through from the bottom of the navigation bar). We aim to respond to all new applicants, whether the answer is positive or otherwise, within three days. Applications for any other position in AWC, such as columnist or artist, should be made directly by email. AWC reserves the right to terminate your wrestler's contract without notice at any time, for any reason deemed sufficient for dismissal. If you wish to take some time out from AWC, or do not want to be booked in a certain week, let a staff member know (the sooner the better) and we will do our utmost to accommodate your wishes.
