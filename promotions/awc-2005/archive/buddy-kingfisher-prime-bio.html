Buddy Kingfisher Biography Real Name William Aaron [name withheld] Nicknames 'The Kingfish', 'The Jolly One', 'That Wal-Mart Fanatic' Hometown Sunray, Oklahoma Height 6'3' Weight 383 Fed Awards Weekly Rankings #9 (12/28/05) Biography Buddy Kingfisher...the man...the myth...the Wal-Mart loving fanatic? That is right folks the red haired, fun loving, big man part time Wal-Mart worker turned Core Wrestling superstar is as entertaining and exciting as anyone has seen in Core in a long time. Whether he is dishing out some punishment in the ring with his size and strength, while messing around and sticking out his tongue and dancing for the crowd, or giving props to the legends of the past and present in Core Wrestling Buddy Kingfisher brings a smile to the face of every fan in the arena and more than a chuckle or two from his fellow co-workers. After signing with Core Wrestling Buddy Kingfisher got involved in the Jason Hughes Star Search tournament and after a early first round set back came all the way back to make the finals against Sir Arafat at the Last Rights Pay-Per-View. In the finals Buddy fought his heart out and left everything he had on the mat inside that cage. Last Rites was simply not meant to be his night, however, as he fell to the great skills of Arafat. Now Kingfisher must turn his attention to the Legacy Title shot he earned by making it into the finals and also to the confrontation with Jason Hughes that no doubt hangs in the horizon for his comedy parody of Hughes and his wife Veronica that took place at Last Rites. A tough win over the impressive, and very strong, ToRn, allowed Kingfisher to move with his Core winning ways. Also he picked up a new mission. Making Jason Hughes laugh. Not an easy task, even for the Jolly One himself, but one that Buddy has decided to take on full force. With that mission there seems to be some unfinished business on the mind of Hiroshi who has returned and beaten Buddy with a crutch as the beginning of his 'getting even' for the broken leg that the recieved from Kingfish in a botched figure four attempt. Furthering their problems, and feeding his hatred, Hiroshi cost Kingfisher a match against Paige and Worrell that would have cemented Kingfisher as a top contender in Core Wrestling. Following that crushing loss Kingfisher spent the next couple of weeks on the mission of making Hughes laugh. After many attempts, even the re-enactment of the first, and at that time only, Fusion vs Hughes match up, Buddy and Jason met in the ring for the first ever Core Wrestling Laugh Off. After many great jokes by Buddy that had the crowd rolling Hughes nailed Buddy in the jaw with his Just Like That kick and him and Hiroshi did a number on the Jolly One. With a sore jaw, a failed mission, and no match in weeks Kingfisher walked into Total Chaos on August 18th, 2005 in a seven person over the top rope Battle Royal for the Core Wrestling Classic Championship. After a hard fought match Kingfisher walked out with his first PPV win for Core Wrestling and his first ever Core Wrestling championship by becoming the Classic Champ. For almost a month Buddy proudly held that Classic Championship. Until on PRIDE 41 in a show of more rage and jealousy Hiroshi Ryusaki decided to not only cost Buddy the match, and his title, but also to injure him and put him on the shelf. How long will Buddy be out of cW action? Only time will tell and not even Munson knows how long that time will be. Finally after being gone for over a month Buddy Kingfisher shocked Hiroshi, and Jason Hughes, by returning at the end of the PRIDE 45 show and ruining Hughes attempt to announce Hiroshi Ryusaki as the new number one contender to the Legacy crown that Munson had just regained for the second time. Stepping in and taking the shot owed to him, by making the finals of the Star Search tournament over the summer, Buddy Kingfisher walked into PRIDE 46 with a date against Munson and a shot at destiny. With everything on the line and both men taking it absolutely to the limits of humanity Buddy was able to hit his trademark, super rare, finisher The Worlds Ugliest Elbow and walk away with his very first Legacy Championship crown. With the Ringmaster tournament now beginning just on the horizon Buddy will look to add one more huge accolade to his young, successful, and promising cW career. In the first round of the tournament Buddy Kingfisher saw himself facing white hot rookie John Combs with his title on the line. In his first title defense of the Legacy Championship Buddy Kingfisher stood tall and proud and came away with a victory that solidified him as the champ. In the second first round match up for Buddy Kingfisher, with his title once more on the line, a Mr. Jackson Cleremont came calling. An impressive rookie that had hit a bit of a snag in his early Core career. With the title on the line, and his performance dedicated to the recently departed, but always loved and respected, Eddie Guerrero, Buddy walked into Pride 49 with everything on the line. The world saw Buddy dominate Jackson for a match lasting barely over five minutes only to end with Buddy asending the top rope and flying off with a Frog Splash, in honor of Eddie, to win the match. Shortly after this match however a bitter contract dispute with Core management caused him to leave the company that he'd fallen in love with and seek to make his living and continue his career elsewhere. When the powers that be in the Atlantic Wrestling Club came calling with a nice sized contract and belief that Kingfisher could be huge for the company Buddy was all to pleased to sign the contract that would begin the next chapter in his young but very exciting career. The big man is fairly quick and athletic, for a big man, so he moves around the ring well. Below is a breakdown of his background, or lack there of as it might be, in each of the main styles of pro wrestling. You'll also find how he approaches facing people who use each of these styles. Powerhouse Being that Kingfisher is a powerhouse first and fore most I figured this is the best place to start. At 6'3' and weighing in at 383 lbs (of which he has alot of muscle, but good amount of fat as well) Buddy is extremely strong. There are few, if any, in pro wrestling who could simply man handle him. In 99 our of 100 matches he is going to have the size and strength advantage, probably at most only giving up the size advantage in terms of height. This is where the base of Buddy's offense is going to come from. Everything he does is based off of his power and brutal strength. He'll pound his opponents into the mat with a wide variaty of suplexes, throws, slams, clubing forearms, punches, elbows, and the works. Anything that is power related Buddy will use to his advantage. How he would approach a person who is also a powerhouse (Like Munson): Simply put Buddy will use his strength and size advantage to wear down his fellow powerhouse. Buddy has a great ability to take punishment and has no problem taking plenty of it to dish out more of his own. With the use of every suplex known to man in his arsenal he'd suplex and slam the opponent into the match until they simply couldn't stand up to fight him any longer. Then it would be Party's Over time. Technical (covering anything from amature to great mat technican This area is a extreme weakness for Buddy. The fact of the matter is that beyond the very basic knowledge of technical moves Buddy is completely ignorant to this type of style. He uses very little technical skill in his matches, in fact anything more than arm drags or hip tosses and your pretty much out of his range of technical ability. You won't find Buddy trying too many things from this move set as they don't benefit his style and therefor really have no bearing to help him win matches. How he would face a technical wrestler (Like Jason Hughes/Gwynn): In all honesty Buddy is going to avoid getting anywhere near the mat with this type of wrestler. Being that he is a bruising power house who loves to suplex and brawl his opponents into giving up being on the mat is the worst place in the world for Buddy. He is like a fish out of water when he is forced into a mat game with someone and in that position Buddy, like the fish, is dead. So when faced with someone who can really go in this area Buddy is going to approach it much the way he would a powerhouse. He'll keep them off their game by using his strength to simply pound on them until they can't even think about how to defend themselves let alone how to get him to the mat and put him in a lock. Technicans are probably the most dangerous opponent for Buddy to face, if they are any good, and he'll do whatever he can to avoid being taken down to the mat by them. Submissionist In terms of submission skills Buddy has exactly, none. He doesn't know more than a submission hold or two and they are the basic old school type of a camel clutch or a figure four. VERY rare for him to even attempt to put someone in a submission hold cause he is so inept at using them. Often he will botch even putting the move in place and this is dangerous for all involved. If he actually manages to lock in something simple like a sharpshooter, Camel Clutch, or Figure Four it isn't hard for the opponent to get to the ropes or reverse it cause Buddy simply isn't skilled in this area to keep it from happening. Usually if Buddy is attempting submissions it is out of desperation. How he would face a submissionist (Like Jason Hughes/William Curr): Much like the technican avoiding the submissions is the order of the day. He has a high pain thresh hold so he'll hold out as long as he can but if becomes clear that there is no chance to get to the ropes, reverse the hold, or escape from it then he will tap. He isn't going to risk serious injury if there is no hope of getting away from the move. Being that he isn't good with submissions himself and is like a lame duck if placed in one Buddy has become extremely good and getting out of the holds, either by reversal, or quick thinking and reverse before the move can ever even be properly locked in. Aerialist (Covering everything from traditional to lucha and on down the line) One move. That is the extent of Buddy's aerialist abilities. His special Pay-Per-View/Grudge match finisher he calls the Cannon Ball (Top rope moonsault) is the only flyer move you'll ever see him attempting. He is too big to be going to the rope often, even for the most simple of moves, and he isn't going to be bouncing around the ring doing planchas, cross bodies, and all that other stuff. He simply doesn't even attempt anything else. When he does attempt the Cannon Ball he'll hit is 9 out of 10 times and when he does the match is over. There is NO chance for a kickout of the Cannon Ball. How he would face a Aerialist (Like Diamond/Lamen): This type of style can be both frustrating or very easy for Buddy to handle depending on the compentancy of the wrestler. Buddy is quick and can move around very well for a big man but he is still going to be at great disadvantage to a cruiserweight type of wrestler in both the speed and certainly the athletism department. As such this is where his endurance and ability to absorb great deals of punishment come in very handy for him. Since a huge man can beat on him for long periods of time and Buddy will keep coming it is very hard for a cruiserweight style wrestler to really hurt Buddy. They will have to rely on a whole lot of hit and run to string moves together and try to wear him down over a long match where match conditioning will play into their favor. What Buddy normally does, outside of his usual ground and pound style, is that he will use their momentum against them as much as possible. Reversing attempted arial moves into powerslams, backbreakers, tosses, and suplex's Buddy can utilize not only his size and strength but the natural momentum that his opponent has built up to his advantage. Hardcore/Extreme Buddy is a big man. He is a fun loving man. But he can get down and dirty when the match calls for it. Not illegal dirty as Buddy would NEVER cheat to win but in a hardcore/extreme rules enviroment Buddy can more than hold his own. Again this is where his size and strength play a major factor as his use of weapons is amplified by his strength he brings to the table. Plus his ability to absorb punishment and keep coming makes him a prime canidate for this style of match up. How he would approach a hardcore/extreme wrestler (Like Trashcan Man/The Hit/Clyde): Buddy would gladly beat them at their own game. He can mix it up in this style with the best of them and he wouldn't be afraid to show it. The experience of a Hardcore extremist could play a factor as Buddy doesn't soley compete in this type of enviroment but not by much. This would be a brutal, bloody, and gutsy affair when you pair Buddy up against a person of this style. All rounder Buddy isn't an all arounder. So there is no need to figure on that with him. :) How he would approach and All Rounder (Like Nova/Worrell): Simply put Buddy would use a combination of how he would approach someone from each of the skill sets. He'd try and mix it up as best he could to keep the opponent off their game and out of being able to become comfortable with what he is going to try and do. Martial Artist Buddy has ZERO martial arts trainning so there for he doesn't use any of it in his matches. How he would approach a martial artist (Like Hiro/Conway/Lamen): Keep them off their game by either pounding them into near unconscious or by frustrating them into forgetting that style and making mistakes. When they do use it he is simply going to be playing the game of absorbing as much of it as possible as he doesn't have the training to defend against this type of skill set. Simple Yay's and Nay's for Buddy in the ring Yay's: (1) Is always a good sport. Doesn't whine and cry at the ref for counts that are slow and what not. Always shows great sportsmanship and composure. (2) Loves to have fun in the ring. He'll play to the crowd often to keep them entertained. (3) ALWAYS hooks at least one leg when pinning and often hooks both. (4) Shows his opponents the proper respect at all times. Nay's: (1) NEVER cheats. Absolutely NEVER. Will not cheat for anything. The use of weapons and such is only available if that is the match style. Otherwise he will not do it. EVER. (2) NEVER gets physical with a ref. He will not take anything out of a ref. (3) Will not take his opponent lightly. Approaches every opponent as if they are a former Legacy Champion. NEVER disrespects his opponents skills or ability to win a match. (4) Will NEVER back down from a fight during a match up. Kingfisher will not run, beg, or what have you toward an opponent during a match. He is honorable and remains so in his matches.
