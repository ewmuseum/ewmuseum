Pierce Lavelle smiles at the fans’ support and gives a thumbs-up to the fans before applying a sit down cobra clutch. Strider reels back in pain and can do nothing except accept the pain. Truth Waters: Once again Pierce Lavelle is sticking to his strategy of working on one body part, quick takedowns and some high risk moves. George Cassidy: This kid is pulling out all he’s got. I am developing some respect for the young superstar. Truth Waters: Really? Perhaps George Cassidy is changing in his approach. George Cassidy: No. I’m kidding. Well, sort of. He’s got potential, I suppose. But he’s raw. Very raw. Strider grimaces. George Cassidy: Alex Strider looks close to passing out – in fact, so does Lavelle. How can they be expected to go on? Sweat is literally pouring down the two men’s bodies. Truth Waters: Look at that sweat drip off them. Lavelle’s hair looks like he’s just taken a dip in a swimming pool – oh, what I wouldn’t give for that right now. I hate this heat! George Cassidy: Luckily, I think the inferno’s dying down a bit. The flames aren’t so big. Truth Waters: That’s right. I can see into the ring better now. The ring is where no progress has been made. Lavelle is persistent with the hold, and Strider is enduring it. Truth Waters: Once more folks, the rules are simple. There are no tapouts, pinfalls or countouts. Lavelle breaks the hold in frustration and runs his hands through his hair. George Cassidy: Lavelle lets go. He knows he can’t do any more with it. Frustration is clearly shown in both Strider and Lavelle. Lavelle looks around at all the bright lights and hopeful faces of the fans, his hands shaken and his mind not fully in tune due to the possible concussion. He collapses to the ring mat, which is now covered in dried and fresh blood from Strider’s and Lavelle’s busted open heads. Michael Ryan checks on both men, worried for their conditions. Truth Waters: The canvas is turning a dark red. George Cassidy: It makes a good show, Truth. Truth Waters: Not when the two top athletes in the AWC have to be rushed to hospital and can’t compete. That doesn’t make good TV. Lavelle and Strider drag themselves to their feet and begin circling. Lavelle holds out his hand for Strider. Strider refuses to shake it and Lavelle pulls it back. Truth Waters: Pierce Lavelle showing some sportsmanship. George Cassidy: And Strider is having none of it. Strider winks at Lavelle, but Lavelle punches Strider right in the side of the mouth. Truth Waters: Ha! Take that! Lavelle fed up with the cocky antics of his opponent and the champion of the AWC. Perhaps not for long. George Cassidy: Oh, The Mongoose will retain. Lavelle continues with the sharp, continuous punches aimed at Strider’s face. Strider lifts up his arms to defend his face as he gets backed into the corner. Strider suddenly knees Lavelle in the crotch area. The challenger bends over, his knees crashing to the mat. Strider smirks and aims his shin so that it hits Lavelle in the side. Lavelle turns over to his left and rolls out of the ring to regain his composure. Truth Waters: I don’t understand this! Ringside is usually an escape, but here he’s just rolling into even more intense heat. George Cassidy: Maybe it’s the lesser of two evils. Which is worse? Facing Strider or being just a couple of feet from a raging inferno? Strider attempts a baseball slide; Lavelle ducks and grabs Strider’s trunks and yanks him to the outside. Strider stumbles and almost stands in some of the flames surrounding the ring. Lavelle delivers a swift uppercut and Strider stumbles once more, his arms wobbling around and around, his back arched. Truth Waters: This could be it. Strider is balancing on one foot! The crowd’s cheers reaches fever pitch as they anticipate the end. Strider throws his weight forward in a desperate attempt to avoid the flames and it works. Lavelle catches him and hits a DDT onto the protective mats around the ring. George Cassidy: Not close enough. That’s fortunate. Truth Waters: But Strider isn’t coming back, surely. That was one stiff DDT. Lavelle sits up again and looks once more, breathless, around the crowd of fans and loyal wrestling followers. Michael Ryan asks Pierce Lavelle to continue with the match. Truth Waters: Lavelle seems to be getting fed up with this match! There must be nothing he wants more than his own bed... or a bucket of ice. George Cassidy: I wouldn’t blame the guy. He can’t pin the champion or make him submit. Those are the two ways that most people win a match. Pierce Lavelle will have to do something unique to get Strider into the flames, and probably risk losing himself in the process. Truth Waters: This is the first time in the Inferno for either man, and I can tell you, they won’t be rushing to sign up for another. Lavelle waits for Strider and delivers another swinging neckbreaker, sending Strider face-first onto the disfigured steel chair. Blood dots splatter around the sides of the chair. Lavelle closes his eyes for a few moments. Truth Waters: We definitely need medics out here after the match. Lavelle has a concussion and his head is busted open. Same goes for Strider. They’ll have to get their head wounds seen to. George Cassidy: Don’t forget burns. Truth Waters: It’s not just the burns, either, it’s the blisters that will cover every square inch of their bodies. Lavelle turns around and Strider plants him with one shot from the steel chair. The fans boo Strider as he laughs out loud. Ignoring the fans and their opinions. Michael Ryan yells for Strider to stop and put the chair down. Strider laughs again and chucks it into the flames. Strider watches as the devouring heat begins to slowly melt the metal. Truth Waters: What a cheap shot by Strider. Steel chair to the head of Lavelle! And look at the chair in the fire... it’s ALREADY melting! George Cassidy: Quick and painful, Strider’s strategy. Truth Waters: And painful it will be when one of them is forced into the inferno! I’m beginning to think that the blowtorch would be a NICER way to end the match! Lavelle is beginning to wake up. Strider jumps on Lavelle’s back and begins pounding his head into the floor. Lavelle tries to wriggle free, but has no luck. Michael Ryan slides out of the ring and breaks up Strider’s vicious assault. Strider opens up his arms in an “I don’t care” manner and walks back up the ring steps and into the ring. Truth Waters: Lavelle isn’t moving. This could be worrying. George Cassidy: Strider has Lavelle just where he wants him. Truth Waters: The thing is, Lavelle has a conscience... Strider just doesn’t care. Perhaps that could give the champion the edge. Michael Ryan checks on Lavelle and tries to wake him up. Strider begins to taunt the crowd. The crowd boo loudly at Strider, who just shrugs it off by turning his back on them. Strider spots Michael Ryan and Lavelle now standing. Lavelle is bleeding profusely now. Strider opens up the bottom rope to allow the referee in but drops it and leaves as Lavelle climbs up slowly. Strider charges just as Lavelle is standing on the ring apron. Lavelle ducks and delivers a shoulder block to the abdomen of Strider. Strider grips the top rope and pulls backwards, grabbing hold of Lavelle’s head in a lazy, low suplex. Lavelle is up and over and out flat on his back in the middle of the ring. Strider climbs up onto the turnbuckle and steadies himself. Truth Waters: Strider taking his time. We don’t often see him up there. George Cassidy: He’s confident, Truth. Look at the mess Lavelle is in. Truth Waters: They are both in a mess, Cassidy. Both men are tired, bleeding and are suffering from mild concussions. This match is an all out bloodfest. Strider leaps off the turnbuckle in a graceful manner and lowers his elbow in mid air for an elbow drop. Lavelle rolls out of the way at the last second and Strider hits the mat, full force with his own elbow! Truth Waters: Misses the elbow drop! George Cassidy: Oh no, Alex...! He looks around in pain and spots Lavelle, now standing. Lavelle has a smirk on his face and he plants Strider right in the stomach with his own elbow drop. Strider now begins to roll to the outside and drops to the floor below. Lavelle stands up and waits for Strider. Truth Waters: Whoever wins tonight deserves it. Both of these athletes put in a valiant effort. George Cassidy: I suppose I have to agree. The expression “to hell and back” is way overused, but really, where can it be more appropriate than here? Truth Waters: Surely some companionship between the two will come out of this. Strider is up on the outside. Lavelle dives off the ropes with a springboard cross body block. Strider ducks and Lavelle falls just short of the ring of fire. Strider leans up against the steel steps. He positions himself and waits for Lavelle, leaning on his knees. George Cassidy: What did I tell you, Truth? The end of the match is upon us. Truth Waters: Lavelle fell so close to the fire. Strider could end it now! Lavelle stands up. Strider moves forward and steadies himself. He grabs Lavelle, but Lavelle turns around and nails Strider with Blackout! Truth Waters: Blackout! No dice, Strider! The fans are on their feet. Lavelle motions for it and hoists Strider over his head, looking for Whiplash. Lavelle turns around, with Strider on his shoulders. George Cassidy: NO WAY! NO FREAKING WAY! Truth Waters: Look at the strength of Lavelle! And surely he can’t be considering this! Surely not! Surely not! Lavelle throws Strider off his shoulders and into the blaze! Truth Waters: OH MY GOD! NOW I HAVE SEEN IT ALL! WHIPLASH INTO A BLAZING INFERNO! The bell rings, as Strider manages to roll straight through to the outside of the inferno, screaming in pain. Striders trousers are burnt a quarter of the way up and the fringes singed greatly. George Cassidy: No! Strider was sent through the blaze and caught on fire. This can’t be how it ends! This isn’t fair! Truth Waters: It’s fortunate that he managed to roll right through. Imagine if he’d been caught in there. After just a second or two our Transatlantic champion would’ve been roasted – our FORMER champion, I should say! It’s time to crown Pierce Lavelle! Lavelle falls backwards into Michael Ryan who raises Lavelle’s hand. Lavelle looks around, dazed and unsure at what has just happened, before he falls to the floor The fans are all clapping and cheering for the young Pennsylvanian. James Brunt: The winner... and new Transatlantic champion... PIERCE LAVELLE! The firemen begin putting out the choking blaze with their massive hose as Strider is loaded onto a stretcher and carried away. Michael Ryan hands Pierce Lavelle the Transatlantic championship. Pierce Lavelle holds onto it for dear life. He is still shocked at what just happened. He looks at the nameplate and smiles. Lavelle takes in the cheers from the fans with delight. Truth Waters: Well there you have it. Pierce Lavelle is our new Transatlantic champion. He proved himself tonight, not just to us, but to everyone watching. Great show by both men. George Cassidy: This is a travesty! I can’t believe it. Strider has lost his title! Truth Waters: It didn’t really matter who won. That these two young men were fit and strong enough to both come out of that relatively unharmed is a miracle. To even stand in that atmosphere for nearly thirty minutes is a challenge, but to contest a wrestling match between two BITTER rivals? Incredible. Now I wonder if Lavelle will have the strength to speak to us – George Cassidy: Strider’s coming back! Truth Waters: WHAT?! Having abandoned his stretcher, Alex Strider marches defiantly through the one section of the fire that has been put out and grabs Pierce Lavelle by the head. Lavelle is caught unawares, and has no strength to react to Strider slamming his head against the ringpost. The former champion now drags the new champion over to a section that is still burning. George Cassidy: Strider wants revenge! Truth Waters: Somebody stop him! This is sick! Absolutely sick! There is no way he is going to do it... surely... Strider, using the last of his energy, forces Lavelle’s head downwards through the choking smoke. Lavelle begins to dry-heave violently, struggling desperately to avoid being pushed into the fire. Suddenly, a man in wrestling attire bursts through the curtain! He sprints down the ramp, his blond-brown hair flopping at the sides and front of his head as he leaps the previously-burning barricade and tears Strider away from Lavelle! Strider’s eyes widen as he is kneed viciously in the gut. Truth Waters: What is this? Who is this? I can barely see him through the smoke! All I can make out is the white tights! That isn’t someone we know, is it, Cassidy? George Cassidy: Certainly not, and someone needs to prevent this attack on the champion! He’s just endured an Inferno match. Truth Waters: He isn’t the champion anymore, and besides, all this man is doing is saving Lavelle from possible DEATH at the angry hands of Alex Strider! Pausing to look out at the crowd, the agile wrestler now turns to his doubled-up opponent and grabs the ringpost. Channeling his momentum, he swings himself up, pushing off Strider’s lower back with his left foot and then flipping off the apron with his right. Completing his mid-air backflip, his arm falls into place around Strider’s neck and the two go to the floor in an amazing DDT! George Cassidy: That’s... surely not... he left the business! Truth Waters: What? Who? The man now pulls Lavelle urgently away from the flames and onto the stretcher Strider vacated. After some quick, sharp instructions to medics, he rolls apprehensively into the ring and stands to face 18,000 confused yet happy fans. And finally, simultaneously, everyone recognises him for who he is. Truth Waters: TIM SHIPLEY! Copyright notice. Cut. Black.
