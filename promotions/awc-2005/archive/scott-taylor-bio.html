Scott Taylor Biography Real Name Scott Adam Taylor Nicknames The New Hero Hometown Baton Rouge, Louisiana Birthday February 11, 1977 (29) Height 6'3' Weight 220 Biography Born into a wealthy family in Baton Rouge, Scott grew up as a well off kid from the suburbs of Louisiana's capitol. A child of fortune, Scott found his outlet in the great American sport of baseball and was a star player for many years in school. After finishing High School in the Spring of 1995, Scott passed up a chance to play baseball for the prestigious Tulane University to attend a wrestling school. After finishing the school, Scott took on the name 'Scotty '2Extreme' Taylor' and began wrestling for locally owned promotions, usually having his matches in parking lots of car dealerships and high school gyms. In the year 1999 he signed a contract with a small, traveling promotion known as Larger Than Life wrestling and became the company's risk taker; putting his body on the line to live up to his '2Extreme' moniker and entertain the crowd. LTL went under in 2001 due to bad management. Without a fed, Scott became a free agent with enough experience to start garneing the attention of more serious wrestling promotions. After a failed run in PCW's 'Diamond In The Rough' tournament, Scott was picked up by a PTC off-shoot called NTICW. At the advice of one of the company's owners Scott dropped the '2Extreme' name and began to focus on more technical wrestling, mixing mat based moves and top rope acrobatics into his impact moves, and that was when his career took off. After an impressive run of leading the company's biggest face stable and winning the World Title in a three way dance, Scott became an NTICW hero. After holding the company's World Championship for three months, Scott was offered a deal with PCW by its then owner and CEO, Tyler Nelson: 'The Greediest Player In The Game.' After penning the deal and discussing wrestling with the legend, Scott was approached by another. Jane Reagan, the owner of the rival company OSW offered Scott a better contract and he double-dealt The Greedy One. Thanks to Jane's team of highly paid lawyers, Scott avoided a Breech-Of-Contract Lawsuit and joined forces with OSW. Now in his first big name promotion, Taylor immediately began wowing the wrestling world with his feud with cruiserweight legend Kid Wonder. The feud won PTC's 'Feud of Summer 2002' award and those matches are still considered some of the best. After a lengthy feud with 'The Russian Bear' Ivan Stanislav over the OSW World Title, Scott was sidelined for several months with a lower back injury; his '2Extreme' days catching up with him after botching a move against Stanislav at the biggest PPV of the year. Upon his return, Scott held feuds with Chris Sloboda and Six-Finger Cisco and eventually won the OSW Title from Cisco in a Best-Of-Three series of matches. However, the reign was short lived as OSW fell under hard times after severing its ties with PTC and undergoing new ownership. Not too long after OSW's shocking downfall, Scott was approached by a former colleague, who was now under contract with the revived RPW. Scott signed a short-term contract with the company and wrestled a few select shows, but quickly left the company due to contract disagreements. At this point in his career, the then 27 year old wrestler took a break from the world of professional wrestling. Using his name and face value, he began doing commercials and tv spots for everything from Y2K Stinger, Bowflex, and even a comedic series of Doritos commercials featuring other sports celebrities. In December of 2005, Scott was given a call by Stephen Caldera, a man who served as a behind-the-scenes worker in OSW. Stephen offered Scott a spot in his new fed, Global Championship Wrestling, or GCW for short. Scott quickly signed the deal for the sake of his old friend but due to poor ticket sales, a lack of promotion, and horrible backstage morale, Scott left the company by February 2006. Over the summer of 2006, Scott appeared in the movie 'Killer Serpent Attack', a Sci-Fi Pictures Original film. He portrayed a bounty hunter who was hired by a small town scientist to battle a threat of giant snakers. Though the movie was viewed as a campy B film with lame CGI effects, it drew the attention of wrestling fans who were eager to see Scott in the film. Now at 29 years old, the 11 year veteran of the wrestling scene is back with a new image, new attitude, and a new promotion; ready to bring old school wrestling flare to AWC.
