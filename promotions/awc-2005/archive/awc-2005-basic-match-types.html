Basic Match Types

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Singles – A basic one-on-one contest which can be won by pinfall, submission, knock-out (very rare), count-out or disqualification. Duo Tag – A two-on-two match where only one wrestler from each team can be in the ring at a time. Legality of entry can be switched between the members of the team by means of a tag from the legal man to his partner who must be standing on the ring apron in or around his or her allocated corner. Tandem – A two-on-two match where there are no restrictions on who can enter the ring when. Three-Way Elimination – A match pitting three wrestlers against each other simultaneously. Elimination takes place via the usual methods; the last wrestler left in the match is the winner. Four-Way Elimination – A match pitting four wrestlers against each other simultaneously. Elimination takes place via the usual methods; the last wrestler left in the match is the winner. Three-Way Fury – A match pitting three wrestlers against each other simultaneously. The first fall wins the match. Four-Way Fury – A match pitting four wrestlers against each other simultaneously. The first fall wins the match. Three-Way Tag – A match pitting three wrestlers against each other. Only two may be in the ring at a time; the right to be in the ring is exchanged via the tag method to the third wrestler who stands on the ring apron. Four-Way Tag – A match pitting four wrestlers against each other. Only two may be in the ring at a time; the right to be in the ring is exchanged via the tag method to the other two wrestlers who stand on the ring apron. Best Of Three – A one-on-one match consisting of three ‘falls’. Each fall is basically a normal singles match. The winner is the wrestler that wins two of these falls. If the first two falls are won by one wrestler, the match ends there and the third fall does not take place. Weapons – A one-on-one match for which weapons are provided. There can be no disqualification or count-out in this match. Pinfalls and submissions can occur anywhere. 10-Count – A one-on-one match where, to win, the victor must ensure that his opponent lies prone for ten seconds (without contact) while the referee counts him down. Submission – A one-on-one match which may end by submission only. Handicap – Any match in which the teams are not fairly numbered. Example: a two-on-one match. Ladder – A one-on-one match where ladders are the only legal weapon. The match ends when one wrestler climbs the ladder in order to retrieve the prize being suspended above the ring. Alternatively, disqualification can end the match. No-DQ Ladder – A Ladder match where all weapons are legal and there are no disqualifications. Table – A Weapons match which ends when one wrestler slams the other through a table. The table must break. Cage – A one-on-one match for which a topless steel cage, usually around fifteen feet in height, is bolted onto the ring enclosing the competitors. There is no disqualification in this match, which ends either when a wrestler escapes the cage (through the door or over the top of the cage) or by pinfall or submission. Escape – A Cage match in which the cage has no door, and the only way to win is by climbing the cage and escaping over the top. These are only the basic stipulations, which can be combined to create, for example, a Tandem Table match. Other match types will undoubtedly surface for AWC pay-per-views; matches are not limited to a choice from the list above. Note that championship belts can usually only change hands after pinfall, knock-out (including the finish of a 10-Count match) or submission. Exceptions: Ladder matches (where the challenger captures the title belt itself), Table matches, Cage matches, Escape matches.
