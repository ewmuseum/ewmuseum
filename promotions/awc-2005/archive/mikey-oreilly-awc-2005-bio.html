Mikey O'Reilly Biography Real Name Michael Lawrence Cavanaugh Farren Nicknames 'Tha' Fokin' Pikey,' 'Mr. One Punch' Hometown Belfast, Ireland, now residing in Boston, Mass Birthday January 12, 1973 Height 6'1' Weight 230 Fed Titles Frontier champion (8/8/06 - 8/22/06) Fed Awards 2nd shortest Frontier title reign (14 days) Being the better Irishman than Paddy O'Shea ;) Biography Born Michael Lawrence Cavanaugh Farren on January 12, 1973 in Belfast, Ireland. He was the youngest of six children - three sisters and two other brothers - and his parents were Wendy Cavanaugh and Seamus Farren. Having six children in the household, food, money and clothing were hard to come by, even education was expensive for the six children. Only three of the children only have a high school diploma, and the girls received the coveted academic accolates. In 1986, thirteen-year-old Michael Farren wanted to help bring in money. He did everything from selling newspapers, mowing lawns and even resorted to sell drugs. In the spring of that year, young Michael found his calling in Irish bare-knuckle boxing. Before his first match, the boxing promoter gave with him the handle Mikey O'Reilly, and it stuck with him ever since. The small, scrappy young boy had his first match against a fighter three times his size...and won. Mikey's punch power - which had come to his surprise - was an asset for the young boy. In two years, he landed his first bare-knuckle boxing title. In the summer of 1998, he went against long time friend and boxing rival, Ian McCleod in a match that Mikey had regreted in participating in. It was thirty minutes of hell, both men giving it everything they had at each other, but Mikey was victorious, but not in a way he wanted to win. He accidently killed his best friend in the ring, not only shattering his jaw with his powerful hook, but broke his neck in the process, killing him instantly. After that day, he wasn't sure if he wanted to box after that and took a two-year hiatus, only to come back in January of 2000. In the fourteen years he'd been in Irish bare-knuckle boxing, he had won eight bare-knuckle boxing titles. But his nineth eluded him on January 15, 2000. On that day, he faced Paddy O'Shea, an unknown bare-knuckle boxing contender to Mikey and was smaller than the average fighter in Mikey's career. He was defeated after a fifteen-minute bout against the smaller opponent, being humilated in from the boxing fans and shunned by his peers. On that day, he had his final bare-knuckle match. At the end of the month of that year, he moved to the United States, looking to have a clean slate in Boston, Massachusetts. At that time, he ran into the world's most notorious wrestling superstars, Zsasz. Mikey explained to Zsasz about his bare-knuckle boxing past and wanted to start a professional boxing career. Zsasz lied to Mikey, saying that no one would sign a former bare-knuckle boxing competitor and persuaded Mikey to enter the world of professional wrestling. Zsasz even promised to train the young man. He had a few matches in local venues all over Massachusetts, but nothing came to fruition for the rookie wrestler. In 2002, Zsasz brought in his student to Superior Championship Wrestling Federation for a tag team match, replacing Zsasz due to storyline and angling purposes. After having that tag team match, he was never seen in the SCWF again. Mikey continued to train under Zsasz's wing for the next three years. Today, Mikey O'Reilly enters the Atlantic Wrestling Club with Zsasz as his manager. Will six years under Zsasz's tutelage will make something of Mikey O'Reilly, or will Mikey silently slip under the wrestling radar? We shall wait and see... Other Feds SCWF Other Fed Awards Had one match, then it was Splitsville. Wrestling Style Irish Bare-knuckle boxing with surprisingly good wrestling skills Finisher Move Falling of Christ Finisher Description Since he's a Christian basher, this finisher is quite fitting. Mikey picks up his opponent and puts him across his right shoulder in a Canadian Backbreaker. Next, he pins his opponent's arms behind their back, interlocking his arms with theirs. Finally, he drops to his knees, driving the opponent on the shoulderblades/back of the neck region with a Crucifix Pile Driver. If he whips his opponent down fast enough, he'll spike them on their heads. Told you the move was fitting. Other Moves Bare-Knuckle Boxing I'll give you a little tutorial on his bare-knuckle boxing. He'd been doing the sport for 14 years, and he's gotten good at it. He isn't afraid to throw hands against his opponent should he have trouble wrestling him/her. He'll use the following combos: Basic - Left jab (face), right jab (face), left hook (jaw), right hand (stomach) & left uppercut (chin) Intermediate - Left hook (face), right hook (face), left kidney punch, right kidney punch, head-butt (face) and Clothesline Advanced - Left stomach punch, right stomach punch, left hook (face), right hook (face), back heel trip into a mount, straight left hand (face), straight right hand (face), 3-5 head-butts (face) & a Double-Ax Handle. If he's still feeling violent, he'll roll of his opponent and kick him/her right in the side of the head. Hard. NOTE: You can take these three sets of combos and mix it up a bit if you like. Playing Bully - He'll throw his opponent into a corner and viciously beat him about the head, face and body. Should his opponent try to escape, he'll push him/her back into the corner and continue his onslaught until he feels contented to free his opponent. And for the Coup de Grace... THE ONE PUNCH~! - This is the punch that ends all punches. If he can't defeat his opponent with his Crucifix Pile Driver, it is guaranteed he can knock his opponent out. When his opponent is going for a right hand, Mikey O'Reilly blocks the right hand with his left arm, uses all of his weight for the force of the punch, and uses his shoulder to lean into the biggest right hook anyone has ever seen. The velocity of the punch is strong enough to shatter his opponent's jaw and/or break the opponent's neck after the nasty whiplash effect. This punch can also be used left-handed. Now for his wrestling skills. Match Writing Tutorial Mikey O'Reilly is a simple character to write if you make it simple. His attitude is pretty cocky, levelheaded and ready to take anyone on. He doesn't get too unnerved easily, but when he does, all hell breaks loose. His temper takes over and all he wants to do is rip his opponent off. So he'll make lots of mistakes if he starts losing the match, only to miraculous recover before he does lose. Oh yeah, he's always in a boxing stance every time he wrestles. Beginning of Match - He'll enter the match, ready to box. He'll start boxing with his opponent, throwing strategic punches, aggravating his opponent to make them make mistakes. He'll continue to box with his opponent, then go into wrestling. He won't be afraid to mix it up every now and again, but he wants to keep the match at his pace. Middle of Match - This is when he'll get aggressive. He's warmed up and ready to kick some ass. His wrestling skills will really come out now, administering some impact moves, mix it up with some submissions, etc. Every time he hits a big move, he'll go for a pin; same with submissions. Every time he locks in a big move, he'll try to make his opponent submit. If he can't, he'll break the hold and continue his onslaught. He'll always try to keep the momentum to himself. If he makes mistakes, it'll show, I.E. beginning to lose his temper and making haphazard mistakes. Every time his opponent think Mikey is done, Mikey miraculous recovers, continuing to stay in the match. Toward the End/Ending of Match - It's make or break for Mikey. He's either going to win or he's going to lose. If he's going to win, he isn't going to waste any time fucking around. He's going right for his Crucifix Pile Driver finisher, 'Falling of Christ' and win with that. If he can't put his opponent away with that finisher, there's always the ONE PUNCH~! Should he lose, he'll continue to muster as much tenacity as possible until he's put away. If you have any questions, feel free to drop a PM, AIM me, whatever. I'm always happy to answer whatever quandary you have. Entrance Music 'The Enemy' by Team Cazares
