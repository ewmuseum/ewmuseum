Make It a Triple

7 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/07/06 Heading back up the entrance ramp, Trent Marshall stops and turns back to the ring, clearly enjoying the moment at hand. As he wipes away some of the sweat pouring down his face, Marshall realizes what he has missed most in the eight years since he last engaged in active competition—victory. From growing up wrestling in his family’s Chicago suburbs basement as a child, to his final match at the University of Iowa as a college senior, to this very moment in the Atlantic Wrestling Club, the goal remains the same. While others may settle for second best or take solace in moral victories, Marshall works only to win. Turning now and continuing up the ramp, Mr. Marshall leaves behind the thrill of his first AWC victory, satisfied by a job well done. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Exhausted by hours of diligent tape study, Mr. Marshall pauses his VCR and sits back in his black office chair. The small dark room smells of coffee and marker, as the barely discernible whiteboard behind Marshall is filled with all sorts of stratagem and scouting notes. After a long night of mental preparation, Marshall has come to a startling revelation. He’s supposed to lose this match. Not that he believes it, but then again he might be the only one. Both Ortiz and O’Reilly justified the hype surrounding their AWC debuts with impressive victories on last week’s Fresh! that have earned them respect the entire federation over. Meanwhile Marshall did the same thing that he’s been going since junior high, methodically putting away the competition. But now, for the first time since making Nationals as a freshman at the University of Iowa, he’s going into a match as a prohibitive underdog, the forgotten third of this three-way fury match up. He’s not supposed to be quick enough to handle Mikey O’Reilly’s fists. He’s not supposed to be tough enough to handle Jason Ortiz’s experience. But with knowledge comes confidence, and Marshall knows one thing for sure—he will enter the match more prepared than either of his opponents. He’s spent hours studying tape. He’s spent hours in the gym. He’s spent hours working on strategy. And if all else fails, Marshall can always just out-wrestle them. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Three Men: Jason Ortiz. Mikey O’Reilly. Trent Marshall. Three Distinct Styles: Shoot Fighting. Bare-Knuckle Boxing. Amateur Wrestling. Three Accomplished Backgrounds: Lights Out Wrestling World Champion, Realm Champion, and X-Treme Champion Eight Irish bare-knuckle boxing titles. Four-Time All-American. Two-Time National Champion. Three Successful Debuts: “Jason Ortiz gets to his feet, feeling that he can win this match. As Patrick Mapleleaf gingerly gets to his feet, the former LOW World Champion bounces off the top rope, runs at Mapleleaf and gives Mapleleaf a high knee right into the face! Truth Waters: Blowin’ Smoke by Jason Ortiz! We all know what’s come up after that! Ortiz gets to his feet, signaling for his finisher. He picks up Patrick Mapleleaf, kicks him in the gut and sets Mapleleaf’s head in between his legs. Ortiz jumps forward, does a 360 flip and spikes Mapleleaf’s head with.... Truth Waters: El Rehabilitator! The Front Flip Pile Driver! He goes for the cover... ONE! TWO! THREE!” “Mikey O’Reilly gets to his feet, doing a cutthroat, signaling for the end. Mikey O’Reilly picks up Dagoth Kinslayer, kicks him in the gut, and sets his head in between his legs. He gets his arms in a crucifix pose, thumbs pointing up. Then he points his thumbs down, picking Dagoth up over his right shoulder with a Canadian Backbreaker. He pins Dagoth’s arms behind him, interlocking Mikey’s arms with his. Finally, Mikey drops to his knees, driving Dagoth onto the back of his neck and shoulder blades region with the Crucifix Pile Driver! Truth Waters: Falling of Christ! He goes for the cover: ONE! TWO! THREE!” “Neurotic mounts on top of Mr. Marshall and starts with the hammering right fists. Larsson pulls Neurotic off of him. Both men gasping for air, they climb to their feet. Neurotic suddenly springs off the ropes and pummels a drop kick into Marshall’s stomach. Truth Waters: The Demise! Neurotic takes a minute to rally the crowd... He wraps Marshall’s arms up. He begins to lift the newcomer into the air! Mr. Marshall puts all his weight down and falls to one knee... and does a leg whip to slam Neurotic on the ground! George Cassidy: How does he keep getting out of Self Infliction? Mr. Marshall crawls to his feet... Neurotic climbs to his feet... the two men run at each other... Neurotic throws his huge arm out for the clothesline. Mr. Marshall ducks, turns around and performs Hawkeye Driver! The crowd erupts in cheers. George Cassidy: The Hawkeye Driver! What a move! That took some strength to pull that one off. Neurotic is sprawled on the mat, fighting for consciousness... Mr. Marshall then sits Neurotic up... and wrenches him in MARSHALL LAW! George Cassidy: That’s called Marshall Law! Neurotic fights the pain, he lets out a loud yell… the crowd is on their feet... screaming at the top of their lungs... Neurotic reaches for the ropes, Marshall squeezes even tighter with every ounce of strength he has... Neurotic taps out! Lars Larsson calls for the bell... the match is over!” Three Undefeated Records. One Match. One Winner.
