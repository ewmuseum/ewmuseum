One for Luck, Ch. 10

21 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 21st Dec One for Luck, Ch. 10 (And so the story continues…) The snow danced lightly against the window, sliding down the windowsill till it landed on the balcony creating a white rug of snowflakes. The wind had died down as had many of the mad dashers for Christmas. Pierce had remained secluded in the small corner of his new hotel room. It had been three days since his grandfathers death and Susanne had rung to say the funeral was to be the next day. Pierce hadn’t answered. He didn’t know what to do, he was a wreck and he hadn’t let that known when he was at his companies Christmas party. Pulling out another bottle of tequila, he rolled the empty vodka bottle along the floor till it came to a stop at the main door. He didn’t care how drunk he was, he could still feel pain and hurt. The silver berretta lay on the floor next to him with one bronze coloured bullet lying next to it. He ripped the cork off the top of the tequila bottle and took a large gulp from it, wiped his mouth and coughed. It was stronger than he remembered, but it would go straight to his head. He picked up the berretta, removed the clip and emptied it. He then picked up the single bullet and placed it into the ninth hatch. He placed it into his hand and against his head and pulled the trigger. CLICK Nothing, first hatch down, he then did it a second time. CLICK Nothing, the second hatch down. The bullet was rising with each click. Tears were running down his face. Usually in high school when Pierce had had one too many he would become quiet and tired, now he was an emotional wreck with the drink. He picked up the bottle of tequila a second time and pointed the gun at the opposite wall and pulled the trigger, another empty “Click” woke him from his drunken state. He ignored the bullets scuttling along the mahogany floor and took another large gulp from the tequila, but with each drink he could feel the nausea rising. He was not used to the high extremes of alcohol, not any more. He stared at the moon’s glow, glistening against the snow, creating a heavenly effect on the balcony. Pierce dipped his head and pointed the gun once more. CLICK “Jesus,” a voiced cried from the hall. Pierce looked up and saw Lois stood in the hall, carrying her luggage and dressed in a posh suit, her hair tied tightly behind her face. Of course, this is what he would have seen, if he wasn’t so drunk! “Lo…” Pierce mumbled from behind a drunken slur. “Are you trying to kill me?” Lois said, dropping her bags and approaching Pierce. “I…” Pierce lay back against the wall, the nausea now resting in his throat. Lois knelt next to Pierce and removed the gun from his hand. She undid the clip and found four hatches, used and empty, but there was one bullet in the ninth hatch, that had risen to the fifth. Pierce rose up, using the wall for leverage and moved around Lois, struggling to maintain his balance as he made his way to the toilet. Lois stood, hands on hips as she listened to the unpleasant sounds of Pierce vomiting. “This place is a mess,” she said to herself as she picked up the two bottles of drink and poured the remainder of the tequila down the sink. She began to brew an old remedy for hangovers and the unwelcome aftertaste of drinking too much. Pierce emerged after a few minutes, his hands on his head as he moaned over and over. Lois turned around and handed him the drink along with a wet face cloth. He drank it none the less and wiped his mouth with the towel. “Ewe, yuck!” Pierce spewed. Lois smiled and removed the drink as Pierce feel off the stool and onto the floor. He had meant to sit down, but his balance and equilibrium were all over the place. Lois held him up with great difficulty and moved him toward the bed, where he collapsed and soon fell into a deep sleep. She stared at Pierce in a deep sleep and set up a bed for herself on the sofa. *** The morning sun rose in the distance and Pierce Lavelle had a nasty aftertaste in his mouth as the suns rays danced around his shut eyelids, waking him from his deep sleep. He could feel the aftershock of a hangover creeping up on him as he rose slowly from under the bed covers. He couldn’t remember drinking or how he got into bed, but Lois sound asleep on the sofa brought back some memories. Intent on staying quiet, Pierce rose from under the duvet, pulled it aside and moved silently, only to trip on the trailing end and crash into the coffee table leg, leaving a load THUD echoing through the small hotel room. Lois jumped immediately and shrieked. Pierce grabbed his head as he remembered the migraine. “Shush!” he said, despite the fact he had woken Lois up. “Me, shush? Its 9:00 a.m., Pierce…” Lois mumbled under the cushion. “I’m sorry,” Pierce mumbled as he looked toward the letter he had yet to open that his grandfather had left for him. Pierce had actually had some sleep and despite the fact things seemed terrible a few days ago, he was starting to calm down and accept things. He knew his grandfather was gone, but it wasn’t goodbye, just farewell. Lois rose from under the covers of the make-shift bed, slipped into a dressing gown and moved toward the kitchen. “I’m going to make some coffee, but you need water and some pain killers for the headache, right?” Lois questioned. Although each movement triggered the conga line in his head, he moved toward the kitchen, still slightly confused as to why Lois was here, especially after the last time they had departed. He was also confused at how he was standing in nothing but his jeans. “You going to explain to me how you got the bullet wound?” Lois asked from behind the partition door. “I…eh…” Pierce said. “Your mother rang me and told me that your grandfather had died, she didn’t know where you were, so I decided I’d come and see how you were, not good by the looks of things…” Lois knew exactly what Pierce was thinking and had answered his question before he had had time to even ask it. “Its a mess Lo,” Pierce said and stared at the Panadol dissolving in the glass of water. “I can tell,” Lois said and took a sip from her coffee. “I read your file, the one seated on top of your luggage,” Lois said. The file Lois was referring to held every single bit of information Pierce had managed to accumulate on his work in Los Angeles on the Prometheus investigation. “Why didn’t you ring Rob or me?” she asked. “I couldn’t, if you read the file, then you know why…” Pierce said drinking his Panadol and regretting not buying orange juice, as it tasted revolting. Pierce coughed on a part of the panadol that hadn’t dissolved and quickly drank as much of the drink as possible as the remainder began to dissolve on his tongue. “I thought after what happened in Albany, you were going to stop this,” she said behind the rim of the coffee mug. Pierce dipped his head and knew exactly what she meant by that, he knew he deserved it, but the one good thing about Lois was that she was honest. “I know, I should have…” Pierce said and finished off his medicine. He moved into the main room again, picked up his grandfathers letter and lay on the bed. As he stared at the letter he could feel the bed move, Lois climbed up beside him and lay down next to him, resting her hand on his, a comforting gesture. “My back is killing me,” she smiled. “The sofa wasn’t exactly a master bed!” she winked. Pierce smiled to himself and leant against Lois for some comfort and opened the letter. He knew it was going to be hard to read. The first two lines made it clear: Pierce, if you are reading this then I must be dead. I am sorry I never got around to telling you of my heart condition, but there were more pressing concerns than my health. Pierce stopped and took a deep breath. Lois continued to hold his hand, as she sipped on some more coffee and gazed at the letter. Pierce skimmed through the four pages of writing and photos and all other types of documents. The one that caught his attention was the information on Kelli Stoner and Zack Midas. Pierce skipped the first two pages and read the paragraph that his grandfather had written on Kelli Stoner: Pierce, by now you probably know that Kelli had been hired by a man to spy on you, but you must know her intentions were never to hurt you. I had her followed for the last few weeks and she was very resistant with the mystery man, I never got around to getting his name, but with the threat of a man named Zack Midas, she had no choice. I know you must be hurting from the deception, but you are a kind and forgiving man, do not make enemies out of friends. Zack Midas is doing time in prison for many various crimes and is a dangerous man. We all do stupid things when our lives our threatened. I am sorry I never told you about this Pierce, but there is always hope and love out there, you just need to believe, kiddo…. The letter continued with the various crimes Zack Midas had done from assault to drunken driving to many other crimes, but Pierce could no longer read on. Somewhere inside him a little light was sparking. A happiness inside him. Although his grandfather was gone, the wording of the letter sounded as though he was speaking to him and the fact that Kelli had been forced into the situation by Adam Dick relaxed his emotions a little - he hadn’t been completely played. “He’s right you know,” Lois said. “Huh?” Pierce woke up from his daze at staring at random words. “About you being kind and forgiving,” Lois said. “Everyone deserves a second chance Pierce. You gave us and our friendship another try and we became closer friends, maybe you should forgive Kelli too. We all do stupid things you know…” Lois said and sat up. Pierce nodded his head, it was something on his to do list, but he knew he could never be with Kelli again, he could forgive her, but their friendship would take time to rebuild. Lois slid off the bed and turned around, hands on hips, her eyes staring a hole through Pierce. He knew what was coming, he was in trouble. “You never answered my question,” she said. “I…eh…well…you see, wait, what question?” Pierce slid the letter to one side. “About the bullet wound,” Lois said pointing to the bandage wrapped around his shoulder and arm, along with a spot of crimson on it. He had hurt it again when Chainz had jumped him at the Christmas party. “Another of Adam Dick’s plans. Two hit-man came to get me, they almost killed me too,” Pierce said and moved off the bed toward the mirror and stared at the bandage. He needed to clean his wound and put on a fresh bandage. The telephone rang and Pierce’s mother yelled through the answering machine. Pierce smiled at his mother’s use of bad language and moved into the bathroom. Lois remained seated on the bed, trying to remain silent as she listened to the message. “Pierce, you should probably listen to this,” Lois said in a low whisper. “I’m not going to the church Lo,” Pierce said from inside the bathroom. He was stood, using the sink as leverage as he shaved off the remainder of his stubble. He couldn’t handle churches, it was strange, yes, but for some reason every time he was in one he would have a bad premonition and couldn’t stand the sermons or listening to the eulogies. They only brought more pain to an already upsetting situation. The one thing about going to the church was the fact that Pierce’s own father would be there and he couldn’t see him. He had vowed in Harrisburg that he would never speak to his father, Brad Lavelle, ever again. “What? You mad? Pierce…” Lois ran into the bathroom and made Pierce jumped as he chucked the razor into the basin of water. “I can’t, I can’t do the whole mass thing and I can’t see my father…” Pierce groaned as he stared at the little red spot forming on his chin. He had nicked himself with the razor. Lois just leant against the rim of the bath tub and nodded her head. She stood up and helped remove Pierce’s bandage. She shivered when she saw the exit hole that had been stitched up. She took the bottle of iodine and began to clean the wound with Pierce making faces as it stung. “Come on, stop cringing, take it like a man!” she smiled. “You did this when you fell off that stupid motorbike back in Senior year, you remember?” Lois laughed and cleaned the entry wound on his front. Pierce nodded his head and winked at Lois. He was actually cheering up. Something unexpected, I know. “It stings you know,” Pierce said and wiped away the excess trickles of iodine. Lois left the room and rooted around a couple of bags, switched on the C.D. player and started singing to ‘Sweet Home Alabama‘. Pierce, still inside the bathroom, had removed his jeans and his boxers and had hopped into the shower. Something he hadn’t done in a while! He pulled the curtain closed. Lois soon re-entered and Pierce jumped, pulling a towel around his waist. “Hey!” he squeaked. “A little privacy,” Pierce said and stopped the water. “Nothing I haven’t seen before,” Lois winked to a half naked Pierce behind the shower curtain. “Eh-hem!” Pierce coughed. Lois turned her back to him and held up a pair of suit trouser, a shirt and a suit jacket. She then laughed. Pierce stared at the suit through the shower curtain, it was the same one Sarah Kennedy had made Pierce wear for the Christmas party. Not fitting for a funeral, but then again you can only wear black. Pierce emerged from the bathroom a few minutes later, fully clothed and smelling of Lynx. Lois stood, dressed in a black long dress that accentuated her feminine features, holding up a grey tie for Pierce. It took him a while to stop staring at Lois, he had missed her a lot, but not in that way. She had always been his best friend, along with Robert and with her here, it was easing his recovery. He hadn’t thought about Kelli or Adam Dick for a while now, not since he had read the letter. “…”Pierce was struggling with the tie. He had worn a clip-on during his graduation in high school and hadn’t bothered at the Christmas party. Lois smiled, took his hands off of it and began to fasten it. She had managed to tie it properly within a couple of seconds and pulled it up to meet the crook in his shirt. “Thanks,” Pierce said. Lois took a step back and noticed Pierce’s hair. “You shaved the mop!” she commentated and then sat down on the bed to put on her high heels and do her hair. “Suits you,” she remarked. Pierce smiled, ran his hand over his shaved head and then returned to the kitchen to take another panadol for the headache. It was fading, but it still felt like he was listening to somebody playing the drums badly. “One question before we go,” Lois said as she entered the kitchen. “Okay,” Pierce said, putting the medicine to one side. Lois moved closer, the gun and clip in her hand. “Four hatches are used and empty, the bullet had climbed four to the fifth. What I mean to say is were you trying to kill yourself?” Lois asked, her eyes staring directly at Pierce’s. “No Lo, I don’t know what was running through my mind last night, but I would never commit suicide, if that’s what you are asking. That gun was given to me by my grandfather as protection. That bullet is meant for one man, but I would never bring myself to use it. I’m sorry I gave you such a fright,” Pierce said putting a hand on Lois’s shoulder. “You sure? Because I was thinking a psyche evaluation is needed,” Lois smiled. “I’m fine, I’m feeling much better now,” Pierce said. “Okay, lets go, otherwise Marie is going to kill us,” Lois smiled and ran toward the door in her high heels. Pierce knocked back the panadol and took a breath mint as he closed the door and ran after Lois in her high heels, she had done track in high school and college and was now half way down the stairs by the time Pierce had managed to stop the aching in his leg. *** The church had been filled by many of Pierce‘s close relations, but also many of his grandfathers relatives and companions. His grandfathers coffin was seated just beneath the alter. Pierce couldn’t bring himself to go any further, he stood in the shadows, next to a large pillar and listened to the words of the priest and soon his uncle Keith’s eulogy for his father. What surprised Pierce was the amount of people at the funeral, each one had had a kind word to say, but the one man that should have been here, wasn’t. Pierce’s father hadn’t shown up and it didn’t surprise him. Lois had joined his mother and little sister. Pierce’s mother turned in her seat and shot a look at Pierce, but he just smiled and nodded his head. He hoped he wouldn’t have to carry the coffin, but he wanted to say goodbye one last time. “Jesus, remember me, when I come in to your Kingdom” rang through the speakers around the church as the priest said his final words. The many mourners stood up and began to leave the church to head toward the graveyard, but Pierce stayed behind. He had been greeted with many hand shakes and sorrows, but most of the people Pierce barely knew or recognised but they were important to his grandfather and he respected that. His mother, his sister and his grandfather’s other four son’s remained near the coffin. Pierce approached, his hands in his pockets, his head dipped down. The church and the atmosphere had brought back the feelings of sorrow for him. “Pierce, lad, you alright?” His Uncle Keith spoke to break the silence. His Texan accent ringing through the hollow walls of God’s holy place. Pierce nodded his head and his mother nudged his arm. “I’d just like a moment to say goodbye,” Pierce said as he stared at the open coffin. “Understood, kid,” His Uncle Lewis patted his injured shoulder and Pierce tried not to cringe and move it away. His four uncles and his mother and sister moved to the side and thanked the Priest for the nice sermon and the prayers. Lois remained behind Pierce and held his hand. Pierce stared at his grandfather and pulled out a small rosary-bead. His grandfather had been a very holy man and had forgotten his rosary-beads that day he died. Pierce placed them between his grandfathers fingers and took a step back. “You okay?” Lois asked. “I will be,” Pierce replied and watched his uncles close the coffin and lift it onto their shoulders. The graveyard was just behind the church and Pierce slowly followed behind his family. The sun had broken through the dense snow clouds that had formed over the last few days. Leaving a yellow glow over the church and graveyard. Pierce watched, as many of his grandfathers friends had soon followed behind the coffin too. An open grave awaited them as Pierce’s four uncles lowered their fathers coffin onto the rack set up by a small crane to lower the coffin into the grave. Many people were whimpering by this stage, but Pierce remained strong, he couldn’t bring himself to cry, because he knew that his grandfather had lived a happy life and he wanted to remember the good times. He watched the coffin slowly lower and watched how many people threw dirt on top of the coffin as it descended, a common ritual. Pierce took a hand-full of dirt and gently sprinkled it on top. As the coffin reached the end of the grave, the chains on the crane were removed and the Priest said one last prayer. “Farewell old man,” Pierce whispered and took a step back, away from those that had broken into prayer and tears. Lois followed quickly and saw Pierce stood at the gate, hands in his jacket and the corners of his jacket turned up against the wind. “Hey,” she said with a sweet voice. “Hey,” he replied. “You want to wait till everyone leaves and say goodbye properly, by yourself?” Lois asked nicely. Pierce straightened his back and looked around at the scene unfolding at his grandfather’s grave. “You know what, I think I’m okay with this. I didn’t think I would be, but I said my farewell to him and the only way to remember him, is through his kind words and memories,” Pierce said and looked away from Lois. “You sure?” she asked. “Very,” he replied. “I’m going to miss him, but he really opened my eyes these last few months and became a great friend. I’ll remember him for that,” Pierce said and began to walk along the gravel and stones back toward the car. Lois followed, struggling in her high heels as she traversed across the mucky, snowy grass patches. “Had to be grass, didn’t it,” she mumbled to herself as she opened the door and unstuck her heels from a mucky patch. “Hey, you hungry? There’s this great little place,” Pierce smiled and climbed into the car. “Yeah, sure, and on the way there you need to tell me about this Christmas party,” Lois smiled and shut the door. Pierce started the engine and drove away from the graveyard, saying one last goodbye in his mind to his grandfather, after that he didn’t look back. *** The red mill was a bar that had been serving martini’s and drinks to the many denizens of Los Angeles - both famous and infamous - for the last twenty years. The main room was based on the 1950’s German beer house. Its seats held red satin cushions with oak chairs and oak tables. People would sit and wait, whilest preserving conversation with moderately fashioned waiters in red and black outfits. The very back rooms were VIP and occasionally used for night club shows or for various celebrations like Weddings. As Pierce and Lois came into the bar, two males had slipped off two stools and left. Pierce offered Lois her seat and sat next to her. A bartender who recognised Pierce smiled and came over. “What I can I get ya?” he asked while cleaning out a few glasses. “I’ll have a martini,” Lois said and crossed her legs, pulling her black slit dress over, to prevent the many onlookers who had already taken to her. “I’ll just have a beer, Russo,” Pierce said. “Gotcha,” Russo replied and poured out the two servings and slid them down the bar toward Pierce and Lois. Pierce and Lois raised their glasses and clinked them and at the same time and both said “To John Lavelle.” Pierce was feeling very at ease sitting next to Lois in the bar - of course they had been childhood sweat hearts and Pierce had fallen for her in High school and in Albany, but they were still good friends and knew the others ways and habits inside out. He found this calming and relaxed a little. “You never told me about this Christmas Party you went to?” Lois said over the squeaking of someone in the back testing out the karaoke machine. “It was going really well and then …” Pierce replied and took a sip of his drink, not wishing to recap his humility. “Pierce, I’m a lawyer, not a mind reader,” Lois joked and had managed to put a smile on Pierce’s face. '…Sarah got me to play a game, well it involved writing the name of a famous celebrity on your head. Anyway, she wouldn't join in and I decided I'd go to the bathroom and check out who she had written on my forehead...' Pierce took another gulp from his beer. Lois just laughs, but says nothing and lets Pierce continue. '…I get to the bathroom, the female bathroom that is, I couldn't let Sarah follow me into the men's! Just a little too open. Anyway, we get there and we play a questions game. The questions got quite heated... It's all going really well and we were kind of getting closer, anyway I turn around and see my name on my forehead,” Pierce stops and looks around at the crowd forming behind them for the Karaoke machine. 'Yup, figures...' Lois smiles at the brim of her martini. 'Huh?' Pierce replied and takes another sip from his beer. 'Continue and it is called female intuition...' Lois smiles. 'Well, I leant in to ... and then this man disrupts the whole moment and then brings up my incident with the Prometheus drug and I kind of left,' Pierce replied. 'YOU LEFT!' Lois slapped his arm. 'What are you? 12? Pierce those were some big hints...you are blind,' Lois says and places her martini glass onto the beer mat. 'Okay, firstly, ouch! And secondly, I know I am an idiot...' Pierce says and places his drink onto the mat. 'Well, what's Sarah like?' Lois asks intrigued by the situation Pierce is in. 'She's tall, extremely beautiful. She's got this cute button nose and when she's frustrated she has this adorable look. She's got straight blonde hair and blue eyes and she has...' 'Okay, stop there Romeo. Why when women ask what's she like, you immediately jump to appearance and sometimes cup size? By you, I mean men in all their glory' Lois smiled. 'Not appearance. What is she like?' 'She's kind, sweet, she's extremely caring, loyal and excellent at her job, she's respectful and funny and has a way with words...' Pierce says with a little smile on his face. 'Do you like her?' Lois cuts straight to the point. 'I - well - I don't know, I never got to and well I've kind of ruined my chance now, right?' Pierce leant against the bar and began to swirl a matchstick around the waxed-oak counter top. Lois smiled and drank more of her martini. 'You LIKE her, you want KISS her, you want HUG her...' Lois began to joke. Pierce turned around and placed a hand on Lois's lip. 'Lo, if you want to do Sandra Bullock impressions try singing it better,' Pierce said with a smile. Lois immediately shot off the chair and headed toward the stage. 'Right, I gotcha...' she smiled. Pierce shocked, jumped off the chair and ran toward her. 'Okay...yes I like her...' Pierce said with a hint of worry and bashfulness. 'And...YOU WANT TO KISS HER...' Lois continued to sing. 'YES...now can you please not announce it to a group of drunks...' Pierce said. 'Sure,' She smiled and headed back to the bar. 'What?' Pierce said, dumbfounded. 'I had never intended on doing that in the first place. I’m practically tone deaf! I just wanted you to admit your feelings ...' Lois said and ordered another martini. 'You are good,' Pierce said. 'I know,' Lois said with a hint of ego. 'Dave seems to like it too,' she smiled. 'Ah yes, your other half. What's he like?' Pierce said with a smile. Lois patted Pierce on the leg. 'Oh no! We aren't finished with you yet,' she said. 'You haven't completely messed things up, most likely she feels a little stupid for throwing herself into the deep end, but she seems cool and I think you may have a chance, Romeo,” she said and took a sip from her second martini. “...So you are hot huh?' she laughed. 'Hey! Okay, this isn't funny,' Pierce said and ordered a packet of crisps. 'Oh, it is, believe me,' Lois smiled. 'When do you see her next?' she asked. 'At the pay-per view,' Pierce said and took a long gulp from his beer. 'Then it’s settled, you'll go talk to you her and tell her how you feel...' Lois said and paid the barman. 'Huh?“ Pierce said practically choking on his beer. “What do I do? Encase you haven't noticed, Lo. I'm not exactly the greatest with relationships. I've only had two, well not even two...' Pierce said and paid Russo for the remainder of the drinks. “I see…” Lois smiled. “My flight is tomorrow Lo, a little help,” Pierce pleaded. “Oh, it’s going to be a long night and I have a court case tomorrow. Alright, come on. We can discuss this on the way back to the hotel,” Lois smiled and pulled on her own jacket and soon left the Red Mill. Pierce followed her out into the snow once more and toward the car. He knew things were starting to improve and with a lack of Prometheus and Adam Dick, he could see things getting better. He just needed to believe in the words of his grandfather: There is always hope and love out there, you just need to believe
