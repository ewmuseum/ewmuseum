The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch. 6

10 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10th June The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch. 6 Continuation of Ch. 5 Black Goose “Hello young man,” A strange voice echoed behind me. I ignored his presence and indulged myself deeper into a white martini on the rocks. “You seem a little upset, perhaps I could offer some advice!” He stated, still standing behind me. “What are you? A priest?” I said, waiting for the alcohol toxins to affect my bloodstream to remove the heart ache. “No, something else,” He said and sat himself opposite me. He was a tall man, with a lot of wrinkles along his grey eyes, which gave him a wise, venerable appearance to contradict his flashy black suit and bright red shirt. He removed his hat and dangled his very expensive platinum Rolex before my eyes, before indulging himself too; into the drinks menu. “Is it alright if I sit here?” He asked. “You already are, doesn’t matter what I think,” I replied, still wondering when the affect would happen. “You won’t get drunk on a martini,” The venerable man said. “They don’t have tequila,” I announced. “A man with taste, see that’s why I am here. From the moment you walked into this bar, the ‘Black Goose’, I knew there was something inside you, something I have been looking for, for a while now,” He said - swirling the little stick with an olive on the end around and around his martini glass before taking it out and eating it. Something a mafia boss would do and then say “Pleasure doing business with you,”. I realise now that I have got to stop the movie marathon, to which myself and Rob do on a Saturday. Everything I do and see reminds me of scenes from a movie, I’m becoming paranoid and slowly begin to think that an alien mother- ship would land and beam me up, or like in the old gangster movies, a load of men, dressed in long posh coats, will pull out old fashioned pistols and I’ll be on the receiving end of their bullets. “I never got your name,” I stated, now pushing aside my drink, to which was having no affect in intoxicating my bloodstream. “That’s not important at this time; see, if I told you, my boys would have to kill you,” He looked me sternly in the eyes. Then looked over his shoulders and it was then I spotted two large men, at least 15 stone each, wearing posh black trousers and blue shirts with a gun holster under each arm. I laughed but it wasn’t because I thought this man was being funny, it was because I was now extremely nervous. “You think I’m a priest and I’ve come to you, to help you!” He stated bluntly. “I don’t believe in God or the words in which Priests speak, god doesn’t help anyone,” I said, pushing back on my chair. “That’s very true, I’m not a holy man either,” He said, with a hint of a cynical tone. “Here’s my card, I’ll be calling you sometime kid. Believe me when I say you hold something I would like to make use of, you have a key ingredient in my little situation. So, see you around kid.” He wrote something on the back of the card and flicked it across the table. He rose from his seat and carefully placed his hat back over his head. Walking past me, I waited for him and his two henchmen to leave, before I picked up the card. It was a small white card, with blue bubbled writing of “Black Goose” on the front, on the back of the card lay his messy writing from which all I could read was - ‘debt-collector’ open for business. I was confused by the term - ‘debt-collector’, but then again nothing is ever what it seems. As the old saying goes: ‘don’t judge a book by its cover’. Is something I have grown to begin to understand. But, this man and this card; I hadn’t even begun scratched the surface. I was in a strange place, Boston. In wet clothes, a broken heart, no friends and no place to stay. The ‘Black Goose’ was turning out to be a disappointment too, the drinks didn’t even get me drunk. I decided it was time to leave and find an ‘off-license’ to buy some tequila from or even vodka. “Excuse me!” I proclaimed, hoping the young male, dressed in a posh white waistcoat would hear me. “Yes sir. How may I help you?” He asked. “Cheque please, one martini,” I said. “One moment sir,” As I waited for the waiter to return, I continued to the turn the card from back to front, trying to make sense of this new situation. My life seemed full of them. As I sat in this seat here in Boston, I couldn’t help but feel that I let my mother and sister down; by not being in Albany. They needed me and instead I decided to come after a lost cause, Lilly. Who it seems has forgotten about me, George and her life in Albany already. “There you are sir,” The waiter came back with a small silver tray carrying the cheque and price for his tip. I paid the man a tip of $5 and paid the bill, which for a martini seemed very ridiculous, $14.50c. I rose from seat and putting the card in my back pocket, left the ‘black goose’ and remained standing under the shelter of its porch waiting for the rain to die down, just a little. A small dark car pulled up toward the bar, its automatic window rolled down softly as the rain trickled into its inside. Lilly’s head peered out of the window. “Pierce, please just come in from the rain. We need to talk,” She said, opening the door to her hired car. “Okay,” I said, now moving forward out of the rain - a smile upon my face. Perhaps it was a chance for me and not a lost cause. I slid into the passenger seat and closed the door, winding the window back up to protect the cars interior from the torrential rain shower. “I’m glad you came,” Lilly stated with a smile on her face as she started the exhaust up once more. The car jolted into reverse and then again into first gear and we began our drive up onto the empty street and into the bright lights. “Where are we going?” I asked. “Somewhere safe,” Somewhere safe, I said to myself, why did we need to go somewhere safe. I peered out of the window and watched the rain dancing along the pathway as we drove up alongside all the traffic. Rendezvous The car came to a stop on top of a hillside, a desolate area, its view stretched out among Boston, giving a clear view of the small little estates and the main city itself. The sun was beginning to rise in the distance and the early morning radio came on, as Lilly sat still in the car. I leaned back, feeling a strange sense of comfort and safety wash over me as I was seated in this car, with Lilly. It was then I realised I would always wait for this woman, it was something about her that made me feel human in contrast to the life I was living alone. “Hope you don’t mind being so far from humanity up on this hill,” She said, not really implying any question or jester. “Not at all, it’s kind of peaceful, compared to the rest of the city,” I replied. “So, why are we here?” I asked. “I needed to talk to you, somewhere, where nobody knew I was going,” She turned off the cars engine. “I used to come up here as a kid on my bike with my brother; we would sit on this very spot and drink some pop and listen to the cheering fans from the closest stadium, times I really miss,” Lilly unbuckled her seat-belt and leaned back, she had a tear rolling down her cheek. “Whose the guy?” I wanted to find out as much as I could about why Lilly had gone. “A man, who I don’t want you to get involved with Pierce, it’s dangerous,” Lilly had become serious now, she wiped her tear off her cheek and stared me in the face with her beautiful green eyes. “Why?” I couldn’t understand why it was she had become so covert all of a sudden. “Remember I told you about my brother and fathers murder, and how the police mentioned to my mother to drop the case. You know why?” Lilly didn’t give me a chance to reply. “In Albany you have three gangs, ‘The Blue Religion’, ‘Murder Dolls’ and the ‘Genocide Hogs’. Not great names, yet they are very violent men and women. Well, in Boston we have gangs too, only we have one left now. A ruler, kind of the mafia and runner of the black market around here. My father was murdered by this man, he calls himself the debt-collector. He tends to certain businesses that need to be dealt with. I found out my father was running something, something I shouldn’t have found out but did and now I know it was him,” Lilly began to become tense, looking out of the rear-view mirror to see if we were followed. “The ‘debt-collector’?” I said, now remembering the old man I had met in the Black Goose, I pulled out the card from my back pocket. “Funny you should mention that name, I met a man in the bar who gave me this. Said he would like me to work for him or was it more, I had something he wanted, not too sure really. I was too busy focusing on the alcohol menu to listen to the old man,” I gave her the card. Lilly froze, as though she realised everything she had planned had gone terribly wrong. She began to cry a little, tears running down both cheeks now; Lilly was always the sensitive type and I liked that. She wore her feelings on her sleeve, most of the time. “This can’t be happening, not to you too,” She said, behind the tears. “What do you mean me too?” I put my hand on hers and held it. “His name is Marvin Harrington, he gives these cards to those he wants to kill, kind of like the kiss of death. Which means you hold something he wants,” She said, throwing the card into the back seat and lunging herself into me for a hug. I embraced her tightly, she needed to be held at this moment. “What could I have done? I only arrived today,” I stated. “The man I was ‘seeing’, was Marvin’s son, he probably saw you and followed you to the Black Goose, notifying his dad, the boss,” This was the icing on the cake. I now held a knack for getting myself into these strange situations. First getting expelled from College, moving to Albany, getting a low-paying job, meeting my twenty eight year old grandmother and involving myself in Lilly’s affairs. I don’t think I ever held normality in my life. For once I just wished I could be that guy, who goes to work at certain hours of the week, wears a nice suit, gets paid well, comes home to a wife and kids with a white picket fence. A dream I wish I could have for real. “Why are you dating this man?” I asked. “Because he was one of Marvin’s henchmen which killed my family,” She held me tighter now, my wet clothing began to stick to my skin. I began to shiver, the nerves kicking in. “But why are you dating him?” I said once more. “I’m following him, reading his mobile, finding out what he did to my family,” Lilly said. “Family? Your mother is dead as well?” I was now extremely anxious. “She died a year ago, found by next door neighbour, two simple shots to the back of the head. That’s when I moved to Albany. I was studying forensics here, but, I didn’t feel safe being here anymore and I moved to Albany where I was abused once more by George until you came into my life. I won’t let them kill you as well, Pierce. You have to leave Boston,” Lilly broke the hug and moved back into her seat, her top was now wet as well. I couldn’t help but take a quick glance, my attention soon turned once more to Lilly’s green eyes. “I won’t go without you,” I stated. “It’s too dangerous,” She said, “I need to stay; I’m helping the police investigation. I’ll be perfectly safe,” “I still won’t go, even if you have two police men staying with you every night,” I looked out toward the morning sun. A few hours had passed now. “Haven’t you heard of explosives?” I looked at her. “If they can’t shoot to kill you, they’ll blow you and the two coppers up, KA-BOOM,” I opened the door for some fresh air. The rain had stopped. “Please Pierce, don’t make this difficult for me,” She opened her door as well. “I won’t lose you to these scum, let the police deal with it, please!” I stood out of the car following after her. Lilly stood with her arms folded next to a large tree, Boston’s national tree in fact. She took a deep breath of fresh air and faced me. “It is up to me, to reveal the truth and to stop all the murders,” I couldn’t help but feel that Lilly felt somewhat responsible for the death of her family, that she felt she should have gone with them. To end the suffering which I knew she held. I felt incredibly stupid after recapping those vital moments in the coffee house when I blabbed on and on about my parents. A family which she didn’t have anymore and yet, she listened to how much I complained about having one. I felt like a fool on the first date for not shutting my mouth and listening to her for once. “I’m sorry,” I said. “For what?” Lilly asked, confused by my sudden change in temperament. “For complaining so much about having a family, when you lost yours,” I approached her and stood beside her, gazing down toward the view of Boston. It looked completely different during the daylight hours. The houses glistened in the distance, small cars of many colours looked like ants beneath my size 8 ½ shoe. “Don’t worry about it, I heard about your mother. I’m sorry for not being there to help you, like you did for me.” Lilly walked back to her car. “I’ll come with you to Albany, Pierce. I’m too scared to remain here anymore,” “What about the investigation?” I implied. “I thought you wanted me out of this?” She asked. “I do, but, won’t the cops come looking for you?” I knew I needed to make up for my rash decision in implying the wrong facts. “I’ll ring them from Albany, on a pay phone,” She looked at me and smiled. “Come on, we’ve got a plane to catch,” “Where will you stay?” I asked. “Can’t stay in the Harbrook residents. Too easy to find me,” “You can stay with Rob, Lois and I,” I looked at her and hoped into the rented car. “Thanks,” Once more the car jolted into reverse and Lilly drove the car down the way we came last night and out into the city. We were heading for Boston airport for a safe trip home. “I’m glad you saw sense,” I stated as she pulled out onto the freeway toward the airport. Misinterpretation The distance between myself and Lilly during the plane trip was antagonising. Countless times I would try to say something and she would constantly ignore me. Our flight was destined to be at least two and a half hours long, which gave me enough time to see what was wrong with Lilly. “What is it?” I asked - noticing now that she had become tense. “You haven’t spoken to me since we boarded, what have I done?” I asked. “Nothing. I’m just worried they’ll come and get me,” She replied, turning to face me and holding my hand. “Thanks for helping me, Pierce. Nobody has ever cared for me so much before, I appreciate all of your help, you’re a great friend,” She said. Yes you heard it, I am a ‘great-friend’. The “f” word was the last word I wanted to hear; Lilly was becoming important to me and I feel extremely close to her, but, when the one person you relate to most turns around and says you’re a great friend, you can’t help but cringe. I knew I would have to wait a while, maybe even a long time before she realises I care for her. Maybe I don’t have the attraction her last few boyfriends held. “What is wrong?” She asked, breaking my thought process. “Nothing, I was just thinking about the food menu on this flight,” I paused a little, I knew I would punch myself for this later. “Everything is going to be okay, you’ll be staying with me and my two great friends and the apartment block is always under high surveillance. Nobody is going to hurt you; I promise,” “I hope so,” Lilly said. “I’ll be glad to see Robert and Gavin again, and it will be interesting to meet this Lois, your first kiss and first love; is it not difficult for you having her under the same roof as you?” Lilly asked, looking out the plane’s window, down toward the grass land and whatever houses she could see from our height. “It isn’t that difficult. Lois is with Rob now and I’ve accepted that fact. Lois and I were just friends and to be honest, we should have never dated, it caused a lot of strain on our friendship after dating. It is a bad idea for best of friends to date I think, but, then again Rob and Lois are extremely good friends and make a great couple. I think she’ll be glad to have some feminine company. You know? To go shopping with, discuss womanly things, I think the whole manly attention is taking its toll,” I covered my tracks well. “It will be nice to meet those you care a lot about,” Lilly looked at me. “You don’t speak much about your mother and sister, well not since that incident with George and your father arriving,” She paused. “What date is the court case, I don’t think I’ll be able to deal with that,” I placed my hand on Lilly’s. Lilly was the type of woman who always considered other peoples feelings, she feared violence and hatred, which made her susceptible to the type of characters like George and Harrington’s son. Men that bully their girlfriends and hurt them. I knew I would have to protect Lilly from that side of her life for a long time; at least until she became strong enough to ignore it. “I’ll be right there with you, all George can do is stare at you, nothing else, I promise. Relax Lilly, everything is going to be fine,” I smiled. A tall man, with a shaved head continued to stare at myself and Lilly, ever so slightly. He would move his head the odd time and look over the rim of his paper. He was an air host staff member, but, I became exceedingly paranoid that I was being followed, the kiss of death kept playing on my mind. Too many movies, too many bad scenes: “Say hello to my little friend,”. I was just waiting for a pistol to show up from under the newspaper. I returned his stare until he stood up. “Is everything alright sir?” The man asked. I was relieved that it wasn’t a gun, instead it was a meal tray. “Everything is fine!” I looked away. “The flight will be landing in a few moments, please fasten your seat-belts,” With that the man walked back to his seat and sat down, he continued his stare and unbuttoned the top two buttons on his clean blue shirt. Albany and safety were only a few moments away. Home Sweet Home “Pierce, where the hell did you go man?” Rob yelled while still seated on the leather sofa. “I went to Boston,” I replied, waiting to tell everyone that Lilly is staying with us. “Why’d you go to Boston?” Rob asked again, still confused. “To get Lilly,” I said. “Is she okay?” Lois asked, at least somebody was more concerned about the true facts, rather than where I had been. “I’m fine,” Lilly replied. “You must be Lois, nice to meet you,” Lilly entered the room and shook hands with Lois. “Lilly is going to be staying here for a while,” I said. “Oh really!” Rob now standing next to me nudged my shoulder. “Hey Lilly, welcome back to Albany, Gavin’s been asking for you,” Rob said. Rob and Lois holding hands disappeared down the corridor toward their own bedroom. Lilly stood in awe at the site of our fancy apartment. “Not bad for a college drop-out!” I laughed. “Indeed,” She smiled. “Your grandfather is extremely generous,” She ran her hand along the glass coffee table and stood next to the veranda which over looked the city of Albany. “You should wait here for a little while longer, it‘s almost pitch black,” I said. “He’s a great man, bar the odd play-boy mansion girl he marries,” “I know, I saw your incident in the bathroom in pizza hut,” She laughed. “Not my most memorable moment,” I blushed a little, remembering how Lilly entered into the bathroom while I had my head down a toilet, throwing up my lunch after meeting “Bimbo Barbie”. “You can sleep in my room, it doesn’t smell, just so you know,” I smiled. Lilly moved forward toward my room. She smiled and sat down on the bed. “You like guitars?” She asked. “I love guitars, my passion apart from wrestling,” I sat next to her and picked up my pride and joy - the metallic red fender with Hendrix on the back. “I could teach you sometime, it is extremely simple,” I said. “It is getting late, perhaps another day,” She looked at the bedroom and realised she didn’t bring a spare set of clothing or pyjamas. I stood up and rooted through my closet. I handed her a pair of light men’s tracksuit bottoms and a wife beater. “I know it’s not your style, but, I think you’ll still look ‘off-the-hook’ in these,” I laughed. “Thanks,” She smiled. “Where are you going to sleep?” She asked. “On the sofa,” I grabbed my pyjama bottoms and removed my old shirt from the day before. “Could you sleep in here with me? It’s just I don’t feel all too safe,” She moved over to the right side of the double bed. I stood still, shocked and very much taken by surprise. I knew it wasn’t the same as being in bed with someone who loves you back, but, Lilly the woman of my dreams wanted me to protect her. To watch over her, the only downside was, she took my side of the bed. What is it with double beds and relationships - having to have a certain side of the bed. I liked the right side. I smiled and sat on the left side. “Of course I’ll stay and protect you,” I smiled. Imagine what Rob and Lois would say tomorrow morning, when we emerge out of this room together. I thought to myself as I stared at Lilly. I knew this murder mystery wasn’t over, but I was glad to have her out of Boston and safely in my arms. Lilly had gone into the bathroom and changed into my spare clothing. I removed my trousers and changed into my pyjama bottoms. Sliding into the covers I got ready to sleep. Lilly returned with my trousers dangling round her hips and my wife beater acted like a maternity dress, but, she looked so cute. She clambered into bed and turned off the light. I lay silently and watched her begin to drift asleep. Her skin caught the rays of the moon light as the night sky began to fall. Another day was over. Only it wasn’t a typical day.
