An Old School Arrival

11 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11th October In the faint distance, I hear a car door slam and shut out on the street. I notice it almost instantly, like it messes with my senses. My time in Siberia seems to have sharpened my hearing, not dulled them like you’d expect. I was over-sensitive to everything around me since I returned, returned to New York. Currently I was sitting around O’Grady’s gym, taking in the atmosphere. Sweat pours from the very core of this establishment, its pungent aroma intoxicating the surroundings. There was no way you could get away from the intense, bitter and overpowering smell. It took over and enveloped you. Not that I cared, it’d been a long time but O’Grady’s still felt like home. It was home. The set up was pretty simple. The old-school ring erected in the centre of the gym, taut and kept in good shape for any wrestlers that wanted to work out on it. The ring was littered with blood and spit and sweat from every orifice on that canvas. It held many dreams and most of them it never gave back. Running my hands over it I could feel the tension and exhilaration, the excitement and anxiety that carried with each and every match. It wasn’t just a tool of the trade; it was the very extension of the wrestler’s mind, body and soul. I used it to express myself in a way no other object can. For me it felt right to be inside that ring, or any one for that matter, and use it to bring my talent to the masses, give back as it were. However, lately all I have seen is disrespect and misuse of such a sanctified ground within the community. And there were two words that epitomised such blatant misuse and attacks on the tradition and glamour of the sport. Hardcore and extreme. These two words categorically obliterated everything that was good in the name of wrestling. They’ve brought it right down to trash and settled it nicely in the gutter between pornography and destitution. It made me sick to my stomach that people could tarnish such a thing but they had and I was sick at this. Sick and tired of the bullshit. The rest of the gym was pretty standard. A couple of boxing bags for warm ups, one or two vending machines for power bars and drinks and little else of value. O’Grady had stuck to his guns and refused to renovate the gym, no matter who complained about it. He was adamant that he would not become a corporate sell-out as he called it to himself and would not change the way the gym looked purely to bring in customers. Simply because he didn’t need them, he got by no matter what. Dusty, faded wooden panel lined the floor and most of the walls. Where it stopped, mirrors began to allow any Narcissuses to get a good look at themselves while they pumped their guns with the one dumbbell set in the gym. In this way the Narcissus could get a full three-sixty degree look at his bulging biceps and firm abs and I remember some guys coming in and doing exactly that. The wood panel was stained and tired as well, more than likely sweat, oozing forth from the foundations, like a necessary ingredient to keep the damn thing alive. It was probably true too. Right now though, the gym was empty and lit only by the old fluorescent lights that have hung from the ceiling for decades in here. It was patchy at best and I can remember how when you used the ring that half of you was bathed in light while the other cloaked in shadow. It was a nice symmetry and looked almost poetic to some. The rattle of the keys outside the door alerted me now and I hoped that it was who I was thinking of. It should be Dan, though it may not have to be. The jingle-jangle of the keys followed by a swift crap let me know it was him. You always could tell with Dan. The man had been born and raised in Ireland, but just like myself had outgrown the paltry wrestling scene in the country. It was too easy for him he claimed and I wouldn’t doubt it. O’Grady had a knack to him that was calculating but instinctual; nothing fazed him in the ring but he was a man possessed because of it. The ring held a hold over him like no other and instead of controlling and harnessing the power for himself, he preferred to as he called it ‘project its feelings through him.’ It doesn’t sound too bad on paper but most things don’t. It’s when they go into practice that they can all fall apart. Dan didn’t so much as project as spew forth everything. He was an amalgamation of emotion and passion and he just let the whole thing flow. It was a wonder to see him whenever he wrestled but woe betide anyone in the ring with him. I had that dubious pleasure once, and once was enough! Finally the keys clicked and you heard the angled motion as the keys continued to jangle as he opened the door. The stout, stocky man entered the room and immediately sucked in a huge lungful of air like there was no tomorrow. And according to him there never was. ‘Ah I love the smell of sweat in the morning!’ Dan was carrying a brown paper bag in one hand, nestled against his shoulder and carrying his keys in another. Standing legs akimbo he was much shorter than myself by at least a half a foot and was my senior by almost twenty years. He was entering his prime just as I was born. Now, those times were long gone. He noticed that I hadn’t said a word and addressed me as such. ‘Something wrong Jack?’ I looked up a little more at him and pondered it for a moment. In this moment came Dan’s reply. ‘Your return match I suspect. Come on, just let me get this in the kitchen and I want you in the ring.’ He wandered through another door in to the back and left me to my devices. Slowly I pulled myself off the floor and headed towards the ring. Strangely, though I had intended to never leave Siberia I still had brought the one apparel that I never could have used there; my wrestling gear. So, it was still in prime nick and would be for some time to come. It’s like I knew that I would need it at some stage. Funny how your brain works. Walking up to the ring using the steps I slowly brushed my feet on the outside apron, before I pulled myself through the ropes. It was always the way to have proper etiquette and appreciation for the ring and I followed it dutifully. It mattered an awful lot and to see that the majority of wrestlers who simply ignore this is astounding. Again disrespect. Once I was in the ring I took a look around and admired the feeling, the anticipation that I felt each and every time I entered the squared circle. Nothing held me like that and this ring was no exception. Within moments O’Grady had joined me and entered also, honouring the etiquette just as I had. ‘Alright so you’re up for a match this week, your very first match in the ring for what…two years?’ ‘Yeah, pretty much.’ ‘Hmm, this could take some time then. You ready for it?’ ‘Go for it.’ So we went. Grappling, trying out holds, each of us wanting to outsmart and outmatch one another. It all flowed back into me and had a rippling effect, subconsciously remembering and enjoying each trick and ploy that we would use. It was all intimately there still; it just had to be woken up again. For two hours we did this, no words, simple technique and skills, tiring ourselves out, then pushing again and again. There was no let up but I was glad, because for once I forgot about my pain, I forgot about the damnation hanging over my head. I was at peace. Finally we halted. ‘So…you think you’re ready?’ And I was. Nothing had been lost in the two years and in a way I felt in the greatest shape of my life. I had been lost to the world but I had found renewed purpose in that I wanted to bring everyone to heel so that I could show them the power of tradition. I didn’t care in the method; they would simply learn that old school was the only school. I would teach them the ways in which they have lost, the etiquette and manners, the simple basic rules that they ignored and blasphemed every day when they walked into the ring. I would show them respect… …And how they had lost their own. I was ready for battle, ready to triumph…ready for AWC. I nodded my head and O’Grady beamed.
