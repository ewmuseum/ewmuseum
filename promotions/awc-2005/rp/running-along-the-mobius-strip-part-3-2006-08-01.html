Running Along the Mobius Strip (Part 3)

1 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/1/06 Eventually I stood up after reaching a moment of clarity where the only thing that made sense to me was to walk the direction I perceived to be north, a straight shot away from the church exit. I crossed the street, maneuvering past herds of vehicles driven by angry motorists all clenching their steering wheels at precise angles to ensure perfect automotive control. I had placed a certain trust in these drivers as to ensure the safety of my own meanderings and had luckily avoided any collisions that could have quite possibly injured me. And with much practice, I soon became quite adept at crossing streets. I saw others attempt to one up me by crossing streets themselves, but anyone else would invariably be struck quite forcefully by the speeding vehicles. No- my knack for crossing streets was unparalleled. I was the best that I knew of. As luck would have it, after the successful passage of several streets, I came across a discarded flyer that chronicled symptoms of methamphetamine addiction. It had become such a profitable problem for drug rehabilitation centers that addicts were being hunted with ample bounties upon their capture. The immediate effects of methamphetamine included euphoria, aggression, increased activity, decreased fatigue, jerky movements, hyper alertness, and grinding teeth among a load of other things. I figured meth addicts, then, would be easy to spot, but the flyer also warned that there is no profile for a meth addict. Anyone could be an addict. Maybe I too was an addict, and I just didn’t know it yet. My plans were finally coming to fruition as I created them on the fly. If I were, in fact, Jesus Christ, I would need to prove my identity by gathering a group of apostles to do my bidding. There are no better apostles than meth addicts. They always need to be occupied with some sort of project and they never sleep. There was an address on the flyer for some rehab center, but I never put much stock in addresses. I hated the things- don’t know why. They just rubbed me the wrong way. Instead, I had a far more fool-proof method to finding meth addicts. I would just listen for the sound of grinding teeth. It would come out at night, when all the world would be silently sleeping save for the meth addicts, wandering around in their chemically insomniac zombie struts. They were like crickets on a hot summer night. I distinctly remember them chirping- chirping all night long, and I couldn’t sleep. I opened up my bedroom window once and started screaming. “Shut the fuck up! Shut the fuck up!” But the inconsiderate bastards kept on chirping, and I kept on yelling and tearing at my hair until I was red in the face. And when all was said and done, the police took me away to a cell. It was unbelievable. They said I was disturbing the peace! I slept that night on a concrete ledge in a holding cell. The gray drabness, I thought, was soothing. If I hadn’t been locked in, maybe I wouldn’t have wanted to leave so strongly. But I was locked in, and all the while, I could still hear those mother fucking crickets, their chirping unrestrained as if their collective desire was to deprive me of my last shred of sanity. And here they were again, chirping, as I tried to hear past their constant screeching my ears combing through the silence interrupted as I waited for the grinding of teeth, some glorious symphony of jaws pressed shut and scraping top against bottom; bottom against top. The sound pinched a little hole in the cricket whine like the marching of an army, each soldier slightly off tempo. And that hole grew as if burning, the chirping ground between the teeth gnashing back and forth against each other. The teeth were grinding so avidly that I felt the earth quake, the shocks and tremors guiding an unsteady path towards the noise. I followed it, trying to keep my footing while dodging crumbling masses of building that shook loose and crashed in a steel, deadly sleet that left the streets around me freshly cratered. Car alarms rang out and dogs barked frantically, but the grinding of teeth still buzzed through, cutting like a circular saw through the softer parts of skull. The rain threaded down like saliva in torrential calypso as I pounded onward through it. The streetlights and the skyline flickered just out of synch with each other at first before the intervals gapped, randomly recycling to a brief instant of near synchronization again, albeit by accident. The rain, now tangled into watery vines, swayed back and forth from the force of the thundering teeth dragging across each other like two rusted utensils. And I saw myself as an old man, sitting at the only chair in front of a small, round table in a tiny room with no wallpaper. The old man sat, scraping his knife in between the prongs of his fork, both utensils crusted with remnants of old food. There was no plate in front of me. All the while, I kept on running. The rain grew thicker, engulfing me like some used placenta as I shook off connected gobs from my wrists. I trudged across the swampy streets as the old man shakily brought the fork towards the vicinity of his wide open mouth, the skin around his lips cracked and wrinkled and the lips around his mouth growing white with the loss of tint. His glaucoma made this endeavor painstaking as his eyes rested like lightless orbs behind clouded flesh. He stabbed at his toothless gums and thought the taste of his own trickling blood was supper. The running had slowed to a crawl as I gripped onto imprints in puddles and pulled myself towards the sound, playing chaos on my stereo fields. It came from one direction, and then the next, and here I was, right in the fucking middle of it all. The man ran a dry tongue, each bud swollen individually visible, around his gums, sucking the last trails of iron flavor from the perimeters of his mouth. His lips wrenched into a challenging smile, one that was broken and healed out of shape. He savored even the smallest victory and expected failure in every aspect of life. But it didn’t wear the tiniest scrap of will that survived inside. And here, with all my physical capabilities, the most minor setback leaves me facedown in a pool of water that collected and bubbled together, the froth gathered to fit around my head like a mold where it penetrated the surface. My ears bobbing in and out of submersion, the sonic frequencies dove and glided, the grinding even becoming an irritant. And then a dull series of prods to my lower back activated my reflexes, forcing me to turn my face from the water to my disturbers. “We’ve got a live one!” At first, I thought it was the old man I had envisioned with the silverware, but as the haze settled and the water drooled out of my eyes, this man was far too groomed to be the same old man. The mistake was easily accounted for as all old men look the same. It always perplexed me too, that as men age, their ears and noses keep growing to frighteningly disproportional sizes. Their posture, in turn, is pulled forward. Their backs arch, and they hike up their pants, securing them tightly to keep their spines in place so they don’t wilt and droop like long dead flowers. In the days before belts, even, old men would droop hideously, their faces dragging against the ground as they hobbled forth with deadweight arms at their sides and lumbar vertebrae jutting upwards at their mutant apex. Then as I came to, being pulled to my feet by a younger pair, one male, one female. They both wore matching ponchos, an insignia from my pamphlet printed in yellow on the chest of each poncho. They were tired, irritable from the rain and from the late night reports of addicts passed out in alleyways, freezing to death in the downpour. The man shined a light in my eyes, examining me for any serious defects while the woman retrieved a woolen shawl from the back of their van and wrapped it around my shoulders. “We’ll get you somewhere warm,” said the younger male. But I wasn’t listening. My sights were focused on the older man, who I came to realize wasn’t even as old as I had first suspected. “Hah! Middle aged at best,” I confirmed aloud, mostly to myself. His ears, however, stuck off the sides of his head, red at the edges from being in the rain at nighttime, even in the summer. His nose, too, it came to a sharp point, the tip bluntly pointed towards the ground as the wide nostrils hooked around from the sides. The bridge of the nose led to bushy eyebrows- Sam Waterson caliber brows- that furrowed closer to each other than most eyebrows tend to do. Still, each brow moved freely on its own; aware of its separation from the other. The prodding man caught my gaze a stern look forming on his face as the lines on his cheek curved into harsh parentheses. “Take him in overnight. We’ll get his information and run some tests.” And then the younger two loaded me into the back of the van where they perched me on a seat running parallel to the vehicle’s sides. I thought of putting up a protest, but was so utterly awed at the unlikelihood of such bewildering human contact that I instead recessed into the confines of my woolen shawl, my shell tortoise-like in its soothing reassurance. But from what, I suppose? *** Insect eggs were by no means an uncommon sight in my confined residence. In fact, a fingertip dabbed in eggs was one of the few pleasures of taste I was afforded in this condition. Such ovular stimuli pleased both my tongue and my ego as I would imagine I was some distinguished gentleman, one wearing a double breasted suit with flourishes and a monocle over my right eye- that’s my squinting eye, sampling Russian caviar. I even invented a wealthy compatriot for such an occasion. Though imaginary, Arthur Thunderfoot Prescott was a venture capitalist who made his fortune in the paper industry. He owned several factories all across the country that produced the world’s best selling brand of loose-leaf notebook paper. In the early nineties, his business expanded with the rising popularity of computers and his company took over the computer paper market as well. He explained to me once how the process worked, and I remember it had something to do with wood pulp but not much else. Regardless, I still clung onto the hope of escaping, and conversation with Arthur kept my verbal skills functional; a skill I desperately needed to hold onto to avoid blowing my chances at rescue with wormy gibberish. “Fine caviar this evening,” Arthur complimented, “Absolutely splendid!” “Indeed, my good man,” I concurred. And then we shared a hearty laugh; a rich man’s laugh where the laughter is spoken rather than forcefully sounded out the windpipe. This somehow makes the process of laughter classier than the refluxed guffaws of the working class. We took sips from imaginary glasses of chardonnay, our pinkies pointed skyward. Pretending always gave some flavor to my own saliva. The mind is a potentially powerful device, powerful to conjure up traces of alcohol in the moisture beneath the tongue. My mind, though, was either weak from attrition- slowly corroding from the undecorated dirt walls- or strong- strengthened by its duty to reconcile the absence of any physical stimuli. “The wine, too, it has a pleasant earthy aftertaste,” Arthur chimed in contemplatively while we swished our own spit around our mouths. “I detect a hint of raspberry, no?” “Yes,” I affirmed. “It’s a 2001.” Arthur nodded in satisfaction at the age of the salivary wine. I looked at him, though, with guile and contempt. The silence that followed between us spoke volumes. He had tasted older; tasted better than his own glandular secretions. Even through the dim, he could see the ire burn in my eyes; reddening brown irises filling with blood from surfacing, twitching veins. “What’s the matter, Johnny?” And I couldn’t tell if he was sincere, though I had overwhelming doubts. Two similar beings confined in a tiny, inescapable spot for a long enough time can start to pick at each other’s minds until you can distinguish one’s thoughts for the other’s. “Johnny?” he asked, his eyes widening as sweat and concern welled in the corners. I rose up from my sitting position, crouching under the low dirt ceiling that had bent my spine into Neanderthal posture. My arms and shoulders were spread out as far as they would go, hands held out with open fingers at the sides of my bent knee strides. “Johnny?” he repeated. “JOHNNY!” louder still, as he tried to cut through to my consciousness, fully convinced that I had gone mad. I tried to mask my readable thoughts to assure him that he was in no immediate danger, but my trembling hands unsheathed my intent as rage waved turbulently through bile seas inside of me. That arrogant, unimaginative shit! How dare he insult my hospitality? “Fly eggs and spit!” I yelped out. “It may not be what you’re used to, but it’s all I have!” He tried to cough out a defense, but my hands were already firmly pressed around his neck. “That’s all I have!” I shook, watching his head bobble and his eyes fade back from bulging out. And then I let him go. I let him go, and he cowered back into the depths of my mind as my focus was broken by a viscous burst. Half of a worm with the open middle frayed lay in front of a lemon shaped sac, cocooned and writhing, with scattered slices of dismantled worm flesh strewn about. The exploded worm was somewhat familiar to me, even in its state of disarray. I knew that worm carnally, and though I felt no emotional attachment to the worm itself, there was something curious about the writhing pouch; something that caused my stomach to shrivel in to noose its own acids. I edged towards the pouch on my hands and knees, resisting the urge to vomit. I put my face up close to it and stared. It was larger than any of the eggs or cocoons I had seen, and perhaps had all the eggs and cocoons been this size, my diet would have been far more fulfilling. But as egg pouches go, this was an obvious abnormality. It was too large for the worm to successfully push out without fucking exploding. I held my head in my hands, regretting the lack of contraceptives available to me in my moment of ecstatic weakness. “Shit.” I chewed out, accepting the most twisted form of defeat imaginable. And there I sat for quite some time, watching the pouch bubble and retract as a myriad of moral quandaries rushed through my mind. *** Upon arriving at the facility- Happy Endings Rehabilitation Center, I think it was called- the first thing I noticed was the gate that kept it apart from the rest of everything. The architecture itself presented premonitions of long hallways and locked doors sealing rooms full of once interesting people, now reduced to desperate shells, draped in white. As I was led through the doors of the building, the place reeked with a sour sterilization. Underneath all that was painted white there was something underneath, struggling to breathe out, but white is deceptively strong. White needs to be coated on, multiple times over, and this gives white power in numbers. Immediately upon entrance, the personnel who ambulanced me there turned me over to a receptionist, a short haired menopausal woman, probably married at one point, divorced now, only child away at college, still visits sometimes, lonely, baggy eyed, et cetera. Her nametag said Jeanine. She was the perfect candidate for the job. Forms and documents seemed to suit her, lines and checkmarks. “What’s your name, sir?” I still didn’t remember what my name was, or if I had one. Names were a completely human invention. I still hadn’t felt completely human. I chose the name I had assigned for myself, the first name I had heard since my memory had seemingly wiped itself clean. Things were slowly coming back to me, but only as I encountered them. Until I encountered evidence that I had a name, I might as well have not had one at all. “Jesus Christ.” “Excuse me?” “Jesus Christ.” “Sir, do you have any identification on you?” She raised an eyebrow, only slightly enough for me to notice. I patted at my pockets to prove the point that I knew they were already empty. Jeanine sighed and scribbled in the name field. “Current address?” “I’m not sure I have one,” I replied frankly. “I never put much stock into addresses anyway. Don’t like ‘em.” Jeanine then shook her head and scanned down the page for any questions that I might be able to answer, but seeing as how she was skeptical about the whole name thing, she quickly gave up. As she was searching the fields on the document, I picked up on the grinding of teeth, subtle at first, but then picking up in intensity in a semi-harmonized cluster of different pitches and frequencies, like spectral colors blending black at war with the walls. “Do you have any relatives we can contact?” “None that are living,” I responded, feeling myself go cold. My mind went numb as memory simultaneously flooded back to me and rushed away from me. I didn’t remember being born or being orphaned. I’d figure being born is one of those things that you should remember. It is such an irreversibly unique experience- the whole bloody expulsion process of being birthed. Logically, I couldn’t prove that I had even been born, or that I even existed right now. She operated some device, paging the middle aged man who first discovered me. I think she said his name was Ron. He arrived within the minute, expecting the page to come. Jeanine slid the clipboard holding the form to him. He looked it over. “Is this some kind of joke?” Jeanine squinted with one eye. Her face went unsettlingly crooked. “That’s all I could get out of him. He had no identification, and he claims to have no relatives.” “Well, I’ll take a sample from him, but if we don’t have a permanent address or a relative to contact, we can’t keep him here. We’ll have no way to bill him for treatment.” Ron led me to a restroom and handed me a cup and a sanitary napkin. I gladly accepted both of them, but the look on my face as I examined the objects was an obvious tell that I had no idea what they were for. Picking up on this, Ron was quick to instruct me. “We’re going to run a drug test on you before we get you situated here. I’m going to need you to piss in this container, seal it, and place it in the collection cabinet next to the toilet.” I nodded and began to walk in, but he caught me by the shoulder. “One more thing, you must clean the tip of your penis before you start.” He had a straight face on after this seemingly odd request, and the emphasis placed on the word “must” let me know that he wasn’t fucking around. “Um, alright,” I inquisitively agreed. This all seemed a little out of sorts for me. I found it rather discomforting that somewhere in this facility, there probably was a room stocked with vials of urine on shelves from wall to wall. I entered the bathroom, concentrating at the task at hand, but midstream, I was caught by curiosity. I carefully examined the shelf, a small metallic unit, no larger than a microwave oven. I sealed the container of my own urine, screwing the plastic cap on securely and placed it in the cabinet, shutting the door. I opened the door soon after, the urine still in tact, untarnished as I had left it. I shut the door again, realizing that I had forgotten to clean the tip of my penis as Ron had so adamantly requested. I began to panic, pacing back and forth, wondering if I should empty the contents of the cup and start again. My bladder, though, had been fully depleted. I turned the sink on and tried fitting my head under the faucet, but the shape and curvature of the sink prevented my head from fitting. I tried cupping my hands under the running water, but it slid through my fingers which had always been to crooked to tightly align. I then looked down into the toilet, the newly rejuvenated waters filling in after the soiled water had been flushed out. Someone I met on a city bus once had suggested that fresh toilet water is actually as clean as the water that runs from the tap, if not cleaner. Unable to confirm or dispel this bit of advice, I knelt in front of the bowl and dipped my face in it, crossing some sort of imaginary border between societal norms and shamelessness. As I drank, there seemed to be nothing different in the water quality. Water was water. It tasted the same, perhaps psychosomatically purer in some ways. I reckoned that maybe it was cleaner- convinced myself of the fact- and resolved to drink fresh toilet water on occasion from there on out. For, I had heard theories about fluoride treatment. The government could easily lace the water supply with chemicals, I suppose, and by the time we found out, it would be too late to do anything about it. After drinking a sufficient amount of water, I reopened the cabinet, but the sample was gone, as if by some form of trapdoor sorcery. I started to sweat profusely, and the paranoia was only augmented by a knocking that came upon the door. “Are you alright in there?” penetrated the voice of Ron through the door. “Shit,” I cursed under my breath before putting together a sketchy response. “Give me a minute.” I needed to get that cup back, somehow, to replace the contents with urine from a clean tipped penis. If the test results came back showing that I had failed to properly follow directions, there’s no telling what would happen to me. I thought of the emphasized must, and the middle aged man with rectangular eyebrows standing impatiently on the other side of the door. But when Ron finally had waited long enough, deciding that I must have been up to something in there, he only found an empty bathroom. *** I felt a nagging obligation to guard the egg sac, and with little else to do otherwise, I sat staring at the damned thing for hours on end. Over the last few days, the pouch had at least tripled inside, and there was a triangular schism calling for some type of resolution. The stinging, moral sense of obligation that gnawed at the base of my brain- where the nervous system started to run down the spine- insisted that I see to it that the creatures developing within the pouch of eggs were to be successfully hatched. The loose shriveled bag that asserted itself as my stomach felt like a deflated organ with a thick, leathery, old balloon skin. It moaned out in whimpered gargles for me to consume the pouch of eggs. The protein, it contested, would save my remaining tissue from atrophying away. My whole digestive system was a disarrayed mess at the moment though. My intestines felt like they had rearranged themselves, thinning out and throbbing and tangling around in new, knotted patterns that squeezed and squelched for activity. I began to be able to translate my own visceral rumblings, torturously unintentionally. I tried to shut out their voices, because my organs did nothing but yell. My pancreas was constantly cursing me- it swore like a sailor. And the only thing my duodenum did was complain. It was getting out of hand. “Shut the fuck up already!” I argued back; my unrelenting organs clamoring not only to me in particular, but also to each other. I had swallowed a Palm Beach retirement villa, and all the residents inside; old, married couples with each spouse at the brink of death, each sick of the other, each too scared to die first, each trying to drive the other one to lunatic aggression to absolve them of that fear. Still there was also a cold logic nestled in my brain somewhere that was working out complex calculations based on mathematical formulae that I was inventing on the spot. I took into account the growth rate of the eggs in proportional weighted values against my slow starvation. If I could tolerate the churning vortex imploding my insides for a bit longer, perhaps the eggs would grow larger, and they would provide a more satiating meal. I decided to go with this third option. Though I knew that numbers were just as easily manipulated as words, there was some comfort in mathematics. Mankind simultaneously takes logic for granted while putting faith in science. We’re all, in fact, completely lost to the concept of logic. Science is not the factual antithesis of religious faith. It neither disproves nor attempts to disprove the existence of God. It only affirms our faith in mankind, and unjustifiably so. And though science is logical, it is not logic. Likewise, logic is not the emotionless barrier of objectivity. The two, though unable to fit together, must react to one another on a perpetually imbalanced scale. The concept of logic itself, is wholly illogical, a faux definable code of statistical probabilities that does nothing to further our true desires, only to delay them. And logically, the sequence of events following my decision to wait for the eggs to grow only goes to prove the fallibility of logic itself! It still infuriates me- I could’ve eaten those goddamned eggs. They would have been delicious. Days later, the eggs were still growing healthily, the pouch weighty and bulbous. But the eggs were almost large enough to contain a newborn human baby in each. It wasn’t, however, the concept of eating fetal meat that repelled me from eating the eggs. Anyone opposed to cannibalism just hasn’t been hungry enough to give it a chance. Rather, it was the dangers of curiosity that kept me waiting on the eggs. I wanted to know what would come out of the eggs when they finally did hatch. I had no doubt in my mind that it would be hideous, perhaps even more so than eating whatever was developing within. Truthfully, I didn’t know what to expect from the eggs. I had let this whole charade get out of hand, and the irony set in that if the eggs hatched out giant worms, I’d be the one being digested slowly in a tube of shit, having to cut my way out with a bowie knife. But how in fuck’s sake was I supposed to get a bowie knife? It was imperative that I dispose of the eggs before things got out of hand, regardless of curiosity or my potential fathering of these unimaginable monstrosities. I hobbled over to the eggs with the intent to perform the only large scale amateur abortion, to my knowledge, in history. I reared back my white knuckled fist of doom, a bony fingered, balled up mitt empowered by urgency, and I sent it at full force towards the pouch. “FUCK!” I yelled out in pain, clutching my hand between my other hand and my pubis. “Fuck, fuck, fuck, fuck, fuck, fuck, FUCK!” The eggshells were solid as cement. Even the dulled nerves in my appendages momentarily regained sharp aching sensations, alerting me that my carpals were in significant peril. As my hand throbbed in my lap while I tried to calm my breathing and dissuade the logical possibilities that may arise when the eggs began to hatch, it was clear to me that all the logical equating I had done earlier had left me with nothing but dead end options that wouldn’t have worked out anyway. Logic! Ha! It’s all just a pipe dream. How gullibly optimistic is it to believe that there are explanations to complex questions? I lost all faith in reason then and there, tired of thinking of a way out, tired of wondering how I got in, and tired of being convinced and spooked by a shadow every time the room shifted. Hell, the room had probably never shifted. Maybe it was all just my sense of direction, my sense of positioning or even my sense of self. Perhaps I was simply drifting from the body of one insect to another, seeing through distorted kaleidoscope eyes, and every so often returning to my own body. Or maybe it wasn’t fucking worth explaining. But then I felt the room, shifting again, swearing up and down that nothing was happening, that this was all just the bastard side of my personality split apart and waging cruel games on the rest of me. I was wormlike, cut in half, both sides regenerating new segments. There were two of me, and the bastard half was up somewhere, having me imprisoned here, torturing what was left until nothing else existed. My will strengthened as the light beamed, or rather appeared to beam, around me, the shadowy, hallucinated head staring over the center above. Or maybe it was him- me- laughing down from above, cackling his unbearable cackle. His wry features twisted into an intolerable delight. Yes! I could see it there, through the dim. I could make out the shapes, the all too familiar shapes that belonged to me. God, how I hated the sight of it all! I couldn’t stand to look at the son of a bitch. His appearance made me ill and baneful. “Come down here, you son of a bitch! Come down here and fight me to the death!” The coward smirked, continuing instead just to stare, still and statuesque. He stared until he was satisfied with the ecstatic pleasure he received from watching me sealed off, and the when he had reached the height of his sick derivations, the lights were gone, the room having snapped onto its other side again. “You insufferable little shit!” I uselessly yelled at him. I sat down again, breathing too heavily to hear the inaugural cracklings from inside the pouch. *** Under insurmountable pressure, I had managed to contort myself into a compact enough shape to lock myself in the deposit cabinet for urine samples. I wasn’t planning on staying in long. I figured that I’d just wait until I heard Ron leave the bathroom before I opened the door back up and snuck out. I couldn’t stand to be shaped into a tiny cube for long, sitting with my neck arched and my head protruding between my knees and my elbows pointed forward, hands resting on my shoulders. Something about the claustrophobic situation, though, led me to fall asleep before the coast was clear. Perhaps it was that the circulation to my head had been cut off by the bend in my neck or the tightness of my body’s fit in the cabinet. Either way, I woke up a few hours later in complete darkness. I reached to my left to open the cabinet door, but there was no latch to let me back out. In my hysterics, it took me a while to notice that the other side, where I had previously assumed there was no exit, was carelessly left open- or maybe pried open from the wall’s inability to handle the compression within while I slept. I sorely stretched my limbs out of the opening, pushing the camouflaged door open wider and searched the near wall for a switch. Flipping it on, I panned across the room with my eyes. I got the same sense of vague uneasiness that comes along with certain things. It felt like I had just walked into an old widow or widower’s basement and discovered that the process of mourning a dead spouse was spent collecting preserved fruit in jars and storing them on rickety shelves in a musty basement that smelled like someone had left a corpse behind a stack of decades old newspapers somewhere near the back. I immediately thought of the old man from my earlier visions. I thought of him storing jars with preserved apricots and peaches and other such fruit that could have been gummed down his toothless mouth if it wasn’t for the fact that his hands were too brittle to break the air tight seals. “Conflabbit!” he’d mutter. “These damn lids are on too tight!” And then he’d shake his head from side to side as he looked at the dusty, cobwebbed, concrete floors. “Damn lids.” And he’d scoot off back up the rickety wooden stairs, a half step at a time because he didn’t have the kneecaps to walk up stairs anymore. Still, he’d make this trip every day, stacking a new jar of fruit on the shelves until he ran out of space or fell over and died of disappointment- whichever came first. But this place was fucking immaculate. It smelled like isopropyl alcohol and rubber gloves. The floors were white tile to match the whiteness of everything else. The room, though, had a yellow glow to it, though as the fluorescent lights refracted through the cups and cups of urine lined up alphabetically by name along the single supermarket sized aisle. I scanned the shelf, slowly, looking for my urine by name. My finger guided my eyes across the labels until I came to the specific jar I was looking for- Christ comma Jesus. It resembled my urine, but I had to be sure. I’d never seen so much urine in my life, and it was all starting to look the same. I also didn’t remember the exact tint or odor of my previous secretion, but I was never one to trust labels. I had a theory about them, but it was too incomplete to divulge at the moment. I was just about to break the seal for further inspection. “Hey, man…you’re not supposed to be in there.” I immediately turned, fearing this detection, when I saw the late night janitor. His voice matched up with his appearance- a burnt out forty-something who looked more like a fifty-something with unkempt gray hair, a craggy, stubble covered face, and a navy blue work suit. He perennially wheeled a mop bucket around, the mop leaning over his shoulder from the bucket’s ringer, dripping behind him and down the back of his uniform. He was meek, non-threatening, and pacifistic. He couldn’t have been more than five and a half feet tall, and his memory was apparently shot completely as he repeated his previous inquiry in a tone that indicated he had already forgotten whether or not he had already addressed me. “Uh…hey, man…you’re not supposed to be in there.” He spoke with ellipsis in between his words, and if I hadn’t said anything, he might have been standing there all night, the dripping mop soaking his back as he repeated himself, over and over. “I’m sorry,” I apologized, “I must have gotten lost or something.” I began concocting a story as I pocketed the container of urine. “I think I took a wrong turn at the second hallway past the fichus plant, and…” He sheepishly interrupted, poking a finger into the air. “Oh, alright…I can help you back to your room, but you have to put that container back, or I’ll be in deep shit, man.” He backed up, putting his palms out as I walked towards him, taking the urine out of my pocket and holding it in my hand. I stopped more than an arm’s reach from him so that maybe he wouldn’t feel as intimidated. “Relax, man. I don’t want to get you fired. You’re the only person that’s been willing to help me out since I’ve gotten here. What’s your name?” He reverted from his cowering and met my extended hand. “It’s Lou…Lou Chambers, man.” “I’m Jesus Christ. It’s good to meet you.” “It’s good to meet you too…man.” He nodded his head, a relieved grin on his face. And then he kept nodding, like the people at church. It began to scare me a little, so I started to back up as his eyes stared past me. I could have grabbed the cup of piss and ran, but instead I just froze until he came out of it. “Sorry, man…just an acid flashback…happens sometimes.” I nodded back, quizzically, and he followed me as I turned to put the urine specimen back in its rightful spot. He spoke apologetically to me, always apologetically it seemed, “Sorry about the pee, man. They keep close tabs on that shit, man…I don’t know why. Even after they test it, man…they still keep it…pretty strange, huh?” “Indeed,” I agreed, the seeds of conspiracy germinating now in my head. “What would the lab need with old piss?” “I don’t know, man,” Lou continued, trusting in nature and endlessly wandering without focus. “But they do all kinds of experiments and shit, man…more than just drug tests.” “Huh,” I mused, liking this Lou Chambers character more and more. I, too, was growing comfortable with the unfamiliarity of interaction. Unfortunately, I didn’t know what to make of the new information and couldn’t yet offer any productive reaction while Lou shuffled his feet in the awkward but good willed silence. “I guess I better take you back to your room, man…what number are you in?” “Me? Oh, um, I don’t think I’ve been assigned a room yet. I didn’t have any way to pay for it, but I have nowhere else to stay. I was staying in the specimen deposit box for a while.” Lou even trusted the unbelievable truth, a quality lacking in any other individual I had ever encountered. The humanitarian in Lou was further brought to life as he scratched the back of his head searching for a solution. “I guess you can stay in the janitor’s closet, but you can’t let anyone know you’re in there, and you’ll have to be locked in sometimes.” “I would appreciate that, Lou.” I watched him as the expression on his face conflicted with itself. On one hand, he was uneasy at the risk he had taken with this offer, but his conscience was beaming through. And then, in a moment, both the thought and the expression passed as a new one forced a smile over his face. “Hey, Jesus…you want to go smoke a joint with me in the janitor’s closet?” I shrugged my shoulders. “Sure, why not?”
