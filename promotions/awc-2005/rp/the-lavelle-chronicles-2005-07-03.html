The Lavelle Chronicles

3 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3rd July ((OOC: Please Note that the newspaper clip in this is fictional and I wrote it. It is to wrap-up the finale of the part 1 - in regards to Lilly Connelly and the mafia bosses. )) The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch. 9 “The finale for Part 1 of The Lavelle Chronicles -” Albany, NY. Au Revoir! Lois Wood lay still in the water of the crystal lake, her eyes gazing upon the starry sky. Her mind clear of thought and worry and then she woke up. She sat up quietly and stared at the dark, empty, hospital bedroom, that she had become accustomed to. She looked around and stood up, gaining her balance, she flicked on the light. She thought maybe the silver birch scratching against her window had awoken her, instead her mobile began to chirp and she was quick to answer it. “Hello…”she waited for the caller to reply. A moments silence passed and she thought about hanging up the phone. “Morning Lois, hope I didn’t wake you,” Robert was on the other end. Lois smirked to herself and checked her watch; it was 2:00am. She was now pretty angry at Rob. She was not a morning person and being woken up at 2:00 am was just the icing on the cake for an early morning blow-out. “No, I was already awake! why are you ringing me at two in the morning, Rob?” “I just needed to tell you that I won’t be coming back to Albany for a while.” “….what do you mean?” she was still quite groggy and wasn’t really paying attention to Robert on the other end. Her eyelids began to gently close and she could feel herself drifting into a sleep. “Lois!” Robert yelled into the phone. She was up and immediately alert again; she sighed and returned to the phone call with her ex-boyfriend. “Yes, what is it?” “As I said already. Would you please just let Pierce know….” “Know what? Sorry my mind isn’t really awake,” she smiled. Rob had woken her up too early and it aggravated her. Now she would do the same thing to him. “I won’t be coming back to Albany for a while, Lois,” Robert repeated himself again. Something she knew he hated doing. “When are you going to come back?” she couldn’t understand why Rob would ring her at 2:00 in the morning to tell her this. “Why did you ring at this time to tell me?” “Lois, I won’t be around for a while. My family and I are heading up to a Florida for a few months. Is that okay? I sent your transfer papers to your mom’s house. When you have a place to stay and an address, she said she would send them to you.” “I don’t get it, Robert. Why the sudden move to Florida?” “Lois, you and Pierce have found what it is you want to do and that’s going to Harbrook and being together. I haven’t found that person yet and I haven’t figured out what I want to do yet.” “So moving to Florida is going to hold the answer?” “Maybe,” Robert sighed into the phone. “I don’t want to argue with you, Lois. I still like you, but I know you like Pierce and I’m happy for you, but I need to move on, too.” All she could do was nod her head in approval. “That’s fine, Robert. Just keep in touch.” “Goodbye, Lois.” With his goodbyes said, Robert hung-up. Lois was left listening to the irritating beeping of the disengaged tone. She closed her mobile and placed it on the small counter top. It was 3:00 in the morning and because of Robert she was now wide awake. She decided she would go and see Pierce, he was always up early and she knew he’d be up to watch the sun rising, well most likely. If he wasn’t up, then she would have to wake him up. New Beginning I sat in my bed, my mind wide awake. It was 3:05 in the morning and there was no chance that I would actually be able to sleep. I opened the blinds and watched the greys and black fade away slowly, as a crimson red and orange immerged over the horizon. “You awake?” I turned around to see Lois, dressed in her pyjamas standing in the hall of the bright hospital room. “Yeah, but what are you doing up?” I laughed to myself. “The Lois I know doesn’t arise from her bed until at least 12:00 in the day.” “Funny, Pierce. I am up because of our good friend, Robert,” she closed the door and sat next to me on the bed. “Robert?” “He rings me at 2:00 in the morning and tells me he’s moving to Florida to find himself,” she looked at me again. “Why are you up?” “I’m always up to watch the sun rise. I always have done.” “Lovely…” she hugged me. “You have never watched it, have you?” I smiled when Lois shook her head. “I’m too tired to stay and watch it, Pierce. I’m going to go back to bed.” “Stay,” I looked at her and held her hand. “Just for the sunrise, then you can go back to bed.” “Okay, but don’t blame me if I fall asleep,” she nestled her head into my chest and watched closely out the window. The reds and oranges became electrifying as the sun slowly rose up. The moon was fading, along with its desultory greys and blacks. “It’s beautiful,” she smiled and continued looking out the window. “Lois, have you gone soft?” She nudged me and laughed. “Guess not,” I rubbed my shoulder and held her tighter. “Lois?” Lois had fallen asleep and I couldn’t help, but smile. Her breathing was enticing; my eyelids began to shut and I knew I could get to sleep like this. I welcomed the soft clouds and the idea of counting sheep soon passed into a black void. Normality The alarm began to break that momentary silence. The nurses and doctors went about their usual routines: checking patients, running bills, going to get coffee for the rest of the staff and performing surgery. Lois slept silently against my pillow. I rose up from the small single bed and went into the bathroom. In front of me, in the mirror, was a man I didn‘t even recognise, with overgrown stubble, messed up hair and blood shot eyes. I definitely wasn’t looking my best. I ran the hot water and pulled out a small cloth. “Damit Rob,” I said to myself. I couldn’t get over the fact he had left so suddenly and refused to even ring me to let me know he was going. I would just have to let him go. I routed through the “Hilfiger” bag, hoping that Robert had bought some gel or shaving cream. Luckily there was shaving cream and a razor, but no gel. My hair would have to be styled with water. “Pierce?” Lois asked from inside the room. “Yeah, I’m just going to clean my appearance up a little.” I smiled at the thought of Lois’s weary eyes looking up from the comfort of sleep, as she looked for me. I opened the bottle of shaving cream and spayed some onto my hand and began running it along my face. The razor was a typical “Gillette for men”. Four minutes later I was finished and all the terrible growth on my chin was gone. I felt like a new man! Clean and crisp. “You clean up nicely!” Lois smiled at me. Lois was now dressed in a casual pair of baggy jeans with some combat pockets on each leg and a tight orange wife beater. Her hair was up in a ponytail and she couldn’t stop smiling. “That’s a new look!” “Well, it’s time for a change. How do I look?” she looked at me and did a twirl. “You look great,” I laughed. “Now what are you looking at?” she looked at me and then down at herself and smiled. “You have a sick mind, Pierce.” I laughed and threw on a shirt. My thoughts were broken by the hospital phone chirping. “Hello?” Lois said into the phone. “Hey Lo, how are things? I just got off the phone with Robert; he said you were in a fire. Are you alright?” Lois’s brother Jason had rung her. “I’m alright Jay, thanks for ringing.” “You’re my little sis, of course I’d ring. Robert also mentioned that Pierce saved you. I thought you weren’t talking to him after he broke your heart at seventeen,” Jason said. I could over hear everything and I was beginning to feel extremely uncomfortable. I knew this day would come. I knew I would have to face Lois’s four older brothers. I just didn’t know it would be so soon. “That was a long time ago. He’s my best friend and he saved my life. Ease up a little.” Lois held my hand and gave me a caring look before returning to the rebuttal with her older brother, Jason. “Okay, Lo. Pierce is a good guy, especially since he saved your life.” Lois could tell that Jay was only lying to impress her and to stop the arguing. I knew deep down that as a teenager I had broken Lois’s fragile heart. But, now that we were in our twenties, it should appear that we were more mature in our approach to love and the world around us. “Listen Lo, I gotta go, mother is having another cleaning fit again. Catch ya later, be careful.” Lois hung up the phone and stood up, fixing her top. “I’m sorry about that.” “About what?” “About my brother, Jay. He’s always been extremely protective of me…” Lois hugged me and then moved over to her “GAP” bag. “You don’t have to apologise. I know what Jay is like. Don’t you remember the day in school that he held me against my locker and kicked me in the stomach?” “Please, don’t bring that up again.” I knew Lois hated living in the past, but I had to make it clear that Jason never liked me and I wouldn’t blame him. I didn’t threat Lois too good when I was younger and that was purely because I was a troubled teenager, not a great home life, no real father and a mother that was hurting more than me. “I’m sorry. I won’t mention it again.” “Perhaps we should go and see your mother, see how she’s doing. She has probably read the newspaper by now and is extremely worried about you. What about Rebecca?” Lois had done the unthinkable; she actually cared about my family. I never thought anyone actually would. She knew better than Rob the dealings that happened when the door was closed and she still accepted me for who I was. “You’re right.” She gave me a nudge on the shoulder. “I’m always right.” “That you are, Lois Mahr-Wood,” “Nobodies used that name in a long time,” Lois smiled. Her last name, Mahr-Wood - was a combination of her fathers and mothers name. Her mother was born in Germany and moved to America a long time ago. Here, she met David Wood, Lois’s father. Lois generally goes by Mahr, for her mom’s sake, as Lois’s father died when she was thirteen. “I guess it’s just nice to see a smile on your face.” “You always know how to make a girl smile, Pierce.” “…I guess I do.” I laughed at Lois who was struggling with her new bandage dressing. Lois and I both had to change our bandages regularly. Lois however had an easier task than I had. While removing my own dressing I would have to apply a medical gel along the fresh skin-graft. I moved closer to Lois and placed my hands on her hips, I felt a slight tingling sensation. “Here, let me help you.” Lois grinned to herself and turned around to face me, moving her body closer to mine. She had a sweet scent of vanilla perfume on her skin. It was enticing. “Are you going to help me or stare at me?” she smiled again. I began wrapping the new bandage over her stitches carefully. I ran my hand along her hip bone and noticed the bruising had almost vanished. I was so relieved that she was pulling through this. “There, all done.” I pulled away and smiled. “Well done, Pierce. Nice job,” she smiled back and added a small nudge to my shoulder. The nudging usually was a playful little punch, which never hurt. “So - er - what do you want to do this morning?” “You asking me on a date Pierce?” she smiled again and approached me. I couldn’t help but smile at Lois. She was extremely cute in her approach to her feelings for somebody. “Will you accept my proposition?” Lois looked at me and nodded. “Of course.” “Breakfast at the fabulous Hospital cafeteria and then off to the cancer ward to see my mother.” “Not exactly what I pictured as romantic, but let’s go.” Lois and I left her hospital bedroom. A shady Truth The morning breakfast had gone quicker than usual and both Lois and I found ourselves in my mother’s room with the New York Post in front of us. I held open the newspaper and began to read its contents: Mafia Denouement and the Albany blaze. In the last couple of nights, Boston police have been tracking the hellacious murders on the Boston streets. Everything they had worked for has finally come to an end. In an attempt to end the mafia's rule, Boston chief of police (Derek Braden) called in the Albany Police department when an apartment block was set ablaze in the middle of the night. Albany City police detectives have announced that the fire was started intentionally by the mafia overlords and their assassins. Marvin Harrington, Sicilian billionaire, is the main man responsible for every mafia related issue that has swarmed our streets and many more. Forensic detectives in Boston have compiled evidence against Marvin Harrington. Also, Isabel Harrington is willing to testify against her husband for the death of their son, Pete Harrington. This man and his many mercenaries are also responsible for the summer murders of the Connelly family back in 2004. In the Albany blaze, two firemen lost their lives along with Lilly Connelly, the last member of her family who survived the attempted assassination by the mafia back in 2004. One man remained in the building and risked his own life to save another. Pierce Lavelle, aged 22 and also a friend of Lilly Connelly, stayed behind in an attempt to save another friend. In interviewing him, he had this to say: 'When the fire broke out, I was extremely frightened. Even though the firemen came to help us, I knew I needed to go back for my friend. She was still in her room and hadn't moved. I guess my going back for her saved my life and hers, because just as we came out of her bedroom, the floor above us collapsed. Fortunately we remained under the large oak beams and were unharmed. I wouldn't say I am a hero, I just didn't want to leave her behind.' The brave Pennsylvanian had much more to say in his interview which is continued on page 9. Marvin Harrington along with many of his remaining cohorts will be facing trial under a federal state judge. It is believed he has two choices in the court; life without parole or face the death penalty. Marvin's lawyer announced that if Marvin admits to agreeing to the murders he will face life without parole. If he confesses to the murder of his son, he will face the death penalty under one count of first degree murder. Boston residents may be able to return to their late night partying. The gang-land bosses are behind bars and slowly but surely, the Boston police department are cracking down on narcotics, gambling and gun smuggling. The many lawful ways of the mafia ruling. I slowly closed the newspaper, not really wanting to read on. Lois looked at me; I knew she could tell that reading the clip made everything that happened a little more believable. I had left out a lot of the general facts and I knew she would be annoyed, purely because she found it out from a different source. 'So, everything that happened to you and Lois, was because of your involvement with Lilly and that mafia boss?' Rebecca asked. 'Yes,' I looked at Lois, who still refused to do or say anything. She remained silent and seated in the small guest chair. 'I'm just glad you and Lois are alright, Pierce,' my mother piped up over the silence. 'I think I am going to go and get some coffee...'Lois stood up and left. 'Pierce, you didn't tell her everything did you?' my mother looked at me again. 'Did you not even mention the reason why Lilly was staying with you in the apartment?' 'No I didn't.' 'It's no wonder she's quiet,' Rebecca looked at me. 'You lied to her, Pierce. She almost died because of Lilly and the mafia boss and you still didn’t even mention why that was. I think she is in shock none the less. I mean, she finds out the full truth about her feelings for Robert, her near death and then after everything that has happened between you and Lilly, you announce to Lois that you love her.” Rebecca stops. I was shocked, how did my sister know? “You love Lois?” my mother looked at me. “Yes. I only realised it a few weeks ago. I - I mean it has been there since I knew her, it just sparked now. Then I meet Lilly, but I knew nothing would happen between us. I think she liked somebody else. Then the other morning, before any of this happened. Lois came into the kitchen and just seeing her brought a smile to my face.” I looked at the door to make sure nobody was listening in. “I didn’t mean to lie to her or hurt her; I just wanted to protect her from this part of the truth. She has a journalistic instinct and I don‘t want her getting too involved in this.” “Go tell her this,” Rebecca looked at me. “I don’t think I can listen to your mushy love stories. I think I am going to be sick…” Rebecca laughed. “Pierce, I am just glad you are on the mend. I’m sorry about everything that has happened to you as a result of your father and our engagements back in Harrisburg. But, everything will be alright,” my mother smiled again. A mother knows best. That’s what my mother used to always say. No matter what, she could tell everything about me. I was surprised that she couldn’t tell that I loved Lois a lot sooner than now. “Thanks mom, it’s not your fault. Brad Lavelle will never be father of the year or anything, but my expulsion from college was of my own rebellious doing.” “Rebellious? Pierce, since when did you become so smart?” Rebecca looked at me. “Pierce, did you tell mother about that huge victory the other night?” I shook my head. My sister was referring to my match at the wrestling venue I work for. I had won the main championship and I felt proud of my accomplishment. My trip home to Albany wasn’t too long either. “Funny Becca, keep quiet will you. The grown-ups are talking…” “Oh, cheap shot. The things I must put up with from you…” Rebecca picked up a small magazine and began flicking through the pages. “Hope I didn’t miss too much,” Lois said as she came back with two cups of coffee, one hot chocolate and an ice tea for my mom. “Please, Lois. Save me from my demented brother!” Rebecca stood up and took her hot chocolate. “Hmm - demented? I would have gone with sweet.” I couldn’t help but smile at Rebecca’s face when Lois had said that. “My god - mom - help! Lois and Pierce have become all mushy!” “Since everyone seems to be in a good mood. I think I should tell you that the doctor said my cancer is on retreat. The chemotherapy is working wonders. It looks like I’ll be back to normal.” “That’s great, Mrs. Lavelle.” My mother smiled at Lois. For the past six years of our friendship, Lois has always called my mother, “Mrs. Lavelle”. Lois had been brought up by her mother and was always told to be polite. Her father was an army general, but died in combat and she always looked toward my mother as a sort of friend and second parent. “Please Lois, called me Marie,” my mother smiled. “Lo, what happened to you in the fire?” Rebecca looked at Lois. “The newspaper didn’t mention much on either of your injuries on page nine.” “That reminds me. Pierce, a man named Tim Shipley called for you…” I was up and out of the room before I had even taken a sip of coffee. Back track into the past 25th June 2005 Harbrook Open Day Lois and Rob had made their way up to the podium. I had stayed behind and looked at all the different courses to take with my skills. I had a choice between medicine, paramedics, biologist, surgeon and the list went on. Lois had gone over to “journalism and media” and Rob took a gander at “astronomy and astrophysics”. I used to think both were the same thing, but Rob set me straight. Harbrook was filled to the gunnels with high school students and some foreigners looking for a place in some American colleges. When Lois and Rob had returned, we decided we would look for the forms we needed to fill in, for next year’s semester. Last thing I needed was to have done so well in my exams and never complete college. Lois had persuaded me to come along and it was turning out to be a good idea. “Lois, I think the administration office is over here,” Rob said. “Yeah, you’re right.” Lois and Rob moved ahead of me. The crowd was becoming just a little too claustrophobic. Too many people crammed into such a small space. “T-Tony?” a man behind me tapped me on the back and spoke “Tony” nervously. As I turned around to greet the man, he was as white as a sheet. I recognised him immediately. “I’m sorry; I didn’t mean to interrupt you. I thought you were somebody else,” said the man, apologetically. “Don’t worry about it. Tim Shipley, right? I’m Pierce Lavelle.” I held out my hand and he shook it. “How do you know who I am?” Tim Shipley asked. “From CORE. I was a big fan of you and your tag-partner,” I stopped for a second. I realised who Tim Shipley thought I was: “Tony Aliso”, but he had died a few months ago. Tim Shipley nodded his head. “Yeah, I’m sorry I interrupted you.” Tim said once more. “Listen, I don’t really mind. Hey, I’m a wrestler too. Oh - but I guess you don‘t want to talk about that.” Tim shook his head again. “No, no, it’s fine. You - er - you go to Harbrook?” Tim said. “Not yet, but I will be.” I looked around to try and see if I could find Lois and Rob. “Okay…er - what about a coffee or something a little later? I - I’d like to ask you about….wrestling” Tim looked at me now. He didn’t seem as nervous as he had done, when he first tapped me on the back. “Sure, that sounds awesome.” I shook Tim Shipley’s hand and gave him my mobile number and heeded to Lois’s calling. Present Day A regarded ally I ran purposefully down the long corridors of the hospital. A nurse behind me constantly yelled; “Stop running!”, but I ignored her wishes and continued. I needed to get to a pay phone. I knew I could get in contact with Tim, I needed to thank to him for his help a few nights ago. “Hey, is Tim there?” I asked politely into the phone. “Tim who?” the receptionist asked. “I’m sorry, but you’ll have to be more specific.” “Tim Shipley. I’m not sure which residential he is staying in,” I looked for some more change. “One moment please…”the receptionist left the phone on hold and I was forced to listen to a concerto by Mozart for a few seconds. “Hello?” “Tim, its Pierce. I heard you rang.” “Yeah. I-I was just wondering how you were doing? I read the newspaper.” “I’m fine, thanks. I just wanted to ring back and thank you for your help at Solarized.” “Don’t mention it…”Tim became silent. “Sorry, our residence has to share a phone with about six people.” I laughed into the phone. “You need to get yourself a mobile or something!” A small voice from the pay phone announced that I had 0 cent left on the meter. “Listen, Tim. I have to go. My money has run out. Perhaps I’ll talk to you later.” Tim said his goodbyes and I hung up the receiver. I placed both of my hands into my pockets and headed back upstairs. My best chance to move on with the world and my life - would be to find a new place to stay and the man to turn to for that was my grandfather. I needed to swallow my pride and say “sorry”. The next day… (Lois) Lois lay out flat on the spare bed and began to groan as she turned over. She moved to her left and then stopped. She woke up suddenly to notice the dented empty cushion that Pierce had slept on. Lois sat up and looked around the dark hospital room. She stood up, gained her balance and flicked on the light. It was 9:00 am in the morning and she noticed Pierce’s jumper still slung over the chair he had left it on the night before. Lois picked it up and zipped it on. She was cold. She made her way out of the bedroom and down the hall. “Where are you, Pierce?” Lois asked herself as she searched down the corridors, looking for any sign of her boyfriend. She stopped and spotted Pierce on the phone and decided it best that she leave him and go back to the bedroom and wait for him. Her journalistic features encouraged her to go to him and find out what he was doing, but she decided against it and knew she would only upset herself if she found out something she didn’t want to know. Her mind had been racing over the last few hours. She was unsure what was next for her in the world and college. She placed her hand on the door knob, but stopped. “Lo?” a man behind her asked. “Jay, what are you doing here?” Lois was shocked. Her brother Jason had decided to come to Albany. This was something she hadn’t planned on. “Geez - thanks for the lovely welcome!” he looked at her. “Sorry, I - I just wasn’t expecting you, that’s all.” “Well I decided to come here and tell you that you are coming home with me.” “What?” “Mom wants you to come home with me.” Jason moved forward and handed Lois a plane ticket for Harrisburg, PA. Lois looked at the ticket and contemplated going with Jason as she could no longer stand to sleep in this dingy hospital. But, she couldn’t leave Pierce. Not now, not ever. “You’ll kick yourself for this in the morning!” Lois once again thought to herself and handed Jason back the ticket. “I don’t want to go!” “Lo, please. You need to come home. Mom isn’t the same without you.” “Mom has always been that way, with or without me.” “Lo, this isn’t funny. Why do you want to stay here? Robert said to me that your apartment was burnt down. You have no place to stay.” “Pierce is working on that. Tell Robert to stay out of my business.” Lois knew exactly what Robert was trying to do. She knew after she got rid of Jason and sent him on his merry way that she would have to confront Pierce about Robert’s intervention. “Look Lo, I know you like Pierce, but you staying here is not your choice to make. It’s mine. I am your big brother and what I say, goes.” Lois smiled and looked over toward Pierce - who luckily enough was still on the phone. “Jay, I am old enough now to make my own decisions. You are not Dad and it’s my own life. I will do what I want. When I first said I wanted to go into Journalism, you didn’t like that, but you accepted it. Just like Stuart, Bob and Dennis. I wish you could understand this, but I know you never will. I am fine here. I can take care of myself; after all, a black belt in Karate is good enough for something, right?” “You’re right, Lo. But, I still don’t know why you are staying here in Albany. You loved it in Harrisburg. You had loads of friends and you were doing brilliant in Thompson College. Why stay here?” “I need to move on with my life, Jay. I stayed in Harrisburg for a long time, not because I wanted to, but because it was expected of me. I have always dreamed of going to Harbrook or Harvard and finishing my Journalism course, but I can’t accomplish that dream in Pennsylvania. My new life starts here with Pierce.” Jason turned sharply around and looked at his younger sister, Lois. He understood now why she decided on staying, but the mention of “Pierce Lavelle” was another issue altogether. “Stuart and Dennis like Pierce. Why can’t you and Bob accept him?” “Because -” he looked down at his shoes. “Because what?” Once again Lois rose to her potential and put on the tough, witty, know-it-all, obnoxious, attitude she had carried with her for years. She could always stand her ground and never really needed rescuing. “I just never liked the guy. I - I mean he never treated you right when you were younger. What makes now any different?” “He’s changed, Jason. Trust me on this one. It was Robert that hurt me. I should have trusted Pierce and not got with Robert, but I did. And in that time I watched Pierce change from a punk-rock, rebellious, teen, to a man. So Jay, if you have a problem with me and Pierce being together, well then I’m sorry, but I love him and you can’t stop me.” “I guess not.” Jason took a step back and threw his duffle bag over his shoulder. He looked once more at the ticket and put it in his pocket. Instead, he took out two hundred dollar bills and gave them to Lois, who had a shocked expression on her face. “Just encase you decide to come home for a while,” he smiled and hugged his little sister and began to leave. “Thanks Jay.” Lois watched her brother leave and she decided to put the two hundred dollar bills into her back pocket for now. She opened the door and smiled. She had stood up to her brother and made it clear that she loved, Pierce. She knew she would always be the army brat of the family and she enjoyed that role, more so than ever before. ……
