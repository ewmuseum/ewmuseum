Vegas

30 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/30 Really, Laura Winters could have gone her entire life without returning to Las Vegas. The Strip still glittered as much as she remembered it, though truth be told, under most circumstances she wouldn’t even have been out had it not been for her guest. “Omigod, Laura,” came the slightly ditzy reply from her best friend. “It’s so awesome that you get to go places like this for work!” In spite of herself, the Queen of Submission gave her friend Rebecca a small face, before responding. “Usually we don’t get so much time,” she explained, “but it’s a pay per view this week. That’s why I wanted you along. It gets lonely, and I really don’t have many friends in AWC.” Rebecca nodded, looking out at casinos as far as the eye could see. For someone who had never seen the city in person, it was an undeniably impressive sight. “So, Laura,” she squealed, unable to control her excitement any longer, “what do you want to do?” Laura laughed a bit at her exuberance. “Whatever you want, really. Doesn’t matter to me, as long as we don’t get back TOO late. I need a good night’s sleep. Training really takes it out of me.” “Really want to beat Mapleleaf this time, huh?” The Queen of Submission smiled. Patrick hadn’t been the first person to defeat her in the wrestling ring, and it was quite likely that he wouldn’t be the last. “It’s not so much that he beat me,” she explained, quietly. “It’s more the fact that he’s an utter waste of humanity that gets to me.” “Yeah, I saw. Typical male.” “Typical male WRESTLER,” Laura corrected. “Trust me, if you hung around a bunch of wrestlers for a while, the guys on U-Del campus would seem like Knights of the Round Table.” “Maybe you should introduce me,” Rebecca said, jokingly. “Anything to make those idiots back home tolerable. But, you know…” Laura knew that voice, the way it trailed off, the tone, everything about it. It was Rebecca’s teasing voice, one that she used quite a bit and for varying and sundry purposes. “Yes, Becca?” “If you find wrestlers so objectionable, why did you date one?” Laura’s eyes rolled back in her head, sighing at her giggling friend. “First of all, I said typical male wrestlers are like that. There are, of course, good eggs to be found. And besides, I met Seymour when we were in kindergarten, a good, oh, seventeen years before he started his career.” “Awww, how romantic! Did you two share milk at snack time?” Rather than snicker at the (admittedly poor) joke, Laura simply fell silent for a few moments, looking out at Vegas at night. Rebecca poked her a few times, trying to snap her out of it. “Hello? Earth to Laura?” “This…probably isn’t the best place to push that particular issue, Becca,” she admitted, letting a world-weary sigh escape her throat. Pouting, Rebecca moved closer to her best friend, wrapping a reassuring arm over her shoulders. “What happened here, Laura? Was this where you two…broke up?” The Queen of Submission shook slightly, catching her friend in a hug. She quietly shook her head no, leaving Rebecca somewhat confused. “Then what happened, Laura?” It took a couple of false starts before she managed to come out with it. “This is where we got married.” “YOU got married in Vegas?!” It probably wasn’t the most polite thing Rebecca could have said, but her shock was genuine. She knew Laura as being relatively strait-laced, so a scandalous quickie marriage in Las Vegas was nearly unimaginable. Wiping the beginnings of a tear from her eye, Laura looked her best friend dead in the eyes. “We had planned it out beforehand! It was in the Golden Nugget, and--” “You PLANNED OUT your quickie wedding?!” “I TOLD you my parents hated him!” “Ooh, wait until everyone at Delaware finds out!” “They won’t find out,” Laura said, almost surprising Rebecca with her calmness. “Oh really,” a suddenly prim and proper Rebecca asked. “Why not?” “Because,” Laura replied, a grin tugging at the corners of her mouth, “you’ve obviously forgotten that they call me the Queen of Submission…” ------------------------ Ten minutes, a hammerlock, profuse apologies, and a short walk later, the two had found a coffee shop. With coffee cups in hand, they engaged in that time-honored tradition of womankind, talking over coffee. And yeesh, they have pastries, too. Looks like we could be a while. “Alright, so you don’t want to talk about the wedding,” Rebecca concluded, clutching her left arm as if it had been shot. “But what about the honeymo--” “If you want to retain use of your left arm,” Laura said, somehow both calmly and threateningly at once, “you won’t finish that sentence.” “Okay, okay,” Rebecca managed, finally conceding the point. “Can we at least talk about your match without you trying to rip my arms out?” It didn’t take much for Laura to detect the slight hint of annoyance creeping into her best friend’s voice. Somewhat sheepishly, she frowned, and leaned a bit closer. “I’m sorry, Rebecca,” she said. “I know you want to know more about him, and us, and everything, but it’s just difficult. I’m trying to move on and make a name for myself on my own, but everywhere I go I’m reminded of him.” “That’s not difficult, you know. You DID travel all over the world with him.” Rebecca was right, of course. Laura had seen places that she never thought she would see during her marriage, thanks to her managing her husband. For the most part, the memories were good ones. Trying to forget about them seemed counterproductive, and potentially impossible. “Rebecca, am I…being ridiculous about this?” Rebecca looked at her obviously uncomfortable friend. It was her turn to sigh. “Not really, Laura. You just haven’t figured out the place you want those memories to take yet, whether you want to keep them with you or push them away. I’m sorry for trying to force your hand on this. One day, you’ll be ready to tell me.” Laura nodded. That was a likely possibility. Besides, she had to admit that right now, she was somewhat distracted plotting match strategy and how to avoid being caught in Mapleleaf’s Full Blown Canadian submission move again. “--And then, everything will be…Laura, you’re off in wrestling dreamland again, aren’t you?” “Ack!” She nodded, and managed to stammer out an apology, but her mind did not change focus. Patrick remained on her mind, and not in the way that the brash Canadian athlete would have preferred. “No one beats me twice, Rebecca,” she said, conviction in her eyes. “I’ve been in the ring with him once. I know what he can do, and I know what he can’t. Now it’s just a matter of putting together the proper gameplan.” “He really isn’t a very nice sort, is he,” Rebecca asked, somewhat innocently. “I’ve seen him when watching the show looking for you. All arrogant and mean and…well, he likes to tell everyone he’s Canadian.” That, of course, had to be in the early running for understatement of the century. “It doesn’t matter what nationality he is. My submissions don’t discriminate.” Gone was the easygoing, somewhat gentle girl that attended the University of Delaware. The Queen of Submission had taken her place, and was more than ready for action. “He needs to be taken down a peg, for the sake of AWC. He’s been running roughshod over everyone and everything without regard for anyone but himself. Mapleleaf triumphed in our first meeting. We shall see if he is so fortunate this time. This is my PPV debut. I am NOT losing.” Forcefully, she slammed her paper coffee cup to the table. Thankfully, there was no beverage remaining in it. The cup crumpled on impact, and Laura simply discarded it over her shoulder, where it miraculously landed in a trash recepticle. “Let’s go, Rebecca. You’ve got gambling and man hunting to get down to, and I need to work off some aggression.” Her best friend wasn’t about to belabor the point.
