History Repeated

13 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13/12/05 History Repeated Black is the colour…of my true love’s hair… Her lips are like…some roses fair. “Well…wha’ ye think?” Paddy’s speech was slurred as it rolled lazily from his mouth. His tongue felt sluggish and obese in his mouth, like a scrunched up sock bunging up his parched throat. Only one cure for that then, isn’t there? More drink I say! “Ye’re too good t’ answer me? Well maybe aye should cut ye down t’ size!” Paddy, whose chin had been quaintly resting on the mahogany bar work surface, jerked upwards into a straight posture, his head cocking back painfully so that he was staring upwards. He should have been in pain. Instead however, he merely began giggling as he noticed the tiled ceiling and how it was partially streaked with light thanks to a strange, floral lampshade that defeated its own purpose by being lined with apertures. And now, his glazed stare returned to his own source of conversation – a quarter filled pint of beer. Paddy frowned, trying to distinguish which of the two pints he was seeing was in fact real. He groggily groped for the left. Lucky guess, Paddy gripped it loosely and seemed to throw it into (and around) his mouth, causing a steady stream of foam to run from the corners of his mouth and subsequently drip from his chin. Paddy swayed slightly, grinning so widely now that every tooth in his mouth could be counted, then steadied himself to the best of his current ability. Once this mountainous task had been dealt with, he thrust his hand into the air, now scanning the bar for the deliverer of the mead of the Gods. “Barman! Another one here me good man!” The proprietor of the bar sighed. For twenty years now he’d owned this bar. He’d seen all sorts of people. Tall people, small people, white people, coloured people, straight people, bent people. He believed himself to be a fair man, a tolerant man but if there was one thing he had no patience for, it was gypsies. Filthy, idle, indecent gypsies. Even worse was drunk gypsies. Gypsies shouldn’t be allowed near alcohol goddamnit!. This wasn’t to say he was prejudiced or anything. I mean, he didn’t mind black people as long as they were working for him and not killing and stealing (which in his opinion, all of them did at some stage); he would only throw insults at the Japanese unlike those yobs who threw rock and bottles. Yes, Jim Prescott considered himself a fair man to all people. Unless you were a women of course because damnit, they better stay in that kitchen before I have to take off my belt! Jim decided to comply. He was a small man, petite and cowardly. This gypsy didn’t look much tougher than him he supposed but they did say pykie’s bite. And he didn’t want to join the good Lord in the sky from some case of AIDs he caught from a fucking vermin bum. Not yet anyway, because of course, a good man like himself would be on the right hand of the Father come judgement day. Let the fucker have his drunk, he wouldn’t be roasting in hell for his crimes. He wasn’t the only ‘good’ man here though. This little English village named Harrods and to the North, was full of ‘good’ men. Men who’d burn the place down before they let their fine home be tainted so aimlessly with ethnics. Because, well, if God wanted us to all be the same, he’d have made us all the same colour. Whites to the North, Blacks to the South and yellow fellas can have the East. And, well, gypsies were just an experiment gone wrong, so they can just find an island somewhere. Jim Prescott put a pint glass to the tap and poured it straight, not caring if the ‘dirty tramp’ complained about their being too much head on it. He better learn his place or…or…that guy over there will teach him lesson, for sure! Jim Prescott purposely slammed the pint in front of Paddy’s face, picked at the pile of change lying beside his punters hand, then returned to his stoop, his eyes never leaving the pykie. He was considering the possibility the man might be so drunk he couldn’t fight back if he hit him…then he concerned the possibility he had a knife. “He’s a meanie,” Paddy whispered to the pint, transfixed as little streams of gas shot to the crown of the beer. To Paddy, an image of golden bliss. Yes indeed Paddy, he is a meanie. But you got your pint my son. You got your pint! Paddy really should have researched the place before he pitched his tent in the village. Should have looked up the ‘Where To Live’ website. There, he would have found Harrods to be one of the most staunchly conservative settlements in modern Britain, so backwards the residents might as well be sharing a room with the Black Death. He should’ve have bothered. Shouldn’t’ have been so damn naïve. But he didn’t and this flippancy would surely come back to bite him on the arse. Alone, he set his caravan in the middle of their park, confusing the passers-by fury for mild confusion. He should have even let them know he was only staying here until the East Atlantic tour was over, a matter of weeks. Should have moved to calm a tiger ready to pounce. But it slipped his mind, and in a village of 2,017 residents, he made 2,017 enemies instantaneously. “So…wha’ do you think then?” Paddy rested his chin on the bar again, his eyes drooping, his lips curving into a lopsided grin. He traced a finger tip over the glasses surface, clearing the condensation, and inscribing his initials. “Do ye think aye…should end it with her?” Paddy bit his bottom lip and dropped his gaze. Meanwhile Jim Prescott was burning a hole into Paddy’s head, all the while thinking. All the time wondering his best course of action. “Do ye think it’s too dangerous, tha’ aye should care fer her too much t’ risk losing her?” Rewind to June. Rewind back to when Hate was the bane of Paddy’s life, haunting his every footstep. See now the times when his Father was his captive, how he would surely have died if not for the intervention of Mickey Fitz. For weeks on end, he sat beside the telephone completely motionless, hoping and praying his kidnapper would just make contact. For his brothers, his sisters, his mother and himself…just give me back my father. I’LL GIVE YE WHATEVER YE WANT! Those times weren’t fun. Now fast forward back to now…and what’s new? Take Hate out of the picture and prepare a way for Mike Wade. And what’s new? One has hair, one doesn’t. One’s a merciless bastard and the others a merciless, er, bastard. And what’s new I ask? Could the changing factor be the victim? Could Shelly Clarke be the next Danny O’Shea? I won’t shy away if danger comes to me… The memory of Shelly’s statement was a bittersweet symphony in his head. She would stand by him. But he didn’t think she realised it would be her taking the bulk of the punches in this exchange. That was just the way Mike Wade worked. “It’s happenin’ again…isn’t it?” Yes Paddy, I’m afraid it is… “Fine…aye’ll…aye…aye need t’ think.” Keep a hold of her and he’ll hurt you through her. Let her go and… But she has the sweetest smile… the gentlest touch… Well, let her go and you lose something you’ve never had before. You lose someone that cares about you as much as you care about them. Loves you with everything they have, a perfect match, a perfect bond. When you’re the acid, she’s the alkali and only she can bring neutralisation and balance to you. She’s the one that sobers you up. Encase you haven’t noticed it Paddy, even thinking about her has set everything in your head straight. She’s the constant, the taper indicating this is right and that is wrong. And, like a giddy teenage crush, he just can’t stop thinking about her. Paddy stood up and drained his pint. Struggling to stand, he staggered the path in front of him, out of the bar into the suffocating cold night air. Mr Jim Prescott, satisfied the gypsy had indeed left the building, waited five minutes, then sat beside his customers, sombre and focused. Harrods wasn’t built in a day he said…but that pykie could bring it all down in a day. And his father didn’t fight to clear this settlement from scum like him. He wouldn’t forsake that legacy. Trouble was like a cancer. Cut it out now, or it’ll spread. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Cold, isn’t it? A cloud of breathe escapes Paddy’s lips. He believes his mouth to be closed, but his trembling blue lips can’t seem to clamp the orifice shut tightly. The condensed air thins out before him, until the hazy whiteness becomes transparent. Paddy’s confused. But what’s new pussy cat? He stands now on the pedestrian pathway that runs the length of the River Swilly, the one that flows though the town he currently inhabits, gazing out at the water, that laps up the sides of the concrete levees. It took him until this morning to realise it. But he got there in the end. He shouldn’t be alive. He shouldn’t have survived those shots to the head. Yes, he’d considered the possibility before that he was exceptionally tough, that’s what gave him the aura of ‘the Little Train that could’. But it wasn’t right that a man take all those shots and just get up again, shake off the cobwebs and get stuck back in. It wasn’t human. BANG! He felt his own blood, drawn forth from his mangled nose, splatter his cheeks and drip from his eyelashes. BANG! He had thought he’d heard his skull fracture as his head penetrated the ground and in his semi-conscious state, disorientated and askew, he could swear his head dented the concrete. BANG! There goes a tooth. BANG! There goes a straight nose. He’s thought about it now. The events of Fresh will not be allowed to pass by like dust in the wind. When Mike Wade had Paddy balanced on that railing of the bridge, clutching him by the scruff of his shirt, he held a man’s life in his hand. And though he desperately wishes otherwise, he knows that Mike Wade didn’t hesitate once. And he wouldn’t have. Cold, cold fate….wash over me. The legacy of Mike Wade has reached its climax. It reached its climax the day he revealed himself to be an enemy of Paddy O’Shea…because from then on son, everything’s on the slide. Everything you are and stand for, get ready to leave all that behind. Get ready for the rocky road because if there’s one thing he’s finally learned, it’s that it won’t happen again. He won’t let it happen again. Paddy looks down at the pram sitting beside him, his adopted daughter, nestled snugly amidst a mass of blankets, her button nose a slight red at the tip from the cold. She’s asleep now thank God. Sleep now my child, I’m getting ready for the rocky road…and this isn’t shouldn’t be something you should bear witness too. “He had you…didn’t he.” A hoarse voice pierces the air and Paddy hears it from behind him. The voice is old, weather torn if that’s possible. Paddy begins to turn around. “Just face forward boy.” The power in the man’s voice compels Paddy to do so. “I know who you are…and I know what you do. I’m not here to make an introduction, it’s unnecessary. I’m only here to make you an offer…you can’t beat Mike Wade and you can’t win the Transatlantic Championship. Not in your frame of mind. Not now.” “Tha’ doesn’t sound like much of an offer t’ me.” Again, Paddy begins to turn around. Again, the man’s voice halts him in his arch. “Just face forward I said. And you didn’t let me finish speaking. That’s one thing we’ll have to remedy if you take me up on my offer.” A nasty silence followed, Paddy gazing forward at the icy water, feeling the man’s eye bearing into his back. He knew he was studying him and he didn’t bloody like it. That’s what the kids did when he young. What’s a gypsy mommy? Why son, they’re the fiends who’ll snatch you in the night. Don’t go near them! Save the sound of the man struggling to breath, the cold obviously having detrimental effects on his chest, the silence lasted at least ten second then- “Which is?” The man snickered from behind. He liked to be the answerer of questions, not the asker it seemed. Paddy didn’t know it, but this was an old tactic used to establish who indeed was the master and who the apprentice was. Already the man knew Paddy was interested. “You let a man in the know teach you how to win a battle mentally.” Paddy scoffed. “Aye’m afraid ye’ve been listenin’ t’ the wrong sources old man…aye’m very much on top of things.” “Thursday at around lunchtime, I’ll come by to your caravan. Then I’ll teach you.” Paddy pondered for a few seconds, the abruptness of all this puzzling. This was idiotic. He wouldn’t discuss something like this with his back to the negotiator. Paddy swung around quickly. “Who said aye-“ And he was gone. Just like that. It would take Paddy a few minutes to emerge from his thought, then, with a sigh, he made his way home with his daughter. A PCW reunion match is calling him…
