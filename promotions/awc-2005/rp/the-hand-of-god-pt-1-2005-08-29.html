The Hand Of God - Pt. 1

29 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29/8/05 1. Aunt May Paddy never was the most socially acceptable child. He rarely ever had the opportunity to indulge in the underrated pleasure of tag or bouncing on a see saw. And why was this? Because these games, like most children’s game, require multiple numbers of people to participate. Paddy O’Shea was a loner and he accepted that. He couldn’t do much about it to be honest antway. Most people in his prejudiced little town, were wary of gypsies, saw them as no good and taught their offspring to think the same. Contempt breeds contempt and all that malarkey. In his class for example, he would often find himself sitting alone because of a non-existent ‘smell’ that the other kids teased him about. His mind was so undeveloped, he actually began to believe their lies. Paddy didn’t even fight back for many years because of his shattered confidence but all that changed one day and anyone who ever knew him would pay homage to this. He later realised that it was this defiance that probably provoked the masses into furthering their unrelenting campaign of bullying in his more senior school years. But even as he stared into the sneering faces of his personal bullies, he yearned for friendship, to make his frankly depressing childhood that much harder to bear. But he never got it in the classroom arena where it would be most likely to spawn from, he got it somewhere all so different. One day when he was ten, Paddy O’Shea was walking home from school (alone as usual) when he was followed by probably the three most vicious of his torturers – Willy, Jack and Brendan. These were boys who would have kicked sand in other kids eyes for building a better sandcastle or punched someone because they got tagged first. However, they had just a little bit more detest for travellers. Riding up alongside Paddy on their bikes, they began their assault on a young and nervous Paddy. “Hey ya Paddy, have a good day at school did ye?” Willy asked with a snicker. Instead of respond, Paddy only gave Willy a fleeting look then turned once again to the path ahead. “Oi, Gypsy! Aye’m talkin’ t’ ye!” Willy shouted. Now the snicker was replaced by a snarl that quickly shook Paddy out of his attempted heroics. Paddy simply mumbled a reply and suddenly the three boys had crushed Paddy by only having Willy raise his voice. Yes Paddy could give as good as got but there were three and he was one. Simply mathematics denied him a dignified way out. “So pykie, what’s new today in yer wee world of gypsiness,” the boy named Brendan smirked clearly enjoying the domination in this playground power struggle. “That’s not a word”, Paddy snapped without thinking. If he hadn’t of had his hands jammed firmly in his pockets he probably would have just clapped them across his mouth because his mistake had suddenly become blatantly obvious. The three boy’s looked at each other with slightly stunned gazes, then Jack smiled a wicked smile that told Paddy everything – it told Paddy that he would savour the thrill of beating this new found attitude out of him. Then very calmly and effortlessly, he whispered to the two other boys. “Let’s beat up this scum, aye don’ like his face.” They moved in slowly with the wheels of their bikes squeaking solemnly. Then suddenly, Jack let his bike fall on its side and he lunged for Paddy, with the other two still a few yards behind. Jack was on top of Paddy now and he quickly raised his fist above his head when- “What the hell are you doing boy!” All four looked around to see where this middle aged, female voice had come from. It sounded powerful and dignified because Jack had climbed off Paddy instinctively. He obviously knew the threat an adult possesses. “Yeah, you better get off of him” Where was it coming from? Paddy now realised the accent was a foreign one yet none of them could see where it was coming from. Then he peered up. Paddy hadn’t really been paying much attention to notice that they were on the pavement just outside a row or terraced houses and now, the occupant of the house directly adjacent to where he lay was hollering out of her top window. She was a stocky woman with a large buxom that was hanging over the window frame. Her black hair and olive skin seemed alien to him but it was her face, curled up in anger now that was the most interesting. She was angry, genuinely angry, that these boys were bullying him…this wasn’t the way this was supposed to function, was it? “What the hell do you think you’re doing to him?” the woman yelled once again at the top boys. Paddy felt her warm spittle freckle his face from his current position on the floor. But he didn’t get up, like the other three boys, he was transfixed by this woman but not in fear like these three, but in awe of this majestic knight. “Don’t w-worry mam, he’s just a wee gypsy,” Jack replied a little nervously. However, he gave the excuse like it was natural, as if he expected her to say, “Oh sorry, didn’t know that, carry on then”. Hell, Paddy thought the excuse was viable! “WHAT! THAT’S YOUR EXCUSE!” The woman was actually screaming now at the boys below, liberalism etched all over her lined face. And now Jack was wilting. The sadistic, ruthless Jack who had respect for no teacher was wilting and Paddy found it hard to suppress a smile as it crept up his chin. Jack was going limp before his eyes just like a man wilts with time. But only, it was Jacks mind more than his body that had just made a bum note. Brendan too was looking at his toes and muttering something. Willy was simply fiddling with his ring, not daring to look for fear of being incarcerated with that stare. Paddy finally got to his feet and stood in the midst of these three boys. He’d stood in this position many times before with the self proclaimed “three musketeers’. But on this occasion he was delighted instead of in fear. Here was Brendan who liked to ride his bike as if was a horse and he was a badass cowboy. Here was Willy who would never be nice to anyone when he was around his friends. And here was Jack, the worst of the lot, the Marlon Brando throwback who didn’t give a shit about nothing. Only now these perceptions were somehow getting mixed up with images of clowns at the circus and Paddy himself was doing back flips inside. Never again he had thought at that moment…a thought that he kept buried in the back of his mind all his life. Now the women was rising up again for an onslaught. “Now you three listen to me! If I ever see you around here, no around ANYWHERE, I’m going to come looking for you and I’m going to cut your toes off! And as for this boy, if I even THINK you’ve confronted him after this day, I assure you, that day will be your last. Now I’m coming downstairs and if you’re not gone by the time I open the door, I’m bringing you in!” The women suddenly disappeared from the top window and before Paddy knew it, the three boys were riding past him on their bikes, heads down. Paddy can’t recall having any major run ins with these boys again. Then click…the door opened and Paddy suddenly came to his senses. He initially felt a nervous tingling corseting through his body. Yes, she had just come to his aid but that wasn’t to say she was welcoming. Yet he stood his ground, even to thank her then dash. When the doorway was fully drawn back, Paddy got a severe shock. For in front of him wasn’t some woman in a man’s body. Standing before him was a woman, at least fifty, with a definite East European look to her. Instead of the towering figure he had come to expect, was a woman no bigger then 4 and a half feet and smaller than Paddy despite him being only ten. Wearing an unusual burgundy dress with a black shawl, Paddy was genuinely startled by this foreign alien. The women came out to her small porch and looked up and down swiftly for the three boy’s and when she seen they were gone, her lined face caused from a scowl instantly subsided into a warm greeting smile. Now that Paddy was this close he could see all her fine details. She had two gold teeth that sparkled in the shimmering sun. She wore large gold hoops – cheap gold probably but the juvenile Paddy was of course fooled. She had short choppy fingers that were pressed together at this moment in an almost religious gesture. And her eyes were twinkling, incredibly dazzling and standing out against her tanned skin. “Come in my boy. Do not be afraid”, the woman cooed at him. Paddy took a step then hesitated and anxiety was revealed on his smooth hairless face. “Aye’m a gypsy missus, me teacher says aye have de tell anybody who asks me,” Paddy said, his voice shaking. He wouldn’t cry after all he had accepted this often precedented practise but he couldn’t help his head from dropping. He never saw the brief stroke of anger that flashed on the woman’s face as she bit her lip in fury. But as Paddy looked up again, she was smiling warmly once again. “Your teacher said that? I’ll have to have word with your school then. But please, come in.” Paddy scratched his head briefly, hesitated once again and then dashed inside the house. The woman let his long hair brush her hand as he ran past and she sighed because she knew (and so did he deep down) that he would never be normal…not here. The woman led Paddy into a sitting room and instructed him to take a seat on a low maroon sofa. Paddy plopped himself up and found himself sinking into it. He grabbed the arm to make sure he was propped and then turned to the woman who had taken a seat in a chair opposite him. She held out a small plate with an assortment of biscuits on it without saying a word. Paddy took one and forced a smile in gratitude. “Don’t be afraid to smile my boy. Don’t you know you can make someone happy by smiling? And do you know that if everyone smiled everyone would be happy?” Paddy again forced another smile and shook his head to indicate he didn’t. Paddy then smiled as wide as he could. The woman laughed at him for just a second. “Okay, not that big…people will think you’re a retard.” Paddy didn’t get the joke and so only laughed along with the woman out of politeness. After she was done she wiped a solitary tear from her eye. “Okay son, what’s your name?” “Paddy” “Paddy. That’s a nice name. I’m May…nice to meet you.” May reached across and extended a hand. Paddy pulled his hand back for a second then identified the kindness in the woman’s face and shook it. “See…now we’re friends. And those boys out there. They’re not your friends I’m guessing.” Paddy shook his head feverishly. “No, they’re not gypsies so they can’t be my friend.” May gritted her teeth for a second then became dead serious. “Now Paddy, you listen to me, just because you may travel about or live in a caravan or whatever doesn’t mean you’re any inferior to anyone else, you hear? “In-fear-e-ar?” Paddy replied solemnly and slowly. “Yes, inferior. It means smaller than someone. You and them boys are just the same. Infact I’d say you’re better than those scum. So don’t you ever let anybody make your life any harder. For God’s sake, life’s stressful enough.” May examined Paddy. It seemed he was slowly getting the message and the first of many messages May gave Paddy that helped him shape his personality in later years. “Let me tell you Paddy, I was born in a caravan too. Not here mind, in Romania. And I’ll tell you what, I’ve never been as happy than when I was travelling around, sitting in front with my dad and the horses.” “Horses?” “Yes Paddy. We didn’t have a car. Not many people did, so horses got us round. Some people were stuck with donkeys or mules but we got lucky and had horses.” Paddy nodded in understanding as May continued. “Then I moved here when I was twenty. I wanted a job but Romania had none. I needed money to live and have a family. I came here because Ireland was becoming a greater country. But I found neither – nobody wanted to give a gypsy a job let alone marry one. Now I live off the benefit your government gives me. They give me a little money to shut me up and keep me in a corner. But I tell you, like everyone else who has ever been prejudiced – why the hell should you accept it? You shouldn’t! I’m growing old and weak now but I still fight on. And one day, we will all be equals Paddy. All you have to do is demand it.” Paddy was now looking deeply into May’s eyes. He should have been seeing before him a fragile woman who had aged terribly. Instead he saw a warrior like one from some of the storybooks. A warrior who could kill a thousand men with only his bare hands. And then she smiled and the image faded as quickly as it appeared and once again he saw a warm woman before him. They chatted casually for a little longer about trivial matters. May was asking about school -teachers and students, who he had problems with, what did they do. She asked about home and if was pleasant. All the time, May was gathering information. Information she would later use to correct Paddy’s life without him ever having known. But Paddy was slowly gathering a friend. /\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\ friend (noun) Your friends are people you know well and like to spend time with. /\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\ Then May turned to Paddy suddenly. “Paddy, you must be getting home now it’s getting dark.” Paddy felt a hand wrench his stomach as he was suddenly hit with the reality of leaving. He almost felt like an adult in drinking tea and having a normal conversation. But now he had to leave and he stood up reluctantly. “Please let yourself out. I’m afraid it’s quite a struggle to get up and down these days. Remember Paddy, value your youth!” So this was the end then? Paddy began to walk to the doorway. He had someone who actually talked to him and he should be appreciative but now a strong desire was washing over him. A desire to demand more. Just as he disappeared through the doorway of the sitting room, May’s cool voice called him back. “Oh Paddy? Don’t forget to visit now would you. I’ll be here tomorrow at the same time if you like. With more biscuits!” Paddy didn’t re-enter the room. He didn’t think he could face her with such a ridiculous smile he felt spreading over his face, threatening to rupture his cheeks. Paddy gave a silent punch to the air and mimed a whoop. Then he was gone, through the doorway and running home, beaming the whole way. He had a friend. Someone who cared for him and would fight for him. Later he appreciated her as someone who helped sculpt a fine young man who demanded civil liberties yet always upheld the pride of his tradition and culture. Before Paddy left for the AWC, she was still alive but weaker than ever. Yet her last message was as powerful as ever and her growl was still ringing around in his head. Everyone is discriminated against, everyone. When an Irishman goes to England he’s discriminated against, when an Englishman goes to India, he’s discriminated against. Not because these people are vicious but because they feel they must attack to shield themselves from prejudice. As soon as you step outside the boundaries of your world, you will be vulnerable to attack. The only thing for gypsies is that their world ends when they step outside their caravan. An Irishman feels safe in Ireland and a gypsy feels safe in his caravan but who can live their life more comfortably? Gypsies face the worst type of discrimination anywhere because their retreat is tiny. But remember this Paddy, all that changes when you leave, because you’ll leave Ireland a gypsy and arrive in America, an Irishman. And even in the light of new hardships you may face, remember one thing – Never run away, face your persecutor and watch them shrink. 2. Resurrection Paddy is sitting on a park bench in Central Park, New York. Although seemingly wide-awake, Paddy is so deep in thought at the present minute, a carnival of elephants couldn’t wake him from his trance. Three things are on his mind right now. Hate, Mike Wade and the Transatlantic title. Hate – a man who has tortured him for months now who almost crippled him, executed his father and destroyed him future. A man he should fear, did fear in fact, has now fallen down the pecking order of worries on his mind. For last week Paddy made a stand against him and swore to him and himself, he wouldn’t run away again. Plus, the emergence of a number of his relatives has changed things significantly. Now he has an insurance policy. Then there’s Mike Wade. This is a man who he has faced and defeated following Wades constant taunting of O’Shea. A man he shouldn’t trust because Mickey Fitz certainly doesn’t but somehow, he feels he has to. Because like him, he’s an Irishman trapped in a discriminatory little federation. Shelter the weak and all that bull. And finally there’s the Transatlantic belt, the epitome of his desires, his calling to be a wrestler, a symbol that can tell him what he’s doing is right and just. Right now, in this confused little situation he’s in, winning that next week is the only thing that can bring him round. And all the stands between him now is two men – Pierce Lavelle and the Educator. Men he’s battled before – twice each. The only thing is, apart from beating the Educator on his debut, he has been unable since to beat either of them. And that memory of Alexander Strider screwing him on his last title shot is still haunting him deeply. But never mind, what happens happens and as long as he can still stand, he’ll be fighting for that belt each and every week even if it seems to be fading away. Paddy raises his head and looks up into the night sky. It’s a bright night and the gleaming stars glisten in their constellations in the sky. Sucking in the midnight air with a sigh of pleasure, Paddy stands up and stretches, allowing his intercostal muscles to protrude momentarily from his chest. He then flexes him arms slightly and then begins to pace down the small tarmaced path with slow lumbering steps. O’Shea is wearing a simple black military type jacket over a white vest and full length khaki combats over simple black boots. His mass of dirty fair hair is also being covered by his trademark black leather hat. His dark stubble has just passed prickly point and he rubs a hand over it with a smile before jamming it back into his right pocket. Paddy has had a lot on his mind these last few days but things are starting to pick up drastically. Yes he still has Hate to deal with but now he has more support than ever against the beast. But it’s not only that that has helped him, it’s been May’s words to him that always seem relevant despite what the situation is. Paddy has just turned a sharp corner to the right, a corner that seems to hug a small oak tree, when he hears it – the gentle yet unmistakeable sound of an infant crying. Paddy stops dead in his tracks and raises an eyebrow – surely no mother would bring a child out at this time and not in a city like this? Yet seconds later, he hears the cry again. To his left, a midst the cluster of trees. O’Shea steps from the path to the grass then begins to walk in between the foliage, guessing which way the sound is coming from. It doesn’t take long for Paddy’s eyes to adjust to a dim white object standing out against the darkness, a cradle obviously illuminated by the moon overhead. Paddy half jogs over to the cradle and drops to one knee with a soft squelch. He looks left and right for an adult of this child then looks at the baby closely. May The thought jumps into his head without thinking but he immediately pushes it away because of its impossibility. The baby is beautiful and dressed in white which makes the olive skin stand out all the more. It’s this skin and dark hair that must have made him think of his most loyal friend but something tells him otherwise. Paddy lifts the baby gently out of the cradle and holds her in his arms. Yes, it’s a her, somehow Paddy knows it already. The child instantly stops crying and casually opens her eyes. Blue eyes. She must be only born two weeks ago tops. There’s something almost heavenly about holding the baby now and Paddy smiles. He doesn’t know it has crept out until he comes back to reality yet he holds on to it because it’s the first time he’s smiled in weeks. Then Paddy gently puts the baby back down onto her vessel once more and she gurgles pleasantly. As Paddy is doing this, he notices a piece of paper squeezed in between the cradle wall and foam base. Paddy pulls it out and opens it without thinking. On the sheet, in printed letters, reads: To Patrick Aiden O’Shea, Here is a present for you, Replace the old with the new. They say all good things come to an end, But just for you, this rule I’ll bend. The Hand. The Hand?…What the hell? 3. Good Times, Bad Times A half an hour has passed now. It was a cold night and Paddy has decided to take the baby to his hotel room and into the heat. It’s only when he is standing outside the door and he hears the commotion from inside that he remembers his visitors and he punches the wall in frustration of his forgetfulness. He’s practically already here now so Paddy enters anyway. Standing or sitting in various parts of the room is of course the small congregation of Paddy’s family who have decided to become his insurance recently. Of the ten, seven are drinking from cans of Harp and two are smoking cigarettes currently – not the best conditions for a child. When he walks in to the lilac painted room all heads turn around in initial welcome and then confusion when they see the baby carriage. “Paddy…wha-“ says his uncle Robert, his white coloured hair shining in the low lighting like tinsel. “Alright. Aye found this here little ‘in in the park so aye brought it here t’ make sure she was alright. Okay?” Paddy explains a little impatiently. All ten men seem to look at each other for a few seconds and then look at Paddy to see if he is indeed being serious. Paddy sees their expression then continues. “Look lads, gimme a break would ye, aye’m not in the mood. She’s stayin’ here tonight then tomorrow aye’m takin’ her to social services. Okay?” The men once again look at each other and then grumble their approval. Paddy wades over and between the men and then moves into a second room after exiting through the eastern door. He is now standing in a second and smaller room. After making a makeshift stand out of a locker, Paddy places the cradle on top of it and then sits there as he waits for the child to go to sleep. It doesn’t take long however. Paddy simply stands there smiling at the child as she wavers on the brink of consciousness and then her eyes close. Paddy stands another few moments in her company and then tiptoes towards the door, gently closing it behind him. “Seriously Paddy, what be the angle?” All men are looking at him once again – four sitting at the end of the bed, three on a sofa beside the window, two lying on the floor and one on the dresser. It however was his uncle Robert voice he heard. “Look, there’s no angle. The wain was lyin’ there an’ aye couldn’ leave her okay?” Paddy says a little angrily. He finishes this with a firm ‘the conversation is over’ but apparently the men pay no heed as they begin to joke about. “So wha’ are ye callin’ her?” May “Don’ be silly Jim would ye, she’s goin’ tomorrow now can we drop it please. Aye’m worried off me head about this match an’ aye don’ need this right now.” Paddy replies, now pleading. One man sighs and says “fine, let it go lads” and they begin a new line of conversation. “Look Paddy, if yer worried about this match, ye know we can make our presence felt,” Jim says with a wink. Paddy looks at him for a second as he considers it then shakes his head defiantly. “No way. Pierce is a fair lad an’ he’s goin’ t’ get a fair shot at the title. Tha’ be all aye want too lads so no interference, okay?” The men shrug and begin to get back to their pleasantries when the phone begins to ring. A moment later, the sound of the baby crying is heard as the phone networked next door begins ringing too. “Fer feck’s sake,” Paddy says with a sigh. “Paul, get tha’ would ye an’ aye’ll go int’ the wain..” Paddy opens the door he has been leaning on and walks back over to where the child lies crying. Paddy motherly picks her up once more and nurses her in his arms as the ringing suddenly ceases. The child too stops crying and instead smiles back happily as Paddy stick out his tongue at her. “Paddy!” The shout of his name from the other room seems urgent so with the baby in his arms, he walks back over to the door and in exits it. All eyes are on him once again only this time there is no giddiness or suspicion. Now there is only a little distress. “What’s wrong?” The men look at each other then Paul comes forward and claps a hand on his shoulder. “It’s May Paddy…” says Paul with a regretful frown. Paddy looks hard into his face and tries to extract the information from there. What he sees scares him. “She’s dead Paddy…heart attack.” Paddy looks directly into his face as his eyes well up. He feels his eyes water and a tear emerge before he looks away, gritting his teeth. “There’s nothin’ ye could have done…she was old an’ her time had come Paddy,” says Robert from the edge of the bed. With his father temporarily absent, his younger brother has been somewhat of a chief influence in his life as of late. Yet his explanation gives no comfort for Paddy. Paddy nods and walks back into the other room, bleary eyed. The full impact of what has said has not come yet but in this suddenness, he can already feel himself cracking. He walks back over to the cradle as the tears begin to flow more easily and places the girl down. The child is now looking upset herself and Paddy identifies it. “Aye’m sorry…it’s not yer fault, it’s jus Paddy’s lost a friend. It’s not you.” Paddy raises a hand to his eyes and begins to wipe away the tears. When he removes it, he’s look directly at the note that he squeezed back into its original position. Only this time, as he remembers the words, the note has some kind of confused meaning. Here is a present for you, Replace the old with the new. TO BE CONTINUED
