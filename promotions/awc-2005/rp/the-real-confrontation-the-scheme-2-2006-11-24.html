The Real Confrontation? - The Scheme (2)

24 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Nov 24 After setting her freshly-poured glass of red wine on a nearby coffee table, Natalie Porter tiredly collapsed on a sofa in an apartment in Berkeley, California, one that belonged to her significant other, Cedric Riley. Her luggage remained stacked near the front door. She had yet to unpack her belongings, a feat that would finalize the process of moving into the flat. Instead, she reached for the remote and turned on the television. She flicked through the channels in search of something soothing. That was the American way of doing things, anyway, the woman from London thought, and eventually settled on something called 'Opera'. She was unfamiliar with the popular daytime talk-show, and perhaps watching it would teach her the customs of American people. Natalie Porter had undergone a rather rough few weeks prior to that day she sat down to watch Opera. Being involved with Cedric Riley was perhaps the main cause, and just recently, an argument had erupted between them and they hadn't spoke since. He was in Greece just off of an inter-fed wrestling match, and Natalie was stuck by her lonesome in his apartment. Oh well – at least she had Opera. She reached for her glass of wine and swallowed a mouthful. The most worrisome aspect of the argument was what she didn't know, which was too much for even a strong woman like Natalie. Being unaware of where Cedric put his trust – if he did at all – was something she had trouble processing. Their relationship had been so honest; the trust between them seemingly unbreakable. And yet something had invaded their solid bond, and drove Cedric into an insecure mess. The sudden uncertainty was not something easily fixed; it would take drastic measures to repair. The couple was not unfamiliar to great obstacles, however. Just last spring they had been passengers on a hijacked boat and together and overcame potential death. Never before had they encountered a relational problem that ultimately came between them, though, and that was what Natalie had trouble coping with. She let her head fall back into the cushions as Opera went into some inspirational rant. Footsteps came pattering from the hallway behind and she turned around quickly; a young boy of about three years was making a beeline for the living room. A smile spread across the young woman's face as the boy rounded the sofa and with outstretched arms fell into his mother's hands. She delightfully lifted him onto her lap and ran a hand through his hair. Her son, Seth, had just awoken from an afternoon nap. His flight had been a restless one, but he was as alive and jumpy like his normal self now. Perhaps his most notable characteristic was his importance to his mother; this little bundle of joy was the summary of her existence. It hadn't been that way forever, though. But here she was now: Seth Riley on her lap, and it was one of the most joyous occasions of her days. The little boy looked from the TV to her and asked adoringly, “Where's Daddy?” “He'll be home soon, baby,” she promised, rubbing his upper back. He let his head fall into his mom's shoulder as she wrapped an arm around him, then kissed the top of his head. That's when the door bust open, clear off it's hinges. Sun flooded through the gap as three men strode into the house. Natalie looked towards them as she held Seth close, her eyes widening. At the front was one Lucas Emerson, a man who had almost killed her only a month before, and one of Cedric's most hated enemies. He shot a fixated glance at her, his cold eyes invoking a gasp as she put her hand over her mouth, trying to subdue her fright. “Good afternoon, Natalie,” he greeted cynically. “I bet you thought I was dead, didn't you?” She didn't say so, but she certainly did. Lucas laughed, mockingly. “Unfortunately for you, it takes much more than a bullet in the shoulder to cut through my thick skin.” Natalie gulped, then began quietly, “What do you want with me?” “What kind of question is that?” he responded, amazed. “I don't want anything with you; kidnapping lying wenches is old hat for me, Natalie. I'm here for what I really want ...” He pulled a gun from the back of his pants and pointed it in her direction, then inched it over so the barrel pointed at Seth. His eyes locked on the young child and he waved his gun, indicating for his men to take action. Like the obedient goons they were, they pushed forward as a team. The small furniture in their way was tossed aside easily. Natalie screamed and gripped her son with all her might, lifting her feet in the air and kicking the men away while trying to scamper off the couch. The battle was short-lived. Natalie was a fighter, but against two men with tree-trunks for muscles she had no hope. One of the cronies scooped up Seth and slung him over his shoulder while the other muscled the helpless woman over to a chair. He held her in place as Lucas approached slowly with a smug grin on his face and a roll of duct-tape in hand. He crouched behind her as the goon forced her hands together behind the chair, where Lucas wrapped her wrists in a bundle of tape. The same was done to her ankles. “You sadistic son-of-a-bitch,” Natalie cursed, having submitted by now and getting verbal as her last attempt. “I hope you rot in hell.” Lucas smiled and brought his face within inches of hers. “Tell your boyfriend that if he comes after me, I'll kill him.” He grazed his two fingers down her cheek and locked his eyes on hers, then brought his other hand up and cupped her face with both hands. He forced his lips upon her for a passionless kiss, the proverbial cherry-on-top, just before he put a length of duct-tape over her mouth for good measure. And they were gone. * * * Cedric Riley was staying at a luxurious hotel in Berkeley on his first night back from Athens, Greece, where he had conquered Killean Sirrajin. While one may question why he wasn't at his home – if it could be called that -- his choice to stay at a hotel was a way to avoid his girlfriend, who he assumed had arrived in his absence. He wanted out of everything. An escape from reality, if you will. Triangles was in his near future, and he was being pursued for a murder he didn't commit. What made his life even less livable was the one reliable, honest, solid relationship he had was on thin ice, which he didn't dare confront for fear of losing his last bit of patience. Cedric broke the surface of the bar-side pool in a hotel just off the beach, swallowing a breath of intoxicating ocean air. Confronting the nuance that was his real life had been pushed aside, and instead, he was accepting martinis from sexy, bikini-clad bartenders. He pulled himself out of the water, onto the deck, and accepted a towel from the poolside attendant. After a quick dry he approached the bar and ordered a Margarita. An open stool stood beside a lone man. This rugged stranger wore a formal suit with a loose tie; his face, unshaven. He was halfway through a glass of Scotch on the rocks. Cedric asked him casually, “Is this taken?” In the middle of a swig from his drink, the man was somewhat surprised. As an easy-going man, he subdued the reaction. “Go ahead,” he offered politely. Cedric sat on the stool and accepted his drink from the bartender, who he thanked. A silence hung over the two men, who both swirled their beverages with feigned interest. The sounds of a gentle piano and happy laughter were in the background. The man brought his glass to his lips and let a little Scotch trickle into his open mouth, then held his glass in front of him With his eyes on the crystal ice-cubes, he began the conversation, “So, what brings you here?” After a sip of his drink, Cedric cleared his throat and laid his elbows on the bar. “Trying to avoid my girlfriend,” he answered briefly. A faint smile came onto the man's face, and he let out a quiet chuckle. “Tell me about it,” he added reflectively. “What's your name?” “Cedric,” the ex-agent replied, opting for another sip of his drink. “Having women problems yourself?” he asked daringly, taking into account the man's believing response to his current dilemma. The man shrugged. “It's the wife. Rather, ex-wife,” he added bitterly. “I wasn't exactly the best husband, or father.” Cedric frowned in realization of his own situation. “Oh?” The man sipped his Scotch again. “Let me tell you, son ... if you got a good girl, don't let her go. It ain't worth it,” he advised. Cedric paused for a thought. The friendly stranger had just unknowingly provided the answer to his current problem. It had been haunting him since his last meeting with Natalie, despite his best effort to put it in the back of his mind. How could I have been so stupid?, he thought frustratingly, in realization of his selfish mishaps. These antics had tentatively torn his one solid relationship apart. He needed to patch up his mistakes, and fast. “Excuse me ... I have to be somewhere,” Cedric informed the man suddenly, getting up from his stool. “Have a Margareta, on me.” Cedric walked away; he seemed confident, as if he had a purpose. The friendly stranger watched him, and couldn't help but grin. * * * The muffled screams of Natalie Porter had ceased by the time Cedric Riley stormed through the opening to his flat at 5:30 PM. Guilt struck him painfully as he hurried towards his tied-up significant other. He muttered, “What the fuck happened ...” to himself. He was choked. Angry. Completely pissed off for not coming earlier; he immediately placed the blame on himself without anymore consideration. He ripped the duct-tape off of his girlfriend's wrists, ankles, and mouth. When she was free, Natalie got up from the chair and bolted towards the phone, completely taken over by a fit of hysteria. She frantically reached cordless phone and went to grab it. She failed to do so and instead, the phone skittered off the table and to the floor. “Natalie ... what the hell is going one?! What happened?!” Cedric demanded, completely unaware of the circumstances. All he got for a response was heavy weeping. Natalie fell to her knees and scooped the phone up off the floor with an unsteady hand. She attempted to dial a number, but her shaky fingers were unable to press the appropriate buttons. In a fit of rage, she screamed, “God dammit!” Tears rolled down her cheeks as she reared back and threw the cordless like a baseball against the wall. It smashed into pieces that scattered everywhere as Natalie put her face into her hands, unable to sustain her emotions. She was completely broken to every extent of the word, and it showed as she curled up onto the floor. “They took Seth,” she managed quickly between heavy sobs. The shock hit Cedric as if he had ran into a brick wall. With mouth agape, his body became immobilized. Once unable to stand, he stumbled backwards into the chair behind him, sitting down cautiously. “What ...” he sobbed. Natalie broke out into a fit of crying again, then told him, “It was Lucas.” Cedric's expressionless face was haunting and without life. He gripped the back of his chair with his left hand, and his knuckles turned white. Slowly raising from the chair, his eyes remained fixated on nothing. And suddenly, he turned round and lifted the chair from the ground and with a battle cry, smashed it against the ground. The pieces scattered everywhere. Bordering on insanity, Cedric made way towards what was supposed to be his door. Natalie got up quickly. “Where are you going?!” Cedric stopped. He laid one hand on the door frame to steady himself, his eyes staring intensely at a spot on the ground in a state of steady breathing. He slowly turned his gaze towards Natalie and announced calmly, “I'm going to kill him.” Natalie made no protest, despite her conscience telling her she should try and stop him. An attempt at stopping the crazed Cedric Riley would be a fruitless effort, so she remained on the floor. Staying out of his way seemed to be her best bet, but what did it bode for him? * * * Racing dangerously along the freeway in disregard of his own safety, Cedric Riley had no consideration of direction or destination. Having failed to consider a game-plan in his reaction to Natalie's awful news of the kidnap, he had his cellular phone to his ear, in a last-ditch effort to gain information on Lucas's whereabouts. Cedric had his suspicions as to what his enemy's next move was; all he needed to do was find him. Cedric had a contact at the Berkeley airport by the name of Lee Hoffer, a man that he had known ever since his college years in California. As a frequent flier out of said establishment, the professional wrestler was known to make calls to arrange last-minute flights when tickets may not have been available. His intent this time around, however, was a little different. “Pick up the phone, god dammit!” Cedric muttered into the phone as he swerved around another vehicle. “Good evening, Lee Hoffer speaking,” greeted the receiver of the call. “Lee!” exclaimed Cedric. “Cedric! How are--” Lee began. Cedric interrupted, “I need a favor. And I need it now.” Lee drew in a sharp intake of breath. “Woah there ... slow down, buddy ... what do you need?” “It's an emergency,” Cedric said quickly. “I need to know if a certain man is aboard one of your flights.” There was no immediate response. “Lee!” Cedric shouted impatiently. “I'm fucking serious!” After a short lull, Lee responded, “Fine..” “His name is Lucas Emerson.” “Alright ... give me a minute,” Lee said, sounding doubtful. “Try and make it less,” Cedric requested ungratefully. The caller was momentarily put on hold as Lee retrieved the information from his computer. A short wait later and the airport employee came back on the line. He began, “He bought a ticket yesterday for a flight to Los Angeles today. It leaves in half an hour.” “Alright, thanks,” Cedric said. “Oh ... and one more thing ...” Lee sighed. “What now?” “Do you think I could get a ticket for that flight?” * * * The six o'clock flight from Berkeley, California to Los Angeles was about to take off, and Lucas Emerson was seated comfortably aboard. With a glass of white wine in hand he rested his head against his chair, and a satisfied grin crossed his face. His rather ingenious plan had successful so far, though it wasn't yet a 'mission accomplished'. But it was only a matter of time. The 'fasten seatbelt' light came on and Lucas followed its instruction. After the plane took off and was sailing steadily towards Los Angeles, he removed his seatbelt and relaxed in his chair. Unfortunately, he needed to use the bathroom, so he got up from his seat and headed towards the front of the plane. He would use this inconvenience as an opportunity to 'look around', for he was almost convinced a familiar face accompanied him on the flight. Lucas was unsuccessful in spotting this person, so he disappeared into the washroom; as the door swung close to it, a man in the back corner of the plane rose from his seat. Lucas washed his hands and checked himself in the mirror. Even he thought he looked like a cunning, evil bastard, and such an appearance was fine by him. He turned and opened the washroom door for his return to his seat. When a slight opening was made, Cedric Riley pushed through the door and slammed the occupant forcefully against the wall. The attacker turned round, closing and locking the door. Cedric turned back to Lucas, who tried to slam his fist into Cedric's face. He missed as his target dodged the strike. Cedric managed to scoop his arm around Lucas's neck and pull back for a choke hold. The struggle between the men stopped as Cedric seemed to have won. “Give me one reason why I shouldn't kill you right now,” Cedric threatened. Both of Lucas's hands were pried on the arm that cut off his air. His attempts to escape were fruitless, and after a short delay of a response he managed, “I can think of more than one.” Cedric released him from the hold. Lucas turned around and faced up for a verbal confrontation, and instead his opposition grabbed a hold of his collar and pressed him aggressively up against the wall. Both men breathed heavily and beads of sweat trickled down their faces. Their eyes locked. “Tell me where my son is, Lucas, or I swear to God I'll kill you with my bare hands,” Cedric threatened again. His murderous tone was a drastic change to his usual nature, and Lucas looked at him as if he were a different man. “Don't be rash, Cedric,” Lucas scoffed. “Don't you tell me what to fucking be!” Cedric stressed in a hush, frightening voice. Lucas swallowed. “You're going to have to cooperate with me, Cedric, if you want your son back,” he said calmly, despite being at the hands of a raging mad-man. “My son is on this plane. He's with you, and I'm going to leave it with him,” Cedric said through heavy breathing. A sadistic grin crossed Lucas's face. “You were never one to make a rational decision, my friend. I was always one step ahead of you, and here we are again ...” Cedric reared Lucas from the wall and slammed him back into it. “Tell me where he is!” The smirk remained on Lucas's face. “Your son isn't on this plane,” he said slowly. Cedric face cringed as if he were fighting off cheers. He bit his lip and looked away, then looked back at Lucas. “You're lying!” he protested angrily. Lucas shook his head. “Unfortunately for you, I'm not. I told your little girlfriend that you shouldn't come after me if you knew what was best for you, but you did anyway.” He paused, his face looked haunting. “And look where that got you. Hundreds of miles above the earth chasing nothing, when your son and Natalie are tied up in the back of a vehicle, driven by my men.” Cedric had no response. He kept his grip on Lucas, but looked at the floor, overcome with guilt and regret. “That's right, Cedric,” Lucas continued. “I knew you would make the rash decision to come after me. To try and kill me or something heroic like that. So I came up with the perfect plan ... a ingenious scheme, if you will ... to have me board this plane and instead have my men take your precious son, then go pick up your pitiful girlfriend. And now, you are at my command. Funny how these things turn out, aren't they?” He paused, allowing Cedric to take in the information. “Let me go, Cedric,” Lucas requested slowly. Cedric did, and Lucas straightened his collar. “Good, you know what's best for you, then. If I don't come off this plane alive, my men will have known it was you. And, in turn, you will never see your son or girlfriend again.” Cedric leaned against the sink and tried to regain his composure. His irregular breaths proved that he felt weak. “Now, how about we go back to our seats and talk like civil people,” Lucas suggested. “I have something I want you to do for me, and if you don't, well ... like I said, seeing your son and girlfriend again will become no less than an unlikely dream.” * * * Natalie Porter sat in the back of a black sedan that moved steadily along the freeway out of Berkeley, California. Her hands and ankles were tied, and her mouth gagged. In the back seat she leaned against the door, forehead propped against the window. Two men were in the front, recognizable as Lucas's goons, and they were having a discussion. “Take that cloth out of her mouth,” the driver demanded. “What? Why?” the passenger responded, bewildered. “I could use some female company,” he said. After a moment of no activity, he shouted, “Just do it!” After a sigh the passenger turned around and removed the cloth from Natalie's mouth. Her struggle was weak. “Where is my son?!” Natalie screamed immediately. The driver shrugged. “In the trunk.” Overcome again by hysteria, Natalie managed, “If he's not alive ...” “Don't worry, miss, he ain't dead,” the passenger reassured. “Yet.” The driver smirked. “Yeah, that depends on how cooperative your husband is.” Natalie fell back into the corner and leaned her head against the window. She looked to the outside and muttered, “Of course.” To be continued ....
