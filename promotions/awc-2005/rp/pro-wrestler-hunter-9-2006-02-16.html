Pro Wrestler Hunter #9

16 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/16/06 Regroup “Did you hear about the baby born in the high tech delivery room?” The voice chortled in delight. “No,” said the doctor. “It was cordless!” The Green Grappler hands the doctor a green phone with a dangling cord. He then bursts out laughing. “Mr. Grappler, must I remind you to stay still. I need to check you out here.” He started groping Grappler’s back. “Doctor,” said the nurse in the background. “His temperature seems to be going up a few points.” “Sell! Sell I say!” Grappler joked. She walked over to the bed and The Green Grappler eyed here with a lascivious smile. ”Well hello senorita! Can I interest you in a sponge bath?” Grappler reached out to grab the nurse but the doctor yelling caused him to jerk back in an exaggerated motion. “You have some nasty burns and bruises, but I don’t think anything is broken. Just stand still while we check you out.” “Good luck,” said a voice sarcastically from the hospital doorway. Butterfly Hamada turned around, rolling her eyes at the same time. Let the doctors deal with the over ripe banana for a while, she thought. At least he didn’t get that badly hurt. Hamada sat down in an orange plastic hospital chair in the sterile dim corridor, and whipped out a map with a little determination to stay on course. Next stop on the map was marked in red letters with an X, “Bloodlust, St. Louis Missouri”. Her eyebrows furrowed in focused conviction as her pupils narrowed on the site of her next big match. Then her line of sight slowly mapped out the different paths that would lead her and her ‘hostage’ to, possibly, their final destination. Take the back roads, she decided in her head. No one will be able to follow them if they dash from one small town to the next all the way north. She thought about The Green Grappler, the man she had mistakenly put in danger. She thought about her friend Jin, and the woman known as Sage. Was the Pro Wrestler Hunter one of them? Or was he about to strike one of them down, and continue to make Butterfly look like the guilty party. Bloodlust was a perfect time to find out. What better place to hatch a plot, while Butterfly was going to be in the ring against The Furious Fists, a combination of over six hundred pounds of pure hatred and rage. It was going to be a hurricane of violence. It was going to be a baptism of fire, in both name and in fact. Butterfly folded the map and stuck it in her bag, and then instinctively reached into her jacket pockets and found the cool, almost wet touch of brass against her skin a nice feeling. Let The Furious Fists of God believe in their own divine right and feebleminded superiority, Megumi espoused. The age of miracles is past. Tattoos “I demand to be heard.” Megumi looked up from her truck stop cheeseburger to see what the problem was now. The Green Grappler looked down away from her nasty stare, but like a still demanded like a child. “You don’t talk to me.” “What do you want to know,” Megumi said, returning to her cheeseburger. “I told you everything I know. Pro Wrestler Hunter, out to get me, yes? You, in danger. Flee. End of story.” “But we are missing all the good hotels, all the nightlife.” Grappler implored, leaning at her from across the table. “Why drive ALL THE WAY to St. Louis when we could have flown there by plane, huh? We could have spent at least two or three days shopping, relaxing, and getting our nails done.” “If we go to St. Louis, or anywhere else for that matter, you make such a huge spectacle, I will never see it coming before it is too late.” Megumi said matter-of-factly.”You will come with me. We drive to St. Louis, get there a few hours before the show.” “I need a new cape.” “What?” “My last cape is burned and has holes in it. And the feathery trimming is all scorched. Bejeezus! I can’t sew it while driving in a redneck Cadillac like that pick-up truck.” Just then two truckers walk into the diner and eye the masked Green Grappler with part humor and part disgust. “There is enough time for wardrobe to make you a new cape when we get to St. Louis and the arena.” She didn’t look up from her food. But she did notice the looks the other patrons in the diner were giving her and the masked man. “OK.” Grappler returned to the tuna and avocado salad sitting in front of him. “You agree so easily,” she said, looking up from her French fries. Stuffing a piece of lettuce in his mouth, Grappler arched an eyebrow at her. “Do I have much of a choice?” “No.” 'Well then, no sense in whining about things you can't change, is there?' he concluded cheerfully. He picked up a glass of wheat grass and celery juice and took a bracing pull at it before he slammed the glass onto the table. “Enough talk about you. Let’s talk about you for a change.” Grappler smiled.” Ha ha! See how I did that flippy-doo thing. But tell me, seriously now. That tattoo you have is marvelous. Is that a traditional Edo tattoo?” Megumi looked up in surprise. “You know Edo?” “I’ll have you know my dear, that I have traveled in many far off lands, not just Mexico. I have worked in Japan many times, and I know more then a few Yakuza with tattoos.” Grappler said proudly. “I’m just surprised you have one. Tattoos are normally frowned upon by Japan society.” “I am not normal society. I am a wrestler. And more, remember? My mother gave me this tattoo. She studied Edo for many years. She was just a student however, as her master was the one who worked on it for many years, drawing little sections of it. You see the shape of the butterfly, and this is what my mother called me when I was young. I was her ‘little butterfly’.” Even to her own ears, her voice sounded a little bitter as she thought about her mother. “There are smaller scenes inside the wings of the insect. Supposedly it is a story of some sort, but for me it is just a nice reminder of my mother’s love.” “I see a great thunderstorm, and a man and woman fleeing on horseback right there. Incredible detail isn’t it.” Grappler leaned in closer across the table. “And here is a fire and a throne, and there is a small boat and a hurricane. A tree climbs up across the outer bicep and---“ “Yes, yes,” Megumi griped and pulled her arm away, a little insecure. It is a tattoo, that is all. “Are you finished? It is time we go.” She pulled her hair back and leaned her neck, craning it to stretch her muscles. “How about that tattoo on your neck?”, Grappler noticed. “That doesn’t match the style of the sleeve. It is in the shape of a tiger’s eye.” Megumi didn’t like his incessant proding. “That was given to me by my teacher, Tyger Shuri-ken.” “Quite the different style, isn’t it? I don’t even think that is Japanese.” Megumi’s attention was caught by this. “What do you mean?” “Like I told you before, The Green Grappler is a world wide performer. I have been to South Korea more then once on a tour. And that tattoo is of Korean design.” He said it with a convincing tone. Hamada looked surprised and impressed. “That is a remarkable observation,” she proclaimed. “Not at all,” he disclaimed modestly. “As I mentioned, I have a passion for culture. I have wrestled all over the world. My cheesiness is famous on five continents.” “That wouldn’t surprise me at all.” The Grappler gulped down the last of his green viscuous concoction. Thus fortified, he rose and faced Megumi. 'I think I'm going to the little luchadores room. I'll just freshen up a tad. Not that we are likely to meet anyone from polite society along this backwoods route you've chosen, but one can't travel looking like leftovers from a Furious Fists of God's Tupperware party.” As Grapps rose to his feet a hoarse voice called out, stopping him in mid-step. “Well, lookie what we got here, Travis. Extreme Prejudice Suddenly two large men walked up to the booth where Megumi and Grappler are sitting. The two rough looking men sweep ugly, judgmental gazes over both of them. 'Is it already Halloween, Bubba?” one of them asks mockingly. “Hee hee! Can’t be, Travis! Maybe it was taco day down at the high school or somethin’. No matter, looks like we got us here a couple o’ WEIRDOS!” says the taller man with numerous brown teeth in his gaping maw. His smile causes Hamada to almost retch. “Well hello there gentlemen,” Grappler intervened with his friendly gesture. “And let me introduce myself! I am The Green Grappler, wrestler extraordinaire. The Split Pea Personality! The Wizard of Odd! The---“ “Shut yer yap, Chico!” yells one of the men. “Take it outside, boys! I’m gonna call the cops on ya,” threatens the manager of the diner, adjusting his white hat, a cigarette dangling from his lips as he leans over the counter. “Circus is in town, Lloyd. Carny folk. Bad for business.” The man called Bubba sneers at Hamada and then shoots a trail of green snot at Grappler’s direction. Grapps looks down at his shirt in disgust. “That was NOT very nice. This is a hundred dollar Armani shirt, I will have you know---“ Hamada cut him off by standing up from the booth, hands in her jacket pockets. “My friend and I were just going.” She said this as disarming as possible. “Ha ha, says who?” one of the truckers chortles. “What kinda freak show you runnin’ here Lloyd?” he yells at the manager but doesn’t take his eyes off Hamada. ”Your diner is turning into a sleazy joint that no respectable WHITE man come eat in!” “Yeah!” “Yeah! Damn hippies!” A mob of men get up out of their chairs and start to surround Hamada and Grappler. Unfazed, the color of hamada’s eyes seem to drop temperature, like the icy stare of a reptile hunting its’ prey. “Excuse me, but do you have an issue with either my friend or myself? Did we offend you in any way?” She asked with a note of sarcasm in her voice. “Yeah, you offended me with your face showing up in here, yellow pan-face bitch!” Spat the man right in front of Hamada. “Good.” CRACK! CRASH! Hamada smashes the man’s face in with a punch, her knuckles lined with the hard glimmering cold touch of brass. She grabs the man’s head before he topples back and slams it into the tabletop, and food and plates crash to the floor. He crumples in a heap at her feet. “Pan face, this is the best you can do? How about Jap? Call me a Nip. Chinky, rice-eater, come on now! $%#! @#$%^! @#%^! Look at him!” She points to Grappler. “Beaner! Taco head! Wetback!” “Hey!” Grappler calls out in defense. “Is that what you want to hear, you racist pieces of shit? You want to look at me, NOT as an individual, but as a member of a group you don’t even know anything about?” Deadly quiet follows, but only for a second. Hamada knows exactly what is about to go down, and she is ready. A front kick sends the closest man flying back into the register by the door. Hamada turns around only to see a big right hook coming toeard her face, but incredibly, Grappler blocks the attack, and pokes the man in the eyes with his fingers. “Whoop! Whoop! Nyuck! Nyuck!” “Fight you fool!” Bodies start flying as Hamada clocks everything that moves with her fists. Grappler ducks and back body drops a man unto a dining table, and then starts crawling around the floor on hands and knees. “Get up and fight, coward!” Hamada yells at her tag team partner. “I think I dropped my contact lens over here!” Hamada kicks and punches one redneck after another but suddenly the numbers game starts to catch up to her. Before she can collect herself, four men surround her. And then, just like that, they drop to the floor, one by one. “Oooph!” “Doh!” “Hyugh!” “RAWR!” The Green Grappler jumps up to his feet. Hamada sees the bodies on the floor, allows herself a small smile, and immediately turns and heads toward the door. “Ladies and gentlemen of the Truck Stop Trateria,” Grappler stands up on a chair and addresses the diner, now littered with a tangle of bodies and broken dishes. “May I say, your aperitifs were wonderful, the desserts delightful, and the service impeccable. From soup… to NUTS! Ha ha ha!” Hamada turns around and grabs Grappler by the collar and drags him out of the diner before they get into any more trouble. “I’ll recommend you to Zagats, I promise! ASS JOCKEYS! Weeeeeeeeeeeeeee!” Clear Path to Heaven, Dark Road to Hell Megumi shook her happy hostage awake while it was still dark. “Whazzat?” Grapps sat up abruptly, staring bleary-eyed into the grim face of the joshi wrestler until his vision focused. “Oh. Hello there. Can I help you?” “Time to leave,” she said flatly. “Oh. If you say so.” Grapps struggled to get the seatbelt untangled from his t-shirt, erupting in a violent struggle of man versus nylon strap. Once he was done with his antics, he shifted his weight and tried to stretch, wincing out some of the kinks. “Where are we going?” “St. Louis.” “Oh, marvelous,” he said, brightening. “We can finally get to sleep in a nice warm bed instead of this infernal pick-up truck. Did you see the bumper stick on the back of this thing? I wonder which Martin brother thinks “Possum is the other White Meat”. “No,” Megumi broke in quietly. “No hotels. No carousing. No parties.” She then popped open the driver’s side door and walked out into the starlit night. Grapps exited the other side with a puzzled expression and walked around the car with his hands held out. “No?” Grapps asked, stopping in his tracks in front of the young girl. “Whyever not? Do you like parking at night next to highway signs and reading poetry off the bathroom walls of the local Flying J Truck Stop?” Megumi just breathed in some of the clean fresh night air, and looked out into the night. After another night of driving she decided to just pull off the road and park the truck next to a large highway sign, the kind usually meant to highway patrol cars looking for speeders. Megumi explained with the patience usually afforded to a rather slow child. “A very skillful tracker has been following me. I was in England last time our roads seemed to come together. I am sure he never lost me. I wouldn’t be too hard to find. I’m assuming he can pick up any magazine and find out where the AWC is heading next. But to throw him off, I need to use the least logical approach. If we travel the local roads or take an airplane he will find me. On the road, anything can happen. Three hours to show time inside a heavily secured arena, he won’t have it so easy.” “Ah. Never overlook the obvious,” Grapps commented, nodding wisely. “Something like that,” Megumi admitted. “So we get going now, we will be in St. Louis right before Bloodlust.” The masked dandy shook his head and sputtered in disbelief,” Wait. Three hours before the show? Three hours before our match with those two utterly distasteful Billy Graham knock-offs? We won’t have a chance to prepare!” “Don’t worry. We don’t need to prepare.” “No preparation?” “None.” Grapps still looked distressed, so Megumi elaborated. “Look around us Grappler. Open country. No way for the Pro Wrestler Hunter to track us down easily. And right now, he is right at the top of my list. The Furious Fists come in a close second. But look, what preparation do we need? Look.” She gestured with her arm at the night sky. “Stars, fresh air, trees, green grass at our feet, the smell of the earth as we drive down dirt roads. What more could you want for a peaceful calm before the firestorm.” Grapps caught his breath and held up both hands in a gesture of protest. “Firestorm? Bring that up now, great! Those two Bible-boozers are probably heating up the flaming coals as we speak. Well, no thank you. I think I’ll just takle my nice green pick-up truck and head right for the nearest train station, or airport…. Or hay ride out of here.” Megumi anticipated the reaction. “I’m sorry,” she told him firmly, “but you’re going to come with me.” He sighed with resignation, then smirked. “I do grow on people, don’t I?” “Hardly. I need to reach St. Louis and disappear for four hours, then I’m gone again before the last match of the night. But, “she added pointedly, “if I let you go, you will sing this song of beating truckers and riding through Missouri to everyone within earshot. I’ll be right back where I started, which will be a sitting duck for the Hunter.” “What about pre-event photo shoots? Parties? Hanging out with celebrities!? I can’t miss Tempest Bledsoe again! We made plans!” “Cancel them.” Megumi leaned against the pick-up, no further argument expected. “In fact, don’t even do that. Whoever this Tempest is, she can wait. Once we reach St. Louis and have our match with the Furious Fists, you can go.” “You are RUINING my social life!” He said despondently. Megumi breathed in, but something was nibbling at her curiosity. “Where did you learn to fight like that?” “Whazzat?” “You showed a lot of skill in our match against Norton & Bridges. And then, at the truck stop, you took out all those men. Most luchadores I know are opening match comedy acts. Where did you learn to fight?” He grinned disarmingly. “I have five older brothers.” “Very amusing,” she said dryly, crossing her arms over her chest as she studied the man. “That is not enough to explain your skill or your confidence.” “All right then, would you believe six older brothers?” Megumi’s shoulders sagged in defeat. “This isn’t getting me anywhere,” she muttered to herself. She straightened and addressed the young man in a brisk tone. “All right. Your secrets are your own. You saved my hide back there at the truck stop, and I owe you. In St. Louis, after our match, you and I are done.” “How lovely,” he drawled. “But you already said that. And maybe I don’t want to go.” St. Louis, Baptism of Fire, Furious Fists. One match, win lose, draw, or burned to toast. Then we go our separate ways.” That was her final deal. “Thank you for the kind thought of being burned to death, but no.” Exasperated, the young woman sank to the ground, her back to the grill of the pick-up. “I take it you have something ele in mind?” she observed. He sat down on a rock facing her, grimacing as he held his right shoulder. “As it happens, I do,” he said lightly. “You.” Startled, she sat upright and eyed him suspiciously. “I beg your pardon?” “Your company,” he clarified. “From now on, we shall be partners and travel-mates, like the tag team we are supposed to be. Celestial Fury!” Megumi stared at the man. Remarkable though it seemed, he was serious. “That is impossible.” “Why?” Leveling a stern look at Grapps, she said, “I work alone. I walk alone.” “Or so it is written in the stars,” he intoned, gently mocking the stiffness of her tone. Megumi looked flush and turned away. “I didn’t mean to sound so pompous,” she continued quietly. “I simply do not wish to travel with another.” “What have we been doing for the better part of a month?” he asked, then raised one hand to cut off the argument she had ready. “Yes, yes. I know. Escape, secrecy, hostage, Pro Wrestler Hunter. All that side, you said you were heading for New York sooner or later, yes? Well, I have some business in New York and who better to protect me from the Pro Wrestler Hunter, then you? Not to mention we cannot have those Alliance titles separated after we are crowned the new champions, can we?” “A straight answer from you would be a nice change of pace,” she replied. “OK, the truth? I am working on becoming a traveling troubadour. What better way to write music in the fashion of the old Mexican caballeros then to travel first hand with a prototypical gunslinger from the West. I will sing about the great Japanese woman shooter, Megumi ‘Butterfly’ Hamada!” “Sometimes, masked man, I believe you are more full of it then Tim and Liam Martin and the whole lot of them. I am not a fool and do not enjoy being treated like one—“ “What? Hmph…” Grapps was looking strangely at Megumi’s tattoo. “What is it now?” she asked, quite perturbed at the odd man’s behavior. “Well, I could swear that just now, your tattoo… it… moved?” “Ridiculous,” she said, pushing his comments aside as if they were insane. She looked at her arm anyway but brushed off his silliness with a smirk. “No, right there. The top left wing. The man and woman on horseback. The storm clouds and the bolt of lighting. Maybe it is the lights, or that side order of cheese fries at the Denny’s, but,” his voice trailed off. “The lightning bolt… and the horse’s mane.” “You must be seeing things,” she replied. “let’s get going. Maybe a cup of coffee will you have straightened out. And another twelve hours behind the wheel.” Megumi ignored his protestations as she jumped back into the truck and they sped off. _______________________________________ Dark Figure Sunrise colors still stained the sky when a large shadowy figure slipped from his hiding place among the trees. He watched as the unlikely pair took off down the road, headed toward St. Louis. To his way of thinking, they would keep backtracking and plying the dirt roads, all the way until game day and Bloodlust. She had surprised him before, of course, but now he was onto her game. The dark figure doubted that Megumi Hamada would place herself in danger without making sure her prey, or predator as how it might eventually come out to be, was stalking her. He’d followed her and watched her since September, through Triangles, across the Atlantic into Europe, England, Holland, and back here to America. She was well-trained it seemed. There had been several times when she had almost seen him, and he would not take anymore chances, not until he was ready to make his move. The morning was half spent when he finally urged his motorcycle forward, out of the brush. Effortlessly he roared the engine and headed down the hill and picked up the trail of the two wrestling desperados. With a sense of reluctance, he followed his quarry once more…
