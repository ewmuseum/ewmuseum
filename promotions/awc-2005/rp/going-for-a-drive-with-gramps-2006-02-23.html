Going for a drive with gramps.

23 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/23/06 Sputtering along, sputtering along, putt, putt, putt… “How long have you had this damned truck for?” “Longer than you’ve been alive!” “Ho ho ho!” The laugh of Santa Clause, that’s what years of cigars will do to you. He just laughed and laughed, though, he was the funniest man he ever knew. The Martin brothers were driving around their grandfather, Teddy Martin. He wasn’t an Eagle…he was before that time…but, rather, he was just an old man. Just an old man. Sitting in the truck that would sputter. Putt. Putt. Putt. “Well, boys, I appreciate you giving me this drive. Been a while since I’ve been out!” “Yeah, gramps, we know, we know. How are you keeping yourself busy these days?” Out comes the fat cigar. Ignite the flame. Light it up. Inhale…breathe…good. The smoke filled the truck, warming it up as the heater was broken. The boys didn’t mind, though, they were avid cigarette smokers themselves. The cigar just kept burning as everyone was attuned… “The people at the home, they don’t know shit about living! I just tell a story, a mild one too, and they say ‘God damn, that ain’t true!’ But what’s the difference with the truth, you know? No one tells much the truth anymore…” “Gramps, you’ve been telling crazy stories for years. Remember that one with your cousin?” Cousin Bill? Oh, Cousin Bill! Big fella. And when I say big, I mean can’t-fit-through-doorways big. This feller was absolutely massive. And along with that size was a hunger that none could fill. Cousin Bill would go to a buffet and just start piling. One place asked him to leave! An all you can eat place! It was incredible! Well, one day, way back when, Cousin Bill went to the local donut place, Dee’s Donuts. Now, Dee’s was famous for having the most delectable of donuts. I’m talking take a bite, your mouth waters type of donut. So, of course, Cousin Bill would always head over to this place. This day, though, Cousin Bill saw the place was packed! Smashed! Filled to the brim! Cousin Bill said, Hey! Why are there so many people around?! The man behind the counter, all he said was, You wanna eat? Take a ticket! So Cousin Bill did. He pulled a ticket off and walked over to the place where everyone was standing. Well, not too long, they brought Bill to a table, and he was sitting next to all these random people! Damn, Bill thought to himself, All these people waiting for donuts as well? Finally, they brought out donuts. And by donuts, I mean cart loads FULL of donuts. They put them all in the middle of the table…but strange enough, no one was allowed to eat! Bill grabbed a donut, and they smacked it out of his damn hand! Finally, though, somebody said “GO!” and Cousin Bill began to eat. And eat. And eat. And eat. Bill just kept on eating donuts! And there were people watching…and cheering! Out of no where, though, some guy stopped him…and stopped everyone else! Cousin Bill asked, What’s this, I’m still hungry! And the man just said, Hey, you won! Won what? The donut eating contest! “And, well, I think Bill got something like $50 for that! And, wow, $50, that was something back then!” “Whatever happened to Cousin Bill?” “Died. Heart attack.” The silence was killer for a few moments. It was obvious Teddy liked Cousin Bill, maybe even loved him in a family way. “You gotta admit, though, that’s hard to believe Cousin Bill was that thick. I mean, how could he not know that it was a donut eating contest?” “I dunno, simpler times?” “Simpler times? That doesn’t mean there weren’t signs or anything like that!” “Nah, it could mean that. Shit, everything was simple…did I ever tell you about that time in Vegas?” You mean the time when you were just a kid? Got out of the Navy…what were you gonna do? Not a damned thing. So, hell, why not go to Vegas? Live the life of a gambler! Make money! The fame, the fortune! But that wasn’t the way it was. You stayed in the shelters, looking for jobs. You’d wash dishes for a night and the next day that money would be blown on a black jack game. Money was nothing as well, you could just spend all your time looking for a job, staying thin while starving. Well, one day you were busing at some restaurant for a night, just trying to make a few bucks, and some feller stops you and taps you on the shoulder! He says, Hey, you a Martin? Why, yes, how did you know? You look just like your brother, Peter! You knew Peter? Turns out he knew Peter very well…he was Peter’s best man! In Peter’s wedding! Billy Conway! Teddy Martin! What the fuck you doing here in Vegas? Oh, you know, the wife wanted to spend a weekend here…how about you? I’m living here. Mr. Money Bags! Living in the city! Is it the life or what? Yeah…it is… “But it wasn’t…I always wondered, how did that schmuck, Billy Conway, end up living so well? I mean, there he is in Vegas, spending so much! At the god damn marriage of my brother years before, he didn’t look like anything special…but there he is, cash flowing from his pockets! Spending a weekend in Vegas! And I went through the damned Navy…to do what?” “It’s all about experience, Gramps. I mean, you can’t dwell.” “I ain’t dwelling, I’m just thinking…I’m living the life through stories, you know? I live that life again and again. Stories are important, boys…” “Mhm.” “They keep you living the young life! You may not know it, but you’ll get old one day!” “Mhm.” “Thing is, though, I never hated Billy. I was never jealous, either. I was just so amazed that here he was, this nothing man in the wedding, now with a pocket full of cash. And the thing that really upset me is how he could be held down…” “Whaddya mean?” “Well, I saw him that day, and he was miserable. I mean, I could tell he didn’t want to be at Vegas. The only reason he was there was cause his wife…there were people like that everywhere. Being told what to do, all that stuff. The happiest people were the most independent, like Tex.” Tex was one of those characters you could only find in Vegas. No one knew his real name…they called him “Tex” because of what he wore: big 10-gallon cowboy hat, boots with the spurs, nice cowboy jacket and pants. He looked like a Texas Cowboy, through and through. Thing is, though, he was homeless. Like me, he went from place to place, living in the shelters. But he never had a job, though. How did he live? Well, instead of an income, he was like an insurance man. People like me would give him a buck or two every time we got extra money. He’d hold onto it, remember who we were, and then, if we needed it, we’d come up to him and ask for a buck. And the conversations always went the same. Hey there Tex, how are things? Oh, just fine partner. Every day is sunny! How can I help you? Oh, Tex, I’m in the slums…how about a buck? I think I can part with a bill for ya. Thanks, Tex, I appreciate it. Anytime, partner. “Now, gramps, that’s the type of stuff I CAN’T believe…no one is that trusting!” “And Billy Conway was a free spirit. And Cousin Bill was a genius. Believe what you want, I don’t mind. I’m just letting ‘em live.” “You think Tex is dead?” “Dunno…but even if he ain’t…he isn’t living out here! Why not let him experience the country!” The truck continued to putter, sputter, sputt, and putt along, making a path to no where in particular. The boys and their grand father had been talking for a long while no. Smoking cigars, puttering, sputtering, putt, putt, putt… “Maybe the country is a scary place. Maybe he’s just comfortable where he is…” “Maybe. S’ppose I never thought of that.” “And all these years you’ve been telling us how wise you were.” “I was wise, damn it! A smart feller, too!” And boy was that true. I don’t know exactly what Teddy did out there in the Navy, but he did something smart. He wasn’t a dope in high school either; it translated into a full scholarship to the Massachusetts Institute of Technology when he got out of the Navy. Ma! Listen to this! MIT! Oh, that’s great honey! But it wasn’t. He didn’t want that. He wanted none of that. At first, he was happy…wouldn’t you be? But after the initial “WOW! MIT!”, he wasn’t so excited. Ma, I’m not so sure about MIT. Teddy, how could you NOT be sure?! It’s one of the most prestigious schools in the world! People work all their lives to get there…and then they get rejected! You have to go there! But Teddy wouldn’t have that. Instead, he jumped on that train, and headed to Vegas…the train just putted…and sputted…and spatt…and patt… “You turned down MIT?!” “No lying.” “Yeah, right gramps.” “Why would I make this up? I know you boys love me, so what am I trying to prove? What is it?” “I dunno, it’s just…MIT. That’s some smart shit right there.” “Don’t think I’m up to it, eh? That’s fine, I guess I can accept that. I guess. But what did you fellers do with your damned life?” “Gramps, we own a construction business.” Puttering…sputtering… “And what else?” “Uh…I dunno…held down the neighborhood, I suppose.” “Held down the...?!...THAT’S IT?!” The cigar flew out of the mouth and onto the road. All the while the truck kept puttering and sputtering and moving along…just putt, putt, putt… “You boys ain’t even lived! You just doin’ nothing!” “Gramps, how can you say that? We love working! Hell, we’re doing this on our free time! It’s not like we hate what we do!” Teddy Martin sat there, a semi smile on his face. He was proposing to his long time girlfriend. He had been working for years, getting out of Vegas and into South Boston. He chugged along at the meat factory, day in, and day out, just sputtering along… Mary-Lou, I’ve been meaning to ask you… Yes Teddy? He stank like rotten meat. There was no time for a shower. Love could not wait a second longer. Mary-Lou, how would you love to spend the rest of your life with me? “But you gotta go places! You can’t get held down by something! No matter what happens, do not get held down by something! By God, you gotta visit the world! See new places!” Marry you?! Yeah, Mary-Lou! Whaddya say, huh? “We don’t want to see new places…we see enough of those off-colored freaks in Boston!” Teddy… “Off colored freaks? Your father…he laid an imprint on you. I had plenty of minority friends! Why do you fellers hate them so much?” I… “I dunno…I guess that’s just the way it is, gramps.” Can’t. Ever. Not to you. Right then, Teddy sighed. “I guess you’re right, boys. But I ain’t gonna stop loving you for what monsters you’ve become.” She turned around and left the apartment. Later she got her stuff, but she never came back. So there Teddy sat, staring. He was grasping onto something he couldn’t handle, but at the same time it was part of him changing. It was his life moving. It was that last glimmer of sun, smacking the night so hard right across the teeth that it bruises. “Well, why are you calling us monsters all of the sudden?” It was the feeling of joy that never left Teddy as he moved on from Boston, the factory closing anyways. Off to somewhere else. Expecting. Clinging. “Why am I calling you monsters? Uh, you gotta respect the other races! The black people! The Asians! Respect ‘em all!” “Gramps, what have they done to respect or help one of us?” “What have they done?! The most helpful man in my life…he was…” I was sitting there on the outskirts of the sidewalk, holding a can for change, when a black man in a crisp, black suit came up to me. He stared at me. Got any change? Nah, man, but follow me. I’ll show you a different kind. So I did. I followed the man. I expected a nice car, or something great, through and through…but I didn’t get it. In fact, he led me to a grimier part of the city. His home. His refrigerator box. What the fuck is this, man? This isn’t anything great! Nah, but it is. It is. What the fuck are you talking about? You were thinking I was Mr. Money bags, weren’t you? Did you look deeper? Did you see deeper? There’s two ways to live…believing the lies or accepting them. Everyone believes them, even for an instant…the difference is those who accept them. “He was WHAT, gramps?” “Nothing…not important. Say, why are you still calling me gramps? How old are you fellers?” “Never too old to call you gramps. Why does age matter?” “Your father stopped calling me ‘Pa’ when he was 18, old enough to leave. And then he just left.” “Just left?” GET THE FUCK OUT! “Yeah, just got up and left.” Dad, I didn’t mean to get her pregnant! “That doesn’t seem like dad at all.” YOU GET OUT NOW! “Well, it’s what happened.” The girl got an abortion. Father Martin left Teddy and went to Boston to live. Teddy came crawling back eventually. But the important thing is that Father Martin ended up in Boston. But even more important…what happened to that kid? The aborted one? Oh, you’re talking about the doctor? Doctor Martin? Went to Harvard, later became one of the premier Muscular Dystrophy researchers ever? Is that the one? Father of four (Grace, Tom, Jamie, and Gordon), with Grace growing up to play in the WNBA? Tom dying in a car accident at age 14? Jamie working in a cubicle the rest of his life? Gordon having down syndrome and works at Ace Hardware? Grace’s son, Steven, grows up to be a mayor? Jamie having two kids, brothers, who opened a hobby store that just made ends meet? Oh yeah, that kid…coat hanger to the fetus. Shame really. Maybe Teddy should’ve screamed softer. Or maybe the mother should’ve manned up and accepted the fact that she fucked before marriage and this is what she gets. She gets a kid and she deals with it. Look, bitch, you know you’re having sex, you know you might have a God damn kid. But when it happens, what do you do? Do you grow a pair and accept it for what it is? Or do you putter and sputter along with your life, wanting everything to be all gravy like it was? Because you can’t. You fuck up on that caliber and that’s the step from being a little girl to maturing into a God damn woman. But, no, you wanted to fuck Father Martin. You wanted the dick in the vagina SO BAD that you wanted to fuck him. And what happens? You have a fucking kid? What a God damn surprise. So instead of trying to save it, try to give it a chance in the world because you fucked up, you give it a coat hanger. The opportunity for forgiveness has lost. You fucked up, and you took away a generation. I can see the God damned conversation now. Hi Fetus. Hi mom. Why did you end me? Because I didn’t want to face facts and accept reality. I wanted to live in a world where I was still an innocent teen. I actually went off to college, and I’m a God damned engineer now. I have a decent job, doing boring shit no one will ever give a fuck about. All because I didn’t want you. You could’ve been great, you could’ve been something special. But I’ve been a selfish bitch, clinging to my urges, my wants. And what happened when I fucked up? I fucked YOU up, Fetus. I crawled to my fucking hole and cried as I hid there, trying to find an escape. And I did. And that’s why I ended you. No, I think end is too calm a world. AND THAT IS WHY I SCRAMBLED YOU! I MADE YOU IMPOSSIBLE TO FORM! Oh, ok, thanks mom. No problem. Love you sweetie. Love you too, mom. Sputter, putter, putt, putt, putt, putt, putt… “Hey gramps, when was the last time you talked to Dad?” “The other day.” “No, I mean, like, before you died.” “Oh, before that?” “Yeah, before that.” “A few days before I got hit by that car and my hip shattered and my skull cracked and I bled to death in the middle of the road. It was the worst pain ever.” “What’d you say to him?” You’re always too hard on your boys. Why are you telling me what I am or am not? I’m just saying, let your boys live! Stop pressing on them so hard! How about this, you let me live my life like I’ve let you live yours, ok? “Just small talk.” Putter, sputter, spatter, patter, patt, patt, patt…it’s all the same…. And the truck kept on sputtering. Gramps sat between the boys…Liam could barely look above the wheel…but he was a grown man now. His ass rested on three telephone books. Inside, the pages were empty. There were no numbers. Three books sat there, holding Liam up. “That can’t be comfortable, your head hitting the ceiling like that.” His hands gripped the wheel, trying to prove something. Gramps, look at me! I’m an adult! You missed it because you died, but I’m an adult! Please! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! I try! But I do not avail. Because you are dead. And all I can do. Is sputter. And putter. And spatter. And patter. And patt. And putt.
