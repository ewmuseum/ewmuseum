Reach To Die

18 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08.18.05 08.17.05 | Kyoto, Japan | Night Time A young man knelt down in front of the base of a large tree, in the shadows of the park he'd ran into out of desperation. His breathing was heavy, as beads of cold sweat trickled down his face, matting his long black bangs of hair to his forehead. Blood lined his knuckles, those of which had shone in the moonlight that illuminated the man's frame. Under the light, the six foot tall manbrought a hand up to wipe away any tears that threatened to swell up in his dark brown eyes. His thoughts were clouded in disbelief, plagued by the sighting he had made only minutes before. Jacob had been running as far as his legs could carry him until he simply could not keep himself upright, sending himself crashing to his knees. There, he blinked fervently in an attempt to not only drive the tears away, but try and get the image out of his mind. 'There's no way...' he thought to himself in shock, 'how did he find me?' Over time, Jacob Moreno had been able to handle life in Japan, able to withstand living every day if only to know if someone he cared about was okay. All of that had become second nature to him; it was for that reason that he took on the name of his missing sister, Adalia. The realization that someone wanted him dead was not. 07.01.05 | Toms River, New Jersey | Evening 'Okay. So you wanna know how to look really retarded?' Pete laughed mid-sentence as he spoke to his older brother Jacob. Pete's tall, lanky frame stretched out on the bed in his brother's room, while Jacob sat across from him in a rolling chair by his desk, a look of feigned interest on his face. The two never seen fully eye to eye, but each of them would be quick to admit that the other man did have his moments of coolness. This was not one of Pete's cooler moments. 'So you know that fishing trip I was on up in Canada last week, right?' Jacob nodded instinctively. 'Well, me and my boy Ronnie were gonna head out early with his 'rents, but it started rainin' so we just stayed in the cabin. Eventually afternoon comes and Ronnie's like 'fuck this shit, dawg' and we head out to get some raincoats, and of course there's only snowmobile shit in Canada so we ended up taking garbage bags and making these little ass raincoats outta those. Of course, the second we head back out with the coats, it stops fucking raining.' Jacob stared at Pete, expression blank as stone. 'Well then.' The twenty-one year old Jacob wasn't so easily amused when it came to humorous stories that were the level of what Pete had just told. 'What happened after?' Pete shrugged. 'Not much.' 'Figures.' 'Oh!' Pete exclaimed. 'I drove Ronnie's SUV through this huge puddle, and the water came through the windows. Luckily we had on our garbage bag coats.' Jacob couldn't understand how that was possibly funny. Well, he could understand Pete thinking it was funny, but maybe it was just one of those 'you had to have been there' types of moments. Maybe the two brothers were just that much different from eachother. Or maybe it was the drugs? Before Jacob could come up with an answer in his mind however let alone speak it, the youngest of the three brothers- thirteen year old Alex- had eased into the room, leaning against the doorway. 'Pete? Mom needs you to lend her thirty bucks.' The middle brother looked over at Alex from the bed, tilting his head slightly out of confusion. 'Why?' Alex shrugged. 'I think Adalia wanted to go to some concert tonight and she needs money for a ticket.' Pete stared in annoyance. 'And I need to pay for this... why?' 'I don't make the rules, dude,' Alex answered, 'I just have to tell them to you and piss you off with them.' Pete sighed, his face contorting as though he were in a great deal of physical pain. 'Great,' he mumbled, 'that makes me feel a lot better.' 'So, you gonna lend her the money?' 'If she wants the money, she can ask me herself.' Jacob frowned. He was far from angelic with his parents, but he was too nice to not do things for them when asked. Of course, however, that didn't mean Jacob had no backbone; he merely had no time to hear disapproval from two people who were supposed to love him no matter what. Either way, he was going to be moving soon, and nothing was going to be able to change that. It did make Jacob feel bad, knowing it'd leave Pete in charge of helping out around the house, with dad confined to his wheelchair. 'Oh well,' Jacob thought, 'if the world was perfect, dad would still be able to walk right now.' 'You could always just do it,' Jacob spoke aloud. 'I mean, you have yourself a job, you can spare thirty dollars. She is your sister, after all.' Pete laughed, but only out of pure sarcasm; it wasn't long before his bitter side shined through in spades.'If you're so willing to have her get my money, why don't you pay her off yourself?' 'Because I do enough to help around here and don't deserve to give them more than I already have,' Jacob answered calmly. 'You hardly give them anything, anyway. What will it hurt to do something good for once?' Ouch, that one struck a nerve. 'You know... just because I don't help mom and dad out doesn't mean I don't do good things for people in general... and not for nothin' bro, who the fuck are you to tell me what to do, huh? You're twenty-one, you're an adult, great. But I just graduated high school, and I don't think my graduation present needs to be forking over thirty goddamn dollars so our adopted freak of nature can have a night on the town. You got me?' Jacob slowly shook his head as he rose from his chair. 'I got you... but you're not the only one who has it tough around this house.' 'Don't be gay,' Pete scoffed out of desperation, green eyes narrowed in anger as his fingers ran through the shaved black hair on his head. 'Don't be gay' was possibly the prime last resort defense of Pete Moreno's dignity. If all else had failed, those three words were just enough to get the man out of any situation, and this one was no exception. Jacob took one moment to let those words sink in, and that was all that was needed; Pete was out of the room and storming down the stairs. In doing so, he completely nudged past Adalia who was standing just outside in the hallway. She had heard everything. As the door remained ajar from Pete's departure, both Jacob and Adalia stared at eachother in the open door frame, Alex standing frozen from dumbfoundedness in the middle of them. Jacob's lips parted, coughing in attempt to find the words to say that could make his sixteen year old sister feel any better, but all that came out was a... '...Hey.' Adalia looked at her two siblings who stood wide eyed in the bedroom, looking back at her with a share of concern on both of their faces. Unfortunately, their concern wasn't enough for her if she couldn't get that expression from all three of her brothers. And at that, Adalia turned to sulk her way downstairs as well. Jacob, however, didn't feel as though he was ready to let things go so easily. 'Jacob?' his little brother questioned as the man made his way out into the hall, stopping his sister just before she could hit the steps. 'Listen,' he whispered, reaching into his pocket and pulling out his black leather wallet. 'Whatever you heard from Pete just now...' he trailed off just long enough to flash two twenty dollar bills, handing them to his sister with a forced smile on his face, '..that's what he says about everyone basically.' Adalia gave off a faint hint of a smile, before shyly accepting the money handed to her. 'Thank you,' she answered back softly as she bowed her head in gratitude. 'So mom's taking you to a concert, huh?' Jacob continued, his smile becoming a bit more natural. 'Surprising, I never thought she'd go outside for anything other than work and grocery shopping ever again.' Adalia looked up at her older brother, and slowly but surely, her smile faded. 'Well.. she isn't exactly taking me.' 'Eh, how did I know that one was coming?' Jacob's smile soon disappeared in turn. 'I see... do you have anyone giving you a ride at all?' Adalia blushed. 'No... I was hoping you'd take me actually.. I don't really know anyone else who would wanna be out so late.' Well, that much was true; Jacob's vulnerability towards sunlight didn't leave him with many other options other than to go out at night. That didn't exactly make him a vampire, either; vampires weren't living, breathing people like Jacob. Of course, that meant Jacob also wasn't as immortal as a creature of the night, but hey, one has to improvise with what they've been given. That said, it was little of surprise to Jacob that he was asked to give his sister a ride. That helped take the sting out of the request, leaving nothing more than one simple question. 'Well... what concert is it?' Of course, Jacob heard the last two words he had wanted/expected to hear. 'Backstreet Boys.' 'I'm afraid I can't give you a ride, Adalia. Sorry.' Adalia took seriousness from that comment and was starting to walk away when Jacob began to chuckle. 'I kid. As long as I'm not forced to stick it out, I'll drop you off and pick you back up.' That was all it took for Jacob's little sister to throw her arms around him in happiness. 'Thanks, Jacob,' she exclaimed. At least the family wouldn't be completely against one another. 'Son of a... why does this thing have to give me problems now?' Jacob popped the hood of his black 1996 Dodge Stratus, staring blankly at the numerous whatchamacallits that made- or in this case, broke- the car. 'Is everything alright with it?' Adalia inquired as her head poked out of the passenger side window. Jacob could sense the eyes on him, and looked out from behind the upright hood. 'Yeah, just a second.' Jacob didn't understand it; although he lived on that dead end street for his entire life and knew that the place was more or less dead past nine o'clock, but that hadn't stopped him from trying to at least imitate a person who knew what he was doing when it came to maintaining a car. 'Because if you don't...' Jacob fidgeted with a couple of the car parts and slammed the hood finally. '...that's when people start to wonder about you around here.' Jacob silently hoped that parents his random jostling with the objects was all the car needed for it to start, as he climbed behind the driver's seat and turned the key in the ignition. Luckily for Jacob, the car roared to life, causing a smile to spread across both of their faces. 'Looks like we have a bit of luck on our side, after all,' Adalia said hopefully. 07.01.05 | New York, New York | Night Time The place was slightly more crowded than Jacob had thought it would be; Adalia had no idea it would be so jam-packed, either, but that was a different mentality all on its known. Jacob's thought of the place being busier than expected was only off by a good half a thousand people or so. His sister, on the other hand... 'I've never been to something this... lively.' Jacob seemed a bit taken aback. He hadn't known if his sister had gone to any concerts, simple due to the fact that the subject had never arose. Either way, he could sense the apprehension Adalia had towards being amongst the sea of people. It was also at that moment that Jacob remembered something else of other importance. 'God, she's never even been in New York, period.' Adalia inched to the door next to her, but it was still quite obvious that she suddenly did not feel so content with heading out there by herself. 'You want me to head in there with you, huh?' Jacob queried, to which his younger sibling could only give off a slow, subtle nod 'yes'. 'I mean.. if you don't want to, we can just go home.. I don't want to force you to...' 'No,' Jacob cut her off, 'I'll head in, it would suck if we came up here for nothin'.' Granted, Jacob definitely wasn't as interested in the concert as others were. Girls were screaming their heads off, whereas the guys spotted in the crowd were either screaming along in just as girly of a manner as the women near them, or they consisted of men who obviously had been dragged along by their girlfriends. Of course, Jacob fell into neither category; he simply made sure Adalia hadn't gotten lost, as if he did let such a thing happen... 'For Chrissake, you good-for-nothing freak, did you go and fuck things up yet again?' The mere thought of doing anything that made him seem remotely mischevious in his parents' eyes made him cringe for that very reason. 'God, what a bitch.' Perhaps it was best off that he didn't think about that, and kept his mind on making sure he paid enough attention that such a conversation wouldn't have to take place. He had no idea that anyone else he knew would be there; it wasn't as though he got to keep much contact with his friends after high school, after all. 'Who would be here that I know anyway? Well.. aside from him..' How he even remembered 'him' was a mystery all on its known, but someone Jacob had managed it. He hadn't seen Ein since graduation day three years ago... ...and speak of the devil. 'That can't be..' Jacob mumbled to himself, unaware that he had been speaking aloud; not that it mattered anyway, since the place was so loud that no one noticed unless they bothered to read his lips, which was more or less nobody. However, whether someone noticed the gothic-looking, black-haired man staring or not, it didn't change the fact that there was indeed someone standing in the distance who had a distinct resemblance to a certain someone: shoulder length red dyed hair, tight black tee shirt, average fitting blue jeans, talking amongst two very snotty-looking young ladies... all Jacob needed to see was a good shot of the man's sneakers before he could truly judge. He slid out into the aisle, trying to get a better look at the person, and there, his suspicions were confirmed: a black pair of low tops with a pink skull and crossbones at the toes of each sneaker. Jacob only knew one person who wore those. Jacob could sense himself growing further worried when the man had turned away from the two blonde women to glance in his direction. The smile that followed set Jacob Moreno off in a fit of panic. 'No way.' 'Jacob?' The somewhat imposing six foot tall man jumped slightly at the sound of his name being whispered, but was immediately relieved to turn and see his harmless sister standing next to him. 'Is something the matter?' she asked, tapping his shoulder to make sure he listened. Her brother quickly nodded his head as a result, in a quick defensiveness that Adalia wasn't used to seeing from her oldest sibling. 'Yeah,' he stammered, 'yeah everything's fine... say.. you mind if we take off? I mean it's just past midnight, and I don't want mom and dad complaining you were out too long.' Adalia seemed a bit hesitant to leave, but she'd been grateful enough of her brother for him taking her at all. 'Sure,' she replied with a smile, 'that's alright.' It didn't take much longer than that before both of them were out of the building and looking for their parking spot; a tough feat to accomplish in the heart of New York City. In the midst of attempting this, however, Adalia found something else that also caught her eye. 'Hey, take a look..' Jacob was confused as to what his sister was talking about, until she pointed it out to him. A Dance Dance Revolution machine glowed through the window of an arcade. It didn't seem as though anyone was inside, but with all the lights on, it had to have been open. 'Can we check it out?' asked Adalia. Jacob chuckled, but it wasn't a chuckle that sounded very happy. 'Maybe some other time.. we got the game at home anyway.' 'Yeah, but I can always play it at home. Please? Just three songs.' Jacob's nice guy streak was going on for too long, why bother stopping it now? 'Eh, sure, let's do it.' Across the street the two went, checking the place out. Just inside was a man wearing a staff shirt, fiddling with the open/closed sign on the front door. 'Guess they're closing up..' Jacob muttered. Ironically enough, the teenager inside seemed to read Jacob's lips, because he soon opened the door and looked out at the two of them. 'Did you guys want to use the DDR machine?' Jacob blinked. 'Er.. how did you know that?' The teenage kid smirked, pushing up his coke bottle glasses with his index finger. 'I've been workin' at this place for three years, I know what people come here for.' Laughing, Jacob nodded his head. 'I guess you know your stuff then. I mean, if it's not too much trouble..' The kid took a look at Jacob, and then the girl next to him. His expression grew even happier. 'Well, I suppose I can let you guys have a quick run, not like I can turn down this place getting an extra buck anyway, hehe.' Smiling, Adalia stepped into the neon-lit arcade, as the teenage boy held the door open for her. 'Thank you, I needed a quick fix.' Jacob chuckled and followed his sister inside, causing the teenager to close the door. 'I'll just be in the back getting my stuff ready so I can get out of here. You guys go on ahead and play.' Adalia and Jacob looked at eachother and smiled. Two dollars later, both of them were discussing which song they would play first; on Heavy of course, those two wouldn't be any good if they couldn't play on Heavy mode. 'Shall we do Sakura first?' Adalia questioned. Jacob shrugged his slightly broad shoulders, before he reached behind himself to clutch the metal bar, leaning back on it. 'Doesn't matter to me.. I'll prolly fail it, though.' Adalia shrugged and picked the song anyway. Both of them set their options to their liking, and soon enough they were both off to a loud start, stomping on the metal pads in rapid fire succession, knocking down step after step with perfect timing. Their skill obviously was attention worthy, and they surely got some; the red-headed man Jacob spotted earlier had taken note of the duo playing across the street, and had begun calmly strolling over upon dismissing his two girlfriends with a wave of his hand and a few words that he'd just take a moment. The smile on his face was almost as if an unchangable expression; so much that it seemed to take on a whole other meaning. By the time Adalia and Jacob noticed him standing there watching, they had completed their second song, both of them already out of breath. 'Bravo,' Ein stated happily, clapping his hands as he did so, 'you always were the best at this game, hm, Jake?' Jacob's cheeks flushed with red. His sister glanced at the stranger, before looking over at her older brother with a legitimately confused look on her face. 'Who is that?' 'Friend from school,' Jacob dismissed quickly, keeping his voice at a whisper. 'What brings you here?' Ein shrugged, motioning to Adalia with disinterest. 'I see you got yourself a girlfriend, eh?' he inquired, his smile becoming all the more sinister. Jacob soon could not find much to say. What else could he say? 'She's my sister, Ein.' 'Hmph. So you not only ignore me for a few years, but you can lie to me, too. I'm very impressed by your manners.' Ein folded his arms across his chest and turned his head away from Jacob, as if quite put off by his presence. 'Well you just said it yourself,' Jacob shot back honestly, 'you haven't seen me since high school.' 'And in that time you just happened to get yourself a sister, huh?' Jacob cringed. The more he thought about it, the more he realized perhaps Ein wasn't so wrong for not wanting to believe him. It did sound farfetched... 'Yeah, I did.' Ein bought none of it, although Jacob had no intention of lying his way out of anything. He needed to be honest; he owed that much to himself. 'She's adopted, alright? I just meant to give her a ride of this concert as a means of keeping from getting bitched at from my parents. If you don't believe that, well I'm sorry.' The red-headed man frowned, and reached slowly into his pants pocket. 'It's funny...' Slowly, that hand pulled out a pistol. Adalia jumped slightly at the sight of it, although Jacob didn't seem so surprised by it. With the amount of beatdowns he remembered Ein recieving in school, he figured the guy would never want to leave his house without any kind of means of self-defense ever again. Jacob had known that first hand long ago. 'I thought you were the one who hated people who thought the word 'sorry' fixes everything.' Jacob noticed Adalia's tentative nature shining at the sight of the weapon, and things begun to get serious. It was okay if Ein tried to hurt him- perhaps he even deserved it- but Adalia had no part in any of it. 'So you think using that will fix things instead?' 'I honestly don't know,' Ein answered, waving the weapon about randomly... just as the teenage staffer of the arcade had come out from the back. 'What the hell?' BAM! 'Get out of here, kid, this doesn't have a thing to do with you.' That was all the warning the teen needed before he took off running outside, and not looking back. So much for getting any help. 'Then again,' Jacob thought coldly, 'this is New York we're talking about.' 'As I was saying.. I figured maybe you guys would wanna settle this... maturely.' With that, Ein pointed the barrel of his gun in the direction of the DDR machine. 'Play a song. Whoever gets the higher score, gets to keep their brains inside their head. ^_^' Jacob's passive look at Ein turned into a deadly glare. 'Don't think about threatening my sister. I won't take part in it.' 'Yeah?' Ein gestured. 'If that's the case, then I'll just kill you both. Would you like that?' Well, Jacob definitely had little room to argue with that. Frowning, Adalia turned to her brother, trying her hardest not to cry. However, her attempt to restrain her emotions was for naught. 'I dunno if I can play now...' 'Don't worry,' Jacob whispered, leaning forward so Ein couldn't hear, 'I''ll throw this on purpose... you'll be alright.' Adalia blinked. 'Throw.. this...' knowing what that would mean for her brother's safety, she threw her arms around his neck in a tight hug. 'No!' BAM! 'I'm getting tired of shooting this ceiling to be honest with you, and if you touch him again, I'll decide that maybe I don't feel like playing games.' With that cruel remark, Ein stepped forward and began fiddling with the controls on the DDR machine, playing the one song neither Adalia or Jacob had ever completed before: Max Period. There was a reason why the song was considered one of the hardest ones to complete in Dance Dance Revolution history, and one didn't have to look further for that reason than the six hundred beats per minute the song boasted. Couple that with numerous slowdowns and hundreds and hundreds of steps, it would wear one out if it didn't mess up their perception of the beat in the early going. 'You two are so good, I'm sure you'll be fine. Just remember, if you both fail, I'll have no problem shooting you both right now.' Adalia wished to yell back at the man, but she had no time to; the song was about to get started and both of them were ready to go- at least as ready as they could be considering the circumstances. Jacob merely prayed that his sister would pass; there would be nothing for him to do if she failed, unless he tried surprising Ein to get the gun from him, and with how far away he put himself, there was little chance of that happening successfully. Either way, Jacob refused to play, and stepped off the dance pad within the first couple of beats. BAM! Before he knew it, Jacob was on the ground, clutching his chest as rivulets of blood flowed freely from his bullet wound. 'Jacob!' Adalia screamed, turning to get off her side of the pad as well. However, Jacob quickly shook his head, knowing full well what would happen if she did. With little other choice, Adalia's feet flew like never before as her older brother bled profusely- and there was nothing she could do for him. Halfway through the song, her limbs were beginning to tire, and rightfully so; she was used to difficult songs, but none that required as much endurance as this one did. 'Keep going!' Jacob yelled, watching on in disbelief of what was going on. Sweat began pouring down Adalia's face, but she took her brother's advice and refused to stop. Her eyes remained locked on the screen for key after key, no matter how much her tears stung. Ein smirked delightfully; it was enough for him, knowing that he'd get back at the person who caused him so much pain- even if it was unintentional. Eventually, however, it was all over. Adalia fell to her knees the moment the song ended, signifying her safety when Ein pocketed his gun. Swiftly, he moved to her side, and spoke cruelly into her ear: 'You don't deserve him. You don't deserve the time to be with him.' Without another word, Ein left. Both brother and sister cried freely in the empty arcade, and neither one could believe what had just taken place. How could they? Who else had heard of someone's life being on the line from a DDR game? The sad thing was, the damage had been done. 'Jacob.. oh God, Jacob...' Adalia crawled over to her brother, staring down helplessly at him as she watched his breathing deteriorate before her very eyes. She wished there was something she could do.. anything... 'Help!' she screamed, 'please, someone help us!' No one answered. All the fuss remained at the Backstreet Boys concert across the street; too far for Adalia's legs to carry her after what she just had to endure. 'Jacob.. please... don't die, God please don't die, Jacob...' she leaned forward, clutching her brother's near limp body as though the entire world was going to die with this one man. Why things had to happen this way, she didn't know... 'And maybe they don't have to be this way at all...' 'I'll make things better,' Adalia promised with a hoarse whisper, 'I promise.. I promise, things will be better.' 07.02.05 | Toms River, New Jersey | Morning 'Holy shit...' When Jacob awoke the next morning, he was shocked to find himself in his own bed, tucked in neatly under the covers. Quickly, he lifted the bedsheets from his body and looked at his chest. No blood. No hole. No pain. 'Man...' he leaned back against the pillows, sighing a deep sigh of relief. 'What a nightmare.' He completely ignored the commotion going on downstairs. That was, until the commotion immediately entered his room. 'Jacob, you son of a bitch..' The young man looked up from his bed, shocked to find his irate mother screaming at him from the doorway. 'What did I do now?' he asked tiredly. The answer he got proved to be another big shock. 'Where the fuck is Adalia?! I let you take her to that concert, and now she's fucking missing.. goddamn you, Jacob!' She lunged at her own son, and it took the help of both Pete and Alex to get her off of him. Jacob could only watch, frozen in silence as his mother was carried out of sight, and he was then faced with another dilemma. He was alright, but what about his sister? Needless to say, he thought it was the best decision to head to Kyoto to find Adalia's real parents, and perhaps figure out the solution to the problem at hand. However, he hadn't expected to find Ein Rivera waiting for him upon arrival. That led to the current day. The day when the Battle of Britain would be held. When Jacob Moreno would be introduced to all the world, for their entertainment. However, he would not be known as an entertainer. He was not Jacob Moreno to the Atlantic Wrestling Club. To the Atlantic Wrestling Club, his name, was Adalia Delorian.
