Date: Feb 28/06 Cedric Riley sat anxiously in the back of a yellow cab, peering out the open window, catching the occasional cool breeze that relieved him of the stuffy feeling the vehicle presented. After spending a few months in British Columbia, it was expected for him to feel unfamiliar with the warmer Californian climate. Winter in Canada could be quite unforgiving, but compared to the stench coming off the driver … the cold weather sounded good. It was a little cliché, but Cedric was accustom to the backseat of taxis, and knew that some cab drivers probably lived in their workplace. “So, what’s your name?” The driver asked, looking at his passenger in the rear-view. Cedric shook himself from his momentary daze, and then peered into the mirror back at his chauffeur. “Charl- I mean, Cedric,” he finished, but not before mistaking his own identity. The driver raised an eyebrow. “Forgot your own name?” Charles smiled. “Yeah, I guess you could say that.” An almost awkward silence followed as they rolled through the streets of Berkeley in search of an apartment block somewhere on the north side of town. Cedric seemed interested in this particular setting, as he laid claim to a bit of a history with the Californian city. Growing up and learning his trade of wrestling there, and also attending its university, he had a lot of memories, some good, most bad. But even with the time when different stages of his life involving a stint in wrestling and a career in the CIA, he never forgot the people close to his heart in his hometown. After going through huge changes, especially in the past three months, it came as a surprise he was so eager to return here. After all the drama involving the CIA and his new career choice, the former Charles Kensrue needed a new home to go along with his new name and new appearance. “You know, you kind of look like a wrestler I watch on TV,” the man in the front said. “You might,” Cedric replied, almost cautious of what direction this conversation was headed. “Charles Kensrue?” He asked almost suspiciously. Charles simply nodded, looking out the window. “What, you wrestlers have secret identities outside the ring?” Charles looked in the rear-view at the driver that suspected him of he didn’t know what, maybe selfishness, or something of that nature. He smiled. “Nah … it’s kind of complicated.” “Figured so,” the cab handler claimed, looking back to the road, flicking on his lights as the sun began to go down. “Any reason you still wrestle as Charles?” “Funny that you mention it, I’m kind of trying to figure that out for myself right now,” Cedric explained, leaning back in the uncomfortable back seat, sighing. The discussion came to a stop at that as Charles leaned his head against the window, looking into the side mirror. The face that stared back at him was different than the same one he remember from about a year ago, with the rough goatee patched on his chin and the messy, slightly curled hair, and black, square, thick-rimmed glasses that surprisingly suit him. His moment of self-involvement came to an end as the cab came to a slow stop in front of a clustered neighborhood of cars, apartments, and a park across the street that seemed almost out of place. “This is it,” the driver said, turning to his passenger. Cedric handed him a few bills, thanked him, then stepped out of the car. He grabbed a single duffel bag from the trunk and turned towards the block of apartments that overshadowed him. While it still was a similar design, it wasn’t the same architecture he remembered from 8 years ago when him and a friend lived here during college. Gone was the shady, run-down look that hinted it might be home to drug dealers. Cedric was almost glad to see a spruced up, more 21st century look as he slung his bag over his shoulder and headed into the courtyard. He found the number 2 door of the apartments, which was more like a series of divided duplexes, then knocked. The door opened. Inside of it stood a man who looked in his late 20’s, like Cedric, who was taller, fit, and had a distinct business look. “So, who is it? Sean, Charles, or Cedric?” he joked, looking at his childhood friend. Cedric forced a laugh in response to the sarcasm. “Nice to see you too, Matt,” he greeted. He shook his hand abruptly and welcomed himself into the apartment. “Long time no see,” Matt said, closing the door behind him, and strolling into his living room. It was a bachelor-like setup of furniture, entertainment center, and a kitchen connected to it. In front of the doorway was a flight of steps with gaps in between them. Cedric examined the place, then dropped his duffel bag on the steps and went into the living room. He easily fell onto the couch and stifled a yawn during a stretch. “Yeah, it’s been a while,” he responded, while his friend went into the kitchen and opened the fridge. “Something to drink?” Matt asked. Cedric seemed to ignore him, finding the remote and turning on the TV. Matt came back into the living room with two cans of Sprite, throwing one to his couched visitor. “So, what have you been up to the past eight years?” Cedric sighed. “Want the rundown? I left University, wrestled for four years, spent four years in the CIA, and now I’m wrestling again, in the midst of changing identities. It’s a long story, trust me, you don’t want to hear it.” Matt took a long drink. “Wow, compared to my stint in Hollywood and return to Berkeley, that’s an epic.” “It’s more of a nightmare, really.” “Ha, being a film producer is about the same,” Matt related. Cedric did a double take. “Wait … you failed to mention that on the phone.” “Whatever, it isn’t that big of a deal.” “So that would explain why these apartments are completely different from when we lived in them back in college,” Cedric said. Matt shrugged. “Thought I’d spruce the place up.” “Seems more like a few borderline condos instead of a motel,” Cedric complimented, adding a laugh. A moment of silence followed, and the timing seemed right for a phone to go off. The ringing was muffled, but Cedric turned his head to his bag, where the sound clearly came from. He got up from the couch and briskly went to the stairs, digging through his things until he found his old Motorola. He opened it up in hoping he got to it on time. “Hello?” “Hey.” Natalie. Cedric was almost relieved to hear her voice, for some reason, probably because he hadn’t heard from her for a while. After everything that had been going on, he seemed to have forgotten how much time had passed since he had last been in London. “Charl- I mean, Ced, I have some news. Good and bad.” Charles raised an eyebrow. “Good first, I guess.” “Well, someone is here to see you. At my place,” she enlightened almost hastily. “Alright, I don’t know if that is exactly good, but go on,” he said edgily, sitting down on the step and awaiting the next bit of Natalie’s news. She hesitated. “It’s your brother.” Cedric ran a hand through his hair. Great.
