3: under my skin, you're breaking in

31 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 31, 2006 Logan, Today has yielded me so many stories, even as I alter tantric timestamps and pretend again that it's night. But how many stories have been written on my face? On every wrinkle of my eyes that see without seeing? On every twist of a forehead and stare? I do my best not to frighten people with my habits; I'm not out to destroy them, simply to use them for a different purpose. But today has yielded me so many stories even as I sit here and do nothing but listen to the slow beat of them in my head. I profiled the life story of an entire man, the afternoon past, because I couldn't stop dreaming. In any other person's world that would have been a necessity to stop. Yesterday – two hours watching two mimes in an exercise in nothingness. There is a man two seats away from me. He bites his finger. He wears a pink shirt. I wish his name was Oliver because then he could give my life a twist, twist my arm into saying something, twist my hair into something beautiful, twist my history. I watch him when I pretend to lift the curtains and he is drawing. I wish I could draw. He sits there for hours and draws. I sit there for hours and watch in my bleeding, pregnant pause. I could write him a letter: Dear So-And-So, I hope your name is Oliver; I'll be sad if it's is not, will you give me your phone number so I can call you up and listen to you talk drawings and paint? Will you paint me? I am a narcissist. I would sing him a hymn if he believed in God (and since he is made up in my mind I will, tonight, when I’m stark alone in the middle of nowhere thinking of something to do). There is a woman who moved me yesterday, who reclined in her painting with a chain of cigarettes between her fingers, looked cool though I was shivering standing looking at her wishing I had a scarf to put on. There is a man whose mouth spills objects; there is a boy who receives them. There is a string of red and white panels which match the colors of my blood cells and are equally static: the small shapes dart like something ancient making a move for the present, the bend with the edges chipped off, white as lace so long my eyes feel like stubs in my face, the entire of me stubby in small and crouching existence, expanding later as I watch people disappear out of sight, like descending into brightness, until I'm the only one left in the clatter of background noise and no music. I wrote a list of five things that made me want to write: stories in faces, the artist in the pink shirt, three artworks, long fingernails, and watching people disappear out of sight. Now, reading back, I can't tell which is which. Is this a lack of sight, foresight or insight? I can't tell. Maybe it's all three; I'm always looking shallowly and blindly into the past. ** El, I woke up today and discovered the sky was falling. The blue ceiling was starting to peel at the edges, creating evolutionary wrinkles in the horizon. The sun had already given up, hanging loosely off the sky, rays brazenly flirting with the water. Sky pushed at my fingertips; I dipped them in the ocean, but the ocean pressed into my skin. I swished droplets onto dry shores that have never tasted salt, only acid, and ran across them, the vinegar feel burning into the soles of my feet. The sky and salt mixed in my palms, molding to the curve of my finger pads; everything I touched turned a glittery blue in the hanging sun and a fleeting silver in shadows overcast by moody clouds. I imagined this is what Mother Nature's fingers felt like. I waltzed inland, cities' lights threading through my hair like modern shower gel, twirling past pastures and blooming apple trees. I stopped for a picnic lunch in Kansas and then hitched a ride with a tornado heading east. I traced America's Manifest Destiny, running through the pages of history before Sky could have an affair with Earth. (at the expense of all life-- Earth was an expensive lover to keep and Sky was a bit blind -- shit happens when you have clouds covering your eyes) Mother Nature lent me her sons and daughters; (she always did care too much about Earth for her own good) I leapt from the tornado to a cloud, rode a thunderbolt down to Indiana and took a nap on a squirrel's back as we made for the Atlantic. On the sandy dunes of an ocean washed by European customs, I built a sandcastle protecting the backbone of baby cries borne in the shadow of a once-great country. It didn't last of course, the tide always comes, always on time, always in this place, and Sky had fallen into the Earth, eternity forming where they met. I thought, as history weighed down on me: as failures go, one ending in an eternity can't be all that bad. -- Logan, Things I remember about today: a telephone hot against my ear, voice in my throat, the light making absurd shadows out of the simplest things. I was thinking about standing in the middle of a grassy field, in the eye of an amphitheatre, the crowds high above; lights bright and casting doubt into a figure smashed flat against the ground and me, standing tall, hawk-like, waiting for something or someone to come from the opposing end and walk towards the center like a diatribe compulsively attacking itself, like an escape from death, impossible to unlock. I'll open my arms and someone will replace the air, the lights so bright above us as I'm spun in wind, becoming younger and younger and younger and - All of a sudden I'm flying, carried by a draft, holding the hand of Peter Pan, the both of us becoming the sighs of a dandelion's wing. I quote Zau. “I only fall in love with people I know will never love me back, and if there's a chance that they might, I fall out of love.” I dream in the evenings about touching the pages of a book only to have my hand disappear, a fantasy novel where I pen my own ending. These past months have been this way: orchestrated, the warmth of a belly against my back a cage from which I would never choose to leave because the sky is free and stark. I'm a bird in a cage, a heart between ribs. Outside I am a bird among a flock of a hundred thousand, a heartbeat of the many muddled thumping people falling in love with each other. I'm falling too, but differently. I stare from the windows of a high building and think what it would be like to fall -- not to death, but to fall and never hit, to have hands wave at me from their windows and to have me grasp each and every one, relive a thousand lives in a thousand different touches. That, I think, must be what it is like to fall into a love the size of the ocean and an ocean the size of a myriad of molecules suddenly shifting in the waves, each atom a dream person, each ocean an imagination. Five oceans making countless possibilities. Yet how a dream never fails to love me back. ** El, five things I would buy if the world was on sale: 1. A face with character. Someone once told me pretty ones never have any. 2. Something intangible and fleeting -- free jazz or love or the miracle angles of an extraordinary play -- to catch and hang in my window, like mobile sculpture, like kinetic art. 3. Some place obscure in the mountains or by the sea where I can take my family after all is said and done. I know I’m fortunate but it charms me, thinking that we could live simply, in some sort of divine poverty. Which is, you once said, a little wearily, just the kind of ridiculous scenario, without thought or practical implications, I would dream up. 4. HBO. 5. A way to smooth away pretenses, sad affectations; a way to fit the lipstick overwhelming a woman's wrinkled face to the outlines of her mouth; a way to end tanning salons and self-help books and the defacement of plastic surgeries; a way to enfold self-deceits and then lower them down, very gently, into nothingness. -- Logan, In that dream I’m twenty-four. You’re twenty-nine. My hair is down to my waist and very straight. Outside, it’s cold but it doesn’t snow. I’m wearing a tan coat, a rainbow scarf, all mismatched. It may be a Starbucks’ door I push against, if only to make myself warm in. Regardless, it’s a rectangular café by the corner, glass on two adjacent sides, a counter and a red wall on the other. Outside, there’s a busy street, the cars zooming past, across, brown buildings in old architecture. It’s morning. I have books in my arms, a messenger bag slung across me, and my feet drag in vestiges of winter. Central heating makes my cheeks pink. I order a vanilla latte. They ask for my name and I pretend to be someone else. I think it’s Audrey. I’m Audrey-like that day, even as I pull off my gloves in an undignified manner and have to hold them between my teeth to get my purse. My change clatters along the counter. They mess up my order and give me a size too big, but I take it anyway. You’re sitting away from the windows, near the counter, behind the shelf of mugs stacked tall, Halloween-themed. You’re reading. One of those intellectual books I never had much taste for but you seemed to like, which made me think, Okay. I see you reading. I think, Okay. The first thing I say is hello. Between then and my next sentence someone opens the door and a gust of cold air dashes in and dashes me in the eyes. I have to keep blinking to make sure I’m not dreaming. The welcome bell rings. You know, that bell that rings when someone opens the door to one of those tacky stores along a shopfront. Well, the welcome bell rings. So in between there is door opening, a gust of cold air, and a welcome bell ringing. The thing is, for the longest moment in the sequence of events I think I get the wrong person. I don’t understand what you’re doing there, with a smart book in your hand, not when you’re supposed to be a continent away, a whole era away. I think, why does the past resurface from water, not having been sufficiently drowned? I don’t realize that I’ve answered my own question. You’re wearing glasses. I can’t remember if I ever told you you should always wear glasses. You look up above those glasses. They’re the same pair. That’s what makes me certain then, it’s the glasses you wear, the lenses you see through, a different mind looking through the same eyes of a different person. I think, then, that you are about to reply, and I lean forward trying to hear, above the clatter of cutlery and the shuffle of morning news, to hear if you’re going to wonder who this mismatched girl with the long, straight hair and vanilla latte is. For a moment I think you’re going to tell me that I’m still small, or that I’m familiar, or tell me that you overheard me and what a nice name Audrey is, each response going further away from the other. Just as you are about to speak, I wake up to darkness. ** El, The man in the prosthetic mask smiled at you that day, do you remember? Your shoulders shook and my hands held them in place. – Logan, What I remember is that Claire's birth permeated the hollows of an open ground. ** El, How have you been, by the way? I miss the way you'd walk into the coffee shop, stories lingering in the curl of your lip and promises crossing your ankles under the thin tabletop. I think you only ever wore skirts on dates, nails clacking like plastic marbles against the angles of your wrists, knees bumping foolishly on a summer day, when the sun lit your heart into a brief golden glory. I left four messages on your phone yesterday. -- Logan, It was in you that I discovered the hues of forgotten horizons; paint splattered our hands as we struggled to color our world. We were so afraid of forgetting. We picked up the stones of our past and putting them in our bags and I never realized how heavy our pockets had become until I couldn't step forward. We climbed our mountains side by side in silent solitude while grasping each other through the rainbow afterimages branded on the back of our eyelids from looking at the sun for too long. In the end we might be blind, but we never really needed eyes to see. I'm fine. ** El, You held me in misty half-life moments and watched as I grew into the burdens of myself, did you know? I long to run my hands over your face again, brushing over cheekbones and the expanse of your mouth because you were there I first discovered understanding and I want to understand you, to dip myself inside the essence of you and find out what makes you so addictive. You're a craving now, grasping onto the edges of my soul. If asked, I think you would take me apart. You are more and less than everything I ever thought of you, and I wonder if I'll ever discover where forever went. – Logan, I am one of many to know that I don't know a thing about myself. The northern winds tell me there are answers in the aligning of the planets with the spherical sun; many days I pass the hours by wondering if there really is anything deeper than the creased waves that rise and fall in steady patterns on the ocean's surface. What lies below that filmy viscosity, the slight sticky touch of water under palms? Sometimes I smile and play the fool: move the chess pieces, the bishops and pawns, like I have a plan. I shut my eyes and stare straight ahead, dividing my mind up into squares, light and dark as the chessboard, and number my disasters in each box. There are yellowed books with torn bindings that inhabit the caves inside me and their words are heroine to my veins. They capture me as I turn my back and they whisper to me the secrets of love and literature that I can't ignore. With the passing of seasons, I shed skin and tears as animals do, I lose myself in pieces with every transitory change in winds and time. I don't understand how every snowflake can be different - there are only x numbers of ways to form a frozen sliver of ice and pressure into an object of admiration. Eventually, nature will run out of designs. Eventually, nature will have to start again. ** El, Our old bedroom whispers of rain and neglected fields, where choking weeds peel the paint off the brittle walls. The faded mask flakes away like sun-burnt skin, raw and tenuous as the city dissipates to ashes behind the curtains. You used to tell me that you'd live there forever because the view was priceless. Yesterday I lifted the cardboard boxes to transport history away, one by one. Later, I hunted for breatheable air at a phonebooth on 30th and Madison. Years ago, one flesh-and-bones human wrote messages on the smudged glass walls; in an exercise of repition my middle finger slides over the obscene words, tracing fuck over and over, unconsciously. -- Logan, It isn't easy to act out theory; the paradigm of surprise keeps us waiting until every wave has broken and erased every footstep in the sand. I don't know any other way to express my feelings than to put a dictionary in your lap and ask you to multiply the number of words written by a hundred. ** Ellis, The future is always something you’ve wanted to control and pull apart with your fingers. You can't control the present, though; can't control the stars. -- Logan, Maybe, but if I could believe in anything it wouldn’t be you. Love is about fighting and forgiving and imperfections and faults and everything that I hold myself too far above. ** Ellis, You know -- you were beautiful once, when you had worried over not enough bone and too much skin, but now it's raining outside and even promises do not care for the dead. -- Logan, Look. Rule Number One: When you are supposed to die, you do. And if you follow them, there are no penalties and no weighing of the scales and distributing of the debt. I'm thankful, we all are, but now I'm standing talking to a department store mannequin with sequins for eyes. Her breaths are numbered; who else is there to blame? It was your mistake that will kill her, it was your error that has stolen the beats of her heart, it was you. It was always you. ** Ellis, Don't you dare blame me. If anything, it is both of our faults. -- Logan, Thousands of apologies mean nothing to the Death. “Ellis?” Yes? “Where do you think your forever went, then?” To death. “To death?” To death. With our daughter.
