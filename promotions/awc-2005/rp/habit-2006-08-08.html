Habit

8 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/8/06 Amber pulled down the sleeve of her tight-fitting black cotton top, covering up the puckered marks on the inside of her elbow. Calmly, she brushed hair away from her face and looked up at the man across the table. He was a hard-looking sort, his features angled and set as if carved by an amateur or a madman. In a heavy jacket and a beanie, he didn't look dressed for the near-heatwave weather, but that didn't bother Amber in the slightest. He had what she wanted, so she would deal with him. ''What's taking you so long?'' Amber asked impatiently, crossing her right leg over her left and folding her arms under her breasts. The man looked up at her and sniffed dismissively, going back to his items. Amber rolled her eyes. ''Hurry up, Creigh. I've been waiting for half an hour and—'' ''Quiet,'' the man said in a plain, almost monotone voice. They locked eyes briefly before Creigh looked back down at his task. Amber shivered, turning away and looking up at the solitary window. The only time he showed anything close to an emotion was when he had smack flowing through his blood in a 1:1 ratio. He was like a ghost now, a spectre of a human that haunted the place where he had died. They were forced to meet in what looked like an aboveground bunker; the walls were undecorated and unpainted grayish bricks and the floor was a flat slab of concrete. A lamp sat atop the wooden table that Amber and Creigh were situated at, providing the dealer with enough light to do what he needed to do. The door to the rest of the shoddy building was left slightly open and Creigh made a habit of glancing at it every few minutes to make sure that no one was coming. Stress had dominated Amber's life for weeks now. She couldn't think at times with the way it clogged her brain; it slowed her down, it nearly made her black out. Maybe she was hallucinating, but if she was, it wasn't a good sort of trip. Between Anton and Luis and that insufferable maggot Nneka, she felt as if she was entitled to a release. If that release took the form of a new addiction, well, it was hers. Creigh nodded, lifting his head up and pushing a bit of his hair back under his red beanie. ''Okay,'' he announced. ''Twenty bucks.'' Amber nodded and reached into her black leather purse, slipping out a twenty and sliding it towards Creigh. He took the bill and stuffed it into his inside jacket pocket before pushing her a blue balloon. She picked it up and inspected it. ''This doesn't look like twenty's worth,'' Amber said dejectedly. Creigh snorted. ''I could have given you a goddamn kilo and you'd say the same thing,'' he shot. ''You got what you wanted, I got what I wanted. Any reason why we should stick around talking?'' Amber shook her head and stood up, slipping the balloon into her purse. Creigh stood as well, quickly gathering all of the items he'd had on the table and slipping them into a box. ''Up the stairs and down the hall?'' Amber asked. Creigh nodded. ''All the way at the end on the right,'' he told her. Amber nodded back at him and slipped through the door, starting up the creaky stairs. She supposed she should have thanked him, but she let it go as she pushed open the door at the top of the stairway. She followed his directions and soon found herself outside the house, starting down the sidewalk towards the street. A tall figure stood up from its position crouching near a vehicle. She gave him a smile as she came closer. Anton was dressed in a dark red t-shirt and black jeans, looking implacable as always. He was tender to her, though; she hoped it would make people jealous the way that he softened his touch with her. She slipped her arms around his neck and they shared a kiss. ''I hope you weren't too lonely,'' she cracked. Anton chuckled. ''I was fine,'' Anton said. His brow suddenly furrowed. ''What were you—'' ''Don't ask,'' Amber cut in. ''Please?'' Amber watched him as he searched her eyes to see if he could dig anything out of them. She kept her expression as pleading as she could and hoped it would mask anything that she didn't want to reveal. ''Fine,'' Anton said at last. ''Let us go.'' Amber nodded, fishing her keys out of her purse. ''Let's.'' She was doing this for Anton. She had to have something to help herself endure all of this until she could dispose of Luis. After that was done, she'd kick this habit. She could do it. She just needed a little time. Nneka closed her eyes as she took a deep drag of the cigarette. Her legs dangled over the edge of the balcony and she balanced herself with a hand. Smoke swirled around her head and wild brown-dyed hair. She opened her eyes and looked over the skyline. It was beautiful the way the sun cast red over everything. She felt a sense of awe that was almost primordial, coded into her genes. She couldn't help it. A smile spread over her face. Luis was in the hospital room behind her, laying in bed and trying to rest. She was so proud of the way that he never gave up, the way he always managed to smile at her, managed to joke that he'd be up and fucking her in the next day. Nneka knew he needed that much more than he needed anything else; the inner strength to keep going on. She thought that he was wasting away, though. She thought he was dying under those blankets, under the care of all those doctors. He was slipping away from her. She twisted and dropped off of the balcony railing into the little box itself, stretching her arms. She pressed the end of the cigarette into the wall before flicking it away and pulling open the screen door, stepping through. ''Morning, Luis,'' she piped up. The nurse, dressed in dark blue scrubs, set Luis's breakfast tray in front of him. The Venezuelan lifted a fork and picked at what looked like scrambled eggs. Nneka had never tried the hospital food, but she had heard it was shit. ''Eh,'' Luis grunted in reply. He scooped up a bit of eggs onto his fork and lifted it halfway to his mouth before looking up at her. ''Hey, Nneka, you ever wonner why they can't feed us nothin' right? I mean, we's payin' fer alla dis, leas' they cuh do is feed us.'' ''You have the eggs,'' Nneka said. She sat down next to him and combed hair out of his face. ''Shit, Nneka, don' you know nothin'?'' he growled; she knew the growl was more for the eggs than for her, but she couldn't stop her eyes from widening just a bit. ''This shit ain't fit for dogs. But I gotta eat it if I wanna get up…'' Luis ate the eggs that were on the fork; he made a face, but he choked them down all the same. Nneka smiled and leaned into him. ''So you've been okay?'' she asked him. Luis arched an eyebrow and looked over at her. ''You beeen here wit me the whole time,'' he said. ''You don' know?'' Her smile wilted a bit and her eyes became serious. She exhaled lowly. ''So, you'll be okay if I go out for a bit?'' ''What for?'' ''I said I would meet him,'' she told Luis. Luis frowned, taking another forkful of eggs and chewing pensively. Nneka rested her hand on top of Luis's head as he chewed. She knew that Luis wouldn't like her meeting with her brother, wouldn't like her talking with him. Luis was trying to deal with this himself and he either thought Nneka was an incompetent or that Anton was going to murder her once he saw her. She knew he would object, at least internally. She was going either way, though. ''Alright,'' Luis said after a bit. ''I don' like it, but you gotta go if you gotta. Come back, though. Eh?'' He lifted his hand up shakily, raising her chin. Nneka's grin returned full force and she kissed Luis on the nose. ''I'll be back,'' she said, hopping off the bed. ''It shouldn't be too long.'' She glided to the door as if her feet were three inches from it, moving out and into the hall easily. Now she just had to talk her brother into letting go of Amber. Suddenly, her soles thudded back against the tile.
