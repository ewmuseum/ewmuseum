Chronical Three: Christmas Shopping

10 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/10/05 [Snow sprinkles the air with a certain sense of magic that is found only in this time of year. The wind gently blows as our cameras start to zoom in on Patrick Mapleleaf’s brand new Toronto mansion. The front security gate is the focus of the camera, as two figures are seen walking towards it. The two figures, upon closer inspection, are girl scouts, bound up in large green coats and carrying plastic bags, which the keen observer can deduct that they contain girl scout cookies. They approach the large iron gate, which has a Mapleleaf on the top. The wall is solid brick, surrounding the perimeter of the mighty dwelling. One of the girls presses the button to the intercom system…] [We now are shown the inside of Mapleleaf’s mansion. The big guy himself is sitting on a brown leather recliner inside of his library, fumbling to read the latest in his literary endeavors. The library is coated in brown wood paneling with a hard wood floor. The scent of pine disinfectant is in the air, and the window is slightly ajar to allow the flow of fresh air. A fire is brewing in the fireplace across the room, which contains a portrait of Mapleleaf above it. Actually…the entire library is adorned in pictures of Mapleleaf. Mapleleaf licks his finger, puts it on the page, and turns to the last page of the mighty book that he has finally finished. He reads the last page, and throws the book into the fireplace in frustration.] Mapleleaf: GAH! What an ending! Never have I seen such rubbish! He actually eats the Green Eggs and Ham! I never could have seen that coming! BUZZZZ Mapleleaf: Yes? Girl’s Voice: Girl scout cookies! Mapleleaf: Oh, very nice, sweetheart! I love girl scout cookies! I’ll open the gate for you. Why don’t you just come on in? Girl’s Voice: Really? Thanks! [Mapleleaf presses the button to buzz the girls in. A devilish grin crosses his face.] Mapleleaf: Generically named butler! [A butler, wearing a black and white tuxedo that makes him resemble a penguin, enters at once. He has a large belly and a shiny bald head, adding to the illusion of a walking, talking penguin.] Generically Named Butler: Yes, sir? Mapleleaf: Release the hounds! [Generically Named Butler nods his head and leaves at once. There are a series of switches on the wall. He presses one. All of a sudden, the walls in the library open up, and a live mariachi band appears. They begin to sing, according.] Band [singing]: OHHH MARIA!! WE LOVE YOU SOOO!!! OOOHHH MARIA!! PLEASE COME HOME TONNIIIIGHT!!! Mapleleaf: I said hounds! You pressed the wrong button! Generically Named Butler: My apologies, sir. The “Live Mariachi Band” button is right next to the button for the hounds. Singer: No! Don’t put us back in! We’re hungry, man! Drummer: I’m having sharp pains in my chest! Mapleleaf: That’s the price you pay, MUCHACHOS! Put them back! [The Butler presses the button, and wall closes again. He presses the hounds button this time, and the sound of screaming girls and growing of seven Doberman pinchers is heard. Mapleleaf rubs his hands in satisfaction and smiles.] Mapleleaf: I love it. It’s like opening presents on Christmas morning. Speaking of which…I need to start my Christmas shopping. Generically Named Butler: Might I suggest…a new tie for me. This one is starting to stink. Mapleleaf: Me? Give other people presents? Really now, that’s not what Christmas is about! It’s about giving to the people most important to you. And, since I’m the most important person to myself, I am going to go do me some shopping! - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - [The mall is, predictably, crowded as Patrick Mapleleaf treks through. He is wearing a black overcoat, stylish designer jeans, and a red wool cap on top of head. His blonde hair is streaming through the cap, glistening with the best hair care products money can buy. Next to him is his best friend, Cedric, wearing a new gold disco suit. His black hair is in a freshly frizzed afro. The African-Canadian has not lost a step, snapping his fingers at all the fly honeys he walks by. Mapleleaf is carrying so many bags that he now has to have a shopping cart, which is overflowing. As they pass a Brooks Brothers clothing store, Patrick Mapleleaf sees a car in the middle of the mall. There is a drawing box next to it, with a sign that says, “Enter to Win!” The car is a sky blue Ford Focus, shiny and glistening in the mall light. The mall worker in charge of the drawing is standing next to the car.] Mapleleaf: Cedric, I have to have this car. Cedric: What it is, daddy-o. That’s one smooth ride, g. Can ya dig? Mapleleaf: Oh, I dig. I definitely dig. I’m gonna buy it right now! Here, help me get it into the cart. Cedric: …. Mapleleaf: Help me! [Mapleleaf brings the shopping cart next to the Focus. He pushes the car as hard as he can, attempting to actually get it into the cart.] Cedric: Yo, homie…you cain’t get that car into that shopping cart. Besides, ain’t you had enough for today? You already bought yourself fourteen plain white shirts, each costing 60 dollars, a facial, a hair appointment, a pair of women’s underwear--- Mapleleaf: ---I told you, I’m experimenting! There’s nothing wrong with that! Cedric: You also bought a football helmet full of cottage cheese, that painting of circles and lines for 7,000 dollars, a pack of dogs to pull your sled--- Mapleleaf: Good investment I may add. Cedric: They’re Chihuahuas! Mapleleaf: Yep, so daddy will have the cutest dog team of all. Cedric: You also bought— Mapleleaf: Cedric, haven’t you ever heard of the season of giving? Cedric: Yeah, but I hardly think--- Mapleleaf: One of the most important things one can do is give to oneself. Cedric: But you give to yourself all year around. Mapleleaf: Okay, am I gonna have to call those ninjas I bought earlier to come out and attack you? Cedric: You…bought human beings?! Don’t you know my people were enslaved for hundreds of years? Mapleleaf: So were mine! Cedric: What?! Who?! Mapleleaf: The rich! Haven’t you ever read about…the… We….well, I guess…umm…oh wait. Hey, I’ve actually had it pretty darn good! Woo! Aren’t you happy for me? Cedric: We were talking about me! Mapleleaf: Typical Cedric…only thinks of himself. Anywho, let’s get this car and get outta here. Excuse me, sir? Man: Yes? Mapleleaf: How much for the car? Man: It’s not for sale, sir. You have to win it in the drawing. Mapleleaf: Bugwash on a stick! I want to buy this car. It is a gift for someone very special. Man: I’m sure they’ll understand… Mapleleaf: Actually, it’s for me, and I won’t understand. Now, as the French say, GIMME! Man: There is absolutely no way you’re driving off in this car… - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - [Patrick and Cedric are driving on the highway, back towards Mapleleaf’s manner, in his brand new Ford Focus. As Patrick adjusts the mirror and takes a look at himself, he feels pretty pleased with what he sees.] Cedric: I still can’t believe he gave you this car. Mapleleaf: Well, as I said, everyone has their price. Cedric: You could have picked this car up for an eighth of what you paid for it. That’s right, knot-head. You paid eight times the car’s value. Ya know, I’m considering not even getting you a gift this year! Mapleleaf: Too late, you already did. Oh, I almost forgot, I need to give this back to ya. [Mapleleaf hands Cedric back his MasterCard.] Cedric: What the? Mapleleaf: You think I was gonna pay for that car with my OWN money? Come on, it was eight times the value of the car! I’m not THAT much of an idiot! Cedric: I can’t believe you did that! Mapleleaf: You should be thanking me! Most people struggle and agonize over getting the perfect gift for their friends. Now you don’t have to worry about it. Of course, my birthday is coming up, and – Cedric: Imma kill you! Mapleleaf: Racial violence…Cedric, you should be ashamed of yourself! Besides, I did get you a gift. Do you think that I would leave you out? Cedric: Yes. Mapleleaf: Absotutely not, my friend. For being my best friend, I present to thee… [Mapleleaf reaches into his wallet. Cedric smiles in anticipation, awaiting a crisp hundred…maybe some fifties…a stack of twenties…or…] [A single white card.] Cedric [reading the card]: 2 dollars off any car wash with a full up of BP gas. You…you… Mapleleaf: I know, I know. I would use it on myself, but that would be downright selfish. Cedric: It expired in August of 1997! Mapleleaf: Did it? You should have used it sooner then! Cedric: But— Mapleleaf: Now, now, better luck next year! Maybe then you won’t wait to use your Christmas presents! [Mapleleaf puts on his right turn signal. He pulls over into the shoulder, which overlooks a mighty river, intertwined in the light from the sun that is beginning to peak from behind the clouds. There is about a thirty foot drop between the shoulder and river. It flows slowly due to the frost on the ground. Mapleleaf gets out of the car, and Cedric, completely unaware of what is going on, follows suit. Mapleleaf then opens the driver’s side door and takes off the emergency break, then putting the car into neutral. Without warning, he closes the door, gets behind the car, and rolls it over the ledge. Cedric watches in horror as the car crashes into the river, smashing into millions of pieces.] Cedric: NO! What the—WHY?! Mapleleaf: Eh, it’s an American-made car. I didn’t like how it hesitated a split second before changing gears…piece of crap. Cedric: My money…ALL GONE! Mapleleaf: Oh, come on, Cedric. Remember, you’re a millionaire! You’re a successful wrestler and you’re in a multi-million dollar contract! Cedric: No I’m not! I’m a produce clerk at the grocery store! I live in a small, one bedroom apartment, and I have to take the bus everyday! Mapleleaf: Oh. Yeah, that’s right. I was talking about me. Sorry, life sucks, doesn’t it? Cedric: You truly are evil. [Fade]
