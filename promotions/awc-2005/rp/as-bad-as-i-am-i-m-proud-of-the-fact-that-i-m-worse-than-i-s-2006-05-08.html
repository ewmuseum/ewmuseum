As bad as I am, I'm proud of the fact that I'm worse than I seem.

8 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-8-06 “Jack Benson was not a great man. And depending on who you ask, you might not even hear that he was a good man. But born in the 20th century and having lived on through the 21st, Jack Benson was a man. A man of uncompromised ideals and unyielding faith. In this day and age, it’s hard to ask for much else.” The rain had finally begun sprinkling drops on the early morning crowd gathered to pay their last respects to the Nova Scotia native. The morose occasion had led the crowd to huddle somewhat close together as they listened to the priest’s final words. One might think…one might even want to believe that the huddled mass was so close together out of the combined fondness they’d had for Benson. More likely it was because the funeral patrons were attempting to shield themselves from the rain under the sparse amount of umbrellas that were available, in combination with no rain showers in early May being quite as frigid as the Northeastern Canadian variety. But just as misty as the sky had become, so had more than a few of the eyes in the audience, and their owners clutching the people around them for support. Perhaps Jack’s passing had brought these people closer together. Darcy Markson, on the other hand, never met Jack Benson. Never even heard of him, in fact, until Amy confronted him about accompanying her to his funeral back home in her native country. At his side stood Amy, too proud to shed a tear but not too strong to clasp Darcy’s hand as the two looked on at her surrogate “Uncle” Jack’s coffin, where he lied in state. Amy had said very little once they’d arrived for the funeral, leaving Darcy to his own thoughts. And because his emotional attachment to Benson was virtually nil, his mind was free to wander to other matters. Like, for one thing…why does today have to be so gray? Darcy thought to himself. Understandably, there wasn’t much that could be done about the weather. Nobody asks for a rainy funeral on a gray gloomy day…come to think of it, nobody really asks for gray and gloom on any occasion. But those days still tend to come around, like those irritating friends who nobody ever invites yet nobody has enough resolve to tell them to get lost. “Sometimes, you just gotta make do…” Darcy accidentally whispered to himself aloud. Amy had caught that he said something, but unable to make out what he said she turned her head to look at him inquisitively. Unwilling to explain himself, however, Darcy continued staring at the coffin, acting as if he’d said nothing at all. But of all the things gray on a day typically reserved for the flowers of May, there was one Darcy did not understand; the eulogy from Father Thomas. Jack Benson was a man? Those aren’t exactly the most touching of words…he thought. He pondered the notion for a few moments. He remembered a touching quote supposedly uttered by the immortal Jonathan Frakes in a Star Trek movie, but first heard by Darcy through the mouths of the equally immortal Parker and Stone on an episode of South Park: “Don’t try to be a great man…just be a man, and let history make it’s own judgment.” An easy credo for actors and amateurs to live by, Darcy mused. I wish it were so easy for me… Darcy gently squeezed the hand of his counterpart, who showed practically no emotion on her face as the casket began its descent into the ground. He wondered what she was thinking precisely at the moment – hoping the focus was on the burying of her beloved surrogate relative, and not the “burying” she allegedly had in store for Frontier Title holder Ellis Nash in their awaiting championship encounter. To tell the truth, Darcy admired Aimz for her borderline-psychotic intensity. There had been a time not too long ago that he himself had been just as passionate about taking the wrestling world by storm that he was willing to do just about anything to pull out a victory. He’d proved that when he buried the Gutter Rat alive all those years ago in Pier Six Wrestling, earning the honor of becoming Pier Six Wrestling’s Extreme Violence champion. Darcy shuddered, just as he always did when he thought back on that fateful night of the Buried Alive match. That bitter rivalry began when the shady, smelly Rat put his then-girlfriend Kalypso through a window, simply to show Markson that he meant business. Darcy hated the man, and likely would not have been terribly troubled if an accident had befallen the Rat bastard…but he didn’t want to be the one who went so far as to do him in. He had no trouble, however, beating the man unconscious in front of tens of thousands of fans and a pay-per-view audience of millions to boot, as well as putting on a show for said fans by “pretending” to bury the Gutter Rat alive. Darcy had been assured by the technicians who set up the apparatus that the man who was to be “buried” would simply fall through a trap door under the stage, safely shielded from the dirt that would be tossed in and fully able to breathe. However, two facts greatly troubled Darcy from the conclusion of the match onward, to this very day: one, because he was so arrogantly and intensely confident that he wouldn’t be the one buried alive, Darcy never physically laid eyes on the trap door apparatus to confirm its existence. And two, Darcy never saw the Gutter Rat ever again. Suddenly recalling where he was, Darcy was momentarily shocked at the coincidence that lied before him: seeing the Benson family members begin tossing dirt over their fallen loved one just as he was reminded of doing the same to the Gutter Rat almost caused him to wonder if he’d traveled back in time. “Ashes to ashes, dust to dust, for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return. You will be missed, Jack Benson, but you will not be forgotten.” Father Thomas added, officially concluding the service. With that, the crowd huddled near the lowered coffin began to disperse a little, although many of the patrons remained to offer each other a few more consoling words, likely hoping that this last moment of togetherness for those who had loved Jack would help ease the grief. Amy had begun chatting with some friends of her family whom Darcy did not recognize. Sensing he would not be missed, Darcy whispered into her ear that he would return shortly, and utilized the opportunity to step away. As he’d told Amy before he agreed to join her in Canada for the funeral, these things gave him the willies…granted, everywhere he turned he was surrounded by dead bodies, but perhaps being around some that weren’t so fresh in the ground might do him some good. Walking along a paved pathway that wound around slow loping hills littered with concrete headstones, Darcy’s thoughts again turned to the conversation he’d had with Amy about her title shot. While he may not have been happy about it, he could certainly understand her recent mean streak. After all, his own intensity did not wane in the slightest following the Gutter Rat incident. Darcy went on to become the longest reigning Extreme Violence champion in PSW history, willing to sadistically and systematically destroy any and all challengers in ways he never so much as dreamed possible. Repeated chair shots to the skull, planchas through burning tables, even viciously tearing arm ligaments with his patented Darcinator submission – there was no ceiling as to what Darcy Crisis was capable of. It was that “Go! Go! Go!” level of intensity that fueled Darcy’s ascent to wrestling stardom, that earned him every accolade he’d ever achieved. But it was his inability to control that intensity, his inability to turn it off that made his life a living hell. Darcy’s entire career had been like a marathon race; he’d trained relentlessly to run faster, stronger and harder than everyone else around him. And somehow, amazingly, he’d pulled it off, and earned the right to call himself a World Champion. He’d earned his spot among the very best in the business, all by metaphorically running his ass off to get to the finish line first. But with his disastrous back injury, the race had effectively ended for good. His wrestling career seemed finished - he’d walked out of Pier Six with Amy on his arm, and while he’d earned quite a reputation within the sports-entertainment sphere, there were no promotions interested in hiring a gimp who could no longer deliver in the ring. And that’s where things took a tumultuous turn for the worse. His career was finished, but the Go! Go! Go! intensity that had been so prominent in the way he competed was now ingrained into his personality. Sitting around on his ass, barely able to complete the functions of a normal human being, and having no future career prospects to boot, Darcy became increasingly bitter and despondent. It finally drove an irreconcilable wedge between himself and Amy, the very girl who he’d given up his place in the spotlight to be with in the first place. Darcy had spent years rebounding from the lowest of low points in his life…but now, ironically enough, the intensity that had consumed his soul threatened to rear its ugly head once more, this time poising to possess the girl who had a front row view of what it did to Darcy. Darcy shook his head vigorously, hoping to chase the perilous thoughts from his head. Come on, Darse…things aren’t so bad, he thought. Those days are behind you…you rebounded, you made it back even though everybody told you it couldn’t be done. You’re back where you belong. And Amy…things are different now. There’s no way she’s dumb enough to let what happened to you happen to her. Besides…she loves you just as madly and stupidly as you do her. She told you herself, right? Darcy nodded. Speaking of Amy, she’s probably wondering where the hell I am at this point, he noted, noticing that he’d walked quite a ways by now. Cold-ass Canada might be home to her and her family, but judging by how eager Amy had been to return to California once they’d reunited Darcy surmised that there was no way they’d want to hang out in this bitter drizzle for much longer, no matter who kicked the bucket. Darcy turned around to head back in the direction that he came, and was about to begin the trek when he caught glimpse of a gravestone out of the corner of his eye. His jaw dropped as he read the man’s name etched into the marble. DARCY MARKSEN 1864-1898 It wasn’t the exact spelling, but it was close enough to leave Darcy astounded. Of course, the two Darcys weren’t connected in any way – the simple vowel change ensured that there was no blood lineage shared by the men. The only thing we have in common, Darcy mused, is that he probably got just as much crap as a kid about having a girl’s name as I did. Intrigued, Darcy examined the grave marker for more clues about the man that lie before him. A small cross etched inside a circle indicated that the man was part of the Christian faith…or at least his family was, wishing it to be known for Mr. Marksen as well. There was no epitaph or explanation as to his cause of death, except that he’d died at a relatively young age. 34…poor guy, Darcy thought. He was only six or seven years older than me. Suddenly, Darcy was struck by the thought that this might be more than just a mere coincidence. He’d studied quite a bit about the Mayan culture of Mesoamerica during his brief stint in college, as well as their remarkably accurate astrological calendar. The calendar abruptly ends on the mysterious date of December 23, 2012…exactly six years in the future. Many conspiracy theorists believe that the end of the calendar marked the end of the present “age of man,” as the Mayans had a cyclical interpretation of time. Not the end of mankind, these theorists argued, but the end of many lives in the coming new order. Darcy himself was born on August 29, supposedly the date of “Judgment Day” in the Terminator movies…was it possible that his life could end abruptly, at the age of 34 just like the man buried beneath him, on another alleged date of doomsday? “Oooooo….it’s possssssssible…” came a voice that sounded an awful lot like it came from behind him, causing Darcy nearly to jump out of his skin. He quickly turned about face, expecting to see Amy behind him playing an amusing prank, but he quickly saw that there was no one even within sight, let alone anyone close enough to whisper into his ear. “Ooooooo….over heeeeeeeere…” came the voice once more. Darcy’s eyes shot open, fearing the worst: the voice was seemingly coming from Marksen’s grave. The voice laughed, seemingly taunting the Darcy that was still living and breathing. “Hey, relax buddy,” the voice bellowed. “You’re not actually hearing this voice aloud. This is just your intuition playing a hilarious trick on you. And oh man, was it EVER hilarious. I wish you could have seen yourself jump.” Darcy became increasingly alarmed, unaware that his own intuition was capable of voice projection. “Normally no, but I’ve got something important to tell you,” the voice replied. Darcy looked about, nervously. Is this some kind of LSD flashback? he wondered, suddenly wishing he hadn’t experimented with so many hallucinogenic drugs in his teenage years. “Oh, come ON!” answered Darcy’s intuition, sounding exascerbated. “Should it come as so much of a surprise that your intuition is able to read your thoughts? Get real. Anyway, like I said, something important to tell you. And hey, just for shits and giggles, let’s pretend I actually am speaking from under the grave. Just think of me as this…Mr. Marksen. Is that okay with you, Jumpy?” Darcy offered no reply, vocal or otherwise, sensing it made no difference either way. “Aww, shucks…you’re no fun at all. Anyhow, I’m sure I know what you’re expecting to hear. A message from beyond the grave coming from a man who died in his prime, telling you ‘Make sure you appreciate what you have and live life to the fullest, cause you could go at anytime and having nothing to show for it, blah blah blah…” “That’s usually how it plays out in the movies,” Darcy responded. “Well this ain’t no movie, brother.” Marksen said. “And that’s not what I have to say.” “Then would you might getting to the point? Believe it or not, having a weird-ass conversation with the dead in a cold-as-balls Canadian graveyard didn’t quite fit onto my agenda for the day.” At that, Darcy heard a chuckle from “Marksen.” He was willing to play along with this bizarre discussion, but laughter from a supposed dead man would creep anyone out under any circumstances. “I thought you might say something like that. You and your ‘agendas…’ I’d roll my eyes if they hadn’t rotted away,” came Marksen’s response. “That’s what I’m trying to tell you – lay off the agendas! Lay off trying so damn hard at everything! I used to be like you, you know…” “Somehow, I doubt that.” Darcy said. “I did! I was so worried that my life wouldn’t amount to anything. I slaved away night and day apprenticing at my uncle’s law firm, working my way up the ladder. No regard for my family, no regard for starting a family of my own, no regard for my own health, even. All I did was work. Work work work work work! Until, that is, I came into a nasty bout with tuberculosis. I even tried to work through that. But then, I keeled over dead. And you know something, Darcy? It was great! Nothing to do but rest and relax. No more constantly chasing after a dream, wondering if I’d ever get the chance to stop for just a moment to catch my breath. Don’t you get it? It’s just not worth it…” “Yeah, I do get it,” Darcy responded, with irritation evident in his voice. “I nearly ruined my life working so hard. I spent years learning how to slow down, even after I’d already lost nearly everything. If you’re going to go through all this effort to get my attention by creeping me out, couldn’t you at least tell me something I didn’t already know?” “Evidently, you don’t. By the way, I’d sneer at you if I could, but I haven’t got the mouth muscles left to pull it off,” Marksen said, cackling. “You may have been able to quell the fire inside you. But your little lady friend…Aimz, is it? That’s a cute little moniker…” Darcy shivered. “Look buddy, it’s cold! Get a move on, would ya?” he asked, rubbing his hands against his frigid arms. “Cute little body, too…” Marksen added, snickering. Darcy glared at the gravestone. “Alright, alright…here’s my point,” Marksen said. “You saw it in her eyes, Darcy. Aimz is gearing up for a run at the big time. She’s a crazy ass bitch who may not have the dedication to the craft that you did, but she clearly has twice the passion. I pity anybody that gets in her way. And you, my friend…you say the two of you are a ‘unit,’ now? Do you really think she’s going to accept any less from you than she does herself? She’s about to go full throttle, and you’re going to break your goddamn neck trying to keep up with her. Am I right, or am I right?” Darcy gritted his teeth. “It doesn’t matter whether you’re right, wrong or any place in between. If Amy wants to make a run at the top, I’m gonna be right there with her. It won’t be easy. It might even suck. But I gotta do what I gotta do.” “But why? It’s not worth it, brother. She doesn’t even love you.” Darcy’s eyes shot open. “Fuck. You.” “Fuck you! You know what, enough of this ‘Marksen’ crap. This is your intuition talking now, and I don’t want to see you get hurt, mostly because then I have to hang out with your sorry depressing ass. Do you really think things have changed since you last walked out on her? You really don’t think she’ll make you do it again? FACE IT, Darcy…she’s fucked up, she uses you to provide some sort of balance in your life. The minute things go wrong…which they will, by the way, because you’re going to kill yourself trying to keep pace with Aimz and her psychotic mean streak…she’s going to turn on you, man. And you’re not going to have anything to show for it but a broken heart and a beaten down soul.” “You’re wrong.” “Am I really, Darcy?” Darcy sighed a deep sigh. “That’s what I thought. Now get your ass in gear and-” “No! You are wrong. I can’t prove it, but you are. Look, I know you’re my intuition, and I know you don’t trust any women since all the ones I dared to care about ended up leaving me high and dry. But don’t forget how I got here.” “I don’t forget anything. I live in your brain.” “Then I’m surprised to see you acting like you’re taking the high road. It’s not just how I got here…it’s how we got here! I spent the last four years of my life endlessly soul searching, trying to figure out where I truly belonged, and my search led me right back here. Canada. With Amy. I followed my heart and it led me back to the business, and back to her. And when I say I ‘followed my heart,’ it’s just a colloquialism that means I was listening to YOU. My FUCKING intuition. So if you’re going to start casting doubt on this situation now it won’t do a damn bit of good. My mind is already made up. I’m too involved.” Darcy waited for a response, but none came. “I love Amy. And I’m going to take that wherever it leads me, whether it’s King of the Tabloids, strung out in a gutter or anyplace else in between. Do you hear me?!” Again, no response. Either his intuition really had abandoned him and leapt into Marksen’s grave, or simply wasn’t listening anymore. “…who are you talking to?” came a distinctly familiar female voice from behind Darcy, causing him once again to nearly have his skeleton leap out from the confines of his body. “Jesus Tapdancing Christ! What is it with people sneaking up on me from behind?” Darcy shouted as he turned around, still bewildered. Amy Campbell, the owner of the voice, looked at Darcy as if he’d spontaneously grown an extra head. “Do you get that a lot when you vanish off into the distance and people come looking for you?” Darcy took a deep breath, exhaling his apprehension. “I’m sorry, babe. Like I said, these places give me the creeps. I took a walk to try and clear my head…I guess I lost track of the time.” “Don’t sweat it. I can’t thank you enough for coming here with me in the first place. Come on, let’s get out of here…I’ve got a surprise to make it up to you.” “A surprise?” Darcy inquired, sounding intrigued. “Let’s just say we’ll soon forget all about freezing our asses off in Canada. Speaking of which, let’s-” “-Hold on! I want to say it!” Darcy interjected. “Say what?” “Make like a tree and get the fuck out of here!” “Amy smiled, kissing his cheek. “You corny fuck. Come on, let’s get going.” With that, Amy headed up the path toward the graveyard’s exit. Darcy gave one last glimpse toward Darcy Marksen’s grave before following her as well. One thing’s for certain…life with Amy is a never-ending series of surprises.
