The Web, ch. 3: 'Step into my parlour,' said the spider to the fly

24 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 24/11/2006 Jane. Just what the hell was going on? Andy’s head was clouded with unsure thoughts and ideas at the moment. He had married Jane McCluskey when he was a mere nineteen years old, which was almost a decade ago now. During their time as a couple they brought Marvin M. into the world; a bright young kind who seemed unlikely to follow his father in joining the wrestling industry, but would seemingly forge out a healthy living of his own (despite recent academic shortcomings) given his general intelligence and willingness to work. She was the first person that Andy had really connected with after emigrating to the United States of America in order to further his career. Andy M. fell almost immediately for Jane – herself a student from London, England who was travelling the globe on a gap year at the time – and the rest, as they say, is history. But alas, it didn’t last. The relationship began to deteriorate seemingly as soon as the couple were married, and within twelve months of Andy and Jane first declaring their vows, they were divorced. Towards the end of the marriage the differences between the two were simply irreconcilable; they would argue and bicker about the most petty of subjects, and for the last couple of months, Andy was forced to sleep on the sofa before he had eventually earned enough through wrestling to buy his own place. Andy’s parents told him from the start that marrying at such an early age was not a great idea. He could see now that they were right. Jade was granted custody of Marvin. This tore Andy apart; despite whatever problems he may have had with his wife he loved his son deeply and wanted to be there for him all the time. However, while his heart led him to believe that Marvin would be better off in his custody and not in that of the “witch” than his former wife had become, Andy knew in his head that his growing career simply would not gift him with enough time to bring his son up in the way he would have liked. As warped as it may sound, if Andy had been the one granted custody of Marvin he probably wouldn’t be at the level he now found himself at in the wrestling business, and he certainly wouldn’t be able to afford his current way of life. And so, for the best part of a decade, Andy and Jane harboured a strong feeling of resentment for one another. They communicated only when necessary – most of the time resorting to barking competitions down the telephone – and went about their own lives sharing the knowledge that they shared a son they both loved very much with a person who they now found it impossible to deal with. But recently, over the past couple of months – particularly during Andy’s downtime from the Atlantic Wrestling Club – the couple had found themselves warming to one another once again. Gradually, conversation between the two had grown from the fortnightly argument over arranging Andy’s entitlement to see his son to almost daily telephone calls, mid-day get-togethers at the coffee shop, and for the first time in a long, long time, family days out. The Scottish King of Cool was perplexed. He found it incredibly bizarre that he was now getting along famously with a woman he had had nothing but grief from for the previous nine years. He was happy; a huge weight had been lifted from his mind, and of course, now that he and Jane were “friends” again he benefited from increased access to Marvin. But naturally, doubts still lingered in his mind. Was she being genuine? Or was Jane merely putting up a front, was she being nice to Andy to get at something? But if this was the case, what the hell did she want? And how come after nine years of bitter resentment the two of them had seemingly been able to put aside their differences, just like that? Neither of these things mattered, however, as the two of them staggered into Jane’s apartment at 3am on a humid, musky earlier Floridian morning. Jane, clearly a little bit tipsy, flicked on the light switch to reveal a pad that, while not as lavishly decorated as Andy’s home, “Andyville,” certainly looked like it belonged to somebody who had done very well for themselves in life. The reception room they had stepped into doubled up as a lounge and a kitchen. The lounge area featured prominently a set of black leather couches at either side of a glass coffee table, and an expensive looking widescreen high-definition television sat proud in an oak cabinet. From the corner of his eye Andy could see that the kitchen too was impressively decorated, with marble tiles and oak-panelled cupboards. He refused to close the door behind him as he came in; he had come to drop his ex-wife off home after an enjoyable night out – nothing more. Andy, looking smart in a black, short-sleeved shirt and a pair of grey slacks, watched on as Jane turned around to face him with a smile. She looked good tonight; her hair, which was slightly longer than shoulder-length, was tied back in a small ponytail, and she wore a white blouse that was buttoned to a level that, while revealing, still left quite a bit to the imagination, along with a mid-length skirt. She gently kicked off her shoes as Andy raised his hand with a small wave, mouthing the word “goodbye.” She stepped forward and embraced her former husband with a warm, gentle hug, before briefly kissing Murrr on the lips. After a couple of drinks, this normal uncomfortable situation didn’t feel so bad to the Scottish King of Cool at all. “I better be off now,” Andy almost whispered. “I’ve got a pretty busy day to get through tomorrow, but thanks… I had an awesome time…” Andy made an attempt to leave, but Jane was having none of it. She softly resisted his attempts to walk away by grasping on of his hands and pulling him a few steps forward. She leaned forward and pushed the door closed firmly behind Andy who glanced down, looking straight into Jane’s deep, hazel eyes. He opened his mouth to speak, but before he could utter any words Jane pressed her finger against his lips. Seemingly under a spell, the Scottish King of Cool was led from the doorway by Jane, who pushed him down onto one of the leather sofas, before leaning in towards her former husband and planting a long, passionate kiss on his lips. The influence that the alcohol in his bloodstream was not great enough, however, for Andy not to be blown away by what was happening. ”What the hell is she doing?” he thought to himself. ”I shouldn’t be here and I shouldn’t be doing this! I should be at home, asleep… and Christ, what happens if Marvin wakes up? What the hell would I do…!?!” Any doubts Andy had were quickly dispersed, however, as Jane leaned away from him, and slowly began undoing the buttons on her blouse, one by one. The web had caught him -------------------- Jane woke the following morning with the green, LED numbers “10:48” flashing at her from the alarm clock. Thankfully for her it was a Saturday morning and she didn’t have to go to work; otherwise she would be in big trouble. At this time of day it was bright enough for the sunlight to dimly illuminate the bedroom through the thin, wine-red coloured curtains which hung over her window. It was not dark enough to go back to sleep, nor was it light enough to be fully awoken, so instead Jane chose to spend a few moments enjoying the warm comfort of her queen-sized bed. She closed her eyes with a satisfied sight, and rolled over towards the centre of the bed, pulling the bed sheets across herself, the faintest hint of a hangover lingering in her head. But almost immediately a thought crept into her mind that shattered her relaxed satisfaction, something was missing… Shit, where the fuck was Andy? Jane sat almost bolt upright in the bed and glanced to her left; he wasn’t there. The side of the bed that she thought she would find her estranged partner in was occupied only by a coupled of ruffled pillows. Her pulse rate began to increase. ”Where on earth has he gone?” she thought to herself. ”Has he just left me without a trace?” She scanned her eyes around the room quickly – other than the ruffled pillows, there was no other sign that Andy M. was about, the pile of clothes he had left were gone too. Quickly Jane slid out of her side of the bed and grabbed the nearest thing she could to cover her modesty – a light-blue coloured dressing gown which hung over the corner of the door of a cupboard that she had left slightly ajar. Throwing the garment over her curvaceous physique, Jane, almost in a panic, pulled open the bedroom door – if Andy had merely vanished on her, she would not be best pleased. Jane stepped out into the lounge – the television was on the ESPN network at a very low volume. She could hear a faint clattering sound coming from the kitchen, as well as the gentle purr of an extractor fan. Somebody was there. Thankfully for Jane, as she stepped around the corner and into the kitchen area, she saw Andy M. standing there, spreading butter onto a couple of fresh slices of toast. Dressed in the same clothes as he wore last night, Andy seemingly sensed the presence of Jane, and immediately turned around, meeting her eye-to-eye. “Good morning motherhubbard, how are you doing?” Andy asked with a half-smile. Jane smiled back at Andy’s attempt at humour, where a couple of months ago she would have told him to “shut the hell up,” or something to that effect. Without a word she paced along the cool, tiled floor towards Andy, briefly embracing him. And for the first time in nearly a decade, embracing Jane didn’t feel awkward or uneasy to Andy; it felt just right. It could have gone two ways after last night. It would have been very, very easy for Andy to have walked out and abandoned Jane this morning - the hour that he had been awake for would have provided more than enough time to slip out unnoticed – but that was never really an option for Andy. He could have put his foot down and walked away last night, but something wouldn’t let him. Now, on this glorious morning, he could see that this “thing” was not alcoholic. He hadn’t planned to sleep with his ex-wife last night, but the content feelings he was experiencing this morning made him thankful that he had. This, of course, would change everything. It marked an incredibly turning point in the relationship between Andy and Jane, and made the past nine years seem like the least important thing on the planet. Murrr wasn’t sure things would go from here, but he figured that surely only good things could come from here on. They weren’t nineteen anymore – they weren’t young, stupid and naïve anymore. They both knew exactly what they were doing. “Do you want anything to eat?” Andy asked Jane as she sat down at the kitchen table. “No thanks Andy, not at the moment,” she responded, scanning her eyes quickly across the headlines on the morning papers. Andy had obviously been out to get them, which she was pleasantly surprised by. Andy took a bite out of one of the slices of toast, chewing quickly, before washing it down with a refreshing mouthful of fresh orange juice. He watched on as Jane – whose hair was still looking surprisingly tidy, despite the fact that she clearly hadn’t combed or brushed it – skimmed through one of the newspapers, not really pausing to read any article in particular. Opening his mouth to comment, Andy was interrupted by the sound of a closing door before he could utter anything. Almost immediately Marvin stepped into the kitchen area, already fully-dressed in a white Nike t-shirt and a pair of blue jeans. “Hey little dude, how’s it going?” Andy asked Marvin, who hadn’t yet noticed his father standing before him. When he did notice Andy, a somewhat puzzled look came across his face. This was the first time he had ever seen his father in this apartment; in the past, Andy had made a habit of avoiding the place as much as possible. “Oh, Dad… what are you doing here?” he naturally enquired as an anxious look drew across Jane’s face. She didn’t know what to tell him… did she lie to him, or did she tell him the truth? But what exactly was “the truth”? She didn’t want to say anything in case it brought about complications… she left the talking to Andy… “Eh, you know, just popped around for a little visit…” Andy responded somewhat uneasily. He knew it was a flawed response, but hoped that Marvin didn’t take into account that this was the first time Andy had ever visited the apartment and that before last night he didn’t even know where the place was. “We just made some toast Marv, you want some?” Andy asked, trying to drag his son’s attention away from the awkward subject. “Sure, why not?” Marvin nodded as Andy and Jane simultaneously breathed sighs of relief – they had pulled Marvin away from the topic, for now. Marvin pulled up the seat next to his mother and sat down, as Andy passed the untouched slice of toast in his direction. Andy could feel the cloud of uncertainly slowly fading away. Maybe walking into Jane’s web wasn’t such a bad idea after all?
