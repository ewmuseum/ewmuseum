Chronical Four: Community Service

9 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/9/05 [Patrick Mapleleaf and Cedric are sitting in the sauna of Mapleleaf’s brand new sky rise apartment in the posh area of Toronto. The apartment, 3100 dollars a month (Canadian) includes a hot tub, gorgeous view, and is equipped with two refrigerators (one with regular food, one with beer), a 60 inch plasma flat screen television, X-Box 360, and a state of the art sound system. The sauna, constructed of solid oak, is small enough to seat about 3-4. It has a small coal-burning surface that provides the steam. Sweat glistens off Mapleleaf’s forehead. He is wearing nothing but a red and white towel. Cedric is wearing a black shirt, black leather pants, and has straightened hair. He is now a goth for the new year, as his sweat rolls down the face and ruins his makeup.] Mapleleaf: Aren’t you hot in that thing? Cedric: No, I can adjust. Mapleleaf: I mean, I know the goth look gets you plenty of pussy, but it’s a bitch to keep up. Cedric: Unlike you, I don’t do everything to get pleasure in bed. I am doing this as a form of self expression. Mapleleaf: Wow, that’s a good line. You should try that one. Cedric: You read the papers today? You’re in some serious trouble, my friend. Mapleleaf: What now? Did the government find out about my illegal video tapes? I swear, those girls LOOKED 18! Cedric: No, no…these newspapers. Look at this. The New York post says that “Patrick Mapleleaf, AWC wrestler, is nothing but a woman beater. After beating down Jessica Phillips on Fresh, Mapleleaf should be ashamed of himself.” Mapleleaf: New York City is Toronto’s ugly, fat cousin. I don’t care if they write a few words about me. As far as I’m concerned, the sweet nation of Canada still loves me, and that’s all I care about. Cedric: Well, the National Organization of Women wants to hold a meeting with you. Mapleleaf: See? What is that, some kind of sorority club? WOO! Well, maybe if they bring the one token ugly friend, I can let you at her. Cedric: This is serious, man. You need to do something to improve your image, or else you can kiss your AWC contract goodbye. Mapleleaf: Relax. AWC wouldn’t cancel my contract. I’m a top player! Cedric: Are you? How come you haven’t gotten any title shots yet? Mapleleaf: I’m biding my time…It’s because I’m too good! If they gave me the title shot right away, I’d have nothing to work at! I don’t want any! I hate you! Cedric: Look, my cousin works down at the homeless shelter. I can arrange for you to do some work there, get some cameras down there, and the whole thing can blow over. [The door to the sauna opens. Frederick the butler is standing there with a silver tray of drinks, wearing a black tuxedo. His silver, thinning hair is brushed back against his scalp.] Frederick: Sir, I have brought drinks for you. Mapleleaf: Close the door! Close the door! [Mapleleaf tries to slam the door shut. It hits Frederick, causing him to lose balance as he falls into the coals of the sauna, face first. He screams for a split second before meeting a gruesome end, burned to death in a bed of coals.] Mapleleaf: Let’s…get out of here. Cedric: Good idea. [Mapleleaf and Cedric walk out of the sauna, locking the door behind them with Frederick’s melting body inside.] ------- [The scene picks up a short time later in the least likely place you would expect in a Patrick Mapleleaf promo…the Pykes-Down homeless shelter in downtown Toronto. Patrick Mapleleaf and Cedric the black guy are entering. Mapleleaf is wearing a greenish-white sports coat, green pants, a white shirt, and a green tie. Cedric is wearing a black trenchcoat, his hair slicked over his forehead. His black eye makeup makes him look threatening. A poorly dressed man, an African-Canadian with short black hair and a white t-shirt with jeans, approaches the pair.] Cedric: Derek! What’s up, man? Derek: Hey, Cedric. Long time no see! You must be Patrick Mapleleaf. We’re so glad to hear that you’re coming down to help out. Mapleleaf: Well, I do love to help people. Say, where is your ATM machine? I have to take out a couple hundred. I’m shooting down to Vegas after we’re done here. Derek: We don’t have an ATM machine…come, let me show you what we do here. [Derek leads Mapleleaf and Cedric over to a table. The table has a large metal pot filled with soup. A man in a flowered shirt with grisly hair is spooning the soup into bowls.] Derek: This is Harold, fellas. Harold, this is my cousin Cedric and his friend, AWC wrestler Patrick Mapleleaf. Harold: Hello. Great to see you volunteering. Mapleleaf: Yeah, yeah. I didn’t ask for your life story. My hands are full of shmutz. Mind if I wipe them on these rags? [Mapleleaf picks up a pile of shirts from the table. He wipes his hands. Then he puts another shirt up to his nose and blows it. Then he takes another shirt, puts it down his pants, and wipes. A large brown stain is seen on the shirt as he withdraws it from his expensive pants. Harold, Cedric, and Derek are in a state of disgust.] Harold: My GOD! Those shirts are for the homeless! Mapleleaf: Oh, I was only borrowing them. They’re homeless! Besides, a shit-stained shirt from Patrick Mapleleaf will bring in big bucks. In fact… [Patrick snatches the shirt.] Mapleleaf: There we go. Ebay, here we go! Derek: That’s…evil. Mapleleaf: Oh, cheer up…I plan on giving the profits to the shelter. Cedric: R-really? Mapleleaf: Probably not. Hey, I have to make payments on my hummer. You know how it is, Harold! Harold: I sleep on a cot here. Mapleleaf: Then maybe you should sell the hummer. Geez, why are you paying for a hummer if you’re in a freaking homeless shelter? Harold: I— Mapleleaf: Well, now we’re just splitting hairs. Anyway, let me try spooning this into the bowls. Derek: Go for it. [Mapleleaf stands behind the table. A line full of homeless people start to line up. One of them is Canadian actor, former star of Growing Pains, Alan Thicke.] Alan: The usual, please. Mapleleaf: Alan Thicke? Weren’t you on that show— Alan: Yeah, Growing Pains. Mapleleaf: I meant My Three Sons. Didn’t you play the big pile of walking, talking dogshit that lived next door? Alan: That…never happened. Mapleleaf: Well, it will! [Mapleleaf takes out a pad of paper and scribbles down the suggestion.] Mapleleaf: Good luck trying to steal this gem of an idea from me! [As “The Leaf” keeps working, he notices a small man with long, graying hair and a thick beard. He comes up to Mapleleaf, smile on his face, and nods.] Man: Some soup, please? Mapleleaf: Oh, sure. Sorry, this suit is chafing me. I had it made out of recycled thousand dollar bills. You know how it is…those things are useless. Who would have thought a jacket and pants made from them would be so hot? Man: Well, last night, I slept on a cot with one blanket. How do you think that feels? Mapleleaf: I know exactly how it feels. One night, my thick quilt was in the cleaners, so I had to use one of a slightly lesser quality. I tell you, if I don’t get exactly 8 hours of sleep in my nice warm bed, my throat gets a little itchy. Man: Well, you should be grateful… Mapleleaf: I’m growing tired of you, old man. I can see your leper-like skin, and quite frankly, it reminds me of the time I saw Robert Frost’s behind in the 40’s. Here, take your soup… [Mapleleaf pours the soup onto the man’s pants, right in front of his genitals. The man screams in pain. Mapleleaf neatens out his spikey blonde hair.] Mapleleaf: NEXT! [Mapleleaf spoons the soup into the bowl. He continues this for about a half hour, and he suddenly stops.] Cedric: What’s wrong, Pat? Mapleleaf: I was just thinking…what is this soup we’re serving? Cedric: I really don’t know. Mapleleaf: It doesn’t smell too good. Here. [Mapleleaf takes the pot. The line of hungry homeless protest as he exits the shelter and pours it on the streets.] Derek: WHAT ARE YOU DOING?! Mapleleaf: Get these people some real soup. Derek: That was the last of the soup. These people have nothing to eat! Mapleleaf: Good. Some of them look like they can skip a meal. You telling me that my tax money goes to feed these people? Cedric: Mappes, you haven’t paid taxes since 1999. Mapleleaf: And now you see why! No worries, I brought my trusty guitar to entertain these people. [Mapleleaf reaches under the table. He grabs his guitar and starts to play.] Mapleleaf: Any requests? [There is a groan of protest] Mapleleaf: Aha! Got one! [Mapleleaf starts to play “If I were a Rich Man” from Fiddler on the Roof with his six string Fender acoustic.] Mapleleaf[singing]: If I were a rich man…dadadadada….dooo dee dooo deee deee deee daaaa. If I were a very rich man—HEY! [stops singing] Pipe down! I’m providing free entertainment! Cedric: Maybe its time for us to go. Mapleleaf: Nah, the fun is just beginning. [Mapleleaf takes a pack of cigarettes out of his suit jacket pocket. He shakes one out, puts it in his mouth, and lights it.] Mapleleaf: I know what these people need. Here, Cedric, take this hundred and go down to the liquor store. Bring back a keg of Molson. Cedric: But— Mapleleaf: DO IT! [One hour later.] Cedric: Well, I hope you’re happy, Patrick. You’ve turned a good deed into a college-like keg party. [Two homeless people fight and start charging at each other.] Mapleleaf: Oh, this should be a good one! I put my money on the skinny guy. Any takers? Who wants to bet me money on the skinny guy? Oh wait, I forgot…it’s like asking the desert to bet with water. Sorry, fellas. [Mapleleaf takes a drag of his cigarette.] Harold: Mapleleaf! Man…I…I…love…yoooou….maaaaan. Mapleleaf: I’d say it back, but last time I did, she asked for a commitment. Derek: I hope you’re happy, Mapleleaf. You’ve made the entire homeless shelter drunk! Mapleleaf: Hey, I’m just doing my duty. Speaking of which, I need to go real bad. Is that a toilet over there? Derek: No, that’s…that’s…. [Mapleleaf walks over, drops his pants, and lets the fun fly out.] Derek: …a bed! You shit on someone’s bed! Mapleleaf: If you’re gonna be like this, I’m gonna leave. Come on, Cedric! Cedric: Finally… [Mapleleaf walks out of the homeless shelter. The place is reeking of poo by now, there are fights everywhere, and the keg is being tapped like Carmen Electra at a college party. Cursing is heard in large quantities. Mapleleaf takes another drag of his cigarette and throws it on the carpet as he is leaving.] Cedric: Well, you sure screwed that up. Mapleleaf: Nah. Just think of it as a gift. That’s all these people want. To booze it up! Speaking of which, why don’t we hit one of the bars on the way back? I need some dick candy for tonight. Cedric: Come on, maybe---HEY! The homeless shelter is in flames! Mapleleaf: Now who could have done that? Cedric: You! Your cigarette! Mapleleaf: I don’t smoke, Cedric. Derek[from shelter]: HELP! Mapleleaf! HELP! The fire is blocking the exit! Mapleleaf: Nope, sorry. You wanted me out, and I’m not gonna help unless you apologize. Derek: But— Mapleleaf: Sorry, Charlie. Derek: Fine! I’m sorry! Mapleleaf: Nope, not good enough. You only did it because I asked you to. Let’s go, Cedric. Cedric: But those people— Mapleleaf: God will provide. Cedric: You don’t believe in God. Mapleleaf: My God is Satan. And by provide, I mean, a slow and grizzly death. Cedric: You truly are evil. [Fade]
