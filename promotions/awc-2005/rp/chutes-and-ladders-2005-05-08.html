CHUTES AND LADDERS

8 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 8th, 2005 CHUTES AND LADDERS SCENE: INTERIOR, POLICE HEADQUARTERS, TAMPA BAY, NIGHT MAY 07, 2005 12:01 PM EST ALEXANDER STRIDER sits in a cold metal chair in a sterile, unfriendly interrogation room. The light is dim, save for the hot light that shines down on suspect Strider, still wearing his ring attire and bloody lip from his ‘successful’ PPV Championship match. DETECTIVE MICHAEL LANE, pudgy at the age of 38, sits sullenly across from Strider, while DETECTIVE DREW FITZHAUSEN, a strong and angry 31 year old, paces back and forth behind. Lane drinks out of his styrofoam coffee cup when he is not asking questions, or pretending to try and calm Detective Fitzhausen down from his ire. Detective Lane is overweight, wearing a striped business shirt with loose matching tie, and is going slightly bald. He has a thick moustache, turned yellow and gray from stress and cigarettes. Detective Fitzhausen was a former jock, whose job doesn’t afford him time to work out anymore. With age came a larger pot-belly, but he’s still got the upper body of a football playing Frat-Boy. His jet black curly hair is thinning. DETECTIVE LANE: You’re smarter than this, making it so hard on yourself. What’s your deal, Alex? Strider nurses his busted lip with a bloody paper towel, putting it down when he speaks. ALEXANDER STRIDER: I haven’t done anything wrong. You’re wasting my time. Fitzhausen rams both fists down on the table, rolled up sleeves over pulsing forearms. DETECTIVE FITZHAUSEN: Bullshit! You were told not to leave Pennsylvania until they’d done the toxicology report on that dead woman they found in your bathroom. And yet, here you are, in FLORIDA! ALEXANDER STRIDER: So what? Fitzhausen circles around Strider, cracking his knuckles as he speaks. DETECTIVE FITZHAUSEN: If you’d had just stayed put, like you were told, I wouldn’t have to look at your broken face right now, and we’d both be happier, right? Alex shakes his head and stares defiantly forward, smirking his acidic smile. ALEXANDER STRIDER: Sorry, I had a prior engagement to make. DETECTIVE FITZHAUSEN: It looks to me more like you got scared. ALEXANDER STRIDER: It sounds to me like you’re reaching. I went on a nationally televised Pay-Per-View! Who was running scared? I didn’t care. This is all just sloppy police work. I wasn’t going to miss my shot! Not for anything in the world. Definitely not because some woman I didn’t even know decided to kill herself in my bathroom. Was I really supposed to wait for some Barney Fife to tell me what I already knew: That I didn’t have anything to do with her overdose, I didn’t supply the drugs? I didn’t even know the girl! DETECTIVE LANE: That really isn’t the best way to convince the authorities of your innocence though, you have to admit. ALEXANDER STRIDER: I don’t have to convince anyone of anything. The facts are there, plain as day. It was an unfortunate incident, but I don’t see why that should inconvenience me. Fitzhausen becomes irate, and pulls Strider’s chair adjacent to the table. Fitzhausen grimaces, his face full of fire, and he rams his face nose to nose with “The Mongoose”. DETECTIVE FITZHAUSEN: You smug prick! A woman is dead! And she died in your bathroom, you sick fuck! ALEXANDER STRIDER: If I had any idea that she was still in there I would have politely asked her to leave so she could have died somewhere more convenient for all concerned. Then maybe I wouldn’t have to be here answering your pointless questions, and I could be off licking my wounds. Why is it my fault that some woman just happened to die next to my toilet? I’m the victim here! I have no idea how I’ll get her blood out of the grout around my Turkish tiles. Taking another sip of his coffee, Lane looks over his thick black rim glasses at Strider. Studying the folder on the case he has, he calmly seeks more answers. DETECTIVE LANE: You were high on LSD, Alex. Come on, tell us what really happened. Alex looks worn out and rolls his eyes at the officer. Pulling his hands back behind his head, Alex dons a sarcastic smile. ALEXANDER STRIDER: Well, Detective, If you read down just a little further you’ll see I stated that I was drugged. By the woman you found in my bathroom. I went through a lot of crazy stuff that night, and it isn’t something I would have wished on myself voluntarily. DETECTIVE FITZHAUSEN: You expect anyone to believe that somebody forced you to take acid? What do you take us for? Strider cocks his head to the side and looks at the towering Detective, but can’t resist opening his mouth. ALEXANDER STRIDER: I take you to be an overbearing ape with a severe testosterone imbalance, which is evidenced through your premature baldness and severe monkey-jawing. But the fact is that I’ve never done a single drug in my life. I’m an athlete! Look at me! You can’t tell me you’ve ever seen anyone fine tune their body with this much precision. I wouldn’t endanger my life’s work to ‘trip’. I’m no hippy. If I ever took a drug the obvious choice would be a performance enhancer, don’t you think? I don’t take those... because I don’t need them to win. DETECTIVE FITZHAUSEN: You know what’s great, you prick? You’ve already been put through hell tonight, so your face looked like hamburger coming in! I’d love to just crack you one, you murderous son of a bitch. Alexander sighs, sensing the violence emanating from the Detective. Years of frustration visibly had built up in Fitzhausen, never quite learning how to best deal with unruly suspects. Strider looks forward again, hands on the table. Fitzhausen continues to stalk behind Strider. Not waiting for a response anymore, Fitzhausen let’s loose with a swinging forearm across Strider’s jaw. Alex Strider’s head rolls with the blow, and he immediately looks back at the Detective, defiant and unfazed. Fitzhausen slugs Strider a second time, using his whole body weight to bear down on Strider’s face, knocking him backwards, ultimately landing on his back, the chair caught between his back and the floor. Detective Fitzhausen stands, chest heaving from anger and adrenaline, his fists on his hips. ALEXANDER STRIDER: Feel better now, officer? Fitzhausen turns away from Strider, and runs his hand through his thinning hair, out of frustration. ALEXANDER STRIDER: Let’s try to be a bit more mature, shall we? You don’t have anything on me other than I left Pennsylvania when I was simply advised to stay. You aren’t going to trick me into hitting you, or you’ll have to work a lot harder than that, anyway. Go work out your frustrations like most meatheads: Go home and beat your wife and kids. Lane motions to Fitzhausen to step outside, and he does so begrudgingly. Alex picks himself up, and sits back down at the table, smiling with incredulity. ALEXANDER STRIDER (CONT’D): This is outrageous, you know that, right? DETECTIVE LANE: Alex, right now the way it looks is you dropped acid, and you got a little crazy. So, you set up a fix for your lady friend, only something goes horribly wrong. So, I’m thinking this is just manslaughter. You're digging yourself to a murder warrant though. With you running like you did, then resisting arrest... ALEXANDER STRIDER: I resisted being escorted by AWC security, not the police. I resisted the urge to punch your ape of a partner in the eye, is what I resisted! I still haven’t been charged with anything. You’re just stalling right now. But I tell you what... Lane looks on at Strider, shrugging his shoulders. DETECTIVE LANE: What’s that? Strider looks on at the Detective with a bloody smile, a genuine smile from a man who is better known for his lies. ALEXANDER STRIDER: I’ll take my phone call now.
