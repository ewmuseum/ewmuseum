One for Luck, Ch. 7

20 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 20th Nov. 2005 One for Luck, Ch. 7 (Prometheus: Section 5) The silence shifted throughout the night. Car alarms broke the silence frequently, with the accompaniment of a howling dog or hissing cat, the knocking over of a thrash can and the odd curse from a neighbouring room. I gazed, eyes open into the darkness that engulphed our hotel room. Kelli lay, dreaming and sleeping softly against the cushions and fresh duvet. My back had given out on me after Testimony and each motion hurt, I could feel the spine moving and rubbing against each single bone. My muscles were tense and my wounds, still bled after bursting twelve stitches. Looking back on Triangles, it might have been better if I had stayed at home. Kelli had given me two pain killers before she had drifted off, but I felt as though not even an elephant sedative could take away the pain. I felt afraid to move - in case it hurt and I winced, but also the fact it was three in the morning and Kelli was a heavy sleeper. I turned my head slowly, feeling the thumping at each temple increase. The migraine was becoming ferocious and my sight almost blinded with pain. The moons rays slowly began to descend as the sun began its ascent into the atmosphere, to raise those sleepy heads out of bed. I felt a chill run up my spin as my body moved to a more comfortable position. I could no longer lie flat on my back. The night’s sky was vanishing as 5:00am rolled around on the clock. I decided it best to try and get some shut eye, but it wasn’t working. I felt Kelli’s hand brush against my shoulder and soon felt her body move up off the bed. But it wasn’t Kelli. A blonde woman rose from the bed and pulled out a Beretta; she stood, legs spread for balance and aimed it at me. Her hair danced around her shoulders, she was fully clothed. I looked at where Kelli would sleep, and she was laying there, blood spilling into the white duvet. I was panicking and sweating ferociously with fear. Tears and emotions were welling up inside me. “KELLI!” I screamed and turned her over; her face was pale white, a small line of blood trickled down her mouth. Right there and then, I just needed to touch her. I leant over in the darkness and felt her cheek. It was cold. I touched the duvet beside her and pulled my hand back like I'd had it in a fire. Even in the darkness I could see the sheen of crimson on my hands. I grabbed Kelli's shoulders. Panic stricken, I leant over and removed the duvet. A pool of blood had now outlined Kelli’s body. The blood was seeping through the mattress and the frame of the bed, creating a dripping sound on the marbled floor. She was cold, her body swelling. The blonde haired murderer removed the muffler on the Beretta and moved off the bed and strolled around the room. I couldn’t move now. Not only were my muscles tense, but my whole body had gone into complete shutdown. A small needle lay empty on the duvet. “The effects should be setting in, but they don‘t last long,” she whispered. I opened my mouth, but found it difficult. I could no longer move my whole body. The blonde was now in control, she pulled up a large black bag, inside was a new outfit and a wig. “You’re paralysed; I’d try to scream, might help with some of the pain. Oh wait, you can’t…” she said with the accompanying of an evil laugh - very persuasive. She turned to face me, wearing a black outfit. She cleaned the muffler and placed it back on the Beretta and moved back toward me. She stared at Kelli’s lifeless body, the blood still oozing out of the arteries that had been punctured. “You’ve ruined me and my girls … now you’ll pay,” she said and removed the safety from the gun. “Bu-” “...You’ve ruined my business…” “Bu-” “… Now you’ll pay,” She raised the gun up so it was pointing at my chest, burning a hole into my flesh. “Goodbye” “NO” I screamed, the effect of the drug wearing off. SFIFT Cold, I feel...cold. SFIFT Blurry, she was blurry now. SFIFT Drifting...drifting...away... ***** “Pierce? Pierce? Wake up …” I heard Kelli’s voice in the distance. I shot up, feeling a shooting pain in my back. Panic stricken from my nightmare. I removed the duvet and checked my stomach and arms, everything was fine, Kelli was still breathing and rosy cheeked. I stood in a daze. It was 8:00 am in the morning, the sun was up and the hotel room was safe. Kelli sat in the messed up duvet with a curious look on her face. “Pierce, you were having a nightmare. You screamed my name and then went cold. Are you okay?” she asked, getting off the bed. Before she could move any further, I hugged her. The dream was so vivid I thought to myself as I hugged her, tightly. I couldn’t quite picture the blondes face anymore, but the way she spoke to me; “You have ruined my business …”. I couldn’t quite understand what that was about, but it was a dream, nothing more than a dream. A bad dream. Probably the after shock of the drug, or something. “It was just a bad dream,” Kelli whispered in my ear. “Yeah, you’re right. That’s all it was,” I said. The nerves still floating around my system, tingling each sensor in my body, until I had goose-bumps along my arms and chest. “You want to talk about it?” she asked. I stood there for a moment, gazing at her blue eyes and wondering what I should do. “Eh - no - not really. I don’t really remember,” I said and backed away a little toward the dresser and took a large slug from my glass of water. “But you screamed my name. Did something bad happen?” she asked, wisely I might add. If there is one thing you can’t do, its keep secrets from Kelli Stoner; she has a way about getting things done and when she wants to know something or go somewhere, she will get what it is she wants. She was right, I did scream her name, but I didn’t want to say why. “I really don’t remember,” I said again, with a fake smile, hoping it would lead her away. I didn’t want to tell her that she had died. Luckily, the infamous Lavelle smile had worked and she ignored the topic and went to have a shower instead, which she kindly pointed out, I needed one. I waited for a few moments and then collapsed onto the bed with a large sigh. Things just weren’t going my way anymore, ever since I arrived in Los Angeles, I’ve been in nothing but trouble and danger, and now it had lurked into my dreams, corrupting my mind. The one thing I wasn’t looking forward to was seeing my grandfather later; in regards to the information on the Promecil pills I had stolen from the factory almost a week ago, before John McGrady turned on me, tortured me and almost killed me. The alarm finally rang in the distance, a contrast to the power shower in the bathroom. I rose from the comfort of the crumpled duvet and turned off the alarm. When I stood in front of the mirror I was shocked. My body was bruised and cut from the pay-per view. My three scars on my left arm, were healing, but would never be perfect. My eyes were a bloodshot red and I had at least five days worth of stubble on my face. The first thing I needed was a shower and a shave, the next some pain killers, but my mind didn’t focus on the necessities; it was drawn toward my lap top, sat on top of the table in the corner. A small message flashed across the screen; “ONE NEW E-MAIL”. I moved slowly to the computer and clicked the saved file of Rubin Demorto, the poor kid that died from the drug. He was 18 years old and had been wrestling for five years, he hoped to make it into the Olympics at the age of 20 (which won’t be happening, I thought to myself, no thanks for Promecil). He was born in Santa Monica and went to school until he was 16, he had to drop out because his father was an alcoholic and he had three younger sisters and a mother. He took an apprenticeship as a Mechanic and managed to keep a stable income. In 2004 he met James Lawler and soon got the break he had been looking for, a match and a manager. He started off quickly, winning the Cruiserweight title in the young gym. Of course, it was down to the unknown drug taken. He died early 2005, but no investigation was taken, until afterward when the mother noticed a small packet of white pills in her son’s sports bag. James Lawler was cleared of charges, but one day got a conscience and decided not to work for GOONE anymore - didn’t work so well in his favour, ended up murdered. I sat still looking at the file. Demorto was a victim and abused by a drug baron, but the drug baron soon became the victim and abused by the drug lords. A vicious circle. He was only eighteen and here I thought James Lawler was the innocent manager of an abuser and drug user, turns out I was wrong. Kelli came out of the bathroom, wrapped in a white towel and smiled at me, before disappearing into the kitchen and switching on the kettle. I ignored the e-mail that I had received, closed the Demorto file and ran into the bathroom to take a shower. The hot water trickled down my face and acted as a calmer for the stress that was building inside my mind. I could smell the sweet smell of coffee, behind the steams of hot water and lynx shampoo. After my shower, I threw on some grey boxers, jeans and a green New York jets t-shirt. After tending to a long shave, I emerged to a cooked breakfast and coffee. Only, Kelli wasn’t around anymore, it was grandfather frying some eggs in the kitchen. “Don’t you have maids to do that?” I asked with a smile and checked my watch. “I thought I was supposed to meet you in Hancook Park at 12:00 pm,” I said staring at the breakfast cooked on the table. “Yes, I have maids, but is more enjoyable to try the simple things in life, Pierce,” he smiled and flipped a fried egg onto a plate. “You were supposed to meet me, but I have a business lunch at 12:00pm, didn’t you get my e-mail?” he asked, turning off the oven. The new e-mail I had received was from my grandfather, well that saved me a couple minutes of time. I sat down in front of him and pulled out a napkin. “Where’s Kelli gone?” I asked. “Out shopping with your sister and Susanne,” he said, munching away on some French toast. “Huh?” I said, almost spilling my chewed egg onto the plate. “I thought it was time your sister came to see who your new girlfriend is. She likes her, thinks she’s funny. Hope you don’t mind?” he asked. I leant back and took a large gulp from the hot coffee and stared at the blistering sun, sat high above the earth’s atmosphere, shining through the cold, harshness of space. “Its fine …” I said and turned back to the breakfast. “I need to talk to you about the drug,” he said. I put my knife and fork down, leant into the chair and stared at him, brushing my wet hair out of my face. “Okay,” I said. He got up off the chair and walked into the living room, and out of my sight, when he returned he was holding his brown briefcase. He plonked it onto the counter and pulled out two small folders. The first he handed me was my blood test results. I scrolled through all the DNA and white blood cell count information and came to the page, the most important page. It was the result page and the last word on the page read, CLEAR. Meaning the drug Promecil had been taken out of my body before the pay-per view, which meant I could confront Pearl now, if ever needed, I had evidence. “Thanks,” I said, pointing to the large CLEAR on the page in front of me. He nodded and handed me the second file, it was the drug analysis of Promecil and a map of Los Angeles. I gazed at the analysis information - everything I already knew was on the page. I then turned to the map and saw a large circle, around Downtown, Hancook Park and Hollywood. Santa Monica was highlighted and Culver City. The overall shape was a triangle. “What’s with the triangle?” I asked. “It’s the area they deal in. Look, I’ll explain …” he said and sat next to me. “…Downtown is where two dealers have been taken in by the LAPD. Hancook Park is where James Lawler was murdered, also where Lawler went to collect his shipments. Santa Monica is the scene of Omnikon and Culver City is where Lawler’s gym is, where he supplied teenagers, unknowingly, with the drug.” “What about Hollywood?” I asked. “Alex Strider has been seen there. Since you saw his face on the Promecil billboard, we’ve been tracking him, he seems to be hanging around that area, but also because he has some fame, it seems the obvious choice,” he replied. “Strider!” I muttered, anger welling inside me. He was at the pay-per view, battling in the main event and all the while he was marketing a drug, illegally and enough of them could kill someone. I had always hated Alex Strider, but I never thought he would endorse drugs. “What now?” I asked, stuck in a tight spot. I had nowhere to go. I knew where Omnikon was, I knew who GOONE were and I knew there was only one way to stop the production, to call the LAPD and have their narcotics unit bust the dealers and producers, but for some reason I couldn’t lift the phone to make the call. “That’s a good question. Right now, we have all we need to get LAPD narcotics involved, but that would almost implement us as the bad guys,” he said. “How so?” I asked, confused by this new angle. “Well, if you recall, McGrady worked for me. You were doing a private investigation and shot two men in the factory. So you can be placed at the scene of Omnikon and with your torture and being injected with Promecil, you can also be charged with taking the drug,” my grandfather spoke those words softly. “Against my choice,” I replied, infuriated by the dim-witted law system that was our justice department. “I know, kid, I know,” he said and patted my back. “All we can do is continue with what we have and build a strong enough case file, just to be careful, so that we have something if it goes to court,” he said and put the file back into his briefcase. I stood up and sighed. This wasn’t my day. The good guys always seem to get burned, not matter how much they put out the fire, solve the problem and save a few people, they always get burned. “I must be off, kid,” he said and slipped on his trilby hat. “Be careful,” he said and left. I remained in the kitchen, gazing out the window and down onto the city of Los Angeles, watching the clouds move with the gravitational pull and of course the wind. If Alex Strider was in Hollywood that’s where I needed to be, but then again, perhaps I should take this normality and a chance for peace. It may never happen again. I pulled the Beretta from under my pillow, placed it into the small of my back. Threw on my black hooded jumper and my denim jacket and locked the door behind me. There was something’s I needed to do, and others, I didn’t. ******** Jeremiah was dealt his hand. Went off to another land this evening. Thought he’d find some inner peace and to see a better way, of living. He only went, to try to change something. His poor young life, was pulled from under him. I pulled the car out onto Route 405 and kept driving straight, ignoring the turn for Hollywood, I kept going. The road was clear and my mind was not, it was stuck in between doing what was right and what was wrong. If I went to follow Alex Strider, I risked being exposed, but if I kept going this way, I was sure to lead myself somewhere I needed to be. The constant worry about Kelli and Rebecca flashed over my mind. Were they okay? What were they doing? It was the paranoia in me flaring that seemed to control my second instinct as I found myself turn off route 405 and turn into Brentwood. It was a peaceful little estate and before I knew it, I was sat outside my uncle and aunts house. The very place I had spent a month trying to avoid. Found him by the motorway, Jeremiah ran away from something. Jeremiah’s mother wept, for the answers they had left behind them. He only went, to try to change something. His poor young life, was pulled from under him. What should I say? I thought to myself, sitting alone in the cold of the car, with the engine running. I could always reverse and run away, but where would be the sense in that. An old saying was to keep your friends close, but your enemies closer. Right now, it seemed all I had were enemies and I was losing grip of my friends and my reality, which once was. I switched off the engine and opened the car door. I saw my Uncle Mike, stood next to his pickup, replacing a tire and listening to some Dolly Parton song. I smiled at his country music and shut the door. He turned around, oil on his face. His mouth hitting the ground. “Pierce, lad, your home,” he said and gave me a hug. “Your mother is going to kill you,” he smiled. “She wouldn’t be the first to want to do that,” I smiled. “Huh?” he said, continuing to dry his hands of oil. “Eh-nothing. Is Aunt Joyce home?” I asked, noticing the quiet atmosphere, the white picket fence and the kids playing on the street. It was the normality I had been searching for. “Yeah, she’s baking. Your girlfriend is so nice, Pierce. Nice catch. If I was thirty years younger…” he winked and nudged my shoulder. “Kelli’s here?” I asked, relieved she was safe. “Yup, with your sister. She’s been learning all about you …” he laughed and turned back toward his pickup. I stood, hands in my pockets, gazing around the lawn, in admiration at the fact normality did exist and it wasn’t too hard to achieve it. I just wished I could be that lucky. And every time I see the sun go down, I think of you. The pilots I have swept out of sight, there’s nothing knew. And every time I see the sun go down, I think of you. I hope some day someone will feel the need to tell the truth… “Your mother will want to see you,” Uncle Mike said, pulling me out of my daze. I nodded my head and began to walk up the drive way, to the porch and opened the porch door, gazing through the glass and seeing Kelli, sat down, laughing away with Rebecca and Aunt Joyce. I knocked on the window and the three shot up, Kelli smiled, but Aunt Joyce went cold and angry, it was a face I had forgotten, but it was shadowed by my mother’s frail face, behind the glass, tears in her eyes. “Mom,” I said softly. She opened the door and hugged me. Little world, do not fear. The ways of the world, will soon be clear.
