Summer's Son #1: 'Make An Impact'

9 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9th August 2005 AWC Solarized 1st July 22:17 James Brunt: The winner... and new Transatlantic champion... PIERCE LAVELLE! The firemen begin putting out the choking blaze with their massive hose as Strider is loaded onto a stretcher and carried away. Michael Ryan hands Pierce Lavelle the Transatlantic championship. Pierce Lavelle holds onto it for dear life. He is still shocked at what just happened. He looks at the nameplate and smiles. Lavelle takes in the cheers from the fans with delight. Truth Waters: Well there you have it. Pierce Lavelle is our new Transatlantic champion. He proved himself tonight, not just to us, but to everyone watching. Great show by both men. George Cassidy: This is a travesty! I can’t believe it. Strider has lost his title! Truth Waters: It didn’t really matter who won. That these two young men were fit and strong enough to both come out of that relatively unharmed is a miracle. To even stand in that atmosphere for nearly thirty minutes is a challenge, but to contest a wrestling match between two BITTER rivals? Incredible. Now I wonder if Lavelle will have the strength to speak to us – George Cassidy: Strider’s coming back! Truth Waters: WHAT?! Having abandoned his stretcher, Alex Strider marches defiantly through the one section of the fire that has been put out and grabs Pierce Lavelle by the head. Lavelle is caught unawares, and has no strength to react to Strider slamming his head against the ringpost. The former champion now drags the new champion over to a section that is still burning. George Cassidy: Strider wants revenge! Truth Waters: Somebody stop him! This is sick! Absolutely sick! There is no way he is going to do it... surely... Strider, using the last of his energy, forces Lavelle’s head downwards through the choking smoke. Lavelle begins to dry-heave violently, struggling desperately to avoid being pushed into the fire. Suddenly, a man in wrestling attire bursts through the curtain! He sprints down the ramp, his blond-brown hair flopping at the sides and front of his head as he leaps the previously-burning barricade and tears Strider away from Lavelle! Strider’s eyes widen as he is kneed viciously in the gut. Truth Waters: What is this? Who is this? I can barely see him through the smoke! All I can make out is the white tights! That isn’t someone we know, is it, Cassidy? George Cassidy: Certainly not, and someone needs to prevent this attack on the champion! He’s just endured an Inferno match. Truth Waters: He isn’t the champion anymore, and besides, all this man is doing is saving Lavelle from possible DEATH at the angry hands of Alex Strider! Pausing to look out at the crowd, the agile wrestler now turns to his doubled-up opponent and grabs the ringpost. Channeling his momentum, he swings himself up, pushing off Strider’s lower back with his left foot and then flipping off the apron with his right. Completing his mid-air backflip, his arm falls into place around Strider’s neck and the two go to the floor in an amazing DDT! George Cassidy: That’s... surely not... he left the business! Truth Waters: What? Who? The man now pulls Lavelle urgently away from the flames and onto the stretcher Strider vacated. After some quick, sharp instructions to medics, he rolls apprehensively into the ring and stands to face 18,000 confused yet happy fans. And finally, simultaneously, everyone recognises him for who he is. Truth Waters: TIM SHIPLEY! Copyright notice. Cut. Black. 22:23 “Hey! Let go of me!” I protested, but it was no use. All my weary body wanted to do was rest. The adrenaline was pumping so quickly through my blood vessels that it was itself tiring me out. Or maybe that was just the force with which I was being held against the side of the ring. “Ouch!” I complained as Butch Radder tightened his hammerlock. I wanted to yell For fuck’s sake, get your hands off me, I’ve a perfect right to do what I did, just as much as the next wrestler. I still had no clue why, once the cameras had stopped rolling, all three of AWC’s security staff had come bounding out from backstage to effectively arrest me. “What are you doing? Leave him! He’s a wrestler!” I heard my salvation and almost wept with joy. David Harber’s voice, though tired, was full of authority. Radder, however, was having none of it. “No, Mr. Harber, we’ve never seen him before, he just ran out here, he could be a fan, this could be dangerous –” “He’s not a fan! Didn’t you hear what I just said? That’s Tim Shipley you’re manhandling!” Harber thundered, and if I’d been able to channel the requisite energy to my mouth, I would have smiled. Reluctantly, Radder released my raw wrist. Pulling my face up from the ring apron onto which it had just collapsed, my vision was blurred, but I could definitely make out a head, a talking head. I rubbed my eyes. Oh... shit. Suddenly, I was longing for the warm embrace of Butch Radder once more. For Alex Strider was up – and Alex Strider wanted revenge. I jumped out of my skin as the Madison Square Garden crowd roared. I’d forgotten an audience was even there, and scanning quickly I saw that only a very small proportion of the fans had left on being told the show was over. They were very aware of the possibility of afters here. Strider’s blazing glare continued to penetrate into my skull, and I couldn’t bring myself to even look at him. Desperately, my eyes glanced over the ringside area – nothing and no one could help me. I caught sight of Pierce being loaded onto a stretcher, and suddenly my mind leapt back a few minutes to when Pierce Lavelle, the new Transatlantic champion, had been moving away on a stretcher when Strider had interfered and pulled him back to settle the score. Suddenly, I knew I had something important to do, and that was keep Strider away from my new friend. And when I’m working with a purpose... I’m a whole lot more efficient. I turned my head back to Strider’s, and immediately registered a reaction of surprise to the new expression adorning my face. Inside, I smiled. This was what I wanted. My face now was set, ready... confident. Heels like easy prey. But now Strider was my prey, because I wasn’t going to give Strider what he liked. Then, two things happened – simultaneously, and totally out of the blue. Strider turned away and sat down against the apron, and I felt a pang of pity for him. Let’s deal with one at a time – something my hyperactive mind was unable to do on the night. First, Strider turning away and sitting down – why? Was he really the cowardly type? From what I’d seen before, I didn’t think so. And – I looked down at my thin, pasty-white body – it sure as hell wasn’t fear. I’ll never know for sure, since I’ll never be brave enough to ask him, but I’ll put it down to both physical and mental exhaustion. I was myself feeling the effects of that blazing heat, having spent maybe... thirty seconds in its midst? Strider had been wrestling within that ring of fire for thirty minutes. And the buzz I’d got, the elation I’d felt before, during and after my few seconds of action, kicking through The Eternal Hope for the first time in months and hearing 18,000 people cheering... well, for me at least, that wasn’t a one-off. It had always been like that in Core: once I’d stepped into the ring, I was in a different world, and I couldn’t think about anything but the match. My ability to think logically and rationally, to analyse and explain, was gone for five, ten, fifteen minutes – however long the ring time lasted. So tonight’s buzz was not simply due to the novelty factor. It was the standard ring buzz, and it seemed Strider got it too, and it seemed it wore him out too. Pity, though. Why? I was looking at one of the most dastardly, deceitful and detested men in wrestling. He’d cheated his way to the Transatlantic title and done his worst to keep it for this long. I’d just done the world a favour with that Eternal Hope – not to mention saving Pierce from serious burns or maybe even worse; I wasn’t to know how far Strider would have gone and I hadn’t been about to risk waiting to find out. Deep down I knew why I felt pity for the man. When you cut to the core, each human being is just like everyone else. Basic emotions intact. Even though Alexander Strider had unlawfully won the thing in the first place, he’d fallen in love with that treasured possession of his, the Transatlantic title belt, and now it had been taken from him. In addition, it was the notion of reward. When we work hard, it’s generally assumed that we’ll get something in return. But wrestling is a harsh game. There’s always, in the end, a loser. Strider had gone through nearly thirty minutes of pain and intense heat. I couldn’t imagine the strain his body was feeling right then. His endurance had been tested to the maximum, and he’d come through. He’d tolerated it. Congratulations, Alex! Here’s your reward – we’ll just take that Transatlantic championship of yours and give it to someone else. Make an impact is AWC’s slogan, and I’d certainly done so with my run-in, but I knew in my head that the very fact that I’d felt pity for Alex Strider at that precise point – and more precisely, that I knew why I’d felt pity for Alex Strider at that precise point – was what would ensure that I, Tim Shipley, would make a more lasting impact on the world of wrestling once more.
