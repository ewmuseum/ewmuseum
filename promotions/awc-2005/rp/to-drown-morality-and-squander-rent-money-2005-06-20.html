To drown morality and squander rent money.

20 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 20th June 2005. i. Imprinted in the thin sheet of dust that carpeted the cheap hardwood flooring was a delineation etched by his own right hand. The tip of his index finger, to be precise. The simple drawing, he felt, quite accurately portrayed his dysphoric mood that dull morning. It was strange how such a basic, familiar image could symbolise his frame of mind and yet be so inanimate; so dissatisfying. He pressed his rough finger against the wood once more and slowly began to draw an imperfect circle around the preceding unhappy face. It was as the circle was mere centimetres from completion that another scream resounded. A shriek of anguish that rippled through his static body and made the stoic countenance cringe in unquestionable disgust, then pity. Before the cringe could fade, a muffled groan ensued by an audible collision of body and hard furniture ripped through the wall and taunted Layne's non-existent valour. It had been those two horrid had also awoken Layne from a deep slumber some fifteen minutes prior. A rest he had craved to repair his bruised and aching body from a AWC debut he had lost. A sleep he had hoped would last until at least midday so that the tedium that overarched his every day would be less prolonged. He hated the very woman who emitted those horrified wails and innocent protests as with each scream his muscles tensed and cheeks inflamed, and all he wanted to do was help her. Defend her. Be the one who ignored the voices that cried 'it's none of your business' and rescue her from her drunkard partner. That ghastly, despicable ogre of a man. That ignominy of masculinity. That fucking bastard. Alas, Layne's gallantry was as apparent as his respect for the dickhead who thought it quite alright to physically assault his partner and make domestic violence an hourly occurence. It has nothin' to do with me, he repeated to him self while endeavouring to parry the unrelenting cries for help that echoed inside his aching skull. Frustration pained him. Guilt pained him more. //8:34am// Fully clothed - in loose, tattered jeans; a black skin-tight T-Shirt, mostly hidden by a black hooded jacket; and, his favourite old, black, ECCO boots - Layne approached the strangely stained front door of his appartment. His face reflected the combination of sorrow and guilt that bubbled inside of him. That ate at him. He sighed heavily as he unlocked the door and stepped out into the corridor. All of his life he had ran away from anything that had truly intimidated him or threatened to change him. Ironically, it was as a result of both of those two reasons that he was avoiding confrontation and helping someone desperately in need of saving. With his hooded head lowered and eyes transfixed on the floor, Layne hurried past the door of his neighbours, and just as he reached the top of the steep stairway, he winced. Only metres away had an ever-drunken man just struck his defenceless female partner for what Layne counted as the third time that morning. Why doesn't she just walk out the door? Why doesn't she just leave... It has nothin' to do with me, he promptly reminded him self, ahead of cautiously descending down the hazardous stairs, refusing to glance over his shoulder. //8:40am// It was whilst he slowly descended the third, and last, staircase that a realisation hit him. A realisation that was triggered by a man's voice. By an American accent with an unmistakable undertone of Italian. 'Shit,' Layne mouthed having remembered he hadn't finished the payment of that week's rent. He pivoted on the stairs instinctively, as if he had been about to flee the scene and find an alternative route. Yet as he did, a tensing pain, unleashed from his belligerently authoritative mentality, drove it's canines into his scalp, and injected momentarily dwelling recollections of pleading and screaming into his mind. The decision to continue his descent down the stairs was instantaneous, and, with it, his stubbornly inconsiderate ideal felled his altruistic desire once more. Before he arrived at the bottom of the staircase, and making as little sound as his black boots would allow, Layne drew his hood over his head and proceeded to hurry towards the door. Fortunately for Layne, the landlord appeared intensely focused on the utterer at the end of the phone line, and with a cautious glance, the wrestler disappeared through the doorway. .ii A nearly clear sky, occupied only by numerous isolated detachments of diversely shapped cloud, spread out above like a a canvas awaiting it's final strokes of blue. A beautiful piece of art that failed to attract the attention of an aggravated Layne. He walked onward; careless for nature's priceless handiwork. A sigh escaped his lips as he pushed through the door of a bar, to drown morality and squander rent money.
