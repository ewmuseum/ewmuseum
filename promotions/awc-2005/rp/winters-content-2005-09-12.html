Winters' Content

12 September 2005

Date: 9/12 It was mornings like this, Laura had decided, when life was very much worth living. The University of Delaware campus was an inviting place, particularly on a nice day, and the young woman was taking full advantage. Sitting out on one of the quads, with a notebook in her lap and a Starbucks café latte in her hands, she carefully studied a book on molecular chemistry. Here, she was an entirely different woman than what most wrestling fans knew her as. To most people who had seen her on television, she was Aerith Heartilly, manager and wife of “The Final Fantasy” Seymour Almasy. Despite having been off television for a year or so, she still got approached by fans and asked for autographs. These she gave willingly. After all, for the most part, wrestling had been quite good to her. The money had helped finance her return to academia. While her parents had been highly skeptical of her career and putting college on hold, they now didn’t have to pay for her final two years, and the subsequent graduate school that she planned on attending. She stifled a sigh as she flipped to the next page. Her retirement, of course, was only temporary. Something she was going to have to tell her friends shortly. Laura had been preparing for it for months, attending wrestling school on the sly at night, working it around her busy class schedule. Considering she was debuting this week, she mused, she’d better tell people soon. “Hiya, Laura!” And as fate would have it, the opportunity presented itself. “Hey, Rebecca,” she responded, smiling up at her fellow chemistry major and long-time friend. “How’s it going?” Rebecca made a small face, before responding. “Pretty good. Molecular’s a bitch this year though. I don’t know how you get through it so well.” “We all have our strengths and weaknesses,” Laura replied, good-naturedly. “I can get As in chemistry, but I’m struggling for Bs in other things.” “You? Struggling? That’ll be the day…” Running a hand through her long, blonde hair, Rebecca’s jaw suddenly set. Laura knew that look. Generally, it wasn’t a good one. “That reminds me!” she exclaimed, hands on hips, doing her level best to look annoyed. “When were you planning on telling me that you were returning to wrestling?” Crap. She’d been found out. So much for the big long talk she was going over in her head… “Well,” she began, sheepishly, “I was actually planning on telling everyone sometime soo--” “And you’re actually WRESTLING this time,” Rebecca went on, undeterred. “You must have been planning this for months! And without telling me! Your best friend!” Rebecca continued to look grave for a few moments, before leaning down to embrace her friend. “Congrats,” she said. “How long has this been in the works?” “About four months,” she admitted. “I’ve been training at night after classes…which is coincidentally why I haven’t been to any parties so far this semester.” “But you missed the best one of the semester!” “Rebecca,” Laura said, shaking her head, “the semester’s barely started.” “I know! But that’s why it’s the best one!” It was hard, at times, to argue with Rebecca’s logic. So Laura simply decided to re-focus the subject back on the matter at hand. “So, you know I’m debuting for AWC this week then,” she said. “I’m looking forward to it. I’ve only really wrestled a handful of matches, and I wasn’t especially trained back then.” “You’ll do great!” Rebecca chortled. If nothing else, the blonde was constantly enthusiastic. “So, uh, what kind of wrestler are you? Do you fly really high and crash land on people?” “No,” Laura said, shaking her head. Rebecca tried again. “Do you suplex people,” she offered, somewhat skeptical that such a relatively slight girl would be capable of throwing around far larger opponents. “No,” Laura repeated. “Then what,” a slightly exasperated Rebecca asked, “DO you do?” A wicked grin curved at the mouth of the former UWF valet, as she leaned in close to whisper in her friend’s ear. “I make them submit.” Rebecca blinked once, twice, looking at her friend in disbelief. “You WHAT?” “I make them submit,” Laura continued, less evilly this time. “I break down a body part, apply a submission hold, and torque until they can’t withstand the pain anymore, and they are forced to submit.” Clearly, Rebecca was somewhat impressed. “You can DO that,” she asked, eyes widened with more than a touch of awe. Laura nodded eagerly, placing a hand on her friend’s shoulder. “Considering my size, it’s actually a pretty strong strategy,” she explained. “I’m not going to be able to out-power anyone, so I use my speed to get them down and then weaken a joint. They’re not going to be able to lift me with an injured arm or leg, you know.” Rebecca nodded. She’d never really thought about the strategic aspect of wrestling before, always getting lost in its more dramatic and entertaining properties. But what her friend was laying out before her made good sense. “So,” she said, slyly, slightly changing the subject, “you inviting him to watch?” Laura froze for a moment at the question. ‘Him’ quite clearly meant Seymour. She had considered exactly that question for a while, before finally coming to a conclusion. “No,” she said, somewhat firmly. “I don’t want him to overshadow my debut. I’ll invite him to watch me someday, but for now…I’m Laura Winters. I’ll be identified with him for the rest of my career as it is, I don’t need that to be exaggerated any more than it already is.” In truth, as much as she had enjoyed her time with Seymour while things were good, it was turning out to be a hindrance. Already, people were discussing the prospect of “Seymour’s ex-wife” wrestling. It was the reason she had decided to use her real name, as opposed to the far more established Aerith Heartilly. “Oh,” Rebecca said. “I understand. It’s just…you two were so cute together on camera. I’m sort of upset you never took him to meet me!” “I would have,” Laura responded, somewhat sadly, “but we were always on the go. A show here, an appearance there.” “It’s okay,” came Rebecca’s good-natured response. “I should have KNOWN something was going on between you two though. You had such chemistry, especially with him playing such an innocent.” Placing a hand on Rebecca’s shoulder, Laura favored her friend with a blindingly bright smile. “He wasn’t acting, you know.” “Omigod! You mean, you actually…” Laura nodded, wickedly. “That’s a time of my life that I could never forget no matter if I tried. It’s made me the person I am today. I don’t regret a minute of it…” “But at the same time,” Rebecca finished, “you want to move on, and be your own person.” “Right. I’ve got to admit though, I’m really sort of nervous,” Winters said, “I don’t know much about my opponent. I know Katerina’s well trained, and of Eastern European descent, but beyond that, most of her past’s a blank.” “Don’t worry about it! You’ll do great, Laura. I haven’t seen you try anything that you haven’t succeeded at.” Miss Winters blushed slightly at the compliment. Rebecca truly was a very close friend, so the words meant even more coming from her. She nodded; perhaps she WAS a bit too worried about the coming contest. Being overly worried meant that she would be tense, and that meant that she would make mistakes. She’d been around the ring long enough to know how fatal mistakes could be. “Thank you,” she replied, grinning. “But unfortunately, I need to get this reading done now. Have to finish it before I get on the plane so Professor Williams doesn’t throw a hissy fit next class.” “Right. Call me when you get there, Laura. I want to hear EVERYTHING! I’ll tell Cindy and Elizabeth where you are.” With that, Rebecca ran off excitedly to spread the news. The woman who now called herself the Queen of Submission couldn’t help but grin at her friend’s enthusiasm. It was contagious. In a few hours, she would be on board a plane on the way to Fresno, California, to take part in her first real match. It would be difficult, she knew. But looking back on her life -- a 4.0 grade point average, a loving marriage at one point, and her constant desire to be the best -- she had come to one conclusion. Everything in life worth fighting for was difficult. She would simply have to rise to the occasion.
