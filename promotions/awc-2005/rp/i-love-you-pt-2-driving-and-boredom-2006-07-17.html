Date: 7/17/06 The wheels on the truck turned, and some drawn out metaphor about how the truck was some kind of beacon of light going towards a new beginning even though the truck, which is quite raggedy, is one its last wheels was going to be portrayed, but alas, there is no need to. For very literal reasons, we know the facts of the entire situation, or at least I, Sam L., know the facts. Maybe you don’t as a reader, I don’t know, I’m not you, I’m not a mind reader by any mean at all. So let’s clear them up: -Liam is in a truck. -The Truck is going to visit his dying former ex, Mandy. -Liam is apprehensive and happy to be doing this. You see, in how bluntly literal I’m being, I’m trying to capture the realness of the situation. I’m not even trying to hide what I’m doing, I’m telling you how it is. The motherfucker is nervous, the mother fucker loves Mandy, and that woman is going to die, period. If I try to fluff it up with my usual attempt at some kind of deeper thing (or “deeper”, because sometimes I give my writing too much credit), it turns into stupid fluff and takes away more. Death is a tricky subject, too. There is nothing more literal and figurative than death and the subject of dying. You can compare death to so many fucking things it’s not even funny. And yet, death by itself is just that—mother fucker, it’s death. So why go into details that don’t really go into details? Why try to lace in some kind of image that couldn’t possibly capture the emotions Liam is going through? I mean, what’s more intense—some kind of analogy with an animal changing, or the fact that Liam is on the verge of tears as he drives up in his truck, remembering the good times? All he has in his head are the things he and Mandy have gone through. If you’ve been in love with someone and then ripped apart, you know what’s it like to reminisce. All the times you spent with her, all the funny things, the sad things, all that bullshit. Like, there was that time when Liam first met Mandy. It was a Sunday, and guess where the fucker was? One of those assholes. “What the fuck, bitch? Who the fuck are you now?” “Bitch?” As Liam’s eyebrow raised, a cocky little smile grew. It was foreshadowing the cocky little fist that was about to ram into that unwarranted cocky asshole’s jaw and shatter it, leaving him against the concrete and writhing around. “Yeah, fucker, I called her a bitch!” *SMACK!* So when the fucking fist flew into Gabe’s jaw, Mandy jumped a bit. But it wasn’t a “Don’t hurt my baby!” jump, no, it was a “Oh my God, violence!” jump, a natural reaction for any human being. Because when Gabe hit the ground, you could see the happiness in her eyes. You could see that smile, that little thought bubble with the phrase “He got what he deserved” just fucking poof above her head. And when she looked up at Liam, Liam saw it to and held out his hand. “You coming to church Sunday?” “Yes.” And that was all that was said that night as they walked away from the scene, Liam keeping Mandy close with an arm around the shoulder. He could still feel her warm body as he clutched onto the steering wheel, smiling. Wow, he thought as he continued to drive, she was something. No, she IS something. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Life is not the same. When my brother left for Portsmouth, I was all smiles. He’s yin to my yang, except that we’re so much fucking alike. But, eh, I guess we’re different. When I wanted to go back to my flame, I took Liam. When he wanted to go back to his, he went by himself. Maybe I have to look for something else. Maybe I have to become myself. I need to find something. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~ As Liam seemingly somehow got closer to Portsmouth on this two to three hour truck drive, the timed seemed to move slower and slower, his mental capacities contemplating and trying to wonder how Mandy would be.
