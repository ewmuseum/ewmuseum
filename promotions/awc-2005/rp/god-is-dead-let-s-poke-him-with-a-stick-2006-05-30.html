God Is Dead... Let's Poke Him with a Stick!

30 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/30/06 It's interesting how one can look into the eyes of his opponent and see a purely inverted image of himself. It's almost as if the fates conspired to put them on a collision, Suleimon thought to himself as he smoked his cigar, blankly staring at the pile of books in front of him. He wasn't in the mood for reading, even though he had no problem plowing through books he's read several times prior. Right now, he thought interestingly about how he himself was a man of little faith, raised by a devout Muslim, and how Duke Williams, a man who made his name in bars, could have spawned such a fanatically religious son. Does faith really breed faith, he thought to himself. Is it rebellion? Not in his own case. He had kept the faith mainly because he wanted to be a good son to his father, even if his father was behind bars. And who rebels to the side of God anyway? These were questions that Suleimon knew probably was no answer to. Yet he still pondered them. For hours upon end sometimes. It sidetracked him from reading, made him fidgety. He needed to move things around, constantly switch his position. He started with his books, grabbing them, repiling them, sorting them like an OCD librarian. He stacked books and then stacked them again and again. He did anything he could to get his mind off the silly, children's topic of faith. He grabbed and repiled, but something caught his eye. At the very bottom of one of his piles, he saw an old, beaten up book. His mouth went agape, the cigar fell out. He couldn't believe his eyes at the book, almost like he thought he shouldn't have still had it after all these years. The book was a Quran. After Tariq left the shanty town for Istanbul, he thought that things would almost immediately pick back up for him. Despite the fact that he was only 13, he thought the world was his for the taking. I mean, how many other 13 year-olds could engineer a hostile takeover of a gang who had guns? He certainly thought the Turkish Army could use someone like him in Cyprus. He thought wrong. The army recruiters scoffed at the idea of letting a pre-teen into their army. Letting him fight and possibly die for their country at such a young age would be a SCANDAL~! on the world stage, and Turkey wasn't about to risk their status as rising star in the Western World to let a mere child, willing as he was, carry a gun and fight in the quagmire that was Cyprus. So there was, a homeless pre-teen, wandering the streets of Istanbul. He wouldn't wander long. He would soon find out that Turkey's child labor laws weren't very strictly enforced, and within a week, he was doing odd jobs for contractors, running as a go-fer, buying tools, doing various sorts of busywork for the folks who would be building stuff for the residents of Turkey's largest city. It was during this phase of Tariq's life that he would begin to fall in love with professional wrestling more intensely. As a child, he watched it casually, always rooting against the jingoistic scum like Hacksaw Jim Duggan or Hulk Hogan, and rooting for folks like the Iron Shiekh and Nikolai Volkoff. Interestingly enough, this was before his run-in with those rogue Turkish commies, but I digress. One of the contractors Tariq was working for, Mehmet Enver, was a huge wrestling fan. Because Mehmet was one of the most successful contractors in Turkey, he could afford tapes of non-American wrestling, especially Japanese puroresu and spot-fu. For his teenage years, while Tariq saved up money prodigiously to be able to bring his mother and brother back to Istanbul (they had thankfully moved out of the shantytown shortly after the Communist incident and now were living with Farouk's brother in Ankara), he spent his weekends and nights watching wrestling with Mehmet. He watched matches that the WWF could only dream of putting out; Tiger Mask, Dynamite Kid, Misawa, Kawada, Giant Baba, Jumbo, Sasuke, up and comer gaijin like Black Tiger and Wild Pegasus. Over those years, wrestling became almost like religion to him. Tariq spent all his time working, reading about the history of the Ottomans or watching wrestling. He crammed as much of each into his free hours despite them not being as plentiful as they would be for a normal teenager. Over the years though, Tariq gained more and more of Mehmet's trust, and would get paid a pretty good sum for being a child laborer and get more and more time off. Mehmet knew in his heart that Tariq would bolt to follow a dream he knew Tariq had but would never say in front of him. One day, Tariq finally did tell Mehmet of his dream, and Mehmet knew he was losing a valuable member of his team. 'Mr. Enver...' Mehmet was startled. It was a Friday night and he was alone, eating his supper in his dining room. He turned around to see Tariq, standing solitary in the foyer, a certain uneasiness in his eyes. 'Tariq! How did you get in here?' 'Mrs. Enver let me in.' 'Oh, she'd let anyone in. I ought to get a watch dog,' said Mehmet as he wryly smiled. 'So what brings you here?' 'I... I have something... to... to ask of you, sir.' Mehmet stopped chewing, paused and then swallowed hard. Not because he was worried. He knew this day was going to come for awhile now. He was just nervous for Tariq. 'Go on.' 'Well... I... I really think that contracting and construction... it's... it's not for me. I... think I want to be...' 'A wrestler, Tariq?' Tariq stood there dumbfounded. 'How... how'd you know?' 'I knew ever since I saw the gleam in your eyes when you watched your first Tiger Mask/DK match.' 'Well... you know why I have to leave.' 'I know, and I'm not sad, or angry. I'm actually very happy for you, Tariq. You see, not many people have what it takes to even begin following their dreams. I know I'll be losing a hard worker but I know those are the qualities that will help you succeed.' Tariq nodded, and he was about to say something else before Mehmet cut him off. 'And before you leave, I want to give you another bit of information. If you're serious about it...' Mehmet got up and went to his desk. He picked up a pad of paper and a pen and scribbled something down. He ripped the top paper off and handed it to Tariq. '...then go here. He's an old friend of mine. He's probably the only wrestling trainer in Turkey who's really worth a damn.' 'Thank you, Mr. Enver. I guess this is goodbye then.' 'Oh no, Tariq. Not goodbye. I'm sure our paths will cross again, when you're a famous wrestler.' Tariq nodded and left. Mehmet just smiled as he went back to eating his supper. The next day, Tariq came up to the address. The building had an old, beaten-down façade. He entered and was taken quite aback by the ornately decorated foyer. There were religious paraphernalia abounding, prayer rugs in neat lines. He looked around, thinking maybe he was in the wrong place. He saw a young man walking from a door towards the back, and Tariq had to ask him. 'Excuse me... do I have the wrong place?' 'Wrong place? What are you looking for?' 'Ahmad Denezer's wrestling school.' 'Oh, you have the right place. The Imam is in the back room there.' 'Oh... thank you.' Tariq was confused. Religious adornment? Imam? He wondered if he hadn't stumbled into some Muslim religious center for homeless boys. He thought about turning back; he didn't want religious indoctrination. He wanted freedom from that, yet he knew Mehmet wouldn't have steered him wrong. Besides, his dream, that was important. Tariq walked slowly, sheepishly towards the back room. He put his hand on the doorknob. Last chance to turn back, he thought. Clenching the doorknob, his mind wrestled with itself... *click* The door opened. Tariq walked through, and to his mild surprise, there was a full gym set-up. Wrestling ring, heavy bags, weights. In the ring was a large, bald-headed, clean-shaven man, skin darker than most people around Istanbul. He was hip-tossing a smaller man, while in the corner, a old man with a large, graying beard looked on, applauding. 'Good form, good form indeed! Once again, you show why you are my best pupil.' The large bald man nodded silently, while the older man put his arm on his pupil's shoulder. 'I'm sure you'll be my first student to make it overseas. Maybe in Japan, or even America! Oh yes, you'll definitely make it in WCW or the WWF.' He patted the large wrestler on his shoulder. 'Go shower. You've done well for the day.' The large wrestler exited the ring. Tariq saw this as his chance to approach the man whom he thought was Ahmad Denezer. He walked hurriedly towards the ring. When he got into the old man's earshot, he cleared his throat. After getting the bearded trainer's attention, he spoke. 'Excuse me, Mr. Denezer?' The old man's face lit up with a look of surprise. 'No one's called me that in a few years, but yes, that's me. You can call me Imam.' 'Uh... okay.' The proverbial cat had gotten hold of Tariq's tongue. 'So what is it you want, young lad,' The Imam said with an avuncular tone to his voice. 'Well... I want to be a wrestler.' 'Well, you've come to the right place. Do you have the desire to train hard and give your physical life to the rigors of the ring?' Tariq nodded. 'Excellent... and do you have humility to submit your life to Allah in order to be in his good graces as you walk the path towards greatness in the squared circle?' Tariq's face hardened and became red. 'No, sir... I... I don't know.' 'You don't know? You don't know what?' 'Well... I... I really... don't follow Islam very well anymore.' The Imam's face started to show concern. 'You aren't a blasted Jew, are you?' 'Oh no, no, I'm not. I am a Muslim... well, I used to be. I... just had a crisis of faith a few years ago, and now I'm not so sure that I have faith anymore.' 'Oh no... that's unfortunate. A man with such desire to become a wrestler but who has no faith in his heart. I'm sorry, but I cannot allow you to train here.' The Imam turned away from Tariq and started to walk away. Tariq felt a lump in this throat. His one chance to make it, thrown away because he lacked faith. Tariq cleared his throat again. 'Imam!' The old man turned around. 'Sir... I know I may not have faith... but Mr. Enver said you were the best trainer, and he thinks the world of me and...' 'Enver? You know Mehmet Enver, son?' Tariq nodded. 'I used to work for him.' 'Well, Mehmet is a very good friend of mine. Perhaps the only person in all of this country who feels as passionately about wrestling as I do. I would feel utterly horrible if I were to deny you. Usually, I don't take in people who aren't as passionate about Islam as they are about wrestling into my academy... but for you, I shall make an exception.' Tariq finally was able to breathe. 'But don't make it well-known. I have a reputation to maintain. I expect you to at least pretend you're a devout Muslim, which means no unclean foods and you will join us on the rugs when we pray. Is that understood?' 'Yes... yes sir.' 'Alright. Then welcome aboard.' The next few weeks were hellish for Tariq. Not so much did he mind the hours and hours of wrestling and honing his skills, but it was the charade of being a good Muslim that made him feel sick at night. He didn't know what he thought was worse: pretending to be something he wasn't, or that that something was a concept he hated at worst and was alienated from at best. It also didn't help that Imam kept spending most of his time with the large, head-shaven man. Salim, the Palestinian Dream, six feet, seven inches, 285 pounds... a wrestler with a look that Vince McMahon himself would probably cream his pants over. The thing is, he also had a workrate that was akin to some of the slugs McMahon has hired over the years. He was slow, relied too much on his power. His moveset was limited, but it inspired awe whenever he did hit a high-impact move. Basically, he was a WWF main-eventer, in look and in moveset. Imam gushed over him though, partially because of his ring potential, but mostly because Salim himself was a devout Muslim. Imam was not an imam. He was called so because his wrestlers always looked to him as a spiritual leader. He was always leading them in prayer, encouraging them to be strong pillars of Islam. The Imam often bonded with those who showed faith like he did, and Salim had it in spades. Tariq didn't. What Tariq brought to the table was flash, innovation. He brought the dojos of Japan to the Prayerhouse Gym. He was the only one who could do the Tiger, Dragon and even Ocean Cyclone Suplexes. He flew gracefully through the air, a Shooting Star here and a Space Flying Tiger there. He even toyed with a reverse senton drop that the Imam said was too dangerous to do at this stage, despite being potentially breathtaking. While the Imam would give Tariq brief words of encouragement, he wouldn't gush over him like he would Salim. Because Tariq had no faith... he was just there for the ride. Tariq began to feel like an unwanted foster child, an insanely gifted but unwanted foster child. He would sit in his room at night, almost to the brink of tears. He'd curse Salim. He'd think back to his father, and why Allah couldn't save him. He was becoming a mental wreck, his thoughts tearing at him like wolves on a freshly killed deer. This wasn't any more apparent than it was when it came time for Tariq's first simulated match. He was up against another up-and-comer, a scrawny lad named Ozlem. Tariq dominated the match early on, oozing confidence. He had Ozlem beaten, going to the top rope. He looked around for the Imam, trying to see if he was going to watch his shining moment... ...and there he was, giving Salim a pep-talk. Tariq's mind shattered. He leapt and botched the press so badly that he landed on his neck. Luckily, he wasn't hurt badly. At least his neck wasn't. Ozlem couldn't capitalize on the botch, but Tariq still couldn't get over it. He tried a Tiger suplex and pulled his back before he could bridge Ozlem over. He fanned on a cross-body block. Ozlem ended up getting the win after Tariq unraveled. After the match, it was Salim's turn. As expected, Salim made short work of his opponent. Every move was crisp. Every impact was supremely forceful. Salim looked the part and acted it as well as anyone could have asked, and predictably, at the end, Imam applauded him. Still, he had not forgotten about his other star pupil, the one who didn't fare as well, and after the sims were over, he approached Tariq. 'You know, child, I saw your match.' Tariq knew it was an outright lie, or at least a partial one, but he didn't want anymore trouble. 'I have to say... you seem troubled.' 'Well... I am.' 'Oh, well that is a shame. You know, maybe if you actually came around on loving Allah and submitting to him instead of just pretending to, you would fare a lot better.' Tariq looked at him incredulously. He still didn't say a word. 'I mean, look at Salim. He's a man of great faith, and he's probably our best shot to gain the school some notoreity! And you know why?' 'Because he has faith in Allah? Because he has faith in an indifferent God who doesn't care if a man grows up without his father? Because he has faith that some magical being will do for him what he can't do for himself? Is that why?' The Imam was dumbfounded. Tariq continued on his diatribe. 'You look after him like a son because he shares the same passion for religion as you. Yet here I am, a man who at your wrestling school shares the same passion for wrestling, the one who brings something new to the school, and you spend your time on him.' The Imam's face grew ashen. 'My son... my faith tells me I should be patient. I know you're hurting because you lost your father at a young age.' Tariq was unresponsive. 'But how about this... I won't berate you. Islam at its heart is a religion of peace. I want you to sleep on it, calm down a little bit. Faith isn't to be forced on someone, it's to be accepted with open arms. Obviously, you're clouded now because I...' Tariq was still unresponsive. '...because I wronged you today. I am sorry. I just wish you would come around.' Tariq, calmed down considerably, looked Imam right in the eyes. 'Maybe I'll never come around. What if that happens?' 'That's for Allah to decide. But for now, just get some sleep.' The next morning Tariq woke up and headed down to the gym. Everyone was there, Imam, Ozlem, the Flying Carpets, Khan, everyone except one. 'Good morning Tariq.' 'Good morning, Imam... I don't see Salim. Where is he? He's usually the first down here.' 'Oh, he went home to Tel Aviv yesterday. Said he needed to celebrate his sister's birthday. But enough about him. How did you sleep?' 'I slept well.' It was a bald-faced lie. 'I think I've calmed down.' That wasn't though. 'Good, good, because I've scheduled another round of sims for Thursday. That's only two days, and I want you to redeem yourself.' 'Oh, well, thank you sir. I promise, I shall not disappoint.' The three days came and went without much pomp or circumstance. The only discernable difference was that Imam had paid more attention to Tariq. Whether this was solely a function of Salim being away or whether Imam was genuinely touched by Tariq's outpouring of frustration, and now Imam wasn't distracted by anything. Tariq was paired with Ozlem again, and this time, there was no botching of any holds. The Tiger suplex... seamless. Cross-body block... crisp. He even broke out a tornado reverse DDT that wowed the crowd, and then the coup de grace. The Shooting Star Press. The entire gym shot up in applause. Tariq had squashed Ozlem. After the match, Imam came right over to Tariq and embraced him. 'Son, that was a fine display of wrestling if I have ever seen one.' 'Thank you, sir. Thank you.' He was flabbergasted. For the first time, he felt a kinship with the Imam. He almost felt like his five-year long crisis of faith was starting to come to a close. 'Such paucity of words... the cat hasn't gotten your tongue again, has it, Tariq?' 'Well...' He was interrupted by another student rushing into the gym. 'Imam! Imam! There's a phone call for you. It's urgent!' The student led Imam to the back. He wasn't seen again all night. The next morning, Tariq came down to the gym, but there was no sign of the Imam. The students were there, but they weren't practicing. They were all ashen-faced. A pall had been cast over the gym. 'Hey... what's going on?' Tariq asked generally. No one answered. Instead, Ozlem brought a newspaper over to Tariq. He read the story that Ozlem had brought to his attention, and his face became wide in horror. He dropped the paper and rushed upstairs to Imam's room, banging on the door.' 'Imam! Imam! Open up!' but the old man would not answer. Tariq kept banging and screaming though. Finally, after five minutes, the door creaked open. With tears in his eyes, the Imam answered the call with only a stare into Tariq's. 'I'm... I'm so sorry, sir.' 'No, don't be sorry... I am the one who should be sorry.' 'Why sir? It wasn't your fault that he did this. Your message is of peace, love.' 'I fed it into him though... I may not have told him to strap that bomb to his chest, but I fed into his fanaticism. I told him there was nothing else in life than to submit your will to Allah. Other people were telling him that too, only they were perverting the message to him. I guess there is such a thing as having too much faith... Tariq fell silent. He didn't know what words to say to an old man, sobbing into his cloak. Tariq started to walk away, but the old man's voice halted him. 'Hold on... come here.' Tariq stepped forward, and the Imam reached back. He handed him an old, beaten down book. 'Take this.' Tariq took the book and held it up to see it. It was Imam's Quran. 'Sir... but...' 'Take it... it's clear I don't need it anymore. I know every verse by heart. I only have it around to teach. I guess Allah doesn't need me to be a teacher. He needs me to be silently faithful, I suppose. Now go, run off. Please.' Imam shut the door and Tariq went back downstairs. The next day, all the students were in the gym, milling about. Suddenly, the door flung open, and in walked Imam, followed by a man with curly black hair and a thick moustache. There were hints of gray in his mane as he strode behind the distinguished trainer. They both got into the ring, and Imam took in a deep breath. 'Hello my sons. As you know, a few days ago, we lost a student and a dear friend to very dubious circumstances to say the least. I... I am still very upset about what happened, and that's why...' Dramatic pause. Almost as if Imam didn't want to say the next words. '...that's why I must relinquish control of this school.' A buzz fell over the students. 'Now now, don't be so alarmed, don't be, don't be. The school is not closing. Instead, I have enlisted a very good friend to take over the reigns. His name is Malik Ismed Rahman. He's a fellow wrestling enthusiast. He won't require you to pray with him, but he will require you to give one-hundred percent now.' The students were still buzzing. Rahman stepped to the forefront. 'Gentlemen, I have actually been here watching you over the last few months, taking a great interest in your growth. I've seen you wrestle, and quite frankly, some of you need work. But some of you...' He looked straight in the direction of Tariq. 'Some of you, I expect big things from.' Tariq smiled. Later that night, Tariq was cleaning up his room. He saw the Quran that Imam had given him. He picked it up, looked at it and thought upon it for a few moments. He then put it in a chest of his and locked it away. In fact, he didn't gaze his eyes upon it ever again until... ...that night. Suleimon looked at the old, battered book. His eyes glowed with memories, some of them happy, most of them sad, until finally he came to the memory of the day he read about Salim in the paper. When his mind settled on just what Salim had done, a fit of rage came over him and he tossed the book out the closed window, sending shards of glass upon the street and on his floor. The spectre of Islam's hold on Salim was enough for Suleimon to keep his faith in books of a more concrete subject matter. And so he returned to his chair, picking up a book on the history of the Mongolian Khan dynasty. Finally, his reader's block was gone.
