Date: a couple of hours before the deadline a. what hurts the most Turn your head, Dr. Burns says. So Ellis turns her fucking head. Then there is a pause as Dr. Burns sucks in his breath. On the side of Ellis’s fucking head is a gash, painted pretty-red with blood, the gash is subcutaneous fat below the ripped-off dermis. Then Dr. Burns grips Ellis’s chin in one hand and traces the pads of his fingers over the gash with the other. We’re going to need to stitch this up, Dr. Burns says. Dr. Burns holds out his palm and says, Needle and thread, Matt. She rubs the other cotton ball out and tilts her head forward and pinches just below the bridge so drops of ruptured blood vessels fall and stain her jeans, they diffuse like perfume or cologne and dye the denim with cherry food-coloring. While that fucking Matt Matthews is trying to find that needle and thread, Dr. Burns is placing his glove on Ellis’s cheek and is tilting Ellis’s fucking head down. Matt Matthews says, Does the color matter? Dr. Burns rolls his eyes and begins to reply but Ellis Nash, exhausted and bleeding from her face and nose says, Purple. I want purple. So two minutes later Dr. Burns is digging that needle into Ellis’s not-ripped-off dermis, he’s got two fingers on the either sides of the gash, he’s squeezing them together to prevent scarring and reduce infection. That purple thread is tugging, digs in, pulls and tugs, digs in until there are six consecutive purple thread lines yanking that dermis, covering that subcutaneous fat. Gutting drawers again, Matt Matthews is trying to find a pair of scissors. Now he’s rifling through X-Ray copies. The scissors are in the back. Matt Matthews grabs them and nearly stabs himself in the palm before he is giving them to Dr. Burns. Dr. Burns takes them and snip-snaps the excess purple thread, it drifts and meshes with the blood stains on Ellis’s jeans, maroonpurple. Then Dr. Burns dabs a cloth with antiseptic cleanser and swipes away the dried blood. There we go, Dr. Burns says, That should take care of that. Dr. Burns says, Now let’s take a look at that nose. So Dr. Burns takes a look at that fucking nose. He presses a Ziploc with ice against the side of Ellis’s nose. This will tauten the blood vessels, Dr. Burns says, Tautening the blood vessels will prevent any further bleeding. Ellis knows this, but she says nothing. She watches the clock’s minute hand shift to the left. It’s 11:46 PM. Her plane to JFK leaves in four hours. With his opposite-hand fingers gliding down each side of Ellis’s nostrils, Dr. Burns says, Doesn’t look like we’ve got any broken bone here, no injured cartilage, which is good, considering. You took a couple of nasty blows there, Ellis. He says, you’re lucky you came out of that ring in one piece. I wasn’t really expecting it, to be truthful. Fucking Matt Matthews chips in, he says, Me neither. Chainz is, well, he’s an animal. He sort-of laughs and says, He really is. Ellis, well, Ellis is just Ellis, and Ellis just blinks. Mister Doctor Burns, he’s clearing his throat and pressing Ellis’s palm against the bag of ice and saying, Here, hold this, and he’s digging into his white Doctor coat, he’s pulling out this little white circle around a smaller circle of cardboard, he’s saying, This is sterilized tape. Now, if you ever feel the stitches loosen, ever, what you want to do is put this tape over them, what you want to do is tape perpendicularly over them, so the stitches are vertical and the tape is horizontal. Dr. Burns says, Taping perpendicularly will hold the stitches in place tighter, it’s reverse pressure. And that fucking Matt Matthews, he’s off in his fucking corner nodding his fucking head along like he understands what the fuck Dr. Burns is saying, like he’s some motherfucking Physics genius. Dr. Burns says, If you want to wash your face, what you want to use is alcohol, you know, rubbing alcohol, it’ll degrease the skin easier. He says, just make sure you don’t get it anywhere near your eyes, and don’t use it for your entire face, just around the stitches, you can use tap and soap for the rest, it doesn’t really matter, just don’t get the alcohol in your eyes. Off in his little fucking corner, Matt Matthews leans against a rolling cart with his hand and sends himself pummeling into the ground side-of-the-face-first. This is reverse pressure Matt Matthews, you fucking Physics genius, you fucking brilliant nurse. There is Dr. Burns and Ellis Nash looking at him, Ellis Nash exhausted and bleeding from her nose and looking at him with a face, there’s Dr. Burns exhausted and annoyed and disheveled. Fucking Matt Matthews, he’s scrambling to his feet, he’s slipping on the tile before he’s up on them, all twos, clearing his throat and nodding his head, mumbling a muffled, Sorry. Dr. Burns, he gives up. He turns back to Ellis and says, Do you understand? Yeah, Ellis says. Good, Dr. Burns says. Now, he says, Now, what I need for you to do, is get some rest. You’ve had a pretty rough night. What’s best for you and that nose of yours, is a little rest. Can you do that? Ellis says, Yeah. Okay, Dr. Burns says. He pats her knee and smiles his Doctor smile and says, I’ll see you next week. So she’s slipping off that white Doctor table with the white Doctor sheets that are ruffled now because of her ass, she’s slipping that little white circle around a smaller circle of cardboard into the back of her jeans, she’s walking out of that Mister Doctor Burns’s office with purple stitches and sterilized tape and a bloody nose. As she does, Dr. Burns watches, he’s shaking his head and saying, God, help that girl, she is so em0, and Matt Matthews is picking up his mess of band-aids and orange bottles and needles and saying, She’s kind of hot, hm. * * b. is being so close At three in the morning Ellis is sitting in St. Louis in the airport in her terminal with her feet folded beneath her and her elbows on either knee and her palms on either side of her forehead with her head down and hair draping like a curtain over her body, it tickles the white document in her lap. Two minutes twenty-five seconds later Ellis picks her head up, she keeps her palms on her cheeks, they sort-of join together at her chin and cup it, her eyes scan over that white document in her lap. There’s a colored photograph in the top right – a girl, blonde hair and green eyes and big boobs that are kind-of falling out of the purple halter she is wearing, or isn’t wearing, rather. Then her eyes move to the side of the photograph, it’s a criminal profile. Height: 5'6-.5'; Weight: 124 lbs.; Address: 116 Abaco Circle, Chelsea New York; Charged With: Attempted Robbery; Past Charges: N/A, but none of this interests Ellis, not really, because her interest is instead drawn to the name glaring bluntly above all of this: Allison Prather. Ellis rubs her fingers over her stitches, purple like this woman’s purple halter top, bruised like this woman’s cheek, everything like this woman in this colored photograph because this woman, this woman is her sister. This woman is Ally Nash, this woman with all that breast inside that shirt/purple halter, this woman with green eyes and dyed blonde hair with horribly obvious brunette roots? That’s Allyson Nash, folks. Not Allison, not Prather. Not Sloan, for God’s sake, because eww. But It’s Allyson Nash. That’s her little sister. Her fingers drawing small circles over Ally’s face, Ellis lifts her gaze, it moves to her plane pulling into its gate, it moves to the closed shades and blue wings, and she’s wondering what the fuck Ally had to do with Dennis, Dennis with his ripped-out ribs and torn-off balls, what the fuck did Ally have to do with this Dennis? Then a voice comes over the speakers, Welcome to American Airlines flight 5162 to New York, with a connecting flight in Washington D.C. We are now seating the A section, that is rows 1-25. The voice says, If your ticket has an A on it, please come to the gate at this time, once on the plane please feel free to take any seat you wish, thank you for choosing American Airlines. Ellis has a B. Businessmen with A’s all around her are shuffling, grabbing their suitcases and flipping their cell phones off. They’re all crowding around the gate with their suitcases at their side and sunglasses on top of their head and ties down to their hips and their tickets in front of their faces, big A’s round out the right-most corner, jaws strict, lips pursed, homogenous in arrogance and Ellis really, really wants to go to sleep, but she can’t. It’s not like Ellis suffers from insomnia, really, but it’s three o’clock in the motherfucking morning and she’s not really tired, and it’s not like she’s one of these businessmen who travel all the time, well she does travel all the time but not at three in the morning, it’s just that she hasn’t slept in twenty-eight hours but she’s not tired, not really. All of the businessmen with A's and strict jaws have been moved through the gate before that voice which is really this really fat brown-haired chick behind the AA desk grabs her little microphone awfully disproportionate to the size of her fat lips and says, Welcome to American Airlines flight 5162 to New York, with a connecting flight in Washington D.C. We are now ready for B, rows 26-40. Then, after five minutes, Ellis grabs her shit and goes to the end of the line, yawning. On the airplane, Ellis ends up having to sit next to this burly Dave Harber looking man with his big burly chest and a pretty face. He smiles at her and his teeth are all uniformly bleach-white, Ellis tugs one corner of her mouth in a kind-of smile but not-really smile and sits down, tugging the seat belt around her waist. The C’s come on the plane soon after, and after that there’s the same fat woman’s voice on the speakers saying, Welcome to American Airlines flight 5162 to New York, with a connecting flight in Washington D.C. That big fat woman from behind the desk with the little microphone, she’s walking up and down the aisles and offering people drinks again, before it was some skinny blonde college student. Those businessmen, now they’re trying to be all sophisticated with their ginger ales and their Just a sprint of lemon, please’s so when the big fat woman stops at Ellis’s row, she is probably expecting, I don’t know, manners or something, but Ellis looks at her, that subcutaneous fat under those purple stitches glimmering off of this fat woman’s Captain Wings. So this Rosie O’Donnell gives Ellis a martini.
