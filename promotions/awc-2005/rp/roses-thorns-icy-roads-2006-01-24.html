Roses & Thorns - Icy Roads

24 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 24/01/06 You know, sometimes love works in mysterious ways. I would not be the first person to tell you that but damn I won’t be the last. What’s certainly true is that it’s never exactly the same for anybody, whether it’s the president of whatever country you come from or if it’s the lowly nerd that stays at home playing with his computer, hoping that he’ll get one step closer to creating the perfect android to satisfy him for life. There are so many variables attached that it’s impossible to deconstruct and figure out what exactly influenced each decision until it’s too late. Such is life; where would we be if we simply waited around and did nothing? Life is proactive, not a place for reactionary forces to win. If nothing else, this world promotes the bold, the strong and the courageous time and time again. Whether or not they win at the event but inevitably it seems they win when it matters most. This is a story…maybe you’d call it a tale in this type of situation. Regardless this is a record of the trials and tribulations that I had to work through to make it to where I am today. Who knows if the decisions I made were the right ones when it all comes down to it but one thing is for certain; it was a hell of a ride. This is a marker for many numerous things that you should or shouldn’t do, the only way you’ll know which is which depends on the situation and the partner. I am not here to provide answers to people’s love lives, I am merely a precursor to the real thing, like a guide to what may lie ahead but by no means am I the definitive article. Consider me your friend, trying his best to do the right thing for you but ultimately the choice lies in your hands. You ready to walk? Alright then, let’s go. *** 28th December, 2005 - The Victoria Hotel, Amsterdam The slow dull beep of the alarm clock heralded another day and another dawn to the Christmas period. Despite the fact that Winter Warfare had passed only a short few days ago, I had declined Jessica’s offer to return back to Kingston and spend Christmas with her and Dan. It felt better to me if I stayed completely away from all the stress and pressure that Kingston held. After the chain of events I had set in place, I realised how wrong I had been to start them, to let them happen. I was too egotistical and arrogant in my own ability to carry through the possibility of taking the helm of a syndicate which I had no claim to in the first place. Luck had been on my side that day but I knew that it wouldn’t be waiting by my side when I returned. So instead I didn’t try to return. I made my excuses with Jessica, professing a want to get in touch with myself, something that did not require her services. She was a little disappointed but accepting. I was alone again. Or so I thought. I rose from the depths of the luxurious bed that accommodated my frame with relative ease. Outside the window all was still dark and gloomy, with the moonlight reflecting calmly off the riverside. When I opened the window, you could hear the slow lapping of the banks and the gentle rubbing of the barges by the stony dock. All was peaceful and well, no real movement or action to disturb this idyllic morning. Taking my gaze from the river was hard to do, the simple yet numerous depictions of colour and shading fascinated the eye, delivering a host of tantalising possibilities. To the naked eye this happened in mere moments, with each blink a different picturesque echoing of the contributions of the nature around it. It’s amazing how simple things like this were catching my attention these days. These days were fast approaching the business end of my thirties and to say I was no spring chicken in the ring anymore was a slight understatement. To be in the sport I was in required you to be at the peak of your ability, to be able to push the boundaries each and every night on a frequent basis and the more I looked at it, the more I realised that maybe this wouldn’t be the way for me. It was more than assured that I would be at this, but the attraction to go out there every night and tear my body apart, slowly and painfully was losing the glamour and appeal it once had. The cloak and shadows that it once wore now truly revealed the grim prospect of a life with dodgy knees, arthritic shoulder and a head that had never been right the past five years. I didn’t want this to become any worse, but at the same time the drive to aspire to something greater than which I had achieved so far was still evident when I did grace the ring, the very fires still burning strongly. Still there was something nagging at the back of my mind, something that was requiring something other than the physicality of the ring. Instead it would require all the mental capacity and, truth be told, just as much physicality as the ring to truly tackle it head on. A love life. This whole trip to get away from the limelight was for the purpose to sort out where it was I wanted to head in life. Did I really want to step in that ring again if I didn’t have my heart in it? I wasn’t sure and that’s why I had intended to stay here. Amsterdam had the cool laid back atmosphere (and it wasn’t because of the drugs or the prostitution) that I needed to put things in perspective. It was well known by now that if I stepped foot in Kingston the way things were right now, I would not be even entertaining the thought of peace and quiet. Here I could relax, step back and look at things the way I wanted to. There was also the factor of the O’Grady’s. Despite Jessica and Dan’s best intentions, I knew that there would be two problems with trying to do any sort of career changing decisions. One: Jessica was a complete and utter workaholic and no matter how hard I tried to get her to relax over the Christmas party weekend, she had the intention of setting up an interview here and a meet and greet there. Things that I wanted left for after Winter Warfare, things I could deal with when I was feeling in a better mood after beating the bejesus out of Hamada. But after that match, I had been left with an empty and pointless feeling; I had never intended on spending time in AWC fighting women who shouldn’t even be in there in the first place, and winning meant nothing to me. Why was I doing it? Second: even a sniff of my foul mood and Dan would be onto me, figuring out everything that was bothering me. Even staying away from him in town wouldn’t work as he had eyes and ears everywhere. No, there was no way to escape him in Kingston and once he found out he would bring upon me the unholy creed of telling me I was disrespecting his legacy by quitting this way etc. He felt you kept wrestling until your body fell apart just like his did in the ring. Until that happened you still had go in you. After all, if you can move, you can fucking wrestle. So in hindsight, I made a pretty good move staying away from the pair of them. Looking out the window again revealed an even more picturesque view. The first cracks of the morning sun were coming into view and it looked like it’d be another freezing cold but bright day. Some of the ice on the ground was beginning to slowly melt and you could see people already choosing to make their way out for their business. Christmas wasn’t such a big deal out over here, the drive for things to get back to normal bringing about basically a two day lull in business before shops and sundries opened again. Guess the whore capital of the world really did have no attachment to religion anymore. Seeing the attractive quality of the outdoors, I figured a jog would do me some good. I know I came out to think but when I exercise I can thresh ideas out properly, give them time to work through and see if they truly could be viable. Some men like to think on the john, I like the outdoor air hitting my face. Turning, I grabbed my sports bag and headed in to the bathroom. *** Fuck I shouldn’t have worn shorts. To say it was cold was an understatement, it was bloody freezing. I think icicles were starting to grab onto the hair of my legs and form a small sheet around them, giving me some shin guards. And I’d only started to jog around the block. I wanted to pick a decent place to go and decided that going down by the river would be interesting, see where it took me. Tentatively I took my first steps, trying to be careful on the ground underfoot. Not only was it cold, I was running on cobbles. Strike two Jack, you know your knees aren’t getting any better. Within minutes I was working up at least a good bit of warmth in my body, the long sleeve jumper doing most of the work for that (fucking shorts) whilst my hat kept it all in. Even though it was eight in the morning by now, there were a good few people who had the same idea as I did. Routine it felt to me, not that there were any resolutions to do something new after gorging on their own respective feasts. There was still a good week for that. I myself hadn’t even had Christmas dinner, instead ingesting a simple but delicious duck with plum sauce and side salad expertly arranged in some bird form. Delectable. It was a paltry dinner in the end but one that was still enticing to all that saw it, including the few waiters that served me in the hotel. Honestly I wouldn’t have been surprised if some of it were missing when it got to the table. The hardened cobble gave way to the more pliable and flexible wooden platform that I now sped over, a leeway to go in between that and the lock that was in the river. As I pounded over it, I could feel the age and toughness go right through my bones. Once it had been proud and burnished with shiny metal, working day in and out, not a care in its world, proud to be a part of the community but slowly over time it had faded, the metal long gone having rotted out of the wood itself and the colour changing and distorting from brown to a duller, dead grey. Such is the way of life. In thinking on this strange analogy (it was after all, an inanimate object I had just described as bring proud to service the community) I had looked a little bit too longingly back at it. The change back to the cobbles was sudden and unprovoked, leading to a skid by my left foot, followed by (probably) a fantastic pirouette to encapsulate the entire surroundings and finally to a dramatic stop, thudding as my entire body crushed itself against the cold hard ground. I spat out viscously and cursed audibly. I’m really losing it now. ‘Better watch yourself…AWC can’t be losing someone as important as you.’ The voice was very familiar, the taint of eastern Europe fresh on its breath, linked with the prim accent of the business world gave it a scintillating quality, a quality that was lost on few people, and not me. I looked up to be sure it was her. It was.
