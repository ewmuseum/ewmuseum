The Long and Winding Road: Chapter Three - Regrets/Remonstrations

23 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23rd May 2005 Pact “So, Pact, how does it feel to be the number one contender for the AWC Frontier Championship?” “…” “…ahahaha! That’s great! And what’re your thoughts on your opponent this week, Kris Krimzon?” “…” “Is that so? Wow, the insight you guys provide is just amazing…” Stupid DJs are the main reason why I hate making appearances on talk radio. Unfortunately, my newfound status within the Atlantic Wrestling Club has led to several of these over the past week. The first one I threw myself into with gusto – it’d been so long since I had the chance to casually plug myself to the general public without splitting myself into several large pieces. It wasn’t until the end of the interview that I realised the jockey had got “Ultraviolent Icon” and “Hardcore Icon” mixed up – he thought he was talking to Mick Foley. Bastard. The excerpt above comes from my interview with Handsome Dan of SPORT 96.7 last Friday night. Shortly afterwards, I boarded a plane headed for Mexico to compete (on an indy booking) for the NLCW Hardcore Championship. To the shock and surprise of everyone present (myself included) I captured the strap. I’d made a few undistinguished appearances on NLCW TV in the preceding weeks, going down to Sugar Shane Perry, Soulless and some others. So on Sunday for me to snap the five month winning streak of the Hardcore Champion, Enigma, was more than a surprise in the eyes of the fans – it was a downright upset. The guys in the back were all happy for me of course – even Enigma. He knew how much the title would mean to me. But still. It hadn’t sunk in by the time I moseyed into AWC headquarters on Monday morning – tired, dirty and stinking of vintage tequila and chillies. “PACT! What the fuck do you think you’re doing to me, man?!” cried Pearl Harbor when he saw me. “I just got word via the Wrestling Observer Daily Newsletter that you won the Hardcore Title in NLCW yesterday!” “…well yeah…” “YOU’RE MEANT TO BE EXCLUSIVE AWC TALENT! HOW DARE YOU UNDERTAKE AN INDY BOOKING WITHOUT CONSULTING ME FIRST!” 'Agh,' I thought to myself, 'it’s gonna be a long day. Alexi Plane journey back from Mexico was a nightmare situation – Pact, Roach, Merritt and I sharing a cordoned off section of first class, stewardesses occasionally pulling back the curtain trying to catch Merritt and Roach puffing on their questionable substances but always failing. I don’t know why they allow smoking on these flights anyway; it’s a horrible habit. Things have been tense between Pact and I of late. I just hope he hasn’t cottoned on to what’s going on right under his nose. It’s getting to the point where I’m nervous to be in close quarters with him. To be honest, I can’t wait for Fresh! to be over. After Fresh! I’m sure that things will get better. Everything will be out in the open, Pact will have his title and I’ll be able to relax safe in the knowledge that his career is secure. At least for a while. It really is quite a BRILLIANT plan, though, heh. Not that it’s ALL my devising, oh no – The Benefactor’s amazing mind played a large role in the shaping of the concepts involved, of course. He’s investing a considerable sum – I had to take his suggestions on board. And he’s the only man who truly knows what is best for Pact – both the man and the wrestler. I know that Chris will see things my way in the long run. And I will keep to my word on Sunday – I will not become physically involved in his match with Enigma. Nor, at the root of it, will I have to. His victory is signed, sealed, delivered. It’s as good as guaranteed. When a man as great as the Benefactor makes you a promise, you know you can trust it, no questions asked. No questions, indeed, necessary. These Yakuza may be ill-mannered slobs, but they’re reliable to a fault. You can depend on them – after all, for them there is no tighter bond than money. It’s just the Benefactor…he is a man who shaped the course of Pact’s life. He changed everything in a big way for him. The changes were both good and bad – he’s the first to admit it, really he is. He understands why Pact might still be angry with him, may even want to kill him. I mean, the blood shed in the Benefactor’s name, the lives lost to his cause…they don’t come without a price. And he realises that. He truly does. He understands that men – many men – want him dead, want his head stuck on a steak for all of Tokyo to see, but it simply will not be that way. He is too clever, too careful, too resourceful. He’s protected himself so far – over twenty years since he escaped the clutches of Interpol. So…we’ll see I guess. We’ll see. Oh Jesus, I hope I’ve done the right thing. Pact I’d taken myself away from the hustle and bustle of the offices and sought some solitude in the building’s basement; somewhere I could be alone with my thoughts. I huddled myself in next to the building’s largely redundant heating system, laying the Hardcore Championship out in front of me. It gleamed in the half-light, reflecting back at me a hundred victories and losses, images of horrendous bloodletting, the fulfilled hopes and shattered dreams of the men and women who’d held this belt in their arms before me. I allowed a smile to spread across my face, thinking about how much I’d pissed off Pearl and how much I was looking forward to facing Kris Krimson this week. 'I’m not the Frontier Champion yet. At the end of the day what I’ve done in the past means nothing here. Here, nobody knows me. To them I’m just another rookie, another guy out to make a name for himself. They don’t understand what my name means. And to be frank I don’t blame them. To say I’m washed up would be an understatement. In order to validate my position in this company I had to muscle my way in to a spot a younger, fitter and probably more talented guy could’ve better filled. What kind of example does that set for the 1 and 2 year rookies in the lockerroom? Is that the kind of man I want to be remembered as? Do I want to be remembered as the man who stole the Frontier Championship from a younger, more deserving superstar? Or do I want to be known as the man that beat an up and coming star for it, fair and square, in the middle of the ring? I only hope that my performance at Fresh! validates rather than undermines what I’ve done in AWC since my arrival. I’m not necessarily proud of the spot I’m in, or having a claim to the Championship, or of being in the position to nip a promising young superstar’s career in the bud…But at the end of the day they’re all necessary steps. I need to ground myself in this league somehow or other and I haven’t done so as yet. And I’ve now got myself in a position where I can’t afford to lose. Loss brings shame not just on me but on the men I’ve tried to drag down with me. Loss would mean everything I’ve put myself and the roster through has been for naught. I’ll be in exactly the same position as I was a month ago. I can’t let that happen. I WON’T let that happen. I will not disgrace us all.' With these last few words reverberating around my skull, I lit up a Marlboro Light with shaking hands. Drawing deeply, I let my eyes play across the gold of the belt. I expelled the smoke slowly, allowing my head to loll against the side of the boiler. 'Krimson seems as insane as I am. It’s a quality I find attractive and positive in a young man. It’s a shame that I’m going to have to finish his nice little run before it even really begins. But that is what I’ll do. Failure is not an option. The concept of defeat does not enter my mind. I am determined to come out the victor.' I dragged myself to my feet and crushed the Marlboro under my boot. Picking the belt up, I slung it over my shoulder and made my way, slowly, back to reality.
