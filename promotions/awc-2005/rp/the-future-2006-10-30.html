The Future

30 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30th October, 2006 October 29th, 2021 I stood in silence amidst the cries of joy that surrounded me. Everyone smiled, everyone laughed, each person had something to be pleased about. I, on the other hand, mustered my best fake smile to get me through the moment. My mind was elsewhere, tracing back through my past to the day that I still remembered vividly. After fifteen years, I anticipated the memory would be long gone, reduced to merely an unfortunate incident in the back of my mind. But things didn’t turn out the way I wanted them to. Day after day, I woke up in despair. Today, the anniversary of his tragic death, I could barely speak I had descended into such a state of despair. No one talked to me. No one attempted to comfort me. No one knew what happened. The children darted by my feet in the comfortable living room, ducking behind the leather sofas and peeking out when the coast was clear. I shuffled past them quickly, seeking the fresh air outside. My wife, Lilly Baines, had become frustrated with my antics and decided that outside in the back yard was the best place to be. She wasn’t interested in my company while I constantly mulled over that day, and what I could have done differently, to prevent Phil Allen’s death. To be truthful, I was unable to prevent it. I still remembered the frosty air crashing into my face as he topped like a rag doll to the floor, the drugs slowly taking effect. Within moments they had sapped the life out of the vibrant man I once knew as my mentor and best friend. As I leaned over him in those final moments, trying in vain to revive him, the most noticeable aspect of his face was the lack of vibrancy. He no longer smiled, his lips steadily shivering in a frown as the last ounce of life was removed from his body. I kept these images locked in my subconscious, shutting them out from the world. Lilly advised me to share it with her, but I refused every time. The trust in our relationship was effectively gone. In the early stages, after Dean Fisher had been put away, we loved one another deeply. It took us only a year to get married. Life was better than ever before for me. The downward slope began two years ago, when suddenly life wasn’t good enough anymore. My ambitions changed and I switched my focus. The news that I was impotent and couldn’t give Lilly a child only served to compound my misery. I stepped into the back yard where Lilly stood holding her martini glass, half-empty. Just as I opened my mouth to apologise, she interjected, predicting my next move with precision. “If you’re here to say sorry or look for sympathy, you’ve come to the wrong place, Wes.” She turned towards me and left her martini glass on a nearby table, her face ashen with anger. As the wind blew from behind me, I just took a moment to look into her eyes. They still had life in them. She wasn’t a day older than thirty seven; we had our whole lives before us. The sad part was that I didn’t anticipate my future would be with her. She was approaching breaking point. “Lilly, I can’t expect you to understand what I’m going through. It’s hard to even understand it for me. Just please be patient and let me deal with this.” “You’ve been dealing with it for the last fifteen years, Wes! Why all of a sudden is it such a big deal? Pull it together fast, or I won’t be around for much longer to listen to your bitching every day!” she snapped, her words reverberating around the yard. Before I had a chance to reply, she was gone. She stormed past me and back into the house to mingle, undoubtedly hoping it would take her mind off the situation at hand. I took a seat alone on the bench in the garden, taking in the atmosphere. I was at a loss for what to do next, so instead of looking to the future, I looked to the past. That night fifteen years ago was the most traumatic of my life, but I couldn’t let it destroy my life now. Phil Allen wouldn’t have had it that way. Neither would I. Everything went as expected outside the arena after our match. Fans lined up seeking our autographs, something Phil and I still couldn’t get over. A fan graciously took the signed piece of paper I handed back and cradled it like a baby. Sometimes I was amazed by how a simple signature could please someone so much. It was a quirky feature of wrestling fans. Phil was well able to deal with the attention, but something was bothering him tonight. He wasn’t his usual fetching self. Some fans noticed, and I certainly did too. As we headed towards our car, the security cutting off the fans’ route suddenly, I spoke to him. “Is everything okay, Phil? Ever since we finished the match, you’ve been acting a little weird.” “I’ve just had a tough week, that’s all. My wife called and – oh, it’s hard to explain. Just, just let me explain it again, alright?” He was irritated by my question, almost as if it had no place in his mind. He didn’t want to delve into the reasons for his recent depression. I had plenty of possible scenarios, but I didn’t want to risk mentioning them in case I angered him further. We got into the car, but didn’t speak until we were on the road for five minutes. “Wes, do you mind if we pull in here at this rest stop?” Phil Allen rolled the passenger window down and the cold air rushed into our faces, catching us off guard. I instinctively knew something was wrong with my tag team partner. I pulled in as instructed at the rest stop, which consisted of a simple toilet and a payphone. Phil moved awkwardly through the hard, frosty landscape towards the toilet. I followed him slowly, simultaneously concerned and wary of him at that moment. Before I could act, he collapsed onto the frost-covered grass, his body now limp and lifeless. I frantically ran over to where he had fallen and turned him onto his back so that I could speak to him. Something brought me pause just as I was about to address him, however. It was resignation. It was fear. It was knowing I had no chance to save my best friend from what had befallen him. His lips quivered as the life left his body quickly. I dropped to my knees and slammed my fists on the ground in front of me. Tears began to roll down my cheeks. It didn’t take a rocket scientist to figure out what had ended Phil Allen’s life. I cursed myself for not spotting it soon. The drugs had turned him around for the worst. I was blind to the danger, blind to his life. I was self-obsessed and greedy. I sat in silence for hours as his body lay beside me, motionless. From that moment on, everything changed. The AWC no longer mattered to me. My budding relationship with Lilly Baines was futile. This experience scarred my mind. It scarred my very existence.
