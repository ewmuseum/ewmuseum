Trials and the Consequence - The Hunger

30 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30/08/86 ‘You know, up until about ten minutes ago I was ready to come in here and tell you I was retiring.’ Well, that woke the conference up. So I’m standing in front of the multitude of photographers, journalists and hangers on inside the press room of the Continental Airlines arena in New Jersey, the site of Coast to Coast. This was nothing more than a simple routine affair with the press, get a couple of blurbs for their own respective articles, maybe have a couple of laughs, maybe get the occasional witty comment from a journalist, but mostly just have a boring hour or two as the questions are pretty functional and bland. I didn’t feel like having one of those days frankly. It wasn’t just a tactic to wake up the crowd either; who as usual were in a peaceful slumber in between questions. No, this one was for real. I know that wrestlers say they’re going to retire so many times and then out of nowhere they come back for that one last match, that one last tasting of the cup before it dries up. There’s a real specific reasoning behind that though; it’s two little things called desire and hunger. Sure money comes into things from time to time but inevitably they come back for those two. I think you can see where this is going, but let’s just continue the façade for the moment shall we? ‘Well, it looks like for once you’ll be fully absorbed in everything I say here won’t we? It’s nice to finally get some attention after falling off of your hot list. Don’t think that I haven’t read each and every one of your articles over the past couple of months questioning my abilities, questioning my effort into this business. For one thing, you’d probably be right about a lot of it.’ I took a simple gaze across the room to see if anyone in particular was catching the eye, if anybody really was sucked in so far by this. Three stood out to me, but only one mattered in the grand scheme of things. That was the reporter Sylvia Carter, of a local wrestling newspaper in the Tri-State area. It was easy to pick her out because she was one of the only women in the entire room, sitting right by the side to hide herself slightly but still in just enough view to be a predatory, watching force. You get the feeling that when you’re talking to her that she has an incredible distaste for the industry in general, cursing silently at it as she plies her trade. It seems things weren’t meant to go this way for the plucky journalist, least not the way she intended to write her own story. So instead she toils away in the harsh unforgiving wrestling industry, hoping for that leap to stardom someday to take off. Knowing what I was about to do, she was the perfect candidate to hear me. I knew I’d have her hooked. ‘You’d be right, simply because for the last couple of months I haven’t been inspired by anything here in the AWC. I haven’t felt any desire to strive for anything in the organisation. It’s taken me nigh on fifteen years to reach the pinnacle of the sport when I won the title at Bloodlust this past year, and then for it to be taken away from me so quickly, so unfairly and not even be tossed a conciliatory gesture is disgusting. Instead what I get is a shot against someone who is so far below my stature that it was insulting.’ ‘But Jack, you got a title shot, maybe not for the belt that you had lost but others have been treated far harsher.’ Some young journalist had piped up right in the midst of the speech and I was not happy. He looked determined, a little bead of sweat trickling from his forehead and in a way he had to be admired. But I wasn’t going to let that slide. I wasn’t about to let him simply interrupt Jack Murphy. ‘Would you feel that you’ve been treated fairly if after getting a top story you get stuck with covering something irrelevant and pointless? Would you think it fair that you get given a promotion and then they decide they’d rather not have you and conspire to get you out of there? I don’t think that’s fair, and maybe you have a different perspective on this from you but I sure as hell would not think it’s fair. And next time, wait your turn you little prick.’ Some of the older journalists chuckled at the remark thrown at the end and smiled. The plucky young journalist meanwhile fumed inwardly. Deep down he wanted to leave in protest but he’d just look like a little cry baby. He knew it, and I knew it. So instead he shut up, crossed his arms and sat there unmoving. ‘Anyway, back to my point. I have been mistreated and insulted publicly week after week on television. I know I said that the weeks after my loss were spent watching and waiting and to a certain degree that was true, but let’s not beat around the bush any longer. Those weeks were spent questioning the worth of the business to me anymore. I had reached the heights and I had reached the pinnacle of what I wanted to achieve and frankly I wasn’t sure if there was any reason why I wanted to stay.’ I took another pause to gaze across the room and everyone was watching. I had them in my thrall now, like the preacher to the flock. I was their master and was ready to lay upon them the sacred word which would tell all how they would serve their lives. It was…it felt good right now and I wasn’t about to let up. ‘There are so many things that I can say today as to why I didn’t want to stay and really, I think you all deserve to hear them. The problem lies at the very core of the AWC and its managers, chiefly David Harber and Sasha Volkyeva. Week after week we see petty squabbles between the two take up air time that should be composed of wrestlers. It’s a wrestling show and no matter how you cut it, it will ALWAYS be about wrestling. As I’m sure you’ve heard hundreds of times since it was first uttered, it still says wrestling on the marquee and I came to wrestle. Because of these two people I wasn’t allowed to do that.’ Journalists were taking down notes furiously, poring over them like they were money waiting to be had. This pleased me and once more I took a look in Sylvia’s direction. She was amused and caught my eye, nodding towards me. Excellent. ‘If you want to see proof of this, you only have to look back at the first couple of months in which I entered the organisation. I was on each and every week, staking my claim to being one of the top dogs and Triangles is the result of that. I stand proud with the grand slam package, the shot to each and every belt whenever I choose. Since then though I have had to toil for my time. I was thrown the Livewire belt as a way of appeasing me whilst Lavelle worked his way through the pathetic challengers that he had. A collision course was inevitable but they made sure that it occurred as subtly as possible. It wasn’t given the time it deserved, it wasn’t given a grand stage and I was tossed back. I admit I underestimated Lavelle but the treatment for me was already simmering.’ This felt good, really good to get it off of my chest. It had been brewing in my stomach for the better part of the year and really I had only been pandering to it by letting it to brew there, stewing and coming to the boil until it would ache at me to be unleashed. This was the time to do it. ‘From there you have my rise to the Transatlantic Championship and that was certainly deserved, however it is from there that you come to the current situation. Nobody can deny that the way in which I was treated during this time was unfair and uncalled for. Even speaking upon it now it leaves me hurting to see how cheaply I was tossed to get Adam Dick back on top. It leaves me sick to remember that nobody fought my side of the case apart from Truth Waters. Nobody cared and this has left me numb and dulled to the wants of the organisation. Why should I give a crap about this place when nobody within it gives a crap about me?’ ‘So, are you saying that retiring would have been an easy way out?’ It was Sylvia, finally ready to impart some sort of question into the equation. She, unlike our plucky journalist from before had waited for the moment to pause at a question, a rhetorical one at that, but a question nonetheless. Now I began to take a closer look at her as she had stepped out from her perch in the room so that everybody could see her clearly and know her intent. She had bided her time and felt that enough information had been processed to call for the question. She wore only a simple business suit, nothing flash or exotic. It hung a bit flat on her body which was pretty non-existent. It was hard to tell what kind of frame she had as the suit was covering it and I suspected that there was nothing to be worried about there. Her dark brown hair was tied up in a bun, trails of hair falling from it in a haphazard way but she was calm and collected. I think simply the state of the air conditioning in the room (in actual fact there was none) had had a distinct effect on her general appearance and demeanour. It was misleading to say the least. In all this time I hadn’t realised I was staring and, more importantly, I hadn’t even answered the question. Her eyes kind of gave it away. ‘Well Miss Carter I think you‘re close but not quite where I‘m getting at. Retiring was the easy way out, it would be too easy in fact but it’s not the only reason that I’m professing today. No, there’s far much more. You see, whilst the managers of AWC are part and parcel of the problem, there is also another situation right now which has left me without the desire and eagerness to even bother.’ A sigh emanated from my lips and resounded through the microphone and all the journalists halted in their frenetic scribbling. I motioned that I was chewing something in my mouth, just to get across that I wanted these words to be heard right and not misappropriated. ‘When I look at the current roster of the AWC my heart and soul sighs much like I have just done. Right now there is practically nothing that excites me about the organisation. I see a main event mired in chicanery and ludicrous actions that don’t make any sense. What pains me the most is that it seems Lavelle merely left for a bit of a break in defending his title. It’s like they only ever wanted Lavelle as the champion and everything in between was a stopgap. Following on from this I find unexciting, bland, dead characters to me.’ Hmm, this was going even better than I thought. ‘Witness the swift change of the Frontier title back and forth between a man who’s highlight so far has been as a bodyguard for the entertainment manager, and a character who is so stereotyped it’s not even funny. What’s more, the stereotype he was capitalising on has now returned to the organisation. I fail to see how it is relevant to have two of the same gimmick in AWC. Especially such a gimmick that is disrespecting and tasteless for an organisation who has a great viewer ship from the place where these two men come from. Perhaps I am particularly slighted by this due to my similar nationality but that’s neither here nor there. The further I go down, the further the situation I am left in is even more undesirable. I see a sea full of mediocrity and wannabes. They all claim they’ve got the talent but in numerous showings they have given me, or the audience nothing to change my mind. It is simply unacceptable that a federation striving to be the place to be in the wrestling industry has such a disparity between the quality at the top and bottom.’ ‘Why then aren’t you jumping ship while you have the opportunity? Why don’t you ride your status to a neighbouring federation so that you won’t be misused and misrepresented like you have been so far?’ Carter was on my side now, you can tell by the questions. Once they become feeder questions as opposed to ones trying to get to the root of your statements, you know that you’ve won them over. They know there’s a story within your proclamation so they’re playing ball to get to it. Sylvia Carter was playing ball and I was going to give her a nice return. ‘One person, one single person in this entire federation has given me hope and the possibility that he can help to change this place with me. Remember when I first entered the AWC, I came in looking to change the way in which wrestling was perceived, to bring it back to the roots and where it should be, as the pinnacle of sports, the pinnacle and height of competition. I said all of these things and in some little ways I have improved upon that. But there is much work still to be done. There are many paths which still have to be walked down upon but up until now I haven’t been able to do such simply because I am only one man. I am only one person in this crusade and you need others if you are to drum up momentum and power in what you are saying. It is only now that I have finally found someone who I can look at and say that he would be the right choice to walk with me and bring a new era into the AWC.’ ‘And who would this be?’ ‘Well, I know some of you will find this surprising but that is merely because you don’t understand. That man is Captain Suleimon.’ A bit of a murmur went round the room again, each of them rushing for a pen to make sure they didn’t forget it. Sylvia didn’t move, she knew that this wouldn’t be a forgotten quip of mine. ‘Ah I see I haven’t put you all to sleep then. I know it sounds like I’m in a bit of a quandary here since I am here in New Jersey to face the very man that I wish to walk along with. The answer to this is rather simple and I alluded to it in the broadcast on the recent Fresh!. In Captain Suleimon I see respect and integrity for this industry. He wants the same things that I do, though he isn’t aware of it quite yet. He wants to bring this place to a point where the skill rules, the power and ruthlessness of the wrestler in the ring wins out. The only way to bring those out though, is to show him in the ring. Show him exactly how it can be achieved through the ring. When I said I would slowly drag him through the match, I wasn’t lying. I am going to show him, through my power and strength the way that we both should be taking. Thank you.’ The flicker of flashbulbs went off as I descended from the perch of the pulpit in the press conference. That felt better, felt very revitalising and as I walked down the aisle of the room I could see people watching me leave. They wouldn’t be forgetting this one any time soon. One person wasn’t going to ever and she followed me out the door. ‘Jack!’ I halted, smiling widely as I heard the pitter patter of her heels clicking up to me in the corridor. I turned round slowly, making it seem like I wasn’t too compliant with her calling. ‘Yes Miss Carter?’ She stopped abruptly and was slightly flustered by my bluntness. Straightening herself, she attempted to flatten out her suit and make it look more appealing. It didn’t really work. ‘Well Jack…I mean Mr. Murphy…’ ‘Please, Jack will be fine.’ ‘Well…Jack, your story was very…intriguing to say the least and I’d really like it if we could meet up some time for a much closer look into your psyche. I mean, it’s no secret that you’re encountering other problems in your life right now and I expect that with the right coverage…they might not be a problem at all.’ Huh, well she knew her history and had done the research. Of course, she didn’t realise that I wanted it this way. You see, the entire press conference and venting was carefully staged because recently I had come into some knowledge. Some very important knowledge. Father Pearson isn’t all that he seems to be and that there is definitely subtle influences dictating where he is going in his intentions against me. I had learned this from a close friend (one which I can’t divulge right now) and this had garnered me a glimmer of hope. The problem was, I had no way of getting near to Pearson to find out the truth. In order to get through there, I needed someone who had contacts, who the wherewithal and, most importantly, the shadow quality to them. Sylvia Carter fit the bill because of two things. Desire and hunger. They may have come up earlier if you were keeping up. ‘Well Miss Carter…I think that you have intrigued me certainly. When do you wish to meet?’ ‘How about the night after the PPV? We can have dinner?’ ‘That sounds fine to me.’ ‘Here, take my business card Jack.’ ‘Thank you. Well, I best be off. It was nice to finally speak to you in person Miss Carter.’ ‘Sylvia, please.’ I twirled the card in my hand and turned to go, but something had piqued my interest. Her hand lingered just a little long on my own and the twinkle in her eye was disconcerting. It was slightly off, but I thought nothing more of it. In the meantime I had to prepare finally for the match which would be the beginning, the beginning of a new era for me and Captain Suleimon, whether he knew it or not. *** Off to the side of the corridor, Sylvia Carter paused, taking out a lighter and a cigarette. She calmly flicked on the lighter, letting the flame catch on the smoke stick. Once that was done she pawed through her purse looking for her cell phone. It didn’t take too long. Speed dial saw to the number she wanted. Whatever you could say about Miss Carter, she was nothing if not efficient. ‘Hello… Yes it’s done. It was a little simpler than expected, but he is only a wrestler. I will keep that in mind, don’t worry Dr. Drake, everything is in the palm of my hand. After all, you’re the one who has the control and the plan.’ Click.
