Politics and Persuasion

14 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Roleplay for Fresh! Title: 'Politics and Persuasion' Los Angeles, California The autumn sunshine whipped over the hills as the morning rose to greet the night. The many alarms buzzed ferociously, endlessly attempting to rouse those who slept in longer than they should. The rush hour traffic had begun and the endless honking of horns became apparent even to those in the hills. In the midst of the morning rituals, Lavelle stirred on the sofa, rolling to one side and landed with a loud thud on the maple wood flooring. Looking bleary eyed around the room he rubbed his back and rose, trailing the blanket along the floor before plonking himself back onto the sofa. Although he now lived in the large mansion that sat in Bel-Air he had yet to find a bedroom, each one held a past memory and none of them were favourable, so that night he opted for the large sofa in the living area. Edward emerged from around the corner cradling a plate of toast and a glass of fresh orange juice. “I hope you’re hungry,” Edward said as he placed the breakfast onto the small coffee table. Lavelle smiled and quickly nibbled on the slice of toast, before turning back toward Edward. “Hope you don’t mind my intrusion,” he said, quietly. Edward smiled. “Not at all, this is your house.” “Do I get a choice of car in the garage?” Lavelle questioned in between slurping down some orange juice. “When you have finished your breakfast, I guess.” “Get first dibs on any one of his cars, right?” Lavelle pondered with a smug grin. Edward sat next to Lavelle, pushing aside the blanket and pillow that Lavelle had slept on. “Everything but the Silver Cloud Rolls,” Edward said, wistfully. Wetting his gums with the remainder of the orange juice he wiped his mouth with the back of his hand and turned to Edward who was now heavily indulged in a copy of USA today, reading the Sports section as he kept a close eye on the many seasons now beginning. “How’s this sound, you can own the Rolls…” Lavelle said. “…and in return I don’t have to see this fashion consultant.” Edward looked up from the paper, his reading glasses perched on the bridge of his nose as his moustache moved under his breath. “Afraid that’s already been arranged,” Edward announced. “Besides, you can’t go into work looking like a Kansas farmer.” Lavelle looked toward his mound of clothing and then at himself, dressed in nothing but chequered pyjama trousers and white boxers. “Hey! I happen to like my fashion!” Lavelle argued. “Then you must be blind…” A high pitched voice shot through their conversation as a man appeared in the doorway cradling a briefcase and dressed in a posh Armani suit and pink shirt. He moved forward with a very feminine swagger in his step, placing his briefcase onto a small stool before turning his attention to Lavelle, his eyes opening wide with delight. “Well, it’s not everyday I get a hunk to work with,” he said with a wink. Edward laughed, musing to himself at how uncomfortable Lavelle must be feeling as he sat rigid at the corner of the chair. Edward rose and introduced Lavelle to the man in front of him as Lawrence Delaine. “Charmed,” Lawrence said with glee. Lavelle rose and shook his hand. “Uh-huh.” Edward nodded toward Lawrence, resisting the shaking of hands. Lavelle peered around the man still holding his hand toward the briefcase with wonder. “So, what exactly is in the briefcase?” Lavelle asked, wearily. “Oh darlin’, that’s my man bag. Contains all my essentials,” Lawrence said with his back to Edward and Lavelle as he rummaged through Lavelle’s clothing. He turned sharply, holding a bundle of Lavelle’s shirts, t-shirts and jeans and shook his head with displeasure. Lavelle raised his shoulders. “What?” “Where are you from again?” Lawrence questioned. “Pennsylvania…” Lavelle said, coyly. Lawrence sighed and chucked the entire bundle into a small thrash can that lay next to the study desk. Lavelle jumped forward but stopped when he felt Lawrence’s hand on his chest. “Hey…those are my…” “Rags! You’re living in the ‘80’s…” Lawrence spoke bluntly, turning to Edward who stood in calmly to one side, arms folded. “Edward, you wouldn’t get the car prepped…we’re going to need some serious shopping, STAT.” Lawrence said, mockingly, clapping his hands in a gesture that Lavelle found all too irritating. Edward nodded and scuttled off to the garage as Lavelle stood awkwardly in nothing but his pyjama trousers being scrutinized by Lawrence. “So…what can I wear?” Lavelle asked, impatiently, watching his clothes being chucked into the thrash can. Lawrence turned around with an eager smile, holding a shit and jeans. “These.” Lavelle raised an eyebrow. “Those?” he said whilst holding them in his hand and raising an eyebrow with a bemused expression. “They aren’t even mine.” Lawrence chuckled as Lavelle held the shirt out in disbelief. “Come along, we’ve got a lot of work to do.” Lavelle looked at the shirt. “Pink isn’t even my colour! HEY!” Las Vegas, Nevada Las Vegas was a world apart from anything she'd seen. The constant flashing of neon lights that surrounded the casinos and the infamous strip dazzled her as she stood quietly at the window, gazing out into the small part of the dessert that never slept and she could understand why it was a saying magnanimous with the City of Sin… Who could sleep with the constant light? She asked herself as she pulled down the black shutter and gazed horrifyingly at herself in the mirror. She'd be down sized to no more than a mere window manikin. A one trick pony. Although the outfit dazzled those lingering and monstrous eyes, it wasn't her. Now sitting against the wall, she gripped the small glass and took a large swig of the clear liquid. She relished the taste as a burning sensation fluttered down the back of her throat, taking her mind off of her woes and captivity. Just one more, she thought, knocking back another swig. She felt cold dressed in the clad attire she'd been given. Even in the Nevada dessert, everything felt cold to her. In the distance she heard the feint cheers and lulling from the crowd just beyond the thin wall that separated her from her hell, a small window opened up below her to the floor. As Lois rose off the floor she watched the many men from a distance, clawing and howling toward the dancers on stage and she shuddered. She'd been doing this for a week and felt sick to her stomach. As she turned, she felt the cold and harsh touch of her captor. 'Enjoying yourself, Lois?' he asked, smugly. 'Oh, it's a hoot.' She said with a determined shove to the side while she gripped her small flannel shirt and a packet of cigarettes. He turned toward her in his slick designed suit and savvy sophisticated appearance. 'And where you off to?' he questioned, menacingly. 'Can't a girl have a smoke?' she said, toying with him. He nodded his head and left. Sighing with relief she placed a cigarette into her mouth and pulled the shirt over her heavily bruised and sore back. Swinging on the pole was no easy task and she never had much upper body strength. Standing in the harsh night air, she lit the cigarette, a small blinking dot in the mass of lighting around her. The cold dessert winds rippled around her thighs, bare and exposed to the harshness around her. Shivering with the cigarette lingering in her mouth she stood silently, playing to the onlooker. It was all an act, she wasn't a smoker. The watchful eyes left and returned to the darkness they'd emerged from. Across the street was a phone booth and in her hand were those precious nickels’ and dimes. Her change...Her chance…Her freedom... The willingness and urgency in her footsteps fluttered through her mind, her heart began pulsating a beat a second. Slamming the glass door shut behind her, she felt blinded, as though she were in a glass house and the sun was shining through. Lois squinted her eyes as she shoved the change into the machine and gripped the phone. Her heart relished in the moment when her ears listened to the tone. 'Thank you!' she muttered to nobody as she dialed the number. The ring tone tingled and a small tear trickled down her cheek. The sweet voice that answered caused Lois to push her head back in relief and close her eyes as she listened. 'Mom,' Lois said, teary eyed. 'Sweetheart, where have you been?' her mother questioned. The sound of a television lay in the background. 'I'm sorry, mom,' Lois said. 'Listen, honey, you've been gone for two weeks. Pierce and Robert have been extremely worried…We’ve all been worried…I'm racked with nerves, where are you sweetheart, I'll send Tommy to get you.' I wish you could! Lois thought as she headed to her mothers words. He was worried about me. She pondered a moment and looked back toward the dark door she'd emerged from and knew she couldn't simply leave, it was never simple. 'I'm fine, mom. I'm staying with a friend in Vegas. Don't worry. I'll keep in touch.' Lois's heart jumped as she saw him crossing the street, his pace quickening. 'Ok. Love you mom. Gotta run!' Lois shoved the receiver down and emerged from the glass box. He stood next to her and with a swing of his fist sent her colliding to the harsh gravel beneath her. She looked up, holding her cheek as she felt the warmth crimson blood trickle down her split lip. 'You ever pull something like that again and I'll fucking kill you!' Lois nodded her head as he gripped her arm forcefully and dragged her back to the darkened door. Los Angeles, California The LAPD office in Downtown was filled to the gunnels with officers and convicts, the beckoning calls of the 5th precinct seemed endless as each new crime brought a new manila file to the table of Mastriani and Collins. They sat opposite one another and piled their way through file after file, each time finding less than they’d begun with and each case would hold the breakthrough and the breaking point. Collins sat rigidly in his seat and swirled his LAPD cup around, it had been empty for eight hours and he was planning on moving. In the distance another siren roared as it made its way onto the street, heading to the plea of another victim or worse Collins thought as he stared toward Mastriani. She was engrossed in her computer, running up convicts and leads through DMV, each hit bringing more winding roads and leads to check-up on. The stress was written all over her face as she rubbed her temples and removed the thin glasses she wore for reading. Collins inched forward, picking up his cup. “I’m gonna get a refill, ya want some?” Mastriani turned toward him. “No, I’m fine.” Collins nodded and rose out of his seat, stretching his arms and back as he felt each muscle pull taut before relaxing once again. The commander-in-chief’s door flew open and his gaze shot immediately toward Collins. “Collins!” he said, vigorously. Mastriani looked up from her desk, removing her glasses. “What’d you do now?” she asked, wearily. Collins shrugged his shoulders and placed his cup of coffee down. The commander-in-chief, Henry Mullin, was a veteran of homicide and under cover operations, but he’d soon grown tired of the hunt and yearned for the power and benefits of being the boss. Ordering Collins to sit down, Mullins waltzed over to his leather recliner and took a sip of his coffee. Collins wasn’t the only man in the room, opposite them was a man dressed in a deep blue suit, white shirt and deep blue tie. His hair and image trimmed to perfection, his posture perfect. Collins looked at his own posture, slumped and lazy, he smiled. “Wipe that cocky grin off your face, Matthew,” Mullins said gazing toward Collins with a stern and forceful demeanor. “Yes, Sir!” Collins replied, sarcastically. Mullins reclined and relaxed before commencing. Collins sat impatiently, fiddling with his thumbs, ignoring the man in the corner. “Matthew Collins, I’d like you to meet Agent Jack Mason, Federal Bureau of Intelligence.” Collins looked up and swore to himself. “Oh, fuck, the Fumbling Bureau of Idiots” he thought with a toothy smile as he shook Masons hand. “Pleasure to meet you,” Collins spoke. “Likewise,” Mason responded with an orderly and pristine tone. Rolling his eyes, Collins turned to Mullins. “They getting another one of my cases?” Mason now took over, turning in his seat. “I can assure you this has nothing to do with one of your cases, Detective. This is a federal investigation.” Collins squirmed. “Look, I swear I didn’t know he was undercover. I just knocked him out for doin’ drugs to the kid. How am I supposed to know he was an FBI witness?” Mullins shook his head in embarrassment as Mason looked dumbfounded by the immediacy and urgency in the man’s tone. “This is to do with a missing agent, Detective Collins.” Collins eyes opened. “OH!” Mullins patted his fingers together before turning to his phone and ordering Mastriani to come to his office. Before long, the door opened and Mastriani stepped into the office with a confused look upon her worn out image. “Rachel Mastriani, I’d like you to meet Agent Jack Mason,” Mullins said, once more running through the formalities. Mastriani sat down next to her partner and shook hands with the Agent before Mullins continued. “Now that I have you both here, you are being stripped of all your ongoing cases.” Collins tore from his seat. “Hold on one second.” “Please, Detective, I require you and your partners help,” Mason interjected. Mastriani took the file from Mason and opened it up; she spoke without looking up from the document. “You want us to interview this suspect?” she questioned. “Pierce Lavelle is not a suspect, we just need to confirm that he hasn’t had contact with the missing agent.” “Look, thanks for the opportunity, but can’t you boys…” Collins stopped; Mastriani was stood in front of him. “We’ll take it,” she said and thanked the agent. Mason nodded and handed them a contact card before organizing a meeting for tomorrow. Collins raised his eyebrows. “What was that?” he asked. “I’m saving your ass,” she responded and left. Collins followed, closing Mullins door behind him. “That’s all good, Rachel, except my ass doesn’t need saving.” Mastriani turned toward him. “We’ve got over 50 files on our desk, none have been solved, and this is a file worth looking at. If you don’t start solving something, Matt, Mullins will boot you down to desk duty.” Collins nodded his head. “Alright, just give most the files to Dickson and Madding.” Mastriani nodded with a smile. “When should we go see this Lubelle guy?” Collins questioned, glumly. “It’s LA-VELLE, and I guess now is as good a time as any!” Lavelle stood in front of Edward a new man. He had been out on Sunset for over three hours, indulging the whims of Lawrence Delaine and purchasing a new wardrobe of clothing that befitted the hubris of a businessman, not a small town surgeon who wrestled on the side, but seeing his clean-shaven and trimmed appearance he admitted to Edward that he did like the new look. Dressed in a lavish black suit and shirt with deep blue tie he strolled through the long corridor carrying a bundle of documents and a small letter he’d retrieved from his grandfather’s vault. He opened the garage door and flicked the switch. An array of colours dazzled him as his eyes lay beholden unto the many varying types of car collected and stored in the multitasking garage beneath the supreme manor. The selection of cars seemed more associated with a Hollywood after party than a business owner’s private collection, but none the less, Lavelle’s eyes prowled the key hooks till he set upon a DV logo that he couldn’t resist. “You’ll be taking the Dodge then sir?” Edward questioned, balanced in the doorway. Lavelle clicked the electrical un-lock button on the key and the royal blue Dodge Viper sprung to life, its lights flashing. “Yeah, this one will do,” Lavelle said, engrossed in the all knowing sense of speed and adrenaline that awaited him. “Do fill it up on the way home,” Edward said and left. Sliding into the seat Lavelle took a moment to smell the fresh leather interior as he began revving the engine for a quick run test. The documents that lay strewn on the passenger seat began to vibrate. Placing the gear stick into drive he pressed a small remote and watched the garage doors wind open, the rain and wind howling through the small crevice created. Whilst he sat, he opened the letter, having read various paragraphs he had now become consumed with a familiar name appearing in the letter – council. Unsure of what it meant, again, Lavelle placed the letter down and carefully touched the accelerator and felt the engine roar to life. He began to pull away when he suddenly hit the clutch, in front were a man and woman, both stood in the rain and hovering around the garage for some shelter. Lavelle emerged from the car and had the woman’s immediate attention. “Mr. Lavelle, I’m Detective Mastriani and this is my partner, Detective Collins.” Mastriani said, formally, extending her hand to which Lavelle obliged and shook. Collins stood with a squared jaw; arms folded and already fed up with his new task. “We need to ask you a couple of questions, Mr. Lavelle,” Collins stated. “Sure, but I’m just on the way to the office,” Lavelle said looking at his watch. “I got about ten minutes before traffic gets really heavy. How may I help?” Collins looked around the garage, eyes wide open as he met the Lamborghini Espada. “I guess here’s as good as any place to conduct the investigation,” Collins mumbled, moving into the garage. “Investigation?” Lavelle questioned. “Mr. Lavelle, are you familiar with a man named Tony Rush?” Mastriani asked, piling through the wet manila file. Lavelle nodded his head and leant back onto the hood of the Viper. “Yeah, we met last year.” “Have you had any contact with this man in the last year?” Collins questioned. “Not since October ’05 when he was the Agent on the Harrington case,” Lavelle responded matter-of-factly. “Have you heard anything from him recently?” Mastriani asked, running through protocol. “No. What is this about?” Lavelle now questioned, coyly. Collins looked toward him, shaking off his soaked LAPD cap. “Just some routine questioning, Mr. Lavelle.” “Has something happened to Tony?” Lavelle questioned Mastriani, ignoring the other detective. Mastriani put the file back under her arm and looked up toward the man before her. “The Agent has gone under the radar and we are just tying up some loose ends.” “You mean he’s missing and you have no clue where he is…”Lavelle responded shrilly, turning toward his car. “If you hear anything, do let us know,” Collins said and thanked Lavelle for his time as the two left. Sliding back into his car, Lavelle left for Dietetics Pharmaceuticals as he slid the Detectives card into the visor and now began to wonder where Tony Rush was… Dietetics Pharmaceuticals had an eerie air about it as another day had become engulphed in maintaining the running for the finance and support of the social welfare system and the endowments of many hospitals. Moore was ladled with a dumbfounded workload, his eyes a blistering red and his head pounding as he rose from his desk. His phone began to ring endlessly, each time, Rachel Lang, secretary and assistant to the company would answer and soon become immersed in yellow post-its. “The world’s gone mad!” Paige cried from the accounting desk as his calculator finally ran out of battery life. “Is there any way we could keep Mercy?” Moore questioned, losing all sense of hope. Paige lifted up a bundle of pages, stacked to the reams with numbers and calculations. “Let’s see, NO!” Paige answered, glumly. “How about we check those calculations again?” Moore said, wistfully. The door to the sixth floor opened and Lavelle emerged into a world of frenzy, worry and mass hysteria as people charged around him, phones and computers working aimlessly to tend to the many needs being asked. Moore walked around his table and greeted Lavelle with a tight, pinching handshake and weary smile. “Mr. Lavelle, welcome aboard,” he said, smugly. “Thanks, Moore.” Lavelle acknowledge the rest of his staff and moved toward Rachel Lang who was in the middle of opening another packet of yellow post-its. “Ms. Lang, would you mind holding all calls, we’re going to have a meeting.” She smiled and took the phone off the hook, finding relief in the mild silence offered. Lavelle nodded and Moore led them all into a conference room. It was cold and quiet, but almost therapeutic to those who were now convulsing with migraine and sore throats. “Where are we on the Mercy Hospital?” Lavelle asked, sliding into a chair and placing a bundle of documents in front of him. “So far, Warner Enterprises has merged with GCC to give a hefty sum to the hospital,” Moore said, bleary eyed and tired, his voice was hoarse when he spoke. “How much money are we talking?” Lavelle asked, flipping through the accountants books. “We estimate that GCC and WE have given their lawyers handled by, Lawler and Sons, the better part of 200 million to deal with the hospital,” Paige said with a stern and unwarranted tone, as he slid a sheet of graph paper containing estimations. Lang took out another couple of documents and slid them to Lavelle who took them with a smile and proceeded to listen to Moore and Paige. “Dr. Hoffman has refused to answer any offers coming from this firm,” Lang said in a desultory tone. “Our best guess is he’s gone with Lawler,” Moore continued. “How much did my grandfather offer Hoffman and Mercy?” Lavelle questioned. “100 million dollars, but Hoffman refused,” Paige said as he recapped the documentation. “Can we afford to up the price to 250 million dollars?” Lavelle asked. “The Lawler-Hoffman project is more of a political agenda than a social welfare benefit,” Moore said. Lavelle nodded his head. “I know, but I want to help the hospital, not aid Hoffman’s rise to mayor.” “Afraid that’s just politics,” Moore responded, slumping into his seat and removing his glasses. “What if we cut some liabilities and luxury’s and concentrated on the Hoffman merger?” Lavelle pondered. Paige was sat in silence holding a HB pencil and a new calculator as he began hurriedly working while the others spoke. Moore’s eyes began to drift and Lavelle was engrossed in documents when Paige shrieked with excitement. “I think we can sink the bastards,” Paige exclaimed. “We can afford to raise the price?” Lavelle asked, hopefully. “We can do better, my friend, we can buy the hospital.” Paige said and Moore looked up, shaking his head as he began to speak. “Paige, you’re delirious.” “No, it’s a perfectly viable exchange,” Paige retorted, sliding the sheet of paper across. Moore’s eyes widened as he read the estimated figures and the luxury cuts. “How will that be possible? The hospitals falling to bits and the price you want to put into it…it’s a goddamn liability.” Moore’s words rang through the room and Lavelle sat back and thought for a moment. Rachel Lang was busy browsing through the computer to see if Hoffman and Lawler had merged, but she shook her head, it gave Lavelle and his team time. “What if we took about 50 million off the buying price and put it toward updating the hospital.” “It’s the only hospital without a great surgical staff,” Moore said, shaking his head. “I’m a surgeon,” Lavelle retorted, placing both elbows onto the ebony table. Moore and Paige beamed toothy smiles. “This could be the straw to break the camels back,” Moore said. “It’s a pipe dream, Lavelle, but it might just work.” Lavelle smiled. “Thanks.” “I’ll run up some estimation and get on the phone with Mercy’s head doctors.” Paige said before scuttling out of the room, shortly followed by Rachel Lang. Moore and Lavelle rose together, shaking hands once more and exiting into the main floor, still a frenzy of activity. “Enjoying your first day?” Moore asked, patting Lavelle on the back. Lavelle shrugged. “Definitely different.” “You can say that again…”Moore joked and pressed the button for the elevator. “I’m going to head out and get up to speed on Dr. Hoffman and Warner Enterprises.” Lavelle spoke with a direct tone as he slid into the elevator followed by Moore who held a Styrofoam empty cup. “Coffee is on the 3rd floor,” he said with a smile and waved the empty cup. Having said goodbye to Moore on the third floor, Lavelle rode the elevator till it reached its final destination. The ground floor of Dietetics pharmaceuticals was like a glass house, with black tints on the windows. The black marble floor shone reflections against the metal columns along the room. The security desk was silent, abandoned. Lavelle soon understood why as a crowd of media had gathered outside. A tall and beefy security inspector moved toward Lavelle, his footsteps quickening along the marbled floor. “Mr. Lavelle, I’m Reynard Walker, head of security,” he said swiftly, moving Lavelle to one side. “Nice to meet you, Mr. Walker, but I was just leaving…” Lavelle spoke, attempting to side step the rotund man. “I’m sorry, Mr. Lavelle, but we’re just making the media leave…If you could hold on one moment.” Walker was stern but forthcoming as he spoke into his walkie-talkie and directed the flow of removal. Lavelle watched the various vans begin to pack-up – CNN, FOX, CNBC – each more disappointed than the other. “What exactly do they want?” Lavelle questioned, shyly. “To ask about the death of your grandfather,” Walker muttered. “I see…” There was a crackle on the walkie-talkie and then a voice announced the all clear. Walker stepped aside and motioned toward the swiveling doors. “Have a good day, Sir,” he said. Lavelle nodded and entered into the outside. The rain had stopped and the smell of wet gravel and trees were apparent as he crossed the small gravel entrance and moved toward his car. By his car stands a woman, gracefully stood next to the hood. Dressed in a suit and skirt, her hair tied back nonchalantly but with a gallant and beautiful wisp that accentuates her facial features, she speaks softly, extending her hand. “Mr. Lavelle, my name’s Hailey Rose, I work for the USA today as a reporter…” “Look, I’m not in the mood for questions.” Lavelle said with a knowing nod and opened his car door. “This is off the record stuff. I just wanted to apologize for the frenzied rumors about your grandfather. Reporters are vermin,” she said. “Then why are…?” “It pays well,” she said with a sweet smile and extended her card. “My interest is purely in the Hoffman merger. I’m a political reporter. If you ever want to talk to me, you’ve got my card.” Moving away slowly with a swagger in her step she slid into a black Lexus and swiftly drove away. Lavelle stood in the abandoned parking lot and gazed toward her card with a smile. His mobile rang with a vibrating tone as he flicked it open. “Hey!” … “You’re here?” … “Yeah I’ll pick you up…How does dinner and a movie sound?” … To be continued…
