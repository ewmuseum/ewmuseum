Running Along the Mobius Strip (Part 2)

18 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 18/7/06 I had developed somewhat of a maddening fever in the living entombment. The temperature swelled to unbearable degrees at times, and my stomach periodically rejected the little it held inside in painful expulsions. The oozing had no particular preference of escape, and it dripped from my orifices indiscriminately. Even the wax melted out of my ears. I began to collect it in a sluggishly growing pile, hoping to eventually have enough to start developing a system of candles. It is altogether an amazing thing, the human body, as with careful inventory, one can survive on his own unnecessary insides for quite some time. As the body may rejuvenate itself through cellular reparation, systems of self-recycling may be successfully implemented under meticulous supervision. Another side effect of the fever was the disorienting hallucinations it produced. Fortunately, I felt confident in my ability to distinguish my imaginations from my reality. The insufficient light I had grown used to must have eliminated color from my interpretable capabilities, and when color did come into view, it seemed reliably indicative of hallucination. The reintroduction of color, though, as it existed within my manifestations, was a strange and foreign sense to me; one that was disconnected from the simple decoding of visual information. I couldn’t reasonably tell you which colors I had seen in the falsified imagery that contended my pragmatic assessment and maintenance of this delicate balance which allowed me to continue living. Perhaps some of these colors were from an alternate spectrum. They were all relative based on difference, each carrying their own hidden connotations. The auditory hallucinations, though, chipped away at the skulled shell that cages the mind from dissipating among the rest of the connected molecular energy that bound everything together with less than microscopic gaps. I realized that these infinitesimal synapses were not entirely porous absences of matter, but that the concept of nothing was a limit that was approached but never reached. That would be laughably impossible, you see, as these theoretical holes were constantly filled with moving things that flowed quickly and interchangeably. With all outside noise barred by earthen insulation, I could hear everything around me. Thus, it was far from unreasonable that the sound of worms chewing was clearly audible. I began, then, to pick up on distinct patterns and repetitions; variances in the intensity and frequency of their wormy mastication. The language in itself was not too dissimilar from Chinese, and after my prolonged habitation with the worms, I would imagine that I could learn Chinese fluently in a matter of days if I ever escaped. From what I could make of it at the time, the worms had nothing interesting to say. They simply repeated whether they were hungry or tired or needed to shit. With practice, I could reciprocate my digestive needs as well, but this seemed to do nothing beneficial. It was upon me to develop the language of the worms by first alerting them of my presence and communicability. I would then have to assess whether or not the worms were capable of building vocabulary. This all seemed like a daunting, tedious task, but the payoff could mean the difference between eternal entrapment and freedom. Hungrily, yet also begrudgingly, I picked up a worm between my thumb and forefinger and sucked it between my lips. It writhed a bit in my mouth, but I had grown used to it. Initially, there is something innately unsatisfying in the feeling of your prey still wriggling in your throat. I had grown so used to eating things that were dead and cooked that I had found myself jaded to the whole enterprise of eating. Everything tastes like chicken, they say. And then they chuckle because a well timed cliché eases tension in many interpersonal situations. However, there is a difference between a live chicken and the food that when slaughtered, skinned, packaged, and burnt, is referred to as chicken. The two bear little resemblance to each other, and without the trust that mankind puts in product labels, processed chicken and turkey, sliced into round, flesh colored circles are easily mistaken for one another. However, both animals in their living forms have very distinct qualities to separate one from being the other. Thus, everything does not in fact taste like chicken; not even what we have come to know as chicken. Furthermore, I came to despise lunch meats and all processed foods. It wasn’t the taste- no- I fondly recalled oven roasted turkey on toasted wheat with all the fixings or ham and Swiss cheese, alternately stacked on top of each other in sandwiches that towered domineeringly over children and small animals. There was just something unsettling in pimento loaf; something that spread infectiously to the surrounding meats. It was like a nuclear relative but one that was not distant enough for my own personal comfort. Even the smallest taint of pimento blood could ruin a perfectly good sandwich. You see, the thing that bothered me the most about the said loaf was the unnaturalness of it all. Unless there was some majestic pig-beast with green olives embedded underneath the skin, all I could think of was a vat of semi-fluid animal grounds being stirred in a large, grey vat; a bulky, balding, Eastern European woman stirring in mounds of olives with a rowboat oar. The plasma would then be left in the sun to harden before being sliced and packaged and distributed to supermarkets all over the country. To add to my uneasiness, I have never actually seen anyone buy or own any pimento loaf, so all the shit that hangs on grocery store hooks, next to the sausages and above the individually wrapped cheeses, has undoubtedly been there since 1980-something. And that’s what went through my head as I started chewing on the worm, ceasing it’s squirming. As the worm’s flavor filled my mouth, I found peace in the satisfaction it felt before death. The human mouth is a dark, warm, moist place; a place where worms thrive, and a place like this humid hole. Of course, the worm still tasted disagreeable because anyone who has ever dissected a worm can tell you that a worm is basically just a living tube full of dirt and shit. I didn’t so much as eat the worm as I chewed it, trying to learn the language of the other worms. The others, however, weren’t very responsive, so I eventually swallowed the crushed worm tube and its intestinal contents, wondering what a live chicken actually tasted like compared to a dead one…or a turkey, or an ostrich. *** After a very disillusioning night, I woke up in the basement where a cot had been laid out for me. The mattress on the damn thing was stiffer than the floor, so I ended up sleeping on the floor underneath the cot so as to quell my anger. The cot, you see, was a flagrant impostor, existing under the guise of being a suitable place for sleeping whereas the tile floor didn’t try to deceive me with niceties like sheets and pillows. Besides, there was something familiarly comforting about the claustrophobic lack of distance between the floor and the cot. The irony hadn’t occurred to me that I was guilty of being just as fraudulent as the cot I was under while I masqueraded as Jesus Christ. I knew neither who he was nor who I was at the time, though, so technically there was a chance that I could have been him, albeit I knew that chance was also marginally slim. This whole self-identification malfunction, however, seemed irrelevant now as my slumber was unwillingly disrupted by the choral voices that waved muffled from the chapel upstairs. It is difficult to reconcile yourself from a sleep so tiresomely gained. In the brief cache of my memory, I hadn’t once slept, though I had fruitlessly tried on several occasions. The lack of success led me to believe that perhaps I had invented the whole concept of sleep, and the slow parabola of melting time that fused its way into my joints as the momentum of things declined en masse around me was just the last of my blood coagulating in some sort of precedent to rigor mortis. The fact that I had waken up was momentarily reassuring in that it differentiated sleep from death. I slept without dreaming, and an eternal dreamless sleep is a concept frightening enough for man to invent grandiose alternatives worth fighting to protect; worth even killing to protect. The moment of reassurances, though, had passed, and I had let my mind wander again for too long to return to the irresponsible blankness between the bottom of the cot and the top of the tile floor, where I shifted out from underneath in motions and shapes like the ticking intervals of a second hand, engineering its dizzy path around the face of some numberless clock with no distinct top or bottom so that it can’t surely tell the time. Still, I ticked, with or without reference, out from the shade of my unbending canopy to venture upstairs and examine the perturbing song that broke my peacefully sleeping silence. No sooner, though, than I had gotten up had the choir rested, but already stirring, I continued towards the stairs and watched from the top step, craning my neck around a wall made of large bricks, generously painted white so that small drips had solidified upon drying. The man whom I mistook for the butler stood overlooking the tired, the desperate, and the obligated who sat quietly and uniformly, feigning interest in his heavily gesticulated outbursts; mostly feigning it to themselves. The man at the altar had eventually introduced himself as Father Pendleton. I never quite understood why. As far as I was concerned, we didn’t share much of a family resemblance, but he still insisted upon being referred to as such. I figured that it was just some zany, psychosexual deviance I would simply have to deal with. “It has been brought to the public eye that Lucifer has been tempting the youth of this community as well as the heartland of rural America with a vice called methamphetamine.” The men all wore white shirts with drab ties. They combed the remains of their hair back and trimmed their mustaches neatly. Their wives all wore dresses with flower patterns; patterns you’d see on tablecloths. I imagined that they were the types of people who covered their furniture in plastic wrap to keep their children from shedding on the couches or accidentally urinating on the upholstery with their insufficient bladder control. It was the fashion here to stare blankly forward and nod solemnly, not fully processing what Father Pendleton was saying being far too busy concentrating on nodding the most solemnly as if it were some kind of solemn nodding competition. “And the Lord, our God, has commanded that it is a sin to distract the mind with such a vice that can put the mind at risk and put thoughts at risk of straying away from the will of the Lord.” And as the solemn nodding continued, the bobbing of heads seemed to synchronize in some sort of natural phenomenon. The bobbing became metronomic, cycling self-perpetuated in a chicken and egg conundrum in which each head individually was no longer fueled consciously by its owner. Instead, each head nodded on because of the nodding heads around it. None could stop under its own power, and I had to strain my neck to still my head from joining the hypnotic trance dance. “Hell has a place of its own for those who turn away from God and give into Lucifer’s medicine. Those who find themselves addicted to anything other than God’s Truth and God’s Love above all else will also find themselves damned eternally to perpetual darkness where the lost wander blindly, feeling for an exit through hellfire that melts the flesh.” My neck started twitching violently, and I feared the uncontrollable bobbing; I feared it would never stop and I’d be doomed to a state of perpetual nodding. I grasped my head tightly, both hands pressed around my ears, but soon the spasms took over, and my body began swaying to compensate for the stillness of my head as my midsection jutted forward and backward. “But there is an exit from eternal damnation, and that exit is repentance. Are there any newcomers here, any brave souls, who will renounce a life of temptation and ask for forgiveness? I know that nobody is perfect, but God wants you to let Him into your heart and ask for forgiveness. And He will forgive you and pluck you from the darkness and the hellfire.” My body was now convulsing violently, and I was growing increasingly paranoid as to the condition of my pelvic tremors. Clasping my head with a vice-like grip I shakily walked to the altar, thrusting my hips forward and my posterior backwards with clockwork alternation until I was standing next to Father Pendleton, dancing in uncomfortable angles with my head and feet parallel to each other and my midsection pushing and retracting across the imaginary longitudinal line of my normally upright form. “Glorious! Now introduce yourself to the congregation, your fellow brethren, with God now guiding your heart. For today, your blindness will be lifted and your impurities will be cleansed.” And I shakily turned towards the crowd of nodding men and flower-patterned women, my pelvis now thrusting in and out towards them. “Hello, um, my name is Jesus. Jesus Christ.” For a moment, I thought it had worked. I thought I had been cured as the solemn nodding stopped and everyone sat motionless and awed. When the nodding stopped, my thrusting ceased shortly after, and I turned to Father Pendleton with wide joyous eyes as God had obviously answered my desperate plea to stop the incessant jutting and place me back in control of my faculties. Father Pendleton returned locked eyes. His brows angled downward to a furious scowl. His teeth grinded against each other behind his twitching lip. “Blasphemer!” he pointed towards the large oak doors. “How dare you show such arrogant disrespect in this, the most sacred of places? Leave this place. Leave at once, and do not return.” I was confused and dejected as the elation flushed out of my face. I walked down the aisle between the rows of pews as the women, pale faced, couldn’t bear to look at me, and the men with trimmed mustaches now discouragingly shook their heads- shook their heads at me as if they had forgotten all about the solemn nodding! I pushed through the heavy oak doors and shielded my eyes from the sun shining at its brightest. *** I had mastered the monosyllabic worm-song, but much to my dismay, I found that the worms were only capable of the most basic communication to satisfy only the most rudimentary of needs. None of the worms seemed to take to my attempts to make casual conversation. They knew nothing of the world outside having not been exposed to it. The dirt was all they knew, and it sufficed for them. My attempts to explain the sky and the sea and the stars to them were all for naught. I even sensed an air of dissention in the chewing of the worms, as if the world above that I tried to describe to them was nothing more than some fantastic invention of my imagination. Their claims to food and vociferations of needs grew ever the more condescending. I could tell that none of them believed me, and furthermore, none of them wanted anything to do with me. No, all I had now were the flies, as I confined myself to one of the corners of the room. I could never understand the flies. I fucking hated them, buzzing around from here to there seemingly without any direction or landing on your arm and smugly rubbing their tiny fly hands. They were planning something, I concluded. What pained me the most about their plans, however, was that they were designed without ultimate intent, somewhat ingeniously crafted without ambition. They had no desire to destroy mankind and take over even though they were capable with all the diseases they carried and their stealth; their goddamned insufferable stealth! It was quite simple, really. They aimed to drive me mad with their airborne darting and their indefinable buzzes. This is how they passed their time. This is how the sick bastards entertained themselves- whirring and buzzing noisily around me, irritatingly landing on me to rub their malcontented little hands at me; the sons of bitches! But as the flies circled around me, I remained cross-legged and unflinching with my arms shaking under the weight of my heavy head, cradled in my hands. My head was unbearably weighty because of the thoughts within as they crumbled and sunk. I began to doubt the existence of the world above as it began to seem impossible that something so magnificent and complex could stem from the reality around me- dirt and insects. I began too, to question my own identity. I had crafted an image of myself in relation to the other beings and structures that may or may not have existed before I found myself here. I started to lose my sense of self, and even my sense of humanity. For what I had known to be human did not exist here outside of my own self-perception, which was being slowly unraveled without any reassurances towards my own appearance. I had long since lost sensation with the unintentional neglect upon my sense of touch. The nerve endings in my limbs, numbing and dying from being unused, had shriveled and weakened. No longer could I feel my arms and legs as I had remembered. Instead, I began to feel my whole existence confined into one tubular mass that dangled from my head. “Preposterous!” I chewed out. And then I chuckled. I had been down here for quite some time, living on a minimalist diet of whatever scrap I could find in my confinement. I had even resorted to feasting on hallucinations, thinking I could fool my way to satiation. And then, the gravity of things began to set in. My body was at war with my mind. The body is the serf of self-perception, you see, and if I started thinking I was a worm, my genetic coding would soon untwist, helix by helix, and rearrange itself into the coding of a worm. I would have to hold onto the crazy invention I believed was outside for as long as I could. I would have to believe in it so strongly that I could convince the worms that it existed, too. And even more tediously, I would have to invent more. I would have to create an even greater facsimile in my mind of something that did not yet exist; reconstruct what it was to be human; improve upon what some God or some scientist had mapped out until I could regain whatever amount of strength it took to break free from the simple, torturous device that I had somehow landed in. Without any resource or instruction, it seemed the only way out. I sat down again, not knowing whether my eyes were open or shut because it didn’t matter. I thought in reverse, trying to rewind what little I had left of my memory back to nothing. This in itself was a confusing task because of the way memory gets distorted, exaggerated in grandiose fashion, colors becoming brighter, things becoming larger, emotions glowing warmer. I had to generalize a lot of things that existed before I was born- things that I had seen on television or in photographs or that I had read or heard of in stories. As I remembered back before me, it was funny how the colors changed, as if someone were messing with the tint settings in my brain as if they were adjusting levels television set. The decades changed, at first somewhat blurred with the resurfacing of colors like olive green and burnt orange; drab, tacky things with the absence of the brightness of primary color. And then earlier in the century, crisp blacks and whites and the grays in between, because that’s what I’d seen in the photographs of an era where people read newspapers because there was shit else to do. And before that, there was no color simply because people didn’t like color. They thought it might anger God or something. A century ago, color was disrespectful to elders, who were nothing like the incontinent, buffet-eating, denture gel generation today who shuffleboard away their last few grains of sanity before their 2:00 pm suppers and pre-sleep Matlock. Before shuffleboard and Matlock, all old people had to do was sit in rocking chairs, smack their gums, and curse the thunderstorms for flaring up their arthritic joints. So here you had a bunch of misshapen, old mother fuckers with disproportional, oversized joints sitting in rocking chairs while Mother Nature ravaged furiously about with nothing to do but wait for death with scowling invitation. And then color returned, centuries ago, back in Europe where it was a distinguished badge worn only by the gentry, mainly because there were no photographs from the time to prove otherwise. The politics of color, however, were interrupted by an impending, familiar rumbling. Stirred from my meditations, I braced myself, holding onto the walls beside me as the ground shook and turned, and there I was again facing the shadow of a head staring down upon me. “Let me out!” I cried, the simplest of my demands inundating the population of questions that had settled in my head. “Let me the fuck out!” but it was in the chewing language of the worms, and I struggled to find the words and they sounded like a babble of noise- jumbled vowels- when I tried forming them in human ways. And then the room turned over again, as I started crying. I felt the hot salt of tears stream down my face and catch in my unshaven, unattended beard. “Fuck!” I yelled, the room stilling and settling. “Fuck!” in clear, conceivable English. But nobody could hear me beneath all this fucking dirt as the echo quaked around me and the harsh friction of consonants foamed to a static. *** I exited the church with nothing but a base knowledge of a place called Hell, a vague clue about kids on methamphetamine, a conspicuous false identity, and a pack of hand rolled Amsterdam Shag cigarettes. I knew that somehow, all of this was connectable. I could feel in my bones. I hadn’t yet developed a sense of cardinal direction, and using the sun’s positioning and the perceived time of day, I figured that a place as hot as Hell would have to undoubtedly be somewhere south based on seasonal tilt of the earth and a variety of other factors that would influence the possibility for such combustible conditions as never ending fields of fire. After some quick calculations based on several mathematical principles too complicated for you to understand and thus, not worth explaining, I decided that the existence of such a place was laughably unfeasible. This left me recalculating my navigational plans as I balanced thoroughness against inefficiency in terms of walking from the center into a growing spiral until I found some clue as to where I could find these meth kids or this Lucifer guy who lives in the fiery rubble of his exploded meth lab as I assume he invented this methamphetamine shit. Logically, this explains why Hell is on fire all of the time. Disregarding the lack of continuity in my memory, I vaguely recalled some news story about how ingredients could be extracted from over-the-counter drugs like caffeine pills or cough medicine. I wasn’t sure which, and I wasn’t sure where the nearest convenient store was anyway. I was too apprehensive to ask anyone for help as my recent experiences with interaction had all ended up landing me as some sort of pariah, left to wander around aimlessly; unwelcome and unsociable. I sat down on the steps leading up to the church and smoked my Amsterdam Shag, watching people walk back and forth, none of them acknowledging one another except in awkward periods of passing where the sidewalk would become too crowded and all parties involved would worriedly redirect their paths in zigzags to avert disastrous collision. This intrigued and unsettled me, the cold repulsion of one man towards another. I wanted to see people collide or congeal into one mass so that everything wasn’t so fucking fragmented and separate. I wanted to find someone who wouldn’t repel me or be repelled by me, even just so I could find out where the fuck I could go to get some goddamned caffeine pills and cough syrup, but everybody must have been filled with fucking positively charged magnets or something. After discarding the cigarette butt into some nearby hedges, I stood up and walked in one direction, the most nagging part of my indecision informing me to go in the opposite direction. I had counted out about fifty paces before I couldn’t ignore it anymore, so I turned around, calmed, and continued back past the church and another fifty paces in the opposite direction before the thought hit me that maybe my first instinct was correct after all and I turned around again. It hadn’t occurred to me until I saw the church crowd exit in one dissipating drove that I was only pacing in growing pendulum oscillations back and forth across a straight line that I was making no progress whatsoever. I noticed Father Pendleton standing at the church doors, shaking hands and saying farewells to his flock and quickly dove to the ground, shielded by some of the hedges to avoid his notice. But the sleepy Sunday morning gatherers nearly trampled me to death, their eyes pointed straight forward, like daggers, towards their destinations. They hadn’t noticed me, or pretended not to, beneath their feet, and after they passed and sufficient time had been accounted for Father Pendleton’s disappearance back through those large oak doors, I stood back up, now covered in footprints. There was something discouraging, though, about being a stampede victim. My legs were tired from walking anyway, so I sat back down in the middle of the sidewalk and stretched my body out as the occasional straggler walked around me startled. I pretended not to notice anyone walking around me as they passed, and I ended up staring at the sidewalk for a matter of time, looking for discrete patterns that lie within to perhaps give me some guidance. Instead, all I saw were ants, so I figured I’d just lie there forever, and humankind could block me off with barricades or build a wall around me for all I cared. I’d lie in wait like a fucking Venus fly trap, and if someone tried to rouse me from my spot, I’d snap my jaws, either figuratively or literally depending on how I felt when the time came. That’s right. Either I’d find someone who could tell me which direction to go, or I’d literally fucking eat someone. *** I swayed back and forth between hope and despair, my meditations constantly interrupted by my insecurities, and my insecurities interrupted by my will in an inescapable triangle. I had managed to remember my way far back before I ever existed, right to the point where history breaks off and theories from the distant future clashed. But then, I was overcome by the problem that I couldn’t piece together what happened from the several incomplete theories, and they all melded into a state of confusion where I was left retracing my steps many times over. And so, I gave up, for the time being, unable to push back any further and too tired to reverse the mind’s momentum and start moving back forward again. I sat staring through a virtual blank page, seeing right through as the room lit up in front of me, revealing the same dirt walls with the same insects crawling around, growing ever the more populous from interbreeding now. There were flying worms and worming flies, and a thousand different dialects of chewing and buzzing intermingled. It had gotten to the point where the worms had forgotten all about my previous ramblings, and the life was so diverse in the hole that I could just have easily been one of the insects. I became numb again in all of my appendages save one which up until now, limped in a state of dormancy. In the colorless dim, the strange reality of the situation had me confusing my hallucination and reality with my imagination tying the two together, but if I wasn’t mistaken, there was a worm right near me, presenting herself to me. Her tiny worm end wavered towards me with the most carnal of propositions, and to be honest, I wasn’t sure which end it was that was, in fact, wavering. I was a bit apprehensive at first, as I had heard stories somewhere about the female mantis devouring her male counterpart after copulation, but I was every bit as desperate. I felt the blood rushing quickly around my body in completely new ways, detouring towards my stiffening core. There was something irresistible about her feminine wiles as her hind end sultrily swayed in the dim. “Take me,” she purred seductively with unbridled libido emanating from the scent of her chewing. “I would, but, uh, I don’t quite know to go about this,” I chewed back, rather unsurely as I removed what was left of my pants. I had eaten off most of the material on the legs and found that clothing was surprisingly edible. What it lacked in flavor, it made up for in quantity as a patch of denim here or there would often be enough to satisfy my aching stomach. “Here,” she offered as her wavering end began to swell and a circular orifice spread open, pointed fleshy enclosures separating from the center and breathing like the straw hole in a soft drink lid. “Trust me.” “I’ve never, you know, with a worm. Are you sure this will work? The physics seems a bit off.” My apparent doubt surfaced, but the hole gaped wider, like an eyeless socket with the lids pulled back. “Trust me.” “I, uh, well…” I knew I was defeated. The absurdity of defense deflated any and every logical argument that crossed my mind at the time, and there I was, fumbling my way in like I was some thirteen year old losing his virginity, except without the whole pubescent excitement thing. I felt uneasy at first, in plain view of the other insects that still buzzed around me, landing on me with their damned rubbing hands. Couldn’t they respect that this situation was a bit awkward for me as it was? It was initially uncomfortable and even a slight bit painful, but the pain was drowned out readily by the worm noises of copulation that took some getting used to. The chewing sounds had been replaced by some strange swallowing sound; more accurately, the sound of someone swallowing a mouthful of glass and tinfoil. And then, as the pain and comfort subsided, I felt a moment of purity, my body tensing quickly, a burst of warm seizures, and a final relaxation as I slouched backwards, my companion peeling off from around me like a surgical glove as her hind end slowly eased back into its normal state, the orifice inhaling back to a tiny point like a bright light shrinking a pupil. It was over quickly, and the worm slithered off as I shrunk back against the dirt wall wondering exactly what had just happened. I reached into my pocket for a post-coital cigarette and lit it, the flickering flame exposing the faces of bugs- all sorts of slimy things- staring towards me and chewing and buzzing after some sort of mating ritual. I froze, half in terror and half still contemplating this euphoric buzz, puffing my way through the cigarette to level myself out with the nicotine. And when I was done, I lit another cigarette with the end of the last one because I still needed time to come to terms with what just happened, positive that it would take some time to recover from the gravity of reality as it set in despite every strained attempt to shake it away.
