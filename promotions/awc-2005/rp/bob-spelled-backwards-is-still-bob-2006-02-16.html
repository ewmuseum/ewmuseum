BOB....Spelled Backwards is still...BOB

16 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-16-06 It was the day after the shooting. The clock on the wall read 8a.m. Teresa reclined in a chair next to her brother’s hospital bed. A nurse entered to take Eric’s vitals and left as quietly as she came in. The beeping of the heart monitor resting on the other side of the bed was the only noise in the room besides Teresa’s ragged breaths as she slept. Someone else entered the room. A tall figure shadowed over Teresa, watching her sleep for a long moment then gently placed a masculine hand on her shoulder, his voice smooth. “Teresa, wake up.” A harsh groan exited Teresa’s throat. She swatted at the hand then blinked as she waked. Once her eyes focused and the figure standing before her became clear, they widened in surprise. She sat up and tried to swallow but her throat was too dry. “Bob…how..how..did you know?” The man smiled softly, pulled up a chair, and sat next to her. His green eyes moved to Eric and he sighed. “He came by the pawn shop after you left. Apparently, he was following you. I didn’t know where you were meeting Darrel. All I told him was that you came by asking about a shot gun.” Teresa nodded. She gently took her brother’s hand and rubbed her thumb in the space between his forefinger and thumb. He had not awakened since the incident and the nurses gave him enough sedatives to keep him out for a few more days. She began to regret borrowing the five grand from Bob to pay Darrel’s debt. Maybe then, Eric wouldn’t be lying in a hospital bed with a bullet hole in his stomach. “The doctor says he’ll be alright. It’ll just be a long while before he heals. Bob, this is all my fault. If I didn’t..” Her words cut short when someone else entered the room. His big build, work boots, sleeveless flannel shirt, and camouflage cap said all too well that it’s Teresa’s cousin Darrel. A solemn expression set on his rough unshaven face. Teresa looked up. The stare she gave him could put a hole through a boulder. She stood allowing her brother’s hand slip out of hers. Darrel glanced past Teresa to Bob who remained in his chair mouthing the words “GET OUT.” However, it was too late. Teresa had already shortened the distance between her and Darrel and with her fist, threw a brutal punch to his face. Her fist made contact with his nose instantly causing it to bleed. Darrel screamed and stumbled back. “Teresa! Please, I called the ambulance for Christ’s sake. I stayed with you until you went to the hospital. I’m just here to check on Eric.” Teresa growled through clenched teeth and shoved Darrel against the wall. A nurse and a security officer arrived just in time for Bob to bolt out of his chair and grab Teresa by the arms. This didn’t stop Teresa from yelling at Darrel. “Damn you Darrel! Damn you to Hell! It’s your fault Eric is laying in that bed with a hole in his stomach. It should’ve been you!” The security officer stepped in-between Teresa and her cousin. He was a slender man but solid. He stood 6’5 and his military cut auburn hair made him appear intimidating. “I’m afraid you’ll have to leave Miss. Your outbursts are not only disturbing the other patients, but your brother as well.” Teresa shot a cold stare a Darrel whole the petite red headed nurse tended to his busted nose. She escorted him out of the room and into another. To the security officer, she sneered. Bob loosened his grip on her arms, but didn’t let go. He then leaned down and whispered softly into her ear. “Come. You’ve had a rough night. Let me take you somewhere where you can have a smoke and perhaps a shot of whiskey. Together, they will help to calm your nerves.” Two Winston Lights, five shots of whiskey, and half an hour later, Teresa was calm. She sat on a brown leather sofa in the back room of Bob’s pawnshop. In a Lazy Boy across the room sat Bob. His concerned expression fixed on Teresa as she finally neglected the shot glass and took to the bottle itself. “Feeling better?” “Yeah. What now?” “You’re drunk” “I know. I don’t feel a thing.” “Always drink like this?” “Just when I’m stressed.” “When aren’t you stressed?” “When I’m drunk.” Bob ran his long fingers through his short red hair. He needed her to make a delivery in the morning but with a hangover, she may not be able to do it. He rose from the recliner and sat beside the drunken woman nursing a half-drunk bottle of whiskey. “Listen Teresa..” He carefully took the whiskey bottle from her hand. Teresa reached for it as he placed the bottle on the floor at his feet, but Bob grabbed her hands and squeezed them gently as he shook his head. “You don’t need so much booze. It’s not good for you.” Teresa tried to pull her hands out of Bob’s but he refused to let hers go. Her drunken baby blue eyes narrowed and she opened her mouth to speak but he cut in. “You almost lost your brother and you want to kill your cousin for it. Drinking until you can’t feel the pain isn’t going to help you. Let me help you. As long as I’m around, you’ll be taken care of and you won’t need the whiskey for comfort.” He watched Teresa for a reaction. He didn’t expect much considering her intoxicated state of mind. Teresa raised her eyebrows then gave a drunken smile. Her eyes then closed and her body fell forward into Bob’s chest passed out from exhaustion and too much booze.
