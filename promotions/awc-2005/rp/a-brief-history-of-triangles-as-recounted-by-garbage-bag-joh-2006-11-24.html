A Brief History of Triangles as Recounted by Garbage Bag Johnny

24 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 241106 Most of the triangles I knew had three sides. I had heard somewhere that all of them were supposed to have three sides, but I wasn’t convinced that this was the case. I believe it was Aristotle who invented the triangle, and the version he invented was just a standard equilateral triangle. Since then, other scientists have expounded upon his work, and to date, there are a few more kinds of triangles that have been put into other sorts of classifications. Those who have researched this particular geometrical figure have focused primarily on two specific ways to split the general concept of the triangle into more exclusive subdivisions. The equality of side length is one of the factors that determines a triangle into a certain archetype. The original triangle, the equilateral triangle, was found with three sides of matching length. While the equilateral triangle is also part of the isosceles family, there do exist many isosceles triangles that have only two equal sides, altering the length of a third side that may be either longer or shorter than either of the two other sides. The final triangle as determined by side length is the scalene triangle. The scalene triangle has no equal sides because it is a disorganized mess and a son of a bitch. I, for one, see no application for the scalene triangle in today’s civilized society and have long advocated outlawing scalene triangles altogether. Since the categorizing of triangles by the differing numbers of equal sides, however, scientists have devised another method of differentiating between triangles by measuring the largest angle formed by the merger of two adjacent sides. It has been highly documented that no one triangle shall have any more or less than a sum of one hundred and eighty degrees between all of its angles, but I know for a fact that there are loopholes in this so called mathematical law that the federal government have purposely tacked on as riders concerning land appropriation. In any case, the right triangle has garnered most of the fame from the triangles that are classified by largest angle. In a right triangle, the largest angle is a ninety degree angle formed by two perpendicular sides. The right triangle has been subjected to disproportionate amounts of mathematical analysis with experts being specifically interested to the unique hypotenuse that such a triangle has. I’ve heard that scientists in the Netherlands are trying to develop an equilateral right triangle, but such an idea is far too progressive to ever catch on in the Western Hemisphere. The conservatives would band together and call it immoral, and support from a suspicious rural constituency would ensure that the equilateral right triangle would never be legal in the states. Other than the right triangle, though, there are the obtuse and acute triangles. The obtuse triangle has at least one angle that surpasses ninety degrees. Some triangles diagnosed with mental retardation have several obtuse angles. This occurs with high levels of exposure to radioactive material. On the other hand, the acute triangle has no angle in excess of eighty nine degrees. With such small angles, acute triangles have never been held in high regard save for the equilateral triangle, where in addition to having three equal sides, all three angles are at a constant sixty degrees. With that being said, my fascination with triangles began long ago, spurring years of tedious study that led eventually to my expertise and mastery in the art (in both the cultural and martial senses) and philosophy of the triangle. I had been drinking a bit, and I had fallen asleep in an alley. I had expected that I would have woken up not remembering that I had fallen asleep in an alley, but I was fairly sure that I would have at some point woken up in said alley and somehow navigated my way home on instinct. There were theories that had been well exacted in the news reports about metallic, magnetic fragments in the bridge of the nose that worked as some sort of internal compass, but this to me was nothing but a mere pipe dream; humanity’s aggregate demand for a fantasy world full of androids and cyborgs. Instead, though, I woke up sitting in a chair placed carefully in the middle of a rug patterned with triangles in a room that masqueraded as some sort of misshapen dungeon. A man sat across the room from me, looking triangular in fashion with his legs folded so that his knees jutted out to two points that complemented his head, which perched upwards as a third point, evenly spaced between the outward extensions of his knees. I would be lying if I said I didn’t panic. I was a bit frightened by the foreign surroundings; frightened and somehow sexually curious. “What the hell?” I asked groggily as my hangover left little space for all the more concrete questions that flowed into my waking psyche. I had no idea where I was or how I had gotten here, and I had never seen the man sitting before me. I was aware that I might have in some way been violated, but there were no residual signs of any lubrication. There may have been a chance that I had awoken untarnished. “I have seen sketches of robots from the future,” the man said with reverberations of wisdom, “and they all have interlocking, triangular teeth.” He stood up as I tried to dissect his mantra. I stayed silent, watching, as he flared out his arms to reveal two triangular folds that stretched from the ends of his sleeves to his waists. He looked flighty, slightly like a vampire, but not in the traditional, Romanian sense. He just had this way about him where he looked like he could disappear and glide off bat-like at any second. Without a careful eye following him, he would vanish and reappear somewhere else, much quicker than would seem physically possible. “Triangulation!” he exclaimed as he surprised me from behind. “The initial point of any action can be determined by the difference in response time from any two other points. That, my friend, is how I am able to move so fast…and read your mind.” I was beginning to feel uncomfortable, and I wanted to leave, but every time I so much as thought about moving in one direction or the other, the man would be there blocking my path. I felt like my mind was working lethargically and had perhaps been numbed by the air in the room. Breathing felt less permissive, and there was a certain heaviness to it. “Consider this a gift,” he said with a convincing arrogance. “Perhaps I should have left you there to die if you are thinking such ungracious things of me.” My mouth was open in confusion, and I could taste the stale bits of air on my tongue. They had a definite palpability to them, those air molecules, and they wedged between the individual buds and creases in my taste organ. It was getting dry, and I couldn’t speak. I could only look forward, gawking, with the same glassy expression on my face. “If it weren’t for me, you would have been eaten by the rats in your sleep, but I found you there when I was on my late evening walk. I walk every morning at three down the same course. There are three points, I have found, that align to form a triangle that would somehow connect though the angles between them total one hundred eighty one degrees. I found you there in that extra degree, and I dragged you all the way back here to kill you.” I clenched my entire body defensively, but eased up slowly as he continued. “While dragging you back here, though, I noticed how weak I truly was. My muscles were starting to give and my legs were growing sorer with every step. I barely made it to the door, and my butler had to help me drag you in and down the stairs. By projecting a point in the past down a trajectory in the present angling towards the future, I calculated an all to near endpoint in the future. I felt my time growing near, and I realized I had spent so much of my life researching a lost art that I had never fathered an heir. In return for your life, I simply ask your apprenticeship and that you refer to me only as Father and never inquire further as to my identity beyond what I reveal to you. Do we have an agreement?” I really didn’t know what to say. I didn’t understand most of what he was saying, so I couldn’t put together any valid argument at the time. “Yes, Father,” was all I could manage to say because as he moved, his gliding wings flourished and accentuated his gauntness. He had a very angular face that seemed sunken in between the bones. I was able to gather, though, simply by my surroundings and his demeanor that he was a misanthrope who had spent years in hermitage. Perhaps he was a mathematician once. He appeared quite pedantic, though there was something artificial about it. Perhaps he was a shell of what he once was; outcast from the scientific community for failing to abide by the laws of physics by which he was bound. He was a highly sought after man, empowered with the ability to manipulate the geometry of our dimensions to bend his way through space. The key, as he had emblazoned in every mark of his existence, was a linear and angular relationship between opposite and adjacent sides. Still, he had his cursed mortality. He knew things about triangles that certain officials wanted kept buried. He knew things about shape manipulation that would put an end to organized society as we knew it. Pride would not let that knowledge die with him. He needed to pass it onto someone, as destructive as that might be, to preserve his legacy. A man spends his life’s work developing a nanotechnology devoted to creating the largest possible explosion with the smallest possible chemical reaction. It must be tirelessly mapped with ego lest the inventor’s life be sacrificed for those at the mercy of his hideous creation. I had been working diligently at unbinding my hands. They were fastened tightly with rope behind the chair’s backrest. I had been bound all this time, but still, I would think about moving, and he would appear there in whatever direction I was thinking. It had to have something to do with the patterns on the rug. The chair was placed equidistantly from every point on a large triangle in the middle of the rug. I saw what he was doing with his eyes and my eyes and the angles of the encasing triangular design. He was calculating, far beyond the need for a protractor or any other such device. It was all intrinsic now, for him, and this made his movements quicker- if not in speed in efficiency. “Excuse me, Father, but how long am I going to have to stay tied to this chair? My wrists are starting to chafe.” He paced around the perimeter of the central triangle design on the rug. It seemed like he was considering my question, but he ignored it in response. “Johnny,” he began, “have you heard of the Bermuda Triangle?” I nodded, and he went on another long winded story about something or other. I tried to pay attention as best I could, but my predicament distracted me. Still, I could hear his voice well enough to recount what he told me, I just wasn’t concentrating on linking the words together to find meaning except for some parts that seemed more relevant to me. “Meteorologists will have you believe that the reported disappearances of various ships have something to do with the meeting of currents that swirl together some sort of perfect storm. This is a load of rubbish; the most feasible thing that modern science can come up with to explain such a phenomenon. The Bermuda Triangle, however, isn’t a triangle at all. It’s a vacuous space that I invented prior to the sixteenth century.” I wasn’t really following, then. It all seemed like some self indulgent lecture. I had listened to strings of incoherent babble for some time now, and I was growing impatient to find out what he was trying to get at. The next part, though, piqued my interest. “The world as you know it is a familiar place because everything was created in mathematical proportion based on the repetition of different shapes and patterns in everything. In your dimension, that shape would be the spiral. Everything is measured by a certain ratio that disguises any cracks or fissures in your dimension’s surface. For the most part, mankind has gone on for generations without suspecting that other geometrical realms are shaped across the same plane simultaneously.” He paused for a bit to let me soak in his simplified construct of the universe and the several layers that exist within the same space, completely ignorant to each other. “With the ability to bend shapes- to stretch them beyond their definitions- we can tear holes through our own geometric dimension and pass on to others. All it takes is simple manipulation of a conflicting shape to open a portal from one layer to another. The Bermuda Triangle is the most infamous example of this, bordering between sailor’s myth and inexplicable reality.” He paused again, and this time I was having a harder time keeping up. I was also still fidgeting with the ropes around my wrists, but I wasn’t getting anywhere, and besides, the rope was still knotted around the chair in snarling configurations and tied around my ankles. “Let’s just say that these shapes can be torn open using an ancient form of rogue mathematics that has been outlawed; punishable by death. Some have mistakenly identified its use in the past as sorcery or witchcraft, but it is neither. It is just a much less restrictive form of mathematics that is too complex for you to worry about. In fact, it has nothing to do with the issue at hand here; which is the artifact called the Bermuda Triangle that exists beneath the Bermuda Triangle. In fact, the mysterious geographical region known as the Bermuda Triangle is just the largest Bermuda Triangle and the only one that’s gained any attention in modern day society. Any manipulated triangle that opens a passage to a new dimension is a Bermuda Triangle. That’s how you ended up here. The artifact, however, is a completely different story.” “So you’re telling me that we’re in some sort of triangle dimension?” Father smiled at my aptness, and he started clapping his hands together. “Precisely; I didn’t want to startle you. Let this be a warning that everything around you will appear foreign and plausibly frightening. My advice to you is that you try to adapt to your new surroundings as quickly as you can. There is going to be a significant amount of physical discomfort as a result of these changes, but the more you resist it, the worse it will become. You’re going to have to accept the pain and let it reshape you.” I wasn’t quite sure what he meant by this, but he moved to untie me. As he was struggling with the knots, I had to pardon my own nondescript attention to the detail of the room. The dimensions were off, and the relative sizes of some thing compared to another were slightly offsetting. I began to see what he meant when he talked about worlds that were based on certain figures, because the binding spiral that related my perspectives in such a portioned symmetry were gone, and instead, I began to see everything from the angle of two triangulated points trying to balance each other out. It wasn’t as if my visual intake was processing differently, but the things it was forced to process were only able to be blurred into focus if I saw them from points outside of myself. It was a strange focus- a triangular focus- where I could anchor the two adjacent points to angle inward on a third and primary point of focus forward along my spatial field. It wasn’t so much that the symmetry was gone that it was there in an imbalance from top to bottom. Completely unbound, though, Father motioned for me to follow and we slunk through a door in a wall angled inward. When we were through, it felt as if we were on the ground again with two more walls angled inward above us. Certainly this was some sort of glitch in gravity’s flow that seemed perfectly normal in this dimension. I felt it push against me, and I fought it to try to gain some semblance of balance. As I resisted, however, I found it more difficult to control my movements and my sense of up and down became dizzily confused. I could feel my head hurt from the inside as my body was tossed carelessly by the waves of a fluid, multidirectional gravity. It felt like a boat desperately swaying to not capsize. “The infiniteness of this universe doesn’t spiral out as cleanly as yours does. Instead, it grows upon itself both outwards and back inwards in overlap. Thus, unlike your universe where the infinite is unreachable, we are able to travel the course to theoretically anywhere here. Unfortunately, we don’t have the discretion of our own movement. Instead, we’ve just built this vessel, and we’re constantly carried along currents.” I looked around, noting that he referred to a “we,” and I saw that there were attendants manning every aspect of the deck we were on. The course of the vessel felt smooth, as if we were cutting through the surface rather than sailing in a wave. Some of them nearby greeted him by simply addressing him as Father, and there was something off about his shape. It was constant and clearly defined, while the shapes of his attendants were changeable so that they could be upright standing any way. Simply put, the whole deck and the people on board and the horizon beyond were a series of interlocking triangles of varying sizes, but it was the inconsistency and incongruent nature of the whole ordeal that allowed for the ship to be guided along by the geophysical currents. Without such incongruence along tri-fold symmetric axes, there would be no room for movement. It worked, in ways I never understood, somewhat like a pinwheel where each tier moved along at a different rate that remained somewhat fluctuating. “The Bermuda Triangle- it should be down here somewhere.” He stood and waited, inactive and unable to will us to his destination any sooner. I still didn’t understand where I fit into all of this, and upon closer examination of the ship’s crew, I started to become a bit uneasy. They were all the damned souls of lost pirates; none with a moral bone to being with, and now none with any bones at all. They had become gelatinous in shape as if hammered triangularly by the thundering pounds of some vengeful triangular god. The flag hung unwavering on the mast with no wind to blow it to and fro. The symbol on it was a snake bent twice, eating its own tail. I never understood, even, what the physical, palpable item known as the Bermuda Triangle was. All I knew was that it was valuable for some reason because Father sought after it with the loudest of appetites. As we walked though, across the deck and past the pirates, they all greeted him with the same salutation- a simple “Father” upon his passage. It made me wonder if those pirates too had been given the same speech before about becoming his apprentice. The greedy need no heirs and will say anything to get theirs. But all these seamen were specialists at their own boating trades. They worked tediously and spoke obediently to Father. I, however, had no skill that I could imagine. As I followed Father, I could barely keep at a stand and could only walk with the utmost of care with the heel of my stepping foot touching against the toes of my planted foot with every lunge forward. I was disoriented; distracted by the borders between separate entities rather than the entities themselves. Everything looked blemished to me; unique in a blurred, decomposing way that I had never seen before. As I walked on, I became more and more skeptical of the damned old twat, but I refrained from questioning him further so that I wouldn’t be lulled by his practiced reassurances. The triangulation of the whole place was far too distracting for me to try and detect glibness in his voice. I was off my guard and thought it best to keep a low center of gravity and let my hairs and flaring orifices frill and snarl. I tried to appear larger than I was, but this was difficult given the amount of shifting my body was doing from one adjoining corner to another. Instead, I sat down upon one of the sides of the triangular yaw as it cut through the fractal canals of the sea, which turned at half right angles either port or starboard at random and through overlapping folds. I sat, and I let the knife blade run of the ship roll me on as I fought off a triangular seasickness that threatened to seep out in some heaving expulsion from any of the holes on my body. *** When it had gone dark, and Father had returned to his cabin for sleep, some of the pirates anchored the boat in place so that we wouldn’t pass the pattern marking the resting place of the Bermuda Triangle. I had learned from listening to the pirates that this was a small artifact, but one that fetched a tremendous price that would make them all very rich men. Oddly, though, most of these pirates had disappeared centuries ago, so they were all over three hundred years old and had little use for treasure anyway. It had just become kind of a habit for them. The junkie needs his hit and the pirate needs his booty. Apparently, there was a spot in this triangular dimension where a single hexagon lay unscathed by any crosshairs to divide it in two. It was at the center of this point where the Bermuda Triangle could be retrieved, but not much was known about the process of obtaining it. Everything I had heard was either hearsay or old pirate stories. The pirates, as a group, were wholly misrepresented by dramatized accounts of history. It was a grave misconception that pirates wore patches over there eyes and hooks and pegs over their amputated limbs. The truth was that few pirates, if any, were amputees. Manning a ship isn’t the easiest of tasks, and without fully functional limbs, a crew member would be a waste of space and resources on a ship. Amputees of that era remained landlocked for the most part unless they were particularly rich from nobility and had a fancy for sailing. Vision was equally important as depth perception is a must have in nautical navigation. Pirates lived a very dangerous, combative lifestyle, nonetheless, and had to be in peak physical condition to last on the high seas. The pirates aboard this ship noticed my condition when camped out at night. They had gathered around me, building a small fire for warmth and rationing me extra ladles of broth to settle my stomach. They sat around laughing at me in jovial pirate manner while getting sauced on drams of rum. Under the command of their liquors, the pirates crept from their obedient and strenuous mechanical cog placements to reveal their truer selves. They spoke openly and freely with Father below deck, and they were really just regular fellows- a bit older and worn to bone in some places. In the movement and buzz of the day, though, they remained silent and servile; able to look you in the eye and deny up and down any memories of their midnight musings and yarns weaved under the three sided moon. “Aye, it be no more than three yanks on an old man’s beard until we’re there,” mused one of the pirates, one of the hands on deck who was a card at woodwork. “Maybe no more than the morn after the morrow!” replied another, who steered the boat along the current. “Who’s the fresh fish?” one of them asked, pointing a zigzagging finger at me. I tried to respond, but I was still sipping on broth when another pirate intervened. “Aye, that be the body!” The pirates started laughing and clanking their flasks together. I swallowed my mouthful of broth. “The body?” The pirates stopped laughing and looked at me as I questioned them. One of them knelt in close to me and spoke as if he was only speaking to me. I saw the others listening intently, though, to a story that seemed newer every time they heard it. “Father gave you the most important job of all. We’ve been rowing for years now as he’s been searching for someone with a body that won’t reject his soul. I’ve heard that the Bermuda Triangle is a great many things. It is a physical item, but it cannot be held in the hand. It can only be swallowed and held in the stomach. Some say that the Triangle, when swallowed, unites all the layered dimensions into one chaotic dimension where everything can be manipulated by the one who swallows it. His power will be unrivaled, and as his loyal crew, we will reign just beneath him over every facet of life that ever has existed and ever will exist.” “Go back a bit,” I said, reinstating the aura that starts to fade at the culmination of a good pirate story. “Go back to the part about my body and his soul.” “Aye- when Father swallows the triangle, his body will no longer be usable as his soul’s vessel, and his soul will seek a new body. After centuries of searching, yours appears to be a perfect host body to house Father’s soul.” “And what’ll happen to my soul?” “It’s a necessary casualty, but you’ll die an honorable death.” “I see,” I responded without much fervor as the pirates around me. They all had some look of wolverine curiosity on their faces. They salivated at the anticipation of witnessing such a sacrifice, and I could tell that there eyes on me right now were like eyes on a dead man. Part of it was an admiration, though not an envious one, but on the whole, the bloodthirsty curs. It was the unattended drool in their beards that sickened me the most. I leaked out some broth, and immediately, the lot of them began to attend to me. I was Christ-like, but all this respect was only given at the anticipation of my death. Any attempts to rally the pirates to save me would surely end in the loss of their trust. I had no desire to become a martyr. It seemed nice except for the dying. That’s mainly what turned me off about it. So with nobody I could convince to help me, I either had to chance that the pirate’s tales were nothing more than the speculation of a few drunken scallywags or I had to swallow the Bermuda Triangle myself. *** I had stayed silent on the ship, praying perhaps that the trinket was only valuable in its inaccessibility, and paradoxically, would be so valuable that it has to remain inaccessible. I was banking on this; that the Bermuda Triangle itself was a virtually unverifiable myth in its rarity so that its value built upon itself as it rested somewhere. Basically, I hoped that its value came from its worthlessness. If this were the case, however, what would explain my presence on the ship? The pirate’s tale had to be true. It was the only thing that I could think of that would explain my predicament in a somewhat feasible manner. Of course, we were in an alternate dimension from the one I was used to. I figured that all sorts of antithetical logic would apply here. Unfortunately, I had woken up rebound on the morning we were said to have approached the hexagon. This time, I was still on deck, and instead of being tied to a chair; I was tied to the base of one of the masts. The crew was gathered abound, and Father stood looking over the stern of the boat as it carved it’s way in towards the gigantic hexagonal figure, dipping into the shape. I saw Father’s eyes glow, and I wondered for a brief second if this was Satan that I was duping. Was this the devil and his undead pirate crew that I was inches away from outfoxing? I’m still not quite sure. I just remember the satisfaction I derived from what happened next. “Fuck!” Father exclaimed, flailing his arms about, striking the deck with some tools that lay about while kicking doors and ricocheting from side to side of the room like a bouncing ball. “It’s gone!” He stared over the side of the ship one more time, looking deep in the empty center of the hexagon for the Bermuda Triangle, but there was nothing to be found, save an empty space where the triangle should have been- where the triangle once was. But with the knowledge of impending execution hanging over my head, I couldn’t sleep. I knew the crew was all sauced last night, and that we were close, so I jumped overboard. I was always a poor swimmer, but this, being a different dimension and all, didn’t call for normal swimming. It was more of a carving. I had to fit myself into a groove and slide along the current until it dropped me into a whirlpool spin down to the bottom of this hexagon- careening around the one hundred and twenty degree angles as they met towards the bottom. It was in this careen that I noted the beauty of the equilateral triangle, finally understanding it in the form of the equilateral hexagon, a convergence of six such triangles all bonded together to form one shape. It was here that I understood that every polygonal figure imaginable can be broken down into a series of triangles. And then I hit the bottom, at the point of convergence, where the Bermuda Triangle was waiting there for me, and I saw it. To be honest, I didn’t know what to make of it. It was small and modest looking. As for the taste, it didn’t have much flavor. The angles made the whole thing hard to swallow, and I could feel bleeding in my esophagus as it went down. “Fuck!” Father cursed again, his eyes widening in terror as the boat descended and his pirate crew all scurried to the starboard to try to weight the boat back against the current; all except one pirate- not the most attractive of fellows, but one with a body I had grown used to. He was a rough, hairy man with dirty locks of brown hair that hang over his face, an unshaven beard, and crooked, yellow teeth. He must have been outcast from the Queen’s Royal Navy. I figured he’d have to do, and now I’ve grown quite used to the body. My old one was worn anyway. Once I had swallowed the Bermuda Triangle, I had to get back to the boat before anyone woke up and noticed me missing. This required carving my way against the current which was a slow, tedious process, but I knew I’d be exchanging bodies in the meantime. The friction left a rather turbulent way for the ship that morning, but this last rocky pass seemed fitting for the anxious pirate crew had the end of the journey not been such a disappointment for them. “Fuck!” was all Father could say as he watched the boat turn down in towards the hexagon, the angles beginning to bend and tear a bit so that the seven hundred and twenty degrees of angle in the perimeter burst open a bit, allowing everything to tear through, a soluble fizzing as the thin walls tore separating one dimension from another. I don’t know where they ended up, but somewhere below a triangular sea lay my old remains and the sunken ship of who I can only presume to be Satan himself. I ended up reborn in a new form where I had grown averse to all five identified types of triangle in lieu of my hexagonal fascination, which to be honest, was rather short lived. Still, I oftentimes wondered about my new body. It seemed to work a lot better than my old one, a bit scruffier than I would have looked at that age, but surely it was born to loving British parents. I wondered what had made it run off and consider piracy. Piracy sounds like something very fitting for me still, even though I have met pirates and did not feel like I fit in with them. It has been easy to return to my preconceived notions of pirates in a world where ironically I legitimately occupy a pirate body. Sometimes I look at my hands at night and wonder if they’ve shot men; if they’ve slit men’s throats. But try as I might to tear open new shapes and climb inside, it only worked that once. I suppose, though, that’s when it counted the most.
