Vincent Webb Part 3

29 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/29/05 Steven Xandrous I follow these… spies, I guess you could call them; I take notes of the surroundings. The cracks in the walls, the doors, the names on the doors, the exits, everything, I have taken account for. I have a feeling this unfortunately is not the last time I am going to be seeing this place. After numerous minutes of jogging, and the occasional burst of speed, we finally arrive to an exit to the maze I was stuck inside of. The spies open the door and I walk out first, relieved to smell fresh air. Well, as fresh as it can get in this city. The men stare at me. I look back and extend my hand to each of them. “Thank you,” I say embarrassingly. They smile and return the handshake, but do not say a word. The guards walk back inside of the building and slam the doors shut. I walk away from the would-be murder scene quietly. As I do, I realize that my life was extremely close to an end… what a life. Whatever. I walk far away from the building that my life could have ended inside of, and I sit on a broken and worn out bench, my thoughts beginning to swarm. For some reason, I felt this yearning. It’s as though life is nothing without it. I don’t really understand it, but it is there and I can feel it. It’s hurting me. Not in a physical way, either, but it aches me. I look up, across the street. I see a little boy on the ground, crying, holding his knee. I also see a woman, presumably his mother, picking him up, her face holding one of concern. That’s what I’m yearning for. Love. I can’t live a life like this. I can’t live with all this hatred surrounding me. I need something that I don’t yet understand. Love. As my thoughts finalized, my breathe is cut short by a strong arm wrapping around my neck, choking me. The arm pulls me up off of the bench and throws me to the ground. I look up to see a large man, a heavy man, about six-foot four inches. His face screams Italian, and his attire is that of a Vincent Webb soldier. The man charges forward. I hurriedly get to my feet and before the man can get to me, I extend my foot towards his stomach forcefully, tossing him backwards, his breathe gone. I quickly switch feet, my other foot catching him in the side of the head. I smile. This would be over quickly. I attempt to deliver a final crushing blow to his head, but the man grabs my ankle and twists it, forcing me to lose my balance. I fall and hit the ground hard, my head scraping the pavement. The man charges forward once more, hitting me with left and right fists to my jaw. I stumble backwards from the impact, but my vision clears up and I see him nearing yet again. I charge at him in an attempt to tackle him to the ground, but he side-steps me and knees me, catching me right above the nose. I fall back to the ground, clutching my nose. The man kicks me in the side of my stomach, and I roll off towards the bench. The man continues his attack, but I catch him in the stomach with another foot. He bends over, clutching his large stomach and I swing another foot up towards him. The man grabs both my feet, and pulls me off the ground in a power bomb-like motion. Damn, this guy is strong. He pulls me up and attempts to slam me down onto the back of the bench, but I curve towards him, barely dodging the bench, and whip him into the bench, headfirst. I hear something pop, but the man does not halt his attack. He charges at me and hits me dead-on, picking me up on his shoulder. I try to leap forward, off his shoulders, but his grip is extremely tight. Suddenly I feel my back hit a wall hard, and all the breathe in my lungs escape. I clutch my back in pain, my eyes shut. When I open them, I see a silver-coated pistol aimed right at my head. “Look, kid, you’ve pissed me off. I’m just going to end this fast, alright? You want it in the head, or in the stomach?” he says, grinning. Before he gets to put his finger on the trigger, I thrust my foot hard into his groin. I swiftly hurl my foot up and hit him, once again, in the side of the head. The man falls lifeless to the ground. I roll him over onto his back and open his pockets. I pull out a wallet and open it. The ID card reads “Leone Andrews,” along with his information such as height, weight, eye color, and so on. I flip the fold open, and inside of the wallet is at least two hundred dollars. I put the wallet in my own pocket, smiling with satisfaction. I deliver another kick to this… Leone’s stomach. As I walk away, I hear a loud crackle behind me and the concrete which I hit earlier is releases a piece of itself to the ground. I turn, and as if in slow motion, I see a black convertible speeding towards me, one arm extending outward towards me. A gun. What is with people and their damn guns!? I sigh, grasp the gun that had previously been Leone’s, and begin to run. As the car catches up, I realize that there is no way that I can escape these guys on foot. I go to the nearest car coming my way and run in front of it, my arm extended outward. The car stops, and I rush into the passenger seat, my gun pointed. The driver of the vehicle lets out a shrill yell, and I realize it is a woman. She looks at me, her arms above her head. She’s screaming, not saying anything comprehendible. “Ma’am,” I say quietly. The screaming continues so I repeat the word, this time yelling. “MA’AM!” The woman stops screaming, but her sobbing continues. I extend my arm out the door, and drop the gun. “Look, I just need you to give me a ride… please. Just fucking go. I won’t hurt you.” The woman looks at me, still frightened. She puts her foot to the peddle and the tires spin to life, thrusting the vehicle forward. As the tears continue to burst from her eyes, she is able to stutter out the words, “Wh-what’s going on?” I sigh, looking down. “I’m not sure. I will tell you the story later. Just keep driving, I’ll tell you where to go.” I lean out the window, and as I do a bullet zings by my head breaking to rear view mirror. The woman lets out a scream, and her hands turn the wheel, forcing the vehicle to go off track. I look up the road, and coming up is a two way turn… and an empty field. “Go straight,” I say firmly. “What?!” the woman says, confused. “GO STRAIGHT!!” I yell. The woman gulps, and continues forward. The vehicle zooms onward, hitting a lump in the grass, throwing it into mid-air as we crash into the field, still going full speed ahead. I turn around and I see my pursuer’s vehicle skid to a halt. Thank God… “Get back onto a road. They stopped,” I say calmly. I notice the woman is still sobbing. As the vehicle slows down, I begin to notice her facial features more vividly. Her long black hair is still smoothly running down her shoulders, and her skin is moderately tanned. She looks at me, frightened, but her blue eyes shine brilliantly past her tears. When I see her, I begin to regret what I’ve done. This woman could have died just as easily as me. “Look,” I say quietly, “I don’t know who these guys are, but I know that they probably have your license plate number, and soon they’ll have your name and address and everything.” “How would they do that? That’s impossible,” she says. This is the first time that I ever heard her speak, and her voice somewhat calms me. “Let’s just say that they have friends in high places,” I say, mimicking the old man’s own words. “Do you have any family members or roommates at home?” “No, I don’t. What’s that have to do with anything?” she asks, confused. “Do you live in a house or apartment?” I ask, ignoring her question. “I live in an apartment. Why? Please, just tell me what is going on,” she asks again. “I can’t tell you yet. Look, you can’t go home yet. I don’t know if you will be able to for a while, honestly. We need to get to a hotel or something. First we need to get some plates or something. Just hurry and get onto a road,” I say quickly. After driving through the bare field, we finally reach a driver way, and pull out onto the busy road, full of moving traffic. The woman calmly drivers to the nearest hotel, an ugly, rusted one, but I guess it will do. I pull the money Vincent had given me out of my pocket, and the woman gasps. I laugh. “Who the hell are you?” she whispers quietly to herself. Too bad I heard it. We walk into the hotel, and standing behind the small counter, which is really just a small desk they probably got from Wal-Mart, is a rather old and rather short woman who appears to me to be Asian. She says in a high pitched voice, “Hello, what can I do for you?” “We’d like to rent a room for the next two nights,” I say, looking at the woman. She looks at me and nods, uncomfortable with the situation. “Unless there are two rooms available?” The woman looks at her laptop sitting in front of her and moves the mouse a little bit before saying, “Yes, we have two rooms right next to each other open. Both have one king-sized bed. Is that alright?” I nod, and so does the woman. “Alright, that’ll be sixty-three dollars a night for two rooms for two nights… that will be two-hundred sixty-nine dollars and sixty-four cents.” I hand the woman three hundred-dollar bills. She hands me the proper change, and then hands us the keys. “Room one-oh-one for the lady, room one-oh-two for the gentlemen. Have a nice night.” We nod and walk over to our rooms. I unlock my door and open it and before I step in, the woman does. I look at her, confused. She sits down at the bed, and this is the first time I’ve seen her body features. The woman is about five foot seven inches at most, and her legs are beautiful and thin, as is the rest of her body, excluding her chest. “So,” she says, “tell me what is going on here.” I sit next to her and tell her the whole story about Vincent Webb, the box that he planted in my room, everything. The whole time, she sits their, quietly and intently listening. This pleases me deeply that someone actually gives a shit. As I finish my story, she looks at me, her face showing sincerity. “So,” I finish, “how about you? What’s your name?” “My name is Alice Penn. I’m twenty-seven, I’m not married, and I have no relatives nearby because I just moved here,” she says, seemingly a bit sad. “Well, at least you have relatives that you remember,” I say, quietly. “What?” she asks. “N-Nothing, don’t worry about it,” I say, not wanting to tell the story. I look at the clock. Nine thirty-seven. “Well, I need to catch some sleep. It’s been a very long day for me.” She nods and smiles. She stands up and before she leaves, she does something I didn’t expect. She kisses me on my left cheek.
