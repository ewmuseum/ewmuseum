The Threat

1 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1st October, 2006 The coffee shop known as Insomnia in East Rutherford, NJ, had a somber air about it tonight. It was unlike any other occasion I had visited this vibrant, energetic spot. As it was past eight in the evening, most of the loyal coffee drinkers had sipped their final sip for the day, returning home until the next pre-work morning ritual would begin tomorrow. The owner, a man named William Monks, quietly went about his business behind the clean countertop, gazing up at his 1980 Blaupunkt model television every few seconds. I sat across from my tag team partner and friend, Phil Allen, in one of the sixteen cubby holes in the shop, examining his pensive expression. It told me one thing and one thing only – he had something on his mind, and it was bothering him. Allen was normally a placid character, admittedly with a fiery streak from time to time, but tonight was different. I didn’t know if it was the aromatic scent of the coffee wafting into his nostrils or just the trauma of the past week of dealing with incessant waves of press questions. Truthfully, it mattered little to me. I had problems of my own. “We’ve had a tough week, Wes, but believe it or not we’ve got a match coming up this week,” Allen said, speaking for the first time in over ten minutes to me. “They couldn’t have chosen a worse time, Phil. Can’t we reschedule?” Allen’s expression was not a tolerant one. “Wrestling doesn’t work like that, Wes. You take your chances when you get them. You can’t select them. You don’t get a chance to step out in front of thousands of people every day of the week, you know. Savour it, Wes.” The world of wrestling and sports entertainment was the last thought in my mind currently. A cloud of doubt had begun to form around that part of my life since the death of both Dean Fisher and Lilly Baines over a week ago. Making money was no longer an object. As much as I knew Fisher’s absence on the political front would only assist my cause, I berated myself for even considering my future when I knew full well that neither of them had one before them. It was cruel and tasteless of me. I took a quiet moment to myself as Allen sipped silently on his coffee. “We’ll probably win the match, anyway,” I mumbled, reaching for my polystyrene cup. Before I could make any such movement properly, Allen slammed his fist down on the table in fury. I looked into his eyes, which were lit up with an intensity I had only seen before while he was competing in the ring. “Never become complacent, kid! That must have been the first lesson I taught you in your training. The second you start to become cocky is exactly the time you’ll start losing matches.” “Okay, I’m sorry. I really am, Phil. I’ll train for this match, I will. It’s just that I may not be at one hundred percent with all this shit going on in my life right now. Is that acceptable?” Allen shuffled awkwardly in his seat, regretting his decision to lash out at me, I expected. “Look, we’ve been through a lot of unnecessary stuff this week, Wes. I realise that. We just need to get over it now and get back to work. I’m – I’m really sorry for blowing my top at you.” I accepted his apology with a simple nod. There was a mutual respect between us that was difficult to describe. Just as I prepared to take one last sip to finish off my coffee, the owner swiveled towards us, his attention distracted by a bulletin on the television. “Excuse me, Mr. Reno? I think you should see this,” Monks managed, although there was trepidation in his voice, almost as if he was afraid to speak to me. The white lettering across the bottom of the screen told me everything. NEWS UPDATE – REPUBLICAN PARTY POLITICIAN DEAN FISHER ALIVE My hands trembled uncontrollably as I stood up and moved out of the cubicle, my coffee cup’s remaining liquid being knocked over in the process. The news was hard to take for me, especially after the week I had just been through. Thoughts dashed into my mind quickly. I formulated questions for a few moments, knowing there was no logical answer to them. Phil Allen walked towards Monks, who went about his business casually, as if nothing had even happened. “This can’t be true, can it?” Allen asked incredulously, turning around to face me in disbelief. “Wes, how could this be true? Surely there’s a mistake – “ “There is no mistake,” I piped in swiftly. “And I know just who is responsible for this. Phil, we have to go.” We left Insomnia without paying my longtime acquaintance William Monks, but the forty year old owner didn’t bat an eyelid. His job faded into obscurity compared to this situation, as harsh as it sounded. The street outside was unwelcoming and I pulled my jacket on tight as I turned around to face Allen, who was just as dumbstruck about this as I was. “Phil, the man we’re looking for is Joseph Lynch.” “How can you be so sure? What if this is all just a rumour that’s gone out of proportion?” I dismissed his rumour theory; it was not feasible. The cogs of my brain worked overtime as I attempted to fathom the ins and outs of this unusual occurrence. The only conclusion that came to me was the fact that on the day that Fisher and Baines had been murdered, Joseph Lynch had somehow intervened to protect them. Lynch was identified as the shooter by eyewitnesses, but escaped before he could be brought into custody. The signs pointed to him. Perhaps he and Fisher had worked side by side on this to create the illusion that Fisher and Baines were killed. The possibilities were endless. “Look, I’m going to go find Lynch, Phil. You can either come with me or stay here. Make your decision.” “Wes, now is not the time to go off on some wild goose chase,” Allen remonstrated in a stern tone. “So you’re staying, then,” I replied, unlocking my BMW with my key and opening the driver’s door. My partner’s complaints fell on deaf ears as I started up the engine and sped down the street. I watched Allen’s figure disappear in the rear view mirror as I drove away. I saw the disappointment register on his face. The room was dark, the lone inhabitant obscured by the shadows of the tall, thin objects all around him. The desk was the only visible portion of the small, compact room, illuminated by a single lamp hanging loosely from a cable. The dingy corners of the room housed torn boxes of supplies, many years out of date. The lone figure at the table arched forward, the light catching the frame of his face, discerning him as male. He cleared his throat loudly as there was a patter of three knocks on the door. “Come in,” the man announced. She entered the room with care, avoiding the various stacks of files and folders left haphazardly on the dirty floor. He welcomed her under the shade of darkness with a long, passionate kiss. The couple sat down at the desk together, the woman sitting comfortably on the man’s lap. “We threaded the eye of the needle, Dean,” the woman said, her voice soft and tender. “We certainly did, Lilly.” The figures of Dean Fisher and Lilly Baines came into view simultaneously as Fisher turned his table lamp in his direction to view the files laid out before him neatly, a stark contrast to the other files in the room. Baines rubbed Fisher’s hair with her long, bony fingers as Fisher folded his arms. “It’s all panning out nicely, Lilly. My sources tell me the bulletin went down perfectly. Knowing Wesley Reno, he’s probably already out on the fruitless hunt for me. That was where he always fell down in his career, Lilly. He had no self-control.” “I know, baby,” Baines replied, continuing to run her hands through Fisher’s straight, combed hair. “What do we do to get rid of him?” “We wait. It’s that simple. Things are already in motion as far as eliminating Reno goes. In fact, maybe it’s time we give our old friend a call, huh?” Fisher dialed a nine digit number slowly, an arrogant smile appearing on his unshaven face as he did so. He switched the phone to speakerphone mode and waited for Reno to pick up. My phone ringing startled me, removing me from the harrowing void of my conscious mind momentarily. I swerved the wheel to the left, turning into a rest stop on the freeway to take the call. The caller I.D. read “Private Number”. I felt a tingle run up my spine as I answered the call after six rings. “Hello, who is this?” I enquired instantly, eager to uncover the identity of the caller. “It’s the guy who’s gonna put you out of a job, Wes,” the voice shot back, a venom in his voice. The voice, of course, was unmistakable. It was Dean Fisher. My mouth turned dry as I struggled internally with myself, asking myself the question why he would call me. His threat needed no repetition; he planned to put me out a job. I dismissed it as a vacuous threat, scoffing into the phone as I replied. “Nice try, Dean, but you’ll have to pull another rabbit out of the hat this time. How could you do this, Dean? You faked your own death, and for what? Do you want recognition? Is that it? Trust me, Dean. Your political career is dead.” “I beg to differ, Wes,” Fisher responded, an unexpected confidence flowing through his words. “In fact, I suggest you discontinue any investigations on the Ruth Beckford case with immediate effect and tender your resignation, or something unfortunate will happen.” This threat was far from vacuous. I slammed my fist on the wheel and cursed under my breath as I considered the options. Fisher couldn’t be underestimated as an opponent, but I knew him well. He was always liable to compromise if it suited him. In this case, however, his tone told me that a compromise was not an option. “I don’t believe you. Why would I believe you?” “Oh, I think you should take what I’m telling you very seriously, Wes, or you may just find yourself in a very, very horrible situation.” Before I had a chance to even imagine the consequences, the voice of my former girlfriend, Lilly Baines, invaded the line. “Do what he says, Wes. Then you can go and live a happy life by yourself and be out of our hair forever.” I hung up the phone on hearing those hard-hitting words from my former flame. It felt as if I had been ripped to shreds by Fisher and Baines during the phone call, leaving myself no viable option for escape. It was the worst feeling I had ever experienced. Phil Allen had been living in his apartment in East Rutherford, NJ, since mid-July, and yet as he stepped into the hallway and threw his keys to the side, he still didn’t feel like he was at home. There was a quality about the apartment that wasn’t right, but he couldn’t put his finger on what exactly it was. He picked up a framed photograph of himself and his wife-to-be and simply looked at it. He wasn’t an emotional man per se, but each and every day he wished he could fly back to San Diego to see her. He was in the mood for some quiet time, a concept that up until recently he would have denied himself. It was mainly due to the extreme stress his body had been under since returning to the ring that he had begun this new ritual. He prepared a cup of green tea and sat down, about to watch re-runs of ECW classic matches when the phone rang. He secretly hoped it was his fiance wishing him a good night, but the caller was an unexpected one. “Hello, is this Phil Allen?” “Yes, who’s asking?” “Whoa, calm down, Mr. Allen. My name is Ignacio Torredo. I was hired by Daniel Reno to monitor Dean Fisher’s activities.” Allen remembered Torredo. Minutes after the false shooting of Fisher and Baines, Torredo attempted to contact his partner Joseph Lynch, but Lynch never picked up the phone. Allen met Torredo with Wesley Reno following the incident. Torredo came across as a polite gentleman, and the police, after rigorous interviews with him, had ruled him out of any involvement with Lynch’s plan. “Yes, I remember you, Mr. Torredo. Could I ask you why you’re calling at this hour? I was just about to sit my ass down for some quality T.V., you see.” “I assume you’ve seen the news bulletin about Fisher and Baines,” Torredo stated, ignoring Allen’s previous comment. “Yes, I’ve seen it.” “Then you know that something needs to be done about this. Where is Reno right now?” Allen berated himself silently for allowing Reno to take off by himself at this hour, and then returned his mind to the conversation. “He’s out… at the moment. We were at a coffee place. Once he found out about Fisher being alive, he took off in his car. He shouldn’t be too hard to find. I’m sure he’s safe.” “Mr. Allen, I need your help with something. Tonight, I’m going to a public records office downtown to research Reno and Fisher, more specifically their history. I’m eager to get to the bottom of their relationship before something serious happens to either one of them. This has gone far beyond the scope of politics, Mr. Allen. This is war.” Allen paused, realising the severity of the ensuing battle between his tag team partner and Fisher. “What do you need me to do?” “Come with me to the records office, Mr. Allen, and just help me find out anything about their history. Two heads are better than one, and you know Wesley better than anyone.” “Okay,” Allen replied, albeit slightly reluctantly. “Where do we meet?” “In fifteen minutes, I’ll be down the street from the public records office. I’ll meet you there. I’m driving a dark blue Mercedes.” “I’ll be there,” Allen responded, hanging up the phone before Torredo had a chance to thank him. Allen felt a pang of guilt about this meeting; he didn’t particularly want to dig up material on his partner. He wondered how Torredo had access to all of these records, but left his reservations behind him for the moment. Pulling on a jacket, he left his apartment, glancing at he and his fiancée in the framed photograph as he did so. The silent humming of a rotator fan on a cool setting was the only audible sound as the man at the desk swiveled around to press a button on his phone, allowing the person on the other side of his door to come in. The woman who entered was dressed impeccably in a short black skirt and red woolen top, accentuating her best features. She gingerly extended her hand to the man, in which she held a cell phone. She faltered slightly, losing her balance as the man roughly wrestled the phone from her grasp. Embarrassed and afraid, she left the room as the man began to speak. “Is it handled yet, Mr. Fisher?” the man asked, speaking with a Spanish accent, his words disjointed. “Yes, it’s in the process of being handled. It will only be a matter of time until he’s completely out of the picture,” Fisher assured, his tone unwavering in its confidence. “Good,” the man replied curtly. He sifted through a pile of files on his desk, one of which sported the name “Wesley Reno”. He opened the file slowly and revealed the black and white photograph of Reno. It appeared to be a recent photograph judging from his attire and hairstyle. “Will that be all, sir?” Fisher pondered, the man not realising his protégé was still on the other end of the line. “Yes. There is only one thing I ask of you, Mr. Fisher. Ensure that Reno is politically redundant within a week. I’ll handle things on my end. Good bye.” He hung up the phone abruptly, his thoughts wandering to other matters. He knew that if Reno was handled effectively, Fisher would rise to the top with ease, rivaled by no-one. Within a week, he would be back on the top of the pile thanks to Fisher’s work. The king of politics was about to retake his thrown. Phil Allen and Ignacio Torredo both knew time was up against them as they unlocked the padlock on the large door of the public records office in New Jersey. Each state had its own office, certain ones containing many more files than others. The New Jersey state public records office was infamous for a controversial incident which took place in 2000 involving the D.A. at the time, George Fern. Fern accepted bribes from influential lawyers in the city to allow them to search the office for information regarding their clients. This search was outside the rules and was deemed to be an offense against the state. The D.A. and the lawyers who accepted the bribes were subsequently tried and arrested, but Allen remembered the story vividly as he opened the doors. The building’s interior was nondescript. The floor was littered with computer desks on one side; the other housed a plethora of files and folders containing personal information on the population of New Jersey. It was the visitor’s choice as to which option was used. It was either the computers or the old fashioned way. Torredo led Allen towards the computers and quickly searched for Reno and Fisher. Within five seconds, the search was complete. Fisher and Reno just so happened to be in the same colour coded section – blue. Allen motioned to head over, but Torredo hesitated. “Wait,” Torredo said. He reached into his left pocket and removed two pairs of rubber gloves. “If we’re going to be rooting through this stuff, we need to cover our tracks.” Allen gazed at Torredo with a look of scrutiny. “It’s purely a precaution, Mr. Allen,” Torredo continued as Allen slipped the rubber gloves on. Torredo followed suit and the two men began their search through the files using the computers. Allen was not accustomed to dealing with computers on a regular basis, but even he was pleasantly surprised by his ability to navigate the system after only five minutes of searching. Allen glanced over at Torredo, who appeared to be particularly adept with machinery. Allen focused on the screen now, which contained a long list of documents with Dean Fisher’s name. Many of the files were simply quotes from newspapers or political conferences. Allen clicked on the “background” tab which brought up a page about Dean Fisher’s upbringing. It was about halfway down the page that something caught his eye. “Detective Torredo, I think I’ve found something you want to see. This is big,” Allen said as Torredo shuffled over to where Allen was seated. The look on his face told its own story. This certainly was big. Allen shook his head in disbelief at what he saw before him on the screen. It was a small, twenty-line report filed by a family counsellor detailing a supposed relationship between Dean Fisher’s mother, Lucy Fisher, and Wesley Reno’s father, Daniel Reno which took place when both Wesley Reno and Dean Fisher were no more than ten years old. As Allen continued to read the document, his mind began to sort through the possibilities. According to the document, during the period following the affair when it had become public knowledge, the families were torn apart. The boys had to be put into a foster home for temporary care as it was decided their parents were not in the correct frame of mind to take care of them. The document went on to reveal that the boys got into numerous fights and disagreements while in the foster home. Their four week stay was described by the counsellor as “the worst period in their young lives”. Torredo and Allen finished reading the document, but reread it several times, such was its magnitude. Allen knew that Reno had to be stopped before he did anything that he would live to regret. He had to get in touch with him as soon as possible. “We have to find Wesley Reno now, Mr. Allen,” Torredo said, echoing Allen’s own thoughts. “Then we better get going, Detective,” Allen dryly responded. The remnants of a group of youth’s night out were littered all over the streets as I drove up my street and swerved left into the driveway of my home. For the first time in months, I took a moment to examine the façade of the place where I lived. It had no homely feeling about it, in truth. It was simply bricks and mortar, nothing more. I stepped out of the car and inhaled the stale scent of the flowers Lilly Baines had purchased for me all those months ago. It brought back memories I wanted to distance myself from. I entered my cold, dark hallway and checked my messages. Not surprisingly, there were plenty for me to sink my teeth into. Even though my involvement in Fisher and Baines’ supposed deaths was cleared, the press still called for interviews, offering greater sums of money each time. I flicked the light switch in my bedroom on and threw myself onto the unmade bed, its sheets laced with the smell of my own body odour. I had no inclination for cleaning or keeping myself clean, for that matter. My life for the past few weeks had been a whirlwind of disappointments and moments of elation intertwined. The disappointment consisted of the deaths of Fisher and Baines, which effectively destroyed my career. As it turned out, the phone call I received from Fisher moments ago definitely put the cat amongst the pigeons and changed the situation completely. His ultimatum was concise and clear; I had to make a decision, or I would be the victim in a terrible event. On the other side of the coin were the moments of joy and excitement, the likes of which were few and far between in recent times for me. They manifested themselves in the squared circle, the stage of the wrestling ring. There was an unmistakable buzz when I went out to perform in front of the thousands of fans every week. The week off had given me a much needed respite from that world, but in the political abyss I was in right now, the thought of being wrestled to the canvas appealed to me more than ever before. It was my only escape route. I shut my eyes to review the last week’s events, only to be interrupted by the chirping of my cell phone. I recognised the ring tone as the one that I heard when Fisher had called moments ago. I could only assume it was him again. I answered the call, a resolute and defiant tone in my voice. “Fisher, I’m done with your little ploys! If you’re calling to issue more empty threats, then you’d be advised to hang up the phone right now.” “On the contrary, Wesley,” Fisher whispered menacingly. “In fact, I don’t deal in empty threats, and if you want proof, I have it right here.” Fisher seemed to move the receiver closer to someone who was speaking in muffled tones. The man who I could only assume was gagged began to shout frantically. My heart sank when I finally recognised the voice to belong to my father, Daniel Reno. “Convinced, Wes?” My fists clenched and I began to seethe with rage. The notion of Fisher kidnapping my father to gain leverage over me was something I never envisaged any politician would do. Fisher hadn’t only crossed the lines of politics; he had crossed legal lines that he could not go back on. Composing myself, I answered. “You son of a bitch, Dean! Do you even realise what you’re doing anymore? You’ve completely lost it! Let my dad go now, or you’ll never see the light of day again, because I will find you and lock you up for life. I promise you that!” “You haven’t got the balls, Wes,” Fisher shot back with fervour. “Do not underestimate me, Dean,” I exploded, the veins on my forehead increasing in size as I became overtaken with rage. “Now that I’ve got your attention, Wes, there’s something I need you to do for me. Of course, that’s only if you want to see your father alive ever again,” Fisher taunted, his control over me fully established now, as much as it pained me to accept. “You want me to tender my resignation? It’s done,” I replied, thoughts of my father being held captive rushing into my head. “Oh, I expect that from you, anyway,” Fisher responded arrogantly. “There’s a lot more for you to do, Wes. From this point in time until I say otherwise, you are my personal… bitch. Agreed?” Utter rage and confusion overcame me as I cursed aloud and made the hardest decision I ever had to make. I had to do his will if I wanted to see my father again. It was the only way. “Agreed,” I mumbled unconvincingly. “Good,” Fisher said, a wave of authority now on his side. “We’ll begin right away.” To be continued…
