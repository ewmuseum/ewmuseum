Trials and the Consequence - The Progenitor

21 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 21/11/06 ‘Are you really sure that this is what we must do?’ Picture a grey expanse of land, a concrete jungle of nothingness. Little light disperses itself over the area, nothing except for the moon. Conveniently it has draped over one corner of the expanse, far away from where myself and Pearson stand. He was looking at me with honest eyes, trying to spark some sort of bond between the two of us. I would have nothing of it now, the plan had been set out and that’s all there was to it. He had told me of a place, a place where I could find answers. ‘Yes, this is the only way priest. You wanted forgiveness right?’ He fidgeted slightly in the darkness of the night, thumbing his hands back and forth to keep away the chill. It wasn’t merely for that though, using his hands as a means to hold the fear at bay. There was no hiding this one and he acted on it. ‘I do but…’ ‘But what priest? You can’t back down now can you? Everyone must see through the consequences, no matter how much they wish to avoid them. You are no different.’ He nodded but wasn’t reassured by my evaluation of the situation. Overall I think he was regretting making this choice, entrusting my judgement to this whole proceeding. That’s because right now the two of us were standing about twenty metres away from a small building on the outskirts of Los Angeles, downtown to be more specific and one might expect it to be nothing more than a warehouse for nothing extraordinary. However, according to the priest, it was something much more than that. There was the promise of being able to find answers to questions that had been bothering me, ones like what exactly had happened to Sylvia. He too was expectant on this one, hoping that he could be reunited with his most beloved pupil. I on the other hand wished to find out more from the…person that had remonstrated with me back wherever it was they had kept me. When I had been talked to by the person in the haze of a building, he had spoken of power, of influence and manipulation being at the core of the tenets of his faith. He valued the enjoyment and pleasure of seeing how his machinations would play out over the weeks and months to his desire and, for him, there was nothing greater. And he wasn’t alone in this vein of thinking, at least not by the intimations that I had gathered from the cold muddled voice that had been harsh, metallic and alien to me at the time. Hell, I wasn’t even sure if it had have been a man at all. The build of the person had been deliberately disfigured and confused to my eyes. It had been an impressive façade from which to build upon. It had led me to this now, knowing that the priest was responsible for some of it, the one whom behind I could find some answers and perhaps… Wait. I held out my hand to go across the body of the priest and turned on him, looking him straight in the eyes. Now Pearson looked scared, his recognition of the fire in my orbs all to clear to him. He jerked his collar much like a cartoon character would and the sweat poured down from his wrinkled forehead. ‘You’ve brought me here, you’ve told me that I can find the answers that I seek here…yet you seem afraid of the consequences. If you were so sure of the possibility of getting your beloved lamb, wouldn’t you be willing to sacrifice other things? What’s holding you back…unless you know something that you’re not letting on?’ He gulped hard. Oh not a good sign. ‘I…’ ‘Priest do not lie to me now, at this, the most critical of times. I need the truth, you owe me that don’t you?’ His face crumbled and it soon rested in his hands, held low to avoid the cynical stare of someone who was tired of being conned out of something. For so long people have been bullshitting and dancing round in the shadows in the name of protection and guile. In fact, the priest was just like them right now, trying to influence the proceedings, seeing how he could push me in the right direction. I’m tired of this, I’m tired of all this crap. ‘Well?’ ‘They wanted me to bring you here. They’re planning a grand show for your arrival.’ ‘What?’ ‘I don’t know to be honest Jack…and I swear to you that’s the truth. All I was told was to bring you to here.’ ‘Both of you, Sylvia and yourself have been nothing but pawns you know that? All you’ve done is foreshadow something for them and for that I’m left looking like an idiot. This has to end. I am the one who should have control over my life, nobody else and it has to stop.’ ‘I wish that it were true Jack…but I don’t think you’ll find that.’ ‘I will priest…if nothing else but to restore your faith in your God.’ He looked at me in slight puzzlement. ‘Why?’ ‘Why not? I will admit that we all must be part of some maker, but what that is not for me to say. All I want to restore is that some things are beyond a human’s control. Call it miracles, call it science-fiction, call it whatever the fuck you want. I’m here to prove choice and happenstance.’ ‘But…’ ‘But nothing priest. I have come too far and too long to realise that we are powerless to something above ourselves, but something that is within reach for a few. I cannot come to that conclusion.’ ‘So you’re going?’ ‘Yes. And you’re coming with me.’ ‘I…’ ‘No doubts priest, this is where you begin your journey of faith and belief. We’ll see who wants to play God this time and who will end up as progenitors when all is said and done.’ No more words, no more responses. The man came with me whether he’d liked it or not. I was going to find out what was up with this now…and it would be final. *** Drakewerx was the name hanging over the entrance to the compound of the warehouse. There was nothing fancy about the entire appearance, nothing flashy. It was simple, and it was there whether you liked it or not. In some ways it was similar to the company. But mostly it wasn’t. This had all started months ago, seemingly innocuous interactions with people whom I had never thought would have anything more than simple emotions of their own to base their actions and decisions upon, but apparently, walking into this building, I was wrong, completely wrong on this matter. Apparently selling your soul for whatever ill gotten gains you wanted was the more proactive and appropriate choice for someone to make. I mean, when even a priest is doing something like this, you’ve got to ask yourself the question: who are you dealing with that is so powerful? That’s what I wanted to find out mainly; discovering their evil genius like machinations and their supposed god complex. Right now that was top of the list. Other than that I really didn’t have anything in mind other than this. I’d let the situation play out, let it wash over me and from there I could figure out where to go from there. In a brief moment of calm I remembered how far away Jessica was from here right now, both physically and mentally. This was not her place obviously, but it wouldn’t be right to tell her about this. In reality she had never even seen me before I embarked on this journey, she was too busy travelling the world for my gym, and it was best that I left her doing that. She didn’t needed to be worried, and I didn’t need to be disturbed. The door loomed large but things were still relatively quiet. I turned to the priest. ‘So, they’ve some sort of grand plan for my arrival?’ He shrugged noncommittally. ‘That’s what they told me. I was to wait out her by their appraisal. They said my reward would be waiting for me once you left my company so I guess that now would be where I leave?’ I blew some air from between my pursed lips, mulling it over as best I could. This could be part of their scheming and machiavellian designs, but honestly I couldn’t think of a reason for why he would need to be with me now. ‘I suppose.’ ‘Good luck Jack. If I see you again soon, I’ll take that as the proof of my God.’ I smiled grimly but nodded in appreciation of the sentiment. We didn’t exchange a handshake or hug like some action heroes might in a moment of quiet. There was no need. Either we saw each other, or we did not. There was nothing left to say. Looking at the door again I couldn’t help but wonder about one thing that still bothered me… What the hell did Adam make of all this? *** If outside was debatable as to the amount of light, inside was pitch black. I couldn’t see anything no matter where I turned and really I was fumbling aimlessly in the dark of a small corridor. So much for trying to look impressive, resolved and hardened by the ordeal. Once again they knew exactly how to make their pawns act however they wanted. Then I had an idea, something that just could work. I stopped moving. Completely. I let the darkness shroud me and I waited for something to happen. It worked too. A small bit of light eked out in front of me, a slit revealing the workings of a door. Again my thoughts turned to Adam as the proverbial white light beckoned me. Did he really think that this was all believable? That this whole bullshit was guiding him in a particular direction, being destined to do something. You know, he probably didn’t care. He was probably too busy getting high. The light still beckoned and really, I had no choice anymore. Damned if you do, damned if you don’t I’m sure the priest would say. Damned it is. *** My eyes struggled to adjust to the lighting but once they had, a whole cornucopia of sights, feelings and confusion mixed all at once in my head, telling me that something was wrong, so wrong. Because I had walked into an empty space. Empty space. This is what I get for people fucking with my head? Illusions of grandeur, bait and switches? What is this bullshit? ‘Where the fuck are you!!’ I had been made a pawn of once again. The priest of course had been told they were going to give a giant show of things. Of course he was going to be told certain things and made to act in a certain manner, even if he wasn’t going to know that he was being conditioned to act in this way. Truly, he had no god other than those who were willing to push him in the direction that they saw fit. Fuck me. I spun endlessly, out of annoyance, out of rage, out of desperation. I wanted nothing more than to see something that could be given to be something more than empty space. It was depressing me the longer this went on. And then suddenly, I saw something. It was right in front of me in fact. The person that had stood around me, cloaked in shadows and distorted to protect himself. At least now I could finally realise that it was a him. And he wasn’t nearly as physically imposing as I thought he would be. Actually he was wearing some reeboks, a plain t-shirt and slacks. Fuck me. ‘Hello Jack.’ I instantly leapt to the defensive, getting as close as possible to the man. Not once did he flinch, not once did he seem worried. His face kept bare and empty. ‘What the hell is going on? I want…no I NEED answers as to what the hell has been going on for the past month or two? Why have I been tortured at the expense of so many different things? Why have I been the one to take the blame when I had nothing to do with some of the things that were happening? WHY ME?’ He simply pushed a bony finger up to his lips and mimicked a shush. Despite my best intentions, I couldn’t keep the venom in my words and slowly but surely I calmed down. What the hell was going on? ‘All in good time Jack, and I will address them as they come to me. First however, I must ask you a question - do you believe in fate?’ I looked at him, standing in his sparse and plain clothing. He had a crew cut for hair, parted to the left all in a perfect movement. Everything about him seemed to be so perfect, so well proportioned. Almost like he wasn’t real. Unconsciously I shoved him…he moved, just like a real person would. I guess I had to go with it. ‘Yes I’m real Jack. I must admit, your scepticism leads me to believe that you wouldn’t be opposed to the idea of fate, but that you would have problems accepting it wholly. This would be a wise course of action of course. But for a minute, assume that fate is something to be both true and plausible alright? Follow me.’ We began to walk around in a wide circle, his arm guiding my body with him, a twisted dance between us. I followed like an idiot and I cursed myself for being led so easily. The fact that this had not been the first time was not lost on me. ‘Imagine a world where everyone is controlled by their fate, everyone has their true calling mapped out for them. Somewhere in this world, a group of people have it mapped out how the people are going to interact, where, why. They know how long the person will live, whether he will die peacefully, violently. They will know if he will be gay, straight, bisexual or something else more…intriguing. They will know if they have children or not. They will know if they will have sex…or not. They will know if the partners that this person will have will be satisfied with their lives.’ He paused for a moment and looked me straight in the eyes, his face still blank. ‘All this decided and accounted for, for whatever reason by a group of individuals. It seems like an awful lot of power does it not? Being entrusted with the fates of people and constructing a fathomable world in which the very idea of fate, while it seems plausible, cannot be too obvious. In other words…’ ‘In other words the façade of choice and personal determination have to be all the more true.’ The first crack of emotion. A smile crept across his face and started to walk again. ‘Exactly Jack. Now, what if this scenario was a little bit more…realistic than you might imagine. What would you think?’ ‘Futile.’ ‘So…how then do you explain your actions of the last couple of months. At any point did you feel like you were acting outside the normal parameters of your decision making? Did you feel like you were doing something that you wouldn’t normally do?’ ‘Yes.’ ‘Hmm…still some kinks I suspect.’ This had me confused. ‘Kinks? What are you talking about?’ ‘Well Jack, I’ll be honest with you. For the last two months, you’ve been drugged.’ ‘What?’ He simply nodded as we continued on our extended walk around the blank room. The soft padding of his reeboks were the noise that I most connected with funnily enough in the big wide open space. I was fixated on them and kept coming back to the noise. He finally continued his one sided conversation. ‘Yes, you have been drugged with something that has been introduced to your system consistently over the past two months. Various ways have been used to enable you to be under its effects, methods which I will not go into detail here, but suffice to say, they were introduced. And they have been effective.’ He stopped in his tracks and looked at me square again. ‘But we won’t be continuing them on you, because we have learnt what we needed to know about you and it is something that we are certain of the answer of. Yet, because of our continued desire to appreciate the backdrop of free will we must allow the asking of the question.’ Only one thing came to mind. And for the first time in a long time, my head felt clear as I asked the question. ‘You spoke of power and manipulation. It’s only now that I realise your intentions in that room that day. What I want to ask is, how can I become one of you?’ He smiled again. ‘There is a way for that Jack. I simply need to make you aware of what we’ve been doing. Where you stand now is empty, that’s for certain, but it won’t always be. In less than a week or two’s time we shall begin the hard graft and research on the potential for the drugs that both you and the priest - yes you were not alone in the experiment - have been under. It really is quite an interesting story behind the drugs.’ ‘And what’s that?’ ‘Well, we cannot take claim or credit to the origins of the drug. Like all other interesting mind warping and shady developments, they can be considered military research, this one particular to a German institution, though probably not the one you would think of, for the origins of the drug. Ares is the name of the project…and as for how it came into our hands, well that is something that is both interesting but something you will not hear of for now.’ ‘So how can I become one of you?’ My question was more pressing and urgent, eager to know how I could make an impression and begin to finally get out of the rut. ‘It’s simple. You must prove your wish to become one of us by acting like us. There is a man through this door. His fate has been decided and you must see it through.’ He said no more, merely pointed at the door which I had come through. With no hesitation in my mind, I walked back through it into the darkness. This time however the darkness wasn’t absolute, rather it was slightly dull, the kind of darkness your eyes adjust to. When I did, I saw my target. ‘Priest.’ ‘Jack? I had not expected to see you so soon.’ ‘Remember what I told you priest?’ ‘That you would have proved God? Yes I do, why?’ ‘I’m your god.’
