... Dire & Ever-Circling Wolves

24 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/24/06 Anton breathed outside, tilting his head up and regarding the night sky. He had his hands jammed into the pockets of his dark blue jeans, but a good bit of the dark skin on his arms and, of course, his face stood out against the air. It was colder than it would have been at daytime, but still warm relative to more Northeastern or Midwestern parts of the country. It was good weather for Anton. The night was still and quiet if one could manage to block out the sounds of the pulsing rave that went on just past the stone wall that Anton was propped against; years of training had honed the Nigerian's focus enough so that he could. A small swish came from his right and he turned his gaze that way, eyeing the slender woman that slipped out to join him in the night. She smoothed her tartan skirt and looked up at him. He remembered her from inside, remembered barely touching her, remembered that jet black hair of hers. She smiled at him and he noticed that her eyes were a bit redder than they had been earlier. He fought down a sneer as she leaned next to him, yet far enough away that they didn't come in danger of touching each other. ''You never explained him,'' she said quietly, in an accent that marked English as her native tongue. ''Does he have something to hide?'' Anton shook his head, pushing himself away from the wall. Her questions weren't for him to answer. ''I have nothing to reveal,'' Anton said curtly, his African accent laid on thick. ''I'm going for a walk.'' He set off down the sidewalk that led away from the squat building both of them had recently exited, dipping his head and trying to keep away from her, but she caught up quickly and was soon just a tad behind him. Her steps were quicker—they had to be because of Anton's long strides—but she didn't seem to tire. ''Stop going so fast,'' she demanded. ''You're being a buzzkill.'' ''Well, enjoy it by yourself,'' Anton told her, a bit more harshly than he really wanted. She didn't let it deter her, though, managing to hurry up in front of him. She stopped, tottering a bit at the quick halt, but managed not to lose too much ground. Anton decided against running over and instead met her bleary gaze. ''You're running away from me,'' she observed. ''Are you scared?'' ''Of a little woman named Amber?'' Anton asked. He smiled, but not fully. ''Unless you have gun, I don't know why would I be scared.'' His eyes did not give his lie: there was no lie to be given. Amber pouted a bit, stepping towards him and shoving him a bit. Anton didn't move at all. ''So, why don't you tell me about him?'' Amber asked. ''Because I don't have anything to tell you,'' Anton said, exasperated. ''His name is Luis. That is all. Just Luis.'' Amber gave him a wry look. ''So he doesn't have a last name, then?'' she asked. Anton snorted. ''Ask him yourself,'' he said. ''Now move.'' ''And what if I don't want to?'' Anton noticed a mischevious twinkle in her eye. She planted her fists on her hips, acting as if the words were a shield that would protect her. She was taunting him behind this invisible bulwark. Anton's eyes narrowed and he reached forward, grabbing her around the waist. She shrieked playfully as he settled her onto his shoulder. ''I don't care what you want,'' Anton said, starting off again. He felt her weight on his shoulder, but he'd carried much more before. She kicked her legs and drummed her fists on his upper back, but he knew it was all a show. Soon enough, she relaxed as best she could in the sack-of-meat pose that Anton had her in. The only sounds she made were slight grunts from being jostled and Anton supposed that the weed had kicked in fully by this time. Wandering, Anton brought them to the midst of a wooded park. He pitched forward slowly, settling her down on a bench. He straightened and looked at Amber. She was regarding him with a calm look that would normally come from someone who hadn't just been carried for what was certainly near a mile. Anton didn't budge, meeting her gaze dispassionately. She relaxed, lounging into the curved wood frame without taking her eyes off of the Lion. It was almost disconcerting the way she stared into Anton's eyes, but he had already resolved not to let her get any sort of advantage. ''Okay, then,'' she said finally, almost tripping over her words. ''Let's talk about you since you don't want to talk about… Lou?'' Anton fought back a smile. She'd forgotten Luis already. ''If you wish,'' Anton said, making his voice blank. ''Sit down,'' she said, frowning a bit. She patted the seat next to her and looked back up at him hopefully. When he didn't move, she sighed. ''A real buzzkill. I'm gonna have to dope you up when you're not looking.'' ''Please don't,'' Anton said. She smiled at him again. He found himself almost living from smile to smile. ''Why are you so clean and your friend so dirty?'' Amber asked him. She made as if she wanted to sit forward, but decided that the bench was too comfortable. ''Are you an evangelic… evangelical or something?'' Anton shook his head fiercely. ''I'm a fighter,'' Anton told her. Amber seemed to roll the word around her mouth, working it between her teeth like a marble. ''Like… people?'' ''Yes.'' It was an effort not to sound overly exasperated. For all of her prettiness, however, Anton thought she might become a bit tiring. ''Oh,'' she said, trying to picture it, as if Anton was not the very image of a lean martial artist. Fleetingly, Anton thought the hesitation might be because of his color: not only was he not Asian, he was African. ''Why do you get into something like that?'' Anton grunted and shook his head, turning a bit to look down the narrow path that they had come on. ''Because,'' he said simply. He'd been asked the question too many times to give more of an answer than that. He was tired of the question. Amber knew that he was dodging it—even in her state, it was a flimsy response—and she shifted a bit. ''Because wha—'' Her question was cut off as her attention was drawn by something. Her jaw remained opened and even dropped wider and she scooted away from her edge of the bench. Anton turned around, fists clenched and jaw set. He didn't know this area too well, but there were always thieves and idiots who thought that holding a knife would be enough to get them any amount of money they wanted. He squinted, dark brown eyes not gathering much light in the barely moonlit night. ''Whatchoo lookin' at?'' came a familiar voice. The shadow coalesced into the form of Luis Ferrara, his jacket pulled tight around his body. His neck twitched a bit and his head tilted to the side in time, but other than that he seemed normal, if a bit looser. He glanced over at Amber with a sly smile and Anton's brow set defensively, but Amber recoiled with near revulsion. ''Who's the broad?'' ''Her name is Amber,'' Anton said slowly. ''You bring 'er out into the park to bang 'er?'' Luis asked incredulously. He snorted. ''Guess it's a little romantic, but fuckin' was never about romance, y'understand?'' Anton's eyes flashed with anger and Luis took a step back. Sex was always a touchy subject, especially after the episode with Anton's sister. ''But yeah, if romance is what ya want—'' ''Nothing is happening,'' Anton said firmly. Luis nodded, but he looked over at Amber to see if she thought the same. She had obviously spaced out, her face too blank to even be considered as a quarter-way attentive. Sighing, Luis ran his hand through his hair. ''Tomorrow at noon,'' Luis said to Anton, pushing Amber out of his mind. Anton arched an eyebrow. ''I've got a favor to do for somebody, which means you've got a favor to do, y'understand?'' ''What is it?'' Anton asked. Luis frowned a bit as Anton used the three-word crowbar. ''Shipping,'' Luis said. He turned around on his heel and stalked off, hands shoved into his pockets. Anton sniffed indignantly before turning back to Amber. To his surprise, Amber was looking straight at him. ''I don't like that guy,'' she confided to Anton, almost in a whisper. ''He's dirty. Greasy.'' ''He's my manager,'' Anton said slowly. Her expression didn't change except to give Anton a taste of the disapproving look she'd been using to stare after Luis. ''I knew he was connected to you,'' Amber said. ''I didn't know why, though. You followed him into the room. Why? You could beat him up so easily. You could be your own.'' Anton shut his eyes, taking a deep breath. She said it as if Anton hadn't thought about it. She said it like Anton hadn't thought about striding out alone, free of Luis's influence, free of his money-grubbing ways. Anton shook his head slowly. ''Because.'' That was just the way, the why, some things had to be. Because. ''You've got some skills with those hands, kid,'' Luis had said to him in blistering Spanish. ''You could go places, win some big money.'' ''I know,'' Anton had said. He was focusing on getting out, trying to get into his cab and go to the hotel he was staying at, but this small local man was blocking him. ''You heard of the Golden Gloves?'' ''Boxing.'' ''Right.'' Luis grinned at him. ''We got a thing like that, too. We call it the Knuckles of Motherfuckin' Blood.'' Anton's eyebrow had raised up until he thought it might tear off his face. ''Uh… huh.'' Chuckling, Luis had clapped him on the shoulder, told him it was a joke. Anton had laughed a bit. ''But seriously, man, in Venezuela, you could be a big star. Knock out a couple of guys, find yourself in a lot of money.'' ''Yeah.'' Anton was starting to tire of this guy. ''But you know, man, in a place like this… you could piss a lot of people off, understand?'' Anton had narrowed his eyes at the little man. ''I can handle myself.'' Luis had grinned. His grin was different, then; mischevious. ''Oh, can you?'' ''You said yourself I had good hands.'' ''But that won't help you with the guys who run these things,'' Luis had explained. ''Vicous guys. You knocked off one of their champions, one of their favorites. They'll be looking for you.'' ''Thugs.'' Anton had remembered snarling. ''I'll fuck 'em up.'' ''No, kid, no you won't,'' Luis had cautioned. ''Not if you want to fight in Venezuela again. Not if you want to live. Sure, for a little while, you might be a hero, but you'll be a fugitive hero, and the law ain't gonna do shit. And eventually…'' Luis had put two fingers up to Anton's forehead and made a low pistol sound—BWOOOW!—as he pulled them away. ''… they'll get you. Hands won't help against a gun.'' Anton had consciously fought the lump that wanted to sink down his throat. ''And how do I tell them to lay off?'' ''Let me do the talking, kid,'' Luis had said, clapping him on the shoulder. He had chuckled. ''You're a Lion out there, but you don't know how to creep like a rat. Lion's a big target, y'know.'' ''I know.'' ''Easy to get shot at.'' ''I know.'' ''Easy to ki—'' ''I know.'' Luis had grinned. He still remembered that specific wily grin. ''You got some skills with those hands, kid. Let's see if you know enough to keep your mouth shut tight.''
