Trick or Treat

7 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Nov 7/06 Cedric Riley was in Spokane, Washington, on October 31st for AWC's Fresh! special, and was stuck in a crisis, not to be confused with his most recent loss. It was Halloween, and the ex-agent had failed to embrace the Hallmark holiday by forgetting to purchase a costume. This was a significant problem, as all AWC wrestlers were expected to wear one for the event. It was subtly ironic in remembrance that wrestlers put on a different face every show regardless of the day. Needless to say, the man known to AWC followers as AgentDash was in a bit of a hurry as he left his hotel room only hours before the opening theme song of Fresh!. Yellowcard's “Way Away” was what would decide if Cedric were outlawed from the Spokane Arena, which he certainly wasn't keen on. As if to increase the burden, Cedric had no idea what kind of costume to get, or where to purchase it. Perhaps fate was on his side as he walked briskly out of the hotel and spotted AWC's Transatlantic champion, Garbage Bag Johnny. There the Dirtiest Dude in AWC stood, waiting for a lull in traffic, as if planning to jaywalk across the street. Cedric caught up to him just before he was about to bound across the four lanes, by taking a hold of his hairy, greasy arm. The ex-agent winced at the feeling, but he felt that Bag's eccentric nature would aid in him finding a suitable costume. “Geroff me!” Bag cried in surprise, turning around to face up to Cedric. “Sorry, er .. Mr. Bag ... I was just-” “How do you know my name?!” Johnny interrupted. “Er ... I'm in AWC with you. We've wrestled against each other at least five times,” Cedric responded. “Oh, right,” Garbage Bag said, looking at the sidewalk as he paused for a thought. “Did I win?” “Yes,” Cedric admitted bitterly. “Aha! I knew it!” Johnny spat at him with a toothy grin. “So what did you want again?” “I haven't told you yet-” Johnny cut in, “Well get a move on! If I'm late for that thing tonight, I won't get paid...” he said worriedly. The Transatlantic champ was in a bit of a tight spot for money at the time, due to a bet lost against the evil Pierce Lavelle. “I need help buying a costume. I don't know where to buy one or what to get ...” Cedric said worriedly. Garbage Bag thought about it, then asked, “What's in it for me?” Cedric hadn't thought about that. “Erm ... a pack of smokes?” He considered it. “Paul Malls.” “Great,” Cedric said excitedly. “What store did you have in mind?” “Store? Who needs one of those?” Johnny scoffed, beginning to walk down the sidewalk. “Come with me.” Cedric obliged, following the superior AWC'ian through the mass of people that dominated the sidewalk. Garbage Bag lead him to a nearby alley, which was vacant, and the ex-agent followed him inside. Garbage Bag stood proudly beside the nearest dumpster, and ran his hands over the top as if it were his pride and joy. Cedric assumed that it was Johnny's sleeping arrangements for Spokane. “Isn't it special?” Garbage Bag asked, grabbing the lid and heaving it open. The noise from lid slamming against the brick wall behind it was loud and almost frightened Cedric. “Er ... it's a dumpster,” Cedric stated plainly. “You'd be surprised at the good stuff you find in these,” Johnny assured him, right before grabbing the ledge of the garbage disposal and jumping inside. Cedric couldn't help himself and looked interestingly into the space, where Bag had prepared a cozy 'bed' of some sort. Garbage was scattered here and there, but mostly contained in corner of the dumpster. Johnny scrounged through the pile and brought out a medium-sized box, in which he looked delighted to find. “Here it is,” Johnny said. “I knew this would come in use. I would wear it, except I have something much better in store for everyone!” “And what's that?” Cedric spoke into the dumpster as Bag shuffled around. “That would ruin the surprise now, wouldn't it?” “Right,” the ex-agent admitted through his teeth, feeling outsmarted by Garbage Bag Johnny, of all people. Garbage Bag Johnny ripped open the box excitedly, which was bare cardboard and bore no indication of its contents. The box appeared to be taped together, though, as if it were already opened. Sure enough, Johnny didn't seem surprised when a clutter of green material fell into his hands. Cedric looked curiously at the ball of light green, which he assumed to be a costume of some sort. While he was skeptical about dressing up in something that came out of a dumpster, he certainly didn't want to be the only one on Fresh! without irregular garments to show off. Johnny waved out the costume and it fell into its normal form – an outfit that resembled Kermit the frog from the Muppets, a famous cartoon that appealed to children. Bag wore a satisfied grin at his belonging, which he was willing to give up for a pack of Paul Malls. He seemed reluctant as Cedric offered to take it from him, which was a part of the deal, but eventually forfeited it. The one who planned on donning the costume examined it closely, as if looking for any spots of grime or dirt on Kermit from the dumpster. “This is actually good ... I'm surprised you found it in a dumpster,” Cedric said adoringly. “I didn't find it, I made it,” Garbage Bag corrected pleasingly. Cedric looked at him, then to this costume, and back at Bag with a raised eyebrow. “Serious?” “No,” Johnny admitted as he moved towards the dumpster ledge and lifted himself out. He closed the lid and wiped his hands off on his shorts, then turned back to Cedric. “But I had you going, didn't I? Now where's my smokes.” “Right,” Cedric replied to the demand, reaching for his wallet. “Oh no,” Garbage Bag said. “You have to buy them for me!” “Wh-” “Never you mind! Let's go,” Johnny interrupted, taking lead in the direction of the street they just came from. Cedric followed, too puzzled to ask questions. Him and Johnny reentered the crowded sidewalk, and Bag headed right for the middle of the street. Ignoring the crosswalk that was only a short trip to his left, he scurried across during break in traffic. With no choice but to follow suit, Cedric did, though the temptation to run with the costume was almost overruling. The ex-agent held a certain amount of respect for Bag that he hadn't quite figured out, and would buy his cigarettes accordingly. After darting across the street, Cedric followed Bag around the corner of the junction. A half-block later, Bag stopped and pointed to a convenience store across the street. “There,” Bag said. “That's the store?” Cedric wondered, looking across at the shotty gas-bar. He noticed a group of drifters hanging around, suspiciously watching anyone that entered the store. “Yeah ... what are you waiting for?” “Er ..” Cedric began, “Okay, let's go.” “I'm going to stay here,” Johnny informed. “Why?” “Because,” stated Bag inconclusively. “I don't like the looks of those punks hanging around ...” Cedric began worriedly. “Why? You're a wrestler, aren't you?” Johnny asked. “Are you scared?” “Yes!” Cedric responded to the 'wrestler' question, then realized what had just happened. “I mean ... no! ... Why are you so worried about coming with me?” “Er ... no reason,” replied Johnny, pausing. “Fine .. let's go.” Johnny lead Cedric across the street in another jaywalk, and they walked up to the convenience store. They both went inside, hurrying past the group Cedric noticed earlier. Going into the store, Johnny seemed to have a destination in mind that was the back of the store. Cedric ignored him and went to the counter. “How can I help you?” the clerk asked. He was a pimply man, about mid-thirties, with greasy black hair and a goatee. His arms bulged in his uniform, which looked awkward on a man who would normally look normal squeezed into a Led Zepplin tee-shirt. “Can I get a pack of ...” Cedric began, but not before forgetting what brand Johnny wanted. “Hey Bag!” he called to the back of the store. “Huh? ... Oh! .. what?” Johnny responded, as if surprised that someone was paying attention to him as he huddled behind a chip rack. “What kind did you want again?” I asked. “Oh ... Paul Malls,” he replied in a hurry. Cedric turned around to the counter to see that the clerk had already retreated to the cupboard of cigarettes. He got the Malls and set them on the counter, then routinely asked, “Anything else?” “No thanks,” the customer said, reaching for his wallet. He pulled out a five dollar bill and offered it to the clerk. “These are ten dollars,” the clerk said sternly. “Oh ... right,” Cedric responded, almost appalled at the price. The price of cancerous agents these days! Cedric handed him another bill and took the cigarettes from the counter, then turned to the door, where Bag was waiting to head out. “Hey! Are you going to pay for those chips?” shouted the clerk. Cedric turned to him simultaneously as Bag opened the door and ran out. In a moment of confusion, Cedric turned as well and bolted out the door, after hearing the clerk jump the counter behind him and follow, running after Johnny, who shoplifted a bag of chips. Seeing Johnny heading for the sidewalk, Cedric ran towards him, still unsure of what to do. “What the hell's going on?!” a man from the gang outside shouted. “That fucker stole a bag of chips! From my store!” the clerk shouted angrily, apparently very passionate and offensive about his business. “Which one?!” another man yelled. “It doesn't matter!” a large man announced. His demeanor suggested that he was the leader of this gang, as he stepped forward with confidence, mashing the knuckles of one beefy hand into the palm of his other. Warrior shouts of approval ensued the battle cry. They all pushed forward at full-speed towards their targets, one man out in front of the rest. The individual seemed rather quick. Garbage Bag was almost to the sidewalk by then, and Cedric was on his tail. As a finely-tuned athlete, Cedric was gaining on Bag with each step. Johnny was moving, though – running on adrenaline and ignoring the little detail that he smoked a pack of cigarettes per day. Bag took an unexpected turn to his right, avoiding the sidewalk, and instead headed towards a fence. It was a lower fence, but nonetheless it took quite the leap for Johnny to get over it, which he did smoothly, as if that weren't his first time. By then Cedric had abandoned the box that he held – his former costume for the evening, and was able to clear the fence as well. Cedric quickened his pace and soon caught up to Johnny, who was losing steam. They finally came to the street, and after a quick look in both directions, Cedric bolted across. Just about to step over the curb and onto the sidewalk, he chanced a look over his shoulder to see where Johnny was. SCREEEEEEEEEEECH! A blue sports car came to an abrupt stop, locking its brakes and melting the tire-tread to the pavement. Johnny barely avoided the bumper by shifting his closest leg at the last second to an awkward stance, and with his forward momentum, stumbled over his feet and tripped over the hood on the driver's side. Cedric stopped in his tracks and turned around, debating whether he should go to Johnny's aid. Considering that the Transatlantic champion's antics had gotten him into this mess, it would have been explainable to submit to the temptation he had to run away. But the gang that was gaining on the fallen Garbage Bag looked keen on an ass-kicking, and Cedric wasn't a fan of “leaving a man behind”. The door to the car opened suddenly and the driver stepped out, whom nobody had paid attention to in the confusion of Garbage Bag almost getting killed while simultaneously being hunted down by a gang. “HOLY SHIT! CAN A GUY NOT DRIVE IN THIS DAMN CITY?!” Holy shit is right. “The Illustrious Face Eater” Adam Dick had joined the festivities, and he was pissed. PRIME's guest superstar for that evening's Fresh! was clearly on his way to the arena. “Is that you, Bag?! WHAT THE FUCK ARE YOU DOING? We have a tag match tonight, and you are fucking out playing in traffic!” Dick berated the man who he almost just killed. “Uh ... Dick ... we kind of got a problem,” Cedric piped up from behind him. Facey wheeled around. “And who the fuck are you? Get the fuck out of my sight, I have a crisis here.” “I'm with Garbage Bag. We're on our way to the arena, and kind of in a bit of a tight spot,” Cedric explained, his eyes flashing towards the gang that was nearing them while they wasted seconds of precious time. “See, there's some people chasing after us.” “Come on ... we can fucking take them. Hell, I can fucking take-” and he turned around in the direction from which the gang was coming from. “HOLY FUCK.” He paused in amazement at the pursuers. “Get in the damn car!” Without hesitation, Cedric and Adam went into action, each hooking an hand under Johnny's armpits. They heaved him into the back of the car, a process that seemed more tedious than it really was with the pending beat-down they would get from the convenience-store-junkies. Adam jumped into the driver's seat as Cedric launched himself across the hood. He landed on the other side as the leader was only a few steps away, and had his arm reared back. Cedric tumbled through the open window instead of using the door and Facey hit the gas, burning rubber and leaving the adversaries in their adversaries in smoke. “What the fuck happened back there?!” Dick demanded, shifting the Honda into third gear as he raced down the road. “Mr. Champ here in the back stole a bag of chips,” Cedric responded with a hint of sarcasm. Adam laughed. “Good one, Johnny. You fucking bum.” He paused for a response, keeping his eyes on the road. “Johnny?” Cedric and Facey turned around to see Johnny in the back, swirled in smoke and tugging away at a bong. Cedric looked in shock as Johnny pulled away from it, coughing only slightly after a long hit. “Jesus Christ, Bag!” yelled Adam. Cedric nodded in agreement as Dick turned his eyes back to the road. He looked in the rear-view mirror and demanded, “Give me some of that shit!” Cedric didn't know what to say, but Johnny pocketed the bong regardless. “Sorry, man,” Bag apologized in a spaced-out manner. “I'm out.” “FUCK!” Facey screamed, then took a moment's pause as if to think. “AHA!” he said with glee, as if to imply he experienced an epiphany. “We need to make a short stop before the arena, if you know what I mean.” Cedric looked at his watch. “What?! We're almost late!” “It's an emergency,” Facey returned casually. The ex-agent sighed and fell back into his seat. It was only a few minutes' drive later and Dick pulled the car to a stop outside of a shady-looking bar of some kind, which had its fair share of junkies hanging outside of it. “Okay, Jeremy, I need you to go inside and pick up something for me ...” Facey began after turning to his passenger “What?!” Cedric responded. “Why do I have to do it?” He looked worriedly at the shoddy bar. “I saved your life!” Dick retorted. Cedric paused and looked menacingly at the AWC legend, then to the back where Johnny was laughing at and curiously examining a spider that crawled on the window. After an intense moment, Cedric made his decision. He sighed. “Fine,” he said, opening his door and turning to leave. “Excellent!” Facey said, rubbing his hands together. “Just ask for Boris, and tell whoever that you're there for me. You should get a bag of weed ... the son of a bitch owes me one.” Cedric exited the vehicle and closed the door behind him. Needless to say, he didn't look happy as he approached the front door of the pub, and neither did the men standing outside at the fact he was invading their territory. Cedric tried to keep a straight face as he pushed open the door and entered the bar. It was a typical scenario – a dimmed room full of pool tables, old slot machines, and booths. It was characterized by that stingy smell, not uncommon in such places as a result of the heavy amount of smoking. The smell of stale beer also invaded the intruder's nostrils, making the contents turn in his stomach. The customers were mostly men who played for the next round of drinks on the pool table, but Cedric spotted a man and a woman talking over beers in one of the booths. He walked cautiously up to the bar, where a few more men leaned over the counter on their elbows. They grasped mugs of foamy lager while watching a football game on the dusty television above. It was a quiet bar at this time of day, although Cedric was sure the psuedo-bikers probably got fairly rowdy when sun went down. Choosing not to sit down for fearing of being off guard, Cedric stood by the edge of the counter, waiting for the bartender's attention. The woman behind the bar was attractive in a cowgirl sort of way, and she looked rather out of place in such a setting. Her cowboy hat, plaid long-sleeve shirt, and fit jeans were enough to get Cedric to look twice. “May I help you?” she asked. “Yeah ... um ... I'm here to see a man named Boris?, to pick up something for Adam Dick,” Cedric said. The bartender didn't say anything immediately. She was noticeably hesitant, but finally said, “Give me a moment, please.” She finished wiping down a beer glass and placed it on one of the racks, then walked down a hallway to Cedric's right that he assumed lead to the bathrooms and the cliche “VIP” room, where all the real gambling and 'other activities' took place. A short wait later that consisted of Cedric lightly rapping his knuckles on the counter to distract him from the sketchy people in the bar, the bartender returned. “It's right down the hall there ... last door on the left,” she informed him. Cedric thanked her, then followed her directions. He came to the door and opened it cautiously. He entered a room that was, if possible, smokier than the one he had just came from. A few men with slicked-back hair and formal suits puffed away at cigars as they sat around a table. Cedric could only assume what was being discussed. He decided not to be the first one to say something, and waited. “So, you're here for Adam Dick ... ?” said the man across the room, who sat intimidatingly with his legs crossed. He took a drag of his cigar, tapped out the ashes, then looked back at the ex-agent. “That man was never one to send others to clean up his business.” Cedric shrugged. “Well, I'm just here for the pick up ...” “The pick up?” the man whom resembled a mafia leader repeated uncertainly. His unbelieving tone was confirmed by the rest of the men who occupied the room – they all looked strangely at the visitor. “Yeah?” Cedric said uncertainly. The man re-lit his cigar and billowed smoke out the end., then extracted it from his mouth. “I'm not quite sure I understand your intent,” he explained. “You see, Mr. Dick is in debt to me. While the amount is not what I'd consider great, it has been a long wait.” Cedric looked like he had just run into a wall. His mouth slightly agape as if he was in shock – his eyes mimicked those of a lost dog. “I – I'm not quite – I'm confused,” he stammered out. “I'm just – I'm just the messenger!” “Ah, mister ... it is unfortunate that you are in this position. But I am a powerful man. This has become a pressing issue since I learned of Mr. Dick's return to Spokane, and I expected him to come! I see he has followed through, although in a questionable circumstance ... therefore, I must insist that you pay me the two-hundred dollars,” the man elaborated. His grin was one that indicated he wanted Cedric to leave the bar unscathed, but his money seemed to be the more important issue. “But – but – but ... I got screwed into this!” Cedric explained uselessly. The man across the table looked at the two other men closer to Cedric and gave them a nod as if to signal for something. Their chairs scraped the ground as they stood up, turning to the ex-agent. Cedric readied himself for combat ... he knew he could easily take them down with two or three strikes, allowing his easy escape. But he hesitated – he didn't know why, but fighting was no longer his answer. He had come to realize that the agency had turned him into a monster of self-defense, when he could easily resolve this problem in a much more sensible way. He reached into his pocket and pulled out his wallet. Thankful he had stocked up on American cash for the evening, allowing him to pull out a hundred dollars. The two men sat back down – they seemed let-down after getting their knuckles warm for a pounding. Cedric approached the table and threw down the hundred dollars. “A hundred,” he said simply, without hesitation nor confidence. “That's all I got.” The man across the table looked at the wad of cash curiously, then to Cedric. He then reached for the bills and sorted through them, adding them up. He set the back down and left his elbows on the table. Observing Cedric, he smiled as if a result of discovery. “What kind of watch is that you're wearing?” he asked, interested. Cedric sighed. Trying to avoid any further delay to his arrival at Fresh! (the watch was a painful reminder of that), he removed the Rolex and set it on the table. “See for yourself.” The man picked up the watch and examined it, then gave it to his cronies, who also looked at it. “Seems genuine,” one of them grunted. Cedric fought the temptation to roll his eyes. The man placed the Rolex aside with the cash and beamed. “Thankyou, sir. It has been a pleasure doing business with you.” Cedric didn't stay for any cliched goodbyes, and instead walked briskly out of the room. He couldn't decide if that man was actually dangerous or just a wannabe, but he seemingly could care less. No longer an agent, but a professional, he would continue going to lengths to escape his former lifestyle. He escaped the muggy bar and embraced the fresh air. Outside, his expectations were proved true – the sports car was gone. Cedric let out an exasperated sigh. He had been played for a fool by the Illustrious Face Eater, and the trickery was certainly well-thought. It had occurred to him that Garbage Bag Johnny may have been involved all along in the devious plot that resulted in the ex-agent having to forfeit a hundred dollars cash and a Rolex, but on the other hand, maybe he didn't. In any case, Cedric began the long trek back to his hotel. With no money, a taxi wasn't an option, and there was no way he would make Fresh! on time anyway. Therefore, added on to his shortage of a gold watch and hundred dollars, he would go without one week's pay, which was quite upsetting. All he could say to himself was: “That fucking Dick.”
