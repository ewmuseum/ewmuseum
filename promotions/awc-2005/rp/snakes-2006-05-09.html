Snakes

9 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/9/06 ''What do you see in him?'' Nneka asked in semi-fluid English. She leaned over from the far edge of the couch where she was seated and placed her elbow on the arm of it, resting her chin on her palm. She was dressed casually, in snug jeans and a deep red babydoll tee, her hair as wild as it ever had been. Her eyes centered on the admittedly cute white woman in a short dress on the couch nearby. The corner of Nneka's lips and eyebrow lifted with the question. ''Really.'' ''Really?'' Amber answered, glancing up at the just-shut door. She smiled; Amber was a sugary girl, a bit too saccharine for Nneka. She'd desperately wanted to ask Anton what he saw in her, but she'd shake her brother down later. Amber shrugged and rested her chin in her hands. Her fingers twisted elegantly in a fashion that Nneka was embarrassed to realize she was trying to copy. ''I don't know. He just makes me feel good.'' ''So he is a good lay?'' Nneka blurted. She wanted to cover her mouth, especially when Amber giggled a bit, but she didn't. ''You're interested if your brother is a good lay, are you?'' Amber asked. Nneka flushed and found herself glad the color wasn't all that visible on her dark cheeks. ''Well, yes, he is, but that's not what I mean. I don't know if you'd understand.'' ''Do you think I'm little?'' Nneka said in a half-growl. Amber shook her head. ''No, I—'' She sighed; Nneka was glad to see a bit of uncertainty on her part. ''I'm sorry. I didn't mean to say it like that.'' ''I'm sure you are sorry.'' They were quiet for a bit, Amber trying to avoid Nneka's eyes and Nneka trying to study the woman. She was sure that Amber was simply being diplomatic; she wouldn't want to annoy the sister of her fuckbuddy. Nneka didn't want to call them boyfriend-and-girlfriend or, even more severe, lovers. Nneka didn't want to think that such a simple girl could entrap her brother. ''Why does Anton hang around Luis?'' Amber asked suddenly, turning her blue eyes on Nneka. Nneka blinked and sat back a bit, letting her arm slip under her breasts. Unconsciously, she pushed them up and out, to boast that she was a bit more full-chested than Amber. She wanted every bit of leverage she could get over the sugar-lady. ''Luis is a good guy,'' Nneka answered reflexively. Amber narrowed her eyes, but Nneka didn't back down. ''He does what he needs to do, what Anton figures he can't. He manages things that Anton would rather not get his hands dirty with.'' Amber tossed her hair, pushing a bit over one ear. She re-crossed her legs. ''Seems like a snake,'' she said. It was Nneka's turn to narrow her eyes. ''Don't talk like that about Luis,'' Nneka snapped. Amber eyed her and sniffed. ''Just 'cause he's fucking you doesn't mean you should get pissy,'' Amber warned. ''After all, it's good to notice your man's… faults.'' ''What do you want?'' Her sugary smile returned and Nneka felt her stomach thrashing. ''What's best for Anton.'' ''And you think you can do a better job than Luis.'' ''I don't think. I know.'' Nneka's mouth tightened. She had it right with the first sentence, but the second should have been thrown out. Luis threw a wild punch that connected with Anton's jaw. The taller man staggered, unused to the small Venezuelan putting so much behind his admonitory strikes. He gave Luis a heated glare in return. Luis withered just a tad, but it was enough that Anton knew he wasn't going to try something like that again in the near future. Luis straightened his white zoot suit, the snarl never leaving his face. ''Fuck you, Lion!'' Luis howled in Spanish. ''She's gonna fuckin' ruin everything I've put together for you!'' ''Are you afraid of her?'' Anton answered coldly, drawing muscled arms over his chest. He wore his dashiki to mark that he wasn't intending on fighting anyone at the moment. It was when he removed it that Luis had cause to be scared. ''Afraid of the bitch?'' Luis snarled. ''I ain't afraid of her, I'm afraid of what she's gonna do.'' ''So you are afraid--'' ''She's ripping you away, Lion! She wants to cash in on a fuckin' statement she ain't ever been a part of! Don't you get it?'' Anton shook his head. ''I don't know if you get it,'' Anton said. Luis threw up his hands and began to pace around the room, taking off his hat and scratching his well-greased black hair. ''She's with me, Luis. You think I can't be with someone because I've chosen not to for so long?'' ''She's a snake, Lion and--'' ''She's mine!'' Anton lunged forward, his big hand closing around Luis's neck as he rammed the smaller man back-first against the wall. Luis's hands came up to the Lion's wrist, but he couldn't pry himself loose. ''She's mine, Luis, and if you can't deal with it, then…'' Anton's voice trailed off. Then what? Then he'd break Luis in half? Then he'd dump Luis in the ocean? He'd never thought farther than the feeling he'd get from leaving this partnership. He'd never really considered doing it. Not like now. ''Alright,'' Luis croaked faintly. He kicked his legs; he was a good foot off the ground. ''Alright!'' Anton let Luis drop and took a step back. ''Keep the fuckin' viper 'round. She's already got her fangs in your neck, man. Don't let it distract you. You have a match.'' ''I know,'' Anton said. ''Contrary to what you might think, I can read.'' ''But you can't see, motherfucker,'' Luis growled. He pushed the door of the cramped room open and looked out into the den. Nneka and Amber seemed to be having pleasant conversation. Anton's eyes blazed into Luis's back. ''Can't see the forest for the trees. I don't know what exactly it means, but I know it's got to do with what's going on.'' Luis jammed his hat onto his head and grumbled. ''You got a match.'' And then he let the door shut. Anton took a deep breath, balling his fist tight. He wasn't listening to Luis. Luis didn't know what it was like. To have your stomach on fire, to look at someone and see something beyond the lust of the moment. To stay up nights without any thought but a name. To want to tattoo that name into your bones, into every chromosome. He didn't know what it was like and he wouldn't understand. He saw money and he saw a threat. Anton, too, saw a threat. It stung that he had known the man for so long. Anton thrust palms out, then knees, then feet, then elbows, then fists, all in a blur of combative fury. Alone on the rooftop, alone to focus, alone to train. He wasn't playing a game; his arms didn't flail like a child. He focused himself on a spot and every strike hit the spot in the air, one after another. All of his weight was thrown into each shot, but he shifted his weight fluidly so that each counted to its fullest. He imagined the spot as Luis's face. It made him want to continue fighting. Sweat rolled off of his ebon skin, enough that it seemed as if it should make a puddle on the concrete below him, but his skin was so hot that the sweat must sizzle right off. He was the perfect image of concentration, with his eyebrows drawn tight and his jaw set. Yet his mind soared to new continents of thought, seeing but never touching down. He focused, but he was not there. He did not focus on training. He did not focus on fighting. He did not focus on his match. He did not focus like he should be focusing. For once, it didn't bother him; he had to think. ''Brother?'' a timid voice in Igbo rose up. The motions slowly dwindled until he possessed himself enough to turn on his heel and face the staircase that led up here. Nneka had climbed up, dressed in her yellow shorts and tube top as if ready to train, but the way she held herself, the expression on her face, showed that she intended nothing of the like. ''Nneka,'' Anton replied. ''I thought you were sleeping. I thought everyone was sleeping.'' Anton's upper lip curled. ''Is Luis… keeping you up?'' He didn't want to say what he thought, but Nneka relieved him by shaking her head. ''I just wanted to… train,'' she lied. Anton folded his arms over his chest, eyeing his sister. She dropped her eyes and squatted near the staircase. ''You have something to say to me,'' Anton pried. She looked up at him. ''Or to ask me. Speak. I want to continue my training.'' Nneka nodded. Her hands shook and she moved them towards each other, but she made them drop before they moved too far. ''Who is this woman?'' Nneka asked. The words struck Anton, but he only barely moved his chin. ''She belongs to me,'' Anton said levelly. ''She thinks that you belong to her,'' Nneka protested. Anton thought he could see her eyes shining with tears from here, but he didn't know what he had said. ''Perhaps that is true, in a way,'' Anton admitted with a sigh. ''You would not understand.'' ''Don't say that!'' Nneka shouted. She didn't rise, but she looked as if she might. She looked angry. Anton blinked. ''That is what she told me, too, like I'm a little girl who doesn't even know how to cook yet.'' ''I didn't--'' ''She is using you! That is what Luis says and I believe him. Where did she come from?'' ''I picked her from a pit of snakes,'' Anton growled. ''And I will put you there if you do not—Nneka!'' He took a step forward, but he was nowhere near quick enough to catch her as she darted back down the stairs. Anton stood there shaking for a bit before he turned swiftly on his heel, beginning to strike the spot in the air again. Focus was lost now, and he was no longer even soaring on thought, but crashing into a sea of emotion. Anger. The sea bubbled with it. His fists flew hard, almost meeting a slap of resisting air at the force. He was lost. He would have continued into the morning, but he forced himself to stop when something came to him. He realized that he'd started imagining Nneka's face there instead of Luis's.
