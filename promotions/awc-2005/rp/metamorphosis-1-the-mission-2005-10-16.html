Metamorphosis #1 - 'The Mission'

16 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 16th, 2005 July 12th, 2005 Los Angeles, California Location: Unknown *** The clutches of darkness embraced me in the small, rectangular room like they had for the last couple of months since I had arrived. To this day, I could still recall the final blow to the cranium before I collapsed onto the concrete, the Los Angeles sun baking me from above. My eyes had become accustomed to spending prolonged periods in the dark, though, so I could make out a few objects in front of me: the steel-topped table, a chair at the other side of the table and the tiny slit in the door which provided the slightest bit of light. Fortunately, I had experience in situations like this. Despite the best effort of Knucks, the criminal organisation who essentially had full control over my life nowadays, they were finding it more than difficult to break me down. I refused to be shaped to their designs, to conform and do what I was told. Ever since I was a child, I was hard to pin down. My emotions were all over the place. One day I was on top of world, celebrating the Lakers winning; the next, down in the dumps over another failed relationship. My capture back in the month of April had come suddenly and without warning. I had been ripped away from my wrestling career and my friends. Any other man may have cracked under pressure. I, on the other hand, was determined to break out of this prison. The steady clicking of high heels echoed through the corridor outside my room of isolation. I could tell they were heading this way. I did my best to remain as composed as possible; I knew this was what my supervisor in Knucks had been warning me about. Incidentally, my supervisor, although tough, was one of the most down-to-earth men I had ever met in my life. How he ended up working for a criminal organisation responsible for the deaths of over two thousand people per annum was a mystery to me. The clicking stopped abruptly, and I gulped as the door swung open violently. The light poured in, and I squinted as a blonde-haired woman approached me menacingly. “Who - who’s that?” I ventured, fear etched in my voice. There was no response from the woman, who firstly made sure my arms were secured tightly behind my back, and then sat down opposite me at the table. She barely batted an eyelid as my eyes finally adjusted to the light. “Name,” she blurted, more a statement than a question. “Aliso,” I replied sheepishly. “My name is Tony Aliso.” Knucks were well documented as the most ruthless criminal organisation in Los Angeles. Most notorious for a killing spree known as “The Berksley Murders”, in which Knucks’ men brutally murdered twenty husbands in twenty separate families in an prominent estate in L.A. known as Berksley, they had a talent for destroying one’s identity. I would not allow them to reduce me to a drone. My will was strong, and not even this moderately attractive woman sat in front of me could change that. “Alright, Mr. Aliso, here’s the deal. You’ve been a… problem for us for the last few weeks. It can’t go on like this. Something must be done to stop this. We’ve decided amongst ourselves - “ “To resort to more aggressive therapy, I suppose?” I cut in, prompting her to sigh audibly. She nodded, confirming my guess was indeed correct. It seemed basic punishment and sensory deprivation had not been a success on me. I had expected it, of course. My time with the L.A.P.D. had provided months of training in these situations. The strange thing was that this had to be announced on my twenty-second birthday. That didn’t matter. I dared to ask what kind of increase in the punishment they had planned. Underneath my cool façade, I was afraid. Afraid that perhaps I couldn’t resist this new form of punishment. Staying cool under pressure was now the name of the game. I felt a drop of sweat roll down my forehead, such was the humidity of the room I found myself in. “You’re going to be taken to see John Wilson. Mr. Wilson has been a valuable member of Knucks for close to twenty years now. No matter how hard you try to resist, Mr. Aliso, you won’t stand a chance of negating Wilson’s tactics. He’ll do anything - anything - to get his way. That’s why I’ve come to you today…” She trailed off abruptly, giving me a moment to contemplate my options here. It was a do or die situation. I had no idea how to approach this nameless woman, let alone this vicious man who would resort to any means to bring me into the clutches of Knucks. I was certain I had heard the name Wilson before; it rang a few bells. The woman tossed her blonde hair over her shoulder and began to smile wickedly at me. “S-so, what exactly are my options?” I asked, though I doubted she would directly answer my question. “To be completely honest, you have none. Prepare yourself mentally and physically for Wilson’s ways, unless…” “Unless?” I probed, hoping for a way out of this predicament. “Unless you do a favour for me,” she said, teasing me now by pacing behind me, her sour breath landing squarely on my neck. Whenever a woman asked me for a favour, it was always one of two things. Number one, a few minutes in the bedroom or something to that effect; or number two, something downright wrong that could potentially harm someone. That was the innate quality in women I had noticed since I was a teenager - they always have an ulterior motive in store. This blonde had played the escape card. I bit. “What’s the favour? Name it.” “I want you,” she moaned, “to go on a little, let’s say, mission for me.” A mission sounded acceptable, as long as it wasn’t something absurd. I creased my brow, which was drenched in sweat, then nodded in agreement. She extended her hand to me and the light caught her lip gloss brilliantly as she introduced herself. “I’m Sarah Wilson,” she scoffed, “John’s husband. And if you don’t succeed in this little mission I’ve set you, you’re a dead duck, to put it mildly.” I gulped quietly, though I don’t believe she noticed my sign of nervousness and fright at what could be ahead of me with this so-called “mission”. Nevertheless, I planned to take it head-on, whatever it was. Mrs. Wilson left the room, bringing her file with her. The light was switched off again, and my eyes adjusted to the familiar darkness once again as I waited for the inevitable challenge that confronted me.
