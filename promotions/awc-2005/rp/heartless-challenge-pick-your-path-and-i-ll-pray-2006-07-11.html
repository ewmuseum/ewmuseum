Heartless challenge...pick your path and I'll pray.

11 July 2006

Date: 7.11.06 I wish I could tell you we met in some kind of memorable way…like it was destiny that was responsible for bringing us together. You know, like in the movies, where the guy saves the girl from a bunch of street thugs, or the girl’s car breaks down in front of her wacky but endearing future husband’s house. I suppose the story of how I met Jane O’Connor is memorable only to yours truly, and probably wouldn’t make for a very good movie. But just for shits and giggles, I’ll tell you about it anyway: It was in my psychology class, back in high school. It was my sophomore year, and it must have been about October or so…I remember, because it was right before Homecoming, and I also remember that I was on crutches at the time. I was playing hockey, and as luck would have it I took a puck off the side of my shin, just outside the shin guard’s protective padding. Any hockey player will be quick to tell you that hockey pucks must have built-in radar or something, because they seem to seek out ways to dodge the plastic shell and nail you right in the meat of the leg. They’re sadistic like that. Anyway, it was nothing too serious, but it left a lovely-colored bruise on my leg for several weeks, during which I had extreme trouble making use of said leg…hence, the crutches. A funny thing about crutches - if you’ve ever been on crutches, you know that for some reason every time you walk into a room, all eyes are on you. Some are feeling sympathetic, most are curious as to know how you ended up in such a predicament, and just about everybody is waiting to see if you can make it to where you’re headed without crashing in a heap like a douche. So as luck would have it on that day, when I walked into class all eyes were indeed focused on the poor cripple. All eyes, including the eyes of what was hands-down the most beautiful sixteen-year-old girl I ever saw. She had radiant blonde hair that came to an end just above her shoulders. From beneath the few strands of golden hair that adorned her face peered a striking pair of green eyes that could stop any man in his tracks. [withheld — a passage held under the museum's content rule] She was already sitting in her desk in the front row, so it was difficult to tell much else about her. I hadn’t seen her around before, so she had to be new to our school. [withheld — a passage held under the museum's content rule] I had no idea what to say to this girl, but I knew it had to be something. Thinking quickly, I hobbled over to where Mr. Passini posted our latest test scores, right next to one of those hokey motivational posters with a picture of a bald eagle soaring over a horizon, with some lame-ass quote written under a giant word like “DETERMINATION” or “INTEGRITY”…I can’t remember exactly what it said. As I expected, I’d bombed the test in epic fashion; Passini’s idea of teaching us psychology was forcing us to memorize every single goddamn psychological disorder there was, who discovered it, all the major symptoms and the most effective treatments. Not that these things weren’t important…but once you reach disorder #20 or so, they really all start to run together. I turned around to find the girl still watching me. I’m not sure why, but something told me that I shouldn’t let her know that I’d bombed that test, for fear she’d think I was a dumbass or something. Hard to say, exactly, the inner workings of my asinine teenage brain. But I’ll never forget what happened next. I let go of the handles of my crutches and steadied myself as best I could. I turned to look at Jane, grinned like an idiot, and gave her what surely must have been the hokiest two thumbs up in East Bay High history, Arthur Fonzarelli style. Evidently overcome by my goofiness, Jane gave me the most endearing smile I’d ever seen. I would spend the next four years trying to make her show me that smile as often as I could. *** It had begun raining, presumably sometime after Darcy started on his yage journey. When he answered the door it was now pouring down buckets, and Jane was a sopping mess. Her words still echoed through his head. Darcy…I need your help. He offered no reply to this, instead electing to stare at the woman who dared re-enter his life. Like Darcy, she too was fast approaching thirty, and she no longer bared quite the resemblance of her teenage years, when the two had first met. Her hair was still blonde, but easily twice as long, and the rain had made a point to turn it into a wet sagging mop that clung to her face and neck. Her eyes were still green and still striking, but now looked weathered. Darcy couldn’t tell whether this was due to the years since college not being terribly kind to her, or perhaps some sort of traumatic event that might have led her here, seeking his assistance. Her clothes were also soaked through as she stood at his door with no umbrella or raincoat, leading Darcy to believe that this sudden rainstorm had apparently caught Jane by surprise as much as it did him. She must have known that Darcy had had no trouble hearing what she said, electing to offer no reply herself to Darcy’s own non-reply. She also appeared to know exactly what kind of weight her request carried with him as she stood shaking on the doorstep, partly from the cold of the chilling rain, partly at seeing the ex-boyfriend she once believed was destined to father her future family. Darcy had long contemplated what he’d do if this day ever came. For many years he daydreamed about the choice words he’d have in store, how quickly he would send her packing if she ever dared to come back into her life, offering some banal excuse at an apology. And had she shown up any time other than right now, just after a giant wolf-god from another dimension had warned him of an impending visitor whose actions would have a direct impact on the rest of his life, Darcy would have been all-to-eager to slam the door in her face, never to lay eyes on her again. But fate surely must have been kind to bring Jane Elise O’Connor to his house, at this precise moment. “Come in, Jane…you’re soaking wet,” Darcy said, finally offering an answer to her plea. He still wasn’t sure he wouldn’t end up screaming at her and sending her out the door with a swift kick to the ass, but he decided that it didn’t have to be right this very second. “Thank you,” Jane replied, her voice barely above a whisper. A bolt of lightning lit up the sky around her as she entered Darcy’s abode. Darcy held up a finger in Jane’s direction (his index, although it practically pained him not to use the middle one instead), signaling he would quickly return. He did just that, with a towel in hand and a fresh T-shirt that he flipped in her direction. “You can use the bathroom…get yourself dried off, and we can talk about what the hell you’re doing here,” he said to her, rather coldly. She merely nodded, closing the door behind her. As Jane dried herself off, Darcy took a seat on the nearby sofa, running his hands through his long, significantly drier hair. He used the opportunity presented by Jane’s brief absence from the room to collect his bearings. He was surprised to find himself being able to say anything to Jane at all; yage trips usually took such a psychological toll that he found it difficult to speak for several hours, almost as if his brain had short-circuited and needed time to reboot. The fact that it was Jane, the person with whom Darcy LEAST wanted to converse with in all the world, only escalated the affair. Perhaps his anger, or perhaps his history with the girl enabled him to forego the rebooting and properly articulate himself…or perhaps fate was once again lending a helping hand. Darcy heard the familiar click of a door being opened, and he turned to see Jane re-enter the room, now much drier. She evidently had elected to dry her hair as best she could by vigorously running a towel through it, making her look like an early 1980s hair-metal bassist. She normally was not one to worry too greatly about how she looked, but the sadness Darcy could see in her eyes seemed to suggest she had far greater things occupying her mind than looking presentable for a man who wasn’t particularly happy to see her regardless. She joined him on the couch, but kept a considerable distance from him, still unwilling to look him in the eye. “Jane, I-” Darcy began, before an inner rage boiling inside stopped him in his tracks. Clenching his fists, Darcy took a deep breath, again remembering the words from the wolf-god. “I can’t tell you how…extraordinarily lucky you are that you showed up when you did. I’ve got half a mind to kick you out of here anyway, but I’ll at least give you the honor of explaining why the hell I should help you. So let’s hear it.” Jane finally looked up at Darcy, doing her best to hold back tears. “Darcy…” she answered, softly. “…do you have to be so cold?” “Cold? You…” he swiftly replied, pointing at her. “…telling ME…” turning his finger back towards himself. “…that I’M cold? That’s rich. After what you did to me…you know what, fuck it. You’ll forgive me for not being in the mood for chit chat. Tell me what this is about, or hit the bricks.” “Fine,” Jane said, looking away from Darcy once more. She let a deep sigh of her own escape her lips, readying herself for the monumental request she had for him. Her composure regained, she made it so: “Darcy…I need the ring.” *** We were 19. It was fall semester of our freshman year. I guess the year was…1996? Yeah, ’96. Good times. Remember Chumbawamba? Oh MAN, good times. Well, except for Jane. And her family. Which might as well have included me at that point. That was the year that Jane’s mom went down to cancer. She put up a hell of a fight, she really did. I hear radiation treatments are a real bitch, but she never let us see it. I can’t remember a single time seeing her without a smile on her face. I don’t think she could bear looking weak in front of her family. She still traveled with them on vacations. Still drove the kids to school. Still ran the house with the sort of iron fist that only worried, loving mothers can possess. She was in and out of the hospital for treatment pretty frequently by then, but she always talked about what she had planned when she got back. There was just one day when…well, she didn’t come back. She must have known that day was the day, too. Nobody openly talked about it, since Jane had two little brothers who were going to have things pretty rough as it was, but things were looking pretty grim. The whole family came in to the hospital to see her before she went off for another scheduled treatment. Jane knew what was going on, and she begged me to come. She said it would break her mother’s heart if she didn’t get to see us together one last time. I don’t particularly do well in those sorts of situations…I can’t stand funerals, or any other way-too-painful goodbyes. But like I said, I was practically a member of the family, and I felt obliged. Jane and everybody else went early in the morning to see her, but it was midterms, and my asshole for an anthropology professor wouldn’t let me out of a test due to a death in a family I didn’t technically share blood with. I didn’t have a car my first year in college either, so after the test Jane came back to campus and we went to the hospital together. When we got there Jane’s whole family was gathered in the room; her dad, towering above everybody at a svelte 6’5”; Jane’s two little brothers, Danny and Rory; they even brought their dog. Everybody seemed to be doing their best to pretend this was just another family gathering, but it was clear to me, as it must have been to everybody there that this would be the last time they would all be together. Well, except for the dog, probably. Eventually it came time for Jane’s mom’s treatment, and the rest of the family was making their way out of the room when she asked me and Jane to stick around. We stood by her bed, and as Jane’s mother spoke, it appeared that for the first time she was willing to show a little bit of weakness. “You two…” she spoke, softly. “So full of life. So full of love…” She bore a warm smile on her face. Jane’s eyes were welling up, and I’d be lying to you if I said my eyes weren’t getting a little misty too. Then Jane’s mom started rustling underneath the blankets, and I couldn’t tell what was going on. But then, her hand extended from beneath the blankets, and in it laid her wedding ring. That was way too much for Jane to handle, and tears were now openly running down her face. “I can’t take that, Mom…it’s too hard. I’m sorry, but it’s too hard,” she tried to tell her. “Jane…I’m not giving you this ring,” she answered, coughing slightly. “Darcy will.” And I still can’t believe what happened next. She then took my hand in hers, gripping it tightly, probably as tight as she could. When she released her grip, the ring was in my hand. It was hands down the most beautiful piece of jewelry I’d ever seen. A golden band – it must have been 24 karat, easily - had something inscribed on the inside; a person’s name I didn’t recognize. But the big prize was on top. A golden eagle’s talon clutched tightly a simply stunning sapphire that you could practically see your reflection in. I’m no appraiser, but even I could tell you that that ring was probably worth five figures. Maybe even six. Somebody definitely put a lot of time and money into making that ring catch people’s attention. Jane and I looked at each other like we’d both seen a ghost. Like I said, we were only 19. Yeah, we’d been together about three years by then, and it’s not like marrying Jane hadn’t crossed my mind. But it was still a pipe dream, way off in the future. That was a hell of a way to open up serious discussions about it. Jane tried to say something, but her mom cut her off. “I’ve seen the way you two look at each other. I know you’re young…but so was I when your father proposed to me, Janey. Maybe you haven’t figured it out yet, but this is meant to be. You two are going to be so happy together…” She then turned to look at me. “Darcy…this ring has been passed down through my family for a few generations now. My mom gave it to Jane’s father when he proposed to me. Her mom gave it to Jane’s grandpa when he proposed. I don’t expect you to give this to Janey tomorrow, but when the time is right…well, I’m sure you’ll know what to do.” She smiled again with that infectious smile. Jane sobbed, but she too found herself smiling. I was just about to join them when the doctors came back into the room, insisting that Jane’s mom really needed to get to ER. Personally, I thought the whole thing sounded crazy. Jane and I were just kids…not even old enough to drink. Women, man…I swear. Between Jane’s mom dropping that bomb on me, Ames asking me to do it in Rio, my own mother…I don’t know what it is. Apparently there’s big business to be made in me getting married and they all bought stock. And as things ended up turning out, Jane and I had no business getting married…not at 19, not now or any age. But there was no time left to argue...not that I had the heart to anyway, with Jane’s mom looking so happy. Who was I to deny a mother’s dying wish? She died later that day. Everybody was holding out hope that they’d get to see her again, but I knew it wasn’t to be. I could tell by what happened when they wheeled her out of that room. Jane couldn’t bear to watch, and buried her face into my chest as I did my best to console her. My eyes were locked with the eyes of the woman who, despite everything, seemed only disappointed that she wouldn’t live on as my future mother-in-law. And I swear to you, as she disappeared behind the door…I never saw anybody who looked so completely and totally at peace. *** “The ring? Why?” Jane stared at him. Darcy immediately surmised that it would probably be a long story, one that Jane was probably loathe to share. If that’s all she wants, the ring, Darcy thought to himself, we probably don’t have to go through it.“Nevermind…” he said. “It’s your family ring, you don’t-” “Daddy’s been in a tailspin ever since Mom died,” Jane interrupted, evidently ready to share the story after all. “He’s spent the last ten years turning to all sorts of vices. Drinking. Womanizing. Worst of all…yachting. I swear, I have an easier time seeing him come home with some floozie than parading on top of a plutocrat’s houseboat like some kind of bigshot. But none of them he enjoyed quite as much as he did gambling…” Darcy nodded. Steve O’Connor, Jane’s dad, earned himself quite a bit of money in the telecommunications business, back when Silicon Valley was still in its infancy. He hated the work, but he garnered enough wealth that he was not only able to retire in his 40s, but enough that to move his family out to the affluent Bay area and still have plenty of cash to toss around. Darcy had an uncle of his own whom fell prey to blowing a fortune gambling, and he had a pretty good idea where Jane was going with this. “At first it was innocent. A few hundred bucks here or there, usually at the Indian casinos or the horse races. But it got worse. He started taking frequent trips to Vegas. Money started getting really tight, if you can believe that. I was happy when he sold that damn yacht, but then…he started selling everything. Danny and Rory don’t know, and I’m not supposed to know either, but he’s run up some massive debts. With the wrong sort of people.” “Like who?” She shrugged. “All I know is that some men came by his house the other day, and, and-” The words seemed to get caught in her throat as she became choked up by emotion. “Did they hurt him?” Darcy asked, attempting to finish the sentence for her. She could only nod, her face now scrunched as she was fighting the best she could to hold back the tears. “I tried to call the police, but Daddy stopped me. He said if the authorities got involved, not only would they hurt him more…but they’d hurt me, too. And my brothers,” she said, with a sniffle. “And you want to hawk the ring to pay off your dad’s debts?” Darcy inquired. “Yeah…it’s a shitty thing to do, I know. But I know I can’t bear to keep that ring, and I can’t imagine you still want to, either. Besides…Daddy’s up shit creek, and I don’t know what else to do.” “I don’t know about this…” Darcy said, with a frown. “This would break your mother’s heart.” “Mom’s dead,” Jane swiftly replied, matter-of-factly. “And if I don’t do something soon, her entire family might be joining her. So that was that. No apology for practically stabbing him in the heart. No apology for sleeping with one of his best friends, either. No burning desire to rectify the gross injustices she’d committed against the man she’d confessed her love for more times than Darcy could count. No, the extenuating circumstances notwithstanding, Jane had only shown up on his doorstep because she needed something. “Let’s get something straight,” Darcy said after several moments to reflect on the situation. “I still hate you for what you did to me, and I doubt I’ll ever forgive you. But you’re right…that ring doesn’t belong to me, and I definitely don’t want it. I have it locked up in a safety deposit box over at the bank, I can bail it out for you tomorrow morning. And then you can get back to staying the hell out of my life.” He stood up, subtly suggesting that he considered Jane’s time with him over and done with. But Jane just looked at him, a subtle reply of her own that there was still more to be said. It was then that it hit Darcy. It was against Jane’s nature to bare her soul like that. Unlike Amy, who was all too quick and all too willing to make emotional outbursts, Jane was far more introverted when it came to discussing her personal problems. It was one of the few things Darcy disliked about her when they were still together, but something he learned to respect. That is, until their relationship turned sour, and Jane remained mum for quite some time just as to when and why she considered their love dead. “Wait a minute,” he said, pointing at her. “This is about more than the ring, isn’t it? You must have known that if you wanted the ring back, all you had to do was ask. I didn’t need a back story, and there’s no way you’d want me to know it, either. Unless…” Just as it looked as if the sadness in Jane’s eyes was waning, it had returned in full force. “…unless you need something else.” “I’m scared!” Jane blurted out. “There are men looking to kill me, Darcy! I have no idea how much debt Daddy’s racked up, or if selling Mom’s ring will even take care of it. What if it isn’t enough? How am I supposed to keep these people away from my family? I know these types of people all too well…they can show up anyplace, anytime, and they don’t enjoy taking ‘no’ for an answer.” “And how is that my problem, exactly?” Darcy asked, now losing his patience with the girl. “If you’re scared for your safety, get somebody who actually gives a crap about you to take you in.” “It’s not that simple,” she replied, softly. A crack of thunder echoed in the distance. “Wonderful. Do go on, this should be entertaining.” “I’ve been working with John Edwards’ office. You know, the senator from North Carolina. Lately I’ve been back home rounding up support for him in California.” Darcy nodded. “Every aspect of my life is at stake here, Darcy. Everybody I know is connected to Washington somehow. And there are those who live for this kind of negative publicity. If news of this gets out, it would destroy not only my career, but Senator Edwards’, as well. I can’t trust anybody…” “Alright, hold it right there.” Darcy said, holding up a hand for added effect. “You want the ring, it’s yours. But I don’t owe you a damn thing after that!” His fists were clenched as he was beginning to see red. It appeared he wouldn’t be able to contain his pent-up anger after all. “And I…cannot…BELIEVE…that you would have the audacity to come back here and put that on me,” he continued. “After what you pulled, leaving me high and fucking dry, you have the nerve to come back, almost seven years later, and ask me to put MY life on the line to keep you safe? What the fuck is wrong with you, Jane?!” It may not have been exactly how he’d foreseen it, but he’d waited a long time to direct those harsh words at her. But judging by her response, it appeared she’d waited just as long to offer the retort. “I may have left you, Darcy. But you’re the one who left altogether. You think that didn’t break my heart just as much?” “YOU SLEPT WITH MY FUCKING ROOMMATE!” “Darcy!” Jane shouted in kind, no longer able to hold back the tears. This was it – a confrontation seven years in the making. Emotions were running high, and the tension in the air was so thick you could cut it with a knife. The exact timing of the matter would prove to be unfortunate, at least for Darcy. Had he not been so incensed, he might have noticed the tiny voice calling to him from his pocket, warning him of impending danger. Several moments passed in relative silence. Jane was now standing as well, and both of them were breathing heavily, silently wondering which of them would be the first to move from here. “Darcy…” Jane began, choosing to break the ice first. “I know you must hate me for what I did to you. We were kids, and I was so stupid. But you have to know that there wasn’t a day that went by that I didn’t regret being in that room. And not a day went by that I didn’t miss you, and want to talk to you, only to know it was impossible. It was the ring that brought me here, Darcy…but now that I’m here…well…” She trailed off, and Darcy wasn’t about to let her off the hook so easy. “Well?” he answered, prompting her to continue. “Things never should have happened the way they did. I have only myself to blame for that. And if I’m to leave here only with what you owe me…I guess I understand. But please, I’m begging you, Darcy….please think long and hard about this, if this is really what you want. I don’t expect you to forgive me. But if my penance for breaking your heart and driving you away has to be my life, I want you to be sure of it.” *** I’d never been more sure of anything in my entire life. I even had my mouth open to tell her just that, when something clicked on in the back of my brain. Suddenly my trip to La-La Land, home of the Wolf-God made perfect sense. If Jane truly was in imminent danger, if her life really was at stake…then my decision would indeed affect the rest of my days. And I realized something else just then. Seven years is a long fucking time, if you think about it. But in all that time…somehow I never found the time to make peace with what happened. I don’t know, everything happened pretty fast…When I found her in the room with Sam, that was the last day I ever set foot in that house. It was also the last day I ever spent in college. My whole world turned upside-down that day, and all I knew how to do was run away from it all, like some damn tiger was on my trail. I don’t think it was until the ink was dry on my very first contract to wrestle professionally that I even took the time to look back on how I even got there…and I’m still not even sure. But I do know this – the only way I could handle what Jane did to me was to get her completely removed from my life. I never wanted to see her again, and I never wanted to hear about her. I couldn’t bear to even think about her. Everything about her, until that very moment when we were reunited, had been completely and utterly locked away in the darkest corners of my mind. It was a pretty good defense mechanism, if I do say so myself, until it failed. You’d think it’d be hard to repress memories of somebody like that, somebody who was once so important. But you’d be surprised. I spent half the time obsessed with climbing those golden stairs of professional wrestling, possessed with the idea of being a world champion, and being recognized for standing head and shoulders above everybody else. The other half of the time…well, I spent it doing more running away, I suppose. But that was for an entirely different set of problems I was left completely unprepared to handle. A broken back and a ruined career will do that to you, you know. With all that on my mind and more, there was no time for Janey O’Connor. But seven years of running turned out to be a dead end solution. The emotions I’d tried so hard to bottle up, the grief that I never allowed myself to take in finally had the gall to unlock the closet where they’d been locked away. I couldn’t bear to look at her, and I wanted nothing more than for this whole matter to be over and done with. And that’s when the decision became clear. Running away simply wasn’t going to do, especially now. If I turned my back on her now, and I had to read in the papers that her body was found floating in San Francisco Bay…then it would weigh way, way too much on my conscience to ever bottle her up again. If that happened… *** Then it will never be over. Darcy opened his eyes, finally able to look again at Jane, still waiting on his reply with baited breath. He hoped upon hope that his eloquence wouldn’t fail him now, since the words that could accurate describe what he was feeling scarcely existed as it was. “Jane…” She made no sound, her only reaction came in looking into his eyes intently. “I…I don’t know if…I can ever forgive you for what happened. And I don’t know if I can ever really look at you without it feeling like World War 3 inside of me. But you’re right about something…running away never did me any good. I have to find a way to make my peace with you. And I guess…I can’t really do that if something happens to you.” Jane’s heart practically skipped a beat. “I won’t be hanging around or anything, really,” she said, attempting to reassure any lingering doubts he might have. “I just…I just want there to be a friend, somebody I can trust if something happens.” “I’m not your friend.” “But…can I trust you?” The flood of emotion flamed up doubts about his newfound course of action, but Darcy was sure that there could be no turning back now. With a slow, single nod, Darcy affirmed that she could. Jane made some kind of sound that was part-sob, part-laugh, part-sigh of relief. In her excitement, she took Darcy’s hands in hers. “You’re a brave man, Darcy Markson,” she said to him, looking into his eyes as her own began to water. “Thank you.” It was about that time that a certain redhead, tipped off by an unfortunate unwitting phone call that had revealed to her what was going on in her home, burst through the front door. Taking one look at her boyfriend and his hands meshed with the woman she could only assume must have been the ex-girlfriend she’d been warned about, her worst fears were, in her mind, more or less confirmed. Darcy couldn’t believe his luck. He had not ten seconds prior potentially pledged to put his life on the line for the girl he wasn’t sure he didn’t altogether hate… …and now his girlfriend could potentially end up nixing the deal by taking both their lives outright. Oh fate. You silly bitch. To be concluded…
