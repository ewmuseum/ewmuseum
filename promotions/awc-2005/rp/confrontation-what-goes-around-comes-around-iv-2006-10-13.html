Confrontation - What Goes Around Comes Around (iv)

13 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Oct 13 As a professional wrestler, catching red eye flights from America to Europe and vice versa was slowly becoming custom. A weekly event that was always more tedious than the last, these flights were also necessary in supporting my long-distance relationship This undertaking that Natalie and I had chosen was for various reasons, but mainly because of her being at a considerably young age. We made it work, but the constant jumping across continents was always a tiresome process, which was the cause of my exaggerated collapse on the double bed in our hotel in Leon, Spain. Natalie followed me inside, dragging in her suitcase, which was regrettably filled with contents that would probably go unused. This was her first real time accompanying me to an AWC endeavor, and she clearly wasn't an accomplished traveler despite flying more frequently than the average person. I didn't see the need for her to tag along as someone who didn't even follow wrestling, but she insisted, claiming that if it was important to me, it was important to her – the age-old relationship mantra. Needless to say, she was right in one respect – Untouchable was a significant event to me, as it was my first real shot at AWC gold. A chance for me to win a tangible object to prove my worth in one of PTC's premier clubs was penciled in the PPV lineup. Unfortunately for me, my mind had wandered elsewhere in the recent weeks. Being inquired about a job for the agency by my long-time comrade Lucas Emerson was nagging me from the rear of my mind. I had chosen the apparent easy way out by ignoring it and leaving calls on my cell-phone unanswered – but such a complicated matter never held a defined “easy way out”. When it returned to my conscience I felt the slight pang of guilt that is almost unavoidable due to the potential of letting down a close friend. What made it more overwhelming was the past between us – Lucas had pulled strings for me before, but most notably had saved my life in a previous encounter that remains untouched in memory. Above all, he was a man that I highly respected and was in debt to. Helping him in a business affair was something that could put us back on even turf, an opportunity that I had waited for since the occurrence. There is a certain amount of self-respect to be gained in considering yourself a grateful person; I wanted to prove to Lucas that my appreciation for his deed was far beyond a simple 'thank-you'. However, it is clear in life that there will always be the presence of certain forces that contrast logical choices with their own degree of decisive reason. In this case, my relationship with Natalie was what stood between me and taking the offer - what made it complicated is that it was much more than that. I was too stubborn to simply let it go. But I was also so dedicated to Natalie, and so concerned with maintaining my new life that had very little room for error. Getting an new start was the answer to a functional family - freeing myself of the imposing threat of a career as an agent. To put that back in jeopardy would be too much of a risk. Taking risks had been a major force in many aspects of my life and careers, but taking risks that counteracted another risk was no longer a considerable undertaking. It was dangerous, and so was Lucas' request. His threats, though intimidating, didn't stand up to the role of keeping my family safe. Why did Lucas want me specifically assigned to the mission, even as a retired agent? Something was rather fishy about the situation that I had mulled over before. For Lucas to keep his reasoning hidden from me during our encounter in London was a choice worthy of investigation, but not on my part. If it came to it, I would play the role of an escapist. I was very capable of that. “Honey, can you help me unpack?” My daze was interrupted by the sound of a sweet voice that came from the foot of the bed. Natalie was unpacking enough clothes to last a month, even though we only planned on staying a few days. I didn't dare question the female authority, though, and shifted from the bed and grabbed my own suitcase. I made the process as simple and quick as possible by taking my stack of clothes in one handful and shoving them all into a single drawer. My first attempt to close it failed, but after shifting some pants into an unoccupied corner it submitted to my arm, sliding back into it's space. Natalie seemed rather annoyed as she carefully placed her clothes in the drawer in an orderly manner. “I hope you're not planning on going to dinner in that,” she said, surveying my sweatpants and hoodie with sarcastic disdain. “These clothes are rather comfortable, thank you very much,” I returned as I reoccupied the bed. I leaned up against the headboard in a relaxed position, and while looking at the ceiling prompted Natalie, “Who said anything about dinner anyway?” Natalie shrugged as she continued her slow process. “I guess I did. You can't be satisfied with that airplane food,” she suggested. “Guess you could say I'm used to it,” I said, then stifled a yawn as I slumped down into the bed. I rolled on my side and buried my head in a pillow. Deciding that removing my glasses would be much more comfortable, I did so and closed my eyes. I heard Natalie stirring. “You're not going to sleep are you?” rang her voice. “No,” I murmured. “Okay, well, I'm taking a shower. You best be ready when I get out, or I'm going down to the restaurant and finding myself a nice gentleman to buy me dinner,” she said threateningly before moving into the washroom. I heard the sound of the taps being turned and the sound of water against the ceramic tub, and lost myself in an unforeseen sleep. * * * BAM! BAM! BAM! Awoken abruptly by a sudden loud knocking on the door of my hotel room, I opened my eyes to darkness. I remembered it being lighter before, and immediately concluded that I had fallen asleep. I didn't take note to check the time, although it felt as if the sun had recently set. I swung my legs off of the bed and got up. As I made the notion to answer the knock on the door, I noticed Natalie's empty suitcase and remembered her dinner request. My reaction was a short curse to myself under my breath, and also realizing she wasn't present in the hotel room. BAM! BAM! BAM! “Okay, okay ... hold your horses,” I responded to the rather obnoxious pounding that sounded throughout the entire room. Such a noise could probably wake the dead. I got on my toes to look through the peep hole, only to see that nobody was out there. Perhaps it was Natalie, and she had forgotten our room key? If so, it was sensible to open the door anyways and catch her before she went all the way back down to the lobby, perhaps to redeem myself for falling asleep before dinner. I opened the door to an empty space, as expected. I stepped into the hall and glanced down the corridor to my left and felt a hand being placed on my shoulder from behind. I reacted instantly, kneeling down and reaching over and behind my shoulder to grab the wrist of my potential victim, and raised myself up in a spin, twisting the arm of the person in the meantime. I stood face to face with Lucas Emerson, who despite being in a painful predicament had a cheerful smile on his face. “Still quick, aren't we?” he suggested smartly. Out of near-shock I kept his arm twisted, but soon released it when I finally realized what had happened. I took a deep breath. “Holy shit, Lucas, you scared the hell out of me.” Lucas brought his suit jacket back into proper form by shaking out his arms, then straightened his tie before approaching me and putting a hand on my shoulder and pointing me into the hotel room. “It wasn't my intent. But at least I know you're still as quick as you once were,” he said. “Let's go inside.” I felt reluctant at first, but that feeling quickly turned into being inclined as I went inside. Lucas wielded a certain mental power over me that I couldn't quite grasp, but in accordance to my current dilemma involving his request, it would be important for me to learn to counter it. I stood at the foot of the bed, watching interestingly as Lucas moved towards the window and opened the curtain, taking a glance to the outdoors. It seemed like an awkward move. What was he trying to prove? More importantly, what was he doing here? Okay, I knew the answer to that; however, it surprised me that he would go to such lengths to track me down. I couldn't help but be intrigued; why was I so important to his plan? Or was it even a plan? “You're probably going to ask me what I'm doing here,” Lucas began, standing omnisciently with his hands in his pockets, “even though you already know. Or think you do.” I didn't say anything. Lucas gave no indication as to whether he expected me to respond or not; I opted to leave the ball in his court. He was good with words, but unless he was certain I was listening, his patience was limited. I would use that strength to my highest advantage. “I didn't come her to babysit you, Cedric. I'm not going to tell you that you should be answering your phone, or that you should give someone a yes or a no when they ask you to do something for them,” he explained rather gravely, trying to put some sort of effect on me. I had dealt with such an approach before in other circumstances, but didn't expect it from Lucas, who was once my best friend. Our friendship seemed to be slowly deteriorating, although I was unsure of his stance on it. If he was going to put on his best business face and game-plan to match his fancy suit, he would get the same in return. Not accepting his request was slowly becoming the easier choice to make – his actions gave me the impression that following through on the request was less of a friendship consequence than a professional one. Lucas sighed, and let an almost sadistic chuckle roll off his lips, staring at me with gleeful eyes. “I don't think you quite understand what's at stake here, Cedric,” he said, then sat down on the chair beside the window. He crossed his legs and observed me. I didn't flinch. “It's no longer in the best interests of the agency that you accept what I asked you a fortnight ago. Oh no ... it's in your best interests.” He paused. “At this point, do you even know what your best interests are?” He looked at me with curious eyes, as if I wasn't comfortable with my own self-knowledge. I almost let out a smirk, but instead gave him a serious look. “Yes,” I responded calmly. “And what is that, exactly?” he pushed. “To not give a damn about the agency's best interests, or, the logic behind the job you want me to do,” I shot at him. A smile crept across his face, bringing out his evil, which I was almost convinced of – his malevolent manner was without flaw. I knew his intentions. “See, Cedric ... that job has already been taken care of. It should be as we speak, actually.” He uncrossed his legs and raised up from the chair, making his presence known. “You really weren't necessary, Cedric, so I bid you farewell.” And he straightened his tie, added a nod, and stepped in the direction of the door. Something wasn't right. I sensed it from the second that I discovered Lucas outside the door. Each of his movements, every sentence he spoke – the almost victorious sadism that effect his expressions wasn't without reason. He showed some sort of arrogance in that he knew something I didn't – something that I would certainly want to know. And I wasn't about to let myself fall into the trap that he set for me. There was a good reason he was in Spain besides telling me what he did; there was a reason he was in my hotel room in Leon at this hour, and in no way did it make sense for a man of his power and cluttered schedule to go out of his way like that. To make arrangements to get here, to uncover my whereabouts ... nothing added up. And I wasn't about to let him slip through my fingers, and leave me in a state of confusion with no lead or source of information. As he moved past me I stepped towards him from behind, quickly using my vantage point. I reached my arm around to his front, intending to put him in a choke hold that seemed like the most effective maneuver from my position. As I was an arm-tug away from strangling the life out of him, I saw his head come rocketing backwards and slam right into my nose. I stumbled backwards and felt the blood come trickling out as I tried to ready myself for his next attack, which would come immediately. He came at me with an assault of punches to my stomach, feeding me one after the other in a rather unorthodox style. I kept stumbling backwards in a doubled over position, blood running out of my nose. The momentary lapse in action gave me perfect opportunity for a short-charge that resulted in a tackle, slamming the old agent into the ground. And just like that, I had him defeated. It had taken that much to get him in a hopeless position, with both hands pressing his throat against the ground, cutting off enough oxygen and blood circulation to make him powerless, but also keep him conscious. “What the fuck do you want from me?!” I spat into his face, feeling the urge to squeeze the life out of him. My mental breakdown was accumulative of hatred for my close friend trying to put my life in jeopardy, along with my recent mental illness. Lucas choked something back at me, as if he was trying to say something. I let the pressure of and brought one hand back up, then back down for a quick punch that would keep me in control. “Give me – give me -” I began, but failed to even stutter the remaining of the sentence. I was shaking with fury, as if a vain in my neck was on the verge of popping. I had never felt such a surge of craziness and adrenaline all in the same swallow, it was unnatural. And yet I still found it necessary to challenge this older man with such force and vengeance, it was subtly surreal. “There's nothing -” Lucas whispered in a hoarse breath between a succession of heavy breathing. He swallowed and put his words together slowly. “There's nothing you can do now.” “What can't I do?” I demanded. More heavy breathing, and a tense pause. “It's over. Give up.” “GIVE UP TO WHAT?!!” I said with such passion, intensity, and desperation I almost lost what little control I had left and felt even more temptation to do further harm. And in that second of delay, as I waited for another response, a noise erupted from a few meters in front of me as the door opened, and in stormed two men decked in black, complete with leather jackets and ear-pieces. Bodyguards, or the CIA alternative, whatever secretive entitlement they may hold. At first glance, they were too large to be agents, but too swift to be run-of-the-mill bodyguards. I got up in time to dodge the attack of the first one, but soon felt my knees collapse as Lucas slammed his arm into the sensitive spots. From there I was vulnerable to the onslaught of the second bodyguard, who took me out with a massive right hand. I felt the back of my head thud sickly against the floor as a result, and my vision was immediately clouded. The ceiling above me spun in circles, and I was mentally unable to focus on a single spot. All I could feel was my ribcage being stomped, and in a matter of seconds I was engulfed in darkness. * * * The slam of a car door was enough to stir me to consciousness some time later, but I would not know at the time the source of noise. I couldn't see anything – the area I occupied was pitch black – and had no concept of time or location I scrambled in my lie down position and attempted to sit up, unaware of the low roof that was over my head. My forehead struck the solid barrier and I evoked a muffled scream in pain, cautious of my unknown human surroundings. I could feel the sensitivity in my ribs as I crawled around in the space, trying to find some means of movement. It seemed as if I was in an enclosed vessel of some kind – I noticed that breathing required effort. This was troubling as my head already throbbed in pain from the earlier beating I struggled to remember. The details about Lucas came flooding back to me – giving me a surreal feeling that provoked nostalgia for my lost friendship with him. The consequences of our encounter was slowly becoming a reality for me, but I still was unclear in regard to any truthful causation. As the instigator it would be expected I would feel remorseful. It was comforting that I no longer felt any guilt, but complete indifference toward my former friend. He was now an enemy. I felt around in the space, trying to find a way out or inform myself of my whereabouts. The floor beneath me was carpeted but not soft, and the very low roof above was a smooth metal. To my right was another panel that also felt like some sort of carpet material, and to my left it seemed as though the roof curved downwards to another panel of metal. And as I aimlessly felt around, I placed my hand on some sort of arrangement of metal pieces. It struck me suddenly that my cell phone had formerly been in my pocket, so I reached for that, hoping for a break. My pockets were emptied of all their contents. I cursed under my breath. Following that moment, as I was beginning to feel my cause was a lost one, I heard the unmistakable sound of a car door opening. I strained my ears to catch any voices and there was some short discussion, and then the door was slammed closed. I didn't just hear it – I felt it. I was in a trunk. Trying to avoid hitting the panic button, I rethought my current situation. My immediate reaction was that some trunk had release buttons on their interior, and if I was lucky enough, I would find one to open. As if fate were on my side and coincidence in favour of the plot, my fingers located a small button near the key hole. I took a sharp intake of breath as I pushed it in – silently hoping that not only would the trunk pop open, but that nobody would be around to cave my skull in with a baseball bat and back into unconsciousness. Sure enough, the latch released and the enclosure above me sprang open to reveal a clouded black sky. A slow rain patted down on the asphalt and made for a rather depressing scene, yet fitting for my current situation. I had no idea where I was, or what the time was as I crawled stealthily out of the trunk, exercising caution in the potential event of an enemy nearby. I walked slowly in a crouch, turning to the side of the car. To my right was an ominous brick building – a church. Uninformed of the civil layout of Leon, Spain, I had no clue of where this church was located. I was rather clueless in more than one way – why had Lucas and his cronies bring me to a church? What the hell were they doing here? Why weren't they raining bullets on me for escaping my automotive jail? Too many questions remained unanswered for me to come to any misguided conclusion, but I had a gut feeling Lucas was up to something nearby. From the things he had said during our encounter, I couldn't help but feel I was involved somehow. I surveyed my surroundings and reached for the nearest car door handle. I needed a weapon, and inside the car was the first place to look. It was locked. I got to my feet and looked around, then examined the vehicle. It was an older model that I couldn't put a name to, but I was sure it didn't have a fancy alarm, so I smashed my fist through the window. Trickles of blood oozed from my hand as I reached inside, targeting the most obvious spot – the glove-box. Sure enough a pistol slid out and dropped do the floor. I grabbed it quickly and stuffed it in the back of my pants – the bad-ass cop move that is seen in every Martin Lawrence movie ever made. My next move was unclear even to me until I saw a light come to life from inside the church. It seemed like an awkward hour for a light to be coming on, and in my current state of unknowing, it seemed like a good place to start investigating. Not at all reluctant to walk into a church with a gun pointed at my right ass cheek, I headed up the flight of stairs and pulled the large door open. Sister Theresa apparently forgot to deadbolt God's house, which made my entry incredibly easier. I trekked slowly down a hallway to my left, carefully checking every room, wielding my gun like someone possessed. Soon I came to another set of larger doors, which was sure to be the entrance to the service area and church pews. I opened the door slowly and my expectation was confirmed. It was a typical set of pews, surrounded by stain glass, and at the front was the minister and choir area. An ominous statue, portraying Jesus looked down on everyone from the front. What really hit me as unexpected was the person who was kneeling at the foot of the statue in a prayer position, sounds of weeping coming from the same person. It was my girlfriend, Natalie, and she was under the command of a man that stood beside her with a pistol of his own. I stepped in without further adieu. The door closed behind me with a thud that echoed throughout the cavernous hall, something expected from a quiet church in the middle of the night. Lucas didn't flinch, but merely looked over at me, sure to keep his gun steadily pointed at Natalie to use her as a hostage. I had my weapon in a similar position, yet directed right at him. With deadly aim like mine, it was an easy shot, but risky in that he could pull the trigger on Natalie in the same breath. Natalie was helpless, with her hands tied in ropes. Lucas smiled at me, a sadistic combination with his gaunt, hollow look that gave an eerie feeling of despicable evil. “Nice of you to join us-” “What the fuck is she doing here.” I interrupted with the demand. The sudden wave of emotion, compassion, and desperation left me in a state of complete insanity that came off as calmness. I was dead serious. I had no time to come up with hypothetical reasons as to why Natalie was praying to God while Lucas pointed a gun at her head. “Natalie's been a naughty girl,” Lucas said plainly, still playing coy with his evil grin. I moved towards him. He didn't move his gun from it's persistent position, but I was slow and cautious with every slight move I made, or even thought of making. “She's lied to me. And you.” “What the fuck are you talking about, Lucas. Don't fuck around with me. I'm not afraid to blast these shells in the house of God, you know that,” I threatened, keeping my crawl-pace of moving towards him. Leading him to believe I wasn't about to make any sudden movements was the goal. Lucas laughed. “And you think I am? You know me better than that, Cedric,” he said, almost disappointed. “But you don't really know your significant other as well as you thought you do.” “Shut the fuck up, Lucas. Your snide bullshit doesn't work with me. What the fuck have you done to her,” I pushed at him, keeping my intense glare locked on. He shook his head and added a sigh. “You see, Cedric, you blind fool ... I haven't done anything to her. I'm simply demanding that she confess to her sins; you know I'm a religious man, Cedric.” “That's tough to distinguish when you're pointing a gun at me in a church, Lucas,” I shot at him. “This is just precaution. I intend on killing her in another, more clever manner. I think it is fitting that she should try and be forgiven by my God, as when she insulted me, she did the same to Him,” Lucas philosophized. “This little bitch of yours, Cedric ... she stole my son from me.” I paused and absorbed that last message. “You – you're lying,” I stammered. It was all I could come up with. Natalie whimpered severely, as if to say something. If anything, I was sure it would be a protest to Lucas's outrageous claim. “And to make her conscience even more foggy, she's hidden from you for many years now of the affair we had.” I swallowed hard. It was utterly outrageous and way too much news to swallow in one. “I don't believe you,” I said simply. It came out effectively. “Believe it. And now, for kidnapping my flesh and blood-” he slowly emphasized. I quickened my motion towards him that turned into a few long strides. “You son of a bitch ...” Lucas turned his gun towards me. This was exactly what I wanted. He fired a shot abruptly at me as I dived in reaction, shielding myself behind a pew. The bullet smashed into a corner of the long bench and splinters of wood scattered everywhere. I heard Natalie scramble to her feet. I knew her hands were tied, which worried me slightly. I heard another bullet fire and I jumped up from behind the pew – from the looks of it, Lucas had turned to shoot her and Natalie had kicked the gun from out of his hands. They were now in combat, Natalie holding her own with only the use of her legs and feet, keeping the upper-hand. I raised my gun and aimed it right at Lucas. Natalie planted a kick into his chest that sent him stumbling into the open for a clear shot. Many years of friendship forgotten, I pulled the trigger in account of the target trying to kill my girlfriend and I. I wasn't sure where it clipped him, but he collapsed to the ground imminently. I dropped the gun and ran towards Natalie. After both avoiding death within the same minute and a sequence of events that made for one of my most extraordinary nights ever, there was sure to be blood lost and tears shed. But the fact that we were alive was the most reassuring – and for that, celebration today. Whatever the consequences may have been would wait for tomorrow, or the next day, as the elation of being alive and together was too overwhelming. We embraced. fin
