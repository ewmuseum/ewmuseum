Side Effect of Stardom

28 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 28, 2005 As posted by Race Alexander on his semi-official fan subpage on wrestlesmarks.com. Pathetic. Red Rock is just another example of exactly what is wrong with this industry today. Do fans really need to see their so-called heroes mock the elderly as a form of comedy? How can children look up to a guy who admits that he became saw an ad in a comic book store for a wrestling school, and enrolled himself with the hopes it would keep bullies from kicking his ass on the street? Pathetic. Guys like Red Rock, living off a legacy of being a curtain jerking, light-counter for Primetime Championship Wrestling come to promotions like AWC and try to sit at the top of the food chain. They try to be the top dogs, living off nothing more than fleeting history of days gone past. Nothing more than three decades in age makes Red Rock different than Johnny Noble. Both stand in my way. Both prosper on reputation alone, talent be damned. Both will fall prey to me. I am a lion, and they are mere herd animals, waiting to fall prey to my unrelenting hunger for victory. 'Maybe we should do some PR work, like Red Rock, and his valet Boobie,' says Connie, as the scene fades in from black to reveal the pair sitting across from each other in what appears to be a diner-like restaurant. 'You know, visit sick kids, or something.' Race scowls. 'Nah, let guys like him and Noble do that kind of crap,' says Race. 'I am a wrestler, not a philanthropist. Why guys like him are out copping cheap press, I am inside the gym, doing situps and skipping rope.' Connie sparks up a cigarette, making it clear that the pair are in a non-smoking section of the restaurant. Then again, they are in England, is there such a thing as a non-smoking section? 'Do you have a game plan for tomorrow night?' she says, as she exhales a plume of smoke through her nose. Race nods. 'I've watched tape of him, most of it ended with him lying on his back like a back alley whore counting ceiling tiles,' he replies. 'I haven't really seen what the kid can do because of that. After splitting Noble's head open like an overripe cantelope, I am pretty confident that the 'powers that be' would want to keep my heat on the rise, therefore sacrificing Red Rock at the Alter of Race Alexander.' Connie smiles in agreement. All of a sudden, a shriek pierces the area from off camera, startling the pair. The camera pans left, to reveal a young boy, perhaps seven, wearing bright red 'Incredibuddy' T-shirt on. He points at Race in protest with his left hand, while his right hand clutches tightly to that of his mother. 'THAT'S THE MAN WHO BEAT UP JOHNNY NOBLE!' sobs the boy to his mother. 'HE HIT HIM WHEN HE WASN'T LOOKING AND MADE HIM BLEED!' Race shakes his head in disgust. 'For the love of Christ,' he mumbles quietly to Connie. 'Do I really have to remain in kayfabe even when I go get something to eat now that I am on TV.' Connie frowns, but nods to confirm the worst. The answer is yes. 'This isn't the indies any more,' she whispers back. 'Pour it on thick.' Race sighs, and turns to the boy, and stares at him with a steely gaze. The kid's sobbing subsides and gives way to fear, as he looks into the eyes of the man who bashed the skull of his idol. 'Yeah, kid, that's me,' sneers Race slowly, breathing a bit hard, for effect. 'Next time, I plan to finish him... Races licks his lips and smiles sinisterly. '...Permantly.' The boy cries to his mother in protest, who looks at Race in disgust. 'How could ya talk to a yooong lad like that, there Mister,' says the mother with a thick Irish accent. 'Ya coooolda told him its all make believe.' Race looks over to Connie for a bit of direction, as he didn't expect the mother to step in. His long-time valet takes a long drag off her cigarette and stands up and gets close to the woman... within a few inches of her face. 'Why don't you just back off, bitch...' says Connie, accentuating the word bitch by blowing smoke in the mother's face. The woman coughs, and obviously looks scared. '...And take your sure-to-grow-up-gay, crybaby of a son, with ya.' The woman coughs again and trembles in a combination of fear and rage. She then grabs the boy by the hand. 'Coooome along, Angus.' The woman and her son scurry off, with Connie's sneering gaze upon her. Once the woman is out of sight, she slides back into her seat, as Race chuckles. 'And what, praytell, would you have done had she called your bluff,' says Race. 'Hey, I'm from South Philly,' says Connie with a wink. 'I can handle myself.' Connie pauses to take a last, long drag off her cigarette, before snuffing the life out of it in an ashtray. 'But, ya know, there is a bigger picture here, babe. You are on TV now. People are gonna recognize you. You always have to be on and play the role and be in character. Busting ass in the ring will win their respect, but a little bit of showmanship out of it will go a long way to.' Race knows she's right. 'Don't get me wrong,' Connie continues. 'I am not telling you to visit old folks homes and shit like that. I am just saying that the Race Alexander on camera, needs to be the Race Alexander off camera when you are recognized in public. This is a side effect that will come with your stardom.' Race nods again and smiles. 'There really isn't all that much of a difference between the two, is there?' Connie raises an eyebrow and smiles without answering, as the scene fades to black.
