The Eagle's Nest

4 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 4th, 2005 The flame was engrossing. I couldn’t pull my eyes away from my lighter as I lit it. While I held it burning in the air I let my cigarette hang between my lips, bobbing up and down as my jaw shifted. Slowly I brought the lighter to the cigarette, lighting the tip of it and taking a deep puff, the smoke hitting my lungs with a force. I felt revived and complete, my mind thinking straight again. “Tim, are you hungry?” My brother was clutching the wheel of the car, his eyes focused on the road as he drove. The truck was old, but it got us around town well enough. There were countless things wrong with it, though, such as all the speakers being blown out and something making a funny noise in the engine. I didn’t really know cars well enough to do a thing, though, so I didn’t think much of it. I responded with a simple “Sure”, dragging on the cigarette which burnt nice and slow. Things were just going fine, which wasn’t surprising at this time of day. Usually, shit didn’t hit the fan until 1pm, and hour from now…but who knows. Liam and I weren’t known to be conservative pacifists. After a few more minutes of driving, Liam and I pulled to the side of the road and parked, the busy city bustling around us. We were in a dirty part of town, the part where everyone knew us as family. This area, known as The Eagle’s Nest, was our turf, where we could go as we pleased. Here we were law and what we did or what we said was to be accepted. This wasn’t strange, though, because that’s how everyone lived around here. We were all Eagles, and no one would be crazy enough to go where Eagles dare. Pockets Street was the center of The Eagle’s Nest, where things happened, where the cops didn’t even bother to even check. They knew that this street full of bars and restaurants would only cause trouble and bloodshed. Here you could find the toughest men around, which was a description me and Liam fit. If you were respected here you were ignored, and if you were like a pink suit at a funeral you were done. Liam and I were the type to be ignored. We decided to go into our favorite place, Pete’s. Even though Liam and I were ignored by most people, the crowd at Pete’s were like family. We had been drinking there before we could even legally do it. All around everyone was a brother and a sister in there. When we walked in we prompted a huge celebration of cheers and smiles, everything going crazy. “Tim! Liam! Glad you could make it! Got here kind of late, didn’t you?” “Last night, Liam, I don’t see how you even made it home!” “Tim, let me buy you a drink! Pete, a drink for my buddy!” It was as if we left for years and came back, but really we were there all last night. That’s just the way things were at Pete’s…we were loved. Liam and I sat at the bar and started drinking, each engaging in our own conversations when the TV came on. It was boxing match, with Lugo Rodriguez and Jamaal Graham fighting, two unknown middle weight boxers. Before I could even comprehend what was happening, Liam took the words right out of my mouth. “A n*gger and a cabbage picker boxing…hell, double KO don’t sound so bad after all!” The whole room erupted with laughter and people gave warm faces of approval towards our direction. It was sad, though, how these two low life fucks could make it on TV. Boxing was always our sport…and now all these dregs are taking what we have. Here in The Eagle’s Nest, though, we don’t take their crap. We protect what is ours and everything about it, not letting any n*gger, spic, jew, f*g, or anyone touch it. What is ours is ours. Don’t think that we are out spoken protectors, though. We don’t give ourselves titles like “White Supremacists” or say shit like “White Power!”…none of that bull. We don’t fuck around; we get down to business. That is because there is no acknowledgment of anything else…the male and female are assumed to be Christian, white, and straight as a diamond’s edge. Those other “races” were just mistakes that spineless pussies let last. But we weren’t at Pete’s to shove opinions down people throats…we were there to get piss drunk. As Liam conversed with funny man Bob Smith, I got up and walked over to my good pal John Carnegie, who seemed to be having a rough time. He was a tiny man with a skinny frame who sat there hunched over his drink. You could tell by looking at his hollow eyes that he was having a rough time. I parked my ass in the stool next to his and put a hand on his shoulder. “John,” I said, “What’s the matter? You look shook up, buddy.” He looked up at me, and his gaze chilled my spine. It seemed like he was on the verge of tears as his lip quivered, mustering the strength to speak. “Oh, Tim, Charlie isn’t doing so good…his condition is worsening, I s’ppose, as they’ll be having him back on that therapy.” Charlie was John’s son who had leukemia. For months, Charlie had gone through chemo therapy, bringing him to the brink of death. And for a while, it seemed like it worked…until now, apparently. My heart sunk for John and I called the barkeep over. “Pete,” I said, “Get John another round of whatever he’s having.” “Oh no, Tim, I can’t, I have to get back to work…but, really, thanks though. Tell Liam I said hi…I didn’t get a chance to say a thing to him.” John got up and gave me a hug, pulling me close to him. Slowly and without life he walked out, sulking his way to the door. John was a hardworking man who did construction. He never missed a day of work, needing every cent for his family. His situation was a perfect example of how the Nest works, though. When it was heard that Charlie needed chemo therapy and that John didn’t have nearly enough to pay for it, every Eagle in the Nest chipped in. Liam and I put in a large sum, and John has been nothing but forever grateful. That’s how the Nest works, though. When an Eagle is down, the others help him or her back into the air. Any Eagle would throw his or her life on the line for another, doing anything they could to help. I mean, if I was down in the gutter, I’d want to be pulled back up in a second. After John left, I turned and called over Pete again, who walked his way over to somebody else. When he arrived, I leaned over and put a hand on his shoulder, grinning wider than the Atlantic ocean. “Pete,” I said, “I’m hungry.” “I’ll tell Missy.” Pete put his head back into the kitchen where his beautiful wife, Missy, worked. “Tim’s hungry!” he bellowed, and Missy instantly knew what to cook. Liam and I come in here enough and order the same thing enough that they know exactly what we want every time. For me it’s a piece of haddock, baked, a large heaping side of coleslaw, and a healthy serving of jello for dessert. Liam’s meal of choice was a hamburger, bloody, a thing of potatoes (how it was served was not a problem), and some good ol’ tapioca pudding. Looking down the bar, I could see that Liam was already eating his meal, shoveling the food into his face. If there was something I could depend on, it was Liam being hungry. “Tim, come over here!” The voice was big, strong, and goofy…I could tell it was Bob Smith. Bob was a large man, all 300 pounds of him hanging off his 5’2” frame like nothing I’d ever seen. He looked like a clown, too, with his goofy orange over coat and matching wool pants. When I walked over to him, Liam was bent over with laughter, barely being able to breath. “OH MY FUCKING LORD! Bob…*deep breaths*…you’re going to kill me!” “Bob,” I said, “You can’t go around killing poor people like Liam here…control your humor!” I took a deep swig of my drink and put my hand on Bob’s shoulder, looking at Liam. Somehow, he was still laughing hysterically, bent over as if someone laid right into his stomach with a barrage of fists. Bob was chuckling over this now, and looked me in the eye with a smirk. “Eh, you want to hear it Tim? Well, so there’s this priest and he walks into a bar, and…” Bob suddenly went dead quiet, as did the rest of the room. He looked towards the door with a glare, and as I turned, I saw what everyone was staring at. An officer of the law, right in the doorway, was glaring at all of us. Usually, we just tell him to get on out of here, but this was different. This situation needed enforcement of the Nest’s law, especially the law of Pete’s. This was a n*gger officer. “There’s been word going around of trouble, and the source of it from here.” He had a cocky tone to him. Every word he said sounded like it was trying to be better than the rest of us. Everyone remained silent as Liam and I took our cue. We walked over to him slowly, cracking the joints in our necks and our fists, staring him down. At 6’5” each, we had about 5 inches on the officer. And with both of us floating around 300 pounds, there was no way he’d be able to physically take us down. We stopped not far from him, towering over him. “Well,” I said, “I don’t remember any trouble…do you Liam?” Liam gave me a look, smirking, and then turned towards the officer. He leaned in close to the officer’s face, shaking his head. “Nah, Tim. But, I gotta say…I think there is going to be some ‘trouble’ coming up quite fucking soon…” The room chuckled softly as the officer stared right back at us. “Do you think I haven’t heard about you two? The whole police force knows who you are, Tim and Liam Martin, or as you guys like to call yourself, ‘The Furious Fists Of God’. You think that since you guys are law here, that what you do in…what do you call it, ‘The Nest’, doesn’t go unnoticed. But we can see through you. We know how to break you down. I was sent as a warning…any more of this shit, and you’re both being taken in!” The room went silent, waiting for our action. I smiled and looked Tim straight in the eye, elbowing him playfully. “Liam, what shit is he talking about?” I said, smirking. “I think he means this shit, Tim.” And without hesitation, Liam threw a right that landed square on the jaw of that cop. He went down hard, and Liam and I dragged him towards the bar. I picked him up and threw him on top of it, the sound of the thud and his groan barely audible over the cheers and woos of the patrons of Pete’s. Liam and I then started beating him down, landing punch after punch on his torso. “You fucking n*ggers think you can come onto OUR land and push US around?” Liam said before throwing a fist right into the cop’s eye. The cop was bleeding and moaning, his body all weak and almost lifeless. I stopped Liam from continuing to beat down the cop and silenced the room. I had something to say. “We let this fucker live.” I said, speaking loud enough so even this thing on our counter could hear us through his mangled ears. “We let this fucker live so he can deliver a message. The police will NOT come into The Eagle’s Nest and push us around. And any fucker who thinks he can get away with being something other than white who pushes us around will get worse than this!” The whole place cheered as I grabbed the head of the police officer and pulled him off the counter and dragged him towards the door. Once we got out to the sidewalk, I threw him into the street, the traffic all around careening to try and stop from hitting him. Without a word after that, I turned around and walked back inside, and everything was going on like not a thing happened…. -----([([([END FEED])])])-----
