Date: 23/8/06 Double Negative ~~~ 1. Hi, I’m Michael and I’ll be your Narrator for the Day Everybody’s got their own troubles. Thing is, troubles like a cancer - if you don’t deal with the tumor while it’s benign, it becomes malignant and before you know it, you’ve lost all control of it. It spreads, blackening your body, rotting your flesh from the inside. It eats you up like a carnivore and nothing short of a miracle can save you. I’m here because I’m that miracle. You don’t know me. My name is Michael O’Shea, nine years the older brother of the famous Patrick. I’ve never entered these stories before and perhaps after this I never will again. It doesn’t matter. What matters now is my brother’s sanity of which I am obligated to protect. I died a few years ago far away. It was my own fault and I make no excuse for it. I had been trailblazing across the world since I was only 17, picking up habits and making enemies. I lost my accent, my identity and for the most part, my family. Yet every so often, I’d receive an early morning phone call from my brother Patrick, sobbing down the handset, telling me of how our bastard of a Father was ruining his kids life once again with his vicious beatings and drunken hysteria. I tried to be as best a Father as I could be in the situation and I felt for the most part it worked. Sure I was no role model, but I knew exactly what I wanted my brother NOT to be. More importantly, a special bond developed between us. While my Father knocked down the building blocks of his life, I was the one who put it all back together. My Father was afraid of me … unfortunately Patrick was terrified of him in turn. For a long time, I felt Patrick was on the brink of insanity and I moved to give him the confidence he needed to make decisions for himself. I was the anchor in his life, the glue just about holding everything together. Things changed five years ago. With a plethora of convictions relating to drug trafficking and GBH under my belt, the world caught up with me and I was gunned down in East London over a feud relating to cocaine dealing. My brother Patrick was 28 at the time and I knew it would hurt him the worst. It made him weaker than what he already was. He was a frail old man in an athlete’s body and businessmen seen this vulnerability as if he had been sending out radio signals the whole time. He tried to put up a strong front but it was in the little things that you seen it. How much he smoked; the restless way his eyes moved, never quite meeting the eyes of those he was talking to, merely touching them form time to time and then leaping nimbly away; the way he rubbed his elbows lightly when he was nervous and the fact his fingernails were brutally short. They’re short because he bites them The wrestling organizations that exploited him were like lions. Lions may not think like humans but they know a target and when antelopes start away from a watering hole, alerted by the dusty stench of approaching death, the cat can observe which one falls to the rear of the pack. Paddy often found himself to be the straggler. Paddy endured fierce misery due to these people but would never turn away from his calling. It bred misery and it’s connected to the reason he’s in his current state now. His weakening personality made him impressionable; he became dependent and then a pawn. It’s the same reason he didn’t see the holes when he was contracted to an assassination. The same reason he would be prepared to eradicate targets then destroy himself with compressing guilt. But I am not ashamed of him for that; he is more of a man than I ever was for I didn’t know compassion nor love. He did. His “weakness” coincided with an abundance of good that is so rare in society today and his unselfish and caring attributes was the one thing that could have perhaps saved me. Like I said before…I owe my brother a lot - I asked him once not to follow the path I had and he didn’t. He instead protected his younger siblings as best he could against the turmoil of a beast of a Father. He devoted a promising life to letting me have my own. Therefore that’s why I’m here. Watching down on him, I watched the madness growing. All he ever wanted now was not to be the man he saw in the mirror. All he needed was the advice of his brother to do anything to take the pain away. I’m here because I’m going to give him a choice, a choice elementary yet drastic. Today he can decide whether to continue to be Paddy O’Shea or he can choose to be someone else, even for just a while. He can choose his own mind, what he thinks and whether he feels guilty or not. Now on the brink of insanity he has only one resort left. I can save him if only he’ll let me. 2. The Birth of Pat Whatshisface Hyndman Hotel, New York, NY - 9.45 pm August 23rd “He took my hand” Patrick O’Shea, the very much alive brother of your spiritual narrator, was referring to the dream that plagued him the night before. In the early hours of the morning, his mind a contorted wreck, his one wish came to him. He seen the baby he had killed, little Joshua of number 46, the son of war and peace. And he was alive. “He took my hand” Joshua was talking. Despite being only six months old and of course, surely incapable of talking, Patrick heard him speak and believed because he needed to feel innocent again. He needed to shed his man suit, expose his insides and show, he too was as vulnerable as the infant that sat in front of him. In a prison cell lit only by the moonlight which wriggled in though the bars, Joshua Robinson, was smiling. “He said ‘why are ye so sad?’” And you said? “Aye said ‘because aye killed ye’. And he giggled.” Clasping his chubby digits over his mouth, he had laughed with his tongue gritted between his gums so that saliva dribbled down his chin. “’Silly man…ye didn’t kill me. Look, aye’m alive! Ye must have been havin’ a bad dream’” Paddy was standing in front of a broken mirror in a darkened hotel bathroom that only let a haze of light in through it’s drawn curtains. It cast an uneasy shadow over his face he could see, blurry, almost unnatural. Paddy appreciated this; it was a tag he deemed acceptable. He felt his head drop as his already salve encrusted eyes brought forth a flood. “He took my hand,” he said once again, lifting his head. “He took my hand and said aye couldn’t hurt anyone anyway”. Now Paddy was sobbing. His vision impaired, he noticed the painting of a vase full of roses become a crimson blur. It reminded him of that bloodied cot. It reminded him of everything. Of being impoverished and accepting the job from the mysterious Q; of breaking into little number 46, home of the Robinson’s and firing blindly. (Of the bullets piercing the ceiling) Of finding little baby Joshua, lifeless and bloody, his eyes open in excruciating horror forever more. And it was sending him mad. It was a bathroom like this in which he had tried to take his life. One failed suicide attempt later and he was being blackmailed by Q to carry on with the jobs he was instructed to carry out or else his family would soon join him in the ground. He was a rat caught in a cage, knowing it will inevitably die of blood loss or by another vile means; knowing the crushing reality is sending it over the edge just in time for tea. While all the world invests in stocks and shares, taking trips to Barbados on “Mamaluka“, John Ranfield Esquires fourth prize yacht, Pat the rat is facing the executioner. And no one seems to care. He can’t struggle through this on his own. It was getting worse and more and more people were wiping their hands clean. “’Wake up’ he said t’ me so aye did…Joshua was still dead and aye’m still a murderer.” I whispered into his subconscious. And I gave him the choice there and then as the fabric of the mind was torn apart by psychosis. Maybe I was unleashing a monster, I didn’t care at the time. He was on a straight path to lunacy and I gave him an adjacent alleyway out. It was then and there that Paddy O’Shea made the life changing decision. Paddy O’Shea no longer wanted to be Paddy O’Shea. Stripping off his clothes carefully and methodically, my brother Patrick made a compromise with his troubled mind - his conscience would give him amnesty but not as Paddy O’Shea. Amnesty would come in exchange for identity. Patrick began running the film backwards. He forgot about the job, he forgot about the wrestling, he forgot about his family and friends, he forgot about his manhood, then his teens, then his childhood. He forgot everything he had ever done, anyone he had ever met. He was resetting his life because Paddy O’Shea went hand in hand with pain and suffering. Naked, he opened shopping bags that lay up against his leg. He pulled out a white shirt which he pulled on, once again slowly and methodically, taking great pains with the crease. He produced a pair of white boxer shorts. He slipped these on quickly. Next, he pulled out a black pinstriped pair of trousers and then a matching jacket. They in turn were pulled on. These were followed by black athletic socks and then polished Gianfranco shoes. With his memory quickly shutting down, he realized what he was doing. He was wearing clothes he would never wear, it was Halloween in mid August, he was starring in a panto. Combing his usually disheveled hair behind his ears and then sweeping to the side, he smiled. He wasn’t Paddy O’Shea. He was a ghost, a nobody, no guilt nor fear of retribution. He was Pat Whatshisface, unmatchable, untraceable. Reboot system. I thought at the time, his mind, still holding self dignity, wouldn’t allow Paddy O’Shea to get on the bus route to insanity. Taking a cab, it made its own trek. He wouldn’t lose his mind, he’d reset it and Paddy O’Shea along with it. Grease your heels partner, Patrick O’Shea is sliding on. He was nearly done now. Very soon he would be complete he thought as he produced his final item from the shopping bag - Akeem sunglasses, as slick as the suit he wore. But before he did so he had to make penance. He breathed heavily onto the glass before him, producing a fine mist. Raising his index finger, he etched shakily: ‘I am heartily sorry‘. Paddy O’Shea looked at his reflection tensely, his hands balled into fists. He almost expects his reflection to remind him of crimes he thinks he committed (though he’s not exactly sure). With a chuckle, he shaked it off then slipped the sunglasses up and over the bridge of his nose so they sat snugly. Almost instantaneously, his eyes went blank behind their mask and he hit the ground with a thud, his head thumping against the tiles below. A few seconds later and a thin trickle of blood would channel into the grooves between the ceramics. And as Paddy O’Shea forgot about everything and ceased to exist, he forgot about me, the brother he must have dreamed he had. This is where my story ends. After all, apparently I’m just some guy who once had a brother named Patrick O’Shea. Apparently we’re both dead. 3. The Dawn of Identity Hyndman Hotel, New York, NY - 10.15 pm August 23rd Pat Whatshisface was going to be Paddy O’Shea’s opposite his mind had decided. The shy, often carefree Irish wrestler would be stricken from memory and a negative would be created. His mind formed a false identity before Pat/Paddy regained consciousness so that answers could be programmed without hesitation if questions were to be raised. Pat Whatshisface entered an elevator for the very first time in his life. He would have sworn otherwise of course, his mind’s false memory database had created many an amusing anecdote relating to elevators which he could muster without uncertainty. Dabbing a cut he carried along his hairline, he told himself he must have slipped in the bathroom and the knock had scrambled a few wires as he found himself needing to remind himself exactly of basic details that should have been like his own personal bible. He was the Jesus of himself after all. His mind intervened to clean up such vexations from the get-go. Your name is Pat Whatshisname and you were born in Galway, Ireland to Paddy O’Shea and Shelley Whatshisname. You don’t remember your parents because Shelley died soon after you were born and your Father abandoned you on a foster family in Bristol, England; hence your accent. Paul and Mary Johnson had no children of their own but fostered dozens of children at different stages. Being the resentful youth you were, you hated them for being too nice and left them at the age of sixteen. You can’t really remember anymore details because it’s been twenty years. The crusty old farts had made him porridge for breakfast three times a week even though the bastards knew he hated it. He guessed it was merely the knock and a few drinks would solve the problem of details. You’re a self made man whose seen it all. This is because you’re arrogant and violent. Your favorite food is pasta with almost anything and you secretly watch soap operas diligently. Your favorite color is green and your favorite sport of football. Time to go get laid.” The doors flicked open and Pat Whathisface unknowingly went through the lobby and then outside for the very first time. 4. Hurray for Henry! The Fighting Irish Bar, New York, NY - 10.27pm August 23rd Say, for some contemptible reason, one was to look for the whereabouts of the Henry Robinson, the man the Belfast Star had named “the most ruthless UVF Brigadier in ten years” (From the article “The New War”, October 15, 1997), you would have had to have escaped from the Gransha Mental Institution of Belfast (this can be done on ones own if duplicity is a plentiful virtue or with the help of an unknown aid as in Henry’s case). Your travels would have piloted you to Belfast City Airport, where you of course would have had to have stowed away (and in Henry’s case, gutted a few stewards and safely disposed of them in order to ensure security remained unbreached). Lazing in the baggage hull, it is optional whether you decided to nap/scope through the baggage. Henry did. He got himself a nice navy tank top and faded jeans as well as over $1000 in cash, not to mention a Motorola V2 cell phone and a laptop. He then packed his new items into a shoulder bag he conveniently found. The next step would involve moving from the hull of the plane into the blend of the crowd. This may prove difficult with airport security rife but with simple stealth techniques such as ones Henry picked up as he developed as a paramilitary brigadier, bloodshed can be avoided at this stage. You would have to get a taxi now. The driver may be an asshole so try to avoid conversation. You would direct him to the “Fighting Irish” bar which is on 23rd street just off Evangelo Avenue. You can sit back and relax knowing the heat is off you for at least another 12 hours at which point the bodies at Belfast airport will be found, CCTV will be checked and your face will be twisted and contorted onto computers in police stations in Belfast and New York. Eventually you will turn into 23rd street. It’s a quieter part of town with only small business on view - Rodger’s Quik Kut Barber’s, a pharmacy (or a “drug store”) and a business. There is one building though that should draw your attention - it will have the tricolour of Ireland painted onto the side of the establishment with the words “The Fighting Irish” painted elegantly in black over the top of this. The driver will stop, thank and pay him (don’t forget about tips); move to the front of the building and enter. If one was to have entered here at anywhere between 8.43pm and 11.37pm on the 23rd, you would have found Henry Robinson sat at the bar, drinking glasses of whiskey. Cleaning glasses whilst keeping a keen eye on the few stragglers left in the bar, the proprietor Thomas O’Doherty found himself drawn to this man that sat before him, this man with eyes only for the whiskey in front of him. He was a trifle surprised to begin with upon his entering; this bar was like a lattice of regulars - to see a new face was indeed a rare occasion and such an opportunity he always made an effort to seize. Worthwhile company was a rarity he had always reckoned and especially here where the usual drunken scoundrels were Americans who used the excuse of “Irish blood in them” to drink until they could no longer stand. “Hallo there friend, how are ye?” Thomas said as a multitude of lines emerged at the corners of his mouth as he smiled. This along with his graying hair confirmed him to be around 50ish. Thomas of course wouldn’t complain - business was business. And anyway, he was as plastic as the rest of them; he had changed his second name from Granger to O’Doherty in 1985, just around the same time his voice developed the twang of an Irish accent and his tongue inherited the “story” of his Grandfather’s daring adventure from the impoverished hills of Cork to the golden walkways of New York. It brought the Irish/”Irish” in from the street as they sought solace in numbers. It was a gimmick, he was a gimmick, they all were a gimmick. He was just another low life scumbag that had the flash of intelligence once to contrive a nice little scam. He carried no shame. Henry raised his eyes from studying his hands and looked at Thomas like he was some new and amusing type of life. Henry was pale and distracted but he had time there and then just to have a chuckle. “Tell me…do ye put on that accent for the business or because ye lost a bet?” Unknowingly, Thomas looked into the face of a man who had been a killer all his life. A man who’d ripped families apart and then fed whoever else was left enough china white to melt their brains out of their ears. A man who had instigated many hits but never seen his own one coming when it did, had never seen the masked men (one of which he now knew as Paddy O’Shea) tear into his house then gun them all down. They say only the wife and baby was killed and the husband survived. They were lying. Because Thomas O’Doherty was actually looking into the face of a ghost. One that had no quandary with the thought of gutting a certain Irish AWC wrestler. “Er…” Oh hunny, ye know aye don’t like ye drinking so late his wife’s voice whispered to him. “Just a few more darling then aye’ll get back onto the job.” Thomas took a step back as he watched the man mutter to himself. Interesting conversation was one thing, fucking whack attack was another. Thomas retreated slightly out of swinging range and then piped up again although with a much more frail demeanor. “Can aye get ye another?” Henry nodded then lowered his head, now emitting inaudible whisperings to his hands. Thomas, regaining his composure and convincing himself the guy just had a bad day, turned to the bar and lifted a stein up to a hanging bottle of Wild Turkey. He pushed up and the whiskey gurgled out in one measure and then up again for two. “There ye are friend, on the house,” Thomas said as cheerfully as he could. He decided it was better to stay on the man’s good side. “So…havin’ a bad day are ye?” “It hasn’t been great.” Thomas patted Henry’s hand. “Bad news?” “Bad news. Bad news from home.” “Aye’m sorry t’ hear that.” “Thanks.” Thomas fell silent. He thought about asking if there was anything he could do, now filled more with pity than anxiety and was just about to open his mouth when the door swung open and in stepped another stranger to Thomas’s delight. Another stranger who wore fancy clothes with fat pockets. “Howdy fuckers!” Pat Whatshisface had graced “The Fighting Irish” for the first time. 5. Finding Paddy O‘Shea The Fighting Irish Bar, New York, NY - 10.37pm August 23rd It’s safe to say Henry liked Pat from the moment he met him. He’d spent his life looking over his shoulder, so was the life of UVF brigadier, and so he really should have been wary of this supercilious stranger. But there was something so enticing about him - it was almost as if the charm discharged from his very pores. Taking a seat beside Henry on the bar, Pat rapped the counter with his knuckles, a broad grin plastered on his handsome face. “Oi oi, barman, a rum and cola for myself and whatever this fine chap beside me is having.” “I’m fine,” Henry muttered, not bothering to look up at Pat. Pat was looking at him now though, looking at this man before him the way a zoologist might look at an interesting new species. He was sitting down but Pat measured him to be at least 6’5. He wore a pair of faded jeans that were too tight and defined his bulging muscles and a navy tank top that failed to cover various tattoos that included an AK-47 running up the length of his inside forearm and a bloodied skull at the back of his neck. He surmised himself to be a hard ticket Pat decided and his shaven head only added to the notion. You don’t like people disrespecting you. Despite Pat being only 5’7, he had no problem now clasping him on the shoulder so tightly, he gripped the muscle beneath with his fingernails. “Ho ho ho now chap, it’s a beautiful day and not one to start off by making enemies with strangers.” Pat squeezed down on Henry’s shoulder harder so that the nails dug in further. Whereas a normal person would have elbowed Pat in the face in this situation or at the very least struggled from his grasp, Henry allowed himself the pleasure of pain. There was no way in hell he could admit he didn’t enjoy it there and then. He looked up at the smiling Pat’s face and smiled back. Despite the smell and the aggressive tattoos, his green eyes (although undoubtedly mad) weren’t without a sense of humor and even the remnants of his extensive intelligence. It was Thomas looking on that summed the moment up perfectly. Great….the screwball found a screwball friend. Thomas scuttled off to the liquor rack and with a glass on each hand, emitted shots of whiskey and vodka respectively from two marked bottles then went about garnishing the drinks with cola and ice. “Pat’s the name, Pat Whatshisface…and yourself?” “Wha’ kind of a stupid name’s that?” Henry chuckled though there was no venom in his reply. Pat remained silent, still adorning that plastic smile so that an uneasiness settled between the two. “Aye’m Henry…Henry Robinson.” As the drinks were placed in front of them Pat scooped his glass up and tapped it against the rim of Henry’s stein. “Here’s to a good night Henry.” Henry nodded. “T’ a good night.” The two nailed back their respective drinks as Thomas backed off. He’d decided he’d had enough ‘interesting conversation’ for one night. These two seemed made for one another and he didn’t want to get involved in what was becoming a blossoming friendship. Now Henry found himself drawn to initiate the conversation and he turned on his stool so he was facing Pat. “Ye really should watch who ye grab in future. Ye never know who may take offense to it and against a littlun like yourself, aye doubt there’d be any hesitation in makin‘ their feelings clear.” “I’ll chance it,” Pat said. He measured Henry once again. Sure he had like three stone on him but most of it was beer muscle, Pat decided in his conceited little mind. “I could take someone like you.” Henry’s face crinkled in amiable humor once more. “Maybe. Maybe not. But if ye think ye can just walk into my hood with an attitude like tha’, ye’ll soon realise that’s not how we play. All tha’ Marquees of Queensbury fairplay? Personally, if aye got me a piece of yer granny’s apple pie, aye’d shit on it.” “Foul mouth you have there, Henry,” Pat said mildly and with that same vexing smirk. “But then again, I wouldn’t expect anything less from a potato-picking inbred like yourself.” Henry made a disgusted blowing sound but did locate it in himself to snigger. “Anyway,” Pat continued. “Why fight? After all, I am a business man, you know? Just investing in a few stocks here, purchasing a few shares here. Good honest work. Don’t tell me Henry…gangster? You’re good at analyzing people “You probably have a little stash in your pocket right now, ready to push onto some kid, ready to poison them, hm? What if you were reported now with all that shit on you? Nasty, nasty, nasty, Henry. Bad boy. Do not pass go, do not collect two hundred dollars. Go directly to New York state jail.” Henry was looking jaded. Pat suddenly brought his foot down with a crash onto Henry’s foot. There was a quick series of emotions on Henry’s face, Pat had been sure to catch. Astonishment, pain and then…pleasure? “You want to listen when I talk Henry.” Henry turned and looked at Pat now for the first time with real interest. His former uneasy calm was nothing more than a memory now and his bulging eyes told the same story. He hadn’t been interested in anything in quite a whole…but this man here…he’s… Crazy as a box of frogs hunny! Ye be careful around him now, Henry! “Okay, aye’m listening.” You like to charm people into doing your bidding. With an extensive list of contacts, you find completing your tasks much more systematic. You after all have a father to find. “Thank you,” Pat beamed. “Now I’m sure there’s something in the world you want…I know there’s something I want…now I look at you and I see a man that could help me get what I want. And in return, maybe I could help you with your wish and-” “Paddy O’Shea,” Henry interrupted. Pat nearly fell off his seat. It was almost as if a complete stranger had recited his best kept secret to him. Now Pat was uneasy. “W-what?” “Paddy O’Shea…aye need t’ find him. Ye help me find him and aye’ll help you with whatever you want.” Paddy O’Shea…your father…the man who owes you money Pat and the only man in the world whose going to keep your sinking ship above water. And he’s looking for him too? Well, well, well Pat. You may have an accomplice here. You don’t have to like the man (after all, there’s no one in the world you’ve genuinely ever liked) but you can use him, because that’s the type of person you are. “You got it,” Pat replied. He was feeling so much better already. The signs were good, there was somebody out there who wanted him to find his father. Things had just got a whole lot more fascinating. Pat pulled two cigars from his inside pocket. He placed one to his lips and gave the other to Henry. “Light me up friend,” Henry beamed. Pat threw his head back and laughed hard. “We’re going to get along great Henry…I have a good feeling about it.” Pat pulled out a zippo lighter and lit himself then Henry up. “So what is it you want?” Henry asked before taking a long chesty drag from the cigar. “Nothing…nothing right now. But I have a feeling when the time comes, you’re going to be the man to bag me my dream. I’m a big man around this city and if you scratch my back, I’ll be sure to scratch yours.” Pat turned to the bartender. “Same again here my good man.” He turned to look around the bar and seen a young woman in the corner. She was beautiful but she was a slut he concluded. A winning combination. “And whatever that fine girl in the corner wants.” The woman overheard and smiled upon viewing Pat’s handsome face. Pat got up to move to her. “Where are ye going? We have business t’ discuss.” “Uh-uh. Business tomorrow…tonight my friend, me and you celebrate.” Pat grinned at Henry once more then moved away.
