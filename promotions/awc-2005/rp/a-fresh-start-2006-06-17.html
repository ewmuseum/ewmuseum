A Fresh Start

17 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/17/06 It was midnight in Belle Harbor, NY. It happened every night, sure, but tonight was different. For some, midnight was a time of fear. For others, a time never seen, because they were already within the deepest portion of sleep. For Rustin Slade, it was home. It was the only time of night where he could really think clearly. And thinking clearly is what Rustin needed this night; the night before Fresh!burst. What better way to do it than by stepping out into the cool breeze wafting off of Rockaway Beach? Although it was a predominantly Catholic town, Rustin had no affiliation. He found it futile to put hopes into a God that had not listened to him his entire life. A God that Rustin believed created him particularly for his own amusement. About the only thing that Rustin did not fail at was wrestling. He liked Belle Harbor for its serenity. The ability for it to put someone’s mind at ease. Somehow, however, Rustin didn’t think that his thoughts could be put at ease tonight, not even in the comfort of Belle Harbor. Shawn Singleton offered him a place in Oakland, so he could be closer to the team, but it was still too soon. He trusted Shawn, but the history between the two was rocky. You can only keep your enemies so close. He was crushed with the closing of 4WW, his home for the last year. For the year he had competed there and had gained much, but lost more. He was thinking of hanging up his boots with the closing, all of his hopes crushed. But then the offer came. The offer to start over. It was a comforting thought. He remembered the phone call like it was yesterday... “Rustin?” the voice asked. “Look, pal,” Rustin started. “Whatever you’re selling...” “Rustin, it’s Shawn,” Singleton said. Rustin stood silent for a minute. “Oh. What can I do for you Singleton?” “Is that any way to talk to an old friend?” Singleton answered. “Friend. Right.” Rustin replied dryly. “I’m not calling to dick around, Slade,” Singleton said. “Let me get to the point. Have you ever heard of AWC?” Rustin thought for a moment. It had sounded familiar. He tried to kick start his memory. AWC...AWC...Atlantic Wrestling Club? Yeah, that was it. It had to be. “Vaguely,” Rustin replied. “Atlantic Wrestling Club, right?” “Very good Slade,” Singleton replied. “What do you say?” “About what?” Rustin asked. “You and I both know that we’re two of God’s gifts to the wrestling world,” Singleton said. “You don’t find people like us anymore.” “What are you saying?” “Don’t play stupid. You’re smarter than that.” Rustin scoffed. “You and me?” “Precisely. The diamonds in the rough. The sole survivors of a dying breed...” Rustin stood silent again. “Spare me the melodrama. I don’t know about this. We had our run. Why push it any further?” Shawn laughed. “Because it can, Rustin. It can be pushed so much further. 4WW was torn apart when we were done with it. Don’t tell me you didn’t like that feeling. It gave you power. Hell, it gave me power. AWC can be our new playing ground.” Rustin let Shawn’s words sink in. It was true. It had given him power. It felt good, but at what cost? Rustin lost so much of himself in the process. Was he that desperate to get back into the ring? It had been relaxing the past couple of weeks, not having to worry about pouring himself into his matches. But he’d be lying if he said he wasn’t going through somewhat of a withdrawal. Wrestling was Rustin’s drug, and like any true-to-self addict, Rustin needed it. The temptation of power and destruction tempted Rustin again, tearing into him like a bear trap. “Singleton...” Rustin began. “Good, Rustin,” Singleton replied. “Wise decision.” And with that, Rustin heard the click on the other end. Rustin shook his head. Rather presumptuous of Singleton to assume his answer. Or had he really known him that well? Known him well enough to say that Rustin could not resist temptation. Certainly it was apparent when it came to girls. Rustin only needed to take a few looks at a hot girl, and he’d go weak at the knees. Then he’d attempt to flirt with her, and that’s where it all fell apart. It was always the same. They’d always laugh at him, never giving him a second chance. But then there was fame. It changed all of that. Girls flocked to him for a while, attracted rather than repulsed by his lack of charm. Or were they attracted to the fame? Rustin could never tell. Another downfall of his lack of perception... The night air dropped a few degrees as Rustin remembered the phone call, but still continued comforting him. He felt like he could say anything to the night air, and it wouldn’t laugh at him, unlike the girls. In a way, he was grateful to Singleton, but he dared not admit it. They were on a level where thanks wasn’t necessary. Each of them knew they were good. Now was their chance to prove it to the rest of the AWC. It would be a challenge, but Rustin did not put it beyond him. It was doable. After all, Shawn Singleton had more than enough confidence for the both of them. He had heard that he and Singleton were pitted up against a team known as The New Black. Rustin knew they were new to the AWC, just like him and Singleton, and that they too would have to prove themselves. Rustin didn’t know their history, their strengths, their weaknesses, but he knew one thing. It was a fresh start. Rustin swatted a mosquito on his arm, ending its reign of blood-drinking. Rustin looked angrily at the dead insect as he brushed it off into the sand. The cool breeze continued to seep into Rustin. The waves on Rockaway Beach slipped softly onto the shore, and then retreated. Rustin looked up at the moon, watching it illuminate the water. Something was wrong...Rustin no longer felt comfortable. The breeze no longer put him at ease. He looked at the water, again, seeking the relief he needed. Rustin tore off his shirt and ran into the water. He waded in waist deep and then submerged himself underwater. He stroked farther and farther into the bay. He came up periodically, grabbing fresh air, only to return moments later back under the surface. The cool water revitalized Rustin, and brought him back to the present; the past now washed away. It was a time for new beginnings...no time for remorse or contemplation. Rustin’s drug had been offered again, and his will was weak. Even if it couldn’t be shot or snorted, Rustin considered it to be the most potent drug he had ever encountered. It offered him a high that was unlike anything that could be offered by synthetic means. There was no taming the lust for power and destruction that was within Rustin now. Not even the cool, calm waters of Manhattan Bay. Rustin resurfaced, and swam back to shore. Dripping wet and glimmering in the midnight moonlight, Rustin was baptized... In the name of wrestling, power, and the fighting spirit...
