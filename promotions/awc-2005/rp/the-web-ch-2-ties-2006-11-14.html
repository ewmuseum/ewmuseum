The Web, ch. 2: Ties

14 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 14th November, 2006 The Japanese God of Thunder was reeling. Downed by another furious barrage of Liu Kang’s bicycle kicks, Raiden could feel the last few drops of his energy slowly fading away as his broken body hit the cold, unforgiving stone floor atop the Pyramid of Argus. The former protector of the Earthrealm knew the end was near; surely even his control over thunder and lightning could not save him now? He sluggishly clambered up to his feet as a small trickle of blood began to seep out of a small wound on his upper lip. Glancing with a bitter malice from under his traditional Japanese hat, he could see that his adversary was poised and ready to finish him off. One thing was for sure though, Raiden, the Japanese God of Thunder, was not merely going to lie down and let Liu Kang defeat him… Liu Kang, who had served Raiden and fought beside him for so many years beforehand… Liu Kang, who’s soul Raiden had battled for against the mighty shape-shifter Shang Tsung, and won… Liu Kang, who, more than anyone else, had felt bitterly betrayed by Raiden’s sudden turn to join sides with their former mutual enemy, Shao Kahn… Liu Kang, who had just launched himself towards his former master with a dangerous trademark flying kick. With just a split second to react, Raiden ducked Kang’s attempt and was able to grab his opponent by the neck before Liu Kang could react. As Raiden lifted Kang off the ground, he sent hundreds of bolts of painful electricity surging through his body, causing Kang’s body to spasm and gyrate on it’s own accord. THUD! Raiden threw his former protégé down to the ground with ruthless effectiveness. Liu Kang felt the pain buzzing through his body, but it was going to take more than that to stop him, and the livewire combatant was soon back on his feet, throwing some lightning fast jabs at the God of Thunder. Able to seemingly anticipate almost everything his opponent could throw at him, even in his weakened state Raiden’s ability to block Liu Kang’s assaults were remarkable. An opportunity presented itself; Kang had tired himself somewhat attempting to hit Raiden, and as Kang’s assaults slowed down, Raiden somehow managed to find a brief opening to connect with a couple of stiff punches to the jaw of Liu Kang. He had him backfooting now… The God of Thunder committed himself, he attempted an uppercut… But it was not to be. Liu Kang leapt backwards quickly, blocking the uppercut, and quickly threw himself towards his former ally, landing a stiff kick to Raiden’s face. Now the Japanese God of Thunder was in trouble, one more move and surely Kang’s redemption would be complete. Raiden was going to pay dearly for doing a deal with the devil and aligning himself with evil. Liu Kang swept Raiden off his feet with a legsweep; the God of Thunder hit the floor face first. If there had been any hope left in the heart of Raiden, it had just vanished. Even he knew he couldn’t go on for much longer against such a strong adversary… he was almost beginning to regret his decision to change his allegiances… and now, the all-powerful Thunder God was on the verge of being finished off. And sure enough, as soon as he had stood back up for one final hurrah, Raiden was hit with a trademark Flying Uppercut from Kang, sending him flying backwards and crashing down atop the Pyramid of Argus with an immense impact. It was over. Liu Kang had brought his former to justice at last. Although he possessed the power to brutally murder Raiden, Liu Kang chose not to, for it was not the Shaolin way. Instead, he had dis- “OMGOMGOMG!!!!!!!1 FUXIN STEWPID GAM!!!” Grobschnit threw the silver Playstation2 control pad against the laminate flooring in the living room of Andyville, leaving the slightest of dents in both the control pad and the light-oak finished flooring. The horrible wee green beastie from outer space let out an exasperated sigh as he hunched forward on the edge of the ridiculously comfortable light grey leather couch, holding his head in his hands. He had lost again – no matter what character he selected, he found it impossible to defeat the Scottish King of Cool at Mortal Kombat. “Come on now Grobby, cheer up…” Andy M. reassured, padding his green homeboy on the back, “it’s not that bleak, you almost had me with Scorpion, you should never have switched to Raiden dude, he’s too damn slow to take on a speedy beast like Liu Kang! Bad move all-round…” Grobschnit peeked out from between his stubby green fingers as Liu Kang’s pixelated figure celebrated on the widescreen television. He let out what can only be described as a faint growl, before leaning back on the sofa, taking a look around the lavishly-decorated room. Even on such a grim, murky afternoon the room was still full of light, mainly down to the massive bay windows on either side. “Eye dun lyke dis gam, Anne D…” barked Grobber, who today was wearing a black bandana, one of those ridiculously long t-shirts that goes down to the knees, and even a pair of stupidly baggy jeans which were hung well below the waist. “Evree tyme wee plai eye alwayz gettt beatenified… izz note fare! Kant eye juzt beet u wonce!?! Eye hayte dis krappo gam!” Andy, who was clothed in a tight black & green slayer t-shirt and a pair of black boot-cut jeans, raised an eyebrow as a pissed-off Grobschnit slammed his slimy green fists down on the couch. “Dude, please tell me you did NOT just say that?” Andy questioned. “Everyone knows that there is nothing more significant in this world than Mortal Kombat! It’s generally knowledge, dude… Mortal Kombat is more important than life and death, or something like that…” Of course, the notorious ‘S-C-H-N-I-T took no notice of this; he had the idea that the game was no more than a mere “peece uv karp,” as he might put it himself. Either way, Andy didn’t really feel the urge to defeat his lil’ green homeslice in virtual combat again – twelve times in a row was enough. At least his brother Cayle could put up a fight… Murrr opened his mouth to speak again, but before he could do so he felt a familiar buzzing in this pocket; his mobile phone was ringing. Andy stood up and pulled his large, navy-blue coloured Nokia old-school brick from his pocket before answering. “Hellooooo! Andy M., Scottish King of Cool, hero to millions, emperor of the free-world and all-round lovely man speaking, how may I be of assistance?!” he answered in a typically over-the-top manner. “Emmm, good afternoon Mr. M., this is Ian Davenport, head master of your son’s school,” replied the articulate voice of man clearly dumbfounded by the way in which Andy had answered the phone. “We have a pressing issue that needs to be discussed, would you be free at some point to come down to the school and have a little chat with us?” The Scottish King of Cool was a bit puzzled by this. “Umm, sure, okay, I guess so… what’s the matter, chief?” “Well, I don’t want to divulge too much information over the phone, I would much rather meet with you in person. But it concerns the progress of your son, Marvin…” “Oh shit,” Andy responded instinctively, “he’s not in any trouble, is he? He didn’t, like, put frogs in the girl’s locker-rooms or anything did he? Or throw eggs at some emos, or something…” Mr. Davenport sighed, clearly he had very little time for Andy’s theatrics. “No, nothing like that at all Mr. M. If you could just come and see us tomorrow morning we’ll discuss matters. I’ve got an opening at 10:30, is that alright for you?” asked the headmaster sharply. Without thinking Andy responded; “Yeah, sure, that works…” He was somewhat surprised at the manner in which Davenport was speaking to him. “Good, then I’ll see you then. Goodbye Mr. M.…” and just like that, Mr. Davenport hung up the phone, without even giving Andy the chance to respond with a “goodbye” of his own. That was probably a good idea for a busy man though, the last thing you want to do when you’ve got stuff that needs done is to get involved in a conversation with the Scottish King of Cool. Even still, Andy was taken aback by the conduct of the man in charge of his son’s education. -------------------- Andy arose the following morning with a head full of questions. What did Ian Davenport mean about his “concerns with Marvin’s progress?” Marvin had always been a good student at school, surely it couldn’t be academic concerns? But then again, he was such a friendly, timid child, so surely there couldn’t be behavioural problems either? And why exactly had Davenport spoken to Andy in such a repulsive way? Did he have something up his sleeve, or was that just the way he was with people? Either way, Andy knew he would find out the answers to his questions soon enough. Figuring he should dress in a somewhat “proper” manner compared to his usual attire, Andy threw on a decent sky blue shirt and some light-beige coloured chinos – he didn’t want Ian Davenport to think he was any more of a bum than he already did. Besides, this was a pretty high-class school; most of the pupils were the offspring of the rich, uptight and snobby. One would think that Andy M. would fit in fine with the other parents of this school, given that he was a relatively wealthy man himself, but the gulf in lifestyles between the school’s average parent and the Scottish King of Cool were pretty obvious, shown particularly well by the way that the Andymobile – Andy’s beloved customised multi-colour Volkswagen hippie van – stood out like a sore thumb in the school’s car park against a sea of upmarket Mercedes, Chrysler and Buick cars. As he finally reached the office of Ian Davenport, Andy glanced at his watch – 10:25am it read. “Thank god,” muttered the Scottish King of Cool under his breath. He wasn’t late; in fact, he was 5 minutes early. Andy gently tapped his knuckles against the frosted glass panel in the middle of the door, behind which he could hear a few soft, muffled voices, unfortunately none of which could be deciphered, such was their inaudibility. A few members of the school teaching staff passed Andy before there was a clear response of “Come in!” from the other side of the door, all of them looking at him like he was somewhere where he wasn’t supposed to be. Upon hearing the call to enter, Andy quickly slipped in the door, leaving the one uncomfortable atmosphere and walking straight into another. Standing up behind a cluttered desk stood a short, skinny man who stood barely over 5’ tall. The man - who by the looks of things was well into his 50’s – had a rodent-like look about his face. He was unshaven and what was left of his straggly brown hair was combed over the bald dome of his head. The add to the man’s unattractiveness, he wore a pair of milk bottle-thick black glasses, which sat squint over his broken nose. However, it was not this man – presumably Ian Davenport – that bothered Andy the most. Sat at the other side of the desk were Andy’s son, Marvin, dressed smartly in his school uniform, and his ex-wife Jane who was dressed casually in a navy blue zip-up sweater and a pair of blue jeans. ”Fuck!” Andy thought to himself, ”this is going to be bloody awkward…” “Mr. M.!” spoke the rat-like man at the other side of the desk in a voice that was familiar to Andy, “I am Ian Davenport, pleased to meet you…” he spoke in a voice that was instantly familiar to Andy. The Scottish King of Cool nodded once, before shaking hands with this rather odd-looking fellow. ”This is the guy that was a twat to me down the phone? Ha, gutted mate, you look like Splinter out of the Ninja Turtles… one nil me!” thought Andy, bringing a smile to his face. “Yeah, hi, I’m Andy…” responded Murrr, attempting rather unsuccessfully to subdue his own laughter. However, Andy’s amusement was soon cut short as he turned around to be met with the sight of his ex-wife’s familiar deep, hazel eyes staring back at him. Jane smiled and nodded at Andy, who responded with a soft, almost whispered “hey,” before looking down at his son, Marvin, who was staring a hole through the floor, looking pretty embarrassed and ashamed of himself. “Sit down Mr. M., please…” Ian Davenport almost demanded of Andy, who was still standing up. Not wishing to irritate the man any more than he possibly already had, Andy sat down without question. “Oh, sorry dude,” Andy said, sitting forward in the poorly-padded and rather uncomfortable chair he had just sat down in. “So, what’s the deal then?” he asked in a genuinely inquisitive tone of voice. “Okay, here’s the thing,” Davenport began, glancing between Andy and Jane, speaking in the unpleasant, Southern rasp that Andy had heard on the phone. “Ever since Marvin enrolled with us, he has been nothing short of exceptional. His grades have consistently been of a very high standard, he’s always taken a keen interest in his studies and there’s never been any behavioural issues…” Both Andy and Jane listened, waiting patiently for big “buuuut”… “He’s generally been a credit to the school as a whole, but…” Boom, there it is. “Well, recently, a few of his teachers have expressed their concern over Marvin’s performance in class tests. In English, for example, he scored a 57% in his previous test compared with his usual average of 86%. Scores in other classes, notably math, have also been falling. Not only that, but the same teachers stated they felt Marvin at lost a lot of interest in the classes; he’s not contributing as much as he used to…” This came as a total shock to Andy. He had also been led to believe that his son was the archetype of a great student, even at his young age. “Marvin, how come you didn’t tell anyone?” Jane gently enquired, getting nothing more than an embarrassed look back from her son before Davenport resumed… “Needless to say, at the moment, Marvin is unfortunately currently falling somewhat short of the high standards we set here,” snarled Ian Davenport, sounding a whole lot less compassionate than he was possibly trying to sound. “Obviously, we all want to see Marvin return to his previous high standard of work, because at the rate he’s going, he could soon fall behind his classmates...” The Scottish King of Cool felt another disappointment weigh on his mind, but he knew that his son had it in him to work hard and get things back on track. “Alright, I get it... so do you reckon there’s anything we could do to help him out?” Andy asked, exchanging a quick glance with Jane. “Well, there’s not a great deal really,” sighed Davenport. “I mean, you could always spend a little more time helping him with his homework, giving positive feedback, etc., but I guess that might be a little hard, what with you two being separated and all…” Jeesh, way to enhance the awkwardness, Batman! Murrr screwed up his face, did Davenport intend to impose such an aura of discomfort or did he not realise he had done so? Either way, Andy had had enough; now he knew the score, he didn’t want to hang around much longer. “Is that it? Anything else?” he enquired restlessly. “No, I think that’s pretty much it. I just thought that we should inform you of the situation face to face, a little more personal that doing it through a phone line. Hopefully our little meeting today has sparked up a little enthusiasm inside Marvin here, too...” rasped Ian Davenport, who glanced at Marvin, expecting some kind of response. However, Marvin kept his eyes faced firmly on the floor, not looking up for a second. “Alright, thanks Mr. Davenport, I’ll make sure that we never have to meet like this again,” quipped Jane as if she had direct control over her son’s academic performance. Davenport nodded, and without saying a word, watched as a disappointed Andy M. got up from the chair and held the door open for his son and ex-wife to walk out. Out in the corridor, Jane knelt down to hug her disappointed son. “Don’t worry, it’ll be alright,” she said. “We’ll have a little talk when you get back from school, okay?” she enquired, Marvin nodding indifferently in response. Not entirely sure how to act, the Scottish King of Cool watched as Jane stood back up and Marvin walked away, along the seemingly endless corridor towards his next classroom. Now it was just Andy and Jane. Awkward. As. Fuck. The two stood for a few seconds, briefly exchanging uncomfortable glances. This was the first time they had been alone in a long, long time… “So, errr, what a creepy dude, eh?” chimed in Andy, attempting to crack the uncomfortable atmosphere that had come between himself and his ex-wife. Jane chuckled in response, Murrr couldn’t tell if it was genuine or forced… “Yeah, he kind of reminded me of that “guy” Splinter from the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles..” “God, that’s exactly what I thought!” Andy responded, feeling the awkward aura easing off somewhat. “From the way he barked down the telephone, I expected this big tank, like a 7’ monster who eats nuts and bolts for breakfast, not some scrawny-ass bitch!” Again Jane tittered. “Tell me about it… say, Andy, I’ve got about an hour to kill, do you fancy getting a coffee or something?” Shit. That was somewhat unexpected. Ex-wives are not supposed to ask you out for a cup of coffee, that’s not how it fucking works. Andy frantically searched the back of his mind for a good enough excuse to decline, but he just couldn’t find anything; he was drawing a blank. Either way, he didn’t particularly feel the need to lie to Jane, despite how awkward the situation may become. “Uh, sure, why not?” Andy hesitantly responded after a short pause. ”That was probably a very, very bad idea…” he immediately thought to himself. ”What the hell are we going to talk about!? This is going to be an absolute silence-fest! Oh well, committed now, might as well get it over and done with…” -------------------- And yet, almost as the two of them stepped into the Andymobile almost all of Andy’s doubts went out the window. Any sense of discomfort or awkwardness had completely disappeared; surprisingly replaced by light-hearted discussion that would have seemed like an impossibility for Andy and Jane twelve months ago. He had a cappuccino; she had some kind of ridiculously pretentiously-named thing – Mochafrappalattechino, or something along those lines. And as they sat down in the faceless coffee shop – which was full of people who looked like they possessed a PHD in “how to be a bit of a twat” – after Andy had gotten over the burning of his tongue following an ill-advised large gulp of hot coffee, the inevitable came up – Marvin. “So, what are we going to do about it?” Jane asked, unzipping her sweater slowly. “Hopefully, it’s just a little glitch Marvin’s having…” she continued, slipping the sweater off, revealing a tight, black tank top below. Despite the route that the conversation had taken, Andy found it very hard not to notice how good Jane looked toward, particularly having shed that extra layer. He couldn’t help but wonder though, had she dressed like that on purpose? Was she trying to lead him on. ”Of course not. Snap out of it. Ex-wives don’t do that…” “Eh, I guess it’s just a passing phase,” Andy began, “he’s always done really well, I think he’ll get through this fine if he just knuckles down. I’ll try and give him a little bit more encouragement next time I see him though, I suppose a little chat every now and then wouldn’t go a-miss either. Besides, I don’t really want to be called into Ratboy’s office again any time soon!” Jane laughed again, a smile lingering on her lips. She took a long, final sip of coffee, finishing it off, before glancing at the time on her silver wristwatch. “Gee, look at that, I have to be at work in half an hour, so I better run…” she said, quickly stepping up, gathering up her purse and sweater from the chair. Andy stood up also… “It’s been nice, Andy…” Jane said, as Andy felt the unwelcome awkwardness creep over him again. He didn’t really know what to do, should he offer her a lift to work? Or should he just let her go? It’s not like she had far to travel anyway… “Yeah, catch you later then…” Andy responded, taking the easy route out. Jane smiled and nodded her head, before leaning in towards the Scottish King of Cool and embracing him with a brief hug. “Bye bye…” she muttered, leaving Murrr speechless. He could only watch as she walked out the door and onto the street… A hug? What the fuck was going on?
