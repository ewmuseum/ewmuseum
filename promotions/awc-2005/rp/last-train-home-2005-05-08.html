Last Train Home

8 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 08, 2005 It was a few days after my sixteenth birthday... “I swear mom, experts estimate today's hydroponically grown marijuana is up to twenty five times stronger than naturally grown crops! Weighing up all the possibilities, I feel I am getting quality as well as quantity.” I smiled at her, yet see seemed none too impressed. “Experts in what? Masturbation possibly, but certainly not marijuana!” Okay, she wasn’t impressed at all. I was thinking of something else to say, and then it hit me. “It’s not my fault! I swear.” I stopped and swallowed hard, this wasn’t a good idea and I knew it. “It was way back in the US all this shit came from, out of racial hatred.” I could see her eyes widen. But for some reason I carried on. I must have been possessed or something. “It will take a while to disappear even after the public…” By now I was slurring my words. “…is aware of the facts, people still believe in that Jesus guy right.” Under the circumstances though, I thought it was a well thought out explanation. “THAT’S IT! Ralph get out and don’t come back. I am sick of your behaviour.” I was surprised as hell. I didn’t think she would take it that serious. “If this is about the me drinking from the milk carton again, I am sorry it wont happy again. Honestly!” I gave off a goofy smile that showed of my huge braces. I wouldn’t of had crooked teeth if it wasn’t for that bully. I only tried to get it on with his sister; I mean do people have to take everything so serious all the time? “Its not about the milk, does this look like its about the milk? Goddamn you stupid son of a bitch.” I guess see was calling herself right there. I didn’t want to say anything at the time, but ha! She called herself a bitch, the dumb bitch. “Fuck off and don’t come back, you can go and dangle yourself from a tree for all I care.” I guess she was a tad aggravated at me. I went to leave my bedroom, but you know like they ‘say never turn back’? I just had to go and do it. “Before I go can I have my plant?” I got caught up in the moment. I guess it was like James Bond… err well it wasn’t but I glided in the air anyway, it was like slow motion as I ducked under the plant thrown towards me. “Nooo… why did you do that?” I crouched over the plant. It had snapped in half from crashing off the floor. I pulled it in close to me as if I was comforting it. “She just doesn’t understand. It will be alright she did not mean to hurt you.” I was whispering to the plants… err I suppose stem. “Get your stuff and leave the house.” My mother stormed out of the room. Leaving me alone with the plant. “Stupidly generous, drunken, drug fucked and utterly without a clue, bitch! That’s what she is. Don’t worry papa is still here.” I didn’t truly know what I was doing back then. I think it was just one of those periods of my life I would rather forget. I sat up as my eyes circled around the room. There was only one thing left to do, have a wash, put on a clean pair of underwear, gather up all my stuff and wash my bong. Well I had a few things to do. I gathered I would be sleeping rough for a while until I decided I would go back to my dorm at boarding school. I walked down the stairs. Well step anyway, I didn’t live in a big house. We only had one room. Which could be considered as the dinning room, living room, kitchen, bedroom, attic and even the basement, it was like a mansion combined into one miniature room. Hell it was cosy I guess. I would sleep in the bathtub. This was what I called the second floor as we had one step to get into it. I suppose the bathroom was a good enough bedroom for me. I was tempted to kill my mother. But I guess I was in enough trouble already so I left without saying another word, with a brown bag full stuff. I didn’t know where I was heading. I just kept walking forwards it seemed to do the trick nicely. My boarding school kept popping into my mind. I had another week to kill before I was meant to go back. It wasn’t too bad but I wasn’t doing too good to be honest. I did tryout for the wrestling team. I got on it as well. But on the night of the first meet I came up with this move, I called it ‘The Lower Below’. Rubbish name but I wasn’t too clever. The move resulted in me getting suspended from the team. I didn’t realise slapping someone on the buttocks, then sweeping their legs was against the rules. Maybe however it was the fact I started to stomp on his head? I was setting him up for a moonsault, then I realised there was no ropes. Yeah, it sucked I know. I believe it was getting quite dark. So I started looking for an alleyway. It wasn’t long before I spotted some flames. When I got to where the flames were, an old man with a long greyish beard, which covered most of his face. Was sat down warming himself up. The one thing I still remember to this day about him was his eyes. The despair, the desire, the bitterness, the happiness, his eyes they told his own story. As I sat down next to him on an old thrown out mattress, next to the burning flames under the pitch-black darkness. I began wonder what was going to become of me. I wasn’t anything special; I was just another idiot with no future ahead of him. I was just another worthless child. That’s what I felt like at the time. I must have been sat down for well over an hour before we even spoke. We hadn’t even exchanged eye contact. “Hello…” I was really shocked. I just looked at him and smiled. That night was different to say the least. Some guy named Ryan McPherson kept trying to hustle us into fighting each other. Nothing happened though, but I would’ve totally kicked his arse. We didn’t say anything else until the next morning. There were a couple of odd groans and grunts, however but we didn’t exchange any verbal contact, until. “I found a opened tin of beans in the dumpster, would you like some?” I had gone on the hunt for some food that morning. As I forget to rob some money from my mother when I left home. I am so stupid sometimes. I sat down on the mattress. He didn’t say anything but I could tell by his eyes he wanted them. So I gave them to him. It’s funny how powerful your eyes can be sometimes. So complex, yet if you poke them they will still water, weird. I fumbled through my brown bag. I found a retro Chocolate Bar filled with nostalgic favours and embedded with the finest gourmet chocolate. They really know how to sell those bars. By the time I had taken the first bite, the old man was staring at me. I knew he wanted the bar, he probably needed it more than me. So I gave it to him. Looking back over it I wished I had eaten it, I was hungry for hours after that. He had consumed the bar in no time at all. We sat in silence again. Being homeless wasn’t as fun as I first thought. You have freedom but absolutely nothing to do. A while later the old man gathered up his stuff. He walked off slowly and turned round and spoke in a gentle voice. I still remember what he said to this day. For once it actually meant something to me. “The golden opportunity you are seeking is in yourself. It is not in your environment; it is not in luck or chance, or the help of others; it is in yourself alone.' I fell back into the mattress. It must have been around noon. I went to look in the dumpster behind a shop where I found the tin of beans earlier. I thought if I was lucky enough I might find some again. I could see the dumpster, it was a few meters away from me. I began to run towards it, like you would when you embraced someone who you hadn’t seen for a very long time. I dived over into it sadly I smashed my head. They had only gone and empted the dumpster. It hurt like hell. I guess that’s what happens when you get too excited and go and do something stupid. I had a real problem when it comes to things like this. I would on average do something stupid once a day, even to this day. I climbed out of the dumpster and walked across the road. A cop car drove by and in the back was the old man. Blood was soaked into his beard. I stared at him as the car drove off into the distances. View Ralph Richards 's Biography
