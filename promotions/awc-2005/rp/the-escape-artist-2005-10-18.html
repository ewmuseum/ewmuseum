The Escape Artist

18 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 18/10/05 Harry was and is John’s closest confidante. He was the first person John came out to and admitted he was a wrestling fan, but before he had there was only one man who knew of John’s clandestine passion. How John stumbled upon him isn’t important. He called himself ‘The Escape Artist’ and he was just that, “Wrestling is the most spectacular and ancient martial art, they found hieroglyphics in Egypt of wrestling holds” he looked like Salvador Dali, they shared the same intense eyes and insane moustache. His house was immensely tall because each floor of it had but one room and a staircase. By and large the house was peeling, it was always moist yet in a very dry way rays of sunlight would expose mists of dust. John cycled to the old man’s house and ran up his five flights of stairs as a warm up en route to the most (infact the only) spectacular part of his house, the roof. The roof was flat, the steel girders that supported the house extended twelve feet above and each had three turnbuckles holding creaky ring ropes. On top of the girders corrugated iron sheets drained away rainwater. “Never let yourself get thrown out of the ring, this is a temple and the principal of the thing will cost you your life here, I shall never forget Alan” he would say staring to the distant ground. Alan was a cat who died of natural causes, but The Escape Artist would never tell John this, only let him assume what he would. John was a natural. The Escape Artist never taught John holds, only escapes, the counter holds would flow into place and were felt, never shown. As time elapsed training became intense and immensely competitive. John wanted to pin The Escape Artist, he began using Judo holds and throws. In the heat of the moment John once tried to throw The Escape Artist out of the ring and found himself in the blink of an eye caught up in the ropes dangling on the wrong side of the rooftop ring. The Escape Artist grew disgusted at his pupil as his ignorance began to make him a grappling machine, callous. Their bouts became so fast, in their penultimate contest they ran the ropes. The Escape Artist had not run ropes since his days as a young happy-go-lucky upstart; John had never run the ropes. Ducking, dodging, side stepping – the pair were sprinting with all their might, but it was The Escape Artist who sprang off the second and finished John with a cross body press. He did not see John to the door that day. It was autumn on the final day, John ran up the stairs. The breeze swept the ring of dead leaves that had drifted in. John lifted the hatch and with immediacy tried a single leg pick up, he knew the old timer was ready for him. There was no build up of speed in this bout, the two countered and countered again with ferocity. They once again ended up hitting the ropes. James took the cross body press as he had before, but this time had the presence of mind, timing and momentum to continue the motion and gain a cross press of his own, The Escape Artist did just that at two and threw his body at the ropes. John hit the floor, the sleep caught his opponent off guard for but a split second and despite making the trip The Escape Artist shoulder rolled up and sprang back off the ropes. Amazed John attempted a hip throw, the old man head sprung his counter and again shot into the ropes, which creaked their final creak followed by a snap. The Escape Artist was never pinned for three by John. That was the important thing to remember. In life there was always the possibility of defeat, it was perhaps inevitable. In death The Escape Artist was immortal, undefeated, triumphant. John walked home that day.
