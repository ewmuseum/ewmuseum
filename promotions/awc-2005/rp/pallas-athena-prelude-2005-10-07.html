Pallas Athena: Prelude

7 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/7/05 KOAN After a long and arduous journey a young Japanese man arrived deep in a forest where the teacher of his choice was living in a small house he had made. When the student arrived, the teacher was sweeping up fallen leaves. Greeting his master, the young man received no greeting in return. And to all his questions there were no replies. Realizing there was nothing he could do to get the teacher's attention, the student went to another part of the same forest and built himself a house. Years later, when he was sweeping up fallen leaves, he was enlightened. He then dropped everything, ran through the forest to his teacher, and said, 'Thank you.' Sunday, August 24th, 1986 Nagano Mountains Japan Kaze Hogo emerged in a snow-laden glade, a small verdant meadow ringed by a tight circle of vast, ancient evergreen trees. His path had brought him to a spot of rare beauty which appeared untouched to the untrained eye. Through the occasional falling snowflakes Kaze could see the famous hot springs of the Nagano Mountains. Here, amongst the conifers and deciduous trees, at the foot of the Shiga Kogen volcano, he would find his prey. Dropping to his knees Kaze found what he was looking for- an almost imperceptible path where the snow had been lightly walked over. Yes, his prey had come this way. Quickly he followed the trail where it slipped between two evergreens. He parted wet, slush covered branches and stepped out of the glade, blinking away the morning sunlight. Once his eyes adjusted to the shadows of the dim light of the woodlands, he saw a small dirt path winding around the hot springs of Shiga Kogen. A familiar site in this part of central Japan is the snow monkey, the Japanese macaque, or Nihon zaru. A small herd of them were soaking up the watery warmth of a hot spring in the midst of a bone chilling, wintery landscape. They pay no attention to Kaze. They were used to occasional visitors. Kaze's quarry did not know they were being followed. Why were they taking such a hidden path? Kaze didn't stay to think too long. He slipped under the evergreen branches and set off down the path. There was little for him to follow here but he didn't let it stop him. The two he was following were amongst the best fighters in Japan, highly trained and skilled. And they knew that they were wanted back in Tokyo, so they were being extra careful to stay out of sight, out of reach. But he had been given information, at a steep price to his masters. This is where they came every morning for meditation and peace. There would be none of that today. The assassin glided silently along the path, his blood quickening at the thought of victory that lay ahead. Victory and money. There were years of planning and vengeance behind this final mission. Its' completion would bring him much fame and gold. The proper means and moment were at hand and he was proud to be the one to land the final strike, the last blow. The path ended at a stone wall and the assassin stopped, paused, observed. The snow monkeys watched with curiosity but didn't stare long enough to not get back to preening each other and enjoying the hot steam springs. Kaze crouched in the shadow of the wall and examined the scene spread out before him. Beyond the wall was a garden sprawled out amongst more hot springs. It was a lovely sight to behold. The gardener was an enlightened man for sure, thought Kaze. It would be easy for Japanese to be seduced by this lovely sight before them. He could see why the fighters had chosen here to hide. He lifted his head to look around and saw the stack of a chimney blowing small puffs of smoke over the top of snow covered trees. He had found his prey. He was right in the middle of their lair. With renewed purpose he planned his attack. He could not see anyone. His situation could hardly seem better. Get to the house, find the couple, and strike them down. Only snow monkeys stood before him and his mission. He would kill, withdraw, and come back and kill again if need be until they were no more and the family tree had been chopped down and destroyed. He started down the slope and around great grey stones that were used to ring the hot springs. The garden had been set up like a maze. He passed a great waterfall where a slew of the monkeys bathed. He made his way up and down the slope of the mountain side, the path winding around great trees and springs and cropped bushes and zen gardens. With a growing sense of dismay he passed winter trees bearing red ripe fruit, ponds filled with small bright fish, and fountains and arbors. he found himself at the foot of a great waterfall, filled with bathing snow monkeys. What the devil? Had he gone in a complete circle? Before he could castigate himself, the assassin heard a faint clicking sound, not far away. He trailed it to a large circular garden, ringed with rare winter flowers that attracted the nihon zaru. Many paths led out of this small garden, which was dominated by pale azure blue flowering bushes in a bed shaped like a butterfly. In the center of the garden stood an old man. He was snipping away at the bushes, busy and hard at work. Surely this was the center of the garden maze and the old gardener would be more then happy to tell Kaze the whereabouts of the young couple he was going to kill. Certainly he would talk at the tip of a long slender stiletto blade. Kaze slipped into the garden. As he did, a group of snow monkeys ran to get out of his way, and the gardener looked up, his large brown eyes lit with gentle curiosity at the disruption. His gaze fell upon the intruder and he raised his hand to shout out a greeting. No! Kaze could not let his presence be known. Quickly he threw his stiletto. A look of surprise crossed the old man's face as he fumbled with the knife in his chest. He fell heavily to the ground. The monkeys started to scream. Kaze sped across the garden. He crouched besides the corpse, a flash of blue catching his eye. He reached for it. Beneath the woolen coat of the old man was a robe of brilliant blue. It's touch was unmistakable. Silk! And those eyes, that face! The assassin dropped to his heels, dizzy with elation. By the most fortunate of mistakes he had just killed Shujin Hamada, head of the Hamada clan! The monkeys... They were calling out to each other in their own language, a high pitched screech. Someone would have heard it by now. Before he could move, a keening scream broke through the snowy branches. It was an anguished female voice. A man had run into the clearing carrying a rifle. A woman was with him. In an instant Kaze leaped to his feet and twirled, but not before the crack of a gunpowder exploded across the mountainside. The stunning impact of the shot spun him around and he fell to his knees. A burning flash of pain screamed at him to get up and move. Summoming up all his self-will he forgot about the pain and turned, drawing his own weapon. He could still kill them both, the wench and her husband. Kill them and then make his escape. 'This way!' The voice signaled more people were coming. The screams and the gunshot had alerted the guards. He could hear them rapidly approaching through the snow-covered paths. He must not be captured and questioned! Die for the cause another time. He hesitated for only a moment and then fled back toward the glade, back toward his getaway car. Another shot rang out and more screams and screeches. Breathing hard he plunged through the bushes and onto a gravely road. How long had he been running? The car was there. He was dragged in and the screech of tires was the last thing he heard in the clean cold air of Shiga Kogen. Strong, slender arms eased him onto the seat of the black sedan as it raced down the mountain. 'Kaze! Tell me what happened!' 'This was the place, this was their hiding place. The House of Shujin Hamada. The father lies dead.' Kaze gasped for air. A triumphant ringing cry escaped the assassin's companion. The car sped across a small wooden bridge over a trickling stream. 'And that bitch? That traitorous bitch?' 'They still live.' The effort of speaking brought fresh pangs of pain to Kaze's face. He grimaced and grasped at the wound at his side. 'Take ease my friend. Nikkou and her gaijin lover will soon follow Shujin Hamada to their death.' He gently moved the assassin's hand away and put pressure on the wound. 'Were you seen?' 'Yes,' the answer coming from between gritted teeth. The hand on the wound tensed for a second, and then eased. 'Even so, you have done well.' With a quick motion, the man plunged his hands into the wound, grabbed Kaze's lungs, and ripped them out of his body! The lifeblood of Kaze Hogo is stilled forever. 'But not good enough.' Wednesday, October 7th, 2005 JFK Airport, Queens,New York United States Megumi 'Butterfly' Hamada and her boyfriend Tozen Fujita are at the airport, having finally landed on American soil. SCW cameras are there to greet the newest addition to the roster. Being a talented foreign star gets her alot of attention. She waves at the cameras as they film her waiting at the luggage terminal. Cut to... Megumi is at a newsstand in the airport. She shows Tozen a women's magazine cover, points at the price and he shakes his head and smiles. He hands her another magazine and she smiles and holds it up for the camera to see. 'AWC Wrestling Monthly' is splashed across the cover. Megumi puts the magazines on the counter and asks the clerk for the price. She digs a few bills out of her pocket and pays for them. She flips through the first few pages. Cut to... Tozen smiles at the camera as he sits down at a McDonald's. Megumi grabs a cheeseburger and waves to the camera again. She takes a bite and laughs. Tozen says a few words and looks at the camera and also laughs. Cut to... The cameraman asks Megumi about her first match. 'I am very honored to be wrestling my first match for AWC this Friday at 'Fresh'. This is a new start for me. I was successful in Japan and accomplished many of my goals, but it is well known that to truly become successful in professional fighting you have to come here to America, where the greatest fighters from all over the world are assembled. I look up and down the roster of the AWC and am very impressed with the wrestlers that work there... I mean here. Ha ha! It will be an honor to step into the ring against them. As far as my match with Juri Hyobanshi? I am looking forward to my first match, with whomever it might be. Perhaps Juri and myself can give the AWC and the fans something impressive to see. I know I will try my best. It is important that I make a good first impression, so I will try hard to win my match. I put alot of pride and allot of work into my wrestling. It is the moist important thing in my life. I believe I have the fighting spirit within my soul and that it is strong. And I am fighting Juri because she is there to fight. I hope that we have a competitive match and the fans enjoy it. I know many people believe that Juri is a veteran and she has intense fighting spirit. I am walking into my first match as an underdog and that suits me well. I am not one for much talking or boasting. I am also not one to appreciate the same from my opponents. I have not heard anything from Juri yet but I know what must be thinking. I won't reveal my strategy for our match but believe me. I will be ready for her Rapture Eternal. Her claim is that no one has ever been able to counter that submission move, but don't be surprised if I am the first. But all competitiveness aside, I hope we can become good friends afterwards. We are both Japanese. We have much in common. Maybe she can teach me her style and I would be honored to teach her mine as well. I also am looking forward to meeting the AWC champions, like Pierce Lavelle, and Tim Shipley. And also the wrestlers I have heard of, like The British Bomber and Red Rock and Mike Wade. And of course, ha ha, Mr. Harber. Thank you. Thank you very much. Doumo arigatou gozai-masu.' ... end ...
