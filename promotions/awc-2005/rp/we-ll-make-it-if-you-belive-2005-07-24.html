We'll make it if you belive

24 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: the now People often call Gary, Indiana a ghetto; often very nonchalantly. Chances are these people have never been to Gary. “You’ve had a lead-foot all night. Do you really have to speed right in to town?” With how much I’ve been concentrating on gripping the wheel, I’ve actually forgotten how fast I’ve been going. But my eyes dart around too fast to be caught by some smug DPS piece of shit. ”Chances are the Highway Patrol are perched right around here.” She says whilst pointing ahead and to the right, towards a big tree that draped over the highway. It’s too hard to see if there’s anyone behind but I don’t care. I’ll slow down later I figure. People in Gary don’t really act with the same standards of living as most cities in the States. While each society is relatively different all depending on which area of the country you’re in, the rather large would-be metropolis still had the vibes of a creepy small town. I pass the tree at 84. Nothing on the other side. By the way I’ve been driving, it’s no surprise we made it here as quick as we have. I didn’t even consider stopping in my hometown, despite driving despicably close to it. I figure whatever loose-ends my self has left can’t be dealt with now. ”And you’re absolutely right. We don’t need to go home. People will ask questions.” Too many questions. ”Like, ‘where were you?’ or ‘why did you go?’ They probably won’t even let you leave again.” She says staring at me. ”Heh.” I snort back what mucus escaped in that outburst. It’s not like my father can really hold me back; that asshole’s been sitting in the same bed for seven years, he’s not in any position to stop me. Me chuckling is somewhat derogatory towards her. ”Right, that’s funny, you’re dad. Stop to figure the friends you left back there, including my boyfriend? I figure he’s going to have a ton of questions for you. And they won’t stop prying until you explain it all, every single detail. And you know you’ll crack, @%@#, you always do. When you break down, you’ll tell them every last detail. They’ll annoy you by asking you so much, you’ll just want to move on and come clean. ”Or maybe they might ignite some spark of hope; just enough to convince yourself that they will be able to help you, or that you could make them understand. And then you’ll spill your guts.” I see street lights ahead. It’s hard to listen to her, especially when everything she says is aggravating. So this is the part where I do my dammedest ignoring her as I begin to come in to town, with the moon setting early against the right side of the windshield that it almost looks like an extra sunrise. Because I can’t multi-task, I set to remind myself to thank God for my ability to be easily amused. ”Try and ignore me, but you know what I say is true. Christ knows what they’ll do when you tell them everything, even I can’t predict that.” She can’t even predict where Cops are setting speed traps; referencing earlier, while paying attention to the white bumper that extended out and decelerating my speed. She scrunches her nose up, letting her tone get a tad bit too snotty for me. “I’m not a fucking psychic, okay, I just understand how people will react when they think you’re crazy. It took a while for me to get the hang of it, but trust me, seeing your friends would have been a whole heap of trouble.” ^f^l^a^s^h^b^a^c^k^s^c^e^n^e^ This is why we avoided my hometown. Once I was convinced to go to Gary, (or as I like to call it, “the Degobah system”) we hit the road and we hit it with a vengeance. No nonsense like stopping to hassle with dim-witted-but-good-intentioned friends who had the means to put my ultimate quest on a permanent hiatus. No sir-ee-bob, they would not see me for another great while. This meant if I was going to get on television, I would need to stay hidden. I guess this means her ‘mask’ idea isn’t out the window after all. And now comes the topic of my untimely return/rebirth, and where it should take place. My concerns, apparently, already were addressed. ”As soon as you finish up in Gary, we’re going to head back down for the border.” This notion was a little ridiculous, considering how close we were already. Lord knows I hate driving in the desert, and coming back didn’t sound like anything resembling pleasant. ”We’re heading for a plane in Mexico City that will take you to Honduras. There you will meet with talent agents from the Atlantic Wrestling Club who will more than likely analyze your new-found skill. You will impress them, and they will be implied to use you rather quickly.” Fuck this place. “I am not wrestling for some South-of-the-border, good-of-the-sport fed that probably doesn’t even believe in paying their talent.” Admit it; the idea of a “Club” sounds terrible. ”It is not a Lucha-Fed! They stay close to that side of the sea, alright? They’re set to tour the U.K. afterwards.” And that is so much better! “Hahahaha! London, sweet! Whatever, okay, they smell over there and it’s like, normal to them. And that’s just creepy.” ”Where else are you going to go? You don’t have many options.” She says, being difficult. “Fuck, man, maybe Pr-“ ”Don’t you dare say Prime.” She interrupts me. “They have standards.” I give her the evil eye. “Fucking Core, then.” She laughs, pretty loudly. I didn’t really think it would convince her, in fact I knew what she had in mind was the best idea and there most likely was no changing her mind otherwise. The truth was, I hated the desert. Yes, that’s the only reason why I say fuck going to the AWC. I’d be driving all the way up to Gary, and then back down here, all the way through to Mexico City. I’m going to have to stop and see my family. ”You are NOT going to see your family! My God, do you hate Arizona that much? Is it so hard to drive in the sun?” ”Oh, go ahead; convince yourself that this is a beautiful scene. But after about five minutes of staring at dead saguaro cacti and dodging coyotes, you begin to notice that the stretch of the small of your back, all the way down to your ass crack, has begun to put the Nile River to shame. You fucking drown in sweat, all right!” “You have air-conditioning, you pussy!” That’s what they all say. They think that the A/C unit can handle it. Well I laugh at that. “Haha, we shall see. Arizona will destroy your sense of will with the fucking power of the sun.” ”So does that mean we’re going?” she asks hopeful. ”I fail to see the reason to go to AWC, still.” And then she does it: she displays a professional flyer showcasing Tim Shipley, the one and only, and his union glorified with Pierce Lavelle taking place on the event titled Solarized. Tim Shipley, doing nothing. Tim Shipley, standing victorious. Tim Shipley, plain as hell. Tim Shipley, doing it a-fucking-gain. Tim Shipley, being famous and doing nothing to deserve it. ”It’s all the same, you know. It’s just up to you to show these assholes what they’re really watching.” >f>a>d>e>o>u>t>t>o>t>h>e>n>o>w> Narrator: People say Gary is a ghetto. But what they don’t understand about Gary is that people who pass by can’t really understand what takes place. The agenda there is so much darker that the average way of life was less than honorable. Two years ago, it was over-run by a man named Josiah. Yes, a single man. It started by eliminating random criminals. Then it became more elaborate, disrupting scandals and stopping major deals and shit. It was hard for people to act without getting caught, but it was all for a purpose. You see, Josiah had this planned all along. He attacked until he caught something that snagged with importance. Rumors of his crimes began to spread among the more powerful politicians of Gary, benefiting off the situation of poverty within the city. It is said that within six months of this man’s crusade, most of the low-lifes of the city began to praise this Josiah as a sort of messiah. People that have nothing search for something to live their life for, and all of a sudden, Josiah has a legion. He has a horde to do his bidding. They began to attack in groups, damaging efforts beneficial to aristocratic pockets, yet they also took their toll on the city as well. Many of the citizens, what was left of the middle class and down, began to recognize that Gary was too far down the path to be saved. They decided to join Josiah’s cause and riots almost spread through out. Then, another mysterious figure entered and altered the equation. The Mutineer was a character for theatrics, as well, and had the appearance to accompany. He wore a mask, with a red star, and was ridiculously big. He became close to Josiah, and turned his mob in to a movement. Together, the two created a religious and political faction that swelled amongst the streets. You see, Mutineer convinced Josiah that a change could only be brought through combined efforts. When everyone would work together, they couldn’t be stopped. People became organized, informed, tolerant… brainwashed. In an almost cult-like faction, the fusion of beliefs created by Josiah and the Mutineer had corrupted almost every household in the area, and it wasn’t before long until the city’s own structure began to change as well. It didn’t matter who was in office, it was a figure-head’s position. Those that lived in the city, they knew who was in control. It isn’t hard to survive in this city, though, as threats on the street have all but squandered. It was obvious that in order to keep your head in such an unorthodox environment, all one had to do is follow the rules. Eddy Estrada learned this rather quickly. But when establishing a new business, it is often hard to stay under the radar of those in power. Eddy didn’t quite have a choice but to play the cards he was given, however. Once an employee of the now un-operational Squared Circle wrestling promotion, Eddy was on tour with the fed when they took an unexpected turn for the worse when the owner passed away. Deciding to plan a final show in place of an upcoming planned tour-date in a future city, the owner’s wife made arrangements for the Squared Circle to perform in Gary. That show never took place, and Eddy Estrada has since waited for it to happen. Somewhat stranded in the mid-west, Eddy has since severed contact with all former friends and ties to the old promotion, and has decided to create work for himself in the form of opening a wrestling school. Can we guess where the Illustrious Face-Eater comes in to all of this?
