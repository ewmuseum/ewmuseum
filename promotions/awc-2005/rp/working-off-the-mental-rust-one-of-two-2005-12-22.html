Working off the mental rust... One of Two

22 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Previously: At The Christmas Party... He'd always been entirely professional. Well, aside from four years before when he relinquished a title reign for the sake of 'love' with a woman who, at that time, would have been barely old enough to be graduated. But that was beside the point because, four years later, Darcy Crisis was in the lounge of the Amsterdam hotel that AWC was calling home for their Christmas party, chatting up a few one his new coworkers. Always the professional, he was laughing along with one of Paddy O'Shea's drunken jokes when an inebriated little raincloud entered the room with a drink in hand, santa hat binding wild red hair and an outfit that included jeans slung low enough to fit plenty of holiday cheer. At first, Darcy hadn't even noticed her, but O'Shea had a bit of a wandering eye after a few pints and it was hard to deny the presence of a lady among a party of musclebound men. Always the absolute opposite of professional, Aimz was weaving her tiny form in amongst the shoulders of men she'd soon topple in a wrestling ring. She looked the part of one of Santa's elves in a crowd so large, but ignored the fact with only one plan in mind - to find out whether or not a crew member had lied to get a reaction from her when he'd said that Darcy Crisis had the nerve to show up in Amsterdam that evening. When she spotted him, he was standing across from PCW alumni and trying to pretend he hadn't noticed her entrance. ''Typical,'' she grumbled, trying to shake off a slight haze in her mismatched eyes as she set them on her target. One too many eyes were on her when she snatched a portion of Darcy's sleeve, yanking him away from his conversation. Paddy knew when not to approach a woman, and that had been one of those times - he ambled off into the sea of people to find someone who DIDN'T have a spiteful redhead attached to their arm for conversation. Darcy, on the other hand, wasn't quite as lucky and didn't have the timing of the Irishman. Amy tugged his shirt and ordered him to follow her, to which he protested but couldn't do too much about without causing a scene that really wasn't worth the effort. So, he tailed her to a subdued corner of the lounge, and that's when she started the earfull that was lined up for him... ''What the hell do you think you're doing here?!'' she snarled with a sardonic tone, ''This is for people who've actually... y'know, done something here.'' Darcy smirked, ''Well, I couldn't pass up a chance to get to know my coworkers. And I couldn't help but notice that nobody's... 'y'know', done any talking to you.'' The sarcastic tone wasn't about to work on Amy. ''That's because I don't need friends here, I've done a fine job taking care of myself.'' The little redhead finished her drink and glared at him with all the ire she could muster, but it didn't stop him from guiding her to an actual table where they could speak without all eyes being casted their way. '' ...Whereas you've just been a pain in my ass,'' she finished. Realizing an argument with an inebriated Amy Campbell would lead him nowhere, Darcy motioned to the bartender to bring over a couple of shotglasses and some Jose Cuervo. Logic wasn't one of Amy's strong points, especially when she was as sloshed as she was now. Darcy's only hope was to catch up in the alcohol department and hope for the best. He was less than shocked to hear such a self-serving comment from Aimz, ''…a pain in your ass?'' ''Yes! Exactly! You've been a PAIN in my ASS.'' The bartender arrived with the Cuervo and a can of Coke, and not a moment too soon. Amy eyed one of the shotglasses as the bartender left. ''Come on, Amy…if you're going to chew my ass out, at least give me the chance to catch up.'' Wrestling was not the only field in which Darcy displayed professionalism, as he was about to display. In a matter of seconds, he had both shots poured and consumed and had moved on to the Coke. He knew Amy wouldn't give him much of a breather, and he was right. ''Darcy, why here? Why now?'' Damn, Darcy thought to himself. She was cutting right to the chase, which would make it difficult to stall for enough time for the tequila to kick in. He attempted to stall for time. ''Come on, Amy!'' he chuckled, ''It's Christmas in Europe! An authentic Dutch bar! Do we really have to talk about work?'' It would be difficult to argue that Amy Campbell ever exhibited much patience, but what little she had was clearly gone. She threw her Santa hat at him, glaring. ''Now you're REALLY being a pain in the ass. Why catch up when all you're aiming to do is get a leg up by fighting me? You came, now fight and run off. That's all you've ever done... fight a little while, then duck and cover with your tail between your legs. Hopefully this time you'll be down for more than four years.'' Darcy poured himself another shot. He couldn't help but toss away the concept of small talk. ''Well?'' ''I believe you have your history a little mixed up. I recall fighting, I recall leaving...but I don't recall any running.'' ''All you've done is replace a word that starts with R with one that starts in an L. You left, you ran... either way, it took you four years to come back - and why absolutely baffles me. I know it's not just 'one more match', even though you were always proud to a fault, but I've got a feeling that it's a lot more complicated than just running back to get laid or put another loss on your spotty record. You're trying to accomplish something.'' ''Hey, news flash Amy: I didn't run, I left. And there is a difference...I left for YOU. But that's not the point. You're damn right I'm trying to accomplish something, but I didn't come back for you. I'm back to face you.'' With his half-heared explaination, Amy cringed. This was drawing too much attention - everyone in the room could sense the tension, even if it was in the corner. Amy's eyes rolled, ''Here's where you tell me what's so important about facing me, and then we're gonna look like we've made nice and go back upstairs if you wanna talk about exactly why you came back. Sitting here gritting our teeth isn't going to accomplish anything, and I'd rather have people think I took you to my room to play than have them suffer through hearing your bullshit excuses.'' Unlike Amy, Darcy was well-known among his circles for having a plethora of patience. However, even his was now starting to wear thin. It was difficult to remember a conversation with Amy that hadn't ended with him wanting to smash something in the immediate area. But this was too important to let his anger get the best of him. He clenched his fists for a moment and released his frustration with a deep exhale. ''Fine, Amy. We'll do it your way.'' ''Alright, then. Follow me and try to look happy about it.'' The party was hardly entertaining anyway... Bedroom Shenanigans Amy's head had started to spin about halfway to the elevator, and it only got worse inside. Her peers were looking at her, seemingly dragging Darcy Crisis back to her room in a drunken haze, as if it wasn't any surprise at all. That reaction sickened her, but that couldn't be the fault of anyone but her... or, maybe stereotyping was still that strong. Either way, her dizziness grew with every step toward her hotel room's door, until she was finally through it and angrily releasing the guiding grip she'd had on Darcy's wrist. She had to pause and drive shut her eyes for a moment, regaining some sense of balance and control over the alcohol that was chasing conciousness scared through her veins. Four drug-filled years raced behind her eyes as she casted them toward Crisis, who was living up to his name by way of the the inner-turmoil his return sparked within the tiny redhead. He seemed content to settle himself down into one of the chairs next to the switched off television, while Amy took a route she was too buzzed to realize the vulnerability in. She laid down at the foot of the bed, pressing fingers to her temple in an attempt to silence the raging headache that crept up her spine as she thought of what exactly to say to the 'old friend' in front of her. ''Spill it,'' was all that came out in the end. All that thought and five straight minutes of walking through sobriety for what, 'spill it'? Two words? She was getting convinced that the clever edge she'd once possessed was failing. No less, she stared expectantly at Darcy. The moment had run through his mind for years...but it was not quite playing out the way he imagined. Darcy often daydreamed of confronting Amy in a wrestling ring, triumphantly marching to the ring to the tune of his old theme song. He imagined spilling his guts in front of thousands of completely shocked, screaming fans, on the edge of their seats wondering what the former World Champ had in store for them. Instead, he found himself in a hotel room in front of no fans, his only audience being Amy Campbell and her close personal friend, Amy's Drunken Stupor. Somehow this was pretty close to the opposite of what he had in mind. ''Amy...'' Darcy said softly. ''If you want to talk, I'll talk. But please don't throw up on me.'' Amy grimaced, standing and weaving her way to sit in a chair she placed directly across from him. She cockily raised her feet up and rested them, crossed on Darcy's knee with a smirk. It was as if she needed to prove she was still sober enough to act the superior and still, it wasn't at all what he'd had in mind. ''I'll be fine within a few minutes, you know me. Now, talk.'' ''I'm not sure what you want to hear out of me. I told you I came back to face you, one last time. One more match. It's really only a question of when.'' ''Yeah, why,'' she scoffed, ''I want to know why the look in your eyes tells me you're full of shit, and there's a whole lot that I don't know.'' ''Amy, I've been called a lot of things by a lot of people. Some of them I'm proud to hear, and some are less than flattering. But one thing nobody calls me is a liar,'' Darcy responded, with the last remark meant to be more than a little snappy. ''And you really don't give yourself enough credit. Give it more than a few seconds thought, and you'll know why I'm here as well as I do.'' Amy simply stared at him, her eyes transfixed in a stand-offish glare. She was not about to play his guessing games. ''Don't you remember a damn thing about where you were four years ago?'' Darcy asked. The redhead eased back into the chair with a look resembling blind confidence. She wriggled out of her coat and tossed it to the floor, letting the warmth of the room sink into arms that her tanktop bared. She had a simple statement: ''Yeah, I know I was sleeping with a self-absorbed jackass. That's about it.'' 'Alright, Amy...' Darcy said, his frustration no longer well hidden. 'If you don't like me, fine. If you never liked me, that's fine too. But I came here to give you the professional credit you deserve. You MADE me as good as I was. And yet you've forgotten all that I've done for you.' Amy's face instantly screwed up with rage. How dare he claim to have done anything for her? Even if it was true, she couldn't recognize it... he'd know she was weak and utterly defenseless around him. Then again, the violent front she put up was probably as transparent as it was energetic. She charged out of the chair and quickly realized that standing up would give her no height advantage over Crisis, so she placed a hand on each arm of his seat and bent forward until he could smell the odd marrige of dried blood, alcohol and mint on her breath. Darcy chuckled and raised a finger to her forehead, ''You know what this does, Amy. Headbutt me right in the nose if you want, but we both know that won't get the words and memories floating around in there to go away.'' A version from years before urged her to collapse onto his chest and take a nap, but anger begged her to throw the first punch. She did neither, and acted on an entirely different, much stronger instinct to give into the little dance and spill her guts. ''You made me a much better person than I'd been before that,'' Amy finally relented, to a reaction near shocked, ''You took the money I'd earned and the stupid pose that went with it and you made it mean nothing. You humbled me, you taught me and I'd wager to say you loved me.'' 'Loved'. Regardless of who it was said to or how it was put forth, that was a word nobody would hear in Amy Campbell's casual vocabulary. Darcy suddenly calmed at the admissions, but the glint in Amy's ice blue eye contrased the softer expression in its mismatched brown counterpart - that indicated to him that she was only just getting started. And that indication was truthful, ''You trained with me, slept by me and had my back for a good while. You were the positive boost that my life needed to put it on course. It was so right for me...'' With the remenissions almost complete, Amy's expression suddenly soured when she finished her sentence, ''...And then you bailed. You left me an absolute mess. How the fuck was I supposed to deal with all of that? I was barely nineteen! I was a kid. You came in, gave me everything and snatched it away for a reason I STILL haven't figured out, and I've spent the last four years reliving and rebuilding all of it. But I can't, because every god damned thing you gave to teach me how to be the person I truly was got tainted and raped the moment you walked out. You made me a great person, and then you did everything you could to put me on my ass, embittered as I could possibly be. Plainly put, Markson,'' she growled his real surname with contempt and clutched onto the sides of the chair, anger growing by the second to a point that nearly had Darcy alarmed, ''You built me, you broke me and then you kicked the parts under the sofa.'' ''Oh,'' continued Amy, barely holding back tears she didn't dare to shed in front of him, ''And then you came back, four years later, with the nerve to claim that you were going to give me 'professional credit' by trying to wrestle me. I SERIOUSLY don't think that I'm the one who doesn't remember all that you did 'for' me. I think you're convinced this will prove - to you, not me - that you're an okay human being, as opposed the fucking coward I've remember for the last half decade.'' With that, she sniffled and tried maintaining the anger in her eyes, sitting back in her own chair and crossing her legs with that same expectant look. Her eyes never left his, though Darcy's were wandering about in an attempt to regain his own composure after what had essentially felt like a verbal ass-raping. Darcy exhaled deeply. She had him there. As quickly as Darcy had entered her life, he had vanished with remarkably similar speed. There really was no trying to explain what happened. He knew she'd never understand…nobody really would. There was only one way out of this: Operation Deflect the Question. Darcy looked intently into Amy's eyes, which were filled with the same passion as they'd had so many years before, and hoped that the forthcoming words would be enough to defuse this situation. ''In a way…'' he began, carefully choosing the words. Darcy knew that as much as Amy was trying her best not to release years of pent-up rage, an inadvertent poor choice of vocabulary could set off the ticking time-bomb. 'In a way…I suppose I did love you. There were a lot of emotions flying around at the time, and I'd be lying to you if I tried to tell you that wasn't one of them…' Darcy paused, sensing that Amy's glare had now relented just a bit. Deciding it was safe to continue, he did. ''I know you probably don't think very much of me as a person. That's why I brought up what I'd done for you, so you'd at least be civil and hear me out. Don't you remember, Amy? You were engaged to that piece of shit Eddie Osbourne.'' Osbourne. Eddie 'Impact' Osbourne. Darcy had absolutely no idea where the former owner of Pier Six Wrestling had disappeared to. He suspected Amy had even less of an idea. But if it weren't for him, Darcy Markson and Amy Campbell would not be here today, having this conversation. ''Every day that I saw you, I knew you were miserable. You tried hard to hide it, and you had a lot of people fooled…myself included, when I first joined up. That's why our little 'romance' happened in the first place. You needed to get away from Eddie Osbourne. You needed to get away from Pier Six. And I don't claim to understand why, but you couldn't do it without me.'' Amy's fists were clenched. No time for a pause. ''Like it or not Amy, you owe me for that. You know it. If I hadn't taken you in and watched over you, protected you from Eddie and his cronies, you would still be there. He would have had you. You'd be MARRIED to that slimeball, if only because you were afraid to stand up to him. You never would have moved on, your star never would have risen as high as it has. You never would have made it to PTC, which you're so damn proud of that you've nearly tried to kill Andy M. over it. And nobody would have ever heard of Amy 'Aimz' Campbell…'' Darcy then stared even harder at the redhead in front of him. He could almost see the scared teenager in front of him on the night they planned their simultaneous exit from the company…the owner's estranged fiancée and his World Champion. ''Like it or not, I saved you from becoming Mrs. Osbourne. I did you quite possibly the biggest favor anybody has ever done for anyone. And I'm here today not to prove anything about my character to you…I'm here because I need that favor repaid.'' ''I brought myself up out of the mess you 'saved' me from, and then the mess you left me IN. I've been married now, that ended after a year. I've been 'Mrs' so and so, and I've never been afraid to stand up to anyone in my life.'' She was all talk, and Darcy knew it, ''Then why are you so scared to tell me what you really think?'' ''I think you're full of shit, that's what I think. I think you want to use the celebrity I earned myself. You're eeking in because you probably flipped open a magazine or went on the internet and noticed 'Oh, damn... Amy's a big name now.'. I've been in this playing field where I'm never big enough for world titles, no matter how many people I've beaten. I've overcomed politics with the talent that came built in with me. You did nothing but throw my life into a pit, so why should I let you take advantage of my name just because you think you're going to launch some spectacular comeback when you lose?'' Amy slumped in the chair, but looked him dead in the eye, ''You came back and restirred this pot. You think it's a favor to fight me, 'one last time', whatever. You used my name to get looked at by the scouts here, that's the ONLY reason you're even able to get within a fucking mile of me. Don't talk to me about favors, I've done you enough by rising up and being who I am, and that was WITHOUT any help from your sorry, washed-up ass. How's the back, by the way? Still gonna crumble if some ringrat squeezes too hard?'' Things were definitely going south. Darcy no longer cared whether or not Amy was going to get pissed off…she already clearly was. It was time to abandon pleasantries in favor of a screeching acceleration to the point. Darcy stood up, hoping against but definitely expecting this to end with a crash into the figurative brick wall. ''You know what Amy? Fuck you. If you want to be a hateful, mean-spirited bitch, that's your prerogative. But I don't have to sit here and take it. Maybe one day when you're willing not to take vicious pot-shots at me just so you can feel better about being dead-fucking wrong, I'll actually be able to explain how the 'mess' I put you in was actually the best thing to ever happen to you. But let's pretend, just for one tiny little moment, that none of that ever happened. Darcy Markson and Amy Campbell aside, it's time to talk about Darcy Crisis and Aimz.'' Darcy moved the hair out of his face as he continued staring at Amy, his focus almost resembling one that he saved for the cameras. It was almost as if he was cutting a promo, right then and there. And in a way, he might as well have been. ''Believe me, I didn't want to walk away from Pier Six. I was the World Goddamn Champion, for chrissakes. But still, I did. I did it for you, and you can't take that away from me. The only reason I was ABLE to walk away, however, was because I was secure in the knowledge that I had beaten everyone there was to beat. I had won every belt they had to offer, and I'd held them more times than anybody else and for longer than anybody else. And that was enough for me. I know I told you years ago about how much wrestling meant to me, and it was true. But I knew it wouldn't last forever. I was ready to walk away…until I realized that I hadn't beaten everybody. Hell, I hadn't even beaten the best Pier Six had to offer. I hadn't beaten YOU.'' ''Say what you will about me, Amy. Believe me, I have just as much to say about you. But there's no denying that upon our exist from PSW, you and I were the best. Nobody in that promotion could touch us. Do you remember the night I became the PSW World Champion?'' Independently but simultaneously, the two thought back to that fateful night in April of 2001. WrestleCade, it was called. The World Championship had been declared vacant, and was up for grabs in a company-wide, 3-tier Triple Cage match. The title was suspended over 50 feet in the air, on top of the third cage. ''Everybody had a chance to make it to the top. Anybody could have. But only two people did…Aimz and Darcy Crisis. I swear to God I thought the roof was coming apart at the seams, they were making so much noise. For the thirty seconds that it was, it was epic. We defined each other in that moment. But it was the only time we squared off in a wrestling ring…that is, if you can call being on top of mesh towering 50 feet over a wrestling ring as such. And it's been eating at me, Amy…it's been eating at me that Aimz vs. Darcy Crisis, one on one, never happened. So there you have it…there's the real truth. This isn't about money, fame, or even glory…it's to give my career the proper ending it deserves. This isn't the beginning of some big comeback for Darcy Crisis, four years past his prime. I just have to know if I can beat the best Pier Six Wrestling had to offer. And deep down inside, even if you'll never admit it, you'll always regret it if you never find out if you could beat the best Pier Six had to offer.'' With that, Darcy moved to within no farther than a foot away from Amy. Even with nearly a foot on her in height, he certainly did not feel like a large, powerful man as he gazed down into the eyes of his once, and possibly, future adversary. 'You can name any stipulations you like. Take any advantage you think you need. All I want is Aimz vs. Darcy Crisis. One on one. It can be at Winter Warfare, it can be at Zero to Hero, it can even be in an empty armory, in front of nobody at all. I just want to answer a question that should have been answered years ago.'' ''Bullshit.'' ''What?'' Amy glared, ''I call bullshit. Federations don't sign people who only want one match, they laugh and throw them out on their asses. This isn't any one-off deal... and if it is, you're not getting it.'' She folded her arms, ''I won't fight you.'' It was Darcy's turn to scowl, ''Scared to give someone what they want, or are you afraid you might try too hard to hurt me...?'' ''This isn't going to be a play on what I may or may not feel, Darcy. I'm not going to wrestle you, not any time soon. For one, I've got better things to do than pitter-patter around with another fucking wash-up, and secondly...'' she hesitated a moment, sighing, ''I saw you back then. I know what happened to you, and I know what the doctors were saying. I'm not going to be... gah... I'm not letting you get your back broken because of pride. I already went through that once.'' ''It feels!'' ''Fuck off, okay? I just don't need that kind of blood on my hands,'' the little redhead curled her knees to her chest, ''My career's been trashy enough as is.'' ''So let me get this straight…'' Darcy said, realizing that Amy was arguing herself into a hole. ''The girl I've seen do planchas and land on nothing but concrete…the girl I've seen 450 splash guys through more tables than I care to count…the girl I DISTINCTLY remember creaming me smack in the middle of the forehead with a steel chair on top of a 50 foot cage is afraid to get blood on her hands? Afraid she somehow might breathe funny on a back that has had plenty of time to heal?'' With her reply, Amy was shooting straight from the cuff, and it was far from what she'd planned to say. ''Oh yeah, pardon me for not liking the idea of fucking up someone I love.'' She instantly crumpled into the chair, ''God damnit, that's not what I meant. I mea--fuckin' hell--just... piss off, I'm just drunk or something.'' Then, to distract from the slipped emotion, she had a better idea. At least, it seemed like a better idea. Standing up to Darcy, inches from him, Amy stared him down and opened her arms... but it wasn't for a hug. ''Forget that was said. Now, if you think you're gonna be able to take me in a ring... go ahead and punch me now. Prove you'll actually take the shot.'' ''Any idiot can punch you in the face once you've asked for it...hell, I've had half a mind to do it anyway. What the hell does that prove?'' came Darcy's reply. '''Simple. It proves you wouldn't go easy if I gave you that match. But, if you're not gonna do it... fine. I wouldn't give you the match even if you DID have the balls to hit me right here. You'll get distracted within a few hours, find one of the girls here to mount... maybe start a bar fight. Either way,'' she sighed, ''I've got better things to do than talk to you about a match that's never gonna happen, so leave me be.'' While he had been gazing at the redhead for some time now, Amy could now actually tell that Darcy was seeing red. He reared back, and attempted to unload on Amy's jaw with piston-like speed. Despite her drunkenness, she was able to move her face out of the way with only milliseconds to spare, and locked Darcy's wrist with a vice-like grip on the follow through. ''Pathetic,'' Amy said, with more than a hint of arrogance in her voice. But she was not prepared for what happened next. Darcy quickly kicked Amy's legs out from under her, and before she could react Darcy rolled through, and while she still kept her grip on his wrist Darcy now had her entire arm and head locked between his legs. It was his once world-famous finishing maneuver, the Darcinator. ''Game, set, match.'' Darcy said, matching Amy's arrogance. It wasn't even locked in hard. Amy could freely wriggle around, and actually started to smile and chuckle as she tried breaking free of the awkward grip. As much as she didn't want Darcy to interpret it as playfighting, she absolutely couldn't help herself when it came to the impish smirk she'd made a trademark. Her attempts to bolt her arm in a crotch-shot hadn't worked - apparently Darcy had been hitting the gym for some limbering up, because the lock was not about to break. ''We used to do this way too often...'' That's when Amy caught herself remeniscing, and swiftly stifled her smile, ''Err... c'mon and let go, you can't expect a girl with a tequila buzz on to get out of a lame-ass move she hasn't seen for four years. And you almost tried hurting me there...'' ''Nothing left now but to count the pinfall,'' Darcy said, chuckling. But suddenly, Darcy realized where he was. Just a week ago, he had barely been able to work up the nerve to make the phone call to the head honchos in AWC, just so he could get a chance to talk to Amy Campbell again. And now here he was, on the floor of a hotel room in Amsterdam at an AWC event, lying on top of the very same Amy Campbell. The last time he found himself in this position, his world had been turned upside-down. Instincts of course told him to give in to carnal desire, but he knew better. ''This wasn't what I came here for…'' No longer laughing, Darcy got off of her. Without any words being exchanged, Amy seemed to sense his apprehension, and several seconds of awkward silence passed before anyone spoke. ''It's getting late,'' Darcy finally said. ''And I think we've fooled everybody downstairs long enough.'' That had been too close. He knew it, she knew it... now it was a matter of figuring out why these things kept happening to them. Something had drawn them back together after four years, and Amy didn't buy that it was simply a need for Darcy to feed his crippled ego. The issue would take a good once-over in thought, and maybe a little dip into the past. Hard work, research... Of course, by the time she'd figured out exactly how to figure it out, Darcy was gone with an unusually awkward, sheepish exit. There hadn't been any yelling or name-calling - none at ALL. Odd. With too much tequila in her, Amy's hard work and research would come in the morning - most likely after she'd emptied her stomach of any traces of the M. family's saliva. An early end to the party, no less. ... Wait a second. 'Before Darcy... there was that M. brother. What did I say to h--' Amy's memory suddenly refreshed itself, and not a moment too soon, 'GOD DAMNIT!' ...She had a match with Andy at the pay-per-view... And she decided to leave all of those ridiculously unpleasant thoughts until the morning. With that realization, she was out like a drunken nightlight on top of the bed's covers. Waking up would be cold, lonely and most likely involve a lot of violence - in vomitting, of course.
