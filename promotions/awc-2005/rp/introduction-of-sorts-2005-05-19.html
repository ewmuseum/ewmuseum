Date: 19th of May Prologue – Crimson’s memory. When I was fifteen years old, the 'crazy old lady next door' died. Sadly, not many people seemed to care. However, even at fifteen, I knew that I would forever miss her. Not that I even knew her name-- she was always the 'crazy old lady next door' to me, but everyday on my way home from school she would ask me how my day was. Knowing the consequential response, I usually mumbled fine continuing down the sidewalk as quickly as possible to avoid answering the inevitable. 'Why?' 'I don't know.' I would say. Her response: 'There you have it, another wasted day.' She always seemed to be a pessimistic type, if you were to ask me. Fretting everything that happened to her garden. Once I remember when I was thirteen, I was swimming in the pool hanging out with Jamie Cellars the hottest girl in the ninth grade. She laid bikini clad in the sun on the deck chair while I swam around the pump dreaming of what her tan lines looked like. Jamie was one of those intellectual types. She was in every club and group, she could in high school. Drama club, student government, honor society, chess team, you name it, she was probably in it. Which didn’t give her much time to hang around my pool and increase my pubescent fantasies. Either way she had the nicest hips and sexiest pelvic bones my disposition “inclined” too. You couldn’t really say she was the large chested girl that every guy at Rockefeller High spank too. But she was built and beautiful, but not really the type to not know why exactly I asked her to come swim with me every day she didn’t have some rehearsal or meeting to go to after school. “You know, I imagine that you’d like it better over that at the intake rather than the water pump. It might feel a little better.” She said. “What are you talking about.” I replied knowing damn well that she had caught on to my sensation. “So what do you do when, I’m not here, skinny dip and add protein to the chlorine.” “What’s it to you?” I said, trying to be a suave and debonair, well or the equivalent to that four a thirteen year old. “I don’t know, just wondering. I’ve always wanted to go skinny dipping.” Just when I was starting to think, “Holy shit, this is my chance.” The crazy lady next door ruins the entire mood. She had come out into her garden and seen that her tomatoes had all died. She started sobbing and wailing. “This is just great, what next?” “Is that your neighbor?” Jamie said. “Yeah, she’s crazy, just forget her. So what were we talking about?” I said, trying to get back to the real topic on hand at that point, the skinny dipping. “She sounds really upset. Maybe we should do something for her.” She says, exactly what I didn’t want to here. I wanted to be the guy who all the school was talking about, the new extra-curricular activity of the Jamie Sellers. Reluctantly I knew if I stayed there in the pool there was no more talk of skinny-dipping to be happening anytime soon. “Alright.” I said pushing myself out of the pool. I was always pretty much a short kid. Tiny, you might say, but it was in my blood. My dad was one of the first cruiserweight types to get into the wrestling business at a measurable four foot eleven inches. So I had to get a step stool to look over the fence. “Are you alright? Is there anything I can do for you?” I said over the fence, hoping the answer was no. “Yeah you can stop doing those damn cannon balls and getting chlorine all over my vegetables. You are ruining them. Look at these tomatoes not a single one good enough to eat. What am I supposed to do about my pizza I was cooking?” She said in a half cry, half scream voice topped with a little bit of that lecture tone my mom always used when she upset with me over breaking something wrestling with my sister or found my porn stash. “You could always get some tomatoes from the Supermarket.” I said like any smart-ass teenager with an attitude and a little hottie to show off to. “Crimson!” Jamie said under her breath elbowing me in the gut. “I mean… We could walk down to the supermarket and get you some.” If I were going to be sweeter than usual, atleast I was going to get some honey time in. Yet of course the crazy old bat wanted to know who we was, and if she was my girlfriend. The old bitch embarrassed the hell out of me. I guess that’s what I deserved for ruining her precious tomatoes. I was so pissed on the whole way to the store I stopped being so into Jamie. When we got back and she invited us in for that pizza I was a little skeptical. Of course, Jamie was all for it and volunteered are lives to a crazy woman before I had time to object. However, despite the old ladies negative attitude I tried to be as nice to her as I could and Jamie began to hold my hand under the table as we ate our pizza. Flashback – Jason’s First Days 'This is the west wing, self-admitted patients.' The hall was white, bleach white. The only other color was the skin of people. Even their jackets were a blinding white. It was all overwhelming, truly a boring, lousy, place, not really what you would expect of an insanity ward. 'Their information can be found on the folders in the door.' Betty was a nice woman with a slight whiting to her hair. She knew her time here was almost done and she could retire. Yet, she felt like she owed something to them. She knew that these people still needed care and I appreciated her for that. She had put in over thirty years to these people. I was suppose to replace her once she left. I was a little over a year from getting my degree in psychology. I wanted to help people, but I needed money and this was a way of getting it without my degree. I knew it would be nice to sit in an office, put people on a couch, and charge them up the ass for doing nothing. Yet my wife was five months pregnant and the bills were coming faster than before. I had no objections; I was still helping people. It would take a little more work, but working never killed anyone. I thought I might like it, might even be humorous. I knew a person that worked at a psyche-ward. He always had funny as hell stories about the people inside. 'This is the last chamber. This guy is probably the most sane person here.' 'You mean patient?' I said, looking at her. She said nothing and walked down the hall. I looked in the room. I didn't know. I couldn't, I mean how could I compare my sanity to a guy in a straight jacket? The man appeared normal, not insane like movie psychos. There was something about him, a next-door neighbor feel. Except he sat there and well sat there. If you blinked you would have to find him again. With his white clothing and him sitting, leaning against a wall, you couldn't see him; it was like camouflage. His pink head was all you could see. He was red headed, with what looked like over grown of peach fuzz on his head. Smaller than average weight and height, but a truly normal man I saw. He almost looked out of place in his attire and his blank expression, just staring. I thought he was dead for a minute, but then he shut his eyes and lay down. I turned out his light, picked up his folder and headed to the main office. 'The copy machine is over there. Be sure to turn it off when you are done.' I had already started walking over when I looked back. She had already started talking on the phone again. How did she know? It didn't matter I had to find out about this guy I started reading the minute I got home. It was already ten thirty, but I was engulfed by the words on the paper in front of me. Apparently the guys family and friends died through an illness that was passing through the family. His father must have died tragically, almost for unknown reasons. The file never really explained. His father must not have died for this same reason. The rest of his family had a special case of cancer or something that passed through like the plague. He must have taken care of most of them, yet never gotten sick himself. When they all had passed he gave up. The tenant at his lodging was a little weary the apartment had been abandoned. When he went to check everything out, he discovered him passed out on the floor thinking the illness had killed him also. The hospital discovered him to be a live and well. Yet he wouldn't eat or drink, would not speak or even show awareness. It was late when I finally did get to bed. Well laid in bed. I couldn't sleep. I stayed up all night thinking about this man. A man whose name I didn't even know I felt like a teen girl with a crush excited to see this man the next day. Patient ZOZ1578B not John or Jacob, I would rather have something unpronounceable than ZOZ1578B. But to call this man whom I knew nothing about a number, I couldn't do it. What if this person had accomplished something in his life before he was…? I couldn't even say it let alone even think it. Yet I knew he was. By the time the alarm went off I don't think I had closed my eyes. I did however know that there were ninety-seven ceiling tiles in the bedroom of our apartment. It at least had a design pattern it wasn't plain white. So I wasn't crazy. I was only thinking too, not staring endlessly or anything. I did though not know how long I had been staring or thinking or whatever you want to call it. I was also not mentally present enough to turn off the alarm when it went off in the morning. 'Honey, are you awake. The damn alarm has been going off for an hour.' My wife was not pleased. She did have an appointment today, so I got up and amazingly wasn't very tired. I was a little worn out physically, but mentally I was more awake then I've ever been. I also think this was the first time I was excited for work. Of course my work history isn't that impressive. Being a pizza boy doesn't mean you look forward to smelling like a big human pepperoni. I wanted to figure out this man. If I couldn't do it, then the profession I've chosen was not the right one for me. My whole ride to work I thought about this man, my own life, everything I've learned in my psyche classes. I walked though the door when Betty looked up at me. 'Sleep well last night?' She said. I looked at her. Was she psychic or psycho, I mean, how the hell does she know everything? 'You aren't the only one that tried to figure this guy out.' 'How long have you been doing this?' 'That doesn't matter. You're taking West Wing observation today.' Thinking I couldn't get any answers out of her I went ahead and went to work. You wouldn't believe how boring staring at TVs all day could be. West Wing must be the easiest. I had one TV with a guy who constantly talked to himself. Another with an infatuation with his penis who went around humping the rubber walls until he got tired enough to fall asleep. A real loon with an obsession with interpretive dance although I don't know what he was interpreting. And a manic-depressive who I just had to make sure didn't attempt to stop breathing. Then there was ZOZ1578B. Once I got bored of interpretive dance and wall masturbation I started searching the Internet for any cases similar to ZOZ1578B, and I found out a little more about his condition. Present Day (Crimson) – Clearer minds. I am wandering down the path. I am somewhat absent-minded. Hands in my pockets, it’s the first time I begin to wonder whether returning to the ring has been good for me. I know the path well; it may very well be that the path only exists because my own feet had beaten the earth. The lack of growing grasses a testament of my journey. A journey I often make-- to clear my thoughts, relax, and allow the world to swallow me rather than me swallow the world. The world is like a pill to some. Acting as a relaxer, a depressant, a stimulant, whatever it needs to be, and sometimes everything thing it shouldn’t. The path widens and forks. A fork in the road normally symbolizes a choice. Today, it seems I don’t even look up. Staring at the ground I ventures left, towards the creek. Water alone is an element not only of the world, but also of the body. Almost two-thirds of the body is made of water. It is also known that two-thirds of the world is made up of this same element. Many believe this not to be a coincidence. The rain, the rolling water, the rush of a waterfall – all of these things can be considered a relaxing sound, an atmosphere of reflection. There is a park bench sitting at the edge of the creek. The water is a stones throw away. Mud tracks the ground, the bench itself, however, is impeccably clean. It is green, a great contrast to the brown ground below. There is a small impression in the green; the ware of paint shows a common seat. This is where I sit as I have down often times before. I take in a large breath and sigh – Sometimes reflection is one of the hardest things to see. I pick up a rock and skip it up creek. The rock is both a religious and national symbol. Peter the disciple was called the rock. Jesus of Nazareth tells Peter that he is the rock for which his church will be built. A church from within that rock blooms a flower that most of the world smells. A foundation of solidity is embodied by representation of the rock. The rock is also a symbol of Ireland. The Rock of Cashel was the palace of the King of Munster from the fifth century. It was handed over to the church in 1101 and became a religious center until it was taken over by Oliver Cromwell's army in 1647. The buildings are perched on a limestone hill within a short walking distance from the center of Cashel. They site includes a twelfth century round tower, a high cross, a thirteenth century Gothic cathedral, a Romanesque Chapel, and a fifteenth century castle. The rock is an illustration of great transformation a show of the ability of adaptability. I begin to speak to myself, knowing no one is within earshot, but only to think things over to myself. “I did not know what the meaning of those words were, for they had been spoken in Italian or German, but they sounded like the softest song from any fairy in the National Opera House. Atleast that is what it seems. I did not know what she meant by facing my past, looking it into the eyes. To me the past is not a human, more of a spirit whom you constantly must look behind your shoulder and see if it about to stab you in the back. To her it seems the past was yourself, a former you without the knowledge you have now. ‘What you have learned gives you the upper hand against the past. The past can not out smart you, you have tools it could only wish to gain.’ she said. My return was unheralded and ignored. I was not yet ready mentally and physically. I did all right; I was not completely embarrassed. Granted, losses are part of the game. It isn’t he loss that bothers me; it’s that the minute I was in the ring, I forgot the rest. It wasn’t what I expected. I expected to be shocked, taken back, almost mesmerized by the entrance between the ropes. There was a moment, an interlude before the fight. The second my opponent was in the ring, instinct took over. I did well considering my rust, not awful. Yet I have failed to have the great revolution I expected. I thought I hated the fighting, I hated the ring. It appears that I have been wrong. Something I have learned that my past does not know. I am happy with the experience. If I expect to do this for the rest of my life, I would have rather gone with out loss, but I will be better off from now on. My next match is with a woman, something I believe I will find – to say the least – interesting. I have not been in the situation to get sweaty and winded with a woman in many years. I don’t know exactly the rhythm of a woman anymore. I am not with inhibition I am not coerced not to fight her. Is it a lack of humanity? I think not. It’s part of the career; it’s part of the territory. Most of your men aren’t little guys who can use the speed and pounce of a tiger. I imagine I’ll easily get over it. I do not know what is ahead, but I fear it. I’m unsure of the surprises the realizations to come. I will walk on. I will push forward. I will.” I pick up another rock, I am going to skip it, but instead I plunk it down and watch it sink to the bottom of the creek. I will not sink; the rock CAN float… Present day (Jessie) -- A New Sun Rises I sleep in later than usual; the last few days have found me feeling more and more tired. A lone beam of sunlight steals through the thick, velvet drapes that hang in front of the window, rainbow hues playing in my face—tempting me to wake up, almost teasing my laziness. With a reluctant yawn, I roll onto my side, anticipating the feel of a husband's strong chest. I have forgotten. My hunger for a husband seems to have increased each day since his current presence outside of the fence. I roll my eyes. Sometimes dreams are stupid. Sometimes they aren’t what you really want. I guess sometimes the mind wants you to figure it out on you own. Perhaps dreams are a subconcious reminder that there is better than your dreams. Today, I don’t believe it. I stretch out my arm imagining a strong body to hold. But, instead of a warm, friendly husband, my hand is met with a cold, impersonal spot of sheet. My eyes open, blinking back tears that formed in the corners rather suddenly. The origin of such an emotional outburst this early in the morning escapes me. My breath caught in my throat, then the reminder hits her. Another trick of the mind – It makes you wonder whether the brain hates you more than the body. “Oh well…” I say aloud. I prop myself up on one elbow to find the book I was reading before I decided to give this whole sleep thing another chance. Luckily for me, this time I prevailed. I lift the book to my chest. Mockingly hugging it, sometimes I like to compete against my evil mind’s torments. It accompanies me to the closet, where I have already prepared for the day my clothes. It also goes with her into the wardrobe, where I chose a light, simple T-shirt and jeans, I guess I don’t really feel the need to impress that pretend husband. The book is sitting on the vanity table while I ponder what to do with my hair. “Oh a ponytail will do, I feel like shit anyhow.” Looks like talking to myself is a major theme this morning. I start to get ready and look at the clock. I seem to forget that I have nothing to prepare for. I get back in bed, fully clothed and begin reading the book again. Pro-Wrestling for Dummies, or atleast that’s what it should be called, opens to the page I was reading last night. This section is on the dangers of wrestling. I flip the page, I know this section all to well, and this morning in my depression, I feel not like reliving it. I begin reading and thinking at the same time. It’s probably not the best thing to do, but I might as well take advantage of a woman’s ability to multi-task. Ever since my sister died I have not been the best person in the world. I have lived of my inheritance and really done nothing with my life. I go and visit a man whom I know will never love me. It seems however that I have a relationship with this man. He may never even be capable of love. I can’t blame him it’s not entirely his fault. Yet I must feel that such a disturbed individual has more depth to him, possibly the depth my sister once saw in his eyes. She often told of him as a child, their times in high school, his sincerity after his fathers fatal accident, his inner torment during his mothers decline… I saw for myself his beauty of heart as my sister fell ill. He would sleep beside her in the hospital holding her hand under the sheets. It took my own hand to remove it from her cold body once she had passed. It brought me some comfort that my sister was embodied by such strength in her greatest time of weakness, in her demise of her own strength. It takes me but a minute to stop reading. I close the book and toss it beside me in the bed. I get up and head for the car. I knew exactly where I was getting ready to go, even though I didn’t want to admit it. If my brain still serves any purpose he’s down at the park bench. Present Day (Jessie) – And the Old Sun Sets… I get out of the car and start the walk from the parking lot down to the creek. Of course I was right. There he is staring down into the water. I put my hand on his shoulder, which is no large task consider I’m a little taller than he is. He jumps and turns around quickly. “Sorry.” I say, not realizing I was going to frighten him. He sighs and kisses my cheek. “I’m fine.” he says. I don’t believe him. Only because I know he’s lying. It’s not like he’s doing it on purpose, he’s a little programmed to overly protect himself. He sees I am unbelieving, so like any guy who doesn’t want to bring up a bad topic he changes the subject. “So did you come to help me with my wrestling or did you wear that to flirt with the janitors.” He says, almost flirtingly himself. Losing myself, I say, “Don’t you wish?” I start wrestling with him, throwing light little punches into his stomach. We both lose are selves and we fall on the beaten dirt path. All of a sudden he stops jerkingly while I am still on top of his back, legs straddled like a horse rider. “I’m sorry.” I say again. Seems like I’m zero for two on the physical contact limits today. “No, it’s just. I started thinking you were Jamie. I guess we shouldn’t have.” He says, like a violin playing the saddest lament written by deaf ear. I put on a little smile of awkward predilection. I must have frozen in that position, because the next thing I know I am staring at the creek myself and Crimson is behind my back. I turn around and catch up. “Did you watch my match?” he says. “I did.” I say somewhat shyly. “And…” “Well you looked kind of cute in that little thong thing…” I am glad we can still joke around each other like we did when he brought my sister home from prom, just like bad things never happened. It sometimes came to bite us both in the ass, but it was a learning experience. “We put socks down there for the TV add ten pounds. I had to use two.” He says jokingly. “Really? That’s quite shocking. I mean I understand those other guys can say the steroids shrunk it down there, but you definitely aren’t hunky enough to claim that one.” “I have to get to group sessions, I’ll talk to you later.” He says suddenly changing the mood.
