A New Beginning #3: 'Could Well Be In'

1 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1st August 2005 From The Lavelle Chronicles, ch. 9 Harbrook Open Day 25th June 11:27 “Lois, I think the administration office is over here,” Rob said. “Yeah, you’re right.” Lois and Rob moved ahead of me. The crowd was becoming just a little too claustrophobic. Too many people crammed into such a small space. “T-Tony?” a man behind me tapped me on the back and spoke “Tony” nervously. As I turned around to greet the man, he was as white as a sheet. I recognised him immediately. “I’m sorry; I didn’t mean to interrupt you. I thought you were somebody else,” said the man, apologetically. “Don’t worry about it. Tim Shipley, right? I’m Pierce Lavelle.” I held out my hand and he shook it. “How do you know who I am?” Tim Shipley asked. “From CORE. I was a big fan of you and your tag-partner,” I stopped for a second. I realised who Tim Shipley thought I was: “Tony Aliso”, but he had died a few months ago. Tim Shipley nodded his head. “Yeah, I’m sorry I interrupted you.” Tim said once more. “Listen, I don’t really mind. Hey, I’m a wrestler too. Oh - but I guess you don‘t want to talk about that.” Tim shook his head again. “No, no, it’s fine. You - er - you go to Harbrook?” Tim said. “Not yet, but I will be.” I looked around to try and see if I could find Lois and Rob. “Okay…er - what about a coffee or something a little later? I - I’d like to ask you about….wrestling” Tim looked at me now. He didn’t seem as nervous as he had done, when he first tapped me on the back. “Sure, that sounds awesome.” I shook Tim Shipley’s hand and gave him my mobile number and heeded to Lois’s calling. 11:56 I don’t even drink coffee. Why oh why does talking to somebody new always have to entail some form of pretence? At least I hadn’t lied or anything, but I didn’t really want to meet up for a coffee, I wanted to meet up for a bottle of water. Never mind, never mind... this was too good to be true. I’d dropped the dream, yet here was fate telling me otherwise. How else would I have met a pro wrestler at an Open Day for my college? Wrestlers didn’t aspire to go to college, it was the other way round. I booted up my laptop nervously, waiting for Internet Explorer to open on the ancient Toshiba. I’d had to return to my room in order to use the phone – I still hadn’t caught up with the world in terms of getting a mobile, despite Jake Scott’s pestering (he owned four) – and thought I may as well check out some details on this Pierce Lavelle before I called him. I’d never even heard of the man, but a quick Google search informed me that Lavelle was a main-eventer in a new promotion called the Atlantic Wrestling Club. Slightly suspicious of how valuable a contact in a small-time fed would be to me, I clicked through to their website. Sixty minutes later, I was still there. This was brilliant! This was just what I wanted! AWC put on shows on both sides of the Atlantic, which would give me the chance to visit my native England, and see some of Europe, which I had never got round to doing. The full transcripts available on the website showed off an exciting and fast-moving weekly show, Fresh!, which had been steadily gaining more and more viewers ever since its 10th May debut. I hadn’t heard of many of the people on the roster, but that was probably a good thing – in Core Wrestling, I’d been swamped in a sea of arrogant ‘big names’. A new fed full of new faces was the ideal scenario. With shaking hands, I finally clicked on to the biography of the man in charge of hiring new talent: Entertainment Manager, David Harber. And I smiled. He was a nice guy. He’d take me on – I was sure of it. But first, I would need to speak to my new best friend, the Zero To Hero runner-up Pierce Lavelle. Taking the receiver in my right hand, I punched in his number. “H-hello? Pierce? It’s Tim Shipley. How about that coffee?” 12:20 “So you want to be in AWC?” Lavelle asked, wide-eyed, as he took a deep swig of his black coffee. “I – I don’t know,” I stammered uncomfortably. I took the smallest possible sip from my heavily-milked cup and forced myself to swallow. “I’d been thinking about – PRIME –” His eyebrows shot up. “PRIME? Wow! That would be...” “Incredible, I know. Taking the literal meaning, unfortunately. Hin See set me straight,” I smiled ruefully. “Ah, that’s a shame,” Lavelle responded, but he seemed more interested in his coffee. “No matter,” I said, trying to pass it off as nothing, but a well-muscled arm was waved at me. “Don’t worry, Tim, I’ll find you a job in AWC.” “You think?” I grinned, a little wider than I had planned. Lavelle nodded furiously. “Of course! You were one of the best in Core. AWC doesn’t have that big a roster and you’d be one of the top talents. In fact, how about we form a duo together?” I gaped at him. One of the top two wrestlers in the Atlantic Wrestling Club was offering to tag up with me on the basis of mistaken identity and a cup of coffee. “Seriously?” “Yeah, why not? The Alliance division is starting up; the first champions will be decided at Solarized.” Lavelle looked at my blank face. “That’s the pay-per-view next week,” he told me. “Ah,” I nodded. I had seen the name after all, on the website. “So – so what are you doing at this – Solarized?” To my surprise, his face fell. “I’m facing Alex Strider for the Transatlantic title...” “Isn’t that a good thing?” I frowned. “Well, yeah, but it’s an Inferno match...” “Wow!” I breathed. “That must be some serious rivalry!” “Well... sort of,” he conceded. “But I think Pearl made the match more to fit in with the event’s whole gimmick of the Sun, and so on.” “Who’s Pearl?” I asked with a frown. “David Harber,” Lavelle smiled, almost apologetically. “It’s his nickname. Pearl Harbor, see?” I laughed. “He sounds like quite a nice guy. He must be, if he’s willing to go with such a cheesy nickname –” “Oh, you’ll like Pearl,” Lavelle nodded. Then he looked at me – with worry in his eyes. “Tim – you were never in an Inferno match, were you?” I shook my head. “I don’t know what to expect,” he muttered. “Strider’s beaten me twice. Both times in contentious circumstances. And now I’ve got to try to win with a circle of fire around the ring –” I didn’t know what to say. Here was a top wrestler, spilling his heart out to a relative stranger who hadn’t wrestled for three months and was still a rookie in the business. However, the one piece of advice that came to me would actually turn out very useful, little was I to know. “Remember that fire isn’t just for the end of the match, though,” I told him, frantically racking my brains for something useful. “That fire is going to be blazing throughout the entire match, and it’s going to be seriously hot in the ring.” Lavelle looked at me, impressed. “You’re right. I’ll need a new strategy. Conserving my energy and hoping I can survive longer than he can in the conditions...” “Yes,” I nodded, though I didn’t really know what I was saying. “Stamina will be very important. The crucial question is, are you fitter than Strider?” He pursed his lips. “It’s difficult to say... I suppose we’re quite even.” “Hmm... well, you’ve got a week, haven’t you? Work on the stamina, and – and you’ll be fine...” Personally, I couldn’t see how he would be. I hadn’t expected the mighty Pierce Lavelle to be just as insecure about an upcoming match as I had been in the past – and this was an Inferno match. Going into one of these things with anything other than 100% confidence was a recipe for disaster. If only there was something I could do to help him, just as he was helping me... Pierce Lavelle handed me a scrap of paper, torn from the menu sitting alongside him. “This is Pearl’s cellphone number. Give him a call. I’ll tell him to expect you.” He stood up, finishing the dregs of his coffee and throwing the polystyrene cup backwards without a glance. It flew straight into the bin. “It was nice meeting you, Tim.” I rushed to my feet, hitting my knee on the table. “It was – great t-to meet you too, Pierce,” I stammered through the pain shooting up my leg. Remembering it was an Open Day, I quickly blurted out, “What did you think of Harbrook?” Lavelle smiled. “I like it, I really do. I may well be here, come October. Then we can be friends at college as well as duo partners!” Surely this was too good to be true. “Yeah,” I said gratefully, but sounding a little gormless in the process. It was time to end the conversation before I embarrassed myself further. “See you – see you at Solarized!” I grinned, fully aware that I was pushing my luck. Lavelle’s eyes twinkled. “Let’s hope so.” He raised a hand in parting, a gesture which I mimicked, and then I was left alone at a table covered in very white coffee. I hadn’t even noticed that the cup had been knocked over when my knee had jolted against the table. A wicked grin masked by my sigh, I grabbed a few paper napkins and started to mop up the spillage.
