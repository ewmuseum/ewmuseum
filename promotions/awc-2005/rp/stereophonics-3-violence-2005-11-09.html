Stereophonics #3: 'Violence'

9 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9th November 2005 No FREAKIN’ way, you tell me. I'm having you on. It’s a joke. A funny one. Ha, ha, ha! This guy’s a real joker! Ha, ha! I'm a natural, you tell me. I should be on stage, you tell me. That one’s so off-the-wall... so oddball... ha, ha, ha! But it’s no joke. Tim Shipley? Virginity? Lost? Yeah, RIGHT! Like those words even appear in the same dictionary! But it happened, and that was how it happened... and there I was. THE KING. Nothing and no one could touch me. 7th October 2005 20:56 We pushed our way into the throng, Tatyana leading the way, parting the crowd with ease despite her slender frame. It was like there was some force field around her... something she had would just make people move aside without even realising it. The way you instinctively move aside if Christina Aguilera needs to get past. And unless you’re gawping like an idiot, she is going to get past regardless of your wishes. The aura of celebrity brings falsely implied authority. We were just in time. The support bands – whoever they had been – seemed to have gone down well; the crowd was loud, happy, and ready for some serious Stereophonics. And when the lights dimmed and the strobes came on and a guitar was violently slashed... Valentine’s exploded. I self-consciously joined in the cheering, enjoying the fact that I couldn’t hear my own voice against the backdrop of thousands of others. I turned to Tatyana, and she was looking at me; a pleasant surprise. I wanted to look away – it’s just what you do when you look at someone and they look at you too – but then I realised I didn’t need to. This girl had pledged everything to me. This girl had willingly taken me inside her, the greatest degree of intimacy; had given me the best experience of my life. This girl was happy for me to look at her, and so look at her I did, and a warm feeling came rushing up into my chest. But there was no time for that. Here they came. Kelly Jones. Richard Jones. Javier Weyler. “Yeah!” I tentatively yelled, furiously clapping my hands above my head like everyone else around me. I did not have any clue how to handle myself – bad choice of words – at this sort of event. Perhaps just standing still would be easier... but then what would Tatyana think? Never mind. Listen. Because “Brother” has started up. And the chorus began, and the crowd rose as one, and like a wave moved through the air, jumping, screaming, singing, yelling, and I was there, reckless, on top of the world, jumping, screaming, singing, yelling, and I was Tim Shipley, but I, for once in my miserable little life, didn’t care. You walk right up to me Abuse me on the street Like a cannonball killing me You got it wrong brother, can’t you see? Can’t you see? The song died back down to its starchy bassline and I turned to Tatyana, exhilarated. She had a smile on her face – a weak, fixed smile – a glistening of sweat on her forehead, her hair ruffled and messy and her cheeks red. Her eyes locked with mine and instinctively I leant down and met her with a kiss. A big, wet, sloppy kiss; duelling tongues, saliva compounds. I was enjoying this more every time it happened. Until – Wwwwwwwwwhat? That was the reaction on repeat in my brain as I span away from Tatyana, a hard shove having caused me to lose my balance. Don’t fall, don’t fall, I told myself desperately, worried sick about being crushed beneath the crowd, whose boisterous actions looked a whole lot more violent from below. I fell. Knowing the next coming of the chorus would bring trampling, I fought desperately to right myself, grabbing the jeans of the man next to me – not a wise decision; with a scandalised expression on his face he pushed his way between the elbows of the two in front of him, moving away from the pervert on the floor. Eventually I staggered to my knees – only to receive a crushing blow to the sternum, or as they call it colloquially, a fuck-off punt in the ribs. Wwwwwwwwwhat? I hadn’t a clue what was going on. I hadn’t a clue who was going on. As I was dragged to my feet I saw a murky silhouette, but that was it as the extravagant lighting lulled. I was being held upright, totally helpless as I was forced through the crowd, people parting for whoever was restraining me just as they had for Tatyana. And finally we reached the edge of the standing area, and he let me go, and I whirled round, ready for a fist fight, but he got there first. His hand slammed into the bridge of my nose and I was thrown back against a pillar by the almighty force of the blow. What had I done to deserve this? 7th October 2005 21:18 “Tatyana?” Her name was my first word as my eyes opened, and even that was painful. Which?, you ask, Which was painful?, noting my ambiguous sentence, and the answer is both. My eyelids felt like huge boulders; the struggle to force them up was akin to trying to lift a house, but there was something inside me telling me I just had to do it, so I did it, and suddenly things felt a bit better. And speaking her name... that hurt too, in more than one way. My lip was split and I’d coughed up blood. The inside of my mouth tasted like mud (oh, believe me, I’d tasted that on many an occasion) and my tongue was like a huge swollen blancmange. With teeth marks in it. But more than one way, as I said. Because when I opened my eyes, when I spoke Tatyana’s name, it wasn’t Tatyana in front of me. He wasn’t even tatty. It was the doorman. “You ain’t lookin’ too special,” he commented airily. I sniffed and said nothing as my breath rasped in and out of my aching chest. There wasn’t a part of me that didn’t hurt. “Did I – pass out?” I managed, my voice reedy and thin. The bouncer opened his eyes wide, nodding slowly, his incredulity at my inability to make the most basic of inferences lost on me. “That you did, mate. Shame. You’ve missed half the set.” “Oh, shit...” Putting two and one-point-eight (my body wasn’t 100% functional) together, I realised that the crashing sounds clouding my hearing were those of “Deadhead”, one of the less-celebrated tracks from Language Sex Violence Other?. “Just listen to the rest of it from here, alright?” He squatted down, placing a concerned hand on my shoulder. “It’s not just that I wouldn’t go back out there in your state – though I wouldn’t, myself – but that guy who fucked you up big-time, well, he’s still out there somewhere.” It all came racing back to me, just as an axe cleaved my skull in two. “Aaarghh!” I threw a hand to my head, wincing and curling up in pain. The doorman sucked in air through his teeth. “Pain delay. That happens.” “What –” I struggled for words. “Who – who was he?” The bouncer frowned. “Not a clue, mate. Didn’t do nuffing to upset ‘im?” “I’d never seen him!” I protested vehemently, and then a racking cough cut my protest short. He courteously (perhaps...?) waited for me to finish before continuing. “You sure, mate?” He paused and sniffed the air. “You been drinking?” “A – a little bit,” I confessed, as more memories came flooding back, of the bar, and Tatyana, and shots, and Tatyana, and Tatyana again. “Mm... you of age, then?” “Yes,” I replied. Too quickly. He sighed. “Won’t say nuffing, mate, but you don’t wanna get caught drinking underage in Valentine’s. This is not the sorta place you want to get barred from...” “I know, I know,” I wheezed. “I'm sorry...” “The other clubs round ‘ere, they all draw their blacklists from here,” he went on, looking out of the window. That window looked mighty familiar... Then I saw a used condom in the corner, and I knew exactly where I was. Unfortunately, he followed my eye line. “What’s ‘appened ‘ere, then?” was his rhetoric as he raised his voice, stamping over to the corner of the room to peer closely at the contraceptive. She’d brought them, of course (I’d never bought condoms in my life). Had plenty handy in her handbag. Funny, that. “Looks like someone paid this room a visit,” he said, turning to me with a conspiratorial grin. I gave a weak smile but this was forced off my face by the sudden impulsive need to vomit. He saw my face turn white and rushed over, his own face rapidly becoming that colour. “’ere, ‘ere, ‘old it! Not the carpet – bucket –” He scrabbled for one, but it was too late. “Awww...” The bouncer threw his arms up in frustration. “Sorry,” I said feebly, contorting my face into one of apology while trying to shuffle away from the small pool of my own sick. Some was on my chin and my cheek. This wasn’t nice. “There’s an ambulance on its way, by the by,” he told me as he resignedly bent to inspect the mess. “Why?” I blurted out. His hacking laugh told me my question was a stupid one. “You’re off to ‘ospital, you are...” “No, no, I'm fine,” I told him. His eyebrows jumped about a metre. “Really!” I insisted, forcing myself to sit up. “I'm a professional wrestler! I can handle this... what?” He was chuckling. Chuckling rather a lot. “I'm not joking!” I maintained, raising my voice as I stood fully – wobbling. “I'm Tim Shipley, I wrestle in AWC and I'm the Frontier champion!” “Yeah, and I'm John Lennon, mate,” he joked, his upper body shaking as he clasped his arms over his torso, trying to contain his laughter. “Oh, fuck this,” I muttered. I was tired of it. And, knowing it was a stupid decision, I strode to the door, pulled it open, and walked out on him. This was a new low for Tim Shipley. I could hardly walk. My body was battered and bruised. My lip was split and I had ugly welts all over my head. Traces of vomit adorned my face. I had a splitting headache. And I was still hammered. But things were about to go from bad... to worse.
