Purgatory III

7 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/10/05 I pulled open the door slowly, tugging at the steel object to move it clearly open. At times like these it tended to get sticky with the onset of winter. Needs a bit more oil. I’ll have to head to the village for some more of that I reckoned. Regardless, the door began to swing open eventually, my arms aching slightly from the amount of effort involved. As I finally pulled open the door, Dan looked up at me and knew that he was in the right place. He stopped shuffling his feet, (a bit of a trait with O’Grady – guess it came from his wrestling days when he just couldn’t stop moving in the ring; that’s why he was so dangerous) and used one of his hands to rip the goggles off and see me with his own bare eyes. It was O’Grady all right, you could tell from the burning brown eyes. ‘Hello Dan.’ ‘Jack?’ The eyes were searching now for recognition. He knew that it was me but obviously I’d changed since the last time that I had met him. That was well over 3 years ago now and it was certain that I had lost a lot of body weight and muscle mass since then. No question about it. Still, I was still myself. ‘It is Dan. You going to stand out in the cold like a hen in a hot griddle or what?’ A slim smile crept over his lips and he nodded before dancing into the bunker along with me. He looked very cold and I pointed him in the direction of the boiler where he could rekindle the fire. As he did so I went to the stove, almost instinctively to offer him something. Only then did I realise there was none. ‘I would offer you tea Dan but…’ ‘You don’t have any. You know you never did and I liked that about you kid, never trying to pander to other people just so that they can be relaxed. This is your home, they’ve got to deal with it.’ It was true, I never did. It was all inconsequential now though. The past didn’t matter. There were more pressing, burning questions in the present. ‘Why are you here Dan?’ The question took him aback a little, like he’d have expected me to waste time with chitchat about other things. ‘That was…sudden. I thought we could catch up a little first Jack.’ ‘Well Dan as you can tell by my surroundings I’m not going to have much to say. It’s not like I’m going to go on about shooting bears and killing yokels now am I?’ ‘I suppose not…but you better hear me out anyway.’ The tone in his voice had already risen to another level, as it always did with the firebrand. Renowned for a quick temper and vicious retorts, there was little that got his goat more than being directly confronted and not given the time to consider what he was going to say. The blood pumped through him like a drug, taking him to new, more intense levels and made it so easy to predict him. I guess he hadn’t changed much in all this time. ‘Dammit Jack you know me! I’m not here to quarrel with an old pupil and friend. I don’t want to go off on you as if we were back in training. I’m here as a concerned friend and also as…a friend in need.’ This was different. O’Grady had turned round now and was facing me from the boiler, his hands clasped together like he was going to drop to his knees and begin the lamentations of Christ so that he may be saved and absolved. In a moment he looked like a scared child, caught by the priest at school smoking round the back of the gym. The leather belt was certain to follow (as was the custom of his time) and that was no what the boy would want. So now he’d come before the priest and pray to God right there in front of him, as if that would save him from the punishment. He should know that punishments are meant to be served, not run away from. ‘What are you on about Dan?’ ‘It’s the gym, my gym. They’re trying to take it away from me and nobody else can help.’ I laughed, bitterly and in his face, coming forward and towering over him. For all the firebrand’s brash bravado, he was still less than six feet tall. It was his guts and gumption that got him so far in the business, not physique and power. ‘And what do you think I can do eh Dan? I’ve been locked away out here for more than two years and it’s all I’ve wanted for the rest of my life. How do you think I can come back just like that just because you say so? Look at me! I’m wasting away from the past, I’m not a big man anymore, and I’m not a tough guy who can sort out your problems. I’m just a man…and a shadow of a man at that.’ I slumped down onto the nearby chair and rested my head in my hands. There was no sense in this madness he was proposing. Did he really think that I would just up and leave when he simply called out of nowhere? In fact, that was worth questioning in itself. ‘And how the hell did you find me?’ ‘It wasn’t hard, just took a while. You know there’s not too many people who’ve emigrated from America with the purpose of living in Siberia. Even fewer that are originally Irish. Just took some decent background checks, that’s all.’ ‘But why?’ He wasn’t getting to the point at all. I needed to know why he was in this trouble, and why he needed me. Why come looking for the fallen? ‘Because you’ve got the power Jack that I never had. You’ve got the ability to control and master people. Able to bend them to your will. Look at you; you’re the perfect example of how I don’t have that at all. Did you ever think that I was in charge when we were training?’ ‘No.’ ‘Exactly, then take it from me, this is about power and control, which I don’t have and nobody else does in the gym. We need you, everybody and only you can do it.’ The hands gripped tighter in front of me, the knuckles turning white he was holding them so tightly. Still I wasn’t sure; still I didn’t know what was going on. I needed to know. ‘Do what?’ ‘Break the bond, break the control that Farrelli has over my gym.’ The name sent a chill through my spine and images flashed before me. My wife being dragged into the hall… The gun, pointed directly at her head…. The sickening noise I heard as I closed my eyes… Now it was my turn to feel the blood boil and bubble up inside me. Embers of long forgotten rage sparked and it was now that they chose to rear their ugly head. Something inside me had forgotten about them, but they hadn’t forgotten about me. They were waiting, waiting for the chance to return and restore some sense of power. ‘What has he done?’ My tone had returned to a more powerful commanding one and O’Grady noticed this. His eyes lit slightly and he pressed on, eager to catch the flames and nurture them before they died out again. ‘Ever since you left, all that’s happened has been Farrelli pressing ever more and more on us. For a while I got away scot-free because I was helping to train his boys but that wasn’t enough. He’d become so big that he didn’t even visit me personally. Instead he sent his goon Antonio. I’ve been dealing with him and he’s taken it upon himself to take advantage of everything that he can get, pocketing cash for himself above what is owed to Farrelli.’ ‘I see…and you want me to do what?’ ‘Come back…and show them what we’re made of.’ ‘But look at me, how do you think I’m going to be imposing like this?’ ‘We have time to get you back in shape. Enough to make you look menacing anyway. Jack, you’ve not lost as much muscle as you’d think.’ ‘So what, what’s in it for me?’ ‘Ah well, I was hoping you’d get to this part.’ He rubbed his hands with glee in an immensely clichéd leprechaun way. ‘I’ll train you again, because I’ve got an offer for a federation…’ My mind wandered and returned to the last time I was involved with any wrestling organisation. Things hadn’t gone so well. ‘I don’t know…’ ‘It’ll give the time to travel around a bit and you’ll have a cushy enough salary. In return for doing this, I’ll not only train you, but you’ll be able to start what you’ve always wanted to do…get back at Farrelli.’ ‘What are you on about? Why would you think I’d want to get back at him?’ O’Grady slammed the table beside me and pushed my shoulders. ‘Oh come on Jack! That’s ridiculous! You know everyone understands why you’re here. You think that Farrelli doesn’t gloat about silencing the Bull of Kildare? You think he doesn’t relish in retelling the tale to every recruit he feels is worthy of knowing such a butchery? The underworld of New York knows the tale as well as a child knows not to play with fire.’ He moved very close to me now and looked straight into my eyes. ‘I can see it right now…a father disgraced at his family’s death.’ That sent me off and I barrelled out of the chair, crunching into him and then following with a left that sent him rolling into the wall. I didn’t even think but head butt him right there, nose connecting on nose and hearing a sickening crack. I didn’t know it was my own nose or his. I didn’t care. He crumpled to the floor and began to chuckle to himself, padding at his nose with a handkerchief that he produced. He looked up at me and smiled, a couple of gaps in his teeth making it all the more comical looking. ‘I knew you still had the fire in you boy! Why don’t you show them it yourself instead of through me?’ And from there all was changed again. Dan convinced me to return to his gym and to return to America. More importantly he got me a job with an independent but confident and capable organisation – AWC. So I was back. But that was only the beginning. There are many more tales left to tell and this was only a footnote in my history. What Farrelli had to contend with now was a possessed demon… …who had been finally released from his prison.
