Only in America

1 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-1-06 “Attention all passengers, please fasten your seatbelts as we prepare for landing. We hope you enjoyed your flight and thank you for choosing Delta Air.” The pilot’s voice over the intercom startled Teresa awake. She had been on a non-stop flight to California from Russia. Dr. Burns insisted she be admitted to a local hospital for observations overnight. He told her it was only a precaution due to the amount of alcohol consumption to her body combined with the physical stress she experienced from her match with Anton Assault. If she would have tried leaving for the states too soon, she would see a week in a California hospital for exhaustion and number of other things Teresa didn’t remember. In fact, the whole night was a blur. Once her eyes focused, she peered out her window seat to see the ocean several yards from the airport. Her eyes instantly narrowed to the bright sun’s rays against her face. She couldn’t wait to take her shoes off and feel the sand between her toes. Just when Teresa stood to grab her luggage from the overhead compartment, something caught her eye. Amongst the normal summer crowd and the anticipating fans waiting for Freshspecial, a group of navy men were playing volleyball with a team of bikini-clad 20 something’s with sun blond hair. Teresa couldn’t help but laugh. Only in America. She couldn’t help but also think of her father. ----------- “But Steven, she’s just a baby!” A young Edna Tomas held her crying baby daughter close. The babe had been crying non stop for an hour. Nothing seemed to quiet her and the arguing between Edna and Steve didn’t make the situation any better. “And you’re her mother Edna! Surely you can do SOMETHING to make her shut up! It’s 2am and I have two hours to get more than just 3 hours of sleep, get ready, and be on the ship.” With a tear stained face, Edna softly shushed her daughter and left their bedroom for the nursery. There, she sat in a rocking chair and began to hum while rocking. A small shadow appeared in the doorway. It was 5 year old Eric Tomas. He rubbed his eyes and stumbled sleepily into the room. “What’s wrong with her Mommy?” Edna smiled to her eldest as he approached and carefully gave the baby his finger for her to grasp. The children held a bond she couldn’t quite understand. Edna believed everything in life had a reason, even life itself and it was a blessing from above that her children shared this bond. Within minutes, the baby drifted to sleep. “Perhaps you should sleep in here tonight, just incase she wakes up.” Eric gave his mother a silent nod. He didn’t like sleeping in a room by himself. It was too scary and figured it was twice as scary for his baby sister to sleep alone as well. As Eric unrolled a sleeping bag across the nursery floor, Edna returned to her and her husband’s bed to find Steven sitting on the edge. His large frame slumped and his hands held both sides of his head. He was mumbling to himself. “No, go away! I can handle this! She’s just a baby, we can leave her here! She’d die..” Steven rocked back and forth and began sobbing silently. Edna couldn’t help but watch helplessly. When he came back from Nam, he wasn’t the same. Private Tomas had experience so much horror and death she simply couldn’t imagine existed. To come home to a changed world didn’t help him emotionally in the least. This wasn’t the first episode and surely wasn’t the last and the fear of the flashbacks worsening haunted her every day. Hesitantly, and yearning to ease her husband mind, the young Edna sat beside her husband and held him to her chest and he continued to sob. Her fingers tenderly stroked his thick blonde hair and she rocked him as she would her own child. “Shhh… It’s over now darling. You’re home with your family. It’ll pass and you’ll be ok. You’re safe now.” When the sobs ceased, Steven sat up straight, grabbed a tissue from the nightstand and wiped his eyes. He looked down to the worry and love in the soft blue eyes of his wife. War had changed everything in his life, except her. No man could ask for a better wife. A deep sigh exits Steven’s chest and he gives her lips a gentle kiss. “I’m sorry Baby, I’m not the man I used to be. I wish things were different” Edna took her husband’s hands and gave them a tight squeeze while she shook her head. “It’s not your fault Steven. I…” Two fingers were placed over her lips. Steven shook his head as he looked at her sternly then stood up. “Not a word. If I had been here instead of playing soldier, none of this would’ve happened. Discussion closed.” ----------- The sand felt perfect between Teresa’s bare feet. Living in Tennessee, the only sand you stepped onto came from either a sandbox or a man-made beach at a resort lake. Real sand came from the ocean and none felt better. “Teresa Tomas?” The voice was unfamiliar. Teresa turned around to see one of the young navy men standing before her. His sun-tanned chest was sleek and bare. A set of dog tags dangled from his neck. The boy’s eyes were dark and what was left of his hair was just as dark. Teresa gave the boy a once over. If only she was ten years younger. “Yes, that’s me. And you are?” The young sailor gave Teresa a boyish grin that would melt the heart of any girl that would fall victim to his man-in-uniform charm. “Steven Price Ma’am and I would be honored if you would sign a picture for me.” Teresa nodded. He, along with the rest of the sailors must’ve come for the show. Steven retrieved a black wallet from his back pocket and pulled out a photo. When he showed it to Teresa, she saw it was an old picture of her. The logo of an old indy fed hung in the background. Teresa stood between a tag team she managed there. It was her first job in the wrestling field. Memories of old caused her to smile. “Wow, you must’ve been a fan for a long time kid. Back then I was just eye-candy with an attitude. I’m impressed.” The young sailor gleamed proudly. “You look much sexier now Teresa. I mean you started working out since then and damn if you can kick some major ass. It would make my day if you would just sign the back of that picture and when I go out to sea in a few days, I’ll have something from home to take with me.” Teresa took a pen the boy also carried in his wallet and signed the photo for him. Something however did strike her attention. “Something from home? Why not a picture of your girlfriend or family?” The boy shook his head sadly, almost reluctant to say much more, but he swallowed hard. Teresa was his idol, surely she’d understand. “I’m gay. No boyfriend either. Family disowned me when I came out.” Teresa handed the boy his picture wishing she hadn’t asked, but he was a fan and she wasn’t going let him go on duty disappointed in her. He was too nice looking to be homosexual she thought to herself. Why he would like a redneck woman so much she couldn’t understand. “I see… Well, Steven, you’re a very handsome young man. I’m surely you’ll find someone’s picture to replace mine in that wallet of yours. Hope you enjoy the show.” “I will Teresa, and thank you for understanding.” The young sailor returned the photo back into his wallet and took off down the beach where the other sailors were now showing their buff to a new group of bikini-clad giggling girls. Teresa shook her head and continued along the shoreline. Only in America.
