Trials and the Consequence - The Manipulator

26 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26/09/06 11th of September - evening time The sun was fading fast over the highway we were taking on the way to Albany, the next stop in Jack’s mind on his quest for freedom. Nothing had passed through his mind as to the suspicious dealings of Father Pearson. Nothing other than it could help him to breaking free and finally receiving his wish. He hadn’t even asked me for why I was doing this to help him. Not even one single question. Such a sad, pathetic child in the eyes of God. As for me, well that’s an entirely different story. I was born Sylvia Patricia Mary Carter, to a simple family in the heart of Albany. Things weren’t complicated when I was younger, you either got to doing something or just got to being a reckless youth. For me doing something seemed the appropriate choice. I pored over books, studying the literature of the past as a way to give me both guidance and purpose for the future. In many ways I let myself becomes characters that I shared some sort of feeling for. It was a device for me to escape from the reality of life in Albany which truly had nothing for me the majority of the time. Mostly, the other kids had chosen to be the reckless youth. This left me with few options for entertainment. So followed my life for the convening ten years or so as I entrenched myself behind the barricade of literature and classics, filling my mind with hopes and dreams of a better, more interesting calling than the one that had been given to me right now. This was my lot in life and I was humbling myself to accept, hoping beyond hope that I would achieve this goal but being downcast the entire time about it. Really, I was just trying to shadow the motions of a great writer, trying to be a troubled, darkened soul that would be inspired by something, something that would lift them out of the doldrums of the humdrum life. Not for me, but I would be inspired. It was a Sunday, the Lord’s Sabbath and I was being dragged towards the church by my parents as usual. The one thing that I had which most definitely shared some of my favourite writers was the apparent lack of spiritual faith. Nothing called to me about religion and most of the time I was just concocting my epic novel that would change the world forever. So really it was pandering to both my family and the Lord in a very disrespectful tone, the kind which you always do when you’re a child. It was then that I encountered the cerebral, devout, inspiring Father Dave Pearson. Before the priests that we would encounter were old, fire and brimstone type characters. They urged repentance with no reasoning behind it other than a fall to hell. It had little effect on me largely because the church had admitted to the fact that purgatory and hell were now non-existent ideals of the faith. It was hard then to listen to someone advocating a non-existent entity. The Father Pearson that walked into my life was far different in appearance than the one who still travels the state of New York. Where a portly, gruff man now stands, then there was a strong, confident and bullish looking youth, full of fire and passion for the Lord our God. He was ready to invoke the fires within each and every one of us, to instil in us a passion for the holy text that is a symbol of our faith. His speech was strong that day and I still remember every word, like I was there now. What stands to me now is still one particular line: ‘In God you are device of the Lord, ready to do his wishes through your talents and abilities. Who are you to deny the world of what you can do if someone feels that you shouldn’t be using such talents?’ It spoke because up until then I had been hounded and admonished for my particular talents of writing. I was able to conjure arguments and, in a manner of speaking, deceive others as to the true feeling laying underneath the piece. But here was a priest, a man of God, a servant of the Lord who was inviting me to use my talents for the betterment of the Lord. How could I deny it? That day I met and talked with Father Pearson and from there we both shared a bond of faith that has defined our relationship to this very day. We both met back and forth whenever we could, Father Pearson encouraging and guiding my own path into journalism, recognising the power with which I could wield for the Lord. We communicated and he cultivated my talent, offering advice and literature I could enjoy and enrich my already deep understanding of the literature of the world. He opened my eyes to many texts such as David Hume, Immanuel Kant and others of their ilk who questioned the authority of the existence of religion. Father Pearson understood that I had had very little faith originally and therefore fed me with challenging topics that I otherwise wouldn’t have explored. It is not to say that Father Pearson employed this side virtually unchallenged. He regularly included his own notes that would bring up valid counters to the arguments presented. It stimulated my brain and slowly but surely he would indoctrinate me. That was one of the interesting things about Father Pearson. He knew that I would understand his techniques so he did not insult me by trying to hide them. He would merely present them as they were and leave it up to me. Eventually I realised that this was a test of faith by the Father and I would have to endure it myself, his testing eyes always watching. So what is it that has brought me to the wrestler Jack Murphy? Well, there is the obvious inclusion of my journalistic abilities and there being used for the wrestling industry. This was through Father Pearson’s design. It is true that I did not achieve many opportunities as a journalist and so I had to take what I was given but Father Pearson reminded me that the Lord has a plan for everything. And he does. So now I’m sitting the car of Jack Murphy, driving to Albany to enact the next part of the Lord’s plan for Jack Murphy. It was set out by Father Pearson and by Dr. Kasidy Drake. As for why, well that will have to wait for another time. *** ‘Sylvia?’ ‘Yes?’ ‘Doesn’t the sun look fantastic setting?’ ‘Well…yes I suppose it does why?’ ‘No reason…I just appreciate and admire beauty.’ ‘Oh. Where has this come from?’ ‘Never mind.’
