Teacher's Proof.

18 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/18/06 Autmann’s Deli had always been a staple of downtown Baltimore since 1971, a part of the famous “corned beef row” district of the city. Business was flowing as usual in this afternoon, with two rather large men occupying a back corner table off to the right. “Who is Shawn Singleton?” he asked. The Hunter swept the crumbs from his sandwich off of the plastic table. Corned beef on white bread, mustard with a pickle on the side. Shawn sat across from him, stirring the ice left in his lemonade cup with his straw, mulling over the options in his mind. The foil to his left still had residue from his kosher hot dog. “What do you mean?” he responded inquisitively. “Who are you?” Hunter asked him. “I’m Shawn Singleton.” “So what? Why should the people who run the AWC care? Why should the fans cheer or boo you? Why should your peers give a damn about you?” “They shouldn’t.” Hunter, in all of his years in this business, hadn’t been ready for that one. A man rarely caught by surprise, a man who prided himself on always being prepared for the unexpected, was not prepared. “Why shouldn’t they?” “Because I don’t care. Like the cliché says, ‘less is more’.” “How so?” “The less you care about what someone else thinks, the more they are forced to care about what you think, about who you are, about what you stand for. They desire to solve the enigma that you become, they wish to know the reason you don’t find a need to appease them.” “So, you subscribe to the adage, addition by subtraction?” Hunter questioned. “Call it what you like. I have nothing to prove to anyone. I don’t need to prove myself to the people in charge of the AWC. All they can do for me is sign the checks and provide me with a forum to execute the art that I have spent years perfecting. I hurt people. That’s what I do, it’s all I want to do, and it’s what I’m best at. I’m a professional. I take my craft personally, and I’ll be damned if I let the perceptions of my so-called employers affect how I do what I do. As far as the fans…” He pauses, pulling the straw from the cup, sucking the last remnants of lemonade from the tip. “Fuck them. I used to feel such bitterness and animosity towards them. I used to detest their presence, the knowledge that for so many weaker men, the fans make or break their careers. That’s too much power for the stupid to have. But now? I don’t care. They don’t factor into my thought process. Whereas before, if they cheered for me, I’d be upset, and if they booed me, I’d be upset. Now, I could care less. I am a manipulator of people, Hunter. You know that, because you are the same way. Everything I do makes sense; no one can ever claim me to be random. Even if you don’t like or agree with what I do, you understand every move I make. To sit there and dislike everything a person does, but be forced to cheer their execution of it and know that the reasons make sense. And you know what? That drives people insane,” Shawn said coolly. “That doesn’t sound like you don’t care. It sounds like you find joy in making them hypocrites.” “I can live with that,” Shawn answered. “So, let me ask you this, Shawn. What’s your plan? You know this AWC deal wasn’t my idea. I’m only in it because you and I are a package deal, and I’ll be damned if I don’t have my stake in your career.” “I am a conqueror, a destroyer, a nomadic destructive force. I took over and destroyed 4WW in seventeen months from the bottom to the top. Worked my way up every run of their ladder and dismantled it. Every title, every challenge was met. I buried two of their top legends, hall of fame performers, literally buried alive beneath two tons of dirt. I declared myself, and rightfully so, the champion of the company. A month later, I made it official, beating the best they had to offer to take what I had earned. And a few months after that, I burned the damn thing to the ground. Sucked it dry of its life blood. But I’m not satisfied, I never am. So I need something new, somewhere to ply my trade, a fresh forum for my devastation. You don’t conquer all at once, it happens incrementally. AWC will suffice, but not survive,” Shawn explained. “So, what is the mark you wish to leave? More titles?” “My greatness has long since been established. No title or accolade will do anything more than further cement what is already etched in granite. They are nothing more than cosmetic resume-fillers, artificial accolades, they mean nothing. The mark I wish to leave is one that I will prove can not be refuted. Shawn Singleton is the best in the business. Not just now, not just in the future, not just the past, but of all-time. I will set a standard so high, the notion of reaching it will be an accomplishment that only the second best can fathom.” “What makes you any different than the rest? What makes you better than Rustin, better than even Martyr?” “We deal with pain differently. Wrestling is about pain, about dishing it out and about how you react to the very real truth that you will be subjected to considerable amounts of it, as well. He seems to relish pain, both doling it out and in receiving it. Martyr is too impulsive to be cerebral about his craft. Indeed, he is a monster, which is why we serve as his eyes, his ears, his senses. He is a tool of destruction, something to be deployed at our every whim and desire. He is indiscriminate as to whom he hurts, who he maims, who he cripples. That, in itself, is strength, but it is also why he is weak. He lacks the focus and the tactical prowess of a true assassin.” “And you?” “I deal with pain differently. I too enjoy dishing it out, but Martyr and I differ in the receiving portion. Where he enjoys pain, I use it solely as a motivational tactic. You hurt me, and I have to hurt you more. Emotionally, physically, mentally, spiritually, there is no end to which I will not go to in order to enforce as much pain, to instill as much fear, to ensure as much suffering as possible. And I attack you with purpose; my violence isn’t just reckless and random. Everything I do is for a specific reason, every move, every word; every glance sets up the next, which ultimately leads to your end.” “So, it’s the physical more than the mental?” “Every sport or activity people view as physically demanding is primarily an exercise in the mental strength of the participant. My craft is no different.” “So, what do you have in store for your opponents?” “Pain. That’s all I promise. Remember in 4WW, how I used to obsess over victories, how I’d demand victories at all cost?” “I do.” “That was a flaw. Wins and losses in this industry don’t matter. No one knows how many wins anyone else has, it doesn’t matter. A competitor is defined by the legacy he leaves behind, the moments he imbeds in the minds of those that watch him, the unforgettable nature of his actions. So, I no longer require victory to solidify my status.” “What do you judge yourself by, then, if not by wins and losses, successes or failures, victories or defeats?” “Unforgettable moments. Frankly, I envision there will be matches that I will accept here in this company that I won’t even want to win. I’m challenging myself here, Hunter. Why do you think I’ve allowed us to, for now, be a tag team? It’s something new, something I don’t do, it’s a challenge. I’m making myself even better by perfecting that which I do not enjoy. I used to fear losing because it removed that aura of invincibility that I had built so highly. Now? I know I’m invincible, and losing no longer convinces me of anything less. Like I said, you don’t conquer anything all at once. It happens piece by piece. A man who looks at the bigger pictures will never stress over the smaller portions. My plans are grander than anything you’ve ever seen me put together. Just you watch. So, I do not guarantee victory, not even against The New Black, not even for my first match. What I do guarantee, however, will be that what is witnessed will not be forgotten,” Shawn says. The Hunter looked across the table at Shawn, a man who had, at one time, been responsible for him not being able to walk for four years, a man who he viewed as family, a man whose career he had overseen every moment of, and smiled. He had been taught well.
