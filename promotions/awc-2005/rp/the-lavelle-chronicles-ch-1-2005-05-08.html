The Lavelle Chronicles, ch 1

8 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9th of April The 9th of April 2005, Harrisburg, PA Thompson Medical College The following takes place between 9: 00 and 10:15 am The morning sun rose in the distance, my mouth tasted strange, my tongue felt numb. As I rose up from my bed I felt the after shock of a hangover slowly creeping up on me. I knew I had been drinking, well at least I thought I had. If I didn’t then I should be proud! Although it seemed I had something not to be proud of, as bottles of tequila lined the floor underneath my bunk. Rob never came out drinking with me, or the band. He was a conservative type person. He studied; watched wrestling and the odd time went out. I knew if I needed something Rob would be the one to trust. The alarm slowly “rang” in the corner of a very messy room. I knew the dominator, ‘as we called the dorm inspector’, would have something to say about the mess. There was a quiet knock against the stained wooden door. I rose from my mess of bed and answered its call!. The last thing I expected was for Professor Sullivan to come knocking at my door. It was 9:00 in the morning. His presence emitted edginess about his reasons for being in the dorms before class. The night before, all I could remember was what brand of cologne I had put on! Something not to be proud of either. Professor Sullivan was a large man. Over six feet and 250 pounds. He carried it well though, with a full head of curly brown hair and contrasting blue eyes. The hair on his chin added a highbrow look of an artist to this image, though art had little to do with the field he toiled in. I didn’t have my lecture on the “Cervical Vertebrae” until 10:00 am. It confused me as to why he was here. Still I wondered though: perhaps I left my Dictaphone in the room the other day, or I forgot my assignment. My mind began to work a mile a minute. “I am afraid you won’t be welcome in our class this morning, Pierce. It seems the Dean wishes to speak with you; I have come as a friend to reason with your thought process. Pierce, I have heard about your hooligan antics over the last year and I must say it doesn’t sound like the actions of a straight “A” student. What do you think?” Professor Sullivan said, staring at the mess of empty tequila bottles on the floor. “Okay, I’ll go see the Dean. Would it be possible for you to get me some notes on the lecture?” I asked, not really caring at all about the lecture. I began to understand why I was here in this college, and it was because my father and grandfather both studied here and got their degrees and such! I spent most my life trying to be my father and not Pierce Lavelle. “I shall see about that,” Professor Sullivan said. “I am sure you will!” I was about to close the door when he spoke once more. “Your meeting with the Dean is at 10: 00 am, on the dot. Don’t be late, or else,” Professor Sullivan strolled down the corridor, his briefcase swinging below his hip. He began to whistle a familiar tune before he disappeared down the long steep steps into the “hell hold”, otherwise known as the freshman residence. ‘A simple back track to the past’….. My father soon became wise to my use of the college funds. The drink and the band. Everything I had started to become, he had taken away. Kept me on track to becoming his lab rat. I wondered why the Dean wanted me though. Perhaps my father had come down to pay me a visit, here in “Thompson College”. Maybe he is here to watch me in my wrestling match against Bel-Air in the nationals. Maybe he’s changed! “Pierce! Where did all this drink come from? It wasn’t here when I went to sleep,” Rob sat up on his bed. “I don’t remember,” I sat down on the small beanie-bag; starring at the group of empty tequila bottles on the floor. “You have got to stop hanging around with that popular crowd, Pierce. I know you like the richer class ones because they are the rebels. But it isn’t you. Let me guess, Rachael Le’ Cross. The prettiest girl in school was having a pool party in the girls’ dorm again.” Rob climbed down off the top bunk and began picking up my mess. Robert Green has been my friend since kindergarten; I owed him a lot though. All the times I was in trouble or hiding from my father’s forceful hand. His parents would allow me to stay the night. Or stay in the tree house. In some ways I began to wish I could do that now. I had hit a large boulder in the road, and I was tumbling fast. First the drinking, then the late nights and now all these girls I don’t even know. “I heard what the professor said,” Rob said, throwing the tequila bottles down the waste shoot in the hall. “Yeah, I wonder what she wants.” I looked at the clock; it was 9:30 already. I sat up and grabbed my baggy jeans and the school jumper and quickly got dressed. “I wonder, too….” Rob said, with a hint of sarcasm in his voice. “You know man, I was trying to look out for you when you started drinking, but after a while I had to give up. Your father and mine are both a like. Ruining our minds and beliefs and infiltrating them with their thoughts. But sometimes Pierce I feel as though you truly are a lot like Brad Lavelle. ” Rob headed into the bathroom. To hear that name sent a cold shiver down my spine. Brad Lavelle as you could guess was my father. I had never heard Rob talk like this before. We had become the two musketeers, dodging our evil fathers, while trying to build careers for ourselves. Now even Rob was turning against me. I left the room the way it was and ran down the corridor toward the Dean’s office. The walls of Thompson College greeted me with a smile, the college gates wide open, stuffed to the gunnels with freshmen, sophomores, juniors and seniors. I loved the early morning classes, and the good humoured girls that came with it. Only I was never one to confront a girl until drunk. The guts and the brawn weren’t with me; never have been. The college halls were alive with that familiar sound. The echo of the ungraceful alarm bell, beckoning for students to go to their lectures or classes. I ran passed my band, but none of them acknowledged my presence. They acted as though I didn’t exist. “Gods of War” we were called, but something after two nights ago changed my view on the people I was performing with. “Ah Piece Lavelle isn’t it?” The Dean said. Her door was open; she was expecting me. “Yes!” I said as I followed her in, shutting the door behind me. “Take a seat, won’t you? We have a lot to discuss.” She sat down and folded her arms. Her glasses ever so slightly began to slide down the brim of her nose, and in one swift motion she would move them back up. “You have been brought to my recognition Mr. Lavelle. I am aware of your grades and excellent performance in the team wrestling matches. Your coach has informed me of your behaviour that comes with it though. See Mr. Lavelle in this college we don’t take kindly to rebels or hooligans at all. In fact, most of the time we suspend them for a while, make sure they learn their lesson. But you Mr. Lavelle, are different.” She bent down and pulled out a large folder. She slammed it down on the oak table. Dust flew up and around the sides of the folder. She adjusted her glasses once more, before opening the large folder. “Do you know what this is?” She asked. “My record!” I stated. I began to guess where this conversation was going. “Yes it is, and it’s quite an interesting read too. Mr. Lavelle, it seems you have caused quite a mess around this college. [She stares at me, while slowly reading out my mistakes.] You’ve snuck into the girls’ dorms after hours, drunk. Broken four windows in the teachers lounge, died the swimming pool green in a drunken state, while yelling something of an un-etiquette language. However, your grades and actions in school activities completely contrast with your drunken behaviour. I have seen many hooligans in this college Mr. Lavelle, but you are somewhat different. Now I am afraid I must expel you, under the reasons I’ve just called out but also your college tuition balance is empty. You have been flaunting the money which was supposed to keep you in.” “But I didn’t do all of that…I swear. It wasn’t just me alone. I’ve been framed by the real rebels.” I said. My voice had become high pitched and squeaky, my nerves kicking in. “Really, Mr. Lavelle, this is not appropriate. You were caught on all three accounts alone. I must ask you to leave the college. You have four hours to collect your things and finish your last assignment. Good bye, Pierce Lavelle.” She stood up and pointed to the door. I got up slowly and without a single word left the Dean’s office. I walked down the corridors of Thompson College. It didn’t look the same anymore. Its walls looked plain, the students dull and depressing. The water cooler gurgled silently in the background; as though it was laughing at my misfortune. I made my way back to my dorm, like a dog with its tail between its legs. I had been set up and framed by my band and some rich kids. Robert was right! I only dreaded the ride home. Following takes place between, 12:15 p.m. and 1:00 pm I sat still in the now empty dorm room and waited for Robert to finish class. I knew he wouldn’t be shocked; after all he was the one who warned me about all this. The word ‘different’, kept playing over and over in my head: “You Mr. Lavelle, are different”. The repetition of the word, made me feel like an outcast, a lower human being than the Dean. Yet, I couldn’t help but wonder if this expulsion was a wake up call! I knew I would have to confront my parents, the very people I wanted to run away from. This college, this dorm, was my solitude, my hide-out from my parents. Now I must go home. I looked at the clock, it was already 12:15 p.m., and I knew I only had a few hours left here, but I didn’t need anything else. All I ever had was a small cardboard box, which fit all my worldly possessions perfectly. Robert would be finished soon; he had a late lecture today. He was studying physics in this college, and I, biology. Something we had in common, I guess. I remembered as kids he would always get the higher mark in the physics exams and chemistry, but I, I could always nail the biology exam. It was funny, thinking about it now, how jealous I would get when he scored a 100% in all of his exams. Well nearly all. Though I guess that’s what you need, healthy competition. A balance in the world, which is our life. The moon falls and the sun rises, a butterfly flaps its wings somewhere and we get a hurricane, the “chaos theory”. The only thing I remember from physics lectures in our secondary school. “Pierce, what are you doing here?” Rob asked. Unaware about my recent expulsion. “I got expelled.” I said. My tone bland and tedious. “You are not serious, are you?” Rob now had a panicked look upon his face. “I’m afraid so. It won’t be so bad I guess.” I was lying, and he knew it. “Not so bad, you’re going to have to go home soon. The worse thing about that is your father and mine. We were their lab rats, this was our sanctuary. I can’t be alone in this college, Pierce.” Rob was now seated on the upper bunk, taking out his A4 pad, and notes. “I know, but I am afraid it’s all my fault.” I looked down at the photos of myself, Rob and our friend Pete. Memories began to flood into my mind, like a river into an open bank. “You’re right about that, Pierce. This is your fault. If you hadn’t started drinking, none of this would have happened in the first place. And you would still be finishing your second last year of college. You had one year left, Pierce, one year. Why did you waste these last three years, just to get…expelled” I could tell Rob was annoyed, and so he should be. The word ’expelled’ echoed in the dorm room. I messed everything up. For myself and for Rob. Our plans had been ruined, but this time not by our parents but, by me. “When are you leaving?” He asked. I looked at the clock once more. “In about 30 minutes. I was told by the Dean, to collect my things and say my goodbyes. Only you are my only goodbye. These girls you speak of, I don’t actually know. My band, “Gods of War” have replaced me in the space of two hours. I can’t even collect my guitar. So all I am left with is this, this box of copies and books.” I smiled, but it was fake. Some when they are upset put on a brave face. Others let out their true feelings, but I, I was too shy for any of that. I could never tell my true feelings to anyone, it was like a curse, something that has been with me since I turned 16. “I’ll come with you!” Rob said, jumping down onto the green carpet. “What?” I was shocked. “I’ll come with you. I don’t have another lecture until tomorrow, my professor caught the flu. Would you believe? So I have a free afternoon.” He smiled and picked up his mobile. “You sure? You know what my dad is going to do, right?” I was beginning to worry now, thinking about my dad, Brad Lavelle. “Yeah, I know, all too well,” Rob began to dial a number. “Who are you ringing?” I asked. “A taxi. I don’t want to take the school bus; it stinks from the last time. Didn’t you hear about it?” Rob laughed. “No! What happened?” I looked out the window and down onto the campus. Herds of people flocked onto the bright green grass, for their lunch. Something I did everyday with Rob and Pete, only now I wouldn’t. “Well the football team guys placed four stink bombs in random areas around the bus. Then on the way to practice, they began to light them, after a while, the placed smelt horrible.” I laughed silently to myself, more of a grunt really. I wasn’t in the mood for comedy, or funny jokes. Nothing could soften the blow of humiliation, dread and the unwillingness to see my parents. “Yeah, I’d like to book a taxi,” Rob said, waiting for a reply. “From and where to?” The recipient replied. “From Thompson College to Williamsburg Estates.” Rob replied. Williamsburg Estates was a large, private apartment area. To which my house, well my parent’s house was and also Robert’s parents lived. It was my little neighbourhood as a kid. It had a golf course for the adults and a large green field, for the kids. Fun huh! Not really. I spent most of my days indoors, but the odd time with Robert. Every afternoon, when the parents went to play golf, we fled down the hill toward the swimming pool, which had to be booked privately. Funny thing was, we never booked it, but always snuck in; without ever being caught! “Pierce, man stop day dreaming. We have to go, the taxi is on its way,” Rob grabbed the keys to the dorm and headed down the corridor, yelling for me to hurry as he did so. I took one last look at the dorm; which was my home for the last three years. I began to regret the life style I had become accustomed to, the life style which got me expelled. My box, sat by its lonesome in the corner of a now empty room. I picked it up and slowly saying one last goodbye, I closed the dorm door. The number “36” starred at me, watching me go. Home time now, home; wherever it was. The Following takes place between 3:00 p.m. and 4:00 p.m. The house in Williamsburg Estate was dark; it’s windows as empty as a dead man’s eyes. The house, or otherwise known as apartment to some, was an old style Californian Craftsman with a full front porch, to welcome the visitors and two dormer windows set on the long slope of the roof. “Home Sweet Home,” Rob said, starring at my home. “Yeah!” I didn’t want to knock on the door, in fact, I just wanted to turn around and walk back down the hill toward the electric gates. “You have to do this, Pierce,” Rob said. He looked down the road and noticed his dad’s grey BMW in the drive-way. “Well I’m not going home,” He said, with some hesitation in his voice. I wish I could say what Rob had just said but, I knew it was time to see them; after all it has been almost four years. I guess I never pictured that the next time I would visit my parents, would be to tell them I got expelled. Something my father wouldn’t want to hear, a kind of nightmare, for that kind of parent. I walked up the oak door and without hesitation, knocked three times. But, I didn’t expect my mom to answer the door. Maria Lavelle was my mother. She was a small, tanned, skinny woman with long brown hair and blue eyes. My mother was the one person I could tell things too, apart from Rob. There was many a time, when my mom packed our things and we tried to escape the clutches of my father; but we never succeeded. To be honest, I felt bad leaving her alone with him when I left for college. But I knew she wouldn’t be fully alone, as my sister, Rebecca, was still in secondary school. “Pierce!” My mother said. She gave me a hug. “What are you doing here?” She asked. “I think you better sit down, mom,” I said, looking at her. “Robert!” She said, now staring at Rob who was pacing up and down the driveway. “What are you doing here?” “Em, I just came back with Pierce, I have no classes this afternoon.” He froze stiff, like he used to, when my parents would question him. Only he didn’t know that my mom was nothing like my father. “Pierce, should your father be here for this?” My mother looked me in the eyes. Straight to the point, just as I remembered. Nothing had changed here, nothing. Everything was still the same, my mother’s hair, the dying plant in the hall. The broken stone in the driveway. Everything was the same! I wanted to tell her right now, because I knew my father wasn’t here. But, I couldn’t. “Perhaps we should wait.” I looked around to see if his car was coming up the driveway. “You’re still afraid of him, even to this day. I am so sorry we stayed, Pierce. I truly am.” She dipped her head down and walked into the hall. I followed her. Rob followed behind me. Whilest he closed the door, I took a moment to get my bearings, in a place that just held bad memories for me. I never wanted to come back, and if I did, it wasn’t to see him….. “Pierce!” My sister yelled as she came down the stairs. Rebecca Lavelle, my younger sister. She never had the treatment I had from our father, for some reason it was different; ‘like chalk and cheese’. Sometimes I feel it’s because my mother learnt her lesson with me. She knows now, how to protect us from him. To keep Rebecca on the track to what it is she wants to do. “Hey Becca! How are you?” I said, watching my mother move into the living room. “Hey Rob!” Becca said standing on the first step of the stairs. My little sister had a thing for Robert. Even though he never knew it, I just kept it to myself. Kind of like a conversation you heard, that you didn’t want to hear. It was like that for me. I walked in by accident one day, on one of my sister’s sleep overs, that was when I heard it. Till that day, its been lodged in my brain; I try to forget it! I really do. “Hi Becca! Pierce, where is the bathroom again? I need to freshen up.” He said, ignoring my sister and walking past me, looking for the bathroom. “Go down, past the kitchen and it is on the left hand side.” I smiled as I saw Becca glancing toward him. “So where you off to?” I asked. “Out to Kate’s and Tiffany’s,” Becca replied. They were her two childhood friends. She’s known them since primary really. “Okay, see you later I guess,” I replied and watched as she skipped out of the door and down the road. “Pierce,” My mom said. “Could we talk about why you are here now?” It was then I noticed my fathers shadow in the living room. He was standing next to her; his grey suit was on, along with a gold tie and black shirt. He had change a lot since the last time I had seen him, his face had become worn. His expressions not so terrorising anymore. I smirked to myself as I entered the room. He stared at me, menacingly. I knew I would get a talking to, for showing up without his permission, but, I didn’t care. I felt I had the power in this situation. He had become old and decrepit looking. He wasn’t the same, tall, stocky built man he once was. “Pierce, what are you doing here?” His deep voice asked. “Well you might want to sit,” I said, starting to sit down. “Don’t tell me what to do, boy,” He replied. “Fine,” I said, with a grin on my face. “Why are you here son?” My mother asked, her voice was gentle and caring. “I got expelled from college,” I was shocked too, it just came out. To be honest I didn’t think it would be so short and simple; but for some reason it was. “You what?” My father yelled. “You got expelled?” My mother said. “Be quiet Maria,” My father put his hand in front of her face to silence her. “You might be able to control me, dad! But, you can’t tell my mom what to do. I got expelled from College, because I hated it there. I never wanted to become a surgeon like you. Everything I have done my whole life, weren’t my wishes, my hopes or my dreams, they were yours. All the times me and mom tried to flee, it wasn’t because she was cheating on you, it was so we could get away from you. Don’t you see old man, you created something, a lab rat. So I could be just like you. Well I am not.” I smiled, this was the first time in my whole life that I have ever stood up against him. “What did you do? What did you do?” He asked it twice. But, I don’t think the repetition was all that necessary. “Well, I spent all the college savings on drink, my band and gigs. I apparently died the swimming pool green, broke the teachers lounge windows. Oh, and I snuck into the girls’ dorm while drunk. So I got expelled, but to be honest, I am kind of glad I did it. It allows me the chance to do what I want to do. I have the grades to go anywhere.” I stood up and was about to leave when my father grabbed my arm. “You spent all the college tuition money?” He said. “Yes!” I replied sharply. “Why?” My mother asked. She stood up and pulled my dads arm off my shoulder. “All this time you felt like this, why didn’t you just tell me when we tried to run? Why didn’t you tell me?” I knew she was upset. I had failed her in someway. “I was too scared, of him,” I looked at Brad Lavelle, my dad. “Hey everybody!” Rob said standing in the hall. He could tell I had broken the news, he was quick to leave. “I’m going upstairs to put away my things.” I left without another word. I listened to them fighting in the other room. The following takes place between 4:00 p.m. and 4:15 p.m. The arguing continued. I listened quietly to my mother yelling at my father, and my father yelling back. This was the usual sort of life, a few years back and now I was back here again. I didn’t want this life again; I didn’t want to be in a house with him, to eat dinner with him, to watch TV with him. I did that once, I won’t do it again. But, I couldn’t leave my mother and sister again. This truly was a dilemma. I knew I would have to get them out of here, one day. But first, I needed to get out, to establish a new life. The yelling stopped. I could hear my sister and her friends coming in, silence I thought. But, it would continue once Becca turned on the music in her room and closed her door. My duffle bag was still parked under my bed. My room hadn’t changed a bit, it was cleaner; but nothing dramatic had been done to change it. I knew my mother sometimes sat in here by herself to think and to remember the good old days, but this place it didn’t hold the good memories, only the bad. I pulled out my pass port and my savings and loans booklet. I knew I had around $3000 still in the account. It should be enough to get me out of this town, this city. Without a single thought, I grabbed my duffle bag and began to stuff it with my clothes, music, tooth brush and towels. Anything I would need. The yelling soon started again. I zipped the zipper up the full way and put on my jacket. My first stop would be the bank, to withdraw all my money. My next stop would be the bus station. I unhinged the window and climbed out onto the slopped roof. Rob stared at me from the ground below, he knew what I was up to but, was way ahead of me. He had his things too. His backpack, his clothes and his money. I was confused. I hoped down onto the green lawn and ran out of the driveway. “What are you doing, Rob?” I asked. “Coming with you,” He replied. “What?” I said, making sure my parents didn’t see me. “Don’t worry, they didn’t see you. I don’t want to stay in Harrisburg anymore than you do. So let’s get out of here!” He smiled as he stared at his mother who was yelling his name from behind the large metal gates of his front garden. “That’s the best idea yet!” I looked around and began to walk down the road. “How much money have you got?” I asked. “About $4000 in my savings and loans deposit. My dad keeps topping it up every week,” He asked the security guard to open the gates. The exit out of Williamsburg Estates. Freedom I thought to myself, freedom. “Perfect, that makes $7000 between us. We should be able to rent an apartment, get a job and work for a new life,” A new life I though to myself, the sound of it just sent a tingling feeling down my spine; like the saying goes “Someone just walked over my grave”. A strange old saying for something such as that, but fitting. Rob and I began our long walk down the hill, toward civilisation. Goodbye it really was; goodbye Thompson College, goodbye Harrisburg and goodbye Williamsburg Estates. The Following takes place between 7:00 p.m. and 7:30 p.m. The bus station looked so quiet, peaceful and sedated. All the customers sat in the rows of seats waiting for which ever bus took them out of town. I stood starring at all the different bus routes: 7:45 p.m. BUS FROM HARRISBURG TO CHARLESTON, WEST VIRGINIA 8:00 p.m. BUS FROM HARRISBURG TO ALBANY, NEW YORK [Note some stop overs along the journey.] It was then I saw the city I wanted to go to; Albany, New York. It was perfect. “Hey Rob, I found it,” I said; pointing to the second bus time. “Albany, New York. Sounds awesome. I was thinking Long Island,” Rob said. A hint of disappointment soon followed. “I’m sure Long Island is a great place, we can do that anytime, but Albany has some great colleges. Also my grandfather might be able to get us some work,” I walked over to the customer service desk. “Two tickets to Albany, New York. The 8:00 p.m. bus,” I said; while taking out the money for the tickets. “You are aware of the stop overs, aren’t you?” She asked politely. “Yes,” I replied, in an urgent tone. I wanted to get out of here, now. But, I couldn’t tell her that. Otherwise she would think I was some sort of criminal. “Here you are two tickets to Albany. Enjoy your trip,” She took the money, and gave me my change. “ANNOUCNEMENT………..The bus to Charleston will be arriving in 15 minutes. All passengers for the Charleston bus, could you please que up on isle 12. THANK YOU” In 30 minutes I could say goodbye. I pulled out my mobile and rang Becca’s mobile. And it rang, and it rang, until she finally answered. “Hello!” She said. “Rebecca, its Pierce. Listen I’m leaving, please don’t tell mom and dad, it isn’t the right time. I escaped through the bedroom window, you should try it sometime. If you ever need me, just call okay. My mobile will be on, all the time,” I looked at the announcement board. “How long will you be gone for?” She asked, turning down her music. “Not sure! Besides you know I can’t stand dad, and well I think I have upset mom. Just watch yourself okay, and don’t let him control you.” I said, knowing that I would never be returning to Harrisburg. “Okay!” She said. “I have to go, Tiffany, Kate and I are going to the mall,” With that she hung up. I felt terrible for not telling the truth about where I was going, how long for and if I could actually see her again. Rob was becoming very tense. He thought his parents had trailed us the whole way here, but I know they didn’t. They wanted what was best for Rob, a little different to my parents. If he wanted to leave, they would let him. Mine, would follow me, take me to the cops and have me arrested, so I couldn’t leave. Nice huh? I laughed to myself thinking about that, it was done once before, come to think of it. My father had me locked in my room for prom night. Didn’t trust me, thought the drink and the girls would corrupt my thoughts. “Pierce, it’s time to start heading down to isle “24”. We should start to wait, don’t you think?” Rob said, picking up his bag and beginning the long trek down the paved pathway. “Yeah, let’s go,” With that I picked up my things and turned off the music. I could almost smell the sweet scent of freedom. View Pierce Lavelle 's Biography
