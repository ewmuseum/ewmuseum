How much is a new chapter worth?

23 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23rd October 2006 Saturday 14th October 2006 His thoughts drifted, much as they always seemed to do when issues of relative important came to pass. He remembered a time when it wasn’t so, when things were far simpler. All he would have to do was sign what he was told to, and the rest would take care of itself. But now, now he was the man telling others what to do, and every meticulous detail was necessarily covered with a fine toothcomb. The past eighteen months seemed a blur at that moment. So much had happened, that it all simply gelled into one continuous movement, of which there was no beginning, and he could see no end. But all those moments had led him here. He was often chastised for believing in such notions of destiny, but his reply was always the same “Men who fear their destiny fall short of their potential” or some other remark of a similar nature. In truth, the past year and a half had shaken that belief to the core, but as he sat looking at his face in the mirror, he knew it to be true. Where had the time gone? He asked himself, as he did time and time again. Things were so great before. The business was fantastic. The only problems in his life were his brother and his conscience, neither of which had truly been solved with the events that unfolded. He pondered there, whether his decision to come back to his love of wrestling in the winter of 2004 was a wise move. He knew that what he was doing now was inevitable, but he had a choice back then, a real choice. It had been nothing to agree to come back, agree to take the mantle once again. But now, he thought maybe he’d made the wrong choice. Sure, he’d held a world title, and numerous others in his favourite hobby (for that’s what he believed it to be for himself, when in actual fact, it was more of an addiction), but the costs had been so high. So high indeed. His Mother now lay in her grave, and he had absolutely no questions about the reasons for that. He’d let the business slip. He’d been taken over by his greed for personal achievement in one facet of his life, that the others had almost crumbled beneath his hands. She lay in her grave now as a pawn in a game he’d undertaken…against a foe you simply cannot beat. His thoughts neglected to cover the entire incident, but his eyes deepened, a tint of sadness colouring his plastic smile. Then he’d left, lost everything. China had been his salvation. But then, China had been the destruction of his brother. It was there he’d returned with his notions of destiny and a new confidence, and back to the wrestling world. But now, the very place he had strived to return to had practically given him no alternative. HSW was crippled. He could see little future, especially none he would strive to be a part of. It was precisely that fact that led him to sit where he was. The contract on the table was sufficient, but money was never a concern…it would never have to be. Even after losing it all, he was rich beyond reprieve. His assets, seized by the Government of the USA under the premise of their linkages to organised crime were returned to him without question. His finances were gone, sure, but there were always more. The Swiss accounts, the Cayman Islands, the charities he’d set up to avoid Corporate Gains Tax in the United Kingdom, the Inheritance fund set up by his father, for precisely these kind of situations. No, he was never a poor man. His empire grew once again, and it would continue to do so. He would make sure the same mistakes were never done again. Perhaps he had been too immature when his Father had died, but now he knew what was needed. But despite everything, he sat in this seat, ready to commit himself to another chapter in his wrestling dialogue; dialogue with the fans; dialogue with his colleagues; but far more importantly, dialogue with himself. The smile crept over his lips. There was only one thing he had to remind himself of. ”You’re Jonny Kae. If anyone can do it, it’s you.” The name was signed, the contract finalised. Jonathan Alexander Spencer Kae was now a part of the Atlantic Wrestling Club. Memories of HSW, VWF, ICWF flashed before his mind, but were quickly replaced with hopes and promises about his new future. This chapter wouldn’t be the end. This chapter wouldn’t be the final monologue of Jonny Kae. No, it was a new beginning… *********************************************************** Nearer the present day. Kae sat, languishing somewhere between sleep and alertness, the light of the projector flickering annoyingly on the white screen of the presentations room. His eyes were heavy, thoughts lazy and sluggish, and his interest gone… The man standing beside the projector, holding a long, thin white retractable indicator pole looked exactly as Jonny had expected him to look. He wore his short, black hair down, fringe resting just above his overly large spectacles, which seemed to enlarge his brown eyes. The man’s face was printed upon by the legacy of a terrible affliction with spots. His mouth in a mono-expression, somewhere between frowning and indifference. He wore a tweed suit jacket with brown tartan trousers and a pair of black dress shoes. But what really got on Kae’s nerves was the blue and red tie the man let fall shamelessly from his collar. This man was Henry Lattice, and he was boring the hell out of the head of Kae Enterprises. Jonny Kae: “I think I’ve seen enough. Please could someone turn on the damn lights?” Usually, Jonny Kae would have left this kind of thing to his venture assessment team, or maybe his emerging markets specialists, but this one had been so insistent. Besides, Jonny had some time, and he wanted to think about things. What better excuse than this? But his patience had worn out, and he wasn’t getting any thinking done. Henry Lattice: “But, Mr. Kae, sir, don’t you want to know the profit margin expectations for the second quarter?” Kae hated it when people tried to talk business and clearly knew little about it. He sighed, and looked down at the dreary grey and white pamphlet the small man had distributed before his presentation. He blinked twice, leaving enough of a silence to make his point, and looked up at the IT graduate, who had made the seven hour by coach from his home in Manchester. Jonny Kae: “Quite frankly, no. I’m not interested in fritting my money away. The figures just don’t add up kid.” The man nervously scratched his head. Henry was in a precarious position. He didn’t know what to say next. Henry Lattice: “Maybe if I finished my presentation…” Kae glared a hole through the maggot with a burning rage. Jonny Kae: “Maybe if you shut the hell up! You’ve been throwing verbal diorhea at me for long enough! You expect Kae Enterprises to put forty thousand pounds into a venture that’s going to generate a yearly revenue of under seventy thousand?” The man looked at Kae for a moment, then down at his notes. He muttered to himself as he looked through. Finding the page he wanted, he again looked at Jonny Kae, who was understandably unimpressed. Henry Lattice: “Um…if you turn to page…um…fifteen…I mean fourteen of the pamphlet, I’ve predicted revenues of a hundred and six thousand pounds for the first year. I…um…” Kae slammed his fist onto the desk. Jonny Kae: “Is it not bad enough that AWC are putting me in a contest with an unemployed maggot and a midcard overachiever? If I believed in Karma I’d give a crap load of cash to third world tragedies to stop shit like this happening! Your predictions are laughable, you insolent reprobate! You’re talking of an emerging market in which the specialisation is crippling! I mean, who the hell’s going to want to buy these things? People like you, that’s who!” Henry took a step forward. He was visibly shaking, and the sweat was cumulating on his brow. His unsteady hand gripped the end of the table as he tried to balance himself. Henry Lattice: “Actually, the Lattaspecs are a huge step in computer safety innovation. Optical specialists across the United Kingdom have attested to its reliability and…” Kae exploded. He jumped out of his seat, making his way up the long mahogany table in the centre of the room, his eyes fixed on the small, quivering excuse for human innovation. Jonny Kae: “They’re glorified sunglasses. Designed for people who spend more than eight hours at a time on the computer? Who the hell does that? Not only have you grossly overestimated the demand for these glorified fashion accessories, but also you’ve seriously underestimated the marginal cost of these things. Maybe if we were producing thousands a day in an Indian sweatshop, but Kae Enterprises has a restraint on its production capabilities in the third world, thanks to these ridiculous ‘Free World’ campaigners, and there’s no way in hell I would ever authorise such ambitious production for something as ridiculous as this. And what the hell were you thinking when you were pricing these things? No one’s going to pay Forty quid for them!” The poor Henry Lattice took a step back, as the intimidating and authoritative six foot seven figure of Jonny Kae approached. He dropped his pamphlet and notes on the table as the lights came glaring on, casting an even more devastating affect on the approaching wrestler – business veteran. Henry Lattice: “Well, I…I thought…” Kae pulled the plug out of the socket for the projector. He turned to Henry, and picked up the pamphlet on the table before him. Jonny Kae: “You waste my time…for this?” Kae hurled the pamphlet at the quivering man, who stumbled back again, nearly falling on his ass. Kae motioned to the doorway, from which two large men dressed in black suits and sunglasses entered. Jonny Kae: “Get rid of this worm! He’s vexing me. When I get vexed people start losing jobs.” The two men nodded to Kae, part out of respect, part out of acknowledgement. One grabbed the shocked Henry Lattice by the arm. He struggled understandably, but received a slap across the face from the second large man. Without further warning, the IT grad fainted. One of the large men looked at Kae questioningly. Kae seemed deep in thought. The man motioned towards Kae. Security: “Sir?” Kae turned suddenly to the situation, and noticed the predicament. Jonny Kae: “I don’t know! Put him on the street and put a tenner in his pocket. If he tries to come back in, make sure he gets the point…” The two men nodded, and carted the unconscious Mr Lattice out of the presentation room. Across the room, the quiet Jolene, who sat shaking her head and looking down at the pamphlet in front of her. She looked the epitome of professionalism, with her shoulder length blonde hair tied back into a ponytail, crisp suit jacket and matching black skirt, white blouse, and pair of compact rectangular spectacles. Kae had often wondered what would have happened between them if he’d gotten past his pride and ego and asked her if she was interested in more than PR work as far as he was concerned, before she got married to that pathetic political renegade she called her lover. Jonny Kae: “What?!” Her eyes blinked. He blinked back. This week hadn’t gone so well. Best cut your losses Jonny. He sighed, half out of frustration, and half out of remorse, and left the room. Jolene sat in her seat, wondering the same thing… *********************************************************** Saturday October 21st 2006. Jonny Kae knelt before the headstone, his head bowed. The black sunglasses guarded the tears that were forming around his eyes, and sneaking out to slip down his cheek. His shadow caressed the flowers, or lack thereof, that now seemed to wither and die beside the expensive marble. Of all the graves in this cemetery, the one he knelt before now was by far the most impressive, but also perhaps by far the most solemn. Something had struck Jonny as fitting about it when he’d chosen it. It seemed to emanate a sadness that memories simply couldn’t feed and encourage. He wanted to feel sadness when he knelt there. He had known he would want to, and so it was wholly appropriate. For all Jonathan Alexander Spencer Kae’s solid exterior, and moral questionability, his Mother had always been the only one who’d been able to tease out his softer, sympathetic and tender side. Now she was gone. Of all the events that had happened in his life, Jonny Kae regretted few. This was one of them. His thoughts drifted back…back to the hotel room…the call from the police. The callous bastards had left her where she was, not even taken care of the blood surrounding her lips. Surely they could have made it a little less real, a little less vibrant. The sight of his dead Mother, brutally murdered, had caused Jonny to throw up then, and he couldn’t help it now. The vomit exploded out of his mouth beside a dead rose, lying silently, wavering in the wind beside her gravestone. He threw up then, and threw up now, because he knew he was responsible. Jonny Kae: “Every time Mum. Every damn time. That memory haunts me still, torments my very being. Sorry is a five letter word, and so easy to mutter, but I do so with great reverence. I’m fucking sorry!” He felt the tears continue to flood out, uncontrollably. He’d forced his bodyguards to wait by the limo with his driver. He had to be alone in this time of weakness. No one could ever see him like this. But then, he enjoyed the vulnerability it allowed him, the ability to be out of control for once. He wiped the trickling bile from his lips with a white handkerchief from his pocket. He let it fall away from his hand into the wind, and never even glanced as it dragged itself away on the grass. In his left hand, Kae’s fist clenched around the three roses he had brought, crushing them within his black glove. Jonny Kae: “If I could…If I could take that day back…all those days. But those bastards will get what’s coming to them.” His words almost sounded like he was trying to diminish his responsibility in her death. It was cowardly, he knew it, but he had no choice…these emotions were eating him alive. Anger was a more useful emotion than fear and weakness. He’d learnt that long ago from his father…possibly the only thing he’d really learnt from the vile man. Though sometimes he looked in the mirror and saw his father’s grey bearded face condemning him, Jonny Kae knew in his heart he was nothing like the man. But of course, it’s the destiny of all great men to have been bruised by their fathers, only to reshape themselves in the image of their abusers. Jonny Kae: “I’ll show you Mother. I’ll show you. I’m gonna make it all up to you. Just keep watching this movie, this scene, this playback. Keep glued to the set. The Kae name will never sink again…Never!” His sunglasses were swelling up with the tears from his eyes. He removed them momentarily, and wiped them with his glove, following the action by wiping his eyes. Jonny Kae: “There are so many dimensions of me that you never fully appreciated…so many you never got to see. I’m gonna show you them all. This is a new chapter, and I’m an entirely different author. Just please don’t give up on me just yet. Please. I’ve earned that much, surely.” Kae sluggishly got to his feet. He looked down, sunglasses still in his hand, at the gravestone. The grass seemed to grow prouder before this marble headstone, than any of the others in the cemetery. But then, this one belonged to a Kae. Jonny Kae kissed the three roses he held in his right hand, and let them fall onto the grass before his Mother’s name, engraved in expensive stone. He placed the shades back over his eyes and took a step back. His suit was somewhat crinkled, the tie slightly out of place. The only time he ever wore a tie was when he was around his Mother. Even when she was dead, it seemed, old habits would die hard. Jonny Kae straightened his tie, mark of respect in his imagination, and let his hands trail down the open sides of his suit jacket. He fastened the buttons, and closed his long woollen Navy Blue overcoat around the suit, the very image of his father it would seem. The only thing that was missing was the white scarf, but even that, he’d left in the car. A moment of sadness overtook him, and he couldn’t look at her gravestone anymore. Jonny turned away, head bowed, and muttered the words ‘I’m sorry’ once again, before taking a few steps away towards the limo. He turned his head back once, stopping momentarily, before continuing. The large man, his brown hair short and neat, holding the door to the limo, motioned to Jonny Kae. Bodyguard: “Are you alright sir?” Kae looked at the man through his shades for a moment. He removed his black gloves and placed them in his pockets. Symbolically perhaps, he wiped his hands together, as if he was a modern day Pontius Pilate…but in this scenario there was no one to condemn; no one, that is, except himself. He nodded to the tall man. Jonny Kae: “I am now. Come on, we’ve got a plane to catch…” As Jonny got into the limo, it was as if a magnetic force field cleansed him of the guilt he’d felt in the cold, nipping Scottish weather. He felt suddenly refreshed and revitalised. He looked out once more to the cemetery. He blinked, lingering perhaps on remaining thoughts of his Mother’s tender face. He looked…and wound up the black windows. Now, if his Mother were watching, she could not see in, but he could see out. It seemed an analogy of their relationship…his life…everything he’d lost. Time for a new chapter…time for the AWC. *********************************************************** Sunday October 22nd 2006 As the plane stormed down the runway, Jonny Kae waited in anticipation. One of the only good experiences he ever had with his father had been at the point of takeoff in a plane. They would look out of the window, at the cars and people becoming smaller and smaller. When they were small enough, his father would fuel Jonny’s imagination of a giant boot coming crashing down and crushing them like ants. He called it the ‘destiny of the underclass’ to be squashed beneath the foot of corporate hegemony. They would both laugh, Jonny more so at the thought of a giant foot just suddenly appearing from the clouds. His father would proudly smile at him, and he’d feel special for the rest of the flight, until corporate matters stole his attentions. Indeed, flying seemed to be the only time when his father couldn’t escape the presence of his son. Of course, it was all an illusion, but something made Jonny feel comfortable about watching the cars and people grow smaller as the plane rose. It had nothing to do with the ‘boot of corporate hegemony’, or even the comic idea of a giant divine boot crushing the witless underclass. It reminded him of his father’s laughter, and of the only time Jonny Kae had felt really invulnerable. This time, as the plane rose, the same comfortable feelings were joined by another feeling, one he’d not felt in a long while – hope. Things were opening into a new leaf. New direction in his hobby of wrestling. He was, in fact, wrestling this Tuesday against the PTC Global Champion and AWC Livewire Champion, Johnny Lexicon. He had great hope for Jonny Kae in AWC. But added to that was the events of the past few weeks. By some rather unforeseen circumstances, Jonny Kae was stepping for the first time into the world of politics. It was a completely new avenue for him, and he had no idea where it would lead, or how long it would take, but as of the twenty second of October, Jonny Kae was a political figure. Sure, he’d funded political regimes in various countries where he had a vested interest, but he’d never even entertained the possibility of being a part of those political regimes. Yet now, Jonny Kae was being put forward as a possible contender for the Republican Party Candidacy for the state of Illinois. It was a prospect shrouded in ludicrous connotations, but Kae only had to look at the success of such prominent businessmen as Burlesconi of Italy, in putting Corporate drive into political means. Burlesconi had changed the face of Italian politics, and at the same time, created the ideal breeding ground for his own businesses. Perhaps Kae could do something similar? He was getting ahead of himself. Plenty of time to sort things out. His thoughts thus drifted back to wrestling. He was looking forward to debuting in AWC. It was all looking up. His only concern was that he was teamed with someone he’d neither heard of, nor seen before. B.O.T was his partner against the team of Almasy and Lexicon, and needless to say, Kae was a bit uneasy about the prospect. On the stake, a place in the Triangles Main Event, and a shot at the Transatlantic Championship and more! Jonny lifted his glass, half full of champagne, to his lips and let it fall gently down his throat. He placed the glass in the little holder provided on the table in case of turbulence, and moved for the telephone. He inputted a sequence of numbers, and the other end began to ring. The silky voice that answered sent shivers down his spine. Jonny Kae: “Hi Jolene. Once again, I’m sorry you couldn’t accompany me to the States. I appreciate everything your doing over in London with the ‘housekeeping’. I just have one little favour to ask of you.” He waited for her reply, and smiled, as if she could see him. Jonny Kae: “Yes, thanks. I know. Sorry about that. But, anyway, if you could be a real gem and find me the personal cell number of Jarrett Clemans, aka B.O.T, from the AWC, I would very much appreciate it.” Again, the silky voice answered, and his mind swam with images of its owner. Jonny Kae: “Thank you my dear. You know I appreciate everything. Just give the appropriate channels however little money they want for the information, and get me that number ASAP. Thanks” Kae put down the receiver after his golden-haired angel had hung up. He sat back in his chair, looking at the champagne bottle in the cooler beside his seat. It always struck him as somewhat creepy and disconcerting whenever he was the only passenger on a plane. But then, money buys such things. He took the glass, and finished its contents, placing it back in the holder. Jonny Kae found himself a little sleepy, and sank back into the chair. In a few hours they would touch down in America, but until then, he had little to do. - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - He closed his eyes, thoughts drifting to his angel, Jolene, the face he’d seen in the darkness when his life had been saved in the muddy outskirts of Beijing. How far he’d come since that day. He’d wanted so much since then to tell her how he felt, but he could never find the words. Afterall, when he had disappeared for 4 months, she’d married, and had almost forgotten he’d ever existed. But, he was allowed to dream, and dream he did. Jolene, smiling at him, that graceful smile, authoritative but tender. Their children running across the room onto the stone balcony. He followed them. He looked over in the distance, crowds and crowds of people, watching, waiting, perhaps for him. Instinctively he smiled, and waved. The crowd roared. They chanted his name, raising banners with his picture planted on them, British and American flags. Families were greeting him. Children happy in the comfort of their Mother’s arms, watching the man on the balcony. His children were gone. So was Jolene. His Sunday clothes had changed to a suit, the red tie hanging down. Red ties only ever really meant one thing to Jonny Kae – Politics. He looked down, there were police everywhere, and special service agents. They created a barrier against the restless crowds. Within the crowd there were those who didn’t chant his name, weren’t happy. Something about them, he could tell that… Then a shot was fired…it was so sudden! BANG! Jonny instinctively ducked. A voice screamed out… “Murderer! Murderer!” A flurry of bullets fired into the crowd, from his own Special Service agents! He tried to shout to them to stop, but his words had no volume. “May God have mercy on your soul President Kae! For the things you’ve done have no place, even under watch of Satan! I hope…” The accusation stopped as another flurry of bullets fired into the crowd. People dropped in all directions, screaming, dying. “One Nation Under God? You’ve made a joke out of the concept!” another voice chimed in, and another wave of bullets. More screams. Jonny got to his feet, looking over. Instead of the crowds of cheering supporters, the crowd was now an angry mob, all wearing ripped clothes, tarnished and worn. It looked like an army of street beggars. “Down with Kae!” One voice screamed over the din. “You’re a disgrace to your country, a disgrace to your people, and a disgrace to yourself!” He recognised that voice. He peered into the crowd, and one figure was more vibrant, brighter than the others. Then he noticed that the entire landscape for miles into the horizon was decimated…scorched earth. It was what Jonny Kae had always imagined a nuclear holocaust would look like. Was that what he’d done? “Your legacy can only be what you make it Jonathan. Your legacy can only be that of evil…” He focused on the figure, and found himself in shock. For there, standing in the crowd, was the perfect image of his Mother, Samantha Kae. She pointed up at him accusingly. “You were the greatest mistake I ever made, son. For what I brought into this world, I deserve eternal torment. I loathe you. I loathe myself. How does it feel Jonny? How does it feel to be a tyrant? How does it feel to bring the world to its knees? To destroy it…destroy everything? You were a grievous mistake…the world will pay for my sins.” Jonny held his arm out, over the balcony. He tried to apologise, to call to his Mother, but it was too late. The bullets flashed out from the Special Agents and Police. He watched in terror, as a flurry of shining spheres crashed into his Mother’s tender body, sending her three feet backwards, and to the floor, blood trickling from her chest and stomach. Her eyes still fixed on him, her breathing gave way. She was dead. Jonny Kae: “NOOO!!! NOOO!!! Not again! I can’t lose you again!” He collapsed on his knees. The bullets fired out repeatedly, as if in a rhythm. “Brrrr…Brrrr…Brrr” People falling to the ground everywhere, screaming, pain. “Brrr…Brrr…Brrr” His Mother’s eyes, dead, watching him, accusing him. “Brrr…Brrr…Brrr!' - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - Jonny Kae awoke violently. The phone was ringing. He almost fell of his chair, having forgotten to do his seat belt up. He looked about him in shock and horror, but there was only the plane. For a moment he thought the bullets were still flying, but soon reality kicked in. It had been a dream. The phone screamed at him, and he reached over, still shocked and disturbed by what his mind had created. Jonny Kae: “What?!” The silky smooth voice on the other end soon softened his temper. His breathing slowed, as did his pulse. He softly smoothed out his suit jacket, which he’d evidently been scrunching up during his dream. Jonny Kae: “Sorry Jolene. I was just having a bad…never mind. Thanks again. What was the number?” He wrote down a number on his table, and shook his head, as if to clear the cobwebs. Jonny Kae: “Thanks Jolene. What would I do without you huh?” Kae smiled, and hung up the phone. He chuckled to himself. Jonny Kae: “Come on old boy, it was just a dream. Get a grip. She’s dead.” He shook his head again, and looked down at the number. Jonny picked up the receiver again, and tapped in the numbers slowly and carefully. He sat back in his chair. They were close to their destination now, he could tell. They were over land. He was relieved…still perhaps shaken…but relieved none the less. The phone rang for a few moments, until a male voice picked up at the other end. Kae waited a moment, as it answered. Jonny Kae: “Is that Jarrett Clemans?” He waited for the response once again. Jonny Kae: “Your unfortunate partner for this ridiculous Triangles Competition.” Again the voice answered, and Kae waited patiently. Jonny Kae: “You might find that money buys more than the tangible.” The truth was, Jolene seemed to be able to buy more than the tangible, finding Clemans’ number within under an hour. Jonny reached over to the champagne bottle, which was still sitting in the cooler. He poured the liquid into his glass, and discarded the bottle. He sat back again, glass in hand. Jonny Kae: “Yes. Let’s dispense with the pleasantries. Now, as I understand it, you and I have a match against Johnny Lexicon and Seymour Almasy on Tuesday. I’d like to check that we’re on the same page.” He took a sip of the glass, the champagne soothing his dry, and sore throat. His mouth turned up in a grin, as if what was being said humoured him. Jonny Kae: “Yes…about that. I want to ensure that you’re in a sane frame of mind. To a normal individual, losing a King Prawn would be an acceptable sacrifice. However, I must enquire as to how it might affect your psychology?” Jonny looked at his watch. They were half an hour from their destination, at least by initial estimates. He took another gulp of the liquid. Jonny Kae: “I must admit, I've never had occasion to. But that's beside the point. You see, we're facing a man who was just sacked from his wrestling promotion, and an individual whose mind obviously isn't on AWC activities at the moment; thus, we shouldn't have a problem. That is, unless you screw up.” He placed the glass down on the table, looking out over the land beneath the private jet. Tiny specs were moving in the far distance. Cars, perhaps lorries. Jonny Kae blinked and returned to his relaxing posture. Jonny Kae: “I hope you're right. It's bad enough that AWC force me into a tag team match on my debut. Even worse that I have a place in the Triangles Main Event in someone else's hands. Personally, I don't see the Almasy - Lexicon duo working much at all, and that we can work to our advantage. All egos aside, ambition and co-operation are powerful driving forces. Before the match and after the match, I don't intend to associate myself with someone like you, but for the duration of our bout, it would be beneficial to be coherent.” Kae sighed at the response he was given. The new athletes in this business were all the same. They thought they had it all. They always did. Jonny remembered a time when the crowd weren’t fooled only by finely sculpted bodies and angry attitudes. In any case, this was his partner on Tuesday, so he lied through his teeth. Jonny Kae: “Such arrogance! Is admirable. But trust me, there is nothing mediocre about Jonny Kae. My resume speaks for itself. Just make sure you're ready Tuesday, as will I, and we should have no problems.” He didn’t wait for a response. Jonny hung up the phone, and picked up his glass. The new breed of wrestler vexed him, but it was unavoidable. If Jonny Kae were going to survive in this new era, he would have to adapt, as he had always done. World Titles seemed a far distance away now, whereas before they had been a guarantee. He sighed again, and finished off the glass of champagne. Jonny Kae: “So much for the new chapter.” Jonny put the empty glass next to his seat, by the cooler, and looked at his watch. Better get all his stuff together. He didn’t want to waste any time. There was so much to do, and so little time. *********************************************************** Monday 23rd October, 2006 The conversation with Richard M Daley, mayor of Chicago, hadn’t gone as well as Jonny Kae had expected. For a man who had been a former State Attorney, Kae should have suspected he would be suspicious, but he certainly wasn’t ready for the unwelcoming tone of Mr. Daley’s questions and conversation. Jonny Kae was in an altogether bad mood. He’d thought that, like him the son of a man who’s shadow he needed to step out of, he would understand Jonny’s drive. But instead, he brought up Kae’s former links to the Italian Mafia in New York, Jonny’s corporate history, and questioned his dedication due to his interests in Wrestling. It seemed that the political struggle would be just that, a struggle. He put it behind him, as the limo drove down the Toronto street towards the RICOH Coliseum, where this week’s Fresh! Was to be held. Jonny shook his head, and put his Oakleys over his eyes. He watched the people passing by the limo’s windows as it waited at a red light. Americans always rushed. Even Canadians. It seemed endemic to Western city life that individuals would rush so much that they would get lost on their roads of life. Not Jonny Kae. He had direction now, purpose, and no morally principled 2nd generation mayor was going to stand in his way. Nor, in fact, was a PTC Global Champion. The limo cruised gently into a space outside the coliseum. A valet opened the door…apparently there was a Boxing fight on tonight. As instructed, the driver refused to let Valet’s drive the limo, and left to the lot himself. Kae wrapped himself in his coat, and stepped up to the large doors of the RICOH Coliseum. Immediately, a man in a black tuxedo asked him for his ticket. Kae, instead retrieved a wad of dollar bills, and shoved $300 into the man’s hand. He didn’t even look at the man, and walked into the foyer. The place smelt clean, fresh. He liked fresh. Ironically, he was appearing on a show the next day called Fresh! Kae handed his coat to the cloakroom clerk, and crumpled a $10 bill into her hand. She smiled dutifully, and he walked on past, his suit crisp, shirt opened at the collar. Jonny looked at the schedule for the following night, and smiled when he saw his name. However, the smile turned to a frown when he read the synopsis of the match. It was Seymour Almasy this, Seymour Almasy that. What about the Most Perfectly Attuned body in Professional Wrestling? What about Jonny Kae? He sighed and walked into the back, handing another collection of bills to a security guard at the door, who let him past (not as if he could stop the six foot seven Kae anyway). Jonny walked down the hallway, hearing chatter in the distance, fighters and their valets and managers in deep discussion. Motivation. It was always encouraging to see young lions motivated. Full of promise, no fear. That was when they were most dangerous. But, nine out of ten times, their first defeat always proved to be the most important match of their careers. They lost their confidence and edge after that, and most never made it to stardom. It was this the PR firms loved to manipulate. Cockiness sold, but so did decimated dreams. The infatuation of America and the West in general with failure and tragedy was sometimes disturbing. He past the voices, and smelt the gleaming surfaces of the lockerooms fast approaching. The smell of cleaning products was always inviting. It reminded him of good times. It reminded him of ICWF. A stagehand was walking in the opposite direction. Kae decided to try his luck. Jonny Kae: “Hey there kid. I’m performing here tomorrow night, any chance of a personal lockeroom for the evening?” The young man looked up at Kae’s towering form, and quivered slightly. However, Kae was surprised that he didn’t recognise the former 10-time World Heavyweight Champion. Stagehand: “Um, sorry, it’s not for me to decide. As far as I know, the only man on the card who the boss would give the private room to would be Seymour Almasy.” Kae’s smile crumbled into a grimace. Jonny Kae: “Seymour Who? How dare you! Do you know who I am? Jonny Kae!” The kid thought for a second, then his face brightened. Stagehand: “Aw! You weren’t lying! You are performing tomorrow. I don’t follow Professional Wrestling all that much, but the boys are all excited about your match.” Kae smiled again. Jonny Kae: “Yes. Well, I am, afterall a multi-time world champion. But, I re-iterate, is there any chance of…” The kid interrupted him excitedly. Stagehand: “I mean, The PTC Global Champion, here in Toronto? That’s awesome! Even I’ve heard of the guy. He’s a legend. You really scored a good match against him. The arena’s gonna be pumped! In fact, I’m sure the guys would really appreciate it if you could get him to come to the staff common room during the show, just for a few minutes. Then, maybe we might be able to put some pressure on management for your private…” Kae growled at the man, and slammed his fist onto the wall in frustration. Jonny Kae: “Seymour Almasy? Seymour bloody Almasy? You do realise there are another three wrestlers in this match? Forget your damn private room, and forget your friends. Next you’ll be asking for tickets to Killzone because I know Kris Carmicheal! Get out of my sight peasant!” The man staggered back, blinking twice, before shrugging, turning, and walking down the hall. Kae thought to himself that when he held some kind of political office, he would make people like that illegal. But for now, enough of politics. Enough of Seymour Almasy. And enough of everything else. Kae resolved to relax, watch the boxing bouts of the evening, and get good rest before tomorrow’s show. He brushed off his suit, and turned back towards the foyer. A new chapter indeed. ***********************************************************
