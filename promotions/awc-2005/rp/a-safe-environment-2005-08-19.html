A Safe Environment?

19 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 19/8/05 1. Changes? Some things never change…The way neighbours spy out of their windows to get the latest gossip on their strange neighbours or the way it always seems to rain when you least want it to happen. Minor things in perspective. Unfortunately, other things don’t change that aren’t so minor, sometimes even dangerous. Attitudes rarely change. Once an opinion is laid on a person minds, it becomes tangled in the thorns and difficult to lodge free. And another thing that doesn’t change – the way people never realise things won’t change. History repeats itself yet it’s remarkable how slow people are to catch on to this. There was Mussolini but people still were blind enough to trust Hitler, then Stalin, then Hussein. Even now we have corruption running rampant through Africa yet people still won’t learn. I suppose this goes hand in hand with being human… But it doesn’t stop there – at a more social level, people repeat themselves everyday. Lovers trust the persistently cheating partner despite the lies dancing in front of their eyes, parents trust their drugged up children who have no intention of giving up their daily fix. People don’t learn, change is slow sometimes unreachable. Hate never truly subsides. This will probably just be a pointless reminder to many. But maybe Paddy O’Shea has got the point this time… 2. Gonna Burn this City His hands are gripping the steering wheel with an incredible tightness. His breathe is thickening, forming a cloud in the car. Hate is suffocating him but somehow it’s still the air he’s breathing. Therefore it’s what’s keeping him alive. But that doesn’t make sense, does it? No it couldn’t but hate will help because he will hurt them but he’s going to need something to keep him going. He’s confused, a thousands images forming a blockade around his brain, stopping information go in and out. Yes he’s confused but one message stands clear. They must suffer… Danny O’Shea is sitting in the driver seat of his cousins royal blue Ford Mondeo. He glances at the speedometer for just a second. 105 going on 110 miles per hour. He can only take a second to look at it though – He’s roaring down a winding back road that seems sure to claim his life every time he comes near the next corner. Yet there is no fear in him now. Fear has passed now. All that’s left is a burning desire for retribution. Danny is wearing only a pair of khaki three quarter length trousers. He doesn’t have a hat on or even a proper pair of shoes – only beach sandals are on his feet. His hair is as scruffy as ever but in the dim moonlight the blond in it makes it look like his hair is on fire. His bare chest is rising and falling in alignment with the ticking of the cars clock. His face is menacing though. He can’t pull himself to ungrit his teeth. The vein on his temple looks fit to burst as it protrudes from his head. Danny squints for a second as he tries to make out an object in the darkness outside. He holds the gaze for perhaps another second then a moment of realisation flashes across his face. Then he viciously pulls the handbrake and slides perhaps sixty feet before coming to a halt. Danny bundles out of the car with a claw hammer in hand, the friendly yellow rubber handle dancing in the dark. Then he walks over quickly to a wreck of a car at the side of the road, every step seemingly exciting him more as the prospect of what he’s about to do becomes crystal clear. The car is a red Nissan Primera and Danny should know seeing as he had one. The car is on it’s back and it seems remarkable the chassis isn’t more distorted than it already is. Danny looks up the small road and clearly sees three figures running away in the darkness, their silhouettes mocking his efforts. It would seem the prey has gotten away. Then from inside of the darkness of the car comes the sound of movement. This is followed as out of the shadow; cast the frame of the window, comes a head. It’s bald but it would be difficult to know because of the blood that crowns his head. The man begins to crawl out through the window of the car as Danny moves closer towards him. Blood is streaming down his face in torrents Danny can see. The man is dazed and confused but there is no compassion for him from Danny’s point of you. Danny finally reaches the man and without any hesitation, plants the claw end of the hammer in the cranium of the mans head just as he is fully emerging from the vehicle. The man drops instantly, quivers for a second or two, then becomes still. Danny removes the hammer from the mans head. Blood has spurted across his face and with weapon in hand, he looks psychotic. Danny glances one more time at the man before walking back over to the car…there’s still more vermin to be exterminated yet… 3. London Calling Paddy O’Shea and his father are sitting side by side in a carriage of the London Eye. They have just reached the peak of their circle and are now gazing at London City in all its glory. It’s fitting Paddy thinks fleetingly, that if he wins at Battle of Britain, he will be as high up as he is now. The air up this high is delivering piercing blows to his face and despite it being mid Summer, Paddy shivers beside himself. 7 million people seem to be clustered around his ankles paying him homage, the massive buildings bending in respect. Is it possible for a city to be a precursor of things to come? As Paddy goes back down again and the meandering River Thames curls around him, the unrepentant Fenian bastard tingles as he wishes this to be true. “It is beautiful ye know da…” Paddy whispers, his eyes never leaving the sights before him. Danny O’Shea who in comparison is far less impressed rolls his eyes mockingly. “It’s no’ beautiful, it’s filled with some o’ the greediest lowlifes ye’ll ever kno’. They are the bane o’ the modern world.” “But the building-“ “The buildings were built with English hands. The same hands tha’ took the bread from us durin’ the famine an’ let us starve,” Danny snaps at Paddy with a little more bite than before. Paddy stays silent and allows his father to have the final word. They wait in silence until the ride stops. Then the two jump out. Danny hurries away, obviously unimpressed but Paddy takes one more glance up at it with a trace of a smile on his face. I’ll be ridin’ ye again big fella come the Battle. Yer country may no’ be mine but they’ve left the door open t’ take me opportunity. Paddy then turns and rushes to catch up with Danny. By the time he reaches him they are standing just outside the grounds and on a pavement. Paddy is wearing the same attire as usual – baggy combats, a white vest and his leather black hat. There is an odd glaze in his eyes of admiration that Danny has spotted and does not like. He is frowning at Paddy without knowing it and sighs for his son – if only he would learn, not everyone is quite as welcoming here. The two haven’t been standing together in silence for two minutes before a large open topped bus pulls up. “Right son, get on” Danny says to Paddy causing him to turn around in confusion. “A tour bus? But ye hate London why-“ “Because ye’ll be riding in one o’ these things on Friday and ye need t’ get used this city mighty quickly. Some o’ these wrestlers live in England and will know every inch o’ London. They’ll know every street, every bend, every loose cobblestone. Tha’ is an advantage they have, so now ye need t’ eradicate tha’.” Paddy looks at Danny with his hands jammed in his pocket. He looks almost like a child completely lost in the pointless lecture of his father. Or maybe just what he perceives as pointless. Paddy only shrugs however. He doesn’t see any harm in a little tour; in fact he’s sure it will be fun as long as it isn’t converted into a match training. He’s had enough training these last few weeks keeping away from Hate to last him a lifetime. The two hop onto the bus, Danny first and Paddy second. Paddy hears the murmur from the conductor to Danny who is paying both fares. Danny walks up the second set of stairs to the upper tier. Paddy follows behind and throws a warm smile to the conductor. However, Paddy has already turned before he can see the sneer that has been returned. The two take seats near the rear of the bus. It’s in immaculate conditions, what with the sparkling metal bars and first class foam seats. All seems well on the big red bus for Paddy. And he’ll be fighting on one of these things? Ha, it’ll be a pleasure! The bus takes off with a jolt and begins to pace down the street. “Paddy,” Danny says softly into Paddy’s ear to remain unheard to the others seated near them. “It be vital ye kno’ tha’ just because yer fightin’ in a bus doesn’ mean yer goin’ t’ be safe.” Paddy raises an eyebrow to his father and examines his lined face to make sure he’s serious. “Well, aye kno’ tha’. Aye mean, aye’ll be fightin’ four other people-“ “Aye didn’t mean just themins. Paddy…ye’ll be on a bus, in traffic and there’ll be people all about ye. It’s vital ye know tha’ these people, these British are no’ yer friends.” Paddy turns and faces the front then sighs. It seems he will in fact have to have the same lecture that always seem to come up on the mention of the word British. “Patrick, don’t act like tha’. Ye need t’ kno’ tha’ yer enemies are everywhere! Complacency costs fights and titles. Look, it’s important ye win this fight for-“ “The glory or Ireland and t’ send a message t’ the British tha’ their crimes won’ go by unforgotten. Ye already tol’ me this!” Paddy replies louder than intended because of the frustration he’s feeling. “Lower yer voice! Yes it’s fer the glory of Ireland but it’s not just tha’!” Paddy looks at Danny quizzingly. Usually, after the glory of Ireland, the conversation ended. What else was there to it? “Ye need t’ win because if ye don’t, tha’ bastard Hate probably will an’ if ye have somebody like tha’ with tha’ much power, ye may never see a show again where ye aren’t jumped and hospitalised. Ye need to win t’ make a stand against Hate because if he ever thinks he can beat ye, he’ll just come back and back fer more. Paddy, ye don’t understand, this is yer chance t’ end it…t’ end him even.” Paddy stared directly at his father as he spoke every word, mesmerised. It made sense…this could be a window of opportunity to end this madness that was eating up his life. This madness that was forcing him to phone his home twice a day to make sure his family weren’t dead or the madness that made him jump when he heard an unusual noise from behind him as walked down a street. But now he could it! “And don’t forget everybody will be against ye, including the Brits!” added Danny. “Ah da!” Paddy replied. He was getting exasperated at Danny’s traditional views. Things had changed - can’t he see that? 3. The End To The Madness? Bobby is shivering in the open air. He shouldn’t be shivering because although he’s standing outside, he’s adjacent to a huge fire, watching his whole life burn away. Bobby was born in 1952 making him 27 as he stands in this field. He was born to a couple called Amelia and Jonathon. They were travelling people and so cast to the fringes of society i.e. forced to locate to a field outside of every town they stopped at. Back then in the 1950s, that was the way and they accepted it with heavy hearts – yes they loved to travel but they also secretly desired to be midst the fun in the cities. It seemed however that Bobby’s generation was the one that broke the mould. All over the country young gypsies were rebelling against the discrimination they were suffering over the country. No bars, no restaurants, no houses? Fuck that! Every country had its problems but in Ireland it wasn’t black and white. When Amelia gave birth to their fourth son, Bobby in 1952, it was snowing outside. Amelia had described Bobby as a ‘baby Jesus’ and he stroked his cheek with one finger. This gentle stroke was a mainstay in his life. Even as he got older, the little finger would turn even the gloomiest mood on its head for him. It was a loving home and he found it difficult to leave when the time came for him to move on. On the advice of his uncle, Bobby moved to England. He got a trailer caravan and an old car in the same deal around 1972 and began to travel around England. He thought he had escaped the discrimination and for a long time it seemed he had. The English didn’t see him as gypsy, just Irish. And that was fine with him. He’d always wanted to be considered Irish by someone, not like some kind of vermin rodent. Of course he had a few problems with conservative English in the 1970s but people were preoccupied with other things, they left him alone and Bobby was happy. Then one week ago Danny O’Shea, his cousin, came to stay with him. He was glad to have him over with him. He had a girlfriend now at this stage (an Italian girl who must have thought living in a caravan was the height of fashion) but she didn’t speak a lot of English. Lack of proper social contact was annoying him greatly so when Danny mentioned in a phone call he was calling over to London to check for work on the roads, Bobby was only too happy to give him accommodation for his stay. Bobby hadn’t seen Danny since he was 14 and the other was 12. He remembered a real rascal and that had unnerved Bobby because although he hadn’t had much trouble, he was sure that was only because of the fact he kept his head down. Danny wasn’t like that as far as he knew. However, Bobby pushed that out of his mind and all was well. Danny arrived soon after. Bobby was taken back. He was taller obviously but he had big broad shoulders and was built like a tank. But he still had that devilish smile and that helped ease Bobby’s nerves. For that week there, Danny was a star. He cooked and cleaned and was a genuine asset at the place. Bobby was even considering asking him to stay permanently before that night. And now Bobby’s staring as his caravan is burned to ash. Now he has to find out for sure, who’s to blame. Bobby is staring teary eyed into the inferno when Danny arrives in the car. He was crying a lot earlier when he heard the screams of his girlfriend from inside the caravan but now he’s cried himself dry. There’s an unidentifiable weight on his stomach causing him to feel sick at the pit of his stomach. Of course he won’t throw up anymore, as the puddle of vomit near him is testament to. When the Mondeo comes to a halt around ten metres from him, Bobby almost trips over himself as he runs to the drivers’ door. This is Danny’s doing. Aye took him inte me home an’ this is his doing! Bobby clenches his fist as his hand skims the cool bonnet of the car. But a sight makes him stop dead in his tracks and his hand falls open. There on the driver seat of the car is Danny O’Shea, staring completely forward, his eyes half scared, half mad. His hands are gripping the steering wheel so tight that his knuckles are white. But that isn’t the worst part. The worst part is the blood splashed over Danny’s body and face and Bobby has to take a step back to make sure this is in fact his cousin. “…Danny?” Bobby whimpers. He had meant to sound assertive and strong but in light of what’s before him, he’s finding immense difficulty in doing so. Danny doesn’t answer. Instead, he puts a shaking hand on the door handle and pulls it until it clicks. Then he pushes the door slowly and it creaks open. Danny slowly gets out of the car and walks past Bobby without even a word. Bobby was prepared to be sympathetic but now he’s ignoring him? Suddenly Bobby has his voice back. “DANNY, AYE’M TALKIN’ T’ YE!” Screams bobby, his face the spit of an angry beetroot. Bobby actually doubles over he shouts so loud. But still Danny ignores him. Bobby looks at Danny in amazement. This man that was nothing but an angel all week and a model guest is now ignoring him after he caused this? Bobby looks inside the car and sees a bloody claw hammer. Looks like he’ll have to beat answers from him. Bobby grabs the hammer and runs at Danny, his huge plodding steps just barely audible over the crackling fire. Just as Bobby draws near and raises the hammer over his head, Danny veers around and grabs him arm with lightning fast reflexes. He doesn’t look angry but the look in his eyes instantly drains the adrenaline from Bobby’s body. “Drop it,” murmurs Danny. Bobby does so and then just stands there looking at Danny’s face. “Aye kno’ ye think aye caused this but aye didn’t. Aye swear on me mothers’ eternal soul tha’ aye did no’.” Bobby looks at Danny for another few seconds then drops his head. If only he was a stronger person he thinks. Another few teardrops fall from his eyes as the magnitude of what’s happened tonight hits him once again, thick and fast. A girl was burnt alive tonight. He didn’t love her but she was something to him. She accepted him even if she didn’t understand why it was socially wrong. She was gone and he’d never find anyone who would accept him the way she did again. “Look Bobby…aye was walkin’ through the park and tha’ group followed me. They were shoutin’ things like get home an’ stuff and pullin’ out knives so aye led them away fro’ here. Then, once they got tired o’ following me, aye rushed home. Aye jus’ didn’ think tha’ they would still be followin’ me in a car…how could aye have known. The traffic was busy…aye jus’ didn’ think it would be them.” Danny says, losing his voice at the end of it as he looks at his cousins broken face. He’s lost everything because of Danny. “So where are they?” Bobby murmurs, his head still turned towards the ground, only looking up at Danny when he can. “Don’ worry bout them. They’re dead…all dead.” Danny states. He says it almost as some kind of consolation…it’s not for Bobby however. “WHAT? Ye killed them? The cops will be everywhere! This is me home, this is where aye live. How can aye stay now, eh?” Bobby shoves Danny in rage but Danny refuses to retaliate. Instead he stays completely calm. “Those pigs killed yer girl an’ burned yer home. They deserved t’ die. They be jus’ like every other Brit. Blood thirsty bastards. Aye should have listened t’ me dad!” “Brits are blood thirsty? Says you with the blood all over ye!” Bobby shouts back with anger in his voice. “They were good t’ me. Better than them over there were so feck ye and feck yer parade o’ justice! Yer just as bad as themins fer killin’ them like tha’! Now get out! Aye don’ want t’ see ye ever again. Aye’ll have t’ clear up this mess and bury an innocent girl. Ye can just walk away. So go! Take the car and go!” Danny waits a split second to check to see if Bobby is serious, sees that he is and walks away. He jumps into the Ford Mondeo and drives off quickly without looking at his cousin once. All that was left for Danny now was a deep-seated hate for Britain. He hasn’t heard word of his cousin since. 4. History Repeating Itself Paddy and Danny are now walking through a park. They have left the tour bus and had entered this park around two minutes ago in silence. Paddy had demanded this because of Danny’s persistent preaching yet Danny isn’t finished. He isn’t satisfied because he knows his message that Britain is his enemy has not sunk in yet. But he has an idea. This is the place Danny thinks this to himself as he remembers the terror that gripped him when he first met those four men, just under thirty years ago. It was here they had spotted him and followed him. Then they had burned Bobby’s caravan, killed an innocent girl. When they heard her screams they fled and Danny pursued and killed everyone of them without any remorse. Across the street from the park’s exit they are now drawing near to is a small bar. Danny instantly recognises this place as the place the men must have been drinking at on that night. And he believes that bar breeds a culture of hate for outsiders…maybe a first hand experience will convince his dreamy son. Danny instantly sets off for the bar leaving Paddy behind. Paddy has to half run to keep up with Danny’s long strides. “Where are we goin’?” “Just for a quick drink over here. Just be careful, they don’ like Irish here.” Danny murmurs. When he hears Paddy sigh, Danny smiles slightly. Is he going to be in for a shock or what? The two enter the bar and a small bell above the door greets their arrival. The bar has about twenty people in it at the moment because of the time of day. Yet when every head turns around and looks at their strange attire, Paddy suddenly feels like every set of eyes in Britain are looking at him. “…hello?” Paddy whimpers hopefully at the men. Yet no one says a thing. The first thing he hears however is a shout from the back corner. “Get out you terrorist bastard!” Paddy looks at Danny confused at the current situation. Danny however offers him no help and simply points over to an unused table that’s actually surrounded by the bulk of other drinkers. “Aye get the drinks, just go over and take a seat.” Paddy nervously walks over to the table that Danny indicated, very conscious of the way he’s looking and walking. He hears a few shouts from the corner such as ‘IRA bastard’ and ‘Irish scum and a definite ’go home’. The men are still looking at him although most seem to have lost interest and have returned to their drinks grumbling. Paddy takes the seat and looks over to his father. He notices that there seems to be a bit of trouble over there though. It seems the man won’t serve the drink but he can’t actually hear any voices at the moment. “I said, I don’t serve Irish now get out!” Paddy stands up suddenly but immediately sinks back down as he feels something hard break over his head. His vision becomes blurry for a few seconds as lights flash in front of his eyes, then he drops down to the ground as everything goes black. When Paddy wakes up, he is sitting on a bench in the park with his father beside him. For some reason his father is smiling. “Sorry aye had t’ do tha’ son.” Danny says, having difficulty keeping himself form laughing. Paddy doesn’t respond, he’s too sluggish at the moment to even hear Danny’s words properly. He runs his hand over the top of his skull and feels a large lump. He winces as he pushes it slightly. “Wha’…happened?” Paddy murmurs in a very dozy fashion. “Well son, ye wouldn’t believe me about how people felt over here about the Irish so aye had t’ show ye. Hopefully tha’ snooker cue breaking over yer head will wake ye up a bit.” Paddy contemplates what his father had just said for a few seconds. “Okay son, time t’ go. Ye need t’ get home t’ rest fer the battle.” Paddy stands up and walks a few paces behind him, rubbing his head as he walks. Then almost absent mindedly, he says to himself… “But tha’ was only one place, there can’t be hate everywhere in this city…can there?” END
