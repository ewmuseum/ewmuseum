take this to your grave

22 June 2006

Date: 6.22.06 Where does life go? Its here for a blink and then it's gone just as quick. Perhaps I'm not the leading expert in the field of twenty-twenty wisdom on the particular subject, as at age twenty-six my life was still little more than a budding seed. I remembered being young as if it was a dream that you sometimes have and then spend minutes, or even hours in frustrating thought trying to tap back into. In some ways it was like a movie, something I was caught up in for a short time but it wasn’t real in the end. But I knew much better than that. I only had to let a few fleeting memories of childhood blow through my weary mind to catch a small, yet powerful jolt of sadness or joy. A reassuring hug from my mother or the death of a dear friend. No, I was no expert on the subject of life, not by a long shot I suspect. But I did know that at this very moment that my adolesance, namely my teenage years, seemed a million miles away. I didn’t even feel like the same person anymore. I wondered if this was just a part of development and hoped that it wasn't that I had lost part of myself. As a tear finally managed to escape from my determined damp eyes, however, I remembered all to well that I had. I knelt down on my right knee and then my left, letting my heels prop me upright as I felt that I may just fall backward if left to my own strength. I had come to remember, and yet at this moment in time I would have given almost anything to forget. I opened the left side of my faded, brown leather jacket and pulled a small bottle of vodka from the inside pocket. Part of me felt impending relief as I unscrewed the lid and brought the bottle to my lips. I stopped just before the alcohol kissed my lips and slowly let the bottle down to the freshly cut grass below me. I wanted this drink more than any other I supposed, but that was the easy way out. I had turned to the bottle too many times if I was honest, and I was feeling honest on this day. I lifted the bottle up a little and tilted it, allowing the clear liquid to pour out and soak into the soil. I slowly screwed the lid back on and let the bottle disappear back into the inside pocket from which it came. I looked at my hands, incased in black leather gloves, and immediately stripped them from both hands, resting them to the left of me on the ground. I let my hands caress the soft, plush grass of this place. The cool blades felt good on my tired hands, tired from driving all night and into the next day's afternoon. It's amazing how you can forget, or at least convince yourself that you have. But I hadn't forgotten. How could I, I thought as I pulled a few pieces of grass from their roots. I let the pieces casually fall from my hands as I watched them spiral and dive silently back to the earth. It had been ten years since I last visited this place, whether it was nine years too late or too soon, I hadn't quite decided. I remembered that day, the twenty-second of June, like a bad dream that played on a loop in my head. I guess a person doesn’t forget a thing like that. I recalled the busting glass and the sickening smell of a cocktail of engine fluids. The attempt in vain to shake my friend from his sleep and the man who told me the hard truth. The heat that burned my skin as I stood by the fence and the ironic hour spent in the freezing air-conditioned cruiser's backseat. The men with the body bag and the hollow look in his father's eyes as I walked through their front door. All I could say was sorry, and through my indescribable grief and readily flowing tears I'm still astounded that I managed even that short sentiment. Backing out of my position to speak, unable to control my emotions for any amount of time. Watching as a stranger closed the lid as I saw him for the last time. The sound of the crank locking the casket that still wakes me in the early hours of the morning. The rain that poured from the cloudless sky as his body returned to the earth from which it came and the soul shattering look in his mother's eyes. I remembered a lot about that day and the days that followed. But, oh, how I tried to forget. I still try to forget. But perhaps today it was best to remember, or better, his due reverence. He was much more than a friend, he was a brother and I loved him. His memory beckoned my tears and demanded my sorrow and I would willingly lay down my armor and be pierced by remembrance on this day. Yes, the morrow would bring me troubles, opponents, and another day to tread this long road that is life, but somehow, time had stopped at this place. I bent forward, almost losing all sense of balance as my head spun from my emotional state. My tears flowed down my cheeks, over my trembling lips, and to my chin where they dripped onto the ground. I steadied my hands on the soft ground and slowly lowered my face to the small, golden marker that told the world who I mourned and could never forget. I kissed the marker and let the coldness of it cool my forehead as I wept tears that needed to be spent. He was remembered.
