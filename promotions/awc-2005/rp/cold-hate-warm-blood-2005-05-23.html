Cold Hate, Warm Blood

23 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 2005 The chill of the night is unnatural for spring. The snowflake-shapes that slowly creep over the windows out of the brisk night air cover the panes in gray, and the few people moving on the outside have to suffer the biting cold with every gulp of treacherously fresh air. The chill and the dark wash over a thin, spider-like shape on the top of one of the more rundown buildings on the block, a three-story monstrosity that never even had hope for itself or the people who built it. Now officially abandoned, it serves as a meeting place for mixed riffraff and a place to spend the night. It also serves as an unofficial headquarters for the Cult of the Fifth Horseman. The creature on top of the building is one of the senior members of the Cult, some of the very first men who responded to Hate and took his message as their own. But for the being now known as Azagtoth, merely believing the message wasn't enough. The shunned, quiet boy from downtown Chigago grew out of his shell within two weeks and soon became a depraved servitor of Hate. Christened Azagtoth by his self-chosen master, the already pale young man has gone with his face painted like that of a corpse ever since. Now, he sits on the roof of the building, huddling under a black cloak, strands of raven-black hair contrasting the white of his face. In his hands he feels the mass and shape of two bricks, stones which he chose earlier from a construction yard nearby. So far, he hasn't hit anything, but came close. It would only be a matter of time until a suitably slow target walked past the building and he could not miss. The clang and the bang of a metal door barely startles the man, but the thuds made by a heavy pair of boots make him draw back from the ledge and straighten up somewhat. He looks back to see Hate, in his trenchcoat and jeans, grinding away some chips of concrete with his combat boots absendmindedly while staring at Azagtoth, still holding his bricks. Hate's bald head is no longer violently red, but the calculating look in his eyes barely covers up the welling lust for violence inside. Hate cracks his neck, creating a noise which almost hurts Azagtoth's ears in the silence, but luckily for him Hate breaks that silence. Hate: 'Blessing passerbys again, I see. You realize, Azagtoth, that people might think you a psychopath for doing that?' Azagtoth turns his head away from Hate and looks into the black horizon, where nothing but imagination separates the earth from the sky. Then, slowly and with rasp in his voice, Azagtoth speaks out his thoughts. Azagtoth: ''Bleak the atmosphere, freezing warmest tears. The Earth a ball of ice.' Azagtoth laughs morbidly before continuing. 'Bodies frozen cold, glaciers of heart and soul. All warmth has long since died.' Their blood would be more than enough warmth.' Hate shakes his head and looks impassively on as Azagtoth draws his arm back and flings one of the bricks off of the roof, hitting a scrapped car on the other side of the street and smashing a dent into the roof. Hate: 'How disappointing that you should choose to spend your time concentrating on such meaningless drivel. They are empty words about empty hearts, and meant for the empty heads. I sincerely hope you do not believe yourself to be in this category.' When Azagtoth turns around, his posture is slumped over as ever. But his voice now drips with hidden poison, and a sort of malice creeps over his sharp-featured face as he eyes up his leader. Azagtoth: 'Well, my leader, I do not consider myself empty. I consider myself full from the lust and will of the battle. Something you seemed to lack this past week.' The hint does not go unnoticed by Hate, who frowns and straightens his back. Even though he is not much shorter, Azagtoth is dwarfed by the heavily muscled frame of Hate, and he immediately changes his tone to a more pleasing one, driving the hint of challenge away. Hate: 'I am not fully sure what you mean, my dear Azagtoth. Do indulge me in this personal amusement of yours.' Azagtoth: 'My leader, I speak of the travesty that was this despicable 'Zero To Hero'-meeting. Why do you allow yourself to be degraded so? Why do you allow those men to tell you what to do and how to fight? You looked like a fool, playing by their rules that night. It made me cringe.' Hate's face does not reveal his emotions, and Azagtoth keeps himself a safe distance away, fingering the brick in his left hand and trying to size up his chances of survival, which are looking rather slim at the moment. Then, Hate lifts his hand up and shakes his head again, this time while looking at the rubble beneath his feet. Hate: 'It does amaze me, Azagtoth, how you can speak like a wise man and an idiot at the same time. True, I did allow myself to be made a fool. But the man who made me look like a fool is a fool himself: did you not see the way he ran away from me? The way he FEARED me? All these things to avoid facing me directly, head-to-head, and he managed to steal what they call a pinfall. True, Hate lost, but I was not beaten. What you consider a loss, I merely consider a beginning. Beginning for something good. And this coming Tuesday, I will show 'the people in the TV land' just who they are laughing at. I will wipe the smiles off of their faces... And off of yours, if the need arises.' Azagtoth gulps rather loudly and then lets the brick slip from his fingers and onto the floor, which Hate notes down with an approving nod. A wave of relief washes through Azagtoth and he walks closer to Hate, holding his hands out in front of him in a submissive gesture. Azagtoth: 'Ah, how does the One-Eyed One bless you with such wisdom, my leader. Once again, I am humbled by your graces. Thank you for showing me the error of my ways.' Hate motions Azagtoth to the side with a flick of his wrist, and the ghoulish man moves obediently. Hate steps up to the ledge of the building and stares over the other houses nearby, into things he only can see. For a brief moment, the thought of giving him a slight nudge on the back, a slight tap on the knees to send him over the edge crosses the mind of Azagtoth, but as quickly as it came it disappears. The chills running up his spine tell Azagtoth what would be the real result of such an attempt. For a fleeting second, Azagtoth can see his brains splattered on the sidewalk in a crimson rose pattern, next to his lifeless body, and he quickly shakes these thoughts away. Azagtoth: 'My leader, may I inquire... Did you send the girl home?' For a moment, Hate stands still, looking at whatever he sees, and then turns towards Azagtoth, who to his surprise sees a slight smile curl the lips of Hate in a disturbing fashion. Hate: 'My little Angel? No, she is sleeping now. She was exhausted, and I do not blame her: Revelations and Falling from Heaven are hard tasks to accomplish. But she is mine now. There is no need for her to worry ever again.' A disgusted look wrenches Azagtoth's whole presence, and he has to battle himself not to give the nausea away. This apparently goes on unnoticed by Hate, who merely turns away again. In Azagtoth's mind, he devices hundreds and hundreds of ways for this whore to die, over and over again. For Azagtoth, the horrible little wench means nothing but trouble. But her time would come, eventually... Hate: 'How are the arrangements for my trip?' Azagtoth: 'Oh, yes, my leader, they're all ready and done. I checked them myself. You will not have to worry about them.' Hate: 'Good. You see, Azagtoth, how useful you can be. You merely stray away from the right path from time to time.' Even though the words are cold and machine-like, spoken to a dog by his master, they fill Azagtoth's twisted mind with joy and pleasure. Azagtoth smiles, a tight, dry smile which reveals his canines, but Hate does not wait for his response and turns away again. Azagtoth: 'My leader, please indulge me... Are you still planning on going back to that place? They did call for you, but I did not tell them of your whereabouts. Just in case you want to, I have my firebombs ready... They'll never know what hit them.' Hate turns completely around, suddenly face to face with Azagtoth. The goth backs away quickly, whimpering slightly before recomposing himself and straightening up, but retaining a wary look in his eyes and a tension in his body, ready to leap away at a moment's notice. Hate: 'Azagtoth, you idiot you. There you go again, suggesting things for me to do. You do not order me more than a fly orders a flamethrower. Remember that, my friend, or I will have to take matters into my own hands and make it an unforgettable lesson. Yes, I am going back. And why, you are about to ask. Simply because they give me a chance. A chance to hurt people. You should know, that any opportunity wasted to spread the flames of Hate is a transgression against me and what I stand for. I would not tolerate such behavior. The only good offense is a strong punch in the head. No questions asked, merely busted knuckles and bruised heads. That is what they invite me to do, and that is what I will bring them. Like it or not, the Fifth Horseman will be there.' Hate walks past Azagtoth, not further acknowledging the man with anything. Azagtoth looks after Hate as he steps into the doorway he used to come to the roof and slams the door shut, leaving Azagtoth into the dark, accompanied by the echoes of Hate's message in his mind.
