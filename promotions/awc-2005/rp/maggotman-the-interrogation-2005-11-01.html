Maggotman: The Interrogation

1 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/11/05 Detective Bach could feel his nerves reach the level of tension usually meant for violin strings. The back of his neck had been throbbing with pain for the last five minutes, the muscles spasming and knotting like no other. His face reflected the tensions in his back, projected the whole mountain of agony and suffering laid on his poor shoulders at the man sitting opposite of him. The suffering didn’t find anyone to receive it, though. Hate stared right back at the detective, their eyes locked in a duel of the minds that the officer was quickly losing… And that irritated him to no end. Throwing his hands in the air, Bach stood up from his seat, biting his lip before turning back to Hate, looking down the bald cultist. Bach: I’ll ask you, one last time… What is your connection to this woman? Hate: Dear sir. I have no earthly idea what you are talking about. Bach ground his teeth some more before slamming his fist on the table, a tactic he had found good and true after years as an officer of the law. But the bald, muscular freak that was staring straight at him didn’t seem to mind at all. If anything, he seemed amused. Bach had seen punks like this before, hell, he saw them every night. Little nazi pricks thinking they owned the whole world, and that white supremacy was the road to a stable life. Screw those kids. Bach knew where the stable life lay at: 10-hour workdays, busting his ass for a boss who maintained relations with Bach through mutual hatred, and then going home to a nagging wife and disrespecting kids. Kids like this bastard right here. Whatever hole he crawled out of, Bach was determined to break him so that he would crawl right back in there. Bach: Don’t play fucking smarts with me, kid. I’ve been in this situation before. You have any idea how many punks like you I’ve broken down before? You’ll tell me what I want to hear, you piece of shit, or otherwise you’ll walk out of here with less teeth than you originally came in with. Capice? Hate: Please, Mr. Brando, I am sure there is no need to make offers that I can’t refuse. You know I’d love it if one of us was to go out of here with less teeth in their mouth. Unluckily for you, it wouldn’t be me. Just a friendly warning, should you try something. And again! Again the bald punk had twisted Bach’s words against him and made him look like a freaking idiot straight out of a buddy cop movie! His hands clenching into fists, Bach walked around his end of the table, with Hate’s amused smile following him around. Hate was smiling because this was the most fun he had had for days, weeks even. He had almost forgotten what power felt like. Power over people. The idiotic police was dancing right the way Hate wanted him, with his over-the-top, good-for-nothing threats and poor man’s Lennie Brisco-routine. Power… This was it, at it’s purest and rawest form. Controlling followers and yes-men was power too. But to make someone so completely opposed to everything you stand for act in a way of your choosing… The mere concept was intoxicating to Hate. Risking a slight smile, Hate shifted his balance and then lifted his legs on the table, slamming the heavy combat boots down with a loud thud. Hate: Now, officer… The way I see it, you have asked me your questions. Now, correct me if I’m wrong, but to my knowledge that would make me free to go. You have ANY idea how many pigs like you I have met and left groaning in the gutter? You’ll let me go, or one of us isn’t walking out of this room. The threat did not find any soil to set in. Groaning, Bach held himself back from launching his fist at the arrogant bastard he was supposed to be interrogating. A single purple vein throbbed in his forehead, pulsating slowly like a great maggot, revealing the internal rage building up within the officer. He could feel his ulcer doubling in size due to the stress, but right now he didn’t care. Bach toughed out the fit of rage and then left the room, banging the door like a wife divorcing her cheating husband. And Hate laughed, for this only strenghtened his stranglehold over the situation. What a delightful turn of events. A few doors down the hallway, things were not going any better. Detective Reeves had for the last twenty minutes listened to rapid-fire insults hurled straight at his face. Not a single question had been answered. The kid hadn’t even stated his name yet. But Reeves knew well enough who it was: Trey Carmichael, alias ’Azagtoth’. Even now, the aberration in front of him made him puzzled. Black clothes, black hair, black lipstick… Yes, the makeup. A face so white it was practically painted on. Eyes and the mouth accentuated with black, turning his general appearance into that of a corpse. No, more like the mockery of a corpse. No corpse could stare at you with such intense hate burning in it’s eyes. Sighing, Reeves lowered his hands, which prompted Azagtoth to laugh in a high-pitched, crow-like tone. Reeves: Mr. Carmichael, I will ask you again. What is your relationship with… Even before the last syllable had left his mouth, the creature known as Azagtoth burst into laughter. It was a laughter devoid of human nature and soul, filled with whatever darkness lies between the tombstones at a graveyard. The eyes of the beast laughed with it, flaring up with surges of unholy hatred and mockery. Punching the little poet inside him, Reeves sighed and shook his head, just as there was a rap on the door. Reeves: Mr. Carmichael, we are doing this for… Oh for Pete’s sake, it’s open! The mention of the door being open shut up Azagtoth, and instinctively his mind formulated a plan of escape. But what good is escaping a room of one police into a station filled with a hundred? Content with things just the way they were now, Azagtoth spat on the table and crossed his arms, still laughing. Detective Bach stepped in, showing his obvious displeasure towards the young cultist with a sneer. Reeves: Ah, hey. Bach: Hey. The punk done talking? Reeves: He will be, as soon as he STARTS talking… Azagtoth: Release me. Bach: Like fuck we will. Joe, a moment? Reeves spread his hands and looked at Azagtoth for a moment, trying to find a flicker of weakness on the painted face, but to no avail. He stared straight into a lighthouse of madness, beaming at him with a wicked smile, teeth revealed. Shaking his head, Reeves went after Bach, locking the room after him in a manner that made it clear to Azagtoth that he’d stay there as long as they wanted… another threat that had no effect. Outside, Bach cornered Reeves against an empty desk. The expression on his face was a mixture of confusion and anger, and Reeves felt pity for his long-time friend and partner. If only he could openly tell him what this was all about… But no. Bach would never understand. He was a hard-line detective straight out of the 60’s cop movies: the ”eats-iron-and-shits-chain” type of guy. He didn’t have a soft spot in his heart for anyone, not even for himself. Reeves sighed as Bach opened his mouth, starting a verbal tirade in a voice that half of the whole building must have heard. Bach: Look, Joe, it’s like this: the big goon isn’t saying a word, no matter what I do to him. And obviously that little fucker ain’t talking either. Now, we’ve already held them for three hours, and it’s BLATANTLY obvious to everyone else but you that these aren’t the guys. Granted, I think we could keep them for the full 24 hours and see what kind of dirt we could find, lock them up et cetera, and believe me I’d love to do that, but we simply don’t have the time. We have got to let these bastards go. Reeves: And what if one of these is really Maggotman? What if one of these freaks cut up Jessica Torres, ate parts of her and kept the rest as sick trophies? How’d you feel if we let these guys go and one of them found another Torres tonight? You want to be picking up bone splinters tomorrow morning? Mentioning the Torres case clearly took the air out of Bach’s lungs, and he shut up, wiping the sweat off of his forehead. Reeves knew exactly how hard Bach had taken it: even though he didn’t care for people, a sick crime like that was always taxing, especially when you had to go through it without finding any suspects, finding any concrete evidence, FINDING ANYTHING… But Bach was right. The persons known as Hate and Azagtoth were obviously not going to talk, at least not about this case. Maybe they truly knew nothing, and Reeves himself admitted that the possibility of either one being a budding serial killer, despite their weird manners and appearances, was ridiculously small. However… He hadn’t talked to Hate yet. He couldn’t let them go before that. Reeves: All I’m saying is, let’s keep them in for a few more hours, pressure them REAL good, you know, show them that we mean business. Who knows, maybe one of them is on the verge of cracking up… Bach: Don’t bullshit me or yourself, Joe. I know these type of guys well. They know we don’t have anything against them. That just feeds straight into their bullshit ego and makes them impervious. They aren’t going to crack, not until we reach the point of breaking kneecaps. Now, I’d love to do that, but the Chief would go apeshit. So can’t we just let them go? Reeves: Oh, come on, Lucas. There’s NO way we can keep them for a bit longer? I want to talk to that Hate guy anyway. Maybe if we switch? Bach: Hell no. If you put me in the room with that even young little shit, I’ll strangle him in six minutes flat. I ain’t in the mood for any more bullshit right now. Just go talk to that bald fucker, and I’ll go have some coffee. Let the paintface rot in his room. Ah, to hell with this… Reeves was going to say something, but Bach had already gone. What use was there talking to thin air? Reeves stretched his arms and then made his way to the sergeant at the desk, that eternal paper-pusher. Reeves: Look… I’m going to detain those two for twenty more minutes, then you can pick them up. OK? Sergeant: Sure thing, Joe. Knock yourself out. Eyeballing the sergeant for his little quip, Reeves made his way back over to Interrogation Room #3. The room which currently held Hate inside. Breathing deeply a few times, Reeves grabbed the doorknob – and froze. What would he say? What would Hate say? What would prevent him from grabbing his gun and putting a nine millimeter piece of lead through Hate’s bald skull? Questions, questions. Another deep breath, a twist of the knob, and the door opens… Hate has been bored for the last ten minutes or so. Without a living soul to torture, he has prodded the world of philosophical thinking, without much results. Too much thinking to be done there. Too much anything to be done right now. Hate wants to get out, to breathe the night air on the streets and not to waste any time in this representative of useless society any longer. And then a victim presented himself. And Hate did smile, as he saw that it was good. Reeves sat down. Hate stared. Reeves coughed. Hate stared still. Reeves opened his mouth and spoke. And Hate stared. Reeves: Where is she. It wasn’t a question. It wasn’t a demand. It was a mere statement, which threw Hate off of his guard. Slowly, he lowered his boots off the table and leaned over it, staring at this new cop straight in the eyes. Hate: From what I understand… In a hospital, missing her appendages, waiting for a lifetime of not living her own life. But it was not my doing. Reeves: Forget Torres. I know you had nothing to do with her. I merely used it as an excuse to pull you in here. Honestly, the Feds will handle that case. I want you for something else. Hate: Prison romance was never my thing, even back in prison. You’re out of luck there too, pig. Suddenly, Reeves stands up violently, sending his chair skittering across the floor. Hate merely stares at him as he slams his fist against the table, not in a way to try and intimidate Hate but in a way that means he’ll use them. In a way Hate himself uses the same motion. There still isn’t any respect in Hate’s eyes, but the pity and loathing is also missing. Who knows, maybe he is a bit curious. Calm and sterile like a hospital room, Reeves speaks again, spelling every word as sharply as if they were made of razor blades. Reeves: No, I am not talking about that either. I am talking about another girl, a girl of just sixteen years of age. A girl who is out there somewhere on her own. I’m talking about Angelica Reeves. And I know you have her. Hate stares at Reeves, with wide-eyed amazement. The balls on this detective! Hate smirks and closes his eyes, crossing his arms behind his back. The muscles and the scars do not faze Reeves, who grows annoyed by the lack of response. Just as the mood in the room reaches boiling point, Hate speaks, his voice tired and uncaring. Hate: Never heard of an Angelica Reeves. Never seen one either, and never owned one. You must be thinking of the wrong person. I am sorry beyond all belief that your search has turned out empty. Reeves: You damn well know who I’m talking about. She ran away from home, and then YOU took her. Into that little ”cult” of yours or whatever. I know all these things about you, Hate. Hand her over so I don’t have to use this information. Hate chuckles, a reaction not wished for by Reeves. Hate opens his eyes and leans his face forwards, pushing himself closer to the detective. Hate: You know ”things” about me? Why, isn’t that splendid! You must tell me these things as well, then! What is my real name, or do you just prefer calling me Hate? Where did I grow up, or do you prefer to believe the ”Philadelphia, Pennsylvania” you have read on a wrestling promotion’s website? Who were my parents, have you talked to them, met them face to face? Do you know what I do with the Cult, or who Azagtoth really is to me, or indeed what I did with your precious little Angelica? No, you know NOTHING of these matters. You do not know ”things” about me. You know rumors, and lies, and deceit. All things I could spread personally if I felt like it. How does that feel for knowledge? Reeves isn’t listening. He hadn’t been listening from the point Hate mentioned Angelica. He knew it! He knew Hate was behind this! His little girl, his precious little angel running away like that for no reason at all. All Reeves wanted her to do was to show more effort in school, show more effort at home, show more effort at LIFE. And she ran away, like an ungrateful little brat, and sought out something like THIS. This smiling, laughing, violent maniac, she had found HIM and looked up to him as though he was the God allmighty in the sky. That wouldn’t do for Reeves. That would never do. He moved, and Hate moved. Reeves grabbed Hate by the wrist and planted his arm against the table, and Hate pulled his arm away. Hate stood up, and Reeves stared at him, anger filling his eyes and fear filling his insides. Fighting with a detainee was never good. And that would stop him from asking the questions he so burningly wanted to ask. So he asked, staring at Hate, fixing his every word into that bald skull. Reeves: Where… Is… She? You just mentioned her. I now KNOW you have her! All I’m asking is for you to give Angelica back to me. She’s young, she doesn’t know what she wants or does yet. Even you must realize that she is way too young to be listening to your… Teachings. Just, please, you must understand, I am her father and… Hate: Her father? Does that make you an expert on the subject of her? Do you know her deepest desires, her darkest secrets, the very enigmas of her own flesh? No, you do not. Again you claim to know things you possibly couldn’t. You speak of her as property, as something belonging to you. That is false. Anton LaVey stated that every man belongs to himself: that is how he laid down the root foundations for his Satanist faith. I do not subscribe to that school of thought, but I know I am my own master. That is where LaVey was correct. Azagtoth is his own master. Angelica, your daughter and my little Angel, she is her own master as well. If she is young, it merely means she has not learned all the lessons of life. It does not make her any less of a ruler of herself. True, it makes her a POOR ruler of herself, but a ruler she still is. And that is one government that cannot be overthrown with revolution, nor with a coup, nor with any violent political action. She governs herself: you have no such right. Reeves stares at Hate, dumbfounded. Hate’s tirade has left him speechless, unable to word a reply. There has to be a reply! Has to be! A bald cultist cannot win an argument over your own daughter, scream the voices inside Reeves’ head. And they’re right. No father should lose their daughter like that. But detective Jonathan ”Joe” Reeves is about to. The doorknob rattles and clicks open. Reeves looks back over his shoulder: 20 minutes already? Can’t be! Couldn’t be! The door opens. The sergeant. From down the hallway, ruckus. Brawling. Cursing. Bach is one of the ones screaming. What the hell? Sergeant: Joe, that pasty-faced fucker just went berzerk! Quick, we need people to restrain him until we get the straightjackets out! Hurry, for crying out loud, he already has Lucas bleeding on the floor! The staff sergeant ran off, and Reeves was about to do so as well, when a hand stopped him. Hate’s hand, laid across his shoulder. Reeves knew that whatever he would say, would be only to hurt him. But still he looked back, back into those abysmal eyes and that bald visage, looked… And listened. Hate: You are not her governor… But I am her leader. And you know what my little Angel does for me? Do you have any earthly idea? I get to taste her fruit whenever I feel like it, pig. The child you thought you raised so well, lifting her skirts up for me. I do not condone it, but the pleasures of the flesh are just that: pleasures. Why would one deny oneself from these pleasures? Now, go, restrain Azagtoth, with this thought in your head. See him squirming on the floor and think that it could be me and my Angel. Think, pig, and KNOW. For once, KNOW. Reeves doesn’t think. He swings and punches Hate straight in the mouth, before running off. His mind shuts down about everything he just heard: now he is merely going to stop a dangerous detainee from messing around. Again alone, Hate smiles, a wide wicked smile barely hindered by a busted lip. He licks the blood, and smiles even wider. The damage he caused was great. The damage he would cause should the pig come back would be greater. It all fit in the picture: and knowing this, Hate laughed, for he saw that it was good. All good. Cleanse the Impure. Raise the Warcry.
