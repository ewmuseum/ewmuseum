Failings

24 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Today ((Apologies to all for the low quality of this, but I’ve basically been in bed most of the week with a rather nasty cough/flu/bug. Writing this in a window in which I’m not coughing up a lung, as I didn’t want to no show. Cheers to all, and good luck.)) Thanksgiving, for most, was a joyous occasion full of food and frolic. Families united from all across the world on a quest to consume as much as possible and visit with people they hadn’t seen in a year. Such was the way it was for most people. Even most wrestlers, with hellish travel schedules, managed to find time to make it home for Thanksgiving. Seymour Almasy, though, had nowhere to go. He had no family anymore; no mother, no father, no wife, and no grandfather. He probably had extended cousins somewhere, but he’d never really tried to look. That all explained why Seymour’s Thanksgiving consisted of a Swanson frozen roast turkey TV dinner that he had microwaved in his suite. If he’d really wanted, he could have had a real meal prepared. But he didn’t. For while holidays were joyous times for most, Seymour mourned them. Holidays reminded him of Laura. She would always prepare glorious meals for the two of them, before they retired to the couch to cuddle. And even if a holiday didn’t remind him of Laura, it reminded him of Cid, his grandfather. Once upon a time, Seymour’s life had been full of happiness. Full of laughter. Now, he had nothing save for a career that the world was convinced he was clinging on to for far too long. Nothing save for the fact that, in a day or so, he was stepping into AWC’s most dangerous playground with a shot at the AWC Transatlantic Championship, or other fabulous prizes. Never mind that he, honest to God, for the first time in his career, thought that he couldn’t win. It was more than just the environment. Clusterfucks were difficult enough to win on their own. But this wasn’t a battle royale. This was a match taking place in a gigantic cell structure, with nine people. Essentially, one had to win twice to take the Transatlantic Title. He was up against eight hungry competitors. Eight competitors who would do what they had to in order to make Triangles the highlight of their 2006. Eight competitors who were nowhere near as thoroughly fucked up as Seymour was. Eight competitors who could give it their all without a thought in the world. That was more than Seymour could say. Annoyedly, he stopped poking at the TV dinner’s mediocre mashed potatoes and rose, crossing the room to take a look at himself in the mirror. His own reflection startled him now. The white hair dye he applied religiously was half faded, revealing traces of his own blonde locks beneath. He had a few days worth of black stubble on his face, and he generally looked like a man at the end of the line. “Heh,” he mused, shaking his head. “I should be dead right now. Stupid gun…whoever heard of being ready to commit suicide, pulling the trigger, and the gun having no bullets in it?” Some people would see that as motivation to somehow reinvent themselves. To dedicate themselves anew to a life that would now be full of meaning. Seymour Almasy was tired of clichés. What he was now was a broken down 26 year old man with a lot less left in the tank than he’d like to admit. A man who was chomping painkillers to keep competing in a sport that he now resented, yet was all he had left in the world. Triangles, then, would be another task in a long line of them. Perhaps one of AWC’s best would put Seymour out of his misery. With a sigh, Seymour returned to his TV dinner, shaking his head. The fact was, he knew he wasn’t quite that fortunate.
