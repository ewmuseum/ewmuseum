How to disappear completely

25 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9.25.06 It was time to go home. Darcy Markson had done enough hiding, as far as he was concerned, for several lifetimes. He hadn’t been anywhere remotely near his home in East Bay since before his Frontier Title victory at Coast to Coast. Positively terrified to be near the girl who tugged at his emotions with control that would make even John Malkovich blush, Darcy utilized the time spent away from home to pay visits to those near and dear to him. There was the unexpected stay to Marie in Crested Butte, of course – one that Darcy soon found that he wasn’t as good at covering his tracks as he liked to believe, as Amy Campbell had come to confront him yet again after their vicious falling out. A trip to the southern end of his native state to visit his parents, however, met with much better success for Darcy in avoiding his counterpart. Their meeting had been cordial, and relatively uneventful. They congratulated him on his shiny new belt he had in tow, inquired as to his thoughts on Marie’s new Colorado home, as they too had yet to visit it for themselves, and brought him up to speed on the various trivial happenings that one might expect to hear from two newly retired parents, finally free to enjoy the coming “golden” years after a life of toiling in the workforce. Curiously, the topic of Amy had been avoided altogether. Darcy certainly was remiss to bring her up, and refrained from doing so. But strangely, so did both his father and mother. Whether Marie had alerted them prior to his arrival that it might be a touchy subject for him, or whether they’d seen for themselves on AWC television just how sorry the state their relationship was…oh, hell. It was impossible to know for sure, and in the end didn’t really matter. If they knew anything at all, they didn’t let it on, which worked perfectly fine for a man completely unwilling to discuss the problem anyway. But living out of a suitcase grows tiresome, even for those who are accustomed to doing it more often than not. San Francisco’s East Bay also had a remarkable way of calling its native sons home if they strayed too far, for too long – Darcy had learned this in the jungles of Brazil, and in the perpetually snow-tipped Rocky Mountains, he was again reminded. And with Amy essentially cheating on him on live television, Darcy somehow guessed that she wouldn’t be returning to the house that the two had been sharing for the past several months. He needed to go home, and he needed to be alone – and while he certainly did not appreciate the circumstances that created this newfound opportunity, he was not too proud to take advantage. He’d been in the house all of thirty seconds before collapsing on the couch – his couch. Say what you will about material possessions corrupting the character of man, but there was something very comforting in the knowledge that Darcy was surrounded completely and wholly by things that indeed belonged to him and him alone. Save for one item, that is. A small suitcase adorned with wheels and a long, outstretched plastic handle immediately drew Darcy’s eye as he exhaustedly opened his eyes. The luggage didn’t belong to him…and only one other person could possibly have had access to his home since he’d left. Just then, the doorknob jostled, causing Darcy’s heart to skip a beat. He’d locked it behind him without even thinking, unknowingly ensuring that anyone intending to enter would need a key, or Darcy’s permission. And as it turned out, the intruder indeed had the former…and not the latter. *** ''Wh... I...'' She couldn't find words. After what she'd pulled on Fresh!, she didn't expect him to speak either. For once, Aimz was stunned stupid. ''Yeah, I live here, thanks.'' Darcy said, his words cutting sharp. ''What's YOUR excuse?'' ''I... I live here, too,'' she sighed, ''Alright, let's just skip to the fight... I honestly don't have the energy to do this...'' Darcy folded his arms. Skipping past the obvious barrier wouldn't be easy. ''One of the provisions of your living here, as my girlfriend, is that you aren't allowed to sleep with other guys. Believe me, I've no interest in screaming at you either, but at the very least, you need find the energy to convince me that you aren't in breach of said housing contract.'' She shivered, already looking on the verge of tears. ''Darcy,'' began the redhead, ''You know me. God, by NOW... you've got to know me. Do you honestly think I'd-- actually, no -- I'm not even acknowledging the idea. I don't HAVE to prove it to you, because you already know I'm not that low. I do a lot of things, but cheating isn't on the list.'' ''Well there's something else I thought you were above too, Amy.'' Darcy snapped back. ''That stunt you pulled on TV. Seriously Amy, what were you trying to accomplish? And if you didn't fuck that bastard Sloan, just what the hell WERE you doing?'' ''Think about it for a second. I didn't seem like myself, did I? And I didn't, because I wasn't myself... and I can't tell you exactly why without both of us ending up in the line to cash unemployment cheques.'' Darcy raised a brow. What? He quickly shook his head, though. It didn't really matter what motivated her to do it. Amy had recklessly and selfishly drawn a line in the sand, hoping to God that he would blink. He didn't. And now they were here. ''Alright, Amy...'' he started. ''I can think of...thirteen dirty words I could call you off the top of my head. But a liar isn't one of them. If you say you didn't do what I think you did, I believe you. But that still doesn't give you the right to put our problems under a spotlight for everyone to see. This isn't some 'Nick and Jessica' bullshit publicity-starved relationship, this is the real fucking deal.'' Amy couldn't believe that he didn't understand even slightly. Telling him what the company was telling HER would cause him to go into a tailspin and have them both out of work, washed up Shit Creek without a paddle, ring or dime. Not being direct hurt her, and she was already far too ashamed of her actions, but her silence would benefit them in the long run... by keeping a roof over their heads. ''Do you need glasses? Seriously... do I look at ALL happy to you?'' She didn't. It looked like she hadn't slept in a month. And Darcy guessed that in her prolonged insomnia, the smiles had come few and far between. This was normally the part of the fight where Darcy would relent, Amy would eventually start lowering her guard, and the two would realize they loved each other far more than whatever it was keeping them at odds. Indeed, Darcy felt the pangs in his stomach, trying to remind him that he loved the unhappy girl that stood before him. But he wouldn't let himself relent. He couldn't. The past few weeks saw both of them cheat death, Darcy's ex-girlfriend being kidnapped by Amy's friend, and a touch of domestic violence that Darcy still couldn't believe came out of him. To top it all off, Amy had decided to throw gasoline on the fire by putting it on camera. The roller-coaster ride of ''Fight-makeup-fight,'' as entertaining as it had been, had finally caused one too many jostles to the head. ''No, you don't,'' Darcy admitted. ''I hope because you realize that you've done nothing but make my life a living hell for the last month or so.'' ''And mine's been a smooth ride, right?'' Amy growled, ''Get over yourself and look at the big picture... if I could CHOOSE what happened on and off camera, I'd be a fucking champion with the sweetest little personality on the planet.'' ''Are you trying to tell me that you pulled that little stunt against your will?'' ''I'm not telling you anything directly. If you've got a feeling that someone else is cashing in on it... you might be right. Either way, it's not gonna stop,'' she said, ''We're both accountable for what we do, and we're not in a line of work that leans toward privacy.'' Darcy glared at her. This didn't make a lick of sense...not to somebody out of the loop, anyway. ''Amy, I'm going to ask you a question. And what ever answer you give me, I'll believe you. But be straight with me...'' Darcy took a deep breath, completely incredulous that he was about to ask her the words that came next. ''Is somebody gambling on our relationship?'' Fortunately for Amy, she wouldn’t have to lie her way out of this one. No one…at least, to her knowledge, was placing money on whether or not the two would be a pair for much longer. The redhead shook her head, and began half-heartedly offering a convoluted story as to what motivated her actions. At least, that’s what Darcy guessed; the faraway stare in his eye, toward nothing in particular, indicated that he wasn’t really listening. He could tell that while Amy wasn’t outright lying to his face, she also wasn’t being completely honest, either. And in the end, it was of no consequence. Nothing in the world could justify her actions, short of a gun being pointed to her head – and unless that gun had been pointed at her off-camera, then no deadly firearm could be to blame. Suddenly silence had overtaken the room, which Darcy took to interpret as Amy had finished babbling about whatever it was she had to say, and was awaiting a response. Hoping she hadn’t noticed he spent the last several moments perusing the finely-detailed pattern of his carpet, he gave the response he had planned, even if she had told him he had ten seconds to live before an asteroid wiped out every living mammal on the planet. “You know Amy, I just don’t care,” Darcy said, refusing to even look at her. “You crossed a line I never thought you’d cross, and it’s too late to go back now. You want to take this public? You want to turn us into a fucking soap opera? Well congratulations, because there just might be something to this little game you’ve got going. We’ll continue this conversation later…cameras and all.” With that, Darcy popped off the couch and headed for the stairs. He did his best to avoid her gaze, knowing it would make him weak – but his own eyes betrayed him, catching a glimpse and subsequently locking on to her own tear-stained pair. With just one glance, much was revealed; he couldn’t tell whether or not she would take back her transgression, had a time machine allowed her the opportunity, but she looked hurt. Not so much pained at how Darcy was treating her, as he’d been guilty of doing far worse, but pained at how she’d been treating him. Darcy again felt the pangs in his stomach begging him to rush to his girlfriend’s aid. But once more, he let his mind override his heart. He needed to be home. But he needed to be alone, as well. And evidently, the only opportunity he’d get to meet both ends…was in his dreams.
