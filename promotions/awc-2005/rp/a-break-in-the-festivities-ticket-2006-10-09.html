A Break in the Festivities - Ticket

9 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Oct 9 I sped past an old Corolla, pushing my shifter into fifth gear and stealing a glance at the clock as I approached other vehicles creeping ahead of me. I was pushing my luck for making the airport for Natalie‘s scheduled landing time, but I was trying everything to avoid that, and at the same time I completely ignored the potential consequences of my actions. The hot Californian breeze blew threw my car and upset my normally-settled hair, but this didn’t concern me. I turned up the volume on the stereo that pumped Modest Mouse through the speakers and checked my rear view mirror as I steered my 2000 Jetta in front of another vehicle. I cursed myself for sleeping in, kind of like this roleplay I’m writing now, as plans were interrupted by not setting an alarm and leaving my head buried in a pillow until thirty minutes until the deadline. With that being said, I checked my watch and noticed that the plane would be landing any time now, and pushed the accelerator into the floor, letting the engine do it’s work and in the meantime spew diesel fumes out the exhaust and into the car behind me, which I had just passed. Hopefully they had air conditioning, or they wouldn’t be pleased with leaving their window down to allow for some air circulation. I felt rather reckless, and, if anything, free on the open road. I was somewhat glad that Natalie wasn’t sitting in the passenger seat, as she would certainly be berating my aggressive manoeuvres and dangerous speeds. And she had a certain soft spot for the environment, and burning diesel at 80 mile an hour certainly wouldn’t agree with such an attitude. However, I could argue that my fuel-efficient vehicle was more environment-friendly than the monstrous SUV I just passed. I continued to move along the freeway, mindlessly keeping the speedometer over eighty miles an hour. As a Canadian writing a Canadian who lives in America, I feel rather idiotic for not knowing how to convert properly, but in homeland terms I’d say that’s about 130 kilometres per hour. The metric system is far superior to American’s way of handling their measurements, and I could go on a multiple page rant as to how utterly stupid the founders of the Imperial system must’ve been drunk when they conjured it up. It is quite possibly the most random established way of measuring anything. This was actually on my mind as I heard an unmistakeable siren go off behind me. I cringed and looked in my rear-view mirror, where the flashing red and blue lights of a police cruiser tailed me. I glanced at the speedometer out of instinct, but I already knew what I was being pursued for. I cursed angrily, looking at my watch and realizing that Nat would probably be furious for me not arriving and her having to take a taxi all the way across Berkeley. I slowed to a stop on the shoulder, and watched the cop do the same with his ride. He approached the window with that slow, pompous strut. Police knew how to walk in an intimidating manner, but that didn’t mean I was intimidated, so I rolled down the window and waited for him to speak. I could’ve greeted with a, “What seems to be the problem, officer?”, but I knew exactly what the problem was, so I kept quiet. “Do you know how fast you were going?” the officer asked. He had a moustache and a pot belly - insert joke about donuts and coffee. Did that rhyme? “About a hundred and thirty kliqs,” I threw at him, with a little Canadian terminology that he would surely trip over. “Eighty mile an hour!” he spat at me. Something told me he was a redneck, but I couldn’t be sure, as I wasn’t fully paying attention. “Interesting,” I responded. “Interestin’? Is that all? The ticket I’m gon give ye is a lot more than interesting’!” “I can afford a lousy speeding ticket. It isn’t a dent in my wallet. But what if, say, I were a man of great significance who could influence whether you’ll have a job tomorrow or not?” I threatened. The cop tried to say something, but fumbled with his words. “Well, I .. Er ...” he stuttered, trying to find something to say. As if he finally noticed my model of vehicle, he piped up, “You’re drivin’ a Jetta! Man of great importance my arse!” How the hell was this idiot even a cop? I thought. I merely shrugged at him. He wrote me the ticket. And that was the end of that. In a couple days from then, I would jump on a plane to Spain and get into the ring with DUI and battle it out in a tag team match. And then, a few days after, I would wrestle in a match for the Livewire title - my first match with a belt on the line thus far in the Atlantic Wrestling Club. Now, that’s saying something, as I had been in the club for many months by then. Had I accomplished much? Not really. I had my moments, but up until then, not a whole lot had stuck out besides my appearance at Zero 2 Hero, a couple of main events against AWC wrestlers that are now firmly in the Transatlantic scene. But I would concentrate on my tag match. Or try. I didn’t know my partner except for the fact I kicked his ass the week before to gain my title shot. And I didn’t know my opponents, either, which kind of left me to believe I hadn’t prepared well for such an event. One could say I was more concerned with the main event of Fresh!burst, which would determine my opponent for the Friday’s Untouchables. But I had to make a couple showings in the same week, and if I could garner a win or two out of the mix, I would be happy. The matches were just proverbial bumps on the journey towards ultimate success - much like the cop who caught me speeding.
