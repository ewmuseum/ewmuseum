The Fallen Hero

22 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 22/06/06 “I know what you think of me, you think I’m some ignorant and illiterate redneck in a suit. You have this belief that just because I shave my head and because I believe in the rights of the American citizen, that I am a fascist and a militant. It’s not politically correct to be patriotic anymore and it’s not acceptable to stand up for your beliefs either. However I’m not going to be swayed by the masses that form the PC brigade. Instead I’m going to stand strong in front of you all and I’m going to tell my story without flinching. I shall bring to you the truth of modern America and my vision for a future created by ALA. For you all may judge me, but you don’t have the first idea about who I am, where I’ve come from and what makes me tick. So for the benefit of those whose eyes are not open, this is my life and here is where it all began…” Curt Mitchell’s narrative had accompanied him as he walked slowly down the sidewalk of a typical peaceful street, in a typically peaceful suburb in the not always so peaceful state of Michigan. His dark red shirt was mainly covered by the black suit jacket and pants, where his hands were jammed into the pockets. He came to a halt outside a small home with a broken down fence that ran along the outside. Although the sun streamed down from above, the house itself was pitch black inside with no sign of life. Curt ended his monologue as he stopped and looked on at the property with an expression of nostalgia tinged with frustration. He sighed deeply and spoke softly as he gazed upon this homestead. “This is where you find out; it’s not all black and white.” ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- The Mitchell household approached the nineties with the same perspective as they had dealt with the eighties, through hard work, blood, sweat and tears. Tom Mitchell was the traditional patriarchal figure who ruled the home with an iron fist and a stern voice. He worked every hour that God sent to put food on the table and provide for his family, just like he had seen his own father do before him. The men of the Mitchell clan had always worked in construction and Tom was no different. With the new decade approaching, housing was increasing and the business world expanding. Tom had had high hopes for the future and that he might finally be able to do more than just ‘get by’ in life. Yet sometimes your hopes don’t come to anything, your dreams slowly fade and die. As Tom came through the door and set his tools to the floor, he saw his wife and son in the kitchen. He couldn’t imagine how he would tell them this but he had no choice but to tell it how he saw it. “Goddamn mother fucking, niggers!” Tom Mitchell stormed into the kitchen and his wife Diana, recognised the clomping sound immediately. She had been familiar with this angry tone and the fire in his voice, one too many times before. He was not a violent man to her but she had felt the full force of one of his verbal tirades. Whilst she did not always agree with his ways, she respected his beliefs and did not stand against them. This was one of those times that she knew she should combine a respectful silence with an interested few words. “What’s happened honey?” He pulled back a chair at the kitchen table and took a seat, banging his fists on the table as he did so. His teenage son, Curt, turned to glance at his father with caution. Unlike his mother who had never been physically punished, he feared the belt that was around his fathers’ waist because he knew the disciplinary whippings it could deliver. He realised however that it was all done for his own good and as a result, he felt he knew what it was to be a man already. Curt believed that his father had shown him the right way to live his life and how to be a good, strong American male in this ever changing society. He had seen his father angry out of passion for his beliefs however this was something else. “I’ll tell you what’s happened shall I?! Bob decided today that it was a time for a reshuffle in the team and he got some of us together. Me, Al, John and David, we were all called up for a little chat with our ‘good’ friend, Bob. He told us that he was ‘concerned’ about the increasing demand being placed on the four of us, given our ages and circumstances. Therefore he had come to the ‘regrettable’ decision that he had to let us go, not for the good of the company but for our own good. We might not be the youngest for God sake, but we have given the majority of our working lives for this job! Look at Al, he’s nearly sixty years old and admittedly not in the best of health, but he still gives his everything to the task in hand and busts his rear end for the company. As for me, alright sure I might have some cramp in my hands at times but I can still lift a load and operate the machinery!” It wasn’t just cramps, it was the onset of arthritis early on but Tom was in denial. He had watched his own father battle illness with dignity and continue to lead the family until his last breath. There was no way he was ever going to concede defeat to his own health problems because that was not the Mitchell mans way. So instead he just followed his usual routine and when times got tough, he would stuff his hands deep into his pockets and take a walk around the site until the pain felt bearable again. Nobody was any the wiser, or so he thought until today. Now though, his work was over permanently and his place already taken. “For our own fucking good, yeah like hell it was! You know why we were all given the boot? It was because of those fucking dirty niggers that have come into our town! These black men have turned up, sullied the reputation of our town, got their own way wherever they have gone and NOW they steal the jobs of good, hard working, honest men. My father and I worked hard for this country, to provide a living for our families so that we didn’t have to ask for a damn thing from our government! My family have lived here, we have worked here and we have given to this community for decades and for what?! For the politically correct to turn us out onto the streets in favour of immigrants who have been here for five minutes, given nothing and taken whatever they want?! They played the race card, the poor little black men who need help from those around them because they are too fucking lazy to help themselves! Take the food out of the mouths of good honest Americans and give it to the niggers that came here to make a good life off the back of OUR hard work!” Curt had seen this before, the tell tale redness in the face of his father. Normally that fury would have been directed at him for having spoken out of turn or done something crazy at school. However this was a rage directed at a group of people, at a culture and at a colour. For all the hard times they had experienced, Curt respected his father and the beliefs that he held. Tom Mitchell was a rock of a man and someone that his son looked up to, holding him in the highest esteem. Now though, Curt looked on at a broken man who was hiding that distress by lashing out at those who had taken so much from him. This was an unusual experience for the young man and it was making him uncomfortable as he was forced to watch this. “We held that construction company together, we were the foundation of it and we’ve lost everything because of this crazy nigger sympathy shit! I’m sick of it Diana, I’m absolutely sick of it, why should we give up our place in the world just because some coloured people come over on the banana boat, begging and pleading for work? Why should we have to lose everything just because the politically correct, ignorant and blind activists say it’s the right thing to do? I mean hell, didn’t they see what groups like the Black Panthers did? It’s bullshit, they’ve all been blinded by these niggers that have infiltrated and spread their crazy monkey views! I’m damn sick of it, it’s a disgrace!” Diana’s head hung low as she felt the pain and anguish in her husband’s voice. She was the traditional matriarch, so these fiery words were not ones she could extinguish, as she was not one to stand up and fight with the man of the house. It was a truly poor example to set her son but she didn’t know how else to deal with life, she just did what she thought was best. Whilst Diana could not look at her husband, Curt instead stared intently at his father, taking everything in. Tom rose to his feet and turned around, kicking the chair out of his way, no doubt visualising doing the same to one of his former colleagues. As he left the kitchen and stomped up the stairs, Curt recalled everything his father had said. It was true that his dad had worked all the hours that God could send in order to provide for his family. Sure maybe this son had experienced physical pain that he shouldn’t have done in an ideal world, but it was only because his father was trying to protect him from making the wrong choices in life. Curt knew in his heart and mind that he was being educated in the types of people that can take you for a ride, that can mistreat you and do you wrong. He was being shown how to be a strong man and how to take advantage of life, rather than have it take advantage of you. All that his father had ever done was teach him right from wrong and to make sure that the family had all that they needed. He began to question how fair it was that this hard working and proud man had been reduced to feeling this lost and angry. Curt wasn’t a fool and despite his teenage years he knew more about life than many adults who were significantly older than him. Now though, in watching his father being slowly broken by the world around him, Curt was beginning to learn even more about life. “I…I should go and see if your father is okay…” The nervousness in Diana’s voice was obvious as she had never been faced with this situation before. Tom had always been a ‘typical man’ and so the idea of him leaning on her was completely alien to them all. So much so that even as she said this, Diana still remained frozen to the spot, still standing at that table, still with that chopping board in front of her and vegetables to be prepared. As Curt watched his mother, he made a life changing decision, to go from being a child to being a man. “No Mom, I’ll go…” The walk up the stairs felt like a long one as the awkwardness of such a change in him emerged. This was to be his first encounter with his father on a man to man level and it felt uncomfortable and uneasy. He steeled himself as he came to his parents room and glanced through the crack between door and frame. Curt Mitchell never made it to his father for the sight that was before him left him numb and frozen. His inspiration, his rock and his leader, the patriarch of their home was sat on the parental bed, head down and shedding tears for his loss of status. His body was visibly shaking and his emotions were pouring out, albeit quietly. As Curt Mitchell watched on, his teenage mind experienced something it had never felt before, anger, frustration and hatred. His father had lost his strength and the things that made him proud, all because of… “Those fucking niggers.” Words that quietly escaped his lips for the first time in his life, they felt alien but at the same time he believed in those three words. They had hurt not just his father, but his hero too. Today was about change, change for his father, change for his mother and now for him too. Curt Mitchell was suddenly seeing a different side of life and now he felt like he was seeing the truth about these people. They had broken his father, but they were not going to break him. Somebody would have to pay for this. ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- “You still won’t understand but I accept that. You won’t see why it is that I have the Leviathan by my side and why I am about to take the stand that I am. You won’t want to believe what I tell you and you won’t want to trust the voice in your head that tells you I’m right. You won’t want to see that the control I yearn for is not because I’m a megalomaniac but it’s because I am doing what has to be done. You won’t want to see me as anything other than a racist and a white power sympathiser. You will want to portray me as a demon and the son of Satan himself but that will change. Leviathan will take you by the hand and lead you to me, lead you to the truth…You won’t want to see it…But you will have to…Times they are a-changing…You’ll see…You’ll see…” As his narrative slowly ends, Curt smiles to himself as he takes a final look towards the family home. He kisses his left fist and raises it to the heavens. “I’ll make you proud, Dad.” Scene fades out slowly to black.
