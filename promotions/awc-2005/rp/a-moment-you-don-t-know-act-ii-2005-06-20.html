A Moment You Don't Know: Act II

20 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 20 June, 2005 everything is hanging on this moment every action makes a reaction mae - the everglow Dream. London. It’s a quiet morning, and Ellis soaks it in. New York is never this quiet, this still. Ellis likes it, the silence. It rained last night, and it looks as though it will rain again. Ellis doesn’t mind, really. She’s never really minded the rain, even welcomes it, occasionally. She sits in the hotel’s restaurant, eating breakfast. A cursory scan tells her that there are three people behind her, four people in front. None of them look threatening at the moment, but that could change. Anything about this situation could change at any moment. She knows she is paranoid, but she also knows that paranoia will keep her alive. A waiter pours her a glass of orange juice, and Ellis sniffs it, just in case. It's a futile measure, really. Odorless, tasteless, colorless poisons are easy to get. Ellis wouldn’t know what hit her. But it’s a habit, and it can’t hurt. Her mission today is simple: get some papers from the Russian embassy. It’s something Ellis could have done in her sleep, but she doesn’t mind. She has been taking every mission she is free for, regardless of the size or significance, just to fill the days. Just for something to distract her. Chandler has new intel, a few more leads. It’s not much, or he would have given it to her already. Ellis should resent him for that, but she doesn’t have the energy anymore. She doesn’t have the energy for a lot of things anymore. She should be hunting down Duncan, perhaps. The man is responsible for getting them into this, for taking Logan away, but Ellis doesn’t care. She tries not to think of Logan, his smile, his presence, his life. It doesn’t work. Logan has always occupied -- will always occupy -- her thoughts. He’s everything she has left to live for. She can’t fail him. She can’t give up on him. They offered her a ride to the embassy, but she turned it down. Today, she wants to walk. Just because she can. Logan liked London. Ellis never really understood it, since he was always loved bright sunlight and clear skies. She thinks that maybe she should have asked him when she had the chance. Ellis sees Logan almost by accident as she walks through the London streets. He stands near the curb, seemingly waiting for a cab, blonde hair floating in the brisk wind and black coat allowing him to disappear into the masses, to fade into the background. Ellis almost doesn’t see him herself. For a second, he glances up from the street and looks straight at her. It takes her by surprise, and she has to fight her instinctual reaction to make a double take. He smiles at that, facing the street again, but doesn’t acknowledge her in any other way. Ellis was expecting some sort of communication. She just didn’t know that he’d be here in person. Then again, Logan always did like throwing her off guard, just to see what she would do. She does resent him for that, but it lacks bite. Ellis still hates him. She hasn’t forgiven him for anything. But it’s dulled, useless, now. She has nothing left in her to feel. All that disappeared when Logan did. The emptiness is better than the pain. It always is. The street is a crowded area, full of noise and people. Some notice him, some don't (even though he doesn’t want to be seen, not today, not here). To Ellis, he stands out, vibrant and deadly. He wanders through the crowd in an almost random pattern, but Ellis can see that he’s moving toward her. So they don’t know each other, then. Just two people on the street, who accidentally bump into one another. ('Oh,' he says, as if it were really an accident, mouth in an almost-smirk. She would just nodded and walked along.) They’ve never met before. The disk he slides into her pocket doesn’t exist. She’s not someone who craves his touch. When Ellis gets back to her hotel room with the papers (from the Russian embassy) and the disk (from Logan), she has the urge to take short nap, though she hasn’t done really done anything today. Maybe she’s just getting old. She dozes off on top of the covers after she’s taken her disguise off, after she begins to feel like herself again. She’s still wearing his socks. They’re grey, though darker than the clouds outside her window. The rain wakes her a few hours later. It’s not loud, just a quiet pit-pat, but Ellis wakes anyway. She’s not very surprised to see Logan there, watching her. A little, she has to admit, but she knows he wouldn’t just show up for a quick exchange of information. There’s too much risk involved for so little gain. “Hello,” she says, sitting up, stretching, breathing life into mistakes. She doesn’t ask how he found her or how he got in here. She doesn’t particularly care. It’s not like he’d tell her anything anyway. He smiles, though there’s something sad in his eyes, and she can’t help but understand. “It's all I have. Make good use of it.” He doesn’t mention that he’s talking about the disk and that the information on the disk is everything he has on their daughter’s disappearance. He doesn’t have to. Ellis nods, a slight tilt of the head. It might contain something, some lead that just might sound to familiar to the friend of a friend of one of her contacts. She doubts it, though. Ellis has always prided herself on being realistic. Only the barest of leads. Ellis guesses she can live with that, as it’s something. Not much, but still something. “What brings you here?” she asks. She thinks she knows already, but she’s not sure. She’s fully aware of how little she understands him. He studies her for a few seconds, before she meets his gaze levelly. She can’t read his eyes. “You,” he says. His frankness takes her by surprise. It’s probably the only time Logan has ever given her a simple, succinct answer, and his directness throws her off guard (again). She doesn’t know how to respond, nods, never tells Logan that she doesn’t love him, because she doesn’t like lying when she can help it and she doesn’t think he’d believe her anyway. “It’s raining,” Logan says, glancing out the window. His lips are quirking now, as if he is amused and he doesn’t want to show it. Of course, Ellis knows that Logan never shows anything he doesn’t want to show. She envies his serenity sometimes, when her control comes close to breaking, when she gives herself away. “It is raining,” Ellis confirms. She’s not sure where he’s going with this, and she might as well let him carry the conversation. He did, after all, start it. She sits on her bed, back to the headboard. She never pulled back the sheets, and the comforter is only slightly rumpled. It seems right, somehow, to leave the bed the way she found it. Like no one was ever here. Logan watches her, and she still can’t read his eyes. This should upset her more than it does. “I was thinking of the first time you kissed me,” he says. His tone is wistful. It was raining that day, an unexpected downpour. Ellis doesn’t like to think about if she doesn’t have to. They were wet, she was young, and Logan was probably trying to manipulate the situation in his favor anyway. It was a good moment, one that she doesn’t want perspective on. She can keep it real that way, doesn’t want to know what Logan thought of it, thinks of it. So she says nothing. The memory is tainted already. No need to taint it further. He says nothing else, and Ellis is grateful. The bile was already beginning to rise in her throat, and it was taking everything in her to stop herself from snapping back. He knows exactly how to cut her. She sighs, and resists the urge to rub her temples. She feels useless. She should be doing something, anything, besides sitting here. She should be looking for their daughter at this moment. She shouldn’t be sitting here, staring out the window at the rain. But she can’t think of anything better to do. He holds her face and kisses her and despite everything that has happened over time, over years, she kisses him back. The kiss is slow and wet, heavy with sadness and something else that she’s not sure she wants to define. It’s a habit, she thinks, like sniffing a drink for poisons. Something you just do, because it’s too familiar not to. But she wants this, she dreams of this. Not a habit then, a drug, a temporary relief from the world. That’s probably why she can’t get away. Maybe she doesn’t want to. His lips drift over her face, her neck, her ears. She can feel his breath on her skin. Is this his drug too? Does he want this release? Or is it just one more manipulation? Ellis wouldn’t put it past him. She knows that he’d use her and discard her without second thought. He betrays people the same way he breathes. Ellis knows this, and she doesn’t really care. He loves their daughter; he loves their Claire and that’s all that matters. That’s what she can trust. Not his betrayals, not his lies. His love. She doesn’t want to think that maybe some of this is for her own benefit, that maybe she can forgive him (that she wants to forgive him). It would mean that she is susceptible, weak, that he has an opportunity to hurt her again. “What are you thinking about?” he whispers into her ear as his fingers slide under his shirt and trace her ribs. There’s a slight edge to it, his nails dig. “You.” It's the truth. “Good,” he says. She thinks he smiles against her neck. It’s still raining when Ellis takes a taxi to the airport. Logan doesn’t go with her. “I have other business to attend to,” he had said, straightening his clothes. She had only nodded because there was nothing else to say. Heathrow is busy; it always is. Ellis makes her way through check-in, through security, through the crowds of people. It’s familiar at least, repetitive, a habit in its own right. She watches people sleep in chairs, waiting for their flights, jackets splayed out on them like blankets. They could be attacked by anyone at any moment, tagged, bugged, robbed, killed. Sleep exposes you in that way, makes you vulnerable. She envies them, their casual trust in the kindness of strangers. Ellis never asks Logan about that time, how he felt about marrying her, about having their daughter. She’s afraid of hearing the answer. It could destroy the fragile barrier she has erected between them. If he never loved her, she’s free to hate him. Her hate can protect her the moment he inevitably betrays her again (and she has no doubt that he will). Maybe she can survive trusting him again, but she’s not really sure she wants to find out. There’s a voice over the loudspeaker, announcing her flight. About time. Ellis gathers her things and walks toward her gate. She’s going back to New York, a place that should be home, but the thought offers no comfort. It’s not home, not really, not without Claire there. She thinks Logan would understand, in his own way. “The story of my life is a story of you. It’s a story buried in gardens, dropped over the sides of bridges, entwined with winding clocks that have seen more time than I will ever hold in my own hands,” over the phone, “It’s a story I tell with every breath I exhale, every moment I encircle with the expansion of my lungs, trapped between the bars of my ribcage. You are my pages. You are my words.” Breathe. Continue. “In 270 AD, Valentine broke the law for love. In 2004 AD, so did we.” And then she wakes up. Sweat, flush, and she’s never felt any more alive. “Everything all right, El?” Chandler leaned against the doorframe of her bedroom. Ellis noted, disoriented, that Chandler’s pyjama bottoms had penguins on them. “I’m fine,” she managed, brushing moist brown from her forehead, “Had a nightmare, that’s all.” And she can’t let go, she can’t stop, rather, and it scares her. She wants to be able to breathe through the mist without thinking of still Cortland mornings, wants to lean against buildings without thinking of the tangled Albany streets, wants to hear the word love and not recall short moments of lying in the sun and throwing grass. She wants something else, something to open her eyes to, something to grasp more tangibly than glossy photographs and words she keeps repeating to herself. She doesn’t want to be safe in this recollecting, to be content to stay here forever. Because she is. And if she doesn’t come up for air, she’s going to drown in three sultry weeks she will never ever taste again. Angst is not about the black or the white. It is about the huge array of muddled greys in between. Evil people doing evil things is far less interesting than good people doing bad things through misguided good intentions. Less interesting than normal people doing desperate things. Less interesting than people talking themselves into things they wouldn’t normally do through fractures of their own inherent logic. Absolute black-and-white, good-and-evil, boring. There is only conflict, and angst is not about conflict. It is about compromise. She can’t be enough anymore. She’s tried and hated herself for not trying harder, but she can’t do it anymore. He told her in the car that she needs to stop wasting time like it’s such a disappointment, like he wants her to yell to make him understand, like she’s invested so much time and money into him that she can’t fail now. What if she already has? What if that’s what she is, his failure, the mistake he made for a check and a bundle of papers, just a girl who shouldn’t be trying so hard to be what she’s not? She’s wanted to be everything for him; she would do it all if he just asked and she has told him that. She’s tried and no matter what she does, it’s still not enough, though. She’s wanted it to be. She’s wanted nothing more than that. Just to be beautiful enough, smart enough, competent enough, good enough, to make Logan happy enough and maybe proud enough so that, just for one second, he would stop expecting more. She doesn’t know how he can continue to worry about her when she doesn’t even know who she is, someone clay, someone who isn’t so much a person as a bundle of expectations straining for electricity, for Pygmalion’s touch and if that is what he wants her to be, she’d be whoever he asked her to be and do what she can to be someone else somewhere else, where even Logan is blind. But she thinks he knows she is cracking. He’s wrong, there; it’s not perfection. It’s just wanting to be enough. Now she wriggles from the harnesses of her white sheets, falls to her feet, ambles into the kitchen where the moonlight bounces off of walls, creating stretching breathing life into the shadows that play against them. Tap tap twist twist and the water is running, close open close again the cabinets and she takes a sip from a glass, fingers clenches around the matter that she holds against her chest and it’s cold. Face-pressed-against-the-glass-cold. Bare-fingers-numb-in-the-winter cold. Soon. Later. Whatever. Ellis Nash is certain it doesn’t matter anymore. And as she puts the glass in the dish washer, she whispers something too soft to be rehearsed, something like, “Life sucks,” and living as Picasso’s painting is a farce of immortality. It’s actually quite uncomfortable, as she feels at time that even the air she breathes is thick like paint, but she gets used to it. Still, she’d rather be a ghost. Or really, just dead. Insanity has not handcuffed her, only tickled the wrists and as she skips from doubt to cynicism back to doubt but more cynicism, and all the way back to the faded quietude of faith like wrinkles in stiff milk white shirts, like going crazy in an empty room with no one to hear, no one to see, no one to care and it is what it is. Masochist, the way she always reminds herself not to care in the memos that took form in kisses and stained touches and the choking pressure of clean nails against the side of her neck like something heavy, too much so, heavy with love, like a knot of words she choked down. She doesn’t know how to feel anything else. And she goes back to bed, tangles herself in his bedsheets, lets nightmares consume her once again. Because she doesn’t know what else to do.
