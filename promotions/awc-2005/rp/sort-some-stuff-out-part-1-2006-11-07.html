Sort some stuff out (Part 1)

7 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/11/2006 Jonny Kae sat, one leg crossed over the other, in his usual decadence. The walls were painted red, with gold tinges here and there. A giant mirror covered one wall, flanked either side by the Kae coat of arms. A large cabinet held half the adjacent wall, full of pieces of antique pottery and the like, most of which was 18th to 19th century and Georgian pieces. Beside that, a painting hung proud and large, of Jonny Kae’s late Father – one of many constant reminders of the man Jonny had so eagerly waited to replace. Indeed, the man’s death had commanded not a single tear from the young Kae, but instead a feeling of joy within – it was only on the surface that Kae gave in to etiquette and appeared sad. The third wall was bare, all but for one small painting on the far side, a quaint country scene, a farmer walking his dog. Jonny enjoyed the tranquillity and simplicity of it, and often studied it when his life became too hectic. It had a calming influence. The final wall was the property of the gauche furniture. A giant couch laid across it, flanked either end by small chests of drawers, upon which lay red candled, burning into the night when required. Jonny, however, sat in the middle of the room, in the Victorian-style red and gold chair his father had acquired to complement the room. The man talking to him sat opposite, on a somewhat lesser specimen. Man: “All I really came to do, Mr. Kae, was deliver this package.” Jonny studied the man. He wore a mask to conceal his lower face, but the eyes never lied. He could tell there were other parts to this individual’s plan, whatever it may be, but he couldn’t put his finger on it. It had been early in the afternoon when Jolene had called from reception, that a man, about six foot four and well built, had walked into the Kae Enterprises Headquarters looking for Jonny Kae. He looked suspect, perhaps only due to the style of dress, although obviously expensive. He wore a hooded top underneath a sleeveless jumper, the hood covering his hair and forehead. The black trousers were, as Kae spotted immediately, no cheap attire. So, curiosity prevailed over caution, and the man was allowed up to the little room. Ironically, his father had used this room to punish those he didn’t like. The fact that there were no windows here, that the door could only unlock from the inside, and that this was the only room in this particular building that had no CCTV, made it a perfect place to do what one must do to keep one’s affairs in order. But in this case, the man wasn’t getting a beating, or being given a shady task. Jonny just felt comfortable talking to him in the intimacy of privacy. He felt he knew him, not sure where from, but he knew. Jonny Kae: “And what, may I ask, is it?” The man obviously smiled underneath the mask, by the way the creases formed on the upper cheekbone. Man: “Information.” Kae lifted an eye at the man. He clasped his hands together, thinking what it might be. Jonny Kae: “Go on…” The man shook his head slightly, making sure to conceal as much of himself as possible. Man: “You may open it when I am gone. Not before then. Do with it as you wish.” Jonny smiled a shallow smile. Jonny Kae: “What if I retrieve my security to force you to reveal yourself? Then we can witness, together, what you have on offer here. Don’t you recognise this room has no silent witnesses to disturb our privacy?” The veiled threat splashed over the man like water landing on rock. It was what Kae had expected, but he had to make sure this man wasn’t flaky. Nervous men made nervous mistakes, and a man in Jonny’s position couldn’t afford nervous mistakes. Man: “We could. But then this little game would stop. I know you’ll let me out of here. I know you’ll read what’s in that package. I’ll be watching you, Mr. Kae. Don’t disappoint me.” The man stood, wrapping his scarf around his neck. He nodded respectfully at Jonny, before making for the door. Jonny stopped him with his voice. Jonny Kae: “So, what next?” The man chuckled before turning back. Man: “That’s up to you. Your actions will dictate whether you see me again. Good day, sir.” He opened the door, and walked proudly out, his pace fast, but not too fast to be suspicious. Jonny took in his strong American voice for the last time, and cupped his chin with his hands. What could this be? Blackmail perhaps? No. It was too subtle if that were the case. That was partly why he’d chosen this room, to put his guest at ease, to reveal any particular plots that might be stirring. Of course, that was a failure. He turned to look at the package. To open or not to open, that is the question. Of course it wasn’t really a question at all, for all who knew Jonny Kae knew the answer before the question were even an issue. The package might as well be filled with anthrax, Jonny would still open it. He was far too ambitious and curious to not do so. Besides, if someone else opened it, that’s another pair of eyes to see whatever information was in there, and if there was one thing Kae knew, it was that information was power. He slowly opened the sleeve of the envelope, and emptied its contents on the table before him. Pictures. Many pictures. And a few files too, to complement the pictures perhaps? And a note, with an address scribbled on it. Kae picked the first picture up, and looked at the face before him. An Italian man, expressionless, but strong features. He seemed to recognise the face from somewhere. The picture below it explained why. Kae dropped the two pictures in horror, as his eyes tried to reconcile what they had seen. There, on the table, was a scene of his own Mother, lying face down on a hotel bed, with two men behind her wearing ski masks, suffocating her. One held the gun to her head, while the other seemed to push her face as far into the bed as possible. A few moments later, Kae knew, she would be dead. The picture of the Italian man pointed to one of the men, the man with the gun via a reconciliation of symbols. Another picture below the two, of another Italian man, pointed to the other. Things were becoming clear. So, maybe this man has enemies? Maybe he wants you to kill his enemies? So he makes lies…deceits…clever little bee. But no. The next picture was another shock. That of the two men leaving the hotel, carrying duffle bags. But there, circled, was a ski mask hanging out of one man’s pocket. Bastards! He now had faces to put to the hands that had murdered the only woman who had ever truly loved him. An assortment of photos followed, of the two men in various situations, talking to various people. They were obviously surveillance pictures of some sort of the Mafioso. The same Mafioso that had put Kae Enterprises out of business. What his father had taken forty years to build, they had taken away in forty days. Then were the files. Vincent Camberloni. Kae opened the file. He called Jolene, and asked him to cancel his public meetings that afternoon, he had a lot of reading to do. ___________________________________________________________ His breath was visible on the cold air, even through the tactical mask. It created little pictures on the wind, disappearing before they could really take shape. As a child, he had loved to pretend he was a dragon, blowing smoke from his mouth. But he was a child no more. This was serious. He was “Roger.” Of course, Roger wasn’t his real name. It was the code name he used for his clients. Afterall, if a client became angry at his work, there were millions of Rogers across the continent that they would have to track down. He never showed his face, nor did he use his real voice. So, it was only right that everything else was false too. He looked down the scope, to the quaint cafeteria on the side of the street. This was certainly a strange job. He’d been contacted by a political figure earlier in the week. The woman on the phone, her name Jolene, had not divulged his identity, but did reveal he was a man of political and financial influence. The price was not the going rate. Afterall, it was very rare that he had to take out one of the mob. This one was an expensive hit. Vincent Camberloni, Mafioso thug and occasional hit man for the mob. Just thinking about taking him out made Roger dizzy. The number of what ifs were staggering. That was why he had gone over his exit strategy a hundred times, making sure to avoid the Italian hangouts, and get underground as soon as possible. There, the sewers would be his maze, which only he knew the answer to. It would be fine. Besides, it was a lot of money. He looked at his watch. Five minutes till he took the shot. His client had requested it come at exactly twelve noon. Something about a news report coming on television at half past eleven, and confusion. There they were, arguing. Vincent and another Italian man. They had obviously seen something disturbing on the television. He lifted the receiver to his ear, to listen to the conversation. He’d bugged them the night before. Vincent: “What the hell? Who would have the balls to put that on National Television?” Accomplice: “I don’t give a damn. You gotto get out of here.” Vincent was sweating. His voice was shaky. Vincent: “What about Tony? I mean, what if we…” His friend slapped him across the face. Accomplice: “Think straight Vinnie! Tony’s gonna want you dead now. And I’ll be damned if I come forward too. You’re on your own pal. I suggest you get the hell Outta here. Let me try and deal with Tony. But, if you get the call from Rizzo or Antonio, you know you’re in trouble. Only come back if I call you.” Vincent nodded. He was truly fucked. Couldn’t turn himself in, or rely on his boss. Either side would have him killed within twenty four hours. Just then, a motorbike slowed to a halt outside the café. Vincent immediately took out his gun, pointing it at the man on the bike. Vincent: “Who are you?” The boy put his hands up, a package in one hand. Roger aimed the scope at Vincent’s cranium. It was almost time. Boy: “I…I just have a delivery for Mr. Camberloni.” Vincent used to gun to dictate the boy put the package on the floor. He came forward. Vincent grabbed him by the throat, and pointed the gun at his temple. Vincent: “Frisk him.” The other Italian man was shaking his head. Accomplice: “It’s just a kid Vinnie. You got more important things to worry about at the moment.” Vincent: “I said frisk him!” The other Italian man shrugged his shoulders, and frisked the boy. Accomplice: “He’s clean.” Vincent withdrew the gun, and pushed the kid to his bike. Vincent: “Piss off Kid.” The boy did just that, driving for all he was worth, out of certain death country. Camberloni opened the package. Roger turned the scope to his right, to read the package. It was a picture. A picture of a woman being, what seemed to be, murdered, by two men. Across the picture, in red felt, was written the words “REMEMBER THIS?” Roger’s watch clicked midday. He clicked the scope into place. He clicked the trigger. There was a bang in New York tonight. ___________________________________________________________ Jonny Kae thought about the next few weeks. At the moment, things were getting turbulent again, but he couldn’t let the murderers of his Mother go free. Especially when he had all the information he needed. But for now, he put it to the back of his mind. They would die, but he couldn’t kill them all at once. It would cast too many questions. Unless he could make it seem like a rival family hit, but that came with so many problems itself. If he created enough turbulence on the family in question, there would no doubt be a family war anyway. What concerned him more, was the photo and file he had received on his opponent in the race for Illinois. The picture showed Charles Sandon talking to Tony De Lasquelle. Tony De Lasquelle was no small casual acquaintance. Some said he was responsible for all family activity on the West Coast. Of course, they were probably wrong, but a man with that reputation might as well be. This was real dirt. His rival, discussing with a mob boss. This was dirt indeed. But for now, he had to put that behind him. Kae was now on his way to Arizona. Another AWC show, another match. Time to clear his thoughts, free his mind of worry for a few moments. This match pitted Kae with Taylor, against two AWC legends, Pierce Lavelle and Garbage Bag Johnny. No matter. Gimmicks and gimmicks and gimmicks. They were coming back. Jonny was being left behind. The era of attitude was all but over, but it didn’t matter. He went out there for an ego rush, and he still got that. In the ring, there were no politics, no problems. It was a man against a man. Or, in this case, 2 men against 2 men. It was so simple. Akin to watching the picture in his private room, Jonny Kae went to the ring to clear his mind. Perhaps one day, it might be more again, but for now, wrestling had to take a back seat.
