Blanket

1 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/01/06 Anton moved sideways through the door, doing his best to help his much shorter manager through. Anton, dressed in a Nigerian flag t-shirt and training pants, furrowed his brow in consternation as his hand rested on his manager's waist; he was doing his best to yank Luis out of the room. Luis couldn't move so quickly, though, and the smaller man braced himself against the wall as he tried to get his legs to work well enough to help him move. ''Careful, Lion,'' Luis warned, his Spanish flowing freely over his tongue. ''C-carefu-ow! Shit, man, let me get out of here myself. You're gonna kill me.'' ''Just move,'' Anton said in the same language, releasing Luis and stepping backwards to give him space. Luis nodded and shuffled forward, straightening his suit jacket as he slowly made his way out of the room. Anton was bewildered by the man. How, in his state, could he even think of dressing in that ridiculous white zoot suit? How had he gotten into it? Anton surely hadn't helped the man clothe himself. Even in his state, though, even with copious amounts of tape under that jacket and shirt, Luis had to be Luis. It was more than infuriating, but Anton bore it. He didn't exactly want to injure the man again. ''Okay, I'm out of the room,'' Luis panted, resting against the wall. Anton sidestepped him and pulled the door closed. ''You have the key?'' ''Yes.'' ''Did you get a walker?'' Anton blinked. ''A what?'' Luis groaned, throwing up his hands. ''Fuck, Lion. Just… fuuck! I can't believe this. How do you expect me to walk out of here? I ain't exactly in the best of shape.'' Anton hadn't even thought of a walker. He'd considered a wheelchair, but decided against it in the end. Luis wasn't exactly a fan of being wheeled around like he was powerless, even when he was. Anton sighed, reaching over to Luis and pulling the shorter man's arm over his shoulder. ''I'll help you, then.'' ''This is gonna hurt like fuck,'' Luis groaned. He hopped up away from the wall a bit, wincing as he got his feet under him. ''But come on. I don't think I could spend much longer cooped up in that hospital room.'' Anton nodded, taking slow steps and supporting his manager's weight. He wanted to move faster, but he had a duty. He knew there had to be a little bit of sacrifice. They walked that way for a while in silence, apart from the groans and vulgar mutterings of the Venezuelan. It was uncomfortable, not so much the silence as the fact that Anton and Luis had never shared a silence between them. Luis was probably not thinking of anything more than the next step, though, so Anton fumbled around to find something to say. ''Nneka will be happy to see you out of the hospital,'' Anton said. ''She's hated having you to be here.'' She'd told Anton that she would be training at the moment, which was well enough. He didn't want to have to deal with her as well. ''Well, thanks, Lion,'' Luis said with a tongue sharpened to a sarcastic edge. ''Nice to hear that from the man who put me into the hospital in the first place.'' Anton shakes his head. ''Luis, shut up. What you did was inexcusable. You deserved what you got.'' Luis lowered his head, stewing for a moment. Of course, Anton was right, but the ebon gladiator knew that Luis would never let Anton get the last word. ''How's the bitch been, anyway?'' Luis asked. Anton's mouth pursed for a bit. ''Fine,'' he said briefly. Luis nodded. ''She been sleepin' around anymore?'' Luis glanced up at Anton and chuckled. ''Naw, you've been keeping an eye on her, right? Which reminds me. What the fuck is she doing now? D'you know?'' Anton didn't say anything. He shouldn't, after all. He shouldn't lead the man on, try and make him say more. His soul was aflame, burning to lash out, but Anton kept himself under control. ''Quiet,'' Anton snapped. Luis laughed a bit harder, holding his ribs for fear that they might shatter. ''Ah, Lion, we know she ain't cheatin' on yuh. Er, I mean, I wish I could say that, y'understand. But no one knows your bitch. She's probably drunk off her ass, panties down, shakin' it in some bar halfway 'cross town. The kicker is that you don't even know what she's doing! D'you even have a cell phone?'' Luis interrupts himself with a short, abrasive laugh. ''Naw, you don't. I know you don't. You can't even… ha ha, Lion, you're a fuckin' sucker. And your bitch is… a bitch!'' Anton scowled. It wasn't enough to get him to hit Luis again, but it made him so angry that he had to blink the red spots out of his eyes. He tensed up and immediately tried to calm himself. It was difficult, though. Luis had to be Luis, though. As the elevator doors slid open, Anton reminded himself that he'd endured this from Luis for years. He could endure it now. Amber pushed down the plunger, watching the liquid race out of the barrel and undoubtedly through the needle into her vein. She smiled as she plucked the syringe from her arm and sat back against the wall. She knew that she would be satisfied with this dose. She'd been having to take more and more, but this time she was certain she'd taken enough to keep her happy. She looked around at the familiar surroundings. She hadn't been in this apartment in forever. Anton had pulled her away to another place after Luis's attack, but that didn't mean she didn't have fond memories of the house that all four of them had once shared. She had had some good times here. That was enough to bring her back. That and the relative safety of the place. Luis was out for however long and he wasn't coming back. She was glad that Luis was gone, glad that he had been hospitalized. Glad that he wasn't able to try and turn Anton away from her again. Glad that he wasn't around to make her feel… drawn towards him, to reel her in like a trout. She didn't know why she'd lain down with Luis those times, but it had happened and she felt like it could happen again and again if she wasn't careful. Now she didn't have to be. Nneka wasn't home, either. She was probably doting over Luis, trying to get him back to health, a health that Amber was fervently hoping would never return. She had been the nicer of the two, but that didn't mean much. Nneka had still hated Amber and Amber had felt the same. With her gone, that had left this place wide open; finding that spare key in her purse had been a boon. At least those two were still useful, even if it was only because they'd maintained this space. She touched the piercing in her elbow with two fingers. Anton would never understand why she was doing this. If he found her, he would yell at her. He'd chastize her and he'd tell her to give this all up. He'd watch over her day and night and make sure she did, too; he was that way with anything he saw that was wrong. How could he understand that the stress had weighed down on her heart until she needed something like this to calm her? How could he understand that her body couldn't support her mind anymore? How could he… how could he understand what she had told herself every time she shot up? This was all for him. She knew it was. But he wouldn't understand that, so she had to hide. She would have felt terrible about it, but the junk smacked her into bliss. The brown leather interior of the shuttle seemed to bounce independently of the rest of the vehicle as they rolled down the highway. Anton and Luis sat in the back, leaving a wide gap of space between them. The driver wasn't talkative, a point that Anton held in the man's favor; obnoxious taxi drivers were perhaps some of the worst earthly specimens there were. Luis, however, was very talkative. ''You screwed 'er lately?'' Luis asked. Anton glanced at him, but he didn't speak. He'd taken to leaving his lips together since they'd entered the elevator. Anything he said would only bury him further. He didn't have Luis's cutting words. ''I asked you a question, Lion. You fucked the bitch in recent memory?'' Anton lifted his chin, staring straight forward. Luis laughed. ''I bet you haven't. Bet your scared, eh? Scared of where that cunny's been? She probably has all sorts of diseases. Least you're clean, aren't you?'' Luis laughed. ''Of course.'' Anton was pointedly trying to think of exactly where Amber was. She had to be in the house. She'd spent long hours in the house recently, in corners that he hadn't even known existed, rooms that seemed to be conjured into reality from her imagination. He didn't know exactly what was wrong with her. He had to ask her when he got back. ''Lion, you're just bein' dull,'' Luis snapped. ''It ain't my bitch who's wanderin' the earth for some dick, it's yours. Maybe you should keep her on a leash or something, y'understand. Something to keep her in line. She's gonna give you somethin' soon if you ain't careful. Crabs or something. She's probably got a whole fucking zoo in her panties. Crabs and sea lions and—'' ''Shut up, Luis,'' Anton growled. The two men locked eyes and heat passed between them. Anton snarled. ''I don't want to hear another word out of you.'' Luis snorted. ''What's got your panties in a bunch? Would you rather be back with your bitch?'' Anton balled his fists, but he didn't strike. He didn't yell. He simply turned and looked out the windshield. He had a duty to Luis only because he put him in the hospital. He told himself that that was the only reason he was even doing this. Euphoria was Amber's world. She didn't feel anything and she didn't want to. Eternity passed by Amber's eyes and she received it with a smile. At several points, a fly—intruding completely without anyone's permission, much like Amber herself—buzzed in front of her face, on her nose, in her ears, on her neck. She could only smile at it. Dimly, she remembered something about flies vomiting whenever they landed, but it was only an amusing sidenote at this point. Stress was less than a memory; it had never existed. In the hours that she had spent like this, she had been free, freer than she'd ever felt. Free of everything, of stress, of worry, even of life. Free to fly had she wished to, which she didn't. Free to transform into something she wasn't. Free to sleep. All she wanted to do was sleep, really. A black blanket slid over her eyes. Vaguely, she wondered who was tucking her in. Anton pushed the door open to the apartment, sighing. Finally. He didn't know if he'd make it back here, having to deal with all of Luis's jokes and cracks and whatever. He felt drained, exhausted. Of course, it had been a long time since he had seen this apartment. He hadn't been here for weeks at least. He took the opportunity to look around at the surroundings, familiarity seeping into his bones. ''Quit standing in front of the door,'' Luis snapped from behind him. ''Let me in. I wanna sit down; I think I might die if I don't.'' Grunting, Anton acquiesced, stepping forward to let his manager get into the house. His eyes continued to rove around the place. Well, at least Nneka had kept it well while everyone else had been absent. His eyes swept across a form slouched against the back wall. The figure held his attention. What… ''Amber,'' Anton gasped. Anton's face turned into a snarl; he spoke English now in order to give her a talking-to: ''Amber, what are you… Amber?'' Anton took a few steps forward, looking down at her. She wasn't moving. Not a bit. There was no… there was no rise and fall. ''Amber?'' Anton went down to a knee in front of Amber and grabbed her by her slender shoulders. He shook her gently. ''Amber, it is me. Amber. Amber!'' He shook her harder now, but her eyes didn't open. There was no response, except for her head moving so limply with the shakes. ''Amber!'' There was no problem. Amber was just trying to get Anton to react, to notice her. He knew that he'd been training more and more, but that was because he had to be alone to decide. He had to figure out things by himself. She had to know that, that's why she was doing this. Any second now, she would wake up and scowl at him. She would say something like ''What if I actually died? Huh? What then? Where would that leave you?'' Anton could hear her voice even now Amber was just very good at this. Anton shook her harder. ''Amber!'' Oh, she was very good. Making up after the fight… she always told him that was the best part. They would have an enormous fight, then, and they would be able to make up. They would. Tears leaked out of Anton's eyes as he leaned forward, his head pressing against Amber's. It was so cold. So very… Anton let out the longest and loudest howl he thought he had ever summoned from those bestial lungs of his. He cradled her face against his chest and roared. ''Ambulance!'' Anton shouted. ''Luis! Call the ambulance! Call 911! Call them! Move! Call the ambulance!'' Anton continued to shout at the top of his lungs until there was no more breath left, until he could only lean forward and press his head against the wall and deceive himself. The problem was that his tear ducts were wide open and every little lie he told himself was leaking out. The puddle that formed on Amber's knee was a mirror of truth. Anton closed his eyes so he wouldn't have to look in it. Luis couldn't figure out what was wrong. His mind raced. His insides burned. On the outside, though, he seemed to be carved of ice. His eyes, wide as Jupiter's moons, were ensnared by this blue woman he once knew. He heard the lion-like roars for aid, but they washed over him like rain. The ground swept upwards and slammed against Luis's back.
