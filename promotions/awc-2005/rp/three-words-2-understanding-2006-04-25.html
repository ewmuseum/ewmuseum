Three Words [2] - Understanding

25 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 25, 2006 It was August of 2005. Charles Kensrue, who we now refer to as Cedric Riley, sat anxiously in the waiting room of a hospital in Langley, Virginia. His eyes mimicked those of a dead person to go with his pale skin as if he had been fighting sleep for the past ten hours, and awake long before that. He is a little different than we know him now. He is more aware, conscious of every barely noticeable movement within perceivable distance. As gaunt his appearance seems, he still seems to have control of the room; he gives off a sense that he knows more than anyone else. His employment with the CIA gave him these hawk-like abilities. It’s hard to describe the skill as anything comprehendible other than a “spidey-sense”, but he sure had an catalytic like effect on the room. And by that, I mean his presence is there, mentally more than physically, but at the same time he could remove himself without being noticed. The waiting room is an unusual place. To quote Ben Gibbard, there’s no comfort in the waiting room, just nervous pacers bracing for bad news. For Cedric Riley, he was unsure as to whether or not the news he was prepping himself for was good or bad, it was simply reality. Being father to a child was something that he never anticipated; never expected to invade his perilous lifestyle; and didn’t know if he wanted that burden at 27 years of age. The bottom line was that he was simply scared of growing up and taking on adult responsibilities that he had never experienced before. The majority of his life was spent solely dedicated to his career in either wrestling or the agency, and preceding those passionate commitments, three years at Berkeley University. He could hardly keep up with himself. He was nervous. It wasn’t a matter of being happy to be a father, because he knew that he would certainly be joyous, as scared as he was to have the responsibility. He simply was undecided whether or not he would want it to force him out of his career that he had devoted the majority of his life too. Sooner or later he would have to face the consequences of an unexpected child. The word unexpected is a little hostile, but the fact of the matter was that Natalie and him never intended for it to happen. Were they in love? Cedric asked himself that everyday … it was such a powerful thing to someone like Cedric who had never experienced it. Natalie and him had an awkward connection; being co-workers for a secret agency was the main reason. In writing, it was illegitimate in the CIA, but they had fallen for each other, and a relationship spawned. And now, a child. He wanted to tell her that he loved her. They were having a child together; but he felt as if it would somehow affect them for the worst. Would it be better or worst to make sure it would be plausible for an eternal relationship before he said it? Maybe he should have thought about that before doing the deed, but in reality, it was like the Hollywood flicks that he always hated - unpredictable. If they stayed at the CIA, they would be torn apart. What would fate bring? What would happen to their child? Cedric knew he would be willing to drop his position for both Natalie and the baby, but would she? And how easy would it be? Cedric’s mind swirled with possibilities, every option more confusing than the one before, while he tried to keep his mind sharp and aware of everything around him. He had always known that in America being aware is the best defense against anything dangerous. He was a firm believer in it partly because of his career, but also because he was dangerous. To be trusted by Cedric Riley is a rare occurrence that should be looked upon in the highest regard. The nurse came round, and everyone lifted his or her heads. “Congratulations, Mr. Kensrue … you are the father of a baby boy.” Cedric smiled as all his worry, grief, anticipation, and all those variables drained from him and were replaced by a flood of happiness. He almost had tears in his eyes as the nurse led him to the labor room, where Natalie sat exhausted. He took her by the hand and kissed her on the forehead as the nurse approached him cradling a baby boy. As a CIA agent, Cedric’s emotions were usually kept at bay, but it was something that he couldn’t hold back. The laughter, joy, and utter awe at the fact he was holding hischild had overcome him and made him push aside everything else that mattered in his life. Never shall he forget that defining moment as a person, at twenty-seven years of age; he had the experience of a lifetime. He was welcoming his own conception into a world with arms wide open. James Graff entered the room, momentarily turning everyone’s attention from the baby. He had a nervous smile on his face and his hands in his pockets. “Congratulations.” January, 2006. Cedric Riley, known then as Charles Kensrue, awaited the press conference that would act as the final step to disguising his departure from the CIA. It was a well-thought plan that simply needed to be executed to a live audience, but pacing his apartment in Langley, Virginia, the conference was the least of his worries. He hadn’t seen Natalie since Christmas, and knew he wouldn’t be seeing her anytime soon for she had taken up residence with her Mother in England. He knew she had her reasons – to get away and look after their son, Seth, in an area where she wouldn’t be distracted was the best option out of a handful of limited ones. But being away from her had Cedric thinking deeply about where they stood relationship-wise. They were together without any serious differences, but their primary focus was their son, which basically gave them no room to talk about where their relationship as headed. As parents, normally the relationship-stance would be serious or married, but neither of them liked to rush into those things. It was evident that they had fallen in love, but Cedric didn’t know where to go from there, and neither did she. He hadn’t even told her how he felt up to that point, but as they were relieved from their duties of the agency, he thought changes were dawning upon them. But the question as to where they would go from there was still up in the air. To be separated by an ocean presented a primary problem, but they also seemed distant in the sense that they were choosing new careers. Cedric decided to return to wrestling and Natalie would remain unemployed to look after their son for the time being. Natalie knew of Cedric’s love for wrestling. But did she know that he loved her more? Probably. Natalie was only twenty-three years old, though, and even though she was ahead of her years to the point she had maturity beyond the twenty-seven year-old Cedric, they had only been together for barely more than a year. For some, that is plenty of time to fall in love and let each other know it. But let’s review the circumstances involved – the one that sticks out is the fact that both were in a field of high secrecy and protection. Is it smart to go boasting an illegitimate relationship and child? IIllegitimate is such a unconstructive description, but at the same time, the truth. They were never supposed to be together. They knew that. But they were. And they had a child. And they had no idea what to do. Cedric felt juvenile. He had put off being a normal adult for a long time, and seemed to be continuing to do so even with being enveloped by love. Was deciding to return to wrestling a contributor to that? He had no idea. He really had no idea what to do and what not to do. It wasn’t a question of knowing. It was a question of showing. Cedric conveniently came to his senses and found himself in the same dining hall that he recalled. He found it hard to concentrate with loud noises filtering into his mind, but scanned the room regardless, wondering if anyone had noticed his absent daze. Apparently not, because everyone seemed a little preoccupied, and he couldn’t blame them. The group of gunmen had complete control over the chaotic crowd, as ironic as that seems; something about brandishing deadly weapons had that effect. One man in particular stood out simply because he had control of the men that had control. He stood at a little over six feet and was the only one now without a mask on – he had a gaunt expression on his face half-hidden by a graying beard and matching short hair. At his mercy was the man in a white suit that Cedric assumed to be the Captain, though he hesitated to refer to him as that in his current state. There was very little captainship in his predicament – being held at gunpoint, looking helpless with blood-stained attire and a look of fear masking his face. The proposed leader of the gunmen had a sick look on his face as he repeatedly kicked the helpless Captain to the screams of onlookers that only made him happier. Cedric deterred his attention away from the scene to Natalie, who sat motionless, biting her lip, looking as if she was holding back tears. Cedric felt a twinge of responsibility and wanted to take her hand, tell her everything was going to be fine. She looked like that’s what she wanted to hear … that they would make it out alive, but Cedric was an honest man, and couldn’t make such a bold assumption to get everyone’s hopes up only to have them extinguished by the group that had overtaken the ship. Meanwhile, to Cedric’s other side was Lenard Reignes, who had his fists clench and a face full of rage. Cedric had witnessed this before – normally laid-back people who explode when threatened. He hoped that Lenard would hold back from acting rash to put anyone’s life in danger. Who was he kidding? They were all in danger, which was a certainty that he wouldn’t ignore. Cedric noticed the front man kick the Captain once more before firing gunshots into the air for silence, then addressed them. “Ladies and gentleman let me start of by saying welcome! You should all be proud to be included in such an event – one that will involve explosions, destruction, and if anyone acts rashly, maybe even a few deaths. Let me make it clear that the first two you have no control whatsoever over, period. The last … well … you might be able to avoid that for the time being, depending on how stupid you act. I think this calls for a proper introduction … my name is Victor Dickenson. Now, I’m not too sure, but you might recognize my brother, this pathetic vermin at my feet. His name is Darren. As far as a last name is concerned, he doesn’t have one.” Darren, the Captain, showed signs of life and crawled towards his brother. “Vic, don’t do this … please …” Victor scowled at him and delivered a viscous kick to his chest, which sent him sprawling back to the floor and spitting blood. Victor simply smiled. “And why shouldn’t I? Let’s ask our fans, shall we, Darren? Or maybe we should ask them why I’ve always been second-string to the success story of Darren Dickenson? Why I’ve always been told I should be more like Darren, the perfect, accomplished, Captain D.You’re just about as weak as dear old Mom and Dad, aren’t you? They would love to see their precious youngest son right now at the mercy of the son who did nothing with his life. All these years you’ve been riding cloud nine being looked up to. And what about me? I choose to try for something different and fail, and get looked down on because of it? You’re pathetic, you know that?” Darren remained on the floor, but tried to respond. “It’s not like that, Victor. Mom and Dad only ever offered their help, and I did to…” “I don’t needyour pathetic help! I run an operation of my own now, Darren, if you haven’t already caught on. Did you not notice my plans? They’re not a secret, although they’ve been up my sleeve for some time, now that I’m unleashing them I’d like the whole world to now.” He turned away from his brother to the crowd of terrified people. “That’s where you come in. See, there’s a famous wooden strip across this river that they like to call Pier 39, and coincidentally, where this very ship is destined! And not only that, it’s wired with enough TNT to wipe out an entire city block! So, I hope you’ve all said your last goodbyes, because you are all going to die.” “No … I’ll find a way to stop you …” “Yeah? And tell me what brilliant way you’re going to manage that, huh, brother? I have armed men and you have this dining room full of rich snobs that you expect to fight your battle? Not unlike you, Darren, always fighting for a losing side,” Victor said. “You can’t let all these people die, you imbecile! Out of your own selfish pride! Victor, I thought you were better than that. You’re just a bloody coward; no wonder Mom and Dad were never behind you!” Darren exploded, trying to verbally fight back. The enraged Victor strode forward and stomped on his brother aggressively, then picked him up by the throat and kept him in a tight grip, cutting off all the oxygen supply to his brain. “Always have to be the hero, don’t you, brother? Well, sometimes the hero doesn’t win, unfortunately. You were always too headstrong … another weakness that everybody failed to see but me. My next command might do your ego some good, which is to have you tied to the front of your own ship so you can get a front-row view of its destruction, if you’re good enough. I wanted you to stick around for the fireworks later, but at this rate, you’ll be dead in minutes. Goodbye, brother,” he finished then slammed Darren to the floor. Two gunmen approached Darren and seized him by the arms as he tried to helplessly fight them off. Darren screamed at his brother, “NO! I WON’T LET YOU GET AWAY WITH THIS!” The only progress that made was one of the gunmen slamming the butt of his pistol into Darren’s skull, knocking him unconscious. They dragged him out of the dining room as Victor shook his head, then heard a distinct crashing noise from the far corner of the hall where some of the band members were trying to evacuate the area. Victor brandished his gun and fired bullets towards the escapees, assassinating them in the midst of screams of horror. He turned back to the crowd. “I hope that goes for a lesson of all you, as it is only a small dose of what is to come if you cross my path. Let it be clear that no one can stop me now; I have the power to blow up this boat and everything on it at anytime I please, so keep that in mind. Who wants to be the hero? Anyone?! Who wants to meet the same fate as my dear brother, who will go down with his legacy as Captain? You’ll die anyways, but if you keep your cool, it will be quick and painless. If you’re willing to die suffering, be my guest. I imagine I will thoroughly enjoy it.” He allowed a second of silence in which only a few cough were heard – apparently no one wanted to be the hero. Victor smirked and turned to his men. “Okay, I have to go back to the helm and take care of some things, and see to it that my brother gets his share of agony. Split up the men, woman, and children. Leave the men up here, and do with the woman whatever you feel like – the lower level has some good options. Two of you to the woman, three to the men.” While Victor was giving orders, Cedric looked around anxiously. He was trained for this sort of combat, and felt helpless without a weapon or anything to help his cause. He didn’t fight well in a tuxedo; besides, only in Hollywood do people without a gun beat those with them. Even though he was a former CIA agent, he hadn’t quite mastered deflecting a bullet with his fist like Jackie Chan. It was shameful, however, to possess a fighting ability and not put it to use. Unfortunately, the man beside him, Lenard Reignes, disregarded the small chance they had of fighting them off. “So, what’s the plan,” said Lenard, his game-face on. “What plan? You don’t actually think I have a plan, do you?” Responded Cedric. As a CIA agent, he had many plans, few that would be successful against armed men, so he didn’t want to put them out there. “We have to do something! Everybody’s going to die!” Exclaimed Lenard, clearly pointing the obvious. Cedric looked at him in disbelief. “It’s not that we don’t have to do anything, Lenard … it’s that we can’t. Those men have guns, and are willing to kill people. Unless youhave a nine-millimeter hidden under those five-hundred dollar silk threads …” “You must not know that I was in the Gulf War. I’m experienced in this kind of thing, and I’m not going to let these jackasses control mylife. Good Americans died saving our country and getting us freedom … I’m not going to ignore that. I’m ready to fight.” Lenard started getting to his feet and Cedric put his hand on his arm. “Don’t be a fool! You saw what those men are capable of!” Lenard looked at Cedric, a little disgusted. “You’re a coward, so sit back and let them kill us. I’ll do it by myself.” And in one bold, daring, and utterly stupid move Lenard rushed from his chair towards two guards with their backs to him who were leading some women to a designated area. Out of instinct Cedric hopped to his feet and followed the veteran. Lenard may have been in the war, but even if he was tough as nails he wouldn’t do much good against someone who was armed. He didn’t know why he followed the pursuit … maybe he was trying to prevent him from doing anything stupid. But Lenard was a man on a mission, and tackled the two guards from behind and complete confusion followed. The nearby group of people stumbled away from the fight as Lenard wrestled the two guards, giving them both a shot to the chin. His advantage didn’t last long as one of them gave him a shot to the stomach. Cedric tried to defend Lenard by swiftly grabbing his attacker and throwing him over his shoulder. His fists at ready and about to engage with the other guard, a shot rang out and struck the wall nearest to Cedric. He looked to his side where Victor Dickenson stood with his gun at ready. At that distance, the former CIA agent could do nothing but put his hands into the air, praying not to be shot. Victor laughed. “No, please … continue to struggle. I’d enjoy shooting you.” Cedric tried to keep a straight face, but bit his lower lip. “I was just trying to keep … to keep my friend here from making a mistake.” Victor looked down at Lenard in disgust. “A very costly mistake indeed. You’re lucky I’m merciful, or you would’ve ended up like those idiots over there,” he said, pointing over to the fallen band members. Lenard tried to get up, saying, “I’ll show you!” Victor smirked and pointed the gun at him. “Please, show me.” Kathy Reignes burst forward, followed by Natalie, who both screamed, “NO!” Two gunmen shoved forward and stood like walls in front of their superior, as if to keep them from doing anything stupid. Too late. Victor looked at Cedric. “Who is that? Wife? Girlfriend?” “Don’t you dare touch them …” Cedric Victor kicked Cedric right in the face. “Did I not tell you that I give the orders?! Maybe they should be taught a lesson as well … to not speak unless spoken to!” Victor looked at his pawns and directed them, “Send these two men down to the boilers and shoot them. Make sure they suffer. As for these two sorry excuses for the female race, take them up to the deck. Throw them over.” Cedric erupted, “You son of a bitch!” “Save it,” Victor scoffed, “This isn’t an after-school special.” Cedric felt powerless as two gunmen seized him and tied his hands behind his back. He was still convinced that fighting back would only give the trigger-happy Victor more reason to shoot him; he had been in these situations ever too often to make that mistake. It was more disheartening that one of his most valuable talents in skilled fighting would be ignored … for now. With a gun to Lenard and his head they were lead out of the dining room. Cedric looked back to catch one last glimpse of the developing catastrophe – wives were hugging their men and ultimately saying their last goodbyes. Kathy and Nat were being pulled in the opposite direction towards the upper deck. Cedric had already made his move, and the element of surprise was gone – there was no choice left but to wait for the enemy to make the next mistake. That was the only mantra he followed when out-numbered by advantageous opposites. The boiler room was typical – hot, sticky, and smelled of fuel that burned the nostrils. The steam engines could be heard coming from near the room, but for now they were confined to a dark compartment full of heating devices. The guards were very precise and cautious, and each planted a foot into Cedric’s back, then did the same to Lenard. Cedric slammed into the ground and clutched his ribs, and in a state of shock he was sure they were broken. He tried to get to his feet but was struck in the head with weapon from one of the gunmen, who seemed to be thoroughly pleased by the mistreatment. They tied Lenard and Cedric together and shoved them into a wall with a thud. Cedric could hear the whir of engines on the other side of the thick, solid barrier, but was oblivious to them because of the gun pointed at his face. “Can I shoot him now?” One of the gunmen demanded. The other gunmen shook his head. “No. I should be the one to do this.” “I can’t even shoot one?” “Shut the hell up. I told you that I’m handling it, now go back to the dining hall and help them secure the rest of the passengers.” “Is there a reason you’re putting this off? We could just get it over now, quick and easy, get back to the mission.” “Do you have too much wax in your ears, or did you not hear Victor say he wants them to suffer?” He pointed the gun at his partner, threatening him. “I told you to go back to the dining hall. Now” The other man didn’t look frightened, but upset he couldn’t be apart of it. He began to talk, but then simply said, “Fine,” and slowly walked out of the compartment. Cedric butted in. “So, you like assassinating people as an individual? Why, is that some sort of sick way of feeling good about yourself?” “I’m sure you’ve been told a million times you talk too much; why don’t you listen? I knew one of these days your mouth would get you killed …” Cedric raised an eyebrow. “Do I know you?” “Unlikely. But I know you very well … Mr. AgentDash - of the Atlantic Wrestling Club, which I used to follow religiously. You think you’re quite the talent, don’t you? Hell, when I first saw you, I thought the same; it’s unfortunate that you’re ungrateful of your opportunities. Wasting your time running off to England, dealing with International affairs. What’s your secret? You think you’re entertaining a crowd? This isn’t a game. You could be the best, and instead, you’re nothing more than a loser. An ungrateful coward.” Cedric clench his bound fists. “Maybe you’re too selfish to take notice, but I have fun. If you want to see a sick bastard in AWC, maybe you should root for Chainz, you two would get alone nicely.” “More lame jokes? You call that spunk?” He shook his head. “That’s how you get your ass beat, Mr. Riley.” “You’re talking out your ass. I’m the one winning matches and competing in the biggest tournament of the year,” Cedric boasted, waiting for the opportunity to bring up his chance at glory. The gunmen laughed. “Ah, Zero 2 Hero. With that attitude of yours, you will never have what it takes to get the job done.” “Leave that up to me. Maybe you should worry about more pressing matters, like blowing up this ship? Sounds like something right up your ally …” The gunmen pointed his weapon right to Cedric’s forehead. “Something is going to come right up your ally right now. I enjoyed watching you wrestle, Dash, I really did. But I think I’ll enjoy killing you more. Goodbye, AgentDash.” Cedric’s eyes traveled straight down the barrel of the gun as he swallowed hard, then looked to the man who wanted desperately to murder him. The intent on his face was sadistic. Avoiding the inevitable was something almost out of reach – the end of AgentDash was almost here. On the upper deck of the ship, someone very close to the man on the verge of extermination was in grave danger as well. Natalie Garner was bound tightly to the rail of the ship, shivering in the cold, barely able to keep any conversation with the woman beside her – Kath Reignes. The storm clouds above were forming and both of them could feel light raindrops falling from the sky. She was watching the guard assigned to them, who was disassembling the barriers around the ships guardrail’s to clear a way so that someone – Natalie and Kathy, more particularly – to be thrown over. Natalie was sickened how they would end the lives of two people immediately when the entire ship was bound to explode anyways. She looked over to Kathy, who was also beginning to shiver, her face blanked with fear and isolation. “We’re going to die … aren’t we?” said Kathy. “Don’t worry too much … we’ll get out of this somehow,” Natalie reassured, secretly hoping Cedric had found a way to escape, and also observing her own situation and trying to use some of her agent abilities. It seemed to be a lost cause – these men seemed to be highly trained as well, getting paid decent money to commit a terrorizing act such as this. Kathy shook her head. “I wish I could have seen Lenard one more time …” Natalie tried to stay positive. “Kathy, we’re not going to die.” Kathy was on the verge of tears, but swallowed hard and was able to face untimely reality. “Yes, we are. We’re going to die here alone. And so are hundreds of other people.”
