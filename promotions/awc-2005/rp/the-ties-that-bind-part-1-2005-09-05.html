The Ties That Bind: Part 1

5 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5th Sept 'I shouldn’t do doing this. I should be in Belfast, Northern Ireland at the moment, preparing for the Battle of Britain rematch against Adalia Dolerian, Mike Wade, and The British Bomber, not driving along the I-10 headed for Louisiana. Thanks, Katrina.' - AM -------------------- 'Wow. This is quite the headfuck. One moment I'm on the interstate heading for a seven-day break woth my wife in one of the greatest cities on this side of the Atlantic, the next I'm sitting around in my brother's ex-wife's house. The 'joys' of Mother Nature. I just hope Andy gets here soon, I don't think I can stand in the incessent whinings of Jane for much longer. For the past couple of days, I have lived my life like vermin, and it's all my own doing. Of course, I had been paying attention to the news bullitens days before Katrina hit New Orleans, but I always take anything that the fear-mongerers at Fox report with a pinch of salt. Somewhat concerned, I gave the travel agents a quick call; 'Don't worry,' they said, 'it'll be fine... this shouldn't hamper your week to a significant degree.' So I took their word for it, hoping that the most we'd see would be an inch or two of rain and a gale force wind here and there. It might dampen one of our days away, but surely it couldn't get in the way of all seven, right?' - SM -------------------- 'Thank Christ for Cayle's company on this monotonous journey; if I didn't have him to converse with I would have dozed off at the wheel a long time ago. I need my bed, no doubt; unfortunately though, my brother still has 12 months to serve on his driving ban, so I guess there's no chance of some shut-eye anytime soon. I'm going to arrive for Fresh! in terrible shape this week, but when a family member needs your aid, you don't just sit around on the other side of the planet and twiddle your thumbs. I'm glad that Sid and Christine are safe - well, for the moment anyway. In Jane's company, nobody is safe for too long, heh. She's the only reason I had any doubts in my mind about making this trip; every time we cross paths there's always some sort of conflict. I hate that woman, make no mistake, but it was good of her to take Sid and his wife into her home, even though she's staunchly refused to drive them back to Tallahassee herself; lethargic bitch. Oh well, at least I'll get to see Marvin for a little bit.' - AM -------------------- ”So, buoyed by the reassurance of our travel agent, Christine and I headed for New Orleans, Nawlins, Nola, whatever you want to call it. I was looking forward to visiting such a cultured place and learning a little more about the thriving jazz scene, and it had been a life long ambition to take a stroll down Bourbon Street. Besides, if what I’d heard was to be believed, I’d come across many worse storms during my time living on the Floridian panhandle. Right? The warning signs began to appear as we approached Nola. Travelling down the motorway, our side was barren; I didn’t see a single vehicle headed in the same direction as us for at least fifteen minutes. I started to panic when I saw the other side was quite the contrast; traffic had almost come to a complete standstill, the sheer volume of traffic strangling the escape of many, many people leaving the city. I remember turning to Christine and telling her that I was going to turn around. She sensed that I had been getting worried by the mass exile from New Orleans and sympathetically rubbed my shoulder – as if that was going to calm the nerves. “Don’t worry Sid, it’s just mass hysteria,” she said, stating the obvious. “It’s just panic, they’ll calm down when it’s over with. Besides, I’m sure the travel agent knows what he’s talking about. We’ll be fine.” As the windows of our 3-star hotel room blew in I promised myself never to trust the word of a travel agent over my own judgement again. Almost immediately, a young and clearly frightened porter knocked at our door and promised us solace at the hotel across the road; which apparently possessed a sterner frame that was, in the words of the porter, “less likely to collapse.” They sure know how to raise spirits, don’t they? Without a thought we joined the four other families staying at the hotel and dashed across the street, battered by the already fearsome wind and rain. And what did we get for our troubles? We got turned away from shelter in the middle of natural disaster. What the fuck is wrong with people these days? Fortunately, Christine and I were not the only terrified travellers to be turned away; we decided it would be the within everyone’s best interests if we stuck together as a group. But as I looked back across the street and saw the structure swaying in the wind I knew immediately that it would be a far too dangerous place for us to stay. Luckily, everyone else seemed to agree but for one stubborn guy and his family. He looked to be about fifty, and had a permanent scowl cast across his face. His features were rugged and weathered, and there were a couple of small scars here and there; probably ex-military. “I ain’t goin’ nowhere, boy,” he announced in a thick Southern accent through the wind and the rain, “we’re goin’ back to that there hotel, it’ll be safe enough.” And so, they did. I fear the worst for them. The rest of us managed to travel a couple of miles through the now torrential weather, keeping to the back alleys. Christine was absolutely terrified, hanging onto my arm like a small child. She almost collapsed with joy when we finally stumbled into a shopping mall full of people in a similar predicament. “We’ll be able to see out the rest of the storm in here,” a chirpy, Hawaiian shirt-wearing 30 year old father to a young family announced – I think his name was Harry. But the worst was yet to come.” - SM -------------------- ””Why can’t Sid make his own way home?” Cayle asked rather selfishly as we grew closer and closer to Jane’s Baton Rouge home. “Couldn’t he catch the Greyhound bus, or something like that… you know?” By Christ, my little brother is far too naïve for his own damn good. I didn’t feel like that questioned warranted an answer, so I dodged it somewhat. “Sidney is our brother, Cayle, and it is our duty to save him from the clutches of the evil she-wolf Jane, no matter what the cost!” I chuckled, trying to make light of the situation. Cayle merely shrugged; he was the only member of the M. clan that could still stand the company of her. In fact, I am beginning to regret leaving Grobschnit in Northern Ireland with Alvaro at the home of my most gracious friend and former tag team partner, Patrick O’Brien. I could have sent the notorious G-R-O-B in to rescue Sid and Christine from it… that would have saved the inevitable clash between Jane and I. Every time she tries to call me I put Grob on the line; she gets angry because she can’t understand a word he says. Damn, I definitely should have taken him along; she would have jumped out of her skin at the sight of the green little bastard. What the hell am I talking about? This isn’t about me or Grobschnit; it’s about Sid and Christine. Jane took them in which shows she at least has some good in that heart of stone. Doesn’t it?” -AM -------------------- ”By morning the storm was over. During the night we had to move to the upper tier after the lower area began to fill up with disgusting murky, brown, swirling floodwater. It was cramped, but we were fortunate; we were still alive. When we headed back out onto the streets was when the bomb was dropped. Debris floated down the flooded streets, wrecked builders poking out above the vermin-infested swamp water… and the smell, dear god, don’t even get me started. It was as if somebody had taken the great city of New Orleans and replaced it with a fucking rubbish dump. Negotiating this putrid, rancid lake wasn’t going to be a lot of fun, but I wasn’t going to hang around. I grabbed Christine and piggy backed her through the waters in search of dry land. When we found that dry land it wasn’t any better. This once awesome city was in ruins; houses had their roofs and windows caved in, trees had been torn from the ground, boats lay strewn across the freeways – how could this once awesome city ever recover from this? That evening, I lost a heck of a lot of faith in humanity. The city had turned into a warzone; people were robbing, raping and killing each other, and I heard rumours that youths had been hoarding guns and ammo from the local stores and using them against the aid workers that had swept through the city. What the fuck? This people are trying to help you, and you’re trying to put a bullet in their head? This was the type of shit you see going down in Iraq and poverty-ridden, corrupt African countries, not the Western world. This is the United States of America, I thought this was supposed to be the greatest country in the world? I guess we all cave in to our animalistic nature when such tragedy overcomes us. We grouped together with some like-minded individuals and slept in shifts in shop doorways; if we all slept at the same time the gangs that were running riot around the city would surely take advantage of the situation. I got talking with one guy, John Doughan, from New York City. He was in a similar situation to me in that he and his family had come down for a break. He broke down into tears when he told me he hadn’t seen his family since Katrina began to batter the city; I send out a prayer for this poor, poor guy. One night on the streets was enough, was we found ourselves constantly harassed by the disenfranchised. We took refuge in different hotels in the area, but were eventually chased out by armed police, but the constant threat of arrest was preferable to sleeping among the fetid waters and rotten corpses that now lined the streets of New Orleans. Salvation finally came on our fourth day in “paradise.” Seems like the National Guard finally got off their ass and took notice, and we were swept away, along with the rest of our group, by helicopter to the outskirts of Baton Rouge. It was there that we were picked up by Jane, who had been travelling with her and Andy’s son Marvin. Thank Christ she had little Marvin with her; he spotted us standing at the side of the road, and thus Jane was left with little choice than to pick us up. If she didn’t, and I’d told Andy that she drove past without taking notice, I think my young brother would have torn her head off.” - SM -------------------- ”When I first heard that Sid had been caught up in this horrific chain of events, my heart sank. The guy’s been a massive influence on my life, particularly when we were growing up back in Aberdeen when he kept me out of trouble. Hell, if it wasn’t for Sid, I wouldn’t be where I am today as a wrestler. For a couple of days I tore myself apart with worry; I couldn’t get through to him at all, so you can probably imagine how relieved I felt when I finally heard from him. Now, I sat in Jane’s driveway, mere feet from my ravaged brother and his wife. I felt relieved but at the same time aggrieved at having to cross paths with the witch. Hopefully she’d be out, or something. Fuck it, Sid’s more important.” - AM -------------------- Andy M. slammed the door of his airport rent-a-car behind him, as his younger brother Cayle slid out the other side. Taking a deep breath in, Andy removed his aviator shades and slid them into the best pocket of his button-up black cotton shirt. He turned around to the familiar sight of his old home, currently occupied by Jane and Marvin. It wasn’t a particularly big house, but its natural wooden exterior was pleasing on the eye, and it’s quiet, serene location was the perfect place to raise a kid. “Do you want me to stay by the car?” Cayle enquired, not wanting to become entrapped in any potential confrontation between Andy and his ex-wife. “No, come on in,” Andy boomed, “Sid’s your brother too…” he reminded his younger brother. And so, Cayle followed his brother up the stairs, onto the front porch and right up to the front door. With brief hesitation, and Andy banged his first against the door; once, twice, thrice. Anxiety raced through his head as he waited on the arrival of his former spouse; Andy wanted to get in, get Sid and Christine, and get out again. The lock clicked. The hinges creeked. And soon enough, Andy was face to face with Jane M.-McCluskey. In spite of her seemingly “evil” interior, Jane was a startling beautiful woman. Her dark brown hair was just beyond shoulder-length and was combed behind her ears, and her face was dominated by a pair of large hazel eyes. As well as that she was blessed with a curvaceous figure that many women would kill for. Yet, Andy took no notice of her beauty as he brushed through, head hung. “Oh, no hello then, Andy?” she cackled at her estranged husband. She stood defensively with her arms folded across her chest as Cayle sneaked in unnoticed. Andy turned, a less-than-pleased look on his face as he met his nemesis. “Where’s my brother?” he enquired. “What about Marvin, eh? No time to ask where you son is?” Andy shook his head. “Don’t throw that bullshit on me Jane, I know he’s at school right now. Just tell me where Sid is so I can leave this f-“ “Andy…” a familiar voice called out from behind the Scottish King of Cool. Andy immediately abandoned his furore with Jane and embraced his elder brother. Sid looked in pretty good shape, all things considering; he’d had a chance to wash his long, black hair, although a thick layer of stubble surrounded his goatee beard. His white Nike t-shirt was covered with dirt, however, and his black jeans too were dusty. “You didn’t have to do this, come all this way for us…” “Yeah, I did… I mean, you don’t have any money, do you?” Andy asked, as his taller brother shook his head. “Exactly, so how else would you escape the wrath of the wench?” Jane overheard this and obviously took offence. “Hey!” She shrieked. “Shut up!” Andy yelled back. From the door through which Sidney had emerged, Christine followed. Sid’s blond-haired wife was in a similar condition to her husband; her body was fine but her clothes were filthy. She sighed a sigh of massive relief at the sight of Andy and Cayle, who was doing his best to keep Jane from exploding on Andy. “Well, I see no reason to stick around, lets get out of here… have you got all your stuff?” Andy enquired. “Dude, we don’t have any stuff,” Sid said sombrely. “We left it all behind in the hotel; we weren’t about to cart a couple of heavy suitcases around a war-stricken city.” “Right, yeah, I gotcha… well, the car parked out front,” Andy said. Sid and Christine took the message, and gingerly left the well-decorated hallway, heading out into the musky Louisiana afternoon, Christine uttering a small “thank you” to Jane on her way out. Cayle soon followed, to check up on his brother, leaving only Andy and Jane. Andy attempted to leave, but before he could escape through the door Jane stepped directly in front of him, not in the best of moods. “So that’s it, is it?” she asked. “No “thank you”? That’s just typical of you Andy…” Andy sighed, letting a great deal of discontent out of his body. “Look, honestly Jane, I’m grateful for putting Sid and Christine up for a couple of nights, but come on, the least you could have done was take them back to Tallahassee; it’s not that far of a drive. These guys have gone through hell, and I bet you sat there moaning at Sid all the time about our relationship, huh?” She huffed; she knew Andy was right, but was far too headstrong to admit it. Andy knew that she knew this, and cracked a small smile at one side of his mouth. “What the hell are you grinning at!?!” Jane yelled demandingly. “You humour me, witch!” Andy proclaimed, as he finally brushed past Jane out towards the car. “What!?! SCREW YOU ANDY!” she yelled Andy, who paid absolutely no attention whatsoever as he joined Cayle, Sid and Christine in the Chrysler. Without thought, Andy pulled away from Jane’s house like Dario Franchetti would. Yet as Jane slammed the door closed behind her, all signs of anger soon faded from her pretty face. She sniffled as a small tear began to roll from her right eye. Perhaps there was more to this than met the eye.
