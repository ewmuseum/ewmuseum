I hate to do it just as bad as you hate to see it done.

15 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: earlier It’s very rare that you come across a great story teller that isn’t on television. Comedians, poets, actors; all have to be within their element. The way Eddy Estrada explained his situation to me, I’m glad we were alone. Particularly, I wouldn’t have felt secure with all those cultish-radicals watching my every reaction; God knows they’ve heard the story a dozen times over with their own inside jokes and commentary. It took them a few more minutes of convincing Eddy to tell me the story, but as soon as he agreed they were all gone. Estrada spoke, to this day, with a tone of disbelief. Dude was almost as if he were watching someone else do it with all the intentions of stopping. ”This mask-“ he says, holding it in front of my face. “-has been in my family for three generations. My-father-his-father-before-him type shit, you know? When the time came for my inheritance of this legacy, both my father and grandfather knew I was more than ready. But like a few past girlfriends who may not be too fond of me can attest to, I am a little timid.” This is accompanied by pinched-fingers near his eye, measuring the extent of his timidness—is that a word? ”They had some farmer league set-up for me to just walk into and destroy, and I tried to tell them that wasn’t what I had in mind. So I played rogue around the home for the last few weeks of summer before their league went on tour for the fall. Dodging meals, only home when they were working. I’d like to call that time my teenage years, revisited.” He laughs. “Then I finally worked up the courage to take off. We were a little north of Yuma, which is a border county in Arizona. I managed to scrounge a few bucks then took a Greyhound with a duffle bag filled with clothes and a light resemblance of what was my wrestling gear.” “So where did you go?” I ask. Eddy pointed at the ground while taking a tilted gulp from his Arrowhead water bottle. They way he drank, you’d think he was trying to drown himself. I could never personally see how people enjoyed Arrowhead as I thought it tasted like tap water. Bottles like Dasani are clearly more tasty than that shitty red-skin production. Just like all Native American products, they’re done half assed! I’m telling you, it’s America’s fault. America pushed those God damned Indians to the edge of the country, and now we have to segment off the places with the shittiest climates just to keep them happy. We forced those fuckers to become lazy. And now they’re making money off our gambling problems and our penchant for cheap jewelry. Fuck, I don’t even like turquoise! I say fuck turquoise! ”Um… what were we talking about?” By this time I noticed that Eddy had since returned his head from the tilt, and sipped Arrowhead as if he were a normal human being. Well, as normal a human being could be drinking Arrowhead. I swear to Christ I hate that shi- ”I came here. To Gary. Have you ever heard of the 4W Production Company? Probably not. It’s a group of local investors with a few affiliates spread sporadically across the mid-west. They opened this local fed, R4W, their third incarnation of a touring promotion. This would be another in a string of federations that have closed months after their promotions open under the 4W banner, but it would not be the last to open.” I’m bored. When I get bored, my eyes start wondering. It’s not like I mean to, really, I hate being rude. It’s just that I get all these pricks telling me the most boring shit, it’s like they want to hear themselves talk. This is a personality test I like to put people through: the people that apologize and get me on task, those are the people I can stomach. The ones that keep talking until there’s an awkward silence and they bring up another socially acceptable topic to keep friendliness going? Those assholes can go to hell. ”Anyways, it’s not like any of that mattered.” Eddy said, catching my eye. I could tell I was going to like this guy. But this, this is where the story got interesting. ”When I was there, it was like I wasn’t even the person I was. My mask was the same, my agenda was, but my personality… my name… I really was Filth. The name was assigned, as a gimmick. A moniker that summed up my personality.” Eddy ran his fingers through his greasy hair, I could tell he’s had a while to think of this. The way he paused in mid-speak, I could also tell he hated talking about it. ”You ever feel like you’re outside yourself? Like your watching your own self act like a person you don’t want to be, but you continue to support it? That’s what this was like.” So this is where I ask him to be more specific. ”I’ll tell you a story, one story.” He takes a deep breath. “One day, we traveled to Santa Fe. It was the day of the show, and I woke up that morning hung over. I mean, really bad. So bad bro, there was still puke crusted to the corners of my mouth when I woke up at the toilet!” We’ve all been there, I say. He tries to manage a laugh, but its hard for him to speak. “No, we haven’t.. When I woke up on the toilet, I wasn’t concerned about the pain in my stomach or the rankness of my breath. I wasn’t concerned with appearance. I was worried, because as my eyes fluttered open upon the cushion of the toilet seat, I lay face-first looking towards what I could only picture as the end of my career.” He then describes to me that he’s staring at, in the bath-tub next to his makeshift bed, what can only be classified as a defaced whore. He tells me her lips are shred all the way up her cheek; one slice up both sides like she were a smiley face. Her eyes are cold and white, the color lost due to the bruises on her neck. The woman was tortured, and then murdered. ”I didn’t stay to see it all. I didn’t want to.” Why? I ask. “I don’t want to remember.” He replies. And then he starts crying. ”You’ve been so drunk, man, that the whole night’s a blur. You call it time-travelling. You black out at times, and when you wake up the next morning, there’s a pale sketch of what you did in your brain. But as you talk to people, who were there with you as your night progressed, certain events will come to you. Like, you wouldn’t even remember going to a certain bar unless your friend mentions the name. After that, you remember everything you did at that bar, as if someone unlocked a door to your memories instantly with that one phrase.” I nodded. I knew the feeling. ”With every cut I saw on that girl’s arms, as I woke up; hazy memories of my nails cutting in to her skin as her frail arms struggle out of my grip. The bruises on her neck; only triggered thoughts of me forcefully exerting my will, my frustrations on her path of oxygen. I saw myself doing these things after they were already done. I thought I was crazy…” Thought, I say? “We’re all crazy. It’s just a matter of potential.” Eddy says. awkward silence….for a few minutes….really, this is rather ridiculous When we finally decided to stop stepping on egg shells, it was during a complementary bowl of cereal. Just sitting around the ring waiting for topics to present themselves was actually a little boring. So I asked for Cinnamon Toast Crunch. He did one better with the Cap’n Crunch. crunch crunch crunch “So what’re you doing this for?” I say, between scoops of crispity crunchity goodness. crunch crunch crunch ”I don’t know. ‘To give back’ is such a lame answer.” He says. Thankfully. “Shit. I guess it is a partial contribution to the business. My own skills, I feel, are too valuable to just through away. It’s like I’ve developed a talent that can be spread, for the better. My own mannerisms can do more than benefit myself.” He takes a bite. crunch crunch ”I guess it’s just the fact that I want to be able to spread my legacy, not necessarily my name. While the fame will be good, it’s always been about the relationships you make, the people you meet. With this school, I thought ‘maybe I could affect someone.’ Turn a person’s career around for the better, make them a big shot. Because most people are going to say, ‘that kid was alright, he caught my eye for a few seconds.’ But who I do it for, are the ones that marvel his talent. I do it for the ones that say ‘Well, he certainly raised the bar for those to come in his wake.’ You know why?” crunch crunch crunch crunch I say, crunch, “Why?” ”Because I am the one who raised that bar.” crunch crunch crunch ”And what about you?” I think about the question. It’s not like I have to, my father has asked me it plenty before and on motor function I repeat the same bullshit each time. ”Me? Well, it’s undoubtedly… the power.” A confusing answer at best. The man’s face shows me that he wants a better retort, so I decide to explain. ”I love to be placed in a situation where you watch one man, a man with hardly any talent, go straight to the top. Because he was in the right place at the right time, or merely because he has a cute face, that man shot straight to the top. Fame, fortune, women all followed him. This man is a hack, undeserving, piece of shit. And I love it that in this business, I will be able to be in the position to ruin him.” I see Eddy’s jaw slow down. No more crunch. ”You see, before it was about the money. People knowing my name, hating me and loving me and buying my t-shirts. I was so concerned with my public perception, I began becoming self-conscious. Well, recently I learned its all about being yourself. And, me, well I’m vindictive. And I’m vengeful. And before, there was one guy who I know I’m better than, stealing my spot light. This guy’s name; Tim Shipley.” ”And what do you plan on doing?” he says. ”I found the federation he is in, currently. As if he were hiding from me, hah! Well, recently, I have been developing a plan to place us head-to-head. My course of action has been meticulously thought out, and if all goes well, I will be in the ring, wrestling Tim Shipley in a matter of months.” ”But why?” “Because that’s who I am. The fans will love it, I don’t even have to consider them. It’s all about being myself. I can say the first thing that enters my brain and they’ll eat it up, I just never realized it before. I’m going to try and not let the public perception overwhelm me; this time it’s all about making an impact. And trust me, there is no greater impact that can be made, than after I finish fucking with the mind of Tim Shipley. All’s I need is your help to get me in the ring, again.” crunch crunch crunch He finished his cereal and gulped his milk. I still had a few corn kernels floating around. ”Well, I hate to be an asshole, but honestly,” crunch “you aren’t the type of person that I mind helping. You’re whole direction behind this, it’s wrong and completely fucked sideways. I don’t want to help you on your deluded path to revenge.” Bull Fucking Shit. I came here for a reason, there was significance behind this. I was honest to this asshole about my means of determination, and he’s undermining that? Fuck him. ”However, my mind can be changed. A little ‘you scratch my back’ type of deal, if you know what I mean.” I examined him for a moment; this was his plan all along. He never planned on screening my psyche. This asshole planned on using me from the start. I turn my head away to ignore this fact, or prevent me from biting this guy’s head off. Instead, I look out the tinted glass door, on to the red full moon that hangs low but shines bright. Something like that, it puts you in perspective. ”Strawberry Moon.” I say. ”Doesn’t seem to be anything quieter, more calm than a full moon, eh?” you have no idea
