The Return?

29 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hate is not amused. His nervous pacing and quick glances as the clock on the wall of what is essentially a room with a bed and a chair reveal a man troubled by something far greater than what he has been used to. Surely a part of it is the on-going interest by the police into the activities of Hate and his Cult. Normally, he wouldn’t be too worried, but now one of the cops has taken a personal interest, and Hate knew better than anyone that bonds formed with blood were the strongest of all, albeit if only one end of the bond honored it. Angelica, his little Angel, sat on the bed, her legs crossed, her eyes wandering up and down Hate, from head to toe. She was the instigator of this all. Hate opened his mouth to speak… Angelica sat on the bed, her legs crossed, her deep-blue eyes shining on her face, framed by her brown hair. The eyes darted up and down Hate, the person she had taken as her spiritual beacon of hope and light. But now her leader was uneasy, and it made Angelica uneasy as well. She knew what made Hate mad: well, at least partially. He had gone to great lengths to find the person who had hurt her, hurt her in the worst way imaginable, only to come up empty-handed. She sighed and lifted her legs up on the bed and hugged her knees, stealing a glance at Azagtoth standing on the far wall. The fanatically loyal freak was without his facepaint for once, and looked almost normal. Well, almost as normal as Angelica could see a person who had tried to cut her throat in an abandoned warehouse. But she didn’t care. She had become immune to that stuff: become immune to the abuse at her old home and then again in her new family of the Cult. Angelica sighed again, but then she heard Hate speak. Her head perked up again… Azagtoth lounged against the wall, his head rolling lazily from side to side. He was without facepaint for once: seeing his pale flesh had been unsettling ever for him, as it was usually covered with layers of white and black paint. But tonight, Azagtoth hadn’t bothered. His raven-black hair covered his face and his eyes, which were fixated on Hate, his leader. Azagtoth had made it known that he would do anything for Hate, even die if it came to that. His leader had never questioned his loyalty or dedication. And now, he had been shocked to find out that all the months of the work he had put into his leader’s goals, alongside fellow members of the Cult, had been for the unbearable whore lying on the bed. Intolerable thoughts of murder and torture ran in Azagtoth’s mind, as they ran every time he saw her. She brought nothing to his life except misery and mental agony. How he had wanted to slit that throat… But Hate had intervened, and Azagtoth wasn’t brave enough to raise a hand against him. Hate spoke; and Azagtoth listened, as he always did. He listened carefully. ”This… Matter at hand is most unsettling in nature. It seems that due to the history of yours that we haven’t dwelled too deeply upon, my dear Angel, your father has taken a rather inconvenient interest in the affairs of me and the Cult. It is not the first time the pigs of the system have wanted to get me in their hands, and I daresay it won’t be the last time either, but now he seems far too determined to see this to the end. I must say, I find it quite perplexing that you would never mention this to me, my Angel. It is a matter of utmost importance, and one that would surely grab my attention should I be able to hear about it in time. Well, nevermind: it is of no use to cry over the spilt milk, as the saying goes.” Angelica hung her head in shame. It was because of her that all this attention was being diverted onto Hate. All the authorities, looming over his head. She mentally chastised herself every night as she fell asleep, punished herself verbally for the damage she had caused Hate. Azagtoth hung his head in shame. He knew that it had been a risk carrying the bag with his trophies around, and an even bigger risk to trust it on this whore. He hadn’t told anyone yet, but he hadn’t seen the bag in weeks. Where it was… He didn’t know. Hate kept his head high, but feelings of guilt ran in his head. He had allowed his whole organization to work for another man, unwillingly and tricked into servitude. Already he could hear the dissention amongst the ranks of the Cultists. They were not happy. And Hate was not happy either. He had preached the words of independence, of violent isolation and of the survival of one for so long that the whole Cult was behind the ideal. And when their leader began to halter and sway, they began to do so as well. And that was something that bugged Hate beyond every other imaginable concern he might have been facing just now. ”Now, my Angel, you must understand. I am still protecting you. The Cult is still protecting you. But we have taken a blow: the person we hunted down for violating you turned out to be a fake, an imposter, a mere illusion. The real culprit remains at large and hidden from our sight. The Irish pig, Paddy O’Shea, though fully deserving of our wrath, was merely a decoy pointed for us by a fellow pig called Mike Wade. How I allowed myself to fall for this delusion, I can only take the blame for it myself. And I can never forgive myself for betraying the things that makes the Cult what it is today.” Hate looked at Angelica: so young, so pretty, so naïve. She thought of the flesh more than anything. Her passion was undeniable: but her lust was for Hate as much as it was for the Cult. If her wish for the worldy things could be curbed… He then looked at Azagtoth: not as young, definitively not as pretty, and yet almost as naïve. His thoughts were on the spiritual: the Gods of old, the teachings of the Cult, and the words of Hate. His passion could not be denied by anyone: yet his pride and distrust of others beyond Hate went against the pack mentality of the Cult. There was no room for just two members to be dependent on each other: they had to all lean together. ”However, I have now assessed the situation, and we shall act upon it. For now, you two, my closest confidents, I leave you with these words. My Angel… I am sorry that I have dragged you through this, but ripping open your old wounds will help them heal in the long run. Remain strong under the crushing pressure today: you will be the crushing pressure tomorrow. And Azagtoth… Continue your journey by my side. There will be blood, and there will be victories. But there will also be losses and defeats and dead ends. You must grow to accept these as well. The Cult can lose, but it cannot be defeated. Never destroyed. Never made nonexistent. That is not the nature of the Cult, or the nature of myself. Neither of us is a quitter: and I know you aren’t a quitter. Stay.” Hate’s words rang empty in his own ears. What a moral-boosting speech that was, he thought bitterly. It was almost time: time to travel again, to yet another wrestling show. He found himself thinking about the business that had sucked him up. The glitter, the glamour, the oiled-up bodybuilders. All of them wanted to exclude him from the party. Isolation was fine with Hate, but as long as he sought it himself. Forced, he would fight back on principle. Such was the way of Hate. Azagtoth listened to his leader’s words, and couldn’t help but feel their flatness. His heart filled with sorrows. The AWC was doing this, he had decided. The wrestling world was draining his leader of his qualities and had begun turning him into a poser loser just like the rest of them. Azagtoth wished he could take his knife to all of them while they slept and get rid of them, for ever. That way, his leader could be free. And when his leader was free, Azagtoth was free. Angelica buried her face in her hands. She knew that things were slowly spiraling out of control, and she was at the center of it. Her father wanted Hate’s head for ”kidnapping” her. She had taken Azagtoth’s bag of ”trophies” and thrown them away, a decision he had regretted immediately after she had done it. But she couldn’t confront either one of them about it: they’d kill her, no matter what she said or did for Hate, the Cult or Azagtoth. A tear of fear escaped her eye. A tear of frustration formed in the corner of Azagtoth’s eye, and quickly he wiped it away. No tears were to form in Hate’s eyes, though they tried. The Fifth Horseman could not cry, not anymore. He had lost that gift forever. How he cursed it at nights. Cleanse the Impure. Raise the Warcry.
