God's still dead. Stop asking.

5 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/5/06 While Captain Suleimon's heart still lay with Turkey, his home for now was Philadelphia. It wasn't so much because he felt some affection for it. The streets were dirty, the people rude, the food too greasy and rich. It was out of a sense of familiarity. As the old saying went, 'Familiarity breeds contempt.' Suleimon had contempt for the city, but theirs were the newscasts he'd watch. Thanks to his Slingbox, his trusty laptop and free high-speed Internet connection at his hotel, he could watch the news from Philly even in Vitoria, Spain. On said news this evening... 'Next up on NBC 10 News at 11, local mothers are petrified of giving birth to the Antichrist on 6-6-06. What can they do to avoid giving birth? Find out next.' Groan. A voice in his head said, 'Look on the bright side, Cap'n. At least Muslims aren't the only ones who are religious idiots.' However, Suleimon doesn't listen to the voices in his head. Probably a good thing. But back to the original train of thought. Groan. People slaves to their superstitions, their mysticism. Who cares if they complicate the birthing process and perhaps cause themselves or their babies to die, he thought. They won't be giving birth to the Antichrist! These were the people who need to be clocked upside the head with a steel chair... he kept thinking these malicious thoughts. They needed to be knocked out of their religious fanaticism. They needed to be brought into the real world. For all the Imam's shortcomings, he at least was always there for his students. He always had words of encouragement for them after match sims. He would listen and advise his students in times of personal turmoil. He even celebrated birthdays with modest parties that included a nice cake and maybe a present of some new ring gear. He was as much a friend as he was a trainer and spiritual advisor. He also conducted training in a very relaxed atmosphere. Sure, it was rigorous; you had to work hard or else he would flat-out tell you that you needed to shape up or ship out. He had shipped out several young men who were too lazy to do what it took to be a professional wrestler. However, if you had the work ethic, he was very easy-going. You could work at your own pace, develop how you wanted to develop. Perhaps Imam's easiness provided very little comfort against the shock of their new teacher. Yes, Malik Rahman was no Imam, and quite frankly, he didn't give a shit if you didn't like that he was no Imam. His hardness earned him the nickname 'The General.' For one, he was very strict in his training regimen. The day started at 8 AM and didn't end until 5 PM. If you were off schedule, you had to stay an hour later and run 100 laps around the gym. While match sims happened twice a month at best with Imam, they were mandatorily twice a week with Rahman. The only words to his students were orders and instructions. When he was in a good mood, the instructions had a pretense of being friendly. When he wasn't, well, let's just say the meanest of pit bulls didn't have the bark he had. And he never spoke to his students outside of the confines of the gym. After 5 PM, he was distant at best, invisible at worst. Basically, he'd only talk to his pupils outside of training time if they needed to tell him they were leaving the school for a bit to go home or attend to an emergency. Tariq was ambivalent to the change. In a way, he was happier without Imam, because for all of Imam's warmth and friendliness, he never really showed it to Tariq until towards the end. He was always showering it on that suicide bomber with his attentions. Plus, he really felt his skills were improving. Working rigorously for eight hours a day (they had an hour for various breaks), building muscle mass, honing in-ring skills... Tariq was a natural hard worker. Yet there were times he missed the Imam, especially on days when he felt under the weather and could take it easy. He liked the freedom, the flexibility. He liked that Imam let him take whole days to study other wrestlers on tape instead of train physically. There was a lot less stress in the air. Despite the new environment that Rahman brought with him, the days were unremarkable at first. The atmosphere of the academy had changed somewhat, but after a month, it became business as usual. Sometimes he was gruff, but Rahman was easy to get used to. A month later though, things got a little less unremarkable... 'Greetings! Allahu akbar!' The doors to the gym flung open at around noon. Everyone stopped what they were doing and turned their heads to the doorway to see a man dressed in a khaki colored dashiki, sandals, head-wrap and sunglasses. His greeting startled the pupils and Rahman alike. 'I am here! I have come to train in the presence of the great and holy Imam! Where may I find him?' Rahman stood up out of his stool in a corner of the ring and approached this interruptor of his daily routine. 'Ah, are you the Imam that I have heard so much about?' the traveler asked as the gruff General was approaching. Rahman got right up to where he was standing, looked him up and down and stared right into his sunglasses. 'No, I am not. I am Malik Rahman. The Imam resigned his post and left the school to me.' A look of curiosity came upon the traveler's face. Before he could say anything, Rahman interjected, 'And who might you be?' 'My name is Habib al-Shikhah, and I made the trip all the way from Riyadh.' 'Riyadh, eh?' Rahman had a wry look on his face. 'Tell me, Habib, doesn't news travel from Istanbul to Riyadh more quickly than in a month's time in this day and age?' 'A month? You mean that the Imam's not been here for a month?' Habib paused, furrowed his brow and rubbed his chin. 'It's unfortunate that I left when I did then.' 'Left when you did?' Rahman was incredulous. 'It's a few hours via plane and a few weeks if you drove.' 'Yes, but it's two months if you travel by camel.' 'Camel?' 'Yes, I travel by camel on the hajj, and since this was to be a wrestling pilgrimage for me, I felt it appropriate.' Rahman thought that this person was insane, but kept that to himself. 'Well, I'm sorry that your journey was for naught, but despite things not being exactly to your ideals, I do extend you a stay here at the school.' 'Well, I'm certainly not heading back another two months to Riyadh. My will, and God's will, is for me to become a professional wrestler.' And just like that, the school had another student. This student wasn't some novice though. For one, his body already had 'the look.' He was toned and muscular, but he wasn't a roidbeast. When he took to the ring, he showed he was much, much more than just another body. Unlike the last Muslim darling who populated the academy, Habib could move. He showed incredible agility. He could leap three-quarters of the way across the ring. And the power... the academy pros, aka the 'wrestling dummies' all took turns taking moves from him, including a sick looking over-the-shoulder piledriver. Today, it's used by pretty much every other wrestler on the circuit, but remember, this is 1993. Back then, an Emerald Fusion was a sight to behold. The buzz was deafening around the academy. This new specimen... he could definitely be the one who put the school on the map and break through in Japan, England, Mexico or even possibly America? Even Tariq was a little awestruck. Despite the fact that his overly theatrical evangelical Islamic entrance put him off a little, anyone who was that good could be fun to work with. He wanted his limits challenged. For about a month, he had been considered the top prospect at the school for his acrobatics and use of innovations where others stuck to restholds and vertical suplexes. After the first day of training, Tariq approached Habib. 'Hello.' 'As-salaam aliekum.' [sic] 'So... you are no novice.' Tariq found it very hard to make conversation. 'No, actually, I'm not. I've been very diligent in my training back home, but I felt the need to be challenged, both physically and spiritually. I heard so much about the Imam that I felt I needed to make the trek.' His voice trailed off momentarily. 'But now I see the Imam is no longer here. I know my physical side will be challenged, but my spiritual side... well I think that I'll have to be the one who does the challenging for you.' Habib smirked. Tariq found the encounter to be very uncomfortable. Habib looked Tariq up and down. 'You're on the scrawny side. How is it that you're the best this place has?' Tariq raised his eyebrow. He was somewhat offended by the question, but he didn't want the newcomer to feel he was, so that was the only outward sign he showed. 'Oh, well, I'm actually studying a new style. High-flying... aerial, high-impact from above, that sort of thing.' 'That's very interesting. I've seen tapes of that. The Great Sasuke and Tiger Mask. Oh, well I'll simply have to work with you sometime.' Tariq nodded and both of them turned to go their separate ways. Habib stopped short though, turning back towards Tariq. 'Hey, I'm going to go pray now. Would you like to join me?' 'Oh, no... no thanks.' 'What? Why not, do you like to pray in private?' Another uncomfortable conversation. Tariq was tempted to lie, but for one, his father taught him he should never do such things, and two, the Imam was not there anymore, so his charade was no longer necessary. 'No... I... I don't pray at all.' Habib furrowed his brow again. 'Oh, that's a shame. Well then maybe it won't be such a pleasure to work with you.' Tuesdays and Thursdays were the normal days for match sims. Rahman liked to keep the most rigorous portions of training in the middle of the week. Even as much a taskmaster as he was, Rahman knew that on Mondays, his students weren't fully recovered from the weekend and Fridays they were too ground down from the week. Wednesdays... well, they need a day to go over what they did in the day before and what they were going to do the day after. Besides, three days in a row would be cruel. The General was a hardass... he wasn't a slavedriver. On Habib's first Tuesday, he was raring to go. As was the custom, Rahman read out the pairings at 8 AM, and then they went on until the matches were over. He went through every name on the list leaving the two biggest for the end. 'al-Shikhah, you get Suleimon.' A giant buzz went up amongst the students. The new tiger taking on the lion at the top of the heap. The new king of the jungle would be decided so quickly. Upon hearing the news, Habib nodded very cockily while rubbing his chin. Tariq could overhear what Habib was saying. Things like, 'This heathen won't stand a chance against me,' or 'I'll crush him with the guidance of Allah,' or 'Those who don't know God cannot defeat the righteous, and I'll prove that.' The people Habib was talking looked uncomfortable to say the least, but they still listened to him and nodded. Still though, he exuded arrogance, and this made Tariq very unnerved. Tariq started to feel pressure in his gut, like he had a stomach ache or a gas bubble that wouldn't work its way out. Why was this young man so sure he'd beat him? It wasn't because of the sheer fact of his faith, was it? Even Imam never said that faith in Allah was a valid tiebreaker in the wrestling department. Still though, there was something in Tariq that made him unsure. Each match sim that preceeded his showdown with Habib felt like it took three hours. The clock ticked so slow; even the cruiserweights like Ozlem seemed as if they were lumbering like clay giants. Finally, it was time. Tariq stepped into the ring, clapping his hands with the rosin bag, tenatively pacing in his corner. Habib strode into the ring, cocksure and headstrong. One of the academy veterans called for the bell, and it was on. Habib overpowered Tariq at first, using his height and weight advantage to keep the smaller high-flyer down. Tariq was flustered at first. He couldn't get a strike in edgewise, let alone get free and start using his superior speed and agility. Finally... *thump* An elbow to the gut. Finally, Tariq had an advantage. Spin-heel kick! Habib held his face, as Tariq's heel caught him square in the nose. Flying forearm! Knocked Habib right on his ass. Flip-over neckbreaker! Tariq had Habib reeling. The crowd of students was cheering every one of Tariq's moves. Tariq went to the corner, climbed to the top rope. Habib staggered to his feet, obviously punch-drunk. Tariq waited for him to turn around, then leapt. It would have been a perfect cross-body from the top rope. Would have being the operative words. Habib caught Tariq, and in one swift motion, he turned him over his shoulder... *WHAM* Emerald Fusion piledriver. Tariq's head thudded off the canvas. The pin was academic. 'Praise Allah!' Habib shot up, arms in the air, fists pumping. 'Through Allah, all things are possible!' He hooted and hollared. The students clapped and cheered, some of them with sour looks on their face. No matter their feelings, they still had to give Habib his due. That piledriver was SICK. They hadn't seen anything like that before. 'Come, let us celebrate by giving praise to Allah!' Habib went into the prayer room, and those who were closest to Imam, the truly devout Muslims among them, went with him. Tariq, though, sat in the ring. His neck was sore, the top of his head felt compacted. Rahman lingered as well. He looked at Tariq with a stern face. 'Do you know why you lost?' he asked the young man. 'I... I...' Rahman wouldn't let Tariq answer. 'You lost because you didn't do whatever it took to win. You were hesitant.' With that, Rahman got up off his stool and left. Tariq tried to call out to him, but the gruff General would not answer him. The next day, Tariq trained off by himself, dwelling on his experience from yesterday, really stewing. No one tried to make any contact with him. They knew how he got when he was really mad. He became cross, so cross that even a 'Good morning' would be greeted with a hiss. They let him be; Tariq was off by himself, working with the heavy bag, lifting weights, jumping rope. He spend that Wednesday deep in thought as he trained. How did Habib defeat him? Was it really his faith that let him have the advantage? Or was it just that he's a little better than Tariq at this point? These were the things that bothered him so much to the point of nausea. Even though he tried his best to channel out the background noise, he couldn't help but listen in on Habib's rantings. 'It goes to show that faith in Allah conquers all.' 'I really showed him, it's not just the heavy bag and the weights that build a wrestler, it's moral training, spiritual training.' 'By the time I'm done, I shall bring God back into this school.' Tariq couldn't take it anymore. He slammed down his towel and headed up to his room upstairs. No one followed after him, probably because no one saw him huff off. No one except the General... The next morning, Tariq was the first student downstairs. His eyes were red and bleary. Hair desheveled. He looked like he hadn't slept the night before. In reality he didn't. Rahman noticed this. 'You're early.' 'I... I know, sir.' Rahman looked at Tariq slyly. 'You do know that you're going to have to run 100 laps, right?' 'Yes, yes sir. That's why I've come early.' 'Well, you may want to save your strength for after training. You're getting Habib again.' 'But... why? You never repeat...' 'I know I usually don't, but you need to learn a lesson.' 'But... but...' Rahman did not answer him again. Tariq kept trying to get him to answer, but Rahman was silent. When he saw the attempt was futile, Tariq went back to the heavy bag. One by one, the pupils filed into the arena, waiting for the announcement of the matchups for the day. When everyone got there, Rahman read off the matchups, again, closing with Tariq and Habib. 'Oh, has the little runt found God?' Habib asked snarkily? 'No...' Tariq said emphatically from the heavy bag. 'Well then, I assume this will be easier than Tuesday.' Once again, the minutes seemed like hours for Tariq, but instead of pensiveness and nausea, he burned with rage, stewing in his anger from the humilation on Tuesday. His mind and heart raced as he thought of ways he would render Habib's bones from their joints. Finally, it was time again. Tariq was ready. So was Habib. The bell rang. Unlike last time, Tariq came out like a house on fire. Habib was ready for him though, because Tariq's movements in the ring were very unwieldly. He flailed instead of punched. He flung himself instead of jumped. Habib countered every single one of his moves with ease. Once again, Habib put Tariq away with his Emerald Fusion. The next thing Tariq remembered was one of the academy veterans putting smelling salts under his nose. Everyone stood around him, Rahman over top of him to his left at his head, Habib on his right at his feet. 'Just like I thought. Easier than the last time.' He sneered. Tariq would have reacted if he weren't woozier than a sorority girl waking up from roofies on some frat guy's cough. 'Boy, if you knew what was best for you, you'd come and pray with me. You may never be as good as I am, but the least you can do is earn the good graces of Allah so that he may find you a suitable replacement profession.' Habib left and the same people who left with him to pray Tuesday left again. The others, Ozlem and the like, stayed with Rahman and the vets around him. Tariq walked woozily over to Rahman's stool, where he sat. The remaining students stayed until they saw Tariq was alright, then retired to their rooms for the night. The veterans did the same. Rahman was the only one who remained. There was a surreal silence for a few moments as Rahman paced around the ring, arms behind his back, staring at his possibly concussed student. As Tariq slowly regained his full senses, Rahman stared even more intently at him. Finally, Tariq opened his eyes fully and jumped in the stool as if he got a sudden rush of goosebumps. 'You still haven't learned your lesson.' Tariq was still too woozy to talk, although he could understand everything Rahman was saying. 'You can't defeat him unless you give him everything you have. Flailing at him is not giving it everything you have. Do you understand me?' Tariq acted as if he didn't hear the General. Rahman stood up straight, crossed his arms, tapped his foot and then leaned over right in Tariq's face. 'DO YOU UNDERSTAND ME? Tariq's posture shot straight. His face became pale. He nodded almost instinctively. 'Good.' The General's demeanor calmed down considerably. 'Now, you have laps to run.' Tariq nodded again. He struggled to get up out of his stool, but Rahman left the ring altogether and headed towards the exit to the rooms upstairs. Tariq looked over, confused. 'Sir... you... you aren't going to watch me to make sure I run them?' 'No... I trust you.' Tariq was dumbfounded. The next day, Tariq came downstairs with an ice pack on his head. He moseyed his way over to the heavy bag, only to find it was occupied. 'Hello, heathen.' Tariq ignored him. Although the hate welled visibly in his eyes, he wouldn't dignify Habib with a response. 'What's the matter, can't get on your favorite escape?' He clenched his fist. 'Hey, you have to let those of us who actually let God into our lives work out.' Tariq balled both his fists. He looked ready to pounce. Thankfully. 'Boys, boys...' The academy veterans stepped between them. 'Tariq, let it go,' one of them said to him. Tariq unballed his fists and shook his head. HE walked away. 'That's right. Now it's time to bless this bag with my fists... cleanse it of your godless stain!' The other students looked on uncomfortably at the situation. Never before had they seen so much strife at the school. Habib spent the next nine hours straight pounding the heavy bag. Every time he hit with his right hand, he shouted 'Allahu akbar!' With his left, 'All things are possible through God!' Tariq spent most of the day either on the weight bench or with the medicine ball. Habib's taunts pierced him continually, like shrapnel from a nailbomb, but he put on a front, like it didn't bother him. The constant bombardment caused Tariq to stay in his room the entire weekend. He lived off semi-stale pitas and warm bottled water in his room, not wanting to see a single face. Friendly or otherwise. He spent the weekend stewing, even worse than before. Students would complain to the elders of hearing random noises coming from Tariq's room, spastic noises, crashes, every three hours or so. He at least slept though, a marked improvement over most of the week, but the human body can only take so much. Monday, it was more of the same. Habib was right there at the heavy bag when Tariq came downstairs. 'I heard you had quite the weekend, Tariq.' Tariq didn't answer. 'We all heard the noises from your room. I knew you were a heathen, but I didn't know you were possessed by the devil. It's all so clear now.' Habib laughed sardonically. Tariq just walked away. 'Allahu akbar! 'Through God, all things are possible!' Nine hours again, non-stop, no breaks. Tariq started to feel the mental torture physically. His neck was in knots, tense. His stomach, ablaze. His feet were sore. Muscles ached. The next day, Tariq was the last one downstairs. He was still on-time, but he was the last one to get into the crowd. 'I guess I can start to read off the matches,' Rahman said, almost disinterestedly. He was sitting in the ring, but not on his stool. He sat in a steel chair. 'Where is your stool, sir?' asked Ozlem. 'Is it that pressing of a matter that you must interrupt the match sim announcements?' 'I'm sorry, sir. I was just curious.' 'Well, since you've already broken this up... I broke my stool last night. Now, are you ready?' The students nodded. 'First match... al-Shikah and Suleimon.' There was a buzz. Twice in a row was unprecidented, but thrice... that was downright uncalled for. 'Oh man... getting the thrashing out of the way early I see. I guess now we see what this really is, a preview.' 'Quiet...' Habib wouldn't shut up though. 'A preview of your sorry career. Me, the God-fearing main event star, against the jobber, the curtain jerker. This is nothing but a mere warm-up...' 'I said QUIET!' Rahman announced the rest of the matches, and then Habib wasted no time hopping into the ring. Tariq didn't either. He paced the ring like a rabid bull. Habib stood up straight, tall. 'You know what, the more I think about it, the more I think this is a waste of time. I'm just going to paste you again. It's boring.' Tariq stopped in his tracks and barked. 'Are you done yet?' 'No. In fact, why don't we make this more interesting.' Habib closed his eyes and spread his wingspan out. Rahman was shocked by the display and rose out of his chair to get a better look. 'I'll give you the first shot. A free shot.' Tariq looked at Habib, unsure of what his intentions were. He didn't know what he was going to do himself. A free shot. How would he spend it? How did he know this wasn't a trap? What could he do? Why would he do somethign like this? Why was he so arrogant? Why did he taunt Tariq so? Why? Why? The whys repeated in Tariq's head. Then the voices began to well. 'Allahu akbar! 'Through God all things are possible! 'Allahu akbar! 'Through God all things are possible! 'Allahu akbar! Through God all things are possible! Allahu akbar! Through God all things are possible! Allahu akbar! Through God all things are possible! Allahu akbar! Through God all things are possible!' 'AAAAAAAAHHHHHHH!' In a swift fit of insanity, Tariq swooped over to Rahman's corner and grabbed his unoccupied chair. In an instant, he folded it, cocked it back... CRACK! Habib fell backwards like a ton of bricks. Tariq held the chair in his hands, hyperventilating. And Rahman... Rahman looked incredulously at Tariq, almost as if he was going to snap his head off clean from his shoulders. He approached the chair-wielding student before he turned right back around to a prone Habib. He stormed over to the fallen Arab and stood over him. 'WHERE IS YOUR GOD NOW? HUNH? WHERE IS HE? COULD HE SAVE YOU NOW? HUNH? GET UP!' Rahman kicked him lightly in the ribs. 'GET UP!' Rahman's nostrils were flared. His eyes were wide. Then, the look of rage on his face dissipated into a look of disgust. 'On second thought, just stay there. Take your bloody time.' Rahman turned to Tariq, who was still hyperventilating. 'You... I shall talk to you later.' He turned to the rest of his class. 'Training dismissed today, early. Enjoy it. It won't be a habit.' The crowd didn't disperse though. Some of them stood there in awe, still shocked as to what just happened. Some of them were actually nodding and smiling slightly. The academy vets were the most aghast. But Tariq... Tariq looked at Rahman, then looked at Habib. He looked at the chair, and at Habib again, at the carnage he had just caused. He dropped the chair, then dropped to the canvas, sitting, hyperventilating. He threw up in his mouth a little. But then, something strange happened. He swallowed his vomit, and then looking at Habib once more... he smiled. He smiled. His stomach eased up for a second. His mind fluttered. He was smiling. The Captain shut off his Slingbox and his computer. He had become too disgusted watching a report of the women who were considering inducing labor or having C-sections a day early, or artificially withholding labor to have them a day late. He wished in his heart that he could knock some sense into them, forcibly if he could. But until then, he had the satisfaction that he'd have the chance to destroy the Leviathan, a monster straight out of the books of their holy, wretched Bible, as the ultimate symbol on the Day of the Devil.
