What Is A Trendy Twenty-Something?

28 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28/08/05 The people queued up in the space between the arena’s exterior wall and the red rope, hung in slight arcs between gleaming silver poles for as far as the eye can see. The common people. They queue for the evening’s spectacle. They queue because they have to. A silver Lexus ES 2005 creeps up to the front of the line, purring like a panther with dead white luminous eyes. The Twenty-Something’s have arrived. Sally and Mike alighted from the vehicle, Sally speaking loudly on her petite mobile phone, “...okay sweetie, alright honey, bye sweetie, alright honey, ciao!” the snaps the slender device shut and places it in her handbag. Her dress is the new Karen Millen; her waves of brown Toni and Guy hair frame her nose job and bleached teeth. Mike, picnic hamper in hand is making sure the TTS entourage are ticked off the guest list as Harry and John step out the car. Each in smart/casual Next trousers; the pair adjust each other’s collar of their matching London Scottish rugger shirts and fetch their sports bags from the boot. All eyes are on the TTS crew, as Harry says thank you to Dwayne, their afro-Caribbean driver, “Keep it real Dwayne!” As the Twenty-Somethings march into the building the sky opens up, drops, fat as garden peas, of cold water begin to soak the filth of Nottingham as they inadvertently inhale exotic scents that cost more than the value of their attire. An attractive lady, perhaps in her early thirties, certainly not as young as her twenties, greets the Twenty-Somethings, “I’ll have the house Chardonnay, if it’s not old then just don’t bother” Sally is straight to the point, “I’m Alison, I’m the floor manager, who may I ask are you?” the response rife with exaggerated false politeness. At this point Harry offers his chapped hand, destroying Alison’s with his crusher shake. “I’m Harry, we’re the Twenty-Somethings, the Trendy Twenty-Somethings” Names on Alison’s clipboard are checked and crossed and the young debutantes are lead through a series of grey cinderblock corridors, buzzing like wasps with jaded strip lighting. The Twenty Somethings are left to their own devices in a shabby dressing room as Alison the floor manager walks off wondering, ‘what exactly is a Trendy Twenty-Something?’ They’re wealthy, but they’re not rich. They live in neighborhoods where crime is low, the cars are four by four monsters, or petite and trendy and the streets are lined with luscious green trees, tall as houses. They are not celebrities, but they carry themselves as such. They live soap opera lives and find drama in the smallest of incidents, yet are a million miles away from the gritty working class Albert Square. Harry and John, content with and prepared for the raw nature of their dressing room go about stretching and changing into their gear. Mike and Sally are distraught, there is no wine cooler, no cappuccino machine, beanbags or jazz music; and it is the same as everyone else’s dressing room. I’m sure you’ve met a trendy Twenty-Something. They are the ones who go to celebrity parties, but aren’t the celebrities; merely hip extras to fill out the elaborate celebrity drama. They are deluded in this regard and spend all day in trendy cafes in the wealthiest parts of London drinking Doublemochalattefrappacinos, and chatting at length about their brief encounters with models, photographers, artists, designers, exclusive night club owners, as though they had been friends since school. By night they are these cardboard cutouts at the most elite spots in town. Sally and Mike have begun to sip away at the bottle of champagne from the hamper to calm their nerves. They feel like tramps drinking alcohol in such surroundings. Mike hasn’t felt so ashamed since he had to buy a sweatshirt from Gap because Next was undergoing refurbishment. While Trendy Twenty-Somethings are the bottom of the food chain in the upper echelons of our social hierarchy; they are better people than you and I. They are far from common. They got better grades than we did at school, yet still got invited to the best parties. They got into better colleges than you or I, and those who had to go to university (most already have various lucrative family ties, Daddy knows some one who knows a guy who...) got into a better one than you or I. Everything you do they throw slightly more money at and snub your efforts at life as being common and filthy. Alison the floor manager works her way through the labyrinth of grey passage to tell Harry and John they had been called for their match. John, perhaps uneasy turns to Harry, his closest friend for as long as he can remember, “You nervous Harry?” The monstrosity turns his head to an acute angle, his neck popping like bubblewrap. The pair and their entourage head through Nottingham’s National Ice Centre and are stopped in front of a stage curtain. Harry parts the material slightly and gazes momentarily at the damp public; a mass of cagoules, cheap t-shirts and naff shoes. Turning to John, Harry replies, “Not in the slightest mate.”
