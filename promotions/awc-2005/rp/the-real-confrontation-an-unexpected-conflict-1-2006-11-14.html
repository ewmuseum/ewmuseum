The Real Confrontation? - An Unexpected Conflict (1)

14 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Nov 14/06 After all the smoke had cleared and November 7th's Fresh! had come to an end (with another mushy wrap-up between Crisis and Aimz, no less), I was ready to depart the arena with a smile on my face – something unfamiliar to me ever since inking a contract with AWC. After working hard and contending for championships, I remained without any tangible reimbursement that could be displayed on my mantle, or shown to my significant other. In any case, winning the Triangles qualifying match was another first step towards accomplishing this. The competition would certainly be heated, but I felt like I was up to the challenge. I was most intrigued by the mystery briefcase, a prize that had perked my interest ever since Wade's announcement. Was I ready for the Transatlantic championship, or the grand-slam package? Maybe, but as a second-tier superstar, I had other goals in mind – the briefcase topped the list. The return to my hotel in Phoenix, Arizona was an uneventful one. A bed awaited me in a room many floors above the lobby, and lugging my wrestling gear through the front doors, muscles aching and legs lagging, the prospect of crashing for the night was quite tempting. In fact, it had been my plan ever since leaving the arena. A quick burrito from the restaurant, eight floors up the elevator, and an interesting encounter with a Pakistanian bell-boy later, I came to my door with a sigh of relief. The door swung open after being unlocked with my card, and I walked into the hotel room. My bag thrown on the floor, I turned into my bathroom and opened the faucet taps. Water came streaming out into cupped hands, which I splashed into my eyes and face, which still felt somewhat greasy even after a wash at the arena. I turned the water off and shook the water off my hands before turning to the tub. Interested in a shower before I embarked on a good-night's rest, I turned it on. Twenty minutes later I was in bed, my eyes doping as I listened to the Daily Show in the background. And then, a light knock at the door. I considered not answering it, but decided against that and threw the covers off, rustling out of the cushioned mattress that wanted to swallow me in its comfort for a sleep I had been patiently waiting for all day. Apparently, this was not meant to be, and after turning on the lights to the room I grumpily unlocked the door and opened it. My eyes widened. “Natalie!” I said with elation, seeing my significant other standing in the hall wearing a cute grin. My grumpiness evaporated – I hadn't seen her in a while. “Hi, Cedric,” she responded curtly, opening her arms. We embraced. “I hope I didn't wake you,” she said, biting her bottom lip nervously. “Don't worry about it ... here, let me take your bag,” I offered. I took the bag and turned into the room. She followed behind. It wasn't long until the arrival had past and I was settled against the headboard, and Natalie sat on the foot of the bed. It was awkward as a result of what had transpired almost a month ago between us and Lucas, but neither of us would admit to it. “I saw your match from the airport,” she said. “Congratulations.” “Ah, it's not a big deal,” I responded modestly. Natalie smiled at me, then looked towards the window. I decided to introduce the conversation that we didn't want to have. “Natalie ... do we need to talk about what happened in London?” She knew what I was referring to as my eyes bore into her; hers averted mine by focusing on the a tree outside the window, its branches swaying due to a sudden pick up in wind. “I've been avoiding you for that reason,” she confessed. I frowned. “You didn't have to ...” “It's complicated, Cedric,” she said shortly, locking eyes with mine. Looking down at the bed-sheet, I shook my head. “Of all people I think I know that,” I said quietly. After lull in the conversation, I looked at her. She was fighting off tears, and my fear of the conversation taking a turn for the worst became reality. “I don't know why Lucas did that, Natalie. I don't know why he involved you. There's too many things that I just don't know.” “Who are you going to believe, me or that lunatic?” she accused suddenly, something I wasn't expecting. “Well, Natalie ... you haven't given me anything to believe in yet,” I replied, trying as hard as I could to lessen the impact. “And do I need to?” she challenged. “You're my husban--” she stopped abruptly, then looked away, and continued, “Or should be.” “Listen ... I don't know what to think, okay?” I admitted. “I just .. need some time. There's a lot going on right now, and-” “Time?” she interrupted. “TIME?! It's been a month since we've even seen each other, Cedric. And you didn't go out of your way to call me, or come see me ...” tears began to fill her eyes “Well, it's kind of hard ...” I replied harshly, not knowing where to take my defense next. “Well, it won't be anymore,” she said quietly, wiping the tears from her cheek. “I'm moving to America, with our son.” Feeling like I just ran into a brick wall and like I should be elated at the same time, I sat with my mouth agape, trying to formulate the response that I was supposed to give. It was as if there was no agreement between what I should say and what I wanted to say, or what I really felt. The latter was most uncertain. “I don't know what to say,” I decided. “I – I can't believe this.” I figured that was the sort of ambiguous reply that was needed to convey my clash of emotions. “I'm glad you're so happy,” she shot at me, sarcasm snaking off her tongue. “I'm sorry, Natalie ... I'm still shocked by what happened with Lucas ...” “What happened to the secure, confident Cedric Riley that I once knew?” she challenged. “Since when did you become so doubtful of everyone, even yourself? Do you even trust me, anymore?” Taken aback, I fumbled an attempt at a response, then recovered. “It's not that simple ...” “It never is with you, Cedric,” she snapped quickly. “Until I have a reason to be simple, it shouldn't be easy,” I defended. “Why can't you just accept what happened? Why is it necessary for you to continue to question everything for most than what it's worth?” she pushed. “I love you, Cedric ... but sometimes I feel like I don't understand you, and it's because you make it seem like you don't understand me. Why won't you let me in?” “How am I supposed to explain myself?” I accused. “Why do you continue to doubt me?” she said hotly. “I may not have always been there for you, Cedric ... but I've been looking after your son ...” I shot a malevolent look her way. “Don't bring Seth into this, Natalie. We agreed that you would look after him while we let things blow over ...” “While you moved on with your life?! And what about me?!” she screamed. I had had enough. I got up from the bed and moved towards the door, completely flustered by everything that was thrown at me at once. Natalie and I had never been a couple to argue, but things had gone completely awry. I wasn't convinced of how strongly I felt or how much I should have questioned what Lucas had done. As time had moved forward, however, it was as if I couldn't let the possibilities slip by without consideration. “Where are you going, Cedric ...” Natalie whimpered. I reached the door. “I need some fresh air ...” “I'm coming with you ...” she began. “No,” I said shortly. “I need to think things through.” I paused. “Alone.” Having gotten up from the bed, she stopped her trek towards me. “Don't leave me like this, Cedric ...” she said, almost threateningly. “I'm sorry, Natalie,” I said. “I never meant for this to happen ...” And I was gone, closing the door to another desperation plea from my girlfriend. I walked briskly down the hall, turning the corner towards the elevator. I didn't expect Natalie to follow me – she was a stubborn woman, but her begging only went so far. She would let me go, for now, anyway. I got into the elevator, thankful that I was alone. The doors closed – I didn't push a button, and instead retreated to the back and sunk to the floor, feeling the elevator move. I didn't know whether it was going up or down. I just ... let it take me, something that I had recently refused to allow by anything or anyone. Was I just the asshole that I thought I'd never be? What was wrong with me? I felt like shit. There was too much going through my head to process it all at once – so many undecided thoughts and mixed emotions. I fumbled them all, much like I had done in my argument with Natalie. I swallowed hard. My wrestling career was on the verge of flourishing, and I was in conflict with the amazing woman who had allowed it to do so in the first place. A considerable amount of time went by before the elevator doors opened again and somebody entered. It was a man in a suit, carrying a briefcase, much like the one I was bound to pursue in a couple weeks at Triangles. He gave me a nod which I returned. He pressed a button, and the usual awkward elevator silence followed. I propped my elbows on my knees and leaned forward, looking at the floor. “Bad day?” the man asked casually, noticing how much of a wreck I must have looked like. I looked up curiously, then backed my head against the wall. “You could say that.”
