Pallas Athena 2: Origins

11 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/11/05 Way in the past: Megumi Hamada was born in the pro wrestling business in Japan. Her mother, Nikkou, was a wrestler as was her father. Her mother was Japanese, and wrestled under a mask as Jaguar Sky for small independent women's promotions. Her father was a gaijin, a foreigner. However, before she was born, her father left Japan never to return. Megumi never met him. Her mother's family outright refused their daughter to marry a 'barbarian' and so he left her to raise their unborn child. Megumi grew up in Japan and followed her mother's footsteps in the wrestling world. But unlike her mother, she did not wear a mask. She was a wrestler-in-training for her career there, doing prelims and dark matches, all a part of her training. She did not adopt the name 'Butterfly' until after her initiation into Tyger Shuri-ken's school, a very famous wrestler and trainer. The better she became, the more people saw something in her as a possible superstar in the future. Tyger Shuri-ken was a great teacher. He was creating the next big joshi celebrity and box office draw. She worked during the days in a restaurant downstairs from her dojo. She trained in the morning, worked shifts as a hostess and waitress, and then trained even more at night, This was the world of joshi wrestling. There were many rules that all the young girls had to follow. No smoking, no drinking, and no men were the big three rules. She stayed the course. The wrestling community didn't care that a half-gaijin was wrestling and slowly becoming a popular figure. Gaijin were constantly brought into the country as monster heels or doing tours and were very well respected, so she didn't stick out in any way. In fact, it helped her become even more popular and promoters from all over Japan would ask for her to work their shows. Her professional life was taken care of, but her personal life was another matter. Her wrestling was her main focus, but her mother was her everything. Her family in Japan refused to even acknowledge her existence but her mother was always there for her. Nikkou also taught her everything she knew about the sport. Megumi's wrestling inside the ring was styled very much after Nikkou. Her mother was very proud. Megumi even traveled to Mexico where she trained for a few years in the lucha-style, to round out her discipline. She came back to Japan an even bigger star then before. Her mother was very proud and decided to continue her training. With each new move she learned, be it the 'Floating Hammer' or the 'Sky Driver', Nikkou would give her love and praise. But she was still a strict disciplinarian, and instilled this into her daughter. Losses in the ring would be met with harsh criticism, but also with a lesson to be learned. But when she won a big match her mother was there for her as well. The Hamada clan was known for very stylistic and artistic tattoos, having many of the family raised following this art form in the traditional Edo period. And with every big accomplishment, Nikkou would take her daughter to a very secret place, a very special tattoo artist who would place a symbol on her inner forearm. Horimono, or traditional Japanese tattoos were magnificent drawings with incredible detail. Especially for their size which were quite small, they were intricate and beautiful. Different inks were used made from all sorts of strange and rare materials, color derived from pressed charcoal, herbs, flowers, or animals. And they were each unique, symbolizing different emotions, elements, animals, or Japanese sayings. But together they all connected in an intricate pattern that over time took the form of a butterfly. This was Megumi's early life and it was good. She had her mother and she had a purpose. It all came to a screaching end one night. Just like that, a young woman's life was turned inside out. A simple burglary gone wrong ended with Nikkou Hamada stabbed to death. The felons were caught and convicted and sentenced to the death penalty for their heinous crimes. The story of Nikkou Hamada, Jaguar Sky, had come to an end. Her daughter's had just begun. It is here that 'Butterfly' Hamada's story begins. After her mother's funeral she was approached by Tyger Shuri-ken. He wanted to continue her studies under his tutelage. But first he had to make sure he had found the 'real thing'. What that meant to Tyger is anybodies guess. Monday, September 2nd, 1999 Nagano Mountains Japan 'Tyger-san.' Megumi stood up where she was just kneeling before a great orchid. She fingered the medallion that was given to her, the one token her mother had left her. Her mother's side had wanted to take the family heirloom, but it was in her mother's will and they could do nothing about it. Realizing that she was fidgeting, Megumi dropped her hands to her side and turned to face the wrestling legend. Tomoaki 'Tyger' Shuri-ken nodded his head in acknowledgment, then glided toward her with the grace of an incomparable warrior. A masked wrestler, it was not common to see one out of costume or without their traditional mask. Megumi was in awe and felt powerless and insignificant compared to the much taller and well-muscled martial artist. Megumi took a few paces forward to meet him and then swept into a low bow. 'I am honored, Shuriken-san,' she said. 'You may call me Tomoaki,' he corrected her gently. 'It has been a long time since I was important in the scheme of things.' The man studied Megumi for a moment, then turned his brown eyes to the trees behind her. 'I thought I might find you here.' 'Sir?' Megumi's brow furrowed in puzzlement. Tyger glanced over at Megumi. 'This was your mother's favorite tree when her father owned his house here on the mountaintop. The Nagano Mountains have never been the same since his death. Then the house burned down. A pity. Your mother was never the same after that.' 'You knew her? You knew Jaguar?' Megumi asked thirstily. In her excitement she clasped Tyger's forearms impulsively. So few people could tell her anything of her mother's early life, and Tyger hadn't been a part of it with Megumi in the picture. Her hunger for information made her forget her manners and that she was still in awe of this legendary wrestler. 'Briefly, many years ago,' he replied. Slowly he freed his arms from her spontaneous grasp and turned to once again stare at the beautiful orchid that stodd before them. He would throw Megumi a glance every now and again, and she wondered what he was thinking. Was he trying to come to some conclusion or decision? Megumi turned toward the orchid and stared at it, trying to picture her mother standing underneath it when she was young and happy. A small smile crossed her face as she thought of her mother in that way, something she rarely had ever seen. They trained together, and she was her daughter, but something was missing from Jaguar Sky for those sixteen years that Megumi knew her mother. Something had been torn away from her. In her mind's eye she painted a picture on the canvas of her imagination of her mother, happy, childlike, growing up here on the mountain with the trees and the orchids, the blue jays and the snow monkeys. She imagined the glimmer in her mother's eyes amidst all this beauty and it warmed her heart. 'Yes, I suppose this was her favorite place.' Megumi smiled gently. The sound of sorrow in her voice drew Tyger from his thoughts and reflections and he put a hand on Megumi's shoulder in silent condolence of her pain. 'I am sorry for your loss. But if I may ask, what will you do now?' Startled, Megumi drew back, staring at the master trainer blankly. She had not thought about it at all. Jolted in a new realization, she shook her head with a far off look on her face. Just then a small sparrow flittered down from a tree and grabbed a small twig and just as quickly, flew away into the sun. 'I can stay here, rebuild the house. Take care of the mountain,' Megumi said impulsively. 'And if a great wind had just cut through the trees with a howl, would you then have decided to become a kite flyer, or ride in hot air ballooons?' A smile flickered across Tyger's face. Megumi looked down at the snow covered ground, embarassed by her child-like outburst. But her vice was strong and insistent. 'No, I think my mother would have liked it fo me to stay here and make this place my home. I could build a small dojo and train here. I could continue my studies. I have always wanted to become better. Here on Nagano, I could have the solitude and the mountain's own strength to guide me.' Her shoulders straightened and her chin came proudly up as she spoke. Her hand drifted to her forearm where she wore her traditional tattoos and she looked down at them with honor. She thoughtlessly ran her fingers across her mother's medallion around her neck. HER tattoos. HER medallion. 'I see.' Tyger noted the movement of her hands, his eyes narrowing as he studied the patterns on her arm. 'Your mother was a very good tattoo artist. She practised in the Edo tradition. Did she teach you much of the art?' 'No, I am afraid I do not.' Megumi looked down at her arm again. 'I enjoy them, they are beautiful. And they were a gift from my mother, so I cherish them. But I do not know much about them. I am not an artist, I am a wrestler. My mother only said that the tattoos were for me as I came of age, each one separate yet woven together with the others.' Tyger looked up at the necklave around her neck. 'And the medallion?' 'What? This?' Megumi looked questioningly at the master trainer as she held out the necklace around her neck. 'This was my mother's and she wore it everywhere, everyday. Until the...' 'Until the funeral,' Tyger finished gently. Megumi swallowed hard. 'Yes, until the funeral. It was written in her will.' 'Do you know the medallion's history?' Tyger asked inquisitively. 'This thing? No, does it have a story? I haven't heard it, I thought it was just a family heirloom.' Megumi handled it like a child, unsure of what she possessed. 'Are you sure your mother told you nothing of the medallion's story?' Tyger asked again, taking a quick look at Megumni's tattooed arm. 'No, nothing,' replied Megumi, and looked up with puzzlement. 'It is just that then, I am afraid. She wore it all the time, like you said. I thought it had some special meaning. I am just... happy to see that you wear it now, like your mother,' Tyger said with sympathy. Megumi smiled at the man and then looked down at the medallion again and sighed. 'But what else did your mother give you?' asked Tyger, this time with authority in his voice. 'What?' asked Megumi. 'Jaguar Sky was a tattoo artist, but more importantly, a great wrestler. And she would not have given you the honor of wearing those tattoos if you were not worthy.' He cocked an eyebrow in her direction. 'Oh, I can fight. Mother taught me. I am very good.' She stated the last part with a child's frankness. 'Are you indeed? We shall see.' Tyger Shuriken stepped back in a fighter's stance, and before Megumi could take a breath he came at her like a hissing cobra. Almost of their own accord, Megumi's hands came up to defend with lightning thrusts and quickness. An intense emotion flooded Tyger's brown eyes for a moment, but then his rounded face took on an unreadable and mysterious expression. 'Your reflexes are good,' he commented in an even tone. 'How good is your endurance?' As if to prove a point Tyger attacked with a renewed ferocity. He lunged with strikes and swung with kicks. He feinted one way and attacked another. With instinctive grace, Megumi leaped aside and blocked all his offense. Dirt and snow kicked up off the ground as both warriors angled for attacks and parries. The master's eyebwors rose, more with speculation then surprise. He spun a kick around and then threw out a vicious chop that no one could see coming. As it smacked across her chest, the mountain erupted with an echo. Although the child seemed to be momentarily stunned by the pain she did not turn away or allow it to mess her focus. She blocked a second chop and connected with a short running knee to the chest. Tyger withdrew, dancing back several paces, lowering his gaurd, but Megumi crouched down in a an alert position, awaiting a second barrage. Excellent, thought Tyger silently. The child showed a great instinct for fighting and the beginnings of good judgement. Still testing her he advanced only to be met with a Mexican armdrag. Both fighters kipped up after the move. Another big chop slapped across Megumi's chest, then Tyger's special triple claw attack. Her flesh flew from her face, like the talon's of a cat slicing across skin. In response, Megumi spins in with a backfist then connects with a handspring hurricanrana. Tyger goes flying in the air but the highly dexterous fighter lands on his feet. Megumi wipes the blood from her face from a crouched position, a smile crossing her face. Tyger circled her carefully, looking for a weakness but not really finding one. This made him very happy. Going for a low kick feint, Tyger got too close and with the quickness of a serpent Megumi launched herself in the air to connect with the 'Twin Killing' double back kick, Tyger flipping over and to the ground in a pile of dust and dirt. He looked quickly at the girl with pride as well as surprise at the move, and stole a look at her forearm. He saw what he had hoped, the glowing tattoo of the crane. Nothing more could make him happy. Her artform was pure. She was the real deal. Tyger had come here expecting to find a weak little half-breed but instead a fledgling hero stood before him. Megumi stood there ready to attack again but Tyger put up his hands. 'Enough. Uneeded violence on this mountain is unseemly. We can continue your training another time.' Megumi looked surprised. 'Training?' A score of questions crossed Megumi's mind and flashed across her face. Tyger looked at her as he dusted himself off. He could see the confusion was one of hope, not one of loss. And he would need her to hope, to think that under his wing she could reach heights that no one in the world had ever reached before. To be continued...
