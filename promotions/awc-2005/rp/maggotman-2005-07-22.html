Maggotman

22 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 22/7 Detective Bach buried his face in his hands. He had been held for four hours already, with no definite reason given by the sergeant on duty or any of the lab people who kept running about and putting reports down on his partner’s desk. His partner, Jonathan ”Joe” Reeves was nowhere to be seen. Sitting back on his chair, Bach kicks away at the bolted legs of the metallic table occupying the center of the small interrogation room he had chosen to wait out this inane assignment. How he had wanted to go tell them to fuck themselves and go home… But he couldn’t. This was something special, something worth keeping the day shift detective on overtime. Bach hoped it wasn’t anything too bad. He had just had late lunch: he wanted to keep it inside. For what seems like an eternity, the only sound is Bach sighing once in a great while and the steady tap-tap of him kicking the table leg. Just as he once again begins to entertain the idea of leaving the station right now, the door clicks open and swings out like a ghost. Bach nearly falls over, but regains his composure and stands up, just as his partner Reeves steps in, decked in a suit that has seen better days. Offering a half-hearted greeting to his partner, Reeves turns around and closes the door before walking over to Bach and setting a black briefcase down in front of him. Bach, however, will have none of it, as he just looks at Reeves with a murderous stare before blurting out some of his frustration. Bach: ”Seriously, what the hell is this? Can’t this wait for tomorrow? I got a wife and two kids at home, and they’ll forget my face soon if I stay here for anymore overtime! And that’s not even mentioning that I’m not getting paid for this shit either, at least not according to Sergeant O’Donnely…” Reeves: ”Sheesh, just relax, will you? It’s not really a cakewalk for me either. I’m supposed to be at my daughter’s soccer game tomorrow, and I could use the sleep, but here I am, just as deep in this shit as you are.” Detective Bach just scoffs at ”Joe” and sits down. Reeves sighs and sits down as well, snapping open the briefcase and pushing it in front of Bach. As he inspects it, Reeves removes a tape recorder and some tapes from it and let’s Bach finger through the paper documents. Crime scene reports, witness testamonials, pictures… GRUESOME pictures. Bach’s hardened eyes linger over the puddles of blood and what look like bolt cutters before he turns back to his partner, unfazed by the photographs. Bach: ”So? What about this? It’s a murder scene. You can get this kind of shit a dime a dozen on any given weekend. Look, Joe, just give it a rest, and let’s go home… There’s nothing important about this case that warrants our interest beyond our normal time.” Reeves: ”You’re wrong, for two reasons. Reasons why this warrants our time.” Bach: ”Hm? What reasons?” Reeves: ”First of all… This isn’t a murder. The victim’s still alive, although in a pretty bad shape.” Bach perks up instantly. The carnage in the photographs hinted strongly at murder, and he had dismissed them as just one more drug-raged random killing performed by some street junkie. But alive after all that? This could be interesting… Or even worse. Reeves: ”And second… We fear that it’s a serial. And he’s gonna do it again.” Bach freezes on the spot as the words wash over him. Serial? Goddamned. Serials and other nutjobs weren’t his cup of tea. They were the worst of the worst: animals in human skin, preying on the weak and the lonely like the human society was the open prairie and the humans were just stumbling little gazelles… The very thought of such a cold-blooded predator made Bach shiver. He shifted through the pictures once again, more reluctantly than at first, and then turned back at Reeves, his face devoid of emotion. Bach: ”Well… What are we going to do about it? It’s the Feds you want, not us. We can’t handle this kind of shit on our own, you gotta go to the higher ups for this kind of stuff…” Reeves: ”The FBI has been informed already, but there are two things working against them: time and knowledge. It takes time for them to mobilize a profiling team and an agent squad. And they don’t have access to the informants we have: it’ll take time for them to gather the data as well. So we’re running our own investigation for a few weeks… You know, get the boys on the street, get the word out that we’re looking for whoever this guy is. Just put some pressure on him.” Bach: ”And what if he takes this as an invitation for a game of Cat And Mouse and takes a new victim? Instead of hiding in a hole, what if you encourage him into new action?” Reeves opens his mouth to speak, but then shuts up and looks down at the tape recorder in front of him. Bach can see that his partner is tired and at a loss of words: he can’t reply to the stinging comments his own friend has just made. Taking pity, Bach sighs and then pushes some of the paperwork around with his finger, resting his head on his other hand. Bach: ”OK, enough of that… So what’s the case? Who did what to who?” Reeves revives instantly and he starts shuffling through the papers. Soon, he puts a file in front of Bach, with a picture of a young woman lying under hospital sheets so that only her head is visible, sporting several bruises and cuts on the face. Bach looks at it intently, memorizing every feature of her face as Reeves begins to speak. Reeves: ”The victim is Jessica Torres, 21 years old. A farm girl from Arkansas. Been in the city for about a year now, works as a waitress for a restaurant. Lives on Paradise Lane, you know, in one of those brick housing complexes? Bad neighborhood, there.” Bach nods and flips through the file, verifying everything his partner tells him. Yeah, Paradise Lane attracted all sorts of weirdoes. Druggies, skinheads, mentally ill people… There were rumours about a cult infesting the area, but the department had long since turned a deaf ear on the informants who tried to peddle the story to them. There was a line on what the police were ready to believe. Reeves: ”We got a call from an upstairs neighbor at about 4 AM. Says she heard screaming from Roscoe’s apartment. We sent a patrol over there to check it out, but nobody answered the door. After talking to the neighbor, they deemed it sufficient to break down the door. They found Roscoe in her apartment, severely maimed and bleeding.” Just as Reeves gets into the injuries, Bach flips open a new page with pictures and descriptions. Immediately after reading the first few lines, his eyes bulge and he flips the file shut. Bach leans over the table and holds his hand over his mouth as Reeves looks on symphatetically. After a few moments, Bach turns the file open, breathing heavily as he reads. Bach: ”my god…” Reeves stands up so that he can tap his friend on the shoulder to show his support before he continues. Reeves: ”As I said, extremely mutilated. Left hand severed at wrist, right hand severed just above the elbow joint. Also, she was missing her right foot and half of her left one. The patrol immediately called for backup and an ambulance. She was taken to a hospital and we did a beefed-up sweep of the area. We got one witness upstairs, several others to verify her story… And the weirdest thing of all.” Bach looks up from the file at Reeves and then looks back at it, scanning the list of injuries and their possible causes. Reeves: ”Outside, we found an old black Volvo parked on the sidewalk. After inspecting the vehicle, two patrolmen discovered a black plastig bag on the back seat. After entering the car, they took it out and brought it to the investigators. Inside the bag were…” Reeves looks over a list in his hand and starts reading from it, as Bach turns ever-increasingly white as the list progresses. Reeves: ”Four severed fingers, two which had been chewed on and one which had been picked clean… Eight severed toes, with bite marks on some of them… A hand, severed at the wrist and missing all five fingers… A jigsaw with blood on it… Bone splinters, presumably from the arm or the elbow… An empty bottle, with a recipe for 500 mg of heavy painkillers on the side… And a bloody hankerchief.” Bach can’t take it anymore and doubles over, clutching at a nearby trashcan. He vomits violently as Reeves patiently awaits for him to finish, looking sadly at his partner and somewhat white in the face himself. As Bach straightens out, Reeves lets out a shaky sigh and takes a few controlled breaths to calm himself down. Reeves: ”Y… Yeah. The car belonged to a man on the other side of the street, who we immediately took into custody for further hearings.” Bach interrupts him, wiping his mouth with the back of his hand before looking at Reeves with strained eyes. Reeves starts feeling pity for his shaken comrade and just looks down on the table as Bach speaks. Bach: ”Who… Who would do such a thing? Chop a human up like that… DISSECT THEM, like a sample on a lab table. WHO?” Reeves: ”I… I think you should hear these. These are some of the tapes we got from the preliminary interrogations we got from this case… The first is the victim, Jenna Roscoe…” Bach: ”How was she able to participate? I thought she almost died!” Reeves: ”She did, but… She recovered fast. Too fast, to be true. The doctors couldn’t explain it… So we asked her some questions. Just listen to this: you might be shocked even further.” Reeves puts in a tape labeled ”Roscoe Questions #1” and rewinds it. Bach leans closer, rubbing his hands together absent-mindedly as he waits for some answers to the questions in his mind. Reeves (on tape): ”OK… This is Detective Jonathan Reeves, at the Park Memorial Hospital room #226… The time now is 16:14 PM … First, is your name Jenna Roscoe?” No answer. Reeves: ”I’ll ask you again… Is your name Jenna Roscoe or not?” No answer, again. Reeves: ”OK… Is there a specific reason why you refuse to answer?” A Woman (very weak voice, Bach has to strain to hear it on the tape): ”I… I don’t know. Names are… Human…” Reeves: ”Please elaborate.” Woman: ”I… I can’t explain it. I… I don’t feel like I’m human. I feel like … Like an object. I objectify myself.” Reeves: ”What… What do you mean? Objectify?” Woman: ”I feel… … Not human. I feel like an object… That’s why I don’t feel pain… … Pain is human.” Reeves: ”You’re… An object? What kind of an object?” Woman: ”What do I objectify myself as? Well, most of the time, I… I feel like… Food.” Reeves: ”You… Never mind… … Was there someone in your apartment this morning between 2 AM and 5 AM?” Woman: ”Yes… yes there was. A man… … … A man.” Reeves: ”What was he doing there?” Woman: ”He asked me what I wanted the most, what I wanted more than anything in the whole world. So I told him, and gave him my hand…” Bach looks at Reeves, puzzled, and Reeves stops the player. Bach shakes his head and then looks back at the player, as if expecting the faint voice still to continue. Bach: ”That… That makes no sense. Why is she speaking so calm? Or so good, for that matter? She was mutilated to near death!” Reeves: ”I can’t explain it, and neither can the doctors. Just listen…” Reeves (on tape): ”What did he do? What was his response?” Woman: ”He smiled… … Smiled kind of weird. Like… … Like weird. He had a knife. A sharp knife. He… I think he took my fingers first. One at a time.” Reeves: *a moment of silence cuts the tape at this point, apparently as Reeves and any others are stunned by the revelation. Reeves recovers quickly, but you can hear his voice shaking as he continues* ”He… He took them? Cut them off? With the knife?” Woman: ”… … Yes.” Reeves: ”Were you aware and concious during this process?” Woman: ”… Yes.” Man (speaking from the background before Reeves can ask his next question): ”Did it hurt?” Woman: ” Of course it hurt. It hurt like no other… It hurt, but it didn’t matter. I wanted to be hurt… I think I screamed at that point, and that’s when they heard me.” Reeves: ”They?” Woman: ”My neighbors. They heard the clamor… I think that’s why the police came eventually… I guess my neighbors alerted them…” Reeves: ”Can you… Can you recall what you screamed?” Woman: ” What did I scream? I, I’m kind of… Kind of hazy. I really don’t remember. Can’t recall. It’s… Too hazy.” Reeves: ”Are you sure you can’t recall?” Woman: ”… … …” Reeves: ”We have a witness who has stated that she heard you scream ’Tear my limbs’ at the assailant.” Woman: ”Tear my limbs? That… That’s what they heard? Oh, now I remember. I remember… Funny that. Yeah, I said that. Well, I didn’t really say THAT. I really said… I said… ’Desecrate me. Tear me limb from limb. Eat me. Eat me…’” Reeves: *another stunned silence in the room, and you can hear furious scribbling as people take their pens to their notepads. Reeves changes the subject, his voice filled with disgust and horror* ”Are you aware of the extent of the damage that’s been done to you?” Woman: ”I know… I know my arms aren’t there. I can’t lift them… … I’m pretty sure my feet are bad too… Funny. He wasn’t supposed to…” Reeves: ”Did the hospital staff tell you about your legs?” Woman: ” Yeah, they told me. I… I can’t see them yet. They won’t let me. Not until the reconstructive surgery specialist sees me. I guess he’s in for a shock…” Reeves: ”I’m sorry for your loss.” Woman: ”… … …” Reeves: ”You weren’t aware of this being done to you at the time of the crime?” Woman: ”I don’t even remember him taking my toes. I guess I was out by then… But there was a point where I couldn’t stand anymore. I felt really tired and exhausted, and happy… Dunno… Maybe that’s where he had taken my feet, because I couldn’t stand…” Reeves: ”We found evidence that… Evidence that suggests that he used bolt cutters to… Commit at least parts of this act. Did you ever see him with a pair of bolt cutters?” Woman: ”Yeah, he… He took it out of his bag… … said it was an instrument of his craft. Maybe that’s when he took my feet. Who knows. He had the tools for it… maggotman…” Reeves: ”Excuse me? Could you repeat that?” Woman: ”… … …” Reeves: ”Please repeat what you just said.” Woman: ”… Maggotman.” Reeves: ”Is this Maggotman your attacker?” Woman: ” Maggotman. That’s what he called himself. And he spoke of an One-Eyed One. Maggotman… I think he said it came from… From…” Reeves: ”Yes? Did he give you a real name? An address?” Woman: ”He said he’s the reaper of maggots. That’s what it came from. Maggotman.” Reeves: ”The reaper of maggots? Please elaborate, Miss.” Woman: ”Maggotman…” Reeves clicks the tape recorder off and looks back at Bach, who has buried his face in his hands and is fighting the urge to vomit again. The image of this crime is overwhelming: a young woman who willingly lets a strange psychopath mutilate herself and eat parts of her… And doesn’t even sound saddened or enraged about it. More like ashamed than anything. Bach cannot understand such complete lack of humanity, a human being stripped so low, into a food packet to be opened and carved up for bite-size bits at its owner’s whim. Bach: ”She didn’t say anything else?” Reeves: ”No. She just kept repeating ’Maggotman’ to herself. We couldn’t get anything else off of her.” Bach: ”What about the other witnesses?” Reeves: ”There were no visual witnesses for the crime itself… But we have an older lady upstairs who says that she heard weird noises from Roscoe’s apartment around 3 or 4 in the morning. Then at 4 AM, when she was in her bathroom, she heard Roscoe scream and called the police. She was very disturbed by what Roscoe was screaming about.” Bach: ”I would have too… What evidence do we have?” Reeves: ”We got… I think it’d be better if I played you the tape.” Reeves removes the tape from the recorder and places in a tape labeled ”Evidence #1”, clicking it on play while watching Bach for the whole time. Reeves (on tape): ”… This is Detective Jonathan Reeves, the time is 20:04 PM… Could you please introduce yourself.” Man (on tape): ”My name is Dr. Isaac Jacobs… I work for the crime scene lab.” Reeves: ”Were you present at the scene of the crime personally?” Man: ” Yes, I was present at the initial investigation of the scene of crime, and was in the lead of the lab team responsible of collecting initial evidence.” Reeves: ”What kind of evidence were you able to secure?” Man: ” We were able to secure several pieces of physical evidence, including… Splinters of bone… Several blood samples, all traced to the victim… Bolt cutters, ID’d positive for victim’s blood, also near the afforementioned bone splinters… We found a pot, that had been, had apparently been used to , in fact… Was used to… excuse me… BOIL parts of the victim. We found animal fats, fats that… That were human and that were ID’d positive with the victim’s DNA… in the pot… that were residues from… boiling… meat…” Bach looks at Reeves, shocked at what he has heard. But more than the list of gruesome evidence that was just catalogued, Bach is shaken by how a crime lab expert can’t keep his cool with this case. Bach: ”Old Jacobs? ”Stoneface” Jacobs? Who plunges into entrails every day, twice on Sundays? How could he be so…” Reeves: ”Just listen.” Reeves (on tape): ”Thank you, Dr. Jacobs. Ike… Would you like to say something else? Off the record?” Man: ”Off the record? This is strictly off the record?” Reeves: ”Yes, off the record and confidential.” Man: ” I think it’s the most fucked-up thing I’ve ever seen. I mean… You all know Jeffrey Dahmer. I was young then, but I still remember it all. That was a sick fucker. But at least… You know, at least these people DIDN’T WANT TO BE eaten by that monster. This girl., she… She offered herself as a living meal, a living piece of meat… Like a packet of food waiting to be processed by the consumer. And this man, whoever he is, he took advantage of that… We have a credible lunatic, cannibalism, mutilation, torture… If it was up to me, personally, I’d pull my gun and shoot on sight. I’d blast the fucker off the face of the Earth. This is off the record, right?….” Bach holds his hand in front of his mouth and leans back on his chair, closing his eyes. Reeves looks on his partner, smiling supportively. He knows what goes through Bach’s mind, because it’s the same that went through his when he heard Jacobs’ fire-and-brimstone cry for revenge: HE WOULD DO EXACTLY THE SAME. Choking on his own voice, Bach makes a few false starts before he can clear his thoughts and speak again. Bach: ”Do we… So what do we have on this guy?” Reeves: ”The alias Maggotman, which doesn’t tell you much. The reference to the One-Eyed One might be an accomplice or signs of a scitzophrenic assailant. We know he came in to the apartment with at least a knife, bolt cutteres and a jigsaw, two of which have been found. No prints… He used gloves but didn’t wipe the blood off the weapons. We got one bloody handprint on the victim’s lower back, but it’s too early to tell whether or not it’s hers or the assailant’s. Plus, we really don’t have a way to check her full handprints right now: we presume that any body parts not found at the crime scene were taken by ’Maggotman’ as trophies. That’s what leads us to believe it’s a budding serial…” Bach: ”What about that guy whose car you raided for the weapons and the body parts?” Reeves: ”Oh, him? Henry Lucas, 30, unemployed. He was just as shocked as we were. He said he had no idea what the bag contained. Said that a man paid him 50 bucks to keep it in his car for a couple of hours as the man went to attend to some personal business.” Bach: ”Did he give a description of the guy?” Reeves: ”Yeah, we got a partial one. Lucas said he had long, black hair, was really thin, about six feet tall. The man also had his face painted white, like a corpse, besides a few droplets of red that he told Lucas were paint. He gave Lucas the bag, the money and then left.” Bach: ”Any prints on the money?” Reeves: ”Either he used gloves or Lucas smudged the prints up with his own. No luck there.” Bach:” Damn…” Bach stood up from his chair and took a walk around the room to help his mind to come to terms with what he had heard. There was a dangerous psychopath on the loose, one that apparently ate and mutilated people in the most disgusting of manners. The case was peculiar in the sense that the victim had been volunteering for the punishment, but it was a crime nonetheless. And who knows, maybe there were no other willing victims. Bach had a chilling suspicion that an unwilling one would suffice for ’Maggotman’ just fine. Bach: ”So, what’s our next move?” Reeves: ”Well… We got a few leads, but the strongest one connects this guy somehow to the doomsday cult we’ve been hearing so much about. The one residing in the slums around this area.” Bach: ”That fairytale? Is it even real?” Reeves: ”They got some sort of organized activity there, most likely a Neo-Nazi movement or some anarchist punks with a bad rep.” Bach stops at the door, opening it slightly ajar. He stares straight across the room and into the locked weapon cabinet of the office, holding a row of shotguns with new boxes filled with shells topping the shelves above them. He shakes his head and then turns to Reeves, grim determination in his eyes. Bach: ”We raid those fuckers tomorrow. And hard.” Reeves just nods and shuts his briefcase with a snap. Cleanse the Impure. Raise the Warcry.
