The Quick And The Dead (i)

30 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30th of August, 2006 Pop. No, not pop as in Snap, Crackle and Pop. Pop. No. Not Pop Tarts. Not Pop music for that matter, and most certainly not Pop culture. No, think slightly more malevolent, slightly more vindictive. No… not Pop Music, I already told you. Though, on second thought, that wasn’t far from the truth. The Pop in question was the knee joint of his previous victim, Emo Kid. That snapping, blood curdle inducing Pop that pierced the minds of every person stood on the sands of Anapa Beach. It was a cry of angst, a cry of pain and most certainly echoed by everybody standing on those golden grains. He had arrived on those sands, coming like a discoverer in search of the new world, in search of a new existence. Every wish harboured by settlers was rooted within his soul, but with that every rancorous purpose lurked close to the surface. He was a coloniser on the AWC shores. A smash and grab raid. Get in get out, a proper Batman job. However, things were no longer so black and so white. His realm of shadows he had previously existed in was now under intense scrutiny. He didn’t like the bright lights, the glitz or the glamour. He liked his shadows. His esoteric corners only a select few would tread. He was now public property, and most likely in a week, public enemy number one. He yearned for the days of old, the cages, the seedy bars, and the darkness, the small pay, the pride, the passion; they all seemed many a moon ago. His shadow was slowly being dissolved away, dissipated by a huge spotlight which was beginning to find itself cast over him. It was overbearing and imminent: two qualities that never sparked interest in Gabriel. His yen was not for this… this lifestyle of award shows, MTV Cribs, comments on his attire as opposed to his wrestling ability. No, he wanted normality. I mean, don’t we all? But what is normal to a Fijian Animal? His world was a far cry from this. He was a simple man, living off the basic instinct to survive. So what was so different about this? He needed to wrestle to survive, there was no quandary. In your mind my friend, there was no problem. Sasha would send him back to Fiji; back inside if he refused to compete. He was surviving, doing all was necessary. This is where those shades of black and white became tones of grey and dreary muted colours, all intermingled to create this character so complex. He wanted away from it all, to return to the plains, to return to the villages, to the smiling faces of a genuine disposition. There was nothing enjoyable about the Atlantic Wrestling Club to him: it was filled with over glorified combatants, each of some talent all vying for the top spot, not only in the eyes of their peers, but in the eyes of the public. They all sought fame from this game, this game of combat, just to be known… they were like Hollywood actors all competing for that one starring role which usually ends up going to Tom Hanks. It couldn’t be further from Gabriel Afeaki than if it crawled to the other side of the world and hid in a cave. This place just wasn’t him. It wasn’t who he was… But… and he hated the word but… it was a necessity to his prolonged stay on God’s pastures. For every second he was wrestling, there was another second passing where he wasn’t confined to a cell. For every minute of competition, was a minute without the ice cold baths. For every hour of his life now, was an hour he’d remember as opposed to erase from memory. If you begin to add up the hours, you get days, and weeks and then years and so on. From this, you construct a life. A life meant choice, choice meant freedom. Freedom meant whatever you wanted to make of it. All these added up to make something Gabriel had never experienced before… a genuine lack of purpose. Was this a joke? Of course not. Gabriel didn’t really do jokes. Nor did he laugh at them… the majority of the time. When everything in your life was guided by a purpose, since your birth, until now… and by guided I mean dictate… and by dictate I mean direct… and by direct I mean that your life is just this… this purpose. You get bored of the routine… but at the same time you embrace it like sleep. In all honesty he was scared: scared of a life free from purpose… of routine… of well… order. Everything was planned down to the last malignant detail, and now The Voice dictated everything. He didn’t know his identity… or did he just choose to ignore it? Either way, this was another one of those choices that slipped by him like an elusive Mexican. A Fijian amongst their ranks… He was fed up of their clichéd efforts; those surrounding with their personalities lacking richness and life. Their every movement dictated not by their own personal creativity, but influenced by others. It was time for a change… How would they feel about GA? Time would only tell. His body was in its usual position, stuck between two concrete slabs barely big enough for a child to lie down comfortably, especially not an animal. His thick mane of hair was lifeless, matted to his skull through thick layers of week old perspiration. They treated you like their personal plaything. The heating went from hot to cold in minutes, the air chilled, your breath condensed, minutes later you were sweating as if the sun itself was bullying you. That was just it. They were the sun in this place, the centre of the universe… and you… you were an asteroid belt, a toy, a plaything… it was like Hostel, only death was a little more infrequent. “Gabriel!” the voice boomed down the hallway… he knew it all too well. It was his own Pavlov’s bell. It meant one thing and one thing only. His face was about to be spread across the kill grounds; there was no shirking details in Fiji. People died here. Maybe not daily, but the fervent putrid stench of death clung to the walls as if even it was afraid to touch the cold, cold floor. It looked like the remnants of a serial killer’s playground: every last vestige from his victim embossed in every chip in the ground, every bloodied handprint smeared along the floor. Now… now the ground was just a dull colour: there had been so much blood on this floor, it all combined to form one large reminder. The Fijian government didn’t give a fuck about its inmates. This was an institute for death, a prison camp, a Hostel… and Gabriel knew it. The Penguin and his band of merry men were closing in for another shot at their favourite target. Ever since he broke the Penguins nose upon entry into the Hostel, the Penguin carried a chip on his shoulder… and one under each wing. He bore the fruit of jealousy: Gabriel was everything that he could never be. He, a pigmy prison guard with a complex about anything you could hand him, and most stuff you couldn’t. The world was his enemy. He knew Gabriel could break every single one of them. That tattoo on his shoulder… a mark of the highest honour among Fijian men was the salient image all the guards discussed. You didn’t earn that for being a policeman, or even worse, a Penguin. They knew… but it just wasn’t worth the trouble. He’d let them have their fun, their ten minutes in the spotlight. They knew a beating wouldn’t kill him, it wouldn’t make him confess, it wouldn’t make him anything… well… it galvanised him. Every person laying a finger on him, he made a mental note. One of those psychotic post it notes, “You’re dead…” each one said, as they each wore their respected note across their chest. The Penguin though, the chieftain, the general… he had much worse coming to him. “So, Mr. Afeaki, it’s come to that time in the week boy, when you have to face the legal team.” Backed up by three burly guards the Penguin exuded confidence. Too bad he looked like a penguin… “Now, are you coming peacefully, or am I going to have to use force?” an evil grin crept across his face, of course, by ‘I’, he means those guys, and by force, he meant ‘brutality’. Naturally one might say. Gabriel shifted his bodyweight to his haunches, before rolling forward pounding the wall as he sprang forward. His attire was something not to be desired; in just over eighteen months there had been no new uniform… they wouldn’t even clean his old one. It resembled the tatters found on every big city corner at night. He was unshaven, unkempt, unloved… he was isolated. The door began to slowly open; it was such a weight it took two men of a robust demeanour to prise from its locked position. Even with the door now ajar, there was no light. The cell he was locked in was underground: way underground. It was so deep, so dark, so frightfully lacking of life that the suns life giving rays feared to tread down here. Everything was faked; fluorescent lighting cascaded from cracks in the ceiling, the guards had an exterior of understanding and kindness to the outside world, to the inmates they were the devil incarnate. Nothing made sense. Sentences seemed to join and end and begin all in one flow… there was no logic to down here. Logic escaped reality… and reality wished it could escape itself. … so it began… “Ah… Gabriel… such a pleasure to see you again,” the Penguin tapped a perfectly formed bruise just underneath the ribcage with his baton. GA flinched back… remembering every last detail of the incident that had seen his face sewn with welts and his back left with lacerations. Who were they kidding? In any other environment, in any other walk of life they would be dead in their shoes, had they not been stuck in here… behind these bars, these rules and this system. “We have some people who’d like to meet you…” his voice chuckled; he knew he’d get the chance to dish out some punishment. “Who…” his voice trembled and quaked, his raw strength meant little here, very little: he often sat asking how the others managed the years they had put in here. It was all about mental toughness they would tell him, once you got over the fact you were in here for the long haul; it was something which you developed. The awful truth was they all deserved to be in here: rapists, murderers, robbers… each one had committed a crime that fitted the term time they were serving. Afeaki was held without trial, without an omission of guilt, without it would seem legal reasons. However, it’s strange the thing that occurs when money is banded about – all of a sudden, your world can come crashing down. He’d asked to see a lawyer… denied… asked to see the arresting officer… denied… enquired why he was here without trial… denied. It cut to the core, his life changed forever: a once burgeoning career cut short and for what? The part that kept him sane was the same part that had put him away. It was like the meaning of life, spend years searching for it without answers. His life was these four walls now, there was no denying that. They’d keep him here until the four horsemen themselves tore the roof off the establishment and sent him on his way, be it heaven or hell, anything was better than this life. His sanity was just that question that needed answering – why? It drove him through every day. There was of course no answer to his question, there never was – everything in clandestine purity, everything in shadows. Strange how AWC’s very own dark night was born from the shadowy climate he came to abhor, ironic you might argue, fuck irony… He had come to the part on the inside where he began to play things smartly, using his intellect as opposed to his considerable brawn. All that hubris, all that fake bullshit was dropped, and as soon as it dropped the batons would stop. Don’t give them a reason he was told, but to the animals who ran the institute, your existence was enough to batter you an inch from within you meeting your maker. They held life in their hands daily: no one cared if you died in here, just like no one cared how many times Jeremy J tries to bone Lara or Lia, it’s something you came to expect. The boning that is… the beatings to the outside world were veiled; this of course was one of those model institutions. Everything to the naked eye seemed in order, no signs of brutality or anything of that nature, though if you dig deep enough… into anything for that matter, then cockroaches began to emerge. Tours were always told that the boiler level was just that… a boiler, nothing to see here. How wrong they were, for on that level the ‘favoured sons’ dwelt. The ones who gave lip and the ones who questioned this shit, the ones who stepped on toes and those who stepped out of line: the wave makers. GA, understandably peeved by the situation was put down here… keep in line, and made him toe it. He played things their way for once, “Okay, I’ll come quietly.” The words fell from his mouth, it was a lie, bare faced. He’d like nothing more than to open up and unleash a right hand, just one person, it’d be worth it… surely, just one bone snap, and some blood… sanity was clawing, drifting, and coherency failing… I felt my fists clench, it was beginning to grip my body, that rush of thunder, that fix of adrenaline, that lion’s spirit combined with this panthers mind. They’d never break me – put me on a rock and splatter my brains across this world would be the only way they’d get inside this mind. I’d bide my time… you know… this fool who has a hold on my right arm, the Penguins right hand man; he was first on my list. “In there bitch…” his derogatory quip meant little to me, what they didn’t realise was that every word, everything they uttered to me was kept in a mental space. So when I’m free of these walls, I can hunt down the little sadists and disembowel them. Of course… I wouldn’t go that far, but it was a quaint little thought. I had to restrain myself on commenting – ah… fuck it, you only live twice. “… your mother said the very same thing to me last time she visited…” it was perfectly timed, one of those old modern classics that never die, like a Saville Row suit… his hand came down across my face, and the stinging sensation cleansed my pallet. He punched like a girl… I did my best to contain my laughter. “What you laughing at punk?” his voice had a tone of displeasure… I felt like muttering ‘you’, but kept my mouth shut, there was no need to antagonise him further. He was like a button, easily turned on and awoken; he threw me into the room. Ah… I’d seen this place before, I’d even heard tales of it – they dubbed it “The Confession Room”, but like I’d mentioned many a time before, I couldn’t give a fuck what they said, and never… ever… would I. His eyes scanned the room, a contingent of heavies sat behind a solitary figure at the table before him. The Penguin had selected his favourite goons for this particular encounter; it would most likely result in a black eye or two. Sitting at the table a diminutive suit sat, he wasn’t of Fijian descent, nor was he Polynesian. His smart demeanour was in contrast to those around him: his pink shirt and black tie combination was a particular fashion trend of the Western metrosexual. Small things… noticeable to the trained eye belied his stress and discomfort at the situation. His forehead was damp with perspiration, nerves and the heat would do that to you… his top button was loose and his tie was slack at the knot. He was new to this game, Gabriel was certain of those facts. Who was he? Some lawyer perhaps. The briefcase at his side, the laptop sitting on the desk, and the document to the side of it was most certainly of a legal nature. He could think of no one else other than a lawyer… the sharp jutting pain of a baton moved Gabriel closer into the room. The suit looked slightly pensive at the sight of this raggedy warrior stood before him. Gabriel turned toward the Penguin, who recoiled ever so slightly. Batman always knew how to strike fear in those around him. The room smelt of confusion, of desperation, of bullshit. How the fuck was he here in the first place? He needed some answers. Now. Or else… he’d you know, snap. Snap, Crackle and then Pop. He’d straight up Rice Cripsy someone. He noticed an empty seat next to the suit: one thing was for certain, it wasn’t for show. They never did things for show. The beatings. They enjoyed them. The lacerations. They loved it. No one was an example in here. They were all an example. A plaything. GA just hoped, he wouldn’t remain flavour of the month for too long. He’d been told he thought too much, he wasn’t vocal enough. Some called him banal, they just couldn’t see the beauty that was a character borne from silence. He wasn’t a piece of technical mastery, his words and patterns changed like a beautifully constructed Joyce short story. Although… it wasn’t a story by Joyce, Palahniuk or Grisham. It was his style, his jutting, awkward style. It said fuck to grammar, to rules… it was free. He looked around the AWC and all he saw were clones; each a poorer imitation of the next… all copying a style, all claiming greatness. They needed something else to excite him… to draw his attention. There were some great technical people… but they were jejune to the point of inducing sleep. Just someone with substance… anyone… The door opened and slammed ferociously behind Afeaki. He knew what was coming; the whole good cop bad cop routine. You’d seen it a million times in movies. The heavy footsteps compounded the idea further. The suit in front suddenly seemed to grow in confidence. This guy must have been a top man. He whirled past the vision of GA and took his seat opposing the Fijian monster. He slammed a folder on the desk, one of those exclamation point actions that never really achieved what it set out to accomplish. “So, Mr. Afeaki… I can’t say it’s a pleasure to be here.” The larger of the two men spoke. He held a hulking physique, though slightly aged… “The feeling’s mutual,” Afeaki quipped. What did he have to lose? “Cute kid, real cute,” he leaned with both elbows on the table before him, “We’ve come to sort things out for you kid. You cooperate with us, and we’ll make this as needlessly painless as it has to be.” Afeaki’s gaze was fixated on the smaller of the Suit’s, his pen jotting and flitting across the page, “What can I do you for officer?” “It’s Agent actually. Agent Fealiki, Fijian Special Operatives.” He seemed displeased with Gabriel’s assumptions – it was all a rouse anyway, Gabs just wanted to see who he was. “We’re here to finally see what happened that night.” Gabriel looked inquisitively toward the Agent: did he really think, honestly, deep down in that governmental malleable mind that he had not contemplated why the fuck he was here? “Look, Mr Fealiki, I’ve made my point very clear on this several times. Your boy Penguin out here, and his little cronies all know where I stand. If I knew what I had done, maybe I would be of service to you. This is utter bullshit Mr. Agent sir. So please don’t insult my intelligence by coming in here, acting all federal on me. I’ve got far better things to do than to speak to someone about things that I have no clue about. Now, if you’re here to release me, because as far as I’m concerned. I’ve done fuck all wrong. Then please speak, if not, which I don’t think so… let me go.” Gabriel goes to stand, but a firm hand pushes him back into his seat. Snap, Crackle, Pop… not yet. “Please be seated Mr Afeaki.” He looked toward the smaller suit and nodded, “There’s been a development in your case.” He motioned to the smaller suit. “The Fijian government is having a large amount of pressure exerted upon it currently, with regarding your indefinite sentence. I’m afraid this has called for some desperate measures. You see before me a confession document. If you sign this confession, your sentence will be looked upon more leniently. If you choose to not sign the document, which I trust you will, the ramifications are well…” a large club came down across the neck of Afeaki from behind. It drove his dreadlocked head straight toward the table; his head ringing like the bell of Notre Dame… this is why it was dubbed the confession room. One of those calls it as it is jobs… “Ah come on man, I’ve had girls who hit harder than that.” The club fell for a second time, this time GA refused to move, “Will you get this thing off of me… look, I haven’t done anything wrong!” “We have her…” “Who, Alicia Keys?” “Your Mother.” Silence… “She died when I was a child…” Silence… “Mei… we have Mei.” At that precise point in time, you could pinpoint with a scary precision the moment when GA’s world was pulled from underneath his feet, crumpled into a ball, and thrown into the waste paper basket. The clubbing blow to the back of the head did him no favours, as his skull was sent crashing to the desk. No smart talk. No sass. Nothing. He lies there now. Still. The only sound is the intensifying beating of his heart as his lungs try to crawl out of his throat, or through his chest. “What… what have you… done to her…” the words flowed weak from his mouth. “Mr. Afeaki… cooperate…” What fucking choice did I have? She wasn’t blood, but she might as well have been the amount of times she had saved his behind. Since his parents died, since those hot African nights, since time began and I’m sure long before that, this woman was an angel walking the fertile pastures of God’s earth. He had to do what he could to save her. “This is fucked up… very fucked up. You know I’ve told you a thousand times, how can I confess to something I have no recollection committing, it just doesn’t figure. Just leave the old lady out of it… I’m sure we can figure something out.” He was playing poker with a duff hand, they held all the aces and it shone through like dawns early break. “I’m afraid Mr. Afeaki, we’re beyond the point of reasonableness here. The government needs a man to pin down for this act and well… whether you recollect it or not, we have evidence saying you committed the crime.” “Let me see it then.” “I cannot divulge that information…” the first signs of a stammer developing. Afeaki rose for the first time in a long time… pushing aside the calming hand, “Then why the fuck am I in here?! Answer me that. You know I’m being pinned for something that I didn’t commit. YOU KNOW IT!” Afeaki roared, his fists becoming tighter as he slammed one down upon the desk. “If you honestly believe I’m going to take the rap for a crime I did not fucking commit, then Mr. Suit, you might as well take that piece of paper and put it back in that little folder you’re carrying. Get the fuck out of my sight.” “Mr. Afeaki, please… I don’t feel you comprehend the precise nature of what’s being asked here…” I could feel that enmity boiling through my veins, it was like a rush of oxygen, invigorating every piece of sensory perception I had left in my body… how could I let this Suit push me down places where I didn’t want to be? I’d be a coward if I bowed under his pressure. The old lady would understand, ‘You’ve got to look out for you now Umaga’ she’d tell me as a child… maybe she was right? Slamming the chair against the wall, I was gone… gone to that place where I’m happy, that level of intensity where I felt neither pain nor pleasure, it was a happy medium. From this moment onward, the interview was a blur. Ideas flew over me; one flew east, one flew west, one flew over the cuckoo’s nest. I felt like McMurphy at times, the only sane one in this combine. He was talking to me, getting a little irate, I couldn’t hear a word. “Well, what do you think about that?” I couldn’t reply, I hadn’t been listening – the extra rolls underneath his neck danced a hypnotic melodic dance that had hypnotised me… I replied with a subtle huh… this angered the walrus. He raised his hand to strike me, I caught it effortlessly. He was washed out. No way would I give him the pleasure, he tried to corner me, but I was too alone to care otherwise. His other hand moved to release the other, rejected. Who did he think he was? Bruce Lee? Throwing him back into his seat, the look of shock on his face was a picture… The Penguin remerged, his band of merry men soon followed. It just crossed me, why the hell not? It seemed like an eternity… the pleasure that was… an orgasmic cataclysm of such magnitude Jenna Jameson could not put on a better performance. There was blood everywhere, from what I recall… bones broken, noses crushed, blood… the confession room had finally met its match. The Penguin and the Suit’s had it in for them the worst… it gave me immense satisfaction to bring the Penguin within an inch of his life, and bring him back. It was the kind of power gods have, or in here… guards. For those precious precocious minutes, I was a person again, not a number, or a prejudice. I was put in total solitary for that. Indefinitely. Chained up. Fed bi-monthly. No contact with anyone. No one. Nothing. Not even the banter of the guards. Was it worth it? I’m not too sure. When the news came… the brutal murder of an old lady, I knew it wasn’t worth this. Sign the confession GA, I knew I should have. Whose to say they wouldn’t have still done it? Fuck… it wasn’t worth staring at the brutalised carcass of her body on my wall every day. It’s all I could look at. Day in and day out. Those eyes looking back into mine, so cold and lifeless. Devoid of any warmth. I struggled to cry… Such was life here… Such was life. Little did I know what eventually would rescue me from this God forsaken cess pit. Just a voice was all I was told, just a voice was all I heard and just a voice was responsible for all this... All this? All what? Just wait, bitches. Coast To Coast, the Empire strikes back .::oti::.
