Chapter 2: The Start of Something Big

21 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1.21.06 You're walkin' along the street, or you're at a party, Or else you're alone and then you suddenly dig, You're looking' in someone's eyes, you suddenly realize That this could be the start of something big. “I don’t believe it…I won.” Sitting at home in his apartment overlooking San Francisco’s East Bay, Darcy Markson stared at the television screen still in disbelief. As per usual, his good pal Johnny Hawk recorded the match for Darcy to review, a ritual Darcy rarely missed partaking in once returning home after shows. But Darcy had already perused the tape several times, making notes upon notes of his tactical skills and lapses of judgment in the ring. He now found himself watching his victory over Jason Warr yet again, still unable to fully take in his personal triumph. “Four and a half years,” Darcy said to himself. “Four and a half years later, I still fucking got it.” The events of the last few days had been a whirlwind for Darcy. There were the congratulatory phone calls from his family and friends. The message on his answering machine from his colleagues at the Tomorrow Institute, wishing he’d return but encouraging him to keep kicking ass in the ring until he did. The East Bay Chronicle even contacted him about coming up with a human interest piece to celebrate the Bay native’s inspiring return to the wrestling world. It wouldn’t make the front page, but impressive nonetheless. Modesty was not exactly Darcy Markson’s strong suit. It definitely wasn’t Darcy Crisis’. But with each of the congratulations he received, be it from his mother, Johnny or the press, he did his best to keep things in perspective. “It was huge to come back after so many years and pick up right where I left off,” he told them all. “But this is just the first step on the road to bigger things. And this first step doesn’t mean anything if I can’t walk the whole road.” But sitting alone, taking it in by himself on an unusually brisk California evening, Darcy watched the events unfold once more. Locking Warr into his perilous signature move…his hand slapping the canvas in sheer agony, as so many had done before him…the referee raising Darcy’s arm in the air as the bell sounded his victory. Darcy couldn’t help but give in to the overwhelming magnitude of what he had accomplished. This could be the start of something big. ******************************************************* “It’s not that I wasn’t confident,” Darcy told the Chronicle reporter. “What I’m doing here means a lot to me. I said that Jason Warr would have to kill me if he planned on beating me, and I meant every word of it. But still…I’m surprised he didn’t.” “Really?” the reporter responded, with just a hint of disbelief in her voice, of the condescending variety. “You’re surprised not to be dead?” “Not literally. But I had a lot on the line out there. My pride, for one thing. I went out on top, you know. I was Pier Six Wrestling’s World Champion. Nobody ever beat me for that title. If I come back and get my ass handed to me by a guy nobody’s ever heard of…a tough competitor, but a nobody just the same…that’s tough to swallow. And certainly not a chapter I’d like added to the story of Darcy Crisis.” Darcy watched for a moment as the reporter scribbled the words he had just expressed down on a notepad. He glanced down at the press pass hanging around her neck, bearing her name and position with the Chronicle. Jennie Tyler, Features Editor. Jennie was like a factory-line production model from the mold that most female newspaper writers seemed to come from – thin rimmed glasses covering the eyes of a reasonably attractive face; a slender yet barely curved body, and one of those outfits that was meant to look chic, but in fact likely cost her less than fifty dollars at one of those stores they have in suburban outlet malls…the ones that are supposed to come off like thrift stores, except that there was probably a chain of them scattered around the country. There’s only one thing she’s missing, Darcy thought, until she crossed her right leg over her left and he saw them; burgundy Birkenstocks. Darcy couldn’t help but let out a small chuckle, and it was all he could do not to say “Cliché complete!” when she glanced back at him. “So…” Jennie said. “How’s your back?” ******************************************************* April 22, 2001. He didn’t know it at the time…and nary a soul could have predicted Darcy Crisis’ greatest day would also mark the beginning of the end of his career. Atop a 30 foot high steel cage, Darcy had realized his childhood dream. It was a scene he would remember for the rest of his days. Carnage was strewn about him – the entire Pier Six Wrestling roster lied all around him, broken and motionless…all that is, except for one. The new PSW World Heavyweight Champion held his title high above his head as fireworks exploded around him and confetti rained down. It should have been the greatest moment of his life. But unbeknown to Darcy, Eddie Osbourne’s “Elite” henchmen Kid X and “The Real Deal” Frank Roberts had scaled the cage and assaulted Darcy from behind. He put up a brief struggle, but the match had been too grueling and the odds against him now too overwhelming. He was at the mercy of the Elite…and the unthinkable happened. Darcy Crisis was thrown off the cage. A number of tables had been set up around the cage to help “break” the fall in case any of the wrestlers plummeted to the ground when they attempted to scale the cage. Miraculously, Darcy happened to fall on a few of them, or he very well could have been killed. But the fact that Darcy was still breathing was where his luck ran out. He sustained a great deal of damage to his back, so much so that he should have began his illustrious title run on the shelf for weeks, perhaps months. But fueled by rage and passion Darcy returned to the ring to confront and eventually defeat his attackers. When Darcy left the company a few months after winning the World Title, many thought that despite his courage in confronting the Elite, the toll his back had taken had been far too great. That was the reason he gave to the press, anyway. But in actuality… ******************************************************* “…Darcy? Still with me?” Back from his trip down memory lane, Darcy Markson suddenly remembered he was supposed to be giving an interview. Darcy silently chastised himself for giving in to the all-too-easy temptation to tune out when he found himself preoccupied. I smoked way too much marijuana in college, he thought to himself, wishing he could have a few of those countless wasted hours back. On second thought, nah… “Darcy?” “Right, sorry. Yes, my back…well, everybody knows what happened to me at WrestleCade. Everybody thinks that there’s no way an injury like that can fully heal. And they’re right, Jennie. It took me damn near five years just to get to a point where it doesn’t bother me anymore. But there will always be some lingering pain. And the doctors have told me that I risk permanent damage if some cheap shot artist takes any liberties out on my lower back. So I might as well have a giant bull’s eye painted on there.” Jennie nodded. “But if you’re going to print that Jennie, let me just say that if anybody starts getting any ideas…really, I’d love to see them try. Because I guarantee the Darcinator can wreak far more havoc on your shoulder than you’ll ever get the chance to put on my back.” “Well let me ask you this, Darcy…” Jennie said as she jotted down on her notepad. “Somehow it doesn’t add up. You’ve stated before that you never intended to make a career out of wrestling. You went out a champion. You have a job with the Tomorrow Institute, which you told me that –” “- I can make far more of a difference in the world there than I can beating the tar out of guys in spandex. That’s right,” Darcy interjected. “And now you tell me you risk a serious back injury by stepping back into the ring. I’m not trying to bring down your comeback…believe me, it’s remarkable. But why, Darcy? What could possibly be worth it?” ******************************************************* April 23, 2001. The day after WrestleCade, Darcy was released from the hospital after some tests had been run on his back. Despite the pain he was able to walk under his own power (with a helpful assist from everybody’s favorite painkiller Vicodin), and even though Eddie Osbourne had tried to cripple him the night before he needed Darcy at a PSW promotional event to hype his championship win and some of the big shows that would follow. It was on that night that the stars aligned, and Amy Campbell would enter his life in a way that he never would have thought possible. Aimz was a member of Eddie Osbourne’s Elite. His fiancée, to be precise. On camera they were PSW’s most powerful couple; behind the scenes their relationship began to sour. The straw that broke the camel’s back (no pun intended…well, maybe a little intended) was witnessing Darcy being thrown from the cage. Aimz and Darcy had never got along by any means, and their mutual disdain for each other was well documented. But unlike the rest of the Elite, Amy didn’t want to see the man killed. Swallowing her pride in lieu of some compassion that Darcy badly needed on the heels of his career nearly being destroyed, she sought Darcy out in his hotel room later that night… “What do YOU want?” Darcy said when he opened the door for her. He couldn’t help but be a little wary. “I need to talk to you,” she replied. Their arguments backstage had been legendary, and despite her intentions Amy had no intention of giving up any ground. “Really? Just long enough so your boys can finish what they started, right? I’m surprised you’re not waiting for Saturday night, so Eddie can have his ratings.” “It’s about that, Darcy. If you don’t believe me, let me come in. You can lock the door behind me.” Sighing, Darcy obliged. “Make it quick.” “You might want to open the closet, while you’re at it,” Amy snapped at him. The Boogeyman is also in our employ.” “You know, I may be in pretty bad shape, but not bad enough to kick your ass. And believe me, you’re not helping things. Now what the fuck do you want?” Things were going nowhere fast. For the first time in longer than she could remember, Amy was going to have to take the first step and show some compassion for the bewildered Darcy. “Look, I don’t like you. You know that, I know that. But I feel awful about what happened to you last night. I just want you to know that I had nothing to do –” “Cut the crap, Campbell. All you Elite scumbags are the same. You were on top of the cage when it happened. I bet you put those bastards up to it.” “NO!” Amy shouted. “It’s not true. I swear it.” “Give me one good reason I should believe you,” Darcy said, sitting on the edge of the bed. “And it better be good. I used to be Elite, don’t you forget. I used to roll with you and Eddie. I know how you two think. You two are a fucking unit, through and through. I know Eddie was behind this, which means you are, too.” “I’m here, aren’t I, Darcy? Why waste my time talking to you if I wanted to see you crippled, or worse?” Darcy laughed, condescendingly. “I wasn’t born yesterday. You’re trying to bring my guard down. I know how you creeps work…if you can’t beat me in the ring, you’ll start playing mind games until I don’t know up from down. I don’t trust you.” “Fine! Here’s another reason. How do you think I feel? The entire PSW roster nearly killed themselves trying to get on top of that cage to reach the title. But who made it up there?” Darcy remained silent. “You,” Amy pointed, “and me. And we had quite the battle up there. As much as I hate to say it, you got the best of me. That’s why you’re the champion. You earned that title,” Amy said, glancing over at the golden strap resting on the chair in the corner of the room. “You earned the ovation you got when you took it on top of that cage. And since you had to go through me to get it, I think I earned the right to challenge you for it. But now Eddie’s gone and fucked everything up.” Darcy still remained silent, but the change in his posture told Amy that he was starting to buy her story. She continued. “I know you have issues with the Elite. And I know you have issues with me. But I know I’m good enough to beat you, and I don’t have to resort to that kind of bullshit. And on top of that, while you might find me a bitch, I’m still a human being. Nobody deserves what happened to you. I just wanted you to know that…” With that, Darcy stood up and walked over towards Amy. Sensing a confrontation, she braced herself as he stood mere inches from her face. He stayed there for what seemed like an eternity before he reached behind her into the minibar. “You look like you could use a stiff drink. I know I could. Keep talking…I still don’t trust you.” The two talked long into the night. It took a great deal of convincing, but after some time not only did Darcy believe her story, but for the very first time the two of them realized that while they didn’t get along by any means, they had a deep, profound respect for each other. And in perhaps the strangest twist of all, the night would end with the two of them, quite inebriated, and between the sheets nestled in each others’ arms. Neither of their lives would ever be the same… ******************************************************* Darcy Markson looked into Jennie Tyler’s eyes. “A girl. A girl brought me back.” “Oh really?” Jennie replied. “Somehow I’m not surprised…” Darcy smiled. “Isn’t that always the way?” Jennie scribbled a little more into her notebook before setting it in her handbag and standing up. “Well Mr. Markson, I think I’ve got enough for my story here. I’ll be in touch in a couple of days so you can take a look at it.” “Really? I didn’t even tell you about how the Iceman and I stole Eddie’s truck and went off-roading in his backyard.” “They only gave me so many pages for this one,” Jennie said, smiling as she let herself out. “Thank you for your time.” “Much obliged,” Darcy said, shutting the door behind her. As he sat back down on the couch, he wondered how many people would see this story. More importantly, he wondered if Amy would see it. There’s no way she’s happy about losing last week, Darcy thought. Especially when the washout cripple is 1-0 in AWC. Truth be told, Darcy had no idea. He hadn’t spoken to Amy since the Christmas party in Amsterdam. There was the possibility that she’d been keeping herself pretty busy. But on the other hand…Darcy knew she was an internet junkie. He knew that she was obsessed with keeping up with her competition. And even though she told him there was no way she would face him in a match…somehow he had a feeling she had his eye on the doings of Darcy Crisis. Darcy smiled, knowing things were coming together for him. The seeds had been planted when he returned to wrestling for one more match with Aimz. The seeds began to bud with his victory over Jason Warr. And if he kept the victories piling up, they would surely blossom into something she couldn’t possibly ignore. And once Darcy Crisis and Aimz had settled their score once and for all…perhaps Darcy Markson and Amy Campbell could settle theirs as well. He could only hope. You're watchin' the sun come up and countin' your money, Or else in a dim cafe you're ordering wine, Then suddenly there he is, and you wanna be where he is, And this must be the start of something... This could be the heart of something... This could be the start of something big.
