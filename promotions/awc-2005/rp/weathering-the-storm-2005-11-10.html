Weathering The Storm

Date: 10/11/05 Weathering The Storm Don’t you people understand? Can’t you see the truth before your eyes? We are the risen people. We have harried and held the torrent of your storm Beware of us, you that have bullied and bribed. For from the graves of patriot men and women spring living nations, And from this day hence, I too am part of this struggle. -Michael D. with one quotation from Padraig Pearse Fear. The essence of existence, the spice of life. It was new to Paddy and he lapped it up. This was fear. Pure fear. Not anxiety of what’s crouched in the shadow, not worry of what awaits around the corner. This was knowing what was there. This was knowing that Hate a man who he had waded through shit to get to would as soon spare his life as he would do cabaret. And this knowing that this behemoth who had haunted his being these last few months, was all because of one man. One man powerful enough to manipulate both he and Hate with a single letter. This was fear. And this was Mike Wade. Yet he loved this newfound fear, shrugging aside the fact that this plan could be detrimental to his chances of wrestling again only intensifying the adrenaline buzzing through his limbs, giving him rushes of euphoria at the possibilities before him. For this was a chance. Yes, he’d weathered his storm and fought back to this point where he had the opportunity to break Hate. Fought on further to find out Wade was all that stood between him and peace. How much further was there to fight? Who cares, now where’s my gun. This was a chance indeed. And this was a chance to show everyone once and for all, the gypsy would never bend over for him. Not now, not ever. And there was fear. But it was good fear because despite it, in the back of his mind, he knew that Wade had a neck ready to be snapped too. So fragile now, so brittle, like a vase on it’s final descent to its shattering impact, he would watch him fall and the pieces would never be able to be put back together. “Aye…aye’m scared. But…fer all the right reasons…if ye know what I mean.” Paddy had his hand wrapped around his face, his middle finger and thumb massaging the outline of his temple, teasing the tips of his hairline in its circular motions. It helped him relax, always had, helped his Dad too who he had picked up this little stress relieving tactic off. Paddy slowly removed his hand, tracing the outline of his cheekbones then grazing the bristle on his cheeks. Only now did he realise his eyes were tightly shut, like a child refusing to acknowledge the facts in front of him, he just didn’t want to see that worried face. “Look at me Patrick you fucking twat.” Paddy glanced up at Shelly, her forced smile instantly holding his gaze. He stared at her, his heart yearning more now. One thing that Paddy used to see in himself was there to see. She was poor, it was clear. Hell, all you had to look at was her monstrous red sweater engulfing her petite curvaceous body to know it was probably the product of the Salvation Army thrift-box. As Paddy returned the smile he heard a sniff, a feminine noise coming from one of the nearby tables. He pulled himself back to reality out of those hazel eyes he was swimming in and continued with his meal. This was La Maison de la Manteen, an extremely expensive restaurant. From the moment Paddy had taken a shine to young Sally across from him, he had presumed that he needed to impress her. So from here on in, it was nice clothes, extravagant food, the finest wines. It was all for a good cause though, because then, maybe then, she might actually be able to stifle her vomit long enough to like this backwater gypsy… “That’s a nice jacket you’ve got. Where’d you get it?” Shelly muttered, sensing the conversation was now laced with awkwardness. She dropped her gaze back to her meal, and began toying with her steak with her fingers. “Jus’ some designer fella pointed it out fer me. Says it was ‘made fer me’”. Shelly nodded slowly not looking up, still engaging quite crudely with her food, grease wetting her finger tips as she poked at it suspiciously. Something was on her mind, that was clear to see. Yet Paddy mistook this for boredom. Here he was in his finest clothes doing what he could to woo this girl. He had to. He supposed the bubbly feeling in his stomach made this love (or maybe it was that dodgy chicken from last night) and this made him want her so badly. It was the same when they were in the park and the chill made everything look grey, or when they sat in the zoo admiring the agility and grace of the primates or even when they went to the library so Shelly could get the latest Connelly novel, on those days when time couldn’t move any slower for him, on those days Paddy would sometimes look sideways at Shelly, stealing her face and she would take his heart hurting it yet embracing it at the same time. Those days were dangerous because those were the days he would set himself up for a fall. Because those days, like every other day was when he had to remind himself of what he was. “I think this steak’s raw…” Maybe this was stupid. “See, it’s kinda pink here isn’t it?” Maybe gypsies weren’t supposed to love. “Paddy?” But it didn’t matter… “Oi, retard!” Because he would never tell. “Sorry Shell, aye was away there fer a minute. Wha’ ye say?” “Ah doesn’t matter lamebrain. Are you going to eat that broccoli?” Shelly smirked, a twinkle back in her eye. She grinned at him, her cheeks curling upwards, highlighting those hazel eyes again. She leaned over the table and snatched it away from Paddy’s fork then stuffed it into her mouth, her hands now clad with the gravy that had hugged the vegetable. Paddy heard a clear humph to his right and his eyes darted to see an elderly women, a human stand of jewellery and luxurious attire. The humph said ladies don’t behave like that, nor do they where Oxfam cast off’s. Then the women turned away, content to have made her point that she was indeed socially superior. Paddy looked over at Shelly who either hadn’t heard it or was ignoring it as she fiddled with a Yorkshire pudding, matting the remaining gravy from her plate. She pushed her chestnut hair behind her ear, not caring to clean her fingers revealing her beautifully constructed face (well in Paddy’s mind anyway). That old women could go feck herself to be quite frank he thought, because Shelly was better than that. That women had nice clothes and some of the finest jewellery he’d ever seen and he supposed she would get her hair permed biweekly but this didn’t change the basic facts at all. She could have her hair done everyday and she still be a conceited old snot. No, she didn’t compare at all to his Shelly. Did aye jus’ say my Shelly…wow…tha’ sounded nice in me head. “You’re drifting again buddy,” Shelly chirped now finished her meal and looking at him, comfort in her face. Her eyes betrayed her though. “What’s wrong Shelly? Ye’ve been actin’ differently. Come on, ye can tell me.” Paddy analysed Shelly’s face, all the composure and the act now fading away to the bare bones. And that was worry again. He’d caught on to it earlier but she changed the subject quickly. But it wasn’t closed yet, not while it was on her mind even if he’d already let it go. “Paddy…Mike Wade…I know you want to kill him for what he’s done. I know that. When I asked you if you were scared, I knew straight away that you have plans. I don’t know you that well Paddy-“ But aye kno’ you… “So you don’t have to listen to what I say…but please, think again. Think about being able to walk and talk and do the things you love. Including wrestling. Think about life for God’s sake. Because if he gets his chance, and it probably will happen, he’ll try and snatch every one of those things from you. So…think about it.” Shelly gazed away quickly now deeply embarrassed, a mild shade of rouge now creeping upwards from her neck, surging up her jaw, tenderly stroking her ear. Another cute thing about her. She sure wasn’t making falling out of love easy for Paddy, that was for sure. Paddy sat and thought for a second, feeling himself blush. It was so awkward because they barely knew each other yet at the same time they were symbiotic – sharing habits and loves, thoughts and feelings, both of them never latching onto this fact because opening up was so alien to them. They were sharing heartbeats, breathing the same air, shivering from the same orgasm, call it what you will, they were one. These conflicting factors were making a joyful and disturbing moment all wrapped up in one. Despite the usual torrents of conversation erupting all around them, everything seemed to silence. Neither wanted to say anything now, time creeping by like a cancer. Then he did the only he thought plausible in that situation, even though the heat building in his face was intensifying, he slipped his hand into hers. They fitted like jigsaw pieces, one in the same. Paddy stroked her knuckles with his thumb gently, dipping in between organic ridges, her milky complexion a joy to behold. She looked up at him, she looked tired, ready to fight a battle, wanting to but knew it would be to no avail. Paddy was soft but his face was stern and she knew there would be no changing his mind. “Shelly…this is somethin’ aye must do. Ever since aye came t’ the AWC aye’ve been tortured on tha’ man’s head. Aye can’t live like tha’ anymore. Aye have t’ be a man even if it may cost me dear, aye have t’ say t’ these people tha’ me name is Paddy O’Shea, son of Danny, descendant o’ one o’ the greatest races of people t’ ever walk the hills of the emerald isle. Because if aye don’, then aye’m as bad as them. Don’t ye see? ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Shelly. Please understand me now. Ye mean the world t’ me. Yer in me every thought, every waking moment. When yer with me, aye can’t breath. It’s suffocatin’ me now. So please, please, don’t think o’ me any differently now Aye remember the day aye met ye so clearly in me head, it’s like it’s unfoldin’ before me now. Do ye remember? Remember how ye fell comin’ out t’ the parking lot an’ how ye grazed yer knee. From the moment aye seen ye, aye knew ye were somethin’ special. Aye ran over, helped ye t’ yer feet and helped ye t’ me changin’ room. Ye were workin’ at the time, deliverin’ sheets over t’ Pearl, wearin’ yer black AWC shirt. Then aye cleaned yer knee off, patched it up. It blurted out then, aye asked ye out fer the first time. It wasn’t me tha’ said it though, aye wouldn’t have had the guts. It was something deep inside me repeatin’ again and again over in me head, do no’ let this one go. Aye intend on keep listenin’ t’ tha’ voice Shelly. Aye really do. Don’t ye realise? Yer so much more than tha’, than a damn stagehand. Yer an inextinguishable light, an’ even though ye may no’ feel the same, ye should kno’ wha’ aye think at least so ye can have mercy on me an’ tell this gypsy t’ hit the road now. So when aye say no t’ yer request, it hurts me so badly because aye would do just about anythin’ t’ make ye happy. But aye cannot do tha’. Don’t take it personal Shelly, please. He’s taken so much from me. Don’t walk away an’ become another name on his list. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Shelly shut her eyes firmly and a noise escaped her lips. She wouldn’t fight, what wasn’t clear was whether she would be making a swift exit or if she would stand and take the punches along with him. “Okay Paddy. If this is something you’ve got to do then do it. After all, who am I to tell you wha-“ “Yer everythin’ t’ me, that’s what” Suddenly Paddy didn’t feel embarrassed, suddenly everything was all right. Shelly pulled her hand away from Paddy’s and nodded. A fat waiter suddenly walked past placing a bill beside Paddy’s plate, a courteous smile affixed on his plump face. Paddy heard the whssht-whssht-whssht as he walked past, his fat thighs rubbing together and his hip swinging girlishly then got to his feet himself. It was time to go; this night was only going to lead to them getting hurt if it went on any longer. Shelly got to her feet now too, slowly and solemnly, then lifted her purse. Paddy placed a few tens down then walked around the table to the waiting Shelly. “Aye can’t promise ye reputation or tha’ we’ll no’ get dirty looks in the street,” Paddy stammered, gripping Shelly’s shoulders, eye to eye contact unbreakable. “Aye can’t say t’ ye tha’ aye’ll never get hurt, tha’ aye won’ break me bones either. But aye can tell ye this, if ye give me a chance, aye’ll give ye everythin’ else. Undyin’ commitment Shelly. Undyin’ affection.” Paddy held his stare. He was nervous, so was she, he felt her tremble from beneath her thick jumper, her shivers reverberating up his neck. Her hazel eyes were beginning to well up now. They said that it wouldn’t be right to respond. Sometimes it’s better to keep quiet in such instances for the sake of destroying it. This was one of these times. And somebody of course ended up breaking it… “My, I thought tramps were banned from such places.” Bastard. Ye feckin’ bastard. Paddy kept his gaze on Shelly never looking away from her unmoving eyes. But from the peripheral of his vision he seen the man who’d said that walk past, his lady friend hooked onto his arm. In his flash suit and upper class tone, he’d just ripped Shelly apart. Paddy could see it now. Water was building up. Blurring. Blurring. Blurr- Shelly pulled away from Paddy’s gaze and dodged a few tables then ran straight out the door of the restaurant, Paddy still standing transfixed, feeling stares everywhere boring into his back. Aye’m the tramp. Me. Ye hear me? ME! Thoughts, ideas, composure, sensibility. All out the window in nanoseconds. Paddy, the white rage tearing through his arms like razor wire, grabbed, swung and connected. He looked again and he had a chair in his hand, the mouthy snob lying on the floor, blood seeping from his cranium. Shelly… Paddy dropped the chair, all screams and shouts deaf to him, and ran in the direction Shelly headed. He darted past a few wary hands, too afraid to stop the ‘madman’ escape. Paddy ran out into the marble lobby, shrugging off confused gazes and then thrust the exit door open with his shoulders and into the night rain. Paddy was soaked through in seconds, the weight of each rain drop so great they were actually hurting him as they bounced off his head. Shelly… Paddy looked up and down the streets, scanning the sidewalk outside of every shop until an obstruction blinded him. He ran around in small circles, swinging on his left leg, trying to see everywhere at once, the streetlights a hazy blur of bright orange light, tickling his cortex nerves. Then he heard it. A sob. Paddy swung around so he was looking back at the restaurant door then looked slightly to the right…and into the alley beside it. Paddy ran over, his already drenched ankles feeling water splash up through his socks. He darted into the alley and saw her. She was leaning into the wall, covering her face with her slender hands. Paddy slowed down and walked over cautiously. Amidst the clutter of rubbish, to him an angel stood. He took her into her arms, the sound of a tin sheet being battered by the rain, flooding his ears. Then he perked her chin up ever so slightly so she was looking at his face, bent down and kissed her softly on the lips. The feeling that swept him was so colossal he had to put a hand to the wall to keep him standing; the feeling was huge, inarticulate and mercifully brief because he thought it might split him in two there and then. He pulled back and now he was looking into her mascara stained eyes. At first he seen disgust then she thrust her lips onto his passionately, grinding her body into his. He ran his hands around her waist and held her, feeling her tongue tease his. She ran her fingertips along the lining of his jaw, exciting the stubble then through his soaked hair. And for those brief moments, Paddy regained the teenage years he’d left behind. Anything…for…you END
