The End Of The Beginning

5 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-5-06 Sean Underwood, age twenty-three, stared at the brown package. It was heavy, solid…weighed about twenty pounds…and Sean had no idea what was inside. In fact, the only thing he was sure of is that it wasn’t a bomb. The only reason he was so sure, was because no self-respecting terrorist would waste good explosives on him. So Sean Underwood reached for the box, opened it, and watched his life change forever. * * * * * Jason Core’s North Pacific Wrestling Academy. In a span of about four years, it had gone from a non-existent entity, to one of the premier wrestling training dojos in years. The NPWA was run by two former wrestlers, Core…a journeyman and an excellent teacher, and Kensuke Suzahara, a dynamic talent who won his first World title less than a year and a half after his professional debut. The combination drew a number of wannabes into the dojo, and Core pounded them into real wrestlers. By the end of the first year, the NPWA had the same reputation that the CWA in Jersey and Tallahassee had under Core: an NPWA graduate will put on a solid technical match anywhere in the world with anyone. And so, there’d been three official “classes” and a hundred other kids and grizzled veterans who’d passed through the big rollup door. Not too many of the NPWA kids had gotten to “the big show.” Perhaps five or six kids got on national TV, and tons are touring in the US. Through it all, Sean Underwood has run the books, sent out the mailings, and desperately, desperately wished that he could get in the ring and train like everyone else. He had showed up the first day of classes, and asked Jason Core if there’s any way a hemophiliac could make it in the wrestling business. Jason told Sean the truth: yes, there is. As a ring announcer. So Sean crunched numbers, and learned how to call play-by-play for the NPWA’s few events that they taped for DVD sales. He got interviews with Dave Kern, Joey Red, and others in the industry. They told him that we was good, but needed practice. So he practiced. And of course, if the action ever spilled anywhere near the announce table, Sean high-tailed it, fearful that an errant blow or piece of shrapnel would pierce his skin, and send him on one of those odysseys of inconvenience, where his body wouldn’t let him stop bleeding. Or worse, it could be a serious one, like the time in fifth grade where he skinned his knee and spent almost a week in the hospital. Sean Underwood loved wrestling his whole life. He would’ve given everything to be different, and to be able to mix it up in the ring. His English degree. His family. Anything that God would’ve asked of him, he would’ve done it. And it was all these things…and more…that made him the ideal contact point for The Legion. * * * * * Sean sifted through the Styrofoam in the box, and pulled out a hard leather briefcase. The case clicked open easily, but when Sean saw the notebook computer inside of it, the confusion set in. “What is this? Something for my graduate school classes? Something for the NPWA that was sent to me instead? A gift from Jason?” He booted it up, and after a moment of confusion, followed the on-screen instructions to set the fingerprint-lock software up. And there it was, on the desktop. Only one single icon. “Listen To This.mp3” With only a brief thought to the fact that it could’ve been porn or a snuff clip, Sean double-clicked it, and the MusicMatch screen came up. The voice spoke in clear tones, but it was wholly unrecognizable to Sean. It told him things he already knew. That the briefcase was intended for him, Sean Underwood. That the briefcase wasn’t stolen or anything, that it was a gift that came with an obligation. The voice told Sean that if the briefcase and computer were not returned to the return address within one week, then he’d be accepting the terms of the agreement. But that the agreement would not be at all unpleasant. The agreement was as follows: Sean would manage four young wrestlers making their debut in the AWC, and act as their spokesperson. All four wrestlers would be hooded, and their identities a secret from each other, and from Sean. The only person who knew the real identities would be The Man In The Box, who would direct the actions of these four men via emails sent to this laptop. All Sean had to do is a few simple things. #1. Keep these men, The Legion, in line. The voice was confident that there would not be any discipline issues with the members of the Legion. They were selected especially for their ability to follow orders. #2. Lead them in training from time to time. #3. Facilitate any communications with AWC staff and the public. As the four men’s identities are meant to be secret, Sean would be the go-between mediating between the Legion and the outside world. If they needed something, Sean would get it for them. If AWC management needed something from the Legion, they could call Sean. #4. Oh yes, figure out a method to obtain AWC wrestling contracts for all four men. Sean shook his head at the last one, and The Man In The Box thanked him for his cooperation. He mentioned that in addition to Sean getting to live his dream as a real-life wrestling manager, compensation for his services would be an insane 25% of each man’s contract, which could run at AWC minimum, $100,000. It would be well worth the price for a man of his skills…all his skills, the voice intoned. Sean’s mouth ran dry, and the message stopped. * * * * * Four weeks later, Sean finally met up with the Legion, after they had already introduced themselves to him in essay form. They still had never spoken to him, but something rang familiar about these men. Sean did, watch a great deal of wrestling, after all. But when Sean met them in person, something had changed about him. And it wasn’t just the Roberto Cavalli outfit that Sean was wearing for his national television debut. It was the attitude, the fact that Sean had signed himself to be a part of something he didn’t fully understand, something potentially dangerous. After all, a disembodied voice had put together what basically seemed to be a wrestling hit squad. They even wore ski masks that fit the bill of criminals. And after running with the devil, in some form or another, for three and a half weeks, Sean felt a little invincible, and a little reckless. There was no going back to his past life in Seattle, crunching numbers for a wrestling school that, by nature, would never make very much money. No going back to a half finished grad degree in instructional design, or a crappy apartment in the shadow of Mt. Ranier. He’d spent money on designer clothes, and flown first class. He’d told lies to men in positions of power and made them believe him. He’d designed a plan to take binding contracts away from people who’d earned them. And now, he’d surrounded himself with strong, ruthless men, who’d do almost anything to earn their place in an industry based on violence and action. He checked his watch…almost two days until the official first-match debut for his Legion. And while his curiosity still ran amok (Who was the Legion’s benefactor? Who are the men under the masks? What is the grand plan of the Man In The Box? Does it have something, anything to do with the power struggle leading into Divide and Conquer?), the exhilaration of the new power and control put those thoughts in the rear view mirror and drove past. And no matter what the master plan of the Man In The Box was, did that mean that Sean couldn’t use the Legion in a way that he wanted to as well? The Legion would rise on the day of the devil, and it couldn’t help but make Sean grin. Tiara and Wayne are a sideshow, jokes like Collision Course. As Sasha and Pearl would find out, if they want the tag titles, they’re going to sign the Legion. Sean Underwood smiled, and it was the smile of a boy finding in himself the power of a man at long last. For someone who’s life had been dictated by circumstance, a slave to his body’s frailty and his soul’s passion, and how they simply could not intertwine…well, this was the end. The end of the beginning for Sean. * * * * * And, from somewhere nearby, a man watched from the shadows…a man with an even voice, guts, and a plan. Sean was feeling his oats as the leader of the Legion. The four wrestlers had their first shot at a real match…and through SOME WACKY COINCIDENCE, the Alliance Champions, the dominant Celestial Fury, had just walked out of AWC. The man in the shadows smiled. Indeed, the end of the beginning. Time for the real action to get going. He smiles, if only to himself.
