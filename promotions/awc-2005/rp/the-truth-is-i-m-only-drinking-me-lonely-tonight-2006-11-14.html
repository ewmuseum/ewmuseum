The Truth Is I'm Only Drinking Me Lonely Tonight

14 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-14-06 The cool November air was even more chilly than usual as the midnight black Chevrolet Silverado pulled off of the smooth blacktop road and onto a gravel lane. At the driver’s seat sitting behind the wheel was Scott Taylor; decked out in a pair of loose fitting denim jeans, black cowboy boots, and a black tee shirt with the words “Freak Parade” in bold, white letters. A leather jacket topped off the outfit, and a slim, black hat atop his head, his mid-shoulder length black hair hanging free. It was the night after Fresh! Wednesday, November 8th, 2006. There was still a day or so left before the flight to Vegas for the next show. With the time he had left he planned to go out for a night of it, but it had to be done Southern style and in a way he was used to it. The screeching guitar solo of Allen Collins on the Skynyrd tune “Gimmie Three Steps” filled the cab of the truck as Scott leaned back against the leather seat, one hand on the steering wheel and the other tapping against his thigh, keeping the rhythm of the song. After traveling what felt like forever down this road Scott finally saw the bright neon lights over the wooden building. The glowing sign read “Courtesy of the Red, White, and Booze” with each word appearing in its respective color, “Booze” being written in blue obviously. As the CD changed over to the next track Scott pulled the truck into the parking lot and parked it amongst the other vehicles already there. Killing the engine he stepped from the truck, the soles of his boots clicking against the gravel beneath his feet. After setting the burglar alarm and slipping the keys into his inner jacket pocket, Scott ran his hands through his mane of black hair, flipping it over his shoulders and started the trek towards the bar. He had barely made it three feet towards the entrance when he heard yelling of slurred voiced coming from a group of about six men standing roughly ten feet from the front door. “And I’M telling YOU that Rocky can’t beat Rambo in a fight!” came the inebriated words of a fellow who looked about twenty-five years old with short hair and a Minnesota Vikings cap. “Rocky took on Apollo Creed! He also fought Ivan Drago! Drago was much more of a bad ass than that backwoods Sheriff from Rambo!” came the retort from the other man, who looked about the same age as the Vikings fan. “Rocky never got shot at from a helicopter or fell from the side of a cliff and into a tree either!” said the Vikings fan. “At least Rambo didn’t need a woman to keep him going! Rocky would have given up and thrown in the towel if he didn’t have Adrian!” The man in the Vikings cap had heard enough. He slammed his fist into the face of the Pro-Rocky supporter and then tackled him; the two spilling out of the loose circle of friends and onto the cool gravel. As their friends tried to pull them apart Scott stepped past them easily by diverting a few feet to his right, and then pushed the door open, stepping into the bar. Immediately he was met with the heavy smell of cigarette smoke in the air and the smell of Draft beer wafting from behind the bar. Looking around he found the place to be a quiet little Honky Tonk with a group of people scattered about all dressed similarly to how he was. After doing the movie shoot in Toronto and touring the Northern states with AWC, Scott felt completely at home to find this little country bar; even if it was in Arizona of all states. Moving further into the bar Scott found a stool at the bar that looked rather appealing so he took a seat at it, leaning forward to rest his elbows on the counter. An old jukebox in the corner, which was covered in dust, was blasting an old Jerry Reed song that only added to the down-home feel of this place. As Scott felt his mind begin to slip away into a daydream his thoughts were interrupted as the barkeep, a man who looked about seven feet tall wearing a “wife beater” shirt and a doo-rag adorned with the Confederate Battle Flag and sporting a rather impressive beard approached him. Hearing the man’s bellowing voice caused The New Hero to look up. “What are ya havin’ tonight, kid?” “Let’s start it off with a shot of Jim Beam and follow that up with a Heineken.” Scott said as he diverted his eyes, looking towards the old jukebox as the bartender moved away to get the drinks. Scott watched as an older man who looked to be in his late 50s or possibly even early 60s stood at the jukebox selecting a tune. When the barkeep sat the glass and bottle before him Scott turned back around and grabbed the shot glass. Quickly turning the small glass straight up and leaning his head back, Scott downed the sweet tasting bourbon whiskey, letting it slide down his throat and slowly induce its famous burning sensation. Taking a moment to let the taste and sensation of the Jim Bean enter his system Scott quickly picked up the opened bottle and brought it to his lips. Just as he felt the cool liquid of the alcohol on his tongue the jukebox came to life with the sound of a sad fiddle and strumming acoustic guitars. Pushing you away each time I pull up this old barstool… Drowning you in every drop I drink… Telling you to go to Hell but I’m talking to myself.. Cause you’re not around to hear what I think… The baritone voice of Chris Young came from the jukebox and once again Scott found himself turning from the bar to stare at it sitting in the corner. The song was a slow and sad number about hitting the bars to forget an ex – something that Scott was doing this very moment and somewhere deep down inside, he found that both humorous and disturbing at the same time. Turning back to the bar to drink his beer Scott let his mind drift off into its daydream again. This past week had been rough, damn rough. He was accused of being the man who attacked Teresa Tomas, not only by Teresa herself but also by Jonny Kae, the man who he had to work a tag match with later in the night. The match was still fresh on his mind. He had given it a hard fight but with his mind distracted by the masked assailant who busted up Teresa’s ribs and his constant fear that Kae would turn on him during the match, his mind had just not been into the match at all. After that Frogsplash from Garbage Bag Johnny he just didn’t have the strength to kick out and found himself pinned – not by GBJ but by his tag team partner, AWC Legend Pierce Lavelle. The steam train of “Mr. OSW” had been completely derailed by Lavelle in one match. If he couldn’t even keep up with someone with that big of a name and career after all the hype, what was left? Those thoughts mixed in with his thoughts about Teresa only made him want to drink more. In his wandering state of mind he hadn’t realized it but three quarters of his bottle of beer was gone. As the barkeep passed by serving other patrons Scott gave him the one-finger-wave that was code for “bring me another beer.” Finishing off the bottle he already had his mind began to wander again, trying to piece together the events of the last three weeks and find some common link between them, hopefully to better understand what was happening. It was sometime that Scott recalled being roughly June when he received a letter in the mail that was postmarked as being from Tennessee. Since he obviously wasn’t about to become a country music star there was only one person who would write him from there. Anticipating a letter from his former partner Scott ripped into the plain white envelope and pulled out a neatly folded letter written in blue ink. “Scott, It’s been awhile hasn’t it? I haven’t seen you since you and Teresa came by the house before Mama got sick. I know we haven’t always seen eye to eye over the years but I expect you to actually read this. So, put the whole “Pro Wrestler Tough Guy” bullshit on the back burner and just listen to me. Teresa has problems. She has big problems. She’s taken up smoking and has been drinking way too damn much lately. She’s wrestling like shit these days and the last time I called her she was so damn drunk she didn’t even recognize my voice. I know you’ve been retired for a while now and all but do me a favor. I’m going back to Iraq soon so I won’t be here to look after her. Do me a favor keep an eye on her. - Private Eric Tomas, United States Army.” The letter had really struck a chord with him. He and Eric had not gotten along at all in the last seven years and now he was writing Scott to look after his baby sister. He hadn’t been keeping up much with the happenings in the wrestling world after retiring the year before, but she had always been on his mind now and again; wondering what she was doing and if she was all right, wherever she was in the country. The letter from Eric had been his chief reason for getting involved with AWC in the first place after his time away from the ring, teaming up with Darcy Crisis and winning a coveted spot in the Triangles match was just a fringe benefit. Maybe it was stupidity on his part, but he thought that his return to the squared circle and his crossing paths with Teresa again would have been well met. Instead he found a cold reception from The Redneck Princess and the AWC fans. Teresa hated him. It was that simple. She was already outdone with him for what had happened three years ago but his sudden return, snagging a spot in the Triangles match, and his alleged attack on her had pushed her too far. He knew from the way that she reacted last night in the locker room that she held a deep-seated resentment towards him in her heart. As far as the AWC fans went, they were viewing Scott as nothing more than a has-been. He was only someone who had his glory day at the turn of the new millennium and was relying on his past successes to get him over in the ring. They gave him mixed reactions each time he hit the ring and his spots were mostly ignored and sometimes booed. Whether he was hitting his trademarked second rope moonsault or that DDT from the top rope from his match last night the fans could care less. As if realizing the only woman he had ever loved had nothing but contempt for him wasn’t bad enough, being a professional wrestler who had no fans, not even among the smarks, was a truly devastating fate. The only thing left to do for The New Hero was to sit here in this run down bar on the outskirts of Phoenix and drown all of his sorrows the old fashioned way; with so much alcohol he wouldn’t remember anything until the next day sometime. Earlier in the day he had received a phone call from AWC management informing him of his next match. He had yet to find himself in a singles match since arriving to the company and this didn’t seem to be changing anytime soon. After two tag team matches it was time to put him in a Triple Threat Match. He had plans to pick up recordings of previous AWC shows to check out this Josh Marquez fellow but his bigger concern going into this match was that English asshole Jonny Kae. That guy had quite the nerve and Scott really wanted to get his hands around that Limey’s neck. “Here ya go. I know who you are, Scott, so the rest of these are on the house tonight.” Scott looked up to see the burly bartender sitting another beer bottle before him as well as a second shot to act as a chaser. “Um…. thanks, I guess. I could really use these after the night I had.” “I saw your match. What did you expect going up against Lavelle and Garbage Bag? Just drink up, kid.” Scott took a swig from the new bottle of beer and immediately followed that with his second shot of Jim Beam. He scrunched his face and winced a moment as the double dose hit him and then let out a deep sigh of exhalation. He was known for his ability to hold his alcohol but it had been awhile since the last time he drank like this so his skills weren’t exactly up to snuff anymore. His head was slowly starting to spin and he was finding it more and more difficult to keep his thoughts together. Kae. That was where he left off, he remembered as he drank from the bottle. That asshole had the nerve to interrupt his debuting ring time three weeks ago, coolly accused him of being the attacker of Teresa Tomas, and last week had barely worked with him in their tag match. Except for launching him into that crossbody Kae had done nothing to assist them in the match, which was, in Taylor’s mind as he thought about it, the reason why they lost. The now empty bottle hit the counter and was immediately replaced with a third bottle of “liquid recovery.” As Scott picked up the bottle the jukebox was finishing up another song, a rendition of “Seven Spanish Angels” by the legendary Willie Nelson and was going into something a little more up-tempo; the country-rock styling of “That’s How They Do It In Dixie” by Hank Williams Jr. with a little help from Gretchen Wilson, Big Kenny Alphin and John Rich and brothers Johnny and Donnie Van Zant: The latter four men known as the duos Big and Rich and Van Zant. The fast paced southern rock song caused Scott to tap his boot-covered foot against his stool as he turned the bottle straight up, wasting no time in downing his third beer after having two shots of whiskey already. The fact he would have to drive back to his hotel room and sleep this off before catching a flight to Las Vegas the next morning hadn’t even entered his mind. He was too busy drinking and trying to repress bad thoughts to comprehend that. If Scott was hurt enough to actually cry over his loss “There’s A Tear In My Beer” would have totally fit the mood, but he wasn’t that downtrodden yet. However, much like the first song that came onto the jukebox said, “The truth is I’m only…drinking me lonely tonight….”
