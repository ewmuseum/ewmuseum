Rock-a-Bye

11 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/11/06 ''You don't have to do that,'' Nneka said. She looked up at Luis, eyes locking on the short Venezuelan. Sitting on the bed with her legs crossed under her and her hands folded in her lap, Nneka looked incredibly cute and worried at the same time. Her wild hair seemed as if it was standing on end as she followed her 'boyfriend'—she supposed she could call him that, even though they'd never made a formal proclamation of going out or anything—with her eyes. Luis paced about the room, dressed in a white wifebeater and blue jeans. He held a gun—the gun—in his hand, his eyes fixed on it. Nneka squirmed, turning her eyes towards a corner lamp and doing her best to focus on that. She didn't want to see the gun, to be reminded of all that had transpired. She didn't want to think of the fact that Luis had had a gun all this time and never told her, that he could have simply reached into the dresser drawer and killed her if he had wanted to. She trusted him far more than he really deserved, but she couldn't help but wonder. It was a gun, after all. Luis wheeled around on his heel to face Nneka; the Nigerian girl gave a start, but she didn't move from her place. He inspected the barrel of the pistol before pointing it at her, one hand cupped under the barrel and the other wrapped around the stock. His hands were far from the trigger, but that didn't make her spinal column any less icy. Had he gone crazy? He'd been pacing up and down like a madman, simply repeating that she had to go. Of course, Luis was a fan of using more colorful language to get his point across. Nneka was doing her best to try and talk him down, but she wasn't doing a spectacular job. ''The bitch hasta die,'' Luis declared. ''This can't go on any longer. She won' leave 'cause the Lion won' let 'er leave. He'll make her stay 'round here and just fuck shit up some more. That's all she's really good at, y'understand. Just gettin' in th' way.'' Nneka's eyes opened wide. She wasn't… she wasn't hearing what she thought she was hearing. Was she? She didn't like Amber at all. She probably disliked her more than Luis did. She had never even had any thoughts about fucking the girl, much less going through with it. But this? ''That's not necessary, Luis,'' Nneka said. ''You don't have to do it.'' ''I do,'' Luis said, gritting his teeth. He lowered the pistol a bit. ''I've gotta run her out of here. You tell me how I shuh do it, then.'' ''Don't,'' Nneka said. ''You've never had to do this. Just make Anton see that he should cut her out of the business part of it. That's all. She'll do what he says. He saved her life.'' ''She wanted to take the train t' hell and I say let her. What th' fuck is the Lion doin' gettin' in th' way?'' ''Keeping the walls clean,'' Nneka quipped. Luis snorted. ''Blood kin get cleaned off, y'understand.'' ''And so can she. You don't have to kill her.'' ''Nneka, you never gonna understand!'' Luis lifts his chin into the air before looking over at her sharply. ''You ain't even wanna understand. She is so fuckin' bad for the Lion that it hurts inside, y'know? I don' wanna see that shit go on. Plus, iss bad fuh my inves'ment. I inves'sed time and money, shit that she ain't got, intuh alladis, so why shuh'd I jus—'' ''Why are you even bringing this up?'' Nneka snaps. ''How is that in Anton's best interest at all?'' Luis frowns a bit. ''I'm jus' sayin' that—'' ''She makes him happy,'' Nneka said solemnly. ''I hate her, but she does. I don't know how. If I have to tolerate her because of that, that's fine. But she doesn't have to di—'' ''Yeah, she does!'' Luis balled up his fist and brought it shaking to his eyes, staring at his knuckles. ''She gotta go. One way or th' other. She ain' lookin' like she gonna leave peacefully, so we gonna do this violen'ly. Das how iss gotta be.'' ''God!'' Nneka shouted, throwing up her hands. She turned over, facing away from Luis as she scrambled across the bed, putting as much distance between them as possible. She knew Luis was looking at her concernedly, but she simply folded her arms across her chest. She didn't want to look at this… this unreasonable… unreasonable person! ''Nneka, don't be like tha—'' ''Don't you fucking touch me!'' Nneka warned, lifting a hand. ''I don't want to listen to a single thing. Do you hear me?'' There was a long silence after Nneka spoke, so long that Nneka thought perhaps Luis had simply vanished from the room, slipped out without her hearing or something. He couldn't still be there. But then his voice piped up: ''I'm just lookin' out for what's best fuh the Lion.'' ''What's best for Anton!?'' Nneka blurted incredulously. ''What's best for Anton is you not killing his girl! What's best for Anton is you not sticking your nose in his business. You aren't concerned for Anton, you're concerned for yourself. Stop trying to sell this to me as if that'll make me care more. You know you don't have to kill her or hurt her or… or whatever!'' ''Bitch, I—'' Luis grunted, cutting off in mid-sentence. ''Look… Look, I just… fuck!'' Nneka turned around, watching from over her shoulder as Luis stormed out of the room, slamming the door behind him. She would have had a bit of satisfaction in frustrating the man like, but one thing was amiss. He still had the pistol in his hand. ''Eighteen,'' Amber said eagerly, her delicate hands under the bar. She wasn't really spotting Anton—just acting like she was—but Anton was glad to have her around anyhow. He brough the bar back down to his bare chest and pressed it up again. ''Nineteen. Right there, Anton… twenty! Another set finished.'' Anton lifted the bar up and set it on the rack before sitting up, wiping sweat from his brow with his forearm. ''Towel,'' Anton grunted. Amber sprang to life, practically scurrying towards the nearby bench and snatching up the white towel. He took it from her with a smile when she brought it, wiping his face down before draping it around his neck. Amber watched him for a bit to see if it was okay before she lowered herself down onto his knee. Anton chuckled and slipped an arm around her waist, pulling her close. Amber had been different after he had stopped her from using that gun on herself. She had not exactly been meek, but she had been a lot more agreeable. Anton hadn't minded the change a bit. He didn't particularly relish that he had had to divert suicide for her to stop being so disagreeable, but it was a means to an end. Amber rested her head on his shoulder and looked up at him. He gave her a quick kiss on the forehead and she smiled. ''You're doing great,'' Amber said softly. Anton dipped his head a bit to show assent. Amber pulled away from him just a bit. ''But you can't just be improving physically.'' Anton's eyebrows raised curiously. ''What?'' Anton asked. ''I mean… other things have to improve for you, too,'' Amber said. ''Physically, I don't think anyone in the AWC can touch you. But…'' Anton's eyebrows slid downwards together and pushed together in a downwards arrow. ''But?'' Amber traced her fingers over his chest, keeping her eyes there. She had always been cowed when he gave her a look like the one he was giving her now. That, thankfully, had not changed one bit; he needed to be able to make her nervous or else she'd run roughshod over him. ''Well, I was just saying like… well…'' ''Well what?'' Anton asked. ''What are you trying to say?'' ''Well, look at Darcy Crisis,'' Amber offered. ''I would prefer if I did not.'' ''Quiet.'' She tapped him playfully on the cheek and a grin spread over Anton's face. He was rarely ever witty. ''I just mean to say, Anton… he doesn't have anything holding him back. He doesn't have… uhm…'' ''Luis,'' Anton said flatly. Amber pursed her lips, but that didn't stop her from nodding her head, however slowly. ''He's gotta go, Anton,'' Amber breathed. She put her palms on his chest, leaning into him and looking up at his eyes. She was beautiful. Any other man with a girl this pretty leaning into him like this would cave immediately. Anton's eyes remained steady. ''He has to! I mean, how can you keep him around? He keeps getting into trouble, he keeps… he keeps interfering! How could you possibly…'' Her voice trailed off and she relied on her puppy-dog eyes to make the argument for her. ''No,'' Anton said. ''No. Do not be so jealous of Luis. He has not done anything wro—'' ''He's done everything wrong!'' Amber countered. Anton set his jaw. She was right, but he wasn't going to tell her that. ''Stop this petty argument!'' Anton snapped instead. ''You and Luis do not like each other, I know. You hate each other, yes?'' She nodded. Emphatically. ''But this is too much. Stop being so… childish!'' ''Childish!'' Amber stood up, taking a step backwards. She planted her fists on her hips and glared at him; none of this was making a case against Anton's claim. ''I'm being childish? What about Luis?'' ''He is being childi—'' ''I don't want to hear you defend him again,'' Amber cut in. Anton blinked; he had certainly not been about to defend Luis. ''I don't want to hear… ugh!'' Amber turned away and stalked off towards the door, arms folded under her breasts. ''I'm taking the car!'' Anton watched her stride away, forearms rested over his haunches. So much for being agreeable. She was never going to give this up, was she? Anton shook his head and laid back down to do another set of bench presses. He would calm her down later. He knew she'd walked in. He had seen her car pull up and one person get out: her. The Lion wasn't around. Naturally, he had seen his chance. Moving into the corner, he pulled his belt out of the loops and stretched it taut between his hands before letting the buckle end dangle. He snarled at nothing. He was going to drive her away, and if she wouldn't go peacefully, he'd do it violently. The pistol was jammed in his pants under his shirt, but he didn't want to kill her quite yet. The door was pushed open and he raised up onto the balls of his feet. He watched her form slip into the room, twisting just enough to push the door closed before continuing in. Luis moved forward fluidly, watching her hips move as she walked. Watching her hair sway. He lifted the belt upwards and lashed forward at her. The buckle gave a little clink as it whistled up into the air. It caught her just left of her right shoulderblade. She cried out and stumbled forward. Luis sprang towards her, grabbing the belt in both hands. He looped it over her head and pulled it back against her neck, throttling her. She gasped, hands coming up and vainly trying to pull the leather strap away from her throat. He pulled on the belt so that her back was pressed up against his chest. ''Yuh fuckin' whore,'' Luis spat. ''Yuh no good… fuckin'… fuckin' whore!'' He continued to choke her, lifting the belt up. She kicked forward, putting all her effort into a fruitless struggle. Luis finally let her go and she collapsed to the ground on all fours. Malice had gripped Luis's heart a long time ago, but now it squeezed like a noose. She started to crawl away and Luis brought the belt up, whipping her over the back. She cried out, her arms giving way so that she rested on her elbows. ''Fuckin' slut!'' Luis cried, whipping at her again. The belt buckle cut through the fabric of her clothing. She whimpered. Luis struck her again. And again. And again. He wasn't anywhere near done. He would cover her in scars. He would make her bleed. He would… a dark shape rushed forward and something akin to a brick slammed into his ribs, knocking the wind past his lips and throwing him back into the wall. Luis's eyes practically swelled open as he realized who it was. The belt fell from his hands as another punch slammed into his stomach. At first, Luis grunted at every punch. Soon, though, he had no breath left; he just jolted soundlessly. His bones were crushed to dust. His insides were mashed into a bloody pulp. And still, he felt the punches slamming into him with a disgusting rhythm. Left. Right. Left. Right. Luis would have tilted forward but for the blows landing to his sternum, keeping him pinned upright against the wall. It happened to be the most painful lullaby he had ever experienced. Open. An instant realization that staring up at white lights was not the best way to wake up. Squinting. Watching helplessly as doctors swarm around. There's something plastic over the nose and mouth a respirator. Never needed a respirator before. Hurt that bad? Really? Never expected to be hurt that bad. It was a surprise attack, yeah, but… a respirator, huh? A familiar shape loomed over now, looking down. Close, open. ''Lion?'' The tall, dark-skinned man looks blankly. Close, open. ''What the fuck is wrong with you, Luis?'' Anton set the bags down near the entrance of the hotel room and closed the door behind him. Amber collapsed on the couch, stretching out and just resting. Anton would have preferred that she let him take her to the bed, but he wasn't going to lift her up now. She had to lay down. Anton moved towards her and squatted down to the side of the couch. He reached forward and brushed some dark hair out of her face. Amber smiled up at him. ''Thanks,'' she said. ''For carrying my things. For… this.'' Anton shook his head. ''It is, eh…'' Anton screwed up his face, thinking. ''How do you say… least I can do.'' Amber giggled a bit, turning over and burying her face in the cushions. Anton smiled. ''Luis is bad,'' Amber said after a while. Anton lowered his face, looking at the ground. ''You told me,'' Anton said. ''I saw.'' Anton's lip curled. He had never expected Luis to pull a stunt like that. Luis was a creep. But… well, at least he was able to take Amber away from all that. He didn't need her to be killed over any of this. Amber turned back around, looking at him with shining eyes. ''He's bad for you, Anton,'' Amber confided in him. ''Get rid of him. Please.'' She had said this to him scores of times to him before. Pleaded with him, practically on her knees, and sometimes literally. She had made sure he heard it as the first thing in the morning and the last thing at night. She had said it too many different ways to count; she'd even learned how to say it in French. This was the first time that Anton had seriously considered it.
