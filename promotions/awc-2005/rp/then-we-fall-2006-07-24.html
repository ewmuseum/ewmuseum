Then We Fall

24 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 24/7/06 Prologue I am a bitter man. I didn’t set out to be that, nor do I wish it upon any of my brood. But that I am. I do not try to explain it, for if I did, I would be forced to delve into the intricacies of the mind, compelled to explain neuron patterns and their effects on our behavior. And I still wouldn’t have a clue. I could spend the rest of my life debating how I got into this frame of mind, over and over again and I still wouldn’t have an answer; I’d still be as frustrated as I was five minutes ago. And round and round we’d go. So I do not try to explain it. Instead I accept it and let the bitterness sweep me away on the crest of a wave. It is the leader and I am the lowly minion. Take me where you deem satisfactory. Give me that feeling of hate and frustration, then give me a blinding red face, a target so to speak. And I will strike. And that’s where AWC comes in. My misery. The place brought me so much misery. The taunting, the betrayals, the misplaced trust. It changed me as a person. It made me this bitter man. But it was good for something I came to reckon for it provided me with a vent. I had my targets and I had my bitterness. But I also had a license to brutality. And right now, there’s nothing more in this world I want more. I am a lion, hear me roar. As a trembling hand completes it’s scrawl of non-intelligence, it pauses in the dark. It hesitates, then scratches and almost indistinguishable signature under the monologue. Patrick O’Shea. May 2006. Apparently someone was literate after all and rudely, didn’t deem it logical to inform us. The Early Days First of all, I’ll explain of the clans. While New York has it’s gangs and Sicily has Coscas, Ireland is made up with it’s clans. Like the previous two, these clans are made up mainly of family members with an overall leader. In these districts these groups ally themselves with powerful allies to form factions. There is no high chief, no elected council. Just like the gangs free spirit and the Coscas very loose association in the Mafia, the clans of Ireland were bound in no shape or form to each other by morality and certainly not by law. And so, like vultures, if one group was perceived as weak, it would be attacked and others would grow in their demise. There is no code of honor among thieves. In Ireland, these clans are traveling people, gypsies if you will. In the year 1995, not long before his 25th birthday, Patrick O’Shea became the youngest ever leader of the O’Shea clan. Normally a challenge for leadership was conducted in a brutal fight to the death, but there was a spanner in the works on this occasion. The former leader was Paddy’s own Father, Danny. And so, with a great deal of suspicion, the very able bodied Danny handed over his leadership to Paddy on request. Only Paddy and Danny ever knew that Danny owed Paddy quite a lot and this was only a step towards repayment. But that’s another story. Paddy entry into leadership was somewhat a baptism of fire. The clans were in an uproar following the murder of the clan leader Dermot O’Neill’s, only son. Their allies, the Fitzpatrick’s, O’Doherty’s and Kavanagh’s all rallied up along with them with their own targets in sight. The O’Shea’s and their only allies - the McDaid’s. Now it was common knowledge that Fintan McDaid was behind the murder; his hatred of the O’Neill’s was publically archived and could be tracked back from when his wife had an affair with Dermot O’Neill. And the McDaid’s were weak, they could be easily dispatched by the O’Neill’s alone. The allies uproar was unnecessary and overenthusiastic. Their response of death threats and vicious beatings of locals would normally be deemed an over reaction. However, there was something else that was public knowledge. The transparent empirical ambitions of the Allies. The O’Shea’s were powerful, with one of the biggest land masses in all of Connaught; Anything that was to be done in the province had to receive the nod by them. If one was to eliminate this stumbling block, one’s drive for total domination would be made that much easier. And the Allied clans all wanted total domination. This little fiasco would prove as a perfect excuse to mount a united campaign against them. And this new upstart Patrick O’Shea was surely the underlining factor - the O’Shea’s days as a clan were fading to black. Four months later and Paddy was kidnapped. It was simple and in broad daylight. As he and his young nephew Michael walked down a country lane back from town, a car pulled up beside them and dragged them both in. There was no struggle or no fuss - Patrick’s every moment on this earth since he became leader was of paranoia, knowing the clans would strike at him, the core of his clan. Resistance was futile. Luckily he was prepared. He did not talk as they blindfolded him and in turn they remained in silence. He placed a hand on his young nephew’s knee to comfort him but found the boy was calmer than he was. Despite the seriousness of the situation, he could not stop himself blushing with pride that the virtues of his ancestors had passed in the blood to this young man. Fifteen minutes later they were both pulled out of the car and their sight restored. They found themselves in a small campsite, bordered by encircling caravans. A small fire was the focal point for all to see. The sound of hoofs knocking off the ground reached Paddy’s ears and he turned to his left to see a man on a horse draw near. He wore a smile on his face - not of triumph but of welcome. “Hello Patrick”. Paddy knew instantaneously who he was. Peadar Kavanagh, clan leader. This was obviously the launch of a war. Whether he would be ransomed or simply silenced remained to be seen. Whichever, Peadar’s smile implied it was neither. A rough shirt loosely draped over a muscular pale body was his attire, his pepper-and-salt freckled hair glimmering in the warm evening’s rays. He hopped down from his horse and came closer to the point Paddy could smell the vodka on his breath. No control Paddy noted. The sudden grip of Michael’s hand on his seemed to reinforce the point. “Welcome…aye’ll be straight and get t’ the point. Aye do not wish t’ kill ye - aye simply want peace; peace tha’ comes at a price.” Peadar clasped a hand on his shoulder. “Ye will be ransomed. Yer clan will see yer face again when they move from Galway. Too long our families have been dictated by yer unjust hand. If there was any balance, aye’d gut ye right now…but aye’m not a vengeful man.” “If ye had ransomed me for 50k ye would have got it. Even double. But my family will never move out o’ the land o’ their Father’s. So do wha’ ye will Peadar. Aye will say this though - whatever action ye do t’ me, remember, my cousins are only too willing to match on ye.” Peadar scoffed but respectfully took his hand from Paddy’s shoulder to end his patronization. “Aye admire yer tongue Paddy, aye really do. But trouble minds often bring strong words from weak bodies. Yes, yer family is influential…but does it really have the power t’ reinforce it? Aye think not. Maybe under your Father in his early years but he went senile and he’s been replaced by an inexperienced upstart. Piece of advice Patrick, if ye ever do regain the mantle of yer clan after this - never show mercy or ye will never last. Gratitude is the most temporary of feelings.” Paddy sniffed. This was ridiculous; he was being schooled by imbecile and he turned away. “Aye think we’ll be alright,” Michael whispered to Paddy. Paddy nodded once again admiring his ten year old nephews foresight. Indeed they would be fine. “Peadar, aye wish t’ sleep now,” Paddy politely informed. “Of course Patrick. We will a nice breakfast prepared fer ye in the morning. Enjoy.” Paddy and Michael retired to a nearby caravan. Without any need for a word, they slept soundly, not fearing the possible outcomes. They slept so soundly that they didn’t hear the clamour outside early in the morning. It took a waking hand for them to stir from their slumber. “Paddy?” With great difficulty, Paddy pried his eyes open so they fall on the face of his younger brother Oisin in the misty morning air. It was an enchanting sight. Paddy smiled slowly. “You took yer time.” Oisin playfully slapped Paddy on the head. Then shook Michael, his only son. “Come on you two. Time t’ clear up this mess.” Five minutes later and Peadar was knelt before Paddy, his eyes pleading. “Please Patrick…please. Let us go. We’ll leave Connaught, we’ll do anything…just don’t kill us.” Paddy put a hand on Oisin’s chest. “Didn’t I warn you? Aye told ye wha’ a mistake ye were makin’ yet ye paid no heed. Don’t ye know I always have men watchin’ my position so they can find me if aye happen t’ be, say, kidnapped. Fer me t‘ give ye mercy would be an insult. Dead men are no opposition.” As Peadar began to sob uncontrollably, Paddy felt a tugging on his left arm. He looked down into the sympathetic eyes of young Michael. “Paddy…can’t we let them go? Aye mean, they didn’t do anything t’ us. Please Paddy.” For the first time in a long time, Paddy considered mercy and he was thankful it was this young innocent boy that had reminded him. But today, the boy would learn a lesson - gratitude is the most temporary of feelings. Without a word, Paddy turned away and looked at one of twenty of his clan members. He nodded simply and then turned to walk away, catching the glimmer of a knife in the peripheral of his vision as he did so. He jumped into a nearby car and was drove away. Paddy never had any trouble of that scale with any of the allied clans again. TO BE CONTINUED
