Date: 21st July The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch. 12 Lost Light: Part 1 On the flight back to Albany, I tried to refocus on normality and my new life with Lois. But it was a fruitless effort. I spent a good part of the night in Costa Rica watching the news reports, trying to sway the thoughts that so desperately wanted to enter my head. I had spoken to Lois the night before and she arranged to meet me at 7:15 pm with our new car, silver GMC 4x4. She seemed so proud of her first car on the phone, and mentioned the looks she got from driving into the parking lot at her work - it pays to have a famous boyfriend she had said and recapping that brought a smile to my grim looking face. The good memories of Lois soon parted, as I stared at a cloudless sky overlooking the Atlantic Ocean. My mind wandered to the events that had taken place a week ago: my mother and sister blamed me for the divorce and left the apartment; taking the first hotel they could find. My attempts to contact them failed, my efforts were dim-witted and dismal. Rubbing my shoulder, as though it was still burning; I remembered how Rebecca had shoved me against the cold marble steps and then ran away crying. I felt so broken in that moment; alone and cold. However, despite my bad luck, and the lost light that had left my family in a dark shroud; Lois was still my hope … my ray of light in all the darkness. I had gotten to the plane on my own, but now here, seated in front of a crying baby, I couldn’t let it go. I tried reading a book I had bought a while ago in England: “Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince”, all the rage in England, but it did no good. My mind constantly darted back to the mysteries surrounding my so called tag-partner, Tim Shipley. He had become extremely tense and uncomfortable; afraid of moving shadows, and now I - well - I was beginning to feel the burden of being his tag-partner. I felt slightly uneasy being compared to his old friend. I forgot those thoughts just as soon as they entered my mind and I scribbled down on the spare vomit bag; ‘fire-ants, Tim Shipley and masked man?’ but as soon as I scribbled it down, I rubbed it out again, feeling out of line mingling in someone elses affairs. Even if it was scribbled out, I couldn’t erase it from memory. ______________________________________________________________________________ Lois sat on the hood of the silver GMC jeep, dressed in a pair of baggy jeans and a tight blue t-shirt. Her hair was tied back to show her tanned neck and a silver necklace danced loosely over her collarbones. I was relieved to see her. I dropped my suitcase onto the floor and Lois jumped immediately, darting toward me, leaping into my arms and smiling. “So, how was your trip?” she smiled and put her feet back onto the ground. “… as well as could be expected. I had to deal with a screaming baby the whole trip, not the most peaceful flight, but I am glad it is over,” I kissed her gently and grabbed my suitcase up off the ground as she opened up the boot. Lois smiled as she checked the customs bag and saw a box, wrapped in gold and silver wrapping, with a bow on top. “I thought we agreed for you not to get me a present every time you go away.” “I know … but I feel guilty having to leave every week,” I closed the boot and we both made our way to the driver seat and passenger seat. Continuing a gentle kiss, Lois put the car into drive, and preformed the usual screeching noise as she pulled out of the car park, around the tight bends and into the dusk. Albany looked so beautiful. Lois pulled out onto the freeway; dodging some of the slower cars, she skimmed down two lanes and took a right into Madison Avenue. I smiled as she had a look of concentration on her face; she was biting her bottom lip, her usual look of determination and concentration. I continued to think of the AWC and what had happened recently. I didn’t like the amount of pressure that had suddenly come with the job. College would be starting soon and I doubted whether I would be able to keep up with my work. “Well, you haven’t said much about the show. How’d it go?” Lois took a turn on Sunset and moved further up the small dual-carriageway and then stopped as the red light broke the rushing traffic, the constant billowing of exhaust fumes and the hissing of motorbikes. “It went okay … I would rather not talk about it,” I gazed longingly at the golden sky as the sun was setting over the horizon, as far as the eye could see. “That bad, huh?” she looked around, her bluish eyes smiling at me. “Well … not exactly … it’s just I’m not sure about Tim,” the green light came on and Lois was once again in full concentration on the road ahead. “Tim? Oh, the guy I met a week ago at Harbrook,” she skimmed down Sunset and took a right into Western Boulevard. “I know,” she pulled to a screeching hault, clipped on the left indicator and quickly jolted forward into a parking lot. “Why are we stopping?” I asked while looking around at all of the Mercs and BMW’s parked next to us. “I thought you might be hungry!” she smiled and hopped out of the car. “Sounds good to me,” I was in hot pursuit. Lois led me down a path and toward a small Italian restaurant. “Is this place new?” I said while holding Lois’s hand and gazing at the night’s sky that was now creeping up, slowly but surely … ‘darkness surrounds all’, I could remember my granddads gloomy approach to life after my grandmother had died. “Yeah … I did an article on it just recently!” she smiled and leaned in closer. “You did? I must read this … come on then, I’m starving,” _____________________________________________________________________________________ The place smelt beautiful. A lovely gust of garlic bread, spaghetti and pizza blew past me as a waiter carrying three trays moved to the family seated next to us. I smiled at Lois who was gazing down at the Italian menu; her face showed the distinct impression that she was baffled. She decided finally on getting spaghetti with garlic bread and I asked for the same. She had already finished a glass of Diet Coke and mine was already half-empty, or as Lois would say ‘half-full’. “This place is a really nice,” I said, staring at the familiar picture of two men, naked, their parts covered by leaves and clouds, one man older than the other, their two index fingers touching. It was apparently meant to be Jesus and God. “Here you are sir,” a young Italian man lay down my meal in front of me and moved over to Lois. “Bella!” he said in a very seductive, Italian accent. I sat their, staring at the young man until he finally left. “You didn’t have to stare,” Lois stated bluntly as she pulled out her napkin from under the cutlery. “Well … he had some nerve,” I knew it was the jealousy in me talking; the hidden demon that everyone holds - which seems to surface when another man/woman gazes at the one you love! “I just don’t like it when men do that …” “Do what? Oh, compliment me!” Lois smiled and took a bite from a slice of garlic bread. “Oh, very funny … he was flirting with you … in front of me.” “He was cute!” I was in the middle of sipping my Diet Coke, when Lois had stated: 'he was cute', I almost choked, my cheeks turning a crimson red. “… Pierce, I wouldn’t have gone off with him. Stop worrying. This show didn’t go well at all, did it? You’re completely unsettled and on edge.” “I just have a lot on my mind, but the show went well, I won the tag match, which for me is a good thing.” “Do you remember that time in High School, when you decided on signing up for the school wrestling team, back then you were skinny and extremely white. Now look at you!” Los smiled and then looked at me again. “You want to talk about it - I mean - whatever it is that is on your mind?” Lois had put down her fork, which now held four strings of spaghetti with a small amount of sauce. I nodded and swallowed the lump of garlic bread that I had shoved into my mouth, while gazing at the Italian waiter, who seemed to say Bella to every woman with a PULSE! To my horror the garlic bread wasn’t manufactured stuff - it was real garlic bread - Mediterranean style. The type where you open up a garlic clove and rub it on the bread. I rushed for my glass of Diet Coke and knocked it back in one go, gasping for air afterwards. The two families nearest me stared at me. Lois meanwhile had already come around the other side of the table and began to pat me on the back - assuming I was choking! “You OK?” she said, worryingly. “I’m fine … it was a little strong!” I said exasperatedly. She laughed and eased back into her chair. She had flicked back a small strand of hair, and pushed her plate aside; pulling out a small laptop from her bag she turned it on and swung it round, so that it was facing me. “Em, what’s this?” I said, while staring at the blue windows screen. “Go to word pad, my news report is in it. By the way, thanks for filling the CD player the other night with a whole load of MUSE CD’s, three to be precise. It didn’t help when I tried to write this.” I smiled to myself at the thought of Lois - hoping for some peace and quiet to write her report - when suddenly, “Bliss” or “Hysteria” blares out of the amps' at 30 volume. Rebecca had fallen asleep against her mother and had begun to talk in her sleep. Marie Lavelle watched lovingly at her younger child and contemplated what her next step would be. She questioned whether she should ring her sister and stay there until she can get a lawyer and take Brad to court. She wouldn’t accept matters unless a judge was involved. Rebecca dozed quietly, muttering words of complete nonsense as she snored. A few strands of hair danced loosely around Rebecca’s face. Marie delicately removed them, her touch made Rebecca move slightly, but then she continued to sleep. Marie looked at her daughter once again and smiled. “This will all be over soon,” she whispered quietly and then closed her own eyes for a while. ______________________________________________________________________________ The morning after I sat at the dining-room table in my flat in Western Boulevard. I had a pot of hot coffee in the kitchen and I had put five CD’s into my five-disc CD player, chronicling Queen’s late work, from “Bohemian Rhapsody to Radio Ga Ga“. I had all of my last assignment splayed across the dining-room table in front of me. Most of the assignments were incomplete and some not even started yet. One assignment caught my eye. It was my work on Photosynthesis and Plant Nutrition. I had only started it when I had been expelled from Thompson College. This assignment was merely a starting point for my grade average at the end of the semester. Professor Sullivan had sent it over to this address two days ago, making it clear I would need to show it to my new professor at the start of next semester. I had so many documents and books in front of me that I didn’t know which ones I would use for my grades and final exams. A splinter in the foot, if you will - and now I couldn’t focus on my college work. Lois had left it there earlier in the morning, just before she headed off to work at the “New York Post”. She mentioned a few things, but I wasn’t too awake at the time, more reminiscing over the previous night spent with her. I decided it would be best to put the Divorce file into the lower drawer of the coffee table and ignore it for now.
