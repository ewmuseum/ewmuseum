Through The Eyes Of Another (ii)

12 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12th of September, 2006 It has begun. What the Atlantic Wrestling Club had witnessed at Coast to Coast was just that; the beginning of something so well planned, so well thought out, so malign, so clandestine that its very existence could only be admired as beauty through meticulous construction. Every last iota of planning so scrupulous in its nature, every detail catered for, everything such a revelation that you could only sit back and admire the plan in its fruition. The time of The Empire had snuck up on the Wrestling Club and this time there would be no return of the Jedi. Who would affront them? Who? would dare play Luke Skywalker in this magnanimous story: no one came close to fitting the bill. Lightsabre clad or wielding fists little would stop the Empire. In the harsh light of a South London morning the most well formed and downright despicable amalgamation of bodies within AWC looked even more sordid. Notice how light did that to things? “Things will look brighter in the morning…” that old adage that clung to every nursing home like the old people clung to their estranged relations: it meant little in modern society and the arrival of the Empire quite literally put it to bed. GA made sure there would be no refraction of this beautiful moment and the light of the suns early morning rays did little to appease the situation. The light and Afeaki’s existence ran as parallel as the train tracks of time, he clung to the shadows and all that procured. His existence was still shrouded in mystery and he could feel the anxiety of those around him: they all wanted to know more, couldn’t wait for more and really… they needed more than he was offering them. He wouldn’t give it to them of course; where was the fun in that. After such a monumental night in his life, where else on earth would he rather be than back home with friends and family. His flight had only just recently landed in Bua Levu, one of Fiji’s trendier dwellings, and instantly the euphoria swept through the masses. He was a hero to these people, a break from the normality, he was proud of his heritage, proud of his Fijian roots. Too many a time Fiji’s youngest and brightest were lured to the shirt of the All Blacks with the promise and riches that it assured, or just left to rot in sordid gangs. No. He was different. That’s why above all the stars that had emanated from these shores he was the one that dazzled brightest across the Pacific night. The title victory meant a great deal to these people: it gave a hope to the young that was previously unheralded. If they followed their hearts and their minds with courage and conviction anything was indeed possible. We’d catch him here… The victory party had already been planned for later in the evening, and all those he knew, all those he cared about would be there. Of course it was practically certain that there would be an element of bandwagon hopping. It was as sure as The Empire’s stranglehold on the AWC. In any walk of life success breeds freeloaders, those who jump on the proverbial bandwagon, doing their merry dance, having their merry pictures taken and gaining their merry publicity. They were cunts, but powerful cunts nonetheless: the Prime Minister’s daughter was there with an entourage of beauties, Fiji’s Rugby team… he had become a cult figure overnight and much how Twista had promised; he’d been made a celebrity overnight. That all seemed in the far off distance, over the setting sun in a land too alien and outlying. A horizon so shrouded in cloud and mystery it had little impact on his day. He worried not about the evening: he’d just go there and have some fun, right? That’s what we are counting on… Anyway, he’d deal with the occasion when and how it came. Tonight was a long way off yet, and further from the man himself than ever before. He wasn’t used to neither the fame nor the fortune that it seemed effervescently intertwined with, if he did this for the money, if he did this for the adoration he would have taken up some pathetic American sport. They always said he’d make it out there, a linebacker, or something like that. Fuck that. He’d settle for being a shadow; just a shadow in the world where shadows reigned supreme. His life was no Fight Club, people knew about it and you most certainly could tell others without fear of reprimand, he was no Palahniuk, but his life was often a gripping tale, a Survivor, if you will. He was drifting into one of those tangents that were slowly becoming his trademark. A sign of his literal style, he was winning plaudits across the circuit as a whole: he like him did this for the love of the game, not the fame. I think people forget about the love of the thing they do, he was seeing it grip the club with petty disputes breaking out about trivial matters. Personal rivalries becoming personal issues for all to see: it didn’t fill him with inspiration, but he couldn’t care less. His love was all about the art and it was shining through. So while they bickered, he improved and he improved fast. The journey was slowly beginning. They all noted he was an introspective character claiming he was keeping them in suspense for too long. They needed concrete personality traits; they needed things to critique, to make fun of, to attempt to bring him down. They’d already tried to nail him for his attire but the superficial nature of their criticism was merely a reflection of the club as an entity. It was a place of intense superficiality and he slowly was realising this. He did not want them knowing him through choice, and not through any means of maintaining a miasma thick with the stench of inscrutability. Who would want friends who so quickly turned into enemies? He’d rather stare at a wall… stare at that image those eyes of Mei enmeshed with a cold reality so torrid and torn they’d be burnt into his soul for eternity and a day still. He’d rather that than listen to three seconds worth of drivel from these egotistical superstars surrounding him. It was why he was walking the golden shores of this beach, alone and tepid, he wasn’t the sociable type. He’d spend his win on the only part of tranquillity left in the westernised isle of his birth. He needed to gather his thoughts, he had an immensely big fight ahead of him, and eyes were slowly turning onto him, under the spotlight intensively, it just wasn’t him. This quiet introspective spectre born to parents of low caste was still very much that boy who was orphaned in that crash. He’d never really grown up. He’d never been that social. He’d never really lived. He’d never… existed. He was Batman after all… he said no to existence: just stick to the shadows, make that play and then back to the sidelines. He’d live later. Would he? No… he wouldn’t… They all wanted to hear him speak, just utter one sentence… one iota of speech on screen. Why would he bow to their pressure? He wasn’t a film star, he wasn’t a gimmick, a character; he was the dose of reality that the world craved. Still they wanted more: this bloodthirsty unquenchable need for a competitor to have the on screen skills of Marlon Brando was becoming tiring. Why should he act? He wasn’t in the Godfather, and he certainly wouldn’t be sleeping with any fishes. He was dressed in his usual garb, hardly party going material, the likes of Lia would never be seen in such couture, his slightly baggy Diesel jeans covered his dirtied pair of white Converse, and his baby pink Hoodie did little to obfuscate his chiselled physique. Yes, he wears pink: today’s colour for the modern man. A loose fitted slouch beanie adorned his head and kept away the chill that each gust of wind brought from the sea inland. He sat on a rock deep in isolative thought, he did not remember the last time he experienced such calm, such inner peace. He was like a miniature Buddha only with more hair and less of a pot belly perched on his rock overlooking the warm swirl of seawater. Any serenity was lost however by the shrill tone of his phone. “Hello?” his voice was sapped of any of that malicious afterthought and nefarious tone that his tenure in the club had often seen. He was a much more relaxed figure now it seemed, and ironically a shadow of the beast which had taken the place by storm. He’d been described as this great animal stalking his prey, painstakingly dissecting foe piecemeal. All that had gone, as if the Fijian soil had cleansed all that anger, all that wounding intent, it was if for those precious moments he was home again he was a completely different entity. No longer this harrowing spectre, no longer this unpredictable shadow clinging to the last vestiges of darkness in the minds of men, it was all gone now. As if now was a time when everything that had caused the volatile nature of his character had indeed never happened… …indeed… it hadn’t… “Where the hell are you man? It’s crazy here, you need to come man… hurry, hurry!” the inane ramblings brought an unfamiliar expression to his face: a smile. The words came from the lips of his one and only truest friend, Sitiveni Vainiko (Siti to you and me). His words brought a smile because in essence their friendship had roots deeper than any superficial gathering like tonight could ever forge. They’d seen the bad, they’d seen the very bad, and hell they’d witnessed the downright despicable, but through it all, they stood tall, and did it their way. Frank Sinatra spoke no truer words; it was as if that old rat pack classic was penned for the two. Siti was far more comfortable dealing with the pressures of modern fame; in fact he rather enjoyed them. Siti was a successful businessman in Fiji in the mould of Alan Sugar. He took a market stall and turned it into a company turning over millions of dollars a year. He was no Bill Gates, he had a soul, and there was no ruthlessness in any of his business dealings. He was just, to borrow a phrase, the ‘boy done good.’ Siti had organised the evening with the host of stars, and he knew how uncomfortable it made him, but it was almost expected these days. Success breeds parties… “Siti…” the words escaped with a sigh of relief, “I’m just at the spot, give me a few hours…” “Hours?! These people need you now man. They’re all asking me about you, and might I add, some lovely ladies are enquiring of your status.” His tone said it all, Siti wasn’t seedy, but he knew a good bit of meat when he laid eyes upon it. The music in the background grew steadily, rising to crescendo. “Get here as soon as you can brother, these people need you. Take care man. Take care.” The phone went dead. Ah well… best make a move. Through the eyes of another our world is a completely different kettle of fish. What we view as hardship, another sees as luxury, our luxuries to another a peasants pot of gold. The world would never be an equal place and through the eyes of other people you were always better or worse. The world does nothing but judge. Would you rather be forgotten or hatefully remembered? Would you rather seek fame and fortune, or live in happiness? Questions we face on a daily basis that we put to the back of our minds. Little did he know, tonight would be his last. It was strange to see this brash and openly pretentious setting holding this exuberant bash for someone who on appearances alone seemed such a humble being. The club was surrounded by flash Harry types and the cream of Fijian society. All sipping their free drinks, all taking in another free night, it was like a Paris Hilton convention, although a lot less refined… if that was even imaginable. The quick, the good and the bold were all here. You could reel off the designers here like a whose who of the fashion world and yet… there he was… baggy jeans, dirty shoes and pink hooded top. It was almost like indie chic. It would be a pain to do this to such a man… but he’d crossed the wrong people. Immediately he was mobbed by the crowds as rapturous applause rung around the quirky club, I felt no actual need to join in the show of appreciation, but feeling mildly out of place I put my hands together. All he did was fight people? What did he want from me? Did he want me to welcome him home with open arms as the saviour of our land, of our people? Thank him for curing AIDS? Thank him for saving the world? He was no Superman, no doctor, no scientist, and yet these people respected him far above those who work tirelessly ever day to make our lives better. Bitter? Me? Not really, I just could not for the life of me see the fuss about him. Talk of him becoming Fiji’s Muhammad Ali was somewhat premature. My eyes scanned the crowd, again it was like a whose who of the social elite in Fijian society. The Prime Minister’s daughter and friends amongst other Fijian film stars, music artists and the like: yet the biggest star of the night was a girl who in many respects was daughter to a man very much like him. He was feared amongst the islands without commanding any real mastery of the craft employed by our gracious host tonight. He had power, tangible fucking power. Not the fake bullshit you find in politics, no, he had the power of life over death, the power of the dollar and the power of a personal army behind him: that my friend is power. Her name was Lisa, a femme fatale if ever one existed. She had that unmistakeable power over men, that ability to whip them into a frenzy with just a flick of her gorgeous hair. She could make men do anything she wished. She was a goddess, she was every man’s Goldie. She smelled like Angel’s ought to smell. Tonight though she wasn’t so much an illusion, this was no sin city, she was very much reality and attracting hoards of attention from males all across the room. Unrequited looks of lust were shot in here direction but not one single man could gauge her attention. Her eyes were set firmly on our host. The night started to take on one of those raucous occasions more suited to the jazz age found in an F. Scott Fitzgerald novel. It was a hark back to a time when life was there to be lived, the rich and the splendid on the Egg’s did their utmost to party into the early hours of every morning. This time though, the concertos and swing dances were replaced by the heavy beats of hip hop and the impious dance moves of the modern generation. It was like a cataclysm of modern Fijian culture, absorbing the new a little too readily and losing touch with the common elements that made their culture such a wondrous part of the world to inhabit. The drink flowed all night with the aniseed flavourings of Sambuca being a favourite of those around the bar. Our host was slowly relaxing into the occasion choosing one of the quieter tables in the establishment to sit down and enjoy the company of his close friend Sitiveni Vainiko. Then it happened. She moved over. His eyes intertwined with her gaze and danced a merry tango; it was how she ensnared all her victims. He looked entranced with her as she let a cute smile slip between her full lips. He smiled back completely lost in the moment. He forgot who her father was for those few seconds, allowing himself to become close to her… and that was it. He was hooked. This was my chance to get to know him. To find out about the man whose life was going to end tonight. “Excuse me sir,” the words escaped my lips slowly and deliberately, “could I have a word or two old sport?” he loved all that old jazz speak. I could see that he was still mesmerised by this raging beauty before him, “yes… of course…” he wasn’t there at all so I quickly offered him a glass of champagne, he gulped it down, “one moment…” He stood moving Siti to the side as he approached the exotic beauty of Lisa. I strained to hear the pair of them involved, their conversation was trivial, the obvious attraction between the pair speaking louder than any words could. My job was done for now anyway. He’d taken the champagne. She’d done her part, although involuntarily, I’ll have to tell the boss about that one. You see many a moon ago, when our fine host was younger and less wise than he is now he did something that you could only dream of and get away with. He embarrassed a powerful, and by powerful I mean the aforementioned power, in front of some powerful associates. That night was the coming together of some of the most powerful men in Russia and the biggest face in the Pacific Isles. It was a joint venture of sorts, and I won’t spare you the details. Our host had a disagreement with the boss and ultimately nearly ended his life in a brawl – embarrassing him in front of these Russian men. Now, someone like the boss can’t be disrespected in front of such powerful company. He especially cannot allow for his only daughter to date such an atrocity. Ladies and gentlemen, this is not the first time your host and Ms. Lisa have seen each other. No, it as things tend to be around your host, is a far more complex tale of Romeo and Juliet proportions. Your host was involved shall we say… with Lisa, and ever since that incident Lisa was forbidden from contact. She made it happen that her father would not seek vengeful action under the condition that she was never ever to see him again. Ever. Alas, the heart wants what the heart wants and Lisa couldn’t continue her life living a lie. She would risk it all for just one more night with him… but the Boss knew this. Who was the boss? Think outside the box… think to the Godfather, to Scarface, to Goodfella’s… The plan was already in motion when the lights on the building were cut. The plan was already in motion when several shots were fired in the venue. The plan was nearly complete when the daughter of the Prime Minister lay wrought with agony as crimson ebbed from every bullet hole wound, deep, emerging from within her lungs and landing in clotted heaps on the ground. The plan was finalised when the now inebriated body of your host lie on the ground clutching the murder weapon with blood stains on his hands… the lights came on and only the Fijian Special Forces were there to put together the pieces. Did he commit the crime? How do I know all this? Because I did this… How did I get an invite? Because I could. Who am I? I thought you’d know by now… I felt pained to do this to such a fine fellow… but I promised the boss a favour. A Volkyeva always delivers promises. Always. 12/09/2004 .::oti::.
