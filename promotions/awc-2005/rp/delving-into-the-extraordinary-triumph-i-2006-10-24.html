Delving Into the Extraordinary - Triumph (i)

24 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Oct 24 Human nature is a mysterious, unpredictable entity that thoroughly effects us as individuals. Defined by elements that cease to be explained without proper experience and cases that illustrate these variable intentions. Guilt, shame, aggression, patience, honesty, drive ... all prime cases of human dependencies. We look unknowingly nowhere to find a divine answer to shed enlightenment on why we act, behave, and make decisions based on our internal desires or social forces. I unintentionally sought out this nearly lost answer, desperate to find the root of my own desires and why fate has guided me towards certain accomplishments and goals. In any case, I was proud of what I had done and was aware that I had opportunities to experience that sense of fulfillment – long and short term goals that had avoided me to that point. I was not a philosopher, however – I had other motives to make such a discovery, such as furthering my ability in the ring and, above all, bettering myself as a human being. Is it not the ultimate goal as mankind to understand our individualistic purpose in this world; to find how we go about meeting such expectations; to gain motivation in striving to accomplish our aspirations? Or instead of goals, are they societal functions that are worked up to be more personal than is actually demonstrated? In practicality, I wondered why winning an AWC championship such as the Livewire or Frontier acted as such a big part of my life. Did these symbols of success have more bearing than simply going about my task as a professional wrestler, week in and week out? Perhaps I wanted power and didn't know how to grasp it, as demonstrated at my first Livewire title attempt. Countering that inability was my practical approach to challenge the roots of human nature, or, at the very least, learn about them. Questions would remain unanswered, but the temptation to know and see rather than simply exist for the sake of it was the present and dominant force in my cause. I had many hurdles to jump. Externally in AWC, but more so my internal battle against not being able to come through in the clutch. I failed to dodge the Livewire-title obstacle only a week prior – and now I was penciled in to face Darcy Crisis for the Frontier title. The latter was a much taller and steadier hurdle. Body and mind would have to function as one and be at their best separately in order to successfully land on the other side without clipping the top and face-planting on the track. My idea was to help myself make the jump, but it all boiled down to being borderline crazy. Exaggerated, it could easily be considered borderline insane (as if such a tale could be exaggerated even more). However, desperate times called for desperate measures, and I anticipated that sometimes, even crazy ides get positive results; if, that is, if I could find my answer in time. The story begins in a somewhat odd setting. Unsure of where I was at the time, I awoke from an unconscious state face-down in a grassy area. It was considerably tall grass, the kind that itches the bottoms of your knees when you tramp through it. Thankfully, it wasn't wet, although the temperature wasn't favourable – a cool breeze swept through the field of tall grass. I wore a thin sweater, which was helpful, and a pair of jeans that kept me somewhat shielded from the chilling October wind. I got up, observing my surroundings; the area in front of me was an open area, with gently rolling hills of grass and trees winding distantly, as far as my eyes could see. I looked to my right – same thing. The left proved to be similar, although a surprise awaited me in a man. He wore a dark brown robe of some kind – an ancient one, the kind that you'd expect to see in a fantasy movie. He was of an average build, but I felt intimidated by his aura of power that he gave off. His smooth, brown beard was the source of this intimidation, as well as his observatory eyes that followed me intently. I looked around awkwardly as the man approached me. “Um ... excuse me ... but ... where am I? What is this? Who are you? What am I doing here?” I paused. “Why?” The man smiled. “Well, I suppose that just leaves 'how', doesn't it?” I frowned. “If you want to look at it that way, then sure.” “I'm not looking at it in any way, Cedric – you are,” he said. I raised an eyebrow. “Wait .. how do you know my name?” “I know many things, and certainly know a lot about you. I research my subjects like no one else can,” he said, and smiled, as if realizing the irony in his comment. “Anyways, back to your original question. First of all – this location here,” he began, spreading his arms and looking in all directions, “is your imagination. Stunning, isn't it?” I didn't take my eyes off the man, and shook my head. “It's a deserted, boring forest with a bunch of trees.” I expected more. “Not so fast, Cedric,” the man warned. “Look behind you.” I hesitated, but decided to oblige. I looked behind me, expecting to see a similar scene of rolling hills. But instead, a walkable distance away stood a gigantic castle. I couldn't make out the fine details of it – but it looked old, made of stone, and the architecture was what is to be expected of a castle – large, square, and impressive. It stood on a rise in the land, looming over surrounding trees and clearings. The most intriguing aspect, though, was I was certain I had looked in the same direction beforehand and hadn't spotted such an obvious structure. I looked back to the man, who wore a smug grin. “I could've sworn that-” “Nevermind, we have more pressing matters,” Tom interrupted. “Fine. Why is that there?” I shot at him. “That is your destination,” he stated forwardly. “What?” I said, stammering. “Why?” “You will find out in due time,” he responded. “How do you know? Who are you?” I asked aimlessly. I felt like I was asking questions for the sake of it – but I felt so uninformed and powerless, it was my only escape. “Shame on you, Cedric. I thought you would remember me.” I looked at him intently as a tense moment transpired between us – I did notice some familiarity in his appearance, his actions ... putting a name to his face was proving to be difficult, though. And then it hit me, a sudden realization. The discovery was as shocking and uncertain as the man himself, and to remember him was somewhat surreal. “Tom?” Months ago I had come down ill and knocked myself unconscious in a hospital bathroom. An encounter with Tom had resulted in some alternate dimension. I had my reasons for believing this was a similar meeting, yet as a consequence of something different. Tom's reason for invading my imagination remained unclear. His power was undoubtedly strong – I felt weak merely in his presence. Our last run-in I had challenged him physically and had ended up getting slammed into the ground; all it took was a flinch from Tom, and I was immobilized. He nodded. “Impressive. You even vividly remember our first encounter, I see.” I hesitated, the realization that my mind was an open book to him dawned upon me. “What do you want with me?” Tom sighed. “I don't know, Cedric ... what do you want with you?” I fumbled the rhetoric question in a similar manner of how the words came out of my mouth. A sentence that was far from English, something along the lines of, “I – er – don – erm.” “You clearly haven't learned much since I last told you what you needed to fix about yourself. Of course, I anticipated our next meeting would be much sooner than now, but I suppose that is because your mind simply wasn't ready for such a challenging invasion,” Tom explained, almost muttering to himself. It made little sense to me. “I don't-” I began. “Enough,” Tom interrupted. “In our case, time is not a factor, but if it were, you would certainly be wasting it,” he criticized. “If you remember correctly – which I doubt – I told you that your first lesson would be on loyalty.” He paused, as if expecting me to say something. “I don't remember,” I answered. “Well, at least you are honest. Of course, it should be known that things have changed since then, and from a general observation of what's been figuratively going through your head recently, there are other concerns that need to be addressed. Therefore, your first lesson will be on execution, which you have been pathetic in recently.” I shot him a hostile look at his mention of my loss at Untouchable. “That was different.” “Quiet,” he said forwardly. “Your intelligence cowers under mine – I want to make that clear before you go on this quest.” “Quest?” Tom nodded. “You are to go to the castle and find what you are looking for; I will not be there to help you. Expect the worst. Good luck, Cedric.” I absorbed the information in one gulp – suffocation was not out of the question. “But-” He disappeared before I could strangle a few more questions out of him – or, in fact, strangle him quite literally. I looked towards the castle and sighed. It had finally dawned upon me that my only option was to follow Tom's instructions, for where else was I to go? Staying put certainly wasn't an option; waiting for a narration to end was never a logical decision, and being an active character in this surreal environment didn't help my cause. The ominous shadow that the huge structure cast upon the forest was in intimidating, but in the same vein, stood as the only destination I had to seek. The vast area around the stone gallery was a friendly reminder of this. And as frightening as it looked from a distance, I couldn't help but be intrigued by Tom's reference to it holding “what I am looking for”. His silly little test was beyond me, but either way, I felt results were on the agenda. I began the trek. It wasn't a long walk. I encountered a few of the expected forest animals, like rabbits and squirrels, but no bears stepped into my path or bats swept down on me, which I felt was somewhat of a surprise. I assumed the environment bent to the artistic will of its creator – me, but I had a subtle feeling that Tom had some input in the design as well. No minotaurs or centaurs leaped out at me to engage in battle, however, so maybe the old man was losing his touch. I was beginning to feel rather smug as I trooped down the nicely-trodden path that wouldn't be out of place in a Zelda game. It was slowly dawning upon me that Tom's lesson – my quest - was rather simple. I almost tripped over my feet as I heard a crackling from the bushes to my left. I halted in a ready position, then crept to a vantage point so I could see the source of the commotion. A figure stomped through the forest, then fought his way through the branches of a bush, making very slow progress. My eyes followed him cautiously as he stumbled out into the path, coming out of the bush. However, this would be followed by a series of stumbles – the old man could hardly stand on his feet. He wore a crown, a cape, a silver protective vest made of thin, woven chain, and black warrior's pants. His right hand clung barely onto the hilt of a beautiful sword. The words almost faltered in my mouth. “King Arthur?” He spat through his thick, graying beard, and muttered something that I couldn't quite understand. “Are you drunk?!” I questioned him unbelievingly. He didn't respond, but merely stumbled backwards and lost his footing, crashing awkwardly to the ground. A moment free of movement from either of us followed, until Arthur rolled over onto his back and reached for his sword, Excalibur, which he had dropped in the process of almost passing out. I pursued him immediately, reaching out a hand in order to help him up. He swiped his sword-free hand towards mine, missed twice, and finally grabbed a hold of it. I pulled back and helped him to his feet, which was not an easy task, as he was unable to stand and fell forward. I caught him by his underarms and heaved him into a stance. He regained his composure and shook out the cobwebs from his mighty fall, then looked at me curiously. Intimidation was very little, as he swayed back and forth, side to side, and looked at me in a strange perspective as if he was worried about vomiting all over. “Are you gonna be alright, Mr ... erm ... Mr. King?” I asked hesitantly, trying not to show disrespect to the King, but in the same vain ... well, he was drunk. “I am fine,” he slurred in a Royal accent.. His voice and composure were as if he was trying to put on the sober act, but failing miserably. “Er .. I think you've had a little too much to cider and wine with the Knights,” I said. “Nonsense! We were at the Round Table!” he boasted. There was a pause, and my eyes flashed to his sword. “Do you like my sword?” he asked, his voice cracking at the attempt of 'sword'. “Of course,” I replied diligently. “What's its name again? Excalibur?” “Ehnoo!!” he said. I think it was a 'no' in royal tongue. “I call this one Cedric.” He paused, looking at me. “Do you know any Cedrics?” I hesitated. “Er ... no, actually.” “Then why are you interested in my sword?” he challenged. “Hmm?” “I was just-” Arthur interrupted, “Do you want to buy this beautiful work of a blacksmith?” he asked curiously, as if anticipating a certain answer. I was unsure of what his expectation was, though. But if he was to be bartered, now seemed like a good time to do so. “Sure.” Surprisingly, he didn't look insulted. Instead, he looked at the blade of the sword, examined the hilt, and offered, “I'll give it to you for one pound.” He paused, awaiting a response. “Final offer.” And at this moment, I felt a bulk in my pocket suddenly. I reached into my jeans and pulled out a handful of coins. I looked at them; they were strange, and I wasn't sure if they would amount to his request, but I held out my hand and showed the coins to him. He approached me and examined them, shrugged, and handed me the sword while he took the money. “Sucker!” King Arthur chimed suddenly, then darted back towards the bushes. I watched as he stumbled through a clutter of trees, laughing somewhat to himself. His possible audience was in the form of two squirrels looking on from an above branch. I examined the sword that I had just bartered off King Arthur ... ... King Arthur. I was still amazed. It was a nice sword, though. Not like I was knowledgeable about swords, as I had never used one, nor had seen very many of them in my life. Not enough to judge what would be considered good, that was certain. But if it I had the ability to imagine what a good sword would look and feel like, the one I held would be it. In any case, I needed to push forward, as the sun was beginning to set and arriving unannounced at a creepy mid-evil castle during the night was not a comforting thought. But according to my anticipated walking time, I was almost there. A few minutes of steady pace later up a gently sloped hill, I stepped into a clearing and the large stone structure cast a huge shadow over me. Let me put it into proportion – it was only a little smaller than say, Hogwarts. Many buildings of ranging sizes rose in all directions, some tall, some short, some wide, some narrow. The base of it seemed to be one large chunk that was both wide and fairly high, only shorter than the odd narrow tower that shot up into the clouds. I cringed at the thought of having to go to the top of one of these towers to “find what I was looking for”, because it seemed like in every one of these movies, shit is always at the top of the tallest tower. Why was I the star of a fantasy flick? Why?! Even though Seymour Almasy had just come into AWC, I was off in middle earth bartering with legendary kings! I started to run. I wanted to be over and done with my 'lesson', or 'quest' as soon as humanly possible. I was a track star in high school, and athleticism allowed me to run for a considerable distance, so I used that to my advantage as I ran up the stone path leading to the front doors of the castle. I finally reached the steps, skipping one at a time as I bound to the top, then reached my hand out to push open the huge doors which would require a bit of manpower. I felt capable, though, and felt the door move forward as a result of my force on it. And from behind me I heard a roar. And a loud breath, one that would torch a tree in an instant. I looked behind me and saw it ... a green, reptilian figure moving towards me at a considerable pace. He was getting close. I ignored opening the door and went to switch my sword from my right hand to my left. I fumbled it and it dropped to the ground. Panic hit as I looked up to see the dragon only a leap away from me. He bounded for me at full-speed and I desperately ran and lunged to my right, clearing the wide cement railing and bailing on the ground in an agent roll that I had perfected. I was immediately back in a crouch directed towards the dragon, whom slammed into the the doors. He smashed through them forcefully and I heard him skid to a halt inside. I scrambled from my position, seeing the opportunity and ran to grab my sword. I swooped it up off of the top platform of the steps and looked inside the broken door to see a vast open space of pillars and little else. I cautiously stepped inside, my sword at ready. The dragon was nowhere to be seen, so I kept moving forward, eyes flashing in every which direction out of human instinct. I kept my crawling pace, very aware of what was going on around me – but I could hear a pin drop. It was chillingly quiet. And then I saw a shadow. It moved. I saw the reptile jump out from behind a pillar and flash his teeth. I leaped behind a pillar of my own in the nick of time as he shot a breath of hot flame in my direction. I heard him coming behind the pillar, my back to the stone pole. I rolled off the edge and did a summersalt, my sword at ready as the dragon spotted my change in location. He charged towards me, a ball of fire shooting in my direction. I dropped to my chest and felt the hotness shoot over my back. I rolled over onto my back and saw the dragon approaching, his sharp claws ready for his next meal as they came shrieking down towards my chest. I rolled out of the way and continued to do so, moving as fast as I could. When I was at what felt like a safer distance away, I scrambled to my feet. I tried to get ready in time but the dragon was already after me. He whipped his tail around and it caught my hand, sending my sword and only weapon skidding down the floor. The sharp edges of his tail had caught my hand and it was bleeding now, a large gash seared from the middle-back of my pinkie to the base of my thumb. With no time to acknowledge the pain I took off quickly in the direction of my sword – it was my only hope. I was moving quickly but felt the dragon moving right behind me. I felt his breath warming up for a shot of flames and dived forwards, the flame going over my back for the second time as I reached for my sword. I turned onto my back and held onto my sword with both hands in a ready position as the dragon came to a surprised stop in front of me. I sat up and jammed the blade into his mid-section, praying to have hit the heart. I pulled away and stood up very close to him ... and he took another swipe with his claws which pierced my back excruciatingly. I fell to my knees in agony as I heard the dragon stumbling towards me. I turned around – he was barely able to stand since being gutted by my sword, aptly named “Cedric”. With one last final show of strength, I plunged my sword into his heart, or where the heart should be. He lost his feet and went crashing down to the ground. I breathed heavily, not celebrating my victory, but admiring myself for defeating such a ravenous creature. A grin of satisfaction spread across my sweaty mug and I began to walk away from the fallen reptile, but the pain in my back from the slash was returning. I struggled to even walk, and dropped to the ground as a result. I went unconscious. * * * I awoke to a silent room. I was laying on my back; the pain from the ripped skin no longer cut through my body. I sat up. The room I was in had a sense of serenity in comparison to the dark cavern that I had just finished battling a dragon in. It was rather small, and had an opening as a window to my left. I got to my feet to investigate. Looking out the window, it was a truly amazing sight. The forest and rolling hills below spawned far and wide, and gave me the immediate impression that I was in the highest room of the tallest tower. “Isn't it magnificent?” a voice asked from behind me. I spun around and saw Tom grinning at me. I returned a half-smile. “Sure.” I paused. “It might look better, though, if I didn't almost get killed not that long ago.” Tom shrugged. “If it's any consolation, I think you've passed the test.” “Think?” I scoffed. “You did quite well, I should admit, although I did have to move you myself to the tower,” Tom reiterated. I looked around the tiny room. “I don't think I've found what I'm looking for, though.” “Have you not?” Tom asked. “Really? I wonder ...” he paused. “I think I've asked you this beforehand, but do you know what you're trying to find?” I considered the question for a second – it was easy enough to answer. “Glory. Power. Respect,” I listed, the words naturally coming off the tip of my tongue. “But you were willing to make certain sacrifices to give those up in order for your own safety, no?” he said, hinting at my situation with Natalie. I nodded. “Cedric, I don't know if you have realized it yet, but with great power comes great responsibility.” I thought about it. “You took that from Spiderman.” “Huh? What's-” “Nevermind.” “As you have found out today, Cedric, there are some things in life that you must pursue on your own. You already know that, but I think it would do you honour to apply that idea to other areas of your life, as you have done here today. Will you continue to struggle with execution? Perhaps, but ultimately, you have completed what you sought to accomplish, instead of merely doing what is expected. And that is an award in its own,” Tom philosophized, instilling a somewhat comforting feeling in me. I was unsure of how to respond, and kept it that way. Tom finally said, “In any case, just like life, there are more lessons to be learned; whether or not I will behold the environment where you learn that will be determined in due time. Good luck, Cedric.” A dark room, invaded by a narrow streak of light that flooded the room from a window nearby, where the curtains were not quite drawn shut. In my hotel in Toronto, Canada, I laid in bed wearing a grin of satisfaction. I knew that I had won. I had found some answers. Not all, but enough to give me a new outlook on life. I turned on my side and whispered, “Thanks, Tom.”
