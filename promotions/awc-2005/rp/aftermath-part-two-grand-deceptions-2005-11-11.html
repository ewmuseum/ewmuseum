Aftermath Part Two: Grand Deceptions.

11 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/11/05 Good Receptionists are Hard to find... The building that belonged to my father’s colleague was more modern than Stakiri towers. It was minimalist, with a lot of shiny metal surfaces and white furniture. You needed sunglasses just to be able to see where your walking. I’d imagine that my father’s colleague, the notorious Mr Chambers, designed it to do exactly that in order to dazzle prospective business contacts into bending to his will. That wasn’t the only method he utilised, of course; I’d learnt that much for myself. On the streets you tend to hear about rumours of the ‘baddest of the bad’ via self-reflective stories or ground-less myths. I guess the trick is to tell one from the other, but that’s nowhere near as easy as it sounds. Believe me. Mr. Chambers was up there with the worst of them. People tended to disappear or sign away their lives when dealing with him. I had no idea whether this one was actually ground in truth or just another truth less myth, but if it was accurate then why would he even need me? If the stories I’d hear about were true, he’d surely have plenty of other ‘associates’ to do his killing for him. I entered his office without even bothering to clear it with his elderly receptionist who was staked out just outside his door, similar to an overly loyal puppy. I saw her, out of the corner of my eye, rise outraged by my blatant disregard for protocols. I very much doubt that she’s used to seeing assassins visit her employer, at least not in daylight hours. I also sense her follow me into the office. As soon as I entered the room, Mr. Chambers glanced up from his desk, still busy signing documents - very important ones no doubt - then nod at somebody on the other side of the door. As I suspected, somebody as important - and as notorious - as Mr. Chambers needed guards around him at all times. And, as drew closer to him, I felt a presence at either side of me, closely followed my a rather stern hand placed upon my left shoulder. The one on my left craned down to speak into my ear; his breath smelt as nearly as bad as his cheap cologne. Those two things alone told me that this was just some two-bit thug. Not the kind of protection one as rich and powerful as Mr. Chambers could afford, but not always the most expensive hired hands were the best for the job. “You need to make an appointment, little girl,” the thug on my left whispered to me. I grinned. Then I whirled around, tapped his hand away from my shoulder and kicked the rather sizeable character in the chest. He flew backward, barely missing the receptionist who’d followed me in, crashing through the glass windows inches away from the door, with beautiful devastation. I shot a warning glance at the other thug who had made a start toward my direction. The receptionist saw the carnage, let out a rather disturbing yelp, then ran out of the office. I then refocused my attention back to Mr. Chambers. He sighed and shook his head. “Do you know how hard it is to find a good receptionist? Vera was the best! I’ll have to double her wages just to get her to talk to me again!” “I’m sorry,” I apologised, “but I’m not one for booking appointments.” Mr. Chambers glanced over toward his decimated glass panelling and his equally decimated bodyguard. “I can see that. Alexa Kendericks I presume.” The other guard developed a grin. “Alexa Kendericks? How do I know that name...?” Oh god, I knew this was going to happen. I expected that at least some of the people in the building could possibly be viewers of AWC, but after what I just did to the guard I didn’t expect that anybody in the room would be stupid enough to actually mentioned what happened at Freshburst. “Yeah, that guy Chainz punked you out on national...” The second thug trailed off. He had to really, I’d hit him so hard his outline had now become a permanent fixture on Mr. Chambers’ office wall. After that, he slumped to the floor unconscious, taking a good chunk of plaster and the paintwork with him. Mr. Chambers chuckled to himself. “Stakiri was right, you are good.” I grinned. “I am good. Maybe the best. But enough small-talk, let’s get down to business.” Mr. Chambers grinned. “Yes, let’s...” Chambers pushed a file in front of me. “Leave and read that. No business will be discussed here. It’ll tell you where to be and who the target is.” I picked up the file and turned to leave. “I understand.” “And,” Mr. Chambers began, “try not to cripple any more of my staff on the way out, thank you.” “I’ll try,” I responded, already out of the office and making my way to the roof top. A Scrambled Assignment... When I reached the building top I immediately undid the string holder and opened up the folder. A picture of the target’s face came first. I memorised the face and placed the picture to the back of the folder. I did not recognise the handsome contours of the target’s face. He’d undoubtedly pissed off Chambers in some fashion, but the reason behind the assignment was a mystery to me. Not that I cared much. Then I read the instructions regarding the kill and let’s just say I wasn’t best pleased. Apparently, I’m not the only one he’s got working on this assignment. He’s got a number of other assassin working the case. Apparently, Mr. Chambers wants to see whom, out of the lot of us, is the best killer. The reasons why, were not provided, but I very much doubt he was just doing this for the sport of it. Probably wanted the best to be on his pay roll or something. Perhaps he didn’t much like going freelance; with those two morons he calls bodyguards watching his back, I can’t really blame him. The kill is a scramble type of affair. No rules. No limitations. Whomever gets the kill first wins. My kind of assignment. I quickly sought through all of the files, disregarding those designated files regarding the other participants in this little game and fingered the piece of paper that told of the time and the place. One hour from now, halfway across the city. I could get there in about forty-five to fifty minutes. This should be a walk in the park... These thoughts evaporated into the air like water boiled in high intensity heat, as I something behind me. Something moved out of equilibrium and I knew right then I wasn’t alone. Whoever it was; he was good. He was obviously quite bulky, but moved as though he weighed nothing at all. I thrust myself into the air, somersaulted and landed, now facing him, across the building from him. The simple logic was this: if he needed to sneak up on me, it probably meant he was going for some kind of closed ranged attack - perhaps using a knife or bladed weapon of some sort - and so putting a little space between the two of us was by far the best option right about now. Now, i finally saw the mystery stalker and recognised his face from somewhere before. “Can’t quite place my face, huh?” His rough voice forced. He raised his head, his long hair and long leather hat hiding most of his face; his knee-long leather trench coat hiding the majority of his weapons. “You ruined an assignment of mine a month back...” I got it. He was the guy that went after Jonathon Winters that time. I stopped him and I‘d bet he‘s pissed off about it. His name was Vandal. “...and so your here looking for revenge.” Vandal grunted. Could’ve been a laugh, although I’m not entirely sure. “Why would I lower myself to that?” He raised a gun, that’d been resting in his left hand the whole time. “I’m just here to eliminate the competition.” “Oh great!” I muttered, preparing myself to move.
