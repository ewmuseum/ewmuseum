Theft of the Lock - Part One

23 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/23 Why was he here? That was the question on the minds of most associated with AWC when they saw his name appear on this week’s Fresh! card. It wasn’t every day that the PTC Global Champion decided to show up as part of a qualifying match for one of a company’s biggest events. Sure, there were the comments he made on PRIME TV about the company, but most in the business wrote it off as words to get under the skin of the PRIME faithful, no more and no less. So why was he here? There was the glory that came with Triangles, the honor of competing in a match on the scale of the Dual Halo and the IronMaze. The honor of facing the best AWC had to offer…but Seymour had set foot in many such matches. He had faced many of the best elsewhere. So… why was he here? There was the chance to cement his legacy in yet another place. Another chance to raise his name in PTC so high that none could ever reach it…but Seymour seemed intent on retiring soon. SO WHY WAS HE FUCKING HERE ALREADY?! The answer is both more simplistic and more complex than you may think… ???????????????????????????????????????????????????????????? Over his wrestling career, Seymour Almasy had seen many crappy venues. He had been in bingo halls and high school auditoriums. He had been in poorly ventilated arenas that probably had mold growing in them. Rarely, though, had he been in a place as bad as this. It was what wanted to be a makeshift laboratory, but clothes strewn all over the place made it appear to be instead what it was…a basement. Why he had come here, he had no clue. Strike that, he did. The man standing before him had promised in a letter that he knew how Seymour could get his wife back. As irrational as such an offer was, and unlikely to be true as it was, Seymour couldn’t reject it. If there was a way, ANY WAY he could get his beloved back, he would do it. That explained why he was here, in the basement of an old apartment building a few blocks away from the main campus of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. That was why he was looking into the eyes of a rather geeky looking man, with broad, black spectacles and a white lab coat. Seymour had been listening to the man’s schpiel for a few minutes now, but he still didn’t quite believe what he was hearing. “So let me get this straight,” PTC’s Global Champion said, looking at the rather elaborate contraption standing before him. “This is an actual, honest to goodness, functional TIME MACHINE?” “Yes,” replied the man standing next to the device, without batting an eyelash. “It is. I’ve been working on it ever since I got out of college. Ten years of my life have been put into this machine, and I am proud to say that it is a smashing success. Why, last night I went back to my college days and managed to get my old self to sleep with a drunk girl so that I’m not a virgin anymore!” Almasy cast dubious eyes at the mechanical contraption. It looked like a car with various attachments, but he had to admit that all appeared to be in order. Except one thing. “Wait a minute,” he asked, eyes fixed on the inventor, “if this is a friggin‘ TIME MACHINE, why the Hell did you tell ME about it? Do you have any idea how much the federal government would pay you for this thing?” “Pay me,” the inventor of the machine scoffed. “They’d destroy it? Damn Bush would be too afraid someone would go back in time and point out that he actually lost the election! My life’s work would be destroyed! Besides, you wrestlers are always killing people and using other ridiculous plot devices, so I figured a time machine would be perfect!” Well, that settled that, this guy was a fan. A delusional, anti-Republican fan, to be more specific. “You’re offering me the chance to use a time machine to save my deceased wife,” Seymour asked, looking at the man with shock in his eyes, “in exchange for…what exactly? Money? I‘ll pay you anything you ask.” “No, it’s not money I want,” the eccentric inventor replied. “I want something that money can’t buy me. I want something that only a top wrestler can get me.” “Oh no,” Seymour moaned, low and under his breath, “he’s a collector…” Seymour knew the type. They valued autographs. Title belts, if they could get them. Anything and everything of value related to the industry… “I want a lock of Ellis Nash’s hair.” Of all of the things Seymour Almasy expected to hear at that moment in time, the prior statement had to be about 4,215,356th on the list. “A lock of her hair?” The scientist had a blissful look on his face, as he responded. “Yes! It’s all I need to complete my shrine!” “…you have a shrine to Ellis Nash?” “Of course I do,” the inventor snapped, as if it was the most natural thing in the world. “I have the official Ellis Nash “Nashty” t-shirt on a mannequin designed to look like her, all of the restraining orders she’s filed against me…” With that, he practically danced over to a blue tarp nearby, whisking it off and to the floor. There, just as he promised, was the mannequin image of one of AWC’s most prominent female stars. “Don’t tell me that’s real hair,” Almasy moaned in disbelief. “Oh, of course it is,” came the response. “I used to go stal--err, follow Ellis on the road when she got her hair cut. She eventually caught onto me though. I just need one more lock to make the likeness perfect! That’s where you come in, Mr. Almasy.” Seymour Almasy rolled his eyes. Repeatedly. Under most circumstances, he wasn’t one to feed the unhealthy fetishes of nut job scientists, but if this one’s invention could help him get Laura back, he was willing to do anything. “What the Hell was your name again,” Almasy asked, extending his hand out for a shake. “Mat,” came the response, as the two men shook hands. “In that case, Mat, you’ve got yourself a deal.” Seymour slowly turned to leave, trying to avoid looking at the scientist attempting to make out with his Ellis mannequin. This was going to require some thinking. And since he was in Massachusetts, he figured he could do it over a piping hot bowl of clam chowder. --------------------------------------------------------------------- Seymour had, it seemed, gone from one shithole to another today. The difference was that while the chowder house he was currently in looked like a run down eatery from the outside, it served up some damned good clam chowder. But the clam chowder, while extremely delicious, was not the important thing on the table. When Seymour Almasy had a goal in the sport of kings, there were none better than he at researching it. That would explain why Seymour had strewn on the table an absolute shitload of information from the Atlantic Wrestling Club’s website, and was now reading it aloud to himself, ever trusty tape recorder putting all of the precious date on tape for posterity. “Name: Ellis Nash. Hometown: Cortland, New York. Height: 5’7”. Weight: 126 pounds. AWC win-loss record: twenty-two wins, two draws, twelve losses. This could be rather difficult…” Seymour sighed, slumping back onto the hard backed restaurant chair. Nash was many things, and loyal to AWC was at the top of the list. The odds of her signing up for one of PTC’s interfeds was nearly nonexistent…not to mention the fact that he’d sworn to retire from those after GTT6. That meant that he really only had one option. “I could always go to AWC….YES! THAT’S IT! I’LL GO TO AWC!” For a few moments, Seymour was triumphant. Of course, that lasted about three seconds before he realized several things wrong with that basic plan. “There’s no WAY that girl’s going to let me cut a lock of her hair,” he said, in despair. “She’s more cynical than a MySpace convention! She’d probably think that *I’M* a creepy stalker, not that Mat guy back at MIT! No, no, that won’t do at all. Hey, maybe I can ask as Emoticon. Emoticon always was a hit with the ladies…” After a few moments, he realizes that instead, Emoticon was usually hit BY the ladies. Seymour’s time as the Soul of the Poet could be categorized as many things, but successful with women was not among them. “…okay, so that’s out. No, if I want this lock of hair, I’m going to have to take it by force. That means I’ll have to get in the ring with Ellis. That’s cool. I can do that.” With the practiced eyes of a veteran, he scoured the former Frontier Champion’s moveset, wincing as he went. “There certainly are a lot of kicks here,” he said, sighing. Seymour didn’t like to be kicked. Nor did he like to be kicked by a woman who would likely talk endless amounts of shit while doing it. “Hmm, this is bad. Besides, if I try to pull out scissors against her in a regular match, I could either get DQed or she could flee. Yevon, why does this have to be so complicated?” Things were looking grim indeed for the Final Fantasy. As fate would have it, though, the PTC Global Champion happened upon an advertisement on his cluttered table. Considering his propensity to switch federations, he was on every federation in PTC’s mailing list, and got more wrestling related news than even more dirt sheet subscribers. One of the adverts had, seemingly, snuck its way into his information on Ellis. It was a most fortuitous consquence. “Hmm…AWC presents Triangles,” he mused, looking at the flyer. “Say, isn’t that the event with the big cell shaped like a Triforce?” Indeed it was. Seymour smiled, not merely because the match involved a structure that looked like a vaunted item in the Legend of Zelda series. No, he smiled because written there, under “Confirmed for Triangles” was the name of one Ellis Nash, who would be residing in the green triangle. He had the chance he needed. In the cell of Triangles, why, he could easily corner Ellis Nash and claim a lock of her hair. And then, he could go save his sweet Laura and all would be right in the world of Almasy again. “Hmm,” he managed, chortling in his joy, “I think I’ve got a phone call to make.” And so it was that one phone call later, Seymour was in the draw for Triangles. Of course, most plans don’t go perfectly, and Seymour’s was no exception. --------------------------------------------------- On the streets below a well known Boston hotel, a pedestrian had to swerve at the last possible second to avoid an object hurtling down at him at frightening velocity. It crashed on the pavement, and the fortunate fellow stooped to pick it up. “It’s a shoe,” the man said, puzzled. “How the Hell did a shoe come falling out of the sky? Shoes don’t fly.” No, good sir, shoes don’t fly. Not normally, anyway. The only time they DO fly is when Seymour Almasy had realized a flaw in his plan. “NOOOOOO! How could they let the LOSERS pick which triangle the winners get to go into? I thought the spoils go to the victors, not the B.O.T. and Jonny Kae!” To be fair, for the most part, Seymour’s plans had gone swimmingly. He’d actually been picked in the draw. He’d been paired with a champion in Johnny Lexicon. They’d drawn two opponents with subpar records that Seymour thought he stood a good chance against. All he’d have to do was win, and then convince Mr. Lexicon to take the green triangle. Of course, AWC’s wrinkle in the Lethal Lottery now had Seymour scrambling. “What can I do NOW?! There’s no guarantee those idiots will put us in the green triangle! And then, I’ll have to go through Lexicon and either Garbage Bag Johnny or Pierce Lavelle to get a shot at Nash, and that’s provided she even makes it out of her triangle! This is bad! Very very bad!” Rising to his feet, the Final Fantasy began to pace. Now, he couldn’t count on Triangles to get the hair he needed. He would potentially have to jump her backstage, hold her down, and get the hair that way. That was a rather unpleasant thought. Not only would it be difficult, it would also make him look really bad. “I’ve got enough people who hate me already,” he said, sighing. “I really don’t need to make any more enemies than I have to.” As if that was bad enough, Lexicon himself was proving to be a potential obstacle. After all, the man’s manager badmouthing Seymour before all of PTC wasn’t exactly boding well for the Almasy/Lexicon team. But it didn’t matter. None of that mattered. None of the adversity put in Seymour’s path mattered. The Final Fantasy rose from his seat by the laptop he checked the Fresh! card on, and promptly flopped onto the hotel’s bed. Propping his head on the pillow, he dug into his pocket for his wallet, and flipped it open. There within was all the inspiration he needed. Gazing back at Seymour was the lovely visage of a woman. Chestnut locks framed a delicate face. Her eyes told the story: the expression was honest, open, and loving; the sort of look that was unmistakable. A look that said, without words, “I love you.” “Soon,” Seymour whispered to the picture, clutching it close to his chest. “Soon we can be together again, I promise.” The more he thought about this situation, the more absurd it was. There was no way that that guy at MIT had a time machine. No way. It simply wasn’t possible. Time machines did not exist. That was a fact. Of course, vampires didn’t exist, either. That was what he’d thought until he’d had Lia Radilu Vlaszy’s fangs halfway in his neck and his slight frame was spasming with what could only be called an otherworldly pleasure. Maybe it was the same with other things, like time machines. Maybe the world truly was more complicated than it seemed at first glance. “Wouldn’t that be interesting,” he mused, toying with the idea. As ridiculous as it was, believing in the time machine gave him hope, not only for Laura, but for the whole world. “Looks like I have a new quest,” Almasy said, a bit boldly. He hadn’t been on a quest in forever, not since Super Series. “A quest for a lock of the caustic maiden’s hair. A quest for the woman I love. I can take no chances. I must be victorious this week, or my quest will end. The “Game Over” screen will appear, and I will never see Laura again…” That was all he needed. Knowing that changed the entire match around. It made this contest must win. And when the chips were down, Seymour Almasy had a funny habit. Most people call it ‘victory’.
