The Date

28 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28/07/06 Location: Wesley Reno’s Home – East Rutherford, NJ. Time: 5:51pm I had to be completely honest with myself. I wasn’t one for dating at all. The expectations on you, the gazes, the subtle signs that give you that fleeting ray of hope that something – anything – will happen between the two of you. It was all a little bit too much for me. Save a six month relationship with a woman a few years back, I hadn’t engaged in any form of courtship. I lacked any experience in this field, and frankly, I was petrified. Rejection, destroying my already fragile reputation, humiliating myself in front of the one woman I actually liked spending time with. The beautiful Lilly Baines, whom I so fondly referred to as my AIM accomplice, had just turned twenty-two years old and I had decided to treat her with dinner in her favourite restaurant. Lilly had been a part of my day-to-day life for several weeks now. Our AIM conversations were the only thing that kept me going as I suffered a dry patch in my political career – the wrestling angle up in the air, no indication of any resolution. She gave me a consistent companion, someone I could confide in. Just over an hour ago, I had left a message on Lilly’s phone to apologise for last week’s episode when I had so absent-mindedly forgotten to inform her that I had to train for my first professional wrestling match. The poor girl was left to eat alone in Barton’s, her smoked salmon acting as her only friend. Tonight, no such thing would happen to us. I would make sure that it would be a flawless date from start to finish. My preparation was well underway, too. A bouquet of red roses and a heart-shaped candy box filled with all the kinds of chocolate one could imagine were placed neatly on my dressing table, ready to present to her. Now I just needed to keep Calamity Phil away and all would go well. Or at least I hoped it would… My thought process was brought to a screeching halt when my phone began to chirp in the living room. I sighed aloud and picked it up, placing the receiver between my right shoulder and ear so that I could finish off scribbling down a “love note” for Lilly. “Hello, Wesley Reno speaking.” “Well, well, well, you are still alive. I’m surprised nobody’s bumped you off since that political debate last week. Man, how brutal were you, Wes! You weren’t drunk, were you, because that would at least put my mind at ease.” The voice at the other end was a familiar one. The arrogance from the man on the line seemed to flow through the telephone lines so that it was almost tangible. Dean Fisher’s call was not a welcome one. Fisher and I were fierce political rivals. We grew up in the same neighbourhood, but at the age of eighteen he moved to Manhattan with his family. Fisher was the antithesis of everything I stood for as a politician. He boasted of his ability to save the people from what he dubbed “America’s sinking ship”; he joined the party I oppose and at this moment in time was my main rival. Even if we had nothing to argue about, he would create something. That was just how he functioned. “What do you want, Dean?” I asked, the discontent detectable in my voice. “Oh, I’m just checking up on you, Wes. That debate was off the charts, if the charts go from awful to absolute bullshit,” he said, laughing heartily to himself. Fisher was hitting me while I was down. It was a typical politician’s move. Were I in his situation, namely able to criticize one of my main rivals, I would have probably done the same thing. Perhaps not to the same extent, though. I feared that he might have already leaked this story to the press, which would not do me any favours for the upcoming elections in August. “Don’t even start with me, Dean. I’m perfectly fine, don’t you worry. I put that little incident down to the actions of a man who wasn’t supposed to become involved in the debate. He was probably some lunatic from around here. It was free for the public to enter, after all.” I spoke with unnerving certainty, a skill all politicians learn to master. The lunatic I referred to in passing was of course my personal trainer and tag team partner, Phil Allen. I believed that Fisher would buy the cover story, however. “True,” he scoffed, “Very true. Still, you seem to be losing the plot slightly, Wes. I called you for a different reason, though. I do enjoy taking the odd jab at your career every now and then, of course.” Trademark Dean Fisher. He always had to be the one to get the last word in. I cursed away from the phone as I dropped my notepad on which I had scribbled the love note to Lilly. “Get to the damn point, Dean. What did you arrange, a tea party?” “Not exactly. Better,” Fisher replied, sounding unusually stern. “There have been a few rumblings in the last few weeks regarding the Ruth Beckford scandal in New York. I received a call yesterday from a news reporter based there who says they could offer us some valuable information about the case. Let’s face it, man, this one is big.” Fisher’s point about the Ruth Beckford scandal intrigued me. Ruth Beckford was a New Yorker who had committed manslaughter and countless times had locked young men between the age of sixteen and twenty-one in her home, tortured them and murdered them. Beckford had disappeared recently just as a private investigator had allegedly been working on the case. “Go on, I’m listening.” “This reporter asked specifically for you and I, man. Don’t ask me why. Maybe it’s because we’re the best both our parties have to offer at the moment. Maybe it was random. It just seems like more than a coincidence to me.” I thought I actually heard a pang of fear in Fisher’s voice, as if he needed my support on this trek to New York. Was it that he didn’t trust this reporter to meet him alone? I doubted anyone had actually asked for me, but I decided to humour him and accept his proposal. We said our goodbyes and I hung up. Suddenly my focus had switched from Lilly Baines to this new case. The ramifications of being involved in such a case and possibly solving it excited me. I imagined how beneficial it would be for my reputation. Granted, I wasn’t a cop, but I knew the right people. If this reporter knew something that could help me solve this thing, my stocks would skyrocket. The time had come for me to seize my opportunity both in the political world and in the relationship world. My sleek black BMW was turtle waxed to death, and I let my finger glide across its bonnet to double check its cleanliness. The sun was low in the sky. I looked at the digital clock as I clambered into the front seat of my car. It read 7:31pm. The date that would undoubtedly decide the course of my relationship with Lilly Baines was about to take place. Beads of sweat trickled down the back of my neck. I had to be calmed down. I needed solace from this pressure situation. My cell phone began to buzz and vibrate with my ring tone, Muse’s “Bliss”. The name on the caller ID made my stomach sink. I felt an uncomfortable churning inside just then as I read the name aloud to myself. “Phil Allen.” I flipped the phone open and switched speakerphone on. Allen’s booming enthusiasm hit me like a ton of bricks. “Good evening, Wes! I don’t suppose you’ve got two, maybe three hours free, do you? We’ve really gotta improve those mat-wrestling skills of yours.” It was a cheap shot, but I was in no mood for Allen’s games. I decided to be straight with my trainer and give him the lowdown on my plans for the evening. No regrets. The date was on, and if I didn’t get moving soon, I would be late to meet the poor girl again. I would be crucified by her for that. “Look, I’m gonna be really straight with you here, Philly,” I said sternly, my voice projecting my unhappiness with clarity. “I am going out tonight on a date with a girl I know. It’s arranged for 8pm, so I really don’t have time to chat about grappling.” I thought my to-the-point attitude would elicit a similar response from Allen, but I was way off. “Ah, you’d rather be grappling her. I know how it is.” I grinned at his poor attempt at humour. “You know, I was quite a hit with the ladies back in my day. Maybe I could give you a few tips, come along with you?” His invitation was both disturbing and shocking. My mind raced, desperately trying to decipher a suitable response which would both thank him for his offer and graciously refuse. Politicians were supposed to be masters of twisting words to make them difficult to interpret. This skill had somehow escaped me somehow. “No, no, there’s no need, Philly. I appreciate your offer, but I’ll decline. I’ll be up tomorrow at a reasonable hour for training. Don’t wait up. I’ll catch you later, coach.” My sign-off was delivered in an abrupt fashion, signaling to Allen that the conversation was over. I wouldn’t allow his incessant questioning to alter my good humour before this date. I hung up the phone and drove out the driveway, turning left to head to Barton’s. I could smell the fish already. As I turned, however, I caught a brief glimpse of another vehicle in my rear view mirror. Very faint, but definitely there. I opted to ignore the vehicle. It was nothing more than traffic. Lilly’s face came into my mind, her beauty illuminating the otherwise dull happenings inside my conscious mind. I had to hit a home run tonight. Phil Allen had been tailing Wesley Reno’s BMW for five minutes now, and he was still content that he was keeping a safe distance so as not to arouse any suspicion on Reno’s part. Their phone call was short and sweet. Reno’s style was usually quite the opposite to this, opting for long-winded spiels to express his feelings. Allen knew something was not quite right with the young man, so following him to this so-called “date” of his was the best option. Allen watched as Reno pulled in to Barton’s Restaurant on River Road just over six miles from Reno’s home. Allen parked his car on Boston Avenue where a group of misled youths eyed up his vehicle. Allen dismissed them, confident in his car’s security system. A klaxon on his keys would alert him to anyone attempting to break in. It was foolproof. Allen stepped out of his car and locked it behind him, shooting the teens a glance that said “Touch this and I’ll break you”. Allen saw the spotlights that surrounded Barton’s in the distance and headed down the sidewalk to where he hoped to find Reno. Allen was confident that his woman know-how was still top notch. A simple hint now and then wouldn’t faze young Reno. Allen’s plan was simple – grab a table close to Reno’s and help him out when he needed it. His nervousness around the opposite sex was apparent to Allen. By the end of the night, Lilly would adore Reno because of his handiwork. Barton’s Restaurant was one of the most popular venues in East Rutherford, New Jersey for a night out. If you were looking for a good time, coupled with some of the best live music around, Barton’s was the place for you. As I approached the entrance, my throat seized up as I spotted Lilly Baines, resplendent in a long, white dress. It was tight against her skin, which accentuated her gorgeous figure. I couldn’t keep my eyes off her. She held out her hand, which I took with a marked degree of eagerness. “Great to see you, Lilly,” I whispered playfully into her ear. “You’re looking handsome tonight, Wes,” she teased, not directly responding to my comment. “I’m glad we could finally meet up.” Lilly tightened her grip on my hand, showing her discontent at our previous date not taking place. Wronging a woman was not ever part of my plan. Tonight I would sweep this young woman off her feet. The waiter led us to our table, which by no coincidence was Lilly’s favourite seat in the restaurant. It was situated on the second level of the building, and provided a stunning view of the city. Lilly had always enjoyed sparkling objects – rings, glittering lip gloss – so logically I concluded she would enjoy this. “Listen, Lilly, I hope we can forget all about the last time. I know I was stupid that night. I should have called.” I reached across the marble-topped table, its cold surface soothing, and caressed her hand. “I should have treated you with more respect. I dropped the ball and I sincerely apologise.” Lilly’s mouth curled up at one side into a half-smile, a clear sign that she forgave me. She held my hand tenderly, stroking it up and down. “Apology accepted, Wes. Don’t dwell on it. Nothing we do can ruin this night now. It’s just you and me.” Those words reverberated in my mind for a moment - just you and me. I thought about the brief suspicion that I was being followed to the restaurant just moments ago. Was there someone tailing me here? Perhaps Dean Fisher had to speak to me about the Ruth Beckford scandal? Could it have been one of my neighbours? Almost instinctively, I turned my head to the left and scanned the seats adjacent to my own. The last person I wanted to see in this restaurant looked back at me, a huge grin on his face. To be fair to Phil Allen, he had presented himself in decent attire tonight. The question remained, why was he here? Then I remembered. Our phone call not more than thirty minutes ago. I had specifically warned him off following me there. I could not avoid the distressed look on my face. Lilly appeared to be concerned. “Erm, Wes? Is everything okay? You look a little flushed.” Flushed was her diagnosis, but I was a lot worse off than that. Phil Allen had been the bane of my very existence since I signed him up as my personal trainer. He had essentially removed any semblance of a life that I had outside the world of politics and replaced it with grueling hours of weightlifting, jogging and wrestling. I had to put my foot down and kindly ask him to leave. “Could you excuse me for just a second, Lilly? I need to speak to someone just over there. I won’t be more than a minute.” Lilly did not look impressed, but buried her head in a menu as I strolled over to Allen, my unhappiness with his appearance evident in my demeanour. He extended his hand to shake mine, but I slapped it away and went face to face with my personal trainer. “What’s up with you, Philly? Why did you show up here tonight when I asked you not to? You screwed up the last date with Lilly by dragging me off to subject me to hours of falling down and bruising myself. Now you’re here to, what, sabotage my date again?” Allen reacted by raising his hands in an innocent fashion. “Why do you always make me the bad guy? I’m only here to give you the hints you need to get this chick to sleep with you tonight,” Allen boomed, his voice far too loud for my liking. I shot a glance over to Lilly, who apparently had heard the comments and looked far from pleased. “What the hell do you think you’re doing?” I seethed, whispering to avoid making a scene in the middle of this upper class restaurant. “I’m trying to help, isn’t it obvious? You know, Wes, women don’t appreciate it when you leave them alone. In fact, your date looks pretty pissed off right about now. How would you rate her? I’d say a nine, but – “ “Stop it,” I interjected, “stop playing games with me, Philly. I’m going back over there to my date, and if I don’t see you gone in one minute, I’m calling the waiter over and making a complaint that you’re intimidating me with your behaviour. You’ll be booted out of this place!” Allen feigned that he had been hurt by my comments, though I knew deep down he would not leave. He cleared his throat theatrically as I walked back over to Lilly. She searched my face for an answer to her suspicions about why I had left the table. I didn’t give her one. “Sorry about that, Lilly. Are we ready to order?” I piped up cheerfully. “Wes, drop the act. I know there’s something you’re not telling me. If you want this relationship to work, we have to be honest from here on out. So tell me,” she said, her eyes piercing my mind almost ready to guess what was on my mind. I had to give her credit. She was an intelligent woman who could read situations very well. Women always had the ability to read me like a book. That was generally my problem with the opposite sex in the past. I could never give them any excitement, all of it sapped out of a relationship in my first night with the woman. I was determined not to allow this to happen with Lilly. I would come clean about my secret wrestling training, my new trainer with whom I had just been speaking and my new life. “Okay, Lilly, I’ll tell you. It started about – “ Lilly pointed to the opposite side of the restaurant, where a waiter was remonstrating with a man dressed in a white shirt and black trousers. The man was none other than Phil Allen. Allen grabbed the waiter by his shoulders and began shouting, arguing that he preferred the salmon to the mackerel. “Sir, please! If you would just allow me to ask the cook!” the waiter pleaded. “I need to eat salmon, waiter! My friend over there has a date, so a few oysters wouldn’t be bad for them. They’re aphrodisiacs, I understand?” Allen replied. My heart sunk. Lilly no longer wanted to hear an explanation from me about my life. What I did was no longer her concern. Allen had literally ripped my reputation to shreds in Lilly’s eyes. She stood up, sarcastically thanked me for the lovely night, and promptly left the restaurant. I was emotionally crushed as I watched her walk down the stairs and out of my life. Sitting in the front seat of his Chevrolet, Dean Fisher was in complete darkness, the moonlight his only companion on this chilly night. His Chevrolet was invisible under a large oak tree in full bloom. Fisher admired the majesty of nature for a moment, something he had always been fond of. Fisher was of the belief that there always had to be an escape from the real world. In his case, this was politics. Nature provided him with a pleasant setting in which to think to himself quietly and absorb what went on around him. Several people had passed by since Fisher first spotted Reno’s BMW pull in over forty minutes ago. Fisher didn’t expect to see Reno anytime soon, but had a strange feeling he knew the woman coming in his direction on the sidewalk. Her sparkling white dress caught his eye. Her beautiful figure was mesmerizing to him. He pondered where she had come from, who she might be. For once in his turbulent life, Fisher didn’t care. Throwing caution to the wind, and accidentally bumping his head on the door frame in the process, he opened the passenger door. Lilly Baines’ face told a story as she regarded Fisher with caution. Fisher flashed his political smile, the one that had been instrumental in his rise to the top. Lilly Baines couldn’t help but smile back at him. Fisher made the first move, realizing from her demeanour that she felt vulnerable. “Bad night?” She reacted by raising her eyebrows in confusion at his question. “What makes you think that, if you don’t mind my asking?” He sensed an uneasy streak in the woman’s voice, one he had heard many times in the past. He approached her slowly and extended his hand. She took it almost unconsciously, as if their hands were opposite poles. “Forget it, miss. My name is Dean Fisher. It’s a pleasure to meet you. Might I say you’re looking gorgeous tonight?” Lilly Baines allowed herself to smile. She owed herself that much after the horrific time she had endured at the hands of her longtime friend Wesley Reno and his associate on what should have been a magical night. Instead, it was a shambles. Fisher stared into her mysterious eyes, their gazes locked on each other. “Thank you for the… Dean is your name, yes?” she asked, her voice trailing off slightly as if she was semi-comatose. “Dean Fisher, miss. I didn’t realise we were already on first name terms,” he remarked, laughing to himself. Lilly let out a girlish giggle, too, clearly smitten by Fisher’s charms. “Hey, would you like to maybe go out for a drink sometime, miss?” came the invitation from the suave politician. “I have a name, you know,” she giggled, playfully reaching out and touching his hand. “It’s Lilly Baines. And to answer your question, I would absolutely love to go out with you.” Fisher felt a rush of excitement, the likes of which he had not experienced since his last promotion. Never before had he been swept off his feet by a woman like this. He smiled once again, unable to hide his joy. “It’s a date,” he chimed, wishing Lilly Baines a good evening as she strode into the darkness. The cold night air rushed into my face as if to mock me after what was possibly one of the worst nights of my life. I couldn’t lie to myself about how I felt right now. I was internally crushed after the first date debacle, but to leave Lilly like I had tonight and make her sit through Phil Allen’s embarrassing jibes at us, I couldn’t even face her anymore. It was times like these that I needed a friend, someone to look up to, someone who had experienced these moments before. “Looking for some company, kid?” The voice belonged to my personal trainer, Phil Allen, who had resolved the situation with the waiter a few moments ago. The waiter and he had parted amicably, with the former receiving a large tip for his trouble. I refused to look over to my trainer, whose presence alone angered me. I didn’t respond to his question/ “Look, I know you’re pissed off about tonight, Wes. I know that. I just need you to understand that if you truly want this team to work, if you truly want to be seen in the wrestling world as wrestling’s first politician and thus expand your horizons, you have to commit. You have to come with me to train even when training is the last thing on your mind. Please understand that.” I was in no mood to listen to Allen’s desperate rants about training and how I would break into the wrestling business as a politician. Not until I heard a tone in his voice that was unfamiliar to me up to this point. Compassion. A desire to help. Phil Allen was reaching out to me. “I do understand that it’s important, Philly,” I replied under my breath, “I just find it difficult to distance myself from my normal life. Politicians breathing down my neck, reporters looking for the latest gossip on my next move. I’m under tremendous pressure every day to cough up results for these people, and training doesn’t make it any easier for me. Do you know where I’m coming from?” Allen regarded me with an air of sympathy for the plight I went through daily. He pursed his lips and placed a reassuring hand on my shoulder. “I know, Wes. From now on I’ll keep my distance. Maybe I have been getting involved a little too much in your life recently, but that’s only because I’m looking out for you. I want to keep an eye on you. Without you, remember, there would be no team. There would be no Coalition.” Our eyes locked for several seconds, the last words spoken by my partner echoing in my mind. There would be no Coalition. These words soothed me slightly, despite what had occurred tonight. I briefly wondered where Lilly had gone, but knew that it was a pointless endeavour chasing women now. The intense stare ended when Allen broke it, grinning suddenly. “What’s so funny, Philly? What, did I get fish on my face or what?” I inquired, although I knew very well that there was no possibility I had fish on my face, as we hadn’t even started our meal. “Nope, but come to think of it, you resemble one!” he chuckled, his muscular frame seeming to shake with the laughter. “What now?” I said aloud, though it was more to myself than Allen. “We eat. Forget about her, Wes. She’s not worth your while. In the wrestling business, we’ll make it big and you’ll have chicks begging for even a chance to see Wesley Reno. Are you in?” I slapped him on the back in a friendly gesture and smiled. “Yeah, I’m in.” “Great,” he replied, his eyes searching the area around us. “Now, are there any Burger King’s around here? I fancy a quarter pounder with cheese.” As my tag team partner and I began searching the streets for a reputable place to eat, I recounted the events of tonight in my mind. Tonight was a sign for me. It redirected me into a new pathway, one that I dreamed would lead to stardom. Deep down, I was still the politician from East Rutherford, but I had more to offer the world. More to do, more to see, more to sink my teeth into. My journey had only just begun…
