An Extended Meditation on the Fairer Sex

24 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 24, 2006 I was walking to a bar the other night to meet some of my friends. I remember that it was windy out, and I was wearing a light jacket. I hated the wind. It made the air colder, and it turned lighting a cigarette into a chore. You'd have to turn your back to the wind, which was usually shooting between buildings from every direction. You'd cup your hands over the flame, almost holding it, but as soon as you sucked through your carcinogenic yet glorious tobacco straw, the wind would always get the best of you, and the flame would die out. The walk seemed to take longer than usual, but perhaps that's because I was walking alone. I had all the time in the world to pay attention to just how long the walk was. I could count my steps and number hundreds and then thousands. And then I'd lose count, and I'd swear out loud, and people would think I was crazy. Or I'd start singing whatever song was stuck in my head, but I'd slow to a quiet mumble when I'd pass someone. I was beginning to feel good. I had taken a handful of Vicodin before I had left, and I was entering that floating sleepwalk that I sometimes do when I'm awake but not alert. I started stumbling an inattentive straight line down Green Street. I was a few blocks away from my destination, and I smiled about how I was screwing the system. I hated the bars. I hated the people who went to the bars, and I hated paying for overpriced drinks and then feeling obligated to put an extra dollar down because some slut or some asshole filled a glass for you. I hated paying twenty five dollars to get hammered. Tonight, I'd probably be out of it without dropping ten bucks. 'Thank you,' I said meekly. And then I walked away, leaving the five dollar bill on the counter with two bottles of two dollar Old Style in my hands. I carried the bottles to a table in a dimly lit corner where a couple of guys were talking behind a cluster of empty glasses. They were both wearing black hoodies and already slurring words. I slid into the booth next to Dave. 'Hey, Suge, did you and Cait ever do it in the ass?' Dave asked my roommate Sugah. 'Come on, man.' Suge was defensive. He was still hung up over his last girlfriend. 'Well, did you?' 'Drop it, man,' I said. I just wanted to drink my beer. I didn't want Suge to get angry. He did that easily when he was drunk. His lip was still swollen from a fight he'd gotten into last month. Hell, I remember when we lived in an overpriced two bedroom drywall box. We both got wasted and started trading punches to Tool's 'Opiate.' The officer who responded to a neighbor's call was more than surprised to see that we were the only people there, and we were fighting just for the fuck of fighting. The son of a bitch laughed at us because we were both half naked, disoriented, and hell bent on explaining that if it was the gooks below us who called the police on us, they were the ones blasting their computer speakers while playing World of Warcraft eighty hours a day. Anyway, Dave continued ranting about his anal adventures. I was genuinely disinterested in his excavations of the exterior orifice, so I excused myself to get a couple more bottles of Old Style. As I waited for the bartender to bring me back my beer, I surveyed the scene of the barroom. There were a lot of sluts. Yep. A lot of skanky bitches. There were some huddled around the jukebox. They were undoubtedly putting on crappy songs they could sing or dance along to. 'Bohemian Rhapsody' had played twice already, and as much as I like Queen, few of the drunken bar sluts would ever understand what Freddy Mercury was singing so passionately about. I was amazed at how many new all the words, but they only really knew the sounds that the words made. They completely lost the meaning behind them. Most girls are ignorant to art. They're far too tuned into themselves, their appearances, and their petty lives. To be fair, this is what works for them. All most guys need is a place to stick their johnsons into. And here's a hint. Most men prefer the mouth because it takes away the chance for women to talk about themselves and their petty lives. These pills, they were making me angry. I was getting sick of everyone, and I was becoming increasingly paranoid and agitated. I could hear bits and pieces of conversations, and they all made me angry. Words made me angry because they all no longer had any meaning behind them. And I was in the midst of an upsetting swirl of them. 'I hate John Mayer. He's just not as good as Dave Matthews Band.' 'I'm so drunk right now. I've had three Long Island Iced Teas, and four Miller Lites, and...' 'That is so interesting. I can't believe the President would do that!' 'Did you see that episode of Family Guy where Stewie...' '...American Idol...' 'Rick James, bitch!' Society had been brainwashed, hypnotized. It had grown too large. People were getting their conversation pieces from television. Their words were programmed. Their inside jokes were enraging little phrases broadcast to millions. People were getting their music from the radio. They got it three minutes at a time. There was no time for music, just singing. And they couldn't be ugly either. They had to look the part. Newscasters, too. Everyone played a role, even the average person. The average person had to act average. We got our politics from soundbites, from television stations with agendas, from entertainment magazines. And then there are people who dig deeper. They're even worse. Their self importance stems from lies. They dig through a hollow skin and claim to have found something enlightening. So what's the answer? Say nothing. Search for nothing and find nothing. Really? Maybe. I don't know. Shoot first, and ask questions later, I say. Curb the ever-growing, festering, billion-headed organism that chews and regurgitates all over itself, living in a layer of its own excrement...necks crooning towards the sun, each wanting more light, trying to evolve from within the dung, trying to stem and grow and rise taller than its neighboring head so that it can bathe first in the sunlight. I feel the urge to vomit. I don't know if it's because my stomach is full of beer and pills or if it's because my head is full of unbalancing voices. I have a feeling that it's a mixture of both. I toss another five spot on the bar and immediately snatch my drinks, like some venus fly-trap sensing a fly landing on its fragrant trap just in time to clap its jawlike petals around it. The ignorant fly is then left to melt in the sweetly scented poison that vaporizes tiny black flesh. Eh, its nothing like that, actually, but I prefer to dwell on that image. I can feel the bile fluid clawing its way up my throat. Maybe there's a fly trapped inside. It feels like something's clogging my esophagus. There's a tangible ball of ire in there, trapped, growing stronger from the acid. I push my way through the crowded spaces in between booths and stools before the stale, sweaty mess of humanity escapes my nostrils. There's a sparser clearing just before the painfully brightly lit hallway in the back where the restrooms are. I pass in front of the pool tables and briefly scan for her, but she's not there. I slunk into the hallway, my head aching from the unshielded tubes of light that blinded from the ceiling and pushed through the first wooden door on my right. In no state of consciousness to read, however, I quickly found I had entered the wrong room. There were no urinals, which was fine by me, because the last time I tossed my cookies in one of those, some of the bar goons pulled me out of the bathroom and threw me out the back door. I quickly exited, unnoticed, and peered in the right door, but it was too crowded. There was some jackass at each pisser, and the stall doors were both closed. I backed into the hallway again and tried to regain my composure. I was in desperate need of a cigarette. I pulled one out and lit it, but I knew I had to be stealth. There was always someone who saw you smoking and assumed that you'd love to buddy up with them if they asked for a cigarette. 'Hey, man, you got another cigarette I can bum?' I feel like I've gone over this before, and if I have, I'm sorry, but I really fucking hate these losers. I pull out the American Spirits and they tell me they've never heard of them, and that they really only smoke when they drink, and I laugh because I hate the asshole even more now. Then, they have the nerve to tell me that fucking Marlboro Lights are the best cigarettes ever even though they don't smoke unless they're drunk. So that's about the point when I pull out a cigarette that they think they're going to be smoking, but instead I light it, put a second square in my mouth and tell them, 'Sorry, but I'm a non-smoker' as I walk away smoking two very harsh cigarettes and nearly coughing black, ashy pieces of my lungs out while Jimmy Dickweed stands there like an asshole. But this lighting goes by without cause for calamity, and I clear my head and walk back into the bar, looking at the pool tables again to see if that one girl is there. I couldn't stand her. I loathed her, because she had me by the balls. She feigned interest in me, paid just enough attention to me for me to think that maybe, just maybe, I had a chance of nailing her. And she kept stringing me on, I'm sure it wasn't just some amusing little game, because maybe I'd end up being a decent backup. She was one of those girls who dug into the hollow surface and reported back with findings that weren't there. She scoffed at mainstream radio and gushed about these local bands because they were much better or she knew the lead singer or some shit like that that's supposed to be interesting. She flew her off-kilter personality like an upside-down flag, laughing at how much more intriguing she was than anyone else at the bar. And she was beautiful, beautiful enough that you'd fuck her if she was in a coma, and it'd still be great. The part that stung the most was that she knew all that. And she played her part. She had to project that image that she could change your life too, make it a little bit more interesting, a little bit more crazy, keep you from comitting suicide by hanging yourself with the tie you wore to work every day because it matched your suit coat perfectly. She had brown hair the last time I saw her, natural brown hair, but who knows, she may have dyed it some wild and impulsive color. Regardless, I didn't see her. It wouldn't have mattered anyway. I wasn't the type of guy who would buy her bullshit. I was threatening. I was crazier than she was, but not in that alluring, life-spicing way. Her quirkiness was a ploy to control, and to balance. She needed someone much dumber than her, someone who would follow her trends because they were more meaningful than his. Someone with a doughy mind and an empty skull that she could fill with her sweet whispers. In essence, she'd fill the void in his skull while he filled her more palpable crevice. I was too much of a challenge to her intellect. I was the kind of guy who'd tell her that I didn't give a shit about her favorite band that nobody had heard of. I was the kind of guy who wouldn't adopt her jaded, impulsive dogma. She asked me to slap her one night. She thought it was cute and spontaneous. So I slapped her. As hard as I could. She was taken aback. She was surprised, expecting me to say some pansy bullshit about how it wasn't right to hit a girl. Fuck that. I hit her. And what could she do about it? What the fuck could she do about it? Nothing. Because it would tear down the image she had built up out of hollow illusion that she wasn't average. Still, I had her number in my phone. I invited her over to smoke or drink once, so she told me to call her. I figured that would make her easier to fuck. *** The night was dead silent back at home. I laid on the couch with the last of my stash packed into a bowl that rested on my chest. It was three in the morning, and I was taking casual hits between glances at the TV screen. Some low budget British film was playing. It was called 'Following' or something. I had calmed down significantly since I made it home. Grass tends to do that to me. The movie wasn't very interesting. I had a hard time focusing in on one thing or another. I began to feel alone. The night sometimes did that to me. That's why I always slept with music on, and even then, I stayed awake, watching the fear linger above me. I needed noise. I needed it to overpower the pounding of my heart. My pulse was always too fast. It was drugs, or poor health. I practiced breathing more slowly. Maybe that would calm my heart down. Maybe it was love. The notion of solitude was kind of romantic to me. I didn't need anyone to confirm my being. I could just exist, trapped with myself inside myself forever. Until the skull breaks open, the cord connecting the soul to the body snaps, and the spirit floats away. It rejoins a sea of energy or whatever element it is that breathes life into dead capsules. That's what I liked to think, anyway. I hoped to do it in my sleep. Pass peacefully between this world and the next, inactively but by my own decision. Just think 'stop,' and then you stopped. I called it suicide with nothing but a mind. But it never worked. It was bullshit. It was all just bullshit. I hated being calm. I couldn't do it anymore. My heart started beating fast again as blood rushed through these bulging veins, tinting my skin a vegetable green...an earthen green, the color of regrowth and vigorous existence. I needed to break this shell of solitude. I needed to take someone and rape someone and destroy someone. I grabbed my cell phone from the pile of crap I kept in my pockets. I always kept it together when I disrobed, and I was naked now, like some wounded beast, hellbent on vengeance at night; sacrificing sleep. I browsed through my contact list until I got to Vicki. Vicki with an 'i.' She was insistent about the 'i.' And then I pressed send...or erase. Because they both meant the same thing, one from within, and one from outside, but tied together by a taut cord that I couldn't break for the life of me. It was up to God's scythe to sever.
