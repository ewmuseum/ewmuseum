Sort Some Stuff out (Part 2)

14 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 14th November 2006 The commotion in the restaurant seemed to give it a life of its own. Similar to the human body, various elements kept this beast alive. There was first the most important organ, the chefs, which turned the crude air ingredients into magnificent works of culinary art, for distribution by the blood cells, or waiters, around the body, or restaurant. There was the heart, which kept the red blood cells moving, in this case head waiter or manager, who made sure each individual waiter was serving his or her table with the utmost respect, vigilance and poetic movement. When one of these vital parts failed, the body failed. In this case, it was one of the red blood cells. The young American man swiftly cut through the turmoil in the restaurant, weaving past various ensembles of individuals, either coming or going. He carried the bottle of red Wine in his right hand, the neck resting on his arm below the elbow joint. His left hand held the napkin to the neck, keeping it in place. He was having a rough day, and this was made even worse by the fact that at the reserved head table, Mr. Kae and his entourage weren’t exactly the most polite of customers. When he had insisted they had no Chilean Red Wine in the establishment, as it had all sold out, the ever insolent Jonathan Alexander Kae had insisted he leave and only return with a bottle of good Chilean Red, or a pair of breasts to explain his incompetence. Imagine his surprise, then, when the head waiter, Josh Alvarez, had instructed him to leave the restaurant, and find a good bottle of Chilean Red wine from a local store. He wasn’t about to do that. So, fishing through the used bottles, he had simply found a used bottle of Chilean Shiraz, and exchanged the labels with a bottle of Argentinean Merlot. Too bad Steve Redbridge knew nothing about wine. Worse still, that he knew nothing of Mr. Kae’s love of wine. Steve struggled to the table, already mentally exhausted from the hard evening’s work. It had been a particularly hectic day, but the tips they received made it all worthwhile. He finally emerged from the throng of people, with the bottle of wine in tow, victoriously, almost shining and brimming with professionalism. Steve Redbridge gave Mr. Kae a smile, who simply blinked. Jonny Kae: “What took you so long?” God, these people were rude. Steve opened the bottle as sharply and sophisticatedly as the greatest waiter on the planet, and, as was customary, poured a tiny bit into a glass, showing Jonny Kae the bottle. He nodded with approval at the name, and lifted the glass to his nose. Smelling it, his expression changed. His eye narrowed, as he looked up at the beaming Redbridge. Then, the worst thing happened. Jonny Kae drank the wine. This was, as many military strategists will tell you, the point of no return. If one could freeze time, and point out first the wide eyes, the scrunched nose, clenched fist and furrowed brow, it would be easy to point out. However, even to the ignorant of military strategy, the fact that half of the wine was being hurled towards Steve Redbridge, kinda gave it away. Jonny Kae spat the wine out, and it landed perfectly just to the side of his tie, staining his wonderful, if cheap, white shirt for life. Jonny slammed his fist on the table, and declared his intentions to Steve Redbridge. Jonny Kae: “Get me your damn manager! Now! What the hell do you call this? Shiraz? It’s about as dry as an otter’s arse!” Steve stumbled back, obviously shocked by the unexpected reaction. Any other day. Steve: “But, sir, we didn’t have any Chilean wine left. I thought…” That was it… Jonny Kae: “You thought? Do these people pay you to think? I bloody hope not, or they’d be bankrupt. You, there! You!” He shouted at another waiter, somewhat more experienced. Jonny Kae: “Bring me this man’s supervisor, manager, or whatever the hell you people have around here.” The man scurried off into the fog of people, as Steve tried to slink away. Kae was having none of it. Jonny Kae: “Stay where you are maggot!” Yes…he used the word maggot. A plump man emerged from the crowd, wearing a suit, and a smile that Kae found insulting. Jonny Kae: “What’s the meaning of this? Your employee here brought me a bottle of Merlot, when I specifically asked for Chilean Shiraz?” The man nodded. Supervisor: “My apologies sir. May we give you a complementary bottle of…” Jonny Kae: “Silence! I demand that you fire him instantly!” Steve Redbridge’s eyes widened further. He stepped forward. Steve: “You can’t…you can’t demand that! Who the hell do you think you are?” Kae glared at the young man. Then back at the supervisor. Supervisor: “I’m sorry sir, but I’m afraid we can’t do that. Steve, could you bring Mr. Kae another two bottles of…” Kae stood up. Jonny Kae: “Who the hell owns this place?” The supervisor blinked at him. Supervisor: “Well, Mr. Roderick Wallwark sir. But he’s not…” Jonny Kae: “Call him instantly! I want to speak with this man!” Supervisor: “But, sir, we can’t just…” Jonny Kae: “Now!” Jonny Kae was upsetting the other customers. The supervisor thought it best to agree to the terms, to avoid any more loss of custom. He left briefly, returning with a portable phone; it was ringing. He spoke into it. Supervisor: “Sorry to disturb you sir, but a customer wants to speak with you.” The other end replied. Supervisor: “I know sir. But this is Mr. Kae. You know, the billionaire?” Somehow, money always spoke volumes. Kae was handed the phone. Jonny Kae: “Is this Mr. Wallwark?” Wallwark: “Yes. What can I do for you Mr. Kae?” Jonny Kae: “How much?” Wallwark: “How much what?” Jonny Kae: “How much for your restaurant? I’m buying it.” Wallwark: “Sorry, Mr. Kae, it’s not for sale.” Jonny Kae: “Of course it is. Four hundred thousand?” Wallwark: “No.” Jonny Kae: “Eight Hundred Thousand?” Wallwark: “I told you, it’s not for…” Jonny Kae: “Two Million! But I keep all staff contracts.” Wallwark: “Sold! Are you serious?” Jonny Kae: “Thanks. Pleasure doing business with you. I’ll have my solicitors draw up the necessary paperwork, and wire you the funds.” Wallwark: “Ok, but this is all kind of…” Kae didn’t want to let him finish. He hung up the phone. He turned to Steve Redbridge, who was now serving another customer. Kae tapped him on the shoulder. Steve turned around. Jonny Kae: “You’re Fired!” *********************************************************** Troy Lucini wasn’t a made guy. He never would be a made guy. His Mother had been Irish, and thus he could never pass the necessary prerequisites of becoming a made guy. That made the situation all the more difficult to digest. It all started last week. He had caught the courier who had brought the picture of him and Vinnie performing the hit on Samantha Kae, billionaire heiress, but the courier said he had been given the package by an anonymous sender. Usually, Troy would have buried that kid in a hole somewhere, but panic had set in. Vincent Camberloni was dead. Shot from somewhere close, sniped. It wasn’t the cops. They never took that kinda direct action. At first he thought it was a vigilante, but it didn’t add up. Why Vinnie? Why not Tony Meresco, just a few feet behind. He was a made guy, and had a lot of notches under his belt, wanted by the police for everything from murder to extortion, but protected by corruption within the higher ranks of the police. It didn’t make sense. Maybe it was a mob hit? Maybe Vinnie had gotten some of the guys scared. He had been acting a lot more aggressive the past few days. And the news Program, showing footage of Vincent Camberloni. Maybe they were afraid he would talk to save his ass? That didn’t add up either. No one can organise a hit in the space of five minutes…not like that. Regardless of who it was, Troy didn’t wanna stay and find out. He’d packed his bags that night, and left town. Now he was in Chicago, to meet his girlfriend. Together, they could skip town, and do like that movie, history of violence. He liked that movie. Except Mob guys were nothing like that. But, it was a good movie. Troy was anxious. He didn’t know what to think, who to trust. Paranoid. He just couldn’t… BANG! The car ran straight into Troy Lucini’s side. He went flying over the bonnet, and onto the road behind it. The car immediately reversed. Troy was conscious when it hit him. Must have been painful. Not for long. The car, an old Mercedes, sped off down the road. A few people screamed when they realised what had happened. Even more stopped to look, and carried on. This was a city, a big city. No one cared. It was almost thirty seconds before someone had gone over to check that Troy was dead. He was. Another twenty seconds to arrange for someone to call the police and the ambulance service, because that’s how it happened in movies. Even though the guy was dead, they always called the ambulance. That gave the car long enough to put a safe distance between itself and the incident. No one had gotten the licence plate, because no one cared. It was so easy. Somewhere in Chicago, Queen’s ‘Another one Bites the Dust’ was playing. *********************************************************** Jonny Kae sat in his comfortable sofa chair. The leather exterior made it such a pleasant feeling against his aching limbs. Three hours of squash with his political campaign manager had worn him down. It was the best time to think, though, when adrenaline was running through his body. The message he received on the other end of the phone brought a smile to Jonny’s lips. His Mother would be proud. He was avenging her dismissal from this world. It would soon be time to hunt bigger fish. He folded the phone, and dropped it into his pocket. A good day indeed. The press conference was two weeks away, and Kae really had to prepare for it. So all this business had to be quick and quiet. This week, though, he would be on television again. Good way to gain publicity, as a professional wrestler for a large company. It wasn’t why he did it, but it was good exposure. Look at Arnold what’s his face, the terminator guy. He was now single handedly dismantling California, why not Jonny Kae? Try your luck match. He hated gimmick matches. They always made him dress up, or fight in a silly place. That was the problem with Sports Entertainment. To anyone over the age of twenty-five, you were making a fool out of yourself. No matter. He was against Scott Taylor and Josh Marquez. Who? It didn’t matter. Time to get that publicity. Time to get that exposure. Time to tie up loose ends.
