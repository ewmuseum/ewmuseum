The Essence of My Obsession (1)

8 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10 - 08 - 06 As you get to know a human being, everything about them gradually rises from their pores. The fuzzed reception of the pictures, which make up the life of said human being, will innocently disipate with every question you non-chalantly ask. Their history, that they've struggled to cover up for years, will forecefully be exposed by tainted attraction. Nobody lives an ordinary life, and it's because life isn't ordinary to begin with. Life was never my choice to begin with. Fact is, we're all brought here whether we like it or not. Who knows how good we had it before it was our duty to exist? We're existing because two people decided to bring us to life. Perhaps this is, in fact, what's supposed to happen. I suppose we're supposed to be a part of this planet, but what exactly for? I never knew, and that put me on my own track. I started digging into the troubled mystery of life, and found myself climbing out of massive craters in the ground, so to speak. Nobody had an outlining answer that I could sit on. Nobody knew anything, and it scared me. Instead of shutting myself inside my family's home, I decided to discover things on my own. I became, for lack of a better term, a night owl. I'd spend most of my days within that light-blue house, guarded by parents who really couldn't give a shit, and wait until the sun would turn its back on our little town. I anticipated that night sky like nothing else. I loved sneaking out of the house, at the wee hours of the morning, to find trouble. For the first few stages of my life, I lived smothered by people who refused to listen to what I had to say. I don't want that, anymore. In fact, I can't have that anymore. Life is far too short to get stuck on certain events of your life; events are what make the cycle of existence turn. Without them, people would have no reason to live. I have a reason to live. I have plenty of reasons to live, and sinking myself in tribulation is nothing but a surefire way to monitor how well I actually am living. At this point, I'm doing as well as I possibly can. I meet women every day, but I don't have a sexual attraction to any of them. Is it because 94% of them are fabricated storytellers whose ears have cellphones permanently attached to their ears? Is it because they're terrified of my beliefs and how I carry out my life? Is it because I'm a deeply honest person? I suppose it's a mixture of the three. I don't like women who think of me as their fish. I don't like women who remotely consider me their catch. I'm not the one who swims towards you, unlike the better half of our wannabe-masculine, whipped-to-the-bedpost population. I guess this is why trouble finds me. For the past year, I've been club hopping 'downtown'. I choose the clubs I go to very carefully, as well. I never go into any of the dance/hip-hop nightclubs, and I never go into rave parties. Neither one of those are even worth the entrance fee. I like sports clubs, rock clubs, and entertainment pubs. I like to play a little pool, take a couple shots of Jack, and a scout the scene surrounding me. I like to find women who seem outgoing and welcoming of your company, but not overly loose-lipped. I care not to hear about their jobs, or the different kinds of pets they have. I care not to know your life. I care to know you, not the way you manage your existence. Looks matter. I don't want fake-looking colors, or blotchy lipstick. I don't want eight-inch heels on your feet, or a giant Kool Light hanging out of your mouth. When you smile, I want you to mean the expression. I want trust; a basic display of honesty. I don't need you to glorify the life you lead, or miserably trash it. I'm not your counselor. I am someone who's interested in you, but only in basic terms. If you come off as a simple-minded, innocent human being and betray me by sliding out of your comfort zone, it'll come off as an insult. I don't take kindly to insults. --- Melody James; twenty-seven years old. Red hair, clysdale legs, and a beautiful figure. The club's called 'The Descent' and has three floors; two are underground. There's a giant neon light, blinking pink and green, that you'd have a hard time missing. Velvet roping keeps the wrap-around line, that always forms from the front to the end of the right side, and VIP's make their special exit through a (usually locked) doorway on the left of the building. The inside is one massive room, with a circular aisle to get to tables, the bars, and the people casually hanging around by themselves. I've been to 'The Descent' four or five times. This was my third time and, like usual, I was by myself when I first got in. Fact is, there's a beneficial perk to going by yourself. You don't have to deal with someone hanging on your shoulder for the whole night. You don't have to cater to their needs, in order to sieze their boredom while crawling through the webs of my own. You're given an unlimited amount of time to search the waters for the right fish. The bigger the catch, the more interesting a night will turn out to be. Sitting there, my back to the awful house band covering Van Halen's 'Running With The Devil', I slammed back my second double shot of vodka. Hours prior to this, I had gotten into an incident with my uncle, Grady. Shoving his clothes in a duffel bag, Grady had threatened to move out after a relapse of depression. I grabbed onto his shirt just as he was storming out of the house and he swung at me. His bicep got me in the chin and I snapped by shoving him against the wall and jabbing him in the mouth. He fell to the ground, completely baffled, and I walked out. This is where I turned up, and with a wonderfully red-haired female beside me. She was by herself, drinking her sorrows away. Michael: Can I buy you another one? In mid gulp, she turned her head and stared into my eyes. Melody: What for? Michael: I don't know. I guess just because you look like you could use another drink? Melody: Because I look unhappy? At the first, I didn't know what this broad's deal was. I'm not necessarily known for jumping the gun with a conversation, and she's skinning the fuck out of my groove with a razorblade. As I said before, I don't like women who're loose-lipped, and like a roaring engine. Michael: I suppose. Look, don't take the offer, then. I'm having a shit day just like you, so I figured we could kill two birds with one stone. I don't want to upset you any more than you are, though, so I'll just mind my own business. My apologies. Melody: No. Don't do that, I'm sorry. It's just that, I lost my job today. My ex-boyfriend's been nagging me about a box of concert videotapes he left at my house, but he was abusive so I don't want to see him, and on top of all of that, I think I might have cancer. Michael: Cancer? Melody: Brain Cancer. I've been blacking out, on occasion, for the last two weeks now. When I look up at the sky, or anything bright, these strange fuzzy sparks appear. Michael: Like, UFO's? Holy shit, she was boring the absolute life out of me. She had already told me too much for there be interest. There'd have to be an emergency ejection for this to work properly. Melody: No. It's hard to explain, so I'm not going to. Besides, I'm sure you don't want to hear any of that. You're right, I don't. Michael: It's no problem. I'd go to a doctor about those blackouts, though. That's not something you shouldn't take lightly. Melody: And what do you take lightly in life? Michael: Everything. Melody: Everything? Michael: What's the point in allowing things to aggravate you? You're here, and there's a guy asking to buy your next drink. He's listening to you talk about all of this negativity, while waiting for you to tell him what drink you want. She smiled. I think what made girls, at least the ones I've managed to lasso, think of me as a 'genuinely' nice guy was my fabricated interest in them. Fact is, and I've already stated this, I couldn't care any less. This is nothing but my ploy to get the handcuffs wrapped around her wrists. Melody was coming back to a motel room with me before the night was over, one way or the other. Melody: You're cute. I like Long Island ice teas. As if this whole thing was for cinematic purposes (ahem), the bartender arrived in front of us. To serve as a gesture of servitude(also known as 'getting a good tip')he wrapped his arm behind his back and greeted me. Bartender: Can I get you a drink, ma'am? --- SILLYWORLD MELTDOWN Michael: She wants a Long Island ice tea, and what is your name? Bartender: Uh, Shane? My name's Shane, and I'll be your bartender tonight. Michael: Do you always come up to people without introducing yourself? Would you do that to someone in the middle of New York City? Someone who didn't see it coming? Bartender: No... I don't think so. Michael: Then you should do the same while you're on the job. I was talking to this lady and you immediately bursted your nut in the center of our conversational aura. I had something going! You ask ME if she wants a drink, motherfucker. I tell YOU what to do, and you'll listen to ME. SILLYWORLD MELTDOWN --- Melody: I'll have a Long Island ice tea. Michael: Another vodka. What do you say we get out of here, maybe get a room and shoot the shit? Bartender: On the rocks, sir? I could feel the sweat creeping down my temples. My pulse was in my throat, and it seemed to get louder everytime I swallowed. I was obviously freaking out, and I didn't have the two most oblivious individuals around me. I don't like interuption, and it almost felt like a blatant and aggressive attack by this nosey bartender. I don't like him, and I really don't have a complete reason as to why. Michael: Give me a glass of vodka. No 'rocks', please. Hurry up with it too, I'm about to get out of here. I directed my attention towards 'her'. 'You comin'?' Melody: I don't even know your name. You expect me to go with someone that doesn't even tell me their name? Michael: It's Michael, sorry. Sometimes, when I'm occupied with something, the numbers don't come out appropriately. A breif moment of silence. Melody: What the fuck does that mean? Michael: Jesus. Look, I just forgot to tell you my name. I like you, and am interested in getting to know you. You were occupying me, and I was enjoying it. And, perhaps you could explain your hesitance in giving me your name? Snap. Pull the trigger. Lead her into your embrace and clamp down like a venus fly trap. Yeah, like a venus fly trap. Melody: You didn't ask me. Michael: Since when was there a rule stating that you have to ask the girl or guy her or his name? Melody rolled her eyes and shook her head. Melody: 'Melody' is the name. She sipped down half of her Long Island ice tea and gave me a look. I think it symbolized a spell of acceptance; a momentary opening through the anti-trust, into the womb of understanding and security. The bartender had finally vacated our business and we were left with one another. Another project in the making, and it's been so long. Melody, my dear, it's time to have some fun. Melody: Okay, where are we going? Michael: I know of a Super-8 down the street. I don't want you to go if you feel uneasy, but it's an open invitation to a night you won't forget. Melody: How can you go wrong? I can show you. I laughed while reaching for my jacket on the barstool to the left of me. Michael: That's the thing, you can't. 1*
