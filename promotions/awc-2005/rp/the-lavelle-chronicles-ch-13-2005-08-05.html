The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch.13

5 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5th August The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch.13 Lost Light: Part 2 Sunlight peeked through the cracks in my curtains. The air-conditioning unit in the living-room window murmured ominously in the background, predicting that Albany, once again, was in for a scorcher. I rolled over and stared at Lois’s back, she was still sound asleep. She hadn’t moved since her fall, but didn’t sleep well all night. I made a mental note to ring the doctor after work in Pizza Hut, just saying it made me seem a little unworthy for Lois. A journalist, dating a man with two jobs and no college degree! I sighed gently and rolled back over, kicking the duvet off of my legs. I kept recapping my last two trips to Spain. They had both been memorable moments, to say the least! The alarm glowed a neon blue. It was already 12:30, they day was already passed its half-way mark and it had started without me. I shuttled my way into the bathroom and checked my face, the redness had gone now. I took a quick shower, threw on a black pair of baggy jeans and a white t-shirt. I had one hour before work began. Making sure I didn’t make a sound, I quickly moved into the kitchen and checked the fridge for some food, hoping the magical fairies had done the shopping for me, but there was only some weight watchers milk and cornflakes, which I didn’t feel like eating. I pulled out a bottle of water and checked the answer machine. You have one new message, it rang out of nowhere. ‘Pierce, it’s your mother. I just wanted to apologize for mine and Rebecca’s behaviour towards you. We have arrived at Harrisburg and I’m staying with my sister, your Aunt. If you need to contact me, well, you know the numbed. Once again, I’m sorry son and say hi to Lois for me’. The tone beeped and I decided to save the message. I quickly turned on my mobile and left a note for Lois. The Escape Harrington is stood underneath the drain exit, his prison clothes drenched in sewage. His eyes bloodshot and his hair, completely shaved. He has a sort of small moustache now. A man and a woman come charging down the drain pipe, carrying four bags of clothing. The woman is dressed in some tight leather trousers, a white blouse and a long leather jacket. The man is dressed in a pair of leather trousers, a tight wife beater and a long leather jacket also. 'Boss,' asks the man into the darkness. 'I take it you’re the best they could get me! What did you bring me?' Marvin steps forward into the light of the torch. 'Some of your clothes, boss,' the woman moves forward and hands Harrington the bags. 'Is this really a good idea, boss?' the man leans against the oval wall and sighs. 'I mean, can’t you just let the guy go, he’s probably never going to tell anyone. And, your face is all over the nine o’clock news.' 'I have unfinished business. I can't ask Reno, Romeo and Antonio to do it, because they're dead. I must finish this mission by myself. Nobody else can do it ... everyone keeps messing up,' Marvin pulls off his prison attire and looks around the drain pipe. 'I'll need to find some showers ...' 'There's a community pool down that drain and to your left. Open the hatch and you'll be inside the shower rooms. Don‘t worry, I have studied the underground pipeline of Boston.' 'Good, because if you are lying, I‘ll kill you...'Harrington stares at José coldly. José steps forward and hands Harrington a torch, a wallet, a new passport and some money. 'That‘s everything, Boss,' José stands back. 'Marissa, I want you to cover Albany with me. We have a mission to finish and I can't go to too many places in the public eye. José, go back to our headquarters in the underground and let them know I'll be ready soon,' Harrington begins his long walk to the hatch for the Swimming Pool. Marissa turns to José; she has a look of displeasure on her face. 'Go, it'll be alright; I'll come with you once I get back ...' José kisses Marissa and then leaves. Marissa sobs a little, but pulls her gun closes to her hip and moves forward, following Harrington's trail. Harrington stands underneath the hatch, leading into the mens’ shower room of the Community Swimming Pool. Grunge lines the handles, and a small ‘pad & lock’ is fastened over the opening. Harrington smiles, nods his head and leans against the ladder. Marissa comes jogging around the corner, her long leather coat flailing behind her. 'You’ll need to use your pistol on the lock,' Harrington says, standing back to allow Marissa to shoot it. Marissa pulls out her 9mm Magnum and shoots the lock, smoke forms around the gun; a loud clicking noise is heard. The chains fall to the floor below. Harrington laughs, hands the bags to Marissa and climbs the ladder. 'Come on,' Harrington holds open the hatch. 'We don’t want to hang around too much; it’ll only be a matter of time before they start to search the pipe lines.' Marissa gives in to her boss’s wishes and climbs the slimy step ladder and sits on the linoleum floor of the mens’ shower room. She shuts the hatch behind her and stays standing near the exit, her gun pointed in front of her and her leather jacket dangling just above her ankle. The hissing noise of the power shower ends and a few moments later Harrington steps into the dim light room, dressed in a black trilby hat, a black suit and a grey trench coat, with the collar pulled up around his neck. His moustache is gone, and his appearance change. 'Let’s get out of here,' Harrington slips all of his new credentials into the large pocket in the trench coat and follows Marissa out of the Community Swimming Pool and into the streets of Boston. 'We must move quickly,' Marissa hisses as she climbs onto the back of a BMW motorbike. 'I don‘t take orders. You do it again and I‘ll have you and your boyfriend killed. Now, one more thing, I don‘t like travelling on a motorbike!' Harrington gazes at Marissa as she flings the tails of her leather jacket over the seat. 'Understood …' Marissa throws on a black biker helmet and begins to rev the engine. Refusing to argue, as his life is sort on the line, Harrington sits on the back of the bike and Marissa speeds off. Patience I waited patiently by the phone, hoping the doctor would soon call back. Lois was out flat on the bed, resting; she had given herself a minor form of whiplash, but the bump she received later, was beginning to worry me. Her pupils were dilated. With my Patience growing thin, I moved to the refrigerator and pulled open the door. I was covered in a cold glow, I looked in it for something to eat, and nothing caught my eye. I pulled out a cool bottle of beer and popped the lid. The phone rang in the background. 'Hello,' my tone was slightly desperate. 'Pierce Lavelle, this is doctor Philips. I will be able to get over to you by midnight. Does that suit you fine?' Philips asked into the phone. 'I am aware of your girlfriend’s condition. If it deteriorates within the next few hours, then I recommend taking her to the hospital. Is that understood?' I had met Doctor Philips a month ago after the fire in Western Avenue. He was the kind of man that became a doctor for the pay. He didn't care too much for the patients, or those in need of medical assistance. I was getting fed-up with lack of human emotion or caring. The first thing he had asked earlier when I rang him was: 'How much will you pay me?' Sick is a word I would use to describe this man. 'Whatever, just hurry,' I hung up the phone and gazed at the clock in the living room. It was 7:00 pm. I had finished the beer after only a couple of sips. The temperature was dropping finally and the fans around the living room had been switched off, to save some money on the electricity bill. Lois was standing against the doorframe of the bedroom. 'Pierce, will you relax, it’s just a bump on the head!' she was smiling, but I couldn’t return the smile. I was naturally panicking. 'Well, I know I‘m not a medical expert, but you‘re not okay,' I moved closer to her and helped her onto the sofa. 'Nothing is going to happen to me, would you relax. It’s just a bump on the head, but I could do without the massive migraine,' she smiled and flicked on the TV. In front of me was a picture of Marvin Harrington … the very man I met in the 'Black Goose' almost two months ago; the very man that wanted me to work for him. The very man that had Lilly and her family killed and now he had escaped from prison. Lois sat back into the leather sofa, baffled. 'Is he the man that almost killed us?' I nodded my head, recapping that very tense moment in the Black Goose. I could still picture the two men that stood behind him perfectly. Who would have thought that Marvin Harrington, the so called 'mob boss', would be arrested, sent to a federal prison and charged with murder and then escape. It was a little foolish of me to think that my life could ever be normal, but the Harrington issue was something I thought was all over. Now two months on, he was still here and he was at large! 'Lois … I think I need to contact two agents from the NYPD. They might need my help and we might be in danger!' 'It wouldn’t make sense for him to come here, Pierce,' she looked at me. 'I mean, he’s on the run. The bureau is probably already searching the underground and sewer system of Boston. Why would he risk coming back to Albany - the place where he killed Lilly and two firemen. He’s probably on a small private jet to Bermuda … or something …' 'I guess you are right. I’m worrying about nothing.' I felt a little more at ease. Lois was now nestling her head into my shoulder as she changed from CNN to WB. Nothing was on, but some old reruns of 'BATMAN RETURNS'. I smiled at the very familiar image of Catwoman, cart wheeling toward Penguin and Batman, just as she stops the shop blows up. A very memorable scene. 'Pierce, is there any aspirin left?' Lois was now looking at me, her eyes blood shot and her pupils, still dilated. She was looking even worse now and I couldn't pretend that everything was alright. 'You sure you’re okay? You have the signs of a concussion and it could be serious.' 'Pierce, please, stop worrying. I feel fine. It was just a bump on the head, I have a headache. It’s either that or I am getting my period.' 'Okay … too much information. I’ll check the medicine cabinet.' I got up off the sofa and headed into the bedroom; she knew exactly how to get me out of a room and I smiled to myself. Maybe it was that, it would explain the sudden mood swings, which come and go every month! I was standing in front of the medicine cabinet in the bedroom, we had everything from plasters, to creams, to slings, but there was no aspirin. I came out of the bedroom empty handed. Lois was no longer seated on the sofa, rather struggling to get to the kitchen counter. 'What are you doing?' I ran over and grabbed her before she fell again. 'I needed a glass of water!' she said as I helped her into the bedroom. Removing the sheets, I slid her into the bed. She looked at me. 'Maybe you're right. Perhaps this isn't the monthly thing ... Pierce ... did you get the aspirin. My migraine is killing me.' 'We're all out!' Lois bit her bottom lip and looked away from me. 'Could you go down to the chemist and get some?' 'Sure. But, you have to promise that you won't move from the bed.' 'Alright, just hurry it up ...' she smiled and kissed me. Grabbing my denim jacket I ran out of the door, locking it behind me and hurried down the steps toward the car park. The GMC jeep remained in the same spot I had left it - of course it would - where would it go? I smiled at my stupidity and jumped into the driver seat, and reversed out of Summer Plaza and headed out into the traffic. It was already 8:30 pm. Guidance Tony Rush sat motionless in his black FBI jeep. He stared at the clock on the dash board; it was 9:00 pm. His day was drawing to an end and the chase for Marvin Harrington had only just begun. He sat outside a small colonial house in the suburbs of Boston. A young woman sat crying on the porch, being comforted by a young boy, her son. Tony exited the jeep and it beeped behind him. 'Alicia … I’m so sorry,' Tony put his hand on her shoulder and she jolted up and hugged him, her tears soaking the shoulder of his blue suit jacket. 'Why was he guarding that stupid cell? Why didn’t he go with you?' Alicia now stopped crying and stared Tony in the face, her mind was racing. Her husband had just died, and she and her son were now without a husband and a father. 'I don’t know Alicia. When I left he was in the conference room talking to you on his mobile. The next thing I know, he’s dead. I am so sorry. I wish I could’ve stopped it, I really do. He was my best friend.' Tony stepped back and his head dropped. 'But you didn’t stop it, did you?' Alicia was now furious. 'I - I …' Tony began to stutter. He was never trained to deal with the relations of those lost in the field. That was another agent’s job. 'Forget it, Tony. Just catch the son of a bitch that had my husband killed,' Alicia picked up her son and the two disappeared behind the wooden door. A loud slam was left ringing in Tony’s ear. The two agents that had broken the news to Alicia began to move back to their own car. Tony straightened his jacket and made his way to his car; he was beginning to well-up. But, his emotions had to be put aside. His mobile began to chirp and he was straight into action. 'Yes …' he responded with urgency. 'Agent Rush, this Agent Ramirez, I’m sorry for your loss,' Ramirez said into the phone, his Mexican accent adding a lilt to the words he tried to speak. 'Yes … but why did you ring me, Agent Ramirez?' Tony was no longer in the mood for sympathy; his pain and sorrow was now replaced with determination and revenge for Leroy, Alicia and their son, Jensen. 'We have a lead!' 'Please ELABORATE! In case you haven’t noticed, we are on a ticking time bomb,' Tony was growing impatient, his blood boiling. The sound of a lead made each muscle in his body tighten; he pulled out his keys and hoped into the car. 'He’s heading to Albany … we found a young man on the way out of the sewer. A man named José Echevarria. He was working with Harrington, but he gave-up all of the information. Something about his girlfriend and her life being at risk. I don’t know. Anyway, Harrington’s on his way to Albany. We have alerted the feds there.' 'Albany?' Tony wasn’t asking Ramirez, more thinking out loud as to why Harrington would go to Albany. It then hit him. 'Christ … Ramirez …' 'Yes?' 'I’m going to need a chopper, NOW …' 'We’ll have one waiting for you in five minutes, Rush,' Ramirez hung-up. Tony looked at his clock. Harrington had been out of federal prison and on the run for the better part of twenty hours; he had plenty of time to get to Albany. Tony screeched out of the residential and sped down the hill toward the freeway. He was against the clock now … Black - Out I had no choice but to stop down in the lower district of Albany for some medicine. All of the nearest Chemists had closed. Time was ticking by quickly and my mind constantly raced back to Lois. She could be a stubborn woman sometimes and I only hoped that she hadn’t gotten out of bed. The green cross glowed against the neon reds, blues and pinks of the downtown strip clubs. I stopped the car and locked it behind me. A young man lay on a small cardboard box, his tattered blanket pulled over his head. I pulled out a twenty dollar note and slipped it into his hand. The small bell chimed as I entered the small Chemist. An old black woman, dressed in a white overall medicine coat stood behind the counter, reading what looked like a newspaper. 'Can I help ya?' she looked up over the rim of her glasses and stared at me. 'Yeah, I’d like three bottles of aspirin.' She turned around and opened a cabinet, pulling out three white bottles labelled 'Aspirin'. She cashed them in and I paid the full price for the medicine - which was considerably cheap compared to the big pharmacists on Summer Avenue and Madison. 'You’re not from round ‘ere are ya?' she packed the aspirin into a bag and handed it to me. 'Thanks for the aspirin.” I was about to leave when two men entered, wearing ski masks and carrying semi-automatics. The old black woman leant down quickly enough and pulled out a shotgun, she clipped it quickly and fired one round. The young men ran out through the smashed glass screaming. 'Scum … always trying to rob me …' she said while still holding the shotgun in the direction of the door. I was stood in amazement. My mouth was wide open and my eyes were wide with awe. A woman in her late fifties had just pulled out a shotgun and shot blankly at two young men, half her age, carrying semi-automatics. 'Sorry for that, I’d recommend you leave now.' 'You sure you don’t want me to call the cops?' 'Listen … I know how to handle myself here. But, if you don’t leave soon, your car will either be stolen or it will have spray paint on it.' 'Okay … thanks once again for the aspirin.' I didn’t feel right leaving the chemist after the little incident, but she didn’t seem like the kind of woman to mess with. She handled herself pretty well. I walked carefully back up the street, keeping my eyes peeled for the two young men. To my relief they were gone and my car hadn’t been touched. I opened the door and quickly reversed out of the downtown district and drove back toward Summer Plaza apartments in Western. I dialled our home phone, but there was no answer. It was coming up to 11:00. I closed my mobile and assumed that Lois had dozed off for a couple of hours. Traffic was few tonight, so it was a breeze to get back onto the freeway and onto the dual-carriageway. Arrival Marissa quickly pulled to a hault. Marvin jolted forward a little, gripping Marissa’s waist. Marissa pointed to what looked like a group of apartments. One light remained on, but flickering. Harrington removed his helmet and took a deep breath in. He had made it passed Boston and over the highway into Albany, without the slightest bit of trouble. 'Hmmm, city air, nothing like the smell of sweet car fumes and freedom,' Harrington said with a menacing laugh accompanying it. Marissa just grunted quietly, not too proud of her so-called boss. She was about to leave again, when Harrington suddenly grabbed her by the throat and pushed her off the bike. 'I’m not finished with you yet!' he said. Marissa was crawling backwards with her elbows. She had frozen. She was trained to fight back, but something about the mafia boss, Harrington, made her freeze in her tracks like a scared kitten. She had forgotten everything she had learnt in the last few years, she began to sweat profusely. 'Wha - Wha - What do you want with me? I got you here! That’s all Don Kyoto wanted me to do,' Marissa was spluttering her words as she fumbled around to find her 9mm Magnum. Harrington stood up and was now brandishing Marissa Magnum. It glinted against the light of the moon. Marissa’s eyes narrowed, she was terrified. 'I don’t give a damn about Kyoto, he was a temporary replacement. I’m the boss and I’m back. Now, you seem like a smart woman, so shut up and do as I say and I might spare you your life.' Marissa could only nod, she felt the tears coming. She was never prepared to face death, but something about Harrington made her realise his threat wasn’t a joke. After all he had killed his own son in front of around forty men. 'What is it you want me to do?' she asked, now standing and a little more relaxed. 'Nothing …' Marvin was smiling, his dimples now very prominent in his cheeks. He raised the magnum and put the muffler tip on it, and shot Marissa twice in the chest. She fell backwards, gasping, and hit the BMW bike. She began to slide down with her back against it. She stared once more at Harrington and then hit the gravel. Harrington smiled and made his way to the apartment block. It would be another hour until he could finally get to where he wanted to go, but he was proud of his accomplishments so far. After Marvin Harrington had disappeared around the corner, Marissa began to move slightly, still gasping for air, she quickly undid her blouse and ripped open her bullet proof vest. She had two large purple swellings from where the bullet had hit her vest. She sighed in relief. She had very large swellings, but the bullets hadn’t gone through the vest. It was then she saw a silver jeep approaching, and remained in the shadows. Marvin Harrington had killed her, well that’s what he thought. Marissa laughed as she stood up, using the bike for leverage. She had cuts on her elbows, with two holes in the leather jacket from where the bullets entered. She reached into her pocket and pulled out her mobile phone. She was still struggling to breathe … Against the Clock Tony Rush looked out of the large oval windows of the helicopter and gazed at the city of Albany below him. 'How long until we land?' he asked the pilot. 'Five minutes. The landing strip is just over this tall building.' Tony Rush had become extremely tense since leaving the FBI headquarters. His boss threatened to fire him if he left for Albany, which he didn’t care about. His mind was more focused on the fact that he only brought a small 9MM pistol and his bullet-proof vest with him. He had no back-up and no idea how to find Harrington. His best bet was to wait for something to happen. The helicopter landed gently on the large white ‘H’ on top of the NYPD police department. Tony Rush quickly mounted a simple strategy as he waited for the rotating of the propellers to stop. The pilot motioned for him to leave, and he did as he was told. Hoping onto the tarmac, Tony Rush quickly pulled out his mobile and checked the time. He wasn’t sure whether he would make it in time, but he promised his wife he would try. Two agents began to climb the small step ladder and made their way toward Tony Rush. Tony straightened his blue suit jacket and brushed back his hair with his hand as he fastened his pistol in the small of his back. “Tony Rush, I’m Detective Tim Rivers,” Tim Rivers held out his hand. Tony scanned Tim Rivers, and shook his hand. His attention was more focused on the Latino woman standing behind Tim Rivers. “Casey Rider,” she held out her hand and Tony shook it. Casey Rider was dressed in very casual attire for an agent, however, her partner, Tim Rivers, was dressed from head-to-toe, in an expensive black suit, with a dark navy shirt, with the top three buttons unbuttoned. “We just spoke to your chain of command!” Tim was leading the way down the tarmac roof and into the building. Tony knew what was coming, he was to be immediately dispatched back to Boston and imprisoned for disobeying his bosses orders. Instead, Tim shocked him. “Anyway, our superiors informed them that they assigned you to come. You must have some friends in high places,” Tim laughed, and held open the double glass doors. Casey followed behind Tony and Tim. “So, I’m on the case?” Tony stared at all of the other agents in the debriefing rooms and the CTU unit. “Yes, we’re about to head off now. We have units of three partners each. I can’t believe we missed this, we should’ve sent a team over to his apartment,” Casey dipped her head. “I just hope we’re not too late.” “So do I,” Tony replied and checked the small of his back for his Glock. Calling card The front of my apartment block sat flush with shrubbery and a side walk. Parking was in the rear of the building, with a fancy electric gate and some large spaces for the handicap zones. Three dumpsters hunkered down the side of the apartment block, along with some dead bushes, due to the searing heat. One dumpster for the non-recyclable rubbish and two recycle bins - environmentally friendly! I drove the jeep around the side and parked it, pulling out the keys to end the ignition. The rain was coming down pretty hard now. I knew we were in for some summer showers, it was expected. It was 12:05 on the dashboard. I tucked the keys into my jean pocket and looked around the car park, and I noticed Dr. Philips’s red Volks Wagen beetle. He must’ve arrived a while ago. I closed the jeeps door, pulled out the two grocery bags, along with a six pack and the medicine Lois requested. The car beeped behind me. Lifting the newspaper over my head for some protection I raced around the side of the building and ended up flat on my face. My foot had caught on something. I watched as the six-pack of beer cans went flying across the gravel. Luckily enough the shopping bags broke my fall. I was now covered in dirt. I stood up and looked around. Some red droplets were forming in a pool. I looked around the dumpster and jumped. I had tripped over somebody’s hand. Dropping the grocery bags I took another look and realised it was Dr. Philips. “HOLY CRAP …” I was no yelling, panicking and soaking wet. Dr. Philips was shot twice in the head. He wasn’t the nicest of men, but he didn’t deserve to die. I then remembered … Harrington! I began a quick paced run through the lobby, and saw the dead doorman. “No … please no …” I ran two flights of stairs and I was greeted by my neighbours, they were all dressed in their nightgowns and night caps. Most of them were older than me. I brushed past them, my hair dripping wet. As I reached for my door handle, I realised it had been shot open. The door handle wasn’t there. “Mr. Lavelle … we’re so sorry! We heard screaming, but we didn’t dare to go in.” I looked around at Mrs. Winchester. She was an old woman in her late 80’s. She had four grandchildren and got on quite well with Lois. Two more neighbours stepped forward and patted me on the back. “Is - Is - Is she …” I couldn’t say it, my tongue had begun to swell and my eyes puffy. “We don’t know,” Mrs. Winchester put her arm around me. “You need to call the cops, they’re two dead men downstairs,” I looked around, apparently nobody knew about that. Mr. Dodd picked up his pace and disappeared into his apartment. I slowly pushed open my door while putting on a pair of domestic goods from the grocery bag. I flicked the light switch, but it didn’t work. I feared the worst as I made my way through the living room. The bedroom door had two bullets in it. As I tried to push it, it wouldn’t budge. Lois had put up a fight! I smiled, but the smile soon departed as I quickly brushed past the blockage and entered the bedroom which was covered in darkness. I pulled out my phone and shone it on the bed, and to my relief she wasn‘t there. This brought up a new dilemma! A small letter was left on the pillow. It was Harrington’s calling card, he had Lois. The very familiar three words, were writing on an A4 sheet in blood … ‘Open for business’
