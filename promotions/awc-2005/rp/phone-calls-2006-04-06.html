Phone Calls

6 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-6-06 “We’re we going again?” Teresa asked Bob as they rode down a long curvy road somewhere in the middle of Virginia. Bob drove his beige Saturn and Teresa rode shot gun trying not to get car sick. Before they left, Bob had her pack her bags, buy a fishing pole, tackle and a swim suit if she desired. He never told her why and left her wondering what he was up to. “I told you. I got a cabin out here. It’s secluded. I figured it’d be better than those cramped hotel rooms and we both could use the fresh air before we fly out.” They turned right on a gravel road. Teresa swallowed hard trying to keep her stomach from turning. She rolled down her window and dug in her pocket for a Winston. “Right. You didn’t tell me a damn thing. Why couldn’t we just go back to Tennessee? What if I wanted to see Mama and Eric before I left the country?” Bob shook his head. He pointed over the steering wheel at a two-story log cabin several yards away. Teresa immediately noticed the wrap-around porch. The second story had a pair of French doors that opened to a small balcony. Pretty impressive, she thought, but it wasn’t home. “Going home wouldn’t be the best thing for you right now Teresa. Miguel and his bunch are probably looking all over for you. He did try to shoot you and he doesn’t miss the second time.” After a deep sigh, Teresa opened her door and stepped out. Bob watched as she walked to the cabin. A part of him hated to keep her from her family but he had to do what was best for him. Right now that would be keeping Teresa’s mind off home and away from finding the truth. He stepped out of the car, leaving to luggage for later, and quickly caught up with Teresa. ~~~~~ Bob brought in her luggage and showed her the guest room upstairs; the one with the balcony. The queen-sized cedar bed was covered with what appeared to be a home made quilt. A small night stand rested at the head of the bed with a small lamp. A six-drawer dresser stood opposite of the bed with a round mirror hanging over it. Bob dropped her luggage at the door smiling as Teresa observed the room. “You like?” Teresa walked to the French doors and opened them. An early spring breeze rushed through the room. When she didn’t reply, Bob joined her on the balcony. He started to place his hand around her small waist, but hesitated and declined the notion. It was a long fall from where they stood and he wouldn’t put it past the woman to knock him over the railing and onto the ground below. “You’re still mad aren’t you?” “What you think?” “Hey, you came to me for help.” “You could’ve told me no” “I did.” Teresa sighed. Her long fingers held the railing of the small balcony. It took some convincing in order for him to loan her the money for her cousin Darrel. “You did, but you also changed your mind.” “You got me on that one Teresa. You just look to damn good when you’re in despair.” Bob said this teasingly. Teresa eyed him for a moment not sure whether to laugh or smack the smart-ass grin he was giving her right off his clean-shaven face. Instead, she just stepped passed him into the bedroom and began to unpack. Bob followed, found the fishing pole he had her to buy and handed it to her. “There’s a pond in the back. Help yourself to whatever you catch. Just don’t empty it out.” He smiled as Teresa’s face lit up. What good ole country gal can’t resist an invitation to go fishing? Just as he expected, Teresa grabbed the pole from his hand. She then dug out a small tackle box from her luggage and within seconds she was running down the stairs and the back door slammed shut. Bob sat down on the soft bed and pulled a cell phone from an inside pocket of his leather jacket. Within seconds he was connected. “How’s things?” “We got a problem.” Bob stood up, and walked to the window. Teresa was sitting on a folding chair holding her fishing pole. She appeared content, relaxing hopefully. A six pack of beer sat on the ground beside her. He returned to the bed combing his long fingers through his dark red hair and sighed. “What sort of problem? I though you had everything under control.” The voice on the other end replied with a deep southern accent weighed down with hesitancy. “It’s Eric. He knows everything’.” “….. What?” “He knows about the organization. He knows the five grand was a set up. He also knows you’re leading it all and I’m your supplier.” “So, what you gonna do about it?” Bob’s voice was calm, but inside he was cursing like the devil. He had half the mind to return to Tennessee and clean house starting with whom he was speaking to. If he were to do that, he would have to kill Teresa too. A killing spree would only make matters worse. And he didn’t like the idea of cleaning up the mess. “What you talking about? Bob, Eric’s my cousin for christsake! I can’t just kill him!” “Well, if that’s what it takes to get rid of him. Or would you rather rot in prison? If you can’t do it, then find someone that will. Remember Darrel, if the Organization gets its cover blown, you won’t make it to trial ‘cause you’ll be dead.” Darrel releases a staggered sigh. At that point he knew he was a dead man. If he didn’t kill Eric Tomas, Bob would kill him. If he did kill Eric, then Teresa would hunt him down like a wild animal and gut him like a fish. “….How’s Teresa?” “She’s fine, doesn’t have clue, just as it should be. Once her soldier boy brother is out of the way, I’ll have her involved so deep, the only way out is death.” “I wish you didn’t have to bring her in this. She’s tough as nails but it’s only a shell. She’s still just a woman Bob.” “Yes, Yes, don’t you think I know this? Once I break that shell of hers, she’ll be putty in my hand, waiting for me to mold her however I choose.” “You’re delusional man, How the hell you gonna do that?” “Now, I can’t tell you all my secrets can I? Do as I ordered and next time I call, Eric Tomas better be taken care of. Got it?” “Got it” ~~~ Down at the pond, Teresa held a beer she took from Bob’s refrigerator. She propped the fishing pole against the folding chair and stared at the water. During downtime, she always went home to visit her mama and brother. She thought about stealing the car once Bob was asleep. It would leave him stranded at the cabin, but what if he was right? What if Miguel and his bunch were looking for her? What if he went to the farm? “MAMA!!” Teresa dug into her front pocket, pulled out a slim flip faced cell phone. Dialed her home and waited. Someone picked up after the fourth ring. “Hello?” “Mama?” “Teresa is that you dear?” “Yes Mama, it’s me. Is everything ok?” “Well yeah. I suppose so. Eric is doing much better. In fact, he wants to talk to you.” A short moment passed. Eric soon picked up. She listened as he stepped outside. “Teresa?” “Yeah, it’s me. How you doing?” “The wound is healing up really well. Gonna have an ugly scar though. Listen, is Bob near by?” “No, why?” “Where are you?” “Virginia…Eric what’s going on?” “Teresa who are you talking to?” A voice came from behind. Teresa looked up to see Bob’s tall lanky form shadowing over her as she sat on the folding chair. He placed a folding chair similar to hers beside her and sat down. Teresa watched as he took the fishing pole and cast the line. She listened as Eric continued. “It’s Mama. She’s sick.” It was all Eric could tell her right now. He heard Bob’s voice. He must’ve snuck up on her. If the man got suspicious, he could harm Teresa. When Teresa didn’t respond, he continued. “I wish you were here baby sister. I took Mama to her doctor last week. Remember how she’s been forgetting stuff, like the time she forgot to turn off the stove? Teresa, she’s got Alzheimer’s. It’s only in the fist stage right now. She still remembers most things.” Teresa continued to stare at Bob, who was by now watching her with a puzzled expression. She didn’t see him. It was as if her brother’s words put her in a trance. Bob reached over and carefully took the cell phone from her hand, flipped it shut and stuck it in his jacket pocket. Still shocked from the news of her mother, Teresa sat frozen. Alzheimer’s was a disease of the brain. It affected behavior, personality, the ability to think clearly, and the ability to carry out daily activities. Mama Tomas will gradually lose not only her mind, but her being before finally, her brain will shut down. With a soft voice, Bob finally spoke. He left the fishing pole on the ground. He did not know what was told her, but whatever it was, it wasn’t good. He stood and carefully helped Teresa to her feet and led her inside.
