Introduction of the Kinslayer: Part 1

7 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: A long time ago Part One-The Birth of the Kinslayer Location: A Museum in Hungary Time: 1:00 pm The kids were tired. The day was a hot one, even with the air conditioning inside the museum; they felt like they were going to melt. The woman that worked for the museum had been talking for so long the kids didn't even hear what she was said. It was something or other about the Napoleonic Era, nothing of immediate interest for the kids. They wanted to see ruthless fighters, not ruthless tacticians. They wanted to see a man whose hands had gotten bloodied and not someone who sent people to their deaths. They had passed no such man yet, and they were getting impatient. They kept moving from room to room and they were all getting tired. They did not expect that the next room they would go into, would be the one that would make them look up to someone with such fear, but at the same time, fascination. When they got into the room, they were immediately drawn to a painting. A man with long black hair and a beard standing as if he were royalty, holding a slightly curved sword in his right hand. The sword was immaculate, its hilt made of gold and incrusted with rubies. The man's skin was brown from all the sun he had taken over the years. He wore a white shirt made of wool and black pants. He also wore wolf skin as a cloak. He had dark eyes, his gaze cold and unforgiving. The kids stared in wonder at the man they were seeing. One of them pointed at the name and the woman smiled. 'Oh yes, Attila the Hun. One of the most feared people in the time after Christ was crucified. He ruled the Huns from 434 to 453AD and united the nomads into one group. They ruled most of Eastern Europe under his reign and bent into submission the Ostrogoths and Visigoths, the Gauls and they almost took over the Eastern Roman Empire from its capital in Constantinople. He was feared by the Romans and by everyone else. He was supposed to be more powerful than the Roman Caesar, but he died in the year 453AD by mysterious ways. His wife, one of many he had, found him dead in his bed with blood coming out of his nose, but it is said that one of his wives killed him.' One of the kids, or most of them were not looking at Attila anymore, they were looking at the strange fellow next to him. He was a few inches taller than Attila, his muscles more defined than his king, but he wore a grim face. His left hand was lying on his sword's hilt. His right hand was on the reins of a black horse. The Huns were horse-masters; they cared for their horses more than their families. The woman sighed, as if the sight of the man was shattering her soul as it was. 'This is Attila's fiercest General. General Dagoth of the Huns was a descendant of the great Magog of the Sumerian Empire, his strength and will to fight was what made the Huns rally behind him. He was Attila's friend since they were young, but before his death, Attila feared Dagoth's popularity with his army so much that he betrayed him and slaughtered his family. Dagoth was never the same, he truly became a nomad, and trusted only his sword and his horse, and he wandered through the lands and became a mercenary........... --------------------------------- Location: Germania Time: Sunset Day: Winter 452AD He had been riding for weeks. Not stopping for anything but some food and a little rest when it was night. He wobbled left and right on his saddle, and almost fell down to the floor unconscious. There he was to sleep through the night until he was awoken once again. He dreamed the most terrible of dreams. In a single day he had lost his wife, his two daughters, and his best friend. General Dagoth was nothing more than a simple peasant, but he would have his revenge. He would not forget Attila for betraying him; it would become his passion to kill the slayer of his family. He was entering a house, they had forgotten the lost art of the huts in place for these things that would stay there always, and Attila certainly had passed too much time with the Romans. Dagoth dreamt all night. About times where he was happiest, about times where he was with his daughters and wife, but after a while, they turned to nightmares, they appeared beheaded but still alive; Dagoth was sweating and turning in his sleep. His most trusted companion, Manderb, started to push him around to see if he would wake up, but Dagoth was out cold. He turned all night and all the next morning until something pushed him harder. He felt the pain, as if a sword had just been stuck through his chest. Ho woke up with a start, and as soon as he opened his eyes, he saw ten armed men all around him. His brain started functioning again, and he noticed that they were neither Huns nor Romans, because surely both would have already killed him and taken his head to their king or Caesar. 'Barbarians...' That was the only thing he could say before he was hit in the back of the head with a wooden club. Five of the Barbarians tied him up in a large pole and they began their journey back to their tribe. His horse was taken by the remaining barbarians and the hunt for the feast of that night began. They walked what seemed like ages to Dagoth, but it was only fifteen minutes. When they arrived, he was immediately sent to the chief's hut. It was big enough to fill three of the normal huts and had a big throne in made of wood and bones. The chief was old, the white locks reaching his shoulders and long white beard. He was looking down, as if remembering the past. 'What a day, when the great General of the Huns falls into my lap.' He said standing up and looking straight down at Dagoth, who was kneeling in front of the throne with hands tied behind his back. 'I am no more a General, and the Huns can burn in hell for all I care.' He said spitting in the floor as he said Huns. 'Attila held you in high regard Hun, and you are the fiercest of warriors in this land. Would you like to die a soldier's death?' 'Yes, there is nothing that I want more than to be reunited with my family. Although taking out Attila would be something worth doing before I died,' Dagoth said with a look of anger and anguish at the same time. 'Well, you know, we could use a man like you in our ranks. You would bring fear into the enemies and lighten our people's morale.' The King said while rubbing his white beard. 'I will help you, only if your enemy is Attila and the Huns, and no one else.' Dagoth said firmly. The King laughed and asked one of his servants to cut the rope binding his hands. He smiled. Then I will now call you General Dagoth Hunter of the Huns, and everyone in this tribe will call you by that name. Give him back his sword.' After he grasped the hilt of his sword, he felt his strength coming back. A great sword taken in battle from a Roman General. It had a golden hilt with a horse's head and a sword in its mouth. It was done, the alliance between this Barbarian tribe from Germania and Dagoth Hunter of the Huns. He was to train ten of their best soldiers in sword fighting, axe-fighting, with the bow and arrow and with horses. For months they trained day in and day out, preparing to battle the Hun armies that had become so powerful and so feared over Europe, the same armies that Dagoth himself had trained all his life. After he had trained those ten men with all his knowledge, except a couple of secrets he kept to himself, he was ready for war. The King called forth men from adjacent tribes that had been attacked by the Huns and would love nothing but revenge. They began the march down to Attila's 'castle' ready to kill them all and destroy them forever. After a weeks march, they arrived. Swords, bows, axes, pikes, everything in hand for battle. General Dagoth rode in front, and as soon as he was in hearing distance of the Huns he screamed. 'Attila! Your end has come! No Hun will live to tell of this battle! You, 'The Scourge of God', will see your end, by my sword!' ----------------------------------------------------------------- Location: A Museum in Hungary Time: 1:20 pm 'All were slain; no more of the race of the Hun ever existed, except for Dagoth himself. When he got to Attila, he was already dead. Died because of a nosebleed. Dagoth was furious and swore that he would avenge his family forever, and that he would kill anyone in his way. After the massacre he became a mercenary, and was called General Dagoth the Kinslayer.' The kids where holding their breaths, and after the story, they sighed with relief after such horrible story, how revenge drove a man into madness, and that madness was enough for him to kill everyone of his kin. They looked into Dagoth's eyes again, and felt the hatred. They shuddered and moved on. The story of Dagoth Kinslayer as he was known around the world was fascinating, but his story did not end up there...
