Introductions...a mortal life

6 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/10/2005 Upon a time, before the fairy broods, Drove Nymph and Satyr from the prosperous woods, Before King Oberon’s bright diadem, Sceptre and Mantle, Clasp’d with dewy gem, John Keats: Lamia – 1819 The sun rises this dawn, as it has done for centuries…and I, its silent witness, cherish its return each morning. My name is Anacletus Alcaeus. As I sit here, recounting tales of my life, I find it hard to convey my identity. For what purpose? For the purpose that it has always been my secret. The guarded treasure of exclusive knowledge, which only I may possess…and yet I am about to freely exchange it with all those who are willing to listen to my tale. There are things in this life, that cannot be explained, and then there are things in this life that those with the tools to do so, simply refuse to explain. My name is Anacletus Alcaeus, and I am a vampire. A being of nocturnal preference, which hounds the night like a mistress with whom a waltz is to be initiated. For twenty five hundred years, I’ve plagued the streets beneath my feet with my existence…my perpetual journey through the vessel of time. Those who behold me in my true form have always been either too shocked, or too mesmerised to remember anything specific…but rest assured, there is no trickery in these eyes. There is truth, love, pain and regret…but no trickery. I am a vampire, and I survive on the blood of the living. But it was not always so. This is to be an account of the first years of my life…my mortal life that is. I wish to relate this to all who will take the time to endeavour to read upon my words, as I have kept my life locked away for far too long. It is time to etch it into stone, where it only exists in memory. The world shall know my darkness…and I shall know the world. It’s the age-old taboo…but to hell with it. This is my time…the time of the dark warrior, and the prince of crimson. For I have, without regard for my own well being, or for others like me, stepped into the light. No longer a resident of shadows and dust, I walk freely amongst my new brethren at the Atlantic Wrestling Club. But that is all for another time. What I wish to discuss, in my monologue of rhetoric, is the events that have transpired to make me the vampire, and the man, that I am today… I am a vampire…but it was not always so. I was born Anacletus Alcaeus, of Leonidas and Kallisto, my Father and Mother respectively. I was sired, in the Greek city of Apollonia. It is where I was born, and where I revisited many times since my proverbial rebirth into darkness. The year of my conception was four hundred and sixty eight BC, by modern calendar years, but of course…to us, the year was different. We shall, however, proceed with the calendar of modern acceptance. After all, I wish not to confuse, or insult my avid readers, with petty attention to such details. Let us just take the date as 488 BC…the year, by standards, that I was brought into this world, a healthy Greek child. Because it had been such an effort for my Mother (she had miscarried four times en route to my arrival), my parents believed it fitting to name me as such…Anacletus Alcaeus, translated loosely, meaning Invoked Strength. I had been the one who survived…the one who battled his way out of the womb, where others before me had failed. This sometimes fills me with a pride and happiness…but also with sadness. To all my dead brothers and sisters, who never ventured into the outside world…I mourn and regret, every time my mind crosses the unfortunate events of my mortal life. Unfortunate, perhaps, in some respects…but in most, I suppose I was a privileged young man. The only son of my parents, they lavished me with praise and affection. I was spoilt, and given everything I desired. My parents were wealthy. My father, Leonidas, was born Onesiphorus, but due to a heroic last stand in the defence of the fledgling city from invaders from the Adriatic, earnt the name Leonidas, meaning Lion. The story went, that my father, and three other men, while on a fishing expedition off the port of Illyria, saw an invading ship from the south. They bore a flag of truce and trade, so my Father and his fellows greeted them to their shores. Upon the arrival, however, a spear was fired across, killing one of the fishermen instantly. My father, and his two allies, managed to fend off the entire shipload of marauders with just a stone club, fishing knife and axe between them. Both my father’s friends were killed by the invaders, and by the time the town guard got to the port, my father lay wounded on the ground, still fighting off the foes. From that day, he was named Leonidas, and given a new respect in the Apollonian community. We were a well off family, my Mother coming from some line of nobility, though she would never say which…she only ever let me know I would be secure for my life, no matter which pursuits I chose. I was sent to the best schools, and given the finest tutors, in Greek history, philosophy, logic and mathematics. My mother had high hopes for me in the politics of what she hoped would be a new Greek state. My father, on the other hand, did not share her optimism for my future. He had always wanted a son and heir, to take over his trading business on the Adriatic sea. He saw me as his last chance to take the business further, and from an early age, his designs for me were apparent. However, they were both deceived, by the mysteries of fate. It wasn’t until my fourteenth birthday, that I became so very aware of my uniqueness in the community in which I had grown up in. I had always seen myself as one of the children of Apollonia, with my future mapped out like any other child’s…in philosophy, mathematics, trade, and perhaps politics. My school was full of the teaming future of the Greek intellectualists. However, it was made painfully clear to me on my fourteenth year, that my talents lay far from the fields of politics and economics. It was the father of one of my friends, Sosigenes, who first pointed out to me my obvious size advantage over everyone of my age. By fourteen, I was already six foot two, and showed no signs of slowing my growth. It was then, that I confronted my father, in asking what benefits a size like that could have. He was reluctant to share with me anything as pertaining to what I could achieve with my obvious height and weight advantage, but I was sure, from my own study, that my perfect career would be in the military. I could lift boys my age without effort, and I could throw the hurling stones further than anyone even four years my senior. The community began to embrace my size as a gift from Apollo, for my father’s courage so many years before. My father reluctantly allowed me to be transferred to the Greek military academy in Athens, and I bid my Mother farewell for the last time. My Mother had always been a major influence on me. It was her drive, to allow me to be everything that I could, that perhaps has allowed me to survive this long. However, she always seemed reluctant to converse with me. Back then, I felt as if I had wronged her in some way, but now, looking back…I believe in some way, she blamed me for her inability to have children again. Mine was a Caesarean birth, and my Mother was prevented from bestowing upon me a brother or sister after my arrival. It was that, plus my late blooming passion for war, that I think drove my Mother away from my affections. I loved her still…but it was my father who reluctantly encouraged me from then, not my Mother. I still think to this very day, that it was my acceptance to go to the academy in Athens that killed her. It took three years, but the decline started from that day…of that I am most sure. But, at the age of sixteen, I set off to become what I became. Two years, studying under the greatest tacticians in all the Greek states, taught me valuable lessons in the art of war…and that, coupled with my size and strength, made me the most promising student at the academy. In my third year at the academy, I was given the opportunity to choose for myself a career in any sect of the army I pleased. This was a rare gift, bestowed upon the most talented youngsters, looking to break through the military ranks, onto the field of battle. I was adamant that my future lay in infantry service…and I was given placement into the service of the Macedon Ruler, Perdiccas I. The uneasy peace of Kallas had been signed a year before, meaning that we, as the Macedon army were on constant alert. The peace, as predicted, lasted no more than a few years, and by the age of twenty-one, I had my first real experience of battle. The Persians had amassed an invading force of some ten thousand troops, which we were sent, amidst an alliance of Macedon, Athens and Sparta, to meet head on. Our force of twenty thousand men stood on the shores of the East, where the Persians were set to invade from. By that age, I was six foot nine, and stood out in the crowd of my fellows like a sore thumb. I remember clearly, the training sessions we had…where I would hurl my opponents this way and that, my unprecedented strength applauded by all onlookers. But it was the Persian force that first felt the brunt of my force. The army was led by a ruthless tactician named Cyrus. His plan was to outflank our larger force, with two separate forces of four thousand each, while charging our body with two thousand men, of infantry and cavalry. It was a nice plan, but ultimately flawed. The force destined to attack the left flank of our force was caught up in a storm at sea, and Cyrus had made a terrible mistake, splitting his forces, and fell upon us like water onto stone. The very first man I killed…he was young…perhaps no older than me. The fear in his eyes was what struck me…they were forced to fight, the Persians…slaves to their ruler. He charged at me, screaming something in a language I, to this day, do not understand, and flung his spear at me. My shield was heavy enough to weather the blow with ease, and my blade cut his arm square off. As it lay, dripping blood over the already stained battlefield, his eyes were so full of fear…that he did not scream. I hesitated, feeling sympathy for the man, before plunging my sword into his gut, and ending his agony. The Persians were forced to fight…I always did it for the glory of the battle, and the spoils of victory. The battle was won within four hours, and the fleet attacking our left flank quickly retreated. After that day, I was hailed a hero in the Macedon army, and gained a new respect from my fellow soldiers, and even my rulers. It was the day that my life really began…the life that I wish to share with you in this dialogue. I must apologise for the prelude to my story, but I deem it necessary, so as to provide some background to my life as a mortal…and my tutelage in the ways of vampirism. Where I would like to go from here, however, is the beginning of my life as a champion of the neo-Greek military. The battle with the Persians had given me new respect and adulation, as I’ve already said. Many of my fellow soldiers and comrades told me afterwards that they had seen something in my eyes that day…something that scared them, as well as the enemy. As my sword came crashing down onto the helmets and shields of young Persian men, they were sure they had seen a glint of pleasure. This made me proud, but as I look back on it now…it was my downfall. My Mother and Father had had such high hopes for me in the fields of academia…but I didn’t care. None of that mattered any more. I was In my element, decapitating men I towered over, like a giant. The officers of the gentry all lavished me with their praise, as they had to…in order to gain favour with their superiors. I was a hero… For three months, I lived in the splendour of all this. Drinking the finest wine, with the finest tacticians and officers in the Greek Army. I became stubborn, proud, and even vain. If a man jokingly challenged my family integrity, or mocked my freakish size, I would meet him in battle, and he would fall at my feet, exhausted and battered. This did not have the most positive effect on my officers, as they felt insulted…insulted that a soldier would challenge, and defeat them. I perhaps did this too often, but I was invincible. Fine women came to my bed chambers, and even young boys if the need came for it (In the ancient times, we placed much less of a difference on the skin of youth as is placed now. A young female was as beautiful as a young male, and vice versa). I was truly a child of the Gods, and I let myself know it. It was about that time, that we received word of the Persian mobilisation across the water. Cyrus’ death had angered many Persian leaders, who had all united under the banner of their new ruler, Prius IV. He was well known to be a ruthless man, who would butcher his own people before he would butcher the enemy. He lived for war, and seemed to me the perfect opponent to test our military machine against. We were told that we must pre-empt their attack, and were charged with weaponry, armour and supplies to last four months of warfare…for that was what they believed it may come to. I was convinced we would scatter them like sheep within the first week. We said our goodbyes to the young maidens with whom we’d shared the past three months, and mounted our ships…ready to set sail to enemy land. My sword had already gotten a reputation of its own, and was to be at my side at all times. My staggering height, six foot nine, would not allow me to fit under the masts of the ship I was on, so I was asked to the head of the ship, with our section officer, a man named Achilles, after the legend of the great swordsman who fell in Troy. He fit this name every inch…he was a man of war. He had fought battles in the entire known world, and bore the scars of many foes, including the strange black warriors, with huge spears made of wood and hay, who would shout curses at anyone who neared them; and the barbarians from the North, who threatened the Greek civilisation with every moment of non-vigilance. So it was, that we were paired into battle, on this fine spring day. The ships landed at around midday, and we immediately got to work setting up a camp at the shore, to be able to defend against any ambush attack we might receive. No attacks came, and our scouts reported no enemies in sight. I was invited into the tent of Achilles, and we sat, watching the sunset, and talking of war and women…for hours. At one point he asked me… Achilles: “Tell me, Alcaeus, why do you stride so gallantly into war? Your allies in the battle do so with such apprehension, but it is as if the battlefield plays your host, and you its willing guest…tell me why?” I smiled at him. He had no idea…I was in my element, slaughtering the bodies of the enemy. “The battlefield is my home, friend. And for all those who dare to trespass on the floors of my greatest friend…I have the very medicine that will send them unwillingly to Hades.” I replied, gruffly, arrogantly. Only now do I realise how presumptuous I was…how utterly misguided. But I was strong headed, and it was the joy of battle for which I yearned. We both laughed, and shared more wine, until the sun had disappeared from the horizon, and there was nothing left to talk of. We slept…a long night, dreaming of what we had accomplished and were still to accomplished…tomorrow would be a day to remember… The morning work to sirens everywhere. The scouts had spotted a large enemy force, charging at our position some eight kilometres away. One of our men had apparently been a Persian spy in our ranks, and had given them our exact position. Knowing their own land, they had been able to fool our scouts, and sneak up on us. We were under attack! I immediately suited up, and took my sword, sheathed around my belt. My helmet was pushed onto my head, and hurt me as it went down…I was so excited for the slaughter. My shield went round my back, and I grabbed two of the spears from the side of our tent. Achilles wasn’t far behind when we rode out on horseback, to the front line of the slowly amassing defence perimeter. Off in the distance, a huge line of cavalry were descending on our position. We were of forty thousand men, sluggish, just awake…some of who were still to be ready and join the ranks…it was in disarray. Our enemy had a force of at least seventy thousand…all ready for battle. A cavalry force the likes of which I had never seen, nor heard of. Nevertheless, I grit my teeth, and smiled. This was a moment of true heroism, and I intended to break their lines single handedly if needs be. I was a true son of Apollo, and these men would scatter like water over stone, onto my shield. I raised my spears, and let out a huge roar. My comrades joined me in this respect, as we stood, defiant of our pursuers. Achilles joined me at my side, and he looked almost apprehensive. I reassured him. “Brother, today we are lions as much as we are men. Today, they will hear the thunder of Apollo’s voice in my roar, and the venom of Hades in our blades.” I chanted. He looked at me, still afraid, but my smile must have reassured him. I was brimming confidence. He was shocked, astounded. My mighty arms were held in position, as I watched intently, looking for the largest men, the heroes of their force. I would remove their heads, before their men had a chance to chant their name… “It seems the Gods will smile on us today, Alcaeus. I see them in your eyes, and I’m sure the enemy will too.” His words pierced my like a spear of self-confidence. My arrogance built up like a storm inside me…I was ready. I arched my back, and let out another thunderous roar. The army approaching almost stopped, I knew they heard it. A few feet later, they did stop. Their archers ran out into the field, and launched a blanket of arrows at our position. Our men were well trained in the orders of defence, and our shields were marooned together, like an impenetrable shell. The cries behind me told me some men hadn’t been protected by this all purpose shell, but that didn’t matter. I roared again. As did Achilles this time…he was ready also now. We stared our enemies as they charged at us, and prepared ourselves. “For the Glory of Greece!” I heard shouted out over the army. But what was the Glory of Greece? It was nothing compared to the glory of Alcaeus. The army crashed into our ranks, sending men flying in all directions. The roars began to emanate over the battlefield, as each side looked to gain its confidence from the voices of its comrades. My spear was sent through the forehead of one advancing cavalryman, and the other, into the body of a horse. I drew my sword, and ran right into their midst. My eyes were full of the hunger of battle. I felt only for a moment, a spear pierce my side…it didn’t matter…I was invincible. I ripped the spear out, and flung it into the chest of the man who had thrown it. Men cowered around me, as my sword flashed through the air, removing limbs, ears…anything I could get. I rammed my shield forward into the face of one man, and plunged my sword into another…I was in ecstasy. I roared with laughter, as men fell to their knees, decapitated, destroyed by the sword of muddy vengeance. One man was bent over, about to spear one of my comrades, but I split his back open so badly, it almost came apart in three places. He fell to the floor, revealing a bloody Achilles. I grabbed him by the arm, and lifted my commander to his feet. I smiled down at him, the twinkle of lust for war in my eyes… “The Gods do not smile on you brother…Alcaeus smiles on you today.” I smiled, and laughed, as I turned into a horde of Persian warriors, sending them in all directions. Achilles backed off, and I heard him scream, as a sword pierced him from behind, moving right through his body. I didn’t care any more…this was all too great. I sliced the forehead of a man open with my furious blade, and took the head off another with my sharpened shield. I heard the horn for retreat, but I refused to do so…I beat men’s bodies into the ground, until I looked around, and all I saw was a horde of Persian faces, all staring at me, looking for my weakness. I would show them none! I slammed my shield behind me, making a path through the young men…until I was out in open air, the sun on my face…the blood over my eyes slightly staining my vision…but I turned. The entire army, still strong, but exhausted…was standing, staring at me. Then I saw Prius, upon his horse, coming forward slowly, his eyes glued to mine. He had a frown on his mouth, I was sure he had seen my endeavours on the field. Where were my comrades now? My soldiers? My brothers? To hell with their defiance…I would take on the entire Persian army myself. How foolish…how stubborn. I roared at the army, who took a step back, stumbling over one another. I took a spear, and flung it into the ranks of their men, flooring three of their soldiers, pinned together by the force of the hurled instrument of pain. I looked up, smiling at Prius. He looked shocked at my lust for war…at my pure joy at it all. “It is a sad day, when one man can be so unlike a man, that this must happen”. He shook his head. He turned, but I stayed him with my voice. I shouted “Fine words, coming from a man who slaughters his own people, and walks on paths made of the bodies of his soldiers. I fight for honour, for glory, for the greatness of Greece! You fight for the misguided concept of staying in power.” He stared at me sharply. He was angered by these sentiments. “Such lies your Greek leaders tell you. Such lies. It is they who wish to keep their power, by sending you into battle against a people who bid you no ill will!” He snarled at me “All I see, from the bodies of Greeks, rotting on this field of battle…is ill will.” I smiled, ready for his next insult. “Had we not attacked, you would have taken us by surprise at every village…murdering…raping…pillaging. I had to protect my people.” He looked saddened…as if some major defeat had taken place…but he was the victor! “Then your weakness is your downfall!” I screamed at the top of my lungs, as I hurled a spear into his chest. The Persian leader fell off his white stallion with a thump, dead, no more. The army gasped, and looked at me. I roared at them all, and held my sword up high. Two men charged me, but they both fell as easily as they had made the choice to attack. I wished for them all to attack…perhaps I wished to die…a martyr for the Greek cause. But they did not. They backed off, turned, and made their way back to their camp, carrying their leader with them. I smiled, my victory was complete. The Greeks may have lost, but Alcaeus never lost! I walked back to the camp, and many Greeks shouted in astonishment as I returned. I exchanged my stories, and told them of my murder of the ruler of Persia. I was unbeatable…invincible. I would have taken on their whole army, I said, but they fled. My name rang out over the coastal camp, and we drank wine to our fallen comrades, and our butchered enemies. This was my time…the time of Alcaeus. The morning came, and we decided we should attack the leaderless Persians. There was no place for the codes of conduct, or anything…they had no leader, and we could win this battle with a decisive blow. Our men mounted up, and we marched on the Persians. The following battle was bloody, vicious…we won, at the cost of five thousand Greeks, and twenty thousand Persians. We had the coast, and the mountains that circled it…but what was it worth? Today, I am sickened by the way in which I conducted myself in those early years. While I have never lost the warrior spirit I gained while growing up, I hate myself for having loved it so much. It is my pride, and my torture… The Greek army went on to claim victory after victory. We beat back the Persian armies on land, and at sea…but we never really gained much position. We could not fight them on the mountains…they knew the terrain too well, and the passes that led to their major cities, were flanked with guarding armies, that would surely tear us to pieces. Despite our victories…we were in stalemate. But it didn’t matter to me. Every battle gave me another chance for glory…another chance for my name to ring out in battle. Men fell, without thought for who they had been, or their families…I destroyed them…their lives…their families. I was the champion of the cause of death, and my students stood around me in droves, tired and exhausted in the light of my fury. It was sometime during this mutual slaughtering, that I received word from home. It was my father…he told me things had taken a turn for the worse at home. My Mother was dead, and my Father had broken down. He needed me home…he needed his son. I disregarded the letter, this was too important. I could mourn for my poor Mother later. I would shed no tears on the battlefield…remorse is for the weak, I told myself. Now, I know it is the ally of the strong. How foolish. I should have gone back home, seen my father, taken up his reigns in the business, and the community. I should have buried my Mother. But my own selfish pride refused to allow me this. Three years passed until I decided to return home. Enough blood had been spilt on my hands to make sure my name was etched in the bodies of my enemies. I returned home with the wounded, and those who had seen too much war to allow them to consciously continue. My arrival at the family land, was one of pain and happiness. Happiness at seeing my father…though he was old now…much too old for his time. He did not smile when we met, but I embraced him still. His arms could not lift to hug me, or did not want to be lifted. He did not say a word. But I was happy to be home. Sadness that my Mother was gone…poor Kallisto. I visited her grave, and paid my respects for a full day of prayer to the Gods. When I returned, my father was still sitting in his chair in the house, and did not even raise his eyes to greet me. Her death had hit him hard…and when I hadn’t returned…he had lost all will to live on. It was the community who had supported him, kept him alive…but he was dead inside. I could see it. At dinner, I told my father of all the stories of my conquests in Persia. The men I’d fought, and those I’d fort alongside. He never even acknowledged me. I told him of the death of the Persian ruler, Prius IV, and the destruction of their seventy thousand-man army, by that of myself, and forty thousand Greeks. I told him he was right to let me study at the academy in Athens, that war was the vessel in which I excelled. He had enough… “Since when, Alcaeus, did war replace your family in your heart?” He asked. His words were as poison, but there was no malice in his voice…, which implied malice anyway. I looked at him, and put down my food. “Father, you know that I went away to follow my calling. I went to war, so as to make something of myself, to become…” He spiked right back “To become what? A murderer? A killer? A petty criminal, glorified on the field of battle? Your calling was with us. Your calling was to bury your Mother, when she passed away. You should have chosen to come back then. I could have forgiven you had you done that…but now…now I have no son!” The words smashed me like a sword against my helmet in battle. How could he say that? I was his son, and I had come back, for him… “Father, you don’t understand. I couldn’t just leave…the men, they depended on me. We needed the victories, don’t you see? I mourned for Mother, and lamented your loss Father.” My words had no strength in them. They were hollow and unjustified…I knew it. “You lamented nothing! You warred because in your heart, all you know is war. You are the shell of the man you were to become Alcaeus, and I want never to see you again. After the morning comes, you will return to your land of butchery, and never speak of, nor think of, me again. For that would be an insult to everything I had wanted for you. We fight not for ourselves, but for those and all that need us to. You fight, Alcaeus, because it is all you know…and your vanity has blinded you. I have no son!” He slammed his food down, and stood up, retiring to bed. I sat, shocked, and pained. His words, I knew, were his truths. He would never receive me again as his son…only as an intruder, a marauder. I had seen it in his eyes. How could I have been so foolish? So selfish? As to deny my own father? That was that then… I watched the sun set on our household for the last time that night, and set off for the boats during the night. I would not be there to say goodbye in the morning, I wouldn’t give him that satisfaction. I walked that night, out of my old life, and out of the eyes of the one man who had believed in me before I became what I became…the one man who’s love could have saved me from my future damnation…the one man, who I could have shared eternity with…my father. This is the first chapter of the story of the life of Anacletus Alcaeus. I think it prudent to relate this to those who would listen, as it represents my most hopeful, proud, and foolish years. This feeling is one echoed when I walk into the Atlantic Wrestling Club. Facing John Edwards but a week from now, I will walk once again into the unknown…and battle the fears that I hold deep. That I will be inadequate, has never ventured into my thoughts. But it is the hope, pride and foolishness with which I will need to battle in the ring. Facing one as primed as John Edwards, would not be the easiest of tasks if I were at my strongest. But facing him with the weight of my guilt under my belt…it becomes a battle against he, and myself. It seems like an almost impossible task, to draw myself from the shadows, and make of everything a success. Alcaeus the wrestler…has a ring to it, don’t you think? I do…but then, when has my opinion counted for anything? I am a vampire…that is the essence of my being. A creature of nocturnal preference, who slips undetected through the night, taking my victims from the scourge of society…the damned in the eyes of those who decide the damned. I feast on blood, but I do not partake in any of these ritualistic fantasies that authors such as Bram Stoker have come to champion in the world of literature. I am so much more than that…we are so much more than that. But, as I come back into the light, I simply hope this recount of my mortal life may bring humanity into the eyes of those who perceive me with some distinct displeasure. Those who would discriminate against me will be well advised, that although it does not matter to me whether I am liked or disliked (this, perhaps, is often a lie), it may matter to yourselves not to misjudge me. These are the words of a lost romantic, misplaced, and troubled by the history of blood. One instant can echo through eternity…a few years of immorality have come to pain me for the next twenty five hundred. I feel love, and pain…and it is through these vessels that I will come to understand myself, as I step into the ring for the first time…through these vessels that I hope to preach that understanding to those who would listen. All I wish for is a chance… Twenty five hundred years on this earth, and still my reservations run deep. I just hope I can be sufficient to warrant myself pride again in my dealings. Too much has befallen my life to allow me to wallow in memory…it’s time for new legacies to be built, and a new strategy of life. My years of power and glory, are long gone…all that is left, is my ambition to put things right. To be known…and to quell the voice inside me, that chastises me for the death of all I have held dear… The sun sets this night, as it has done for centuries, and I, its silent witness, will cherish its return again in the morning.
