Further Assumptions(V1: a3)

18 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Many a person had come to pay their final respects to Hibiki Jue. She was a popular girl, and was loved by all. Her family adored her, and her friends worshipped her. However, we have come to learn that popularity isn’t all it’s cracked up to be. Jue had her own secrets… and it was those secrets that led to her untimely demise. Everyone assumed that it was suicide, and they had good reason to believe so according to the letter found with her body. But there are a select few who know the truth of the matter… and those people aren’t about to step forward and admit their crimes. They’re not cowards… they’re geniuses. The tombstone was basic, gray granite and characters etched onto its face. The only thing that separated it from the rest of the slabs of stone was the picture frame that stood below it. The tombstone itself read: “Hibiki Jue. Loved in life, adored in the afterlife.” It was rather creative a message to place upon someone’s grave, but the picture frame was the thing that specified her grave from the other stones. Her picture rested against the headstone, a chipper picture of Jue smiling at the camera wearing a colorful sundress, a memento from a day at the beach… a happier time. The happy times were gone now… Jue had taken them with her. Everyone that she had crossed was touched by her life, and now that she was dead, there was a hole left in his or her hearts that could never be filled again. Then again… no one ever questioned the vanity of a girl to have her own portrait placed upon her grave. A reminder of how cute she was, and how she would rather remind people of how better she looked than they rather than residing to her final rest plainly like the others. There were those that were aware of the darker side of Hibiki Jue… the obsessive vanity that consumed her life, the desire for perfection, the fight to be flashier than anyone else around her. She craved the spotlight, and in her pursuit, she found herself in a place where no light could reach her. It was rather sad in a way… and despite her selfish faults, everyone pitied her, and mourned for her. The failed aspirations of greatness were heavy on everyone else, because they had such high hopes for Jue’s success. Jue had pushed her success onto them from day one; she didn’t want anyone to doubt her. But it didn’t seem like everyone supported her… It was sunny that afternoon, and the stereotypical gloom of the graveyard was taken away, instead giving it a garden-like flavor with a line of stone sculptures rowed in militaristic procedure. And at the current moment, the graveyard was empty except for one visitor. And as fate would have it, she was a most honored guest of the young diva Hibiki Jue. The guest looked down at the picture of Jue, her eyes were saddened by something weighing down on her. She sniffed, her finger brushing up the underside of her nose to catch anything that might have leaked out. While her eyes were indeed watery, and on the verge of leaking themselves, she managed to keep the pipes from bursting and unleashing the emotional floodgates; something had her troubled, and looking at Jue’s face only made things worse. Ikari Yuna kept her composure, sucking up all her tears and her sorrows, and bowed as she walked away from the tombstone. Title: “Further Assumptions 3/3 {Est.}” Subtitle: ”All Over a Girl” Children flocked to playgrounds and parks in droves. They called to their young hearts, saying, “Play here… run here… exploit your youth and vitality here… because as time passes, so do those two traits… and you will only have nothing but the time spent here to look back on in old age… and the memories of them itself will make you feel young again.” Children don’t see it that way, though. Consciously they see it as having fun, while the metaphysical level makes it into the concept recently expressed. But more or less, the park was full of children that afternoon. There wasn’t a piece of playground equipment that wasn’t flocked by the youngsters, everyone crowding around trying to get a turn on the merry go round, or filing in line to go up the ladder only to slide right back down; and the jungle gym… that was just a free-for-all itself. At the jungle gym, a small child wearing a blue t-shirt with white shorts was trying to jump up and reach the bar above him, but with not much luck. “Mmph,” he grunted as he pushed himself off the ground, his short twiggy legs only taking him so far, yet not close enough to attain his goal. “Mmph, mmph, mmph!” Attempt after attempt, but each did nothing but to tire out the little boy. Finally giving in to his defeat, he crouched down on the ground, hugging his knees close to his chest, and started crying softly to himself. Such a thing was sensitive to a child, and could latch onto his mind and follow him into adulthood. “Hey.” The boy looked up with watery eyes to see a girl about the same age standing on the other side of the monkey bars. She was in a pair of jean overalls with a pink shirt underneath and matching shoes, her hair pulled back into two youthful pigtails. She climbed over the first bar and walked over to the boy. “What’s wrong?” she asked innocently. The boy stood up, wiping his face of the tears. When he stood up the girl still had an inch or two over him, but those sorts of things didn’t concern children at that point. Give it a few years… The boy didn’t answer, but just looked down at his white sneakers. The girl stared at him before looking up at the ceiling of steel bars weaving their way around them. A clever girl, she put two and two together… and hugged the child. Taken off guard, the boy didn’t know what was going on, and before he knew it, he was off his feet. He looked up, reaching up with his hand to find the bar in reach. Looking back down, the girl put on a smile as she strained to hold the boy up. “Grab on!” Seeing her struggling to keep him high, he grabbed on as quick as he could. With both hands firmly grasping the bar, she released him only to grab a bar herself and climb up to the top. The boy pulled himself on top of the jungle gym as well, and the two of them sat there smiling at each other. He bowed his head at her slightly. “Thanks.” He muttered. “I’m Yuna! What’s your name?” “Hyotaru.” And that… was the origin… of Ikari Yuna and Shiganori Hyotaru, AKA Chibi. -=-=-=-=-=-=-=- Ever since that day, I’ve been there for you. I’ve been your best friend. When everyone else teased you, I was the one that stuck up for you. I helped you out of jams when bullies took advantage of you. I shared my lunch with you when your lunch was taken from you. I let you copy my homework when yours was ripped right in front of your face. I’ve been there every single day for you… and I know you would do the same for me. Ikari was supposed to be going right home after her walk earlier that day, but her legs just wouldn’t take her in the right direction. She had been wandering around the city for half the day now, both in mind and body. everything had become so complicated now in high school. Emotions were on high, and there were situations that brought about confusion in the tiny little brains of pubescent teens. Chemical imbalances, hormones, and a lack of understanding made everything more difficult than it actually was, and Ikari didn’t know if she really was bothered by the events happening around her… or if she was just going through ‘the change’. Why is everything happening like this? Why are you acting like this? Everything was fine before high school… before SHE came into your life… and ruined mines. Why would you do that? What would make you do such a thing? Are you mad at me? Still? She couldn’t count the amount of people she’d passed during her long walk. She didn’t care enough to stop at the red glare of crosswalks, but she found that her feet would hit the brakes just at the edge, where a car would come speeding by with inches to spare. If she bumped into someone, she didn’t think to stop and apologize. She was running on autopilot in a way, Ikari wanted to know why… and the why itself was almost consuming her very being whole. I can’t bear to look at you right now. I feel betrayed… every time I see you I can’t help but remember what you did, and that makes my stomach churn. But… half of me wishes that it was me you wrote that letter to… not her. I can’t believe you wrote it to HER… after everything we’ve been through, you would pull such a stunt with a girl like HER. I can’t believe it… I won’t believe it… and I won’t allow it. Does he relish the abuse that he takes from others? Has he become a sadist? Is he so much in love with the taunting and the bullying that he would fall for a bully himself? I can’t understand it at all… If only I had the power to do something about it… but I can’t. I’m nothing but a coward myself… just like him… Ikari finally knew where her feet were taking her. She pushed herself up onto the top tier, and sat at the top of the monkey bars with her legs crossed, hanging over the edge. It was the same park where she had met Chibi… She sat there staring at the sky with melancholy masking her face. The whole ordeal had left her drained completely. And so in defeat, she gave up and stretched back on the top of the jungle gym. Her head hung lifelessly as the bar underneath her supported her neck, and as her vantage point gave her a distorted view of the world, she saw one thing right side up. Chibi was standing right behind her, staring right into her eyes. -=-=-=-=-=-=-=- So much for the sun… Clouds had muscled their way into the sky, but the light pushed through turning the black clouds a silvery gray. Rain fell soon thereafter, giving all the headstones a nice shine. Glossy granite is a pretty thing, even though they’re monuments of mortis. One headstone was protected though. It was almost as if the power of Hibiki Jue’s vanity exceeded the grave. A clear plastic umbrella was set over her grave, so that the rain did not ruin her tombstone and her portrait. How supernatural things were turning for Jue. In death she still had people fawning over her, bowing to her every wish. It would turn too much into a problem… the living holding attachments to the dead so dearly. It would cause problems in the end possibly. Despite the bad weather, it seemed that Hibiki’s grave still drew visitors. This one stood before the grave wearing a black windbreaker jacket with the hood pulled up over her head to keep the rain out. She wore black shorts and black sneakers, drops of water running down her smooth toned legs. She stared down at the grave, gripping the strap of her gym bag tightly. She had spent enough time there, because she bowed her head and walked off, squishing through the grass back to the stone path leading out the graveyard. As she approached, she noticed someone standing at the gates of the yard… and she could’ve sworn it was… The person took off, and that caused the other girl to give chase. -=-=-=-=-=-=-=- “Are you…” she was having trouble getting the words out at the moment. “Are you… still mad at me?” Chibi braced his arms against two bars as he leaned back. “About that? Nah… I forgave you for that a long time ago.” Ikari’s face seemed to relax. “I’m glad. I thought that after all this time, you still blamed me for your nickname… you know I never meant for it to be anything bad.” “Yeah, I know. I don’t really care when you call me that.” He admitted. “It’s just when everyone else calls me that to tease me. I get so used to the teasing version that I can’t tell when people are just going with the flow or making fun of me.” “But… what were you thinking?” Ikari looked at Chibi with quizzical eyes. “Why… why Kirima Nagi? Of all the girls in school, you pick the First and Last??” With a slight smirk Chibi shrugged his shoulders and sighed. “She’s not really all that bad, if you know her a bit. She gets a bad rap, but really she’s a good person.” Ikari shook her head, folding her arms as she leaved forward and swung her crossed legs. “I’m sorry, but I can’t seem to picture Kirima Nagi being a ‘good’ person.” “To be honest… she stood up for me once… against Saotome and some other boys…” Chibi’s eyes looked down at his knees, explaining his feelings. “Saotome was trying to pull something wrong, and I tried to stop him and he got the best of me. Then Nagi stepped in and took care of them… and there I was trying to save her, and instead she saves me. Kinda pathetic, isn’t it?” She looked over at him, finding an embarrassed look on his face. He glanced back up at the cloudy sky and thought a bit more about things. “But after she had beaten them up, I saw something in her that made me admire her, something that made me fall for her a bit. No one had stuck up for me before except for you, and now that I think about it… what I saw in Nagi then… was you, Ikari.” He looked up and smiled at Ikari softly. Ikari was stunned, and she couldn’t do anything but stare back at him speechless and listen to him. “You’ve been my hero since I was a kid. You were the one helping me all the time, sticking up for me, looking out for me because everyone made fun of me. You had my back since then… and when Nagi stuck up for me it reminded me of all the times that you helped me.” “But why did you fall for her?” she just couldn’t understand that part of things. Even he didn’t, a shrug coming to his shoulders. “I guess… well… if someone like Nagi was willing to stick her neck out for me, you would think she had a soft spot for you or something. I mean… Nagi hates everyone. But if I knew that you liked me… maybe I wouldn’t have fallen for her after all.” “Wait a minute… you’re not blaming ME are you??” Chibi’s finger pointed at her face with a cocky smile. “If you didn’t keep it to yourself we wouldn’t be in this situation, now would we Ikari? See… we’re both at fault here.” Ikari smiled, laughing slightly as she leaned back on the monkey bars again. “Yeah… I guess so. Sorry.” “Yeah, I’m sorry too.” Chibi leaned back on the monkey bars next to her and looked at her. “So are we friends again?” She looked to her left into his eyes, and nodded. “That, and then some.” Ikari reached over and took his hand, squeezing it tightly. He smiled, looking back up at the cloudy sky again. “We better get inside… it looks like rain.” -=-=-=-=-=-=-=- The girl was still on hot pursuit as it rained. The shadowy figure was bolting out ahead of her, making it hard for her to catch up. She almost had her when she had dashed across the street. The girl would’ve caught up to her, if not for the oncoming car pulling up just when she stepped into the street. The car hit the brakes, but she didn’t. jumping up, she slid across the slick hood of the car and landed on her feet. She would’ve kept going too, if not for the man stepping out of the car calling her name. “Ishikawa!!” She hit the brakes and turned around, looking face to face with her teacher. “Sensei?” Sato stood gripping the edge of his door looking at the girl as if she were crazy. “What’s gotten into you, Ishikawa? Trying to kill yourself now?” The soccer girl shook her head, bowing to him. “Sorry, sensei. I was just chasing after someone… I thought it was…” She drifted off when she saw that he wasn’t alone in the car. Tachibana was over in the passenger’s seat, looking at her with surprised eyes. Sato glanced back in at her before looking at Ishikawa. “Try and be more careful, Ishikawa.” He warned with a soft smile. “We don’t need you going off to meet Hibiki Jue in the afterlife now.” Hearing her name, the look on her face turned sad, but she wiped it off quickly as she nodded to Sato. “Yes sir. Sorry again.” Turning, she dashed off, hoping that she would find some trace of the person’s trail she was chasing. If she suspicions were right… then it would have to be… -=-=-=-=-=-=-=- “Chibi… I almost did something horrible…” The newly formed pair were huddled under the same umbrella walking home. Chibi looked at her, holding the umbrella high so it would cover them both, not to mention hit the taller Ikari in the head. His arm was getting tired, but he wasn’t about to admit it. “Oh? What’s that?” “Well,” she began, “I almost did something rash… because of what you did with Nagi… I just couldn’t bear to see her win your affections over me… so I went to the place you told me about, the place where Nagi drops by every so often.” “What, were you gonna fight her?” Chibi smirked. She shook her head. “Nope. I was going to kill her.” Chibi stopped dead in his tracks, staring at her with wide eyes. Ikari turned around, stepping out from under the umbrella slightly. “You’re kidding, right?” Her eyes went to the ground as she shook her head. “No… I had a box cutter in my hands… and I was in the bushes ready to jump out at her…” “Why… would you even think of doing something like that?” Chibi asked her, the tone of voice clearly upset with her. She looked up with apologetic eyes, starting to water up as the rain placed a few tears of its own on her face. “I’m sorry! I just didn’t… I had no idea what I was going to do. At one point it was all I was thinking about, and on the other hand I just couldn’t bring myself to do it. But I… I saw something while I was in the bushes…” Nagi… Chibi just remembered that Nagi was out of school today. Dropping his umbrella, he grabbed Ikari by her shoulders tightly. “Ikari… did something happen to Nagi?” Ikari stared at him grief stricken. “Today… I went to Hibiki’s grave. I kinda envied her because she was so beautiful. People still talk about how pretty a girl she was. I wish I could have been as pretty as her… then you could’ve liked me a lot sooner before anything rash happened.” “Did you do something??” Chibi asked again, this time rougher, shaking her a bit. “No!” she shook her head quickly. “I… I was just thinking. I heard that if you envy the dead, the dead comes back for you, to make you just like them… dead.” Chibi wasn’t following. “What’s that got to do with Nagi?” The point was coming up. “My memory might be playing tricks on me, but I could’ve sworn that the girl that tackled Nagi into the fountain… was… no, it couldn’t have been…” -=-=-=-=-=-=-=- Ishikawa couldn’t believe it herself. There she was, standing right before her, just as beautiful as she was the last time she saw her. “Jue…” She was walking slowly along the stone path leading back to her grave. Ishikawa knew the girl’s style personally; tight blue jeans with a powder blue baby T halter top, her midriff exposed with matching white and powder blue sneakers. Her hair was pulled back in a ponytail, but the figure was definitely that of Jue’s. The perfume was distinct, if not for an extra air of something Ishikawa couldn’t recognize. Just then, Jue stopped. She had caught eye of something just ahead. Whatever it was, it brought a shriek out of her and caused her to go running. Ishikawa followed, and found Jue’s grave dug up. “No… no… no…” Ishikawa heard Jue whimper as her hands slowly went to her head. “My grave… why? Who would do such a thing??” Ishikawa noticed a leather jacket draped over Jue’s headstone, the umbrella and the portrait knocked to the side. Just then a gloved hand came up from the empty grave. Jue watched with angry eyes, Ishikawa closing in slowly from behind. Slowly, the digger climbed out of the grave. “It had to rain now, didn’t it?” Nagi grumbled to herself as she stepped out of the hole. “Just my damn luck.” Standing up to her full height, she tried brushing off the mud from her jeans, but gave up after a while. Sighing, she shook her head as a dog would shake its fur, flicking droplets of water in all directions. Jue’s finger pointed, slicing through the air like a whip. “You…” she hissed, seething with hate. Seeing the crowd she’d gathered, Nagi stabbed her shovel into the moist ground and gave her a short nod. “Oi. Fancy seeing you here…” she nodded her head down in the direction of the grave, “… instead of there.” “I’m going to kill you!” she snapped, balling her fists up at her sides. “My grave… it was to be my SHRINE! And you desecrated it! You’ll pay for that!” “Jue!” Ishikawa finally made herself known to Jue. “It’s me!” “She’s not Hibiki, not anymore.” Nagi responded, stepping from the edge of the grave to take on Jue. “So beat it, ‘fore you get hurt.” Well you know, people aren’t always so obedient when they see someone they care about go against evil incarnate, or rumored evil incarnate, in the case of Kirima Nagi. So going on instinct, Ishikawa threw herself between Jue and Nagi. And in return Nagi threw Ishikawa right back out of her way. She grabbed Ishikawa by her collar and twisted around, throwing her right into the grave without a change of expression. She turned back around to see Jue come running at her screaming. Nagi hooked her arm under Jue’s arm and slung her around, throwing her straight for her own tombstone. Her head smashed right into the edge and her body flopped over into the grave, right on top of Ishikawa. The rain was washing away all the mud from Nagi’s hair and clothes as she stepped to the edge of the grave and looked in on the two girls. There was a gash across Jue’s forehead oozing with blood, and Ishikawa was out cold. Sighing, Nagi grabbed the shovel and started pushing dirt into the grave. “Two for the price of one…” -=-=-=-=-=-=-=- Ishikawa woke up muddy and bloody later that night. Fortunately for her, she was also lucky not have been buried alive. She sat up slowly, groaning as she held the back of her head. She glanced up to see Kirima Nagi leaning against a tree looking up at the starry sky. The rain had passed, and everything seemed to be back to normal… at least for that city it was. “What… happened… to Jue?” was the first thing Ishikawa asked Nagi as she tried to stand. “I put her back to bed, where all good little girls belong,” Nagi muttered as she kept her eyes on the sky. Ishikawa looked down, and she found herself standing on the filled grave of her friend. Defeated, she dropped down to her knees with her head hanging. “No… not again…” “You’re welcome, by the way.” Nagi responded nonchalantly. “I could’ve just left you down there with your little girlfriend… but you didn’t know any better. You were just being an idiot.” “I’ll never forgive you for this,” Ishikawa looked up with tears streaming down her face. “Do you hear me? I’ll repay you for this… someday.” “Fine by me.” she pushed herself away from the tree, walking away with her hands stuffed into the pockets of her leather jacket. “Just don’t get in my way again… or else I’ll put you in one of those graves for good.” Nagi left Ishikawa crying on Jue’s grave. She assumed that she wouldn’t dig the girl back up… and she was right. Everyone had assumed she was dead… but they still reverenced her as if she were still alive. Such a strong connection between the world of the living and the dead is enough to bring the dead back to life, but with dramatic changes. Of course… after that night… everyone still assumed she was finally dead... -=-=-=-=-=-=-=- “Good morning Nagi---whoa…” The look she gave Chibi was like a heart attack, and almost as deadly. He stopped in his tracks as she stared at him with those sunken, dark eyes. She was packing a full set of luggage under her eyes, and the expression on her face screamed to be left alone. But Chibi wouldn’t be the first to disturb Nagi… and he definitely wouldn’t be the last. “Geez, what happened to you? You look dead.” She was leaning over the railing on the bridge when she looked at him tired. “Long night…” She went back to looking over the overpass, watching the students on the sidewalks heading to school. Chibi cocked his head to the side before joining her, resting his forearms against the railing. “You got something to say, little man?” Nagi said as she chewed on some gum. Chibi looked at her and blinked bewildered before shaking his head. “Uh, no, not any more.” He looked back down to the street and exhaled deeply. “Sorry about the whole, crush on you and everything.” “Whatever. They’re your feelings. You can do whatever you damn well want with them.” Chibi watched as Kawada and her friends emerged from under the bridge. As usual, Jinnai was jawing off about girls, and Kawaii was listening as if he were an astute college lecturer. Kawada ignored his ramblings as she always did, thoughts of the day’s lesson and the previous night’s work in her head. But Ishikawa… there was only one thing on her mind. She glanced over her shoulder, scowling in the direction of Kirima Nagi. Chibi followed the icy stare from the soccer girl’s eyes right into the eyes of the First and Last, and his jaw dropped, stunned as if she had just punched Nagi right in the face. “What’s that all about?” Nagi closed her eyes, huffing as she turned around and leaned back against the railing. “Beats me. Chicks… ya know?” At first Chibi didn’t understand, but he decided to go along with it, grinning with a nod. “Yeah… chicks.” “Oh Hyo-taru!!” Hearing her call, Chibi found Ikari walking up to them. With her school bag slung over her shoulder, she smiled as she gave her friend a short bow. “Good morning! And how are you?” The chipper act seemed to die down a bit when she noticed Nagi standing there next to him. Her smile lingered for a moment before it began to dissipate. Chibi looked from her to Nagi and smiled, rubbing the back of his head. Caught in an awkward bind, he picked out the nearest person on that bridge to help break up the tension. “Ahh… uhh… say, good morning, Juri!!” Juri Hyobanshi stopped in her tracks, looking at the three of them with a blank expression. Then she bowed to Chibi, a polite smile on her face. “Morning,” she responded softly. “Nagi… did you do your homework from yesterday?” She blew past her lips, rolling her eyes with a slight smirk. “Nah, I was out sick. I was gonna see if I could copy yours.” Juri shook her head. “I’d love to help, but I wasn’t here either yesterday.” Chibi’s eyes widened as he turned towards her. Hearing her response, Nagi sighed, pushing herself off the railing and walking off. “Shit. Thanks a lot, kid,” and with a slight wave she stormed off. Juri watched her walk off; in the meantime Chibi was still thinking. In all that confusion, no one noticed Juri was missing. What was her reason for being out? Ikari took Chibi by the arm. “Come along, darling.” Arm in arm, Chibi was dragged towards school with his girlfriend… at least, she was somewhat of a girlfriend. And Juri Hyobanshi? Well… she just stood there with a smile on her face. That smile was as friendly as they came though, but it was her eyes… those narrowing eyes glaring with malice… they gave everything away.
