Prelude

1 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Aug 1, 2006 Spinning endlessly with figures of white linoleum passing by, I stumbled into a small area, struggling to keep myself from slamming into the walls that surrounded me, among other solid, stationary objects that I would observe in due time. I looked to my left, or what I thought to be my left, and countered by snapping in the opposite direction, unsure of my intent. I settled on focusing in front of me, feeling tunnelled by the walls that I was imprisoned by. I noticed an oval-like object in front of me, as tall as my shins, and sunk to my knees. As my uncontrolled arms cradled the circular object, I noticed it contained an opening, wide enough and just deep enough for me to plunge my head into the small pool of water settled at the bottom. Darkness. Not the kind of darkness you experience in a shabby downtown venue holding that week’s death metal band; not the kind of darkness wandering down a back alley; I felt like I was turning through a swirling abyss of white stars spattered across a black backdrop … in three dimensions, of course. Space? Maybe. It had more of a galactic feeling, like watching Star Trek in the middle of a drowsy Saturday night. The only thing missing from the scenario to fit a geek’s wet dream in a sci-fi was Darth Vader. WHAM! I slammed into a hard surface that was surely the ground. Surprisingly conscious, I opened my eyes and pressed my palms against the cold, marble surface, which felt rather moist. I felt almost too weak to push myself to my feet, so I rolled over and sprawled across the floor on my back. It was rather comfortable with my arms and legs spread, although I was breathing heavily and my cotton shirt was plastered against my skin from sweat, similar to the lick of hair across my forehead. My succession of breaths slowed considerably, and I saw that as an opportunity to glance around. The marble floor was surrounded by gleaming white walls of what looked to be the same material, although my depth perception failed me as to how far away they were. It was rather deceiving, and that didn’t comfort me at all, which is ironic, because white marble is usually a very soothing combination. “Quite the place, isn’t it?” I sat up at the sound of this mysterious voice, and scrambled to my feet, circling stupidly as hoping to find the source. There stood a man, or something like one. He couldn’t have been more than five feet tall, which made my height - still short of six feet - look tall. “Where the hell am I? Who the hell are you? Why am I here? How-” I stammered uncontrollably, very unsure of myself. The man stepped towards me slowly, almost suspenseful. “So many questions, Cedric. Too many.” “Yeah, and not enough answers,” I shot back. A smile cracked across his face. “You always were a quick one, Mr. Riley.” I frowned. “And how do you know my name?” He sighed. “Cedric, there’s probably more I know about you than you know about yourself.” I didn’t even know how to respond to that. Instead I stood there indecisively, tempted to knock the man out, and my blank face, intent eyes, and aggressive posture showed that clearly. The way the vertically inept man observed me with light, content interest and a gleam in his eyes gave me the impression that he saw right through my clever disguise. Beyond the intense attitude I presented I felt anxious and almost frightened at the overwhelming sense of power that this man held over me; this situation caused questions to swirl through my mind, never failing to come to a dead end. Hell, I couldn’t even begin to satisfy my own curiosity … however, something told me that this four foot robed dwarf could fill me in on the details. “I have questions …” I said slowly and rather stupidly, spitting out each word more unsure than it’s predecessor. “And I want answers.” There was a pause. I expected him to say something - to interrupt my pitch, but he merely gave me that look of triumph and calamity that angered me even more, and if possible, caused me to fumble any message I was trying to get across. “And you’re going to give them to me,” I stated firmly, rather proud of my ability to cover my real thoughts up. “Of course you have questions, Cedric,” the man began, “And of course you want to know the answers. You’re a curious man; you are mentally unable to be unaware, and as far as that pretentiousness has got you, it’s become quite the problem …” “Me? Pretentious?” I shot back at him unbelievingly. “You see, Mr. Riley, the mind is a powerful thing, and I hope you know that …” “What do you take me for, -” I began, but halted, unknowing of his name. “What am I supposed to call you, anyway?” “Call me Tom … but as I was saying … the mind is a powerful thing. You are one of the few in this world who has managed to exercise control over that weapon, and it seems that ability has trickled through your fingers.” He waited as the moment dragged on longer than expected, as if he anticipated a reply. He seemingly changed his mind. “Don’t respond to that.” “I wasn’t planning on it.” “Excellent, then we can move on …” he began. “What?” I butted in. “Moving on to what, exactly? I have a match to wrestle in a day, and I’m in la la land talking to a dwarf named Tom who seems to think he has control of the universe,” I shot at him. Tom didn’t react, he merely ran his fingers through his scrubby beard. “This is exactly what I was afraid of …” he said softly, almost to himself, or another person who wasn’t there. “You heard me … or did I stutter?” he said confidently, the sarcasm dripping from his lips, not something I’d expect from a man with his personality. “I’ll show you strong, you son of a-” I said through clenched teeth, as I move swiftly towards him with aggressive intent. I drew my fist back, but before I could even begin my forward swing I was blasted with a palm right into my chest. The pain was immediately excruciating, taking my breath away … but that was the best of it. I was set backwards, as if sailing through mid air, and slammed into the wall. The impact on my back allowed every gasp of oxygen to escape through my mouth, and, in combination with the palm-thrust, left me winded to the nines. I hit the ground hard, giving my tailbone a good smack, and crumpled forward with my arms crossed against my chest, gasping for air - the room seemed to lack oxygen as well. My eyes felt as if they were circling through my sockets freely, and as a result, blackness and white stars clouded my vision. My head felt as if it had just been caved in with a baseball bat. My entire body was screaming at me, and I could hardly move a muscle save for the rocking back and forth to keep me occupied, fighting back tears. And just as the pain seemed it couldn’t get any worst … it was gone. I collapsed onto my chest with my arms and legs spread. There was no remainder of what had just happened - no stiffness, after-effect, headache, soreness, bruise, or blood. I had been beaten lesser and still had the scars to show for it, and felt odd as I opened my eyes and climbed to my feet. I surveyed the room - the experience was frighteningly similar to my entrance. And there was Tom. He looked at me, expressionless … if anything, he seemed rather content, and acted as if nothing had just happened. “Consider that your first lesson,” he claimed, moving towards me. I backed away uncertainly; not daring to take my eyes off of the mysterious man in front of me. The man who was older than me, shorter than me, and certainly in lesser shape then me. I felt hopeless in his presence. I had been outsmarted, beaten, and punished by someone who I thought lesser than me in fighting - and it was all in the same breath. Unfortunately for me, it was rather the lack of breath that made me so cautious. “What is this, a test?” I asked, still alert. “Not necessarily,” he responded. He added a sigh, then continued, “Sean …” “Wait,” I interrupted. “How do you know my real name?” Tom looked at me with a disappointing look, then explained, “I thought you were a little better at listening, Mr. Riley - or should I say, Mr. Aspinall. I told you I probably know more about you than you do yourself.” “I …” “Clearly you did not believe me - another weakness. Hopefully we will cure that in due time, for now, you need to focus on your first lesson.” “WHAT DON’T YOU GET ABOUT ME NOT HAVING TIME FOR SOME STUPID LESSON!?” “Now now, Cedric - we went through this once before. You ended up sucking wind and helpless on the floor. Let’s try and avoid a similar scenario, shall we?” “Fine,” I muttered. “Besides … this world, Cedric, this is not like the one that you are accustomed to. We control time, so as far as you can concern yourself, time is not a factor opposed to the 24 hour clock that you schedule your life around. Now your first lesson will consist of observation and will also have a practical element that you will have to bend your mind around to decipher …” “This is ridiculous,” I said, running my hand through my hair, as if my patience were running out. “Enough. You need to be patient with this lesson, Mr. Riley, something that you also need to work on … but in due time, in due time. For now, what you are capable of will do. Or, for your sake, I hope so,” Tom said, turning away from me and walking towards a wall. I thought about that. “And what the hell is that supposed to mean?” “What is that, your quote or something?” he asked casually, awkwardly running his hand across the wall. “That’s a good one,” I said, then under my breath I added, “Smartass.” “That’s your role in this relationship … more than likely any relationship you’re in.” I couldn’t help but agree, and shut up before I dug myself a hole too deep. Tom finally found a spot on the wall that I figured was important, and he stepped back. “See … you’re learning already,” he commented without looking at me. He focused on the wall and drew out a large box with his finger that created some sort of dark opening. … I didn’t ask questions. Tom motioned me towards the black square. I walked slowly towards it, stopped in front of it for effect. “Your first lesson will be on loyalty.” I walked in. BANG! I opened my eyes abruptly to what sounded like a door slamming. The resulting noise seemed to penetrate my ear drums and ring throughout my head, upsetting the headache that I already had. I felt dizzy, nauseous, and like I could barely stand up … but I was in control. I looked around and realized I was face-down on a bathroom floor, my head propped against the bottom of a toilet. I tried to remember why or how I had ended up there, but my memory of the past few hours failed me like trying to keep water in cupped hands. I saw a blur of a face lean over me. “Tom?” I heard escape from my lips without even thinking it. “No, Mr. Riley … my name is Dr. Crosher, and I need to get you into the emergency room,” said a slow female voice. I snapped back to my senses - I had to wrestle tomorrow. “No … I … I have to wrestle tomorrow,” I stuttered. “We’ll see,” she said. “Let’s get you out of this bathroom.” “Okay.” And I blacked out. Again. All that ran through my mind was … Who the fuck is Tom?
