Routines: An Intro

1 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/1/06 A routine of a man, of his entire entirety of living, is essentially the driving force of the being. To live, we need routine, and for a routine to live, it needs us. This basic concept is regurgitated again and again throughout history, but it still remains true. The routine is like a basic life force in this, something that we find similarity in. So, with an introduction as forceful and exhilarating as that (cue sarcastic shifting of eyes), it should come as no surprise to find me drifting along the roads of South Boston, cigarette in hand, staring off into the distance at nothing in particular. The fact that I am smoking or that I am blankly in a daze is not the routine, rather just a character habit of mine. The routine is the time, precisely exactly almost certainly without a doubt 9:16 a.m. While the time may bend a minute or two, the change is never drastic. Today I must have been moving at a leisurely pace, as I am a minute behind my intended schedule. Nevertheless, I am on time, and the on timeness of the entire situation is what is important to routine. With every routine comes a place as well. My train of thought wanted me to incorporate an action into this explanation of steps, but I held off, as the action is the most important part. The smoking is not the action, nor is the staring; again, those are character traits, ones that are committed many times through out the day, therefore not really making it a routine. Rather, the fact that I am outside of the corner bistro, about five feet away to be specific, is the location. And the location is important, imperative rather, to any routine. With location there is a definitive place you go, and therefore a route. Otherwise, it’s a cluster fuck of actions that could demolish the idea of a routine. Say you go to a Dunkin’ Donuts for coffee instead of the bistro. What happens then? Would you grab the Globe, as I do in the Bistro, and read up on the local happenings? Or would you high tail it out of there, totally ruining the flow of the entire day? The latter would probably be your choice, unless you were a wino or a crazy guy, because those are the only Larrys who fucking sit around in a D&D. (That’s Dunkin Donuts, not Dungeons & Dragons. The Boston accent makes for the natives of the land to retard the Dunkin into a slur of Dunk And Donuts, therefore making the incredible acronym.) Of course, hung over teenagers do as well, but that’s less Boston and more suburbs. Finally, there is the action. For most cases, especially in this one, it is easy to determine what the action will be if you are aware of the time and the location. If it was 11:30 at night and I was at a whore house, I don’t think you’d be thinking my routine would consist of buying coffee and watching the world go by in front of the paper. (to clarify, I don’t traverse, me trying to be smart right there, into the world of whores; I am a Lord’s man, no matter my prior reservations I’ve achieved on the subject, and therefore would never even step foot in a foul place like that.) In reverse, you wouldn’t find me in D&D pulling up the skirts of jezebels and ramming my schlong into pink holes. Some of this is common sense, but you never know with some people—an explanation is always worth is. So, today, outside of the Bistro, I embark on my daily routine of coffee and newspaper, sitting with my back to the window at the table in the corner, glancing up every once in a while to see the people of the place. As I step in, it’s like any other coffee place. I could bore you with the details, but the thing about the author is that he likes you to create your own image in the entire situation. He believes in writing that focuses on characters, and from the characters the reader can make his own situation. The Bistro could be fluttered in brown with highlighting reds, a real dark atmosphere with a smoking air drifting throughout the customers. Or it could be a tiny place with light creamy walls. How it’s like is not so important, but rather the routine of the people inside is what to be focused on. In terms of routine, I walk in and I stand in line. This isn’t a waiter/waitress establishment, a place where you can get scrambled eggs if you felt like it, you adventurous fool you. No, this is just coffee and pastries, stuff you can clean up yourself if you make a mess, served on plates of paper and cups of the same cloth. The simplicity is the elegance in that it’s not trying to impress anyone with presentation beyond the food. Sure, the pastries are works of art, but that’s the focus and that only. With coffee, it’s the same in that it’ll be the best cup you’ll ever have every time you drink it, but you’ll never find it in some ceramic bullshit, nothing like that. Just the basics. While in line I get a cup of this coffee, a large one, and pay the dollar ninety five happily. Expensive? Not in today’s world, a mother fucking deal, that’s for sure. After the coffee, my routine causes me to find the table, which is always empty. I don’t know how it happens, I guess it’s just luck. There is never some asshole from out of town who just sits there with his foreign wife, saying things like “Café? Café?” or “Puis j’acheter un croissant?” to the customers, unaware that this isn’t mother fucking France, this is mother fucking America. But, as previously state, those Larrys are never around. It’s just me on my seat, coffee on the table, paper in hand. The only two things that change in my routine are the paper and the people. With the paper, the change is so mighty fucking obvious that if I actually had to explain this one out to you, I might have to quit fucking writing, or rather Sam might have to quit, as this is a first person narrative, and then go assassinate some world leaders. Papers are daily…’nuff said. But the people…the people are the most interesting part. Because while there is change, the similarities in routines can get a little scary, too—how they act, what they order, the days they have exact change Take this fucking slob, for example. 9:25, about that time, the mother fucker walks in and stands in line. To whoever is closer to him, he’ll start to bitch, “A cup a fahkin’ cawfee, two dollahs?! Fahk that! I can make mah own fuh…” The Boston accent is thicker than molasses, streaming out of his mouth much faster than the product. He always has the same clothing on too, a type of plaid shirt and some other vetements. Real bullshit you know. But the fact that he’s here, every day, bitching about the same thing, the insanity. Then there’s that crazy bitch who always enters on her cell phone. Yappy McJap, screaming that asian bullshit. All she does is just fucking yell it into that phone, contradicting her elegant blue ensemble that she adorns oh-so-well. And when she orders her coffee, it’s incredible. “Uh, yes please, one tall mocha latte, thanks!” The English rolls off perfectly, you’d think she was on a talk show, but then only moments later… “ME SO WANG NUT BLAH BLAH BLAH!” It shrills. And every day, around 9:28, it shrills, echoing off the “mathah fahkin’” asshole from earlier. The patterns these people run, just streaming rather, come in and come out. While observing the world like this, you tend to sometimes turn the binoculars on yourself, and more often than not you’re a chameleon. And what scares me is the concept of me blending in, of me being one of those assholes. I see myself, the cigarette being put out, the walk in, the order of the coffee. I wonder if someone else sees me every morning and thinks “Who is that ‘roided out freak? Why the hell is he doing this? Why the fuck does he do that?” I mean, if it’s so easy for me to turn it the other way on a whim, who’s to say they can’t do the same? As I finished up my routine and went through my day, these thoughts stuck with. Am I really that caught into my routine that some Larry who happens to se me can build up histories based on the little he interacts—no, rather, the little he sees me interact with my world? Am I the puppet to his imagination, my strings being loosened and pulled to his pleasure until, in his mind, the artificial reality of my existence is obtained? And as I slid into bed with these thoughts, I didn’t come up with a solution, but I thought up almost a pledge to try and go by, maybe something that could change. Tomorrow the routine breaks. There’s a coffee shop on the Charles that I haven’t yet visited, but it seems fairly popular. One of those places where it’s never insanely busy, but there is always someone in there. I guess in the city that’s how it is for most places, but regardless, that’s how it was for this place especially. So at dawn of the next day I dragged myself out of bed…but I didn’t reach for that white tee, and I didn’t grab my levis, and I sure didn’t grab my work boots. For if I need to break a routine, the routine needs to be broken. And like any historical fact, a routine is an endless web. Sure, I couldn’t break EVERYTHING…but, if I got far enough, things would change enough for me. As I looked through my wardrobes, though, I noticed that, in fact, I didn’t really have much variety. White T’s, Levis jeans, some work boots, and not much else. I wasn’t going to be wearing my formal wear for coffee, and actually ended up just adorning the same clothing. Ok, maybe this part wasn’t meant to be breaking. But the rest! While I end up going along, I pull out the cigarette and light it, eventually losing myself into that deep gaze, the 1,000 mile stare that people serving life sentences become so apt at doing. For a moment, though, out of instinct of attempting to ruin routine, I felt like throwing the cigarette down as I snapped back into reality. But I stopped. Didn’t I say that character is not routine? How could I forget that character was its own being? Foolish me. But that’s when it scared me. That’s when I arrived at the same coffee shop. And like routine I walked in without thinking. The characterness of it all…it scared me. And yet I knew that the routine was not so easy to be broken. (OOC: I’m not going to kill myself writing to face a no-show. This RP is more of an introduction to a series I want to embark on)
