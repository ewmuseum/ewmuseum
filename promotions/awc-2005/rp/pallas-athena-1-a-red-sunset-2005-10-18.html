Pallas Athena 1: A Red Sunset

18 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/18/05 KOAN A monk asked, 'What is on my mind?' The master answered, 'Who is asking?' Monday, September 2nd, 1999 Nagano Mountains Japan The moon rose slowly in the sky and in its wake came the tiny points of pure starlight. Slowly the moon wiped away the reddish warmth of an autumn sunset. In the darkening garden the mists of the Nagano Mountains, where the hot springs near the base of the Shiga Kogen volcano gave the place a mysterious aura, began to gather, shrouding the forest and muting the sound of footsteps on the fallen leaves and branches. There were few places as beautiful as this in all of Japan. But what was once the House of Hamada was no more, and for the little girl huddled near the foot of the statue of Budha in the center of the small glade, she cared little for the beauty of her surroundings. Only that these were her mother's favorite place in the world, her haven. That is all that mattered. Megumi Hamada was grief striken and in shock. She had come here to possibly find some lingering presence of her mother's spirit, an echo of her mother's love. Less then seventeen years of age, Megumi could not comprehend how her mother had been killed at the hands of common criminals. But there could be no doubt. Her killers had confessed to the robbery and the murder and had already been sent to prison for life. It was just too much for Megumi to absorb. The young girl hugged her legs closer to her chest and let her forehead drop to her knees. She was weary of trying to make sense of it all. Nikkou was the only family she had ever had. And in the shadow of her mother's death came a second shock. The sudden appearence of Nikkou Hamada's other family. They hid behind veils and robes and never acknowledged her existence. A family without faces, they had never wanted her to be a part of their world. She was an outsider. And the one place where she could have called home, here on Nagano Mountain, had been burned down in a mysterious fire only one month before her mother's death. The pain was too real, and it was too much for her. Right after the funeral she had shed her black clothing and donned her street clothes. Simple jeans, a shirt, a leather jacket. She looked like any other teenage girl, except for the ancient medallion she wore around a platinum necklace. Megumi's hand strayed to her necklace, her only legacy from her mother, and her fingers absently traced the arcane symbols that ran along it's circular frame. The medallion felt like a part of her and her heritage. After the funeral her mother's relatives had hotly debated who should be the heir of the medallion. None of them could legally claim it, and none of them tried. She had earned it and none of them had ever stepped foot off the continent. It was a symbol of her mother's independence and free-spirit, and also her time spent in Mexico where she had trained as a wrestler for fifteen years. What they wanted from it she did not understand. In the end, they all left. She felt no more or less alone then she had before. 'Megumi Hamada? Excuse me, child. I am sorry to intrude upon you in your time of grief. But I must speak with you.' The words jolted Megumi back to the here and now. She sat upright and squinted in the moonlight at a short, well-muscled figire standing near the entrance of the garden. He stood there waiting for her permission to enter. She quickly recognized her guest. Her usual self-possesion evaporated in the face of her childhood idol. To meet with Tomoaki Shuri-ken in such a state of chaos and gloom. She scrambled to her feet and wiped her hands on her jeans. Tomoaki 'Tyger' Shuri-ken was a professional wrestler of the highest order. A true fighting spirit, a teacher, one of the best wrestlers in Japan. Renowned for his great fighting style and his ability to come back from incredible depths and brutal beatings, Tyger Shuriken was part sports superstar and part cult figure. Many stories abound of his abilities inside and outside the ring. Such stories would also explain how he could keep the 'Yakuza' at bay, the underworld mobsters that tried to wrap their tentacles around everything in Japan. His legacy for fighting Yakuza pressure and violence was well known in the present day era of Japanese puroresu. But what did this man want with to do with a half-gaijin orphan was beyond her imagination. ... to be continued ... Sunday, October 17th, 2005 Gramercy Hotel, New York United States Megumi Hamada combed her hair in the mirror, lost in thought. She studied each silk-like strand in a trance. This was all a dream to her and she could not shake the feeling that it would end with her awake back in Japan. Back to the way things were. Her life as a Hamada family member was all but forgotten, all but a memory now, except to her fans and to the pro wrestling world. But to her, it was the past and it lay buried very deep inside. It didn't matter anymore. Her mother was dead and she never knew her father. Tyger Shuri-ken had taken his place in he heart and mind. But leaving for America was a huge change. In Japan, women wrestlers were not allowed to socialize with men. They were not allowed to drink or smoke cigarettes, or heaven forbid, have sex. They worked hard in their dojo during the day and at night worked at the restaurant that kept the wrestling end of the business going. They had no lives outside that very small circle. However, joshi puroresu, or womens wrestling in Japan, was a dying breed. The hot period had come and gone. The big sell-outs and dome shows were a thing of the past. The business had collapsed due to changes in the industry, mixed martial arts, and over-saturation of the product. So the new land of opportunity was the United States where only very few people had ever seen this style of wrestling before. Megumi thought of everything she had left behind and about how big of a risk she was taking, with her life and that of her boyfriend, Tozen. Was it really all worth it? Was it really all worth it to keep the legacy of the Hamada name alive? 'What's wrong? You seem to be in a far away place,' asked Tozen, walking into the small hotel room with a bucket of ice. 'Nothing, nothing is wrong. I am just thinking.' 'What about?' She didn't want him to worry. These were her problems, her troubles. 'My match against Juri.' 'Well, what about it?' 'It's just so very important that I win. I don't want to dishonor you or my art. I don't want this girl to try and dishonor me like she did in our last match.' 'Don't be silly. You can't win every match. And nothing you do will dishonor me, little girl,' he smiled at her. 'And as far as that rough stuff goes, I've seen you in a few scraps with people like that. I have seen you wear the crimson mask with honor and still have you hand raised at the end of the night.' 'I have to win. Every match. I want to prove that I belong in AWC. I want to show that my ten years spent wrestling in Japan were not a waste. I want to bring honor to my people and to my country. I want to bring honor to my teacher. I want to bring honor back to the Hamada name.' Tozen's brow wrinkled. 'What for? They have spurned you, forgotten you. The only one doing anything for the Hamada name is yourself, no one else. ' Megumi fingered the medallion around her neck. She slowly got up, put down the brush on the dresser, and walked out to the veranada to watch the sun set. Tozen came out a second after her and kneeled on the railing beside her. 'Relax. Have fun. We are in America now. You don't have your teachers here to scold you, or to tell you what to do. You don't have those old hags regailing you with their tales of matches long forgotten from twenty years ago, when they sold out this arena and that. Now we are free from all of that. Together.' Megumi smiled and pointed at the red sunset in the sky. 'I am free, I know. And with you by my side. I couldn't be any happier. But give me just this little bit. Let me worry, just a little. Tomrrow night, I start my new life, my new career. For me, for both of us, this is the most important thing in life right now. I'm not nervous. I'm not afraid. It is just somehow ironic, don't you think. My second match in AWC against a woman from Japan who dishonored and disrespected me. New world, new life. Same old story.' 'And I just feel like I am about to make my first step toward... my ultimate... destiny.' ... end ...
