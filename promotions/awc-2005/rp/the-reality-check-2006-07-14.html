The Reality Check

14 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 14th July, 2006 Location: Wesley Reno’s Home - East Rutherford, NJ. Time: 9:45am The soothing smell of morning coffee in the kettle entered my nostrils as I booted up my brand new laptop for the first time. I had a feeling she would be online this morning, though she had teased on the phone otherwise. The screen came to life and my background of the simple text - “Wesley Reno – Future President of the United States of America” – made me feel good on this uneventful Saturday morning. I poured out a cup of coffee and began drinking it as my laptop finished booting up. I wondered why a machine of this quality would take this long to start up. Then, checking my watch, I realised there was no rush whatsoever. It was Saturday, the day of chilling out and reflecting on what in most cases, and especially for me, would have been a successful week. My AOL Instant Messenger account logged on automatically and my eyes caught sight of that beautiful screen name – LillyLumps298. The rush of caffeine the coffee supplied only made my hand shake even more as I clicked her name with the mouse. It was a strange feeling actually getting to know a girl properly. My life thus far had been predominantly consisted of my greatest love – politics. The fact that I now had time for the opposite sex meant either I had far too much time on my hands or I was making a conscious effort to quell my political fire. I dismissed the latter immediately and concentrated on the flashing icon on my screen. Renoman102: Hey Lilly, what’s up with you? LillyLumps298: Ah, Wes! Wats up? Thought you mite b busy r sumthin so I decided not 2 tlk 2 u. If it were anyone else, I would probably berate them for butchering the English language with all that text speak, but I made an exception for Lilly. She had this innate cuteness that one couldn’t ignore. I was compelled to go easy on her. Renoman102: Nope, I’m off today. Got this political debate at around 11am, though. I might head down there. The topic is “The public and politicians – their relationship. Discuss”. There was a long pause at that point, and I took a nervous sip of coffee as I contemplated Lilly’s reaction. She wasn’t too keen on my political career, but it was something I couldn’t escape. She was probably coming up with some political joke. LillyLumps298: Wel, it sounds ok, Wes. Go get ‘em! Renoman102: Thanks for your support, Lilly. What are your plans for today, anyway? LillyLumps298: The grls n I r goin 2 get a full makeover in Dolly’s. U wudnt b interestd, I take it? I almost spit my coffee out onto the expensive keyboard when that message popped up. I wouldn’t be seen at one of those all girl outings, unless I was under heavy sedation. Lilly had a habit of coming up with these random ideas, none of which I was very keen on. I decided to be a man and propose a date for tonight, something that I had never done before. Renoman102: Lilly, I don’t mean to sound too forward, but are you doing anything this evening? Again, a long, drawn-out pause. LillyLumps298: Hee, y do u ask? Renoman102: I’d like to ask you out for dinner. Just the two of us. I can make reservations for your favourite place. You know, that fish place… LillyLumps298: Barton’s. Renoman102: Yeah, that’s the name! So what do you say? A loud bang at the door distracted me from the online conversation I was having. Typical, I thought. I reluctantly detached myself from the laptop, checked myself in the mirror, and headed to the door. I opened it, and before I had the chance to say a thing, my personal trainer Phil Allen burst in, two large gym bags in tow. He handed me the heavier bag, and my arm also broke in half under the tremendous weight of it. Allen paraded into the sitting room as if it was his home and placed his gym bag down onto my leather sofa. “Watch the leather, Philly! Hey, what exactly are you doing here at this hour of the morning?” Phil Allen was a well-built man and today wore a white tank top with black tracksuit bottoms, both of which were covered with rings of sweat in various places, including the crotch. I averted my eyes quickly as Allen surveyed my living room. “It’s a nice pad you’ve got here, kid. Is that what you call it these days? Pads? I’m thirty years old, man, but I need to be filled in. This new generation beats the shit outta me!” In the background, I heard the “bleep” sound from my conversation with Lilly, meaning she was sending me messages. I rushed back to the computer. Allen pursued, and a look of confusion came across his face as he read her messages. LillyLumps298: Alrite, I’ll go wit u! C u there bout 8pm, Wes. LillyLumps298: Oops, gota jet. Nice chatin wit u. C ya! LillyLumps298 signed off at 10:01:37 That was enough for me. She had accepted the invitation, which meant I had a date tonight. The only thing dampening the mood right now was the arrival of Phil Allen. I remembered how we had arranged a training session for today at 10:30am. Allen looked like he had been working out for hours prior to his arrival, which made me question his mortality. Was he, in fact, a robot? “Stop staring at my sweat patches, Wes! You’ll have plenty later on and you can stare at your own. So are you ready to start your training to become a professional wrestler, eh?” There was enthusiasm in his voice, the likes of which I had never heard before. “Yeah, let me just, eh, take a piss,” I replied, already headed down the corridor to the toilet. “It’s actually better to say excrete urine, kid. You’ll never score with the ladies with lingo like that!” He was probably correct, but while I was in the comfort of my own home, I opted for the “don’t give a shit about what I say” attitude. It suited me down to the ground. Once I was out of sight, I knew this was the time to make my escape. If I wanted to get to that political debate, I had to leave now. Stealth was the key to my escape from my home without Allen noticing. I tiptoed into my bedroom, threw on a respectable looking jacket for the debate, and quietly slipped out the door in my bedroom which led out onto the patio. I was literally five meters from my BMW. Throwing caution to the wind, I dove into the vehicle and sped down the driveway. I was certain that Philly had heard this, but I hoped the old man’s hearing had taken a turn for the worse. What was I thinking? He was only thirty; it would be a matter of moments before he would notice my absence. He wouldn’t stop until he got me into a gym and made me work like I had never worked before. What had I gotten myself into? The BMW’s smooth engine hummed softly as I turned down Brook Avenue. The highway was in my sights, but I also felt a pang of guilt over leaving Allen behind. He did only want to train me to become a professional wrestler, after all. After considering this for a moment, I dismissed the thought. The chase was by far the more entertaining option. I drove for a couple of minutes on the highway, pondering my next move. The political debate on Clifton Avenue was my destination. An angry motorist across the way from me shouted slogans at his radio, obviously irate that it wasn’t properly transmitting his Spanish lessons. Quite sad, really. I rivaled his brand of educational CD’s with a bit of Led Zeppelin. The guy was by no means happy, and switched his attention to me. Watching his tubby cheeks wobbling as he spoke reminded me of a hippo, so I couldn’t help but laugh at both his chubby cheeks and his docile demeanour. I pulled in at the side of the road and caught a glimpse of the building where the weekly political debates I am so fond of take place. Its grey, dour façade, people believed, reflected the kind of pointless and bland issues discussed inside. The interior left something to be desired. The woman at reception stared blankly at me as I walked in, and I felt like giving myself the once over to ensure I had dressed properly. “I’m here for the, um, political debate. It’s room four, right?” I asked, wiping my jacket down as I did so. “Yeah, room four. Make yourself at home,” she replied in a hoarse voice, with absolutely no trace of sincerity. I made myself comfortable in room four amongst several other young men and women, all of whom were sifting through documents, newspapers and magazines. It impressed me that all of the relevant material pertaining to today’s topic was neatly laid out on a glass table in the middle of the room. There was also a spring water dispenser in the corner and several chocolate biscuits to keep people awake. Plenty of sugar would do the trick. I munched away on a biscuit or two. I felt sorry for Philly, who was most likely stranded at my house, still waiting for me in the toilet. Then I heard the faint sound of an engine coming to a halt outside, and everything changed… Phil Allen, now even sweatier than before, pulled into the building he knew Wesley Reno was hiding on Clifton Avenue. It wasn’t difficult for him to find, either. A polite gentleman with cheeks resembling those of a hippopotamus had kindly given him directions to the political debate taking place today. Fortunately, Allen had just endured a twenty-week Spanish course, and understood the man’s speech completely. Allen adjusted the straps of his tank top, wiped the sweat from his brow, and emerged from his vehicle, a ’96 Toyota. Granted, it was not a vehicle that would blow you away, but it got Allen from A to B, and sometimes even C aswell. Allen walked in a stealthy fashion to the reception desk and spotted the woman behind the desk. Middle-aged, and by no means attractive. She was in the middle of reading a magazine, most likely on some bland political issue, as Allen approached her. “Young lady…” he cooed almost seductively. The receptionist looked up at the monster of a man who was Phil Allen, slightly taken aback by his attire more than anything else. The receptionist cleared her throat and replied. “Y – yes sir, may I help you?” Allen was in business. Once he found Reno, who had so rudely pulled the wool over his eyes back at his home with the famous “toilet escape”, he would make him pay with hours upon hours of tough, in-ring activity. Allen had contemplated giving him to Terminator, a close associate of his who had a reputation for crushing a man’s spirit in five minutes of training. That would wipe the smile off his face. “Yes, I’m looking for this political debate. I’m aware I’m not wearing the right gear today, but I’ve just come the gym, you see, and my dedication to politics is such that I can’t afford to miss a single one of these. So please, Miss, allow me the pleasure of entering this thrilling debate.” Allen’s uncanny ability to be completely sarcastic and yet still appear genuine had worked like a charm again. The receptionist simply pointed to the door and Allen headed towards it, preparing to corner Reno. There would be no escape this time. I froze suddenly as Phil Allen entered the room through the double doors at the far end. At this stage, we had arranged our chairs in rows in order to face the screen at the top of the room. Allen stepped past the lecturer for today and found himself a seat. The presentation shown in the last couple of minutes was concerning just how aware the public are of the politicians around them. Allen purposely made grunting sounds and motioned with his hands that I was as good as dead. I gulped unconsciously out of fear. Running away from Philly was not the best idea. “Does anyone have any points to make on that?” the lecturer boomed. Allen shot up out of his seat like a little kid who badly had to pee during a class in school. He feigned enthusiasm so well; everyone in the room was fooled. “I do! Yes, I would like to make a point on that,” Allen squeaked. “Do you not think, sir, that politicians would be better off if they got a grip on life and simply concentrated on what is important. Instead of running away from their problems, why can’t they just confront them? That way, the public would trust them more.” Allen stared at me with intent throughout this monologue. I tried to convince the others around me that I had no relationship with this man, but by now it was obvious. Allen’s gaze tore through me like he would tear through me later today. “Anyone else have anything to add?” I stood up to make a point, more in defense of myself against my trainer than to actually add anything to this debate. “If I may, sir, I would like to say that perhaps if the public were easier on politicians in terms of what they demand, then politicians could not only function better to serve the public, but to serve themselves, too.” I shot Allen a pointed glance as I said these words. He knew what part he played in this debate. He represented the public, I represented the politicians. What Allen needed to accept was that I had better things to do with my time than train to be a professional wrestler. I knew the importance of getting training in, but I didn’t even have a match to wrestler next week as far as I was aware. Why would I need to train? Unless of course I did indeed have a match to wrestle. I cursed under my breath as I realised the seriousness of the situation. My cheeks went pale as I imagined myself in the ring with two buff men trying to mutilate me. Allen got my attention, pointed towards the door, and I gave up. I excused myself from the debate and left with the vile taste of submission in my mouth. Allen followed me, giving his two cents on politics as he shut the door behind him: “Just so you all know, politics is for low brow pussies who only want cash. Empty promises are your forte, ladies and gentlemen! Enjoy the life of politicians, anyway. Or can that even be done?” he remarked, his voice no doubt echoing in the minds of each and every person there. Allen grabbed me roughly outside the double doors of room four and pushed me up against a hard, marble wall. Our eyes locked; I knew what was coming. “Why oh why did you run away this morning, kid? Are you honestly trying to escape this after signing up? You signed a contract, Wes! You have to compete in AWC. Believe it or not, our first match is this week. Tuesday night, kid! We don’t have a lot of time to prepare!” Allen’s to-the-point remarks seemed to kick start my common sense once again. Wrestling was a tough business. He had been there before. He knew all the tricks of the trade, to use a cliché. I had to trust this man, because whether I liked it or not, he was the only way I would get into the wrestling business and hopefully take advantage of my fame there to enhance my political career. This was a highly important stepping stone for me on the way to becoming a world-renowned politician. “I’m sorry, Philly. You know me. I got cocky, I lost control. It was probably the coffee, you know,” I joked, but he wasn’t buying it. “End of the line now, Wes. We start training tonight. We’re not facing Tweedle Dum and Tweedle Dee, you know. It’s us, the Coalition, against the champions in a non-title match! This is just as big for you as it is for me. So let’s put this little episode aside and concentrate on what matters. Besides, politics sucks.” Allen smirked and began chuckling as I tried in vain to come up with a diplomatic response to his insult. “See, Wes, all you lack is that cutting edge. Stop trying to mince your words. In the wrestling business, it’s every man for himself. By the time I’m done with you, you’ll be wearing a leotard and oiled up so good – “ “Okay, okay, enough of the oil, Philly! You’re obsessed with that stuff, aren’t you? Where do we go now?” I wondered, as that trademark smile again crept across Allen’s face. Allen rubbed his hands together in glee and responded: “We’ve got a lot of work to do, kid. First stop, New York City. I’ll drive. I wouldn’t be seen dead in that piece of junk!” Incredulous with his criticism of the ultimate driving machine, the BMW, I retorted. “Um, excuse me? This is a BMW! This is – “ “Just get in the car, kid. I’ve got “Eye of the Tiger” to listen to.” Allen said it as if it was a good thing. I, on the other hand, despised the track. At any rate, our journey had begun. First to the Big Apple, then to Europe to compete in what would be my first match. Phil Allen had seen it all before, but it was his first chance to redeem himself in the squared circle. The formula was perfect, could we provide the goods? All I knew was that we had a ton of work to do – and fast.
