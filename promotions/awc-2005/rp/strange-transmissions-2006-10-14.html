Strange Transmissions

14 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10.14.06 co-written with my good friend, my storied rival and my immensely talented muse, miss Katie C. thank you, and enjoy ------------------------------ I’d barely had my first legal drink before I met her for the first time. She was barely 18 herself, I think. She certainly didn’t look much older. I couldn’t believe Osbourne had the audacity to put his company’s most coveted prize around the svelte waist of a kid who looked like she belonged in high school, behind the desk getting chewed out for blowing off her English homework rather than taking planchas off the top rope. And around the waist of a GIRL, no less! A pretty waist, no doubt – but she definitely didn’t look like a “World’s Champion,” whether she’d earned the prestigious honor or not. Still, I felt obligated to go over and introduce myself. I think when I finally worked up the nerve to do it she was being fawned over by some of the male, overly-macho undercard guys. Wait, no…I think she had just finished bitching out one of the costume designers who made her a pink singlet as a joke. Christ, my memory is failing me. Amy loved being drooled over almost as much as she enjoyed ripping somebody a new asshole, and there were so many instances of both that it’s impossible to keep track of them all. What I do remember, though, is that our first meeting lasted all of about ten seconds. She gave me a half-hearted wave, with a complementary stare that seemed to ask right out loud just what I thought I was doing interrupting her. It was disappointing, to say the least, that I couldn’t even get the time of day out of her. But looking back, I can’t really blame her. After all, she probably got approached like that by every other hotshot rookie she’d ever run into, making themselves look like fools at the sight of a feisty, attractive redhead who went about the same line of work as they. Run of the mill office sexual harassment was bad enough, but throw in guys with huge muscles who spend most of their time on the road working for a company that by and large is degrading to women anyway, and you kind of get the picture. But still…there was something about her. Something that told me that she wasn’t just a cold, vain bitch, and there was something beneath that hardened exterior. It turns out I was wrong – she was a cold, vain bitch, with some other less than flattering adjectives you could throw in there too. But I was also right. There was more, much more to Amy Campbell than met the eye…and even back then I could tell. I would spend the next seven years finding out just how true my sentiments were. *** “Here’s the mail, it never fails…” As Darcy Markson strode towards his mailbox at the edge of his East Bay home’s front lawn, the theme for the mail segment from the hit kid’s TV program “Blue’s Clues” got stuck in his head for only the 79,459th consecutive time he went out to check his mailbox’s contents. However, unlike good ol’ Steve, who was so excited to receive some form of correspondence that he was willing to wail “MAAAAAAAAAAAILLLLLLL!!!” at the top of his lungs despite what his friends might think of him, Darcy was far less enthusiastic. The amount of pure crap he could expect to find awaiting him in his shiny metal box adorned with a precious little flag of course could never begin to approach the legion of sewage within his electronic mailbox, where he could expect to be presented with a daily regiment of at least a hundred offers to refinance his mortgage or (and usually infinitely more likely) to enlarge his most revered of appendages. Yet still, Darcy couldn’t help but scowl at just how many trees had to die so that he could receive all the letters that weren’t even addressed to him – rather, the space for the name read “CURRENT RESIDENT” or “VALUED CUSTOMER,” ensuring that the junk mail companies would be able to make his day just a little bit worse without even having to know his name. Nothing like anonymous pestering, Darcy thought to himself as he re-entered his house, turning to sift through the mail for anything he might actually want to read. In between every other aforementioned piece of junk were, of course, the old reliable bills – the other reason Darcy despised bothering to make the trip out to the mailbox. After one alternating credit card offer and statement after another, he had half a mind to toss the entire pile into his trusty paper shredder and be done with it when he caught a letter addressed to Amy. As far as Darcy was concerned, it was only a matter of time until Amy was both out of his house and life, once and for all. And while he could only see this as a good thing, one thing he was not looking forward to was all the mail intended for Amy that would continue to come his way until the good people at the U.S. Postal Service caught up with her change of address, and would redirect the mail to wherever the hell she ended up. And like Darcy, Amy received her own share of useless mail, and had echoed Darcy’s sentiments about the poor deceased foliage that made it possible for her to briefly hold such envelopes before sending them the way of the trash receptacle. Darcy would have done so on her behalf, but he could already feel the guilt trip coming on in destroying somebody else’s belongings, even before he’d completed the task. While it seemed unlikely that Amy would ever step foot in the house ever again, Darcy couldn’t bring himself to destroy her mail. (It should be noted that this isn’t a sign of any particular affection being shown to Amy by the man called Crisis – he also couldn’t bring himself to destroy the mail of one David T. Schneiders, the man who had previously owned Darcy’s house…not even a good six or seven months after the house had become his). Darcy tossed the letter onto his kitchen table, intending to find a better fate for the letter some point in the future. Yet just as the letter was in mid-flight toward its mahogany landing pad, yet another aspect of the envelope’s exterior caught Darcy’s eye. Taking a closer look, he saw that Amy’s name was printed in a tan-colored piece of paper that protruded through the transparent address slip, contrasting with the stark white that covered the rest of the envelope. If Darcy didn’t know any better, he could have sworn… A check, his mind finished for him. Darcy quickly scanned the return address, which read “JONES PLUMBING & AFFILIATES” in a tiny bold forest green. Already things were looking incredibly fishy; Darcy had had absolutely no trouble with his waterworks in the slightest. And even if he had, any bill to take care of the problem would have come in Darcy’s name, not Amy’s. Even though the two had been living under the same roof for some time, Darcy had staunchly refused any attempt of Amy’s to shift some of the financial burden onto her shoulders. Call it old-fashioned, or call it a safeguard that Darcy held to protect himself from getting sucked in too far into a co-dependent relationship, the fact remained that such a letter had no business being sent to Amy at his address. Suddenly finding his inner guilt quickly subsiding, Darcy tore open the envelope to investigate its contents. Tossing the now clearly unsealed and subsequently useless sheath to the wayside, Darcy’s earlier hunch was now confirmed. It was a check alright; but the amount was for far more than he ever would have guessed. “FIVE THOUSAND DOLLARS?!” Darcy said aloud, catching himself by surprise with the level of volume he’d unknowingly uttered. The mystery had taken yet another left turn, provoking far more questions than the answers provided. Amy Campbell was a full-time wrestler, gainfully employed by the Atlantic Wrestling Club. This did more than its fair share to keep her busy, and there was simply no way she had the time to even shill, let alone work for, some local no-name plumbing company. Darcy could easily attest to this; his own attempt at holding another job with the Tomorrow Institute while again taking on a full-time wrestling schedule had met with miserable failure, and consequently led to his getting canned. So that begged the question – exactly who was taking time out of their lives to throw an unexpected five grand Amy’s way, especially when there was no way in hell she had the time to do anything that could possibly have warranted it? Darcy quickly fetched his laptop from its resting place on his living room coffee table, intending to find out. One of the immensely valuable lessons Darcy had learned in his time with the Institute was how people went out doing business under the table. Or, as this case seemed to suggest, around the table. Because Amy had seemingly done nothing to warrant a check for five thousand dollars from a plumbing company, a far-fetched explanation suddenly seemed the most likely. “Either Jones Plumbing isn’t real…” Darcy said aloud as he threw the name into Google’s search engine. “…or they’re covering for somebody else.” *** ''Sick again?'' ''No, sick still.'' The last shred of any reality was quickly slipping between the fingers of Amy Campbell. She hated LA more than most people, and it didn't help that she was running a fever in the California heat. ''Tell me... how the hell do you get bronchitis in Southern California?'' Amy grunted, ''I mean, back home this isn't too out of the ordinary... but it's not exactly flu season out here!'' Her friend Ben, a curious little Korean bling jeweler, was with her. She'd been crashing on his couch since Darcy essentially came home and took over the week before and, though it was a fine couch, it wasn't exactly home... This isn't my idea of a relationship - not that I even have one to worry about anymore. I showed up at home last week and it seemed to be the one time Darcy wasn't avoiding me... but I was completely wrong on that note, he just hadn't thought I'd be there. Unlike him for the weeks before that, I'd had the balls to stay at home where I belonged instead of running off to find solace in other people and things. I was perfectly content with my kitty and our couch, until he came back and made my stay much less welcome. I think that's what always pissed me off the most about him - the fact that he doesn't know when it's time to put himself aside and play like a big boy. As much as he does and cares for me, he's equally selfish and whiney despite what I do or say. I regularly get bitched at for being 'selfish', like when I don't want to share a Pepsi that he just bought me... but he never looks at his own selfishness, and exactly how far it goes in comparison. I don't like people drinking after me, but he seems not to like people looking after him and remains oblivious to the important level of difference between those two tendencies. Darcy's complete departure from our relationship -- twice now -- only goes to show that he really and truly does NOT want to be in this. Sadly, I can't settle for that answer, because I need him. Yeah yeah, I'm being the pansy and saying it... but I do. I'm paranoid and jealous and all things insane, but the fact that he can drive me to that point (though it might not be too much of a journey) only goes to show exactly how deep his influence on me goes. As a result of Darcy's ego and his need for this ridiculous match, I've been sleeping on a leather sofa all week... if you can even call it 'sleeping'. I'm staying with a friend who works all day and parties all night, with rappers of all people. Not to stereotype, but I try to get rested and all I hear is how some guy's gonna get shot or stabbed or gang-beaten because so-and-so is so tough and you don't mess with his woman, blah blah blah. Talk about choosing your friends the wrong way. Of course, I'm not much better... I accidentally got Jane kidnapped when she seemed to be closing in on my territory a couple of months ago, and I guess that's how I'm in this whole mess in the first place. On top of it all, I've got bronchitis. Yeah, in LA. This is the second time in six years that I've had it, and both times were in California, just after being burned by Darcy. Maybe I should've learned my lesson the first time, but life back then was different without him... I could forget things in an easier, chemically-induced way. These days, I'm sober and very much awake for every agonizing second of loneliness. Not only that, I'm surrounded by a bunch of coked out assholes with guns and shiny necklaces. It's far from being a caring environment, but I remember it being a lot worse a few years ago. When Darcy first left me in... 2000, was it? Anyway, when he left me the first time I found a good friend I'd known. I was still staying with my Korean sidekick Ben on the rebound, but the friend I found was white. Yeah yeah, it's a cute introduction to cocaine. It was an October, too. The tenth month of the year has always been the worst for me, it seems like it's the beginning of hunting and dumping season. Now, the only drug I'm on is cough syrup and an antibiotic that'll kill the infection squeezing my lungs. Darcy thinks I don't know about his queer herbal tea, but he's transparent to someone who's seen and done as much as I have. His constant underestimation of me is sure to rub off on the match at Untouchable, because it's already had the power to put him completely in the dark when it comes to having a civil relationship. I honestly don't know how he HASN'T gotten my hints about the company pushing me to promote our relationship, but if I'm not dead by the end of Friday night I'll be letting him know and accepting whatever consequences go with it, whether it's unemployment or him calling me a liar for keeping things away in his best interests... again. Drama, drama, drama... Maybe I should take up smoking. *** “I dreamt about her again last night.” Darcy took a long puff from the tranquility pipe handed to him from “Bob,” his English-speaking ayahuasquero guide, upon the commencement of his seven words. Darcy didn’t particularly care for the taste of tobacco, nor was he a regular smoker by any stretch of the imagination. But here, deep in the recesses of the Brazilian rainforest, tobacco was regarded as sacred by the natives, capable of warding off evil spirits both during and after their mystical journeys. And as things stood, Darcy wasn’t so sure evil spirits weren’t indeed dogging him. “You mean…this woman from your past? The one that brought you here?” Bob inquired. He took the pipe from Darcy and took a lengthy hit of his own from the pipe. Whether Bob too was plagued by evil spirits or not was information Darcy was not privy to. “She’s not the reason I’m here!” Darcy snapped, almost without even realizing. “I mean…there were plenty of reasons I came here. I’d say an overwhelming distaste for Nosy Nancies who seem all too willing to jump to conclusions, for one.” Bob shook his head. “I know. It’s not important. Tell me about your dream.” *** “I don’t know if I can go through with this…” Darcy Crisis hopped from side to side, anxiously awaiting his entrance to the Six-Pack Challenge that awaited him in the evening’s main event. Five of Pier Six’s best would soon be doing their damnedest to pry his newly won heavyweight championship from his clutches. Aimz wasn’t going to be one of them…but according to the plan hatched by her and Darcy, she would definitely be gracing the main event with her presence. Or would she? “If you’re going to back out, now’s the time,” Darcy said, not even breaking his focus toward the arena to look at her. “I don’t need your help… and I’m sure there’s a more tactful way to go about solving your problem.” The ring announcer’s voice cut in as the other members in the bout were made known to the San Jose crowd. “The following is a SIX-PACK CHALLENGE, for the PSW CHAMPIONSHIP!” he bellowed. “Introducing first…at a COMBINED WEIGHT of 726 pounds, they are Kid X, “The Real Deal” Frank Roberts, and Adam Trimm…THE ELITE!” In theory, the match was supposed to be every man for himself. But with three of those said men belonging to PSW owner Eddie Osbourne’s band of merry henchmen, the Elite were playing their hand openly for everyone to see; and it was a hell of a hand indeed. Eddie’s words from earlier in the evening suddenly rung painfully though Darcy’s head loud enough to give the Liberty Bell a matching crack: “Unless he receives a miracle ordained by God, Darcy Crisis is going down tonight...” ''My problem is that Eddie's a prick and I want out,'' came a sigh from what Darcy hoped was his miracle. ''And tonight I'm gonna smack the shit out of him. I can't think of a better way to solve the problem, actually. It's just not exactly easy, I'm not sure you know the kind of reach he and his morons have...'' “So then, you do know you can go through with it?” Darcy asked, stretching his neck but still not looking at her. “Make up your mind.” The tiny redhead grabbed her nemesis by the arm, ''I don't think you get it, Markson - we're about to royally fuck up both our lives. I know mine personally can't get much worse, but you'd better think twice about the things you're putting out on the table here.'' “Their opponents…from Chicago, Illinois, weighing in at 199 pounds…AARON MARTY!” Finally, Darcy turned to look at her. He certainly didn’t appreciate having his manhood challenged any more than his space invaded before the biggest match of his short career thus far. “Eddie’s a jerk. And he’s tried to kill me besides. If I stick around here much longer, he’ll probably finish the job. If I leave with you and make a run for it…he might still get me, but at least I’ll have a running start. What do I have to lose?” ''If he thinks you've convinced me to leave, he'll probably think we're somehow dating... and then I guarantee he'll do nothing but go after you, running start or not.'' While Marty's music blasted, Amy took another moment of pause to consider how her life was about to alter. The man before her was worthy of moving mountains to stand next to, but he had no idea she thought that way of him. As far as Darcy seemed to be concerned, he was simply a convenient strongarm for Amy's great escape. There was no way he could know what was going on in her head each time she looked at him, and he surely felt in no way the same. Knowing that made her hesitate again, ''I don't care if my neck gets snapped out there, or if I'm shot in the parking lot. It's you I'm worried abo--'' She stopped entirely then. ''Err... I mean... well, you know. Don't want to ruin someone else and all... uhh... yep.'' “Hey.” Darcy now gently had a hold of her shoulder. Amy likely couldn’t hear his placid greeting, as his voice had been overtaken by the far thunderous announcement for the next competitor. “From the abyss…his weight is unknown, his name is AZRAEL!!!” It was difficult to tell with the combination of crowd and Azrael’s choice of bad rap-metal entrance music, but he could have sworn he heard Amy gasp the moment he touched her…almost as if her heart had just tried to leap out of her chest. Pretending not to notice, he uttered a solemn promise. “You got my back, I got your back,” he told her. “I’m not going to let anything happen to you. Got that? I went through the whole goddamned roster at PSW and still came out on top. I’ll do it again if I have to. But we’re gonna make it out of here. Of that much, I can definitely assure you.” Rather suddenly, Amy shivered and spoke, ''I can't believe I'm saying this, but I trust you. It's gonna be a hell of a fight, though... this isn't any one-off deal.'' “Speak for yourself, Toots” Darcy said, trying to sound as non-chalantly as he could. “I’ve got to go through…what, five guys who all want to beat my ass tonight? Six, if you count Eddie? All you have to do is level somebody with a chair shot and high-tail it.” Darcy puffed his cheeks and took a short, sharp exhale before shaking practically all of his joints loose one last time. To say he was nervous was as much of an understatement as saying that Pope John Paul’s hat wasn’t all that big. “It’s just about go-time,” Darcy said as he turned his gaze back to the curtain. “Shouldn’t you get back to Eddie before he figures out where you are right now?” Amy sighed, ''You're right. Just... be safe, y'know. Can't have you getting hurt out there, my legs are way too short to run the mile alone.'' Suddenly, the opening cryptic message of the Guano Apes’ “Open Your Eyes” overtook the arena, effectively putting an end to the conversation. And just like that, Darcy felt transformed – world champions don’t crack under the spotlight, he told himself. So you might as well stop pretending that was even an option. He was about to make either the smartest or the dumbest decision of his life – and only time would tell which it would turn out to be. But with a damsel in distress, a clear mission and stakes as high as they could possibly be…at the very least, it would probably make for a pretty good story someday. Ready for the cameras, Darcy turned to give his soon-to-be accomplice a suddenly-confident grin. “See you out there,” he said, with a wink, before bursting through the curtain with his PSW Title held high. When Darcy disappeared, a lighting technician turned to Aimz - who was staring blankly at the inky black curtain in front of her. ''You okay?'' She grinned, ''Fine. Just getting ready to completely ruin my life.'' *** “She was in this…this white dress,” Darcy began. “I don’t remember all that much of it. But her hair was flowing all around her. I don’t really dream in color…at least, I don’t think I do. But I could see the red, Bobby. But even so, he still couldn’t get over how real it actually felt. “I could feel the heat against my face, Bob,” he said to his friend and guide, with more than a hint of awe in his voice. Just as stoic as always, Bob didn’t so much as flinch upon hearing Darcy’s take on the haunting vision. After a beat, he queried Darcy further. “Was there something she wanted?” “She was…” The words suddenly became lodged in Darcy’s throat. As far as he was concerned, Amy Campbell was gone and out of his life for good, and it had been that way for some time. He was fine with that; completely comfortable with it, in fact. So in silence, Darcy pondered the question. He was happy here…at least, he thought he was. Unlike every other place his travels had led him, the Brazilian jungles provided a nearly perfect sanctuary. Here we was free from the mind-bending frustrations that had plagued his old life. No wrestling promoters trying to take advantage of him, no vindictive females waiting with the cold steel to plunge into his back, and best of all…no expectations. He was free. Free to explore the vastness of his own mind, free to live quietly and peacefully among the world around him, rather than living up to bold expectations of running out and seizing the world by the throat all for himself. “I am,” Darcy said aloud. “I came here looking to bring peace to my soul, and I feel that this place, and your people have helped me accomplish that. Amy Campbell, aka Aimz... odd as ever, but twice as responsible -- when she isn't getting shot or punched or cut. *** Darcy couldn’t believe the words staring back at him from his laptop’s monitor. As much as Darcy felt that his life resembled too much of a bad spy movie for his tastes, he still couldn’t help but be sucked in to a plot that grew more and more intricate by the day. This latest twist, however, hit him hard like a sucker punch to the stomach. Of all the sources Darcy could possibly have imagined, this seemed the least likely to sneak her quiet money. By far. This was unbelievable. He felt sick. Suddenly, the match he’d labored to make for so long, the match he’d waited his entire wrestling LIFE for…suddenly it looked as if it would turn out to be the greatest mistake he ever made. Darcy closed his laptop, unable to stare at his discovery any longer. He closed his eyes as well, hoping upon hope that he could somehow erase the image of what he’d seen. But the cruel, twisted letters composing the name of the company behind the check, and moreover, this sick, masochistic plot were burned hard into his memory. DRAKEWERX
