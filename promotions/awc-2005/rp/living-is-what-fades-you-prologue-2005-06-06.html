Living Is What Fades You: Prologue

6 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6 June, 2005 The muffled alarm woke him up like it was meant to. He whapped it, then caught up with the fact that he needn't have bothered. The other side of the bed was empty, though the clock-face confirmed it had gone off when he'd set it to. He just hadn't been early enough to get a jump on her. He rolled his eyes - the 'in bed' part of the birthday breakfast was pretty much out. So he adjusted, dragged the bedclothes away from his body, and got up. He figured that she had probably snuck down to do some work. She sometimes had patches of sleeplessness, even though these days, her missions were more often in class than they were out in the field. She'd been assigned a new batch of recruits lately, and biased as he was, he thought they were going to be damned lucky. She was putting in the wee hours for them already, pushing the limits of what level of classified documentation she could check out. Arriving downstairs, he saw that it was still too early for her cards to have been delivered yet. From the other side of the thick door, he could hear barking, so he obediently unlocked it to let the mutt and the cold, early air in. 'Whoa, Sawyer,' he muttered, as the dog, acting like the puppy he hadn't been for a few years, bolted past him out back. 'Someone's hungry.' It was too cold to keep the door open, and he was the self-designated chef for the day, with wife and dog waiting. Wondering if he could coax Ellis back to bed and salvage the breakfast plans, he entered the open-plan living area. Sawyer had long stopped barking. She was sitting at the dining table, papers scattered in a white jagged ring in front of her: the first sign. The second was the way she was leaning - sideways, awkward, wrong. He called her name, walked up to her, reaching out too slowly for her carotid pulse. There was none. They would tell him it was her heart. He wouldn't believe it until he was taken to a dust-free room, with shielded light bearing down on him, and held a sheet of another man’s handwriting surrounding an impossibly detailed sketch in his gloved hands. The picture would not leave him, even when he returned home and began opening the envelopes that were addressed to her, surrounding jokes and beautiful cards, the writing knowing nothing of what was to come. -- Twenty-one years earlier. Ellis met Logan in New York. It was cold and she hadn't worn enough; she never did. In the sharp, cruel January air, her skin looked paler and numbly sallow, her hands old. Snow uncurled itself across the pavement and between the car tires like a ghost gliding with the gusts of wind. Ellis could see bits of the sky through the buildings and the thick, electric energy of the city, bits of the sky that were bruised red and purple and faintly orange. Like the flesh of the blood orange she'd eaten two days ago, juice getting under her nails. It was strange of her to notice things in such a detached manner, beautiful things made her loud and earnest, most of the time. But things had started to be strange years ago and hadn't stopped since. She liked it, generally, thought that it was life's way of keeping her alert, awake. Grateful, even. The constants, though - those she wanted to keep. Hotel. She could feel her feet sinking slightly into the carpet with each step. Two, five, nine, fourteen floors up and she was knocking on a door, fourteen floors up and it was Logan, the same Logan she had known for her entire twenty-five years, Logan but changed. Before Ellis began forming a single word, a single smile, there were arms around her and the familiar feeling of Logan's hair against the side of her face. He smelled like mangoes and nicotine and a little bit like the sun, if it could ever have a scent, if it could ever be this real. Logan was thinner now, and Ellis could feel his breath skittering lightly and pooling in the hollow of her collarbone. When Ellis pulled back, she could see the new kind of fierceness around Logan's eyes, the lines of his face. A kind of urgency, almost. Striking. A sort of vital, burning energy in the way he moved, his words. His mouth. And it was like this city, like that summer, like everything people were paid to find words for. Logan wore bits of Che all over him now, but it was something deeper than just second skin. It was more like a sort of driving relentlessness that would be exhausting to look at on anyone but him. Ellis believed the interviews; she read the words and saw the inescapable, bright, hard truths between them and below them and it made her glad. She wasn't like Logan, there was nobody quite like Logan, but Ellis understood the way his mouth tightened sometimes when he read the news, why he wore his glasses as much as he could, how he was impatient and blunt and poetic all at the same time. It was his home, too, his country. 'You're freezing.' Ellis grinned. 'Still not used to it.' Logan looked at her, long, mouth curving a little. 'You will be, though,' and took Ellis' hands and held them between his own, eyes dark with fragments of mountain peaks and riverbanks and echoing secret roads, dark with those places and smiling. There are certain principles that she acts by. Not lives by, but acts by, because Ellis is one of those people who are certain that life is the collection and compaction of all the events you've gone through and nothing more. Dates. Accomplishments. Places. People. There's no deeper meaning unless you make it for yourself. She's got a list of beliefs in her head, neatly spelled out on a piece of lined paper, and it might, it could, look a little like this: 1. If you're going to do something, do it right. 2. There's no reason to stop trying. If you know you can do it, that is. Futile attempts are pointless. 3. Discipline. 4. You're never who you think you are. Everybody needs boundaries, that's the only way you'll ever figure yourself out, at least for the time being. And, lastly: 5. Bending the rules is never a bad idea. These things have become more than thoughts, now, they're almost feelings, rushing headily away from her heart and into her limbs. They're beyond behaviors acquired through conditioning, Ellis thinks one day, they're something hereditary, bred into double helixes. Dominant genes and sequence codes, this is how she succeeds. Ellis operates through a suspension of belief, day in and day out, seasons melting into each other. Nobody ever pins down her ambition with 'you can't's, so she can, and she does. They say that the unthinkable happens. When he leaves, Ellis does not crumble and for that, she's glad. But for the CIA team, it's still like losing an appendage, something you had always assumed would be there. They can function without him but it takes a lot of adapting, of changing and it's still not the same in the end. Logan is somebody that's hard to replace. Few people can, and even those that are able to are reluctant to do so, because they don't want to ruin the legacy that belongs to him, they are afraid of making mistakes where they think Logan has made none. He leaves them on a Thursday afternoon, emptying out his locker and stepping away. Sees Chandler watching him head towards the door and nods. Is almost surprised to see Ellis just a few feet away, holding a gun at her side. 'You're late to the briefing.' 'I had to get this,' waving the gun, 'Left the other one at home.' 'That isn't an excuse.' Ellis smiles. 'I know,' then, suddenly, 'It was stupid of you.' 'It?' 'Your shoulder,' and she does not look afraid, intimidated, 'it was stupid of you to fight so hard against FKA, if this means as much to you as you say it does.' 'I don't like losing. My elbow was supposed to hold up.' 'But it didn't, and you lost anyway. I can't take your place. You wanted me to, but I won't,' Logan frowns at her, like she'd said too much, maybe, shoulders straight and still, 'You should enjoy Germany.' 'I had hoped,' Logan pauses because he knew this already and isn't sure what he is referring to now, Ellis or that match or his shoulder, 'I thought that maybe you could stand in for a while.' 'I'm sorry.' And Logan knows that she isn't, but there is nothing he can do now to save this act, because Ellis turns and walks towards the briefing, and he wants to say something but knows that there is nothing to do here. Logan steps forward and catches Ellis' wrist, turning her around. For a brief, sickening second, he entertains the thought of snapping this narrow circle of bone, of the crisp, perfunctory sound of bones cracking. It's surreal, this, and Logan feels like he's constantly in a dream, now, that he hasn't woken up from this comedy of a nightmare. There's an annoyed look about Ellis' eyebrows and forehead, so he leans forward quick, bending down a little and kisses the edge of her mouth. Logan does not relegate boys and girls into different species; motivation is impossible to categorize, break points are the same against all opponents. This is not quite a game, he has enough of those, it is more of a test. Logan does not bend. 'No, you aren't,' and walks out of the doors, across the street, where a bus has just pulled up. Looking out the window, he can still see Ellis standing there -- one hand at her side and the other curled around the base of the gun loosely, useless against her side, watching her face filtered through the dark glass. Logan wonders if Germany will break him and force him to let go or if he will still handle the weight that faith exerts, even thousands of miles away because that's who he is and that's not Ellis at all, Ellis who people call a prodigy, Ellis who plays selfishly and doesn't give up simply because she has never had to.
