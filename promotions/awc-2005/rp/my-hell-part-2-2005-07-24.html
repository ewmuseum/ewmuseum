My Hell, Part 2

24 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 24/07/05 Hugo Silveira: Amy, just do it! The raised tone in Hugo's voice, which Amy doesn't seem to be used to, makes her react and head down a different corridor. Hugo looks back to the waiting Mickey Fitz-McCarthy, aka The Farmer. Amy had just finished her first ever professional wrestling match with a win nobody was expecting her to get, apart from maybe her father. And, when she left the ring and the cheers of Costa Rican fans who had never heard of her, she didn't get congratulations off anyone. Instead, she found her father talking to the person she'd just fought against, making a big deal, as always, out of a minor issue. And she'd been sent down the corridor toward the nurse's room. She was fine, which she didn't expect to be, but her father had no doubt took a small mistake by her opponent to his head, and wanted her to be checked out anyway. Amy hadn't ever met the medical staff before, and so didn't know what to expect when she got there. The corridor around her looked a little bit more derelict than she had expected. With the show being held in Costa Rica, the facilities, were never going to be fantastic, but prior to the event Amy had at least expected corridors with walls that had been painted some time in the last ten years. The peeling, yellowing paint on the walls gave the impression that the arena had been run-down for years, and the lack of any sound around the corridor made the area of the building seem pretty much abandoned. Amy had got about half-way down the shabby corridor when she heard her father's voice from behind her. Obviously he'd finished whinging to McCarthy, and now wanted to whinge at her instead. Amy simply ignored him at first, continuing her slow and tired strut toward the medical room about twenty paces in front of her. The persistence of Hugo Silveira, however, overcame her attempts to ignore him and after he started jogging to catch up, Amy stopped, spun around on her heels and stood with her arms crossed, waiting for him. Hugo Silveira: What are you ignoring me for? Hugo sounded out of breath from his jog, and spoke with a fairly neutral tone of voice, which would be rare considering that normally he'd speak in a very positive way to his daughter. Amy Silveira: Well, seeing as your only going to whinge if I don't.. Amy was dripping slightly with sweat, with her head shining from the flow of it from the roots of her brown, soft hair. She wiped her forehead, wiping the sweat on her jeans, whilst looking up and away from her father as if she had nothing to say to him. Hugo Silveira: Who said I'm going to whinge?... I'm proud of you. Amy Silveira: Yeah? Well a great way to show it, showing me up in front of the person I'd just beat, for you. Hugo considers the 'for you' comment for a moment, choosing to ignore it, before smiling a little. Hugo Silveira: Look, he could have hurt you badly there, that was a.. Amy Silveira: A badly constructed brainbuster. I know. Hugo Silveira: ..My point is, I don't want you to get hurt. And you may end up facing him again. Amy laughs at his comment a little, looking down and then at him, and wiping her brow again. Amy Silveira: If you didn't want me to get hurt, why would you.... nevermind. Hugo Silveira: No, no.. Tell me. Amy sighs, and Hugo looks slightly concerned. Amy Silveira: No, it doesn't matter. Anyway.. I don't know if I can do that again. Hugo Silveira: Can't do what? Ignore me? Amy gives him a blank look. Hugo considers what she meant, and after realising, smiles a little. Hugo Silveira: Look, Amélia, darling.. You know you can do it, and you've just proven to the world you can. You've got the hard bit over and done with. Amy doesn't reply, just looking down a little. She is visibly tired and in need of various things, such as a drink, and as Hugo realises this he remembers why they're standing outside of a door entitled 'Medical Office', he quickly responds in a hurried sense. Hugo Silveira: I think we'd better get you checked over, I can't have my girl injured, can I? Amy nods slightly, not even bothering to give him a reply. The two enter the office, and are met by a small, practically empty office with very little medical items present. A small, single bed sits at the right of the room, with a desk and some closed cardboard boxes on the right. Directly opposite the door is a bench with a sink, a kettle, and a man with his back to the door. Hugo Silveira: Excuse me..? The person jumps a little, spilling tea on the lino floor under-foot. With a mug in their hand saying 'World's best auntie', they turn, noticing that Hugo and Amy are standing in the doorway. After realising who they are, he stands up straight, brushes himself down with one hand, and motions over to the two chairs in front of the desk on the left. Nurse: Sorry, I was.. The Nurse pauses for a moment or two. Amy Silveira: Making tea? Nurse: Er, yes. The nurse moves to the chair behind the desk, sitting in it and spilling more tea before he places the mug on the desk. He smiles faintly, noticeably at Amy, before looking to Hugo and sipping the tea, as if to suggest for him to get on with it. Hugo: Ok, Mr.. Nurse: Matthews. Matthew.. Matthews. Amy laughs a little at his name, clearly slightly happier than she was a few minutes ago, but a glance from her father shuts her up. Hugo Silveira: Sorry about that. Basically.. Matt Matthews: No, no, it's ok. After interrupting Hugo to reply to a comment five seconds too late, Hugo sighs a little under his breath, and restarts. Hugo Silveira: Ok.. Basically, Amy got a nasty bump on her neck from a rookie wrestler, and I want it to.. My dad can be such an idiot sometimes. If he isn't over-reacting, treating me like I'm ten years old or generally belittling me, he's showing me up in front of people or doing something which generally makes me wonder if he's right in the head. I mean, he can't be right in the head if he gets his eighteen-year old daughter involved in wrestling, but doesn't want her to get hurt. It just doesn't work. I'm sure he realises that he's putting me at danger, but to say he doesn't want me to be in danger even though he pushed me into it is just amazing. In a bad way. But anyway, enough about him. I managed to win my first match, which I guess I'm slightly happy about. I'm sure he must of be bribing someone, though, to get me a reward on my debut which effectively said I was the best wrestler there on the night. I think everyone knows I was lucky to be facing someone who's probably as clueless as I am. I was expecting to get killed in there, yet I came out far from it. It took me a while to get congratulated - in fact, the only people who bothered were my friends and mother - but nevertheless, I'm quite happy with myself for surviving one nine-minute match. And that brings me here, to Málaga. I've barely started my career yet I'm closer to home than I could of imagined, hanging out in a city which is very similar to the one where I grew up. If the people didn't speak Spanish, I'd be able to say that I feel like I am at home, but the fact I can't understand anyone sort of rules that out. Nevertheless, I'd still been able to see some Portuguese friends I hadn't seen for a year or so, since my last visit 'home'. I'd never forgotten my friends, but I guess I might lose them now that I'm a minor celebrity. I'd been able to actually have a good time for the first time in a while, during my time in Spain, as it had been a welcome change of scenery. On top of that, the old man had to stay in Los Angeles on business, so I'll be rid of him until Monday night. The bit of space given to me by that had allowed me to check out the city of Málaga, including the chance to drink more than usual, even if the lads in the clubs were more interested in my sister than me. And it was with my sister that I'd been sitting outside of a fairly upper-class café for about an hour or so now, taking in the afternoon Sun I'd grew accustom to both in Portugal and in America, whilst generally talking like we sort of had on-and-off throughout our life. I didn't know what time it was by now, but when me and Catarina set out it was about 2:30 PM, and so I guess it was around 4PM. The Sun flared down onto us, as if we were in the spotlight, as to us it appeared that we were getting more of the Sun than anyone. I'd chosen to wear a small light-blue top and white skirt today, which was probably a good decision as it meant I didn't boil as much in the hot weather. That said, wearing a short skirt had a lot of Spanish people looking at me, although lately I couldn't tell whether that was because of my looks or the fact that I'm now a 'professional wrestler'. With it being a Sunday, I wasn't sure what I was going to do tonight, although as I'd chosen to spend the past few days with my sister and friends from Portugal, I'd probably end up in the clubs again. I wasn't sick of it, but I guess I was getting a 'visual dad' in front of me, telling me I should be preparing for my next match. The way he drilled ideas into my head, in a way which you couldn't refuse, had made me resent him lately. At the same time, he'd made me agree, and so he'd won. If these were mind games, he was winning. Catarina Silveira: ..Amy..? Catarina was leaning over the table, and her expensive white wine (which I'd paid for), and looking at me whilst smiling a little in her way. I'd been daydreaming, and she'd obviously guessed. Upon realising, I looked around a little, and gave her a cute smile. I don't know why I gave her one, but I did. Amy Silveira: Sorry, I was.. er.. daydreaming. Catarina Silveira: I noticed. Amy Silveira: Ooook.. So, what were you saying? Catarina Silveira: I asked if your coming with me to the bar again tonight. Amy Silveira: Yeah, yeah.. why not. I smiled a little, but as always with Catarina, it wasn't a very strong one. You'd probably get the impression from seeing the two of us that we just don't like each other. And, you'd not be completely right. Or at least, we don't have any reason to not like each other. Or at least I don't. Over time, I've realised that despite our looking nearly identical, bar Catarina's blonde highlights and her more mature facial features, we are very different people. I learnt that in my pre-teens, when we just weren't like normal sisters. She always wanted to get one up on me, and being older, she normally did. And even now, she has more boyfriends than me, knows more people, and has a better life. She doesn't have a dad like mine. Even though we've got the same dad, he hardly ever bothers with Catarina. He never has, and I don't know why. I guess that's one reason why me and Catarina don't get along as well as we should. Whereas my father was never giving me space while I grew up, and pushing me into wrestling over time without my knowing it, he just.. never bothered with Catarina. I don't know why, but they never seemed to get along like they should. They speak, and they have their moments, but they don't have the father-daughter relationship that they should. Come to think of it, without being nasty - Catarina's a bit of a misfit. She gets along with my mother, but so do I; me and Catarina, and dad and Catarina, just don't click. I think it started when we were growing up. Catarina got involved with boys at the age of 12, and I'm pretty sure she lost her virginity a year later, although I've never asked. I've called her a slut before, but then again, she's called me 'daddy's girl'. Catarina's more of a man-eater than me, and gets herself more attention than me, which is sometimes a good thing as she picks up some right vilers at times. I go for what's on the inside of the person; She goes for what's on the inside of the pants. Catarina Silveira: Ok, that's that sorted then. What time is it? I suddenly remembered I was wearing a watch. A silver one, which I'd bought new yesterday with some of the good wages from AWC. Another good thing about being in wrestling. One of few good things. I had a look at it; 4PM. Amy Silveira: Er, about four. Catarina Silveira: We might want to get back, then. I'm going to be bringing Charlie and Marie, if that's ok? Amy Silveira: Yeah, yeah.. That's ok. Charlie and Marie were, basically, two people I'd never liked. They were Catarina's best friends, and whereas she got on with a lot of my friends, I hated hers with a passion. Charlie had given birth when she was 15, having had an abortion a year before, and whereas Marie wasn't as slutty as her, she had the personality of a corn flake. And the intelligence of one, too. She was, basically, a lemming to the two of them. Of course I didn't want them to come, but like my opinion would make a difference. Catarina stood up, urging for me to come on, and I finished my glass of wine and followed her down the street. Her skirt was about an inch shorter than mine, which probably pushed it over the edge, but it got her more attention off the guys with a nasty case of acne. I guess she saw that as victory. I would say I'd rather not be around my sister longer than I have to be, but with the prospect of my father getting here in 24 hours, I'd mayswell get out of my head while I can. After all, I'm about to wrestle a pair of homosexuals in two days' time.
