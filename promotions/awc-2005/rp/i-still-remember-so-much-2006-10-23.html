I still remember so much

23 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10.23.06 From the front page of www.darcycrisis.com... So I recently did this autograph signing in Poughkeepsie, NY. It was for Mark Jones, an old high school buddy of mine who just opened a comic book shop in the city whose name can’t be spelled by anybody who hasn’t lived there at least six months. Everything went swimmingly, and we had a huge turnout and everything. Normally this isn’t the sort of thing that makes a front page splash, and I’m definitely not trying to toot my own horn or anything…but something interesting came out of the event that I want to share with you guys. We pretty much ran the gamut in terms of fans who showed up. Plenty of the old “Crisis Squad” fan club that have hung with me since the PSW days, some AWC faithful…and of course, the regular middling sort who were just there to pick up the latest issue of Fantastic Four and had no idea what the hell some prick from California with a fifteen pound oversized belt was doing clogging up their poor comic shop. But the ones of special note were the younger crowd – 10, 11, 12 year olds, who I’m guessing were getting their first up close and personal encounter with the ol’ Darcinator. And I say this because over and over, I kept getting asked the same questions by these young folks – “How did you become a wrestler, Darcy?” “How did you break into the business?” “How did you get discovered?” “Is it true that if you stare at Pierce Lavelle’s hair for too long, you get sucked into a vortex of hair product and styling gel from which you will never return?” Okay, I made that last question up. But the rest were true. And it strikes me as kind of funny that there’s a new generation of people who have heard of Darcy Crisis, but not the infamous tale of how he came into being. Funny because when it was actually happening, and I was trying to break into wrestling, it was all people could talk about it. Or rather…how I didn’t deserve it. How it was all handed to me on a silver platter. When I first met Amy, she wouldn’t even show me the least bit of human decency because of it. Now here we are, almost ten years later…the criticism faded with time and perseverance…my biggest detractor became my fiancée…and worst of all, nobody even remembers why they used to hate me so bad. That’s right, you heard me correctly…I kind of miss all the hard knocking people used to throw at me. The more people told me I didn’t deserve my free entry into professional wrestling, the more I was determined to prove them wrong. People said I was moving too quickly up the ladder, which only made me want to climb even faster. So now that everybody has stopped talking s**t on me, I run the risk of growing complacent. And since I’m incredibly loathe to let that happen, I thought I’d kill two birds with one stone by cluing in my younger legion of fans of just how I broke into the biz, and see if I can’t drum up some of the old nay-saying that I so much enjoyed feeing off of. So here goes…the infamous story of how Darcy Markson became Darcy Crisis…including the back story that until now the public had never been privy to. Alright, wind back your watches to February of 1998….okay. Are we set? Is “Bittersweet Symphony” stuck in your head? Good. I don’t know what you guys were doing at the time, but I was really in a bad way. College was kicking my ass, I had absolutely no clue what I wanted to do with my life, and I was failing miserably in my attempt to move on after I split up with Jane, my high school girlfriend. Yeah, yeah, yeah…say whatever you want, but you should know two things: One, I hate Chris Carraba and every single of his fans with a passion, to the point that I’d kill them myself if they weren’t already slashing their wrists themselves. And two, when you’ve been seeing a girl long enough that her cancer-stricken mother hands you her wedding ring, with the dying message “I’m so happy for you two!”, well…it can be kind of difficult to let go. Things sort of came to a head when I came home one day to find Jane inexplicably in bed with my roommate, of all people. Actually, I shouldn’t say inexplicably, because there probably was a reason…I just didn’t care what it was. Before I finally calmed down I had not only smashed the Roommate Who Shall Go Nameless’ face in, I had also in my emotionally charged state saw fit to remove myself from the situation altogether by dropping out of college outright. Still way too afraid to return home to face the music with my parents, who surely would have been even more livid with me than I had been at seeing Jane shacked up with Sam [name withheld] (Oops! I named him! I sure hope nobody finds out where you live…chump.), I bummed it with some friends for a few days. By this point I was really a mess…completely and entirely inconsolable. The only thing I could think of that would possibly make me feel better was a hot pastrami sandwich on sourdough bread. So of course, of COURSE the kids I was staying with didn’t have any of the necessary ingredients, leaving me with no option but to haul my mopey ass to the grocery store to get some. My life depended on it, I’m telling you. So I get to the grocery store, I guess around 4:00 in the afternoon or so. Again, just looking for some bread so I can get home, have a sandwich and get back to figuring out just where the hell I was supposed to go from there. And that’s about when that sly, shape-shifting devil known as Destiny decided to intervene, this time in the form of one Timothy Chavez… *** I can’t believe it’s come to this, the balding, heavyset man thought to himself. Life has evidently seen fit to lift himself from the lavatory, wander over to the poor, naive man in question, of all people, and take a dump directly on top of his unsuspecting head. It had been so easy for him back in his college days. There was never any shortage of young adults looking to expand their minds, bend the rules of reality, or simply laugh their asses off watching police chase videos for hours on end. And with Mommy and Daddy unknowingly fronting the bill for most of these kids, there was practically no stopping the money train from steaming in. When the opportunity presented itself to move up the chain, expanding his trade from his friends, to his friends and their friends, to becoming the man to talk to in southern Torrance, it was hardly even a choice. This was the easiest, most lucrative job he could possibly imagine having. But all the positives that came from full-time drug dealing all stemmed from one inescapable source – the high, high risk of danger. It could come from anywhere - a desperate addict in search of his next fix could rob him blind; someone lower on the chain who neglected to show the necessary care in these most delicate of operations could fall prey to the police…where they would be all to willing to supply his name to the authorities in exchange for their freedom. Certain changes to his life were inevitable. One day he found himself purchasing a handgun, simply because he had too much merchandise at his fingertips that could fall victim to snatchery from a complete stranger, a business associate…or even a friend. And then he woke up one day to realize that the sum of the latter had dwindled to a pithy few, simply because it had become too dangerous to trust anybody else. Not when his lucrative occupation was forced to remain a deep secret, harbored by a poor kid whose personality had now absorbed the unwanted but all-too-necessary paranoia. The biggest change of all, however, took hold of his life on the unavoidable day where the long arm of the law finally caught up to him. It was commendable that he’d made it as long as he did without running into trouble until then. Outstanding, even. But to avoid getting caught, a drug dealer had to be perfect. A speeding ticket was just as treacherous to his scheme as an undercover officer with a well-hatched sting. Our drug-dealing friend fell victim to the former. A passable mistake, really – even the most law-abiding citizen can find themselves on the wrong end of a traffic citation if they’re not careful. Yet he cursed himself the moment he saw the headlights flashing alternating hues of red and blue into his peripheral vision. Even with practically every avenue covered, with seemingly no inch of his vehicle containing anything the least bit suspicious, there was no chasing the swarms of butterflies darting within the confines of his stomach. It was like they were purposely screwing with me, he thought to himself as he recounted the incident. The officer was friendly, their conversation was all too brief, and it appeared as if he would escape the affair with nothing more than a hundred and twenty dollar fine; a large annoyance, no doubt, but a far superior fate than some of the others he could have faced. But just as the officer looked like he was about to head back to his squad car, he turned to ask the question that our protagonist had dreaded all along. “You’re not carrying anything illegal in your vehicle, are you sir?” He denied it, of course. But it only delayed his fate long enough for the inevitable follow up query. “Then you wouldn’t mind me having a look, would you?” He was perfectly within his rights to deny the officer his search request. But telling him no would obviously raise some suspicion. And considering his contraband was hidden in the most devious of locations, he thought it wiser to let Snoopy Sally have a look around the car…after all, there was no chance of him finding anything. He hadn’t counted on the officer’s being joined by his canine companion. The officer’s initial search, as expected, came up fruitless. But the moment the K-9 unit entered the investigation, the black market businessman knew the jig was up. It only took the aroma of some trace amounts of blow to reveal the super-secret hiding place lying beneath a false floor of the trunk. Fortunately for him, the trace amounts detected by the drug-sniffer weren’t part of a much larger stash, or he might as well have donned an orange jumpsuit right there and then. But what the dog did uncover gave an all too literal definition to the term “dirty money.” Fifty grand, to be exact. Too much for the driver to explain with any shred of credibility what was doing hidden in his trunk. Far too much for him to be able to cover if something (or in this case, someone) causing its being intercepted. But conveniently, it turned out to be enough for the police department to seize and hold onto until somebody came to claim it…knowing full well that anyone with half a brain would refrain from doing so, resulting in the confiscated pile of money becoming the sole property of the LAPD. It seemed that fate had provided a silver lining in the officer did not have the power to arrest the man for his extremely well-hidden money stash that he could easily deny knowing anything about. But now that the money had missed its intended target and fallen into the hands of the Los Angeles law enforcers, he found himself in the most precarious of situations - one that was perhaps even less enviable than donning pinstripes for the rest of his life. For if there was one thing that druglords simply wouldn’t tolerate, it was money going missing…especially five figure sums. They knew he was in charge of delivering it, and when the shipment didn’t come in, they would surely be looking for him. Somehow, someway, it was up to him to come up with enough money to buy a brand-spanking new Dodge Viper before his intended recipients found him first. Timothy Chavez was no criminal. At least, he had never thought of himself as one until this very moment. As he loaded the clip into his lethal accessory, he wondered if he would ever be able to live with himself after crossing the threshold of seemingly no return. Even when he reached the point where he was moving thousands of dollars worth of drugs, Chavez had been okay with his participation in what he saw to be a series of victimless crimes. But this was nothing like his early forays into the criminal world. Even though he hoped to come away from this without spilling so much as even a single drop of blood, he would surely be creating victims by inflicting severe mental and emotional duress. Vigorously shaking his head, Chavez shook such thoughts from his head. There was simply no alternative, and any musings of doubt would only detract him from accomplishing the mission. Still, as he pulled the nylon stocking over his face, he couldn’t help but wonder how a series of decisions that had began with acquiring some illegal imports on behalf of some desperate friends had led him to this unfortunate point in time. He approached the automatic glass doors to the grocery’s entrance, which obliged his admission as they would any other shopper. Perhaps this was the grocery chain’s first error, in allowing free and automatic entry to all, whether they come packing several credit cards or a firearm. Knowing there was simply no use in backing out, Chavez wasted no time in carrying out his plan. The report of the gun firing once into the supermarket’s ceiling commanded the attention of employees and patrons alike; his bellowing voice made clear his intentions. “THIS IS A ROBBERY, BITCHES!” Chavez screamed, confirming the age-old theory that all armed robbers are inherently sexist. “EVERYBODY ON THE FUCKING GROUND, AND NOBODY GETS HURT!” To a man, the entire store’s population that Chavez could see cowered toward the ground in deference to the crazed gunman. Moving to the closest register, he motioned for the trembling cashier unfortunate enough to be stationed there back to her feet, pointing his gun first at her face before motioning it over to the register, making his intentions perfectly clear without uttering a word. Her hands shaking, the poor cashier did her best to oblige. But as the girl began emptying cash into a paper sack, Chavez got a sour feeling in his stomach. Something isn’t right here, he thought to himself, anxious to solve the mystery. Just then, out of the corner of his eye, he saw it. Some young, foolhardy kid, perhaps four or five years his junior, was actually standing… clutching a loaf of bread and what looked to be some cold cuts he’d acquired from the grocery deli. And unlike everyone else in the store he didn’t appear to have an ounce of fear within him as he stared a hole into the Chavez’s masked mug. “Hey kid!” Chavez roared, moving over to the defiant young man that evidently loved sandwiches. “On the fucking ground, let’s go!” In response he dropped his potential dinner ingredients to the floor, but the lad remained upright, offering no verbal response. “Alright, hard-ass,” Chavez answered, brandishing his gun in the direction of his uncooperative hostage. “You want to be a hero? Well now you’re gonna pay for it. Let’s see your wallet.” From beneath the long, black locks shielding the young man’s face, he gave an answer to the assailant’s demands this time. “You’re not robbing me, Professor Stockingface. Take a hike,” he said, rather chillingly. Chavez clicked the hammer of his pistol to ensure that this man who seemed to insist on becoming an needless victim knew that his firearm was loaded and ready to take his life. “Are you fucking daffy?” he asked, incredulously. “I’m about two seconds from putting a bullet straight between your eyes! Now if after those two seconds your wallet ISN’T in my hand, and your lips AREN’T puckering up on that concrete, heads are gonna fucking ROLL! Understand?!” Incredibly, the young hero called his attacker’s bluff, taking a step forward to stare Chavez’s pistol square in the face. For his part, Chavez couldn’t make heads or tails of this situation. Perhaps this man knew that he was merely an amateur criminal, forced into undertaking this heinous crime more or less against his will. Or perhaps this man could sense the fact that he truly had no intentions of hurting anyone, even if they had the gall to ignore his overwhelming control over whether or not they left the grocery store alive. Or perhaps still…and this seemed the most likely answer, judging by the almost far away stare in his emboldened face…perhaps this man simply had nothing to lose. Now staring past the barrel into the burglar’s eyes himself, he confirmed the latter of the suspicions to be true with only two words, as short as they were direct and powerful. “Do it.” This bastard’s left me with no other alternative… Chavez said to himself silently, before offering the verbal response to the man whose life was about to end. “Say goodbye, punkass…” But just before his finger could exert enough force on the trigger, he suddenly found himself facing a far more precarious situation…or so he thought. Just past this brazen boy’s face, he could make out one of the other cashiers who had clandestinely pulled himself back to his feet during all of this reaching for something. Chavez altered his aim away from the young lad in an attempt to intercept, but it was too late – an ear-splitting alarm overtook the market, alerting seemingly anyone within a ten-mile radius that they were in deep trouble. His only option now was to haul ass here and now, perhaps obtaining a slim chance at escape before the boys in blue arrived to spoil his fun once more. But he never got the chance. Less than a second after he lifted his aim away from the boy, he seized the opportunity by pouncing on his aggressor, freezing his arm and the gun in place. Chavez struggled mightily to free his shooting arm away from the man’s clutches, desperate to re-assert control, but it was to no avail. In his attempt to reclaim the gun, he mistakenly fired it as it was pointed toward the ceiling. It ricocheted first off of a fluorescent light, then a register, then one or two other spots by which point Chavez had lost track of the bullet’s path. When it came to rest he heard a low, guttural groan, but was unable to determine just who the hell the bullet had hit. Unfortunately for Chavez, this man he’d picked a bone with was faster, stronger, and evidently knew just how to incapacitate a potential attacker. He could hear the sirens approaching as his face and arm were locked between the young man’s powerful legs. Of course, he had no way of knowing, but as fate would have it, he would not be the last to fall prey to this maneuver which would come to be known the world over as “the Darcinator.” *** …and as it happened, it was probably the best tasting sandwich I ever devoured. So there you have it, ladies and jelly spoons. I wasn’t looking to be a hero that fateful day. And if I’d felt like I had even the smallest inkling of a care in the world, I probably wouldn’t have stood up to Chavez. Definitely, the smarter move would have been to hit the deck and live to be a hard ass on another day, one where the stakes were hopefully not so high. But then again, if I had, I never would have made all the local papers the next day. I never would have been able to parlay a simple wrestling move I learned from my high school wrestling coach into a contract to do what I had just done for a living, a contract that would lift me from all of my troubles at home into a one-way ticket to the frozen confines of an armory and gymnasium-based wrestling federation stationed out of northeastern Canada. And then you wouldn’t have the opportunity to peruse this lovely website. Two lives went in distinctly different directions on that fateful day. Timothy Chavez morphed into Prisoner #0057HF3, fated to spend the next 10-25 years behind bars for attempted robbery, aggravated assault and attempted murder. And Darcy Markson began his own metamorphosis into the highly-polished wrestler and soon-to-be devoted husband that you know and love, the man called Darcy Crisis. Well I’d love to stay and chat, but it’s off to Toronto for me for yet another can’t-miss episode of Fresh!, in which I beat the hell out of Agent Dash for either the 8th or 9th time. Do me a favor and tune in, it should be off the hook! Take care and God bless, -D *** “Wow. It’s like…Albany. Only cleaner!” Darcy laughed off his cohort’s homage to the old Michael Moore comedy/satire Canadian Bacon, in which the United States inexplicably declares war on Canada in an effort to boost the current administration’s ratings. “I loved that movie,” Darcy said to Johnny as they waited for the stoplight to signal the little stick figure indicator that it was safe to cross the street. “I have to say though, I’m glad that movie was only a fairytale. If our president in his right mind ever purposely and knowingly deceived his everyone into believing that a foreign nation posing no threat to the United States had ill-intentions, thus sparking a prolonged conflict that only leads us to a diplomatic disaster…well, I don’t know what the hell I’d do.” “Yeah, that’d be a real bitch,” Johnny responded, playing along with Darcy’s sarcasm-laden sentiments. A few moments later the stickman was illuminated, and he crossed the street with his longtime friend. With a wife and newborn son back waiting for him at home, John Harrell (or as Darcy usually referred to him, Johnny Hawk, a nickname an old T-ball coach gave to him in the first grade that stuck ever since) was usually far too busy to accompany Darcy on his cross-continental excursions that came with a full-time wrestler’s schedule. But this particular trip to what many called the “New York of Canada” was for a special reason – and as they left the intersection and reached the northwest corner of Yonge and Front Street, that reason loomed over them as they stared in awe. The Bank of Montreal building was constructed in 1885, originally designed to house a branch of the bank that carried its namesake. The building replaced another bank which had called the same location home for 40 years prior, and its own tenure more than doubled its previous tenant, lasting almost a century. But Darcy and Johnny didn’t travel all the way to Toronto to see an enormous bank building. Rather, they were there to see what the building became after it was restored and recommissioned in 1993 – the National Hockey League Hall of Fame. Somehow, despite spending nearly their entire lives in a state that was notorious for its wonderful warm weather and almost complete lack of frozen precipitation, both Darcy and Johnny grew up diehard hockey fans. Some of the greatest years of their lives were remembered fondly by the immortal Wayne Gretzky ushering hockey into California as the captain of the Los Angeles Kings, legitimate championship contenders in the early 1990s. Together, the two played, watched, ate, drank, breathed and slept hockey. It was more than a sport – it was practically a religion. And here and now, Toronto and its legendary Hockey Hall of Fame had become their Mecca. Inside, they had it all – the list of hockey’s most legendary names enshrined for as long as the walls could stand. An astounding replica of the old Montreal Forum locker room of the famous Montreal Canadiens, far and away the NHL’s most successful franchise with 23 Stanley Cup Championships. A permanent house for the various trophies awarded to the league’s annual top scorers and conference champions…all, of course, paling in comparison to Lord Stanley’s prize. Truly, the moment Darcy and Johnny entered the Hall’s hallowed confines, they would be transformed into awestruck, aww-gee-whiz 10-year-olds all over again. “Last one inside’s a penis pump!” Johnny yelled just before he bolted for the door, evidently already transformed into a 10-year-old just by standing outside. Darcy was about to break for the door himself when he was stopped by a cold shiver that shot down his spine. As if on cue, the air plug to the one-hit wonder Rockwell’s one-hit popped into his head. I always feel that…somebody’s WAH-tchin meee…. One would think he’d be over the apprehension by now. After all, considering Darcy had spent so many years earning a living by performing in front of a camera, it could and should be inferred that he would come to expect it when someone recognized him when he had the audacity to venture into the general public he charged himself to entertain. This is not to say that he did not; for not only did he, but in face he loved it. Couldn’t get enough of it. After spending so much time hidden deep in the threshes of the Brazilian rainforest, seen by none but a handful of humble natives, it was a breath of fresh air to be recognized and fawned over by an adoring public. He enjoyed the recognition. Never minded someone approaching for a friendly chat, or even an autograph. Totally fine with screaming “YOU’VE BEEN TARGETED FOR DARCINATION! But do leave a message…” into some grateful mark’s cell phone to serve as the hottest voicemail recording this side of East Bay. But he couldn’t stand the feeling of just being stared at. Deep down, it reminded him of his old job at the local bagel shop, and how he absolutely loathed being forced to wear a name tag, enabling any Joe Schmoe off the street to call him by name like they’d been acquainted for years. It was different inside a wrestling ring, where people paid good money to watch him work his craft. But the outside world was a different story, and he could always count on getting the chills if he thought someone was staring at him, no matter how hard he tried to quell them. The incident with Jane and the kidnappers several months back only made things worse. As far as he knew, his assailants who had come perilously close to taking not only his life, not only Jane’s, but the girl he was now slated to marry, were still at large. And since they’d escaped on…less than positive terms, to say the least, it stood an excellent chance that they might be looking for him in an effort to exact revenge. Shake it off, Darcy…he told himself, in an effort to chase the less than desirable thoughts from his consciousness. It also stood to reason that they weren’t looking for him, and in fact had no business with him at all; after all, the lead henchman in the affair had asserted that Jane’s family had paid the ransom, which in truth was the dirty money owed to them by an out-of-control father with a gambling problem. And even if they were looking for him…the last time they saw him, the last time they’d had him in their clutches, to be precise, he’d negotiated his way out of trouble with flying colors. Those mob cronies may have been sinister, but they were about as effective as Bebop & Rocksteady. Besides, here and now, he had nothing to worry about in a public place. And not just any public place…the Greatest Place on Earth. Darcy’s Disneyland. With a sheepish grin, he bolted for the door to catch up to Johnny before he found him convulsing on the ground with seizures of pure joy. And if there was anything Darcy hated more than being stared at…it was being called a penis pump by Johnny all day. *** There he is. That sniveling little prick. He was a fool for ever showing his face on a camera again. I thought he learned his lesson by dropping off the face of the earth all those years ago. But now he has the GALL to show himself again…to parade himself around for everyone to see, as if he were God’s gift to creation itself. I can still feel the pain shooting through my shoulder like it was just yesterday. It haunts me every day…every minute of my horrible existence. He ruined my life…and as God as my witness, he’ll pay for it. No, no, no…now isn’t the time. I could see it in the way he was standing there, nervously shooting his eyes around. He’s a tricky little devil, far too clever for an uncalculated ambush. I merely had to come here to confirm it for myself, with my own eyes…yes, it’s him. Still making a mockery out of what happened to me, turning the worst day of my life into his own personal glory trip. The great Darcy Crisis…the very thought makes me want to vomit with rage. But I’ll put an end to this, once and for all. From this moment forth, wherever he goes, I won’t be far behind. I’ll be on that son of a bitch like a hawk, watching, waiting…poised for the one moment he lets his guard down. And when he does, I’ll be there to strike like a cobra. And the best part of all…he’ll never even see it coming
