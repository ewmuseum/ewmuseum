Disfigured Mutations 0-1: A String of Synonyms Makes a Fine Noose

12 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 12, 2006 3:33 AM CST I hadn’t slept in near a week, and I didn’t miss it much. Insomniac delusions were well worth the measures of deprivation required to attain such a state of somatic euphoria. The mind has a thirst for dreams, and without sleep, life becomes a dream. The mind of the insomniac merges the two worlds into one where the tangible and the imaginary knot together into one indecipherable mesh. I was at that point, and I was quite enjoying myself, though you never would have been able to tell from the look of me. The skin underneath my eyes began to form bags that I could have used as change purses. My hair was greasily matted all to one side, and all of my joints had tensed up so that I had to drag myself along, clasping and pulling onto ledges, and then when there were no ledges, the shoulders of fellow pedestrians at crosswalks. Those damned street-crossers took no pity on my condition and usually tried to flag me off in the middle of the road. I had thus far averted disaster but had only narrowly escaped falling into an open manhole. There were all of these goddamned open manholes all over the street, and I had lost all faith in my hamstrings. Some time ago, I had confidence in my jumping ability. I was a hurdler by trade and never shied away from any obstacle, be it an overturned trash can, a misplaced log on the sidewalk, or a mysterious chasm in the earth. In my spryer days, I would have been foolishly secure with such circumstances, but I was weathered now and overcome by the fear of falling through spots in the earth that had disappeared. Worse yet, were the spots in the sky. I was always skeptical that the world might one day run out of gravity, and the holes in the sky completely worsened my anxiety. A seaman had told me once that the sea was a wan mistress, and if I had ever met a spaceman, I would assume he’d feel very similarly about gravity. I never wanted to meet a spaceman, though, as there comes a certain celebrity with space travel. Astronauts always came off as smug and self-absorbed narcissists to me; their conversations laced with esoteric, mathematical jargon. Still, they would be helpful right now at repairing the holes in the sky. And there I sat, crumpled up like balled paper, in the middle of the road underneath my pants and jacket. The cars whizzed by me on both sides as I was immobilized luckily between the lanes. I waited for the light to turn and slid across the rest of the way by gripping onto the ankles of several passersby. Most of them only slid me a step or two before shaking me loose, and then I would grab onto another set of ankles. It all felt very underwater, like I was an eel. My body itself felt eely; my bones were very agreeable to being dragged. I overanalyzed the situation; a habitual weakness of mine that oftentimes lands me in a variety of sordid entanglements. I began to panic, feeling so eely and all, because sea creatures lack ankles and therefore the capacity to comprehend them. The passing ankles confused me as I was sent into a tailspin of grief and delusion at the sight of the bony protrusions that poked out from materials of socks or, worse yet, the bottoms of bare shins. I turned down to my own ankles and pulled the legs of my trousers up to expose them. The air felt cold against the ankles, and I rubbed them for warmth. The stony bulbs attaching my legs and feet were wondrous to me. I still, however, had no clue as to how to use them, though I knew that I had the ability. I was conscious of my own mutations as I rubbed my ankles. My fingers were beginning to feel fizzy, and I could see cells dissipating from my fingertips if I held my hand close enough to my face. At this rate, given roughly forty six thousand, twenty one days, my body would completely disappear into a flutter of subatomic particles. This thought was highly unnerving. I vomited a little, there. I don’t think anyone saw. I saw movement in the puddle of gastrointestinal refuse, but I tried not to call any attention to myself. Indeed, this corrosion was the work of parasites! “Damn them!” I muttered as the specks too small to hold identifiable color swam around in nondescript patterns. It was a wonder that they were all so small; that functioning organisms could be gelatinously contained in such microscopic encapsulations. Perhaps this is what made insects such a hallucinogenic focal point. The altered mind is magnetically distracted by such moving dots. The mind of an insomniac, mind you, works in much the same way. After twenty hours without sleep, the insomniac becomes lethargic, exhibiting depressed behavioral patterns and a decline in physical function. After twenty seven hours, the body is falsely recharged- fueled by a sleepless amphetamine, probably released by some gland along the brainstem. At thirty six hours, the mind returns to the previous state experience at twenty hours. This repetition, however, is a bit more extreme after the false alertness stemming from twenty seven hours fades off. After forty five hours, sleep deprivation acts as a hallucinogen. The mind cannot separate real stimuli from imagined stimuli. Both exist in juxtaposition as the insomniac at this point is fully awake and fully asleep at the same time in a confusingly whole complex consciousness. After fifty four hours, I’m not sure what happens, but I suppose I’ll find out fairly soon. Thus, sleep deprivation is a drug and a relatively inexpensive one at that. All it costs is your sanity. I couldn’t resist. I had heard several people from certain social circles (mainly drunken ones) claim that people have died from lack of sleep. It seemed like an urban legend to me at the time. I had certainly never heard of someone overdosing on sleep deprivation. From this point, I decided to document my findings for the name of science. I kept a small notebook in my jacket pocket in which I usually recorded limericks as I thought of them. The limericks were oftentimes in multiple languages. I did this to keep my mind sharp and expand my rhyming palate. Nonetheless, I flipped past to the first empty page when I found myself without a pen or a pencil. “That’s just my rotten luck!” I called out, slapping at my pockets with futile battery. “I suppose none of you fuckers would have a pen or a pencil that I could borrow?” But the pedestrians continued on as if I didn’t exist; or perhaps as if none of them existed. I opted instead to speak my findings into my watch. There was no recording mechanism inside of it, and to be honest, it didn’t even tell the time. It was perennially stuck at twenty minutes past the seven. Still, I felt better about pretending to try something guaranteed to be fallible than having not pretended to try at all. *** “Hour forty eight,” I spoke into my wrist. I was a bit off before. I forgot I didn’t wear a watch, so I spoke into my wrist. I found this paradoxical. Without a timepiece, there was no telling what hour it was. Still, I felt as if I could gauge the passage of time by some force more powerful than estimation. It was in the air and in things like the temperature and the strength of people’s breathing. “From the southwest, a clock faced man walks with shrunken human heads wrapped around his wrist. The time on his face is indistinguishable, his hands poking out of his sleeves and not pointing towards one particular feature or another until he gets an itch.” I spoke in addled hallucinogenic code. The clock faced man was one of many things that looked both ways before emerging or submerging into the blind spots along the ground or sky. “The temperature is no longer constant. Instead, I find when I walk in certain directions, it becomes suddenly frigid and in others it oscillates back into a sweating warmth. I think it has something to do with the size of things. There seems to be some sort of correlation, but it’s incalculable.” I moved in the fashion of a knight in chess, taking two steps in one direction and then hooking one in either perpendicular way, or taking one first and then two next. The combinations seemed dependant on the way I turned and in which way I would turn first. If I went forward two steps and left one, I would get smaller and colder. If I went right instead, I would get smaller and warmer. If I went forward only one step and then turned to my left two, I would get larger and colder, and if I went forward one step and then took two steps to the right, I would get larger and warmer. I had to plan my steps accordingly to keep things at a relatively normal size and at a core temperature that would keep my body from freezing or overheating. This kept me walking in a distractingly diagonal path as I continued to document the changes I saw in the scenery and the population around me. “My eyes appear to be playing nasty tricks on me. I see distortions in people’s faces, but it isn’t a misplaced, sideways eye or a disproportionate, angular nose like you would expect from a Picasso painting. Instead, I see a new concavity in faces, the features sucked into central walls of suctioning anuses that squeeze into puckered centers. Others have an opposing convexity, the nose growing with Pinocchio beaks that expand and pull the mouth and the eyes along the olfactory bridge.” All of this was too much to process as I walked in my right angle patterns, forming non-equilateral hypotenuses all along the walkways. “I imagine this new evolutionary stage in humankind redefines gender roles. Those with the convex faces take on the seeding aspect of reproduction while the fertilization takes place after some facial transfer of appropriate fluids.” I felt my face, but could not gather whether it pointed outward or inward. I assumed that I was either genderless or had not yet developed the glands necessary to dispense my multiplicative oats. I was sure I was male, though; damn sure of it! Before the forty-ninth hour, my knighted stepping was interrupted by a collision with another one of the blasted commuters popping in and out of the blotchy blind spots; this time black holed on a building’s wall. I was thrust backwards and therefore shrunk a bit. I spoke more into my wrist. “Hour forty eight point five; I stand before a man. His face is fittingly convex, but he wears his top hat over the front and has an antenna crowning from his occipital crest. He controls his movements with a handheld remote; presumably his speech as well. He is very sharply dressed but smells of a variety of genital salves and confabulated percentages.” “Why is that you, Howard?” he asked me, bending down to look into my face with a penetrable glint in his beaked eyes. “I think you have me mistaken for someone else.” “No. The resemblance is too uncanny! There is absolutely no way that you are not Howard. My, you’ve become a gentleman and a scholar now, haven’t you? I remember you being this tall.” He held one hand very low and one hand very high so that my current height was somewhere in between. As I moved around edgily, my height rose and sank, but never above or below the extremes of his extremities. I saw no way out of this predicament. “I guess it has been a while. It’s been a while since anyone’s called me Howard, but you can call me Howard if you like.” “Yes. I would prefer to, Howard. It’s funny running into you here. I was just at the bank to dispute an error in my statement.” “What’s so funny about it?” I asked, the arching of my eyebrows triggering a paranoid worry that my face was pulling itself inward to a conical vagina. The man chuckled a bit. I still didn’t know his name, but there were ways around that. “Always a comedian, aren’t you, Howard?” He toyed with the remote, and his head tilted backwards as he laughed, the top hat now standing correctly vertical, but somehow there was some certain incorrectness to it all. Perhaps it was the vestibule where his chin should be that allowed a porous, soupy sound to drip out from the inner yolk of his face. He tilted his face back forward and keyed in his speech. “Some things never change. What do you say we catch up on old times over something hard. It’ll be just like old times!” “It’s barely the afternoon,” I argued. “It’s much too early to drink.” That, of course, was a lie, but it seemed like a fair enough refusing excusal. “Oh, come on! Don’t be a pill and have some sips with me!” And then he inexorably pulled me by the arm, down into one of the opaque holes in the ground. It felt like my body was permeating through a skin rather than falling through emptiness, but I precisely shredded through this placental barricade without so much as a scratch of remembrance for having felt any brushing discomfort. *** “Hour fifty, approaching hour fifty one; I find it quite disconcerting that the sky here shows no signs of having fallen through a hole. There is no upper layer to remind me that I was once concentrically above where I am now. It’s as if the previous layer of earth has peeled off in the habit of an onion. In addition, I have found that the stranger who dragged me here has an irritating affliction that causes him to refer to everyone as ‘Howard.’ The only drink at the bar is not a liquid, but rather some kind of wax, and the bartender is a completely hairless eunuch, save for his authoritatively large arms covered in hair even on the curve of his biceps and the sag of his elbow skin. He wears an apron. I genuinely dislike him and would pick a fight with him if it wasn’t for his arms.” The bartender perked up at this, having heard me communicating into my wrist. The skin underneath where eyebrows should have been scrunched into a scowl around his inversion of facial features. In the center of the concavity, a nub poked out as if his once powerfully angular convection had been forcefully plunged and packaged into its current, androgynous build. It was still recognizably male, though, while being tryingly feminine. It made me uncomfortable. I stopped until he looked back down at a glass of half imbibed wax that he was scraping out. I sighed and continued once I was comfortable again, and the damned eunuch wasn’t eying me. “In addition to the current complications of my sleeplessness, I have been experiencing some other rather strange phenomena. Anything wiry turns to snakes on the sight of it. More pressingly, though, there are two miniscule narrators that have attached themselves to me here and are reporting what they perceive to be my thoughts with a false omniscience. I have found that I can stomp on them, but they only flatten and slowly inflate themselves again, even if you step on them both simultaneously, which I have tried twice.” I tried walking forward one step and then left two and then forward one step and then right two, but I wasn’t gaining much ground, and the narrators would only grow in proportion to me. I was getting uncomfortably large (though my temperature remained fairly constant), and I now had to hunch to maneuver around the room while standing. This made planning my movements all the more difficult, and I was in quite a rough spot trying to calculate my steps to shrink myself down to a normal height due to my bigness. “Why, old Garbage Bag Johnny has gone delightfully mad!” one of the narrators said while waving his tiny, flaccid arms above his head. “He’s had too much wax, I say! He’s gone blithered!” added the second. “I have not!” I argued. “I haven’t even had any wax. You’re slandering me!” “If I’m slandering you, then why is everything wiry turning into snakes?” The little son of a bitch had a point. I couldn’t argue with him even though I hadn’t had a glass of wax. Sure, the man who called everyone Howard ordered two glasses, but he drank his and then mine before I could even consider going about how to consume it. “Howard!” he called the bartender over, “Another round of wax for me and my friend, here!” The eunuch came over and topped us off with more wax. The man who called everyone Howard downed his wax skillfully, and I could tell he was glinting with a belligerent desire to consume more. He had a bad habit, and I guess he still couldn’t knock it. “Nursing that one, I see?” he rhetorically asked while sliding my glass in towards himself and beginning to polish it off. “Hey, stop taking my drinks,” I admonished. I made no attempt to retrieve the glass, though. This was only a matter of principle. “Well, I certainly wasn’t going to let them go to waste!” he defended himself. “Let him have it,” chirped in one of the tiny narrators. “Why, you’ve already gone saucer-eyed already!” I turned to the tiny narrator, my obtuse head pointed down at him through gaunt, acutely bent knees. “I have not!” “You have too,” backed the second. “And your breath reeks of horrid wax. I think you’ve had enough.” I turned to my drinking compatriot after the very informative sidebar with the narrators. At this point, I had agreed with them or at least been outvoted to agree with them. The problem was that they outnumbered me, and I was trying to be in my best democratic manners at the time. I didn’t want anyone seeing me act out of sorts in a place where I was regarded a stranger. “Sir, I hate to cut the night short, but I’ve had a bit too much wax, and it’s getting a bit late.” He slapped me on the back with the bottom teeth of a hearty grin baring themselves beneath the brim of his hat. “I suppose so, Howard. I’ve got to tuck in early myself.” He controlled his one hand to extend to shake mine, and I would have been prepared to part ways there, but I was at a loss for an exit. There were no holes, mind you, and there were no doors in this place that went out- only those that went in. Instead, I just stood there shuffling my feet awkwardly (they bent just so that I couldn’t have them both facing the same direction). “Yes, Howard?” “Which way was the exit again?” I chose these words fairly carefully. He had never showed me where the exit was before, but if I acted as if though he already had, he might not be so hesitant to show me in the first place. “Ah! I forgot there wasn’t one.” He noticed my apprehension. To make things worse, I had just spotted two things wiry. “It’ll be fine. Just crawl up into my vestibule, and I’ll hatch you out.” “Sounds a bit messy, but I guess it’s fair.” I shrugged as he tilted his head back, exposing his oily vestibule. It looked small at first until I walked forward two steps and left and then forward two steps and then right so that I’d fit more easily inside of it. The skin surrounding the hole was easily maneuverable. It felt papery, but it had a binding quality to it so that it would not tear unintentionally. “Hour fifty one and a quarter; I have made my way into the yolk of the vestibule. It smells like sulfur, and even in a less heightened state, I find it difficult to move based on the unfortunate location of my joints. They’ve been cursed with the ability to only curve one way and not the other. I feel fluid movement as he tilts. The air in here is made of a thick embryonic goop, but there are air pouches that rise every so often. Unfortunately, they’re from the discharge of my other orifices.” *** There’s not much to do inside an egg but peck at the walls, and this is a timely and uninteresting process, so I’ll spare you the details. *** “Hour fifty four; here it finally is. It is the hour of my hatching, and I cannot tell if I am back to where I once was and where I am supposed to be. I think this is the world I would recognize had I any recollection of it, but now it seems as if everything is drawn in crayon. People have little dot eyes and upside-down seven noses, but they have no mouths, and all inanimate things are wearing smiles and sunglasses- things like the sun and that mailbox over there.” I felt good. There was an encompassing goodness to the smell of crayon, and it was all over the pavement and the grass and even thick in the air, making it hard to breathe. But all the feeling went away when I saw the man there, his well dressed body slumped over the sidewalk with his egghead cracked open at the neck. A sickening yellow-orange mixed over the gray sidewalk, filling up the aqueduct black cracks that ran from grass to grass. My mouth went dry- like I was chewing on the bark of a cactus. A burnt sienna colored over the periwinkle around me and up into the sky. It was odd, seeing everything through a tint of one certain color or another. Normally, there are no color screens in vision. Where exactly does the blueness of the sky begin? It didn’t matter anymore. Philip, the crown prince of sunset, was making his way down the walk on his evening roundabout. He wore a lot of burrs on his princely black singlet. “Oh, look what you’ve gone and done! Murther!” “It wasn’t me! I swear!” “You’re covered in his yolky substances!” The crown prince of sunset had a high voice; nearly alto. It spiked with flutters and drew low to a sweepingly long decrescendo. “And you smell like you’re waxy!” “It was an accident. He told me he’d hatch me from his vestibule!” I was breaking into a cold sweat. “That’s impossible!” the prince asserted. “You can’t be hatched from a vestibule!” “Sure, I can. Why couldn’t I?” “You haven’t filled out the proper forms!” I didn’t even know there were proper forms to fill out. I knew I shouldn’t have slacked off on learning the new penal codes. It always came back to bite me in the ass. I searched for a veritable escape crayoned in somewhere, but I couldn’t come up with one in time. He blew a shrill whistle that swelled in pitch and timbre until it resonated off of the walls and found its way through every nook in the vicinity. “Guards!” he commanded. Several brutish oafs showed up in full uniform. They carried impressive hooked staffs with blades at the end- one that pointed out and one that curved at a necking angle the shape of a half crest. I let them drag me off in shackles. Though I provided no resistance, they still insisted on humiliating me by dragging me backwards by the arms as though I had. All the while, children lined up on the sides of the streets to lob tomatoes at me. Luckily, children have notoriously bad aim, and the only vegetable that managed to hit me was an accidental turnip that had snuck its way into one of the tomato baskets that families kept at the base of their driveways. “Hour fifty six or fifty seven; the prison that I’ve been assigned to has no bars or locks. All it has are walls, but the walls are arranged in such a random, disconnected fashion that I there was nothing physically barricading me inside. It was my lack of sleep that didn’t allow me to leave. It was the mumbling of the walls. They talked to each other, and I couldn’t pass through the sound waves. They were lullaby waves, and if you tried walking past one, you would fall asleep forever. You’d fall asleep anyway, unless your will was made of iron. The walls would get to you eventually.” There were couches here and there. I tried to avoid them. Luckily, my cellmate was there to keep me entertained. He was ninety six years old and hadn’t been to sleep since his imprisonment. He was in here for dancing a one man rigadoon naked in public. He was possessed by the quick duple meter and had rigadooned across a stretch of road three quarter miles long. His public exposure was nary the issue. Rather, rigadooning had been outlawed years prior, and it was a law that the federalists took rather seriously. He preferred not to talk about rigadooning anymore, though. He had learned to regret what he had done, but he never forgot what the gravity of his punishment would be had he let his guard down. “Don’t let the waves get to you, boy. If you fall asleep here, you might never wake up.” I couldn’t tell if the old man’s presence was a side effect of being past hour fifty four or if he had indeed been awake much longer than I had. He appeared worse for the wear. He had long passed the phases I was at and now walked in more complex patterns with great futility as his body parts would either be way too large or way too small, and his tooth grew in and retracted back into his gums with certain steps. Most of the time, he only had one oversized tooth, and his gums appeared to be overcrowded so that he could never close his mouth. His tooth shined out at me, calling for my uninterrupted focalization. “It’s something in the sound waves that affects your brain. It turns certain switches on and off so that you live in a dream until you pass on.” “There are worse ways to go, though.” “Are there? It’d be hell for you. You’re a young man. You could be helplessly watching yourself drift along for decades.” “But that doesn’t sound so much different from what I’m doing now without sleeping.” “But at least you have control of yourself. And if you fall asleep long enough, the guards come and lay bricks around you. All these walls have people trapped inside them- people who fell asleep and never woke up. But I’m sure they’re awake now. Why do you think the walls make sounds?” “That puts it all in perspective. I have no desire to become paved into a wall.” “Well, is there any way out?” “I’ve been trying to devise a pattern of steps for years, but the sequence is too complex, and I keep forgetting parts, so I have to go back and refigure them every time I miscalculate.” We then knelt down, and he went over all the steps he remembered with me, but fuck all if I was going to remember them. The steps were very complicated indeed. With some, you had to land in certain angles, and in others, you had to step forward with one foot and backward with the other. There were times where you’d have to step twice with one foot and times where you would have to step down with your knees on the ground and your feet pointed upwards. “I’ve taught you all I know. You’re on your own from here.” And then we both continued on, trying to find new combinations and hidden patterns in the sequences we stepped, but neither of us had any luck. I saw him taking steps towards the couch, and he stood in front of it and looked at it a long while before deciding to lie down. “What the hell are you doing?” I asked him. “I’m lying down. I figure I may as well get some rest now that you’re working on your steps.” “I thought you said that if you go to sleep, you don’t wake up.” “Yeah, but I’m old, and my feet are tired.” “What about the walls?” “I suppose I’ll just get used to them. I used to be a bricklayer myself. I much enjoyed the smell of mortar. Perhaps it’ll remind me of when I was young.” And just like that, he fell asleep, and I was left stepping out the most convoluted patterns I could think of. I was sure thinking about the old man, but I forgot he was sleeping there fairly shortly, so I thought about him as if he wasn’t there at all. Soon, the guards were there to build him into a wall, but after they were done, I never heard any sound come from behind it. *** “Hour sixty three; I thought about the barflies who claimed that people could die from lack of sleep. Then there’s the old man who told me I’d die if I fell asleep. And here I am, walking in patterns comparable to the fractional portion of pi. They couldn’t both be right. Why was I more inclined to believe the old man? What do the elderly know about death? They know nothing! Ha! Only the dead know anything about death. What was the noise within the walls? It had to be from the living, even had it been just reverberant snoring. Perhaps I would like being trapped in a wall. It was better than remembering numbers and walking in counted steps and turning.” So I sat there. There was no harm in sitting, was there? And I thought again about the old man’s words, and how eventually they would ring true and how he hadn’t actually told me that sleeping directly lead to death. Still, the ballsy fool knew nothing about death, and he walked for decades until he finally gave in and marched onwards with the bullheaded confidence of a man much younger than me. And the bastard walked off only after consigning me to the same tiredly drudging fate. After walking and thinking for decades, my head would undoubtedly grow large from cranial exercise as would my legs from their physical taxation leaving my uppers and lowers to crush my atrophied torso; thin as a straw of hay between two bulbous entities. I’d have to go to sleep now, to find him and tell him, “Hey, you son of a bitch! You think you can just leave me walking there for years?” And then, I tell you what, I’d hit him right in the jaw. It was a rash decision, but I had made several rash decisions before, and everything had turned out alright; for the better, even. There was another couch nearby, a brownish gray number that was well past her prime, but what she lacked in luster, she made up in a cushiony experience. I tried to ignore her fluffy gashes. Her arms were busily large and solid too as if there were mineral deposits balling up inside. She was of an awkward size so that I’d either have to lay flat with my feet in the air or shift upwards so that my neck bent sorely upwards. Nonetheless, this was more than sufficient. If you’re really tired, and you think about how tired you are, you’ll fall asleep fairly easily. If you’re not tired, this doesn’t work as well even if you think you are. I tried to hold onto clarity a little longer, chanting mantras about how I was going to be lost in a dream and how I’d have to realize I was dreaming so I could find the old man and pop him one. I wanted to be as awake as I could possibly be for this because I have a tendency to forget my dreams, and this whole chain of events would have been utterly pointless if I weren’t to remember it. “I don’t know how long it’s been in a place where hours are obsolete. I’ve become immersed in a dream, and I’ve spent so much time awake that I’ve forgotten the purpose of dreams altogether. I only have the capacity to dream what’s already happened. It’s worse then all hell, and I’ve been trying to do anything I can to intervene. It’s just worse then all hell.” There we were, me and the old bag, back in our imprisoned stepping routine. It was then, in a moment of clarity, that I noticed the brilliance of its encoding. It was indeed impossible for one man to walk the correct combination. One man could walk for a lifetime and never figure it out. It was damned perplexing, and it could drive a man mad. How long had I been walking earlier? Was it only a few hours, or was it something far greater than that? Which one of us was the old man, and which one of us was the young man? “Damn it!” I thought rather loudly, thinking it was too late, but perhaps it wasn’t. Here we both were, walking in our own attempts to further the initial correct steps and build upon them. I had to will myself to tell him; tell him that both of us had to walk in coordinated patterns to silence the damned walls and release ourselves from this incarcerated mania. I tried to call out to him, but he mulled around, concentrated on the floor panels and counting out loud to himself. “Damn it all!” I thought again. The son of a bitch could talk all he wanted, but all he’d say was numbers. And here I was, unable to say anything even though I knew what would be the answer. My body kept walking around and turning, and I kept dodging the rising and falling integers that cycled through my unconscious thought while my lucidity pressed onward, stuck in a blizzard of numerical proportions. I strained my throat, trying to push sound out of my vocal chords, but I could only push out air, and with all this effort, my unconscious body became distracted and started to give up more easily. I unsuccessfully tried to will my body back away from the couch, but it plowed past my neural alerts, step by step, as if it was dragging bulky anchors behind. The body lied down on the couch, and I resisted sleeping inside my dream with all the might I could muster. I focused all my attention to my throat and lungs and let out the most harping siren I could muster, shrieking obscenities in common tongues so that the nameless old shit would give me his ears. All that came out, however, was a little pip sound that he could’ve mistaken for some sort of chirping insect if he had heard it at all. But his ears! They perked like animalistic radar! He turned to me. “Did you say something?” I tried to respond, but the pip had taken about everything out of me. Instead, I lay there, plastered to the couch’s backrest, my arms reaching out and grabbing as if there was a sharp wind coming at me. I tried to say yes, but my mouth wouldn’t form the words and my tongue, the sounds. Instead, I just sat there, looking like I was chewing on a ball of peanut butter as he inched closer. “Did you say something?” He came closer, and it was the damnedest thing. Before I could even fail to respond, his body started to diffuse into a black smoke. Everything around me had changed to an open sky on barren ground. The sky was gray with a tint that signified neither night nor day. There was no sun or moon, and there never had been. There were no trees, but there were shadows of branches, and his voice echoed a few times before fading off into a smoke, albeit a tinier one, just like he had. Suddenly, my vocal disabilities were revoked, and the force of my scream was strong enough to tear at the edges of my mouth as it all rushed out at once. The lingering cloud moved like paramecium. It hovered above me, and its intent was clear to pass through me. I anticipated this with a paralyzed fear, and there was little I could do as it overcame me. Everything stopped inside of me except the insurmountable terror. This was death coming over me, and I knew it. Fuck it all if it wasn’t death. What would there be left to fear after this? But the mess of spirit flushed through me eventually, and I choked it out of myself, and it flew away off where I couldn’t see. My relief was only temporary in nature, though, as these peppering drifters floated sharply through the colorless sky. There were hordes of them, like stings of angry bees! The terrible fuckers were everywhere, and as they passed overhead, the ones that were close enough would enter me again, and I’d go through the same fear of death until they left, and it never got any better until the blackness passed over, and I was left on the couch, breathing heavily and still unable to move, regaining sensation. It was then that I realized that both my eyes were open throughout the whole ordeal.
