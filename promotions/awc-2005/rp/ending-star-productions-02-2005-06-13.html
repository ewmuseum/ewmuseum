Date: 06/13/05 …The Following is a Presentation… …Of… …Ending Star Productions… Copywrite 2004-2005. ___________________________________________________________ There are certain reasons I find my situation funny. For example; I leave for a few weeks and suddenly rheumatism dominates the Atlantic Wrestling Club. Out of absolutely nowhere, this decaying shell of a wrestler begins to dominate what should have been all mine. Out of absolutely nowhere, this guy takes contendership of two championships. And all this happens while I’m on a small sabbatical. BUT that’s not all, is it?! No, of course it isn’t. That wouldn’t have been enough to push me over the edge, would it? We do have to honor our senior citizens after all (zing,) what really gets me pissed is the fact that you have to have some sort of abnormality to become something of a name in the Atlantic Wrestling Club. “Our” world champ-equivalent has an ever-growing superiority complex; “our” two number one contenders are a college student and a guy who openly admits to being a social menace. “Our” Relentless Champion is a miniscule closet homosexual. And another one of “our” stars has a mental deficiency. What’s a normy to do? Do you see what I’m implying? Grandpa and the homo brigade here is basically taking what belongs to me, but hey, who here is to blame? Why, it’s me, of course. Christopher Ashton, the final claim to greatness emanating from so-called “prestigious” Ashton family. Yeah, it’s me; it’s my fault I wasn’t at Fresh! for two weeks running. It was my fault, that Harber backed me out of the match with Pact, the match that should have elevated me in the eyes of staff, but instead only elevated the spokesperson for Viagra in Harber’s eyes. And that was all because of one man. Christopher Ashton. Christopher Ashton pissed everyone backstage off by not cooperating and doing what he was told to do, Christopher Ashton failed to change his rebellious behavior and was removed from the card where he was supposed to face the veteran, Pact. And when asked, no, better yet, ordered to arrive at Fresh! the following week for a meeting with AWC authorities, Christopher Ashton not only didn’t show up, but he didn’t even call with an excuse to explain why he wasn’t at the weekly AWC program. As far as I’m concerned Christopher Ashton has no one else to blame for the success of other AWC employees, but himself. And he’s done it again this week. Christopher Ashton has played on my nerves, restlessly, in the past few weeks, refusing to show up at events even when it would benefit him. Now I don’t plan to show Mr. Ashton any mercy. There is now only one way out of the predicament he finds himself in, and that predicament is a man named Hate. If Ashton refuses to show up at the next AWC broadcast, he will be ejected from the Atlantic Wrestling Club, with absolutely no hope of returning. Now that I think about it, I see that I was too soft with Mr. Ashton. He’s shown all signs of being unreliable in the past, and it was stupid to think that he would abandon his personal life just for a “meaningless strap of gold” in Chris’s own words. Well, as I said, he doesn’t really have a choice now. Or so says David “Pearl” Harber. The truth is that’s this whole fiasco revolving around me is just his attempt to blow off some steam from his on-going war with Transatlantic Champion Alexander Strider. Only I don’t go down without a fight. David Harber can de-book me, he can suspend me, he can blow his proverbial load of problems with me on my answering machine, hell, he can even fire me. The truth is, I just don’t care. I might have cared if this was my early Neon career; I might have cared if this was my career in Japan, a few years back. I would have definitely cared before I met a guy named Pierre H.. Here’s the bottom line, Pearl. I’m not your ordinary Ashton. I’m not some simple tool you can use to boost credibility and ratings for AWC, just to put cash in your pocket. I’m not some toy you can show off to your friends and then toss against the wall to relieve your anger, because one of the people you know crossed you or made you cry or played you for a fool. I’m not about to be your puppet, I’m neither ready nor willing to throw myself into your whorehouse for an easy buck, unlike Pact, or Pierce Lavelle, or Hate, or even Kris Krimzon. I’ll do everything in my power to make your professional life a living, breathing, hell. Would you like to know why, David? It’s because I, unlike you, have absolutely nothing to lose anymore. You’ve taken every bit of respect I had for your federation, and flushed it straight down the crapper. You’ve managed to drain away any ambitions I’ve had in your federations, both competition and championships, that need for gold I once had is gone. There’s only one thing left in my mind, David. That thought is you. Consider this the shot heard around AWC. Consider this the assassination of the arch-duke. Consider this your deadline for retreat. Because in reality that’s what it really is. After this Tuesday, the AWC will never be what it once was. It’ll never be that great environment for talent you dreamt of. AWC will be a kingdom. A kingdom of fear and destruction, a kingdom built on the legacies of countless generations. A kingdom fitted for only one king, only one true heir; a king that truly knows the meaning of greatness. This will be the final frontier “your” creation will live to witness, this kingdom, this legacy. …My Legacy. Consider me the cancer, Pearl. And, David, I’m not planning on going anytime soon. Send your first wave, you aren’t going to halt me. Send your entire roster against me, put a bounty on my head. Start trying to kill me, David; because I don’t plan on resting until I defeat you. Your huge threat is just words. After what I’ve been through, I’m not afraid of any human being anymore, and when you come to Hate’s root, that is all it is. Human. Prepare, David Harber… The war has begun. ___________________________________________________________ June 10, 2005 6:08 A.M Narration: Alyson Rivers Location: Seattle, Washington. “Chris, Chris… CHRIS!” My eyes snap open and I shoot up into a sitting position. I look around the penthouse apartment bedroom, the only safe-house I’ve received since my proverbial “deal with the devil.” I then look to my left at the other half of the king-sized bed my new-found money has brought me. No Chris. “Of course,” I say in a hoarse whisper, yawning and rubbing my eyes. Of course there’s no Chris here in our bed. Of course there’s no Chris in the penthouse he managed to buy me. Of course there’s no Chris in the apartment he bought for himself, as what he calls “a security measure.” I’ll even bet that there’s no Chris in Seattle; or for that matter, North America. I swear he does this just to get on my nerves. He’ll disappear for weeks at a time and then come back out of the blue completely disheartened and worn-out, as though he’s been through hell. It would always be the exact same look he had a few months back when we were running from my step-father, the reason I changed my last name. Everything revolved around that. I thought our troubles were over after that, I thought we could just go back to the way things were before the dilemma with the Yakuza happened; I thought Chris would just go back to wrestling. “Yeah, right,” I scoff softly, not realizing my own bitterness. Chris changed after that. He was no longer the happy-go-lucky guy that everybody would love to be around. He turned cold, amazingly cold. He would let no one in on his “business,” he called it. But a few weeks after each of his “business trips” a large sum of money would appear in our bank account. And when I would ask Chris, he would just close off from me, changing the subject as quickly as he could without me even realizing he did. And after a while I just stopped asking. It just doesn’t quite add up. The additional money we receive from Chris’s business trips always fluctuates, it seems that most times the longer he’s been gone the less money we receive, but even this isn’t consistent. It’s just weird, that’s what it is. “It just doesn’t completely add up.” I swing my legs off of the bed and place myself into a sitting position. I run my hands through my hair and give an exasperated sigh. “Why must you do this?” I whisper again, as though talking to Chris. I sigh again and stand up. As I walk to the bathroom a repressed thought slinks its way back into the foreground of my mind. “What if the ordeal with my step-father never really ended, what if Chris just thrust me out of it and dove deeper in?” Yeah, that’s what I’ve been dwelling on the past few weeks, that’s what has been eating at the back of my mind, the part where all the repressed thoughts and memories go, the part that’s been under titanium lock and key up until recently. Now all those memories and thoughts have been triggered. I’m reliving my teenage years yet again. Only now, it’s as if I am alone. It’s no longer as easy as it was when I was a teen, at least then I had Chris to confide in, now I don’t even have him. “Damn you, you selfish bastard,” I spout bitterly, beginning to shed tears. I look down into the bowl of the marble sink and then at the mirror. I see the reflection of myself in the mirror, the reflection of utter loneliness. I tap marked cold, and let the water run for a bit while I stare at myself in the hanging mirror. The truth is that I don’t know who I’m looking at, I don’t know who I am anymore. I’m in my twenties and I’m going through an identity crisis, what a straightforward life I lead, huh? I know change happens, and I can accept that, but who am I changing into? I look down, cupping my hands together under the ice cold water and splash it on my face, after which I look back into the mirror. “I guess money can’t buy happiness.” May 18th, 2005 12:06 A.M Narration: The normal narrator Location: Seattle International Airport. Just a few hours removed from his last “Fresh!” appearance in just about a month Christopher Ashton, dressed in formal attire, is seen pacing around a crowded airport, or at least crowded for this time of night. Ashton has his cell phone to his ear, he is mumbling incoherently. There is a single blue-gray duffle bag in one of the airport seats; a pair of wrestling tights can be seen emerging from the bag. Ashton seems to be infuriated by something. “You can’t be serious!” his voice noting exclamation even though he’s speaking in a whisper. Ashton stops pacing after he says that. “Yeah, I know I said I’d be right there, but…” A look of annoyance passes the eyes of “The New Age Star” as he kicks the ground softly. “I thought you could handle it, apparently I was wrong, look I’m heading over there now, just sit tight,” Chris says, trying to end the conversation abruptly. Chris walks over to his duffle bag and takes a seat next to it. He reaches in and pulls out another cell phone, this one Motorola, instead of the Verizon one he is using currently. Chris’s look of annoyance is replaced with one of anger. “Look, it’s not my fault you fucked up. You don’t like your mistake, fix it.” He spits his words out, as if they were balls of flame. Chris gets back up and walks over to the windows showing the planes at their gates. He stares a hole through one that that reads Aeroflot, a crappy Russian airline company. “Yeah, I thought you would be. I’ll be there by the end of today, make sure nothing else goes wrong. The last thing we need is the media right now. Nothing leaks out, got it? Yeah, alright, see you soon, Borya.” Ashton hits the “End” button on his phone. He puts the Motorola in his pocket, and takes the Verizon phone apart. Christopher takes out the SIM card and puts that into his pocket as well; he walks over to the trash can on his right and throws both halves of the dismantled phone away. Then he takes out the SIM card and drops it, and then steps on it crushing the flimsy cell phone part into tiny pieces. Ashton takes out the Motorola and keys in a phone number, after several paces before the trash can Ashton stops in his tracks. “Yeah, Alyson, it’s me. I’ve been called away on business,” Ashton says with a slight apologetic smile, perhaps not even realizing he was smiling. Chris’s smile changes to a frown. “No, it isn’t AWC business…” After a few seconds Chris cringes and takes his phone away from his ear. “Yeah, I know how much it means to you, but this is unavoidable…” Chris says, trying to explain the situation. Chris looks at his watch, walks back to his bag, and pulls his plane ticket out of the side pocket; apparently to check the flight departure; 12:24. “Yeah, I know the AWC is supposed to be my main priority, but this can’t wait. Just make something up for Harber…” Chris trails off. Chris rolls his eyes at the lecture of how wrong it is to lie from is girlfriend. “Look I gotta go. I’ll talk to you later, I’ll be back as soon as I can, see you soon,” Chris quickly says, cutting the conversation short. He drops the Motorola into his bag and zips up all compartments. He begins to walk to the gate on his left, where a small cluster of people has materialized. “Flight 623 to Saint Petersburg, Russia now boarding at gate 23, please have your tickets ready.” To Be Continued… View Christopher Ashton 's Biography
