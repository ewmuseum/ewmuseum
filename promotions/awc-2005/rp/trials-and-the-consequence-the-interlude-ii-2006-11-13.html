Trials and the Consequence - The Interlude II

13 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13/11/06 The present day, some time after the incidents being talked about There are times when certain kinds of events just can’t be avoided. They infect your life and make sure that no matter what happens, you’re stuck in the moment, forced to live through it and endure. Today was one of those moments. Much like a past time where I met Sylvia, today I was stuck in a sweaty, cramped conference room. Today, I had to make a response to my bookings for Fresh! I didn’t have a choice of course you see, because ‘Mr. Harber’ had deemed it mandatory for me to answer my critics as it were. I think he just enjoyed the fact that I had to simmer in a hotbed of people who were itching to get me to crack. Oh how I was going to repay him for that. Not only that, he’d made me fly to Atlantic Wrestling Club’s headquarters just to do this one thing. That’s it, just this one thing. Why did I go then? Well, it basically came down to a couple of reasons. Firstly, I just didn’t want him to have any good reason to apply even more pressure and maybe strip me of the title that I currently held. Frankly, I wanted to hold onto this baby for as long as possible. Because the longer that I held onto it, the longer I could rub it in Harber’s face that I had beaten his chosen pet. And that was worth it to me. The second reason? I felt like shooting the shit with these journalists and blowing off some steam. There was nothing I liked more than to insult and fling mud at these people who would call themselves in a weird way historians. So you’ve got me, standing behind the pedestal, dressed as best as I could (which is a lie) in slacks and a shirt. Draped over my right arm is the Relentless Title and in my left hand is a simple pen, flicking it back and forth to work my hand while I talk. I get antsy pretty easy in these kinds of situations so I adapt as best I can you know? There’s a sizable crowd, roughly thirty or so. They all huddle close to their pens and paper, guarding them like it was a question of national security. Every now and then you’ll notice the odd one who’ll peer just over the paper with spectacles and take in the surroundings through a semi-circular span. Curious types, but to each their own I guess. It opened pretty normally, them asking bullshit questions that they fawned over like they cared. They didn’t give a rat’s ass about though in practice. It was all about warming up the bait to them, all about getting me into a nice wax about something so that they could then lay down the real questions. It happened as soon as they asked me something inconsequential about the current state of wrestling. ‘So Jack…what do you really think about your boss, Mr. Harber?’ It came from a weedy journalist to the front, someone who’d be nothing to a man of my size if I were to take him on. The geeky exterior of the man was in plain view, right down to the pocket protector. Man, sometimes they just go to far in their obsessions these guys. I pondered the question for a moment, taking in the possibilities that could follow with it, mulling over whether or not I could get away with this and that. Frankly I was trying to just build a bit of tension, playing them up and then perhaps responding mono-syllabically for no gain just to see them slump in their chairs. That was usually a good one. Strangely though, I decided to ignore that notion for once and instead actually answered the question, much to the surprise of many in the audience. ‘Honestly? I don’t like him. Never did because he’s a bit of an asshole.’ They sat silently, waiting to see if there was anything else. They knew better than to write yet, they knew that I had more to say. ‘But everyone’s an asshole in this business aren’t they? You have to be. It doesn’t matter whether you’re the cream of the crop, the bottom of the barrel or somewhere in between. You’ve got to put on the macho bullshit to get stuff done round here, and if that means being an asshole to serve that purpose, then so be it.’ Now they scrawled, seeing red at the sight of the first cut. ‘I can’t blame Harber for being an asshole to me because I rub him the wrong way; he’s just trying to conduct business. He’s trying to make some hard earned cash as we all are. That does not mean that I can’t have my opinion and voice it when I feel like it. To not have that voice…well what would be the point of trying to promote yourself in a business that is all about the promoting?’ The flicker of pens as they dashed across the open pages. ‘Let’s face it, in this business it isn’t just about your ability to wrestle inside that squared circle. Hell, sometimes the fact you can’t wrestle may be the least of your worries. Any Joe can wander in there and swing wildly for the fences and he might get lucky. It may be a sport but there’s nothing that does not have luck involved to a certain extent. But there’s a certain trait that you need to advance in this business to the higher echelons. It’s something that can’t be taught and only comes to certain people.’ They peered over their paper. ‘It’s oratory. It’s been the defining quality of this sport for so long and yet everywhere I look in this business…it’s fading away, becoming obsolete. Or at least people are claiming that it’s obsolete. As the business evolves and tries to compete with more mainstream sports, you’re going to get the comparisons inevitably. But trying to be too like them is not the right decision. Right now we’ve got athletes that don’t have that extra bit of oomph that the old guys used to have.’ This time I took a pause for effect, letting them see me flicker the pen and take a brief gulp of some water that lay on the pedestal. Ah that was enough I felt. ‘In the Atlantic Wrestling Club I see…nobody right now with any sort of oratory talent beyond myself and a certain Ottoman. None of them come across as anything more than either steroid freaks or anaemic emo boys. And that includes the women.’ A hush went round the room and the scribbling returned as I again halted. This time the geeky journalist had a chance to pose another question. ‘All of them? Even people like your opponent at the upcoming event?’ I laughed a little, the kind of husky middle-aged laugh that you being to develop as time wears on. ‘Yes, even Teresa Tomas and Darcy Crisis. Seriously if you even consider them talented I must be God’s gift to wrestling! They’re nothing but pieces of crap, pieces which I’ll be tossing into the bin as soon as they come near me.’ ‘But don’t you think you’re just a bit disgruntled having to wrestle both of them in the same night?’ ‘Please, the first match is barely anything, especially against a woman. It’s a little warm up at most for me. The main course is going to be disappointing too. I mean, the guy who can’t even decide whether or not he wants the woman he loves? Ugh, I have time for more important things.’ ‘Like?’ ‘Like screwing over Harber. Like I said, you’ve got to be an asshole right? And believe me, I’ve still got plenty of tricks left up my sleeve to sort out everyone’s favourite entertainment. But that’s for another time.’ I was about to leave, pretty much happy with letting off some steam, even in such a light manner. Somebody halted me with one last question however. ‘What of Adam Dick’s remarks on PRIME television?’ I raised an eyebrow before smiling. ‘Let him retort all he wants. He has the time after all, being out of GTT.’ Ah insults.
