Venezuela

21 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/21/05 Luis gritted his teeth, his hands overtop of his slicked-back black hair. He was wearing a Hawaiian-style button-down shirt and blue jeans, a pair of black sneakers and a toothpick sticking out from between his lips completing the look. The swarthy-skinned Venezuelan gave a little grunt at every burst of gunfire that cut through the paneling and shot through the windows of the house, denting everything around. He would have prayed had he believed in God and, unfortunately for him, Darwin was not a comforting individual. The muscular black man, Antony Ozubike, was crouched right next to him, his hands drawn over his head in similar fashion. Antony wore black track pants and a black tank-top, very informal clothing for him. He was taller than Luis and far darker and he wore a disgusted look when he looked over at his compatriot. Luis looked up at him and then gave a double-take, noticing Antony's expression. He gave a short laugh and patted Antony's knee. 'You and me,' Luis said reassuringly in quick Spanish. 'You and me, we're gonna live this shit out, understand? Just gotta wait until the Lopez boys come around and chase these dirty assholes out.' Antony shook his head. 'I told you this would happen,' Antony accused, his calm voice holding a terse disappointment. 'I said it and you didn't listen.' Luis shrugged his shoulders and cursed as a bullet ricocheted off of something metal; neither of them could see what it was. 'The Lopez boys, Lion,' Luis reiterated. 'They're gonna come, they've gotta come. Owe me a favor. We're not gonna die, if that's what you're worried about.' Antony frowned a bit, shaking his head and peering up over the couch, noticing that the hail of gunfire had ceased. He didn't know that it was safe to come out yet; maybe they were just reloading. Luis was never the most reasonable of people, never had been and it seemed like he never would be. He had… habits. Addictions. He wouldn't kick them. Antony knew he had to, but if Luis wanted to keep on like this, he had no recourse but to let him. After all, Luis was Antony's manager. As much as Antony didn't like to admit it, he needed money and he was no good at making deals. He needed Luis. Alive. He gritted his teeth as a warning shot was fired, saying quite clearly that they wanted them to keep their heads down. Anton pressed his back against the couch once more. 'We can't stay in Venezuela, Luis,' Anton said in a low voice, turning the conversation to something more practical. 'You know that now.' Luis waved a hand at Anton, taking his own turn at peering out. 'Yes, Lion, I know. You're right. I don't like to say it, man, but you're right. Venezuela is out of the picture. Colombia, then? I have family in Colombia.' Anton shook his head vehemently. 'Not in South America,' Anton said. Luis glanced back at him and sighed heavily, taking his seat once again. 'You're right, you're right,' Luis said. He rapped on Anton's head a bit with his knuckles, causing the fighter to swat his hand away. 'Sometimes you've got a little bit of sense, y'know, Lion? You should use it more often.' 'Come out, Luis, you little bitch!' came a cry from outside. Luis clenched his teeth angrily, kicking a bit. Before Anton could restrain him, Luis leaned over and gave them the middle finger. A storm of gunfire responded and Anton yanked a laughing Luis back. He held his middle finger up, grinning broadly. 'Those assholes almost got it,' Luis chuckled before kissing his finger up and down, glad to still have it whole. Anton sighed. Luis would never learn. Not in Venezuela, not in Colombia, not nowhere. Luis was going to be Luis and Anton… well, Anton just had to be Anton. It was proving to be more and more difficult by the day, though. Luis loved to two-step. He adored the little dance. He hated hardcore music. He thought it was inane, senseless, stupid. His style was Latino rap, gangster rap, any ype of rap you could throw at him, he appreciated. Old-school thrash metal, too, he was very into. In fact, it seemed like he liked every style of music but hardcore and its direct brethren. But he loved to two-step. Wearing a suit jacket, khaki shorts, and a little red beret he'd picked up from a thrift shop nearby, Luis two-stepped barefoot in the apartment livingroom to Sepultura. He was high on cocaine and fairly drunk. The History Channel was on in the nearby television set. The apartment was plain, with white walls and a crappy beige carpet that looked like processed vomit. It was easier on Luis's feet, though, so this is where he did his little dance. Anton looked up from his push-ups, watching Luis two-step as he continued to press himself up. Escaping Venezuela had not been easy. It had cost them a great bit of the local fortune they'd procured from Anton's regular high-draw illegal bouts to get a plane ticket for the two and to set them up here in L.A. The Lopez boys had mostly been killed by Luis's friends that the Venezuelan had still neglected to name. Hopefully, that was the end of the repeated run-and-gun life that Anton and Luis had led in South America. 'This is the life, man,' Luis said, not breaking his two step. 'In America, got the girls, still got my coke, you got your little bouts, we're making it work. No more guys breaking down our door looking for money we owe.' 'Money you owe, Luis,' Anton said with a little bit of difficulty. He, too, did not break his motions, still doing push-ups. It was critical that Anton remained in tip-top condition for whatever fights he was handed. Injury was out of the question. There was no money and, besides, that might open them up for investigation. They were still involved in a lot of illegal fights even though they got some legal stuff thrown their way. Sometimes. Luis grinned broadly, rubbing his nose. 'Nah, Lion,' Luis disagreed. 'You're with me, so we both owe it. We both gonna pay the penalty if something goes wrong. But nothing will, 'cause like I said: you're with me.' Anton shook his head again, continuing to press himself up and down. The two fell silent for a bit, involved in their own activities. Anton was not averse to Sepultura, so it didn't bother him that this was Luis's choice of music for the moment. They were living pretty good, existing much the same that they had been in Venezuela, but now they were three floors above the bullet-ridden streets. It was a pretty good neighborhood, so they didn't feel threatened. Anton didn't always agree with Luis's observations, but this one was pretty on-the-mark. 'Lion,' Luis mused, his two-stepping losing its quick tempo. 'I've been thinking for a couple weeks. I think you should think about wrestling.' 'Wrestling?' Anton asked. 'Yeah, wrestling, you dumb fuck!' Luis snapped, tripping over his own feet a bit. He took a few stabilizing steps forward, but he didn't fall. 'Can you not understand my Spanish? Professional wrestling. It's a big money-maker.' Anton thought it over a bit. Luis would want an answer now. He might have been thinking about it for weeks, but he never gave Anton that choice. It was just as well, though, because Anton never really needed it. His decisions were swift and decisive. Every point he thought of for and against entering the sport was punctuated by a grunt that also served to mark a finished push-up. 'Okay,' Anton said finally. 'Sounds alright. I can do it. It costs money to get training, I hope you know.' 'You think I'm as dumb as you?' Luis asked. 'I know it costs some fuckin' money. I've got just the guy, too. He'll train you well. Least, that's what his advertisement says.' Sometimes, Luis was pretty dumb.
