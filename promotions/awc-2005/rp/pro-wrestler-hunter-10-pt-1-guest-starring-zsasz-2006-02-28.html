Pro Wrestler Hunter #10 pt. 1 (guest starring Zsasz)

28 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/28/06 The wind blew from the sea, carrying with it a chill drizzle. Philadelphia International Airport was a cold, busy, stranger. Butterfly Hamada walked out from the shelter of a bus stop, her wet hair falling in streaks across her grim and determined face. She hailed a taxi and climbed in. She slid into the warm cab’s leather seats, happy to back on the ground after a long flight. Her comfort was immediately shattered when Grappler jumped into the seat next to her, slamming his door shut with too much enthusiasm. She could picture the thoughts going through the eccentric man’s head as he couldn’t stop fidgeting in his seat with glee. Drinking, gossiping, and showing off his newly won gold belt would occupy the next week of his life. He was a walking tabloid cover, or so he wished. Despite the weather, everyone in AWC seemed to be in a merry mood. Bloodlust was a success for the company. Everyone was looking forward to the next few days of revelry and relaxation. Everyone was looking forward to the next month and the next PPV when again, maybe something special would happen. Plots were probably being hatched and chaos was most certainly brewing under the surface, but the impressions were that there was nowhere to go but up for everyone involved. Fans were waiting in the rain when many of the cabs and limos pulled up to the hotel. Some performers signed a few autographs, some made plans for later that evening, and others were mobbed by zealous fans. But for a pro wrestling company there wasn’t much more then the usual business. Butterfly and Grappler even signed one young boy’s magazine, and Grappler took a picture with him. The boy’s mother gave Grapps a peck on the cheek. “Notoriety has its’ privileges,” he said with a grin. A bellboy took their suitcases. He recognized The Green Grappler, and greeted him with great deference and solemn promises of getting them two good rooms. Amazing, Megumi thought with irritation, is there anyone in the wrestling, or a bar owner anywhere, who doesn’t know about the infamous Green Grappler. Megumi kept a watchful gaze upon the happenings around her, even as Grapps grabbed her hand and they sprinted across the rain drenched street. Grapps dragged her behind him, her eyes scanning the street, always looking and watching. They burst through a pair of black steel doors and right into the entranceway of a nightclub. Megumi jerked her hand free from the masked man’s grasp. Not seeming to notice anything unusual about her mood, Grapps removed his cape and green cloak and handed them to the hat check girl by the door. He was wearing a fancy Italian green silk shirt, and nice pants. He still looked ridiculous with that mask, but Megumi was used to the eccentricities of the pro wrestling business. The party inside was just getting started. Before Megumi knew what was happening, Grapps removed her coat with a gallant flourish and handed it to the coat check girl. “Nice and warm in here,” he noted. He chafed and and blew on his hands as he waited patiently for Megumi to ready herself. Even without the benefit of a mirror, Megumi knew her hair must have been a soggy mess. She slicked it back behind her ears and tied it with a blue ribbon. She wore blue jeans and white Nike sneakers, and a white sweater with a blue butterfly in sequins on the sleeves. Grapps pursed his lips and avoided any comment. When she was done he put his hand on the small of her back and ushered her toward a set of double doors into the main ballroom. “Bad beginnings, bad endings,” Megumi said allowed, to her self. Grapps looked at her with a curious and discerning look. “Loosen up, Mamacita. Try being a social butterfly for once.” he said with a raised eyebrow. They opened the doors and were hit with sound like a blue ocean wave. Hip-hop music came at them in numbing beats. The marble floors and white columns were cold but the dance room emanated with warmth caused by the writhing bodies in motion. Off to the side, a bar washed in blue and gold granite, from chairs to ceiling, seemed more inviting for those who liked to watch. The air was redolent with perfume and seat, alcohol and adrenaline. “It’s not Paris, or Tokyo,” Grapps apologized, “but it’s better then the back of that pick-up truck.” “I’ve seen worse,” Megumi responded curtly. “If you find a good table I will call ahead to make sure our rooms are satisfactory,” Grapps offered, trailing along behind her. Megumi spun around to face him. “This is Philadelphia. We part ways here tonight. Your most pressing goal may be to get drunk, but I’m here to search for a ‘hired gun’, remember?” she said in a low voice. Unperturbed, Grapps gave her his most winning smile. “Do be reasonable, my dear. Just because we’ve arrived into the city and survived Bloodlust, I see no cause to pretend we don’t know each other. In fact, since we are now the AWC Alliance Champions, such pretense might be otherwise difficult to manage. Why, think about all the photo shoots the paparazzi will want of us together! And, are you going to go to Mr. Harber and tell him you are in his company looking to track down a ‘Pro Wrestler Hunter’ as you call it, and that our tag team is nothing more then a farce? You tell Mr. Harber this is just the usual kidnapping of an employee by a crazed Japanese ninja assassin?” With an unexaggerated wave of his arm, Grappler gestured around the club. It was nearly full to capacity, and there was a good number of AWC staff coming and going. People were crowding the bar and hunkering down for a long night of imbibing. “You see?” Grappler concluded. “You’re stuck with me if you want to keep up appearances. At least for one more evening, just to make our rounds as the newly crowned tag team champions.” Seeing her hesitation, he pressed on. “Come now. We’re both cold and wet and tired. We need some good food and some drink. And I mean food that doesn’t come out of a vending machine. We share a little m4eal, some talk, some drinks. And afterwards, we go to bed.” Megumi gave him the look of death. “Come come, my dear. Relax. I did get us a room with separate beds.” Before Megumi could protest, Grappler had skipped away after a waiter. “What ho! Waiter, oh waiter! I’m parched!” he headed off toward the tray juggling man. Will I never be rid of this fool? Megumi stalked off toward the remotest spot in the bar in search of an empty seat. A number of small tables were scattered there in the shadows, drawing her with their isolation. Perhaps one of the nooks would be unoccupied. “Oai-deki-te ureshii-desu, kyoudai, Nikkou-san. Ogenki desu-ka?” The distinctive voice stopped Megumi in midstride, and she floated back in time to when she was a little girl on Nagano Mountain. She had been away from home so many times. Hearing the language reminded her of better times. She turned to find herself face to face with a massive man, with short platinum blonde slicked back hair and sapphire blue eyes. Dressed in the finest suit and in a dignified black, the man carried his size gracefully, like a diplomat. But he also had the look of a fighter, his frame was bulky and he had that superstar aura. ”I’m sorry,” Butterfly said with regret and surprise, “but it’s been many years since I heard that dialect.” “Of course,” the handsome man replied, switching smoothly to English. “That’s a tongue I picked up when I trained in Japan many years ago. Forgive me. I must apologize. For a moment, you reminded me of someone I once knew.” Megumi just smiled, still a little unsure. “I was disappointed it was not her,” he paused, “but only for a moment. A fortunate error, indeed.” Megumi tilted her head at the man, and she flashed one of her rarely seen smiles. “Are you always this gallant and well-behaved with strangers?” “Always,” he responded in kind. Would you like to join me? It would be my honor. I’ve just ordered a bottle of the best saki in the house, and they have some of the best in the world. I haven’t participated in a traditional saki ceremony in ages. You would be one of very few who would appreciate the nuances and the tradition.” Megumi smiled. Hearing her mother’s native tongue had lowered her natural inhibitions. Her homesickness reminded her it had been too long since she had been home. “A gracious offer, indeed,” she replied. “Um, my name is Megumi Hamada.” He extended his hand, placing his palm over hers and bowing low over their joined hands. “Your name is known to me. I am indeed honored,” he said in a respectful tone. The tread of approaching footsteps interrupted them. “Good news and bad news I am afraid, my dear Megumi,” Grapps announced haughtily, setting up for one of his bad one-liners. “Hello! Who is your fr-“ The masked man stopped abruptly, his eyes hidden behind green slits darken like a forest before the dusk, his eyes focused on the stranger. The mask of The Green Grappler darkened, and, to Megumi’s horror, he balled his hand up in a fist at his side and taking a combat posture. What was this maniac doing? she thought with dismay. Silence fell across the nightclub for that one moment when two sets of eyes met. A fight was brewing in the air. The world around them seemed to slow down for a brief instant, conversations trailing off, heads turning to watch. And then just like that everything was fine. The threat passed, Grapps acted as though he was sullied by the response, and stood up straight, extending his hand out to touch Megumi’s arm. “Someone you know, I take it?” he said, gazing down at the two of their joined hands. Self-consciously, Megumi snatched her hand away and stuffed her hands in her pockets. Before she could come up with a quick reply to his clever question, her new friend spoke. “For a moment, I mistook the young lady for an old acquaintance.” Grapps eyebrows flew up, disappearing behind his mask. “Holy guacamole, an original ploy!” he said with great admiration. “I think I’ll try that one myself next time I spot a pretty lady I would like to nibble nosh with, hmmm?” The large man’s eyes narrowed at the accusation, but Grappler’s over the top acting could be taken as nothing more then a joke. For a moment, the three of them stood there unmoving. The big man made a curt bow of dismissal to Grappler as if he were of no further consequence or concern, and he took Megumi’s arm and escorted her toward a nearby table. Megumi felt a flood of relief that the took had not gotten into a fight. This man looked like he took take on almost anyone, he was so big. She glared at Grappler and mouthed the words Go away! Grappler either acted like he didn’t notice or didn’t, and followed them to their table. He pulled up a third chair as the stranger pulled the seat out for Megumi to sit. Grappler plopped down in his chair with an arrogant complacency, as if he was a courtesan in a royal court. “Grappler, what has come over you?” she asked. “What’s come over you sweet cheeks, that’s what I want to know. Accepting invitations from complete strangers, gentlemen though I am sure he may be, without a proper introduction.” He shook his head and tsk-tsked. “I make no secret of my identity,” the man said, speaking only to Megumi. “We were merely interrupted before I could complete the introduction. I am Zsasz, at your service.” “Well, woopity-woop!” Grappler interjected in a goofy tone. “I’ve heard of you! Aren’t you known as ‘Sadistic Brutality’ in some circles? “In certain objectionable ones, yes. Mwa ha ha ha!” the man admitted coolly. Sadistic Brutality. With an effort, Megumi kept her face expressionless. She had also heard of the great Zsasz. His reputation in Japan for cruelty and treachery was legendary in some circles. He was a student of Daisuke Vejita. His Japanese connection were even known to Megumi’s teacher, Tyger Shuri-ken. He had told her a few years ago to stay far away from the man called Zsasz, as his reputation preceded him, and could ruin hers. Megumi, however, refused to be judgmental by the dark rumors and old school traditional fundamentalist thinking of Tyger Shuri-ken. Besides, she could handle herself, and most of these so-called tough men of wrestling were nothing of the sort. What could go wrong if they were to have a drink together? “Grappler, why don’t you go mingle or something. Mr. Zsasz…” she looked at her new friend who smiled and nodded. “He asked me to have a traditional sake ceremony. Please forgive my tag team partner for his bad manners, Zsasz.” “Your tag team partner, yes! Lucky you! Congratulations on your title victory.” “Hmm, thank you so much,” said Grappler. “But I would love to hear stories about the great Sadistic Brutality master himself, Zsasz. Money, power, looking perfect, is that how the slogan went?” “Really?” said Zsasz with his own sort of flair. “I hadn’t known. I wouldn’t expect tales of my exploits to reach the places you have been. Oh, I am sorry Megumi. I failed to ask you your friend’s name?” “This is The Green Grappler,” Megumi said quickly before Grapps could throw out some new ridiculous comment. “Oh, The Green Grappler, yes. I was sure I had seen those green eyes somewhere before. Your reputation precedes you too, Mister Grappler.” Zsasz smiled with gentle amusement. The man left that remark for Grappler to take it as he would. He turned his attention to the sake ceremony and began to pour the rice wine in cups. Megumi gave Grappler a look, as to warn him not to interupt. Then she followed the ceremony, remembering back to a time when her mother would have great teachers and wrestlers over for ceremonial tea. 'Would you like to join us, Mr. Grappler?' Zsasz said, as an afterthought. 'No thank you, I'm going to have a green apple martini, myself,' Grapps said. 'Of course you will,' Zsasz said smoothly, and he summed a waiter. 'A martini for our friend here, and dinner for three,' he instructed the nervous waiter, who nodded and escaped to the safety of the kitchen. Megumi turned to Grapps as Zsasz was talking. He cut her off before she could say anything. 'A place of danger, my dear. You enter the lion's den so willingly?' He smiled at her but the question hung in the air like forst on a cold winter morning. ( to be continued...)
