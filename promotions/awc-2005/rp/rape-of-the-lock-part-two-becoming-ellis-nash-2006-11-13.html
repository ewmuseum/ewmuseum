Rape of the Lock: Part Two (BECOMING: Ellis Nash)

13 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Today They told you that the world was a fair place, long ago. They told you that the good guys always won, and that the bad guys got what was coming to them in the end. They told you that the world was sunshine and rainbows and daffodils, and all of that other shit. Cotton candy and sugary sweet. As if no one had a care in the world. “It’s all going to work out in the end,” they told you, and by God, they actually meant it. Even losses were eventual wins. After all, you always won in the end. The big bad boss cowered before your uber-mighty party, and the ending credits rolled. As if they could make you forget about the casualties. As if they could make you forget about the Aeriths, the Tidus’; those who had to be sacrificed for the victory to occur. Nothing can bring them back. Well, nothing except a deus ex machina ending in a sequel, but life doesn’t have sequels. No, in the real world you’ve only got one shot. One chance to make sure you don’t fuck everything up. Life doesn’t have a Raise spell or Phoenix Downs. All life has is the promise of a time machine that’s not even guaranteed to fix things. All life has is a chance to become a champion, or a chance at chances to become champions, or a briefcase. What’s in the briefcase, you wonder? Is it, for example, the King Prawn? Is it a boot? Aimz’ virginity? Teresa Tomas’ dignity? The world may never know. Except of course it will, because someone will win it. Someone being you, perhaps. You look on, and know that this match really doesn’t matter very much unless you can achieve your goal. Triangles isn’t a guarantee. You’re not in the proper triangle. If you want the treasure, the real reason you’re here, you’re going to have to take it. Never mind that it’s illegal. Never mind that it’s immoral. Never mind that it’ll be a tooth and nail knockdown and drag out to get it. It’s your only hope in a world that isn’t sugar and cotton candy and rainbows. It’s the only way for the good guy to win It’s the only way for things to be fair for you. For, you see, you is me, and me is you. I’m you. You’re me. I am, you are. -------------------------------------------------- The Situation (As Told By Limericks) There once was a man from MIT His nerdocity was plain to see. And what he loved the most Was hair the color of burnt toast: From a woman of the AWC. There once was a man named Seymour Whose prospects for life were quite poor But at the chance to bring back The woman whom he didst lack He launched himself back into war. There once was a woman named Nash Towards the Triangles main did she dash But along came Seymour fair For a lock of her hair And all that she could do was thrash. There once was a show known as Fresh Among its stars did not number John Tesh And that was the place Where would be found a trace …fuck it, I can’t think of a word that rhymes with Fresh. *sad* ------------------------------------------------------------------ “It’s just a lock of hair,” Seymour said, knowing that it was anything but. It was his dreams. His ambitions for the future. His hopes. All in a fucking lock of hair. No sooner had he thought that, though, then somehow the ENTIRE crowd from PRIME’s ReVolution 111 appeared around him, ephemeral, ghostly, unreal. “WHAT?” “A lock of hair.” “WHAT?” “IT’S A LOCK OF HAIR! I DON’T KNOW ANY SYNONYMS FOR IT! WHAT DO YOU THINK I AM, A GODDAMNED THESAURUS?!” That made the crowd be sad, and leave. Crowds don’t like being sad. “Where the Hell was I,” Almasy muttered, shaking his head. He was conversing with himself now, having a deep conversation with his inner Seymour. The Seymour that wanted to roam free and go on a quest for a palm tree, simply because he could. “Nope,” Seymour told himself. “Not happening. The quest I have today is more important. You can go hunt for a palm tree anytime.” Palm trees were stationary targets. Ellis Nash was not. He wondered if he should have gone to AWC officials and asked for a hair match. Caballera contra Caballera they called it down in Mexico. He’d never been in such a match before. Never had he ever wanted to be. After all, wanting to cut off someone’s hair was sort of creepy. You’d think if you wanted to do that so bad, you’d have been a barber, and not a wrestler. But, ah well. It’s not like wrestling ever made sense anyway. “So, just one lock. One lock,” he repeated, again and again, like a mantra. So close, and yet so far. So easy to obtain in theory, and yet so difficult in practice. “I wish I was Ellis Nash,” Seymour said, suddenly. “Then, I could just cut my own hair off, and I could use that.” Little did poor Seymour know, though, that the changes had already begun. The attitudinal shifts…the constant sarcasm, all of it. BECOME, Seymour. Become. ------------------------------- *snip* The scissors cut off a lock of hair from the wig, and it only took 3.8 seconds. *snip snip* It was so easy, the more he thought about it. What did he really care if he beat Ellis Nash? Her hair was all he needed. *snip snippity snip* 3.8 seconds was nothing. Less than nothing. Hell, he could probably incapacitate her for that long with a hiptoss. Or a body slam. Or some other basic manuever that wrestlers do because of tradition that never get near falls anymore. *snip snippity snippity snip* He tried again. This time, the cut only took 3.4 seconds. He was getting better. Practice helped; so too did having a really good pair of scissors. Maybe he could get down below 3 seconds. Then he’d really have to consider just whipping the scissors out mid-match and eating the disqualification. *snip snippity snippityippity snippity snip* One more time. 2.9 seconds. Perfect. ------------------------------------------- It’s absurd, of course. How can Seymour Almasy become Ellis Nash? Ellis is a woman. She has breasts. Seymour is a man. He has a penis. Generally, breasts and penises can’t be found on the same person unless you’re an overweight man or have had surgery done. Becoming someone, though, is more than just physical changes. To BECOME is to get into the mind of. Think like. Act like. Dress like, in some cases, but Seymour’s not into women’s clothing. Unless of course Ellis was willing to dress up as Rinoa, in which case… No, no, we don’t need to go there. To become Seymour Almasy is simple. One must play RPGs and make nerdy references and get his ass kicked a lot, only to make miraculous comebacks. So too is becoming Ellis Nash simple. One must speak in cryptics, be bitingly sarcastic, and never make any actual sense. Neither has any substance. Neither has any logic. Neither has any reason. In short: both are much ado about nothing, really. One is a fading star in the twilight of a career he would trade in a heartbeat for happiness. The other is a woman who doesn’t seem to care, yet is extremely good at what she does. What will happen when they face off? God only knows, since neither really cares. Seymour wants Ellis’ hair, and Ellis is likely being paid extra to have to step in the ring with the nut job. Most anticlimactic main event ever? Probably. --------------------------------- It all comes down To this. Who’d have Ever thought that A former PTC Global Champion would come To AWC in pursuit Of a woman’s hair? Actually A lot of people wouldn’t Be too surprised. Seymour’s Got this bad tendency To chase people for Silly reasons. ------------------------------------- “Oh Yevon…remind me NEVER to do this again! Damned Nova and that shit in the NFW locker room. This is totally the most entertaining thing I’ve done in weeks, though….” Cut to Seymour Almasy, hero to thousands, sitting in his hotel room….staring at a wall. The evidence of tonight’s insanity lay next to him in the form of a bong that appeared to have been rendered in the shape of a Mage Masher, a dagger from Final Fantasy XII. “Mmmm, mang, I should go play Final Fantasy like this. Maybe the chocobos will come running out of the screen so I don’t have to paint an ostrich yellow when I want to ride one.” Almost predictably, a knock came on Seymour’s hotel room door. Seymour didn’t go to answer it at first, until the call of “Domino’s!” snapped him out of his revelry. “Munchies!” he exclaimed, hopping up like a giddy schoolgirl on her way to her first gangbang. Opening the door, he threw a $20 at the pizza boy, managed to spit out ‘keep the change’, and returned to the room with his prize. “Pineapple?” he asked, frowning, looking down at the pizza box. “Why would I ever order pineapple. Pineapple’s for dessert. Ah well, munchies are munchies…” Almasy took a piece of his pineapple and sausage pizza, promptly chomping into it. It wasn’t so bad, really. Now, with food within arm’s reach, he could return to the fascinating wall. “This is so the best show on TV,” he mused. “All of that other shit sucks. Lost? Dumb. Deal or No Deal? Lame. Besides, that Howie guy’s gayer than Johnny Lexicon at a cock-sucking convention…” The world, it seemed, began to spin for Seymour. No longer could he focus on his beloved wall. Instead, he collapsed, falling to the bed, and then rolling off to the unforgiving carpet. ------------------------------------------------------- (Cue Final Fantasy VI Battle Music) Ellis Nash vs. Seymour Almasy HP: 2343/2343 HP: 5672/5672 MP: 56/56 MP: 145/145 Ellis Nash attacks with Emo Beam.
