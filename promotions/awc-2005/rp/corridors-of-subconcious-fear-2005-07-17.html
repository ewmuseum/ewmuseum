Corridors of Subconcious Fear

17 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17/7/05 For you [name withheld], God spare your life 1. Twisted Perception Hope is a powerful thing. Paddy always believed this with the utmost conviction. He believed it was the thing that kept people going at even the bleakest of times when it seemed like even God has abandoned you. It was the thing your body said to you just before you jumped from the stool in your garage with a rope fastened around your neck. Like the wind whistling in your ear…hope. Every cell of your body telling you things will get better; every nuclei begging you there’s always another way; every damn chromosome that shape the life you live in to keep your chin up. And now as Paddy’s existence deteriorates before him and his principles he’s so far upheld his whole life are shredded before his eyes, Paddy is still hopeful even if the flame is diminishing. Looking through Paddy’s eyes was never easy – the hurt he has felt throughout his life had made him believe the sadistic perceptions of those who have persecuted him leaving him at times a shell of the man he is. Yet always, he’s pushed past these stages, each time with increased ferocity as he morphs through each stage of his life. The event now on everybody’s mind though is his father who has been kidnapped. And now as we delve into Paddy’s subconscious we can realise what emotional scars Paddy is hiding behind his mask, his shield to the world so that they don’t know the hurt he is feeling inside. The scene opens in a small corridor. It immediately looks like a hospital though more sinister. The expected gleaming white walls are decaying leaving gaping cavities highlighted by the yellowing fungus that surrounds it. The floor too, once strong linoleum of a pleasant grey colour, has been worn to white at some places and even absent in others. There are lights in this corridor lined horizontally three quarters up the wall. Yet many of these domed fixtures are not working and of those that do work, around half of them flicker, causing temporary shadows to appear on the opposite wall. It is pitch black outside and the wind is strong. This is evident because the two windows that can be seen are both broken, allowing the screeching wind to enter into the fragmenting building. The darkness outside only contributes to this feeling of terror that seems to be the blood in this hospitals veins. The hospital is sick; it’s falling apart yet there’s no one there to remedy it. Then suddenly, a new sound becomes an addition to this carnival: the creaking that accompanies the opening of a door. A door just to the left that from the outside has a number of gaping holes that protrude from it, which ruin the mahogany’s beauty. As the door opens, the inside of the room is revealed. The room seems to reciprocate everything the hospital is. Yes this is a room of the hospital to be inhabited by a patient but everything here is what the rest of the hospital once was – clean and healthy. Everything is sparkling. Yet the strangest thing is that it isn’t dark as you look through the window in this room. In fact it’s a glorious day and the powerful rays seem to pierce the window like an armada of arrows. And standing alone in this alien world? Paddy O’Shea. His face is reflecting the confusion that these walls are emitting. He is standing perfectly still, not scared but uneasy at the minute. He is wearing only the typical hospital nightdress that always made Paddy think of paper ponchos. His hair is combed back behind his ears and is neat and despite him obviously looking like a hospital patient, he seems so out of place in the dark halls that loom ahead. “Hello?” Paddy asks. He realises how weak that sounded in his ears so he clears his throat and says once with more authority “HELLO?” There is no answer but Paddy never really expected anything. This morning when he opened this door he had walked into a bustling hall of nurses and doctors and patients moving from hear to there. And it was clean! Now, his perception of what he had known is being twisted before his very eyes. Paddy didn’t like what he was about to do but there didn’t seem to be any other option. Sure, he could stay in this room, confused by what was behind the door, making himself scared in his own mind. But he had always faced his fears and he wouldn’t shy away now. His principles must be upheld at all times. So Paddy padded bare-footed over to the doorway with his arms wavering in the air, readying for something to spring out. Eventually he reached the doorway and looked left and right. As he put his head through the doorway he suddenly heard the whistling wind, which had been birds whistling birds in the other room. Then he stepped through… 2. Crossing Over Paddy walked up the corridor, always to the centre track of it. He didn’t like the worn out floor but he hated the walls even more. He felt in the darkness of the holes that there were hands, ready to pull him in when he got close. A silly thought perhaps maybe but doesn’t the mind lose all sense of reality when it is uneasy? Paddy didn’t intend to search the rest of this hospital, even if he didn’t find out what was wrong, he would go back to the sanctuary of his room. In fact, he was just about to turn back after only taking a few steps when he’d noticed the blood. He’d been watching the floor the whole time, always on the lookout for glass debris, which had been scattered especially around where the windows once were so he noticed the blood straight away. In this building that had become stagnant in time, this blood was fresh. Paddy knew this as it glistened crimson red in the flickering light. Paddy looked up to see if this blood led anywhere and was shocked at what he seen. This blood was part of a trail but after about a foot from where he stood, it was smeared on the ground, as if someone had been dragged… Paddy thought his tongue had swollen as he felt the moisture being sucked away, leaving only a parched mouth. So Paddy clenched his teeth so hard that he thought they would shatter in his mouth. Feck this…aye’m not made fer this shite Paddy pirouetted on his bare left foot. Just as he began to step away, he noticed his shadow and again fear gripped his heart. For this shadow was but yet not his own. This shadow was definitely his silhouette but it was laughing at him silently…or was it screaming? Paddy took a step back, not realising he was standing directly behind one of the windows. Not until he felt something scream into the back of his neck. Loud, shrill, deafening. He broke his paralysis and swung around yet no one was there. He turned around again and saw his shadow had normalised. Yet Paddy’s heart was still beating as fast as before. Something was seriously wrong here. Then came another sound. A voice instantly recognisable to Paddy. “Paddy.” “Da? DA! Where are ye?” A thought hit him at that moment like a ton of bricks…this could be the work of the Masked Man…and he is only human. With increased courage and restored faith, Paddy looked once again at the trail of blood, refusing to fear the worst at this moment in time. Paddy moved quickly down the corridor, not the padded cautious footsteps like before. He needed to find his father quickly and get out of this thing. He walked past a range of windows, a few that actually still had their panes…he never realised his reflection wasn’t showing up in any of them. “DA! Can ye hear me?” shouted Paddy persistently, yet there was still no answer. Then the blood trail suddenly ended at the entrance to a door labelled JANITOR. As Paddy looked at the brass doorknob, lacking in its once prominent lustre, terror once again grabbed him. He wiped his brow to feel a cold sweat was lingering there. Paddy looked around for a weapon, anything, an axe or a fire extinguisher but there was nothing in sight. “Da?” Paddy asked once again. He never expected an answer but he didn’t like the alternative of opening the door, which he knew he now must do. With a shaking hand, Paddy gripped the knob and turned so slowly he felt every creak as it twisted in it’s bearing. Then he pushed hard and prepared himself. Paddy opened his eyes that he’d closed by instinct and saw…nothing. The room contained all the usual janitor equipment; a mop and bucket, a brush and a locket cabinet among other things but there was no father there. Paddy breathed a long sigh of relief. He’d been holding his breath and even as he sucked in the musky air he was grateful to lap it up. Back to yer room Paddy…aye don’ know what’s goin’ on here but aye’m not getting’ involved any further Then Paddy spied an axe on top of the cabinet. That would be handy for getting back, just incase. So Paddy stepped in and walked over to the cabinet. He noticed a beige janitor uniform folded neatly on top of it. Sewed into the breast pocket in red lettering was the name Bill. Paddy traced this name with his index finger carefully. With the axe in hand he gave the uniform one more look. Then he heard the door shut behind him with a slam. Paddy turned around so fast he almost fell over. Yet he wasn’t quick enough to turn away from the thing that was before him and the horror that it was. For crucified to the inside of door, with heavy bolts through only his hands was Danny O’Shea. His eyes were both missing revealing a decrepit view into his inner thoughts. His whole head and face was smeared in blood, some fresh some drying quickly and he was smiling. “Hello boy,” Paddy’s father said with a smile. He looked at his sons’ reaction (despite having no eyes) and rolled his head in poisonous laughter. Paddy didn’t stay to chat. He ran to the door to which Danny was pinned. He felt his jaw lock in his mouth, his body became cold…and the stench was suffocating, Paddy grabbed the knob that was right beside Danny’s left hand. It was covered in blood and Paddy reeled away from the feel of it on his skin. Then Danny turned around, his hair caked heavily with blood and still holding that huge smile. “You did this t’ me boy!” Danny said then once again returned to his maniacal laughter. Paddy grabbed the doorknob and was out the door nearly before it even opened. And he ran down the corridor. He didn’t have a thought on his mind. Then he screamed before he even knew it. It was high pitched and it scared him even to hear himself do it. Then he stopped suddenly and looked back. He realised the laughter that had been circulating in his ears as he ran had now ceased. He was breathing heavily now yet he felt he could have ran a 100 miles with all the adrenaline he’d collected so far. Behind him was desolate as if nothing had ever happened. Got…to…get…out…o’….here! There wasn’t a snowballs chance of him going back down that way so he would continue on forward. That thing was not his father he said to himself. Even in the midst of his fears he was almost sure that was true. Yet he could not rid himself of that burning image of his hallow eye sockets and that sunken smiling face. And the blood was still on his hands, being kept fluidity because of the relentless sweat. Although he didn’t believe that thing was his father, he wasn’t questioning its reality. Surely the masked man couldn’t be behind something like this? He was doubting his presence anywhere in this hospital until he turned around. “Hello Paddy,” the man said through his voice changer. The voice that sounded was robotic yet cheerful. He was dressed in his black boiler suit with the black balaclava on his head. His eyes were covered by sunglasses as usual. The mask was cut out at the mouth and the smile he had was huge. “Won’t you answer me?” He asked. Paddy had hoped for this situation for weeks where he would have his chance to kill the man before him but as his knees buckled he knew he didn’t want it. Fear had drained him of his energy and he began to shake once again. “Where…be me da?” Paddy said quietly. He said it looking past the man as he couldn’t look at what lay behind those glasses. “Now Paddy, you know it’s rude not to look at a person when you talk.” “Where be me da?” Paddy repeated and obediently looked at the masked man although with difficulty. The man let out a bellowing laugh “Perfect Paddy, you’re top of the class. Now what were you saying? Where’s your ‘da’? I’m sorry Paddy. I have a few David’s and Danny’s but no ‘da’s’.” He repeated with a chuckle at his joke at the end. “Where’s me father?” Paddy asked with more confidence now. He was growing tired of these riddles. He was so close now; he could break his neck he thought. “Name?” The man said then pulled a notepad from his pocket. He flicked it open and as pages tumbled down, Paddy saw the pages were full with names. Perhaps this was a cheap attempt to scare him. Whatever this meant, the man laughed at Paddy’s reaction. “I said name. Don’t you want your father back?” Paddy looked at him. That Cheshire cat smile was really starting to tick him off. He’d suddenly forgotten the earlier episode amidst the sudden loathing of this thing. “Danny O’Shea ye bastard!” Paddy screamed, his cheeks flushing as he did so. “No need to shout. Well Paddy, you’re out of luck. We have one Danny O’Shea here but no Danny O’Shea ye bastard’s” The laugh that accompanied this joke was accompanied by the man doubling over. He seemingly believe this was absolutely hilarious Paddy bit down on his lower lip until he felt blood trickling down his chin. Stay calm…don’t get sucked into his games was the advice his brain gave his fists. “Danny O’Shea” “What?” “Me father’s name is Danny O’Shea.” Paddy said with his eyes now firmly resting on the masked man. If only he could tear those glasses away now. But he mustn’t do anything when there’s a chance to get his father. “Why young Patrick – you’re in luck! Daaaany O’Shea…Come on down!” The man said then keeled over again in laughter. He pointed behind Paddy’s head as he did so. “What’s the matter Paddy…aren’t you going to so hello to your dear father” The masked man quizzed. By this stage he was lying on the ground in his unrelenting laughter. Paddy turned around to see his father. This wasn’t the thing in the janitors’ room. There was no blood on him but dried cuts and bruises, just the way he’d seen him last when his hospital room was invaded. He was wearing the clothes he’d been kidnapped in too. But his eyes were closed…and a rope was around his neck, hanging him from the ceiling. He was lifeless yet he was swinging from side to side slightly from the incoming draft through the unglassed windows. Paddy took a look at the man. He wasn’t scared now but sad. He couldn’t save his father, and he was certain this was his father. He stretched out a hand and touched his father’s leg, reflecting on his love for him. Then he became aware of the sickening laughter behind him. Then sadness was replaced by anger and that by murderous rage. He swung around and grabbed the man, pinning him to the ground. The man’s smile had contorted to a sneer. “WHO ARE YE!” Paddy screamed. “Find out yourself you Irish fool,” he said. Paddy grabbed the man’s balaclava at the top and pulled it off swiftly. He didn’t know what he would expect so anything could be possible when this man was unmasked. But what he seen was soul destroying. “Hello Paddy.” Said the unmasked man…Paddy O’Shea. Paddy didn’t believe it to start with as he rolled off this copy of himself utterly numb. “Don’t ye know Paddy? Ye did this t’ yer own da?” this impostor said and then erupted into laughter. “Isn’t that right da?” Paddy strained his neck around to look at his father. He turned around just as Danny opened his eyes and a smile crept across his face. “Paddy?” “PADDY?” Then darkness 3. Final Showdown “PADDY?” The voice repeated again. Even in Paddy’s semi-conscious state, he knew there was worry in that voice and that was comforting. Paddy opened his eyes slowly. He was covered in sweat and was afraid of what he would look at. When he did open his eyes, the light was almost blinding. He was lying in his bed again. In his hospital room. Safe. It was light outside and it was sunny. He looked at the opened door. It wasn’t the corridor of horror he’d ran through but the one he’d expected before. Bustling and clean. He looked at where the voice was coming from and saw a familiar face…that of his brother. “A dream?” Paddy said out loud but obviously a question intended for himself. “A nightmare more like it. Ye were screamin’ Paddy!” said Owen. His face had worry on it but now this was subsiding. He returned to his seat beside the bed and sat down slowly. “What are ye doin’ here?” Paddy asked. He realised he sounded suspicious yet he found it hard to convey appreciation at this moment in time. “Aye came over t’ see ye. Aye wanted t’ see how ye were doin’. Ye’ve got tha’ match after all in a few days. Aye wanted t’ kno’ how ye were doin’.” “Aye’m fine…it’s just…me da.” Paddy said this with a sigh. “Look Paddy, aye’m just as worried about him as you but ye heard tha’ bastard last week. This Fresh! will be the last time ye’ll see him. He’s challenging you, t’ get Da back. So Paddy, ye need t’ be on top game this week t’ do it.” Paddy considered this thoroughly, latching on to Owen’s every word. He always was wise past his years. The last time he would see his father? Paddy had been scared stiff all week about those words, he had no hope at all, none. He’d always maintained that he was this masked man’s toy, a mannequin on strings to use whatever way the puppet master decided. By God, he was sitting in a hospital bed, afraid to leave the room! “Not anymore” Paddy said out loud. “What?” Owen asked clearly confused. Paddy began to get up from his bed with a groan. But he was swift. His bandages had all been removed and now he was waiting for the final stages of healing. Paddy supposed he’d tried to slow the recovery so he wouldn’t have to face the inevitable. But now, he was awakened and hope was restored to him. “This bastard thinks he has me…he thinks Paddy O’Shea is a broken man. Maybe he was right. Last week aye was friendly to people. People tellin’ me how much they hoped aye got better and beat this masked man. An’ like the sucker aye am aye welcomed their sympathy. The simple fact is Owen, these people couldn’t give a flying fuck.” There was anger in Paddy’s voice now. “But Paddy, it’s not the peoples fault its-“ “Aye know it’s not their fault. But my reactions have made me realise how soft aye am. Aye just stood back on the defensive these last weeks, takin’ this man’s blows whilst curled up in a ball. This week, aye can’t cause there be too much at stake. This week, aye’m gonnae get this masked man an’ aye’m goin’ t’ get me father back.” “And yer match?” Owen inquired. “Don’ worry Owen…aye’ll fight. An’ aye’ll win. Aye’m gonnae send this masked man a message: tonight be the night aye kill the bastard. He can bring all his little feckin’ minions but it’ll make no difference…on tha’ night it isn’t gonnae be the night aye last see me father…tha’ night be the night where the masked man last sees at all.” Owen’s eyes were looking at this man before him. This wasn’t the happy go lucky gypsy that had entered the AWC a month ago. He supposed there always was only a certain amount a man could take. Paddy began pulling the medicinal apparatus from his Paddy. All the tubes and needles. “Paddy?” “Don’ try an’ stop me Owen. Aye’m goin’ now, RIGHT NOW! Aye’m no’ gonnae sit around and wait fer a solution. Aye’m goin’ out t’ take it.” And just as Owen sat in the chair, confused at his brothers’ change of approach and just as Paddy walked out the door, Owen asked himself, was it something he said? END
