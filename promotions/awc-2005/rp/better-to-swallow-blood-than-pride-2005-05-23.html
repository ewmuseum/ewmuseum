'Better To Swallow Blood Than Pride'

23 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-23-05 He stood stiff, his narrow eyes staring at the reflective image of himself; beaten and sore. Grimacing, he contorted his body; turning his damaged back towards the hotel mirror so he could get a visual to match the ceaseless, stinging pain he felt from the small of his back. It was the annoying kind of pain, but somehow debilitating, as if you had a deep hang-nail on each one of your fingers and were scheduled to play piano concerto in front of an audience of thousands. He frowned when he saw how much damage had been done. Ugly, pink and red scratches ran width-wise just above his waist, where the band of his wrestling tights stopped the splintered table from slashing more of his tender skin open. Light yellow bruising marred his once perfect skin, millions of capillaries having been crushed between the weight of his bones and the unforgiving table Pierce LaVelle forced him through with a wicked power-bomb. Sure, Alex knew he’d been through worse, but this time he wasn’t as lucky as he was in the past. This time, his attackers had left a mark. Alex was embarrassed and angry; the very thought of someone getting the better of him, even for an instant, made him sick to his stomach. He thought back to the day before, and his match with Pierce LaVelle. Alex had given the match his best treatment: Attacking Pierce before the match was even officially underway was a stroke of genius. He held the upper hand for so long, but didn’t end it fast enough. Sloppy. He shook his head, rubbing antibiotic cream along the gashes on his back. It stung, but not worse than knowing that for one moment, one fleeting moment, Pierce LaVelle held the upper hand in the match. When he went through that table…he wasn’t meant to get up again. He knew only blackness. Strider bit his lip as he wrapped the gauze tightly around his superficial wounds before tying it off cleanly inside itself. He would have liked it if the pain were sharper, more focused. Instead he was left with feeling a general soreness, stiff and unrelenting. It constantly reminded him of how close he came to defeat. ”Shut up,” he thought, closing his eyes. The sting was there, still, mocking him. “If it hadn’t been for Hate…” he thought, blood rushing to his cheeks, the anger and frustration boiling over. Hate come out of nowhere and leveled both Alex and Pierce. Both men being left so broken they could not complete the match. “If only it hadn’t been for Hate...” “If it hadn’t been for Hate, what then?” His thoughts now answering themselves inside his head, he continued, “If Hate hadn’t interfered when he did, LaVelle would have pulled you back inside the ring, and taken your prize for his own, that’s what!” Strider seethed, gritting his teeth as he silently berated himself. “You owe it to Hate, that zealous bastard, for saving you when you couldn’t save yourself, you pussy!” Strider turned, frantic, reaching for anything that was handy. His hand groping across the counter top it found an unused coffee mug. He tensed, reeling back and throwing the mug as hard as he could against the furthest wall. It sailed beautifully, like a small ceramic bird. Well, maybe a small ceramic bird that was thrown full force by a professional wrestler, at any rate. The mug shattered against the wall, and Strider strangely felt some relief for the moment, the tense energy that had built up inside of him spent in a moment of pure rage. Alex sighed, as he looked around his Spartan hotel room. It was dark, his blinds pulled shut tight to keep the early morning sun as far away as possible. While he usually would allow himself the luxury of enjoying the finer things in life, this time Alex forwent the amenities thinking his stay in Atlanta would not be a long one. Well, things change, and here he was, biding his time until he felt his body was road-worthy. No, that’s not true. Strider just didn’t feel like moving anymore. Strider was hiding out. He felt like everyone could see his shame. “Well, not for long,” he promised himself. “I’ll show them. It’s better to swallow blood than pride.” Tap-Tap-Tap, I weakly knocked on his hotel door. “Who is it,” he called out forcefully, “and what do you want?” “It’s the woman you asked to come a thousand miles out of her way specifically to see you. and I suppose what I want is to talk to you or something,” I rang back with a sarcastic reply. “Even though you’re the one who really wants to talk to me,” I thought to myself. “One second, Pam, I’m getting dressed.” Strider glanced at himself in the mirror as he pulled a white undershirt on; hiding away the most visible signs of the preceding night’s onslaught he endured. When he answered the door he smiled warmly. “Hi, Pam. Thanks for coming on such short notice, I really appreciate it,” he said, I looked up at him quizzically. “This is a new side of you, Alex,” I said, testing the water, “Trying what it’s like to be nice on for size? Do you like it?” “Don’t push too hard, little lady,” Alex said coyly, “you don’t know what you’ll end up finding underneath the rocks.” I stepped inside his modest hotel room, my laptop at my side, ready to ask the questions to delve even deeper into the strange case of “The Dead Groupie In the Wrestler’s Bathroom”. I sat down, and we went straight to business. Alex told me about his interrogation at the hands of the brutish Floridian police detectives. I told him that Sophia Riley, who was only nineteen years old and a freshman at Pitt University had never been in trouble before. That didn’t set his mind at ease. ”That only means that there is something larger going on, Pam. Somebody pushed her into my lap, wanting us both to take a fall.” I catch myself staring at him, my eyes following the rigid curves of his chest down to . I hate to admit it, but he does look fantastic in just a white t-shirt and jeans. I mean, he better, he probably paid out of his ass for the jeans, the conceited bastard. I want to scream inside my head, NO! I can't find him attractive. He's so not attractive. He's selfish, arrogant, conceited. There's not a single redeeming quality I can think of off the bat. ... Ok, manly and handsome, but there is so much more missing than is there, it isn't even worth thinking about. Besides, even if his personality was completely different, or if it was somehow magically upgraded (like he fell into a coma and became a vegetable,) there's no way he'd ever think I was attractive. I saw that Sophia Riley. All legs, butt and breasts. I have none of those things. 'Pam? What are you doing?' he asked. My God, how long had I been staring? I beg my brain to shut off, and start thinking about the report. 'Nothing, just thinking about...the case. You were saying?' I ask, brushing my hair back out of my face, my face hot from blushing a bright pink. We spent the next hour going over the facts and making a battle plan for the investigation. I told Alex I was going to be printing a story on what we found out, and he agreed, “So long as I’m able to move on as quickly as possible.” I reminded him that Sophia’s parents would also like to know what really happened to their child. “That’s fine. Then they’ll have to drop the wrongful death lawsuit they’ve got out against me,” he answered as I’d expect him too: coldly. The plan we came up with was to find out who she was friends with, get to know what she had been up too, and most importantly, how such a young and seemingly innocent teenager got her hands on hard drugs. “O.k., I like that this is starting to come together now,” he said, rubbing his neck. I could tell he’d been through another wringer. “The next show’s been cancelled. I have almost a full two weeks where I can do nothing but work on clearing my name back in Pennsylvania.” I nodded. ”So, what’s been going on in the AWC Alex? Have you at all changed what we were talking about before? You know, acting less like you really could be capable of committing the crimes you’re under suspicion of?” It was a loaded question, I knew he hadn’t but I was interested in finding out why. “No. I don’t change my actions just because there’s suspicion. I’ve made my bed. I’m going to sleep in it. This is who I am, you know? If we’re smart about this and do all the legwork, you’ll have a clutch story and I’ll have the satisfaction of showing everyone how wrong they are.” “And not having to pay off money, to boot. Why do you get off on being such a jerk to everyone?” I asked flatly “I don’t get off on it, Pamela. It’s just in my nature. That’s how I was raised, to aspire to greatness. You break somebody down and then they either expire or they’re built up in to a rock solid machine. That’s what I am. If the others can’t handle it, then too bad.” He paused, reflecting on what he’d just said. ”BESIDES! It’s guys like Harber who are making shit tough on me, for no good reason! I’ve got to slum it with the Midcarders, this week AND at the next show! Do you believe it? I’m the champion! I am the most revered wrestler in the entire federation, and guys like Pact are main eventing in my spot. Disgusting.” “So you’ll keep throwing tantrums until things are put back in their natural order?” I ask dryly. ”Exactly! Thank you, it’s good to see somebody understands my reasoning!” He was earnest. It made me a little embarrassed to think he believed that just because I could identify where he was coming from that I felt the same way. Strider never took responsibility. Everything was always somebody elses fault. From Sophia, to Harber; Hate to his father. It was never Alex, always someone else. No use in trying to change him this late in the game, I suppose. I just nod and listen as he gives laundry lists of all the people that he knows and hates.
