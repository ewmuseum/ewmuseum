The Seduction of Culture: A Tempered Steel Overview

23 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23rd January 2005 The Seduction of Culture – A Tempered Steel Overview The scene opens with a close-up shot of a leggy blonde in a mustard yellow suit coat and short skirt. Randi: Hello AWC fans, this is Randi. I’ve sort of been following Tempered Steel around for the last 10 years and the guys wanted to do, like, a non-typical first promo, so, like I was totally stoked when they asked me to shoot a documentary because I’m totally an aspiring filmmaker and I have a lot of ideas that I don’t think folks really listen to, maybe because I’m good looking or something... I don’t know. Anyway, because I used to bartend at the bar Hammer and Anvil were regu... well, honestly they were only semi-regulars comes to think of it with all of that silly wrestling training they have to keep up with, so since they knew me and they asked nice I decided to compile this special report to sort of introduce the boys to you. Hope you don’t mind. Randi winks at the camera before the scene re-opens on a deserted steel mill. Randi’s crow-like voice can be heard narrating. The on-screen images switch in a slow progression of Hammer and Anvil in various wrestling poses or actually in action in various independent promotions. Randi: (voiceover) One of the strange paradoxes of a brand-spanking-new tag team is that every single match is at once the most important and the least important of their careers. There is always going to be the pressure of making a lasting first impression, of clinching that seductively sweet first victory in the face of those who shamelessly engage in schadenfreude. But there is also infinite time to recover from poor showings by simply hitting a groove and stringing together a few unexpected victories. The problem is, no one ever seems to consider culture. The camera cuts to an image of Randi looking proud of herself before switching back to images of a steel mill or Tempered Steel in action. Randi: (voiceover) Hammer and Anvil are two hard working blokes from the Steel City of Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. They are products of their culture – nothing more and nothing less. And, despite some of the academic academy’s fanciful thinking, the very values that Andrew “Hammer” Shepard and Ryan “Anvil” Hayes live every day of their lives have been shaped by their culture. It is common middlebrow philosophy, when one takes the time to consider it, that if one grows up in an isolated community one tends to adapt the local norms of said community as the universal norms of “what should be” for all, creating a certain air of mutual desperation and arrogant intolerance. It is exactly those cultural factors that no one considers – helping out Sonny from the neighborhood when the potential consequences were grave was not instinct. Sonny was just another loudmouth creep, just another wannabe who hated his job and his poor life choices. The behavior to help, to “stick up for one of their own,” had been learned. Hammer and Anvil grew up in the tightly knit Oakland section of Pittsburgh, a closely-knit working-class community by all accounts. And people from the same communities often share the same sorts of local ideals and myths and it was no different in Oakland. Ultimate life success would be to become foreman of a steel mill, or, better yet, chairman of your own steel company. Rouge entrepreneurs have made a decent living doing auto repair and electrical work but in Pittsburgh they are clearly a full social rung below the captains of steel. In a lot of ways, Tempered Steel ignorantly or naively embodies a number of outdated modes of thinking as if they are the freshest concepts ever – the absurd thought that hard work alone will result in ultimate success. The primary school thought that there exists a “cosmic piggy bank” and that all energy expended shall result in equal energy received. Hammer and Anvil are tightly wound machines in need of a week-long defrag. Luckily, they’ve been booked this week. The sounds of violins screeching in the background are abruptly interrupted by Anvil. “Randi, this is garbage,” Hayes says with spittle escaping from his hissing. “It’s not going anywhere.” “Oh please,” Randi pleads, flipping her blonde hair back over her right shoulder in an attempt to look as available as possible. “Stop this now,” Anvil says with a bit of a growl. “Tempered Steel have come to AWC for a reason, and the reason isn’t to be the biggest freaking freak show going. We’re here to make the Alliance Championship something that someone actually needs to strive for.” “Yeah,” interjects Hammer, feeling a bit left out of the proceedings, “but we should get back and keep thinking about all this philosophy and crap, man. Let’s go.” Randi watches Hammer and Anvil leave before she pulls a secret microphone back out from her pocket. “You see folks,” Randi begins, looking down the hallway to ensure Tempered Steel didn’t know what was going on, “these boys are serious but they have no idea what they’re getting themselves into! They’re actually too innocent! When they realize that a whole lot of folks have been making a whole lot of money off of them they are really going to be ... oh hi Hammer!” Andy Shepard returns looking around as if he may have forgotten something. “Hey Randi - you see a pair of Ray-Bans around here? They cost $100 I can’t afford to lose another pair – Anvil won’t let me get another one.” Randi looks at Hammer with a quizzical look. “I thought ‘someone’ was taking care of your expenses these days? Hammmm – er?” Hammer looks back at Randi with a strained expression. “You be quiet about all that rumpus now. It’s time for Tempered Steel to get to work.”
