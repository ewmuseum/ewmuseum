Going Home (Part 2 of 2)

30 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30/6/05 1. Where has he gone? Sitting in a police station in his hometown of Athenry was Paddy O’Shea. He knew every inch of it: from the decolouring cream walls, to the way the jail doors creaked loudly when opened. He could even recite every sentence of graffiti in the toilets. And why? Well, Paddy hadn’t been the most law-abiding youth with ventures into stealing and illegal fighting mainly the key reasons. But now, he wasn’t here for him, he was here for his father. Paddy is dressed in the same suit he wore on his way to Athenry – the open collared black pin striped suit with the polished shoes (which have rapidly become bland from the fall of dust in his wait in the lobby). He is wearing no hat, which is highlighted by the fact that Paddy is running his hands through his scruffy, dirty fair hair with his body propped forward. The distress on his face is clear. Suddenly, a police officer approaches him. A quick glance at a wall clock reveals the time is just after three O’clock and in the morning because of the view of darkness out of a window. The officer is wearing the traditional Irish Garda uniform, with a light blue shirt and a black tie and trousers. His face tries to look sympathetic but the transparency of this forced emotion is extremely obvious. “Any news James?” Paddy asks immediately as he sees him. His face has picked up to show the many stress lines but hope is now in his eyes “I’m sorry Paddy…we’re no further in progress than we were at the start” James replies in an English accent in stark contrast to Paddy’s Irish one. There had never been any love lost between the two in earlier times and maybe the cultural divide was the reason for this. After all, there was never a very friendly attitude to the English in the more conservative parts of Ireland. “There are no prints on this note they left, nor is this a handwriting we are familiar with” “Well wha’ are ye’s goin’ t’do? Me father has been kidnapped. They can’ be far. Why don’ ye’s go get him?” Paddy asks more than perturbed. “Tha’ be wha’ yer paid fer. Somebody has taken my father now ye’s should be earning yer pay check” “Look Paddy”, says James as he straightens up. He obviously doesn’t like these situation and wants out of there quick. “We have men out looking and he’s been reported missing. We may have a lead near your home of unusual tyre tracks. We are trying” “Tyre tracks? Wha’ t’ye mean it may be a lead? We don’ have a car!” “Well, yes…it would usually look very likely to be linked however…Well, we have identified the type of person to kidnap your father as lowly and desperate. This is because your father has rarely been out of Athenry in his life, yes?” “Aye” “Well, therefore, your father had no high up enemies. Therefore the operation is likely to be botched. Well the tyre tracks have been identified as being of a Porsche 911 and as I’m sure you’re aware, no one in any nearby area has a car like that. The fact is, it doesn’t make sense someone your father has never met has kidnapped him.” “So wha’ yer sayin’ is tha’ he’s been kidnapped t’get back at someone” “That’s right. At the minute, we’re focusing on that possibility. Now, the rest of your family have also rarely been away from this area…and that leaves you” James replies a little more firmly than intended. “Me?” Paddy seems a little confused. “But aye don’ have an enemy in the world…especially someone who has a Porsche.” “But you have moved to America to…wrestle? And well, in a federation like that where money is plentiful, enemies will be plentiful and rich. If that is the case, we cannot do much for the case. We can refer the case to some branch of the US police but since kidnapping is more common there, you may be waiting a long time. I suggest you stay here in Ireland and wait – if you go over there you might be wrestling the same people that did this act and if they’re capable of that, they’re capable of anything. At least if you stay here, we can conduct enquiries if the kidnappers make contact with you” Paddy seems to think this over. He doesn’t want to believe this is because of him but he knows what James is saying makes sense. In wrestling, you make enemies - powerful enemies. Powerful enemies that know where you live, whose in your family and have the resources to pull off this operation. He thought about telling James to secure airports but he knew now that the kidnappers were long gone. Paddy took a small bag from James hand. James let him do so. As Paddy smoothed the bag out flat, he saw clearly a note inside…the note they left behind. He reads it one more to himself, murmuring as he does so: “Danny O’Shea is now our prisoner and will be released (if ever) on our discretion. You will pay for what you have done” This crime that Paddy thought was initially motiveless was now pointing at him. This was Paddy’s fault… 2. The calm before the storm Paddy had arrived at the welcome home party at 7 O’clock in the evening. As he stepped from the Toyota Carina he lent from his cousin, he smoothed himself down and looked in the mirror to make sure his hair was neat and combed. This rarity was reserved for special occasions and Paddy included this one as one. He couldn’t wait to see his father now who had seemingly turned a corner in his life and was behind Paddy and supporting his career. Paddy didn’t care about the wasted years of neglect; all he wanted now was to see him. He wanted the chance to make the lost time up. Paddy walked over the loose stone path and it crunched under his feet. He made sure he hadn’t kicked up any dust to take the polish from his shoes. He seen a speck, bent down and wiped it with a handkerchief. He wondered what his family would think of this pansy before him…and he smiled Just before he pushed through the front door that led into a lounge, he acknowledged the building. Harry’s. A bar he knew had a lot of meaning in the O’Shea dynasty. It had been their drinking spot since the original proprietor Harry McGuiness opened it sixty years ago. It’s old-fashioned thatched roof and crude rock structure was only used for show and he knew. It had been renovated not five years ago and he knew all others on the surrounding areas envied this bar. When he pushed open the door, it seemed to him that ten thousand heads spun around, first with faces of confusion then with faces of welcome. There were actually only about thirty people there but the fact that it seemed everyone of his close family was there excited him. Even his cousin who had lent him the car was here. He was then hailed with every welcome under the rainbow. He heard ‘Top o’ the mornin’, ‘long time no see’ and ‘Comere ye wee whore’ among others. Paddy could only smile at them. His family had personality and that was for sure. “Hallo”, Paddy relied simply with a grin he just couldn’t straighten out He received a handshake from seemingly everyone within five minutes before things started to calm down a little. Then his brother Owen came over. For a minute he thought he was looking at the innocent six-year-old boy he had been at the beach with sixteen years ago. The boy who had trouble swimming but attempted it anyway with all the enthusiasm of a hyped up stallion. He still had that same cheeky smile that he wore as he walked over. His sandy colour hair was still untidy but at least he was wearing a shirt and tie. “Comere bro. Wha’ the feck t’ye want with folks like us superstar” Paddy sniggered beside himself. “Ahh, shad up. Someone has t’keep ye in check don’ they. Thought aye’d come back t’make sure yer out of trouble. Speaking of which, are ye still workin’ fer tha’ sleazebag McDermott?” “Aye. He’s alright ye know jus’ a little…misleading” “Jus’ watch yerself, the man be trouble. Aye think even ye can see tha’” Owen only laughed long and hard. “Ah shut up would ye. Are ye here t’nag or t’get plastered?” Paddy only winked at Owen before turning to talk to someone else. Then he remembered something and swung back around “Where’s me da?” “…. He should be here, he organised the thing. Aye s’pose he may still be at the caravan.” Owen answered almost indifferently “Right, aye’ll go get him then and-“ “No chance”, Owen interrupted. “Aye’ll get him, this be yer party. Now enjoy yerself. There be a buffet over there. Go eat something would ye?” Paddy let Owen go but already he had a feeling that something was out of place. When there was an event at a bar, Danny O’Shea was first there and last to leave. But Paddy quickly pushed this suspicion to the back of his mind. Owen was right, he should enjoy himself. Paddy went over to the buffet despite a few people trying to ask him how things were going in the US. Paddy nibbled at a few cocktail sausages and sipped at a pint of Guinness for the next while. For the next half hour or so, Paddy was asked the same tedious questions but every time he answered them with as much enthusiasm as he could muster. These folks had begun watching the AWC shows. They wanted to know how Paddy was getting on, would he take the Alliance title belts and blah blah blah. This lack of enthusiasm isn’t to say that Paddy didn’t enjoy seeing the faces again but he quickly became reacquainted with the annoying behaviour of family members. An example was Uncle Dermott who he was talking to just before Owen returned. “Paddy…(hic), aye would like t’ say on (hic) behalf o’ all the family, how proud we be of (hic) ye” Dermott had said as he swayed from side to side with a pint of Harp sloshed about in the glass. His receding hairline was temporarily covered by untidy hair but the smile he wore was one only a drunk could muster “Aye…ah…thanks Dermott. It be…ah…appreciated” “Ahh…don’ get shy with yer best Uncle in the world. Aye’m yer best uncle. Wha’ am I? “My best Uncle” Paddy said with an exasperated tone, something Dermott didn’t pick up on Then he heard Owen calling him which he thanked God for for getting him out of there. “Aye’ll be back with ye in a second Dermott” Paddy strided over towards the door, expecting to see the bent over figure of his father. Instead, he saw only Owen. His eyes were red and his face was desperate. He was clutching a note in his hand “Wha’ be wrong” Paddy asked desperately “They took him,” Owen moaned “Who did? WHO?” Paddy shouted angrily. Everyone was listening now and the look of concern of replicated on every face “AYE DON’ KNOW! HERE TAKE THIS!” Owen handed Paddy the note before running out. Upon reading it, Paddy went numb. 3. We shall overcome As Paddy ran the days events over and over in his head, he began to feel sick. He couldn’t explain the complexity of his emotions now but he could identify anger as one. James had asked him to stay put for his own safety. He expected his wrestling career had led to this event and that enemies were plentiful. He expected Paddy to sit back and wait for things to happen That was not going to happen Paddy would go back to the AWC and fight in the AWC and look for answers. And the first suspects? The three other teams involved in the Alliance title division. He didn’t expect to get answers from sitting around. He would beat answers from them and he would win those belts for his father. He would send a message…the people behind this act better run and hide because people would pay… END
