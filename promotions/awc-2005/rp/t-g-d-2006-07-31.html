Date: 7.31.06 FAIR WARNING: Please don't look directly at this RP. I don't want to be responsible for what happens if somebody tries to figure out what the hell I was talking about here. Please kids, let's learn from this tragedy and together we can put and end to drunken RPing. Thanks, and try to enjoy. *** Alright, humor me: it’s 2:00 AM, you can’t sleep. Maybe you’re drunk, maybe you just got off work., whatever. For Lord knows what reason, you’re flipping through the channels on the TV in search of late-night entertainment. Nothing is really catching your eye – sure, cable advertisers are desperate to capture your attention with Sluts Gone Wild, massive blimp explosions and oh-so-captivating infomercials, but you’ve seen them all before. Tonight, however, deserves something new. And at the back end of the cable box, on the channel numbering somewhere in the deep hundreds, you find something new indeed. Evidently just in time for the opening segue, the scene fades into view as a man sits atop a black stool, dressed in a slightly formal black suit and tie. His hair is slicked back, and he puffs away at a cigarette. He looks up, staring into the camera, using his end of the connection to match your gaze. He’s been expecting you. And he has a message. “Three days,” he says, with the sharp eloquence a decades-old radio show announcer. “Such an arbitrary measurement of time, if you think about it. Three revolutions of a small blue globe floating in infinite abyss. An extremely tiny fraction of said globe’s revolution around a much bigger globe, one exerting the fire of life that is breathed into us all. “Merely a portion carved from the dimension we know to be time, a point of reference if you will, utilized to examine the flow of existence from one moment to the next. This provokes an emotional response. But what kind, exactly? It depends on who’s asking. An infinite amount of events can take place within this established three-day period, anywhere at all, capable of affecting an uncountable amount of organisms capable of formulating a response. “But how can one accurately respond to the events that take place over such an arbitrary period of time? Again, it depends on the point of view. A good way to examine these sorts of intellectual quandaries is to observe a lone example as a case study. So I think that’s what we should do. From all the life to be found in this universe, we shall pick one at random and inspect how they react to a passage in time that we’ve come to measure as ‘three days.’ ” A leggy assistant enters the scene, dropping a stack of papers as tall as Verne Troyer on our host’s lap. He removes a pair of reading glasses from his suit coat pocket and examines the list. “Oh, let’s see, I don’t know…I’ll just pick a name at random here…” he mumbles a little less eloquently as he scrolls through the list. “How about…Aha! This one looks interesting. One Amy Campbell, a professional wrestler hailing from Nova Scotia. Currently residing with her boyfriend in a much nicer, warmer place to live. She’s had quite the interesting past three days, indeed. Three days have passed since an ex-lover of said boyfriend was kidnapped by a friend of Miss Campbell’s. Yes, this will do quite nicely.” The man looks back in your direction, removing his reading glasses for poise bonus points. “So we have our subject. We have our period of measurement. And we even have a guiding event, the kidnapping of one Jane O’Connor to guide us in the examination. And to do this, it is best to investigate the evolving of the situation, start to finish.” The scene fades from the announcer to a first-hand perspective of the forthcoming events, with our host continuing his narration via voiceover. “Of course, Miss Campbell has a large stake in making sure that her boyfriend and fellow professional wrestler Darcy Markson does not know who is responsible for kidnapping O’Connor. She is unable to prevent him from knowing of the kidnapping, as he had stumbled upon the scene shortly after her abduction, but she was able to take some solace in knowing that she’d covered her tracks fairly well. She took no part in the actual kidnapping, nor had she made anyone else other than the kidnapper aware of Jane’s location. When Darcy did not return that evening she assumed that he must have stumbled upon the scene of the crime, but when he made no attempt to contact her she rested assured that he did not know that she’d played a hand in the affair. “But there was no sign of him the next day, either. The only logical conclusion was that he was throwing himself wholeheartedly into finding Jane’s whereabouts. Amy awoke to a message left on the answering machine from Darcy in which he apologized for not returning home the prior evening, as well as a promise to contact her later that day…but that contact never took place. However, once again, even though she missed Darcy, she was ironically relieved by the lack of contact – for it meant that she still appeared uninvolved. But one day became two. Two became three. And three days, for our friend Amy Campbell…is more like three goddamn days. *** “Darcy Rules! I knew it!” Amy yelped out loud, to no one in particular. She knew his e-mail password had to be something dated, completely corny, and probably self-gratifying. Ever since she’d cracked his last password of “IMAWESOME,” she figured something to a similar effect wouldn’t be far off the mark, even though it took her some eighty attempts until she cracked the latest. She wasn’t one to normally invade his privacy like that. But two very key facts changed the situation to fit her adjusted code of ethics. Number one was that this was not the situation she was hoping for at all. Although Jane was out of danger from being kidnapped by the people who were actually looking for her, it had come of the expense of keeping Darcy out of the house for what had now become days on end; days in which he presumably labored for hours in trying to rescue Jane from her predicament. If there was any graceful exit from this to be had, it would only come if she could get Darcy to return home, where she could somehow find a way to explain precisely how things came to transpire as they did. And out of Key Fact #1 arises Key Fact #2: It seemed likely in those few days that Darcy sent at least an e-mail or two from his personal account, e-mails which might shed some light on where he’d been all this time. Amy’s password crack allowed her access to his e-mail account, to which she hastily navigated to the “Sent” folder. And true enough, in the past three days he had sent several e-mails to the outside world.
