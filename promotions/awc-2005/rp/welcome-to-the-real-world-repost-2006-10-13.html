Welcome to the Real World (Repost)

13 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 13, 2006 (ooc:the original post was compromised. Myself and Pierce posted at the same time, his got the 803 spot. Mine, not so much.) I could only just sit there… Sit there and stare, through my plastic throw back Ray ban’s and a 5’5, 110 pound sack of flesh, bone, and lies. Never mind the fact that two other awkward chumps sat at either side of me, sipping down cold, stale coffee and tip-typing away to go knows who behind the little monitors in the back of this inconspicuous white van. “You get it yet?” She barked at me. I glanced back down at her tweed pocket again. I didn’t get any of it. Especially not the little piece of paper that lied in that Banana Republic-born pocket, wrapped around the skin of an unfortunate cow, that told me that she had the right to do pretty much whatever she wanted, thanks to the government. If there were any letters more dangerous to me than B.O.T., it would be F.B.I. “Sure…” I lied. “This is being taped, and Ashton Kutcher is gonna open that door back there and tell me I’ve been Punk’d.” I learned in my career that when bad things happen, its best to pretend you really don’t care. Makes dealing with the consequences a little easier, because like with most things, if you pretend they’re true long enough, they might actually become the truth eventually. “You think this is funny, then?” She grabbed my shoulders aggressively. Like fore-play, but not as fun, because she had a gun at her waist, and a temper in her eye. And I don’t care who you are, a gun is not a good sex toy. “What I think is funny is that you and your monkey suit shit-eaters decided to abduct me in broad daylight against my well. Then you flash a stupid little badge in my face and tell me to play snitch for you.” I shrugged her hands off my rather expensive shirt. “Where I come from… Hell, where WE come from, you gotta give to get sometimes.” “Yes, well, where I come from and where I am are two very different things. Now if we were back in the projects in East Paterson, drunk off our puppy love, then maybe I’d give you a pretty please. But in two days, the only chance I’m going to get to bust The Society is going to present itself within you, and I’m going to need to make sure your not going to screw this up.” “I just want to pay them what they want, and get them out of my life.” I really had a hard time caring about the moral decisions the men and women of the Society made daily. The less they make these decisions around me, the better, I always say. “You can’t get them out of your life if your brother is among there ranks.” She claimed. “You’ve no proof that he is!” I retorted. I would have stood, as I was starting to get un happy with this interrogation, but It didn’t take genius to see that a man of my stature didn’t really have room to be one of those stand-and-screamers in this little van. “We have photos from years ago that prove that he was at the very least, making contact with these people.” She takes the only few steps she can take in the cramped up space backwards, to lean up against the walls of the interior.” And don’t you find it a little suspicious that he knows ALL the itty-bitties about where and when to meet them for the drop?” “They called him… Phone numbers are easy to steal nowadays. And fuck your pictures. Dimitri isn’t a mobster. You know this.” “I’m not saying he is now. I’m just not ruling out the possibility that he wasn’t in the past. Your brother knows something, and I need to know it too. It appears you don‘t know anything, though. So at the next light your free to go.” She turned around, as if that was going to end the conversation. As if her back turned towards me meant that everything we ever had was shut off, wholly and completely. “So that’s it, huh?” She didn’t react. “You leave on a sabbatical for which I now know was to become a federal officer, you come back, using the feelings you know I still had for you to trick me into involving myself with mobsters so that you can make a stupid catch. You’ll probably get some kind of reward, right? A citation, a plaque and a raise, maybe? When you eventually retire, and they give you your little gun-in-a-glass case, along with the picture of your peers, you’ll be remember for the person who did everything she had to do for justice. Crush friendships? ‘Sure why not?’ Break hearts? ‘Ok, what else?’ Feel good about that when you wake up tomorrow morning.” “ I will…” She turned, giving me a soulless look. “I’m going to do what I have to catch these bastards. Bastards who will take no heed when it comes to the lives and feelings of others. Sounds familiar, right? Don’t even paint me as the bad guy here for doing something you’ve been doing to people for your entire life. The only difference between me and you, is the fact that I’m doing this to save lives all over the world. Why do you do it, huh? Why do you sit atop your high horse, throwing money around like its confetti, shitting on people that you deem un important? What makes you a person who can do that, and me a person who can’t? What, because you’ve won some stupid fucking championships in some stupid fucking wrestling matches?” She paused, and signaled to the man in the drivers seat to stop. “All I’m asking you to do is to co-operate with me in the real world for one day, so I can do something that‘ll help people. Then ill let you go back to your sick, sad little one.” I couldn’t look at her. How dare she even insult me and everything I do so viciously. “What do you want me to do?” I asked. “When were out of sight, call Dimitri, tell him you found me and to make the arrangements to meet you at the drop spot with the money in New Orleans. Don’t tell him anything that happened here, or I swear to god, ill have you arrested for obstruction. You’ll proceed as normal throughout today. Go to the Leon Arena and compete as per usual, and as early as possible, we will leave Spain towards Louisiana. When we meet up with Lady Harken, don’t immediately give her the money. I’m going to have to send our location to the task force, and they’re going to have to let me know that their in position. Ill give you a better briefing tomorrow.” “Are we done here?” “Yes, your dismissed.” I turned and cracked the door open quickly. They sped off as soon as my feet touched the cobblestone road. I was still very much offended, but I couldn’t really form the words to defend myself from this insults. Maybe because, deep down, I knew she was telling the truth. I needed this to end, so I could get back on track at least… This drama was affecting my game. Speaking of my game, I was two hours past the time I intended to begin warming up, as my first AWC pay per view event would take place tonight. There’s an old cliché about first impressions that’s usually made for this situation, but personally, I was too hurt to make it. Sashenka had thrown my heart under the bus for her country, without remorse or second thought, and left me with the broken pieces. I felt like a little girl feeling this way, but I felt relieved at the same time. Relieved at knowing that after 13 years of doing this shit, I still had a heart somewhere. Maybe when this was all over, ill investigate her claims about my moral fiber, right now, I had an ass to beat. It was gonna be hard to focus on the task at hand, though. I’ve had an awkward couple of weeks, and trying to scout the competition in the brand new horizon of a wrestling federation I’ve fallen across has become difficult, hell impossible, with all this drama and hoopla. I didn’t know what to make of this Society or Sashenka, but I did know it was taking its toll on one of the only things I’ve become really good at: Hurting people. I’m embarrassing myself in front a world stage because of my life’s little distractions, and it didn’t look like it was going to get any better for my next outing. Thank god this was going to end soon. If Sash is right, and this is the real world. A world where I can’t do what I’m known for because of someone else’s irresponsibility, than I’ll take my sick, sad one over it any day.
