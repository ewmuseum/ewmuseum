The Underhand That Feeds

31 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/31/06 Honor... is there such a word more misused in any language or meaning nowadays? Everyone claims to have it, yet what they do belies what they say, at least in the most traditional sense of the word. Everyone's connotation of honor seems to fall along the idea of moral superiority through kind and selfless acts. Captain Suleimon saw this in a different way though. 'You can't tell me that the pashas of Ottoman Turkey had honor,' said Dogan. 'The current government of Turkey has done so much to undo the wrongs they did.' 'You call that honor? I call it spinelessness.' Yes, it's yet another argument between Dogan and Suleimon. When aren't they arguing? 'Yes, diplomacy is spinelessness.' 'Yes, I'm glad you see things my way. Case closed, now leave me alone, you jellyfish.' 'That was sarcasm, Suleimon. I'm sure a man of your supposed letters didn't detect it.' 'I did, and I chose to diffuse it with rank honesty. I'm not surprised at your reaction. Typical of someone who's afraid to admit that his ancestors, his Imperial ancestors, had more honor in their pinkies than all those in the administration now have in their entire bodies.' 'Honor? You call raping and pillaging southeastern Europe honorable? You call forced imperialism honrable? You call widespread genocide of the Armenian peoples honorable?' 'Yes, and you know...' 'No, stop, I've heard enough. You're obviously deranged, and I'm ending this con...' 'No you're not. You stand here and listen to me, you jellyfish. Our Ottoman ancestors may have done things that you and the whining women of the world today may call despicable, but they had honor, real honor. They had honor to their country by taking what they wanted, by building up themselves and their people! Not by acquiescing to Britain or the United States because that's what the rest of the world wants them to do!' Dogan looked at Suleimon, shaking his head in disgust. 'I don't even know why I talk to you.' When we last saw young Tariq, he was sitting, balled up in the corner of the training ring at the General's Academy, ill, worried to the point of vomiting. Just in case you had forgotten, he walloped another trainee, the fanatical Muslim Habib al-Shikah, with a steel chair much to the surprise of everyone in attendance. Even more surprising, the General yelled at Habib and not Tariq. He also said he would talk to young Tariq a little later. Well, it was a little later. Tariq sat in Rahman's room, fidgeting, nervous. He sweat, dry heaved a little. All his life as a wrestling fan, it was ingrained in his head that using weapons was dishonorable. In Tariq's youth, when he used to watch wrestling with Mehmet, his former boss would always yell at the TV when someone resorted to using foreign objects. 'That's not right, bastards,' he'd scream. 'They have to go and ruin the match like that.' It pissed him off so much that he'd throw things across the room. Cans, bottles, whatever he could get his hands on. One time, he became so incensed that he threw a brick at the TV, putting a crack right in the tube. It was a good thing he was well-to-do. Seeing that Mehmet was the one who really molded his interest in professional wrestling, these displays stuck with Tariq over the many years he was in Mehmet's employ. Even in dust ups on job sites, and fights on the street, Tariq would not resort to using weapons. He would jump in to break up fights between disgruntled workers, men who'd routinely arm themselves with hammers or trowels or even knives. One time, two men got into a fight on a job site, renovating the house of a local prelate. They began to argue heatedly over the correct wood to use for his upstairs railing. Words spilled over into shoves. Shoves into fists. The two men traded punches until one of them, a larger, bald man, knocked the shorter, buzz-cut worker to the ground. As the bald one turned his back, thinking the fight was won, the shorter man pulled a knife out of his pocket. Seeing this, Tariq, who was upstairs, saw this, and in an instant, leapt on top of the railing, used it as a springboard and jumped all the way on top of the knife-weilding assailant. It knocked the short man back to his bum, but Tariq screamed out in dire pain, not because he had leapt from a high place. The assailant had lodged the knife right in his side. The pain spilled forth from his eyes like water going down Victoria Falls. He looked down at his side then at the assailant and knew what he had to do. He drove his right forearm square into the buzz-cut man's nose. Even before the blood started rushing out of the crushed probiscous, Tariq shot to his feet, favoring his stabbed, left side, and stomped on the wrist of the arm his attacker was holding the knife. He kicked the knife away just as soon as the attacker let go, and then stomped his other foot on the attacker's chest. Standing, bloodied, over the knifer, holding his left side, Tariq barked to him. 'If you ever pull that stunt again, I will be sure to crush every single bone in both your hands.' Shortly thereafter, Tariq was rushed to the emergency room, but word soon spread among all the petty laborers in Istanbul, those who'd be apt to work for one Mehmet Enver. You had better behave or else that crazy kid will throw himself at you. As Tariq sat in Rahman's room, those two incidents played in his mind. Swinging the chair and cracking Habib over the head, then leaping from the upstairs onto someone just because they dared bring a weapon into a fair fight. He kept thinking to himself, how could he have gone from being an idealistic, young lad who threw his body to stop someone from being hurt by a foreign object to being the one who was bent not on winning, but on maiming? How could he go from the victim to the assailant? He was still trying to figure it out. Suddenly, the door opened and slammed shut, and in the instant between the two, Rahman stepped in. 'I can't believe it,' he said menacingly. Tariq winced as if he was going to be stricken with Rahman's right hand or worse. 'I can't believe it took you so long to figure it out.' Huh? Tariq was puzzled. He opened his eyes, and there was no anger in Rahman's eyes. There was no rancorous tone in his voice. He was stern, no doubt about it, but there was no vitriol. 'Fi... figure what out?' 'That you need to do everything it takes to make it in this world.' 'I... I don't understand.' Tariq was genuinely confused at this point. What exactly did he accomplish by whacking Habib over his head with that chair? 'Did you enjoy Habib's sanctimonious proclamations?' 'Umm... no.' 'Did you wish for him to stop?' 'Yes... I wanted him to stop.' 'Very well. And how did you try to get him to stop?' 'Uhh... umm...' Rahman stared intently in his pupil's eyes. Tariq tried desperately to find the answer. 'What did you do, hm? You didn't pray to his silly God, did you?' 'No... I... I tried to best him...' 'Best him where, on the heavy bag?' 'No... in the wrestling ring, sir.' 'Yes, in the wrestling ring. In the wrestling ring twice, he outclassed you, he broke you, because you were too preoccupied by this notion that you had to play fair to win.' Not entirely true, Tariq would have thought had he been in a stable frame of mind, but it did have some merit to it. Rahman continued, 'Then, the third time, your rage made you realize something, it made you realize that in order to get what you wanted, you had to bend the rules just a little. So you did what you did, you let your inner beast out. And it felt good, didn't it?' Tariq didn't know what to say. Should he tell the truth and say no, he was sickened by what he had done, or should he lie and say yes just to get the overbearing old man off his back? 'N... no... what I did was wrong. It was out of frustration... and... impulse.' 'Stop lying to yourself. You got what you wanted out of it.' 'No! Uhh, no I didn't. In a regular match, I would have been disqualified, I would have lost.' 'Tsk, tsk, so naïve. You're still so young in this business that all you care about is wins and losses. Don't you know you can get what you want without winning a match?' 'Uhh... uhh...' 'I can tell you're still upset. You know what, wait until the morning, and we'll speak more then.' Tariq couldn't sleep that night. His stomach still bothered him, making him toss and turn and toss and turn. His conscience was tearing him apart. All he could hear was the voice of Mehmet, shouting him down in disapproval for taking the chair to Habib's head. Mehmet was the closest thing to a father Tariq since his real father went to and died in prison. He got up at the crack of dawn and made his way down to the gym to begin training. He went over to his refuge, the heavy bag. For the few hours before the training started officially, Tariq unloaded his frustrations, hin insecurities, everything on the bag. At around 7:30, Rahman made his way down the stairs. He saw Tariq over at the heavy bag and was going to approach him, but someone else had followed him down the steps. 'You...' Rahman turned around to find Habib staring him right in the face. 'You have made a mockery of wrestling and of my God.' 'Are you sure it wasn't you who was the one doing that?' 'BLASPHEMY! You shut your mouth, I don't care if you're my elder or not, I would blast you in the face with my fist if I weren't such a peaceful child of Allah.' Rahman smirked and rolled his eyes. 'What I will do though is leave this maggot ridden corpse of a school,' said Habib. 'It's clear you don't want me here, and it's clear that I'm so much above you that your 'star pupil' had to resort to foul play to take me down. I'm going to make sure your reputation in Riyadh is besmirched so much that no one else will want to come here. Good day.' With that, Habib stormed out of the gym. Rahman muttered 'Good riddance' to him. He turned back around to see that Tariq had moved away from the heavy bag and was listening in on the whole exchange. After seeing and hearing everything, the lump in his stomach went away. Suddenly, some words came back to him. Not from Mehmet... but from a much more recent time. 'Don't you know you can get what you want without winning a match?' Rahman didn't talk to him the rest of the day. The day after though, it was a Thursday, and it was time for match sims. Tariq wouldn't be going against Habib for the first time in four sessions, so he went against Khan, who was the biggest of Rahman's students. The match started off with Khan overpowering Tariq. Clubbing forearms, headlocks, Khan used his size advantage over Tariq. Tariq didn't know how he was going to get out of the blocks, but then he got an idea. He looked out of the ring to Rahman, who gave him a gentle nod. They locked up again, but this time, Khan recoiled back, holding his eye. 'Hey! No fair!' cried Ozlem outside of the ring. 'He thumbed his bloody eye!' That Tariq did. He found his momentum and he drove forward. Strikes, carefully placed. Kicks to the legs, flying attacks. Tariq had him lined up for a flying axehandle at one point, but Khan countered it... by grabbing his throat mid-air. It was going to be a chokeslam, but Tariq had other ideas, like a kick to the jimmies. 'Dirty pool!' one of the Flying Carpets exclaimed. Still, no bell had been rung. Tariq followed it up with a neckbreaker. More advantage for Tariq, who worked Khan over, still not being able to put him away. Tariq Irish whipped Khan, but Khan reversed it and leveled Tariq with a vicious lariat. He signaled for the end of the match, giving the throat slash to the rest of the students. At this point, someone discretely slid a steel chair into the ring. Tariq looked at it, then looked at Khan, then looked at the chair again. Khan bent down to pick Tariq up and... *CRACK* That was the impact of Khan's chin on Tariq's skull. Tariq had just nailed him with a reverse full nelson chinbreaker out of nowhere. Right before he did that, Tariq pushed the chair out of the ring. He scored the pinfall without the aid of the chair. The students were leery of his heel antics during the match, but they cheered his victory. Rahman gave Tariq a sly look and Tariq shot a defiant glare back at his teacher. Later on, at around 5:15, Rahman approached Tariq in the gym. Everyone else had gone upstairs to pray or to eat their dinner or to just relax, but for some reason, Tariq knew to stay downstairs for a little while longer. 'You know, I was impressed with you today.' Tariq nodded, trying not to look at Rahman, but failing to keep his eyes totally away from him. 'You did what you had to do to win. I was equally impressed with how you won the match.' Tariq nodded again. 'Although it wasn't the way I planned...' 'Of course not,' Tariq answered. 'I was taught better than that.' Rahman smiled and laughed. 'You know, there will come a day when you'll need to use that chair, right? And then shortly thereafter, there'll be a day when you want to use that chair. And when that day comes, I won't tell you I told you so, but trust me, I will be there.' 'That's where you're mistaken. I can bend the rules, but I won't destroy honor.' 'Honor... there's a word that's misused. But like I said to you the other night. You're still very young.' Rahman walked away, smirking. He found out everything he needed to know through both the incident with Habib and the match with Khan. He knew there was a way to unlock all of Tariq's rage. He knew where the key was too. The tricky part would be trying to get Tariq to let him near the key.
