The Lavelle Chronicles, ch5

4 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4th June The Lavelle Chronicles, ch5 Patience is best I paced the corridors of ‘Albany State Hospital’ awaiting for my mothers helicopter to arrive. Every so often I would stop at a mirror and check the bruises along my face, the cuts and gashes above my eye and lip, the purple swelling along my left cheek. I looked as though I had been the victim of a vicious assault to those that didn’t know my background. A kid in the emergency waiting room, where I had sat to get some stitches, approached me and asked “Did it hurt?”; believing that I had been in a gang land fight, which was becoming common amongst the downtown areas of Albany. The ‘Blue Religion’, a group of misfits that followed those they didn’t like and would soon hurt or kill them. I had read about these gang-land killings everyday in the early morning paper. “Settle down, Pierce,” Rob would announce every so often as he peered his head over the brim of a wrestling weekly magazine. “Easy for you to say,” I would respond, my temperature rising and my palms sweaty. “I’ve just asked the doctor when he thinks your mother will arrive,” Lois said, sipping her coffee. “What did he say?” I now became more anxious than I had been when I first met Lilly. “Not much, he wouldn’t know until he got a phone call from the helicopter emergency patrol. That is all he would say, his beeper went off and he left in a hurry. I’m sorry I couldn’t have been more of a help to you, Pierce.” Lois said, now seated by her boyfriend, Rob, who was still mind deep into the pictures of woman wrestlers. Lois seemingly enough didn’t mind that her boyfriend looked at other woman or had playboy magazines, she could understand the reasons behind it, as she had four brothers herself. Although sometimes it would aggravate her, she would just turn her attention to a handsome looking mechanic, doctor or waiter. Though some would think they weren’t attracted to each other, they assumptions would always be very wrong. The things to which Rob and Lois would get up to, would be too graphic for a descriptive novelist to write. Which is why I now sleep with a cushion over my head, rather than under. “I wonder where Lilly is?” I stated, scanning through my mobile and reading all of my sent text messages to her phone. “She still hasn’t responded?” Lois asked, a little intrigued by the feelings that I carried for Lilly. Perhaps Lois wondered why I didn’t feel that way about her, but, I didn’t want to flatter her. “Not a word!” I said, annoyed by the sudden coldness from a woman, I cared so deeply for. Two days ago, after my brawl with George-Scott Lewis, Lilly had disappeared to her mothers house. Since then all I got was a letter saying that she has pressed charges against her boyfriend and how sorry she is that she got me into the large mess, that is her life. I had been frantically searching the phone book for any Connelly names; and each one I got and called, was just another dead end. “Pierce, look!” Lois said, starring at a skinny shadowy figure in the hall. Rebecca moved forward and sat down on a chair, she had been crying a lot by the looks of her mascara lining her cheeks. I gently approached her and sat down next to her. “Are you okay?” I asked, knowing I needed to comfort her now. “I’m fine, just very tired. They took mom to the cancer ward, fourth floor. The nurse sent me down to you, said you were waiting for me.” Rebecca said, a smile appearing on her face. “I haven’t been able to contact dad,” She stated. “I handled that, he won’t be coming near you or me, he can see mom, but, only when we are not here,” I said. “Why are you so horrible to dad, Pierce?” Rebecca asked. Rebecca Lavelle never understood the goings on between me, her old brother and our father. Rebecca was six years younger than I and never had the same treatment from our father. She was allowed to dream and to go places, ‘daddy’s little girl’. I used to be jealous about the fact she never got hit by our father and never had to watch our mother get hit either. I always tried to keep Rebecca out of that part of my life, I didn’t want her to know the real reason behind my hatred for our father. Otherwise, Rebecca might hate him to, and, I didn’t want to come between them. “It’s a long story, Becca,” I said, scanning through my inbox once more, hoping for a message from Lilly. “She’s not going to contact you, Pierce,” Lois said, still drinking some coffee. “She?” Rebecca asked. “You’re a girl?” I stated to Rebecca. “Last I checked, yes,” Rebecca said. “Cool, well I need advice!” I said once more. “What’s wrong with him Lois?” Rebecca asked. When Rebecca met Lois, she was four years of age, of course at that time Lois and I were just good friends, I had met her at a small bike shop just across the road from my house. We started dating at fifteen and ever since Lois and I broke up, Lois has still remained close to Rebecca and Maria. Acting like an older sister to Rebecca and a close friend to Maria, and I, I liked her for that. I knew deep down that Rob was a lucky man, but, I wasn’t jealous anymore as I found someone, but, she won’t speak to me. “He suffered a concussion,” Lois stated now standing and talking to one of the medical doctors. “You need to go see her Pierce, I’m no expert on relationships, but, by the sounds of things she likes you a lot.” Rebecca said. I had explained the story of Lilly and myself in as much detail as I could muster. “You think so?” I asked, intrigued by my sisters sudden wisdom. “Rebecca and Pierce, do you want to see your mom now?” Lois asked. “Yeah,” Rebecca said. “I need to talk to her,” I replied. Myself and Rebecca rose from the plastic chairs and went up the next flight of stairs, leaving Lois and Rob in the waiting room. I stood next to my younger sister, for support as my balance was a little off. We approached our mothers room. Maria Lavelle was seated on her bed dressed in hospital pyjamas, she looked weak, but, when she saw her two kids enter, her eyes lit up like a burning match touching the tip of a candle. “Pierce, Rebecca,” She said, coughing in between. Rebecca reached for some water and helped her mother take a sip. “Hey mom,” I said; now seated at the end of her bed. “Your face,” Maria now staring in shock at her sons battered and venerable appearance. “I was in a bit of an accident, but, I am fine,” I stated gently. “He got beaten up and then he went to a wrestling match, where he got beaten up even more. Doesn’t he look gorgeous!” Rebecca stated, bringing some humour into the room. “It doesn’t matter mom, I’m fine,” I replied once more, in light of my sisters joke. “You don’t look it,” Maria said. Mothers seem to hold this special inner knowledge of their children, they know exactly what is wrong with you when you don’t want them to know. They know whether you’re happy or sad. They can tell how you feel about certain people. A psychic bond, but the fathers don’t have this, that’s why it seems a lot easier to confront your mom about your feelings than your father, but, that could be different for some, but, not for me. “I think I messed up mom, see there is this girl. She’s really intelligent, goes to Harbrook and is studying forensics. She works in a local newspaper department and she had an abusive boyfriend. So I came to befriend her and well stopped her boyfriend from hurting her anymore, leading to a brawl in which my face got a little banged up. Then dad shows up out of nowhere and well called the cops. Oh, and granddad is married to bimbo Barbie and well now Lilly isn’t talking to me,” I took in a deep breath. “Your best chance with this girl is to go to her, tell her how you feel.” Maria said. “I’ll be fine here with Rebecca, Lois and Rob,” Maria said looking at Lois and Rob in the hallway. “Thanks mom,” I said, while jumping off the bed. I kissed my mother gently on the forehead and ran out of the room, down the two flights of stairs and to the nearest phone. I quickly dialled operator. “Operator, how may I help you.” “I am looking for a Lilly Connelly, mobile number 234-578-967.” I took in a breath. “One moment sir, is this an urgent matter?” “Yes, she’s gone missing,” I lied. The phone went dead for a few moments until she came back online again. “I have traced that number to Boston, Massachusetts. Her mobile is active at the moment. Good luck in your search detective,” She hung up. I couldn’t stop laughing, me a detective! But, why was Lilly in Boston? I thought her parents lived in Madison avenue. It explains why I haven’t got a text message back from her. Boston, eh, well I have always wanted to go and see the Boston Red Sox’s play live. Anatomy a of lie On the way home to my apartment I decided to ring the Boston operator and find out the whereabouts of a Connelly household or households, still keeping up the detective charade! Which, in fact had been working for me so far. “Yes, hi, my name is Detective Lee Valance. I am looking for a missing girl, name Lilly Connelly. I was wondering if you could trace the name ‘Connelly’ to any of the households in the Boston area.” I waited patiently for the operator to reply. I was being sly though, see my uncle is a detective Valance. Which is why I used his name, in the American law system, give or take a few details. The operators only take a detectives last name and scan it through their computer. My knowledge of this came from Pete Hillburg, from my old college. “Yes, hello, you still there?” The operator replied with a heavy Boston accent. “Yes!” I replied, now changing the tone of my voice. “We have three Connelly’s in the following addresses!” Things couldn’t have gone smoother, the Boston operator gave me three addresses and then narrowed it down to one. A Mrs. Jane Connelly. Lilly’s mother, I knew this was my chance to redeem myself and hopefully get Lilly to see sense and come back to New York. At home I quickly undressed out of my shabby jeans and bloodied white t-shirt. With only a certain amount of time until I had to book a plane, I threw on a dark navy shirt and a pair of black suit trousers. I grabbed my wallet and a small Nutrograin bar and stumbled into the hall, while trying to eat the cereal bar and clip on my Rolex watch - I was finding the saying ‘that woman are able to multitask better than men’ to be very true as I continued to stumble while placing my ‘two left feet’ into a pair of black polished boots. My lies about being the detective worked well, but, it was something I would have to forget. I am just glad I used a pay phone to contact her and not my mobile. Thinking about my mother’s advice I dialled the airport and asked for the next flight out of New York to Boston. Needless to say it took her a while just to click two keys, but, in the end there was a result. My flight to which I had booked would be leaving in three hours. I had one hour before I wouldn’t be welcome on the flight. “I would like to book a taxi please?” I said into the phone. “From and where to?” The woman on the other end replied, not really caring as she sits at the same tedious desk, filling her long purple nails with the phone pushed against her ear by her shoulder. “From Apartment 12 on western avenue to Albany International,” I replied. “A taxi will be with you in five minutes sir,” She hung up. That was quick I thought and without another word I slammed the receiver down, grabbed my keys and ran out of the apartment, making sure the door was locked behind me. The taxi cab driver beeped the horn repeatedly until I came bursting out of the double front doors, like a rhino charging across the Indian flat lands. “Sorry I was in the middle of a phone call,” I said, while opening the passenger door. “How long until your flight?” The taxi driver said, trying to make some conversation to ease the long boring ride. But, I couldn’t blame the guy it was in their profession to talk to their customers. “It takes off in three hours time, well around three hours,” I replied. “Where you going?” He insisted on continuing this conversation. “Boston,” I replied, keeping my words short an simple. “Going to see the Boston Red Sox? I’m a huge fan myself, but my wife Betty will never let me go to see a game, she insists on a Friday night family time. I work the Saturday and Sunday shifts. Kind of a strain on my favourite sport.” He said, now turning up the radio to listen in on the latest basketball game. Twenty minutes later and a long discussion on his wife Betty I had finally arrived at the Albany airport. I spotted a flower stand and thought it might be nice to greet Lilly with her favourite flowers, roses. “How much for six of your roses?” I asked, pulling out my wallet. These flowers never seemed to come at a cheap price, even when they were half withered and you only wanted one. “$25!” The stall owner said. “I handed him the money, and he wrapped them up for the flight,” My flight would be leaving soon, only a few moments left to look at the lights of Albany at night. The Long Trek to Boston I sat patiently in my uncomfortable plane seat, next to what looked like a 500 pound woman and a man, which smelt of whiskey and rum. I couldn’t stand it. My arm room was gone as her blubber billowed over the edges as she squeezed into the seat. When the seatbelt sign was removed, I struggled desperately to get up, but, I managed it. Grabbing the roses I walked calmly over to one of the air hostesses and asked for a new seat in the first class section. Surprisingly enough she allowed me pass, without a bribe. Perhaps she fell for my charm and good looks! “Ladies and gentlemen this is your pilot speaking!” the intercom began to speak to the squashed people in the small plane seats. “We will be landing in around fifty minutes. I hope you enjoy the meals and please feel free to enjoy the view,” I couldn’t stop laughing, view. If you looked out of the window all you would see is a black sky and a few lights beneath the aircraft, an unsettling feeling for those afraid of flying. I was soon to be with Lilly, it would only be a matter of time. I knew these next few weeks would be hard, after all, myself and Lilly would have to appear as witnesses to George’s court case for abuse and violent behaviour. “Excuse me, what would you like to drink?” The polite air hostess said. “I’ll have a diet cola, with ice.” I said, scanning through the brochure on Boston. “There you are sir, enjoy your meal!” She replied and moved up one row to serve the next few people. I couldn’t believe what I was seeing before me. For so long I had been used to the rubber meals of second class, but what was seated before me was a roast chicken and potatoes, along with a cool glass of cola. I was surprised by the sudden difference in luxury for a few extra dollars. I couldn’t help but smile at the thought of seeing Lilly. ‘When clouds pour over mountain tops and rain falls’, is something I could remember her say when we first went out by ourselves. Of course, I couldn’t remember the rest of the line but it was something poetic and just. The rain pattered against the thick plastic windows of the plane, as it slowly descended into the Boston airport. I braced myself for impact, as I hated the final touchdown, when the plane would bounce off the tarmac and come to a screeching halt. I could always picture a certain scene in my head, where the wheels would break and the plane would explode. Theory of physics denotes that fact - but, being scared is better than theory. “Ladies and gentleman, I would ask you please to remain seated until we reach our terminal. Thank you,” The pilot spoke once more, politely and less cheerfully as though his long day of flying, had come to an end and he could finally get those few hours of rest he needed until his next flight. A pilot was a job I would have loved to do, all the travelling and flying to different countries just holds something magical to your career. But, that went out the window along with my astronaut idea, a long time ago. I could hear the pressure of the air-lock realising on the plane door, as two Boston air workers opened the double doors leading to a long tunnel and then out of the tunnel and into our terminal. I rose from my seat and took the flowers, making sure I was the first out. Not having any luggage with me I parted from the group and ran out into the rain and toward the taxi rank. Just hoping that I remembered the address. A distant heart The house was colonial built, with a slanted front roof and a gentle pattern for the style decking along the porch. The rain continuously patted against the taxi windows as I paid him the fair and stood out into the rain. I couldn’t help but stare at the house in Newbury Port, it offered a completely different style to my old house in Pittsburgh and my apartment in Albany. The taxi drove off behind me, splashing me with water as he did so, I was now soaking wet along with one broken rose. “Thanks man,” I yelled back at the taxi. I was nervous, but, I had to approach her house or I would never make it. The rain continued to patter against the porch surroundings, as I stood under the dry surface. Reaching up to the door-knocker. I knocked twice. Hoping for an answer…… Knock Knock “Are you looking for Jane?” A neighbour said, as she passed by dressed in rain gear. “Yeah, do you know where she or Lilly is?” I asked, suddenly getting fed up with the Boston weather and my freezing body. “Jane is at church,” The neighbour said and continued to walk on. I sat down on the porch and waited. Hoping the heat from the boiler underneath would dry my clothing or, at least keep me warm. The waiting had come to a stand still. A car approached, a silver sports Honda Coupe. It had black tinted windows, so I couldn’t see who was in the car. I continued to stay seated, in the shadows, so not to be seen. A tall young stick of man stood out of the car, dressed in a long leather jacket. The passenger door opened and Lilly was standing before me. What happened next was the icing on the cake. The two embraced in a long kiss, before he hoped back into his car and drove off. Still in the shadows and out of Lilly’s view; I stood up and left the flowers along with my card on the ‘welcome’ mat and walked off into the night. I could feel the rain running down the back of my neck and I didn’t care now - Lilly had gone from one boyfriend to another, completely passing me by, without a single thought. I began watching from across the street as she picked up the roses and turned around to look for the person who dropped them. She spotted me in the distance and put her hand to her mouth, she had been caught and I didn’t care. I continued to walk away. I needed some comfort and something to take the pain away. The ‘Black Goose’ bar seemed like the perfect idea! Lilly in shock, picked up the note and began to read. Dear Lilly, I have missed you a lot and hope you return to Albany, Love, Pierce View Pierce Lavelle 's Biography
