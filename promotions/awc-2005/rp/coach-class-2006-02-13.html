Coach Class

13 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13th February 2006 “Yeah, uh, he’s right here,” Anvil says as he looks over at Tempered Steel’s high-flyer Hammer who shrugs his shoulders inquisitively before Anvil ignores him and continues into his rather bulky looking mobile telephone. The camera pans back to reveal an airport departure gate. Dressed quite casually in blue jeans and sweatshirts amidst a sea of suited and tied business travelers, the self-conscious duo of Hammer and Anvil sit together in front of gate F32 at Philadelphia International Airport awaiting their flight to Chicago’s O’Hare. “You know,” says an unseen voice coming through the other end of the cellular phone, “you better keep your eyes on this kid Hammer. He’s supposed to be the one with the higher talent ceiling, if you catch my drift.” These were always harsh words for Anvil to hear but he basically knew that what was being said to him was the honest truth. He was the powerhouse and better wrestler of the team and it wasn’t going to change. Hammer had much more natural athletic ability, though, and had always been touted as a better overall talent especially after developing some prowess from the corner turnbuckles. For much of his career Anvil had longed for a manager that would be one hundred percent honest with him and help guide him to the best and most logical path for his specific talents. But every so often, like this instant right now, Anvil’s ego just wouldn’t be ready for it. He took a long gulp and a deep, calming breath. “So, anything else, boss?” Anvil asks in a gruff, ‘let’s get this over with’ sort of tone. “Listen, one other thing. I’ve had you booked for first class and Hammer for coach class for your flight to Chicago.” Anvil moves the phone away from his ear and looks at it as if he didn’t quite believe what he had heard coming through from the other end. He brings it back to his ear before he continues: “Uh, what was that again? I got some interference here in the terminal on this old phone of mine.” “You’re in first class,” says the raspy voice on the other end of the phone, a voice that drips slowly with a distinctly western drawl, “Hammer, the man who failed two weeks ago on Fresh! in his singles debut in a Four Way Fury match, sits in coach class. Maybe that will mean the guy stays hungry this week and actually helps you out?” The honesty this time made Anvil smile widely. I’m happy this shit works both ways. Anvil thinks, He might put me down but he gives it to Hammer just the same. Anvil scratches the top of his head and Hammer backhands him lightly on the arm as if wanting to be clued in to what was going on. I don’t think I’ve ever work with such an unintelligent partner. “Hello?” the voice with the western drawl asks in an impatient manner, “you still there?” “Yeah,” Anvil responds, snapping back to reality, “yeah, sorry, no problem. Got it about the seating. Great idea, by the way!” “What the hell are you talking about?” Hammer interjects, unable to contain himself any longer. Anvil completely ignores his partner. “NOW BOARDING...” crackles over the airport PA system in a nasally effeminate voice, “WE’RE ABOUT TO BOARD FLIGHT 2842 TO CHICAGO. WE’D LIKE TO INVITE ANYONE FLYING TODAY WITH SMALL CHILDREN, ANYONE THAT MAY NEED EXTRA TIME TO BOARD, AND OUR FIRST CLASS PASSENGERS TO BOARD AT THIS TIME.” “Hey man,” Anvil says, “they’re starting to board. I’ll call you after the match and let you know what happened.” “Yeah,” says the twangy voice with a bit of a sigh, “sorry I can’t be there tonight. I still have to heal up a bit. But I’ll be out there with you soon.” “Cool,” says Anvil, “sounds perfect. Talk to you later.” “Later.” Anvil clicks off the phone and pushes it into his tightly packed carry-on bag. “So what the hell is going on, man?” Hammer questions Anvil. “Why don’t you respond to me when I’m talking to you?” “He booked you for coach class,” Anvil says as he looks Hammer straight in the eyes. Anvil then bends over and picks up his bag and sorts out his boarding pass, “and me for first class.” “What the fuck?” Hammer says, pulling out his own boarding pass to confirm Anvil’s story. “What’s that all about?” “He said you don’t deserve it after losing that Four Way Fury match two weeks ago,” Anvil responds, “sorry bud.” “What?! That’s totally untrue. I was dominating that match they just held me back. Other teams were conspiring against me!” “That’s another thing he said he hates,” Anvil seethes as he starts walking away from his partner, ascribing his own feelings to Tempered Steel’s manager, “he said you always have excuses. A true professional doesn’t make excuses.” I hope this reverse psychology crap works. Or else I’m just going to have a worthless partner at the UIC Pavillion. Not unlike some other nights, though I suppose... “Excuses?!” Hammer stammers. He slumps back down into his seat awaiting his boarding zone to be called. “I’ll show those friggin’ bitches,” Hammer mutters to himself just loud enough for Anvil to hear. The camera fades to black with a shot of Tempered Steel’s big man Anvil raising his arm up to dismiss Hammer as he boards the aircraft with the other first class passengers.
