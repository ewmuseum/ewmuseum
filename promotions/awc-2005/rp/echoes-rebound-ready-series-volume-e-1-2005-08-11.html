Red and blue sparkled around the neighborhood like fireworks casting their dazzling hues down on the earth during the evening’s twilight. People were gathered around standing on the side, watching and waiting with nervousness and uneasiness. This was no time for celebration… this was a crime scene. One blue suited officer made sure everyone stayed away from the scene, white gloved hands raised in motion backwards out of the way. Obediently everyone retreated from the officer’s path, though their eyes were still glued to the doors of the abandoned factory waiting for something interesting, a juicy tidbit to call their friends about or talk about at the water cooler the next day. As soon as a path was cleared he rushed back and poked his head through the door, saying something to his comrades. Finally he pushed both doors open, with the help of two other officers, the large gates creaking with rust and wear from years of being sealed shut. “What’s going on? What’s with the cops?” “I dunno, I just came out because I heard sirens.” “Why’re they in the factory? Are they looking for something?” “Geez… what a bunch of noise. Can’t they do this while I’m at work?” Everyone watched as two officers carried out a stretcher, the silhouette of a body outlined underneath a white tarp. Breath was taken from watchers, shocked at the thought of something as grisly as murder occurring in their peaceful little neighborhood. “My word… who’s that?!” “Looks like a girl to me.” “How do you know?” “Man, check out that rack! Gotta be a chick!” “Well… at least they’ll get rid of this eyesore now that there’s bad spirits here.” Amidst the murmurs and whispers as the police walked past with the body, a young student with scruffy auburn hair stood dwarfed amongst the bystanders. He was hunched up between two guys, trying to see the body himself. As the police passed him, his face went a ghostly pale, eyes like two white poker chips. He knew something… he knew someone… As the crowd began to dwindle as the body was placed in the back of the ambulance, young Chibi, as he was called by his peers, noticed something that made those poker chip eyes turned into dinner plates. Standing across from him in the makeshift path through the pedestrians, Chibi spotted a tall shorthaired girl in a black leather jacket and tight blue jeans with black gloves. Staring up at the tomboy, only one name came through his mind, echoing in the back of his head like a bad omen. Kirima Nagi… As if she could read his mind, her eyes shot down on him just as he thought of her name. Chibi, known for his fleeing in the blink of an eye, bolted out of the crowd and through the factory’s gates. Nagi watched with stern eyes as the runt ran off down the street and disappeared into the neighborhood. Her face glowed red and blue, gaze shifting to the ambulance as it screamed away from the scene. -=-=-=-=-=-=- I was called into the office at precisely 12:38pm. It was lunchtime, and the rest of the students were out enjoying their prepared meals, talking amongst themselves and fraternizing with the latest gossip and mischief. I never was one for things like that; I was usually too busy with my own duties. Duties during that time were being interrupted, by this meeting with the principal. Doctor Iwamura was an older man, in his near 50s with gray hairs salting his black hair. Normally his expression is emotionless, a poker face handed down from the ancient times of the samurai. Out of all the people I knew, I would say that his expression rivaled my own in expressionless nature. You couldn’t read anything from neither him, nor me yet I was nowhere near his level. He knew beyond my facial expressions my mood; it may have been because I’ve studied under him for so long that he’s learned my mannerisms. All in all, he was a fair principal that nurtured as well as instructed. So with me sitting across from his desk, I could see that there was something definitely different about the reason I was called there. Normally he goes about his work as if I wasn’t there, and speaks to me in the process, but this time he actually stopped everything he was doing to give me his undivided attention. It definitely had mines, I’d say. “Kyosuke,” he began, clearing his throat after saying my name. “Are you familiar with Jue Hibiki?” I had to scan my faces and names to come up with a match. “Ahh, yes. Younger girl, junior, class B.” “Right,” his voice gave praise a bit, but the matter was still heavy on his heart. “Well, yesterday afternoon we received the call from the police that she turned up dead.” Well now, missing is one thing, but dead is something else entirely. It wasn’t common for students to end up missing from their homes; a lot of kids couldn’t take the pressure of grades and such and ran off, sometimes with their boyfriends or girlfriends. They went somewhere else and started over and usually made a family, but those were with the couples. Most of the time if a person alone ran off, they ended up joining the yakuza as either a soldier or a hooker. Sad thing to think, but that was life here in Japan. Doctor Iwamura saw that I had no response, and continued on with the subject. “Hibiki was found in the abandoned factory on the edge of Shibuya impaled on a steel post. It seems like she fell off, or quite possibly she threw herself off. The police haven’t ruled out homicide, however… seeing how her parents don’t believe that their daughter would just kill herself suddenly. They say she was very happy, and enjoyed hanging out with her friends.” I nodded, “Yes, I’ve seen her with her classmates during class events. She seems to relate to them very well.” He nodded back, reaching into his drawer and pulling out a steno pad. “Getting to the reason you’re here… you’re the head of the morals committee here, and that makes it your job to go around school and question the student body on whatever they may know. I want you to figure out if this was a suicide, or if this was something else.” The doctor held out the pad to me, and once it was in my hands I could see it held the names of every student in her class, along with a group outside her class in the same grade in black ink. I looked up at him after scanning the list, pushing the glasses back up the bridge of my nose. “What’s the deadline, sir?” He shrugged, a gesture that a man of his knowledge and intelligence rarely did. “Within reasonable time, I say. She’s dead, and the parents want answers, but they would rather have them sooner or later, preferably sooner.” “Understood.” I stood up and bowed, walking towards the door of his office. “I won’t disappoint you, sir.” “You never do, son.” He turned back to his computer and started typing again, mind focused back on work now that that little sidebar was out of the way. I closed the door to his office and stopped after taking a few steps, looking down at the notebook. There were at least 20 names on this list, most of them I was familiar with. This probably wouldn’t take me no more than a day, day and a half. No doubt it was suicide, and her friends would be the key to giving the reason why she did it. Forbidden love, academic pressure, low self esteem… it’d be one of those reasons.
