One for Luck, Ch 3

15 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15th October One for Luck - Prometheus Serum (Section 1) Testimony | after the show Quietness fills the small, enclosed corridor. People stand in silence and gaze at the entrance, as footsteps are heard, pounding along the marbled floor. The crowd wait in anticipation, expecting something of worth and forthcoming, instead the silence is broken by the loud echo of Pearl’s sharp tongue. 'What do you mean you haven't seen him?' Pearl asks the adoring crowd. 'He's missing,' a backstage worker replies. David 'Pearl' Harber, Sarah Kennedy and Maddy Estelle all stand in a circle, searching each nook and cranny of the backstage area. The crowd now begin to join in on the man hunt. The only evidence to go on was a small blood trail that had been left coming up the ramp and it was three guesses as to who it belonged to; Pearl had one main man in his mind and was in a desperate urge to find him. The last time the man was seen, was charging up the ramp with Adam Dick in hot pursuit. 'Where's Adam Dick?' Sarah Kennedy asks a regular staff worker. He turns to her, shakes his head and goes back to packing up the wiring and electronics. Maddy Estelle came charging down the hall, her mobile in hand. She stopped dead centre in front of Sarah and Pearl, took two large breaths and straightened her back. 'Adam Dick is gone, he took a flight ...' 'Where?' Pearl demanded. 'I don't know ...' Maddy Estelle stated. She closed her mobile and relaxed a little. 'BOSS!!' A scream came from down the hall. 'We found him,' the mysterious worker said. 'Thank god,' Pearl yelled and soon ran after the young backstage worker. A couple of corridors later we are greeted by the slumped body of Pierce Lavelle. He is splayed out with his back against the wall, his head to his left, resting on his shoulder. His legs in a 'v' shape. A pool of blood surrounds his body and Maddy Estelle finds it hard to stare at the mess and runs off, gagging. Sarah Kennedy steps aside to let the paramedics deal with Pierce. David 'Pearl' Harber kneels down and says something to Pierce, Pierce's body moves a little. He lifts his bloodied hand and grabs Pearl's gold tie, but after a couple of seconds, that momentary movement fails and Pierce's hand hits the floor like a dead-weight. Pearl stands up and watches the paramedics treat him; his tie has a bloodied hand print on it. Pearl turns to the chief medical examiner. 'How come he wasn't found earlier?' Pearl demanded. He crossed his arms and stared at the chief medical examiner. 'The only evidence we had was the replay of Testimony. He wasn't seen after running up the stage-' The medical examiner stops dead in his speech as he watches Pierce being rolled off on a stretcher. Most of the backstage have now turned their attention to the scene before them. Sarah Kennedy turns her back, her face a pale white. Maddy Estelle is seen with a tissue over her mouth and Pearl remains side by side with Pierce the whole way to the car park, where the red and white ambulance is parked. 'I want the best care you have, immediate attention,' Pearl grabs hold of the paramedics arm. 'Make sure he takes this with him,' Pearl hands the paramedic Pierce's duffle bag and also the brown folder. The doors shuts behind Pierce and the ambulance soon zooms up the ramp and out toward the hospital. Pearl sticks his hands into his pockets and looks on, shaking his head. The silence is broken by Pearl's mobile ringing. 'Hello?' Pearl's facial expression immediately changes and he storms off passed the camera's and back into the building. |Treatment| I had swallowed seven pain killers with a full bottle of water and still the pain was defiant and remained - evidently to torture me for my stupid decision of bravery and insolence. I lay gazing at the linoleum ceiling, counting each puncture hole. The doctor continuously examined my collarbone. The two nurses remained standing, checking my vital signs and blood pressure. I felt like a test subject, being mutilated for the beneficiary of science, but in this case, the doctor was working to help me. My night at Testimony was most likely, the luckiest of my life. I knew Hate had damaged me and I was now paying the price, once again. 'Please, try not to move,' the doctor said. A quieter tone than last time. Well, where could I go? I asked myself. I had enough sedatives in me to sedate a horse, and I still felt like I had been hit with a truck. The small electric bed began to slide under the oval roof of the cat scan. I lay perplexed as the small red light scanned my body, up and down. After a few moments the whole ordeal was over and the nurse's lowered me back onto the usual bed. The head rest lifted automatically and the doctor examined each scan. By the looks of things, my nose was broken in two places and my lungs were badly bruised - a large black cloud shrouded my left lung. My collarbone hadn't been broken, but bruised and it hurt to move my arm. 'You are lucky to walk out of this with just bruising,' said the doctor. His back remained to me. The last thing I remembered was racing up the ramp to try and save Paddy O'Shea. Next thing I know, I'm on a stretcher with David Harber alongside me, telling the doctor to treat me immediately; and here I am here with immediate treatment. Just goes to show you, money buys everything. 'We'll put your arm in a sling, it should help with the bruising around the collarbone,' he said. The first nurse lifted my arm and I winced in pain; she slowly slipped on a blue and white sling. I felt the pressure release from my collarbone and bicep. I could relax, a little. 'What about my lungs?' I asked. 'This area here...' he said and pointed to the large black covering of my left lung. “...is seriously bruised, it’s the reason you coughed up so much blood, but luckily you didn't break a rib and haven't punctured your lung. We'll give you some pain killers to help with the breathing, but the pure oxygen that you are taking now, will help ease the bruising.' He was referring to the strings of tubing coming out of my nose. The second nurse lifted me forward and wrapped a long, white bandage around my ribs and then fastened it with a piece of tape. She handed me a shirt and then left. The other nurse soon followed. 'Can I leave now?' I asked. The doctor pulled down the scans, checked his watch and turned off the pure oxygen and helped me remove the nose tubes. 'Yes, I think you'll be fit to leave. Just one moment and I'll sign the form for your pain killers.' He handed me a medical slip and left. I stood up and looked at myself in the mirror. My eyes had a purple glow around them, from the broken nose. My nose had been popped back into place, two buds were now in the nostrils and two pieces of white tape lay over the brim of my nose to reinforce it. My arm lay immobilised and my chest looked like a grape, purple and swollen. I slipped my free arm into the shirt and gently removed my other from the sling, slipped it into the second sleeve and applied the sling once again. I made my way to reception, signed the book and received my medicine. It was late in the day and I needed to return to Harrisburg. I pulled up my duffle bag and saw the brown folder that Harber had given me earlier. I glanced down at it, shoved it into the duffle bag and zipped it up. |Imminent Peril| It had been a three hour flight back to Harrisburg and my mind was now swirling with the amount of pain killers dancing through my blood stream. During my flight I had opened the brown folder and the first word that had reached me was the big red writing of 'TOP SECRET'; I could hear Harber's voice in the back of my head. Flicking through the science stuff on the drug, I was greeted with a map of Los Angeles, 'The City of Angels', kind of ironic for the amount of drug smuggling and gun deaths - nothing angelic about it now. Harber had mentioned the Educator being one of the men in the wrestling business to have taken the drug, and now that I look back at the match, it did seem he was on something, but Prometheus Serum had some serious side effects linked to it, some would be enough to kill a regular athlete. I worried now; could this be a dissolvable supplement? It was something I had tried to ask Harber the night of my match, but with my condition afterwards, I barely remembered anything. I knew my first stop would be a trip to see Adam Masters, but in the condition I was now in, it wasn't the best option. I slid all the documents back into the brown folder, placed my biro back into my jacket, pulled the zip closed on my duffle bag and headed to the Taxi depot. I hailed a yellow cab and it stopped next to me. I threw my bag in and sat in the passenger seat. 'Where t-?' the taxi driver managed to say, before he saw my appearance. 'You alright?' he asked. 'Yeah, I'm fine,' I pulled on the seat belt. 'Williamsburg's Estates,' I said and looked around at the storm clouds gathering in the distance. 'No problem,' he said and pulled out of the airport and onto the freeway. The nights sky had been blinded with the lights from the city - meaning that no matter where you were in the city, you could no longer see the stars, lucky for me, I lived in the outskirts of the main city. Williamsburg's Estates was on the tip of the hill and it took a while to get from there to the main city. After a forty minute drive and the Spanish inquisition from the taxi driver, we had finally arrived outside the intimidating gaping gates. I slid out of the taxi, paid the driver and saw Robert and Kyle playing some basketball. On my approach, both stopped, leaving the basketball bouncing in the distance. 'Shit!' Robert exclaimed. 'What happened to you?' Kyle ran in to get his mother. Robert sighed and approached me. 'Let me guess, all in a days work?' he asked. I nodded my head. Robert was familiar with my grotesque appearance after a bad night at work, he had seen me before, but this time I was a lot worse than I had been letting on. My response was covered by the screeching of Mrs. Green. She padded over the lush green lawn and stared at me, gob smacked. 'Oh my! Oh my!' she repeated in a state of shock. 'What - What - Happened?' she stuttered. 'He'll be fine mom,' Robert said, not too bothered by my appearance. 'Joshua call the cops!' Mrs. Green yelled to her husband. 'What?' I said. 'What?' Robert repeated. 'Mrs. Green, I'm fine. This happened to me at work. Please, there's no need to call the cops!' I stated. 'At work?' she asked. Mrs. Green, poor woman, was still in shock. I'm just glad my mother wasn't here to see my appearance. 'Wrestling,' I said, but Mrs. Green had already woken up from her trance and no longer needed an explanation. Her motherly instinct immediately kicked in and she hooked her arm around mine, I let out a yelp. 'Oh, sorry ...' she said and led me into the house. Robert followed behind, my duffle bag in his hand. Mr. Green was struggling with the answering machine at the police station. 'Put the phone down, Joshua,' Mrs. Green said calmly. 'What's going on?' Mr. Green said. He placed the phone back on its stand and then turned to me, his mouth hit the floor. Shock written all over his face. 'What the bloody hell happened to you?' he asked. 'It happened at work!' I said. 'Pierce is a professional wrestler, daddy,' Kyle commentated. 'Ah, I see! That explains the war wounds ...' Mr. Green added - he too was a fan of wrestling and categorically watched PRIME every week. 'There is no time for comedy,' Mrs. Green snapped us out of our conversation. 'Look at him,' she stated kindly. 'Marie would kill me.' Marie, my mother would have had a coronary if she saw me. I knew this and I thanked the sky that she wasn't here in Harrisburg. 'My mom doesn't need to know,' I said. 'What?' Mrs. Green said. 'You mean she doesn't know you're a wrestler!' 'No,' I dipped my head. 'Robert, go and get some fresh clothes for Pierce, this shirt is covered in blood,' Mrs. Green helped me take off my jacket. 'Joshua, run a bath for Pierce, will you?' 'Excuse me?' I asked. 'It’s an old remedy. It will help the stiff muscles,' she replied. Mr. Green stumbled up the stairs, followed by Kyle who asked if he could play with the rubber ducks. Mr. Green just laughed and kept climbing the stairs. Mrs. Green led me into the kitchen, sat me down on the chair and unhooked my sling. I removed my shirt and she gasped at the purple swelling on my chest and back. She turned around and opened the freezer. Next thing I now, I have a packet of frozen peas on my nose. My eyes shut immediately in response to the cold. 'Ooooohhhh' I chattered. 'Relax, this will help the swelling around the eyes,' she said. Mrs. Green was the old fashioned mother - caring and extremely motherly, but when I saw the bottle of iodine being pulled out, I knew enough was enough. The last time I had been subjected to iodine was after I had fallen out of Robert's tree house and grazed my elbow and my knees, and the only thing I remember is the immediate pain that followed. 'WOW!' I said, backing away from Mrs. Green. 'Oh, stop being such a baby,' she said and applied a tissue - soaked in iodine on my grazed collarbone and the other cuts on my chest and back. I grinded my teeth and remained in an up-right posture. 'There, that wasn't so bad. Was it?' 'N-O-' I muttered each vowel separately. My teeth were crushed together. Mr. Green soon approached, his sleeves rolled up. Kyle was dressed in his pyjamas and said goodnight to his mother and me and then ran back upstairs to bed. Mr. Green turned to me. 'The bath is ready,' he patted my back and the bruising almost gave me enough strength to punch the laughing Mr. Green. Mrs. Green helped me stand up and put away the frozen peas. Up stairs, Robert was seated on his bed playing the play station. My New York Jets grey t-shirt was folded on the chair, along with a pair of baggy black tracksuit bottoms. I grabbed them from him and locked the bathroom door behind me. I stared at the orange coloured dried in iodine on my wounds and looked at my nose. I removed the buds in my nostrils and blew out - blood splattered onto the mirror. 'Damn!' I whispered and wet a piece of tissue paper and cleaned the mirror. The bath was filled to an inch below the rim. I removed my clothes with great difficulty and slid into the bath. Mrs. Green's remedy seemed to be working as my back began to loosen, along with my bicep muscles and chest. After a good twenty minutes soak, I stood up and dried myself off. Checking myself in the mirror - the swelling around my eyes had gone down and the red lump on my nose had also shrunk. I pulled on some clean boxers and my tracksuit bottoms. I struggled with the t-shirt, but eventually managed it. Downstairs, Mrs. Green stood in the hall, phone in hand. 'It’s a call, for you,' she said and left the phone on the table. I fastened my sling once more and picked up the phone. 'Hello?' I asked into the phone. 'Is this Pierce Lavelle?' the voice asked. 'Yes!' I replied. 'Are you alone?' the voice asked. 'Yes, nobodies listening in.' 'You sure?' the voiced asked. 'Okay, cut the third degree, who is this?' It was time for me to ask the questions. 'I'm the manager of Adam Masters, otherwise know as the Educator.' 'Yeah ... So why are you calling me?' I asked. 'You see -' He went silent for a second. 'Hello? You still there?' 'Sorry, I'm using a public pay phone,' he replied. 'As I was saying, I heard you were helping Harber with a case.' 'Uh-Huh?' 'Wait, don't hang up. Just hear me out ... it’s about Adam, I don’t know that much and I want your help?' 'What? Why?' 'Adam's gone missing,' he whispered. 'Since when?' I asked. 'A couple of weeks ago - he hasn't been seen by anyone. It’s the drug. I know it is.' His sentences were short and his pauses were long. 'Why do you need my help?' 'He was last seen in Los Angeles,' his manager said. Once again, Los Angeles is the link for Prometheus, but this time I was being sent there for a different reason - Adam Masters was MIA. My mind jolted back to the conversation. 'Wait! How'd you get this number?' The phone went silent. Adam Master's manager had hung up on me. I ended the connection and looked around. I now had to go to Los Angeles, for Harber and for Masters. This Prometheus Serum was proving to be a tricky situation and I know wondered what I had put myself up for? My last mishap with a mafia syndicate didn't end well for me; I didn't want a repeat of the Godfather! 'Is everything okay?' Mrs. Green asked. 'Yeah, everything is fine. I need to get a flight to Los Angeles!' I stated and picked up the phone book. |Crossed and Double-Crossed| The streets were quiet, too quiet after Pierce Lavelle had left Kelli alone by the block of motel houses. She stood arms folded behind a large dumpster. The streets remained motionless. She pulled out a packet of Camel cigarettes and lit one, taking a couple of puffs of nicotine she eased a little and leant against the dumpster. “You shouldn’t smoke those,” a stranger’s voice said. A man moved forward, the collar of his jacket turned up against the chilly breeze. A baseball hat covered his face, his hands remained in his pockets and Kelli put out the cigarette. “This wasn’t part of the deal,” Kelli said. “Sweet lips, I pay you, you’ll do what I want,” the stranger said and moved forward out of the street light and into the dark. “You promised this arrangement would be over after you dealt with Zack,” she said. Her tone was plain and her posture slumped against the dumpster. “I dealt with Zach, I said I would, but I’m also paying your mortgage. So, this arrangement isn’t over,” the man stood up straight, but he was still covered by the dark night. Kelli dipped her head, sighed and faced the mysterious man. “What do you want me to do?” “Simple really, I need you to be my under-cover spy!” he said. “What?” Kelli didn’t quite understand what was being asked of her. “Our plan has been set in motion, he’s helped you and from where I was sitting, it seems he likes you …” the man said. “I won’t play him, he’s too nice …” she said. “It doesn’t matter about him. You are working in Fibbers because of me,” he raised his tone. “You had me work there, to spy on him,” she stated. “Indeed, now, you will work for me or I’ll post bail for Zach,” he said, sadistically. “Please, don’t,” The fear in Kelli was evident as her hands began to shake against the breeze. “Good,” the man said and handed Kelli a plane ticket. “What?” she took the ticket and stared at the region. “Los Angeles?” “You’ll be arriving in the airport at the same time Pierce Lavelle will be. I’ve checked with a source that made a call to make sure he would be going to Los Angeles. You’ll force yourself to partner with him, then when he finds out things, report them back to me. It’ll be worth it. You do this for me, I’ll make sure Zach Midas never gets out of prison …” the man stepped back a little. “Fine,” she muttered. “Well done, sweet lips. He’ll be putty in your hands,” the man removed his cap and smiled menacingly at Kelli, who shuddered. Kelli put the plane ticket into her purple handbag, stepped back and looked around at the empty street. “Why can’t you do this?” she said. “Because I work with him, I also have my own investigation. You will report back to me, Kelli. Understand?” He placed his hand on the side of her face. “Yes, Adam … I’ll do anything you want,” she muttered and walked off. “Excellent,” Adam muttered. He turned around in the opposite direction and began to walk down the lonesome street. A cars engine revs up and Adam waits patiently as a black limo pulls up alongside him. He slides in and closes the door. The limo drives off passed Kelli. She wait’s a couple of minutes and sighs before opening the door to her rented room. |Angels Flight| Harrisburg International airport was buzzing with life at ten in the morning. Mr. Green kindly decided to drive me to the airport, on account of my bad shoulder. I had packed a small carrier bag with some necessaries. I decided I would buy some new clothes in Los Angeles, and perhaps visit my mother in my aunt and uncles house. Los Angeles to me, had always been “the city of Angels” - no harm or danger, but now, after years of gun running, drink, drugs and violence, it had turned into a hell, rather than heaven. “Here we are lad,” Mr. Green said and opened the door for me. “Thanks for the lift, Mr. Green,” I said. “No problem and try to stay out of trouble, you hear?” Mr. Green winked at me and then climbed back into the four-by-four pick up and drove off, back into the city and toward his work. I hoisted my bag over my good shoulder, checked my passport and ticket and entered the airport. The main lobby was filled with those planning on checking-in their luggage and then making a dash for the restaurants and bars. I squeezed passed a fairly large couple and then scanned all the banners, looking for my flight number and check-in desk. I turned around to my left and saw the large banner reading “US AIRWAYS - LOS ANGELES”. I skittered around the marble floor, bag trolleys and little kids and joined the que for LA. The que was cutting down and before too long I was at the front desk, I threw my bag onto the electronic belt, gave the check-in lady my ticket and passport and stared at the flight times. My plane was to leave at 12:00, it was another two hours away, but it gave me time to listen to my MP3 and also have a look around duty free. “Enjoy your flight, Mr. Lavelle,” the lady at check-in said. She stamped my ticket and handed it back to me, along with my passport. I waved goodbye to my bag and joined the long line of people heading to their various gates and duty free. The thing I never liked about the airports was the risk. It was always there in the back of my head. Planes didn’t appeal to me for that reason, what if? Was the question for many people boarding. I passed through the security gates with no problem and collected my keys and MP3 player. Duty Free - the place to buy normal things for very expensive prices. I gazed at Foot Locker for a while; a New York Jets jersey had caught my attention. “They’re a good team!” a voice said. To my shock, Kelli Stoner was standing behind me. Dressed in a pair of jeans and a nice turquoise top. She looked in the window and smiled. She stared at me and my plastered nose and my bandaged arm and placed a hand on my shoulder. “What happened to you? Are you alright?” she asked. “Yeah, I’m fine,” I replied. “I’ve never seen a wrestler this beaten up before, it’s just a shock,” she looked at me and then adverted her eyes toward the Jets Jersey. “Yeah, they’re a good team. Who do you support?” I asked. “Bears,” she smiled. “How come you are here?” I asked. “Needed to get away for a while,” she said. “What? Last time we spoke, you said you were tired of travelling!” I commentated. Kelli had frozen stiff, she stared at a Giant’s jersey and then back toward me. “I know, but I don’t want to be around when Zach gets out,” she said. “I understand,” I smiled. “Your Jets lost this week,” she said and sat down on the small plastic chair. “Yeah, to the Ravens. Just some bad defence …” I said and sat next to her. “Where you flying to?” she asked. “Los Angeles!” I said and stared at the large screen replaying a Chicago Bears highlight. “Really?” she said her voice squeaky. “Yeah, why?” “I’m going there too. It was the only flight I could afford,” she picked up her carrier bag and placed it on her lap. “Where you staying?” I asked. “Nowhere, yet. I don’t know. I haven’t thought that far ahead,” she dipped her. “You alright?” I asked. “Yeah, I’m fine …” she said. I could tell she wasn’t, but I didn’t want to press the matter. “You could stay with me,” I said. “I have a hotel room, its two beds. I don’t mind, I could do with some company,” I smiled and stood up. “Really? Thanks,” she hugged me and sat down again. “You want a cof-” Before I could finish my sentence our flight was announced to be leaving for Los Angeles. I picked up my small carry bag and helped Kelli Stoner stand up. The two of us marched down the platform, toward our flight. Looking at Kelli, I felt happy to have somebody to keep me company on this trip, but I also worried for her safety in regards to my covert mission. We handed in our tickets, took our seats and as it turned out, Kelli was next to me. The plane door suction kicked in and the door shut, with little difficulty. Kelli tightened her belt buckle, took a deep breath and sighed. “Here we go,” she said and the plane took off.
