Roses & Thorns - Eulogy

7 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/3/06 4th March, 2006 Post-Script - The home of the O‘Grady’s It’s only now that I’ve had the time to sit down and really talk about this moment in the past couple of months and to be honest I’ve been dreading thinking about it. Looking literally at it, it’s one of the darker days in my life and to re-read what I wrote about the hospital visit pains me. Put bluntly…I chose to sleep with somebody, rather than stay by the side of my one true mentor, Dan ‘The Firebrand’ O’Grady. I feel sick about that and knowing that fact I really don’t feel it’s right to even conjure the thoughts of writing about my own personal problems or how they affect me. I can’t continue without doing one thing: Paying respect to my mentor, my trainer, my friend…my true father in life. Sitting at the house of O’Grady right after the funeral, I was sifting through my things when fate flicked me a bone and told me to go play fetch with it. I found some letters and photos of the past which seems like forever but included the guts of Dan’s career. It’s strange that I found a collective of a diary, right in the process of writing my own but, that’s just the way fate is. You don’t tell it what you’d like, you can only hope. I know Dan told me to live in the present that night at his deathbed and I respect his wishes and I’ve tried to do it. It has even helped when the times were a bit tough since then and no greater testament to that fact is the day when I won that Transatlantic Championship. It embodied the spirit of how Dan lived his life; hard, fast and blunt. You didn’t have time to stop with him, his mind was always racing. He truly lived in the present…even if he didn’t think so. So right now I’m sitting in my bedroom, the floor littered with the remnants of his life, all in written form…and I feel lost among thoughts. Because I have lost the will. Because I have dishonoured. That belt sits on the table, watching…waiting. I need to provide it with the justification and purification that it requires to make it feel worthy enough again. I hope I can provide it with that. *** May 14th , 1967 and sitting by a dockyard in America…somewhere The New York Post is right on the bloody corner and splashed across the front page reads the words “Mantle Makes it a Beauty.” You can see the crowds in the background as he‘s dancing around the stadium, taking his applause like a true gent. To be perfectly honest, I haven‘t got a feckin’ clue who he is, but I know he‘s important and that’s something that sticks out. He’s warranted the front page, he’s got the status to be there and looking at his smug face…I want that. I came here to be somebody…not work on dockyards and pass the time as some people felt. I came to be somebody. I take the corner of the paper and look at it…it has an advertisement for a show. A wrestling show. Sometime later I’ve quit my job and am on my way to being somebody. I just know it. Almost a year and a day before I was born and there he was, a fresh faced young boy with so much to live and learn. The photo attached to it is flowing and tightly curled about his head, with a kink right at the side which would come to be his trademark ember. The majority of this letter has been discarded by the looks of it because it abruptly ends. There is no other half, no response and follow up to this possibility, but really I knew the answer. There is an added bit scrawled in the corner, just four lines long and it catches my eye. The entire piece is underlined and looks newer than before. Yesterday, when I was young, The taste of life was sweet as rain upon my tongue, I teased at life as if it were a foolish game, The way the evening breeze may tease a candle flame* Hmm, I guess Dan had a bit of an idle mind and had felt this needed to be said. But the way the lines were written it looked like they had more significance than that. It held more…something had to be there in them. Instantly I got this flickering in my head and I filed through other pieces of paper, hoping it would be right. I was, because there were other pieces of paper with these very same underlined notes. I stared at the very next one I could find and read it…hoping it could speak more to me. October 31st, Oiche Shamhna He says to me “Are you ready Dan?” and I says right back that I’m feckin’ riled up to boiling point. He says “Well, sure I’ve forgotten to give you a ring name.” I says “Just use me name.” He says “I can’t do that, it’s too plain. You’ll need a nickname with it.” “A what?” I says. “A nickname, something for you to go by without any problems,” he says. I says “I still don’t get it.” “What did they call ya when you were young?” and I replied with “The feckin’ firebrand because I was hot as coals when it came to an event.” “Perfect” he says. I stepped through the curtain. This was in full form and to the point. I could only guess when exactly it happened in times of the exact date but it was nice to see the Irish was still in him. My eyes scanned the page and found the underlined part. Yesterday, when I was young, So many happy songs were waiting to be sung, So many wild pleasures lay in store for me, And so much pain my dazzled eyes refused to see* The only thing I had going was that first line, the repetition of the previous piece. I’d seen it before somewhere and it begged to be let out of my head so that it would provide some relief. Staring at it held no better chance of figuring it out though. The event did remind me though of the time I stepped into the ring the first time myself. I was freaked out because I was only a young whelp as Dan had called me back then. I can remember being freaked out clearly because he was lambasting me for puking my guts out in the gutter outside the gym hall. I can see his shaking head as my head hung low to the ground, emptying the contents of the dinner he’d paid for and was now grumbling was going to waste. In fact, he never paid for dinner with me after that. A smile crept across my face. Good old Dan. Still the words lingered with me and it left me frustrated sitting in this pile of memories. So much could be hinted at, so much could be true or false and I had nothing to hold onto. Not yet anyway…but maybe with time. The lines read as if he were happy…and sorrowful all at once. Like he held captivated by the moment, unsure which way to go. He danced and flirted with them both, not letting them decide which took him. Another piece lying on the floor reached out to me and I picked it up. November 18th…. All I hear is a door banging…constantly banging in the back of my head and there being no return for it. No quick wit, no snappy comeback…just silence. And now….loss is in my heart. I could never say it Grace…I never could and you deserved it…but I won’t have the chance to now. It’s too late. Who knew fate would have that in store for you…or me? I never thought… I never thought Grace. Sorry is one word too late to save this mess. And I can’t say it to you now… The game of love I played with arrogance and pride, And every flame I lit too quickly, quickly died, The friends I made all seemed somehow to drift away, And only I am left on stage to end the play* A penny dropped in the well and the reverberations plumed towards the sides, perhaps waiting to stick and not return. This letter left me like that. I knew that Dan was married…and I knew that his wife had passed away… But I never knew like this. It felt voyeuristic looking into the soul of this man…almost perverted and wrong. But I couldn’t let go of this worn page, one single page that opened a doorway. In a sense, each page had so far and each one would as it went along. Maybe that’s why the writing was fresher, the lines worn under to remind the reader of the point being put across in perspective. This piece of words were intrinsically linked to each passage and the one in my hand had a very sour tone to it. There was regret, desolation and loss all at once struggling to get out of the page, to wrap themselves around me and take me out and down to the floor, to be washed over by their power and doused. Dan was lacking his life in this page…he was completely lost. It hurt because I knew of this too….all too well. I remember my wife Laura walking out in a huff with our son, walking out to get fresh air and peace from their stubborn, hot headed husband and father. Instead what they got was a kidnapping and an end to the American Dream that I searched for. I never got to say goodbye. But that’s not the word he wanted to say, I just knew it. This was around the seventies…sometime late in the seventies. And this was a time of extreme change for him, he simply didn’t recover from this event and it showed in this telling page. I knew this now especially from his plea when I sat beside him in the hospital. This tainted, distorted his life forever. I still don’t understand though Dan…what does these underlined words tell you? Another one, picked up from beside my left leg reads a little happier: June 20th, 1975 - St. Peter’s Memorial Church Haven’t felt this feeling in a long time. The quaking in my boots is rickety and unnerving. I’m not like this at all but I feel it coursing through me, wanting to eat me up and take me whole. I can’t let it though and I know that I won’t…because who’s waiting for me is so…beautiful. I wish I could tell her that. Later, I promise the world…just like Jason has. It’s really simple I’ve told her and in no time I’ll be able to take care of her and the baby that’s coming on its way pretty soon. I’ll be able to make ends meet and not live out of her car. The world is like a burning flame I tell her; once you feed it the right kind of wood, it roars into life. A wedding…a white one at that and in the very church where he would be buried beside. I wonder was Father Pearson that priest on that fateful day…? I would have been barely eight years old at that point and so far away in the distance that he couldn’t possibly foretell the future which lay ahead of him…yet he claimed so much, yearned for something bigger and better. It fit so well with the first letter I had read, when he told himself that he would find his purpose and be a somebody in this world. This was the young Dan O’Grady…the Dan that lived in the moment and tossed a middle finger right up at the Gods, each and every one of them. The picture confirmed the setting as he stood in front of the church and was beaming. It was in black and white and I gawped at it, but not for him. It was his wife that was remarkable. It was simply put, Jessica as she was now. Right down to the smallest detail could be found on her mother’s behalf. It amazed me that I’d never noticed it until now but Dan really did look nothing like his daughter. Maybe that’s why he held onto her, to hold onto his former life. The underlining on this one was something that immediately held a clarity. The thousand dreams I dreamed, the splendid things I planned, I always built, alas, on weak and shifting sand, I lived by night and shunned the naked light of day, And only now I see how the years ran away* This post script knew exactly the inclinations he’d made and the failure he had felt at his deathbed. It knew of what he had promised and how he had failed. How he had lost so many things and I couldn’t help but feel…deflated. He’d realised that there was something missing and had let it get to him. I felt deflated because I always thought he could holt out against anything. Turns out I was wrong…turns out he hid himself well. Still the words perplexed and vexed me as I wasn’t sure of the meaning behind them. There was a pattern emerging I thought with the first two but the last couple had thrown me right off. It could have been a repetition of remorse and regret but now it seemed scattered and loose. There was still a tie, but only a sliver of a fabric. It compelled to be continued though and I could see there were more possibilities locked in the pages on the ground. I ran so fast that time and youth at last ran out, I never stopped to think what life was all about, And every conversation I can now recall, Concerned itself with me, and nothing else at all* 1977, August 19th Dear Grace, I’m writing this to you just to make sure that you know I’m alright and things are going well. I understand that right now I haven’t been calling but I miss you so much. I hope Jessica is doing well. Just remember that I’m on my way to the top and to keep hoping for that jackpot alright? Don’t forget me because you’ll need to remember when I return. The Firebrand. The words above the letter said it all. He never stopped to ask how they were doing, he never stopped to ask how things were back at home and he never told them about how much he wanted to be with them. Nothing spoke louder than his signature. Not even his real name…just a persona. It must have hurt him whenever he scrawled that piece above it and it was on purpose too. There was plenty of room on this page to impart those words but only the top would do, almost like a precursor to what you were about to read and take in. He knew he’d fucked up, big time. There was one more piece of writing left that say among the cradle in my lap and it calmly drifted from side to side, waiting to be read but looking at this one, I wasn’t sure whether or not that I could. I had to…because I knew it would make sense. August 13th , 1995 - Park Bench A famous man died today and it seems strange, but sitting here, the memories come flooding back. December 28th, 2005 It’s close…and all I can share is grief with everyone. They all grieve for me and I thank them but I know that deep down I must grieve for them all. Because they must find out what it truly was like. I wish nothing but happiness and comfort once this is all laid to rest. It has to be this way. There are so many songs in me that won't be sung, I feel the bitter taste of tears upon my tongue, The time has come for me to pay, For yesterday, when I was young* I dropped the page as the words were already inside me…taking me apart. I knew the words finally and it all began to make perfect sense. All these words…made a song, a particular song which were referencing the very first event in the letters…Dan’s run in with Mickey Mantle. I knew this because Dan had told me the story about this before. It was a song, that he had played at his funeral and one that he felt typified his life to the hilt. Looking at it now, it felt like Dan empathised with Mickey, in many more ways than one. But it wasn’t the importance of the song along that struck me. It was the very meaning of them and their inherent tags that came along as part of the deal. I knew this journey because I had lived so much of it already…all these words up until the point of the last four lines could easily have been mine as much as Dan’s. They easily…could have been the ones to strike my own leaving of earth as we know it and that scared me most of all. I may have understood his meaning of living in the present and taking care of things in a way but it took on a whole other meaning now. Dan’s life was ruined by considering the past too much and the future too little. He didn’t want me to strictly adhere to the present and forget everything else. He wanted to instil that I had to be mindful and conscious of the consequences of my actions, but not the way I had before. Before I locked myself in my ribcage and waited until it became the past and then I could truly understand. He wanted me to take action, considered action, but action in the present, not of the past. He failed to realise this until it was too late and paid for it the rest of his life. He paid for it right on the last day, realising that mistake. He gave me the chance not to become the same thing. One more thing clicked and in a sense this click brought it all together and rounded it off nicely. He had stated about a specific phrase that he wanted put on his gravestone in his will and it sat on the desk in front of me, right beside the belt which had my name on it. I took it up and looked at it closely, nodding as it became so much clearer…. “a fragile hero to whom we had an emotional attachment so strong and lasting that it defied logic. In the last years of his life, always so hard on himself, he finally began to appreciate the difference between a role model and a hero. The first, he often was not. The last, he forever will be. And, in the end, people got it kid.”** Bob Costas was scribbled down beneath it and it was said at the funeral of Mickey Mantle. Dan, knowing what he had been, knowing what he could have potentially done to his entire network of people that he knew…knew that this was something that he could’ve been and that he fell a bit short. He never saw in his own mind that he was a hero…or a somebody. But he knew that other people did, people that cared and he saw fit to put it there. The key was from his statement of being a role-model and a hero. Only now did he understand the distinguishing characteristic of hero and role-model. When it was too late. He needed to pass it on before it was too late for anyone else. Like me. That’s what he needed put across. He needed me to know that despite intentions, everyone makes mistakes. It’s the learning that’s the important part. The belt shined in front of me and the purification had been started. I wasn’t done with this belt yet, even if it did scare me. To hold it too high was to be scorched from the reflection of the sun off of it. I knew now that it was meant to be held at a lower height…where it becomes a part of you….not you a part of it. I wasn’t done yet…and I thank you Dan for showing me the light. * - Taken from “Yesterday When I Was Young” by Roy Clark ** - Quote by Rob Costas at the funeral of Mickey Mantle
