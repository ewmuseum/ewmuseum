Dear Johnny...

17 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 17th 2005 INT. – JOHNNY NOBLE’S HOME – MORNING October 2005 Johnny’s Noble’s mildly mentally handicapped adopted orphan son, Jethro walks into the living room where Noble is sitting in a recliner staring out the window of his Seattle home as the rain falls. He’s wearing jeans and a t-shirt, playing with his massive soul-patch of death (A “Soul Patch” is the tuft of hair that grows directly under one’s lower lip and above their chin), and holding a picture, as if he were deep in thought. *cue pensive violin sounds* Jethro: Dad, you ready to go to the park! Noble nods. Johnny: Yeah, buddy. Jethro was confused at his father’s unusual reaction. Jethro: Johnny? Johnny tucks the picture up his shirt and spins ‘round in his chair to look at his son. Johnny: Yeah, Jeth? Jethro walks over and sits on the couch just a few feet from his father. Jethro: What’er you thinkin’ about? Johnny: Oh… PANNING SHOTS OF DOWNTOWN SEATTLE IN THE 60’S August 1966 It was 1966 and the first episode of Star Trek, entitled “The Cage” and starring Jeffery Hunter as the rogue Captain Pike had just been rejected by three networks. That has little or nothing to do with the story at hand. Johnny Noble was halfway through his 20th trip around the sun and he’d just met ‘The One’. He was hopelessly in love, head over heels and no one could tell him different. The only person that did was his Pastor. Johnny had been studying to become a Youth Pastor himself at a Charismatic Bible College and at the same time he was interning at a small, urban church in Downtown Seattle, Covenant Christian Fellowship (CCF). He met a girl, a beautiful girl who was at the tender age of 16 and was attending the group he was Pastoring. Ahh, so we can see the conflict of interest rearing it’s ugly head. Johnny fought the feeling for as long as he could. But he was hopeless to resist and resistance was, indeed, futile. He asked this young lady, Desirae, to come out with him to the HarborFront. He intended to heed his Pastor’s advice and break it off before it really had a chance to get started. That was his intention. But the road to hell is paved with those. EXT. - THE HARBORFRONT - EVENING August 1966 A soft, warm wind wandered around and across the piers and the beaches. Johnny and Desirae walked side-by-side along the beach. They’d been walking for nearly 10 minutes and neither of them had said a word yet… besides “Hello”. Johnny: Des… Des: Yeah. Johnny: It’s like this… Des: I know. You can’t see me. We’ve been through this before. Let’s just get it over with. I’m tired of being dangled on this string. Johnny: I’m not dangling you! You mean the world… Des interrupts him and he stops walking, shifting his gaze from the sand up to Desirae. She kept walking, also looking at the ground. Des: No I don’t! Johnny, I’ve wanted you since the first time I ever laid eyes on you. And you’ve lead me to believe that something would happen between us and you just. You… I feel like you pull me in just to push me away. That’s not fair. She looked up and saw that Johnny was not beside her anymore. She turned back and saw Johnny, a little less than ten feet away with tears in his eyes. He heard every word she said and every one of them rang true. He doubted that she knew just how crushing those words were to him. He’d told himself all of them already. He tried to rationalize it, but it just didn’t work. He knew that nothing he could do would make him happy. He had to choose and he knew that the right choice was to stay with the church. But nothing had ever felt so wrong before. They just looked at each other, a lonely longing pulling them together, but a conscience torn ripping them apart. He walked up to her, vulnerable, his very soul lain bare and cupped her face with his right hand. He leaned in and kissed her. He spat in the face of better judgement. He tossed his inhibitions to the wind and cast his convictions into the sea of forgetfulness. He held her close and kissed her. MONTAGE – TO RICHARD MARX’S “ENDLESS SUMMER NIGHTS” August 1966 - February 1969 Summer came and left without a warning // All at once I looked and you were gone // And now you’re looking back at me // Searching for a way that we can be like we were before Johnny & Des getting ice cream at a stand by Husky Stadium. Now I’m back to what I knew before you // Somehow the city doesn’t look the same // I’d give my life for one more night // Of having you here to hold me tight; oh, please // Take me there again // Oh, oh Johnny & Des laying beside each other under one of the piers at the HarborFront. CHORUS: And I remember how you loved me // Time was all we had until the day we said goodbye // I remember every moment of those endless summer nights Johnny & Des dancing in his kitchen to the sound of ‘Mony Mony’. I still recall the walks along the beaches // And the way your hair would glisten in the sun // Rising in the afternoon // Making love to you under the moon, oh Johnny presents Des with a promise ring on her 18th birthday. Do you remember all the nights we spent in silence // Every single breath you took was mine // We can have it all again // Say that you’ll be with me when the sun brings your heart to mine // Oh, oh Des unbuttons Johnny’s shirt in his bedroom. CHORUS: And I remember how you loved me // Time was all we had until the day we said goodbye // I remember every moment of those endless summer nights Johnny presents Des with a ring, she says yes. There’s only so much I can say // So please don’t run away from what we have together // It’s only you and me tonight // So let’s stay lost in flight // Oh, won’t you please surrender Des walks out of the bathroom with a beam on her face. “It’s blue!” she exclaims, “I’m pregnant!” INT. - HARBOURVIEW MEDICAL CENTER – DELIVERY ROOM October 1969 Doctor: Look, Mr. Noble I know it’s hard, but you need to decide! Your fiancée is haemorrhaging; you’ll either have to save her or the baby. We need to know right away. What an insane decision to make! Johnny knew he wasn’t God. It wasn’t his place to decide who’s to live and who’s to die. He knew he loved Des, but he loved his son as well. He couldn’t be a single father. He was confused… he didn’t know what to do. The options raced through his mind in all the bewilderment he blurted out the one choice that kept on revisiting his mind. Johnny: Save her! Doctor: Your fiancée? Johnny, enraged at the choice he was forced to make, lashed out at the doctor. Johnny: My son’s not a girl is he!? The doctor, regretting asking such an obvious question went back into the delivery room. Johnny stood at the window and watched on. In horror. There was so much blood and he wasn’t even allowed in. His son’s final moments of life and this is how they would end; torn from his mother and abandoned by his father. It was too much. He held his head in his hands and slumped up against the door to the operating room. He was a mess. Emotionally screwed up and in ill repair. He stayed slumped up against that door for almost two hours without moving one muscle one inch. For two hours he was alone with his thoughts. The hospital was crowded and busy and people were constantly around him but he was completely unaware. He was alone with his thoughts and they tore him apart. He’d given medicine the nod to kill his son; to sacrifice him for his fiancée. Of course the alternative wasn’t any more appealing. More Appalling, actually. For 120 minutes this is what occupied his brain. Finally the doctors emerged. The last one stopped and offered Johnny a hand up. Which he accepted. Doctor: She’s awake now. You can go see her. She’ll be fine in a day or two. Johnny went into the room. It had been cleaned up and she had obviously been informed of her husband’s choice. It’s written all over her face. Johnny: Honey, I… Des: I know. Johnny sighed and leaned over his fiancée’s bed. Johnny: I- I love you. Des: I know. Johnny: That’s not really the answer I was… Des: I know. Silence. A long, awkward silence. Des: Look. I’m gonna need some time. I’ll give you a call when I’m ready to talk to you again. Johnny stood up. And started walking toward the door to leave. Johnny: If you need anything. Anything at all you can… Des: I know your number. Johnny: Right. With that he left the room not knowing when he might get to see his fiancée again. EXT. – CALVARY CATHOLIC CEMETARY – MIDDAY October 1969 Johnny decided he needed to have a funeral and burial for his son. It was the smallest casket you’ve ever seen and it wasn’t endorsed by the government. They insisted that the boy was never born and was, therefore, not entitled to a birth or death certificate. It only further compounded the aggravation and frustration he and his fiancée had been feeling over the last several days. As the Priest delivered the Eulogy Johnny and Des stood side-by-side and when it was over she started crying. Johnny went to hold her, but she pulled away. They hadn’t touched or spoken since that day in the hospital. That was about to change. Des: Johnny… She played with her engagement ring. Des: You… and the baby… It’s all a part of a life that I can’t be attached to anymore. It’s too much for me to handle. Maybe some day that will change, but for now… Des pulled the ring off her finger and put it in Johnny’s hand. He stood, speechless, as she walked away. Johnny gave up his life, his school, his job, his career and his friends for this woman. This woman who would walk away from him after he made the most important decision of his life. The woman who would walk away when he was at his most vulnerable. Surely this wasn’t right. But what could he do? He had to watch her leave. Johnny stayed long after everyone left and talked to his son. He promised to return and visit him every year on his birthday. And every year he did. And every year he waited for Des to show up. And every year she didn’t. He hasn’t heard from her since. But still he clings to that hope. Hope is a great thing, perhaps the greatest of things and without it we have nothing. Hope gives us tomorrow. INT. - JOHNNY NOBLE’S LIVING ROOM – MIDDAY October 2005 Johnny: Yeah, Jeth? Jethro walks over and sits on the couch just a few feet from his father. Jethro: What’er you thinkin’ about? Johnny: Oh… nothing special. Jethro: Okay. You ready to go to the park? Johnny: After, buddy, I got a promise to keep first. October 17th, 2005. His son would be 36 today. Happy Birthday.
