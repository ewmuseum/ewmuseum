This is where you saw my naked soul (Part 1)

6 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6.6.06 Out of nowhere, you get offered your dream job - but you'll have to move to take it. Unfortunately, your significant other can't go with you. Do you... A: Stay, because love comes first? Or… B: Go for it, because a dream career is more important? What a loaded question. Yet, there were times in my life that the answer always seemed so simple. When I was 14 and head over heels in love with my…third? Fifth girlfriend in three months? I can’t remember, but I knew I was sure of my choice: Love. Love is the most important thing there is. Who would give up something so vital to their soul…for a job? Of course, my testicles were still in the process of dropping. I thought I was in love with somebody else every ten seconds, falling hard for anybody with a pair of tits and lily fragrance who took the trouble to traverse my path. And of course, I was 13 – who gives any thought to career options when they’re not even old enough to realize that listening to Michael Jackson’s feel-good racial equality melodies on repeat makes you among the biggest losers planet Earth has to offer? And of course, I can’t even remember the name of the girl I swore was my “soul mate.” That should give you an indication of just how smitten I really was. Flash forward about half a decade. I’m 19. Jane, the girl I was sure beyond a shadow of a doubt that I would take as my wife, had just conspired with my son-of-a-bitch roommate to trample all over my heart with diamond-encrusted golf cleats. Now, presented with the same question as before, I was just as sure of my answer despite the fact that it was now the opposite from before: Love? Yeah right. Love won’t take you anywhere…except maybe a one-way trip to your own personal hell. By this time I had dropped out of college and my career aspirations were no more settled than they’d been when I was in love with Ol’ Whats-Her-Name during the summer that it Didn’t Matter if You were Black or White. But I knew that if my “dream job” (whatever THAT might be) came up, I wouldn’t let some floozie get in the way. Yeah, I was pretty despondent back then. And I eventually came to realize that one extreme end of the spectrum was no better than the other. Granted, there is no power in the universe that has the uncanny ability to drive an ordinarily rational human male completely bat-shit crazy quite like a woman – especially a woman with the key to his heart worn not proudly around her chest, but buried deep within her purse of indifference. I hated that anybody could have that kind of control over my emotions…not to mention sex life. But I knew it was wrong to trade in one extreme for another. Not allowing yourself to care anything for somebody is equally as bad as caring way too much. Those pesky Buddhists seem to have it right – the middle path is always the best to take. There’s a recurring theme here, if you haven’t gathered it by now. The reason I was so sure of my decision, be it whether I was convinced to follow my heart or my dreams, was probably due in large part to the fact that I didn’t really have to make that decision. Anytime I found myself falling in love I had no career ambitions to get in the way. And anytime I found a job that I felt worthy and worthwhile signing on board for, there was no one around in my life to hold me back. But I would soon learn that when finally faced with making a choice, it wouldn’t be so easy… *** Part 1: This is my home… Several weeks ago… The ultimate matchup: Hunter vs. prey. The small creature found itself backed into corner, literally staring death in the eyes. Her parents had warned her all her life that this day might come. They’d forced her to memorize her surroundings, inch by inch. She knew every escape route from danger, every nook and cranny of her environment. Every possible place she could be, she knew the shortest possible distance back to her safe little hole in the wall. Even though it had now become instinct, such endless application of the information seemed frivolous at best; there had been merely rumors of the shadowy predator and its renowned killing capabilities. She had seen this marauder herself a handful of times in her young life, but only from a great distance. From such a distance, the hunter almost looked cute, definitely as elegant as it was graceful. The way it sleekly glided across a room and lazily perched atop a high overlook, it might almost be mistaken for some sort of deity. But on this day, the predator had traded in his elegance for raw power and brutal killing potency. When he wasn’t sleeping the afternoon away or exploring the confines of the environment he shared with his timid prey, his mind was focused on only one series of events: Hunt. Stalk. Pursue. Kill. The first three were complete. He had seen the prey out foraging for sustenance, completely unaware of his presence. Slowly but surely he followed his target, as stealthily as a poltergeist moving in on an unsuspecting victim. Eventually the smaller animal noticed its adversary and make a break for it, but the predator had its ground covered extremely well and followed in hot pursuit, ultimately trapping it into a corner. There was no more running left to do. The larger creature bared his sharp, piercing fangs as it coiled back, ready to make his final move. Shaking, the smaller of the two stared up into the eyes of what could only be described as a monster, surely bent on her destruction. She closed her eyes in fear as the beast prepared for its assault… “Reaper!” Completely startled, the monster, or more appropriately, the cat named “Reaper” by his seemingly sadistic-minded owner leapt practically three times his own height into the air. This gave his prey, an utterly bewildered but thankful mouse, just enough time to bolt beneath the cat and escape outside an open glass door to a presumably safer outdoors. Reaper turned to look at who had ruined this perfectly good kill. First revealed at his eye level were a pair of tan Vans sneakers with bare shins protruding from them. As he gazed up he saw black pants sheathing the rest of the ruiner’s legs, and a white tank top that would have conveyed at the familiar skull and crossbones logo of Zakk Wylde’s Black Label Society…that is, if cats were able to register human insignias. Atop that lied a black choker, fuchsia lip gloss and fiery, not quite shoulder-length crimson hair. “Come on,” Amy Campbell said, affectionately lifting the midnight-black feline into her arms. “Why don’t you pick on somebody your own size?” Helpless in her arms, Reaper made no move to escape as Amy walked him down the hall into another room. In this room rested one Darcy Markson, fast at work perusing a book that Amy was unable to catch the title of. Out of the corner of his eye Darcy noticed Amy standing in the doorframe with Reaper in her clutches. “He-ey!” Darcy said warmly; Amy couldn’t tell whether it was reserved for her sudden presence or the cat’s. “Is he up to no good?” Darcy added with a smile, perhaps suggesting that he intended it for the both of them. “He was just about to put an end to some poor mouse’s life, before I put a stop to it,” Amy responded, as she moved closer to Darcy’s position on the sofa. “Mice creep me out, but not quite as much as dead mice.” “Well if you would let Reaper do his thing, the mice will learn not to hang around here,” Darcy responded in kind. “Besides, you can’t take hunting away from a cat. That’s what they live for. Amy made a face. “Why don’t you get this place fumigated so we can come back to a vermin-free home for once?” “Amy, that’s a terrible idea and you know it. You know how often people confuse you for vermin…the last thing we want is an exterminator with dangerous chemicals to make the same mistake.” Darcy laughed at his own joke as Amy tossed the cat toward the couch, where he gracefully landed on all four paws. “I’d smack you, if you didn’t bruise so easily,” Amy mockingly threatened. “Good lady, you have insulted my honor!” Darcy said with a chuckle, putting his book down. Aimz and Darcy Crisis may not have ever squared off one-on-one in a wrestling match, but she was fully prepared to take him on in yet another of their countless but classic living room wrestling affairs when the phone suddenly rang. “Hold that thought, Darcy said as he looked toward the phone lying on the glass-topped end table next to his book. Amy nodded and headed for the door. “Come on, Reaper!” she called to the cat. “You want some chicken treats? They’re taaaasty…not to mention already dead…” With a curious meow, Reaper leapt off the couch and followed her out of the room as Darcy picked up the receiver. “Hello?” “Hmm…that smells like a Markson on the other end to me…” Darcy laughed. “Jim-bo! How’s it goin’ brother? What rock did you crawl out from under?” “Your mother’s,” came the reply from the other end. “And please, enough with the ‘Jim-bo.’ It’s ‘Jim,’ or if you’re feeling a little crazy, ‘Jimmy’. ‘Jim-bo’ makes me sound like I come from Turdville, Nebraska, population seven.” “That’s kind of the point,” Darcy said. “So what’s up?” “I just got handed down a direct message to give to you from the new big man himself.” “…Jameson?” Darcy asked, after a beat. “Yup.” Darcy whistled in astonishment. David Jameson, a longtime senior vice president with the Tomorrow Institute (or as its members more often referred to it, “The Institute”), had recently been named the new CEO when health problems forced the previous executive officer to take an early retirement. Jim Torres, the man whom Darcy was speaking with had met the latter several years ago, during Darcy’s excursion into the South American rainforest. ‘Jim-bo,’ as Darcy liked to call him, had been working with a fact-finding mission for the Institute to organize a movement against the rapid tree and soil eradicating interests in the area. There he came across Darcy, who had fallen in with one of the local tribes who had as much at stake in the destruction of their native rainforest as anybody. It was Jim who made an attempt to convince Darcy to return with him to the America and work with the Institute on behalf of the tribe he now lived with. Accepting the offer, Darcy soon found himself on the Institute’s payroll and his home once again in the United States. Impressed by Darcy’s enthusiasm for protecting the rainforest as well as his natural charisma that helped win over many a supporter, Jim did all he could to help Darcy work his way up the Institute ladder. Eventually Darcy met several times with Jameson, who was extremely interested in pulling Darcy in to work full-time directly under him. This was nearly six months ago; Darcy was on the verge of accepting the offer before he became aware that Amy was still wrestling in the up-and-coming Atlantic Wrestling Club. Jameson had a personality that was, to say the least…eccentric. His eyes seemed to always blaze with passion, whether he was speaking of the bold vision for the Institute or savagely chewing out a fellow Institute member over a disagreement as small as where to organize a clean air grassroots campaign. Ordinarily, David Jameson would be the kind of man that Darcy would despise associating himself with, the kind that seemed to value people only as items that would ultimately either help or hinder his agenda. Darcy also got the sense that Jameson didn’t care much for him either; he’d often been both publicly and privately chastised by Jameson for what he liked to call “frivolous pursuits.” At first it seemed like it would be impossible for the two to get along, but as time went by David Jameson and Darcy Markson learned to respect each other’s enthusiasm for protecting the world. At least, enough to get along. “And what might that message be?” Darcy queried. “He says he wants you on the next flight out to Rio de Janeiro,” Jim told him. “He also says to find yourself a half-decent hotel…he wasn’t able to take care of it himself as he’s just flying in from Switzerland, but the Institute will reimburse you. Show up at the Rio office tomorrow and tell the receptionist you have an appointment with Mr. Jameson, and he’ll take it from there.” “Take what from there?” Darcy asked, suddenly confused. “That’s all I was told. I didn’t get this message directly, it was handed through a few people. I asked for more info, which they said they all did just the same, and they all got the same answer: he wouldn’t say.” Darcy frowned. “What’s with this cloak and dagger bullshit? Why does he want me to fly to another hemisphere without so much as a hint about what’s going on?” “I don’t know…but I can’t imagine he would do it if it was going to be a waste of time. Here’s what I do know – when he took the CEO job he told the press that the Institute is going to have a new set of goals. He promised to ‘shake things up,’ whatever the hell that means. But if he wants you out in Rio ASAP without saying why, it’s got to be something big.” “That may be,” Darcy said. “But it’s not that simple. Jameson knows I have another job, one that I’m contractually obligated to make appearances for. Not to mention a certain lady-friend of mine who won’t be happy if I disappear to Brazil without some sort of reason.” “I think he’s aware,” came Jim’s reply. “He also said to bring her with you, if it will prevent some kind of problem. And the only thing you’ll be missing is the AWC anniversary show, which as far as I can tell you’re not even booked to appear for.” “How did you know about the anniversary show? And how do you know I’m not booked?” Jim chuckled from the other end. “What?” Darcy said, starting to get a little annoyed. “Have you heard of a little thing called the internet? The AWC posts their events on the website, with a card of who’s supposed to be there to boot. You weren’t on the list, so Jameson figured you wouldn’t be busy.” Darcy sighed. “Alright, fine. Fair enough. Tell Jameson, or whoever you have to that I’ll be there. But I’m bringing Amy with me, and we’re ordering two of everything on room service.” “A wise decision,” Jim responded. “Bring me back one of those tiny little tequila bottles, I like to pretend I’m a giant.” *** Part 2: This is where I long to be… Rio de Janeiro, Brazil… So in case you were wondering why Aimz and Darcy Crisis no-showed AWC’s anniversary show, now you have your answer. The meeting was supposed to be hush-hush, where in fact it had become anything but. Amy, being the internet junkie that she was, alerted Darcy that while he might be intentionally kept in the dark by the Tomorrow Institute, it certainly wasn’t keeping professional wrestling “news” (in reality, “tabloid” is probably the more appropriate nomenclature) sites from obtaining leaked information about Darcy’s upcoming meeting with Jameson and going into a frenzy of wild speculation. Amy knew to take these so-called “journalists” with a grain of salt, but it was impossible to pretend that headlines such as “Crisis gone from AWC?” that were already reporting that Darcy had negotiated a release with the company weren’t a little disconcerting. She continued perusing a few of the websites that she saved while on the flight over on Darcy’s laptop as the two rode to the Rio office in style, in a jet-black limousine. She appeared intently focused as Darcy leaned over to glance at the monitor. “I wish you wouldn’t look at those things,” Darcy told her. “They’re not ALL about you,” Amy retorted sharply. “Some of them are about the hateful things Ellis Nash has been saying behind my back.” “Well I wouldn’t give them too much credit when they’re printed right next to a confirmed item that Jameson’s offered me seven figures to hang it up and work for him full-time. Believe me, these guys are fucking hippies. They don’t have seven figure salaries to throw around to anybody. I get paid more per chair shot than I do for any idea I come up with for them” Amy didn’t even look up from the monitor. “I don’t like this. He’s probably got some kind of stunt planned. Shouldn’t you be a little more worried? I thought you told me yourself that it never sat well with him that you wouldn’t ‘bend to his will’ or something. Or maybe you were talking about that vacuum cleaner sales guy on TV.” Darcy chortled at her lame joke as the limo arrived at the Institute’s famed “Rio Palace.” The only real comparison this “palace” had to a monarchial mansion was that like the estates of a kingdom’s house their most powerful individuals, so too did the Institute’s bastion in Rio house theirs. The Rio Palace, if it were any place else in the world, would look completely ordinary among the towering architecture intended to capture the imaginations of the area patrons. Here in the so-called “City of God,” there was a certain splendor that the Rio Palace seemed to capture. It was a plainly painted teal building that towered all of five stories above the Brazilian shoreline. A few stone balconies protruded from the large bay windows that adorned the sides of the higher floors. It was capped off by a rooftop containing an orchard of some of Brazil’s finest flora and foliage. Compared to some of the rundown and abandoned buildings surrounding the Palace it might even be considered a place reserved for kings by some of Rio’s less affluent residents, but it wasn’t meant to wow onlookers with inspired architecture. Jameson, who had played a large hand in the building’s designing, was adamant that the building remain modest, and any awe-inspiring should come from Mother Nature. One of the few things we agreed upon, Darcy thought to himself as the limo came to a halt. “What do I owe you?” Darcy called up to the driver as he and Amy made their exit from the automobile. “It’s been taken care of!” the driver shouted back. “No charge!” Darcy and Amy exchanged uneasy glances as the limo driver pulled away. Together they trudged up the sandstone steps into the Palace’s main entrance. The inner confines seemed to suggest that more of the building’s expenses were spent on the interior, as the main lobby resembled that of a multiple-star class hotel. Marble pillars directed the eyes back toward the golden-colored elevator shafts, to which Darcy and Amy were directed by Lisa, the kindly, buxom receptionist. Darcy briefly pondered stealing one last glance at the receptionist’s backside, but the mental picture of Amy snarling made him think better of it. Other than their initial greeting to Lisa, the two offered no additional words until the elevator doors shut behind them. “I thought you said this place was a palace,” Amy finally offered. “I’ve seen Comfort Inns that look more dignified than this place.” “Like I said…hippies,” Darcy said. The elevator dropped them off on the fourth floor, where the offices were primarily contained. Darcy exchanged greetings with a few of the familiar faces as Amy glanced about the room. From the look of it the environment, which resembled any other room packed with cubicles, separated officers and water coolers, one who didn’t know any better would be hard-pressed to guess that this office space was working for the betterment of nature. After a brief walk that led them down several corridors, the two arrived at Jameson’s office. Darcy was just about to let himself in when a scrawny intern jogged up to them from behind. “Mr. Markson!…Mr. Markson!…Hold on one second!” gasped the intern, trying to catch his breath. Once he more or less had it, he continued. “Mr. Jameson didn’t…know you were bringing…your friend here. He requested a private meeting with you.” Darcy was about to offer a protest, but before he could utter a word Amy stopped him short. “Don’t worry,” she said to him, still clutching his laptop as she sat in a green armchair that lied against the wall just to the left of Jameson’s door. “I’ll find a way to stay occupied.” He’s gotta be screwing with me…he KNEW she was coming with me, Darcy thought to himself as he gave Amy a nod. With that, the practically gawking intern attempted to engage Amy in conversation as Darcy entered Jameson’s office. Jameson was taller than Darcy, and his radiant blond hair caught the glow of the South American sun as he turned in his chair to face Markson. “Darcy! It’s good to see you!” Jameson opened with, beaming with his renowned sly little smile. “Forgive me for finding that hard to believe, sir” Darcy replied, stressing the last of his words to emphasize that he had no intention of dealing with any of Jameson’s crap. “Let’s get down to business. I flew to another continent and skipped out on some important business to be here. Would you mind telling me what this is about?” “Darcy, please. Have a seat. Let’s discuss this like gentlemen.” Darcy didn’t move so much as an inch. “…Or not,” Jameson said. “Listen, I know you and I have had a hard time seeing eye to eye. But you and I have both had the interests of the Institute first and foremost, and we can at least recognize each other for that. But now that I’ve been named chief officer of the Tomorrow Institute, I am in an excellent position to bury the hatchet, so to speak. I have something to offer you…something mutually beneficial to the both of us. Darcy might have found his words hard to believe, but Jameson was certainly not above doing something positive for somebody else…so long as it benefited him, of course. “Go on,” Darcy said, electing to take a seat after all. “We’ve both done a lot for the Institute, even if we’ve gone about it different ways,” Jameson continued. You’ve been a valuable spokesman for this company, and even though I thought your moonlighting as a professional wrestler was a little…odd, it’s done wonders for our PR. TI’s name is being spread all over the world, and we have you to thank for that. But there’s way you can contribute even more, a chance to further our agenda threefold. That’s what you want, isn’t it?” Darcy was unwilling to bite. “Get to the point.” “There’s been an opening in the officer ranks around here, if you haven’t heard. The board elected me to take Meyers’ place. And as of yet, nobody’s been offered my spot…” Darcy was taken aback at that, knowing precisely what he meant. Senior VP? Is he serious? he wondered. “You’ve got to be kidding,” he eventually added aloud. “There’s no way I’m qualified -” “- I’ve already discussed it with the board. Maybe you didn’t work your way up the ladder traditionally, but people are for some reason very willing to listen to you. Think about it, Darcy: Sure, the PR’s been great, but you’ve just been a suggestion man up until now. You take my old job, you’ve have a direct say in what we do around here. I wish I could tell you the pay was more stellar, but we’ll definitely have you set up on a pension. You’ll be taken care of. Unlike your current occupation, where one false move to that old back of yours could get you knocked right off the payroll altogether. It’s a full-time gig Darcy, but we…forget that. I know you’re the man for the job.” Darcy’s eyes flared open somewhat. Jameson was a master at manipulating people’s emotions, and he knew just how to go after Darcy. His back, while it seemed to be adjusting into accepting the workload of a full-time professional wrestler, never stopped bothering him; and he always carried the risk of being one sick bump away from a wheelchair. “I- I’m not sure what to say,” Darcy said, faced with perhaps with what might be the most difficult decision of his life. Had Jameson suddenly done a 180 and elected to work with Darcy rather than against him? It seemed too good to believe. He'd been adamant with Jameson that he wished to remain wrestling as long as he could, but he'd also admitted that he didn't intend to spend his entire life turning proverbial tricks for wrestling promoters, who in fact so closely resembled pimps that Darcy didn't care to think about it very often. Deep down, he wanted to help people and the planet as much as he could, and this seemed like his best ticket to do so. “You’ve certainly presented me with a very tempting offer…” Upon those words, Darcy could have sworn he heard someone scamper down the hall. At the time, he thought nothing of it. *** To be continued…
