A New Beginning #2: 'Dropping The Dream'

Date: 18th July 2005 PRIME ReVolution 66 15th June 21:20 Knock, knock, knock. Hin See: Come in! Looking up from his cluttered desk, the PRIME owner brushes his papers aside and stands to greet the new entrant to his office – and then frowns. The person who stands in his doorway is not in PRIME. The person who stands in his doorway is Tim Shipley. Hin See: A Primetime Central guest back for more? Well, well. Do sit down... Shipley: (awkwardly) Good evening, Mr. See... He sits, and Hin See does the same the other side of the desk. Hin See: So. To put it bluntly, why are you here? Shipley: Well, Mr. See, after I was invited for ReVolution 64 – for which I thank you; the show was great and I really enjoyed myself – Hin See nods. Shipley: w – well, after that, I got thinking about how much I missed wrestling... the thrill of being in the ring, and how great it is to entertain thousands of fans... The authority figure yawns. Hin See: I'm sorry. Do go on. Shipley: I was - um - I was thinking maybe I could come back to wrestling. Just for the summer; I can't combine wrestling with college again, it didn't work before. Hin See: So, do so. But I fail to see why you're talking to me, rather than Matthew Lanier. Shipley licks his lips. Shipley: The thing is, Mr. Lanier wasn't all too happy about me leaving Core, and the fact that I chose an education over his promotion. And with all that happened with Tony – Hin See raises an eyebrow. Shipley: Tony Aliso. A warm smile of resignation comes over Hin See's face. Hin See: Ah! Yes, yes, I am sorry. Yes, I understand. Shipley: Yeah, I just can't really go back there... so I wanted to ask you if I could come to PRIME. He rushes out the last words, now focusing his gaze on the floor, red-cheeked, as he allows Hin See to digest what he just said. Hin See: You're asking me for a spot in PRIME? Shipley nods. Hin See: And why should I choose you over the dozens – hundreds, even – of others who are working their asses off to even get a tryout? Shipley: I, er – I was in Seven Of The Best, with Karina Wolfenden; I'm sure she'd tell you I'm – Hin See laughs loudly. Hin See: You expect me to trust the word of that unreliable, mercurial spotter? Besides, I highly doubt Karina Wolfenden even knows your name. Shipley: I – Again, the boss laughs, but not unkindly. Hin See: Even if I were sufficiently impressed by your abilities, I'm afraid that no wrestling promotion would employ you just for the summer. We need to know there's a future there. An end product. We're not going to push you on TV for six weeks and then let you disappear completely. That isn't how the business works, and if you think it is, I'm afraid you're not cut out for wrestling. Look at things from my point of view. Subdued, Shipley continues to stare at the floor. Hin See stands, wanting to end the interview. Hin See: No, I'm sorry. I just can't see it happening - not in PRIME, not anywhere. I'm very busy; I'm afraid I have to ask you to leave, Mr. Shackley. Shipley's head snaps up. He glares at Hin See's apologetic face and stands up, furious - the more confident Shipley from his later times in Core. Shipley: You invited me to your show and you don't even know my name? Oh, that's just great, isn't it. Don't worry, I'll leave, Mr. See, but I'll just remind you who I am before I depart. My name is Tim Shipley. Not Shackley, but Shipley. Remember that name, Mr. See, because one day I'm going to show you just what you missed out on. Shipley storms out of the room and slams the door. Hin See shakes his head. Hin See: So young... so naïve... Well thank you very much Hin See. Sighing, I searched my bottomless pocket for the cool metal relief of my key. Having obtained cool metal relief, I pushed open the door of my room and began to slowly unbutton my shirt – working my way up, rather than down, because tonight I felt in an ‘omfg I R teh r3b3l!’ mood, and I was fully aware of it. It wasn’t a very efficient way of undoing one’s shirt, I mused, as my misfiring fingers brushed against the thin layer of hair that had recently begun to reside on my chest. Check the time: midnight and three. Risk stripping naked before walking the green mile down and across the corridor to the bathroom? Yeah, go for it. No parties tonight. Half of the students gone home; finals over for the lucky ones. Others in their rooms – frantic revision, bi-hourly snacks, no sleep. I smiled ruefully, remembering my last exam. Two days ago. Enough revision? Maybe. Not as much as most, but more intelligent than most. Silently scolding self. Not so self-confident when the results come out and you’ve got a C, eh? Oh, shut up. Nothing you can do about it now. The exams: past. The results: future. Look to the present. One decision to make. Undress now? Ease. Efficiency. Embarrassment... Stop. Think. You want to be a wrestler again? You take risks. Anyway, what does it matter if people do see you naked? So what? Your body isn’t half bad. Sure, you’ve deteriorated since leaving Core, but you’ve kept a good 50% of the muscles you gained at that time – and you’re working out again. But do I really want people who I’m barely on speaking terms with to see me naked? Not really. But we’ve covered this. No one’s out there! So go and take your shower – maybe there’s hot water, maybe not; who knows? – and admire your body in the mirror like usual... I don’t admire my body! I assess it! If I’m going to get back into the ring I need to be making some progress! Whatever. You know that you secretly like to think you have a nice penis. ...Where the hell did that come from? I don’t know! I’m tired, hot, sweaty and angry! Go and have a shower right now! Alright, alright. Give me a chance. I was just thinking about – PRIME. Hin See. I know. Give it a good night’s sleep first. Work it out with a clearer head. But now, go and have a sh – Okay! Okay. A shower it is. Wait... let me check the time... You just did that! Midnight and three! Actually it’s midnight and six now. Whatever! Does that make any difference? Yes. The last train from Albany to New York City departs at 00:05. I’d have missed it, thanks to you. ...We’re not going to New York City. That’s not the point. What is the point, then? I don’t know. I’ll tell you what the point is. You’re too scared to open that door and walk, what, three yards to the bathroom – naked. I’m not scared. What do I have to be scared of? There’s no-one around, and besides, none of these people – I’ll tell you what you’re scared of. And while you’re scared, at the same time you’re secretly excited. About this fantasy... this dream of yours that’s been popping in and out of your head the last few nights... What? You’ve been too long without female contact... you’ve drifted away from Sam... and since Tony died, Kate isn’t even a girl to you anymore... it’s too stressful, too upsetting... We’re friends. No! But it’s not that! At times it’s like you’re the closest of friends – soulmates! But then the next minute you’re like strangers to each other... No, we’re just friends. The spirit of companionship following a traumatic event... that’s what it is. Tony’s death has created a lifelong bond between the two of you – but really, you don’t know anything about her, and she knows even less about you. And that’s not going to change, because whenever you see her, all that’s on your mind is Tony, and all that’s on her mind is Tony, and her eyes brim with tears, and you hug her, and stroke her hair, and she buries her head in your shoulder, and cries, and then you sit her down, and you try to cheer her up by talking about the good things that Tony did for her, and did for you, and was about. And it’s – – the same every time... Yes! And that’s not a bad thing, and I’m not blaming you for that, because Tony meant a lot to you, and a lot to Kate, and it’s only right that the two of you should remember him together. But you’ve still got to have a life. You’ve got to meet new people! So what’s this ‘fantasy’ you mentioned? Oh, come on. You know what it is, because I know what it is. I’ve seen it, because you’ve seen it. I’m you. So we both know what I’m talking about. You’re just choosing not to remember... I don’t know what you’re talking about. Oh, but you do... it’s locked away in the depths of your mind. This fantasy is why you’ve been looking at yourself in the mirror. Naked – always naked. I really haven’t! It’s a dream. Nothing more than a dream. You need to recognise that. It’s not going to happen. What is not going to happen?! I’ll refresh your memory, shall I? It’s you... you’re in the corridor... naked. We don’t know why. Night-time. And who should come walking down the corridor but – Sam. That’s right. Sammie Farrell comes walking down the corridor, in jeans and a light pink summery blouse, the tails just sweeping to either side slightly, affording a fleeting view of the very base of her midriff – unintentional, for sure. That’s right. She doesn’t like her body. So what happens next, since you seem to remember now? She says hey, and I say hi. There’s no reaction to my being naked. It’s like it’s perfectly normal not to have any clothes on. And she looks into my eyes – a look that could melt any man. Sensuous – no, what’s the word? Intense. Intense, yeah. And she moves in close against my chest, and undoes her shirt – starting at the bottom, working her away up. And it falls away, and I help it off her arms and drop it gently on the floor, and she steps back and unzips her jeans. Laughing, she discards them and stands there, grinning at you, and you smile back, and look her from bottom to top, taking in the silky lilac of her matching panties and bra – Panties? Come on now, I haven’t been living in America for that long. Oh, alright. Knickers, if we must. She breathes deeply, in and out, and the smile is dropped as she moves towards you. Closing her eyes, she leans against you, and your arms close gently around her sides, caressing her soft skin, looking down at her pert breasts, and her glistening lips, and her vulnerable eyes. That’s right... and her bra falls away, and her bosom sinks against mine, two white breasts, released from their prison, pressing into my chest – So you remember. I remember. So what’s going to happen right now, when you walk out into the corridor, naked? Sa – No. Oh. No one will be there. No one will see you. No one will have sex with you on the dirty linoleum floor. That’s disappointing. It is, isn’t it? Because you’re twenty years old, and you’re still a v – Alright, alright! So you’re going to have a shower now? I’ll have a shower. “N – gah!” I hopped backwards sharply as a cascade of water from the showerhead greeted me. A cascade of cold water. I turned the dial right round to its ‘hot’ end, hoping and praying for at least some reserves of warmed water tonight. I needed it tonight... I needed it. My wrestling dream had stopped before it had even had the chance to get started. It was my fault, though – I hadn’t even allowed it to get started, by imposing this summer-only rule on my time with any prospective promotion. Sure, they would be happy to sign the big names for short stints, but I was a realist, and I was nowhere near there yet. At twenty, I was still a hot prospect – which was good – but who signs a hot prospect on a short contract? I stepped into the flow of, at best, lukewarm water and pondered: what was I going to do with myself? I’d been so set on PRIME that I’d not considered another promotion – and I didn’t want to consider another promotion. I felt so attached to Hin See’s top US enterprise – they’d invite me, an unemployed former wrestler, as a PTC guest to watch their stars do what they did best: perform. When I’d turned up two weeks later to plead my case for a spot on the roster, I’d been allowed into the backstage area, no questions asked. The guards had recognised me. That meant a lot. But PRIME didn’t want me. Well, perhaps wrestling wasn’t for me after all, I thought. If I got back into the swing of things, I probably wouldn’t want to drop it again at the beginning of October for the new Harbrook term. So I was better off this way. And that was how I left it as I went to bed that night. The idea of wrestling just for the summer was an unworkable ideal, and instead, I would go and live with my parents, in their dingy little Albany apartment, and find a summer job flipping burgers or waiting tables... my new life’s dream would have to wait. Years. Until after college. Little did I know that the person I met the next day would change that.
