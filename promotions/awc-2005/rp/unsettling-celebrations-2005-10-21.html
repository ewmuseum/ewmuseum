Unsettling Celebrations

21 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 21/10/05 There are times when hard work is needed to be put in… There are times when pain and suffering are necessary… But sometimes, you just needed to celebrate. So I’m sitting in Langan’s, the Irish pub of this quarter of the city of Kingston. Of course it’s nearby to O’Grady’s and over the course of the years it has seen many people come and go through the doors. It’s a true Irish pub, right down to the furnishings. In true tested tradition, the walls are lined with old mahogany wood and look beaten by the weather from time immemorial. They’ve been lacquered with some varnish to keep them from fading even more, but really that was the whole point to begin with. To complement this, plenty of relics of Irish culture are posted around the bar, carefully placed behind glass so that they’ll be preserved for the good of all. That’s not to say there’s fights going on in the place, just that the proprietor Jim Langan is immensely protective of his treasures and tokens. Ah Jim, the carer of the community. When you had a problem, be it a murder on your hands, or you simply weren’t able to decide whether to buy black or brown shoes, Jim was your man. Many nights were spent by Jim, wiping down the bar, chatting a couple of the locals and advising them on what perfumes to buy for their wives, how to avoid their wives when they were having affairs and the like. For the people in this small district community in Kingston, in the county of Ulster, right in the heart of the State of New York, he was their priest. Which was fine by them because he wasn’t nearly as irritating as the local Catholic priest. Jim didn’t lecture them on their wrongdoings with God and how they were going to burn in hell. Not to say he wasn’t a devout Catholic our Jim, no, no. He was born and bred on the prayers of his Father in heaven and it kept him straight from morn till night. He was a contented man with simple, down to earth values and was loyal as a bloodhound. He was a shining example of how people can be. I always tried to aspire to him. Just like every night, he was counselling another man for the evening. This time it was Barry Kennison. Barry was one of those who stuck so rigid to the rules of the bible, his nob was going to fall off from lack of use. And it wasn’t because he was ugly or anything, not by a long shot. Barry was about 21 years of age, right in the ripe age for all the frat girls. He had a mane of brown wavy hair, not overly long, but covering his ears. Golden brown eyes added to this and his complexion was spotless. He looked as innocent as the day he was born and in truth, he was. I couldn’t resist from riling up the kid now and again ever since I got back, slapping down a couple of ‘coarse mutterings’ as he called them. That only urged me on all the more. Anyway, currently he was being given a sound talking to from Jim so I moved along the bar, noticing Dan at the heart of a group of lads, most of them much younger than myself, listening in awe as O’Grady retold one of his old wrestling tales. As I walked nearer with my Guinness I caught the end of the story… ‘….so in the end, I blasted him right in the nuts!’ The whole circle burst into a cornucopia of laughter, illuminating the bar with a vibrancy that had been missing in my life for a long time. It felt good… …but then passed just as quickly, remembering why it had gone. This soured my demeanour and I resolved to settle into a corner of the bar, sipping away at my pint and relax the body after its workout in my return match. It hadn’t been so bad, in truth it was relatively easy considering his size. I expected this Titan to try and use it against me, but in reality he used it only to backfire against himself. And when he went up for the Fall from Grace…I could see the shocked faces, watching me control the 300+ pound man between my arms, high up in the air, and they gazed with awe and wonder as he spiralled downwards into the piledriver finish. It had the desired effect. It always does. The disrespect however was simply uncalled for. I had done nothing to annoy or irritate these people, in fact, until Dan had introduced them to me, I didn’t even know who they were! And when I was introduced, they were so sycophantic and pathetic. It made me sick. To see Pearl Harber telling me all the plans that they’d have for a former OSW wrestler was cringe worthy and the way in which it was promptly forgotten even more annoying. Got me pissed off in fact, though that did help for the match in the end. And not only just then, because I had my impetus now, I had my objective… I was going to prove to Pearl Harber what a mistake he’d made in mistreating me. He felt I was washed up, a nostalgic act, somebody who could be used for a bit of money to build up one of his protégés, give them a name to slap on their resume to make them more than just any other wrestler. I was nothing more than a jobber for him, but he didn’t count on me winning, much less destroying someone bigger and heavier than myself. That was step one, but the real work was ahead. I had to take no prisoners, no exceptions along my path to that Heavyweight belt. Nothing else would suffice. I needed it to show him how wrong he was. How wrong everyone was. When I entered the locker room, I could feel that no one was interested in having me there. They all felt I was below them, an older man implanted purely so that they could all get their own spotlight, no matter how fleeting it was. I expendable. At this point in time I felt a nudge from my shoulder and I looked up. It was Jim, cleaning a glass (as every bar man on the planet seems to do to pass the time) and giving me his plain, but genuine smile. ‘Something wrong Jack?’ ‘No, nothing.’ ‘Now come on Jack, I know you and that cries out you’re lying, so what’s wrong?’ ‘Just…the perception of the other guys in AWC. Total disrespect.’ ‘Ah…so they think you can’t go?’ ‘Yeah, that’s what it felt like to me.’ ‘Well Jack, you know better than I do how to deal with that.’ Without another word Jim left the area and returned to serving customers. You see that’s the way with Jim. He doesn’t waste time by probing around the subject too much, he gets right at the core. Why take forever to explain something that can be described in two lines? It’s part of why everyone liked Jim, his frank honesty and critical eye. It’s certainly why I liked him. So I was resolved…they’d all be shown up by me… …plain and simple.
