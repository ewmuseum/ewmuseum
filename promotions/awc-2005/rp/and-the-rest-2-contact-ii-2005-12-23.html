And The Rest #2: 'Contact, II'

23 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23rd December 2005 “Tell me everything,” I said urgently, slamming the door behind me and throwing my jacket aside. “Kate?” 22nd December 2005 14:39 I looked up. She was sitting on the edge of her bed, shoulders hunched, hands between her thighs, face red and streaky as if she’d been crying. She hadn’t even dressed, and I tried not to notice how thin the material of her faded pink t-shirt was, tried not to look down at the milky-white of her legs inadequately protected by her white cotton knickers. “Kate, how long ago did he call?” I asked curiously. I had assumed she’d called me right after she had received the call from Tony Aliso, the man whose life and non-death had dominated our lives for the past year, but there was no way she would have remained in the clothes she slept in until past midday on a normal day. She shook her head and chewed a little on her bottom lip. “He didn’t – call, Tim,” she began quietly, “he was – here.” My eyes almost shot out of my skull, and she impatiently dabbed at her eyes with the back of her hand. At least she hadn’t made herself up this morning, so there was no mascara to worry about running. “Tony was here? In this apartment?” I asked in a daze. Kate nodded her pretty little head. I shot over to the bed and sat down beside her; she at last seemed to notice she wasn’t wearing very much and flushed a little but I was way beyond that by now. “Kate, he can’t have –” “He was here, alright?” she insisted with a strangled shout, and turned over to bury her face in her pillow. Such was my unease, I didn’t even notice the way her pert bottom sat up above the run of her thighs, or how her t-shirt had ridden up so that a glorious half of her lower back was open for observation. “I'm – I'm sorry, Kate, it’s just – why didn’t you tell me earlier?” I lamented, staring intently at the bare white of her bedroom wall. An inaudible groan came from beneath the pillow, and then she rolled over onto her back, arms behind her head, tousled hair everywhere. Navel warning! Nipple bulge! But I wasn’t even looking, damn it. Long would I rue missing out so badly on this day. Never would I previously have thought anything could be more important than the chance to see Kate Barnard like this, but when I was in the same apartment a wanted man had visited only this morning – a wanted man with his murderous eyes set on me – these things fell away like lamb from the bone. “I was thinking,” she said. That confused me. And then I realised I hadn’t told her all that she needed to know. I turned to face her, locking onto her warm, innocent, frightened eyes to shoot my homing missiles of revelation. “Kate,” I said gently, “Tony is wanted for attempted murder.” She stared at me. “No...” Kate whispered to herself. “Yes,” I insisted, “yes, he is. I know it’s hard to believe, but... I saw him with my own eyes. It’s been –” I paused. No words could describe how awful the last few weeks had been for me. “It’s been difficult.” The splendour of understatement. “I don’t... he can’t...” I hadn’t prepared for this. It hadn’t even crossed my mind that Kate wasn’t aware of the latest happenings of the Tony Aliso saga. I’d become so insular ever since the bomb scare that I hadn’t even thought to inform the girl to whom it would mean the most. “Kate,” and I involuntarily put a hand on her knee, and then realised what I’d done, and flinched, but then left it there, because on reflection it just seemed the right thing to do, “Tony tried to murder me.” Here followed the most intense moment of my entire life. Kate stared. What was she thinking? How was she thinking? How could she process that the gentlemanly, kind-hearted, fun-loving Californian with whom she had shared a bed back at my birthday party in February, had tried to kill his friend? Impossible. But then, how had she dealt with the knowledge that he had faked his own death two months after that very episode? A dose of the same medicine was required to swallow this bitter pill. I fervently hoped that Kate’s mind was open to something this shocking. It wasn’t. “Oh, Tim, that’s pathetic and sick,” she told my shocked self with a look of scorn as she pushed away my hand roughly and stood up, walking to the door. “I can’t believe you. At a moment like this, a moment so special to me and a moment that I thought would mean a lot to you too, when Tony’s finally got back in touch, when we’ve got a chance to find out from him why he did what he did... you let adolescent jealousy creep in and you make up the BIGGEST load of BULLSHIT that I have ever heard! You’re disgusting, Tim Shipley... disgusting and obsessed. Don’t think I haven’t noticed you staring at me. Can’t you appreciate that I'm just a little emotional at the moment? Can’t you appreciate that I just need someone to talk to, straight and sincere, someone who knew Tony and someone who WANTS to know him again? If you’re not going to be that person for me, Tim, then –” She began to open the door as I broke into her speech, standing up from the bed. “Kate,” I breathed, mystified, “what happened this morning?” She rolled her eyes and this was when she really began to shock me. “For FUCK’s sake, Tim Shipley! You can NEVER HAVE ME! Now get the FUCK over it and get the FUCK out!” “KATE!” I yelled, taking her arms. “Will you PLEASE get off this anger trip and just LISTEN!” “No, I will not fucking listen to you,” she spat defiantly, shaking me off. “It’s none of your business –” “Kate, am I hearing this right?” I shouted back, shaky. “He’s on the run, Kate, he’s –” “YES, if you must know, we had SEX, Tim! That’s what people who like each other DO! Like each other. But I don’t like you, Tim, you’re a fucking stalker and if you don’t get out...” She broke off and stormed over to her window, peering out of it. I slumped onto the floor, landing heavily on my knees, my head in my hands. Never before had I seen her like this. Timid Kate Barnard, yelling like there was no tomorrow, throwing fucks around like it was a competition. “Kate,” I muttered through my teeth, “you have got to listen to me...” But listening, she was not. “Are they to do with you, too?” she demanded, jabbing a finger at the window as she turned to me. “Are they part of your stalker enterprise, with their long-lensed cameras, told to wait all day until I get naked so you can touch yourself all n-” She stopped as I scrambled to my feet and pushed roughly past her. “Who? Where?” She prodded the window, annoyed, and I saw a black car, with two men in the front seat. One had his hands over the wheel, leaning back in his reclined seat as if asleep. The other manned the very camera Kate had been referring to, and it was trained on... “This window,” I whispered, then turned to Kate, horrified. “How long?” “What?” she gasped, turning white. “How long, Kate! How long’ve they been there!” As I spoke, I was fumbling for the blinds with my hands, but they kept closing on thin air. I looked up to find no material to cover the window. “No blinds, Kate!” She shook her head, numb. I grabbed her arms and shook her. “Kate, you’ve got to tell me how long they’ve been watching you!” “A – a few days – Monday they came – or Tuesday, maybe –” “Why haven’t you called the police?” As I spoke, I was striding across the room to her telephone. “I... I didn’t think it was...” Nine. One. One. “Kate, you know who they are? They’re working for Tony. They’re keeping a watch on you. Why, I don’t know –” “Who are you calling?” she demanded suddenly, bounding over to me. I held the receiver away from her. “The police,” I said firmly. “To tell them where Tony Aliso was last seen. To tell them his men are watching your apartment.” “Give me that!” she cried, reaching for the phone. I held out an arm to stop her, inadvertently grabbing her breast. “Get OFF!” she yelled as she jumped for the receiver. I adjusted my arm to slip around her stomach, stopping her from reaching. “Kate, I have to, it’s for your own – AAARGHHH!” The scream was mine as she slapped me, hard. Momentarily dazed, I let Kate grab the phone out of my hand and slam it back into its dock. “You have got some fucking nerve!” she shrieked. “And if your filthy hands ever touch me again, I swear to God I will –” “KATE!” I shouted at the top of my voice, and this stopped her mid-sentence. “LOOK at me!” I pleaded. “My fucking LIFE is in danger! Tony planted a bomb in a briefcase I was supposed to open on an AWC show! If I’d opened the case, the whole place would’ve blown up! But at the last moment I saw a wire and the bomb was defused! I'm alive only because of that! But now I'm thinking maybe I’d have been better off dead, if only to make you believe what I'm telling you! If you don’t listen to me, Kate, noticing that wire will only have delayed the inevitable! Tony’s a wanted man, he’s on the run and he wants me dead! If you tell me what you know, the police might be able to stop him before it’s too –” The words died in my throat. “Too late...” I whispered, and staggered sideways onto her bed, burying my head in my shoulder as I sobbed furiously. The tears were half-real. This was the last card of my hand. The swearing, the crying – very much out-of-character for the Tim Shipley she thought she knew. If this didn’t work, nothing would. But as I semi-sobbed, I semi-noticed the edges of a pink card poking out from beneath her pillow. I stopped crying, grabbed it and pulled it out. It was hand-made, for crying out loud. The message was inside, and as I read it, I knew I’d lost. My final card was no match for Aliso’s trump. Dear Kate, it read, I'm so sorry I’ve been gone for so long. I’ve missed you more with every sunset.... And there followed A FUCKING POEM. “Get out,” she told me wearily. And this time, I did so. Later, I would call the police, but with only sketchy guesswork to describe what had gone on this morning, they’d barely give my story any credence. After the next day’s Winter Warfare show, I would have to go into hiding, and organise some kind of protection for Kate – but without her knowledge, of course... and the downside was, that way, she might think it another surveillance team. But if it was for her own good, then so be it, I’d do it. That was my resolve as of Thursday afternoon. As the shrill scream of my telephone woke me in the early hours of Friday, everything changed. “Timothy Shipley?” “Who is this?” bed breath, bad breath, need some water, need more sleep “It’s the Albany police department. I'm afraid we have some bad news. We’ll also need to question you.” words swimming around my head, but what are they talking about? water “Okay... what is it?” maybe they mean “I'm afraid it’s Katharine Barnard, sir. She’s been kidnapped. By Tony Aliso, we suspect.” oh my god oh my god oh my god “How – how do you – know?” this cannot be happening “We also have reason to believe her parents are being held. They haven’t been seen for over twenty-four hours and the neighbours haven’t a clue about a reason. Would you know of one?” who gives a fuck about the parents “N-no, no, no idea. What happens now?” it’s all my fault it’s all her fault it’s all my fault it’s all her fault “We play his game.” “What do you mean, officer?” “He left a note.” “What does it say?” He took a deep breath. “This line isn’t secure –” “Fuck that, officer, what did he say?” “We can’t quite work out what he means...” “Tell me what the note says. Please.” “Well – it’s why we called you. The first part refers to a ‘TS’. I suppose that’s you?” I said nothing. “The note goes like this: ‘TS will hear from me at WW. T.’” “WW?” oh fuck shit oh fuck shit “Any ideas?” “Yes. Winter Warfare.” A sick dread flooded my stomach. “What’s that?” “A wrestling show. Tomorrow.” “We’ll be there.” I couldn’t think of what to say to that, so I didn’t. Nor did he. Click. I didn’t sleep again that night.
