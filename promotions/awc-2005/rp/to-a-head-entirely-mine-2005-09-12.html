To a head entirely mine.

12 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: past/present Shaunna told me not to read that book before we left. Shy didn’t imply anything like she usually does. No, she didn’t go “I wouldn’t do that if I were you,” as I’ve come to expect from her in the time we’ve been acquainted. Instead she says it bluntly. There’s no mystery anymore, only the impending feeling of doom lurking just ahead. “Do not read that book.” It’s an order. But I always have to go and cheat. Shaunna tells me that this book contains information that one would need to learn in time; that someone learning all of it once is particularly dangerous. Between all the metaphoric and passive bullshit that comes out of her mouth, that’s exactly what she said. She didn’t tell me anything more than that. So I’m left to think “well maybe it’s because I’m so immature and I won’t understand it.” I begin to sell myself short in terms of my approach in general life. i’m too pale—i can’t finish anything—my athlete’s foot will never go away—why do all cats hiss at me? This kind of reasoning explained what I read last time; the whole passive wording that made me feel as if I were reading Nostradamus. I didn’t go to law school; how the fuck am I supposed to understand paralegal bullshit speak? It always reminded me of Satan trying to take my soul through his loopholes and pretty words. The words are literal and/or metaphorical and have context between and all that subliminal bullshit blah blah blah—it makes me sick! Being predictable ole’ me, I get very insecure. I AM very insecure. So once that thought is established, my mind starts nurturing the thought that Shaunna is selling me short. She thinks I’m stupid, I begin to think. “Well fuck her, fucking I’m not fucking stupid.” Consequently I begin tuning her out. I block her out of my brain and I hide these memories in the recesses so she can’t find them. Forget her warnings if she thinks I’m stupid—well, I thought she thought. I’ll show her, I’m thinking, but that’s never done me any good. I’m thinking she doesn’t know what she’s talking about. She’s a ghost, she’s not real. How can this thing, that exists in an entirely different reality, comprehend my measly choices in my mediocre and highly unimportant life? It’s actually laughable, right? Now that I’ve explained to you all my train of thought, you can understand how easily I came to throwing aside her advice? Well, take this as a lesson; DON’T PAY ANY AfuckingTTENTION TO WHAT I SAY/THINK/DO. Because you’re only taking this advice if you’re like me. Because if you’re like me, you won’t listen to the ghost either. LISTEN TO THE GHOST. Because when she tells you not to do something, it usually is for your own good. Because if you’re taking this advice, you’re like me, and if you’re like me you hate being confused. It’s an incurable itch. It’s like that Steve Urkel jigsaw puzzle you’ve had for eighteen years that you’ve never been quite able to finish. Only you can’t easily blame your hang-ups on a couple of missing corner pieces; these are consequences you’re going to face. I cheated when I shouldn’t have, and I think I’m learning the hard-way why they call it CHEATING. It’s a negative connotation, or whatever a connotation is. But when I see Eddy that impending sense of doom returns. This fucking book says something about him. At least I think it’s about him, and it makes me think I should tell him. Ah hah, have you been paying attention? If you have been, what’s the most satisfactory action to take when you’re me? I should re-word that… what would be the best course to take, to insure that I don’t fuck up anything that I have going? I do exactly the opposite of what I have been doing. I stop thinking. I stop doing. Eddy could be in trouble? I won’t entertain the thought. Because if I do he most likely will be placed in that situation; like I cause it to happen by my own worries. So I’ll just let Eddy be. I’ll let Eddy continue telling me his story, and I’ll ignore everything that I may have learned. Because when I think about things, it causes trouble. In case you haven’t caught on to that fact by now: it is very true. Did I already mention that Eddy’s a good storyteller? “Why are you asking so many questions? You were standing right next to me.” Eddy says in one corner of his wrestling ring, tightening the post with one hand while the other pulled the top-rope back as tight as he could. One hand kept twisting, the other hand was slowly being pulled back from Eddy as the resistance was modified. The Face-Eater’s eyes widened for a second, as he had never seen anyone tend to the ring like that. He never actually considered the aspects outside of the people in terms of wrestling, or utilizing your environment to your advantage. “What are you doing?” he asked. “I’m maintaining my ring; I like to keep everything in top shape.” They don’t call it the Estrada Institute for nothing. Eddy releases his ‘tightening’ hand, using both hands to jerk the rope as hard as he could. Finding a suitable resistance, Eddy nods in confirmation. “So tell me, why all the questions? You forget what happened?” “Exactly.” The Facey says. “That’s exactly what happened—you see, I have this… I don’t want to call it a condition. But let’s just say that in the heat of the moment, I tend to… to lose focus.” Face-Eater had a rough time categorizing his situation. “No shit.” Eddy was obviously referencing his own encounter with Facey’s “condition,” having to snap him back in to their situation weeks prior. “Well, I can’t tell you that you missed out on much.” you’re right. nothing important about being hunted in a fucking church; especially when who’s ever out to get you is “out to chop your fucking sack off”, as you put it—right? “We tried to break in to Josiah’s makeshift stronghold because they stole something from me.” He finally mentions. “It didn’t work out, we didn’t get what I wanted.” “What did he steal from you?” He began to walk away from Facey. His body language said he was avoiding the question, but truthfully he just needed to tighten the rope on the other side of the turnbuckle. So Facey followed him around the ring from the outside. “What’d they steal, Eddy?” “He stole my book.” four months of training and three months on the road later… september 15th, 2005 and we’re back in Gary On a quiet night, in a dark city, on a dark street; there sits a white car. A single white car that parks idly on the side of a road, sitting brightly under the only operating streetlight on the entire block. Every car that sits in their houses driveways all pale in comparison to this Luxury Sedan. In essence, the car is a fish out of water. Someone occupies the passenger seat of the car, their figure too hard to distinguish on the outside due to tinted windows. While the man on the inside waits patiently, the car’s engine hums incessantly with occasional revs in the RPM to sustain power. It’s almost as if the car was anxious. That is, until its predictable cries die in the darkness when the sudden and precise succession of footsteps begin towards the car. The building these steps come from, is the backside of a locally owned and operated Pizza-bar going by the name of Eatza Pizza Buffet (particularly known for a tomato sauce comparable to ketchup and a recipe for dough where the main ingredient is cardboard). The pacing of the footsteps that walk towards the car is perfect in timing, as if the man had been stepping to music in his head. Aside from serving shitty pizza by the shovel-load to the trailer-dominant inhabitants of the area, Eatza Pizza was also known to deliver shitty pizza to those to lazy to leave their homes (we hear that’s more common these days). Though he wore no headphones, he continued clad in a green windbreaker jacket, with the words “Eatza Pizza Delivery” embroidered on the back, as well as across the front of his green hat. He carries a green bag, specifically to tailor pizzas, that has the same decal of that company. He also doesn’t look very pleased. The Pizza guy takes his bag and opens the backseat of the purring car, setting it down safely. He shuts the back door, turns to the front and enters the driver’s seat. “I fucking hate my job.” He mutters upon shutting himself inside. Immediately, the aroma of mozzarella, baked bread and marinara paints a smile on the passenger’s face but does not prevent him from antagonizing his friend. “Stop whining. You get paid a grip.” “Fuck the money, its bullshit!” the driver says. “I’m paid to be a lackey.” The passenger laughs at this notion. “You think it’s funny? You think work like this makes me happy?” “I never said that.” The passenger says in defense. “I don’t enjoy what I do, and I’ve done it for too long.” The Pizza guy looks up, noticing the visor of his hat obstruct his vision. Angrily, he jerks his hat off of his head and throws it in the back seat, making the passenger laugh at his ridiculous demeanor. “And now they’ve got me dressing like a fucking tool, Jesus. And do you even think they’re paying me enough to do what I do? Do you think that I deserve what I get paid?” “You get paid a considerable amount.” The passenger justifies. “It still doesn’t cover the risks that I take. Just look at what I do: they may pay me a quite a bit but it doesn’t cover how many resources I exhaust.” The Pizza guy smacked his steering wheel then quickly jerked the car around a right corner with a sharp turn, slamming the passenger towards the middle of the car. “And what’s worse is that I’m so old, been doing this for so long, that I’m stuck in this cycle that I’m never going to break out of. I’m stuck to doing jobs like this, and it isn’t fair.” “You should be thankful that you had this opportunity.” The passenger tried his hand at reason, which was lost on the Pizza guy in his self-loathing state. “Anyone can get a job like this, you just have to look.” Then the passenger couldn’t contain what he thought was funny. “You do look rather ridiculous.” He mentioned. The Pizza guy snarled. “And its all hilarious, right? Its all a big joke? Don’t lose sight of the big picture, man. I may hate my job, but I take it very seriously.” The car turned one final corner, the driver taking a more sensible approach this time. The passenger was able to tell his stop was ahead, and found it redundant and useless when the Pizza guy had announced “We’re here.” He shuts off the car and turns to the passenger for word. “Get low, if my bosses find out I had someone with me in the car while I was working…” “I understand bro.” the passenger said very empathetically, and shrank in his seat to the point where he was below the windows. The Pizza guy nods to his friend. “I’m too old for this shit.” He forces out of his mouth and steps out the car. Searching the backseat for his hat, he securely places it on his head then withdraws the Pizza bag as well. The passenger waits for him while the Pizza man strolls up the sidewalk. He walks to the front of the building and knocks on the glass. The door shuffles a bit and gives indication of being locked. The Pizza guy turns his head and scans the street-front, surprised to find that no one is around. He finds this odd, but smiles to himself a bit. Rather suddenly, Eddy Estrada shuffles a bit behind the tinted glass and finally manages to open the locked door. “Can I help you?” “Yes,” the Pizza guy begins. “I have a delivery for Mr. Edward Estrada.” “No one here ordered any pizza.” Eddy pokes his head out the door, giving a quick glance at the white car again sits idly. He doesn’t examine closely enough to notice the passenger. “Well, are you sure there isn’t anyone else here that ordered the pizza?” The Pizza guy tries to reason. “Look, I’m the only one here. My name’s Eddy Estrada and I didn’t order any pizza. I think someone’s fucking with you, homeboy. “Oh no, sir, I just realized that I made a mistake.” The pizza guy opens the bag and looks inside. “I don’t have any pizza for you.” Eddy leans in with support of the doorframe and notices the light frame of the pizza bag. But it’s too late, as the Pizza Deliver guy has since withdrawn a Colt .44, and placed it right to Edward’s chest. “But someone ordered this.” Eddy Estrada didn’t hear much after that. two days later, back behind the mask… Three weeks, baby. Three weeks in a row I’ve whooped Tim Shipley’s ass in combat. This alone more than makes up for what happened at the Battle of Britain with that twat Educator. And now they’re finally restoring balance to the universe in giving me my rightful Frontier title shot, which is starting to make me think things are coming around. Its almost like its proof; that douche bags like Shipley aren’t the only talentless hacks that can become famous in the blink of an eye. I’ve proved that those undeserving, paper-nosed pansies aren’t the only way to make a splash. I think that I’ve noticed, over these past few months… you get famous by being yourself. I’ve stayed focus on Shipley, but that doesn’t mean I don’t come across a few fags in my day. Too many of these kids are so hell bent on being liked by everyone who has an opinion. My approach was different: I wanted the attention of one person, and one person only. It took me a while, but I got it. And now that I have it, I haven’t this much fun since high school. The giddiness hasn’t really gone away yet. This time around, it’s so much more successful. But it isn’t over yet, oh hell no, not by a long shot. Tim Shipley is so ignorant, he still doesn’t know. I’m going to keep this game up until the signs pound my fucking name over and over and over in his brain. By the time he knows, he’ll be suicidal for not realizing it earlier. And then, my mission will be complete. Summer’s Son will be nothing but an afterthought of a shitty season, and my reign will continue. I’ll be the Frontier Champion, because we all know Andy M. ain’t shit. And then, I’ll be Transatlantic Champion; the first true competitor to hold both titles, because we also know that Educator is a twat. My plan is only starting to unfold, and by the time this mask comes off, it’ll only be a matter of time until—ring ring ring ring ring How rude. I hate interruptions. So why should I answer? I don’t recognize the number… hmmm, it may be important. Hel-SCHTZZZZZZKRRRRRRRRRRRRRK FUCK, I HATE STATIC! HELLO!?!? Yes, this is he. What? What? What? . .. … …. …..What? Freddie Prinze Sr. is in debt? Freddie Prinze Sr. has fled? OH! Freddie Prinze Sr. is DEAD! Um… okay, thanks?! So I hang up. What the fuck was that about? I couldn’t understand half the words he said. And didn’t Freddie Prinze’s dad die, like, in the 80’s? Maybe they meant his son died, which is horrible news for the production of “She’s STILL All That. Hmm, I should google it and find out if it’s true. Or I’ll just call static-asshole back tomorrow and find out. Hmmm, I sleepy.
