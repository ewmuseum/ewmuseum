A Mutiliated Victory

1 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/8/05 Towards the end of World War I, the Italian army won the key battle of Vittorio Veneto, which provided patriots with their glorious victory in war. The Italian citizens were in hysterical delight and came to expect great rewards following the war’s conclusion. These people became furious upon the allegedly unsatisfactory peace terms which Italian politicians secured at the Paris Peace Conference. This peace was seen as a betrayal of the victory of Vittorio Veneto and those whose blood secured it. Although Italy made more territorial gains (of around 14,500 square kilometres) than any other of the victorious nations and although this was in line with what was promised to Italy at the 1915 Treaty of London, Italians wanted more. Inspired by the exaggerations of Nationalists who made the Italians victory more significant than what it really was, ordinary citizens took to the streets to demand from their leaders their action in taking their ‘full reward’. The politicians went back to the Allies and demanded a share in the former German empire in Africa, which was being divided up. They didn’t receive any more land. Italians felt the Allies had been treacherous and that their achievement in the war had been a mutilated victory. But they couldn’t do anything about it. 1. A Predicament – One Week Earlier The scene opens in a hotel room that is being inhabited by Paddy O’Shea and his father Danny. The room is wallpapered with a reassuring peach glow that was put in place to make the most stubborn of moods be reciprocated. But this vibrant colour is having little effect on the two figures. The floor in the room is made up strips of wood of approximately four feet each in length. They are unvarnished and very smooth and work well with the ambiance of the room. There are two single beds in the room, both of which are crowned by duvets of a red and orange patchwork design. The various pictures of flowers and actual vases of flowers add to the apparent sophistication at hand. Danny O’Shea is sitting on the bed completely straight. He is wearing only a pair of pyjama trousers currently and his bare toes seem to be the only part of his body in motion as they dig incessantly through the cream fluff of the mat. His hair is salt-and-peppered and is also receding. Instead of shaving it bald however, Danny likes to keep it to a length of around two inches. This is in contrast to Paddy however. His hair is a few inches shy of shoulder length and a blur of blond-brownness. Their faces however are near identical apart from the damage Danny has taken from time. Paddy is on one knee on the floor with a wetted cloth in one hand. Completely concentrated on the job at hand, Paddy is dabbing the many cuts and wounds littering Danny’s body. Only now as the camera turns around and the scene becomes more illuminated can we see how different the two actually are in appearance at this time. Danny is the owner of a black eye on one side, a fat lip, various bruises that mainly situate around his ribs and what appear to be stab wounds in less lethal positions such as the sides of his abdomen and in the crook of his arm. He is unbandaged. Danny too is concentrating and is staring fixedly at the wall, only wincing when the pain escapes from him. “Ye can shout if it helps,” Paddy says a little perturbed. He takes a glance up to meet Danny’s eyes but he never takes them out of the stare. “Aye’m fine.” Danny whispers. Ever since Hate’s plans to kill Danny had failed and Paddy had managed to get him to safety, there had been relief in Danny’s eyes. But there had also been madness and Paddy could see it as plain as day, dancing around now, yellowing the whites of his eyes. Danny general cool attitude was also annoying Paddy. He wouldn’t tell him what had happened or his plans. As ignorant as the old days he had thought. “Ye do understand why aye can’ get ye a doctor don’ ye?” Paddy asked in a voice that suggested he’d said it a hundred times before. “No Paddy…actually aye don’t.” Danny looked down at Paddy’s face for a few seconds. He seemed to be challenging Paddy and this really started to get under Paddy’s skin. He’d spent almost a month trying to rescue his father. He’d had ribs broken. A mashed face and almost a fractured skull. And he’d done it for this man in front of him that hadn’t even had the courtesy to show his gratitude for it yet. “Because they’ll want t’ put ye into hospital and they’ll keep records. Hate will be after ye again. Aye didn’t spent all that time just for ye t’ be caught again…Aye’ll tend to yer wounds and ye’ll be fine. The room fell into silence once more apart from the patting of the cloth on Danny’s skin. Paddy realised he’d raised his voice a little higher than he’d intended but refused to apologise for it. This was reinforced when Paddy was reminded of the years of alcoholism of Danny O’Shea and how much bearing that had on what Paddy was today. It was Danny who was the first to break the stalemate. “Look son…thank ye. Aye should have said tha’ t’ ye two days ago ye got me rescued fer feck’s sake. It’s just…aye’m worried.” Danny voice trailed off as he said this. “Worried about what?” “About wha’ this man is goin’ t’ do next.” Danny looked at his son and this time his eyes were desperate. This was him with his thoughts unclothed, standing naked for Paddy to see. This wasn’t the way father-son relationships worked thought Paddy. His father was the one who was supposed to comfort him. Paddy hesitated for a moment and then forced a small smile. “Tha’ be nothing t’ worry about fer feck’s sake! The family has already gone into hiding in the North and we’ll join them tomorrow. We’ll lay low fer a while until police have enough evidence to arrest Hate then-“ “And wha’ if they can’t arrest him?” Danny’s voice was strong and firm. “What do ye mean? Of course he’ll be arrested. Remember attempted murder?” “Paddy, this man was able t’ kidnap me and t’ get ye in tha’ hospital in a way tha’ even the police don’ know. If this man didn’t want t’ be arrested, he wouldn’ be.” Paddy stayed silent and put his head down slightly. “An’ wha’ about wrestling then?” Paddy’s head shot up immediately. “Wha’ about it? Feck it!!” Paddy’s voice seemed a little startled at the question. “Feck it?” Danny’s shouted at Paddy. Paddy fell back onto his buttocks in surprise. “Yer gonnae run away like a cat from dog are ye? Yer gonnae let some sick bastard run ye out of the only job ye’ve ever been good at? Yer gonnae let him win are ye? EH? Paddy stayed silent. Danny was standing over the top of him. For a man that had been severely weakened by the poison of alcohol, Danny had never looked stronger. For an instant Paddy wondered how Hate had managed to take this proud man and wondered if these wounds were testament to Danny resilience to give in to Hate’s interrogation. Then Danny sat back down as the anger drained from his face and Paddy remembered his state. “Well wha’ would ye have me do then?” Paddy asked. Danny coughed heavily. His shouting extravaganza had apparently taken it out of him and Paddy had to wait a few more seconds for an answer. “Aye would have ye act like a man. Like an O’Shea. Aye would have ye stand face t’ face with this man called Hate. Do ye have faith Paddy? In God aye mean” “O’ course” “Well use tha’ faith because tha’ man has the devil on his side. If ye have faith in God and yerself and ye act like a man then ye’ll be untouchable to him. This man will always be able t’ get us but if ye make a stand against him and cut off the source, ye’ll beat him.” “He’ll never be able to find us.” Paddy said and let out a small chuckle. Their was no joyous laughter in that though. Suddenly an envelope slid under the door. The unmistakable noise caused Paddy to spring to his feet in shock. He paced over to the door cautiously then picked up the letter. The envelope was inscribed with his name on it. Paddy glanced at his father who was silent but obviously interested. Paddy tore it open quickly and pulled a small note. Whenever…wherever 2. A Man’s Limit Paddy was about twenty-five at the time he reckons although he was actually only twenty-seven. He does remember it clearly as the seventh of November however because the event was two days after his birthday. He was in Dublin at the time, doing a delivery for his cousin although he can’t recall what he was delivering. He remembers unloading the large sealed boxes onto a flat trolley when he heard a voice hissing into his ear, the spittle moistening the skin. “Hello…pikie.” Paddy turned around so fast he thought he neck had broken. However the pain he felt came from a fist, entwined with a small chain, sinking deep into cheek. He found it difficult to talk and knew instantly something had broken. “Nice country you’ve got here gypsy. Pity about the Irish in it.” Paddy was slumped on the ground now, his head looking directly down. He looked up at who had done this to him and all confusion was instantly cleared away. Skinheads, three of them. He knew their type, he’d seen them on television a few months ago in Brussels rioting over some human rights law. And now of course another European Law was being passed here in Dublin and of course they would be here…how could he have been so stupid. They all wore similar attire; those puffy black coats with Neo-Nazi insignia and those massive steel toe-capped boots. He knew they were steel toe-capped because one had just hit him in the rib and the pain was excruciating. Scarier for Paddy was the tattoos they had that littered their baldheads. ‘Skinhead’ and ‘Death To Blacks’ and one even had a picture of a skull on his forehead. They looked so similar to Paddy as if they were copies of each other. They also had unshaven facial hair and large horrifying eyes. It was their eyes that scared Paddy most for inside them he saw the concealed anger. Anger he couldn’t understand. “So what’s in the box then”, the voice said, he recognised the English accent. He never was too fond of the English but he wasn’t used to this treatment from them, especially in his own country. “Ye can’ have it. It be fer me cousin.” Even Paddy knew how timid his voice sounded in his own ears “Is that a fact,” the apparent leader of the three said. A large grin spread slowly across his face and his eyes widened. Then suddenly the grin disappeared and he bore his teeth like a snarling dog. “Well if we can’t have them…no one can…burn the bastard.” For a while Paddy thought they were going to burn him but he was proved mistaken. One of the skinheads grabbed Paddy and lifted him to his feet. Paddy tried to resist. It was half-hearted and he knew it but he still received a ferocious head butt to the nose for his troubles. “Stay still gypsy or I swear I’ll cut you up.” Paddy couldn’t resist now if he wanted to. He slumped onto the figure holding him in a bear hug and allowed himself to be carried about ten metres from the van. Once there he was dropped onto the ground he turned to see the other two figures. The ‘leader’ of the three was walking over to him now smiling. The other man was trying to spark a lighter to light a rag that was hanging out of the fuel spout at the side of the van. Paddy started to shout various curses and received a direct kick to the spine for his troubles. His shouts were immediately turned into howls of pain. “Close your mouth!” “No, no. Let him shout. I want to hear how a man sounds as he sees his livelihood flushed down the toilet,” The leader said without a hint of emotion. He turned around to Paddy and laughed in his face. “This world has no room for gypsy’s. The quicker you people learn that the better.” Just as the man finished talking to Paddy the van exploded. Paddy thought his eardrums had exploded too because the noise was deafening. He turned around to see the other man dancing like a maniac around the blazing inferno. All he could see was the chassis being blackened by the engulfing flames. There were various parts of the van that had been blasted off in the explosion including a door and the bonnet, which had landed only two metres from where he lay. “Now for you,” The leader said and he brandished a switchblade. But Paddy wasn’t scared now, he was absolutely furious. He felt his breath thicken and his eyes bulged out of his head. He felt himself redden and the adrenaline soar through his body. He began to kick wildly where he lay, swinging around like a madman. One kick actually caused the leader to drop the blade, which Paddy noted with acute accuracy. Of course, for his little rampage, he received a flurry of boots to his unguarded body. He felt a rib crack and blood begin to pour down his forehead and sting his eyes but he kept this up like a madman. Then there was the sound of sirens “How did they get here so fast?” the leader cursed. There was annoyance and fear in his voice now and Paddy noticed this. The two other men jumped back from kicking Paddy and looked at their leader with the same scared, deer eyes. “Let’s go!” The three began to run off down an alley. The fury however was still with Paddy and he jumped to his feet, grabbing the switchblade in the process and he began to run after them. The blade was inside the casing but he knew with one press of the button, it would come darting out. As Paddy ran after them as fast as he could, he felt immense pain surging through his body. He’d been kicked in some places that would bruise atrociously but his broken ribs were a great burden. Yet Paddy wouldn’t slow down a touch and he ran after them at full pace, fuelled by hate. Then the sound of sirens came very close. He looked down to the end of the alley that opened up into a small car park and saw the three men just exiting. The police were in the car park now but it was only one car and they wouldn’t get them all. So Paddy kept going. He knew he would catch them because the men’s large boots were slowing them down – they weren’t used to running away. “SPLIT UP!” The voice was that of the leaders. Paddy eventually entered into the car park. As he entered, he just caught a glimpse of the police car driving into the Eastern alley after at least one of the skinheads. He looked to the Western alley and was just in time to see the leader disappear into it. He’s goin’ t’ get away Paddy ran for the alley with renewed vigour. The blood was blinding one of his eyes and he felt it dripping from his eyelashes. He also knew he wasn’t too far away from the man as he entered into the alley. And sure enough, there he was with his hands on his knees breathing hard. He hadn’t heard Paddy yet and he was only fifteen yards away. Then he heard the footsteps and he looked up. At first there was fear on his face but that quickly subsided into a smile. “You? What do you want you gypsy?” Paddy didn’t answer him. He just felt the anger of the prejudice and discrimination that he’d received all his life being built up to one. He kept running directly at him and the man stood up on the balls of his feet, ready for the strike. Just before Paddy reached him, he put his hand in his back pocket and pulled out a small claw hammer. He’d been using it just before he was attacked. He swung wildly and it connected with the man’s temple. The man fell to the ground hard and awkwardly. Paddy then stuck his hand into his other pocket and pulled out the switchblade. He would wait till the police came and he’d keep this bastard here by threatening him with this. “Oi…turn around onto yer front.” Paddy said quietly. His voice however was very assertive. The man didn’t move however and as the blood flowing from his temple began to be joined with blood pouring from his mouth, nose and ears, Paddy got scared. He couldn’t be dead…could he? Paddy bent down and placed his hand over the man’s mouth and nose…but there was no breath warming his palm. Then Paddy looked at his feet. They were being stained by the man’s blood - he hadn’t realised how fast it had flown out of his head. That was when Paddy panicked. Run Paddy was away before the idea had even surfaced in his head. Who would believe a gypsy was only defending himself, only trying to bring this man under custody? This wasn’t how a public arrest happened in the movies. So he kept on running until he reached his cousins house. There he told him everything apart from him catching up with the man. A day later, he was home. With a murder on his conscience. 4.
