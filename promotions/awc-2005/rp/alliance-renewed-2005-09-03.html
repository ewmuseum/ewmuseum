Alliance Renewed

3 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3rd September 2005 Tim Shipley was a cautious person. Never one to rush into a battle, it took a lot to make this twenty-year-old angry – visibly, at least; inside his head, conflicting emotions would wage wars day by day, minute by minute, as wrestling, college, girls, lack of girls, friends, lack of friends all battled it out for a spot at the focal point of his attention. But outwardly, he was cautious. And he didn’t like it. It wasn’t often Shipley got to feel like the one calling the shots – especially not in the dog-eat-dog wrestling business, where 300-pound monsters would dwarf his 5’10” frame and frankly scare him with their age-old attitudes to respect, their insistence on a backstage hierarchy, and their smell. Only this Tuesday, though, he’d finally felt able to assert himself in the feud that he didn’t quite understand the reasoning for. The Illustrious Face-Eater’s eccentric violence was growing very old very fast, and Shipley had now found his happy medium: no longer would he try and understand why he had to face this weekly torment from the ridiculously-dressed wrestler, he would simply take each event as it came and deal with it as he saw fit – but assertively. The “run and hide” mentality had been hindering his progress, and with just under a month left on his AWC contract, Tim Shipley had nothing to lose. It was time to ride the adrenaline stream. And so he slammed the Face-Eater against the lockers. Yes, that’s right. Shy, introverted college minor Tim Shipley, with his paper-thin nose and his hideously high IQ, had slammed the Face-Eater against the lockers. And it had felt good. He hadn’t stopped there. Relaxing is not the best thing to do while applying a hammerlock, as the Face-Eater had quickly learnt. A quick spin, an elbow into the temple, and not so Illustrious after all, hmm? Mindless violence was not what Tim Shipley liked about wrestling, but sometimes, he justified to himself, it was necessary. When a man you don’t know with a name you don’t know starts bringing up a past he doesn’t know – it’s necessary. If Tim Shipley’s brain had legs, it would have just kicked itself. You don’t bring up Aliso... not now, not ever... Fortunately, the state of panic could be averted. There was a remedy, a new remedy to help Shipley cope with thinking of his lost friend, and albeit unwittingly, the Face-Eater had provided it. The man who had been given the interestingly poor nickname “Summer’s Son” now leapt off his bed and threw open the wardrobe. His actions furious and his face intense, he crouched down and drew out two identical heavy items from the very bottom. He returned to the springy hotel bed and draped them both across his thighs, the weight and touch of the cold metal plating providing a sense of urgent calm for the oxymoron-ridden Englishman. The Core Wrestling Tag Team championship belts had come into Tim Shipley’s possession last Tuesday in Birmingham, when The Illustrious Face-Eater had viciously assaulted him with them right at the beginning of that night’s edition of Fresh!, the flagship Atlantic Wrestling Club television show. He knew they weren’t his to keep, and he knew that by rights he should return them to Matthew Lanier – but recent encounters with the head of Core Wrestling hadn’t been so cordial. Shipley’s famous or infamous (depending on which promotion you follow) appearance on Pride in late July had worsened rather than healed the rift between the pair, Lanier’s uncaring stance on his (now deceased) former employee Tony Aliso the main reason for this. Shipley wasn’t all that inclined to return the belts, especially seeing as they weren’t being used anymore by the promotion in any shape or form. The Pioneers’ one-day championship reign had heralded the untimely death of the Core Wrestling tag division. He sat there for a length of time. Its exact duration, we cannot say; from Shipley’s unchanging demeanour it could have been anything from ten seconds to ten hours. More likely somewhere in between. Pierce was late, and I wasn’t too happy about it. I’d called this urgent meeting as a result of there having been a little bit more than a grain of truth in Face-Eater’s taunts about a possible forced reform of The Academy. It had turned out that Pearl wanted a high-profile duo who were fan favourites and showed AWC in a good light. Apparently, we were that; strikingly, champions Emerald Isle, or Eire Og, or Emerald Isle II, or – whatever, were not. I didn’t like it and nor did Pierce. But then again, I did like it and so did Pierce. There were pros and cons, as there inevitably tend to be with any situation requiring modern thinking. Philosophy was a fun discipline which I should aim to indulge in at least once a week, I noted. Pierce and I had a funny relationship. Our duo, The Academy, had lasted all of one match – but we had defeated the champions Paddy O’Shea and Crimson O’Malec in that non-title encounter. I blamed myself for the break-up, but equally I blamed Pierce; I was sure Pierce had an equally paradoxical attitude to the whole affair. So now, I didn’t even want to get into the whole affair. We’d do the match, we’d work together well, we’d win, and hopefully we’d become friends again – properly – as a direct or indirect result. And for the first time in my wrestling career, I actually expected to win. High Society? With UWF on the bottom of a serious recent downslide, I didn’t expect much out of the admittedly much-vaunted tag team. It wasn’t self-confidence that was fuelling me, though – goodness knows that arrogance isn’t a Tim Shipley trait. It was confidence in Pierce Lavelle, the Transatlantic champion and my partner – and friend. Almost. A college dropout, with nothing going for him than a dead-end job in Pizza Hut and a dingy rental room in Albany. That was the life of AWC's now top star, Pierce Lavelle. From the beginning he was seen as nothing more than the simpleton, the easier target and he did lack confidence showing up on the AWC doorstep, with nothing more than college wrestling experience. This style, however, proved to be a successful advantage for the young wrestler. Pierce Lavelle was the shy, nice guy that always tried to get on with everyone, but not everyone was as forthcoming as he was. He arrived in the AWC just before their first major pay-per-view, Zero To Hero. He wasn't the most notable name there, amongst stars such as Alexander Strider and Hate, but Pierce Lavelle shocked everyone when he got to the final to face Strider. Although he lost, it didn't matter, because Lavelle and Strider would now be the top two men in the AWC for a while to come. In his defeat, Pierce Lavelle decided to remove his front, his shyness and try to stand up for himself, so he grew in courage and his experience in the ring soon grew and before too long he was back up with the big shots and facing Alex Strider at Solarized. Here, Pierce Lavelle shocked the world and took home the gold, but it wasn't all colours, as Alex Strider’s rough-and-tough mean streak almost put Pierce Lavelle into permanent retirement, but Tim Shipley, who hadn’t been seen since supposedly leaving the business altogether in early April, came to his aid and saved the young champion. This was something Pierce Lavelle felt compelled to accept; he knew he owed Tim Shipley and soon came to Shipley's aid when things began to become a little suspicious around the arena. The relationship Pierce Lavelle had with Tim Shipley was something of a base-line friendship – meaning they were quite similar, both were shy and very aware of their surroundings. Shipley, however, had more experience in the ring than Lavelle, and Lavelle looked towards Shipley as a mentor, but the relationship wasn't all peaches and cream and soon, problems and tension began to rise. Old memories and new rivals soon put a great divide between the two young men and this lead Pierce Lavelle toward The Illustrious Face-Eater. He knew, in times of great calamity and hardship, to confront those that cause the mischief and to try to help those around you. Pierce Lavelle had The Illustrious Face-Eater, the wrestler without a name, figured out and backed into a corner and the tension grew. Lavelle came out looking paranoid and delusional, but for the Face-Eater, it was simple mind games. Why is this important? Because it leads to the break-up of The Academy, almost a month ago. Pierce Lavelle always defended his friends, stuck up for those that hadn't done anything wrong, and Lavelle knew Shipley was suffering with all the reminders of Tony Aliso, he who should not be named! But the strains were evident for Lavelle, too. He soon felt the pressure of Shipley's past and decided to face The Illustrious Face-Eater, with the intent of ending the mind games and the hurt, but the Face-Eater’s intentions weren't as noble as his and Lavelle found himself locked into the Breaking Point, Aliso’s old finisher. Shipley froze and Lavelle fell into unconsciousness. The decision to end The Academy was mutual. Now, almost a month on, and Pierce Lavelle having gained the Transatlantic title for a second time, both men have been reunited for one night only to fight in a tag team battle against the men of High Society. Of course, neither man knew of this new development until after AWC's most recent Fresh!. However, even though Pierce Lavelle put on a brave face for his job and those he worked with, deep down in his personal life, things had taken their toll, and now he had become distant and silent to those who most needed him. I was running late, and this time I knew the one reason for it. Fifteen hours ago, I sat in an empty apartment, staring at the hole which used to be my life. Now, I found myself seated in a yellow taxi, asking the driver to rush through traffic and for what? A match, Tim Shipley, or was it to keep my mind distracted from the mental breakdown I was sure to face? I knew Pearl needed me there and I owed him a lot after he allowed me to wrestle Paddy O'Shea and The Educator to regain my championship, despite my severe injuries. I knew Tim Shipley also knew the stakes at which Pearl requested this. He needed the top two fan favourites and in the haste of his plan and the whole UWF invasion issue, Pearl chose Tim and I to face High Society. I guessed we could take them: after all, in our very first tag match, Tim and I beat the previous AWC Alliance champions in a non-title contest, but I knew there would be problems, issues that needed to be resolved, and there was also the fact Tim would kill me for being late... punctuality seemed important to my friend. 'Take a left,' I announced to the taxi driver in front of me, who just sniggered, swerved around a large horse and carriage, managing to cover his wheel in fresh horse dung, and when the driver looked into the rear-view mirror and let out a curse in Italian, it seemed only normal. Despite my haste to rush out of my life back in Albany, I felt relieved to return back to normality, forgetting all that had happened and just to take a breath and concentrate on that which I now had to prepare for. It had been a while since I had spoken to 'Summer’s Son'. I had greeted him in the hall not too long ago, but it was a casual good luck for the pay-per-view; other than that, neither of us spoke. I suppose you could say pride and shyness stood in our way, but I guess neither of us wanted to face the fact we may have been both wrong about everything; to say 'sorry' is always something hard to do, but I thought, to rekindle things and get back on track with life, I could do it. I just hoped he wouldn't bring up the Illustrious Face-Eater issue – it was something I felt angry at Tim for, but with all the new developments in my career and life, I had accepted what had happened and forgotten it, and I just didn't want any heartfelt apologies. 'Hey, Mr, we've arrived,' the cab driver said and slid the Plexiglas separation across, requesting his pay and tip. I was awoken from my daydream to stare into the eyes of a money-hungry cab driver and also a very fancy hotel. 'How much?' I asked politely, trying to ignore the Italian curses. 'Twenty dollars,' he replied and took my change. 'Thanks,' I said, and slid out of the back of the car. 'Could you pop the trunk?' He did as I asked and I reached in and took my bag; on the underside of the trunk lay a picture of Florence, a beautiful city, almost tranquil and serene, everyone so still and relaxed, calmly going about their business, as the artist painted. 'Hey, hurry it up, will you?' the cab driver yelled and shut his door. I closed the trunk, apologized and watched him speed off down the driveway and out into the hustle-and-bustle of rush hour traffic. I turned around and gazed at the large building, illuminated by the city lights and the fountain that shot large amounts of water into the air, leaving a silhouette effect on the building. Here I stood, in front of the very building I now had to make home for a night and inside, I knew a livid Tim would be standing, muttering and checking his watch. We had missed training and as I stood here, all the memories I tried to forget from fifteen hours ago flooded back. I swallowed deeply, pulled the straps for my duffle bag over my shoulder, and moved slowly and surely into the hotel, walking into the unknown. “Pierce! What took you so long?” Tim Shipley snapped, but the bite disappeared from his voice halfway as the AWC Transatlantic champion collapsed into the only armchair. “I – I got delayed,” Pierce Lavelle murmured, his hand going automatically to his aching head. Forgetting the title belts now propped either side of him on the bed, Shipley abandoned his post and ventured cautiously towards his friend. Friend? Well, in this scene… certainly. “Pierce – you look awful,” Shipley said apologetically, squatting down next to him and placing a hand gently on his shoulder. “You need to get some rest…” “No, no!” Lavelle exclaimed. “We need to go through – go through –” Cue one big yawn. “– TACTICS!” Shipley smiled ruefully, having accepted as soon as his partner walked through the door that they weren’t going to get any match preparation done. “No chance, Pierce. When did you last sleep?” Taken aback by Lavelle’s unusually dishevelled appearance, the 20-year-old peered closer into the eyes of the man two years his senior. Eyes that could barely stay open. “I’ve been sleeping, it’s just –” Tim Shipley waited for a response that never came. Aware that whatever explanation his friend could give was going to be lengthy, Shipley straightened up and offered Lavelle a hand. “Come on, Pierce. You go to bed; I’ll leave you for a while.” Unable to protest, Lavelle allowed Shipley to help him gently towards the bed. For a moment, Summer’s Son even dallied with the idea of pulling the duvet over him, but perished the thought. Too much American TV! Fifteen hours later Pierce Lavelle swallowed the last of his orange juice and stretched back over the back of the wooden chair. Darkness had long ago fallen outside, and Tim Shipley, in the chair opposite, wasn’t looking too good for wear himself. “Shouldn’t have slept all day,” Lavelle mumbled. “I won’t be able to manage any sleep now...” “Oh, I dare say you’ll manage a wink,” Shipley replied brightly. Lavelle’s efforts to appear sprightly were falling way short of the mark for the perceptive Englishman. “But – before we go to sleep –” Shipley paused, checking his watch and casting an uncertain glance at Lavelle, who nodded. “Alright, Tim, I’ll start from the beginning. This could take a while...” I swallowed, and told him everything. Lilly Connelly, Marvin Harrington, Lois and Rebecca and Tony Rush. Tim was a good listener. Despite his complete lack of dramatic aptitude, he managed small gasps as appropriate, and his eyes flashed as if he wanted to say something once or twice, but really, I was happy that he didn’t interrupt. As I unravelled more and more of the chronicles of my recent life, I did begin to feel guilty that Tim hadn’t been kept up to date all along. But in the deadly game in which I had been entrapped, you can never take risks, and I tried to justify it to myself this way: Tim might have become a target. I still felt guilty though. But I was too tired to care.
