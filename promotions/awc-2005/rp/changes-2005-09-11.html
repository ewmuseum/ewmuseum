As I tossed a handful of soil onto the coffin I allowed myself to fall to my knees, clasping the dirt with my hands. I had lost my child, my unborn child. My son. The gravestone simply read. ‘To a dear child we’ll never know, may you sleep soundly away from the dark’ ~~~ It became too hard to stay where we were, our lives had been turned upside down, this accident, this miscarriage had torn through us as it naturally would. Louise hadn’t said a word since the funeral, not to me, not to anyone. The only time she wasn’t crying is when she was asleep. I would stand and watch her sometimes, as she found the brief moment of peace, a break from her solace, but even then her dreams were probably plagued with memories of an unborn child, memories of things, which never happened. You know exactly what I’m talking about, the what ifs. What if you hadn’t lost your virginity in a drunken orgy, simply because it seemed like a good idea. What if I had paid attention at school? What if my unborn child hadn’t died? For an entire fortnight not a word between us was spoken. Lingering glances across the room, that was, when we could be in the same room together. Don’t get me wrong; I know now that neither of us blamed the other, I know that it was just one of those things that life sends to kick you in the vitals. At the time though we could feel each other’s resentment, burning at each other. The honeymoon period was long off our marriage, now all that was left was the emptiness, the shell of life which marriage causes. Then out of no-where on a normal Thursday night. Everything changed and I mean, everything. As I was walking back from the gym, I could not even begin to comprehend the extent of which, the next conversation was going to change my life. “We have to move” After two weeks of silence, this was the first thing to come out of Louise’s mouth. It wasn’t the outpouring of emotion I was expecting, nor was it the quiet talk I had hoped for, where we could sit and talk about our feelings. Out of nowhere, she had simply demanded that I drop everything, turn my life around just to suit her. When I asked her why, the expected answer came out, there were too many bad memories here. Which in her defense was true. Our marital home had simply been a place in which we would argue. They say a house is just a shell, but it does begin to take on a personality of it’s own. This was a house of hate. There was of course one small problem. PRIME. Like it or not I was still contracted to them, I couldn’t simply up and leave. PRIME had become a home to me, a lot of promotions were just jobs, a means to an end, but PRIME had a real family atmosphere; everything seemed to gel there and click. PRIME was a home, how could I simply walk into that office and tell them I had to leave. After all they had done for me I simply had to up and leave. For many sleepless nights I allowed my thoughts to linger on this, my mind telling me that I could carry on, that it would simply mean a longer commute in the mornings to the gym, to meetings. This was what had caused problems between Louise and myself before though, so much traveling, so much distance. Maybe a new start was what we needed, we could never forget the past and everything that had happened, but we could put it behind us and start again. That night I watched Louise as she slept, memories of our wedding day flashing through my head in a powerful montage that wouldn’t be out of place in a Hollywood blockbuster. “For Richer For Poorer. In Sickness And In Health And Forsaking All Other Till Death Do You Part.” “I do” That day I’d promised to do whatever was needed to make the marriage work. She had given up everything to spend time with me, to be a part of my life. Regardless of how difficult the decision was I knew that now it was my turn to make a similar sacrifice. It would probably work out for the best, things like this generally did. That morning I handed in my resignation, Hin-See was very understanding about the whole matter and whilst I had received a handshake goodbye, I also got the impression that I would not be returning to PRIME. My time there had been fun, but it was time to move on, nothing would change that. Not now. So we began looking for apartments, flats somewhere with a roof and hot running water. For four years of my life I had lived out of a suitcase, frequenting whatever random hotel was in the area of the latest show. Yet there was something about having a house of your own, little homely touches which made it yours, that and being able to walk around everywhere in your underwear. In the back of my mind however was the money situation and what exactly we were going to do for income. The money that I had in the bank was dwindling quickly and the sale of the old house, would just about pay for the new house. Regardless of whatever the future held I needed to work. The idea of entering a factory job, or becoming a mindless sales clerk made me shudder all over and yet I knew my profile wasn’t large enough to enter movie work, the best I would get would be a commercial, advertising some weight loss program on late night television. I wasn’t about to let that happen. So I put my name out there, in all honesty I wasn’t expecting too much of a response. A few Indy bookings would give me some nice one off payments; the problem with those was just that though. They were one-off; they didn’t give me the financial security I was looking for, that I needed. It was as the offers began to flood in however that I began to realise, just how far I had come in a short amount of time. In the space of twenty-four hours between announcing my services as available, four companies had declared interest, not just interest but generous contract offers, pre-negation figures that a year ago I wouldn’t have dreamed of. It was then I made a choice. It was then I chose AWC.
