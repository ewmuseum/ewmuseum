The Game

23 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23.06.06 You begin to notice the intricacies of things when every single feeling is trebled, when skin becomes so sensitive that the touch of a soft bed sheet became like swimming backstroke in sandpaper and broken glass. The only welcome feeling is the total shutting down of the nervous system or the single needle prick and the rush it brings. Both appealed. Lacuna had been out of the hospital for a few days, and still the side effects from her heroin addiction were rampaging her already fragile body. Muscle spasms caused her limbs to rage, her bones felt like string and she would regularly lose control of her bowels. Life was hell, but Sorrow wouldn’t let her end it. She’d been locked in a small room upon arrival, given a choice of ‘like it or lump it’ and pushed backwards onto a single bed. Here she stayed for as long as she could, staring up at a textured white ceiling, making pictures from the accidental patterns and noting every intricate detail. Even months later she could recall where the inoffensive yellow paint occasionally stepped across and merged with the white of the ceiling. The yellow paint soon became a point of hatred for Lacuna. After focusing her deep brown eyes on it for hours on end, it became almost human. She had given it thoughts, a personality and a set of excuses for letting everyone down time and time again. It was a stereotypical guestroom, decorated sparsely with cheap flat-pack furniture and other such inoffensive ornaments. It was so forced, so ridiculously blatant. This was what people were locked in to die when their children finally had enough of them. Where the infirmed began to feel that they had a molecule of freedom. But this was more of a prison than the hospital, caged inside her antibody-pumped cadaver, strapped to machines she would never understand. She had gone from being a figure for study to a figure hidden from view, out of embarrassment and respectability. This was the output of the comedown. This was what happened when the hole left in a young girls life revealed its true depths, sucking in everything that remained. Drugs had filled this chasm, pulling her from the reality she despised into a world she could make her own. She attributed to her using a large quantity of escapism, although the cliché burned at her every time she thought it through. It was the same old excuse used over and over. Lacuna did not hate her life before drugs, and she certainly did not want to die then, but she dabbled as a way of experimentation as well as, to an extent, searching for a way to make her life fresh. She had made it fresh by trouncing everything that she had left. Now all she wanted was to do it again, one last time. Her thoughts had been interrupted by a heavy-handed attempt and consequent fumble at opening the door quietly. Sorrow had never quite grasped the logic of doing things quietly, so when he tried he regularly made a mess of it. He swung open the pine door, striding into the room with his head bowed. He had his sense of honour left, and did not want to witness something unfitting of a relation. Lacuna eyed him blankly, as she had eyed everything since leaving hospital. She had taken up a permanent glazed expression and wasn’t in the mind to do anything about it. He shifted uncomfortably, his booted feet scraping on the thin blue carpet with a burst of nervous energy. Slowly, he lowered himself into a wicker chair, which bent awkwardly, and he levelled a sullen gaze directly into the eyes of Lacuna. She turned her head away out of habit, having never have been good at eye contact. But now she knew that she was in for a psychological analysis and this encouraged her yet further to avoid any possible link from forming between her and the man she regarded as her captor. A few minutes passed with no change in the room bar a distinct rise in tension. The atmosphere was almost electric with anticipation. Sorrow knew this, and he rolled it over in his mind. He had wracked his brains for so long, forcing himself to examine and cross-examine any trace of feeling that had coursed through him. He had never been the emotional type, cold and calculating held so many advantages when placed with a cunning violent streak and a determination that was second to none. This was what had hurt him more than anything when he realised that he did care. Lacuna was his cousin, and the only trace of family that he was aware of. He had thrown away so much in his life, but he was not a quitter. He was smart, but also naïve and placing himself as the one and only priority in his life had taken its toll on a lot of things. There were some things that could never be placed fully at the back of even the most ruthless mind, and that last remaining blood-link was all he could focus on now. He watched her as she lay motionless, awake but not fully conscious. She was alive by the sense of breathing and beating heart only, there was no life behind her once stunning eyes. She was a shell now, existing to not exist. He could recall her at her prime, a young woman with more money than sense and a love of making it well known. She wrestled because that’s what she had always known, and she loved that too. There was a grit in her that made her ideal, but a spark that made her unique. She had gone a long way; a long way further than her eternally mediocre cousin, but the spark had soon died. He raised a hand and scratched at the back of his neck out of habit, directing his gaze back down at the carpet. The urge to rant and rave had gone. He just wanted his cousin back. Lacuna could feel his gaze. It was cold on her and she shivered, despite being decked out in as much as she could find to wear. She wanted to snuggle up, adopt the foetal position and finally grab some sleep. She couldn’t though; her mind was too active to give in. She needed to break the boredom, she needed another fix. But for now she just needed distracting from herself. “Why are you watching me?” she croaked, her voice rickety and unsteady, as she hadn’t used it in days, maybe weeks. Without sleep to break up the days she couldn’t quite tell anymore. Sorrow continued to stare at the floor, slouching forwards in the lightweight chair. He barely heard her speak, and even when he did take it in he paid little heed to her words. He was too wrapped up in his own thoughts and this infuriated Lacuna a little. “I asked you a very specific question and the least you could do is dignify it with a response.” Her voice was recovering, as was her pride. Sorrow continued to wait, biding his time and noting the responses with a hidden intrigue. This was the start of his ploy. He needed to rile her, and however much it hurt him, this was the only way he could get her to show some of the pride she had been famous for, as was the first step in which she would be rehabilitated. “Grunt” said Sorrow, his voice as full of emotion as his head. Calm was key, and he could hold his calm through the greatest of tests, but he couldn’t hide how he was feeling at the moment. Lacuna sat up tentatively, resting her arm on the headboard and shivering with a jolt of pain. Her head swam from the sudden movement, but she was becoming more focused. Moreover, she was confused at Sorrow. These were not actions he had even brought out before, a side of him yet to see the light of day. Sorrow continued to look at the floor, but Lacuna was having none of it. “What the bloody hell is that supposed to mean? No, seriously, what are you doing?” A slow shake of the head from Sorrow, and he finally lifted his face to look at Lacuna. He ran a hand through his long brown hair and removed it from his face. His eyes were deep and welled with thought. “Nothing” he said simply and rose from the chair before turning to leave the room and closing the door softly behind him. He could hear Lacuna spluttering with frustration behind the door. She had her standards, and was very used to hearing exactly what she wanted when she demanded. He allowed himself to smile lightly as he sat heavily in a padded armchair, kicking his legs out with relief. He had achieved most of what he had wanted. He had riled her. Her tendency to expect a certain brand of power over others didn’t work with him and it was slowly beginning to bring about a level of passion that could have been argued as her strongest character trait. Sorrow was winning. Soon he would have the old Lacuna back. For now, though, he was going to watch TV and think about something else, because a taxing emotional session was never high on his list of priorities. __ Lacuna collapses back onto the bed, catching her head sharply on the thick wooden headboard and cursing loudly. She was annoyed, both because she was being played and because she had started to feel again. It had been so long since she had felt a normal emotion, one that didn’t comprise of pain or boredom. Sorrow had stirred in her something that she vaguely remembered as Lacuna Firenze Devries, herself of old. The same person she believed was left buried in a dark shit-stained room in the middle of nowhere. She rested her head down on the soft pillows and closed her eyes. For the first time in days, maybe weeks, she was tired. She felt compelled to give in, the drawing from within was too much for her and without a mental debate, she caved in and slept. __ She was back in the room, the dark blurring her vision and the stench ripping at her senses like a claw from within. Looking down at herself, she was naked and pockmarked with oozing, yellowing scabs and old wounds. No longer just her arms, but her thighs, her chest, even her neck felt sore and wet. She gasped aloud as the heat flooded her lungs, the air almost too difficult to breath in. She felt dizzy, and cast a small hand aside to lean on the wall, but the wall moved backwards and she fell heavily to the concrete floor. The thoughts and mental anguish came flooding back. She was trapped, nobody was looking for her this time and she couldn’t see a door, just four walls. Not even the window she had despised so much. She didn’t have the energy to get back up; she couldn’t even find the strength to throw an arm beneath her to prop her frame upright. She caught a cough in her throat before gagging and spewing up a stream of steaming vomit. Her head snapped back and then came another torrent, followed by a couple more until she fell back, choking on bile and gasping for oxygen. She couldn’t work out why. She was so confused, but it still seemed right. Rising to her feet with a sudden burst of energy, Lacuna ran towards a wall, spitting and screaming, all her rage and frustration coming frothing from her lips, but before she hit the wall she tripped over a large mass and landed heavily on her face. Blood mixed with her vomit and sweat and she tossed and turned, trying to find whatever it was that had both tripped her and broken her fall. There he was, the face of Angelus Sorrow, pale and shrunken, the skin stretched far too tightly over his skull. His eyes were open and full of hidden horror, as was his mouth, the corner bloodied and stained with a trickle that must have ran days ago. He was beyond dead; he was far beyond any death she had seen before. He was rotting in front of her eyes. She puked again and forced herself into a corner, whimpering and spluttering out in a confused torrent of emotion. “Don’t let me…” Lacuna looked up. She hadn’t spoken; the voice was deep but croaky and forced, sounding like some sort of death rattle, yet definitely male. She crawled back over to the corpse of Sorrow as it spoke again, not moving but issuing forth the challenge. “Don’t let me…” The dead were talking, it was too much to take in, she thrust open her eyes and sat bolt upright in her bed. The inoffensive yellow coming flooding back, she was alright. She was back in reality. “…down”
