Ever the Opportunist

1 November 2006

Date: November 1st Title: Ever the opportunist For: Fresh Author: Lara C Harrisburg, Pennsylvania She sat rigid on the edge of the bed, her wrists bound tightly to the frame with handcuffs, shaving against her skin every time she was to wriggle for comfort. His eyes lay on her, his hands by his side, longing for the savouring touch of her soft pale skin but he had to refrain. His rewards and luxuries of lust would come with payment, but his mind still played and his loins tightened. “You are quite a boisterous woman, aren’t you Ms. Wood?” His voice spoke with a thick and crisp Spanish lilt. Cocking her head to the side, her green eyes were laced with a red and watery weariness, her once playful grin worn. “You must have a voice, Ms. Wood. You did that night!” He mocked her, joyfully, enjoying the tantalizing taste of playing games. “I don’t feel like talking,” she replied, coldly. He edged forward in his seat, the wooden chair rattling under his strong and domineering frame. “Don’t you, Ms. Wood? Surely you must wonder why you are here…” He laughed with a cold and shrill tone, his hands rubbing along his thighs as she looked away in disgust, her stomach turning. “I deal with sick men like you all the time,” Lois responded with a fiery gaze, her boisterous attitude returning. “Sick? I assure you Ms. Wood, I am quite sane.” He grinned, mockingly. Removing a small packet of cigarettes he chucked his cape aside, but the Phantom mask remained. The stench of tobacco rose up her nose, but a warmth kept her calm and at ease. A memory of a face lay before her as she closed her eyes, remembering his gentle words and sweet touch. “Are you familiar with Roman history Ms. Wood?” She opened her eyes finding the question quite amusing and equally as strange. “Thought not,” his response was dry. “Just trying to make conversation.” Her mind was closed to his playful and insightful gestures, her eyes gazed toward the small crack in the curtain, the minor trickle of light flickering along her feet, yet his words still entered her mind, his games alluring her to the questions. “Why am I here?” she questioned, coyly. He sniggered and blew out a line of smoke, fading through the light. “I knew you couldn’t resist the temptation, Lois…” Her eyes widened as she turned to face him, his smile held a foreboding sense in his gaunt crooked expression. “How do you know my name?” Lois pleaded. His smile faded as inched closure, licking his lips, the mask moist. Beads of sweat lined his palms as his loins tightened; his eyes gazed toward the small amount of skin showing on her leg as her body twisted away from him, his hands lingering in the air. “I know a lot about you Ms. Wood,” he said. She turned away, the handcuffs chaffing her skin, a pinching sensation against her skin as a tear rolled down her cheek. His touch was hot and sweaty, his breath smelling of cigarettes as he inched closer. “Aren’t you curious, Ms. Wood?” he questioned, playfully. He tossed her loose hair to the side running his thumb along her cheek, her eyes held his. “Come on. Ask me more questions?” He toyed. “That’s quite enough.” In the distance a deep voice spoke, his words causing the man to relinquish his grip on Lois’s face. The man moved with a pace toward the voice, Lois kept her eyes closed shut, her heart pounding with fear. He gazed toward the man before him. “What are you playing at?” he questioned. “I was just having some fun, Sir,” he replied, removing the moist mask. “We aren’t here to have fun,” His words rang with a ferocious punch to the gut. The man keeled over, dropping his mask to the floor, coughing as he regained his composer. “Are we clear?” “Yes, Sir,” he said and scuttled out of the room. The door slam startled Lois as she looked around the room and stood before her was a different man, refined and European looking. His dark black hair decorated with wisps of grey as his olive skin shinned against the opened curtain as he took a seat comfortably opposite her. “Such animals make my business that much more dirty,” he said with distain as he wiped his hands. “Why am I here?” Lois asked. “I do apologise for the long wait, I had…prior arrangements.” His dark brown eyes lay on her as he unbuttoned his white suit coat and pulled out a small flask of wine and took a sip, savouring its refreshing taste. “Thirsty?” he asked, extending the flask toward her. She shook her head and looked away. “A ‘no thank you’, would’ve been polite,” he said, smugly. “Polite?” she spat. “You have me kidnapped, tied up and put in a room with a sex offender and then ask me to be polite, you can - - -“ “Ah ah ah....Such words might cause me to react, bite your tongue.” She remained quiet, his eyes holding a fiery rage. His inched closer, resting his elbows on his knees as he looked out the window. “Quite a mouth for a district attorney,” he said while rubbing his hand along his stubble covered chin. She ignored his comment and took a breath, deciding silence was her best opportunity. This man, although not as sexually active as the other, was more intuitive, refined and manipulative. “Perhaps we should continue…I’m afraid my employers’ are quite impatient.” Removing the cap once more on his flask he took a sip and placed it onto the floor by his polished brown shoes. Her eyes remained on the flask, her mouth dry and her lip swollen from being knocked unconscious. Her head still pounded but she refused any offering of help. “Do you know why you are here, Ms. Wood?” he asked. “Money?” she responded. “In a manner of speaking, yes. You have a friend, perhaps more than a friend and you are going to help us get what we want from him…” he said sternly. “Who are you talking about?” she questioned. “Surely you must know, Lois, he’s the only one you’ve been with,” His grin was sickening. Lois looked toward him, worry in her eyes. “And if I don’t help you…” Lois responded. He inched toward her, his breath warm against her skin. “Then his fate, shall we say, will be cut short.” Los Angeles, California The autumn leaves danced along the ground, spreading a blanket of colours against the faded and worn green grass. The orange and burgundy leaves shimmering against the low lying sunshine as the chilled wind whistled against the naked trees as though voices were speaking amongst the silence of the graveyard. He stood in the morning air, alone and quiet before his grandfather’s resting place. Dressed in faded blue jeans, a black shirt and working boots Pierce gazed toward the dishevelled mess sprawled across his grandfather’s headstone. His arm draped into a small sling covered by his cream jacket that lay over his shoulders, flapping in the wind as he crouched down and clawed away the leaves that lay across his grandfather’s name. “It’s been a while old man.” He said with a sorrowful and weary tone, his eyes cast down toward the black engraving on the white granite. “To be honest, I’m not even sure why I came.” The lingering silence echoed in the distilled scene around him, the rustling leaves fluttered quietly along the blanketed ground as the wind whistled an eerie response. “I guess I’ve got a decision to make.” Still crouched, he noticed the lack of care and decoration on his grandfather’s headstone. The small letters were beginning to fade overtime with erosion. “I’ve just been trying to understand things,” Lavelle said, solemnly. The crunching of tires against stone allured his senses as his eyes darted to a small Toyota travelling along the gravel toward another headstone. “What I don’t understand is why you would do this.” His voice was quiet as he spoke, his eyes cast down, away from the stone. “Why choose me?” he stated abruptly. The cold wind rattled against his bones as he pulled the collar on his coat taut around his neck. The quiet and calm reverie was almost inviting as he stood up, his thigh muscles aching. “I could really use your help, your advice would sure as hell be useful ‘round about now,” he pleaded. The sky roared with the vacuum of noise as a Boeing 767 passed overhead in a loud and thunderous roar. The birds rose feverishly out of the sheltered trees in a squawking frenzy, disturbing the eerie tranquil blanket that lined the graveyard. “No peace for the wicked, right?” he said with a smile. His attempt at remembrance became interrupted once more by another car, entering the small plot where he now stood, speaking to his grandfather’s headstone. “Why’d you leave me?” Lavelle said in a howling cry as he glared toward the stillness beneath him. For the first time in a long time he felt the trickle of a warm tear slide down his cheeks. The birds danced above him, singing a tune as they soared toward the naked trees around him. He watched as the world went by, the warmth of his tears streamed down his cold skin as his eyes lay on the still and bleak headstone beneath him. The silence is disconcerting as he wipes his eyes with his sleeve and takes a sniffled breath. “I miss you, granddad.” He sobbed through muffled words, his fingers caressing a small key. “I’m just not strong enough to do this on my own. I need your help and…you can’t even hear me.” Lavelle said with cynicism as he knelt down and removed a twig that lay perched against the granite stone. The stillness soon paused around him, the chattering of horns rose in the distance, the murmuring of radios filled the silence. The city was awake and the church bells rang to stir those who remained asleep. “I just hope you knew what you were doing when you gave me this key, ‘cause I sure as hell don’t.” He rose once again and slid the key onto the small chain around his neck as he gazed toward the wrought iron gates in the distance and the glowering taxi driver, waiting with a cold cup of coffee and a crumpled newspaper. Yokohama, Japan The stench of burnt flesh aroused him from his drifting consciousness. His eyes were struggling to open as they swelled up. A darkness once more surrounded him as the pain and aching in his muscles faded with a tingling at the tips of his fingers as he remained hanging loosely. The water bath beneath him had removed all feeling in his feet. “Am I dead?” he thought as he struggled to remain awake, his mouth parched with thirst. “He refuses to talk, Mr. Fleming, there’s nothing we can do.” Came a hoarse voice as a light switch flicked on in the distance, the harsh beam stinging his eyes as he looked vaguely toward the light. Shadows moved in front of it, their cologne a refreshing scent against the smell of his burnt and dying body. “Why won’t you talk Mr.Rush?” Another voice questioned, the grip of a hand resting on Rush’s chin lifting his weak head up toward the eyes of the man responsible for his suffering. Black beads gazed back at him, lifeless and cold. Coughing with pain he drove his head to the side, loosening the grip on his chin. “Why don’t you see that if you just talk, you’ll be a free man?” Rush glared back toward him, feigning a weak response. “Fuck…you!” The man moved forward, his hot musky breath blowing on Rush as he spoke. “You’re a foolish man, Mr. Rush; you’ve offered me no other choice.” The door in the distance opened as two more men entered, dragging behind them a body. Rush studied with a blurred vision the body being dragged, it was thin, slender and curvy. It was a woman… Rush rattled his chains as he tried to inch closer toward the man but for his efforts he received a sharp blow to his abdomen. “What are you doing?” Rush bellowed, finding his voice once more. “You gave me no choice, Mr. Rush.” The chains went slack as Rush fell to the ground, face first, his chin colliding with the cold concrete floor. A pinching sensation gripped his arms as he felt himself being dragged toward a small chair, tugging at his trousers as they raised him onto the hard wooden frame, attaching his hands to the arms of the chair. In front of him she sat, bound and gagged with a black bag over her head. Rush took a few calming breaths as he felt the migraine and pain subside slightly, his arms tingling as the blood soon rushed through them. A wet patch clung to his thigh and legs as he realised he was sitting in his own urine. “What is an American FBI agent doing in Japanese waters?” he questioned. Rush glared toward him. “You tell me!” he spat, vehemently. The man inched forward and slapped Rush with a belting clout across the cheek. “Enough of your stupid games,” he roared and waved his hand. One of men moved toward the woman and removed the bag, terror and pain written across her delicate features as she squirmed under her restraints, eyes lingering on Rush. “I’m sure you remember Emi Natsuko, Mr. Rush.” Rush rattled in his seat, trying to squirm free as he watched one of the men raise a silver berretta. “No….” Rush said with a determined glare. The man smiled coldly. “We don’t have all day, well, Ms. Natsuko doesn’t…” he said with a foreboding stare. “Look I’ll talk just…don’t hurt her,” Rush said. The man nodded and moved toward Rush with a swagger of confidence in his step. “Now, why are you here?” he questioned. He nodded his head as he coughed violently. “On holiday…” Rush said with a sick grin. Emi’s eyes looked toward him, her breathing heavy. Rush managed a small wink but soon the smarmy grin was slapped off his face as the man inched closer, becoming increasingly agitated. “No more games…” he said. She felt his finger squeeze on the gun and then…darkness. Rush watched as her throat splattered around him, her head hanging as the bullet ripped through her. His screams muffled by the loud explosion of the bullet from the barrel. Rush turned away, his breathing slowed and panicked. “You son of a bitch!” Rush screamed. The freeways were littered with cars as the helicopters swept over each freeway giving the daily traffic report. The business executives busied themselves while they crossed the street handling a hot cup of coffee from the popular, Starbucks. At 8:00 am in the morning, Lavelle had left the Hilton Hotel and walked through the streets enjoying the frenzy around him as he ordered a Cola and taco from a small vendor and sat on a bench in the park along Sunset. A cold wind rippled the small foil that lay underneath his taco as it bit along the back of his neck. Ruffling the collar of his jacket against the wind, Lavelle finished off the last bite of his taco and washed it down with a swig of cola. In the distance an old man approached from across the road dressed in a suit and heavy trench coat with a cane aiding his hobbled approach. Crumpling the foil under his grasp he chucked it toward a trash can. He missed it by a mile, but the many pigeons that hung around like vultures devoured the left over crumbs and watched as their breakfast was swept up by the gale force winds. He raised his head to greet the man stood before him. His cane balanced to his left hand side as he slumped into the bench across from Lavelle. “It’s been a long time, Pierce,” the old man said, placing his cane across his lap. Lavelle extended his hand and shook the old man’s frail, cold hand with a smile. “It’s been too long my old friend,” Lavelle spoke gently. “How do like the City of Angels so far?” he asked, smiling. His smile was barely visible beneath the large grey beard. Lavelle smiled back and finished off his can, offering to buy one for the old man who refused kindly and pulled the neck of his coat up against the wind. “It’s good, but I still prefer my quiet home town.” “Don’t we all. What brings you to Los Angeles?” he asked, inching forward. “I went to see my grandfather yesterday and I’ve made my decision,” Lavelle said. “And what have you decided?” he asked, urging Lavelle to continue. Lavelle moved toward the old man, placing the crumpled can of cola to one side as he pulled out a small key - a key that caused so much intrigue and curiosity, but almost seemed worthless. “I’ll take over my grandfather’s business, but there’s just one thing, Edward,” Lavelle spoke quietly. The old man, otherwise know as Edward, leant back placing his cane back off his lap and firmly onto the concrete beneath him. Moving his hand through the air he felt Lavelle’s arm and patted it gently. “And what might that be, Pierce?” Edward asked, coyly. Lavelle produced the silver key and Edward’s eyes widened with intrigue but also recognition. “What’s this key all about?” Lavelle asked. Edward gazed toward it, noticing the small insignia on the front accompanied with the initials “J.H.L”. Moore was an eccentric business man, well groomed and mannered with a rugged handsome look and glinting green eyes. Dressed in a lavish new cream suit and a blue shirt, he stood proudly gazing out into the vastness of the Los Angeles skyline. Before him, the hustle and bustle of business boomed around him. The ringing of telephones, the churning of fax machines, the biting of printers and the chattering of employees. Yes, it was a Monday morning. Moore thought while he gripped two manila files and buttoned his suit coat across his waist before taking a stride through the floor. He’d just received a phone call he’d been waiting almost a year to get, but now that it came, things would change. The odd employee raised their heads in acknowledgement of him, while they chatted feverishly on the other end, keeping the business afloat. He was a man on a mission today as he rapped twice on a small door and opened it. Before him sat Matthew Paige, an accounting graduate from Yale, well established and astute for a number churning, bumbling idiot. Moore smiled and entered the room, shutting the door behind him. “You got the numbers?” Moore asked seating himself down on a chair opposite Paige. Paige nodded, his glasses falling off the brim of his nose while he routed under a bundle of faxes and prints. Gripping a wad of documents he slid them across to Moore and fixed his glasses with his middle finger. “You’ll be quite pleased, Mr. Moore, we’ve become quite the profitable business,” Paige commentated, flipping a couple of pages over for Moore. Moore looked toward the figures and profits earned in the last month and a smile rose along his lips. “Now this is a turn over, what about the Hoffman project?” Moore questioned. Paige looked puzzled for a moment, before answering. “Oh, the Hoffman project, Lang was on the phone with them recently, but they aren’t budging on the merger.” “You think they’re screwing us over?” Moore questioned, looking up from the accounting documents. Paige raised his shoulders in a maybe-manner. “Could be, who knows, they’re a bunch of lawyers’ speaking for the Mercy hospital. The only thing they care about is their bank balance.” Moore nodded in acquiescence to Paige’s comment and flipped another few pages over. Paige just remained in silence for a moment, processing an idea. “With the profit margins, we could up the price a little. Sway things in our favour,” Paige said, coyly. Moore continued reading, as though ignoring Paige, without raising his head, he spoke. “This isn’t our company, Paige. We can’t just go willy-nilly with the money as we deem fit. It’s not ours.” Paige leaned forward. “Encase you haven’t noticed. Lavelle Sr. has been dead a year now and we’ve been managing the dam firm without him.” Moore stopped, the sheet he was reading hanging loosely between his thumb and forefinger. He looked up with a displeased expression on his face. Paige raised his shoulders and leant into the chair. “What? I was stating the obvious, Moore, and you know it.” Moore inched forward, placing the two manila files on Paige’s desk. “We’ve got a new boss, Paige.” “Who?” Paige questioned. “This isn’t our business. We’ve just been keeping the seat warm,” Moore spoke bluntly. “Who’s the new boss?” Paige asked again, ignoring the manila files in front of him. “Lavelle’s grandson has come to town,” Moore said, standing up. “Either you’re joking or Lavelle Sr. really went senile in his old age,” Paige stated with a cocky grin. Moore shook his head in disapproval of his colleague’s comments and attitude. “Keep an eye on those figures, Paige.”
