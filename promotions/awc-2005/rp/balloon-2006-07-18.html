Date: July 18, 2006 if you think you know what i'm doing wrong you're going to have to get in line 'glass house' -- ani difranco ** Girl has a bad habit of mentally letting go of all of her limbs if she doesn’t think about them. This isn’t the same thing as letting your limbs fall asleep, because legs that are asleep twitch and tingle and make their presence known. When Girl mentally lets go of her limbs, she just doesn’t feel them anymore. She sits still for a long periods of time, no twitching or anything, just straight sitting, ignoring every part of her body except for her chest and her face and eventually it’ll be just as if she didn’t have her hands folded in her lap or as if her feet didn’t exist at all. After a while the curiosity will almost kill her, and she’ll be forced to shift her foot a little to reassure herself that it’s still there, or her finger will involuntary snap up, or there will be this itch going from one leg to another and she’ll have to scratch. And then Girl’ll do the whole thing over again. -- They are both young and artless, as clumsy as they are graceful, forgetting things, rushing things, searching for things that are never there. The air is heavy with the crush of leaves and cut grass. It’s idyllic and careless and the honeysuckle is alive. What better time was there to fall in love with him, figures Ellis, eyes as aromatic as the air and his hair the dark night that delivers the day. Night, knight, no puns intended, but there they are. There she is. They go on a trip out to the sea, little sleepy towns strewn across the streets, their shops proportional to their size and tiny little alcoves of peace and serenity where there’s refuge from the everyday horde of duties, poucing across her skin like mosquitoes. There are places everywhere, quaint as nursery rhymes and quiet as lullabies. Escape, they’d both whispered, laughing the way lovers do. Escape. Escape to the docks towards the sea, where they spend hours tossing rocks into the water and counting the skips, where there are yachts and boats floating as serene as feathers, where there is obsidion and emerald and aquamarine. She can count the colors and list their features, much as she can count people. A handful of black. A thumbnail of sunlight. A pointer finger of green, a pinkie of blue. Colors. Cold colors and warm colors and Adam colors and Ellis colors. One for me, one for you, she sings, mouth open and head thrown back like fellow drunkards, arms tossed across shoulders. Without purpose. But that’s wrong, because Adam has a purpose. A ghost of a dock, at last they stop where there is nothing but the horizon of heavens, where a sun is slowly being forced down. A scattered array of wooden poles lay out in front of them, jutting from the glass surface of the water; places where people had once walked over, but it’s gone, having been torn away from the pillars in the water. And where a wooden surface once remained there is only wooden markers to enlighten future visitors. Rest in peace, she thinks to mutter, may the memory of boats and passengers rest you forever. The dust of wood drifting away. Rest in peace, Adam whispers too, one hand tightening across the edge of his shirt. Here is a graveyard of a dock, and buried underneath are the structures of a dock. Like a body. Requiescat in pace. Rest in peace. Quick and leathe as a whistle Adam is on the posts, hands outstretched like a magnificent eagle, one foot balancing and the other in the air. You’ll fall in, Ellis shouts, giddy with excitement, forgetting to let peace be. Adam, on the grave, balancing life with dryness, death with wetness. A gigantic scale. You’ll fall in, she says again, but already the insects in her limbs are stirring, muttering amongst themselves, follow him, follow him, followhimfollowhimfollowhim. Stupid, she whispers back to the flies and cicadas lodged in her joints. Stupid. I’ll get wet. Follow him. Wet. Follow. Wet. Follow follow follow. Adam jumps from pillar to pillar, careful to keep his wings outspread, to allow the sunglinted water to peer at his arms. His feet, curving to the ends of the posts, have not yet slipped, and he glides through the air, one foot after another, rising down, going up, a staircase of pillars. Follow. Wet. Follow. Wet. Ellis argues with herself, all the while watching, consuming, the image of Adam jumping from pillar to pillar smooth within the roughness of wood. He skips his feet across like stones. Follow him, the insects hiss at her. Follow follow follow. Jump across the pillars. Taste the sun. Feel the eyes of the water. Let the exhilaration be your guide. Follow him. Look at his eyes. Can they be wrong? Can they really be wrong? Follow, they scream and shout and bite and claw. So she does. Her legs stretch out to meet the wood. Leap, heartbeat. Again. Up ahead, almost to the edges of the wooden markers is Adam, poised at a moment of a jump. Snapshot of boy in action, she thought, now jump. Follow, Eleanor, follow. The sun is an unbearable laughter, the water beneath looking at the flash of her legs. But she follows, follows the upwards spiral of some, the downwards curve of others. Becomes a jumper. Becomes a follower. And at the edge, now, there is no Adam, only a black dot in the water, immersed in splashes, filled with color, alive with laughter. His hands beckon towards her, elusive as a dream, beckoning her still more to follow. Follow, it yells at her, follow follow follow. Taste the parchment of life, taste water on your lips. Feel it burn your nose, your lungs. Follow. Follow. Like a diver, she unfurls the bow of her body, realizing her motion is her arrow. Midway through the jump she loses her limbs and the truth hits her. She can only see herself, the air rushing away, the water rushing up, and Adam with his arms outstretched to catch her. Follow, the bubbles seem to say as they pop against the coldness of the water. The leap. And done, now, with the jumping, with the following, they both start to swim for the unreachable shore at the other end. Then, Ellis wakes up. Lives are not love stories. They are contraptions designed to be completed and ornamental playgrounds driven too deep into ravines. -- Ellis realizes this too late. It occurs to her after the dream, waking up beside Adam, that she was once happy. She can almost taste it on her tongue, but shivers only. She climbs out of bed, thinking that the night before was cold. She doesn’t remember the remembrance of unremembered happiness. It doesn’t even occur to Ellis that Adam is wrong for her or the right type of wrong that it he could maybe be the type of mistake to fall in love with, just like it doesn’t occur to Ellis that mistakes don’t last. If mistakes are forever, there is nothing as useless or as resourceful as experience. Ellis has never learned by experience. It’s Sunday morning. She steals the joint on the nightstand, slips on her clothes and goes home. Love is, among other things, a trod on the foot when you’re looking the other direction. -- Monday morning. Going back to his and El’s and Ally’s and Jon’s apartment on the 36th floor is always an uncomfortable affair for Chandler. He refuses to adapt to the smooth sliding of the elevator and altitude, even after all of this time. What makes him more uncomfortable are the dull glass walls of the elevator itself and the floor-length windows positioned strategically in the hallways. After all, there’s nothing separating a person from a body and a bloody headline in the newspapers but a quarter inch thickness of glass. He could throw a punch at it and it would probably shatter. Chandler looks away. The people who built this must have been smarter than that. And, as usual, which isn’t how everything seems to be these days - so utterly unusual - Chandler’s forgotten his key. When he knocks, no one answers the door. It’s unlocked, anyway. Some redesign has apparently been going on for the past few days and the living room reeks of distemper. Either that or someone has discovered a passion for painting. The wooden floor is blotted with paint. Chandler steps carefully, dodging the pan of it lying next to the CD rack. Wandering into the kitchen, he realizes that the walls are white again, with a skyline painted in pale green, identical to what he can see through the wall-sized window that’s directly opposite it. Why are there so many fucking windows? Chandler purposely avoids looking too closely, but he thinks that when the sun’s at just the right angle, maybe the silhouette from the world outside would stream in through the glass and match those green lines exactly. Still. The blurry lines of green (is that watercolor?) clash. “I hate it.” Chandler twitches, just a little, because he was starting to get used to the silence of the apartment. It’s tough, every cubic centimeter of it perfectly built and flexible, so that there’s no creaking of the floor or vibration of the walls when you step, or even if you jump or run or anything at all, really. It fits perfectly with Ellis and her Oh! I am so mysterious and enigmatic! way of being, and he bets that whatever hundred boys that Ellis’s brought back to the apartment for the last couple of years have drawn blood from the inside of their mouth as a result. It’s a terribly handy way of surprising people, usually when they’re staring at the rooms, or a wall with a skyline. Then again, a robber could probably break in and hop away with a safe without anyone noticing. Assuming they got past the guards and security and surveillance system and – And it’s just like Ellis to not like something after obsessing and perfecting it. Chandler tries not to roll his eyes. Back at PD, people liked to think that he and Ellis were the same, absolutely dripping in mystique and cool. Most of the time, Chandler’d simply said that Ellis had a way of rubbing off on people that she likes, a way of making them seem almost as aloof and distant as she, if she wants to. Which she usually doesn’t. Chandler’s part had been easy enough. He sat there and shut up, nodding at Ellis’ claims and occasionally saying a bit of bullshit, but not much more. “It’s nice. Although you might want to think about the color.” Ellis doesn’t respond, and pads over to the refrigerator instead. It’s almost empty, and Chandler doesn’t ask why there are 4 bottles of Pom and a pack of pork Ramen on the top shelf. He takes the beer Ellis hands him but doesn’t open it. “I don’t like it.” “I know.” “But if I leave it there long enough.” “You might. Any plans today?” “Maybe. No. I might go stay with Adam again.” “Come on, get some shoes. Have you had lunch yet?” Ellis shoots a look at the clock built into the stove/oven and worries her thumb around the lip of the bottle. “It’s 4:13 in the -” “Exactly.” “-no.” “We’re going, then?” Chandler grins a little. He isn’t sure exactly what he and Ellis are, but in the world that he knows, you don’t have friends. You have people that you fuck and you have people that you work with. Ellis defies both categories, which seems to be her goal in life. (crazy or deep? drunk or sober? talented or--). -- Ellis decides she wants to walk down all thirtysomething flights of stairs, which takes them far longer than it’s supposed to, quite probably due to the amount of time Ellis spends staring out at the city. And, again, what the fuck? They have windows in the stairwells? By the time they reach street level it’s 4:25 PM, but the day’s still warm and a little golden. Having their feet on the ground is nice, because it’s good to have something other than air below you. Chandler can almost feel the earth underground, alive and shifting, protesting at the silver nails being driven into the rock underneath all the buildings, can almost feel the vibration of the sidewalk, side effects of a fiery heart somewhere down deep. It takes them almost ten minutes to walk to the small cafe, weaving around taxis and breathing in the city smell, fried food and salt and concrete. The cafe’s front is brightly painted, with a sign that says Hotpots is happiness for you and much friend! There are round metal tables outside, people eating and talking, and pretty waitresses. Ellis and Chandler sit down at a table near the door. A girl comes over quickly; she's got short hair and chews neon green gum rather enthusiastically. “Hello. What do you desire to eat today?” Chandler mentally raises an eyebrow, and flips through the laminated menu. “Pork gyozas, small, and white rice.” “Green tea. Do you have any noodles?” “Of course! We have noodles fried with seafood and vegetables, water chestnuts and chicken, green beans and –” “Do you have Ramen?” The girl looks at her oddly, but nods and leaves. A little boy yells, I want more Coke! a few tables behind them. An old man pedals by on a bicycle, whistling. Somebody's cell phone rings Sean Paul. Ellis watches the traffic lights change, blinks, and tries not to fall asleep. She’s comfortable here, sitting in a green chair in front of a restaurant in the center of Cortland. When they leave, she gives the waitress a fifteen dollar tip. -- 5:13. They walk down the street and narrowly avoid being mauled to death by a woman carrying the largest Vuitton bag Ellis’d ever seen. At the intersection of West 43rd and Broadway, they see a person dressed as a panda and carrying a briefcase. They spend their night in the apartment drinking Pom with Ally, talking and not talking about the stupidest things. The TV is on and loud - they watch a movie about a bunch of robots killing people and don’t understand what the man with the huge muscles is talking about. Even money can’t buy them good TV. Ellis falls asleep leaning against the couch with Ally’s head on her shoulder. She doesn’t dream. Love is, among other things, betrayal of the self. -- Tuesday morning. The sun sets a fraction and Ellis can feel a sort of warmth through her t-shirt and on the back of her neck. It casts a shadow on the painted wall. The lines don’t quite match up, but it’s an almost glorious sight, lazily red and orange with a human sized hole. The whole thing is all terribly like a badly written book, poorly drawn valentine, and for a second, that’s why Ellis can believe it’s real. Fifteen minutes later and she can’t feel her limbs. Twelve minutes. She shifts her foot a little, to reassure herself that it’s still there. Four minutes. She gets up, nerves now dead in her foot, and grabs a bottle of Pom from the refrigerator. Six minutes. Chandler ambles in flattening sleep-ridden hair with a palm. Through a yawn, he asks,“When’s your flight leave?” El draws in a sigh, tapping her fingernail against the Pom bottle. “2:45.” “You better get going, then. JFK’s far.” She wants to. Get away, at least, just pack her bags and go. The most beautiful poem in the world is an airplane ticket away from the world, a ticket with perforated edges and a rubber stamp across a passport she doesn’t really need anymore, something like that. Ellis is tired of keeping faith and of finding herself time and time again, listless and passionless as she watches Adam fly. Some things bombard across your life and disappear as if they were never there in the first place. That’s what Ellis feels has happened to her life. All the passions and the dreams have left, gone, taken wing and flew away, and Ellis wants to be find the bird that will chase them down. There is no such bird. Ellis hopes that maybe the thing growing inside of her will be that bird. “I will.” Love is, among other things, knowing the difference between giving in and giving up. -- Today. Georgia. Ellis will sit in her locker room prior, losing her limbs. Love is, among other things, an end unto itself.
