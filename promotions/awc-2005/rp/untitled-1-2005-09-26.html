26 September 2005

Date: 26/09/2005 I sat across the table from the man I had been introduced to just a few months ago; before I knew it we had moved in together and began a movement in to sports entertainment. It wasn't as if we had just decided one morning 'Hey, pro wrestling! Now there's a money spinning idea!', we had both had specific training. He as a valet, and me as an in ring performer. Our talents went hand in hand, similar appearances, similar fashion sense, add to the equation that we had been living together for about three months now, seeing one another every morning and training with one another every day. Working the indy circuit together as a team ever since we were introduced by a mutual friend in a mutual federation. We had worked hard these past few months to get into shape, get our gimmick worked out to a tee, got our attire designed, tailor made, specially fitted, and paid for. We worked our asses off to pay for the clothes which would make us at least look the part. All we needed now was a place to compete on a weekly basis that paid well. Easy enough. As any indy circuit wrestler will tell you getting your name out there in this business in this day and age is the most difficult task anyone can ever imagine. There are so many punks out there who will gladly use your shoulders as a ladder rung on their way to the top, and I can hardly blame them. It's cutthroat. Getting your name out there is the most difficult part, once it's out there keeping it there is hardly taxing. As we sat across the dining table from one another, paper scattered across the pine surface, we talked about debut ideas, about how I should be the one to 'work the mike' whilst he stayed out of view for a while. Let the fans grow accustomed to me; grow to like me and accept me as a professional athlete before introducing anything too spectacular or amazingly different as a valet like him. I agreed. I wanted to get some respect for my in-ring performance first, get the fans won over by wrestling to the best of my ability and pulling out the most interesting moves in my repertoire. The paper. The paper on the table, this is what I'm trying to tell you about. Words scribbled across some pieces in a disorganised brainstorming session, some printed neatly with watermarks and letterheads and official signatures 'IN BLANK INK ONLY', our names 'IN BLOCK LETTERS ONLY', our souls 'NEATLY PRINTED INSIDE THE BOX'. Contracts to sign away three years of our lives, on bright orange paper. Oh how signing them made my retinas feel uneasy. But AWC had something special going with their bright colour gimmick. Every federation and their development federations had black ring curtains, black logos, dark this, dark that. The bright company represented something completely different. Not to sound cliché, but they were Fresh. Yeah, they sure were Fresh. Fresh enough to pay us a pretty penny to whore ourselves out to millions of people every single week. It's hardly the most difficult job in the world you know. Anybody with a marginal amount of business sense and a set of dumbbells can break onto the scene. It's all about networking these days. Sucking the right cocks, bedding the right people. In both the metaphorical and literal senses. Not that I care. The papers had all been signed, and I was so excited to finally live the dream that I had been working hard for since I was just a small boy idolising the wrestlers that entered my living room every Saturday dinnertime. And now I'm rich. Rich and fucking famous. I began to tidy the papers, sorting them into neat piles. Official documents, brainstorms, and throw-aways. I wanted everything to be spick-and-span for my first day at the office. Oh how it made me feel giddy to know that I had finally made it. To be up there competing with the big boys. To be a big boy. My mother would be proud. If she wasn't dead and buried. As I stacked the paper into three neat piles he sat and observed me, as he did often, saying nothing. It made me nervous sometimes it felt as if I was being examined on my ability to stack paper in the correct manner. I feared that if a leaf of paper were to break free from the rigid piles that he would take me in to the car park and give me a good thrashing. A bead of sweat formed over my brow as I obsessively ensured that the piles were perfectly straight and there was no fear of them tipping over or becoming dishevelled. I picked the throw-away pile up and slid myself to a vertical stance before heading over to the kitchenette part of the apartment, stepping on the pedal of the trash can and dropping the pile into the clean white bag. A satisfying clunk resounded as the heavy wad of paper slapped against the base of the bin, which previous to this had been empty. Now the bin wasn't empty. Maybe I should empty it. I better fucking empty it. I lifted the lid off and scooped the white bag up, pulling at the yellow string to close it up, before placing it next to the bin and replacing the bag with a fresh one. I lifted the bag up and headed across the apartment to the window. Already open after a previous discussion about the temperature of the room, I dropped the bag out of the open window and directly in to the large container at the bottom of the apartment block. A satisfying rustle of the collection of plastic bags filled with trash, their contents cluttering against one another found its way to the window. I smiled a little before returning to the apartment. Oh shit, I left the lid up on the bin.
