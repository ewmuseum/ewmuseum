Leaving

2 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-2-06 It was another hot summer day on the Tomas Farm. Teresa sat on Eric’s bed as she watched him neatly fold a hunter green undershirt into a suit case. The unspoken silence lingered in the room like thick smoke. Moments before, Eric told his baby sis he was being shipped out yet again. For how long, he didn’t know. The situation overseas wasn’t getting any better and the only thing that ran through Teresa’s mind is that dreadful day she would get the call every family member of a soldier prays not to receive. “You’re packing like you’re leaving tomorrow. We still have the 4th to celebrate.” A knot began to form in Teresa’s gut as Eric carefully removed a formal uniform out of his closet and placed it in a long zippered bag. About this time Mama Tomas walked in with a blue and gold football jersey with the number 85 sewn on the back along with the name E. TOMAS. She proudly held it out for her children to see. “It smelled like mothballs so I washed it for you Honey. I left your helmet and football in the living room also. Don’t forget your swimming trunks and sun block. The last thing you want to get at summer camp besides poison ivy is sunburn.” Teresa watched Eric take his old high school football jersey from his mother and placed it beside his uniform. The old woman noticed the National Guard uniform and the combat boots beside the bed. Her brows furrowed as she gave her son a stern look. “What is this Eric?” “Momma, I’m leaving in a few w days. I told you that.” “I know son. You’re going to football camp. Why are you packing your father’s uniforms? If I told you once, I’ve told you a thousand times, when you grow up, you’re gonna get a civilian job. The Services is only gonna get you killed.” “Mamma… Daddy’s…” “Eric…” Teresa chided interrupting her brother and shook her head. Loosing their father the first time was hard enough on Mrs. Tomas. To be reminded her husband was dead, would be that day lived all over again. She slid off the bed and led their mother out of the room. “Don’t worry Mamma. Eric will be just fine. I smell something good. Are you cooking our favorite?” Mamma Tomas smiled. Her face softened once more as her son left her mind. “Oh yes, I just remembered, chicken and dumplings. I best go check on dinner.” A deep sigh exited Teresa lungs as she watched her mother descend the wooden stairs. Her condition wasn’t getting any better. That’s why Teresa was so bothered about Eric leaving. With her going away with the East Coast Tour, and Eric to fight in Iraq, who will take care of Mrs. Tomas? “Don’t worry Teresa. Mamma is taken care of.” “What you mean?” Teresa leaned against the door facing as her brother placed his old jersey on top of his dresser. “I hired a family take care of the farm and Mamma while we’re gone.” “What?” “Hear me out. The mother is a registered nurse; the father runs a repair shop in town. They have two young kids, two and 5.” Teresa shakes her head. “That would be too much for Mama. The kids would make her nervous and to have strangers taking over her home. It won’t work.” Eric smiles “It would bring life back to this place. The farm hands aren’t enough. Listen, I need to tell you something.” Teresa watched Eric’s smile fade and stepped into the room and sat beside him on the bed. Her mind raced on what could be more important than him leaving for war. “I’ve know about my deployment for some time now.” Confusion set on Teresa’s features. Eric sighed knowing the outcome of his ‘confession’ would be. When she doesn’t respond, he continues. “I got the call right before you went to jail. I didn’t think you needed any more stress than you already had so I didn’t say anything.” Teresa continued to look on with a blank expression as she waited on him to make his point. “I called Scott to post your bail. You know that, but the reason I called him is because I know he is the only one that can look after you when I’m gone.” Here it came. It started with that certain spark in her baby blues that made her eyes appear fiery red. Eric braced himself for the explosion. Then it came. Teresa bolted off the bed and the stare she gave him could burn a hole through steel. “YOU DID WHAT?!!” “Shhhhh! Mama’s downstairs…” Teresa pursed her lips fighting the urge to smack the taste out of Eric’s mouth then spoke in a harsh whisper. “I can take care of myself Eric! I don’t need no damn babysitter! How dare you? I thought you didn’t like him.” “I don’t, but I do respect the man. Hey, I could’ve called Jack Slayter.” Her ears were burning red as her blood pressure soared. She took a lung for her brother but he effortlessly held her back by instantly jumping up from the bed and wrapping his huge muscular arms around her chest. “Calm down Teresa! You’re gonna give yourself a stroke.” “Jack wouldn't have posted my bail. He’d simply tell her I probably deserved being in jail and to let me rot there.” “I know Teresa, I know...” “Screw you Eric for bringing Scott back into my life, now let me go!” Teresa wriggled from Eric’s grasp and raced downstairs and out the door grabbing her truck keys. Once inside the trick, she slammed the door and pulled out her phone from her pocket. After a quick call, she peeled out of the driveway and heading west to the other side of town. Minutes later, the Midnight Purple Chevy truck was parked in a driveway of a rental home. Nothing was fancy about the place. It had light blue vinyl siding and a few shrubs outlined the small one bedroom home. Scott waited at the door as Teresa approached with a purpose. He saw the stern expression on Teresa’s face and instantly became worried. “Hey Teresa, what’s wrong?” She stood at the door refusing to enter the house. Her eyes bore into Scott’s green eyes. “Is it true? Is the reason you came back because Eric called you to look after me?” More confusion crosses Scott’s features. He had been preparing to go to work on his new film when Teresa called. He even called his director to tell him he would be a little late. “What are you talking about Teresa? Eric called me saying you were in a jam and didn’t know who else to call. Teresa, I could've just sent the damn bail money but I brought it myself...because I wanted to see you.'.” “BULLSHIT! Eric told me. He's leaving for Iraq on the 5th. He called you cause you're the only one he trusts to look after me. I told him and I'm telling you. I don't need no damn babysitter!” Teresa’s screamed and punched the wooden door behind Scott. Her knuckled left an imprint. Scott flinched mostly out of startlement. He knew too well about Teresa’s temper to be surprised. However, he didn’t let her accusations pass so easily. “I don’t know what the hell you’re talking about Teresa! I didn’t come back to be a freaking bodyguard. If that’s all you and your dumb brother thinks of me then I’m gone. Once my film is complete then my rich ass is going back to Louisiana.” A few seconds of unspoken silence pass between the two. Their eyes communicating what their pride refused themselves to admit. Finally Teresa’s voice softened almost pleading. “Scott, don’t...” His voice too was softer but the sharpness still remained. He held the door in his hand looking down at Teresa. His other hand started to reach for her but instead combed through his shoulder-length dark hair. “No Teresa, you don’t. You refuse to believe the truth. I don’t need that shit and if you can’t believe me, then I don’t need you either.” The door quietly shuts in Teresa’s face. She stood motionless for a moment staring. Her fingers curled into a fist and rose to knock, but after a hesitation, Teresa’s hand fell down. She turned her back and returned to her truck. ON the other side of the door, Scott picked up a picture that sat on a small table. It was a picture of him and Teresa when they were wrestling together. He turned to look at the door as if he would reopen it, but instead looked back at the picture and tossed it across the room. The glass shattered over two smiling faces.
