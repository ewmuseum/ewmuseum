Attention Defecit Disorder

24 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1-24-06 We see a young Japanese boy, dressed in his classic middle school uniform. He's running down the tight Kobe streets...past the waterfront. No one around him pays any attention. He darts up the stairs of his family's apartment complex, nearly tripping over himself to get up the stairs. After a few flights, he reaches the third floor, and navigates the cluttered hallway to his door. He pushes the door open, and runs into the small apartment, kicking his shoes off as he runs. He reaches his mother's skirt, the skirt for her work uniform, but she brushes right past him. Confused, the boy spins around to watch her leave, and all the mother (your typical middle-age Japanese woman) can do is offer a disappointed frown and the words “Sumimasen, Jiro-kun.” She is out the door like a ghost, and gone. We cut to a different middle-school boy, much whiter, and so skinny that his football pads look simply ridiculous on him. He's standing on the sidelines of a Pop Warner football game, the action going on all around him on the field. The gruff-looking football coach takes a look at the skinny young boy, and just shakes his head. He says “I'm sorry, Joey.” Joey doesn't even notice that the coach is talking to him. He's looking in the stands, looking for someone sitting on the bleachers. His eyes search and search while the football game goes on without him. He doesn't find who he's looking for. Back in Japan, Jiro is older, sitting on his grandfather's couch, out in rural Japan. The ancient Japanese katana is the primary element of decoration in the house, they are everywhere. He's watching something on television, with an unhappy look on his face. There's a knock at the door, and his grandfather answers it. His grandfather returns from the door, and a young American man with bright green hair enters behind him. The American man holds his hand out to Jiro, who lies on the couch. Jiro jumps to his feet. He shakes the American's outstretched hand, and they both smile. Back in America, we see a flourescent light tube shatter over the head of the skinny young man from before, except now he's even skinnier, and taller. He and another young man stand in front of a trampoline in As blood starts to gush from a new gash on his forehead, a portly woman steps out onto the back porch with her cigarette. She clucks her mouth in the general direction of the two boys, but says nothing and returns to the house. Joey bleeds from his forehead, and drops to the grass. Tears well up in his eyes, and they have absolutely nothing to do with the wound on his forehead. We flash forward to today. An imaginary FRESH! Joey Six and Jiro Sennosuke step out into the lights and sounds of the arena. The crowd cheers madly. They both smile. Both men look out in the audience, searching for people. Specific people. They do not find them. Though you'll find that Collision Course's members are wildly different, they've both got something important in common. They're looking for attention, from specific people. Attention that just isn't coming.
