Stacy was on the couch, reading a book with her feet propped up on the coffee table. “Did Rory ask if he could use your computer?” I asked my lovely future wife. She looked up at me, smiled and nodded, then went back to her book. I walked in on Rory who was playing a game of Mah Jong. I watched him matched up the tiles with the greatest accuracy and lightning speed. I was amazed at how many matches he could find in a matter of seconds. Finally, he’d completed the puzzle and the time he did it in shocked me. He finished the puzzle in two and a half minutes. “Rory,” I gasped, still shocked at Rory’s quickness and intelligence. “How in the hell did you solve that puzzle so fast? It would take me about thirty minutes trying to complete the thing.” “I dunno,” he replied, about as shocked as I was. “It’s hard to explain. I guess I can see the matches and how to get to them.” I left the office still shocked at Rory’s advanced intelligence. I did read research about severely autistic people and a small handful of them have genius IQ’s. Rory obviously fit the bill. “Who’s ready fer some pizza?” It was dinnertime in the Farren household. Stacy had planned on making a huge twenty-four inch pizza for everyone to have for dinner. She contemplated whether to make another one - after watching Rory scarf down three peanut butter and jelly sandwiches for lunch - she decided against it. The pizza she made had a Chicago-style deep dish crust, a blanket of cheese and toppings that everyone liked. Everyone sat down, as Stacy brought out the large masterpiece, placing it in the middle of the table. “Let’s dig in!” Everyone took a slice to start off. Practically everyone - including Sully, who was sitting next to Rory and Meryl - watched Rory eat. It seemed to everyone that Rory had an insatiable appetite for food. He took a huge bite out of his pizza, chewing happily on it and swallowed it. “The pizza’s good, St...St...” Rory trailed off and started to twitch. His left hand went up to his head, rubbing it. “My head hurts...” Then what happened next shocked everyone... Rory quickly got up from his chair, and started to roar in pain. He staggered backward, tripped over his chair and gripped his head, continuing to scream in agony. I was the first one to act first. “Rory, what’s wrong?” “MY HEAD HURTS! SOMEONE MAKE IT STOP!” He started to head butt the floor, trying to alleviate the pain. He then got to his feet and started to run for the door. Everyone was screaming when he ran out the door. I ran after him calling for his name. Rory continued to scream bloody murder, screaming that his head was hurting him. Everyone within a three-block radius stepped out on their front porch, wondering what the screaming was about. Eventually, Rory’s screaming started to subside, and his running was reduced to jogging, then power walking, then... stopped. I ran up to him, kneeled in front of him, begging that he was alright. He didn’t look good, his face was slacked and his mouth was moving, but no words were coming out. Finally he started to speak, “Mikey...help me...” He fell forward, passing out. I managed to catch him into my arms. He wasn’t moving. I called his name several times, trying to revive him by slapping his cheeks. He wouldn’t budge. “Somebody call 911!” I yelled at anyone who was listening. I continued to attempt to revive him, repeating the same words over and over in my head... Don’t die, Rory... Please, don’t die... “He has a brain tumor deep inside the cerebral cortex.” Doctor Murphy Solomon, a respected neurosurgeon in Boston, was showing me CAT scans of Rory’s brain after bringing him into the hospital several hours ago. After getting the CAT scans, Rory had slipped into a coma. Doctor Solomon circled the cerebral cortex, where different shades of color were seen dominating it. I was completely baffled at this discovery. How did Rory get it? How long has he had it? “I don’t understand,” I said, looking at the pictures of Rory’s brain. “He’d always been a healthy young man. How did he get it? How long has he had it?” “I’m not exactly sure. As advanced as the brain tumor is, he should’ve been dead a long time ago, depending when he got it. But I guess it’s possible that he’d gotten the brain tumor when he was younger, laid dormant for several years and then took affect.” “Can you operate on it?” I asked. Doctor Solomon shook his head. “It’s inoperable. If we tried, it’ll turn him into a vegetable quicker. This tumor is effecting his basic motor functions. If he go in there in operate, he wouldn’t be able to see, feel, hear, dress himself, feed himself, etc. He’ll be a vegetable.” “Will he ever wake up from his coma?” “I seriously doubt it. He has little to no chance to ever get of it.” I let out a sigh. “Let’s say that he does wake up from his coma. What options does he have?” “Well, if he does walk up from his coma - which I doubt he will; his brain went through so much because of his tumor - his only option would be radiation therapy to shrink the tumor. If that works, he’ll have another five months to live. If it doesn’t... I don’t think he’ll make it until the end of the month.” “Can I see him?” “Sure. One of the nurses can take you up.” A few minutes later, I was in Rory’s room, sitting in a chair next to him. He looked peaceful laying there. He was hooked up to a breathing machine to help him breathe. “Hey, Rory...” I couldn’t say anything else. All I could do was sit there and watch him die. I got up and left the room. I couldn’t bring myself to watch him die. About halfway down the corridor, I heard doctors and nurses running into Rory’s room, working feverishly on him. I knew the truth - he was going to die. With a heavy heart I left the hospital and went home. I was on my way home when I saw Rory standing on the side of the road, waving at me, as if he was telling me goodbye. It was his ghost waving at me. On the drive home, I cried for my cousin Rory.
