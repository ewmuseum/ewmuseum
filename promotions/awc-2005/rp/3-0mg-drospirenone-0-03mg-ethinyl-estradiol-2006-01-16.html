Date: what's that? Pathetic Wanna talk about cliche? How about overdone? Well... on January 10th, Amy Campbell looked the part of a punk rocker with her tattoos, tattered tanktop and equally decrepid jeans. To top it off, she was at a firing range - you heard right - with her best friend who, by default, was also littered with tattoos and a rather unusually overgrown mohawk, worn down to one side of his head. The 'firing one off' and premature shot jokes were filling the air as Jeph packed his handgun back into a locker and smiled to his tiny redheaded friend. ''Ready to peel?'' She looked abnormally nervous, and nodded eagerly without a word - hurried to get out the door and as far away from deadly weaponry as possible. As tough as she pretended to be - or was - an unforgettable night years before was etched into her brain. At a hotel after a wrestling event back in fWo, when it was worth being back in, she literally took a bullet for the heroic Brand Frontier - forever proving that you didn't need muscle and tights to be a hero, just a heart. Unfortunately, it also proved that Amy wasn't made of steel... or kevlar, for that matter. She still had the scar on her hip and the sound of gunfire in her psyche. Before there was a moment in broken thought to realize it, Amy had been sitting in the passenger's side of her own car, staring straight ahead for a solid minute. She'd been ignoring everything said to her... until, of course, she got pinched on the arm. ''Ames? You here on earth or what?'' ''Fucker!'' ''... All these years, and that's basically all I ever hear you call me. Come on,'' he teased, ''What's my name?'' ''Bitch.'' ''Now you sound like one of those two-bit wrasslers who're alw--'' ''Not now?'' ''What's up with you right now?'' grumbled Jeph, ''PMS or something? You're far from your fun-yet-bitchy self.'' ''Jeph...'' She shook her head, but already knew the answer... It was the prick. That fucking guy. ''I love him, and I hate it.'' You Know Who... Simple dreams. Things that hadn't happened, but pointed out how dischordant she would've been if they had. And what in the hell was up with that headache, anyway? She hadn't been drinking, hadn't been high... hadn't been doing anything but sleeping. Sleeping soundly, like a little baby - all alone and perfectly happy about it. Nothing was amiss, and nothing should have been, but the lonely tingle up her spine wouldn't subside. Perhaps because the tingle she felt was her own arm, softly and unconsciously massaging out the kinks that years of fighting had bestowed upon her. A little light reading might help put her back to sleep - she thought so, at least - so Amy let a hand idly roam to her bedside table, a poorly assembled shelf built at an all-ngiht Wal-Mart with no honest function in sight. The piece of junk couldn't hold anything heavier than the ten or so printed pages she'd set atop it. They were biographies - not of Motley Crue, Tommy Lee or Madonna, though. Much less interesting, actually - pages printed from website on Sage, Ellis Nash and Teresa Tomas. In all honesty, Tomas was the only one Aimz had even a moments' hesitation to face, since she'd garnered plenty of accolades after almost as many years as Amy'd worked in PTC. Sage appeared out of nowhere and seemed to want to be someone, though that could be dangerous if she had any actual ability, which was doubtful. Nash was... well... just the same, being nobody Amy knew or cared much about. ''I take PCW down step by step, and they give me this shit. What's next, a women's division?'' Everybody in the match with her had something to prove, which would make it all the more dangerous... but that didn't mean it'd be difficult. She was dealing with bottom-feeders, people who couldn't even call themselves PTC's finest - and that was sad. There were no real gimmicks and no discernable motives outside of beating the big, bad Aimz and collecting a paycheck. Her popularity would make her a notch in their belts, while they were very little more than gum on her shoe - maybe because she hated belts, they were always too stiff and awkward to fit properly. She only ever worked well with title belts, and those were a very rare sight around the little redhead's waist thanks to the human propensity for semi-political garbage in every competetive facet of life. 'Your head hurts because that chip you're carrying around on your shoulder's too heavy.', She could practically hear Darcy saying it, cocky as ever. But really, which one of them had been working for the past four years? ...And why the hell was she thinking about him again? ''Jesus...'' she thought, ''Haven't I got sleeping pills somewhere?'' Nope. And her cell rings in the middle of the night. How's that for a life? ''Jeph? Why the fu...'' Hey, speakerphone. ''Meet me at the coffee shop on your corner.'' ''Huh?'' ''Now.'' Whytwokay? In the new milennium... Her face was rimmed with highlights of neon pink. His was tipped by months of embittered loathing. Hers, snarling. His? Truly hating what he'd become - a doormat. ''I can't believe this shit,'' she said, ''These indy pieces of shit are booking me against these chick-only lineups. Do they have any idea what I've done? I don't fight girls...'' ''Come on Amy...they're not that bad. And they usually smell nicer.'' Darcy clearly was not in the mood for another Amy rant. He knew his attempt to quell it with a wisecrack was probably ill-advised...but still, he had to try. ''They're a bunch of stupid little girls trying to make it in a world where the only women who rule have to have the killer instinct of one in a million,'' Aimz groaned, ''I might be young, but I can fuck guys up... these pixies can't touch shit. Do you seriously think I could look good at ALL being made to look like I'm equal to these twits?'' She flailed a photo of cornstalks with breast implants and hair extensions, ''Seriously, Darcy!'' No dice, Darcy thought, cursing under his breath. Still, maybe I can put this one away early... ''Come on Amy, that's bullshit,'' Darcy said, muting a classic Kings/Oilers edition of Hockey Night in Canada. ''Do you REALLY think you're the only girl strong enough to play in the sandbox with the big boys? Think of all the guys who refused to work with you because they thought 'you'd get hurt going up against a man.' And here you are pissing on all these girls for the same reason. You only need to look at yourself to know that not every girl in this business is a prissy bimbo. And even if you're somehow right about this, all you have to do is show up, squash them, look like a monster and get paid on top of it. So I fail to see the problem.'' With that, Darcy unmuted the TV. 'Game, set and match...I hope.' Even in her delusional and half-awake flashbacks to years before, he always had to be right. It's What You Need Anything to get Darcy Crisis out of her head - even a midnight walk through the rain. ''What the fuck is this?'' Aimz had walked in the dead of the night, almost a full block. It's not that she wasn't in shape, she just hated putting any actual effort into anything. When it came to possible drama, though, she wasn't far... just a little fed up that her best friend had picked the middle of the night to call her out for coffee. ''Drinks,'' Jeph sighed. He looked like hell, which wasn't unusual - but there was a pain in his eye, ''And honesty.'' ''Honesty?'' He nodded, ''Honesty. Like, honestly... when's your motivation making a comeback?'' She knew exactly what he meant, because she'd been feeling the twinge of tired defeat on the edge of her conscience since she'd gone to AWC. Everything she got involved in fell out. ''When are they gonna book me against someone who's seen a win since PCW? When do I face a real fighter, and not some fucking chick?'' ''That depends,'' said Jeph, ''When are you gonna get over yourself?'' Amy instantly shot to her feet. How could he have honestly just said that? ''What... did you just say?'' Jeph rolled his eyes, patting the end of the table that Amy had just abandoned, ''C'mon, Ames - sit the hell down. You've taken worse from me, and you know I'm the only one who's straight with you like this.'' She simply stared him down for what seemed like an eternity. His eyes wandered over the table, to the sugar packets next to the napkins and then back to Aimz. Hers, however, didn't move... he was the only person quite so 'straight' with her, but that didn't change that she was naturally inclined to be offended. That HAD, after all, been a direct blow to her ego. The kicker, though, was that she sat back down almost immediately - spreading a smirk across Jeph's face. ''Thank you. Now...'' he sighed again, ''You've gotta figure yourself out when it comes to this stuff. It worries me that you just sit there all day - you don't go to the gym, you don't do anything but obsess over the internet and finding out everything about the people you're fighting. You read and read and read... but you're not learning anything solid about these people. You know title achievements don't mean much when it comes to measuring a real fighter, so you ignore basically everything you're reading anyway, and that only leaves you sitting around with no idea, aside from a few message board descriptions, how these men and women fight. You're lucky as hell that Andy M. was so out of his element, or you would've barely eeked that one out. You might have a winning streak, but this isn't fWo or PRIME you're dealing with - talent means the world here, what your name is doesn't mean a damned thing and I think you know that. The management knows it, that's why you're facing people like Sage and not Pierce Lavelle.'' Amy raised the brow above her blue eye. It was odd, because he was right... even though Jeph Howe knew jack about wrestling. The guy who'd barely watched a minute of a fight was - in that moment - smarter about the business than the girl who'd been in it, winning - and mostly losing - matches for six years. She decided to humor him a little. He was her best friend, after all, ''And what do you suggest?'' Jeph grinned, ''I suggest you get your ass to a martial arts class at least once a week, keep working on what you know. You're quickly losing the killer instinct I always loved about you, mainly because of what you call the 'bottom-feeders' you're fighting. You've become too accustomed to busting your ass to beat Karina Wolfendens. It spoiled you, because now you're just assuming you can plough through any PCW alumni or 'chick' that comes your way. You've been lucky with AWC so far, but you're gonna slip up sooner or later. Don't obsess, but treat everybody like a Karina - because, no matter how lame most of them might be, you're setting yourself up to lose because you underestimated some PTC underdog. THAT would be embarassing.'' They hadn't ordered coffee, she couldn't drink it anyway. They had, however, made progress... Amy just wouldn't admit it. She stood up, cocked her head and patted Jeph on the hand with an unspoken appreciation - but again, she couldn't admit it openly, so she 'humored' him. ''I'll try taking the advice,'' smirked the redhead, ''But... since when did you become a wrestling fan?'' Jeph's face hinted no humor. When he looked her in the eye then, he was entirely serious, blunt and... correct. ''Since you needed one.''
