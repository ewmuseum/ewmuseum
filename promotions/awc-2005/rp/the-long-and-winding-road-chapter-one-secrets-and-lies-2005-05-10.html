The Long and Winding Road: Chapter One - Secrets And Lies

10 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10th May 2005 THIS IS A CROSSPOST FROM THE BACKUP BOARDS - THIS IS NOT A NEW ROLEPLAY OR THE ROLEPLAY FOR THIS WEEK! Cheers - Kit PACT “Just tell me what I need to hear, doc,” I say as Andrews removes the blood pressure cuff from my bicep. He writes something in my notes and sighs. “Chris…why the hell are you doing this to yourself? You could’ve retired years ago but you’re still going out, night after night, pulling out crazy stunts like you did last Friday – stunts that often don’t pay off.” I thought back to the Asai corkscrew moonsault I tried in my match against Strider. “Live fast, die young, leave a beautiful corpse – that’s what I say, doc.” “Carry on the way you are and the corpse you leave behind will be neither young nor beautiful.” In consternation he threw my chart down on the bed. “How many years have we known each other?” “Look, Jim…I don’t need this right now. All I need is your professional medical clearance so I can wrestle this week.” Andrews took a deep breath. “Pact…Chris…I can’t do that. I’m sorry.” I paused, hurt. “Why?” He didn’t say a word. Instead he walked over to the computer that sat on his desk and opened a file. “Look – this is a normal spine.” He brought up an MRI scan of a healthy spine – vertebrae perfectly aligned, good cushions of fluid, bold black blood vessels, intricate nerve networks. “…and this is the scan of your spine we took earlier.” Flashing up my own MRI scan on the monitor I immediately understood his concern. I’d seen enough of these scans before to know the prognosis wasn’t good – two herniated discs, one ruptured disc, one broken disc and the nerve network very nearly bisected by a rib that had been forced backwards from some long forgotten impact, which had subsequently knitted in an awkward position. He observed the shock on my face and allowed his voice to drop to a concerned whisper. “Pact…if I clear you to wrestle this week, there’s a very real chance you’ll be permanently paralysed. As things are, it’s a miracle you can sit there without writhing in agony, never mind the fact you can still walk and wrestle.” I nodded. “I understand your concern. So, are you gonna clear me or what?” He let out a short, derisive snort. “There’s no way I can clear you until you’ve had surgery on that back. No way in hell.” I nodded. “How long will that take?” Burns thought about this for a moment. “Factoring your age and rehabilitation time…at least 18 months.” “I see. Well, if that’s the way it’s gotta be, then that’s the way it’s gotta be.” “I’m glad you see things my way. Wait there a sec, I’ll just go and get my diary so we can pencil you in for the operation.” With that he left the room. When he returned two minutes later, I was gone. ALEXI Things have been going from bad to worse for Pact in recent weeks. First the CLW folds, leaving his issues with Devastation and Gunner Malone up in the air, then loses the LCW Championship, then he’s defeated by his nemesis Sugar Shane Perry in NLCW, then he crashes out of the AWC Transatlantic Championship tournament in the first round…the guy’s fast becoming a liability. I mean, it’s not as if all this is unexpected – fuck no – he’s nearly 49 years old, he’s had a good run (of that there’s no doubt). But it’s like mama used to say, the time comes when you have to realise last week’s borscht isn’t flavoursome - its just sour. So what can you do? You can throw it out, wasting money and valuable ingredients…or you can add a little sugar and help it go a bit longer. And this is the problem I’m presented with, y’see? Cause I know it’s getting to the stage where Pact either needs to be stuck in the trash or rescued…and I DON’T KNOW WHICH I SHOULD DO! Heh. So I thought I’d come to you for help, seeing as you know him so well. What was that? Ah yes. Better than anyone, some would say. Those in the know would say so anyway. I mean, you were the fi… Right, thanks for the reminder. We can’t talk about that stuff on the phone, can we? Sorry, sir. Yes, sir. No, sir, I wouldn’t want that. It’d definitely be a shame. So…will you help me? I mean, him? Will you help him? Cause I just KNOW if you were part of his life again he’d just – y’know – regain the drive he lost somewhere along the way. You will? Oh thank you! I promise you, you will NOT regret this Mr. Ki…uh…I mean…sir. -click- Pact I sat on the trolley, clutching my medical file tight to my chest, the gravity of what I’d just done beginning to sink in. He’s just trying to help. I know that. But at the same time, I absolutely refuse to lose 18 months of my dwindling career on the whim of a quack. It’s easy for these guys – they sit in their comfortable company-funded offices and decide who should wrestle and who should stay off the card. The concept of risk taking is completely alien to them. This is a gamble I’m more than willing to bet my body on. I’ve got family to provide for, a legacy to put together and a reputation to uphold. I won’t give up until my body does, and when it does I’ll know I’ve given all I can to the industry. I’ll be able to retire with a clear conscience. Until then, I’ll carry on. And I’ll do so at any cost. I alighted the trolley at Yonkers and decided I wanted a cup of coffee, so I went to a little place I know, set back from the main streets. The April sun beat down on my back with surprising force. All around me the faceless Stepford wives that infest this quiet little suburb during the daylight hours went about their business in sundresses and trendy designer sunglasses, each with a perfectly formed, symmetrical smile burned into their pathetic faces. I found myself wondering what my life would’ve been like had I chosen to be one of them. Hell. Unadulterated hell. I opened the door of Giuseppe’s Coffee House and took my favourite table. Conchita, his illegal immigrant waitress, brought me over a cup of my usual (double tall skinny mocha, no idea how to say it in Italian, the way it’s written on the menu – other languages are all Greek to me). I sipped at it thoughtfully and recalled the email I’d received from some random member of the AWC booking team that morning. “You’ve got a fatal four way tag match on ‘Fresh!’ this week.” Whatever the hell one of those is. Not that it really matters. After my frankly embarrassing loss at Zero To Hero I found myself aching to cut loose and prove myself. Strider exposed the extent to which my age is becoming a factor in my matchups. I don’t like it one bit. If things are gonna go my way in this league I’m gonna need to start thinking strategically. First things first, though…I need to get cleared. ??? So, the prodigal son returns to the flock once more. He may a little beaten up, a little broken, a little slower…but he’s still my favourite. And I still love him like the son God refused to bless me with. I’ve seen his matches of late – that twinkle, that little gleam in his eye is still there, just as it was years ago. Just as it was the last time I saw him. How long ago was that? More years than I care to count, if I’m honest. It’s been too long since the world has known me, since the world has known my name. Not that too many knew it before anyway. That was the way I wanted it, of course…but a small part of me always yearned for the limelight. And now I’m going to have it, through my greatest creation. Through him I’ll live the dream I was destined to see through. I made him a star – now he’s gonna make me rich! ALEXI It wasn’t until later that the doubts started to creep in. There was a set of rules my father had imparted to me on the day I left my hometown (so long ago, but not so long for me to forget): never leave home without a pack of tissues, always dress as well as your income allows and don’t fuck with members of the criminal fraternity. Until today I’d kept all of those rules (more or less – occasionally forays into Spanish Harlem for coke and meth), and I’d lived a good life. I can’t help but feel that that one phonecall has marked a definite change in everything, and that it won’t be long until everyone feels its effects. PACT I sat across the desk from Dr. Burns, AWC's resident physical therapist. “Okay, Pact, going by the info you've given me I can see no reason not to clear you for action this week. Have a good match!” “Oh believe me, doc,” I replied, “I will. I guarantee it.” View Pact 's Biography
