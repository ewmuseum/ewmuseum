I Pray You Learn To Trust

16 January 2006

Date: 16 January, 2006 a. if God has a master plan Ellis is twenty-six, now, when she finally realizes that this is what being a cop means. It means letting go and cutting herself loose and saying goodbye to people that are as fundamental to her as a gun. This is what it is: standing up and bearing it, readjusting her reflexes and learning all over again. It feels like fighting for breath, it feels like losing something important like a hand or a limb or a heart. It feels like everything that came easy, before, everything that was effortless and smooth is a struggle all over again, like she’s just had a stroke or like some basic, crucial part that made everything work right is gone. It’s frustrating as all hell, and sometimes she just wants, wants. Wants, but Tuesday Ellis stands at the bottom of Harper Bridge with a radar gun, tagging cars like Whitney Houston popping pills anyway. When she’s done, she speeds at a steady sixty-five to Bronx PD. There, she meets Agent Howie Landon. “Brief me,” Landon strolls through the hallway. Ellis follows on his heel, shuffling through a manilla folder. “I’ve got a crew down at Dennis’ place right now. Chandler interrogated him a day or two ago, moderate reason for suspicion. There’s Geoff Gibson, yet to be brought in, ex-boyfriend, blah fucking dah. I had Vanessa go and talk to his boss an hour ago. Forensics was at Katherine’s a couple hours ago too, results should be in tomorrow or Tuesday on the shirt she was wearing. In conclusion,” Ellis snaps the folder shut, smiling, “We’ve got shit.” “Tell me something new,” Landon collapses into his chair. “I brushed my hair this morning,” Ellis collapses into the other. “Say it ain’t so.” “It’s so.” Landon types something. “Do we have any sufficient leads?” “I wouldn’t call Dennis a prime suspect. I won’t know about Geoff until Vanessa gets back. I’m going to go over the evidence again, see if I can get anything off of that. I can’t really do anything without the Forensics results, though.” “Howie,” Laura Carmichael interrupts, comes bursting into the office, tits nearly falling out of the NYU ringer, “We’ve got a problem.” “What now?” “There’s a hold-up at the Compass Bank on Fifth. Hostage involved. Kennard and Teddington radioed for back-up.” “Right,” Landon keeps his eyes on the monitor, jabbing at the keyboard, “Uh, get Irving and Davenport down there, I guess. And Nash. I want you there, too. It’ll give you something to do while you’re waiting for those results. Supervise them. Make sure they don’t do any stupid shit.” Ellis sighs. Laura smirks behind her. “You too, Carmichael.” “Fuck.” They take Ellis’ car. Laura asks how she could afford an Infiniti. Ellis answers she stole it, why the fuck do you care? Laura narrows her eyes, replies, it was just a question, Ellis. No need for snark. Ellis responds, you are dumb and speeds through a yellow light. When they get there, Laura stays in the car, says, you go see what’s going on, I have to load my gun. Ellis says, whatever, and goes to see what’s going on. Kennard says that David Ryan is a thirty-three year old business tycoon who really isn’t a business tycoon anymore because his company just crashed. “Really crashed. Like, he-doesn’t-have-a-penny really crashed. We IDed him off the car tag,” Kennard explains. Then he goes on to explain that Tech is working on tapping into the security cameras, “Over there in that van.” Ellis goes over to the van. Mike Sullivan, Tech’s head and surprisingly almost identical to Richard Rubin, is untangling wires. “How’s it coming, Sully?” “Well, it’s coming,” Mike answers, swiping at his forehead before returning to the wires. “I should be able to get a link in a few more minutes.” “Hurry up,” Ellis perches herself on the van, “I need to see what’s going on before we can do anything.” Laura comes running over two minutes later, shoving her gun into its holster. “What do we got?” “Shit,” Ellis replies, inspecting a cuticle, “All we know is that he is a bankrupt thirty-three year old. Sully is working on the security cameras so we can assess.” Blondie perches across from Ellis. “Who’s our RP?” “Teddington was in the neighborhood. Saw a car crashed through the West wall of Compass. Figured it was something bad.” “He crashed a car into the bank?” “Affirmitave,” Ellis responds, irritated, “What? Are you fucking blind? Did you not see the Volvo jutting out from the building?” “I didn’t notice, no.” “You are a fucki – ” “We’ve got signal,” Michael interrupts. Ellis and Laura jump up. Ellis shoves Laura out of the way so she can see. Laura growls and hunches over Ellis’ shoulder, blonde meshing with brunette. “Alright,” Ellis grabs her radio, “Copy, Kennard. We’ve got the suspect with a gun to the hostage’s head while another subject is filling a sack with twenties. There are about ten to twelve others on the floor. Over.” “Copy,” Kennard says, fingering his gun, “What are we going to do? Over.” “Tell Irving to find the phone number of the bank. We’re going to have a little chat with him.” Irving finds the phone number and radios it to Ellis, who jams it into her cell phone number by number. She shrugs her radio back into her pocket and lifts the reciever to her ear. It rings three times before someone picks it up. “What?” “David?” “Yeah, what?” Ellis climbs out of the van, heading over to Davenport and Kennard. They have their guns ready and aimed at the front door. “I have a compromise for you.” There is struggle on the other end of the line. David grunts, “I’m listening.” “I’m going to come in,” Ellis pats Kennard’s shoulder and nods towards the door. Kennard nods in response. “You’re going to put your gun down.” David laughs bitterly. “Right, okay.” “Now, David. I wasn’t finished.” Silence. Ellis walks around the building, finding the front door, ducking windows when need be. “You can have the money. Take it. I just need you to put your gun down and let everyone go. Okay? Can you do that?” David contemplates. His voice is shaking. “How do I know this ain’t a trap?” “You have my word,” it is, “this is not a trap,” it is, “We just need everyone out safely,” it is. “There’s cops out there,” David says, suddenly angry, “How the fuck am I supposed to know that they ain’t going to shoot my ass?” Laura has slinked over. Gun in hand, she leans against the wall next to Ellis. “They’ll hold their fire,” Ellis says, “We’ll find you an escort out of here.” Silence, again. David says, “Okay.” Ellis snaps her phone shut. She hands it to Laura. “Hold this,” she says, pulling her gun out of its holster, “I’m going in. Stay here. If I need back-up, it’ll be obvious.” “No. Ellis, stop. Howie just radioed. He wants you to hold off.” “What? Why?” “Davenport is working on getting his son down here. Howey thinks that we can pacify him if we’ve got his son.” “That’s stupid.” “That’s what Howie wants.” “Fuck Howie. He’s got a motherfucking gun, Laura. He’s got a hostage. We don’t know what he’s capable of.” Laura holds her hands up, defenseless. “Don’t maim the messenger.” Ellis stares at her, turns, stares at a tree, turns back around, stares at Laura. “I’m going in.” She starts for the door. Laura grabs her upper arm. “Ellis, stop!” Ellis spins around, frustrated. “No, you know what? Fuck Howie. Fuck PD. We don’t have time. I’m going in and I suggest you let go of me before I bust a cap in your ass.” Laura stares at her. Ellis stares back. Eventually, Laura lets go. “Don’t do anything stupid.” Ellis snickers. Gun by her cheek, she turns and wiggles the doorknob. Her grip tightens around it and she twists her wrist to the left. The door opens. Ellis walks in. David is pressing his gun against his hostage’s temple, barking orders at the Bank’s staff. Cautiously, Ellis inches her way closer, holding her gun out in front of her. David doesn’t notice. To get his attention, Ellis cocks the trigger. Then, David notices. He shifts so his gun is pointing at Ellis instead. “Whoa,” Ellis smiles, “Didn’t we have an agreement?” “Put your gun down,” David replies, shaking his at her, gripping the hostage’s waist with his other arm. “Now, now,” Ellis approaches him slowly, gun still up. “I need you to put your gun down first. Okay?” “No!” David yells, “Put your fucking gun down!” “Well, alright.” Ellis holds her other hand up and bends, putting her gun down on the carpet. She stands back up only to find David’s gun now pressed against her forehead. “So much for compromises,” Ellis mutters. “Everyone get out!” David yells, pushing the hostage away from him, “Everyone! You!” and points a finger at the bag-filler, “Stay! Keep putting money in there!” The witnesses scramble to their feet and nearly sprint towards the door. Laura stops one of them outside, touching his forearm. “You. What’s going on?” “That motherfucker is crazy!” He scatters off. “Oh. Great.” Laura sighs. She grabs her radio. “Copy Kennard, hold your fire. Witnesses. Over.” “David,” Ellis says, holding her hands up, “Calm down, okay? I told you, you can have the money.” In response, David cocks his trigger, wrist shaking. “Okay, seriously. I am so not in the mood for dying right now.” David diverts his attention to Bag-Filler. “That’s enough. Give me the bag.” Bag-Filler fastidiously hands the bag over to David, who snatches it with his free hand. He looks inside, breath hitching, looks at Ellis. “You,” the gun rattles against her forehead, “Go outside. Get rid of the cops.” “Fine,” Ellis says. David lowers his gun as Ellis backs away, attention snared by the amount of money in his hands. It is then that Ellis decideds to whip the other gun from the back of her jean band, pulling the hammer back with her thumb. “Now,” Ellis says, staring down the barrel of David’s raised gun, “On the count of three, we’re both going to put our guns down.” David stares at her. “One,” Ellis begins counting. “Two.” “Thr –” It doesn’t work. Bag-Filler lunges towards David, taking him down around the legs. A gun sets off. Outside, Laura says, “Shit, Ellis,” radioes, “We need back-up, pronto! Copy!” David rolls over, shoving his knee into Bag-Filler’s chest. Seconds later, Laura runs in to find Ellis with David in a headlock, wrestling his gun away while Bag-Filler struggles to push the both of them off of him. Ellis, seeing Laura from the corner of her eye, struggles with David a wee bit longer until the gun is in her possession. She tosses it back to Laura, who catches it, secures it, and throws a pair of handcuffs at Ellis in return. They don’t make it. David elbows Ellis’ nose like a little bitch. With her head snapping back, David grabs the advantage by its horns and sends the back of his fist into her right eye. Ellis staggers off of him, clutching at her face. David grabs at his gun once again but, because she is the protaganist, Ellis miraculously regains her stamina and charges David. On top of his back, Ellis blindly feels for the handcuffs and once her fingers find the steel, she grabs them and snaps one handcuff around David’s wrist before wringing his other arm behind his back and fastening the other one. “You’re under arrest, bitch,” Ellis says, “And you’re paying for my plastic surgery. Shit.
