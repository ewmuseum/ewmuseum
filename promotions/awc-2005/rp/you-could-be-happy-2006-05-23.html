Date: 23 May 2006 Ellis started smoking when she was twenty-one. It was easy, and it’s still easy. Too easy, almost, despite the fact that she nearly hacked a lung onto Logan’s shoe the first time she tried, and Logan had to forcibly remove the cigarette from her lips and pound on her back. After a while she had stopped coughing and the taste was oddly accommodating, as if she’d smoked before. The paper was dry and sweet on her tongue, and Logan was smiling at her knowingly, all of the boys around her smiling like she had just lost her virginity or something, and she smiled back, not because of them, but because the smoke curled around her face just so, like a recurring dream, an idee fixe, or a thought she had had before. She should have known then, but of course she didn’t. That would have been too easy. Smoking, Ellis found out soon enough after, is really just a whole bunch of metaphors for sex. The way she drug it out, the way she rolled the cigarette between her fingers, the indecency and the secrecy of it all; smoking was like having sex with your best friend -- it was that taboo, that amazing, that convenient, and that superficially unauthorized. -- that probably should have been her second clue.
