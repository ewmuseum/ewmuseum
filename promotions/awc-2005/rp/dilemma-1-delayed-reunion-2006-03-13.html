Dilemma [1] - Delayed Reunion

13 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 13, 2006 Struggling to ignore the annoying snore that erupted every few seconds from the man in front of him, Cedric Riley, known now to the Atlantic Wrestling Club as AgentDash, was destined for London, England for the second visit in the same amount of months. Preceding the escapade to the British capital, he had just been informed that his brother was in the very city he was bound for, with intentions of seeing Cedric. Some filling in the blanks is necessary, and it is safe to assume that the history between the brothers was rough, deserving of its own chapter. This inconvenience, however, didn’t deter the ex-agent from flying across the Atlantic to see his mislead relative. Rather, to see what he was up to. Cedric Riley knew Nathan too well to misjudge his intentions, especially when their visits were annually limited, and Nathan usually initiated them with wanting guidance out of a tight spot. Whether it is money, a job, or a place to stay, he never failed to surprise his older brother. But with the death of their father, Cedric made a promise to keep his kid brother out of trouble or danger, and he didn’t blame his old man for this request. Nathan, only 20 years old, had already been in jail for auto theft and it seemed every path he explored lead to the wrong end of the law. But as Flight 189 from California touched down in London as the sun barely peaked over the horizon, there was something the AWC wrestler failed to anticipate; the trouble Cedric was about to encounter would make his potential confrontation with Nathan almost forgetful. The former agent sat relaxed in a window seat in coach, his fingers wrapped around the handle of a traveler’s mug, and a book in his other hand. He shifted to a more comfortable position on the pillow behind him, not distracted from the novel he had already ploughed halfway through. It was difficult to look away from the Da Vinci Code, nevermind actually putting the thriller down. Yeah, the AWC wrestler joined the millions of readers already sucked into the life of Robert Langdon, even though he was a late-joiner to the works of Dan Brown, he simply couldn’t help it. It was a number one selling fiction of all-time for a reason. The main reason for reading it, however, was to distract him from the potential disaster he knew his reunion with Nathan promised to be. This is also the reason for the coffee he sipped casually; there would be no sleeping once him and Nate started rowing, and he would need enough caffeine in his system to survive at least three hours of banter. How could he forget his younger brother’s reaction when he had joined the CIA? The immature little bastard would die of laughter when he discovered Cedric’s new career in wrestling. But Cedric had an inkling that he needed to see him, rather, make sure he didn’t do anything Nathan-like towards Natalie. Besides, Cedric was inclined to see his significant other, who had been absent from his life for over a month. She and his son needed him. Cedric flipped the page in his novel as the plane began to decelerate, which momentarily turned him away from reading. He glanced out the window and down upon the sprawling European metropolis, glad the painfully long flight was about to end. He was starting to get sick of airplane food and coffee, and mentally noted to make a Starbucks stop at the London Airport. Whether or not the England capital had the famous American café franchise he didn’t know, but he required another caffeine blast to keep him awake. He book marked the Da Vinci Code and obeyed the “Fasten Seatbelt” light that flickered on as the plane began to descend. Soon the plane came to a halt and the plane’s passengers began to gather their bags from the overhead. Cedric avoided the hustle and bustle by grabbing his bag quickly, then headed to the airplane’s exit. As the hatch opened, he was blessed with a breeze from the outside, and a glimpse of the dawn of the sun. He motioned towards the portal, but stopped when a stewardess, rather flight attendant, laid her hand on his arm. He looked at her curiously. “Cedric Riley?” She asked confidently. He raised an eyebrow, and nodded. “Who wants to know?” She looked away from him, bit her lip nervously, then looked back. “I’m sorry sir, you’re going to have to wait.” “What? Why?” Dash asked, confused. “If you could just take a seat for a few minutes, somebody will be with you soon.” Cedric looked at her, bemused. He then looked into the portal, where he could see three security guards with their eyes on him, as if to intimidate the ex-agent. Another large man stood nearby in the plane, surveying him. He did the math. He was a suspect. Cedric simply nodded to the stewardess and turned back, pushing through the impatient crowd of passengers, sitting down in a seat nearest to the exit, trying to figure his situation out. The catch was, he couldn’t even begin to figure it out, because the details were so insufficient. The plane was soon empty, with the exception of flight attendants that walked passed him briskly, stealing a glance at him like he was dangerous. He couldn’t even begin to think why they had this impression, but all of his questions would soon have answers, and the process began when a tall black man approached him, who was clearly a man of importance. His black suit and earpiece were good indicators of this, but the serious expression that masked his face made his intentions even more prominent. He was all business. “Sir, we’re going to have to search your bag,” the man stated bluntly, his deep voice and noticeable English accent causing Cedric to stifle a laugh. He obeyed the man and handed over his carry-on bag. “Is this the only bag you brought, sir?” Cedric nodded as the man unzipped the duffel and went through its contents. Once satisfied, he set the bag on an empty seat. “I don’t mean to sound rude,” Cedric began carefully, “but what the hell is going on?” The man reached inside his suit jacket and pulled out a leather wallet, then flashed a badge. “Vince O’Neil, Interpol,” he said professionally, then put the badge back into an inside pocket. “We had an anonymous tip-off of a bomb threat on this flight, and the name Charles Kensrue was brought up—” he paused, “Cedric, is it?” “Yes,” he replied, bewildered. He tried to find something to further his response, and all that came out was, “You’ve got to be kidding me …” “Do I look like I’m kidding?” Cedric looked at him. Vince’s face bore a look of the most serious man on this side of the world. “When it comes to bomb threats on planes, Mr. Riley, nothing is ever a joke.” “I know, but this is ridiculous! ‘Anonymous tip-off’? Does that make me guilty?” Cedric shot at him, thick with sarcasm, beginning to lose his temper. “No. But your background check isn’t in your favor either.” “What?! I’m an ex CIA agent, for fu—for crying out loud!” “Yes, you were: formerly inside the system, recently changed identity, and the messenger, not just some random Joe. Ignoring this type of situation would not be very police-esque, would it?” Vince explained persuasively. “Yeah? Well, false arrest: not very Interpol-esque,” Cedric returned, his patience running short. He got up from his seat. “Now if you excuse me, I have someone to see.” Like a scene out of SWAT, a lot of things happened instantaneously. Two more officers entered the aisle as Cedric reached for his duffel. All three of them seized the wrestler’s arms, with Vince getting the honor of clasping his wrists with handcuffs. Cedric’s immediate instinct was to struggle, but that reaction didn’t last long, because he knew trying to escape would taint his innocence. “We’re taking you to the station,” said Vince. He didn’t spout off any rights to Cedric, so at least the wrestler found comfort in knowing he wasn’t jail-bound. Yet. “I can’t believe this,” Cedric muttered mostly to himself, unfortunately heard by the Interpol officer. Vince tugged him towards the exit. “Believe it. You’ll be spending time in custody with us until we investigate the plane for any threats.” - The holding room at the Interpol headquarters in London, England proved to be nothing special: a small space enclosed by brick walls, with a one-way window glassed across one side, and a single table set up in the middle of the room with metal folding chairs on either side. The only aspect of the room that was particularly interesting was its occupant: Cedric Riley of AWC, who sat on one of the chairs, leaning forward with his arms folded on the table. The only thing missing from the scene was an interrogator sitting across from him, other than that, it was right out of NYPD Blue. The suspect shifted in his chair, taking his arms off the table and leaning back. This wasn’t a matter of being more comfortable, because looking at his watch occasionally, Cedric was beginning to get anxious. Time ticked into the late afternoon, and he had to be back to America to wrestle the next day. It was, unfortunately, the way that he had planned his trip: in and out. That left him in a bit of a tight spot for time, considering he still intended on seeing Natalie and his brother, but depending on how long he spent in custody, that might not be an option. He knew she would understand, but he was apprehensive about how Nate would react. How his younger brother ended up in London in the first place he was unsure, but Cedric knew that Nathan would never be able to afford to immediately follow his brother back to America. But solutions to those problems would have to wait for a later time, because Cedric had a much bigger issue on his hands right now. Somewhere between being a suspect for a bomb threat and finding out whom falsely tipped him off, Dash’s head swarmed with things to do. He wouldn’t allow the ‘anonymous tip-off’ fly under his radar, that was certain. Cedric was too intelligent to pass the circumstance by as a mere twist of fate, because he didn’t believe in coincidences, and for good reason. He was trained to think cautiously, and never thought of any condition at face value only. Cedric was also a firm believer that time spent doing nothing was a waste, and being holed up at Interpol applied to that peeve. With a laundry list of things to do, impatience was setting in, and when he finally heard the doorknob turn, he was immediately attentive. A short man with a baldhead and a mustache entered the room, a coffee cup clutched in hand and a clipboard in the other. His eyes surveyed the wrestler intently. This initial meeting process was a matter of intimidation to the interrogator, because he needed the ball in his court to have a successful discussion. Cedric Riley was a tough customer, however, and stared back at him blankly. “What took you so long?” The suspect asked, mockingly. The man grinned. “We’ve been busy,” he replied with a rough, daunting voice. It was clear he fit the job description. “Yeah? Catching real criminals?” The officer leaned over the table, and got right up in Cedric’s face, so furious his mustache started twitching instinctively. “You better shut the fuck up and let me do the talking, asshole, or it’ll be straight to the bin for your sorry ass.” The officer sat back down in his chair and didn’t take his eyes off the wrestler. Even when he sipped his cup of coffee his eyes were over the brim, staring a hole through Cedric. “First off, you’re gonna tell me why you’re in London.” “To see my girlfriend.” The response he gave lacked any interest or scowl; he was simply answering the questions as if he was barely paying attention. The officer scoffed. “Your passport says you’re from Berkeley. That’s quite the long distance relationship.” Cedric shrugged. The officer began another question, but was interrupted abruptly when the door opened and Cedric’s ‘buddy’, Vince O’Neil, came into the room. He looked at the suspect. “We’ve finished our investigation of the airplane.” An awkward silence followed the announcement, with everybody trying to catch a glimpse of each other. Agent O’Neil broke the silence. “You’re free to go. We’re sorry for any inconvenience.” Cedric’s reaction wasn’t one of happiness, anger, or even blatant disappointment. He simply looked at his watch, sighed, and then looked back to Vince. “Can I use a telephone?” He asked tiresomely. Vince nodded, and then led the innocent man out of the holding room into a hallway. Cedric followed him into an office after a short walk through the building. The officer pointed to the telephone. “Will you be needing a ride anywhere?” Cedric nodded. “To the airport.” Pause. “Give me fifteen minutes.” Vince O’Neil nodded, then left the office, closing the door behind him. Cedric barely even took the time to examine the room, as he pulled up a chair to the desk, picked up the telephone, and dialed a number. The phone call to Natalie’s house came up with nothing but the answering machine. “Hey Nat, it’s Cedric. Listen, I’m not going to be able to make it until next week … it’s a long story, I’ll tell you sometime else. Tell Nathan. Hopefully he’ll understand,” he messaged into the answering machine. He ended he call, then dialed another number … a long one, using impressive memory. He let it ring. Once. Twice. Three times. “James Graff,” the call recipient replied, introducing himself professionally. “Hey,” Cedric greeted, trying to find the right words to continue. “Charl—I mean, Cedric?” “Yeah.” “What are you doing?! I thought I made myself clear that you weren’t to contact me until at least six months passed,” Graff said, berating his former agent. “I know, that’s what I planned on … until I got arrested,” Cedric finished dramatically. It didn’t take long for Graff to process the announcement and react. “You what?!” Graff exclaimed, almost unable to believe the news. “And how the hell did you manage that?” “I was on a plane to London, and somebody phoned in a potential bomb threat, along with the name Charles Kensrue,” Cedric explained, almost pushing for another explosion from his former boss. “Interpol got involved, but they investigated the plane, and I’m free now.” “Any explanation?” Graff wondered, the agent in him coming to life. “Anonymous tip-off,” Cedric quoted, his tone thick with sarcasm. “Maybe I’m just trying to fill in the blanks here, but it sounds like it was somebody of significance to the system that tipped them off.” “Well I’ll be damned. How do you always end up in trouble?” Cedric sighed. “What the hell is this, 60 Minutes? I have no idea, Graff. The best I can think of is that I don’t find trouble, trouble usually finds me.” Graff chuckled. “The age-old expression.” Momentary silence. “Well, if you got off scot-free, then why are you calling?” “I need some information. I need … to know something,” Cedric said slowly, initiating his original intention for calling. “Well, we here at the CIA know a lot of things,” Graff replied, almost cautiously. “But it depends what you want to know.” “I want to know who made the call … to falsely tip off the bomb threat.” “Well, I—” Graff began hesitantly. “Listen, it’s not that big of deal, but I think … I think it’s important.” The CIA boss hesitated again, but soon reached a decision. “Well, I’ll take your word. Give me a second.” “Thanks.” Once Cedric was on hold, he had a chance to look at his watch. He still needed to find a way back to the airport, and book a flight back home as soon as possible, or he’d be pressed for time to make his match. Although the former agent was accustomed to always being on the move, Cedric still didn’t like to be rushed, and now that he was out of the agency he was growing comfortable with having an itinerary. His relationship with Natalie, however, required him to make the odd trip across seas, although he wanted to avoid it being a frequent thing, which would mean them eventually finding a place to settle down together. Cedric was 28 years old, and not getting any younger, but that situation is a whole different story. He heard a muffling sound come from the other end of the line as if the phone was being picked up, and his ears perked immediately. He recognized Graff’s voice on the other end. “I’m back. Those Interpol fellows, their interesting characters, I tell you. I got the information, but not without telling a little fib. Get this, I told them that we had a similar situation over here, and that the two might be related,” Graff rattled on, as if to delay telling Cedric. “Cut to the chase. I have a plane to catch,” Cedric spouted impatiently. “Okay, okay … but you’re not gonna like it. And you probably ain’t going to believe it, either, so brace yourself,” Graff cautioned. “Just tell me.” Graff hesitated. “It was Natalie.” “Natalie who?” Cedric responded, almost feigning bewilderment. Sigh. “What other Natalie do you know?”
