[GTT6 #1] Political Framework - Beginning

14 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Round 1 October 10th, 2006 Leon, Spain 11:00 AM James Sawyer, a reputable private investigator and CEO of his own agency fittingly entitled “Sawyer and Associates”, reclined on a soft chair in the corner of the deck that branched off his vacationing home in Leon, Spain. The clear, blue sky and hot sun provided a dreamy setting for the detective to let loose and soak in the luxury. While one would expect somebody to be drinking a martini or tropical beverage in such an environment, Sawyer's choice of poison was a glass of Scotch that swirled in ice cubes. It had always been his favourite, and felt it unnecessary to break routine despite being bathed in sunshine, soaked in lotion, and offered massages on the hour. He was that type of man – traditional in light of his personal knowledge, and stuck to the mantra of what he knew was always best. He had a strong sense of faith in this, and used it effectively in his line of work, as a businessman and detective. The Scotch, perhaps, was a reflection of his character – brash, old-school, and particular. Ask any of his friends, employees, or clients, who would argue that he was one of the best at what he did. He focused on quality instead of quantity, which customers appreciated and employees sometimes despised, but respected him nevertheless. He trusted them enough, however, to leave the agency under their control to embark on a well-deserved holiday. Before arriving in Leon, he had ended a case under intense scrutiny from higher powers that had present hellish circumstances that required skillful and precise investigation to weasel out of. Needless to say, he had wrapped the job up just in time to pack his suitcase and catch his flight. “Monsieur Sawyer ... vood you like drink?” James removed his sunglasses and smiled at his maid, whom as a French-woman, struggled with her English. He reached for his Scotch and emptied the glass's remaining drops before handing it to her. “Thank you, Miss De Lorme,” he added politely before she left the deck briskly. He leaned back into his chair, relaxing his back on the comforting cushions. He liked this maid, and enjoyed her company. Whether or not he trusted her was another matter – there wasn't many people that he did trust, but aside from that, appreciated her services and willingness to do simple tasks that left James to enjoy his complete tranquility and separation from his normally hectic life. Too many late nights and cigarettes over his most recent case had taken its toll on him, and he was only just beginning to recovery. But that didn't mean he couldn't smoke. From under his chair he brought up a carton of Italian cigarettes, labeled so that he was unable to pronounce the name of the tobacco. He removed one from the pack and ignited it with a lighter from the pocket of his shorts. Breathing in the manufactured toxin and exhaling a cloud of swarming smoke, he felt instantly relieved. It was almost sad and desperate of him to crave a cigarette even under such relaxing conditions, but certain addictions had a funny way of catching up with people. The maid, Miss De Lorme, returned with a glass of Scotch on the rocks for Sawyer's enjoyment. Although she walked directly into the cloud of second-hand smoke, she didn't flinch nor cough. Her movements were careful ones, even a casual task such as handing the man his drink. He received it and thanked her, setting it on the short table beside him. She stood in front of James, looking at him blankly, as if to request something else. Sawyer blew another cloud of smoke out and asked, “Is something wrong, Miss?” She timidly shook her head. Her facial expression turned instantly, as if she was having in an allergic reaction. Her nose sniffled, tears filled her eyes, and her breathing became inconsistent. Was she panicking? Allergic to smoke? Sawyer looked at her curiously and raised himself off his chair, but the maid retreated immediately into the house in a brisk walk. Sawyer looked confused. He looked at his smoke and took one last drag before putting it out on the railing and tossing it off the deck. He reached for his Scotch and brought it to his lips. He hesitated for a moment, as if still wondering about the maid's estranged breakdown, or waiting for her to return with some sort of explanation. He let the Scotch flirt with his lips then fall into his mouth. The bitter alcohol trickled down his throat; the burning sensation felt strangely satisfying. He set the glass down and moved towards the house. One step and he stumbled. Drunk? Unlikely. He struggled with the second, and on the third step he collapsed, face-first on the deck. If he were still alive, he would be able to hear the screams of the maid from inside. * * * October 15th, 2006 Berkeley, California 9:00 AM BAM! BAM! BAM! The insistent knocking on the front door of Cedric Riley's flat in Berkeley, California was enough to rouse the tenant from a deep sleep. After a long flight home from Leon, Spain – the location of AWC's last extravaganza – he felt due for a lie in, which would explain his unpleasant attitude of being awoken. He grumpily rose from his chesterfield to admit his visitor, muttering short curses under his breath that came off as 'never get a damn good sleep round here'. BAM! BAM! BAM! “Alright, alright ... I'm coming ...” he grumbled as he slowly made his way to the door. He turned the the deadbolt and opened the door, allowing the morning sunlight to pour into his apartment. Shielding his eyes he got a first glance at his guest, a man he immediately recognized as Alex Reid. He was of an average build but rather tall, and sported a suit that would be considered casual to his standards. With one hand pocketed and the other rubbing his chin's stubble, he looked at Cedric worriedly. “Alex .. to what do I owe the pleasure?” Cedric greeted. He liked this man – he knew him from his ties with the CIA. Alex was a lawyer from the Los Angeles area, although Cedric didn't know him well, he knew that much. “Bad business, Cedric,” he responded grimly, turning the reunion quickly to a serious matter. Cedric frowned. “Mind if I come in?” “Sure.” Cedric opened the door and permitted his visitor, then closed it behind him. “Anybody else here?” Alex asked, surveying the apartment in a somewhat paranoid manner. “No,” Cedric said. “What's going on, Alex?” Alex sighed, avoiding eye contact with the homeowner, pacing short steps somewhat anxiously. “James Sawyer is dead,” he announced finally. Cedric absorbed the news quickly. He knew James Sawyer, but not well; their limited familiarity was more often than not a conflict of interests. One could say there was some bad blood between them – their disagreements were beyond subtle. Cedric hadn't talked to the private investigator in a couple years. “That's unfortunate,” Cedric replied shortly, unsure of what to say. There was a lull in the conversation, until he followed with, “When did he die? What happened?” “Almost a week ago. He was murdered at his vacation-home in Leon, Spain,” he responded. The mention of the Untouchable host caught his interest, and turned Cedric a bit moody – he was still a little hostile about losing the Livewire Title match, and for a respectable reason. “Huh,” Cedric reacted dully. “Interesting ... I was in Leon only last week.” Alex gave a weak smile. “That's why I'm here.” Cedric frowned. “I'm not quite following ...” He sighed. “Cedric, I'm going to be forward with you – they're considering you as a potential suspect because you were in the same city during that time, and considering investigating.” Cedric's reaction was a short laugh that interrupted the rather serious tone of the conversation. “That's ridiculous ... how does that even make sense?” “They have zero leads and one suspect – the maid, who they've already bombarded with questions and are getting nowhere. There is minimal evidence, which is pointing them in the same direction – nowhere,” Alex explained. “Wait .. who's 'they'?” Cedric wondered, although he had a slight idea of the answer. “FBI. Sawyer was a pretty big deal, as you know. And they know the history between you two. This whole mess is turning from judicial to political in a hurry ... Sawyer himself is close with some of the more respected figureheads in America,” Alex explained. “And it was a good thing for him, because he sure doesn't have many inferior friends.” Cedric moved past Alex and collapsed onto his house. He put his face into his hands, rubbing the sleep out, then looked back at Alex. “Why do I always end up in these messes?” A lull in the conversation provided an awkward silence between the former friends. “First of all, how do you know all of this?” “I'm a lawyer, Cedric. In Los Angeles. I'm quite 'informed' of the inside-system bullshit,” he said, rolling his eyes. “So why are you telling me all of this, anyway?” Alex looked at him in disbelief. “I'm warning you! As a friend, I know you weren't involved, and...” “And you're right,” Cedric confirmed. “I'm a professional wrestler now. I have responsibilities. I have to be in Singapore for a tournament in a few days. And after that, I'm back to Canada to wrestle for my home-promotion.”Another pause. “I can't deal with this shit!” Cedric said blatantly. “You know as well as I do that these things are never simple.” “Well, there's no evidence, so I don't need to worry about it, do I?” Cedric concluded. Alex winced. “Don't blow it off ... not yet, anyways. Keep your eyes and ears open.” He stepped towards the door. “I need to get back to LA.” “Are you involved, Alex?” Cedric asked suddenly, looking intently in his direction. An intense pause followed, as if the trust between these two had been breached, and neither really knew who was to blame. Alex turned back to Cedric before leaving. “No, Cedric. I am not.” He paused. “I know James Sawyer better than anybody. Enough to not risk getting myself hung up in his consequences.” Cedric rose from the couch, standing confidently. “You're his lawyer, aren't you?” he accused. Cedric had been through a lot recently, including being betrayed by one of his best friends, and found it difficult to trust anybody in such a vulnerable situation. Alex stared at him blankly. “No, I am not. At one time, yes, but not now,” he admitted, his tone “Why?” “I'd rather not-” “WHY, Alex?” Cedric stated firmly, trying to break him with a stone-cold stare. Alex looked away, breaking the intense stare-down, then looked back, his face expressionless. “James Sawyer doesn't trust anyone, not even his own wife. Why would I give him the honour in return? You can't work with a man who has that kind of attitude. You can't tell him something without him trying to argue it with his own view on the matter. He has a lot of enemies, Cedric – if you thought he was being particular with the relationship between you two, you're wrong. He's like that with too many people, and it makes him a damn good detective, and an even better businessman, but gaining enemies with the wrong people is a big risk.” He paused, letting Cedric soak in the information. Cedric did nothing; he didn't have anything to say or do but stand there and look at Alex, and trust what he said was honest. Alex continued, “In his last case, I defended the person that he put in the court room with only limited evidence. All I'll say is that it wasn't pretty – this man was put in jail because of a grudge Sawyer had with him. Sawyer had people on the inside, and if he wanted the strings pulled, he had the power to make it happen. He had this impeccable reputation that gave him a huge ego so that he would stop at nothing to satisfy his clients.” Cedric stood in disbelief. “I never knew. Not that much, anyway ...” “Yeah, well, now you do,” Alex said, perhaps trying to provoke guilt in Cedric. “I hate to talk about the man like that now that he's kicked the bucket, but it's the damn truth.” “Listen ... I didn't mean to accuse you like that ... but I've some things have happened recently that have made me lose trust in people,” Cedric admitted. He sounded and appeared hesitant, almost as if wanting to avoid the confrontation with Lucas Emerson he had undergone in Leon, Spain. It was something he wanted to put behind him. In fact, he wanted to put that type of lifestyle behind him entirely and focus on the future. That didn't seem to be in his near-future, however, and he would deal with the delays as they came – he had been doing so ever since leaving the agency. “I get it,” accepted Alex. He was an understandable man, and Cedric seemed to like that about him. Cedric stepped towards him with an outstretched hand. Alex grasped it with an approving grin, then opened the door behind him. “I'll see you around, Cedric.” * * * Elsewhere, just a few hours drive from Berkeley, in Los Angeles, an intimidating man wearing a blue jumpsuit strutted pompously into the visiting room of California State Prison. He looked rather confident for an imprisoned man, and the guards noticed this, sneering at his attitude with disdain. A wall separated the prisoners from their guests, and he sat down at one of the stalls where another man awaited him from the opposite side of the acrylic glass. They both unhooked the receiver from the phone mounted to their right. “Good afternoon, Juvian,” said the visitor. Juvian nodded to him. “Hola, Victor,” he greeted with a thick Spanish accent. He rubbed his thick goatee. A noticeable scar on his lower right cheek would, perhaps, induce fear in others and certainly make them remember him. “How are you?” Victor asked. He was an older, beefy man with a thick beard, sunglasses and slicked back hair. His accent was subtly Cuban. The Spaniard scoffed. “I'm in jail, homes, what d'you think?” “Perhaps it would make you happy, then, if I told you that the hit went as planned?” Victor offered, leaning back in his chair with a smug grin on his face. Juvian's eyes widened like a little kid on Christmas. “Perfect. That despicable man ... Sawyer, is it? ... he deserved what was coming to him.” “Indeed. Listen, I can't stay long ... I have pressing matters to attend to-” Victor began, leaning towards the window and lowering his voice. “More pressing than getting me outta here?” Juvian stressed into the phone in a whisper, keeping his voice as hushed as possible for fear of an eavesdropping guard. Victor closed his eyes as if to exacerbate his next point. “Calm down, Juvian.” He paused. “They are moving you to the Twin Towers Correctional Facility in a few days. I have people set in place to aid your escape during that transport.” “Good ... I am sick and tired of this place. A powerful man like you should be able to make them let me walk out of here without a word.” Victor chuckled. “It isn't quite that easy, my friend.” “Well, in any case ...” “I should go. Remember what I said – ready yourself for anything during your move,” Victor said prominently, a tone of confidence voicing his declaration. “I always am,” Juvian boasted. Victor nodded to him and got up from his chair. Juvian did the same. Game, set, match? Not quite.
