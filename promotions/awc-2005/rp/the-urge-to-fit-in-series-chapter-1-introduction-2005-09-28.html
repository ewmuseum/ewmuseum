'The Urge to Fit in Series' Chapter 1: Introduction

28 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28/9/05 Scoring the winning goal in the World Cup final. Hitting a home run at the World Series. Converting that last field goal in the Super Bowl or scoring that final winning touchdown. These are the dreams that flicker through the minds of all the young men around the world. Whether they be American, Irish, English, Spanish, Canadian or wherever! Every young boy's dream is to grow up to be a big sportstar at some point in his life. (Unless he's gay of course and plays with dolls, or other mens balls) Usually this passion is inherited from their Father, Grandfather or some form of father figure. This would be a 'normal' child. As they say. Not to say that I never dreamed of scoring the winning goal in the European Cup final for my beloved Liverpool Football club, I did. The only thing was that dream was always overshadowed by another. Actually overshadowed isn't strong enough. That other dream took my footballing dream and choked it into submission. My passion didn't lie for football after all. It didn't lie for school or for anything else for that matter. There was one thing in this world I could never stop thinking about one thing to end all other things. Wrestling Wrestling isn't like mainstream sports. In wrestling the fans aren't content to just focus on what goes on in front of the curtain and camera and what's visible in the spotlight. Now that they have to look in the dark corners to find dirt, wrestling is a dirty business and it ain't about to hire no cleaning service! I knew this when I got into it but I didn't care. Winning titles, popping crowds and breaking heads that's what I wanted to do. This decision although popular when I was 8 years old kind of made me an outcast when I got to secondary school and my teenage years. And boy did that hurt. You see there's one thing in this world that will be on par with anyones dream. Whether you wanna be a lawyer, a policeman, a baseball player, a rock star and even a fuckin wrestler and that's fitting in. I don't care who you are, what school you went to and what age you are now. All your life whatever else you strived for we all had the 1 same goal, to fit in. It's a topic that will hit home for a lot of people. Because most of us will have an experience of bullying. Whether it was in school, at work, at home or anywhere. Buzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzz The sound of the alarm clock ringing. My heavy arm somehow reaches the button. 'Snooze' it reads. Of course my eyes are still cemented shut from the marvellous sleep I was having. Nobody could touch me there. There I was in the middle of the ring defeating bad guys left right and centre. No particular federation, no particular arena, downing faceless bad guys like there was no tomorrow. One all dressed in black slides in to the squared circle, my own personal battleground, his face clouded by my meaningless dream. He swings a right fist connecting hard with my jaw. Only hard enough though to stand me to attention. I pull back, my eyes shut while I put every passion in my being into this punch. I pull back, set to release and........... 'Michael, school!' Gone were the faceless bad guys, the screaming crowd from nowhere in particular I saw only one thing, the ceiling. I was awke and it was the dreaded morning. It didn't matter if it were Monday or Thursday all that mattered was my destiny for the day. I knew what lay before me. 'Hey wrestler boy wrestle this!' The 'this' in referral was not a greco-roman grappler but in fact a penis. And I didn't wrestle penis's. In fact I didn't wrestle anything at all.....yet.....so the joke was on him! I found myself having to force my dead weight body out of bed every morning. That empty feeling in my stomach. It wasn't the lack of Coco Pops from that morning it was that empty feeling of having nothing to look forward to. The routine became essential. Wake, turn alarm to sleep, fucking Mam wakes me up, drag self from bed, spray left pit, spray right pit, lynx of course, get dressed, brush teeth, gel hair (in later more handsome years) then get dropped THERE. To school. In we walked. Like mini-prisoners only as cons carried the burden of the crimes they committed to get there we carried the heavy schoolbags that in later years will cause me to have a serious lack of spine! In we walked like droans up the stars 'Left, right, left right'. In my head I'd think: 'I hope they're not in-- I mean maybe if 3 or 4 of them aren't here I'll be ok-- God I wish for once they'd treat me good'. The classroom drew closer and closer, no matter how slow I walked it seemed as if I was running on par with Carl Lewis. That scary classroom was like the unwelcome light at the end of a comfy, sheltered tunnel. I arrived, my head burried so deep in the ground I could have been a fuckin Ostrich. 'OK here goes' I thought. 'Just lift your head- show em you don't give a flying feck' No sooner were the words put into my head then I heard it.... 'Well if it isn't Chief GAY Strongbow'. That was it. Something had struck a chord with me. This 14 year old boy was about to do what every 14 year old boy who's being bulliled dreams of doing- answer that fecker back. Now before we go any further see can you figure it out what it is. It's 1994 ok? Chief Jay Strgonbow hadn't wrestled for God knows how many years and was definitely not a mainstream figure in Ireland. I've copped on. This guys been making my school lilfe hell the last 3 years. The voice in my head but all doubt into the dark.. 'Fuck this prick I've had enough' I sucked in a long deep breath, the most nervous breath I've ever had. So nervous in fact that I think I had to stop in between for a break! Here it came... Mike: Hey how the fuck do you know who Jay Strongbow is? He was a wrestler in like the 70's. I bet you're a closet wrestling fan aren't you? And you'd love to be a fucking wrestler but you're afraid that they don't let queers join. Am I right? My comment was met with a look of disgust. My breath after that was one of the sweetest I've ever tasted. The look of disgust turned to shock as there was no comeback. One of his cronies soon chimed in. 'Yeah you dumbshit, you could've at least said Hulk Hogan or something.' That was that. Mike Wade had broke through the shell. Not to sound gay, but I felt like the butterfly bursting through his cucoon for the first time to see the light of........there really is no way of doing that without being a faggot. From aged 4 I'd had this urge this rumbling. I'd been stood on, kicked, punched, spat on and disrespected some more, the rumbling just got worse. After that one sentence the rumbling went away, at least for that day. The rumble was my urge. That urge to fit in, to be accepted and on that day I was accepted, at least for that one day in that one class, I was the man. And all because I put the fucker in his place. If only it was that easy every day, to just be accepted. September 13th 2005- Post AWC Fresh! A normal night of AWC action. Mike Wade, not competing that night. He had been involved in a confrentation that night with The Farmer. Mike had befriended sworn enemy of a couple of weeks ago Paddy O Shea and the Farmer, Paddys tag partner, was having a hard time believing this fact. Given of course Wades trust track record. Mike was determined to prove to his Irish brothers that he was genuine. Paddy accepted Mike as a friend but Mickey Fitz-McCarthy was different. It was Secondary School and Primary school all over again. Wade on the outside looking in, longing for acceptance and with one guy holding him back. I took my bag up from my locker room. My unused gear rustled as I lifted. My wrestling boots were almost crying out to be used but not tonight. I knew though that in the near future I would need them to prove a point to my opposer, The Farmer. But again not tonight. I had that feeling over me. Not quite the flu, not quite jetlag but somewhere in between. My phone rang as I tried to open the lockerroom door. I took it out and saw the name flashing Ian Enlgish calling My former mentor. One button came a calling. 'Ignore' I know I felt bad. But if I was to make a fresh start I needed fresh friends. With Ian, I would always run into trouble doing his dirty work. I had had a sudden change of heart and was siding with my fellow Irishmen now. Not with a man who took pride in the fact that his forefathers raped and pillaged the land in which my forefathers lived. After I explained to Mickey Moore that myself and Ian did not have 4 fathers each and I meant ancestors I made my way outside finally. It was late. The show had finished at 10.30 it was nearer midnight as I left through the back door. To no surprise really some hardcore fans had waited and waited to see some wrestlers. Hell even if the British Bomber walked out they'd probably ask for an autograph or at least say hello to him. I was all for the fans....OK then I just love the attention! 'Oh Goodie' My time to shine I thought, maybe some praise from fans will cheer me up out of this funk that this feckin Farmer put on me. Mickey was none too impressed. 'Feck this laddie, not in the mood to get confused for a small child in this mess. I'm goin back to d dressin room for a wile, ring me when dis mess is cleared.' Mickey's status of security guards thinking he's a lost child is legendary. But not tonight, he obviously had other business to attend to.....alone.....backstage....probably in the bathroom....involving an issue of 'Nudy Midgets Monthly'....but that's Mickey's own business. I stepped forth from the threshold awaiting my wrestling heroes welcome. And I got it. Even though there were some detractors I got major props. 'Yo Mike Wade man I gotta get your autograph man you rule!' I could feel my cheeks blush. Even though I loved the attention I always blushed at the first notice or compliment. But as they roll in I take em in my stride! 'Wade man never mind that Farmer guy. He's just jealous of you. I'm glad you finally a good guy.' 'Thanks' The only answer I could muster up. Thanks? Fuck sake Mike talk more to the fans. You usually can't stop talking come on!! I was thrown off even more by the next statement. 'Hey Wade where's Ian English?' As if I knew, or cared to be honest. The novelty of that ginger git had worn off many moons ago if I have to be honest. Of course I didn't tell our loyal fanbase that. I was a firm believer in Kayfabe and in character we were mates. Hell we were supposed to be in real life too but maybe I was on my period or something that day. So I gave the Kayfabe answer. 'Fuck that prick. He ain't even working for AWC and he's got a title and is still stealing my heat. I think if that prick dies he'll be stealing my heat from beyond the grave.' Oh did I say I gave the Kayfabe answer? I meant I just lost the rag! It was at that point that I realised I needed to step out of Ians shadow and make myself the star again. It didn't matter where I went. I even started my own federation 5ive Star Wrestling. The question asked most often was not: 'What can we expect from 5SW Mike?' It wasn't even: 'When ya gettin back in the ring man?' No no the question on everybodies lips was: 'You bringin in Ian English?' FUCK IAN ENGLISH. There I said it. He's been the bane of my existence since 2003. My entire title reign in PRIME was based on Ian English. He had to get involved always. He even took Mickey Moore from me. Maybe it was when I saw his name on the phone, maybe it was my growing friendship with Paddy, or maybe it was my green envy that the fans were asking ME about HIM when they should've been asking ME about ME. I had come to this decision: September 13th 2005 12:04 AM: Mike Wade hates Ian English The funny things people do to fit in was about to get even funnnier. But that's a story for another day. I felt weird that night as I said before. It wasn't my fluey jetlag. It wasn't my jealousy towards English. It was as if a presence hung over me all through that night. I couldn't shake it. It was almost as if that arena was cursed on me that night. I racked my brain and I couldn't find my problem. That was until my problem, my flu, my mental block that night found me. Through the flock of few hardcore fans I saw him. He stood with a woolen hat covering his head from the cold, covering some of his identity in the process but I knew it was him. His hair never was his most distinguisihing identity. 'Mike Wade- I always knew you'd make it.' It was him. Conor Fell. The boy who had bullied me for years until I shoved it in his face. Fell hmmm. Now I know why he had that chip on his shoulder. Hang on. I gotta hear that again...... 'Mike Wade- I always knew you'd make it.' He always knew I'd make it so he decided to tell everyone in the school I was a weak pathetic loser who wants to be a wrestler. You made my life a living hell for 3 years and you always knew I'd make it? So many answers to that went through my mind: 'Fuck You' 'Make it? You better make it outta here or I'll punch your fuckin lights out!' As profanity filled my head and the blood rushed to it too I noticed what was by his side. Held in his right hand was not his autograph book or his camera. They were in his left hand and jacket pocket respectively. In his right, was another hand, though much smaller then his own. There stood beside him Conor Fell Jr. His eyes wide open despite it being well past his bed time. He didn't know what kind of a prick his Daddy was growing up. I caught a peek through the little ones jacket to reveal a t-shirt. What it said shook me a little. It wasn't what it said that bothered me. What took me was it was a crude home made shirt that simply read: 'Mike Wade is my favourite wrestler' One look at that shirt changed the whole situation. That young child looked at me in the same way I used to look at my heroes on TV. I then looked into the yes of his father, my former mortal enemy growing up his eyes no longer read that same old story of picking on me. His eyes looked at me with respect and seemed to cry 'I'm Sorry'. I breathed a breath similar to the one I took more then 10 years earlier. The sentence that followed differed somewhat: 'Nice to see you Conor- and even better to meet you....' The childs eyes lit up with joy. But not as much as his Father. An hatchet burried and game, set and match to Wade. I took the high road for the first time in my life and it felt good. Now for Ian English..............
