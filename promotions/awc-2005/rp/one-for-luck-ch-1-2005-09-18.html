One For Luck, Ch. 1

18 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 18th September One for Luck, Chapter One -Home Coming- I was heading home - home. It seemed such a foreign word for even me to use. My home wasn't a home, well, it had been one time or another, but now I had nothing to go back to. Lois had left me and taken most of the things in the flat, including Shelby. I missed Lois and Shelby a lot, but I knew I had to move on, my best bet was leaving the hard struggle that was Albany and try to regain what I gave up in Harrisburg. It had been a couple of hours since I had packed all of my belongings up and sold the penthouse in Summer Plaza apartments. It seemed strange to be returning to something I had first run away from, but when I look back at my recent chronicle, that is my life, it seemed I was doing better in Harrisburg, well, if you ignore the expulsion, drink, rock and roll and my extremely annoying father - well, soon to be divorced parent. However, in light of the things that had happened to me, I felt I had grown from the experience of romance with Lois, the difficulties involved in trying to maintain a steady income and life, whilest dodging fires, bullets and obsessed mafia leaders, but the good thing that came out of this was the fact Lilly Connelly, well Lilly Rush was still alive and kicking and on her way back to Boston with her husband Tony Rush. Well, back to how I got to be sitting on the 2:00 pm flight out of Albany to Harrisburg, Pennsylvania. After I had read the full letter Lois had left me, I decided it best to just leave my life in Albany and try to make-up for the things I had lost in Harrisburg. So, I sold the penthouse to Mrs. Winchester’s daughter and sold most of the old furniture to her too. I then grabbed my duffle bag and my small amount of personal items and left, closing the door to my old life behind me and headed down toward the bus-stop. I guess, now looking back at it, perhaps Robert and I shouldn't have left Harrisburg that night in May, perhaps if we had have stayed, things could have been different, but who knows, that's the fact of life, you have choices and sometimes we don't always make the right ones, but we live by them and try to discover things along the way. 'Excuse me sir?' the young air hostess said as passengers sat down into their seats. 'You'll need to put your bag in the overhead. Do you want me to take that?' Without my realising, I was still standing and just completely staring at a small blonde Pennsylvanian woman, who politely took my bag and slipped it above my seat, A23. I smiled with embarrassment and sat down, next to a nun and a fairly overweight man and wondered why I hadn't paid extra for first class. 'Ladies and Gentleman, this is your pilot speaking. We will be landing in Harrisburg in about two hours. Please enjoy your flight and I hear the movie is 'Rush Hour 2', the pilot said and the intercom system soon fell silent and I was left with the buzzing of 'Hail Mary' and the irritation of the large man snoring, my flight was to be one of peace and quiet, I was sure! After a full one hour and forty minutes of eating some biscuits and enjoying a couple minutes of silence, I returned to gazing out the window, which was half covered by a black head dress, but I ignored it and gazed at the world beneath us. There was something thrilling about being this high in the earth’s atmosphere and actually being able to move. On the last flight I had taken, Robert had introduced me to the reason behind aviation, the men who first invented a plane and the theory of physics. So, needless to say, this was a more exciting trip. To be honest, I had no idea what I was coming home to, all I knew was that I needed to go home and get back on track with things. I had already decided on stopping by my house in Williamsburg’s estates and then going over to visit my old school, perhaps even get my course finished, who knows. The pilot came on once more, and by the end of the flight even I was saying Hail Mary, not because I thought the plane was going to crash, but because George, namely the large man, had eaten burritos and had let off a few. Gasping for air, I raced off the plane, slinging my bag over my shoulder as I ran toward check-in. Another man, dressed in a very posh looking outfit, checked my identification and then let me passed. I had no need to go to luggage bay, so I skipped the crowds, passed the many restaurants and rest-rooms and for one reason or another took a sigh as I saw the large sign reading 'HARRISBURG'. I was actually happy to be home, seemed weird for me. I hailed a yellow cab and told him the address. Well, I had made it passed the first hurdle, now for the next step. Going home to Williamsburg’s Estates. -Past conflicts with Present- I had arrived home. With my MP3 switched on, I leapt out of the taxi, paid the fair and stared at the large gaping security gates that separated the rich and shameless from the regulars. In front of me lay a black cloud of carbon monoxide. 'Hear You Me' by Jimmy Eat World began to play and I made my way, slowly but surely across the tarmaced road toward the security gates. Its large oval shape designs like eyes, staring and judging me as I approached its domain. George, the security guy, stood next to the gate reading a recent news article, he nodded his head and I stood by and watched the gates move open, a little buzzing sound could be heard as it screeched over the metal bar line. I slid in passed the small gap that had been made for me. I slung my duffle bag over my shoulder and listened intently to the song on my MP3 player. 'May angles lead you in', words that seemed fitting for my long accent up the tarmaced hill. I ignored the many onlookers and the gardeners, working extremely hard on a small plot of grass. I passed by a couple of friendly neighbours and nodded my head, but feelings of nerves swept over me as I stared at my house, shrouded in darkness. Its windows empty of life. I then turned to my left and stared at Mrs. Green's house, it was full of colour and radiance. Taking a deep breath I walked up the small drive-way and avoided the broken cobbled stone that had never been replaced. After three knocks, the door opened, slowly. I put my MP3 into my jean pocket and stared in shock at a small woman, probable mid 30's, dressed in nothing but a dressing gown. My mouth had hit the floor and rolled back up, kind of like a 'MASK' moment. My father soon came up from behind and hugged her, but soon stopped when he realised who was at the door. He stood shocked and looked at me, with one of his 'What the hell do you want?' looks. 'Pierce?' he managed to utter with embarrassment. I turned around on my heels, and began to walk away, laughing to myself inside that I had finally caught him in the act and continued walking away from my old house toward the bottom of the estate. The yelling had stopped and a slam of a door echoed through the estate. Many of my neighbours kept their heads down and continued with their work. Without realising, I was now standing outside of Mrs. Green's house. Mrs. Green - Robert's mother - had been my mother’s best friend since High School and I knew that from all of my previous experiences I should be able to ask her anything or talk to her about anything. I knew my best bet in finding my mother would be to ask Mrs. Green. I knew she would be home, because in my last phone call with Robert, he had said his mother and father returned home because they didn't like the humidity of Florida. I hadn't spoken to Robert since; in fact I think he was annoyed at me for taking Lois, even though that had never been my intention. I approached the front lawn and knocked loudly on the door since Mrs. Green wore a hearing aid. After a couple of moments of humming to myself and staring at the garden gnome, I was soon greeted by a familiar face. Robert stood before me, the door pulled tightly to his chest. My mind was now drilling a mile a minute. We both stood still for a few minutes, gazing at one another and catching quick glimpses at the ground. Confusion and shyness rushing through us. We were like an old couple that had fallen out. 'Pierce,' Robert muttered, breaking the silence and tension. 'Robert,' I replied and in doing so, I felt a large weight lift off of my shoulders. 'I thought you were in Florida?' 'Long story,' he replied. 'What are you doing here?' he asked, pulling the door shut and stepping onto the step in front of me. 'I-eh- I came to see your mother. Is she home?' Robert gave me a look, the kind where you are unsure of what to say next, but instead he smiled and hugged me. I was shocked but soon embraced my old friend and watched as he ruffled my hair. 'You still have the mop then!' He laughed. 'Yeah, my mom is home. She's making hot chocolate and there is some fresh apple pie in the oven. You hungry?' he asked. 'Yeah, starving,' I laughed. ' Wait, one more thing. I'm sorry for being a jealous idiot Pierce. I shouldn't have reacted the way I did,' Robert said and opened the door. The smell of baking swept through the hall and up into my sinuses. 'Hey, they don't have a manual for everything Rob, we react badly to things sometimes, but it doesn't matter now, we were both idiots.' Mrs. Green came out of the kitchen, dressed in the motherly outfit - the nice blouse, slippers and a cotton dress. Once I saw her it was like a huge waterfall had just burst the banks of a dam, I had burst into tears and I couldn't hold them back. Perhaps this was my break down. I had cracked and my heart poured with all the stress and emotion I had kept bottled up for months. Mrs. Green sat me down onto the sofa and handed me a box of Kleenex tissues. I cried onto her pink blouse and felt the warm tears roll down my cheek. Robert looked at me and I saw the sympathy in his eyes, he then held up the Harrisburg post and on the front was Marvin Harrington, me, Tony Rush, and all others involved in the last three hectic months of my life, of course, they weren't all photos. Robert just nodded and whispered 'I know ...' and hugged me as well. After a couple of minutes of bawling and feeling like a right idiot, I put the Kleenex tissues aside and watched as Robert placed 'Alien' into the DVD and turned over to the channel. Mrs. Green came in and handed me a hot cup of hot chocolate and gave me a pat on the back. 'Poor lad,' she said and walked away. Mrs. Green had listened to my emotional sob story, as had Robert and both had gasped at each horrible, gut-wrenching twist and turn that I had endured. I was just happy to be home with Robert and to be safe once more. I knew Robert and his mom would look after me, they were like my family after all. My mother and my sister had gone to Los Angeles to live with her sister, Mrs. Green had given me the phone number and address and I looked at the digits and contemplated ringing her, but I knew the tears would come again. In moments like these the best thing to do is unwind, as Robert would put it and his idea of unwinding was watching Alien. Although I did enjoy a good Sci-Fi film now and then, this genre was one of the best. -Here I stand- The movie ended with the typical theme - the heroine saves the day and escapes with most of the crew dead. The usual, cheery-up-stuff we like to watch. By the end of the film, I had drunken five cups of hot chocolate and could feel my bowls disagreeing with the substance of warm chocolate and milk. Perhaps a little too lacitive. Robert stood up and cracked his knees and yawned. The embarrassment soon left and I felt in a good mood now, despite my last traumatic months, things were starting to turn around. I knew deep down that Robert was happy that Lois had left me and perhaps, (despite the way I am looking at things) her leaving me was a good thing. Some say that life is written out for you before you are born, things happen for a reason and we don't always know why, but sometimes, they make a lot of sense and maybe this new journey for me was a card of faith. 'Hey, sleepy head!' Robert said and poked me. 'Huh?' I was sleepy and a little dozy at the moment. 'My mother wants you,' Robert said and sat down in the spot I had been seated in for two hours. I entered the kitchen and saw Mrs. Green reading the newspaper with her fairy-focals on. She slid them down the brim of her nose and stared at me. 'Ah, Pierce. I thought you'd come. How are you feeling?' she asked kindly and pointed to the chair opposite. 'A lot better, thanks Mrs. Green,' I said. She smiled at my politeness. 'That's good to hear. You have been through a lot and it makes a good read. Did you write all of this?' she enquired. What Mrs. Green was referring to was the news article I had put in the New York Post, it was a written account on every waking moment that had happened with my interactions between Lilly Connelly and Marvin Harrington, including those others around me - of course, I left out a lot of crucial information, like the undercover agents, Lilly being alive and other little notches in the truth. Apparently I had a knack for it, according to Mrs. Green who continued to ask questions on the 'evil-man' she called Marvin Harrington. 'I'm just glad it’s over,' I said finally and leant back with a sigh. 'I'm just glad you and Robert didn't get hurt. My son failed to mention the huge fire incident and the death of that poor girl. I guess he was in shock for a lot of his stay in Florida, but he soon hit the wrong side of the tracks himself,' she uttered without realising I didn't know anything about that. 'What do you mean?' I asked. 'Oh dear, I always do this,” She dipped her head. After folding up the newspaper, she turned back toward me. “Robert ended up in Florida state prison for a month, until our lawyer could get him out,' she said and turned over the newspaper page. 'What did he do?' I asked. 'I accidentally helped with a drug run,' Robert said as he placed the ten empty cups onto the draining board. 'WHAT?' I said in a squeaky voice. 'Yeah, you remember Lucy Carter; she was a bucket of trouble. I thought we were going out for a romantic time, you know, like normal couples down to the pier. Instead, I find I'm seated in a car, stuffed to the gunnels with different types of drugs. I get caught and Lucy got away, but thanks to my mothers lawyer the police caught Lucy and the other men involved and I got released without charge. It was a close escape.' 'That's why you came back here,' I pointed out stupidly. 'You're quick today,' Robert joked and hugged his mother. 'Hey, it isn't the best day of my life,' I said in retaliation. 'I know,' he said sympathetically. 'Hey, I know, let’s go down to campus,' Robert jumped with glee. 'Really?' 'Yeah, sure, come on, it'll be a laugh,' he said. 'Okay, who are you and what have you done with my up-tight friend Robert?' I laughed and stood up. 'I loosened up a little,' He added and moved toward the hall. 'I'll see you later mother,' he shouted. 'WAIT!' Mrs. Green bellowed from the kitchen. I soon heard the pitter-patter of her feet across the wood. 'Pierce, where are you staying?' she asked. 'Em- I - I haven't thought about that,' I wondered if she knew about my father’s liaison. 'Well, you can't go home; your father has invited his cheap-whore to stay with him.' 'MOM!' Robert questioned. His mother had used a word, she had always been forbidden to use. 'You can stay here, Pierce. Just until you manage to get back on track, if that suits you?' she asked. 'Yeah, thanks Mrs. Green.' Robert and I turned on our heels and exited the front door. Robert moved over the lawn and looked at the garage. 'Something came for you,' he said and beeped the electronic garage door. My Ford Mustang sat in the garage. It had been cleaned, waxed and the tires had been replaced. I stood in amazement. I thought I had lost this, but apparently I was wrong. 'Who sent it?' I asked. 'Joey,' he added and handed me the keys. 'You can drive.' I beeped the doors open, we both slid into our seats and I put the keys into the ignition, hit reverse and slammed my foot onto the acceleration - leaving a nice screeching of a 180 degree turn. Robert laughed and cranked up the volume for 'Eye of the Tiger'. We were both singing leaving Williamsburg Estates, and probably looked like right fools, but we were enjoying ourselves. -Thompson College- With a sudden screech, crash, bang and a wallop, I had managed to park the car into the small space, without a dent or scratch. Robert remained clutching onto the leather interior. Almost digging his nails into the stitching and with some humiliation sat back and coughed, the kind of manly moment “Right, yeah, beer” thing that we men like to do, if we got a little scared or showed some sort of feminine feeling. I took out the keys, sat back and stared at the welcome sign posted on top of the large pillar. It felt weird to be home, but good. Robert got out of the car, his legs like jelly. I soon followed and locked the doors. “Man, where did you learn to drive like that?” he asked. “Driving at high speeds to rescue people or running from mafia bosses. Why, don’t you like it?” I laughed and slipped the keys into my jeans pocket. “Sure,” he replied with a croaky voice. “Although, next time, I might not eat so much,” he added and made his way into the grassland - well the feeding ground we called our grassland. Everyone, who is everyone, eats in the sunshine or plays their instruments on the lawn. It was the hang-out Zone of Thompson College. Robert had gone off to see a couple of his friends from his class. I left him and headed toward “Reilly Hall” - it contained the many different lecture rooms for medicine, general science and many other science subjects. I approached my old lecture room and stared in through the window at a full room. Mr. Simmons was dressed in the usual tweed coat and nice trousers with his white, crisp, clean shirt. I listened to the lecture and as the bell chimed for the end of class, I stood aside and watched everyone depart. Once the coast was clear and everyone had left for lunch, I entered the lecture room and saw Mr. Simmons packing up his briefcase and various assignments. He coughed gently and I listened intently as it echoed off each wall. Mr. Simmons turned around and almost had a palpitation in front of me. “Pierce? What you doin’ boy? You tryin’ to end my career?” He laughed. “Sorry sir, I just didn’t know how to approach you,” I dipped my head. “Ah, don’t worry bout it. So, what brings you back here? Last I heard, Mrs. Winters had expelled you,” he said, pointing me toward one of the seats. “Not one of my finer moments,” I added. “I decided to come back. I stayed in Albany for a while, it didn’t work out, and it was a little too stressful. Anyway, I just came back to say hello. Robert Green brought me down here.” “I see. Well, Albany is great for those that have finished their course,” He joked. “My expulsion has been lifted. My old girlfriend, Lois Wood spoke to Mrs. Winters and gave the names of the real culprits,” I said, recapping the very memory when Lois had spoken to the Dean of the school in a way, nobody would have dared. “Brave woman,” he added. “I still haven’t got passed hi and goodbye.” Mr. Simmons stood up, dangled his briefcase by his hip and headed for the door. “Shall we walk?” he asked and closed the door behind me. “I have to eat before the next lecture.” “Well, it was nice to see you again, Mr. Simmons,” I said and made a move to leave. “Just one minute, Pierce,” he said and pulled out a couple of sheets. “Huh?” I asked staring blankly at my professor. “Revision notes, just encase you decide on coming back,” He smiled and left into the teachers cafeteria. “WOW,” I whispered to myself and continued walking. My mind wandered and my eyes remained focused on the sheets on “Ecology”. Without realising I had bumped into somebody and completely knocked their books out of their hand. I turned briskly and stood back, shocked. “Selene?” I said. Without realising I had just knocked all of her assignment sheets all over the floor. “Pierce?” she said, adjusting her glasses. “I’m so sorry,” I said and began immediately picking up the sheets. “Don’t worry about it, happens a lot in these halls,” She smiled. Selene Kyle - she had been a friend of mine through high school. Selene had introduced me to Lois Wood, the girlfriend of my teenage years and the last couple of months. Selene Kyle and I had always gotten along. We had gone to prom together, only our relationships was one of the young hearted - is it love or isn’t it? That was the way it had been and when she and I broke-up, I was a man. Lois had never forgiven me for sleeping with Selene and I knew that was the partial reason for her not wanting a full relationship with me as teenagers, but since that night with Selene, both of us have been quite shy around one another. “Where are you off to?” I asked. “Home, actually,” she said and stood up. The two of us bumped into one another again, until our faces were touching. We both moved backward and I gave a nervous cough. “Somebody’s getting reacquainted,” a voice said from behind me. I turned around and saw Robert and Tom Lincoln. Tom Lincoln, had also been a friend of mine through High School, we used to wrestle on the same team, until one night, after our senior prom, we all went to a large house party. Tom could never handle his drink and while I was getting comfortable with Selene, Tom had taken way too much to drink, snuck out of the party and got into the car the four of us drove in. Anyway, within a couple of minutes he had driven it into a pole at top speed, completely splitting the bonnet in two. He had completely ruined his left leg and could never wrestle again and I blamed myself for that night, because, in a way, I should have been looking after him. “Tom, it’s so good to see you,” I said, breaking the silence. “And you,” he said. Laughter and hysterics filled the room as the four of us all laughed and smiled. “How about some lunch,” I stated. “Yeah, go on,” Tom replied. “I’m in,” Robert said. I turned on my heels and looked at Selene. “I know you said you were going home, but how about some lunch?” I smiled. “…Yeah, okay then,” “Cool,” Tom said. The four of us began to walk away from the cafeteria and out into the sunshine toward the Deli.
