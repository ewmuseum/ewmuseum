I'm Afraid of the World

28 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/28/06 Note: There is going to be some sensitive language of a raical nature in this RP. It does not reflect the views of the writer. Discretion is advised. Tariq could think of a million places he wanted to be outside of the locker room at the New Alhambra Arena. Yes, he was impressively victorious in his first outing with Liberty Bell Wrestling. He aced his tryout with flying colors, literally. Crashing out of the sky like a Cessna plane that could magically do somersaults, Tariq had innovated a move that looked so impossible even Reed Richards, Mr. Fucking Fantastic himself would have marvelled at the way he bent his body. Crowds have stood in awe of less impressive maneuvers from men of lighter and darker skin color than Tariq's, yet, they shat all over the young Turk. 'I don't want to say that I told you so.' In his heart, Rahman didn't want to. It pained him to see Tariq put everything on the line for those people and have those people reject him. He knew it was going to happen though. 'Go away. I don't need this.' 'I'm not trying to r...' 'GO THE FUCK AWAY!' Rahman backed off. While he warned against everything Tariq was feeling, he realized that his pupil was still a human being and deserved sympathy. Rahman figured the best way to give his student some sympathy was to give him his space. The next morning, the grizzled old Turk got out of bed to see the distraught young Turk moping about over some soggy Cheerios. The life was gone from Tariq's boyish eyes. Like a child who just watched his puppy get run over by a pickup truck, Tariq didn't move his sullen eyes or even attempt to scoop the mushy Os from the standing milk in his bowl towards his mouth. He sat slumped but stationary at the small kitchen table in their shared apartment. Rahman sat down next to Tariq. 'Are you alright?' No answer. No movement or even acknowledgement of Rahman's existence. Rahman shook his head and went over to the cabinet to fetch a cereal bowl. As he sat back down next to his pupil, the lifeless lips of Tariq started to move. 'Is... is every American like that?' Rahman was taken aback by the question. He knew what he had said to Tariq a few nights ago, about how promoters would sabotage him and how wrestlers wouldn't talk to him if he just tried to wrestle straight up, no gimmicks. Apparently, his embellishments took root inside of the young man's head with the resounding truth of the first part of his spiel... that the fans would shit on him no matter what he did. 'Well no...' 'But you told me the other night...' 'Yes, yes... I was trying to make a point. I was trying to get you to come out right away and embrace your predestined role as a bad guy.' Tariq turned his head towards Rahman. Despite the fact that Tariq wasn't sure he believed in Him at that point, God applauded as the youngster moved for the first time in about three hours. 'But the fans... no matter where you go, they're an uneducated breed. Even at home, you'll find that they'll blindly cheer for you rather than a white man, even if you killed the white man's mother before the match. Here, you have to stand up and be the villain or else you won't get ahead. That's what I was trying to impress upon you. I made a mistake.' Tariq's posture straightened up a bit. 'I just want you to get ahead. In a perfect world, what you did last night would have been enough, but it's not.' 'Well, I did win...' 'In spectacular fashion. All those months of hard work accounted for something, eh?' Tariq rolled his eyes at the obvious self-plug and got up from his chair. He dumped his cereal bowl into the sink and went into the bathroom to freshen up. The next day, it was time to go back to the arena to meet with James Hopkins. Rahman hoped that maybe James and Tariq could work more on his in-ring persona, something ominous. He had called James the night before, while Tariq was out fetching dinner at Tony Luke's. 'Maybe you could nudge him in the edgy direction.' 'Edgy? Like terrorist edgy?' 'If you're comfortable with that, James.' 'I don't know. I mean maybe that could work, but I don't want him getting legit killed in the ring.' 'It's something to keep in mind. I'm not sure how many ideas he'll have. He was fairly obstinant when I proposed the idea to him, and he was fairly shell-shocked when he busted his rear end in the ring and got booed for his efforts.' 'I know, I know. I didn't wanna say anything to him, because hey, stranger things have happened. This is Philly. It's a smart town.' 'A smart town with some of the most boorish fans in any sport.' 'Hey... just because we threw snowballs at Santa doesn't mean we don't appreciate good wrestling.' They got to the arena, and James was right there at the entrance, waiting for his new future star and his teacher. 'Tariq! How are you doing today?' 'I'm... I'm fine.' 'That's good. Anyway, I wanted to talk to you.' 'Oh, really? Because I wanted to talk to you too. I came up with a gimmick...' James looked up at Rahman in sneaking suspicion. He mouthed to Tariq's teacher, 'Did you put him up to this?' Rahman, looking just as surprised as James, shook his head 'no.' 'I... I want to be... an Ottoman captain.' 'Oh... okay... well then, let's discuss it a little more.' James and Tariq went towards his office to discuss things a little more. Rahman heard the words 'Ottoman captain' and suddenly everything came together in his mind. He wasn't surprised anymore, seeing that young Tariq ate, breathed and slept Ottoman history, at least before his crash course in English came along. Rahman waited pensively in the locker room, wondering how everything was going. It wasn't too harsh a wait; he could thank his Lucky Strikes, Time Magazine and a double cheeseburger from Burger King for that. Still, it seemed like an eternity. Then, suddenly, the locker room door flung open. In the doorway stood a stately figure decked in khaki pants and jacket. The jacket had several ornate medals on it. A red sash lay underneath his jacket and a fez of the same color sat upon its head. He wore high black boots that came almost to his knees on his feet. He appeared as an angel before Rahman, an angel dressed as a captain of an empire long since forgotten. 'So, how does he look?' James' voice jarred Rahman from his daze, as the angelic vision of a glorious Turkey past was indeed Tariq. However, before Rahman could answer the question... 'He looks like a grade-A, raghead faggot.' Tariq, James and Rahman all looked over to the far corner of the locker room. There sat a man dressed in blue jeans and a white t-shirt. He had a brown mullet and Fu-manchu moustache, and his cowboy boot-fitted feet were propped up on a press table. 'Lonnie, no one asked you for your God-damned opinion,' said James, an indignant look on his face. Yes, Lonnie Jackson was outspoken, racist and possibly inbred. He didn't give a good God-damn what you thought about him, but he certainly tried to make sure you cared what he said about you. 'Alls I gots to say there, Jimmy, is that I still don't appreciate you lettin' this terrorist goon onto the roster. I don't wanna get blow'd up while rasslin' here, especially by no dune-coon in a monkey suit. I sold out the Mid-South Colosseum. Mid-South! Memphis!' 'Yeah, and that was in 1982, Lonnie. You don't need to keep reminding us. Tariq here is not going to blow the arena up, you don't have to worry.' Lonnie got up out of his seat and marched over to the doorway. He got all up in Tariq's grill, yo, and then turned to James and pointed his finger into his chest. 'Yeah, well, I'll believe it when this raghead is shipped back to his own country.' With that, Lonnie marched out of the locker room. Tariq was aghast. James had a look in his eyes comparable to the one OJ Simpson probably had the night he 'didn't' murder his wife and Jewish man-friend. Rahman shook his head as he held it in his hands. 'Hey, don't worry about him, man.' The voice belonged to Barry Peters. 'He's a douchebag redneck. He gives all the minorities a hard time. I mean, when Kenichiro Montezuki was wrestling here, his pet name for him the Gookster. I think it was also rumored that he burned a cross on Junkyard Dog's lawn in 1986. I wouldn't sweat him.' 'But... his slander... I would never blow up anything.' 'Tariq, I know that. James knows that. That's why you're here and not on a boat back to Istanbul. Just calm down and don't let him get to you.' 'Yeah... the only reason I even keep him around is becuase he makes me money. He's a name. An aging one who moves slower by the minute, but a name nonetheless. Regardless, there are days that I just want to wring his neck, but I remember the fans love him and shell out coin to see him.' Tariq looked uneasy. How could the fans love a creaky bigot like him and not an aerial marvel like himself? He had resigned himself to his fate as a villain, but it still didn't set well with him. He wanted the throngs of fans to cheer him for his moves, not boo him because he was from the Middle East. Why was wrestling such a haven for simplemindedness? The one thing he took solace from was that he could combine his two passions, wrestling and Ottoman history, into one neat little package. Before, his history reading was a hobby. Now it was part of his livelihood. He couldn't wait to fit in different nuances of his ancestry into his mic spots. It was the silver lining in the cloudy sky that was his American wrestling career thus far. Tariq was transported back to the days when he first started training with the Imam. Days were filled with wrestling training. Nights were spent boning up on the Ottoman Turks, their Seljuk ancestors who fought the medieval European kings in the Crusades, but now, it was with purpose. Much like his days at the Academy under Rahman though, his teacher kept nudging him towards the darker side of wrestling. It wasn't as overt as it was back home; Rahman had a reputation to uphold amongst his peers and friends. It usually took place back at the apartment. 'You know, when you were sparring against Peters today, I couldn't help but notice that he kept leaving his groin unguarded.' 'Fag.' 'I'm being serious here. The groin is among the most vulnerable spots on the body.' 'You know, you keep watching guys' crotches, people might think badly about you.' 'I'm being serious. You know he kept countering your every chain wrestling move, and he was at least two steps ahead on your aerial maneuvers. You could have gotten the upper hand if you hit him once below the belt.' 'I'm not going to strike my best friend in the nuts during a spar. Besides, I could get DQ'ed with such a blatant strike.' 'One, most referees will only warn you on the first blow. Second, thinking you have friends will be your ultimate downfall in this business. You may be friendly with people outside the ring, and that's fine. If you want to drink a beer with Peters after the match, I have no problem with it, but in the ring, you are his enemy. And there are going to be days where you won't have any other recourse but to go below the belt. You must do what it takes to beat him.' Tariq looked at Rahman intently. 'Beat him... or beat him off?' Tariq started to giggle uncontrollably. 'You've been spending too much time around those pigs in the locker room.' LBW ran shows every other week. Tariq was held out of the next show just so he could work out all the kinks in his gimmick, to get everything down. Wrestling-wise, he was definitely ready. His first match, against another ham 'n egger, would be the second one on the card. The first one belonged to a returning hometown hero, one Jericoholic Anonymous. Tariq was in the locker room, waiting for the first match to get over. His stomach was in a double knot. It felt like it was being tugged in all different directions. His first match as 'Captain Suleimon,' his first match expecting people to jeer him and possibly throw garbage at him. Could he do this for the rest of his career? How long would his career last? Finally, it was time. James came into the locker room. 'C'mon Tariq, it's time for your match.' Dressed in his khaki pants which tore away to tan, leg length tights, his jacket, sash, fez and boots, and with a Turkish flag clutched in his hand, ready to be waved as he went down the aisle, Tariq got up to his feet and gulped as if he was swallowing a bitter pill. He stepped out of the locker room and went down to the foyer where he'd wait until his music started playing. 'Now, when 'Turkish March' starts playing, you walk out, alright? Henny Clark will already be in the ring, you just walk out and do your best.' Tariq nodded at James and held his flag close to his chest. He waited for the announcements, and first... 'Introducing first, already in the ring... from Vorhees, New Jersey... weighing in at 230 pounds... HENNY... CLAARK!' There was no audible reaction from the crowd. They were waiting for the opponent to come out. Tariq felt it. The speakers started to vibrate, and he was about to step out from the back when suddenly, he felt something was wrong. It wasn't the dulcid tones of Mozart. It was an acid-drenched guitar riff. Mountain. 'Mississippi Queen.' Shoving his way right past both James Hopkins and Tariq Suleimon was one Lonnie Jackson, with a microphone in his hand. The redneck strutted his way to the ring to a monstruous roar from the crowd. He raised his hands over his head and basked in the cheers until he was good and ready enough to say what he came out to say. 'Y'all know what? In my twenty-plus years of rasslin', I done seen a lot of things, an' done a lot of things too. I held title belts, I sold out the Mid-South Colosseum. I even made Ric Flair tap out like a lil' baby in the Stump Puller. But there's one thing I ain't never had to deal wit' in this ring, an' that's sandniggers.' The crowd gasped at the language. 'Y'see, LBW has done gone and hired itself one of them terrorists, a guy who's gonna come out here and rassle this bum. Well, fuck me if I'm gonna stand here for this! This is America! It ain't Ragheadland! We don't want none of his type here, infiltratin' our ranks and throwin' bombs in our cafés!' Tariq peered outside the curtain at Lonnie and his diatribe. He looked out into the crowd... they were going nuts cheering him. 'In fact, I don't want him havin' the dignity of beatin' this feller fair and square...' Lonnie stuck the mic in the pocket of his stone-washed jeans, kicked Clark in his gut and gave him a piledriver that shook the ring. Clark was laid out cold and Lonnie got up hooting and hollering as the crowd went bananas for him. 'Yeah! So when this raghead bastard comes out here and pins this ham 'n egger, it won't be cuz he did anything special. It'll be 'cuz a REAL American handed it to him on a platter!' The crowd kept cheering. Tariq was incredulous. 'An' in two weeks, when we come back here to rassle, I'mma gonna do the same thing to him! That is, if he's gots the stones to face a real, American wrestler in the middle of the ring!' Lonnie threw down the microphone and stomped out of the ring to monstruous cheers. He strutted down the aisle and through the curtain, staring a hole right through Tariq. The young Turk was about to strike at him like a cobra in the bathroom of an airplane, but James restrained him. 'Save it for the ring... even if he didn't leave you much.' Lonnie walked off smirking like a bastard. 'The Turkish March' then began to play on the PA. Tariq walked out waving his Turkish flag, lethargically at first, but as the boos intensified, he waved it harder and harder. 'And his opponent... from Istanbul, Turkey, weighing in at 188 pounds... CAPTAIN... SUUULEIMONNNN!!' Tariq placed his Turkish flag in the corner and looked down on the still-unconscious Clark. He didn't know what to do. Should he attack, even though it would be overkill? He didn't want to take the freebie from such a hateful man, but at the same time, he didn't want to kill Henny Clark either. Sure, he may not have been in the best of shape, but no matter what anyone tells you, jobbers are people too. Seriously, they are. They're not much of people, but they're people. He even thought about walking out of the ring and forfeiting the match. He didn't want to win like this. His pride would be wounded too badly. He wanted badly to rush to the back and take a mulligan for his debut as the Captain. He wanted even worse to take a cricket bat to the obtuse skull of one Lonnie Jackson. Yet, the cries and screams of a crowd that wanted him run out of Philadelphia at the very least permeated his skull. They yelled nasty things at him, words that Tariq tried to block out of his skull. He looked down at the KOed body of Clark, and thought to himself... what would piss these people off the most? A split second later, he was on top of Clark, and the ref counted three. As soon as the show was over, James broke the news to Tariq. 'I signed you to that match Lonnie wanted.' Tariq just stared at James. 'Look, he threatened to play hardball, and really, I don't need him moving onto one of the other area indie feds and taking his gate with him. It took a lot for me to keep him here. Besides, I know you can take him.' Tariq just nodded. James looked at him, shook his head at himself, and then walked out of the room. The next day, Tariq arrived to the arena late. His eyes were bloodshot and he looked like an extra from one of those cheesy Fifties zombie flicks. He dropped his bag right off at the ring. James and Rahman were both concerned, especially Rahman, who saw Tariq sitting in the same corner of his bed when he woke up as he did when he went to sleep the night before. Tariq turned to them both and said, 'I'm sorry for being late.' 'That's okay,' replied James. Tariq dropped his bag off at ringside, having come to the arena already in his training gear. He hopped in the ring to spar with Peters. 'Hey man, you okay?' Tariq nodded half-heartedly. 'You can't be okay man. You look like a zombie. What's wrong?' Well Barry Peters, you're just about to find out what's wrong. 'Hey sandnigger!' From out of the back came Lonnie Jackson, wearing a Stars 'n Bars t-shirt, jeans and cowboy boots. 'Sandnigger! Ha, look at 'im! He can't even beat a ham 'n egger by himself!' 'Lonnie, shut up or else I'm docking you your next purse.' 'Ah fuck off James... ah well, I'll see you in two weeks, cameljockey!' Lonnie made his way to the back. Tariq was seething on the inside, but on the outside, he made no emotive gesture whatsoever. 'Just shake him off.' For the next two weeks, Tariq was put through living hell. Suddenly, it felt like he was back at the Academy with Habib screaming 'Allahu akbar' as he was pounding the heavy bag again, only it irritated him even more. He couldn't figure out why. Every day, the 'sandnigger' and 'cameljockey' taunts came and stabbed Tariq like little knives. Every day, Lonnie would find new and inventive ways to rain the words down on Tariq, seeing that he didn't want to be docked any money, money he so desperately needed to fuel his alcohol habit. Finally, the night came. Tariq's last shreds of humanity were clinging to him ever so delicately, almost as if a strong breeze could come along, blow them away and make Tariq stalk around the hallways drooling and grunting 'brains... BRAAAAIINNSS!' When the time came, James came into his locker room. 'It's time.' Tariq stood up and slowly walked out to the foyer. He stared blankly at the curtain, almost Pavlovianally waiting for his theme music to cue up. As the 'Turkish March' played on the PA, Tariq forced himself to walk with some pep in his step, Turkish flag held over his shoulder, boos raining down on him. He climbed into the ring and placed the flag in the far left corner from the entrance. Then, 'Mississippi Queen' blasted up on the PA, the crowd erupted, Lonnie Jackson entered, Tariq seethed and a little bit of himself died on the inside. Lonnie made it down the aisle and up to the ring. He no sooner climbed on the apron and through the ropes than did Tariq bumrush him, flailing like a retarded third grader in his first fight. Rahman covered his eyes, not seeing a spectacle like this since he faced off against Habib. After the initial disorientation, Lonnie fought off the distraught Turk and took over, first with his fists of stone, then with some of his patented restholds of DOOM~! The pain in Tariq's body was magnified tenfold by the fact that this person who was hurling insults at him all week, who had hated him and thought him inferior because of his race was artificially proving it tonight. He seethed with every wrench on every chinlock, with every punch and stomp. Finally, Lonnie decided that he'd had enough with Tariq and was going to throw him out like yesterday's garbage. He picked Tariq to his feet, grabbed him by the scruff on the back of his neck and... HEAVE-HO~! Tariq crumpled to the mat like a ton of bricks. The hate in his mind burned, but so did the pain his muscles. He looked around at all the fans cheering on the beating that Lonnie was laying on him, and his frustration grew. His hate brought him to his feet and turned him around. He looked up into the ring, first at the referee, whose twenty count was only about a third of the way exhausted. Then, he looked up at Lonnie... and was aghast. Right after Lonnie threw Tariq otu of the ring, he went over to the corner where the flag was. He looked at ti, picked it up and tore the flag right off the pole. He raised it up for the maddening mob to see... ...and stuffed it right in his crotch. The crowd erupted. Unfortunately for Lonnie, so did the hateful volcano in Tariq's mind. Like a deranged sociopath, Tariq looked around for the nearest weapon. He saw a chair. He grabbed it forcefully and folded it up. Sliding into the ring, he held it up eye-level. The referee implored him to put it down, but Tariq just threw him out of the way. He stalked Lonnie with the chair, and Lonnie, with the Turkish flag still sticking out of his crotch, made his last mistake. He told Tariq 'You don't have the sac to hit me wit' that chair, raghead.' CRACK! Lonnie was wrong. Tariq nailed him in the head with the chair and the force of a thousand clubs. The bell was ringing throughout the New Alhambra Arena, calling for Tariq's imminent disqualification. Tariq's arms slumped down and the chair fell from his grasp. He stood there, staring at Lonnie as he lay unconscious on the canvas. Much like the time he cracked Habib with the chair, everyone looking on was in shock. But unlike the time he hit Habib, Tariq wasn't feeling any sickness or shame. This time, Tariq felt a sense of pride. It felt good this time. It felt good.
