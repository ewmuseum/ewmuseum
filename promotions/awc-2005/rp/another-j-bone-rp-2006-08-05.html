Another J-Bone RP

5 August 2006

Date: 8-5-06 Author's Notes: I'd like to thank Sam for helping me out on my roleplay and for giving me some advice. Hopefully, I've used his advice to its fullest extent. lol. The war between humans and vampires was over, and Madison Farnsworth, a detective from the Homicide Division of the Boston Police Department, was the sole survivor of her makeshift army. She knew that in time, Fionnbar’s horrifying grip would loosen itself, but it would leave a menacing bruise. The bruise would heal itself, she was sure of it, but the pain would forever be imprinted in everyone’s mind, and the city of Boston would never be the same again. Stacy smiled when she finally wrote the final paragraph of her experimental horror novel, The Bloodthirsty. She leaned back in her chair, placed her hand on the mouse, guided the arrow up to file, dragged it down to Save, and released the mouse, saving her work. She reached for her bottle of Canada Dry ginger ale, took a healthy swig and swallowed. To her discovery, the ginger ale tasted much better after she completed her first horror novel, all five hundred and twenty-seven pages of it. People would soon ask Stacy why she wrote a horror novel. It had always been an interest of hers ever since she became an author. Her intention for her first novel was to be of the horror genre. When she began writing it, she found it to be extremely difficult, the words didn’t flow right and the story didn’t make any sense at all. Eventually, she would scrap her horror novel idea and write something else. She decided to be eclectic in her writing - do dramas, comedies, mysteries (of course murder ones as well) and thrillers. Horror novels wouldn’t be a part of her repertoire. Years later, she wanted to give it another try. What resulted was this book - The Bloodthirsty, - about an Irish Vampire named Fionnbar migrating from Ireland to Boston. He would then create a lab of sorts, play Dr. Frankenstein and create biologically engineered vampires to take over the city. It was a premise that seemed farfetched at first, but as Stacy continued to write her book, the story flowed evenly with the help vampire research, and used her own twist on vampires. For example, her twist consisted of vampires that could appear in broad daylight, like Blade from the movies and comic books. They have all the strengths of a vampire, some of the weaknesses (minus sunlight, crosses, holy water, silver, etc), and can go through the amorphous state of turning themselves in bats by growing wings out of their shoulder blades. Vampires always intrigued her, so writing a horror novel about vampires was the correct way to go. She had read her share of vampire books: Bram Stoker’s Dracula, Stephen King’s ‘Salem’s Lot, and the plethora of vampire novels Anne Rice had written throughout the years. All those influences were the perfect recipe for Stacy to write this novel. She liked how the book turned out. If it were a success, she would continue to write more horror novels. If not, oh well, it wasn’t meant to be. It won’t discourage her at all. It was an experiment; she did her best to carry out said experiment. She got up from her chair, stretched (her bones and joints were popping louder than packing peanuts. That’s what happened when she sat on her ass for almost four hours) and stepped out of her office. Later in the afternoon, she would pick up her husband from the airport. His flight touched down at 3:15, which was three hours away. She missed Michael horribly. When she had her encounter with Roger Mirkwood last week, she hadn’t felt a strong need to be in her husband’s arms before. There were days she wished she had never met Roger. With his bipolar disorder and being manic-depressive, Roger’s erratic mood swings always put a heavy strain on their relationship. Becoming pregnant with Roger’s baby wasn’t an easy task either; she’d be on the receiving end of several verbal assaults from Roger, telling her it was her own fault that she became pregnant. When Meryl was born, Stacy could no longer take the abuse from Roger anymore. She told him she was leaving, and she was taking their daughter with them. That almost cost Stacy her life. Roger was arrested for attempted murder and served ten of a twenty-year sentence in jail. Stacy remembered those horrifying words as Roger was carted behind the back of a police car... When I get out, Stacy, I’ll become your living nightmare! That encounter she had with Roger last week was a living nightmare. She brought herself back to reality, shaking off the chills she felt in her body. Mustn’t think about him, she told herself. That man is evil. I should’ve seen that years ago, but alas, I didn’t, and I paid for it. She checked her wristwatch. It read 1:33 PM. Michael’s plane would be arriving in less than two hours. Where in the hell did the time go? I should just retire. I should just retire and take up boxing again. This wrestling business isn’t worth it anymore. That thought kept repeating in my mind. It was a like a chant, forcing me to obey it, but the more I thought about it, it didn’t sound all that appealing to me anymore. Just because I’m having a losing slump doesn’t mean I should just up and retire. That wouldn’t be fair to my family and it most certainly wouldn’t be fair I’m just upset, that’s all. My career isn’t going where I want it to go, and eventually it’ll get better...right? I hope it does; I can’t stand losing anymore. First the Battle for Independence, then the Countdown Trophy Match, the West Invitational Battle Royal, and now the Duo Tag Match with that punk bitch, Paddy O’Shea. I had a great desire to blame O’Shea for our loss against Jack Murphy and Captain Suleimon, but it wouldn’t be fair. We both lost, not just one of us. What is becoming of me? What does my career in wrestling mean to me now? Does this mean I’ll forever be some sort of “jobber” (as my old manager put it) in this sport of professional wrestling? If it meant that, why am I still in wrestling if all I’ll ever do is lose? At first, when I arrived in the AWC in February, I thought I had all the opportunities in the world. Zsasz told me that, and I believed him. My success came early when I defeated Dagoth Kinslayer in my debut match and defeated both Jason Ortiz - a former LOW World champion, no less - and Mr. Marshall in a Three Way Fury. Then my success came to a halt when Aimz defeated me, then defeated by Chainz (twice!) in two graphic matches. I would regain my success by beating both Ozric Mortimer and Paddy O’Shea in an Irish bare-knuckle boxing match. The win wasn’t to my liking, but it was a win nonetheless. And now, I’m losing again. Why? Why am I losing? Perhaps I’ll never know. Bing! “Flight 747 to Boston is ready to board. I repeat, flight 747 to Boston is ready to board. Thank you for choosing American Airlines.” Finally. I can board the fucking plane and get the fuck out of this state. I’ve had my share these Malibu assholes, thank you very much. I get up from my chair, grab my tote, my suitcase and walk towards the terminal, gave the dude my ticket and went through the terminal to board the plane. In just five hours, I’ll be home in Boston, where I belong. She wanted to go home for lunch today. Some of her friends wanted her to go to Burger King with them and she happily declined, stating that she had a lunch date with her mother. That was a lie; she didn’t have a lunch date planned with her, but the lie was enough to get her off the hook. She liked her friends dearly, she really did, but she wanted to spend some time alone. If she said that, she’d get hell from her friends, telling her that she’s a prude (an English word she didn’t quite understand) and then get all mad at her. She supposed it was how Americans dealt with the rejections from a Japanese girl. She’d been only living in Boston for a couple of years now, and everything was different here in Boston than it was in Japan. Everyone’s more uptight here, she observed, than the conservative people in Japan. There were throngs of rude, vulgar people in Boston, while in Japan, everyone was respectable and nice. The thought of living in Boston displeased her and she missed Japan more than ever. She had pleaded with her father to move them back to Japan, but her father insisted that living in the United States would be better. The economy in Japan (especially in Tokyo) was way too high, and the family would eventually go broke. He did promise, however, if things didn’t work out in Boston, he would move the family somewhere else in the United States, but Japan was no longer an option. It broke Kiyoko’s heart, knowing she would never see her home again. She would spend the majority of her time locked in her bedroom, only to leave for school (her parents always told her that her education was very important), to come downstairs for dinner, or to go somewhere with her parents - you got the idea. One night, she tried to commit suicide by slashing her wrists with a razor blade, but her father stopped her, gave her a tongue lashing and sent her back to her room. Out of rage, she said something she never thought she would say: “It’s better to be dead than to live in this god-forsaken dump!” The relation between Kiyoko and her father, Hige, was tarnished after that night. She was about to step onto the sidewalk when she was a tall, blonde-haired and blue-eyed man walked towards her. She didn’t even see him until she heard his voice. “Hi there!” She wheeled around to see the large blonde man. She had the strangest feeling that she’d seen this man previously, then it occurred to her: She saw him leaning against his car, smiling at her last Friday as she was walking home. She had the impression that this man in his mid-twenties was stalking her, wanting to kidnap her and do all sorts of evil things to her. Kiyoko may be small, standing only five feet, three inches tall, and weighed a little over one hundred pounds. She was trained in Jiu-Jitsu by her father, so she knew how to defend herself, if she had to. Judging by the size of his individual, standing approximately six feet, three inches tall, and weighed about two hundred and forty pounds, the man would prove to be difficult if she fought him. It looked like he didn’t want to fight. From his sunny disposition, it looked like he wanted to talk to Kiyoko, but that’s how all kidnappers acted, didn’t they? She watched videotapes about these people who appeared to be nice, but in actuality, they want to kidnap you. But not Kiyoko; she knew better. She’d fight to the death if she had to. Remaining on her guard, the large man, teeth bared by his creepy-looking grin, advanced closer. If he was to do something, he would’ve done it, wouldn’t he? Kiyoko was sure of that. Perhaps he was biding his time, looking for the perfect opening to strike, or maybe he’s too reluctant to strike. Or maybe he wasn’t going to strike at all. This man confused Kiyoko. She wasn’t sure what to make of him. Taking a chance she let her guard down, her eyes remained locked on the approaching man. When he got within a foot away from her, nothing happened. The man’s hands were in his pockets, his poster was loose and non-threatening, and the twinkle in his sapphire blue eyes represented kindness. Maybe this guy wasn’t going to do anything bad to her at all. “I see you’ve loosened up,” the man said, chuckling at Kiyoko. “I assure you, miss, I mean you no harm. I’m not even carrying any weapons.” To prove his point, he turned his jean pockets inside out. Seeing that Kiyoko was becoming more comfortable now, his creepy grin turned into a warm smile. “What’s your name, miss?” “Kiyoko,” she answered him. “Kiyoko Norazu.” The man smiled. “A befitting name for such a beautiful girl. Mine name’s Jack Parker.” He extended his large to Kiyoko. When Kiyoko placed her small hand into his, Jack knelt down to kiss it. “It’s a pleasure to meet you, Kiyoko-san.” She flushed slightly when Jack called her beautiful, and flushed a deep red after having her hand kissed. Her heart began to race Oh quell my beating heart, she thought to herself. I think I’m in love! “Where are you going on this beautiful spring day?” Jack asked, still smiling at the beautiful, blushing girl in front of him. “I...I was going home on my lunch period,” Kiyoko replied. There was a little squeak in her throat that made her blush more. If a fire truck came by, the color of her face would be an exact match. “I wanted to get away from my friends just this once and have some time to myself. You know how it is...” “Indeed, I do, my dear Kiyoko,” Jack agreed, even though he didn’t. He spent most of his life juvenile correctional facilities for his explosive behavior. If a kid made a wisecracking remark, Jack would pop him a good one...by slamming the kid’s head into a locker. Repeatedly. “I could give you a lift to your home, if you don’t mind me doing so.” A red flag went off in her head and Kiyoko’s eyes widen slight. Not enough for Jack to noticed, she’d hoped. This was how people got poor, defenseless kids into their cars, she was sure of it! “That’s not necessary, Mr. Parker,” she said shaking her head. “It’s a beautiful spring day, like you said, and I don’t have far to walk---” “I insist,” Jack cut in, his smile was slowly fading now. He sensed that Kiyoko thought of him as some sort of pervert trying to get into her into his car. Well, he was, but that wasn’t his intention. Not today, anyway. “It’ll be fun, Kiyoko-san. We can get to know each other on our drive to your house.” Multiple flags when off in her head. This man is dangerous! RUN AWAY NOW! “R-Really, Mr. Parker---” “Call me Jack.” “Okay...Jack. It really isn’t necessary.” Jack made a faux pas frown, a frown that would make a five-year-old laugh. Not even a chuckle emanated from seventeen-year-old Kiyoko Norazu. “I see how it is,” Jack said, as he chose his words carefully. “You think of me as some pervert that’s going to kidnap you and do horrible things to you, am I right?” Her eyes widen more, and this time, Jack did notice. Kiyoko bobbed her head, feeling her face and ears burn in embarrassment. “You struck me as one, yes. I’m sorry.” Jack let out a small bray of laughter. It wasn’t the first time he’d been categorized as a pervert, even though being called a “pervert” or “sex offender” angered him; he wasn’t one either one of those things. Rape was such a disgusting trait. He killed for power, not pleasure. Perhaps this Kiyoko girl would prove to be difficult to have him gain her trust. “I’m not one of those kidnappers, rapists or sex offenders, you have my word on that. Besides, if I was, I would’ve attempted to kidnap you, wouldn’t you agree?” Kiyoko nodded her head in agreement. “Since I haven’t attempted to kidnap you - especially in broad daylight, mind you - I’m not some perverted sex offender, wouldn’t you agree? Again, Kiyoko’s head bobbed her head up and down, agreeing with Jack’s logic. Jack smiled. “Good! We’ve come to an agreement. My offer still stands by giving you a ride to your home. If you don’t want to... I understand...” He purposely turned his back on Kiyoko, knowing he had her hook, line and sinker, and began walking to his Dodge Charger. In a few short moments, Kiyoko would accept his offer to--- “Wait!” Damn, that was quick. Jack turned around to see Kiyoko running towards him. Am I good or what? Jack reflected, and he was, indeed, good. Manipulatively good. “On second thought,” Kiyoko panting, trying to catch her breath by running after Jack. “You seem like a nice guy, Jack, and I would love for you to give me a ride home. If you play your cards right, I’ll even invite you inside.” Her comments made him grin from ear to ear. “That’s very kind of you, Kiyoko-san, but I don’t think your parents would condone you bringing an older man to their household, especially an older who’s American.” “They’re not home. Besides, what they don’t know won’t hurt them.” She gave Jack a conspiratorial wink. Jack couldn’t help by laughing at that wink. “All right, then. You win. Follow me, if you be so kind.” He offered him his arm, she took it, and they walked side-by-side to his car. Being the perfect gentleman, he opened the passenger side door for Kiyoko. She thanked him, slipped inside and Jack was kind enough to shut the door when Kiyoko was settled in the passenger seat. Jack got inside the car, started the engine and drove away from the high school. Less than five minutes later, Jack parked his car on the curb next to Kiyoko’s house and killed the engine. Jack got out of the car, opened the door for Kiyoko and allowed her to step out. Closing the door, they walked arm-in-arm towards Kiyoko’s house. My plan is turning out better than I thought, Jack reflected as he went inside the house. His plan was turning out well indeed. Bing! “This is your captain speaking.” The voice roused me out of my nap. I checked my wristwatch to see it was three o’clock. “We’ll be arriving in Boston in about fifteen minutes. Thank you for traveling American Airlines. Have a great afternoon!” What the captain said was music to my ears. In fifteen minutes, I get to see my beautiful wife, and I finally get to hold her in my arms. I know that she misses me, she’d called me several times when I was in Huntington Beach, telling me how much she loved me, how much she needed me. I told her the same thing. My time away from my wife and family was a painful experience. Ever since the roster split, Stacy and I weren’t on the same page. My stress levels were going through the roof, and I brought my problems home. That wasn’t fair to anyone. I would involuntarily snap at my kids and, this is what pains me the most, I would fight with my wife. The stress I was going through was tearing me apart, tearing my family apart. I couldn’t live like this anymore. I couldn’t bear the fact that I was hurting my family. That’s why I kept going away, telling everyone to stay behind. Having them go along with me would exacerbate things. It was the most painful decision I ever made; it was the right decision, though it bore consequences. It pushed Stacy and me away farther, and my kids... my kids didn’t want to talk to me most days, and that killed me inside. I could understand Meryl had nothing to say to me, but my boys? You might as well stab me in the heart and turn the knife slowly. Bing! “This is your captain again.” The captain’s voice broke me out of my reverie. What does he have to say now? “We have arrived at our destination. In a few moments, you will be able to get off the plane. Thank you for flying American Airlines. Have a wonderful afternoon!” Cool, I can get off this flying death contraption. Everyone started to unbuckle their seatbelts, rising form their seats and, in a neat, single file line, ready to depart from the planet. I wait for a few minutes until I’m the last one left. I unbuckle by belt, got up, stretched (various bones and joints cracked, and I discovered my ass fell asleep. I hate five-hour flights) and left the plane. I’m now in the Boston Airport, looking around to see if I can find my lovely wife. Since I can’t find her, I go over to the luggage conveyor belt to get my luggage. I got my tote bag, my suitcase, sat in the terminal and wait for my wife to arrive. “MICHAEL!” I heard a voice exclaim. I smiled when I recognized my wife’s voice. I got up from my chair. Before I could turn to see where the source of her voice was coming from, she bum rushed me, giving me a tight embrace. She was crying on my shoulder, telling me she was so happy to see me. I couldn’t have agreed more. I wrapped my arms around Stacy, giving her a kiss on her forehead, and we stood there for several long seconds. All my anger, all my stress, seemed to disappear when I was with her. It is good to be home. “Michael, we need t’ have a talk.” About an hour later, Stacy and I were in our master bedroom, sitting next to each other on the bed. I knew this day was going to come, the day that we needed to talk about what had been going on since we’ve been married. “Do ye remember when we got married,” Stacy began, looking deep into my eyes. “Remember how happy we were?” I nodded. “Yes... yes I do.” “Well, it seems like yer not happy wit’ me anymore. We’ve been fightin’, ye’ve been snappin’ on the children an’ yer stress has been killin’ ye. Ye’ve changed so much tha’ I dunno if yer me husband anymore.” She broke down, tears were streaming down her face, and it broke my heart. “Wha’ has happened t’ ye, Mikey? Did I do somethin’ wrong t’ deserve all o’ this?” I too, started to cry. “No, Stacy,” I replied in a watery voice. “None of this is your fault. It’s mine. I’ve been so upset with how my career is going, and I’ve been taking it out on you, and all I did was run away, run away and go back on the road, while we left things unresolved.” I was weeping openly at this point, and I couldn’t get a hold of myself. “I never met to hurt you, Stacy, I’m so sorry!” I buried my face into her shoulder, crying so hard that my body was racked by the spasms. She wrapped her arms around me in a tight embrace, and she was crying hard too. We both were having a good cry, a cry that felt good. It was our healing process. The more we cried, the more we felt healed “I promise to be a better husband, Stacy,” I said. “I promise never to fight with your or with the kids again. Can you ever forgive me?” “Aye... I forgive ye.” She broke from her embrace, looking deep into my tear-filled. “We both need to work on being a married couple. We need t’ take our relationship one step at a time, an’ eventually, we’ll love an’ care for each other like we did before.” I smiled at Stacy. “I like that idea.” We hugged each other again and we stayed like that for a long time. Things were going to be all right.
