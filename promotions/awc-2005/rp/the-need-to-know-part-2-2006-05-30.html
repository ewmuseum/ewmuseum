The Need to Know (Part 2)

30 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30 May 2006 “So, you see, you and I are more alike than you would believe,” I mumbled to my reflection in a curbside, muddy puddle that was nestled into a concave decline in the street. It had pooled there, the water, and with no sewer drain to stream towards, a whitish, bubbling froth swirled atop the opaque settling of rainwater. The rain had stopped well before sunup, and I awoke to the bustling of skirted and trousered legs hurrying upwards or downwards along the sidewalk, which from my perspective, ran vertically. From time to time a passerby would stop and watch to make sure I was alive. Many were content simply knowing that I was, and they would continue onwards, but still a few attempted to rouse me from my spot with a shake of my shoulder and a few words in a language that seemed foreign to me. I shrugged these concerned activists off with verbal outbursts containing no consonants whatsoever and an outstretched arm swatting blindly back and forth like some decorative novelty pendulum set. It was with these repeated jostlings that I decided to sit up and cough out some garbled warning to keep strangers at a safe distance. As I sat on the curb, most of the people walking by formed an arc around me to avoid my reach, and as the arc would deflate if unattended, I would have to pay enough attention to reiterate my precautionary sentiments by baring sharp, vulgar fragments in order to reform an empty radius around me. I found this concept to be highly amusing, claiming for myself the responsibility of a natural sign of detour. People were conscious of me and had to intentionally reroute their paths, now forced to shift their hips and pay careful mind to the process of walking around an invisible bend. Oh, how I relished imagining my way inside their thoughts as they approached! One moment they would be musing over investments and portfolios- No! Better yet, they would be subtracting yet unpaid bills from their account balances when suddenly my presence would startle them, erasing their calculations and forcing them to think about the least awkward way to maneuver around me. “Ah, shit,” they’d mouth or even whisper before moving on and having to recalculate. And there I was disrupting pedestrian flow of traffic when I noticed a morning dryness in the back of my mouth. I cupped my hands and dipped them into the filthy puddle that collected near me. I threw the water back into my mouth, but it was a fluid texture, not consistent enough to constitute a solid, but still imaginably chewable. Regardless, my throat was too dry to swallow without great effort, and the more effort I exerted towards swallowing, the more overcomplicated the whole process became; a flourish of muscular contraction and relaxation that kneaded a chewed mass (for all things chewed appear remarkably similar in their indistinguishableness- all balled into a malleable dough) down the esophageal tract. It was borderline masturbatory, even; a tubular squeezing through a moist, dark, oblong cylinder. It was an ancient tribal ritual, a volcanic sacrifice to sate gods and gastric juices; all things large or small or someplace relative. It was all too much, this pan-existential multi-planar calibration, you see, for me to handle, so I refused to allow myself to swallow, and instead let the drink-like concoction stream back out of the corners of my mouth, running down through my beard before hanging off my face in slow brownish-gray stains that crept to gather again near the crossed sprouts of the hair on my chin. I looked down at my empty cupped hands, now holding the dissolved remains of the in between muck of tire treads and the underneath kickings of shoes. No longer were they melted, scythed flanks that swung and fleshly clattered against my wrists. They were the same five pronged bone puzzles- knuckled and wrapped in skin- that I would have usually regarded as the natural benefit of evolution. I had opposable thumbs again, god dammit! Strangely, though, my index fingers crooked outwards away from my thumbs. I couldn’t help but fret at the terrible inconveniences I had only just noticed this deformity would pose. For example, if I pointed at someone, my crooked index finger would curve to point actually at the ground between us. I used to take great pride in my ability to point at people. I never owned a laser pointer or other such assisted pointing tool. I never had to- leave that for the cripples, I’d say. It was the damned electricity, you see, that mangled my hands, and they apparently never healed quite properly. This was the first coherent sequence of events that has taken place in the ignorant reality of sobriety in quite some time for me. My body wasn’t used to it all. The sun was brighter than it had ever been, and my ears were ultra-sensitive to sound. Common sounds that the acclimated mind would usually ignore instead jutted out at me so that I heard everything around me mixed together inseparably as some noisy soup. I couldn’t focus on one thing or another, rather I was cursed to tolerate the entirety of it all; the jackhammer, rattlesnake beat of God shaking the universe like a kaleidoscope. *** I had previously spent one hundred and seven continuous hours alternating between electrically shocking myself and recovering from the numb sensation of temporary paralysis that followed shortly after. I would have kept at it, too, if the main breakers hadn’t shorted out. I had several names for the experience: plugging in, powering up, but what I referred to it as was “knowing.” The high, though brief, was incredible. It only lasted a few seconds, but while under its effect, time seemed to stretch on forever. The electricity sent a rush of coded information into the brain, and all neurons would surge simultaneously, allowing me to think and exist through seemingly infinite arrangements of thought at once. I felt like how I imagined God’s mind operated- completely under some omniscient power of observation and infinite wisdom, skeleton keys unlocking all combinations of potential. I was able to fluidly move through my own thoughts as well as thoughts that felt foreign, perhaps the thoughts of others. For seconds at a time, I knew everything at once, but still, there was no way to realistically apply this knowledge anywhere but within. I knew everything. Maybe I knew a bit too much. But after the electricity passed through me, I’d infallibly crash backwards and fall into minutes or hours of complete extinction, unsure if I would ever reemerge. But as soon as I did, newborn and thirsting for nutrients, I’d find my way back into my conducive role as a human lightning rod. I rose once again, some time ago, and wearily fit a length of copper wire into an outlet a foot above the floor, just high enough to force me to complete the arduous task of slightly raising my arm. This process in itself took minutes of steadying, as my arm was drained of strength and trembled under stress at any signal of movement. But when I was sure the wire was in the outlet, I felt nothing. I pushed with what little all of my might I had, and the wire fed deeper in. Still, I felt nothing. I was sure I hadn’t gotten used to it this easily because I knew that once my body became immune to the shock, I would possess a superhuman godlikeness, a real omniscience and omnipotence. Instead, I lay in a human puddle on the floor holding a faulty wire in a dysfunctional outlet. It was the god damned freemasons. I was sure of it! So I lay there for a while, cursing them as it was all I could do, but still holding the wire in the outlet in case a surge of power would happen to sneak through that I wouldn’t miss it. It didn’t. Slowly, I recovered, and within hours, I was able to prop myself up against the wall. Hasone was still out along the opposite wall. He had taken to knowing as well. He’d always been the inquisitive type; probably played with chemistry sets and the like as a kid. I sat with my back arched in tangent to the wall with my knees up under my chin and my arms clasped around my shins, waiting for him to come out of it. Maybe he’d know what to do. He was always handier than I was at fixing shit when it went out. I was somewhat used to letting the broken lay ruined. I let ruin collect. My floor was a graveyard for things that were no longer of use to me, and my feet grew callous and unable to be punctured (though my balance remained uncorrected for the unevenness) from walking on stiff corners and used needles; dirty silverware and glass shards. By contrast, Hasone kept his living space relatively clean, but his walls were patched with pictures of famous artwork, posters, and other random draperies. My walls were plain white. The only decorations I kept were empty nails and tacks. If I ever tried hanging something, it would eventually fall, and so I kept my things all on the floor. I sat on the floor, still in the same position, thinking all these slow, erratic thoughts. The limited capacity of the unelectrified mind frustrated me. I felt that everything moving at normal speed seemed to move much more slowly. The digital clock on the microwave was out. Without any measurement of time, it might as well have stopped. Hasone eventually came to after what was a good hour and a half of sitting there or comparing the speed of movements to the speed of the movements of their shadows cast from the dim light shining down through windows. I saw the reaction times of the shadows decrease noticeably. Hasone tried stabbing a bent fork at the wall outlet. “The power’s out,” I informed him dryly. “What?” he rushed back, excitedly and frantically. “I don’t know what happened. It’s been out. We should go mess with the box.” I shot back, hoping that the situation could be fixed. “Yeah, okay,” he agreed, but his voice sounded too tired to get up, and it was a while before either of us were willing to move. So we sat there for a few minutes staring at the floor because we couldn’t bear to look at each other, each ashamed that neither of us had done anything but shock ourselves for the last few days, and we could smell that we both stunk of burnt skin but neither of us cared. We were both too occupied with worrying about when the power would be working again so that we could know again. *** We eventually made it to the breaker switches and gave them a thorough toggling, but no combination of switches warranted even a flicker. The basement was cold, dark, and dirty, so we sat on top of the washer and dryer. I was on top of the dryer, cushioned by clothes that have been dry for a week, but have collected the basement smell for having not been brought up. We were surrounded by several containers of laundry detergent, each almost empty, and possibly altogether combinable for one or two more loads. “I’ll tell you what, Hasone. It’s those damned freemasons,” I said with a disgruntled puff on my cigarette. “Freemasons? What the fuck are you talking about?” Jason shot back. “I’m just trying to fucking keep to myself here. They could’ve killed me when I found them out, and I could’ve fucking gone public with what I saw in there, but I didn’t.” “No, man, what the hell are you talking about? Saw in there?” Jason asked. “The other day? I thought I would have told you already. Remember all that Ben Franklin shit we were talking about?” “Yeah.” “Well, I…when I was plugged in, I was putting shit together in my head, and it came to me that I heard somewhere that Benjamin Franklin held a high role in the freemasons right during an integral shifting point in masonry history.” And as I said that, I yearned deeply for one more shock, for the fluid remembrance of things I never knew from a being inside that knows all- a tunnel into the gathering of all human thought where time is traversable in all dimensions where thought exists on the other side of palpability. “You found that out by knowing?” “Yeah. It’s one of the only thing that stuck. I think it happened during the shock from the fence. That was the strongest one I had.” “Shit, I don’t remember anything…except that I knew everything, but I don’t remember what everything was.” And Hasone’s remark pretty much summed it up. All we were doing in the kitchen was keeping our addictions temporarily quenched for the moment. Hasone continued, “But what do the freemasons have to do with our power going out again? Maybe I should call the electric company.” “No, it was the damned freemasons. I fucking know it. I broke into one of their lodges. I just caught a glimpse, but they were all knowing, too.” “They know about knowing?” “I think they control it or something. They’re deep into it. Really crazy shit.” “Wait a minute. When’d you do this? You’ve been passed out on the kitchen floor for most of the week,” Hasone recalled, “and when you weren’t passed out, you were knowing again so you could pass out again.” “It was after I came to from pissing on the electric fence.” “I took you back home and put you on the couch while you were out.” “I don’t remember that.” “Maybe you only saw all that shit while you were knowing, man.” I sat there, dumbfounded, as if Hasone had just hit me in the face with a loaf of cement. I tried to put words together, but they formed even more slowly until something coherent came out. It was a question. “If I saw it while knowing, doesn’t that mean it was still real?” “Maybe, I don’t know.” “Yeah, it had to be. It was still real, even if it didn’t actually happen.” We both sat there thinking for a while, spitting out occasional possibilities, but only speaking them halfway through before deciding against their accuracies. Then it struck me. Figuratively. “Lightning.” “What?” My eyes lit up with the intensity of flashbulbs cutting through thick layers of darkness. “I need to get struck by lightning and live through it.” “What?” Hasone interrupted. But I continued as if he hadn’t. “And then it’ll stay. It’ll all stay forever, and I won’t have to constantly appease this curiosity. Hasone, is it supposed to storm anytime soon?” “How the fuck should I know? We’ve both been drooling-fucking-unconscious for the better part of the last few days.” It stormed that night, and we ventured outside to the most wide open field we could find and stood out in the middle like two fucking antennae- we even made helmets with rods jutting through, out, and up. We were the tallest fucking stacks of negative ions within sight. *** Withdrawal from electric shock addiction is a crazy bitch. I never got hit by lightning, and there I sat the next morning on the curb, having gone without a single shock since the afternoon before. I had passed out on the way back home; passed out because it was too difficult to get back. My muscles were shaking with spasms, and I walked back looking like I had just caught down syndrome. The next thing I knew, there I was parked in front of a dirty puddle on the curb. I needed to get home. I needed a fix; needed to know. I shakily tried to tremble up to my feet, but I did it too quickly and ended up falling back on my ass on the hard sidewalk. Someone nearly hurdled me without even bothering to try to help me up. And shit. I still had the damn helmet on. Embarrassed, I tried moving my sore arms up towards my pounding head to get the damn thing off, but I couldn’t bend the joint in my shoulders past a certain point, or my elbows for that matter. The frothy water puddle stains still dewed up at the bottom of my beard, and every so often my back muscles would spasm as I lay there like some sort of retarded suffocating fish. And shaking, I planted my palms on the ground and slowly pushed myself to my knees and further up to my feet. The process was lengthy, but soon I was unsurely wobbling in my legs and nodding my head in both directions looking for some familiar sign. When you’re plugged in, you know everything, and when you’re not, you remember knowing everything except what everything was. And when you need to know…when you REALLY FUCKING NEED TO KNOW; that’s when you remember you really did know something at some point, but you don’t know what that is either. I stumbled backwards and pressed myself against a large glass store window. I peered through the hours of operation sign and the logo, printed on transparent stickers, touting discounts and great deals. I peered through towards a stack of used TVs that were for sale, constantly plugged in, but turned to the static and blank screens that turned on sometimes when there was no programming on at the time. The store would open in a few hours, yet, so I sat back down at the puddle, not even sure of the sequence of events of things from the time I woke up on the curb until now. When did I drink the pool water? When did I fall over? When were people arcing around me or hurdling over me? I started to clench my teeth and grind them noisily there on the street side as cars whizzed by and the sun beat down intensely upon me- but more dull than a hammer- it more so bounced off of me like a mallet. I thought about ramming my body up against the glass and breaking into the store just to plug in, but I was too weak, and I wouldn’t be able to ram myself forcefully enough into the glass to break through it. But sixteen hours was far too long a dry spell for me to handle without the irritating, ticking pressure of addiction and of time running out before I forgot I was even going to go into the store when it opened in the first place. I’d end up forgetting even that, and sitting in front of a puddle until it dried and left me to die then, of thirst. So I pushed myself up again, hobbled towards the window and more or less leaned my body into it before sliding back to the sidewalk and staring up at the deceptively complete looking sky. It seemed so encompassing and unblemished, but there was sky above the places I wasn’t, too. And I heard there was a hole in it somewhere…caused by chemicals from hairsprays and other interesting things to huff and shit like that. If it’s already burnt through, let it fall. Fuck it. Who cares if I ever know again unless I can know it all forever; encompassing and unblemished? The door to the shop opened some time later, but I was too tired to go in. I’d go in when I could get back up, but until then, I waited for lightning to strike. And after I went in and messed with some wiring, I’d wait some more because regardless of whether I live through it or not, I’d rather not make it through than live without knowing if I would have.
