We Malfunction Like Machines

23 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 23rd, 2006 Cortland has eight hours of sunlight and rains like a thousand dead souls have come visiting from heaven. Mr. A will drift onto her window sill, skidding so hard an arm or a leg comes off, but souls are simply the same mass pressed into a different volume. Mr. A, a different shape, is still the same Mr. A. She opens the window and looks at him. “Hello, Mr. A,” she says, wanting to touch him, but her breath is hot enough to melt a man. With the demise of Mr. A, she decides to wait for Mr. B. Mr. B arrives when she goes outside. The flusters become drifts thick enough to swallow her legs like a sea shark in slow consumption, caught in a jagged pause. Mr. B lands on her eyelash and she can see him when she squints. He’s a blurred figure so large it looks like he’s taking up most of the space on her horizon. “Naughty Mr. B,” she laughs and laughs, laughs like a maniac. Her body trembles so hard that he trembles with her and cannot cling to this sudden influx of feeling. Mr. B, commitment-phobic and sensitive as a seismograph, offs himself on the cliff that was part of her. He is forever lost to gravity. She expects a Mr. C, because bad news always comes in threes. She closes her eyes and thinks about what her brief affair will be like, trying to cling to him until he vanishes like steam into cool air. He would’ve been some dead spirit brought alive by the onset of fall. He would’ve been a poet, dead young from pneumonia or writing too may odes to himself in the dead of the night, for Cortland has so many nights and that is when the trouble begins and ends. But Mr. C doesn’t come. He doesn’t knock on her door and flood in when she opens it in the morning. He isn’t the single drop of rain saturating her morning paper, nor one of the last few ones drifting lazily from a cloud. Ellis spends all morning searching for Mr. C. It’s getting sunny again, warm enough to melt just a little bit. Halfway down the cracked gravel with sweat collecting in the dip of her back is when she realizes that she has already found him. ** a. if these walls came crumbling down/fell so hard to make us lose our faith This is clandestine through reverse psychology, like dragging a dead body through the open, paved streets without anybody knowing. Girl doesn’t so much wear the sweatshirt as it’s draped on her like a circus tent. When she speaks her eyes wander, weary and agitated. She’s not smiling, but people who’ve been through the shit she’s been through today rarely do. “I, uh,” Girl begins in a fit of respiratory epilepsy with geometric syllables, stuttering and searching for something, anything. “I.. I kind of had an appointment – earlier in the day, I mean, and, I --.” Receptionist cuts her off before she can find it. The desk lamp lights the thin edges of Receptionist’s face, the curve of her eyelids, rim of a mouth and loops them into a warm, remote summer. Receptionist says, “I’m sorry, ma’am. We’re closed.” Girl’s eyebrows twitch. She crunches cartilage beneath molars. There’s some sort of pause before she says, “Closed?” “Our hours are on the door. We close 8PM Monday through Saturday.” “But.. I – I need this.” Receptionist shifts in her chair and refolds her legs. She says, “I can reschedule you, if you’d like.” Girl’s eyes sparkle, somewhat, with a glint of hope. “Tomorrow?” Miss Receptionist sighs and starts to rifle through the schedule book in front of her. Fourteen seconds later: “The earliest I can do for you is July 20th.” Then, it’s this: the sound of syllables slipping wetly down her ears, something so familiar and foreign, something Girl knows she should understand. She ought to understand, and she doesn’t; it’s something maddening like wrinkles in white shirts or going crazy in an empty room with no one to hear or see or care. Receptionist says, “Ma’am? Are you alright?” Girl wants to say no, I’m not alright. I’m not fucking alright. I’m knocked up with some clown’s baby and you’re fucking telling me that I can’t get rid of it? That I have to wait? Do you know the ridiculous bullshit I’ve been through today, just to get here? Do you even have a fucking idea? All I want is to get rid of this baby. All I want is to get rid of is – Boy walks in, snapping a cell phone shut. Girl turns only her head to look at him, for a second, and she’s far too wrapped up in herself and this baby and this stupid fucking Receptionist to register the poignant woe curving the pleats of his face. Girl’s head turns back around. She stares at the Receptionist and says, to her Boy, “They’re closed.” And really, this is the last thing that her Boy needs to hear. “Closed? What the fuck are you talking about? It’s eight-FUCKING-thirty!” “Sir,” Receptionist begins, following a short sigh, “Our office hours are posted clearly on the door, and –” “It’s eight-FUCKING-thirty!” repeats Boy. Receptionist is exasperated. “Yes, sir, and the office closes at –” “Nine.” “No, sir. We close at –” “Nine?” “Eight!” yells Receptionist, “We close at eight! For God’s sake, what is wrong with you people?!” Insert pause here. Boy petulantly folds his arms across his chest, while Girl awkwardly shifts weight between her legs and picks up for the both of them with, “Look. This is really important, okay? We can’t wait until July 20th. I need this... this, I need it out of me. Please.” Receptionist shakes her head, closing the schedule book. “I’m sorry, ma’am, but there’s nothing I can do for you. I wish there was, but the earliest date available is July 20th. You could always try another clinic.” Girl looks at Boy, but Boy is staring at the wall behind Receptionist’s head. He says nothing, but Girl knows him better than that: the slant of his eyes and the down-quirk of his lips’ corners say it all. “Come on, Adam,” sighs Girl. ** Thumbs tucked into back pockets; narrow hips and easy, violent hands, shoulders soft and kind. Adam's like that on weekdays, deceptive and easy to love. Ellis wants to stand by him, a step behind with her heels nicking the outlines of his shadow under the early morning sunlight. It’s never bright in Cortland and when it is the light is everywhere - artificial and unrevealing, the sort of light that’s kind to liars and hiders. Ellis is neither, she tells herself, and sometimes dresses to match him but nothing she owns is as exquisite as the spread of fabric around his shoulders, crisp and spotless, cruel enough for some semblance of beauty. That’s fine by Ellis. She wouldn’t have it any other way. Mornings are spent in bed smoking joints, paint chips flaking her hands from the ceiling anonymous and unfeeling above their heads. She can feel it pressing down. He could be standing on the edge of a building, feet narrow and arms crossed and telling her come here so she will and when she’s walking over, she’ll think that she’d probably jump off if he’d ask, that she would break her bones and shatter her skull so he could see the beauty of dark clothing and red liquid against the white pavement from seven stories up. He’d say what are you going to do with your life and she would look at her knuckles and say i'll do what you do and all he would do is place both his hands on either side of her neck and close his eyes and she’d be thinking, she’d be breathing one two three one two three and thinking this means you trust me, this means you -- because she could lose her balance right then and they would both fall to their deaths and make worthwhile news for the first and last times in their lives, she would be breathing and thinking when he opens his eyes again and steps away from the edge, steps away and walks away and doesn’t look back. It’s like that. What she doesn’t realize is that he’s never cared much for grabbing life and holding onto it, that he maybe thinks she’s a fucking coward because she’d never do anything to hurt him, that he’s the only honest person she’s ever known because he never says a single goddamned thing that means anything. She’d watched him get dressed that morning, the relaxed curve of his back, muscles neatly mathematical lines; bones and tendons stretched, loose. It’s stupid, Ellis knows this, but it's something akin to art, to the reflection of light, shadow. It’s easy to want things, she knows this too, and it’s easy to want him. She doesn’t know who wouldn’t; the careful eyes but reckless smile, the easy way he has of pulling hair from his eyes and propping an elbow on the kitchen counter, messy mouth and clean jaw. She doesn’t know who wouldn’t want him and she doesn’t know why he stays and, mostly, she doesn’t care. She has him and she’ll keep him this close for as long as it lasts, for as long, and: And one day it’ll be the afternoon and she’ll find him leaning against a wall, watching the cars drive by, thumbs tucked into back pockets and a fatty hanging from his lips. He’ll walk away and she’ll follow. She’ll be back to treading on the fingers of his shadow – his shadow, growing longer and light growing darker and the weight of love heavier in her chest. ** Boy and Girl stand outside the abortion clinic, Boy unusually silent and Girl, hands drawn together in the circus tent’s pocket, equally silent. Something is in the air between them, suspended and heavy, but Girl knows Boy better than this. Girl knows Boy is sometimes a little motherfucking brat about things, and he yells and capslocks and whines too much and he wears gay shirts and a stupid gay crown and watches Rachel Ray instead of football. Girl knows Boy is headstrong about what he wants and is sometimes selfish and annoying and manipulative, and Girl knows Boy isn’t going to say anything until she says something. “Adam.” Simple and uncomplicated, because that’s how Girl is. Mr. Boy’s chest heaves with a broad sigh. His arms unfold and his thumbs go to hook in his jeans’ pockets. Silence, deep in its leverage. Girl doesn’t want to push it, really, but this is a fucking baby they’re talking about. “Will you talk to me?” Pause. A’fucking’gain. “What is there to talk about?” answers Boy, insinuating that he doesn’t know Girl knows him better than that, and Girl would maybe be offended in any other situation. “What isn’t there to talk about?” retaliates Girl. Boy shrugs, diverting his gaze. This is how things go. People are meant for love, built to live on the idea of it, hands and mouths designed to take it in and spit it out. Girl watches him, the moon at his back, Boy a dark edge against the luminance. She says, “Why don’t we just find another clinic, okay?” It’s the way the tip of Boy’s shoe scuffs at the cracked cement that makes Girl wonder if Boy was ever really in this one-hundred percent. “Okay,” murmurs Boy, air filling his lungs. Boy may be selfish and annoying and manipulative, but Girl knows Boy is just another one of those emotional train wrecks. He is sensitive and funny and he loves her, and Girl wants to say I love you, too, I do. I want you to know I love you, and don’t believe anything else I say. They grab each other’s hands and walks towards Girl’s car, and Boys’ fingerprints leave faint and decayed traces of oil on the hill of her knuckles. ** Boy fiddles with the radio dial and changes the station for Girl, even though she didn’t ask him to. Boy falls asleep halfway there, hair stubborn and light against the tinted windows. When he wakes, Girl is staring at the red traffic light, mouth dull and tired. Boy looks at her, strangely, and asks where they are. Girl tells him that we’re almost home, actually, and Boy smiles a little, turning away and watching lights past, messy and flickering but uniform enough to form patterns. His lips part like he’s going to say something secret to the world, like he’s going to slip shards of words through the slitted window opening and Girl thought that maybe he was in love with the world, and maybe, well. Maybe she doesn’t have Mr. C afterall.
