Conversational Rush Hour

Date: like five seconds ago ''I still don't get it...'' Johnny was Darcy's very best of friends. He was practically a brother. That brother, however, had been kitty-sitting all weekend and couldn't - within ANY state of mind - understand why such an absent-minded and outlandish couple would dare to try keeping a living creature between them. ''...You can't keep a plant alive, so you get a cat instead?'' Despite the harsh words to come, Amy was speaking in a playful tone. She had no ill-will toward Johnny, just a casual friendship by association. ''Cats can get their own food if need be. I've never seen a bush run after a squirrel, no matter how high I've been...'' she grinned, back-handedly swatting him on the arm, ''Now take off, jackass!'' Johnny chuckled, ''Not yet, where's Darc'?'' ''Banking, he'll be back and cranky soon.'' ''Then I'll stick around a bit. Besides,'' he smirked, ''I've kinda taken a liking to the pussy...'' Amy cocked an eyebrow and immediately scooped Reaper up into her arms, pointing at Johnny with an accusing hand while she spoke orders to the midnight black cat. ''If he touched you in any way you weren't comfortable with, meow twice and wink...'' ''Wha...'' ''The kitty WINKED, Johnny, that's half way there!'' ''Are you ever even a little normal?'' ''When I'm asleep, maybe?'' ''I dunno, that's not something I ask Darcy about,'' Johnny shrugged, ''But I did hear about your beachfront stunt.'' ''... Here we go...'' Darcy's best friend was hardly the most qualified cat-sitter in the world, but he was guaranteed to do the job because he knew the only other option was death, so when the pair went to Brazil... well... their Reaper couldn't be left alone with the house to himself. Giving Johnny fifty bucks to watch the feline made some sense, being that he'd be the only person Markson would dare trust with the key to his home, and one of a few that Amy would trust with the life of their cat. She should've expected that Darcy would've spilled about the proposal during one of his 'check-up' calls to his buddy that weekend, but her eyes still rolled nearly all the way to the back of her head when Johnny was so daring in bringing it up. He smirked, ''Were you drunk or what? I mean, you can tell me.'' ''I'm not gonna stand here explaining myself to you, John.'' ''Then we'll sit down.'' THE PURGE ________________________________________ Darcy was home. Alone, for once. Amy and Johnny were off bickering aboutsomething at a coffee shop, according to the text message the fiery little redhead sent. Finally... alone. There was never a spare second. Not only did Darcy find free time hard to come by; he’d been having a similar problem with time to himself. However, this predicament had been rectified as well. Gone were the relentless hounding of reporters and Institute higher-ups. The constant barrage of requests for advice and confirmation were vanishing like flies in winter. The call log on his cell phone was an excellent reminder for him. He’d been off the payroll for nearly three weeks, and the number of calls he received so far over the weekend dropped from a staggering ten to fifteen, which he’d come to expect as of late, to a svelte two: one from his mother, asking if he’d be around for Father’s Day, and the other an uneventful wrong number. And with the combination of Darcy having visited his parents for Father’s Day the day before, Amy out of the house all afternoon and no immediate plans to see anyone else, Darcy found himself alone, in his home, for the first time in a long time. So how to occupy himself? The possibilities appeared endless. His guitar was overdue to be recovered from abandonment; Darcy couldn’t remember so much as touching it since he serenaded Amy on Valentine’s Day. The entire DVD set of the subversively hilarious sock puppets Sifl & Olly had recently arrived in the mail, still remaining in the package for Darcy’s perusal. The World Cup awaited Darcy on the television if he so chose…granted, it would certainly turn out to be the invariable thumping of Saudi Arabia by Ukraine, but soccer was rapidly earning a more respectable role in Darcy’s love of athletic competition. But these were all merely idle pursuits. In the back of his mind, Darcy knew along that there was something he’d put off doing for far too long. And with no certainty as to the next time he’d have such a large block of time to himself again, he knew it would be foolhardy not to capitalize on the current opportunity. It was time for Darcy to receive some much needed guidance. Darcy had learned how to accept guidance from what local ayahuasqueros in Brazil called “the great spirits” in their ceremonies that celebrated the union between the two realms of existence, Life and Death. These ceremonies centered on the consumption of a bitter, hallucinogenic drink known as the “vine of souls.” Fortunately, Darcy kept a jar of the drink in an otherwise seldom used mini-fridge that he kept tucked in his laundry closet. There, the tannins within the liquid that had the same chemical makeup as bathroom soap could settle out from the rest of the liquid, preventing what could potentially surface in terrible nausea and vomiting. The Brazilian yage warriors did not bother with the separation of the soap from the tea, believing it to be in fact beneficial to wash out their insides with soap and sacred tea. Darcy, however, who had grown up in a society that seemed to frown upon purposely throwing up, understood that he would have to make changes to the formula. After all, he’d get nothing out of the experience if he couldn’t hold the tea long enough in his body to take advantage of its extraordinary contents. To the outsider, it would appear that the ceremony Darcy was about to prepare for was simply the abuse of a rare, but ultimately harmful hallucinogenic drug. Darcy himself was a bit skeptical when first exposed to the tea by the natives with whom he resided in the rainforest, but he came to see the drink as less a drug than it was a tool with almost religious implications. The yage warriors that consumed this drink they called yage indeed had formed an entire religion upon this very ceremony, and in participating in the ceremony Darcy saw for himself things that could not be explained by mere science, and events such as people being healed, souls being cleansed and faith in the divine restored tenfold over what it had been before. Darcy himself now regarded yage as sacred, but was still wary of making his use of it known to the public. He frowned on those who flaunted their drug use…especially the Royal Court. Particularly Adam Dick and Ellis Nash, who were not above stoning their IQs into oblivion on camera for all - including young, impressionable children – to see. Even though he saw nothing wrong with what he did, Darcy kept his use of the tea hidden from everyone. Even Amy was not aware that he still partook in the tea, with Darcy having only hinted that he’d experimented with the drink on a few occasions while living with the Brazilian rainforest natives. If nothing else, the last thing Darcy wanted was for his secret to leak out to the wrong people, and the press having a field day with headlines like “Darcy the Drug Addict!” There was also the unwanted matter of police possibly electing to get involved. Yage was considered a Schedule I hallucinogen, illegal to possess under U.S. law. While it had recently been ruled legal for use by religious congregations in nearby New Mexico, and Darcy was fully prepared for a legal battle should the law attempt to take his yage away, he still preferred to avoid any sort of battle over the matter at all, if possible. He didn’t enjoy keeping something that had given him so much a secret from his family and friends, but the old adage proved true yet again: Better safe than sorry. Or severely irritated. With the jar now in hand, Darcy returned to the front door of his apartment, and locked it to ease any paranoia of someone dropping in unannounced. This was not Darcy’s first attempt in preparing his special recipe for the tea, and by now he’d had it down to a science. Within minutes, the brew was prepared. First, Darcy strained out the undesirable tannins with an old “AYATOLLA ASSAHOLA” T-shirt barring the visage of Ayatollah Khomeni that Darcy’s dad gave him a few years ago. While it could be now consumed in this state, Darcy elected to place the remaining liquid into a porcelain pot on the stove to boil as much of it away as possible. As he waited for the tea to boil Darcy put some Syrian Rue into some empty gel caps. The powdery seeds that hailed from Syria functioned as monoamine oxidase inhibitors, or MAOIs. These inhibitors were charged with the task of preventing the MAOs located within the body to “steal” the hallucinogenic properties of the tea for their own purposes before they could reach the brain. The gel caps were to prevent Darcy from tasting the rue, which in his mind tasted even more bitter than the astringent tea. He consumed about three grams of the rue, which would require about thirty minutes to take effect. Darcy used this time to prepare everything exactly to his preferred specifications. He headed for his bedroom, the room in his loft that allowed in the least amount of light from the East Bay sun. Darcy turned down the blinds to get the room as dark as he possibly could. The only light Darcy would need for this endeavor would come by candlelight, which he achieved by lighting a few candles that also happened to give off the aroma of lilacs. Both Amy and Johnny, the two that frequented his East Bay abode the most, gave Darcy flack for his strange penchant for scented candles. Darcy sheepishly shrugged to their queries about the candles, allowing them to believe that it was simply a strange taste of his…in truth, Darcy didn’t care much for candles, and used them pretty much only in his yage ceremonies. The lilac scented variety was just to throw them off his trail. With the tea now boiled down and cooled, Darcy carefully poured out the precise amount with the aid of a turkey baster. Even with the amount of experience he’d had with yage that only a handful of white men could match, he was still quite frightened by the prospect of taking too much of the stuff. He settled on an amount containing around ten milligrams of the tea’s special chemical properties. Once he’d seen that enough time had elapsed for the MAOI to have taken effect, Darcy drank his carefully prepared tea. It tasted more foul than anything one could possibly imagine, and he had to fight his gag reflex more than once before finally getting the tea to travel down his throat. If Darcy had any reservations about the journey he was about to undertake, there was no going back now. Darcy idled over to his bedroom, where he planned on remaining firmly for the next several hours. Using one of the nearby candles, Darcy lit a cigar and puffed the smoke about the room’s confines. Darcy was vehemently against smoking, but cigars served a special purpose in yage ceremonies. Apparently tobacco was also regarded as sacred by the yage warriors, who smoked it during the tea-drinking ritual to ward off evil spirits who might attempt to take control of their souls while they were under the tea’s spell. This came as a surprise to Darcy, as it must to anyone else who trusts the validity of science and their finding that tobacco does more harm to the body than good. But Darcy was far more wary of being possessed by an evil spirit than he was of tobacco’s carcinogenic properties, and decided that partaking in a cigar solely during this ritual was not only acceptable, but probably the safer route as well. After a few minutes the yage began to take effect on Darcy. When he closed his eyes his “vision” was overtaken by brightly colored patterns of red, green and blue, patterns that he could not describe with the least bit of justice no matter how many times he’d seen them. They appeared to resemble etchings one might find on the inside of an ancient Aztec temple, in a language lost to all but those who once possessed the native tongue. These patterns were truly a spectacle to be seen, and they began to shine brighter and wave about in front Darcy, even pervading his vision when he opened his eyes. One might very well be prone to get lost in these early visions, but Darcy had had enough experience with the sacred tea to know that these visions were merely the tip of the iceberg. Rather than attempting to focus on any one of the flowing patterns or discern their meaning, Darcy allowed his thoughts to flow freely while waiting for the next stage of the ritual. As his thoughts often did when allowed to flow freely, they turned to Amy…specifically, what had transpired in Rio several weeks ago. He was unable to shake the mental picture of just how hurt she looked when he begged her to retract her asking of him to marry her. It wasn’t that Darcy had a problem with marriage…despite the abysmal fifty percent of marriages that ended in divorce in his native United States, Darcy had seen marriage work in countless places. His parents had been married for over thirty years, and both sets of his grandparents were pushing fifty years themselves. Nor did Darcy doubt his love for Amy, nor her of his. The two had first met when she was a lost, impassioned 18 year old, and he a hotheaded, narrow-minded man just barely old enough to legally purchase alcohol. He knew all along that their first attempt at a relationship could only end in disaster, and his walking out on her - not to mention the rest of the civilized world, as he was in desperate need of soul searching after his traumatic back injury pulled him from the wrestling ranks – was in fact the best scenario he could have hoped for, despite the screaming, sobbing and abundance of broken beer bottles that accompanied his exit. But things were different now. For one, they were both older, and had gained much wisdom in the years they’d spent without each other. They were both very different people – Darcy saw that there was more to life than simply occupying the spotlight of a champion. For her part, Amy had toned down her fondness of public indecency and lewd conduct, which added up to far less time in the county lockup. They were both calmer, more collected in their thoughts and above all else, able to understand how the other worked, and how to best serve each other’s interests without outright bending their wills. To put it simply; where before it had been an impossibility, Darcy Markson and Amy Campbell were actually able to be with each other. They didn’t always get along, and spent a lot more time at odds than Darcy particularly cared for. But it was love. And it worked. For now. This was the key to Darcy’s tremendous reservations about marrying Amy. He had no problem with marriage, nor did he doubt his love for Amy. Rather, it was his qualms about fate…in particular, how fate had a way of cruelly shaping Darcy’s life. Like any American boy, Darcy had been brought up to believe that anything was possible, that he could do anything he wanted so long as he had the courage to follow his heart. For many years he’d taken that belief to heart, always daring to dream of the possibilities that awaited him. But a series of consecutive, monumental failures led Darcy to believe that fate worked just a little bit differently for him… The disastrous breakup with Jane. Dropping out of college. A career in professional wrestling that brought him to highs he never believed he could reach before the rug was cruelly pulled out from under him, permanently injuring his back and forcing him into early retirement. And most recently, his firing from the Tomorrow Institute by David Jameson. All this added up to a new belief that governed Darcy’s life in recent years, a belief that hinged on two very simple, yet unyielding rules: 1) Nothing lasts forever, no matter how good it might seem. 2) Any time Darcy doubted the validity of Rule #1, a cruel twist of fate would unfold to keep him well reminded. And this was the problem. Darcy had always believed that marriage was the ultimate symbol, the ultimate statement that the love between two people would last forever. By agreeing to marry Amy, which he could confess to considering on more than one occasion, it would be as if he was directly tempting fate to intervene and take away one more thing he held near and dear to his very being. Darcy had let Amy get away once, and was determined not to make the same mistake again…even if his reasoning seemed a bit ironic in refusing to be at her side until death forced them apart. Any further thoughts he had on the matter were quickly forced away when Darcy felt himself gag. It was time for what the Brazilian natives liked to call la purga…literally, “the purge.” He’d done the best he could to remove the properties of the tea that caused the terrible vomiting, but there was no avoiding it altogether. In fact, as the natives suggested in their willingness to have their insides cleansed by the soap-like tannins, la purga was considered to be a necessary part of the ceremony. With no time to spare, Darcy made it to his bathroom just in time to violently expel the contents of his stomach into the (fortunately) already open toilet. While he had avoided the taste of the Syrian Rue on the way down, there was no avoiding the taste of it now that it had come back up and into his mouth. Darcy silently cursed to himself as he attempted to expel every bit of the rue from his pallet along with the equally foul-tasting brew. It took several minutes for the task to be complete, by which time Darcy was now a heaving mess crumpled on the bathroom tile. But he could feel the Vine of the Soul splitting his soul from his psyche, removing it from the physical confines of the body lying immobile near the toilet and taking it to a new place altogether. Just as the yage warriors had always claimed, it was only after la purga that his journey could truly begin. LATER... ________________________________________ They ended up in a diner, sipping on Cokes and a beer each. Amy was midway through trying to exlain her motivation for proposal when Johnny's phone rang. ''Hey, br--'' stuttered Johnny, his tone growing concerned and frantic, ''Wait, what?'' He glanced at the little redhead, then down to the tabletop... but his tone gave it all away. It was Darcy, and something was wrong. ''Lemme talk to,'' Amy began before John interrupted, consumed by whatever Darcy was saying. Very accidentally, he uttered the wrong word. ''Jane?! You've gotta be kidding m--WAIT, AMY! DAMNIT!'' Whether Jane was there in that second or not, Amy was on her way back to Darcy with murderous intent toward his well-known ex. Johnny continued to his best friend through the reciever of the cell, ''Don't worry, I'll catch Amy in a minute. It's not like she's got wheels...'' THAT was when he scratched his hand across the table and noticed something significant. ''Uh oh...'' He checked his pockets frantically, and looked around in a frenzy. ''SHIT!'' No keys.
