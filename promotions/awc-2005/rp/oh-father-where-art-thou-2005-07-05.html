Oh Father, Where Art Thou?

5 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/7/05 Here’s to the wings of a bird -Irish political prisoner, 1916 1. Bittersweet Symphony Post-Solarized With hindsight, Paddy O’Shea knew things had gone well. Their recent attempts in the Alliance League hadn’t been futile; after all, he had an Alliance belt sitting on the bed adjacent to him, glimmering in the hazy light of the caravan. He had reaped his reward – the ultimate reward in the Alliance division and as far as everyone was concerned, Emerald Isle was the best team the AWC had to offer. They would forever be remembered as the first champions – the originals; and this made Paddy flush with pride. It was good to be part of something like this. Paddy is standing at the doorway to his makeshift bedroom. The actual caravan has actually been in his possession for around a month whereas in Ireland, he lived in his parent’s caravan. This mobile caravan was kindly granted by the AWC as a clause in his contract. Yet, a month on, and the caravan is already in dire straits. The door that is sitting open has a mahogany finish but what takes away from this beautiful specimen is two large holes and several small ones that protrude from it. They seem like huge gashes, boring into it. The bedroom was painted cream but already, this is decolouring and there are a number of stains on the walls including tomato sauce and vomit. A normal person would be disgusted at it’s condition and the it’s rapid demise but to Paddy, this is what reminds him of home and he wouldn’t change it for all the curry in China. For the good and the bad memories Paddy walks over to the bed and sits down on the (unusually) clean duvet. He is wearing a pair of khaki three quarter length shorts and a pair of beach sandals. He obviously hasn’t shaven for a number of days because of the appearing stubble bristling his lower face and his hair has the ‘just-woke-up’ appearance, the dirty fairness blurring into a haze of frizz. But his eyes seem so tired He rubs his stubby index finger of his right hand over his worntorn face. He first feels out a number of scars. One on his cheek that was cut by a gang of Dublin boys with glass on a drunken night out. Then there is an even larger one parallel to his hairline. Paddy thinks he received that one after falling off a tree when he was around 7 or 8 but isn’t exactly sure. Then he feels his new wounds. His feels the bulbous swelling below his eye, which he got when he was DDTed onto a steel chair. Then there’s his cheekbone which has been damaged in some way and which he is going to check out later that day. He remembers vividly that he felt numbness in his cheek when he was thrown off the ladder and hit the mat below with shocking impact They say only those who work will eat. And now as he identifies the side effects of the work he realises how much he deserves this title before him. Paddy picks it up and spreads it over his lap with complete reverence for the belt. It’s silver and beautiful he thinks. He traces his finger around the outline of the eagle then traces his name which has been engraved at the bottom “Alliance Champion” he says to seemingly no one Yes, right now, he should be celebrating seeing as it’s only the day after the actual event and he’s almost sure Crimson will be doing so right now. But he can’t because an even more pressing issue is on his mind. His father is still missing. There’s been no contact from the kidnappers and that is unnerving. Recently, revelations by the Irish police have almost definitely connected the kidnappers with the AWC and therefore connected the kidnappers with him. It is only human for him now to feel guilty now. In fact, he only came back for the Solarized PPV to fight for two reasons. He expected the kidnappers to reveal themselves at the PPV but only if he was there. Very rational but yet the fact there had been no appearance had scared Paddy beyond belief. He thought he knew how they would be thinking but now he wasn’t at all sure. Perhaps they wanted Paddy to sweat it out longer; perhaps they didn’t even have any intention of revealing themselves. The fact he would never see his father again was almost too hard to bear right now. Then the second reason: he had promised Crimson O’Malec a team partner and after he put his faith in a relative newbie, after he’d risked his reputation for him, Paddy just had to deliver. Paddy knew Crimson would be up against it with the Solar power match and Paddy’s absence would have been devastating. So now, he had done what he had set out to do but he refused to enjoy it, not while there was still no word on his father. This was despite the fact that he had a basic grasp of the concept that his father would be over the moon if he knew that he had won. He pushed the title away and lay back on his bed with laziness that reflected the fatigue running through his muscles. He put his arms behind his head, which revealed his protruding intercoastal muscles – both well developed yet weary. They twisted and coursed as they surged up his ribs with growing ferocity as they ascended. Paddy let out a sigh as he stretched and then sighed again as the thought of his father resurfaced in his mind. Paddy forced it back again temporarily but this process was becoming increasingly difficult as his mental strength deteriorated. He was staring at the flaking ceiling but not actually looking at it. Instead he was thinking about the conversation he had with his brother Owen on the day of the PPV, hours before he won the Alliance title… 2. Hyping Paddy He was sitting in a telephone box yesterday when he had the conversation. This was located about two miles from the peripheral of New York. He had gotten up early at around seven because he knew that he was around five hours behind compared to GMT. At the time he dialled the number with a shaky hand, he was still worrying about his fathers’ whereabouts. But as he hung up the phone, a feeling came over he had not felt since his father went missing – did he even care? “Hello…ma?” Paddy had asked when he heard the rise of the receiver from the phone’s body on the other end of the line “No, Paddy, it’s me, Owen. She’s away out t’ the shop fer milk”, Owens voice sounded weary and disappointed. Paddy supposed he ultimately had hoped it was his fathers’ voice and he knew that feeling “Aye take it, tha’ there be no word then?” Paddy enquired. He had picked up the lack of enthusiasm in Owens and now he was the one feeling disappointed. “No…sorry, Paddy. The police say they may have a lead somewhere in Cork. Maybe it be going somewhere”, the glint of hopefulness was apparent in Owens voice. Hope was something Paddy had always admired and he refused to diminish this from Owen despite his sceptical outlook on life in general “Aye…it looks like it Owen. How are things at home?” “Well, obviously things aren’t back to normal. Aye mean, how could they be? But, aye suppose everything’s goin’ as well as can be expected” “That’s good aye s’pose”, Paddy instantly regretted asking that question. How the hell could things be alright? It was insensitive and ridiculous all rolled into one. Paddy was about to try and change the subject and perhaps cheer Owen up until he did the honours for him “And what about you? The PPV’s coming up and ye’ve got a shot at the tag titles. After three weeks in the federation, tha’ be feckin’ brilliant”, Owen said with forced cheerfulness. Owen always would betray his emotions to make other people feel better and Paddy had always admired him for that. “Oh aye…that.” “What’s wrong Paddy? Aren’t ye lookin’ forward t’ it?” “Aye am…well, aye was. Its just tha’ aye’m finding it hard t’ plan or even wrestle with da still missin’…” Paddy said with a sigh. He then realised he had brought the subject of his father again and cursed his stupidity. His father wasn’t here and as Owen’s older brother, he had to appear strong for him, even if it was tearing him to pieces inside. “But Paddy – ye have t’ wrestle! Ye kno’ how much it means t’ us t’ see ye fight each week. Seeing ye makin’ something o’ yerself is helpin’ us get through this time” Owen sounded exasperated and slightly flustered. “Aye’m sorry Owen. O’ course aye’m fightin’ grand. Don’ worry, aye’m ready fer the PPV”, Paddy was attempting to cover up his previous statement and he knew how unconvincing it sounded in his ears. He supposed this lack of genuinity that persuaded Owen to resort to a last resort. “Look, if yer still worried about da…aye…ah…well, he wasn’ the best o’ da’s was he? Aye mean, it be not like we should worry about him tha’ much. He wasn’ around much and even when he was, he was pickin’ on ye. So jus’ ye go win and don’ think about it” Owen said. Paddy with a straight mind would have been able to tell instantly that Owen was trying to hype Paddy up for his match by taking his mind away from other matters and making him focus on what lay ahead. Paddy however didn’t pick this up until a day later and he was shocked at what Owen said “How can ye say tha’?” Paddy asked more firmly than he’d intended. “Da be more important than wrestlin’ fer feck sake!” “Look just, go out and win fer us would ye? It would mean so much to the family. But remember, just keep yer mind away from Da…he…he…isn’ worth it…remember when he put ye through a wall…?” Paddy froze in the phone box. For the last week, he’d been thinking about all the fantastic things his father had done and how much he needed to get him back. Now Owen had reminded him of one thing where his father had left Paddy with a murderous rage. And this thought of one event resulted in a chain reaction where more and more events came flooding back, events where Paddy could have killed his father if he’d been pushed any further. Owen didn’t mean for Paddy to hate his father, he only wanted to hype Paddy up. The idea was a good one Paddy now thought in the present time but that event was still haunting him… 3. Friend Or Foe? The event that Paddy was reminded of that triggered a new wave of hate towards his father was clear as crystal in his mind again. It hurt him even to think about it. About how his father lost it. He’d been hurt physically slightly then but the emotional carnage was at a whole higher level. So high, he temporarily had a feeling of genuine hate for his father. That subsided luckily but now he was just confused…really, should he care? Paddy predicted he was about 19 at the time. He’d fought with his father before in arguments but it had only came to blows once when he was 16. Danny had left Paddy a bloody wreck for his trouble. Danny had always been a key believer in the idea that discipline should be severe so a resurgence of the act didn’t come about. This event hadn’t been as bloody but it sure as hell hurt him in a way the first event didn’t Paddy had been sitting in the caravan at the time. He was sitting on a makeshift sofa reading a newspaper and the time was around ten O’clock. The fact that it was mid winter was also the reason it was pitch dark outside. Danny had burst in like a madman but that was normal. Paddy had glanced at him in his drunken state, a precedent of every Friday for the last five years “Where’s ‘em logs boy?” Danny asked aggressively. There was a burning rage in his eyes. Paddy thought that he must have been drinking vodka and had ended up causing a raucous at the bar. Paddy had seen it a million times before but Danny had never turned this drunken anger to anyone in his family “Aye’ll get them tomara da. Aye forgot all about it,” Paddy said nonchalantly. He then turned back to reading the newspaper, which he held up to his face to avoid looking at the intoxicated mess before him. He could deal with drunks especially his father and him getting aggressive was the last thing on his mind “…Boy…” Danny said calmly Paddy lowered the newspaper to see what his father wanted now, only to receive a blow from a baseball hat onto the crown of his head. Paddy pictured his neck compressing like the folding of an accordion and it hurt like hell. Paddy fell back, fighting away the consciousness, which was trying desperately to overpower him. But Paddy clung to the sound of his fathers’ voice, still clinging to the idea that his father would always protect him from harm, even after his father was the attacker. Paddy supposed that was the day he lost all the dependency of a child Danny lurched over to Paddy and fell forward, stopping himself from head butting his son by planting his hands in the front of the sofa. Paddy was completely dazed and he felt his arms going numb but his other senses were fairly acute and he remembered how his fathers’ breath, rabid with the stench of alcohol filled his nostrils, getting stronger as he breathed out but temporarily weakened as he sucked it back in. Paddy was shocked at the appearance of his father. He felt like he was looking at himself 25 years from now and it was genuinely disturbing. His eyes though were mad, darting about yet bulging with complete intensity and passion “Aye told ye t’do somethin’ didn’ aye? Don’ aye deserve enough respect t’get tha’ done?” Danny barked. He was a man on the edge and Paddy was genuinely scared as he swayed from side to side. He didn’t think he was actually bleeding until the crimson plasma came flowing down his brow and into his eyes, stinging his eyes in their sockets. Yet he was too weak to do anything about it “ANSWER ME!” But Paddy couldn’t, all he could to do was mumble the same word over and over again: why? This perceived ignorance by Danny only intensified the fire of anger burning brightly within Danny’s mind “Fine ye wee bastard! Ye won’t give me respect? Being completely honest are ye? Want me t’be a bit honest?” Danny said, freckling Paddy’s face with a fine spray of spittle. Danny stood up and wiped himself down. It was unusual that such unprovoked brutality was inflicted by a man now ironing out the creases on his T-Shirt. Paddy noticed that just under Danny’s receding hairline was a protruding nerve. Paddy thought he could almost see the adrenaline pumping through his head. “Come here with me boy, aye’ll show ye something” Danny grabbed Paddy by the back of his head and pulled him to his feet roughly. He then pulled the back of his neck and thrust him forward. Paddy tumbled forward and landed on the kitchen floor with a thud. His nose connected first because he didn’t have the energy to raise his hands to guard from the fall. Paddy felt the blood surging from his mouth but because of his vertical base, he felt it re-entering so he tried to breath through his mouth. Before this happened, Danny hoisted him to his feet once again “Look at this boy” Danny snarled, pointing at the door of the fridge, which was empty Paddy looked at it and tried to focus on the plain white screen “Why isn’t there any pictures or school reports or feckin’ anythin’ fer me t’be proud o’? How come all me children are FAILERS!” Danny shouted. Paddy would usually take this talk as drunken chat but for some reason, what he said touched a nerve. It was like his worst fear was realised and is ready to be exploited. Even in his current state, Paddy felt his mind fall flat on its face. “Look at ye! Yer a failure! Yer nothin’ t’ me! Why the hell should aye even let ye stay here?” Danny shouted at him Paddy looked at Danny’s face as he swayed from side to side. He looked at his angry eyes and felt his own droop at the same time. The strong versus the weak. So Paddy hurt him as much as he could in that instant. He spit the circulating blood in his mouth directly into Danny’s eye Danny yelled in anger and punched Paddy with all his strength into the stomach. Then Paddy felt his hands on the back of his neck “AYE HATE YE!” was what Paddy heard from Danny just before he was put through the caravan wall and just before Paddy was defeated in his battle to keep from passing out. 4. What to do? But that event wasn’t the only one. Danny of course sobered up for the next day and when Paddy woke up from the grass of the field at the step of the caravan he found himself looking into the eyes of his mother. His father wasn’t the apologising type and Paddy knew that from an early age. Everything his father had to say went through his mother. His mother tended to his head and his face. Paddy stayed in bed for two days, afraid to see his father again, still tortured by early childhood memories that convinced him that his father could hurt him anytime he wanted in any way possible. But Danny didn’t say anything. Eventually, after about a month, the wounds healed, the caravan was fixed and the memory was temporarily pushed into Paddy’s subconscious probably because the hurt was too much to bear and it was the only possible thing his brain could do so Paddy could continue his life. It hurt in a way only a father can do to a son. And so things went back to normal. However, every so often, Danny would come in drunk and a similar yet less extreme event would occur. So Paddy made sure to be out when his father came back and it became like taboo in the caravan for anyone to be around when his father came back. Those types of events were in stark contrast to the events Paddy had recently been picturing in his mind – of tranquillity and fun with his father and now he was confused. Paddy knew Danny had a kind and a mean streak inside him. And he didn’t know what he should do But right now, as Paddy thinks about the conversation with his brother, Paddy realises that Owen definitely was only trying to hype him up and he knew how much his father meant to Owen and his mother and all the other kids. For Gods sake, young Danny junior was only three years old…and everyone needs a father figure… So for them and maybe not for himself or even Danny, Paddy would do what he could On Fresh, Paddy will face Pierce Lavelle in the main event in a shot for the transatlantic title. It should be for Paddy, everything he’s always wanted, everything he’s ever dreamed of and a sign for all the people that called him useless that they were wrong. But Paddy is obviously preoccupied. He doesn’t know at this minute how he feels about his father but he does know he will do what he can to save him and deal with his relationship after. Paddy wants that belt obviously and he’s acutely aware of the heart Pierce Lavelle has and how much he wanted that belt in that inferno match But Lavelle is only a kid and children shouldn’t have responsibility like that to carry the weight of a federation on his shoulders. Paddy would transfer all his confusion and anger into determination and take that belt from Pierce Lavelle…wrestling is Paddy’s calling and he must seize the ultimate prize at any chance no matter what the situation… END
