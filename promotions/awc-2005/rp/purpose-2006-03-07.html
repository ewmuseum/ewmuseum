Date: 03/07/06 Anton Assault stood in an empty space near the back of the apartment, his head inclined and dripping with sweat. His whole body was dripping with sweat—his stark, dark-skinned form was dressed only in a pair of boxing shorts and he wore ankle tape, if that could be counted as clothing. The white gauze also adorned his knuckles, but this was for protection. His fists thudded repeatedly into the red heavybag that hung back there, setting it to swinging violently and making the chain creak like something out of a horror movie. His eyes were narrowed as he pummeled the heavy bag. In fact, he wasn't just punching it, slamming his fists hard enough into the sand-filled piece of equipment that they might leave dents in anything more substantial, but he was out-and-out fighting it. The heavy bag was his enemy. He saw it scowling at him, mocking him with that Everlast brand as if it had more endurance than he had. He! The Lion! He slammed his fist into it harder, imagining that it was doubling over in pain from the vicious shot. He focused himself, centering his mental faculties on this one thing: defeating the heavy bag. It would yield to him, it always did. He just had to focus. His punches grew faster, stronger, slamming into the heavy bag with more force. The creaking grew louder and he began to grunt with each punch. His shadow danced effortlessly against a back wall, skinny shadow arms thrusting into a black mass that seemed implacable. Anton drilled his fists into it harder, watching its mocking scowl grow fainter, weaker, more pained. The Everlast brand didn't look so hot now. It couldn't outlast the Lion. Finally, Anton gave it one last strike before backing away, giving the bag a disapproving sneer. Fire burned up his forearms and biceps, all the way into his chest. It seared him as if he was being doused in acid, but Anton did what he always did: ignored it. He walked calmly over to the refrigerator and pulled it open, welcoming the cold blast of air onto his sweat-laden skin. He stooped down and pulled out a bottle of water, twisting open the cap and shutting the door. He tilted it backwards and took a long, gulping drink. He pushed himself all the time, trained himself. For what? To become better. That much he knew. Other than that, though, it didn't help him. It served no purpose other than allowing him to fight more effectively. He didn't even have anything to fight for. He was a contractor, basically, fighting where people told him to fight, doing what people told him to do. He didn't have a cause like the celebrated heroes of old. He would never be a hero. Anton didn't know if he even wanted to be a hero. What merit was there in it? Only the luckiest 1% of heroes—real heroes, not every bullshit soldier or policeman or firefighter that retired—ever got recognized in their lifetimes, and most of those were when they were old and decrepit, unable to enjoy the fame any longer. What did one really get from being a hero. Anton continued to drink before slinging the bottle of water away callously. He walked to the living area and collapsed on the couch, tilting his head backwards. He didn't want to be a hero, not really. He just wanted to be worth something. What he was doing now… it was worthless. He wanted to be something more than a name that people chanted because they liked the way he punched another man's face in. He wanted to mean something more than 'fighting nigger.' He wanted to be worth something. This wasn't the way one went about it, typically, but he was stuck here. A fighter. He had to make the best of it. First of all, though, he had to figure out what 'the best of it' was. Luis stood on the apron of the ring, looking in. He was dressed in non-descript clothing, a black windbreaker over a white wifebeater and blue jeans with a chain hanging down that led to his wallet. A white feathered cap was mashed overtop his greasy hair, giving him a sleazy look despite his casual clothing. A towel was hung over his shoulder in a traditional boxing cornerman style and he watched the action inside the ring interestedly, his eyes narrowed a bit. Nina darted around the ring, looking like a scared rabbit. She was dressed in short yellow athletic shorts and a matching cropped tube top with white boots. Her hair was constraied by red headgear and she wore similarly colored gloves as she danced around the ring. Her opponent, a slightly tan-skinned white girl, wore all black except for her headpiece and part of her gloves. Luis slapped the turnbuckle, trying to get Nina into action. Nina stepped forward and jabbed, but the other woman snuck through with an uppercut that knocked Nina backwards. Nina stumbled and she was caught in the stomach with a straight punch. The Nigerian girl blocked the next one, but she couldn't do anything about the successive strikes that battered her backwards into the corner. Nina managed to force the other girl away and lead out with a soft jab, but a stiff right cross took her down to a knee. Nina sputtered, her long leg sticking out to brace herself on the ground. ''Fuck!'' Luis snapped in English, tossing in the towel. Nina looked up as the other girl started to back away, confused. She spotted the towel on the ground and groaned, flopping onto her back in defeat. Luis climbed into the ring and hurried over to her, squatting down and helping her get her headgear off. Nina quickly brought her hands up and assisted him, eager to get the thing off of her. Luis finally managed it off her and Nina sputtered, spitting out her mouthpiece as well. She returned to her spread-eagled position, exhausted. ''Come on,'' Luis said, slipping out of the ring and helping to roll her out. Nina got herself together as Luis helped her along and swung her legs over the apron, hopping down to the floor. She slung her arm over Luis's shoulder and the Venezuelan assisted her in walking out. It was more of a gesture of defeat than anything: Nina was perfectly capable of leaving under her own power. Eventually, they came to a point where Luis had to let her go into the dressing room alone. As much as he would have liked to join her, some of the other women would throw a fit. Luis stood outside, leaning against the wall and fishing a cigarette out of his pocket. He set it to his lips and brought out his lighter, igniting the end and drawing heavily on it. He exhaled and watched the smoke rise from his lips, ambling skywards. Cars rushed by in front of him and he tilted his head down a bit, sighing. He didn't quite understand why Nina had dragged him out here, why she was doing this, but he had come. He had to support her, after all. He would have felt responsible if she got the shit kicked out of her and he wasn't there to pick her up. He glanced up as Nina stepped out of the building, a black duffle bag slung over one shoulder. She wore the same clothes that she had in the ring, but she had changed her boots for regular sneakers. Luis nodded and tapped his cigarette a bit, flinging smoldering bits towards the ground. He came up to her and looked straight at her, his eyes boring into hers. She tilted her head, giving him a slight smile. ''You're going to tell me that my eyes are beautiful, aren't you?'' Nina said in English. ''Luis, you know you don't have to—'' Luis lifted a finger to silence her. ''I waddn't gonna say nothin' like that,'' Luis replied. Nina looked dejected, sticking her lower lip out. ''I just wanna know… why you doin' this? I don't get it. I mean, you got the Lion stickin' up for ya when ya need it. 'Cept that one time, gran'ed, but…'' ''I want to be able to do it for myself,'' Nina said. She sighed and glanced towards the pavement, rocking back and forth on the balls of her feet. ''Anton… Anton is not going to be there when I go back to France. Anton is not going to be there all the time when I'm here. I want to be able to defend myself so that he doesn't have to. So he can concentrate on other things.'' ''It's Anton's job, though,'' Luis contended. He took a long drag on his cigarette and exhaled through his nostrils. ''You know? I mean, thass what he was put on this earf to do. Protect people like you and me. Fight. Thass his purpose.'' Nina gave the Venezuelan a long, gauging look. ''I don't believe that,'' Nina told him. She turned immediately and started towards the parking lot, deciding to find her way to the car by herself. Luis watched her as she walked away, his eyebrows narrowed. He didn't know exactly what he had said that had set her off, but he had said something. Grunting, he followed her, shoving his hands into his pockets.
