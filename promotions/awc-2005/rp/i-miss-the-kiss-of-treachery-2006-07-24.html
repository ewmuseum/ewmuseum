I miss the kiss of treachery

24 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7.24.06 “Come on, Darcy!” He hated when she did this. It was like somebody lit a fire under her ass, prompting her to practically leap further up the winding mountain trail, as fast as a gazelle, yet somehow no less graceful. He would have found it cute, except for that the sun had long disappeared behind the horizon, draping them in the evening darkness. That, and she was the only one who knew where they were supposed to be going. “Jesus Christ, hold your goddamn horses! I’m coming,” Darcy responded, in between heavy breaths. “Where are we going, anyway?” A good thirty feet in front of him, Jane let out an exasperated sigh. “You’ll see, if you can ever catch up!” she replied, before bounding off again toward the summit of the cliff. Jane was on the cross-country team in school, and practically lived for racing ahead of companions and competitors alike on these sorts of long, winding trails. Darcy surmised that if she wanted to, she could easily have left him in the dust, with enough space to stick the Statue of Liberty between them, extended torch and all. The fact that the distance between them was only a few yards suggested that she intended only to tease him with her lead; enticing enough in appearance to string him along, but just as insurmountable as if the gap had been as wide as Lady Liberty. Nevertheless, Darcy persevered in his quest to close the gap between him and Jane. With the summit now in sight she was moving quicker, apparently having had enough of tempting Darcy to catch up and foraged on to her destination. As she turned into a faint speck in the ascending distance, Darcy resolved to spend a little more time hitting the gym to build up some endurance, lest he wanted to seek enjoyment in Jane thrashing him in their competitions. But that was one of the things Darcy loved best about Jane – unlike other girls he’d been with, Jane had a competitive streak that rivaled even his. The two battled over nearly everything; card games…board games…mind games…and of course, foot races up winding trails. Both of them strived to defeat the other, and neither was willing to accept defeat for very long, unless they could find a way to be comfortable with the vicious verbal abuse that the winner was more than willing to send the loser’s way. Outsiders to their games found the competition bordering unhealthy, which at times could be construed as such. There had been more than one occasion that spirited contests turned into explosive arguments, so full of name-calling and intentional veracity that they closely resembled the great fire that razed Chicago and its wooden foundations to the ground. But their contests continually pushed them to be better at what they did, in effect making them better athletes, better under pressure and ultimately, better human beings. It was for this reason that Darcy privately cherished the contests he had with Jane. And while she never overtly said as much, he suspected she did as well. With Jane now stationary at the cliff’s peak, and the race effectively over, Darcy now had all the time in the world to catch up to Jane. He eventually did a few minutes later, collapsing at her feet in a pathetic, chest-heaving heap. Darcy soon lifted his head to see Jane crouched in front of him, peering down into his defeated eyes. “Maybe you need to spend a little more time at the gym, eh loser?” Jane said, beaming her perfect pearly whites in victory. Darcy wasn’t quite sure how she seemed to have read his mind from before, but he was far more preoccupied with coming up with an excuse for losing the race than her apparent psychic powers. After several moments, he came up with one. “Yeah, well…next time I get to pick where we go. I’m not going to tell you where it is, either. And we’ll see how YOU deal with home-field advantage,” Darcy said, pushing himself to his feet. He glared menacingly at his adversary for a few seconds, as if he was prepared to let this defeat get the best of him. But then he returned Jane’s smile with one of his own, and pulled her in for an embrace. Jane was the first to disengage from the embrace, pushing herself to arm’s length from Darcy. However, she did so gracefully, almost like an impromptu dance move as she sleeked her arms down the sides of his until their hands became entwined. “Don’t worry, Darse,” she said. “You know how they say ‘It’s not about the destination, it’s about the journey?’ Well, this is the opposite. This was about the destination. C’mon!” Jane led Darcy a few feet closer to the summit, perilously close to its edge. The ledge overlooked what must have been at least a hundred foot drop into a rocky ravine. Darcy didn’t do so well with heights, and when he calculated in his head just how far a slip off the ledge would take him he instinctively recoiled. But after a few seconds and a couple deep breaths, he regained his composure. After all, Jane didn’t bring him to the top of the cliff to look down…she brought him there to look out. And when he did, he was rewarded with the most breathtaking sight he’d ever seen. The ravine overlooked the entire bay that the two called home, but this was the first time Darcy had the opportunity to see his home in such a way. By day, it looked like an entirely different city altogether; scores of shipping barges coming in and out of the bay to engage in their daily trade; commuters constantly coming to and fro from the bay area’s majestic Golden Gate Bridge; helicopters persistently buzzing the San Francisco skyline, no doubt sent by mustachioed news personalities determined to outdo each other in getting the best traffic reports out to the gridlocked commuters desperate to get home in time for The Simpsons reruns. But this…this was something he’d never seen. Sure, there were still cars traversing the Golden Gate, still active ships tearing through the bay waters, and still lights on in plenty of the buildings adorning the city skyline. But these were all waning minorities – already few in number, and sure to grow even smaller in number as they further passed the midnight hour. For the most part, the city and surrounding bay looked peacefully asleep under the blanket of stars draping the night sky. It was even darker than usual, as a few pesky clouds that would soon make up the morning marine layer were blocking out the light that should have been coming from the rising full moon. It was a scene that Darcy would surely never forget, a landscape that inspired a level of tranquility that no other could possibly rival. Darcy opened his mouth to share such sentiments with Jane, only to find that she was gazing in a different direction altogether. He turned his head to follow her line of sight, and soon saw that Jane was preoccupied with the vast, seemingly limitless ocean that, stemming from its connection to the raving overlooking the bay, extended in practically every direction. The waves relentlessly crashed into the shoreline below them, but this too was the only action to be found in the immeasurable watery expanse. No cruise ships partying the night away. No fishing boats stocking up for the grocery stores. No surfers looking to catch one last ride back to the shore. Not even a single seagull hovering over the drift, hoping to find a midnight snack. And with the repetitive sound of the mighty waves lifting themselves from the surface to crash into the coast, again and again with nothing to disturb the process. A minute before Darcy was sure he had just laid eyes on the most peaceful display that life had to offer; but it appeared that this newfound view of the Pacific Ocean would require a second thought. “Just think,” Jane said, breaking the long silence with thoughts of her own. “Somewhere, on the other side of that ocean is practically a whole other world. A world we have practically nothing in common with.” “I don’t think I’d go so far as to say that,” came Darcy’s reply. “Oh?” “I’m not sure what time it is over there…” Darcy said, racking his brain for a few moments in search of the answer, which soon proved to be futile. “…I guess it isn’t night time. But I imagine somewhere over there, somebody is staring back at us across the ocean, having this exact conversation.” “You think so?” Jane asked, resting her head on his shoulder. “Seems hard to rule it out,” Darcy replied, placing his arm around her as the two continued staring into the distance. And as they gazed into the expanse, it was almost as if a switch in Darcy’s brain turned itself on, clueing him in to the significance of this very moment. The aura of tranquility that lied before him reminded him just how little peace he’d been able to find in his life since he’d parted ways with Jane. Constantly on the run, moving from place to place, in and out of perhaps the LEAST peaceful business life had to offer. There could be no peace for a man who consistently found himself in the spotlight…not when there were thousands drawn in by the hype that surrounded him, thousands of people all too willing to praise, adore, chastise or insult – whatever their personal opinions might hold – all behind his back. Even when he left civilization behind to find himself in the Brazilian jungle he still could find no peace, although it was perhaps there that he came the closest. He desperately needed a place to hide himself from the promoters and the accolades, and no place could offer protection quite as effective as the shield of the endless rainforest canopy. But nightmares of unfinished business and a long lost love continued to haunt him on a nightly basis, and his search for inner peace would chase him from the confines of the jungle as well. He did not expect to find peace in returning to the business, only to finally deal with the blocks in the road that stood in his way. And for once, his expectations were granted, as his wrestling homecoming had been anything but. This was not to say that he wasn’t happy – he was. Amy was back in his life and, while certainly unpredictable, had proved to be an effective rock in his life that he felt comfortable leaning on when he needed the support. And it brought him great joy to learn that he wasn’t washed up at only 27 years of age, as he found success in the ring against a vast array of characters – some enigmatic, some ridiculous, and some downright asinine – but always entertaining. But this moment…THIS very moment in time, where he found himself overlooking practically infinite serenity, at the side of a woman who had once meant so very, very much to him…was this the feeling of inner calm, the feeling of being completely and totally at peace that he’d been seeking for almost a decade? It seemed hard to believe. After all, standing beside him was the girl who had been responsible for knocking him out of his happy little world, effectively putting him in desperate need of peace in the first place. But here he was, after eight long years, and there was no denying the feeling that had now overtaken him. Jane had started this all…and perhaps now she had ended it, bringing everything full circle. Darcy drew in a deep sigh, not willing to consider the matter any further. All that was left to do was enjoy this moment, no matter how bizarre it may have seemed, because in the end it was what had eluded his grasp for so long. He should have suspected that fate would cruelly snatch this moment away from him, but he was caught unaware until it was prepared to intervene. An ear-splitting boom overtook his ears, causing him to practically leap out of his skin. He first suspected that some kids had stumbled upon their peaceful ravine and saw the scene as the perfect place to launch fireworks into the night sky overlooking the bay. But the slumping figure standing beside him quickly dissolved any notion of a childish prank. Jane collapsed to the ground face-first, showing no signs of life whatsoever. A massive wound in the square of her back was pouring with blood, placing an ugly, life-removing stain in the middle of her pink cardigan. Without thinking Darcy immediately dropped to her side, desperately pleading for her to assure him that she was okay, but she made not so much as a whimper to ease his fears. He then wheeled around to see just who was responsible for this nefarious deed, when he received the biggest shock of all. “…Amy?” The lone figure standing behind him moved slightly closer, revealing her figure and confirming Darcy’s beliefs to be true. She clutched a small revolver close to her chest, looking down as if she was ashamed of what had just transpired. “Amy!” Darcy shouted, bewilderedly. “What the fuck is wrong with you?! How could you?” She offered no reply. “Answer me, god dammit!” “I warned her to stay away from you,” she said, softly. “What?” “I warned her to stay away from you, Darcy!” Amy shouted, loud enough for him to hear this time. “…and I warned you to do the same.” “Wait a minute, Amy-” Amy slowly lifted the gun, now training her sights on her lover. It was difficult to say for sure, but Darcy swore he could see tears welling up in her eyes. “Goodbye, Darcy Markson,” she said, as the gun trembled in her hands. He heard the gun speak just before everything went black. At this point, he had two choices: accept this cruel twist of fate as the end of his existence… …or wake up. *** President Bush’s chief of staff said today that international peacekeepers might be needed in Lebanon to help end the fighting between Israel and Hezbollah militants, but that U.S. troop involvement was unlikely. Josh Bolton reaffirmed comments by- An outstretched arm reached from beneath a tangled mound of blankets and sheets to put an end to the dreary report of the day’s morning events. With silence overtaking the room once more, Darcy Markson took a few moments to confirm precisely where he was. In a bed, obviously, he initially surmised with cunning accuracy. He then slowly rolled his head to the left, allowing the morning sunlight pouring in from the bay window to drape his face. He squinted at first, instinctively attempting to adjust his eyes, but upon catching a glimpse of the cerulean panel that adorned the glass barrier to the outside world he confirmed that it was indeed his own bay window, and his own bed. Darcy ran his hands across his body, checking to make for certain that it was only a bad dream. He was unable to find any major penetration wounds or other serious injuries, save for the softened shoulder that had come at the hands of the beast that was Michael Sloan in their recent tag-team matchup. Every time he had been forced to stand against the man they called “Chainz” he found himself much worse for wear, and after each affair he had standing against the near 300-pounder he found himself questioning the sanity of anyone who would willingly do the same. Especially his girlfriend’s. It was an unfortunate situation, to say the least. Unlike practically all the rest of the couples employed in AWC, Darcy and Amy did not spend a great deal of time together backstage, nor were they particularly keen on becoming involved in each other’s affairs. It was difficult to tell exactly why. Part of it had to be that they’d worked together for so long, spending most of that time wishing each other dead – sometimes secretly, other times publicly, but always overwhelmingly. Pride was also certainly a factor. Both the Red Raver and the Darcinator were esteemed wrestlers in their own rights, and both would be equally miffed if someone were to suggest that their respective success was due to their being joined at the hip, always overeager to bail the other out of trouble at the first sign of a hangnail. It was hard to say for sure, as the two had never really discussed it. But for that matter, they’d never had a reason to either. But this was different. He was completely at a loss to understand what this affair against the monster Sloan was all about. At first, Darcy put the matter out of his mind as best he could. After all, he thought to himself, Amy might be crazy…but she isn’t stupid. She was more than capable of handling herself in precarious situations, and quite frankly…if she needed Darcy’s help she would ask for it, which she had not. But things changed when it became clear that Chainz derived some sort of sick pleasure in making Amy suffer. It had been more than once that he’d put her on the brink of serious, permanent injury, so much so that Darcy found himself pressed into service to keep to stand against the freak of nature. But he fared no better in his attempts to defend Amy’s honor than Amy had in defending her own. His recent “win” against Chainz and partner Anton Assault, the win that broke Darcy’s months-long losing streak, came only when Chainz destroyed everyone standing in the ring – including his own partner. Unwilling to let a pinfall victory over the Frontier Champion go by unclaimed, even if it seemed a little dubious, Darcy covered Assault for the victory. But instead of being able to celebrate the end of the back-breaking streak and the newfound opportunity that potentially lied ahead, Darcy was instead screaming for EMTs to help his fallen lover, who looked to be in more physical pain than he had ever seen her, and wondering where the hell Chainz would stop when it appeared that neither of them could stand in his way without facing serious consequences. But for the moment, they were safe and sound in East Bay. He wouldn’t have to give in to the sickening feeling in the pit of his stomach until they were bound again for the Russian tundra. For now, they had time to lick their wounds and enjoy each other’s company. Darcy reached out with his left arm to stir the redhead from her slumber, but was puzzled to find her missing from his bed. That’s odd, Darcy silently mused. Where could she possibly be so early in the- Suddenly, it hit him. The dull voice that was in charge of delivering the world’s top stories over his clock radio should have clued him in much earlier. Had he awoke when he intended, he instead would have been greeted with the early morning show Party Line, in which elderly folks who had apparently never heard of eBay attempted to hawk whatever junk they had lying around between each other. Not exactly riveting entertainment, admittedly, but Darcy never quite figured out how to get the radio to pick up a better station. But the fact that he awoke to world news, and not Party Line meant only one thing – he’d overslept. A quick look over at the clock confirmed such sentiments. 10:54. Fuck! He’d overslept, alright – by a good three hours. He was supposed to meet Jane at her hotel room earlier that morning to deliver the ring back to her. Darcy leapt from his bed and moved to the dresser across the room with remarkable quickness to grab his cell phone from the charger. He remembered from their teenage years that Jane absolutely could not stand when Darcy was so much as ten minutes late for anything, let alone three hours. He expected to find half a dozen missed calls, but was surprised to find only the normal welcome screen to greet him – indicating his phone had not rung so much as once while he was trapped in his perilous nightmare. Perplexed, Darcy dialed the number Jane had given him at the end of their last meeting and tucked the phone under his chin as he frantically attempted to throw on a pair of jeans lying on the floor. Were he not in such a hurry, he might have been overwhelmed enough to pause himself at the fact that this was the first time he’d made a phone call to Jane since his last days in college. But necessity outweighed the significance of the moment, and Darcy gave it no thought as the phone rang. And rang. And rang. Odder still, Darcy thought to himself as he flipped the phone shut. Why didn’t Jane call me? And why isn’t she answering now? Instantly, Darcy feared the worst – the money grubbers were tired of waiting, figured out where Jane was, tied her up and took her back to their lair, and would soon detonate a nuclear warhead in the hotel kitchen to cover their tracks. Still bare-chested, Darcy stared into the mirror in front of him mounted above the dresser and took a few moments to calm down. “Easy, tiger…” he said aloud, staring into the reflection. “…she probably just stepped out for an early lunch, since you’re an inconsiderate jackass.” This sounded a lot more likely to him; after all, Jane no longer lived in East Bay, and it seemed doubtful that any would-be kidnappers would know to track her there. Throwing on an old golden-colored Hard Rock Minneapolis shirt, Darcy came up with a course of action. He still needed to get to the bank and get the ring from his safety deposit box, preferably as soon as possible so as to avoid the dilemma of surly tellers desperate to go on their lunch breaks, causing further delay. And since this business was far more pressing for Jane than it was for Darcy, he couldn’t imagine where she would disappear to for very long. In the time that it would take to for him to bail the ring out of the deposit box, she would probably return from wherever the hell she was to continue waiting for him, no doubt rehearsing the earful she would have in store for him when he got there. First things first, he thought to himself, as he exited his bedroom in search of his shoes. *** Rock on, gold dust woman Take your silver spoon And dig your grave… The trip to the bank proved fairly uneventful, and Darcy soon found himself back in his trendy hybrid sport utility vehicle in search of Jane’s hotel. Hearing a halfway-decent Fleetwood Mac song on the San Francisco radio waves was a rare treat, and normally Darcy would cherish the opportunity to happily croon off-key along with Miss Nicks. But his mind was far more preoccupied with other matters to enjoy the Rumours classic. Most importantly, he couldn’t get the nightmare he recently awoke from out of his head. For one thing, it was certainly nerve racking to actually be killed in a dream state. Darcy could have sworn he felt the icy hand of death place itself gingerly between his shoulder blades, ready to pluck his soul from the confines of his mortal vessel. Luckily he was provided the opportunity to wake up this time, but it was a definite reminder that one day he would not be given the same choice. The cold reminder of his own mortality sent shivers down his spine. What pained him even more to think about is what he was doing on top of that mountain with Jane in the first place. More specifically, why he felt the way he did. This sort of situation wasn’t exactly new for him – after all, he spent years of his life feeling precisely that way about Jane, always seeking out adventure with her at his side, and feeling like time stood absolutely still when they found the perfect place, on the perfect night, in their perfect little world where no other living creature could occupy. But they were teenagers then. And Darcy’s dream didn’t star their teenage selves. On the contrary – he’d seen Jane just once in the past eight years, yet she looked just as he remembered from that encounter. Was this to suggest that she still occupied a place in his heart? He thought back to what he’d discovered since her return to his life. In his long journey to find the inner calm that had been eluding him, he never once allowed himself to think about Jane for more than a few seconds at a time. Every time it came up he pushed it back down, farther and farther until he believed it drowned in his ever-flowing subconscious. In this way, he was able to move past her…but if she could resurface in his dreams, with a grip on his emotions as strong as she’d ever had to boot, then it would appear that moving past her did not imply that she was gone forever from the place she once held. And then there was Amy…Amy and her gun. He could still see the silver metal shaking in her quivering hands just before she took his life. Darcy had been so preoccupied with this situation with Jane, especially how it was affecting him…he’d barely given a thought until now of how it affected her. With his rejecting her marriage proposal several times, combined with the sudden return of the girl he once loved so much…well, it was enough to make an ordinary woman go off the deep end, not to mention one with a spirit as volatile as Amy Campbell’s. He’d talked her down from a temper explosion more times than he cared to endeavor, but he resolved that just because her anger appeared to be held in check did not mean that it wasn’t a daily struggle to do so. Darcy took a deep breath and attempted to gain some perspective. Even though Amy had threatened to tear Jane’s throat out as well as his own, he couldn’t in good conscience believe that it would honestly come to their actual murders. And as far as Jane was concerned…he was smart enough to know that combining the Jane of today with the Jane he fell in love with as a lovesick teenager was an amalgamation that could exist only in a world of dreams. Even if she fell at his knees confessing a repressed, lifelong love for him…things were just too different for a man now pushing thirty. He had come too far since he’d left Jane behind, and was in no hurry to reverse direction now. Above all, Darcy was too smart to interpret his dreams in a completely literal sense – but not too stupid to ignore the potential warnings they were evidently willing to bestow. Properly confronting his past with Jane was something he would be unable to avoid forever. And for Amy’s sake, as well as his own, he would have to go about it as delicately as he feasibly could. A quick turn off of Baker Street brought him to the address Jane had provided him. This was certainly not a time to be throwing money around for her, so Darcy was not surprised in the least to learn that she forewent some of the more luxurious San Francisco-area hotels in lieu of a Super Motel 8. Bringing his car to a halt in the hotel’s parking lot, Darcy reached over for the small black box lying just below the cigarette holder. He was sure the box contained what he’d held locked away in the bank’s vault for so many years, and so he remained comfortable in keeping it hidden within the tiny box. But he’d certainly look the fool if he handed an empty box to Jane, even if it was covered in a thin layer of soft velvet that nobody can ever stop rubbing against their skin. So even though it pained him to look at it, he opened the box for the first time in as many years as he could remember, just to make sure. And there it was. The eagle’s claw holding the ring’s greatest prize shined as golden as the day Mrs. O’Connor placed it in his palm. The sapphire held tightly in the talon was a piercing blue that surely would inspire awe in even the most expert jewelry enthusiasts. Truly a sight to be seen…but still too painful for Darcy to look at for very long. He quickly closed the box and placed it in his pocket as he killed the ignition, and removed himself from the box of metal and plastic that had transported him to his destination. Two-twenty…two-twe…two-twelve! Darcy silently deliberated before remembering the room number that Jane had given him. The summer heat wave that practically the entire United States were currently toiling through had reached even East Bay, and Darcy welcomed the cool air against his skin when he entered the Super 8 lobby. He made a move for the staircase, but was unable to dodge the all-too-jovial lobby attendant. “CanIhelpyouwithsomething, sir?” the pot-bellied man said, with such cheerful rapidity in his voice that suggested he’d had more than the daily recommended amount of caffeine for the day. “No thanks…Carl,” Darcy said, peering down at his nametag before looking back up at the balding man’s face. “Just heading up to my room.” Carl politely smiled and hurried off to pester a touristy-looking couple, leaving Darcy free to continue up the stairs. He exited the stairwell upon reaching the second floor and started for the hallway when something caught his eye. A bearded man with gauged ear piercings, evidently looking to enter the stairwell that Darcy had just exited, looked Darcy square in the eye as they crossed paths. Darcy couldn’t make out what it was exactly, but he had some sort of multi-colored tattoo running down his neck, and presumably further beneath his red hooded sweatshirt. The two locked eyes for so long that Darcy opened his mouth to offer a greeting, but just before the sound could escape his lips the man turned away from him and pushed the door to the stairwell open. He forced it open with a lot more pressure than Darcy had exerted in pulling it open himself, and the lock released with a surprisingly loud click. The man soon disappeared behind the door, leaving Darcy to ponder exactly what the hell THAT was about. But with a shrug, he gave it no further thought as he pressed on in search of Jane’s room. It was only a few doors down the hall before Darcy saw the number “212” chiseled neatly in raised black letters on the tan door. Without thinking he attempted to enter, but the door handle refused to budge without the lock being disengaged with a card key. Darcy slapped his forehead and was about to knock on the door when he got the sudden feeling that he was being watched. He peered over his shoulder back in the direction of the stairwell, wondering if the skinny tattooed bastard was still looking at him, but saw that he was alone in the hallway. He shrugged this feeling off as well, and knocked on the door…but received no reply. He knocked again. “Jane?” he called out. “It’s Darcy…I’ve got the ring.” He tried knocking again, but heard not so much as a whimper from anyone inside. Where the hell could she be? he wondered, eventually deciding that wherever she was, nobody was going to be answering that door. Darcy headed back down toward the lobby to investigate. Brushing off Carl, whose employers clearly needed to find something better for him to do, he moved back out the front door and into the parking lot. He elected to move around the outside of the building, hoping he could catch a glimpse into Jane’s room through the sliding glass windows that accompanied the back of every room that shared the outside wall. When he arrived at the back of Jane’s room, a North Carolina license plate bearing the visage of the Wright Brother’s first flying contraption on the back of a Lincoln Towncar caught his eye. It was no foregone conclusion, especially since he was unable to remember what kind of car Jane had when she came to his house, but she’d mentioned to him that she was working for Senator John Edwards of North Carolina, and a North Carolina license plate just outside of where her room was located seemed to eerie to be a coincidence. But just then, something else caught Darcy’s eye – something that made him feel like he had an anvil in his stomach. The glass to one of the sliding windows had been shattered, with glass spread all over the balcony that adorned Room 212 and surrounding parking lot. Fearing the worst, Darcy spied a nearby dumpster that could possibly grant him access to the lofted balcony. He hoisted himself on top of the closed dumpster, careful to avoid some of the smaller shards of glass, and from there he leapt as high as he could, just barely reached the top bar of the balcony’s protective gate. Darcy grunted as his momentum carried him hard into the cement, nearly knocking the wind out of him. But Darcy steadfastly tightened his grip, and was able to pull himself up and over the bar and onto the balcony. Shattered glass was everywhere, which was certainly dangerous but also provided Darcy with an opportunity to get inside the room. He quickly did just that as he frantically called out for Jane. The place looked like it had been ransacked; the bedside nightstand had been upended, all the drawers were open and disheveled, and the only sound to be heard was the incessant beeping off the phone which had been knocked off the hook. Darcy moved quickly into the bathroom, silently praying that he wouldn’t find Jane’s corpse lying in the bathtub… …he wasn’t sure whether to consider it a stroke of luck or not, but Jane wasn’t in the bathroom either. But a white envelope lied ominously at the foot of the toilet that looked an awful lot like it would shed some light on the matter. He frantically picked up the envelope and removed a small piece of yellow paper which had been sealed inside. The note read as such: We have the girl We want the money We’d like to exchange them Don’t try anything funny The rhyme scheme would be humorous if it weren’t so terribly tragic. To be continued…
