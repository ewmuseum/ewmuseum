The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch. 7

25 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 25th June The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch. 7 Contd. Ch. 6 Albany, New York Summit The dark alley swarmed with men dressed in posh coats and heavy armoury. The rain added a sense of suspense to the issue of about forty men in a back alley in downtown Boston. Marvin Harrington entered the crowd of young men, an umbrella shielding his head from the rain. Two men stepped aside, gravel crushing beneath their feet. The noises slowly began to aggravate Marvin, as he tuned into the silence; hearing the chewing of tobacco and the spitting of saliva after his men had taken a few drags from their cigarettes. “QUIET!” Marvin yelled. His men stood back, shocked by the sudden break in silence. The rain was beginning to die down, the back alley was crowded in darkness, bar the odd reflection from cars head lights; illuminating the shadowy figures that were leaning against the wet walls and trash shoots. Marvin moved forward, removing the umbrella. His face was stone-cold, no emotion or expression on it. His eyes like icicles, not doing anything else but being cold and reflecting an image in front of them. “What is the status on the Connelly issue?” Marvin solicited. Not one man moved forward, each one hiding a certain secret of disappointment to their boss and leader. “What? Cat got your tongue boys?” Marvin bellowed. “Who was in charge of taking care of this little mess-up?” Two men stepped forward, removing their hats and bowing to Marvin Harrington. Who now had a gleam in his eyes - the ice-like features reflecting the two men and the cars head lights. Marvin was silhouetted against the background of the alley, like a god or mystical figure. “We were sir,” one of the two men announced. Now standing before Marvin. “Your son let her escape,” another man bashfully came forward, clearly scared by the reaction of Marvin to this sudden meeting. “ESCAPED!” Marvin bellowed once more. His voice reverberating against the close knit alley walls. “How?” he asked, calming himself down, while pulling out a sawn-off shotgun, decorated in a gold and platinum dress. “With a young man. Reno is following them at the moment,” another man in the back moved forward offering some light into the mafia’s mess-up. “We need to get this girl, before it leads to chaos or …” Marvin stopped talking when he noticed his son and his son’s two best friends leaning against a small waste shoot and laughing. “Something funny?” he stared at them, his eyes like fire, burning a hole right through them. “No father,” his son announced. “You let our one mess-up escape!” Marvin moved forward, swirling the shotgun around and around; like a professional at that sort of thing - a cowboy revolving his revolver. He stepped closer to his son and the two boys, but stopped as he noticed a large puddle and refused to mess up his black shoes. “Dad, that was an accident. We didn’t mean to kill the boy and the old man,” his son said, panic stricken. “Pete Harrington, you have messed up three times now. First you killed Jonathan and Ben Connelly, a son and father. Then to make up for that mess-up, your two clown body guards shot and killed Jane Connelly, in daylight. Explain to me why you did that again?” Marvin had a sinister look upon his face now. “To clean up my first mess-up, father,” Pete announced. His head dipped to the ground. “We weren’t supposed to kill Jonathan Connelly. He had a special secret we needed and you killed him and his son. Now you let the only survivor escape to somewhere with another man.” Marvin looked around at all of his followers. “WHO HERE KNOWS WHERE SHE WENT?” he yelled over the noise of his cars engine. His voice being enhanced by the alley’s close knit walls. “Albany, New York,” two men yet again stepped forward. “Albany?” Marvin looked baffled. “Who did she go with? A family member? A Boyfriend?” Marvin looked around for an answer. He stopped at his son. Disappointment shown in his eyes as he gazed at the mess of a son who he shared a blood line with. “A man named Pierce Lavelle. We followed them to the airport. Reno killed an air host and took his uniform. He has them under surveillance, boss.” Marvin turned around and moved forward. Planting two kisses on either cheek of the man that just spoke. A common ritual of praise among the Sicilian based mafia. “You have disappointed me too many times, Pete,” Marvin turned around once more. “No father, please father, NO!” Pete yelled. Each man stood back, knowing what was coming. They bowed their heads in a farewell sort of manor. The rain bounced off a fairly wet head of Pete. Tears running down his cheeks, his limbs frozen, his heart racing a mile a minute, his palms sweaty and his throat swollen with fear, unable to swallow. “You have failed me and your mother,” Marvin said In one second, smoke billowed out of the barrel of the sawn-off shotgun; its empty cartridges fell to the floor. Pete’s body flew back at the point blank range of the shotgun blast. His back bouncing off the waste shoot before crashing to the ground and then landing in the pool of water. Marvin stood back watching the blood from his son’s body flow out around the pool of clear water, turning it crimson red. Splashes of blood lined Marvin’s face. Pulling out a white handkerchief he wiped off his sons blood and threw the cloth on him. “Find her and kill her,” Marvin moved back to his car. Each man stood still, now used to the mafia execution. Pete’s body was left in the rain of the lonesome back alley in Boston as each of his family members and mafia friend members walked back toward their cars and left. His eyes remained open… Suspicious Minds I awoke silently, so I didn’t wake Lilly up who still continued to sleep. I climbed out of the bed and while I threw on a black wife beater, I watched her sleep and how every so often there would be a slight tremor or gurgle mixed in with her deep breathing. As gentle as I could, I put the duvet back into its original position and opened the door handle. Rob sat at the small kitchen table munching away on some Cornflakes while listening to the early morning news. He stopped chewing with a half filled mouth of milk and cornflakes and smiled when he saw me emerging from the same room as Lilly. “Got lucky!” Rob mumbles with some milk dripping down his chin. I smiled at the imaginary thought of such a thing and laughed as I noticed the little white drops, crashing to the table underneath Rob’s chin. “That would have been great, if it had happened. She didn’t feel safe, so I stayed with her. That’s all,” I said while sliding into the opposite chair. “Wait. Not safe? What’s going on?” Rob stopped me half way through pouring milk into my bowl of Cornflakes. “It’s too dangerous,” I announced. Hoping that Rob would drop the subject and not persist. Of course, Rob’s intuitive mind and frivolous nature allows him to bypass any common form of danger and swoop into any mishap. “Damit, Pierce. How do you always get into dangerous situations?” Rob continued on eating. “Please Rob, don’t continue this conversation. You really don’t want to know,” I finished pouring the milk. “Where’s Lois?” I asked, concerned for one of the most important women in my life, even if she was dating my best-friend. “Still asleep. You know what she gets like if you wake her up too early,” Rob said, in between the crackling and chewing of his cereal. I smiled at a certain memory when I and Lois had to share the same room as teenagers, after a huge storm hit Harrisburg. I had made the mistake of waking her up too early and paid the price with a pillow to the face and some verbal mumbling. “Yeah, I know,” I replied. “Yeah what? “ Lois emerged from around the corner. Her hair ruffled. She wore a blue pyjama top and bottoms with little white bunny rabbits on them and bunny rabbit slippers. I couldn’t help but smile. “You want some coffee?” I was immediately up. Used to Lois’s morning response. “You know me so well, Pierce,” she said. “Yes, I guess I do,” I continued to smile. Rob sat with his spoon swirling around and around in what was left of his milk and cereal. “What your plans for today, lover-boy?” Rob implied. *Lois looked around and realised that Lilly and Pierce had spend the night together. Her head dipped and her stomach began to spew acid amongst the nerves. Lois jealous of Pierce? No way. This was the first time she had to accept the fact that her ex was with another woman. Since Lois had ended it, she never thought he'd date anyone else. * *What are you doing Lois? This is Pierce, you can’t get jealous!* “Actually, I don’t feel too good. No coffee for me, Pierce,” Lois left the room quickly, hiding her face from her boyfriend, Rob. “Wait Lois, I’ve just finished it…”I stopped the coffee and became a little stunned by Lois’s sudden mood change. “You drink it, Pierce,” Rob said with a cynical tone. I noticed the sudden fall in temperature as I turned around to see Robert’s angry eyes upon me. “I don’t drink coffee at this hour in the morning, remember?” I was so confused by the sudden mood change in both Lois and Rob. The coffee issue had been resolved when I turned twenty. Till that age I had been drinking coffee every morning before school, hoping it would wake me up. At the age of twenty I developed a condition in which my body had too much caffeine and high levels of sugar. So I had to cut down and in doing so it meant I could only have coffee in the afternoon after I had something to eat. Some would say I was a diabetic, but I wasn't. If I didn't have the caffeine my body could go into withdrawal. So I try to have at least one cup of coffee or just a glass of water with melted sugar. My case isn’t as serious as a diabetic, which sometimes made me wonder if one day I would need a needle of insulin. “Yeah, sorry!” Rob finished up his Cornflakes and placed the bowl in the sink. Rob and Lois had been distant all week and I was beginning to see a pattern, but after the last time I interrupted in their affairs, I refused to get involved again. The music had ended and the early morning news report from all major cities was now airing. First up was Albany, New York. It was your average news; temperature, traffic and stock market. But, the Boston news report was what caught my attention. I cranked up the volume: “At the early hours of the morning. Boston residents awoke to yet another murder on our streets. The police announced a report in stating that once more we have watched a mafia execution. The victims name and credentials are yet unknown and the Boston Police department will be releasing another statement later on. Mafia executions have erupted all over the city in recent weeks, once more, gang-land war fair has taken over Boston nightlife. George Harrison is on the scene…” “Who died?” Lilly asked with a concerned look on her face. “They didn’t say,” I turned away from her and listened to the Harrison report. “Pierce, I don’t like this one bit,” Lilly stopped for a second after hearing the name of the victim. “I am live at the scene and it appears the victim of this brutal execution is Pete Harrington, son of Sicilian billionaire, Marvin Harrington. The police will reveal further information about this murder in the coming hours…” “Man, I’d hate to be that man,” Rob joked but then sensed the tension in the room. “This is all my fault,” Lilly said. “That murder had noting to do with…” I stopped. Beneath my window-sill, in and out of the apartment block car park, the same man from the airport stood. I was right, he was following us. “What?” Lilly put her hand on my shoulder. “Huh?” my mind racing faster than the speed of Superman. “You were about to finish off your sentence,” she looked at me now, concerned. “We need to talk, quickly,” I turned around to see Rob now dressed in some work clothes. “You, don’t go anywhere. It’s not safe. Stay put and lock the doors,” I pointed to my room and closed the balcony window. “This isn’t Lethal Weapon, Pierce. You’re no cop and I’m nobodies lap-dog,” Rob smiled. “SIT!” I bellowed. Rob sat down, head in his hand as he turned on his mobile. Complications “What is it? Why did you suddenly become a covert agent?” Lilly smiled. I couldn’t return the smile. I now panicked. I had led the man from the plane straight to me, Lois, Rob and Lilly. He knew we were here. I only feared what he would do. “Pierce?” Lilly said again, snapping me out of my trance. “It’s really, really, really, bad,” I looked around for some clothing. “We were followed,” I picked up a grey hoody and zipped it on. My pyjama bottoms would have to do, not the most attractive, but good enough for a quick escape. “Followed?” Lilly now realised why I had frozen in mid sentence while in the kitchen. “By who?” “That air host from the plane. I knew it, I god damn knew it,” I turned around and around in circles before collapsing on my bed. “Oh this can’t be good, he’s going to kill us, isn’t he?” Lilly sat next to me. “I don’t know, don’t be so pessimistic!” “Me? Pessimistic? Look in the mirror, Pierce,” she turned around angrily. “Okay, us getting angry at one another won’t help this situation,” I stood up again and reached into the beside table for my mobile phone. “You think?” Lilly still had her back to me. “Lilly, call the police that are helping you, please?” My blue eyes blurred a little. I could feel my throat swelling up and the nerves kicking in. I had never felt so unsafe in my entire life. Being at home with a horrible father definitely looked better than being in a room surrounded by mafia assassins. Lilly took the phone and put one hand on my cheek. “I’m so sorry I got you involved in this,” she said. With that she dialled the Boston secret service number and hoped for a resolve to the situation. “This is Lilly Connelly. I need to speak to Agent Rush, now, please. Tell him I’m in danger,” Lilly paced up and down the room. Perspiration beginning to trickle down her forehead. Lilly still remained in my clothing along with a small photo of myself, her, Rob and Gavin in the back pocket of my tracksuit bottoms. “Pierce, I’m putting it on loud speaker,” Lilly announced and clicked two buttons. “Lilly, this is Tony Rush. What’s wrong?” his voice on the other end generally sounded worried. “Tony, I had to escape from Boston. I’m here with Pierce Lavelle, in Albany. We have been followed and I think they’re going to kill us. Because Marvin probably had his son executed last night for failing to keep track of me,” Lilly stopped, trying to recap any more of the events. “Pierce, you there?” Tony Rush asked with a heavy lilt of a Bostonian accent. “Yes,” I replied. “I need you to stay put; you’re in Albany New York, right?” Tony sounded like a man who knew what to do in situations when a witness is under threat. “Yes, my address is apartment 12 Western Avenue,” I proclaimed. “Hang tight, I’m coming in a helicopter and some reinforcements will be arriving soon.” Tony Rush ended the phone call. I could almost picture some Hollywood type cop - dressed in a posh suit with a nice shirt and tie. His gun holstered perfectly on his hip next to his special agent badge. I could picture him leaping off the desk and grabbing his jacket as his task force prepared the chopper. “What now?” Lilly asked. I stood still and realised this situation had elevated beyond the belief I had once thought while in Boston. “I don’t know.” The silence was unbearable. I had never sat for so long in a room with someone in this much silence - neither of us had a word to say. Both of us trying to figure what the other was thinking, instead of saying. I knew this could be my end and I regretted bringing Robert and especially Lois into this. A tight grasp Marvin Harrington sat at his large oak desk in his mansion, located in Bancroft Road, Andover. A desirable location for the rich and famous in the “Hill District” of Andover. The mansion lies adjacent to the fields of Phillips Academy and the charming Bancroft boarding school. Marvin puffed gently on a large Mexican cigar while polishing the barrel of his sawn-off shotgun; which saw action earlier on in the early hours of the morning. His feet remained crossed and hinged onto the table as he perched back on his chair and took in a deep breath of tobacco. He watched closely as his wife approached. “Marvin, how could you?” Marvin’s wife asked with a lot of tears running down both cheeks. “He was our son,” she moved closer but stopped when Marvin pointed the gun at her. “Shut up woman!” he proclaimed with a cocky grin. “He was a failure to this family and our ancestors.” “You killed him,” she said. Still shocked by the visit from the detectives earlier on in the day. “He needed to be taught a lesson; god will see fit to punish him. I want him buried in Sicily,” Marvin stood up and slipped a 9mm pistol into the belt of his trousers and held the shotgun down by the side of his thigh. “You aren’t invited to his funeral, Marvin,” she looked him in the eyes. “The men are waiting, now get out…” she stood her ground against her husband and mafia boss. Isabel Harrington could stand her ground against her husband, but no matter what, she would always have to suffer the consequences. “Don’t make me teach you a lesson, Isabel,” Marvin strode out of the room and down the long corridors, passed the many adjoining rooms and into a large room. Its only piece of furniture was a large round desk in which a group of men sat. Each one looking dodgier than the other. “Commence the phone call,” Marvin said as he seated himself at the head of the round table. “It’ll take two minutes to connect from Boston to New York,” one man said as he pressed the button for the loud speaker. “Boss, this is Reno. I have the targets in site but I can’t get a clear view. What should I do?” Reno asked from the other end of the phone. The sounds of cars passing by made it obvious that he was still outside the apartment. “Is the intended victim in the apartment?” Marvin asked. “Yes,” Reno replied. “Have Antonio and Romeo arrived?” another man at the table asked. “No Godfather,” Reno replied. The godfather was an old man, who sat the other end of the table, but faced Marvin. You could see a clear resemblance between the two. Obviously father and son. The Godfather sat still and his hand was messing around with a small cigar, half burnt down. “Wait for Antonio and Romeo and then commence the assault. Take no prisoners. Our two previous employees failed us and paid the price, Reno. You don’t want that do you?” the Godfather spoke once more. A Sicilian accent imbedded in his deep voice, with a touch of a Boston lilt. “Yes boss,” Reno hung up Marvin nodded slowly toward his father, understanding what he meant by the two previous executions. His son and his son’s friends, who were later dealt with. All the men sat around patiently and silently waiting for Reno to ring back… “Now we wait,” Marvin stated. Confusion Reno stood against the black mustang and took another puff from his cigarette. He was tense after the phone call from the boss and Godfather. He waited patiently for his partners in crime to arrive. Two men dressed in red velvet shirts and black baggy jeans, their hair not too long, but long enough to be pulled back to perfection with gel, began to approach Reno. “Reno,” one of the men announced while crossing the street. “Antonio, Romeo,” Reno announced. Each one greeting each other in the common mafia manner, two kisses on either cheek. The three men rendezvoused back at the black mustang and opened up the trunk. “It’s time. We must get rid of the problem, now,” Reno declared. “Godfather is becoming impatient,” Antonio pulled out a large machine gun. “Not that weapon,” Romeo stated. Antonio frowned at being told to put the MK47 back into the trunk, it was his weapon of choice for many a mission. “I have another idea,” Reno turned around and dialled the boss one more time. The three men began to put on masks while waiting on their boss to answer. Commencement FIRE FIRE FIRE People rushed out of each apartment screaming at the top of their lungs. Smoke began to bulge under the tight gaps in the large oak door. Robert began to panic and in one quick motion headed toward the door before it blew open and flames before to ignite the area surrounding the exit. “NO!” Rob shrieked. “How the hell?” I ran out of the room. My chin dropped and my eyes widened at the site of the burning flames. The smoke was beginning to surround the room. I couldn’t stop coughing, my eyes watering. “When...did this happen?” Rob asks. “I don’t…know,” I turned around to look down at the three men now seated on a black car enjoying the view of the burning building. One man held up a Molotov-cocktail and launched it at the window. “DOWN,” I grabbed Lilly and moved her away from the window. The window shattered, the small glass bottle containing petrol and fire began to ignite around the furniture. The heat began to become unbearable. “Pierce, what do we do?” Lilly had tears running down her cheeks. “I don’t know,” I could barely see. I began to worry now about Lois and Rob. “ROB, LOIS,” I began crawling on hands and knees to avoid the flames. “Put out the fire,” a man yelled. Suddenly water burst into the open room; engulphing the flames around the door and covering the room in a soft foam and water liquid mix. I felt relieved and scared at the same time as I saw Rob curled over on the ground. Five firemen burst into the room. Two grabbed Rob. I looked around nervously, feeling a sense of helplessness as I couldn’t see Lois. “Lois, hold on!” I stood up and ignored the firemen’s help. I then remembered the breakfast this morning and how Lois had gone back to the bedroom. I hadn’t heard from her since. Fear and panic for her safety began to hit me as I began running on the unstable surface. I could feel the heat from the flames in the apartment below us. “The floor is going to collapse,” another fireman spoke. “Don’t be a hero, kid,” the other one added. I burst open Lois and Rob’s bedroom door and I saw Lois half unconscious while coughing as she struggled to stand. She was bleeding profusely around her stomach. She had gotten dressed since this morning, but she was badly injured. I noticed the large gash had cut through her string top. “LOIS!” I ran over immediately and tended to her. “Pierce…” she could barely acknowledge my presence. “Hold on, I’m going to get you out of here…” CRASH BANG WALLOP The roof began collapsing; I rose to my feet and held up Lois, without thinking of the consequences of grabbing Lois so quickly. I held most of her weight on my left shoulder and began to pick up the pace as the room around me was becoming a furnace. The floor in the hall began to collapse just as we made it to the door. I balanced her against my shoulder once more and looked around me at a scene you would only see in a fireman movie. The stairwell was engulphed in flames. The oak banisters adding to the ignition. The trees in the hall were burnt to a crisp. I watched as the roof began collapsing. I pulled Lois into my chest covering her from the falling sparks of fire and the crumbling splinters of the wooden roof. Two firemen fell with the roof. I watched as they crashed to the floor below us. Neither of them moving. “HELP!” The only thing I could say in this situation. I was feeling groggy. Every breath I took, smoke would enter my lungs and deprive my body of oxygen. I tried desperately to stay awake, not just for me, but for Lois. I couldn’t lose her, not now. Not after all these years of being friends. Ambers began to fall from the caved in roof from the fifth floor. The ambers fell onto the unaffected bits of wood which soon caught ablaze. “Hold on, please,” I said. Lois was coughing a lot worse now. Her breathing was slowing down and the loss of blood was like a scene is some bad Dracula movie. I could feel the blood dripping onto my white socks. I sat Lois down and began removing my grey hoody. I then started ripping my wife beater into a long bandage and began wrapping it around her stomach to stop the bleeding. It then hit me: “Where did I learn this?” - I only thanked that extra class I took in Thompson College. I took a paramedics course to increase my chances of working as a medical doctor. I pulled tight on the self made bandage and hoisted Lois back up. Some of the ambers began to fall onto my bear back. I cringed in pain, but swallowed it in until I had reached the bottom stairwell. I knew there was a fire-exit on this floor. I just had to get their… Outside the building “You don’t understand, my friends are still in their!” Rob shrieked while being tended to by a medical officer. “There is nothing we can do, the building is collapsing,” the chief fireman said to Rob. “No, please, you have to go back in and get them,” Rob said again. His mind became hazy as the medicine began to work in his bloodstream. “What floor are they on?” the fireman asked. “Fourth!” Rob said. “…the fifth floor collapsed, killing two of my best firemen. Your friends are most likely dead. I’m sorry…,” the fireman walked away. Rob sat by and watched, like everyone around him, as the two top floors burst into flames. Glass shattering everywhere. Back inside the burning building I listened intently to the loud explosion at least two floors above me, but didn’t continue to stand around. I had reached the fire-exit and to my relief the door was unhinged. I placed my hand on the door handle and realised then that it wasn’t hot. This was our best chance…I held Lois in the way a man would, when bringing his bride over their threshold. I burst open the door with my back. I felt a slight tingling down my lower spine as I raced with all my might down the copper steps and out into the rain. It was then the building blew-up. Each window smashing in a simultaneous fashion. Lois and I were thrown forward at the force of the blast and crashed to the concrete below. The building was beginning to crumble. Rubble and debris flew everywhere. I lay on the cold concrete, most of my body shielding Lois from the sharp, continuous, explosions which ensued for at least three minutes. Debris danced around our motionless bodies. “Over here,” a medic said. Each medic lifted me and Lois up. I held her hand as she was carted onto a stretcher. I didn’t want to let go. Something felt so right about being close to her again. I never thought I could feel this way. “Is she going to be alright?” I asked the rushing medics. “You are bleeding profusely,” the other medic stopped me and looked at the side of my head and back. “I’ll get checked out later. Is she going to be alright?” I asked again. “You saved her life, man. We’ll take care of the rest.” I stood next to the medics as they loaded Lois into the ambulance. We were now a few feet away from the building and any danger. Rob was seated on the stone wall being treated for some minor bruising. “Pierce, you’re alive,” Rob was shocked. It was then I realised, that in my panic and worry for Lois’s well being, I had completely forgotten about Lilly. “Where’s Lilly?” I turned to look at the burning building. In a matter of moments one more final explosion echoed throughout the street. The building began a large collapse. Each floor falling onto one another, a dominos effect and Lilly was still inside… “No………” I yelled while searching frantically through the crowd. I reached into my pocket and pulled out the picture which Lilly had with her this morning. She had handed it to me before the Molotov-Cocktail flew into our apartment window. I looked around once more but all I saw were blank faces. Lilly was no where to be seen… “This afternoon, Albany residents of Western Avenue witnessed an uncontrollable fire. It is believed that two firemen are dead, as well as a young woman. Most residents were unharmed by the sudden blaze; however, there were some major casualties. The apartment block crumbled under the blaze and pressure of the gasoline which was used to ignite the fire in the beginning. It is believed the fire was started intentionally and crime scene investigators will be looking into this horrible incident…” Reno, Romeo and Antonio watched from a distance, while seated in the black Mustang a few minutes down the road. They were listening to the New York post news report when Reno’s mobile rang: “It is done,” Reno says with an evil grin on his face. …
