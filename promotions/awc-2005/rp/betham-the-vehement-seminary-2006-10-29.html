'Betham' - The Vehement Seminary

29 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29 -10 - 06 Editor's Note: Taking a break from the 'Obsession' series. It'll be back, as there's a few chapters left. Until I decide to dive back into that, enjoy this. BETHAM -Prelude- As a high schooler, you develop this mental trigger that extorts you to think that being 'in' is the only way. You'll wake up fearing that you don't have anything 'cool' enough to wear, and it'll make you want to try and skip school. Unfortunately for me, I didn't have a car. My nagging, methol-scented mother would haul her cranky ass out of bed and take me in. This process always included a session of blabbering complaints by her, followed by a bunch of ridiculing, and then the traditional 'you better start doing something in the house or your out next year' bitch festival. I swear, the day I turned eighteen years old, she's wanted me out of that house and far away from her. She thinks I'm bad news; she thinks I'm poisoned by voodoo. It all dates back to when I was younger; it was when a lot of strange things were happening. I wasn't behind any of the occurences, but my mother seemed to throw the aggression of her frustrations on top of me. I suppose the basket case within her was more than enough to turn me into the un-balanced human being that I am today, and it completely fucked with my schooling. I couldn't concentrate, and tests were an obstacle of impossibility. These nerves seemingly evolved from her stress, and I'd sweat uncontrollably. Because of this, the fellow students thought something was wrong with me. That, and only that, is the stake in the chest of any 'cool' potential. There's no longer a chance for you to be a part of the 'cool' community anymore, and this experience is what can cause a kid to hang himself, with a dish towel, in their room closet. Once you're out of the box, it's cold and it's lonely; it's nothing short of a nightmare that you know you can't shake off. You walk into that cafeteria, and you can hear the echoes of everyone laughing at you; the laughter drains out everything and becomes a hollow shell of a bitter person. You no longer have a place to stand, because everyone that hates you are taking up all the spaces. As a man, I certainly drive to be an interesting person. No, not necessarily 'cool', but acceptable. Being hated throughout school turned me into a zombie. I'd barely sleep, often focused on who was going to poke fun, or threaten me, the next day. I'd walk into the school doors, and literally search for ways to bypass the kids who didn't accept me. It was never any use, though, as the hall monitors would always turn me around and bring me back onto the track I endeavoured to avoid. Massive 12th graders knew that I would sweat profusely under pressure, so they'd try their hardest to get me riled up. Once I would start to sweat they'd chant 'GLAZY--BAGEL *clap clap clapclapclap* 'GLAZY--BAGEL' *clap clap clapclapclap* until I'd run away and find an abandoned bathroom somewhere. I would hide in that stall for hours, missing three or four periods, just to make sure they were definitely gone from the premises. I was a chicken shit, but it's easy to get that way when the whole school, with the exception of teachers and the like, is against you. When you grow up like this, you take absolute caution throughout the rest of your life and recede to babysteps all over again. It's a tragedy; a tragedy not so sweet as it is sour; a tragedy that'll surely peel away the flesh right down to the bone. Why, exactly? Why. It's because you can no longer trust anyone. When thousands of kids, your age, are looking at you like you're a giant, multi-colored praying mantis, things shift towards the edge easier with age. Twenty eight years old and I'm just NOW trying to find my way into the world. I'm just NOW seeing things from the opposite perspective of my parents. I never fought any of the kids. I figured there wasn't much of a point to it, anyway. You punch a kid a couple of times, it's going to leave temporary marks and it'll suspend you for a week. It wasn't worth the consequences; I had to see these grinning, little bastards every morning for years to come. I just sat back in my desk, spaced out during most of the school day, and thought about the most gruesome methods of payback. Although most of these morbid aspirations would never come to life, it was satisfying to imagine the sequential events unfold. -Prelude- --- a. A quick rundown of a most delicious peer pressure recipe: - Insolent laughter - A tirade of ridiculing - A ton of kids on the offensive - One absent-minded boy on the defensive There's always a subject at hand, and a brick wall for you to bounce off amidst back-pedalling. To be a bona fide superstar in the form of peer pressure, you have to keep in mind what, exactly, you're trying to force out of the kid on the defensive. If you're already in touch with what's going on, it's imperative that you discuss with the rest of the offensive team. They need to know all of this, if only to make the event as 'quality' as possible. Okay, so now we have the four primary ingrediants. What's next? - The subject As I had previously stated, withOUT a subject to fire at, the whole thing is pretty pointless. In fact, if the offensive is quick enough, he/she/they could spot it out and use it as a tool of defense. Unfortunately, there's barely ever an incident, such as this, where there isn't a target that the offensive is shooting at. It's never the same, either. There's such an array of possibilities to fire at, that it never seems to get tiring. It did, however, get very tiring to Mike. A student, who transferred from Matlock High (the other side of town), Michael kept to himself like any new student would. He'd pay attention in class, but never dared to raise his hand, and turned in his homework when applicable. When the bell rang, he'd already have his books, pencils, erasers, and notebooks packed. He'd jump up as soon as he heard that first note and sprint out of class. There were a number of assholes, I mean students, who found it necessary to banter Michael and it was because he was so intriguing. When someone doesn't do so much as acknowledge your existence, like Michael mindlessly did, it turns into an excursive enigma. Why doesn't he talk? Why hasn't he introduced himself at all? What's wrong with him? I want to know. I want to know! You get to the point where you want to know the mysteriously quiet kid so much that it necessitates you to acknowledge him, no matter how wrong the methods may be. I suppose the kids, on the offensive, thought their methods were accurate and precise simply because they didn't want to be the ones 'begging' to know him. The kids that seemingly were disgusted by who Michael was, were the same kids feasting off of Michael's obscurity. They wanted to know Michael because they were interested, and not because they were disgusted. This among many things, were the offensive's way of being the enemy. They really didn't 'dislike' Michael because he was 'Michael'; They disliked him because they had nothing to like in the first place. --- b. Jeremy 'Betham High' School was erected in the late sixties. An architect, tagged simply as 'Mowris', developed and devised this massive, three-level brick house in fifteen months with a deadline of twenty-four. A month and a half prior to making this beautiful structure, he had planned to retire after one more project. He already had a vacation home marked out in West Hollywood and planned on spending most of his time golfing, and taking to the waves. An obsession for swimming, Mowris always made time to jump in the waters, whether or not it was a pool or the ocean, no matter how hectic his life was. He saw himself as a baby fish delicately wading in the waters; diving under the sea and observing a life without the pollution of sound. A month and a half thereafter, Mowris is presumed killed by a shark in the middle of the ocean; nobody ever found his body and nobody ever knew how he'd gotten that far in the ocean. As if his spirit were the hinges holding that building together, the school collapsed once word got around that he was presumed dead. The principals at hand couldn't control the maximum inflow of students they were having, and realized that three-levels of students were overwhelming them. 1,000 students, at the opening of the school year, is more than enough to cut your eyes out with a switchblade. The stress from the dedication you're required to have, as a teacher, seemed to overpower any good name Betham High had at first. Every corner, there was a bully waiting for a target. There were kids that feared going to some parts of the school, and would their avoid locker if they knew it was a hot spot for the troublemaking juveniles. This was in the sixties. Then the seventies came.. ..and kids started dealing marijuana and acid out in the parking lots. Every kid seemed to be driving, and everyone thought it was hip to smoke ciggarettes. Disco pop was booming out of stoner's hatchbacks and afro-styling bad-motherfucka's were doing the walk while snapping to Grandmaster Funk on the giant boombox, resting normally on their shoulders. There were divisions of peace, but the crime was still overwhelming; it pinched a lot of the school's lifeline, sadly. The positive thing, surprisingly, were the teachers. Suddenly, we had teachers who loved music and publicly promoted pot-smoking; there were teachers who would give you tremendous grades if they liked you. The negative, some of these nice teachers were degraded by the punks. It had a lingering effect on two of the teachers, actually, and they ended up quitting no more than two weeks afterwards. That, ultimately, led to the downfall and domino effect of the school. Most of the kinder kids were transfering to schools where there wasn't the possibility of being killed. The eighties were violently eventful; there was an assortment of gang-related incidents that took place all over the school grounds. There was a couple attempted murders on the football field, in fact, and a severe knife fight at the end of the mathmatics hall. All of this, and more, simply because of territorial difficulties between two groups of people; it's comparable to two dogs growling, foaming at the mouth, at eachother. Simply said, the eighties were nothing but dog-eat-dog moments that we all try to forget. Nobody really can, but 'damn it' if we don't try. 1993. One year prior to the first Woodstock reunion. One year prior to Kurt Cobain's death. One year prior to the Rwandan Genocide. In 1993, a van bomb parked below the North Tower of the World Trade Center goes off, killing 6 and injuring over 1,000. In 1993, the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms agents raid the Branch Davidian compound in Waco, Texas, with a warrant to arrest leader David Koresh on federal firearms violations. Four agents and 5 Davidians die in the raid and a 51-day standoff begins. In 1993, The Buffalo Bills become the first team to lose 3 consecutive Super Bowls, as they are defeated by the Dallas Cowboys, 52-17, in Super Bowl XXVII. In 1993, Michael Spicoli arrived at Betham High. None of these events clouding his mind, Michael cautiously stormed the doors and focused on what was in front of him. As soon as you open those doors, you'll see what you're in for. There's multi-colored graphitti painted all over the place; decorated on the walls, the lockers, on the windows, and even on the floors. Nothing will hit you at first impression; you'll just wonder how you were conned into going to this place. You'll wander aimlessly, continously floating on the same concerns over, and over, and over again, until you get a sure-fire explanation as to why this place looks like some partying fraternity took it over. The only positive was how clever these gang-bangers were. The gangs weren't the ones out to harm people that served none of the same themselves, so they made sure that their colors were painted in their territories; only rivaling gangs would take a step past the colors. This school swallowed those that wanted out; if you weren't satisfied with it, you'd learn to be sooner or later. I suppose the faculty figured the gangs were beneficial. If anything, they were there to beat and break us into the scene that Jeremy Betham High was plagued in. The faculty themselves had no control any longer. It wasn't their school, as far as most of the rebellious individuals were concerned, and there was no way for the teacher to turn the tables over. The innocent bystanders would have to ignore it, deal with it, and concentrate on the educational positivities. That certainly was easier said than ever done. --- c. Going to high school, you have to deal with a lot of changes. There's a lot of different things going on in that transition from middle school to high school. You start to think about how many times you're going to get lost, or how many times you're going to be on a bathroom pass, only to never actually find a bathroom. Instead you wander around, searching for your homeroom, only to be spotted by a hall monitor and hollered at for intentionally skipping a period. It's known that keeping to yourself is the best way to stay out of trouble, so, I went for that option. I did what I could in school, and managed to stay under the radar for my entire freshman year. It was great, in that sense, but it was still an absolutely awful experience. I still had to walk by the punks, in fear of them getting tired of seeing me and taking me down, every day and pray that I'd get by them in one piece. So, in essence, my first year was somewhat of a success. I befriended this red-headed, freckle-constellation faced kid by the name of Henry Bigglesworth. He was a meaty kid, with a massive chest, who loved comics and grunge music. He wore black-rimmed glasses, alike the ones Rivers Cuomo of Weezer wears, until a muscle-headed eleventh-grader smacked them off his head, and stomped them into the ground. Since then, he's delt with not being able to see as well. We met during a study period; I was in the library, sitting at one of the tables, reading the first three chapters of my biology book. In mid-turn of my first page, I heard a faint whimper come from behind me. I turned around, and saw nothing. The whimpers continued, however, and it forced me to get up from my seat and walk over towards the magnificent collection of book shelves. In the dark end of the second row, I saw him with his head in his hand; he was crying as quietly as possible, sniffling back as many tears as he could. 'Excuse me?' I quietly responded to his distress. I tried to 'sshh' him, but his cries were relentless. 'What.. What do you want?' Henry said through an onslaught of sniffles. He lifted his head up, and I saw his lenseless face. It was gleaming in tears, and he seemed to be shaking. 'Are you all right? What happened?' 'You shouldn't be here. Get away from me. I'm bad news.' 'Bad news? How so?' He quickly got to his feet and grabbed his books. 'I've got to go now.' I tried to stop him. I don't know why I was so concerned, but I was. 'Wait. Don't go.' He didn't listen to me. He just slammed into my shoulder and ran out of the room. He barely said anything to me, and even that was enough to get my attention. I was barely ever on the offensive, and wasn't intentionally trying to be this time around either. I didn't go and try to find him, as I'm sure he could use the space. His unwillingness to introduce himself wasn't enough for me to give up, though. This was only the start to something I should've stayed away from. This was only the start. Betham welcomes you.
