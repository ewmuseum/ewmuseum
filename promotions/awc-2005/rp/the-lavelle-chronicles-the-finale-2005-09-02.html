The Lavelle Chronicles, The Finale

2 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2nd of September The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch. 17 The Finale (This part, taken from Ch 16 - if you haven’t read it) The street lights flickered in the background, newspaper headings flew gently above the ground with the early morning breeze. The streets were so quiet, that the little pitter-patter of rat’s feet along the dumpsters and sewer could be heard. Every whisper and car engine echoed silently with the early morning birds. I pulled the Ford Mustang to a hault as the red light flicked on, all the while keeping track in my rear view mirror, I felt extremely uneasy about my surroundings, the perfect scene for a murder, well sort of. If you ignore the many homeless people covered in newspapers and cardboard boxes that snore softly with the rats and pigeons. The green light flicked on and I put the car into first gear and took off down the cobbled road and turned into lower Hudson Avenue. A stagnant smell of salt water, dead fish and dirty boats seeped in through the tiny dent on my window. I cranked the air conditioner onto full blast, hoping to clear the cobwebs and horrible fumes that now made my stomach turn. I pulled off the road and watched the small amount of traffic lights disappear. There were no more car lights behind me and I couldn't shake the feeling of nerves as I turned into the Hudson docks and drove silently over the bridge, my mind in full concentration on the worry I could end up in the freezing cold river and the saying 'sleeping with the fishes' would become a fitting truth. My phone buzzed along the dashboard and my heart leapt into my mouth with a large thud. The caller ID read, Tony Rush. I flicked it open and to remain suppressed, I place the ear piece into my ear and listened. 'I'm in place,' he said quietly and the phone went dead. I turned off the car lights, drove on until I reached a large hanger bay and in front of me was an outline of a large van, or jeep. I put my phone under the car seat, removed the ear piece and put my Beretta into the small of my back. As soon as my feet touched the slimy gravel, my knees turned to jelly with fear. Breathing in the lovely sea air didn't help; for a couple of moments I leant against my car, sipping as much bottled water as I could - hoping to keep my late night pizza down. The hanger bay door suddenly opened and I was blinded by the light. After a few moments Harrington stepped forward, dressed in a black tweed suit, with a trilby hat and some black leather gloves. Lois was seated on a chair, her hands tied and her mouth gagged. She had a large gash on her stomach and her upper eyelid was cut. 'Ah, Pierce, good to see you,' Harrington moved forward, leaving Lois seated on the chair in the warehouse. 'I see you came alone, you are very wise. It is a pity you didn't work for me, we could have ignored all of this and you could have saved all those poor innocent people that died.' 'Don't put their deaths in my hand; I never wanted to work for you. Now, you have me ... please ... let her go.' Harrington laughed and turned to Lois. 'Did you hear that? He's a begging man,' he said and turned back to me. 'I wonder if he'll do tricks,' Harrington chuckled once more moved forward and pointed a silver Calibre pistol at me. 'Sit, stay and play dead.' He found this all too amusing. 'Harrington, you promised you wouldn't hurt her, you gave your word that she wouldn't be touched.' 'I lied,' Harrington continued to point the gun at me and kept a calm and serious look amongst his stance and appearance. His eyes were covered by the trilby hat; all I could see was the smug grin of his lips and the dimples on either cheek. 'I thought it was a mafia tradition, that all members keep promises and remain close ...' 'You watch too many Godfather movies, kid,' Harrington said. While keeping one arm steady on me, he pulled out a cigar with the other and lit it. 'Hollywood emphasized the mafia relations a little too much. We aren't all about family and respect, if we were, we'd all be dead. Including myself.' 'You have me here, just let her go and we can continue this talk until the morning sun rises ...' 'You still haven't got it, have you?' Harrington blew a large circle of smoke into my face. I coughed with the inhalation of a dry smoke. 'Two and two is equal to four; thing is kid, I never needed this here woman. All I needed was the fishing rod, the bait and now the catch ... you.' 'You have me, so let her go.' My voice had become deep and I was becoming aggravated by the mind games of Marvin Harrington. Lois let out some muffled words under the gag, but Harrington just smacked her across the face. I leapt forward, but found the barrel end of a pistol in my third eye. I eased back, continued my gaze at Harrington, all the while each muscle and limb were shaking with uncontrollable fear, my heart pounding in my chest, ready to burst out through my rib cage. 'Alright, you want a promise, here it is; this here woman will be safe from harm, she was only a pawn in something big. I came here to finish off what my boys failed to do two months ago and that's to kill the last piece of the puzzle, you.' 'ME? Why me? Two months ago? You mean the fire?' 'Of course. I wanted both you and that other one dead. Unfortunately, my boys seemed to have missed you and ended up dead themselves, but I was always a firm believer in finishing off messy jobs by myself. Pierce, this is your last couple of minutes alive.' I swallowed deeply, drew a breath of the pungent-odour of dead fish and moved closer to Lois. 'Can I just see her?' 'You have less than a minute. The sun will be rising soon,' Harrington watched me move closer to Lois. Lois had tears in her eyes. I knelt down beside her and hugged her, but instead of hugging her, I untied the knot that was holding her hands tied, she looked at me through the corner of her eyes, fear and anticipation of the unknown lay shrouded in the mystery waters of her blue eyes. 'It'll be alright,' I whispered. Suddenly, I found myself being pulled back by my collar. Harrington had a tight grip and threw me against the side door of the van. The hollow steel let out a bang as my back connected with it. Being winded for a few moments, I failed to rise to my feet. In the distance I watched as the purples, yellows and bright oranges began to seep over the horizon, replacing the darkness that surrounded the city at night. I stared once more at Harrington, who began to remove his hat. I stood up, looked into his eyes and watched as he pulled the calibre pistol to the height of my chest. 'No .....' Lois screams after removing the gag. Three loud shots are heard and darkness soon fills in around me… Suddenly, I found myself being pulled back by my collar. Harrington had a tight grip and threw me against the side door of the van. The hollow steel let out a bang as my back connected with it. Being winded for a few moments, I failed to rise to my feet. In the distance I watched as the purples, yellows and bright oranges began to seep over the horizon, replacing the darkness that surrounded the city at night. I stared once more at Harrington, who began to remove his hat. I stood up, looked into his eyes and watched as he pulled the calibre pistol to the height of my chest. 'No .....' Lois screams after removing the gag. Three loud shots are heard and darkness soon fills in around me (Now, the continuation) Another shot rips through the sky and connects with Harrington in his third eye. Blood seeps through the small hole made by the bullet in the front of his head. Lois continually screams, completely unaware at what just happened. Harrington, still holding the weapon, has a shocked expression on his face as he falls to his knees. He collapses face-first with the sand; waves travel up and blood slowly drifts back to the river. A large hole now replaces what was Harrington's brain in the back of his head. Lois has no choice but to throw-up at the sight before her. I found myself lying underneath the large van, my chest was pounding with pain, and I felt like the bass drum. The scene had gone quiet, I heard nothing. I had a stinging sensation on my face and hands. Next thing I know, Lois is sliding underneath the van too and helps pull me out, my eyes still remain closed, but her voice and her whispers reassure me that something happened. When I finally open my eyes, I find I am lying in a pool of Harrington's blood and salt water. I quickly scramble to my feet and look at my red shirt; three bullet holes are located on my chest. I pull open the Kevlar vest and sigh with relief that the bullets were stopped. In the distance, Tony Rush comes charging over a mound of fish nets and trawling nets. 'Is everyone okay?' He stands up and looks exasperated. 'You could say that,' Lois states. 'Nice shooting Rush,' I said while staring at the mess, that once was a criminal mastermind. 'Yeah, you didn't give me enough time to act. He had you cornered. I had to wait till you got shot and fell to the floor.' 'Thanks,' I said and hugged the man that had just saved my life. 'Okay, listen, I don't do hugs,' he said and began to laugh. I turned to Lois, who was as pale as a ghost; she looked at me and then looked away toward the line of police cars, an ambulance and at the black Lincoln Navigator that belonged to Rider and Rivers. 'Oh great, it's Rivers and Rider,' I stated and looked at Rush. 'I had to call for back-up once I couldn't see you anymore. I guess it is lucky that I made you wear that Kevlar vest.' Rush laughed a little and then greeted Rivers who stepped out of his car, dressed in more casual clothing than usual, seen as it was a Sunday. 'WHAT THE HELL?' Rivers yelled as he moved forward. 'You're welcome,' Rush stated, confused by the NYPD's tone of voice. 'PIERCE, you're an idiot, you know that. We had this situation under control,' Rivers said and gazed at me, Lois, Rush and the dead body. 'We've got a god damn scene here, haven't we?' 'Hey, listen Rivers, you didn't have anything, and you weren't going anywhere. I watched you for three days in a row, sneaking to the janitor’s closet with Rider, you had nothing. Tony Rush solved this case and saved two lives.' While Rivers and Rush argued, I turned around to find Lois staring at the ground. “Are you okay?” I asked and put my arm around her shoulder, she immediately moved it away. “I’m fine,” she said and looked around. “Come on Rivers, we all know what you like to do,” Rush stated and looked at Rider, who was still in the car, head in his hands. Tim Rivers stood back, shocked. The rest of the NYPD police stood and laughed at the insolence of their leader. Casey Rider stayed in the Lincoln Navigator, refusing to move. Probably the best thing to do. The four medics soon approached and took Lois to the second ambulance. The forensic team moved passed Rivers, sniggering at him as they examined the mess before them. Rush patted me on the back and looked around as the sun had now risen and the street was filled with life and early morning rush hour traffic. Everyone completely oblivious to the scene that had unfolded. 'Well, I guess its back to normal,' Rush says and moves towards Rivers. 'I think you'll have a report to write, don't you. How you are going to write all the positions you had with Rider and none with Harrington and this case, oh, I wonder.' 'Don't mess with me, Tony Rush,' Rivers states and walks back to his car angrily. He chucks a badge at Rush. 'You'll be doing the write-up; I want it on my desk, first thing tomorrow.' With that, Rivers shut the car door and both he and Rider were soon out of Hudson Avenue and driving down toward the freeway. I sighed with relief that this whole masquerade was finally over. The fish air didn't seem so pungent now that I had no sense of smell and my hands looked like barbecued meat. 'Em, could you come with us to the ambulance? We need to clean you up,' a medic said from behind me. Tony Rush turned around also, said he had something he needed to do and would meet me at the hospital. I followed the medic toward the first ambulance and sat on the small bed inside the back of the ambulance. The medic applied some gel to my hands and the ambulance was soon heading out of Hudson and onto Lincoln Avenue toward the nearest medical vicinity. {Albany Medical Centre} The medical nurse had bandaged me up, checked my stitches and gave me a fresh t-shirt. I sat on the small examiner bed and sipped a warm Styrofoam cup of coffee and took a breath of chlorine and detol floor wipes. It was a smell you could never forget, no matter how many hospitals you were in. I hadn't heard word from Lois, but a doctor told me she had been released and got a taxi home. I felt relieved that she was okay and would be okay for a very long time. I knew she had a lot to catch-up on, including writing one of the best essays she could for her Law Assignment on Crime and Violence. It was something we all knew about, but rarely suffered from. As I was about to leave a familiar voice came from behind me. Tony Rush was standing behind me; I turned around and saw he had changed, showered, gelled his hair back, shaved and was now dressed in a pair of black jeans and a white Hawaiian shirt. I smiled at his familiar, copper look. 'What brings you here?' I asked and packed the medicines the doctor had given me into my pocketbook. 'Well, just wanted to check-up on ya,' he said and laughed. 'I see you in these places too much.' He joked. 'Yeah, I seem to have a real connection with hospitals. So, when do you head back to Boston?' 'Not for another 20 hours, but there is actually somebody I want you to meet.' 'Em, don't you have a report to write?' 'Yeah, but I'll leave it late, I like to see Rivers squirm.' Rush laughed and pulled open the door. 'I brought my car with me; I'll take you to your apartment.' 'Oh great, it isn't the Toyota is it? I thought that lost its tail pipe and exhaust.' 'True, but it leaves a nice black cloud behind us and it annoys the hell out of Rivers.' Rush began to move out of the room. 'I hate hospitals,' he said and moved toward the elevator. 'So, who am I meeting?' 'It'll be a surprise,' Rush states and presses the elevator button. 'Ah, I see.' After ten minutes of examining the hospital, the back of his rented car and the whole previous situation we were once in, Tony Rush put the car into first and listened to it chuckle and make some funny sounds, reminded me of the car from chitty-chitty-bang-bang. Rush laughed as black car fumes left a lot of tourists coughing behind us. “That can’t be sanitary,” I stated and tried the radio, which was also bust. “Yeah, that doesn’t work.” Rush laughed. “So, who gave you this tin bucket?” “NYPD state rental cars for cops or FBI agents undercover. Although, I doubt you could get very far in this rust bucket, if you are chasing somebody in a Ferrari or Barracuda,” Rush took the car off the freeway, down to 1st and up onto Lincoln Avenue. “Hey, I thought you were bringing me back to my apartment?” I asked, staring at a five star hotel in front of me. “I am, but I need you to meet someone first and I’m hungry,” Rush pulled the car into valet parking and turned off the ignition. He stepped out of the car and watched the door fall off. “I guess it has some screws loose,” he said and burst into laughter. I laughed at the driver’s side door lying on the floor in front of the valet bus boy. He looked on amazed and refused to question Rush. Rush lead me down a marble corridor, passed the very rich and shameless and up into a gold plated elevator. “Did you pay for this?” I asked looking around at all of the women; wearing at least $40,000 dollars worth of attire. “Nah, complementary of my boss from Boston.” “He must have owed you,” I stated. “Yeah, his job,” Rush said and pushed the button for floor ’6’. After a long story from Rush about his boss and his little quick shuttle to Albany and saving my life, the part I already knew. I found we had arrived outside Rush’s hotel room. He went inside and led me in. I sat down on the leather sofa, took a beer from the small mini fridge and popped the lid. “I’ll be back in a second,” Rush said and went upstairs. After a few moments of baseball, I heard footsteps and switched to another station and watched more of the destruction of Katrina. “Tony, you are missing some real chaos here,” I stated. After not receiving an answer, I turned around and in front of me was a ghost. A woman dressed in a white wife-beater and ¾ length jeans. I almost spat out all of the beer that was in my mouth. My heart skipped a few beats and everything that had happened in the last couple of months, made no sense. “Lilly?” I managed to ask. “Hi Pierce,” she said softly. {Tony Rush’s hotel room} Two minutes had passed and it seemed like a life-time. I rose from the sofa and looked at Lilly and Tony standing next to one another. I put the beer on the table, my mind was completely puzzled. “This … this … can’t be. You died, you were dead, you … you,” I was gasping for a breath. I was in a state of panic and shock. For two months I believed that Lilly Connelly had died, and kept thinking it was all my fault, now after almost losing Lois, I hated the dead Lilly Connelly, but in front of me was Lilly, alive and kicking. “Pierce, calm down man,” Rush said. “CALM DOWN!” I yelled. “Please, Pierce, let us explain,” Lilly moved forward and tried to place a hand on my shoulder, I moved back, almost tripping over the small coffee table. “I know it is hard to believe. I didn’t think it would be appropriate to ever tell you …” “What changed your mind?” I asked, curious about this new situation. “My husband, Tony. He got to know you, like I did and believed it best we tell you the whole truth.” “Truth?” “About me,” she said and sat down on the sofa. I looked at Rush and then out the window and saw a flock of birds circling one another - probably leaving for the winter. I realised I had nothing to lose by getting to know Lilly, or whatever her name was, again. I sat down opposite Tony and Lilly and took a bottle of water, taking a few quick sips. My throat had gone dry with nerves and confusion. “So, what do I call you?” I asked. I know, a stupid question, but one I needed to ask. “My name is Lilly Rush, well originally Lilly Connelly, which was before I married Tony.” “When was that?” I asked looking at her and noticing the different changes in her appearance. She had a small bump on her stomach, her hair was cut short and she wore a little less make-up. “I married Tony in 2004, just before Harrington had my father and brother killed,” She looked down at the ground. “Once Harrington killed my mother, Tony decided to put me into witness protection, just until Harrington was behind bars. So, we came up with a new identity, new people to help keep that identity and I was sent to Harbrook, to go to fake seminars and work at the pizza hut.” “Pierce, we never wanted to involve you,” Rush said. “Is that why you felt compelled to help me?” “Yes,” Rush said and let Lilly continue the story. “Gavin and George are two agents that work with Tony, they are lovers and it was hard to keep their identities a secret too. George had to play the role of a football jerk and my fake boyfriend. It was all working perfectly until you showed up. It was then we had to plan another motive, because we knew Harrington had scouts. When George had to hit you, he had to put on a show. He never meant to hurt you. Gavin had to keep an eye on you at the pizza hut and that is why you got the job. We didn’t know what to think about you. George believed you were one of Harrington’s men. So, to test that theory I had to get to know you, get you to like me, but it didn’t play out the way George expected and that’s when the higher-board asked George to play the jealous ex-boyfriend.” “I was a ploy? Your lab-rat; the experiment to see if I could be trusted. Why didn’t you just leave me alone? None of this would have happened, if you had of.” “Pierce, I know you are angry, but it was necessary,” Rush said again and took a sip from his beer. “Necessary? Oh, I get it, to protect Lilly.” Rush just nodded his head and let Lilly get back to telling her story. “After the night your father arrived, we came to a lot of problems. Harrington had found me and found out about the witness protection programme I was in. So, Tony called me and told me to come back to Boston. We didn’t think you would follow. Unfortunately, you did and in the process ran into Harrington. Harrington’s son Joey was working for us and we in turn helped him.” “So he wasn’t your boyfriend,” I stated. “No, he was playing a part. Just like the rest of us,” Lilly stopped for a moment and took a breath. “Anyway, once you arrived it caused some serious problems. It now meant Harrington was after you. We had to get you back to Albany, where Gavin could keep an eye on you. So, I came back with you and we decided it would be best for me to watch over you …” “That was why you stayed in my apartment.” “Yes, but we didn’t think there would be two other people there. When the blaze happened. Tony arrived, dressed as a fireman and took me away quickly enough. We had to make it seem I had died, to keep Harrington off of my trail. We never thought he would break out from prison and come after you and we are so sorry that it happened,” she said and stopped talking. “So, what are you?” I asked. “I’m an agent, like Tony,” she said. “Pierce, I am so sorry that you had to get involved in all of this, but it is finally over and perhaps you could return to a normal life,” Rush said. For all this time, and the people you think you know, it turns out you don’t. Rush spoke of normality, as though it was something we all should have, but my life was never normal. I felt like the lone wolf in this world. Here I was, seated in front of somebody I thought was dead for two months, for most people they would be ecstatic and hugging the person, but I remained subdued to the sofa and confused. I had heard everything she had said, but couldn’t take it in. I guess that day Gavin said his boyfriend was George I should have come to the realisation that something wasn’t right. It seemed almost strange that I was here and everything seemed so … normal. I think the realisation that my whole ordeal with Lilly Connelly and Marvin Harrington was finally over, but what I could I go back to? There was Lois, my apartment and by the looks of things I didn’t have a job in Pizza Hut anymore, if it was even a pizza hut. In all my midst of a mental battle, Tony Rush had stood up, gotten three beers and taken a pizza from inside the kitchen. “You hungry,” he asks and hands me a slice of pepperoni and peppers pizza. “Yeah, thanks,” I ate the pizza with extreme speed, my stomach rumbling with each piece that entered. I guess I didn’t realise how hungry I could be after a long twenty four hours of murder and mystery. “Pierce, I never meant to hurt you, that is the truth,” she said and knelt forward and hugged me. Even though she was a different person, I still remembered her touch and her hugs and embraced her back. She smiled after the embrace and looked at me. “So, I hear you and Lois are together. How is she?” With all the commotion and story-telling I had completely forgotten about Lois. “Oh, Lois, damn …” I jumped up from sofa, almost knocking Lilly over. “I’ll drive you home,” Rush said and grabbed his car keys. Rush ran toward the door, as did I until I realised it was probably the last time I would see Lilly. I turned around, hugged her and said my goodbyes. Thanked her for the truth and shut the door behind me. I followed Rush down the stairs and sat next to him in the broken down Toyota, which was still missing its driver side door. “Em, can you drive without that thing?” I said. “I don’t know, we’ll see,” He laughed and pulled out of the valet spot and drove off of Lincoln Avenue and back down toward Johnson, across the bridge and into Western. One man once said that the greatest friend is Truth, the greatest enemy prejudice and the greatest companion humility. I knew that Truth was now an important role, it was shown to me by both Tony and Lilly, but I still didn’t believe the rest of what Charles Caleb Colton said. I guess we all howl all three features, so perhaps; it isn’t an exact metaphor for the characteristics of friends, but the characteristics each person does hold or can use. I remained focused on the road ahead, smiling with the recognition that normality was possible and without the extremities in life we wouldn’t be strong enough to go through the normality of life. I gazed at the silver sheen that cast a white blanket over the moonlit sky. It was already night-time, after all this time, the day had come to an end and the moon, once again, cast a watchful eye over us. Tony Rush and I had stopped at the nearest Kmart and bought some supplies for my fridge, seen as it was empty, last time I had checked. I worried about Shelby, so I bought some jerky and dog biscuits. We arrived at my block and Rush pulled the Toyota next to Mr. Normans 1950 Buick, which was still in mint condition. I smiled at the thought, that a car, almost a hundred years old was in better condition than Rush’s 2004 Toyota, which had no driver door. Needless to say, it showed our police system seemed almost too lenient and the cops lazy - not once were we pulled over, considering the fact we had no tailpipe, exhaust or driver side door. It was something Rush joked about the whole trip over, I too joined in now and then, especially when I saw the odd NYPD man sitting horseback, reading a newspaper, completely oblivious to what had just passed him. I arrived on the second floor after taking a short trip up with the elevator. I admit it, I was lazy. Wouldn’t you be? Tony Rush decided to take the stairs, to burn off the extra calories from two beers and a pizza. I arrived just outside my door and realised how much I had grown to like what I considered ordinary and normal. Mrs. Winchester was outside her door, watering some plants. She gave a smile and a wave of the hand. I placed the grocery bag onto my hip and leant on one leg, the other tilted as I pulled out the keys and tried to open the door. “Hey Lo, let me in,” I mumbled, holding one key in my mouth. There was silence and after a couple minutes of cursing with the difficulty of working with one hand and six keys to use, I finally decided to use good ol’ fashioned know-how, so I put the groceries on the floor and opened the door. The place was covered in darkness, bar the silver sheen that glazed the wooden floor in the living room. I flicked on the light, turned my back on the apartment and grabbed the grocery bag. When I turned around, everything was gone, except a couple of things that belonged to me. Dust marks lined the cream walls where pictures once hung. Shelby’s dog bowls were gone, along with all of her squeaky toys and dog bed. Most men would be cursing, or calling out their girlfriend’s name, but I stood frozen, my heart shattering into a million pieces as I stared at what was important to me, now just an empty apartment. I felt a warm tear roll down my cheek. Without hesitation I moved forward and in some desperate attempt called “SHELBY”, it didn’t work. The little dog was gone, like Lois. They both left me. How could someone become so attached to a small little animal like that, now she was gone too. I slouched down onto the beanie bag and put my head in my hands. “HEY, Pierce, I got some wine for you and Lo -” Rush stopped halfway into the room. He stood back shocked and saw me seated on the beanie bag, my head in my hands. “Is she gone?” he asked. “Yeah,” I muttered under my tears. I leant back and heard something crumple. Standing up, I saw a piece of paper, labelled with my name. “Pierce, man, I’m so sorry,” Rush said and put the wine into the fridge. I looked at the letter, refusing to open it, I handed it to Rush. “Wha-?” he asked. He knew exactly what I wanted him to do. Carefully and slowly he opened the envelope and removed an A4 sheet with writing on it. “You sure you want me to read this?” he asked. I nodded and he knelt down and sat on the partition step that separates the kitchen and living room. Dear Pierce, It pains me to write this as I sit here looking at our home, but I can’t take this life that you live, the life I have now come to be a part of. I have decided to leave you, but not because I don’t love you, or like you, but it is too hard to remain in this hazard zone, that you have grown to recognise as normality. You knew we would never work, not after our lengthy friendship. I guess after all these years, the feelings I had for you were just that of love for a friend. ………. Tony Rush stopped and handed me the letter. “I can’t read this, Pierce. I’m sorry.” I stared at the one word at the bottom “GOODBYE” Lois had left a lengthy explanation as to where she was going, and if I ever forgave her to call her sometime, but I knew she didn’t really want me to. I knew she would continue her Law degree in Harbrook; I knew she would meet somebody else and completely move on. I placed the letter into my pocket and went into the room. My life had suddenly taken some dramatic twists in the matter of a few hours - I barely had time to take in most of the information. “Where you going?” Rush asked. “Home,” I said.
