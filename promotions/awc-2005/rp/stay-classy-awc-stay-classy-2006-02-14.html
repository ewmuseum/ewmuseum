Stay Classy AWC...Stay Classy

14 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 14th, 2006 The scene opens in a very poorly lit, and furnished, classroom. On the right wall hangs a dingy and cracked chalkboard, while on the left wall is an outdated world map and a yellowed flags of the world poster. In the back right corner of the room sits a water cooler, while in the back left sits a small combination television/VCR set atop a small metal stand. Looking past the rows of desks filling monopolizing almost all of the floor space, we see a solitary figure sitting behind an old wooden desk. Leaning back in a creaky office chair, the man’s professional look starkly contrasts the room around him. With his short brown hair neatly trimmed and lightly gelled and his face cleanly shaven, the man appears to be in his early thirties. Wearing a crisply ironed blue dress shirt and neatly pressed black dress slacks, the man obviously takes great pride in his appearance, from his fashionable wire rim glasses down to his polished black, leather shoes. Looking up from several stapled paper stacks on his desk, he begins to speak. “Oh good, I’m glad you guys could make it out here after all. I just wrapped up grading the last round of tests, so you couldn’t have come at a better time. I suppose most of you wrestling fans are wondering why we’re here in a high school classroom, much less talking to a boring old professor. I’m here thanks to a couple of wrestlers you should already be quite familiar with—Norton and Bridges. As you know, those two crooning cowboys have been looking for a wrestling coach, and it seems my application won by default. Now what good is a history teacher to a promising professional wrestling tag team? Well aside from granting a better understanding of Dagoth Kinslayer’s promos, probably not much. But what about a four time All-America wrestler? Yes, like most teachers, I lived a much more exciting life before entering the classroom. After a standout high school career during which I was blessed to become the first prep to win the Illinois state wrestling meet four times in four different weight classes, I achieved my ultimate dream—a wrestling scholarship to the University of Iowa. As a Hawkeye, I went on to nationals four straight years, winning it as both a junior and a senior. So why haven’t you summer Olympic buffs ever heard of me? Well it’s awfully hard to compete in the trials when your knee is so shredded from a freak weightlifting accident that you can barely stand under your own power. But that’s not the end of the story, no, not even close. Because while I was grading essays, taking attendance and sending out midterms, I never stopped eating, sleeping and breathing wrestling. I’ve worked as the varsity wrestling coach since I started as a teacher here eleven years ago, and in the last five I’ve since resumed training as well. And while my true passion will always be amateur wrestling, over the years I got over my strong bias toward professional wrestling and began to appreciate it as well. And that brings me here today, as the newly hired coach of Norton and Bridges. It’s time for a new challenge in my life, and the Atlantic Wrestling Club is providing me with just that. My name is Trent Marshall…and I’m here to raise the standard.”
