I'm Afraid I Can't Help It

18 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/18/06 'Afeaki, eh?' Captain Suleimon furrowed his brow and rubbed his chin, ripe with stubble that accumulated from a day of not shaving. Holding the cell phone to his ear, he listened intently to the caller on the other end of the line. 'I like the way he moves. Stealthy yet lethal. He would have made a fine Hassassin.' He pauses to listen and shakes his head. 'Oh, I'm not overestimating him at all. I'm allowed to show a little admiration for the enemy, aren't I?' He smirks. 'Listen, I'm not going soft, and I don't have a man-crush on this Afeaki fellow. I'm saying, if he were on my side instead of in this artificial, American-made Empire, he'd make things a lot easier for me.' Nodding. 'Of course I know how I'm going to counter him. Weapons are my forté. No man has a size advantage that's too great for me to overcome with a chair, a wooden club or something else of that blunt nature.' 'Well well, and I remember a time when you'd piss your pants at the thought of using a weapon in the ring.' The condescending tone of this interruption could only mean one thing. Dogan was eavesdropping on the Captain's cell phone conversation. 'I'll call you back later.' Pressing end and snapping the cell phone shut, Suleimon turned around to face his antagonistic cousin. 'You know, it's awfully rude of you to be listening in on my conversations, you know.' 'Oh please, I could hear you from all the way in the kitchen. You talk loud enough to wake the dead.' 'It's the principle. You could have at least not been so brazen as to let me know that you were spying on me.' Dogan rolled his eyes and shook his head. 'It's true though. There was a time when you wouldn't even touch a chair other than to sit on it at ringside.' Suleimon squited his annoyance at Dogan and then looked over in the corner. A black chair with a few dents in the seat sat there collecting dust. Suleimon looked at it, specifically at the right leg, and smiled. The inscription on that leg said 'Black Betty.' As soon as Lonnie Jackson slumped onto the canvas, unconscious from the thunderous hit that Tariq had laid on him with the steel chair, the thick and tense air was shattered by the din of the ring bell. Tariq had been disqualified, but he didn't care. The disparagement of his character and his race was enough to push him over the edge, but he liked it. He liked seeing the redneck asshole suffer for his sins. Even though the record books would forever mark that a loss for the newly minted Captain, it felt like a victory. He meted out justice. As he got backstage, he found Rahman and James Baldwin waiting for him in his locker room. He wasn't surprised, but he acted like he was. James stepped forward first. 'You know Tariq, I'm going to have to fine you for your actions out there...' Tariq started to look away, formulating the best 'But I can explain' spiel he could think of in his head. '...but off-the-record... and this is off-the-record, Tariq... I'm really glad you walloped him out there.' Tariq's look of scheming devisement disguised as remorse turned to genuine shock. Tariq knew that James, just like most others on the LBW roster despised Lonnie, but this was out and out assault. 'But... you're not mad that I broke the rules?' 'Oh God no. I never get mad like that. Usually, the fine just comes out of the purse for stuff like that. Routine stuff. I mean, it's all part of wrestling.' Tariq, already feeling good about what he did, started to feel reenergized. 'I mean, don't go doing that all the time. You're going to want to win some matches so you can make some more money. But that's the kind of stuff I like to see from people who play the 'bad guy.' And with this character of yours, you're definitely playing a 'bad guy,' especially to these fans.' Tariq simply nodded. 'Alright then, I'll leave you. Good job tonight, Tariq.' James exited, leaving student to confer with his teacher. Tariq sat down, eyes wide open. A smile formed on his face. Rahman put his hand on his pupil's shoulder. 'I'm proud of you.' Tariq looked up at his teacher. 'Why?' Before Rahman could answer, Tariq commandeered the conversation in the silence. 'Why is it that you want to see me break rules and wrestle dishonorably?' Rahman sighed. 'Honor is in the eye of the beholder, Tariq. I've been trying to teach you that ever since you came into my employ. I'm teaching you a way to be the best you can be, to be the the wrestler you know you can be, other people's definitions of honor or talent be damned.' Tariq smiled as he got up to go get his shower. The next day, Tariq was the first person to arrive at the arena. Beaming inwardly, he didn't show any of it as he didn't want to be the person who was proud of blatantly trying to injure one among the fraternity. He kept to himself as he headed back to his locker room to get changed into his ring gear. Before he could though, a friendly voice called out to him. 'Hey, Tariq!' The young Turk turns around to find that it was his good friend Barry Peters. 'Hey Barry. What's up?' 'Nothing much. I just got here, and I'm glad I found you.' 'Why's that?' Tariq knew the answer, though. 'Dude, Lonnie's pissed. Him and some of his redneck boys were gonna fuck you up last night. I'm not sure if they still plan to, but you gotta stay close to me and my boys just in case.' 'He's that mad because I brained him last night?' 'Not really... I mean, he's been brained so many times in his career, but his exact words were that 'I ain't gettin' upstaged by no sand-nigger.' Honestly, most of us thought he deserved it.' Tariq felt more satisfied. 'C'mon man...' Before Barry could finish, the doors came bursting open. 'Where's that God-damned sandnigger?!' Lonnie had a crazed look in his eyes and a black chair in his hands. 'Where's that motherfucking sandnigger son of a whore?' 'We gotta get you outta here,' Barry said to Tariq. 'No, I'll face him myself.' Tariq stormed over to where Lonnie barged in with Barry following behind him. Lonnie saw him and made a bee-line. Seeing that, Barry sped ahead so that he could be between his friend and the redneck. 'You God-damned sonuvabitch! You wanna play rough?' 'You're damned right I do. No one talks about me or my people like that and gets away with it!' 'Your people ain't nothin' but a bunch of terrorist motherfuckers! You ain't never done nothin' but blow my people up.' 'You ignorant slime! Not all of us are the same!' 'Right you ain't!' By this time, Barry was having considerable trouble keeping the two apart. Another wrestler popped through the door and saw the fracas. He flew towards it, and in an instant, he tackled Lonnie as if he were Spider-Man. Of course, it would be easy to mistake him for Spider-Man that day because Jericoholic Anonymous was wearing a red lucha mask. JA's intervention gave Barry the time he needed to restrain his friend. 'Get the fuck offa me you stupid masked midget!' 'Dude, we've been over this a hundred times. In three seconds I could suplex your ass on the concrete floor and you'd be drinking the rest of your meals out of a feeding tube the rest of your sorry life, alright? Now, settle the fuck down and leave the poor kid alone.' 'But he done made a fool outta me in front of all my fans!' Barry interjected, 'And people have been doing that to you for years.' 'Yeah, if you want me so bad, get me back in the ring.' JA turned around with a half-shocked, half-impressed look in his eyes under his mask. 'Whoa-ho-ho, look at the balls on this one.' 'Fuck you. Fuck all three a' ya! If you want me in the ring, then why not do it on my terms? No disqualifications. If you really wanna throw down, then lemme at least have my Black Betty!' Lonnie held his chair up. The sun coming in from the window shone off of it, making it look like Excalibur just as Arthur plucked it from the stone. Lonnie held it by its left leg, and it its right, the words 'Black Betty' were inscribed on it. There was minimal denting, if only because he liked hitting people in the back rather than over the head. Tariq saw it and furrowed his brow. 'You know what? You're on.' 'Good. Woowhee! I get to kill you in front of all my fans. This'll be sweet.' Lonnie whipped his chair down and marched off to his changing area. JA and Barry both looked at Tariq. 'You know what kid, you got nutsac,' said JA. The masked wrestler patted Tariq on the shoulder and walked off. 'Man... this is awesome. You really stood up to him. I didn't know you had it in you.' Thing was, Tariq didn't know that either. He couldn't help but remember before when he would shy away from Habib, and then Lonnie before the chairshot. He didn't know what came over him just then. 'Well, I guess I did.' Word got all around the roster of the fracas and the challenge, and James Hopkins had no choice but to book Suleimon/Jackson II at his next event. Not that he minded. The match had MEGA heat behind it. He was a little fearful for Tariq. Rahman had told him of his pupil's aversion to weapons, that the last time he walloped someone with a chair, he refused to keep using weaponry afterwards. Knowing that Lonnie wouldn't hesitate to use anything at his disposal, he thought Tariq at a disadvantage. Yet, the attitude in Tariq in the two weeks preceeding the match was totally different than it was before his last match with Lonnie. Lonnie would try his normal shit, but Tariq wasn't having any of it. The day after James had signed the match officially, Lonnie went into Tariq's locker room. 'Hey you sandnigger? You ready to get yer ass beat?' 'Yeah, I'm about as ready for that as you are to admit that your cousin is also your grandfather.' 'What the fuck! You take that back! Yer makin' a big mistake!' 'Yeah, that's what your mother said before her brother screwed her the night you were conceived.' 'Sonuvabitch!' It would go on and on like that, back and forth. Lonnie would try to bait Tariq, but Tariq would always have a comeback for him. Lonnie never resorted to violence. He just kept telling himself that he'd get his chance to wallop Tariq once and for all in the middle of the ring. Him and Black Betty, riding victorious over the carcass of a dirty raghead. After two weeks, it was finally here. The big match, Lonnie Jackson and Captain Suleimon. Both men came out of the back with their weapons of choice, Lonnie with Black Betty and Tariq with a steel chair of his own. Not anything flashy or engraved, but it was metal, blunt and it could do the job. As the bell rang, both men swung with their steel chairs. *CLANK* The shock of steel on steel sent waves down both men's arms. Each man drew back again and swung, but again, they clashed their chairs on each other. This time, the shockwaves sent down both men's arms made them drop their respective chairs. After that, they said 'fuck it' and just went at each other. Closed fists, choke holds, raking of the eyes. No hold was barred and neither man held back. AT one point, Lonnie whipped Tariq so hard into the steel guard rail that it moved, almost allowing some drunken front row fans to get into the ringside area. Thankfully, security was swift. Tariq returned in kind by choking Lonnie with the tag rope for so long that his face turned purple. To say that it was war in that ring was an understatement, but each man plodded on, exchanging their bodies for the chance at keeping their pride in the face of a mortal enemy. At the fifteen minute mark, both men were worn down. In fifteen minutes, they had done to each other what the Franks and the Abbayyids had done to each other over fifty years of Crusades. Still, the wily and crafty redneck veteran took his shot. As Tariq was struggling to get to his feet using the ropes, Lonnie snuck up behind him and... *BAM* An uppercut to the lower Anatolia region of one Tariq Suleimon. To his credit, Tariq didn't fall to the canvas. Instead, he stayed up, doubled over due to the groin shot he just took. Lonnie took this advantage to grab the lost Black Betty. Grabbing it, he kissed it before he raised it face level. He snuck up behind Tariq again, but this time, Tariq turned his head in just the nick of time. Then, by some miracle of God or adrenaline, the young Turk spun around and leaped in the air, sore groin be damned. He jumped and put both his feet into Black Betty, sending the chair smashing right into Lonnie's face. The crowd was stunned and so was Lonnie. He dropped the chair and held his nose, checking it for blood. Almost instinctively, Tariq grabbed Black Betty rose it over his head... *CRASH!* Lonnie went crashing to the canvas, a victim from a shot over the head with his own damn chair. Even though Tariq saw that Lonnie was nearly unconscious on the mat, there was no mercy or relenting in his eyes. He rose the chair again... CRASH! on the prone redneck. Finally seeing that Lonnie was out cold, Tariq covered. Tariq won. Tariq started getting pelted with garbage. Under the hail of soda cups, programs and other debris, he looked out at Lonnie Jackson, a man who had tormented him in his first month or so in the American wrestling game. He saw the referee administer smelling salts. He saw Lonnie struggle to regain his senses. He saw... a broken foe. In response to everything he saw... he smiled. He grinned from ear to ear. Seeing his hated foe broken on the floor filled him with more joy than anything else he had ever experienced in his life. The conscience in his head telling him not to revel in another man's pain and suffering was being drowned out by the screaming throng of voices in his head telling him he had done well. At that moment, Tariq realized that he was reveling in being sadistic. But for that moment, he didn't care. In fact, he deserved to feel this way. Then he got to thinking that he deserved something else. Spoils from war. Out of the corner of his eye, Tariq spotted a contrast on the ring floor; black metal on off-white canvas. He turned around and saw Black Betty, sitting there, dented next to Lonnie. He went over to it, picked it up, and raised it over his head. If the crowd wasn't booing him at full capacity before, they were now. Some fans were getting restless enough to try and hop the gates. Security saw this and immediately hopped in the ring. 'Tariq, we gotta get you outta here.' He was still in a dreamlike stupor. 'TARIQ!' Finally, he turned around to the guard. 'These fans are going to riot. You have to get out of the ring.' Tariq nodded and then made his way down the aisle, Black Betty in hand. The next day, Tariq, JA, Barry and Rahman were gathered in the locker room. Tariq was taping up, Rahman catching a smoke while reading a magazine and JA and Barry talking about things. Then, suddenly, James Hopkins barged into the locker room. 'Tariq! There you are. I've been meaning to talk to you.' Tariq shot up from the bench and turned around to face his boss. He felt something in the pit of his stomach, like he had done something wrong last night. He knew it, he shouldn't have stolen Black Betty. 'I just wanted to congratulate you for the show you put on last night. It looked like you were a natural out there with those weapons. Did Rahman teach you that technique?' Tariq shook his head. 'Acutally, Master Rahman had been pushing for me to learn that, but I refused. Everything you saw last night was borne out... well, I guess out of my hatred and frustration.' 'Oh, well, either way, you're a pro at this. How would you like to do that all the time?' 'What, you mean cave Lonnie's head in every night?' JA overheard this and stood up. 'No man, I think what he means is do you want to wrestle in hardcore matches all the time?' 'Hardcore matches? What's that?' JA took a second to compose himself. 'Well, hardcore is this style of wrestling where there's no Dee-Kyoo. Everything's legal. Weapons, interference, all kinds of crazy shit, man. It's catching on here, there's Extreme Championship Wrestling based right here in Philly. Kinda cult favorites nationally, but they might catch on. This shit is HUGE in Japan too. I mean, a friend I got, he wrestles under the name Maggot. He's done some crazy ass shit. I got some tapes of him from when he was in Warfare, Inc. You wanna watch?' 'Wait a second... you mean every match is going to be no disqualification?' James nodded. 'And I'd have to use... weapons?' He nodded again, then stopped, tilted his head diagonally a bit and then squinted. 'Well, yes and no. You don't have to, but you should. I mean, you'll probably have to use them to get ahead.' 'No... no, I can't do that.' Rahman, who was listening the whole time, stood up, cigarette falling out of his mouth. 'Why can't you? You just did the same thing to Lonnie last night!' 'Well, yes, but that was only because I hated him. I can't... can't raise a weapon to someone I don't hate, someone I just met, or even worse, someone that I actually like.' 'Goodness gracious Tariq, then you haven't learned a thing I've taught you.' 'But... but...' Rahman wouldn't have any of it. He stormed out of the room. James had an awkward look about his face, but he soon composed himself and went back to addressing Tariq.' 'Look, you won't have to use weapons. If you want, you can go cleanly by the rules all the time. But I want to put you in that division, just in case you do come across someone you hate, then you might be able to let loose. Okay?' Tariq had a tenative look about his face. 'Well... I guess.' 'Alrighty. You'll be booked on the next card then in the first ever LBW hardcore match. I'll find an opponent for you.' Tariq shook his head, wondering what he had gotten himself into. For the next two weeks in training, Rahman stayed as far away from Tariq as possible. Tariq knew why, but he also wasn't clamoring to have Rahman back in his corner either. He didn't want Rahman to try and get him to give in and use weapons. Rahman didn't want his words to fall on deaf ears again. Tariq was headstrong at best and stubborn at worst. He knew that only after a massive failing or a change in his life could he be molded. So he kept his mouth shut, both at the wrestling arena and at home. Not a word was spoken in the Turks' apartment outside of questions about food. You could hear a pin drop. Tariq would retreat to his room to read about the glories of his country past. Rahman would go outside and smoke with the grizzled old Italian men who inhabited South Philadelphia. Two weeks came and passed, and it was Tariq's official hardcore debut against a guy called Ironhead. He was 270 pounds of fat and muscle, and he carried all kinds of junk on his person, mostly in a shopping cart. For that match, he brought with him a tire iron, a crowbar and a baseball bat. Tariq saw the weapons, but he was undaunted. The bell rung and the match began. Using his superior speed and agility, Tariq kept Ironhead off-balance. Using his superior wrestling skills, Tariq kept Ironhead off his feet and on the canvas on a regular basis. Rahman watched from the front row in disbelief. All night, he was waiting for Tariq to implode, see the error of his ways and then come crawling back to him, yet nothing was happening here to suggest it. Missile dropkick right on Ironhead's temple. Tornado reverse DDT from the top. Somersault no-hands plancha over the top. Each impact struck Rahman as much as it struck Ironhead. As each blow progressively weakened Ironhead, it had the reverse effect on Rahman. He thought... maybe Tariq was right, and maybe that's not such a bad thing afterall. Maybe, just maybe he could run roughshod over the division without weapons. It could work, he thought. And it could be marketable too. That line of thought went out the window when Ironhead countered a top rope double axehandle with a brass knuckles shot to the face. Tariq fell to the canvas like a ton of bricks. Ironhead gained control of the match, beating Tariq down with any weapon he could find, be it his hamhock of a fist to random steel chairs. Within five minutes, the match was over. Tariq was bloodied, and Rahman was emboldened, but for a different reason than before. The next morning, Tariq didn't go to the arena. On Rahman's orders, he went to a vacant warehouse somewhere out on Gray's Ferry Avenue. 'What are we doing here?' Tariq looked around at the broken windows, the debris, mice. He then looked upon Rahman, who was sitting on a chair. A black chair. 'Your showing last night was impressive, but not impressive enough. I've brought you here so that you may be fully impressive.' 'Why?' 'Because I fully intend on you staying in the hardcore division.' 'But... I don't want to... it's not...' 'Quiet. You will do what's good for you, and staying in the hardcore division is what's good for you. Do you understand?' Tariq shook his head. He was about to say something, but Rahman cut him off. 'If you haven't learned last night, then you may never learn. You must not hold back your potential. Whenever you refuse to use weapons, you hold back your potential.' 'But I can't. I can't hit anyone I don't truly hate with a weapon. It's not honorable. It's not fair.' 'Yes, and a man plowing you with brass knuckles is fair, Tariq? Take a look inside of your mind at that match last night. You let that fat slob walk all over you because you didn't hate him, and yet he had no problems using everything but the kitchen sink on you, despite the fact that he just met you at belltime. Do you know what he did last night?' Tariq shook his head. He had a slight concussion, of course he didn't know. 'He offered to buy you a beer last night. Are those the actions of someone who hates someone else?' Tariq had no answer for that question. 'Well, just because he's like that doesn't mean I have to.' 'Oh my Lord... this isn't University. That idealistic bullshit won't fly here. It's either kill or be killed, and if you continue down this road, you're going to be killed before your career even gets off the ground.' Tariq was silent, soaking in the words of his teacher. Rahman wouldn't let him soak for too long. He stood up and folded up the chair. Holding it up by the left leg, Tariq noticed the inscription on the right. 'You must learn to conquer your inhibitions.' Rahman walked intently towards Tariq. 'You must learn to become a warrior.' He handed the chair to his pupil. 'You must learn to attack those you love.' Tariq looked up at Rahman like he was crazy. 'You can't be serious.' 'Oh, I am. Strike me over the head. Hit me with this chair, the same chair you destroyed Lonnie Jackson with.' Tariq was incredulous. He couldn't believe what his teacher was asking him to do. 'What are you waiting for? Do it!' Rahman was egging him on, but Tariq wouldn't have any of it. HE held the chair lackadaisically by his side and turned his head away from his teacher. 'You insolent fool! Don't turn away from me! Look at me!' Tariq turned around to face him sheepishly. 'Strike me! Strike me down with all your fury!' Tears began to stream out of Tariq's eyes. He shook his head. 'What are you doing? Hit me!' 'I... I can't...' 'Yes you can! You will hit me! You will not leave this building until you hit me!' Tariq kept shaking his head, more violently than he was before. 'I swear, if you don't, I will take you right out of LBW and we will go home. You will have no wrestling career. Hit me.' Tariq stopped shaking and looked up. He clenched the chair with one hand and moved other on the other leg. 'Do it!' Tariq stopped and looked right in Rahman's eyes, into the eyes of a man who was more of a father to him than anyone had ever been, more than Farouk Suleimon, more than Mehmet Enver, more than The Imam. He looked into Rahman's fiery eyes and saw perhaps the only man who cared for him enough in his life to give him everything he wanted. Who was he to deny Rahman of what he wanted? With a deep breath and one fell swoop, Tariq lifted Black Betty over his head and slammed it down onto the top of Rahman's skull. The old Turk crumpled to the floor of the decrepit old warehouse. And Tariq? He felt a deep sense of... satisfaction. Not the satisfaction that he felt when he brained Lonnie... it was different. It was a rush, something that he had never felt before. He felt exhilarated. He felt... alive. He looked upon Rahman and smiled before noticing that his mentor wasn't moving. He took Rahman over his shoulders and ran out of the warehouse to the nearest hospital. Of course, Rahman didn't file criminal charges, and while the police had a tough time believing that the assault was requested by the victim, they had to buy it. After that, Tariq and Rahman worked closely together for a certain goal, a goal of dominating the hardcore division of LBW. Suleimon looked at Black Betty, a chair that hadn't been used since that day in the warehouse. He'd used several other weapons over the years, but this one was special to him. Sacred. He looked at it, and then took it into his grasp. He then turned to Dogan and raised it quickly over his head. Dogan flinched, but Suleimon held back, laughing at the apparent cowardice of his cousin. 'Yes, there was a time when I was loathe to using these things. But those days are long past. Your days of wetting yourself, though, oh, they're still here.' 'You're a douchebag, you know that?' Dogan turned indignantly and walked out. Suleimon put Black Betty back into the corner and looked at it, most specifically at the dent right in the middle of the seat. The one he put there that day on Gray's Ferry Avenue.
