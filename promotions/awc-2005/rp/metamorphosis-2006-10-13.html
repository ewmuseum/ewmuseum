Metamorphosis

13 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Chapter 4: “Metamorphosis” For: Untouchable Match: Michael “Chainz” Sloan vs. Pierce Lavelle Rome, Italy The sweet butter scotch taste swirled around his mouth, electrifying his taste buds as he tasted the fine liquid. Fleming turned his chair around and placed the small glass firmly onto the table, his eyes gleaming with satisfaction as he licked his lips. “Well, what’s the progress?” Fleming asked. Jorge Ciccone slumped forward, his rotund stomach perched against the mahogany table. “We’re moving slowly, the boy is becoming a problem,” Ciccone said, solemnly. Fleming sighed with an irritated disgust and straightened his posture. His thin white gold Rolex began catching the setting sun’s rays, as slits of light jutted through the blinds. “What do you mean a ‘problem’? I thought Montoya was handling things,” Fleming questioned, sternly. A large bottle of thick red wine sat in the centre of the table. Ciccone reached for it and poured himself a glass, wetting his mouth as he let the taste linger before addressing the irritant Englishman. “He’s handling things, but the boy’s stubborn. He won’t join in a heart-step as you English say,” Ciccone said in a thick Italian tone. Fleming rolled his eyes and lit a cigarette. “It’s heartbeat, Ciccone. I think we’ll need a contingency plan.” “Contingency plan?” Ciccone questioned, raising the rim of his wine glass toward his mouth, intrigued by his partner’s cunning. “We’ve been waiting a month now…We need that bloody money; otherwise we lose our shipment,” Fleming snarled. Ciccone nodded his head in acquiescence and pulled out a large Cuban cigar, wetting the tip as he lit it. The nicotine fumes circling his lungs as a wave of comfort washed through him. Exhaling he turned toward Fleming, who was now focused on a small sleeve of documents. “Give Montoya time, he’ll earn the boy’s trust,” Ciccone said. Fleming coughed under the stench of cigar. “Time is a virtue I don’t have, Ciccone.” “What do suggest we do?” Ciccone questioned, glumly. Rubbing his chin intuitively, Fleming pondered the possibilities of his options. The little he knew and the many he had, all circled through his mind. Yes, what shall we do? He thought before a smile of realisation emerged along his puckered lips. “Information is the key my Italian friend,” Fleming said, gleaming with delight. “What?” Ciccone said in a daze. Fleming smirked. “Yes, it’s brilliant…” Yokohama, Japan The wind ripped passed the Toyota pick-up as he cranked it into second gear, driving it up the small mountainous range till he came to a screeching halt, comfortably outside the small house. Tony Rush froze, eyes peeled on the rear view mirror, a sense of apprehension fluttered through him as he kept his mind focused on the mission at hand. The simple meaningful task to be carried out, a careful observation, however something felt omniscient to him as he reached under his seat and removed his .38 calibre pistol. Emi’s words still loitered in his mind. ‘It’s only a simple scouting mission. It might be a dead end for all we know’. The eagerness he once held to carry-out this task soon dissipated as he watched the approaching storm… The storm rose over the hilltops from the Pacific Ocean, the clouds a musky grey above him as a light patter of rainfall began to dance along his shoulders as he trundled across the loose gravel. The house remained void of motion, its windows dark and dreary to the observer. His eyes scanned the perimeter, gun heeled high in a firing stance as he moved through the dense shrubbery till he found the black truck in the back of the house, two figures perched outside, their eyes focused on the man in the white suit holding a metallic briefcase. “Have you got the shipment?” the man in the white suit said, calmly. One of the men nodded and opened the sliding door of the truck to reveal a large array of crates. The man moved forward, a smile pursed along his lips. “Excellent,” he said, gleefully. “What about our money?” the hired gun asked with his eyes lingering on the man in the white suit. From the distance, Rush could make out the conversation faintly, but his focus was on the man in the white suit. He had a demeanour of utter-importance and power to his pale and scrawny features. “You’ll get your pay when the mission is complete,” he responded, coldly. “We brought you the shipment,” the other spat. A smirk lined the man’s face, a humble laugh accompanied with a straightened posture as he scanned the shipment. “That you have, but we aren’t finished. My boss wants them transferred to Rome.” “Rome?” The hired guns questioned instinctively, confusion lining their tense and toned characteristics. Rush watched on through the focus of his binoculars, watching the two men holster their firing rifles to their shoulders as they shut the sliding door of the truck and escorted the man in the white suit towards the comfort of another car. A crackling noise aroused his senses as he turned his head instinctively. A tall figure loomed over him, the butt end of his rifle pointed toward Rush’s head. With a sudden clatter, Rush watched the ground move as he fell toward darkness… Harrisburg, Pennsylvania He sat alone at the small bar, a pair of sunglasses shading the public from his battered and venerable appearance. A pot of coffee sits steaming by his hand, his other hand sheltered under his suit coat, hoisted to his neck with a sling. His brutal attack no more than a bad dream with the uncanny repercussions as he sips the coffee and chows down on some pancakes. Sloping into the chair, Pierce closed his eyes allowing the pain killers to sooth his anguish and anxiety ridden state. “This seat taken?” A tall male figure emerged from the crowd of morning setters. Dressed in casual jeans, a shirt and a jacket, his eyes focused on Pierce. “You’ve disturbed my reverie, mate,” Pierce said, mockingly. “How’s the head?” questioned the man as he took off his jacket and sat comfortably into the metal chair. “Better, thanks. So, what’s with the urgent phone call?” Pierce questioned, forwardly. No longer willing to waste time on meaningless chit-chat, the figure admired the politeness and forwardness of his fellow partner and comrade. Smirking, he ordered a coffee, black with no sugar and turned his attention to Pierce. “What do you remember from last week?” he asked. Pierce’s eyebrows rose intuitively as he pondered the question and its answer. “Not much…I just remember Sloan screaming a couple of things at me and the rest is a medicated blur.” “You don’t remember our conversation?” he questioned, his lips lingering on the rim of his cup of coffee, while blowing gently into the steaming hot liquid. “What conversation?” Pierce questioned. Placing the cup down, he watched Pierce slump into the chair and rub his damage arm subconsciously. “Doesn’t matter. I’ll say it all again. We aren’t playing things safe,” he said. “We? Excuse me, but I’m doing all the work,” Pierce spoke with anger. Raising his arms in a calming gesture he waited for Pierce to settle down and then spoke. “Alright, so things didn’t play out as planned, but…” he said, quietly leaning forward in his seat. “…you are faltering. That’s a sign of weakness. We can’t make any more slip-ups.” “What more do you want? Sloan already used me as his punching bag. I’m trying here, but you’ve got to get him off my back,” Pierce demanded, coldly. The figure sat up straight and pulled out a small packet of cigarettes, taking a calm approach to his disgruntled partner’s state. Watching with a quizzical look, Pierce sighed and sipped his now cooled cup of coffee with distaste for the melted sugar at the bottom. “Sloan knows the truth, man,” Pierce said, bluntly. Eyes focused on the simmering glaze at the bottom of his cup. The figure raised his eyes and blew a large plume of smoke into the early morning air. “I know.” “You do?” Pierce questioned in an awestruck state. “When? How?” The figure moved forward, emptying his ash into a tray. “That doesn’t matter. I’ll deal with Sloan when the time is right. Just keep up appearances.” Pierce nodded his head in acquiescence. “I understand.” Yokohama, Japan “Rise and shine, Mr. Rush” A daunting voice rose within the darkness, a pair of beady eyes shimmering against the feint amount of light. Tony raised his head with a pained expression across his face, his vision blurred momentarily as he collected himself before standing in the darkened room. “Or is it Alex Hughes…” The voice spoke again as Tony listened trying to figure out where the man was standing. The voice was familiar. It was a dry and husky tone, but with a posh and reverent eloquence to his speech. There was a shuffling of feet against the cold concrete floor, a whisper of orders and a flicker of a switch. “You’ve many names, Mr. Rush. Quite clever for an Agent on the run, but not clever enough…” Tony felt a pulling sensation against his arms. He tugged violently but felt the cold metal of handcuffs restrain him. The flickering of a light bulb illuminated the darkness. Closing his eyes against the harsh and blinding light, Tony watched through a squinted vision as the man in the white suit and three more men moved toward him. “Not much of a talker, are you Mr. Rush?” He asked, mockingly. Tony raised his head, eyes gazing toward the man with determination. The man returned the stare, blankly. His face was void of any emotion. Suddenly, Tony felt one of the men place a clamp on the handcuffs. His feet felt numb, bare and cold. Looking toward his posterior he saw a small water basin in which he was dangling. Realisation occurred… The fear was overwhelming… “Electroshock torture, Mr. Rush. I’m sure you are familiar with it…” he said, gloatingly. They’re going to torture me Rush thought, the realisation of his predicament placed a cold, fearful feeling through his body, but he suppressed it, keeping rationale as his main objective. “You expect me talk?” Rush vehemently spat. The man laughed with a menacing gaze. “No, Mr. Rush, I expect you to die.” Manhattan, New York The rain clattered against the windows of the black limo as it moved through Broadway and 6th. New York City was rife with activity, crowds of busy people bustling through Broadway and 6th avenue, their briefcase’s bouncing off the yellow cabs and limos as they moved through the dense crowds, scurrying under shops to avoid the pelting rain drops. “Keep driving, Charles,” Montoya ordered while he sat comfortably in the back seat. Montoya reclined, revering in his success. “Where to now, Sir?” Charles questioned, sheepishly. “To the hotel Charles, I’m quite tired.” Montoya’s response was shrouded by the echoing ring tone of his mobile phone. The visor rose instinctively as Montoya answered his phone. “Yes?” he questioned, directly. … “What about him? I’m making progress. He’s become quite an athlete,” Montoya spoke, proudly. A sense of astute clarity rose within Montoya as he listened to the recipient on the other end. The order he was given was no more than a simple adherence, a minor mission in the light of greater more distinguishable merits. “How shall I proceed?” Montoya asked. … Montoya opened a small manila file buried underneath his long trench coat, opening the file he skimmed the first few pages before finding the one he needed most. A wave of excitement washed over him. “I have the answer, rather simple in fact,” Montoya said, gleefully. … “I understand what needs to be done,” Montoya assured the recipient. … “It’ll be done,” Montoya spoke with a calm tone as he hung-up. Placing the manila file back underneath his coat he pressed a small microphone and spoke with a lax and gentle tone. “Charles, change of plans… We’re going to Pennsylvania.” Harrisburg, Pennsylvania The Fall Costume Ball The main hall became immersed in people drinking and bobbing their heads as they avoided the Fall decorations. Golden brown leaves hung around the circular banister along with pumpkins and flashing black-lights. The music echoed with a mesmerizing beat throughout the gargantuan ball room as people jived vivaciously in their ornate and decorated costumes to the DJ’s spinning sets. Pierce walked with a slightly limp pace as he dragged his long black cape behind him, his mask blocking the many onlookers to his battered and bruised facial appearance as he joined in, dancing to the familiar and yet catchy “Sexy Back”. He watched from a distance with feint recognition as a woman emerged from the toilets dressed in a cream and decorative long gown. “This is interesting,” a voice yelled upon approaching the main door. Blinded by the rotating black-lights Pierce watched where he stepped as he moved through the crowds grabbing a small bottle of Budweiser to settle his frazzled nerves as he approached the familiar figure stood over the punch bowl. “I didn’t think you’d come,” he yelled. She turned around with a sweet smile, holding a small cup of fruit punch in her hand. “Always the doubter, Skippy,” she said, sweetly. He smiled for a moment and moved out of the line of traffic toward her. “How’ve you been?” he asked, coyly. She sipped her fruit punch, allowing the tropical flavours to tickle her taste buds as the glass lingered on her lips. “I’m doing really well,” she yelled her reply over the heavy bass within the speakers. Lois looked toward his slouched posture and masked appearance, a long black cape draped over his body. She leant toward him, finding the yelling strenuous on her voice. “Phantom of the opera, right?” she asked, smilingly. Nodding his head he smiled with a sweet glisten in his eyes. “Merion from Robin hood, I take it?” “Got it right in one go, Skippy,” Lois laughed with a gleaming smile that fluttered Pierce’s senses. “So where’s Robin Hood then?” he questioned, glumly. Lois looked around the room in a gesturing manner before turning her attention toward Pierce. “No idea.” “Did you come with someone?” he questioned. He realised the nosy and yet interesting side to his prying for information. He had felt the same feelings not long ago when he’d last seen her and although they remained in slumber they were very much alive and alert now as he gazed toward her ravishing appearance and gorgeously glistening green eyes. “Yes. I’m here with Robert,” she said quietly in his ear. She watched his expression drop, his eyes panning along the crowds as he slumped onto the corner of the table. She noticed a small red gash emerge from behind the side face mask. Her eyes lingered on it a moment before seeing the sling as he tossed his cape aside whilst pulling out a packet of cigarettes. “Since when do you smoke?” she asked with a forceful tone. He looked toward his packet of cigarettes and couldn’t even remember how long it had been or how many he was taking in a day; all he could recall was the relaxing feeling that came with it. “Quite recently,” he said, shrilly, while placing a cigarette into his mouth. He broke a match and lit it but as he went to light his cigarette it blew out. She stood, hands on hips smiling while she removed his cigarette. “Well, as of this moment, you’re stopping.” The crowds relinquished their heavy dancing as a rock song emerged along the speakers, taking breathers they dispersed around the room, some retiring to the near-by seats. Pierce watched a group of three people emerge toward them, instant recognition. “Where’s your band of merry men?” Pierce questioned with a smile. Robert Green laughed as he took his green hat off and made himself a drink. “Robbing the rich and giving to the poor.” “Where have you been you bastard?” A rusty and low voice spoke as Pierce turned around and looked toward Tom Lincoln seated in his wheelchair dressed in a suit with a bald cap on his head. “Nice to see you too, Tom,” Pierce said with a grin. “So, what am I?” Tom questioned. “Go on, take a guess…” Pierce rubbed his chin and mask intuitively. “I’m drawing a blank here.” Robert laughed and handed Tom and Lois a drink before turning to the last figure emerging through the crowd, dressed in a gold slit dress with a hair style the likes of which was long gone. “Cleopatra you’re late,” Tom said with a grin toward his girlfriend, Selene. “He’s Xavier from X-Men, I swear, it couldn’t be more embarrassing,” Selene said as she kissed Tom’s cheek and turned to Pierce. “So when did you drop into town?” Robert questioned as he placed an arm over Lois’s shoulders. A quivering and prolonging clot rose within his throat as he sipped his drink, turning his attention away from Robert. “Just recently,” Pierce replied, raptly. The dance floor soon filled once more as “Rock this party” by Bob Sinclair began to play with the accompaniment of many people whistling to the beat, drunk as they slurred on their many beverages. Lois watched him from the corner of her eye as he sipped his beer and laughed with Tom and Selene. She felt Robert began to grope her shoulders and shivered a little. “You know what, how about a dance?” she said immediately gripping Pierce’s good hand and tearing him away from the crowd. “But…Merion!!!” Robert wailed. He let her lead him, savouring the touch of her soft skin as they moved through the twisting and grinding of bodies. They didn’t stop; he watched as she checked behind her and dragged him through the thick mess of people before pulling him outside to the main grounds. A swimming pool reflected the lights and the moon, his eyes blinded by the brightness around him. “I thought we were dancing,” Pierce said with a confused smile. “Hate to tell you, Skippy, but you can’t dance,” she said while she led him, holding his hand. “I know…what…hey!” he said, glumly. The main grounds were filled with limos, flashy sports cars and the typical town cars. Bodies swarmed the benches and many had now splashed themselves in the pool and Jacuzzi. He soon felt like he was being led through the Playboy mansion. Lois stopped at the small balcony and turned toward him. “That was a good song. I could’ve danced to it,” Pierce retorted. She smiled and cocked her head to the side, questionably. “Oh really…” “Yeah really, I’ve got the moves…” he said laughing and turned toward the crowds moving about, chatting, drinking and complimenting each other’s costumes. “How long have you been back?” she asked moving forward toward him. “Couple of days now, had some business to take care of,” he said as he watched her move. “Business, huh?” Lois gripped the small mask and pulled it off gently to reveal bruising, swellings and many gashes. Her mouth opened with shock for a second before gently running her hand along his face. “This the business you’re dealing in nowadays, getting your ass kicked?” Pierce looked toward Lois stood before him holding his mask with worry in her eyes. “I’m fine, Lo. Just a couple of cuts and bruises. It’s nothing serious.” “Nothing serious! Then why the sling?” she questioned with determination. He stood aghast for a moment, pondering the many answers he could reply with and all the emerged from his lips was: “It’s nothing. A little accident is all.” He stood silent for a moment, kicking himself inside for not coming up with a more relevant excuse. A couple scooted through the small pathway, linked arm in arm as they merrily sang to the music, interrupting the uncomfortable silence that lingered between Pierce and Lois. “I’m not stupid. You got the crap kicked out of you and you pretend it’s nothing,” Lois spat, angrily. “Well, it is nothing,” he retorted. “This is the business you are in?” Lois said angrily as she shoved the small mask back into his stomach. He grips it in a tight clutch, holding his painful arm as his face cringes, showing his pain. “Since when do you care so much?” he questioned, bitterly. “I always did care and I never stopped,” Lois retorted with a vehement and menacing gaze. She reached in and slowly kissed him across the lips, the scent of her perfume allured his sense, tickling his nose as he returned the kiss passionately; his good arm wrapped around her waist as he savoured the soft and gentle touch of her lips. Feeling completely at ease and serene as he pulled back and gazed lovingly toward her. “I’ve missed you, Lo,” he said, sweetly. She laughed and patted his shoulder. “Me too, Skippy. Look, this ball isn’t really me, you want to grab a bite to eat in about twenty minutes?” she asked, coyly. “Sure. Can you get away from…?” “Of course. Meet me at the main entrance in twenty,” she said as she kissed him on the cheek and adjusted his mask before disappearing with a swagger in her step toward the mounds of bodies churning along the dance floor. Yokohama, Japan Panting and sweating profusely he rose his head with distinct determination, his face cringed in pain as he felt the electricity surge through his body, stinging each muscle and fibre of his being. The menacing eyes of his torturer lay upon him, a crooked grin placed across his thin lips as he pressed the button once more. His body shook violently, his muscles contorting as he coughed violently, his lungs gasping for air, his heart pounding heavily against his rib cage. Silence emerged through the small dark room; the stench of cooking flesh rose up his nose as his eyes struggled to focus. “That’s enough,” the man in the white suit ordered as he moved toward Rush. The two hired guns flexed their legs and continued to play a game of cards as though nothing was developing around them, oblivious to Rush’s pain and wailing cries. “Now, Mr. Rush, are you willing to talk to save your life?” he questioned, smugly. Rush panted as he felt his saliva draining, his mouth dry and sore as he struggled to speak. “I thought you were going to kill me.” “In time Mr. Rush but for now…you have the chance to save your life,” he replied with a sadist glow. Rush straightened his posture, the handcuffs digging into his wrists, slicing the skin. His mind barely able to focus as the pain became overwhelming, his insides sizzling. “Riddle me this, Mr. Rush. If you have two traitors on the run from their countries and you back them into a corner…will they turn on one another or will they stick together?” he questioned, smugly. Rush gazed toward him and saw a grin emerge as he opened a small packet of cigars and lit one up. “Screw you,” Rush bellowed. “Quite alert now, aren’t you,” The man paused for a second, taking in the fumes of the cigar. “How much do you know about Emi Natsuko?” he asked with a sickening laugh. Rush took deep breaths, his anger rising with a fluid wave of pain floating through his tensed muscles. “What did you do?” “You choose death then, very well.” The man in the white suit smirked as he turned his back and began heading toward the small door, waving his hand for the torturer to resume his post. Rush spat at the torturer and received a stinging clout of a punch across his cheek, blood oozing out of his mouth. “You son of a bitch…” Rush yelled into the darkness… Harrisburg, Pennsylvania The Fall Costume Ball He watched her leave with a heavy sigh as he gazed toward the bustling crowds. Slipping his cloak around his sling he moved toward the crowds, sipping his beer as he followed her direction, savouring the scent of her and the touch of her. The ball room was engrossed with bodies as the night-time began to enthral the younger generation to the costume ball. Slipping through a small parting on the dance floor he felt a tug on his cloak and turned around to see Robin Hood (Rob) standing on the tail end of his cloak, his eyes tearing through him with a raging fire. “Rob, do you mind?” Pierce asked, gently. Robert released his foothold on the cloak and watched Pierce side step out of the dance floor toward the refreshments table, his eyes focused on the exit. “Where you off to?” Robert questioned with a dreary tone. “I’m leaving, Rob. Feeling tired,” Pierce retorted in a high-pitched and persuasive tone. Robert stumbled forward, having had too much to drink, he gripped Pierce’s bad arm and watched Pierce winch momentarily. “I know what you are up and I won’t let you,” Robert said, forcefully. Yanking his arm loose from Robert’s strong grip, he gazed toward Robert’s inebriated state and sighed. “I don’t know what you’re talking about,” Pierce insisted and continued walking, he was already late. “As if, man. I saw you kiss her. I watched you. I’m not stupid,” Robert slurred aloud and drove his finger into Pierce’s back causing him to stumble slightly before regaining composer. “Look Rob, you’ve had too much to drink, maybe you should sit down,” Pierce said while backing up in a defensive manner. Robert huffed and squeezed his knuckles, his facial expression hidden under the fake bear and mask he was wearing. “Fuck you, man,” Robert said as he lunged forward, throwing an upper cut punch toward Pierce. Ducking under the punch Pierce watched Robert stumble before colliding with the small set-up stand that contained the fruit punch and many cups. It all collapsed under his weight, many of the dancers now turning around to see Robert’s drunken state. Whispers began to arise as Pierce leant in to help Robert up, his eyes focused on the exit sign… She emerged through the enormous crowd of people and reached the main entrance and waited. The cold refreshing air blew through the large open crevice where the oak doors lay open. The gusts of air blew her long brown hair gently around her shoulders as she sipped on a small glass of water and waited patiently for him to arrive, her eyes scanning the crowds with a feverous glare as she savoured their moment in the back garden, her skin still tingling from his touch as she watched the many couples and groups arriving and leaving. In the distance she spotted him emerge from the circular staircase; his stature a little less slumped as he flailed his cape behind him, scanning the crowd with his eyes. She walked toward him, a swagger in her step as a smile beamed across her cheeks. “Hey, you made it,” she smiled placing a kiss on the cheek of his mask. He nodded his head and pointed toward the spiral staircase he had come from, his voice barely audible over the music. “Yeah, just like what I was thinking,” she said. Biting her lip she took his hand and led him through the crowds once more to the upstairs, moving over the seated people she led him up the stairs and onto the main landing where they stopped. She turned toward him, something was different but she couldn’t quite place it as he took her by the hand and pulled her speedily along the landing toward a large door. “I assume there’s food up here, otherwise we really need to talk,” Lois interjected retracting her hand from his. He turned toward her, sharply, both his arms free from under the cloak and it was then she realised…this wasn’t Pierce.
