Chapter 3: Baby Steps

6 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2.6.06 It was all a blur. He remembered how he got there, anyway. Her match with Adam Dick had broken down. She was out cold. The referee was out colder. And the Illustrious Face Eater, steel chair in hand, stood poised to unleash a plethora of pain on her prone body. But Darcy Markson could recall hardly more than that. A lone glimpse was all that remained; being down on his knees, presumably near the ropes…looking up just in time to see the shot that was meant for Amy Campbell come crashing down on him. The next thing he remembered was waking up just in time to see the sun setting behind the Pacific Ocean as the plane was touching down at San Francisco International. It was certainly disconcerting to deal with a damn near 24-hour blackout, but everything seemed like it was okay. While his head still reeled with a dull ache and his brains still seemed a little bit scrambled, there were no signs of a concussion; the first thing Darcy did when he stepped off the plane was spend half an hour in the airport bathroom checking to make sure his pupils were still dilating properly. This caught him more than a few stares from confused businessmen, who surely must have wondered why he was staring so intently into the mirror. Hopefully, Darcy mused, , some things might clear up once I can get a look at the tape from last night. At the moment, all he had to go on was the transcript of the show posted on the AWC website. From what he understood, Darcy had held his own in a short brawl with the TransAtlantic Champion, at least until a low blow and the vicious chair shot took him out of commission. Instinctively Darcy winced and reached for his lower back when he read that Dick had taken another shot with the chair to Darcy’s back after he was knocked out, but surprisingly he felt nothing more than the normal aches and pains of a man who had spent the last five years recovering from a 30 foot plummet onto wood and concrete…somehow the Miracle Spine™ of Darcy Crisis had survived what could have been yet another crippling blow. But there were still so many more questions that needed resolving – and this is where the memory gap proved most difficult, for only Darcy could have possibly known the answers. Why the hell did I get involved in the first place? Things had began innocently enough; Darcy was never one to skip out on a chance to be part of the action, and with his name off the card for that week the only action to be found backstage was a heated argument with Norton & Bridges about who would make the angrier drunk – Hate or Paddy O’Shea. Darcy had gone with the former, but looking back he could see the tumultuous tag team’s point; maybe a little fun juice would somehow get Hate to calm down once in awhile. Actually, that was only half true. It wasn’t just about being in the spotlight. Darcy was out there to get in Amy’s head. Things weren’t quite going as planned, and her reluctance to step in the ring with him caused Darcy to have to alter his plans. She’d been doing her best to avoid him, and was doing a pretty good job of it until she surprisingly agreed to accompany Darcy back to his home in East Bay only a week ago. Yet still, nothing was really resolved. “It doesn't matter what I think of you, but take it as a good sign that I'm here.”…I believe those were her words… Was it a good sign? Darcy had to agree that it was. After all, she HAD traveled quite a distance just to be with him. Quite a distance indeed - even Forrest Gump would have been wary if he had to make that run. It wasn’t just the distance that made the ordeal impressive, either. She didn’t just come to California…she came BACK to California. Back to the very place where she’d had her heart shattered, to see the man responsible for doing so. But nothing had really happened. They were together for quite some time that week; talking, arguing, occasionally engaging in fisticuffs. It was just like old times…but circumstances remained the same. All the trip to East Bay had proved was that she could at least be in the same room as him. She was still wary of his intentions, still more than hesitant to trust him, still undeniably and vehemently against stepping into the ring with him. Good sign indeed. Baby steps, Darcy…A long journey begins with the first step. Gotta walk before you can crawl…whatever petty maxim will make you feel better. The lack of results had forced Darcy’s hand. This was Darcy’s further, perhaps true reason he showed up for Amy’s match. He had to let her know that he wasn’t going to back down, and he wasn’t going to go away. As far as he was concerned, his “un-retirement” days were more than numbered… …they’re written in permanent goddamn marker. I came here to square things up with Aimz, and I’m not leaving until I get what I came for. So that explained what Darcy was doing out there in the first place. But getting directly involved in the match? That was where he could use some answers. He knew Amy would stop at nothing short of murder if he even had the very notion of trying to get involved. If it were possible, she’d probably even find a way to bring Darcy back to life – simply to berate him and kill him again. In fact, even though there were thousands of witnesses who could attest to it, there was no way Darcy would have believed that was how events transpired had he not seen the recap for himself on his laptop. He was especially astonished at how fast he got down there to stop him, which theoretically didn’t even seem possible. Granted, Adam Dick took his sweet time apprehending his weapon of mass destruction - Is it wrong that every time I hear the word “weapon,” I can’t help but think “WMD?” God, I hate George Bush… - but in the time it took him to fold it up and slide back into the ring, Darcy somehow managed to untangle and remove his headset and sprint half a football field to interject himself into the foray. …almost like I was a man possessed. But possessed by what? It was almost as if higher forces were at work. Yet Darcy was not a man who really believed in “higher forces” – the only religion Darcy ever needed in his life he learned from what Linda Hamilton carved into the table in Terminator 2. NO FATE. There is no fate but what we make for ourselves. For as long as he could remember, Darcy believed himself to be the master of his own destiny. But it didn’t add up. Something must have come over him to make him move as fast as he did, to sacrifice himself by taking the devastating chair shot meant for a girl that, given the chance, might have taken the chair to him herself. Somehow it didn’t add up. With a sigh, Darcy sank back into his chair just outside the terminal and gazed forlornly towards the floor, realizing the ramifications of his predicament. Maybe his brain needed more time to reset. Maybe his memory of what happened might clear up a little bit. But for now, all he could do was wait. But as a pair of legs entered his field of vision, Darcy suddenly thought he may not have to wait for much longer. A pair of olive khakis and dark blue Vans skater-style shoes caught his attention. I know those pants, those shoes…Darcy thought. But why? Why did she follow me back to California again? To make sure I was alright? Why can’t I remember ANYTHING? “Yo, D…you feelin’ alright buddy?” The voice had come from the owner of the legs standing before him…but it wasn’t Amy’s. Darcy looked up to see before him not the girl he was looking for, but instead his longtime friend Johnny Hawk. “Hey man,” Darcy said, with the exhaustion in his voice quite evident. “What are you doing here?” “You called me last night after the show, dude…you said there was no way you’d be able to drive home from the airport, and I guess your cheap ass is too, well…cheap to spring for a cab.” “I did?” “Sure did.” Darcy pushed the hair out of his face, mulling over just how much he had missed in the past 24 hours. “Sorry…I took a bit of a beating last night. Things are a little hazy…” “Well, do you need to go home and sleep it off? Or can a dear, old friend trouble you for a drink?” Darcy smiled. “Such a question need not ever be asked.” ******************************************************************* Darcy wasn’t sure where Jackie Treehorn actually came from first; The Big Lebowski, featuring John Goodman as the immortal Walter Solczak, or the hole in the wall bar off of Flanagan Street in upper San Fran. He’d heard rumors that the owner was a good friend of one of the Coen brothers, but Darcy never cared enough to fully investigate the matter. As far as he and Johnny were concerned, Jackie Treehorn’s was the only place worth going to in search of boozery when the two ventured out of East Bay, regardless of where the name came from. As Johnny went to the bar to pay for a pitcher, Darcy took some time to gaze around at the various pictures hung up around the establishment. He hadn’t seen anything new in quite some time; just the run-of-the-mill Oakland Raiders and Athletics photos and other memorabilia. But in between the picture of John Madden shaking hands with the Jackie’s owner and a Catfish Hunter jersey, Darcy saw his favorite picture of all – the photo of him walking out to the ring the night after he captured the Pier Six Wrestling World Heavyweight Championship. It was also the night he became PSW’s first ever Triple Crown Champion, and in the picture Darcy was wearing the Tag Team and Extreme Violence Championships around his waist, with the World Title proudly held high. The picture also was adorned by his John Hancock. Granted, Pier Six Wrestling was as much of a hole in the wall establishment as Jackie’s, and for that reason he didn’t have too big of a head about it. But Darcy was still proud to be considered part of the local “sports” scene, and enjoyed relishing in the small bit of fame he had obtained. “You’re always staring at that damn thing,” Johnny said as he returned with a pitcher of Bud Light. “Don’t you ever get tired of it?” “It was a good night, Johnny. You’re just pissed cause the only picture up around here is you getting jumped by the bouncers for trying to get in the wet T-shirt contest.” Like Darcy, Johnny worked hard to keep his body in good form. Save for his long, flowing red hair and oft-crass attitude, it would be easy to mistake the two for brothers. The two were definitely close; and the fact that the two had spent so much time in this bar, in this city led Darcy to come up with the perfect analogy: Johnny was Chuckie Sullivan to his Will Hunting. Johnny poured himself a beer before handing the pitcher to Darcy. “So what’s the deal, man? You can talk to the press about your big comeback, but all I get out of you is ‘Hey, I’m wrestling on TV again, can you tape my matches?’ ” Darcy poured himself a beer as well. “Sorry, brother…the EBC wanted to do a little human-interest story on me, and you know how I get when people want to talk about Darcy Crisis.” “So I take it you’re glad to be back out there?” Johnny asked, as he enjoyed his frosty beverage. “Yeah…” came Darcy’s reply. The look on Johnny’s face made him explain further. “I mean, you can’t beat the rush you get inside the ring. And it was even better to know that I could come back after a four year layoff and have my level of intensity be so close to what it was when I left. But you know…this isn’t what I came back for. Every week I look at the card and it’s Darcy Crisis against the poor up and coming sap trying to make a name for himself. The Darcy of old would have relished the opportunity to make a name for himself by putting new jack scrubs in the loss column, but these days…my heart’s just not in it.” “Ahhhh,” Johnny said, slamming his empty mug down on the table. “So that explains what you were doing going after Adam Dick the other night. Trying to mix it up with the Champ? You sly devil…” Darcy rolled his eyes and started on his own beer as Johnny continued. “…but you may have bitten off more than you can chew. If you think what he did to you last night was bad, there’s nooooo way he’s going to let it go that you cost him the match.” “Believe it or not, Adam Dick is the least of my concerns. If he’s got a problem with me I’ll have no problem paying him back for that chair shot. But the only reason I got involved was to save Amy from what ended up happening to me.” At that, Johnny’s eyes lit up. This occurred when only one of two things happened: something had finally dawned on him, or, as Darcy often liked to suggest, anytime he saw Peter North undress. Hoping that one of the most famous penises in all of pornography wasn’t about to appear behind him, Darcy prepared for which one it was going to be. “So that’s what this is about…Amy. You came back for Amy.” Darcy looked at Johnny as if he had suddenly grown another head. “Umm, I know Johnny. I kind of made that clear from the very beginning. My career deserves to have the proper ending. I want one more match with Amy, to find out who really was the best of the old guard.” “Oh, what a load of crap!” Darcy slammed down his own empty mug. “First, score’s tied at 1. Second, excuse me?” “Look, I know how you believe you lead a dual existence. I know how you think Darcy Markson and Darcy Crisis are two different people. And that’s why you should note the very crucial difference in what I said. You didn’t come back so Darcy Crisis could finally go one on one with Aimz. YOU came back for AMY.” Darcy poured himself another beer. “If you’re implying what I think you’re implying…” “I am.” Darcy scowled. “That was a long time ago, man. She’s grown up…and I’ve grown up too. I made a choice that took us on very different paths. Our hearts are in different places now. Believe me, the only time I’m going to spend living in the past is already written down as such: The Darcy Crisis of old makes one final appearance, finally goes one on one with Aimz, locks on the Darcinator, gets his hand raised, and rides off into the sunset. After that, my life moved forward.” “Oh, Darcy, Darcy, Darcy…you talk it, but you don’t walk it, my friend. Fact: You walked away from wrestling and stayed away for what, four years? Five years now? You always said you were proud of what you accomplished and everything was behind you, but now you’ve got in you head that you need to put your life on hold to lace up the boots and step in the ring with Amy. Fact: you haven’t been with a girl for more than two, three months tops since you broke up with Amy. Fact: I call your house last week and and the voice that picks up I hadn’t heard since Summer ’01: Amy’s. And fact: this time last night you were getting your head bashed in sacrificing yourself for Amy. You see?” Johnny asked, as he counted off his next words on his fingers. “Amy, Amy, Amy, Amy.” Taken aback by what Johnny just said, Darcy said nothing. “I mean, say what you want D, and I’ll believe you. I just have a feeling that not too long from now I’m going to show up at your house, you’re going to be sitting there with your arm around her, and you’re going to have that stupid look on your face like you don’t know how the hell it happened.” Darcy remained silent, until he thought it weird that it had been some time since he had spoken. “I-” “Enough about that. I want to tell you about my latest business venture,” Johnny interrupted, as the two began making quick work of the rest of their pitcher. Darcy pretended to follow along as Johnny talked about his latest get-rich-quick scheme, but his mind was very much elsewhere. Is it true?…Is that what this really is all about?…I could have sworn this was just about wrestling her, putting the final stamp on the Darcy Crisis era… But now…I just don’t know…God, I wish I could remember what happened last night… Darcy tuned back in just as Johnny was finishing his story. “-and the best part is, the Feds won’t catch on until I’m in sunny Acapulco, while those bastards are trying to figure out what to do with 20,000 Bruce Campbell action figures.” Darcy smiled, knowing he could have used his forthcoming reply whether he had listened to Johnny or not. “It’ll never work, Johnny. But I wish you the best of luck.” “Yeah, right…” Johnny answered back, as he looked at the now near-empty pitcher. “Well, I think we better hit the road Darce, before we end up one of those ‘You drink, you drive, you lose’ PSAs.” The two headed for the door, but just as they were about to exit Johnny ran into an old friend whom Darcy did not recognize. As Johnny began laying down the same business idea on his friend, Darcy took the opportunity to look back on his Triple Crown doppelganger hanging on the wall. I was on top of the world that day…so full of confidence. I felt like I had such a grip on life…funny how it throws you curveballs when you get too cocky about it. Now I don’t know what the hell I’m gonna do… Suddenly Johnny reappeared next to him. “Everybody’s a critic. Come on, let’s roll.” At that, Darcy followed Johnny out of Jackie’s. He had nary a clue how his life was going to unravel in the coming weeks…but he had a feeling that he was about to take the first steps of a long journey.
