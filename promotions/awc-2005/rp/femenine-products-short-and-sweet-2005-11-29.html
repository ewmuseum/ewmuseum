Femenine Products - Short and Sweet

29 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Nov. 29th ONE ''Five?'' Time passes slowly for anybody with even a mild degree of fame. While the bassist of Evanescence was probably at home playing video games, Amy Campbell was staring at her laptop's LCD display, practically spitting on the keys. ''Number fucking FIVE?'' Her brow furrows, ''Looks like whatever kid they hired to design this piece of shit website pulls double-duty as the Top 10 guy. No way would anyone who knows jack about this business put me in the middle of some jobber top ten list. It's not even like I'm two, or three... hell, I'd take four - but FIVE? Come the hell on... I blew that place to shreds this week! When they announced I had a MATCH on the next card, shit sold out so fa---'' Amy paused, noticing her friend's absolute disinterest. It was Jeph... they'd made up from his overdose in PRIME - where he'd called her selfish, bitchy... basically everything she truly was - and got out of hospital only to move into the apartment he'd once shared as her boyfriend. 'Lucky' for Amy, she'd lost her job just as Jeph had moved back in - so the pair had essentially been stuck together for weeks. The deal was that he'd watch the place when she was gone, just so long as he could get back on his feet emotionally and rediscover who he was as a man... ... Where in that agreement did anyone mention having to listen to her rambling about wrestling, exactly? ''Are you even listening?'' Jeph nodded from behind his comic book, ''Uh huh. Number five... job sucks... gotcha.'' ''You really aren't any comfort in my life, you know that? If Karina were h---'' ''If you hadn't slept with Karina, she probably wouldn't have run off like a convict, so shut up about it.'' Alright, so that argument stood. But it didn't change the fact that Karina would've had that number one spot... and Amy had beaten her, so what was with the lack of recognition? Andy M. was ahead of her. ANDY M.! The man she verbally put away. They may as well have put Red Rock - broken ribs and all - above Aimz. It was pathetic to think about, considering the undeniable fact that she was a superstar who'd made her debut the week before with a bang: Injuring one man, verbally berating a legend and smacking down another. ''Seriously, Jepha...'' She couldn't even be angry at him for the low-blow - not at Jeph, as much as they'd been through, and no matter how much they occasionally loathed one another. He was generally disgusted by the majority of her actions, but they were more like brother and sister than ex-lovers... absolutely inseparable. Except... TWO ...Except when she trained. Why? Because nobody wanted to get near Amy Campbell with a punching bag. She grimaced as blood ran down her right knuckles after the fifth strike. It was time to play in the ring, but she couldn't seem to find a willing partner in the boxing club she frequented, not for the punching power but simply because it was the only place near home with a ring. Having spent her earlier years terrified of heights, she found a freedom that no drug could give when launching herself off of turnbuckles and aprons and, as was the way in her fleeting relationship with punching bags, not a soul on the planet was willing to work with Aimz in that condition... except, of course, for the K-Wolf - who was notably absent from the lives of every wrestler, fan or human who'd known her. A 450 splash into a duck and roll off of the flimsy-roped post of the boxing ring. The mat was even harder than the modest padding of a wrestling ring, and nobody watching as they lifted their weights and hit their speedbags could deny that hitting shoulder-first as she rolled out of the somersault's landing would have hurt easily as much as a thick-gloved haymaker. She was known to train as if she was a UFC fighter and, in reality, she probably should have been. Amy was hardly a wrestler - what she knew on the mat was mostly from Dave Gibson and grinning and bearing it while trying to watch old WWE tapes that she could barely stand to see for a second. To her, wrestling was a redneck's sport - but fighting was for warriors, and that's what she was. She'd fight, she'd sweat and - more than anything - she'd win, because unmitigated passion won out over strength any day of the week. To her, dirty fighting and cheap-shotting was simply the way to even out the size advantage God didn't give her. She loved to scrap and kill off the competiton. Her ideals were questionable, and her motives were much worse, but people didn't pay her to have a kind attitude or a smile on her facsee. Most of them only wanted blood; And blood, they would get. Nobody had ever been entirely sure what it was that made Amy so good at a fist fight, whether it was her sub-par morals or insatiable lust for victory and being able to say she was the best at something, even if that 'something' was simply beating her particular opponent down. Either way you looked at it, she'd had a long-standing career... maybe not as a wrestler, but she'd never failed as a fighter through childhood, highschool and more importantly, in the underground rock scene she'd been introduced to when attending art school in New York City, by a young man called Kellen Kinkade. At the turn of the century, Amy Campbell had simply been a young girl who was eeking through her first semester at an art institute. She'd moved from Canada with very little passion for anything she did, especially when it came to getting her life's education from professors and books, and spent more time in America's club scene than she did studying or going to school. In fact, the art school her parents had funded had given her an ultimatum - start going, or get out and don't expect daddy's money back. Needless to say, Aimz didn't work well with straight choices. Her room mates from home were fed up with her propensity to whittle away her student loan by paying a little bit of rent and finding dealers in the unfamilliar city to buy their choice of drugs (They were art students, after all.) from. It was all she did, and not even well. Instead of finding a trafficking king, she usually found them lazy, rather unkempt dates instead. That, though, was her specialty; Amy went through boyfriends like pints, and - just like things ended up when she'd collected too many pints - it usually ended in a violent altercation between someone. So, one night, she went looking for a new dealer, a new boyfriend or... well... a free drink, and ended up walking back from a club alone, in a neighborhood even 50 Cent wouldn't dare to walk in. That's where she found an underground fighting arena, Kellen Kinkade and a whole new way to get money using the skills she'd used to survive high-school and her fair share of underaged barfights. She needed a tag name and, being the only one who even slightly desired to know her real name, Kinkade dubbed her Ames - but Brooklyn style. A.I.M.Z. Nobody had thought she'd last fighting, but the little redhead ended up paying rent each month, dropping out of school and eventually getting a contract with a Canadian wrestling company thanks to a fat commissioner trying to pick up on her in a bar. Since those moments, she made a name for herself by starring in federations from basements to the Skydome. Knowing she wouldn't have lasted more than a year, regardless of how good she was, fighting in New York warehouses, she'd elected to make a name for herself in something she saw as a little tamer... something she could make a career in. Sure, it was watered-down and dominated by overmuscled giants of men who usually threw fake punches, but Aimz had never turned down a challenge. From there, she took a stiff - and mostly unwelcome - approach to professional wrestling and shouldered a quieter side to so-called 'sports entertainment'. It was simple: She loved beating people down... and the fanfare for a job well done wasn't bad, either - so she did her job as best she could, always finding new and more creative ways to lay opponents down without indulging most of the 'wrasslin'' stereotypes she'd grown up hating. Nobody could knock her creativity... THREE ''What in the name of...'' Jeph had walked in from a photography session to find his room-mate (At least for the duration of her first few feeble AWC paychecks) sitting at their kitchen table, carefully drilling holes into a Louisville Slugger. ''Maybe I shouldn't ask, but why the hell are you maiming my little league baseball bat?'' The response he got was less than what he'd expected. Then again, asking Amy to stop one of her little projects mid-swing was begging for a black eye. ''You sucked at sports, you don't need these memories.'' '''Then for curiosity's sake,'' Jeph sighed, ''What're you doing to that bat?'' ''Giving Andy M. and PCW another good reason not to fuck with me.'' ''By... poking holes in a children's baseball bat?'' Amy rolled her eyes, ''Dumbass... the holes are there so the barbed wire won't just fall off if it gets lodged in his skin. It does need a brace - didn't you ever pay attention in carpentry class?'' ''I must've had mono during the Deadly Weapons unit.'' Another eye-roll came from Amy as Jeph eased back onto the sofa, turning on the TV and effectively attempting to distract her from the task she'd put to hand. However, she wasn't going to budge. ''Can you pass me the roll of wire under the sink?'' He sighed, knowing that there was nothing to do but listen to her, ''Razor or barbed?'' ''Barbed. They'd never let me get away with razor wire... too much damage, too fast. Management gets cranky when you really start mangling people.'' ''Well, you'd know.'' With the wire in hand, she carefully wound it into the holes in the thick wood, creating a rather menacing device in more than its appearance. A barbed wire-wrapped baseball bat - much harder-hitting than a 2x4 could ever dream of being, yet so compact and lightweight. The perfect tool for a little lady's work. Sure, her day job was a little unusual... but that wouldn't stop her from loving every minute of it. Who wouldn't want to be the best at what they did? When you were as obsessive compulsive as Amy Campbell, you had a hand in absolutely everything you planned to do, every day of the week. From hardware shopping to her unlikely brand of construction and all the way to being a practical hitwoman when it came to downing a target in the wrestling ring, she'd vowed upon leaving PRIME that she'd never do anything half-assed again. That determination had earned her wins over the likes of Karina Wolfenden... ...And it would NOT fail her again. No way, no how... not so long as she had a breath in her body, regardless of how many imaginary badgers or steel chairs her opponents tossed her way.
