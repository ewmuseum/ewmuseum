Confrontation - Deranged & Disordered

24 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sept 24, 2006 Wind. Lightening. Rain. That would be the ideal backdrop to the events that were transpiring in the bedroom of Cedric Riley. But reality proves to be on a different tangent; on this night in particular, the weather was crisp and refreshing. The weather was not unlike what you'd expect in Berkeley, California. Nevertheless, our protagonist tossed and turned in his bed, battling sleep as the night dragged forward. There he lay, sprawled across the steamy mattress, his hair plastered against his face in the middle of a terrible rest. His actions seem almost pitiful. Desperate. A man at the ripe age of twenty-eight, unable to find true meaning in himself or the world around him. He was a poetic nightmare, and certainly experiencing one at that point in time. These events had become a routine he was unable to elude despite failed efforts; hope was all but lost. And all the while Cedric tossed hopelessly in his bed, a rather mysterious man sat in the corner. With his legs crossed and hands folded across his lap, he gave off a certain aura of respect. This man looked older: his worn face and graying hair gave off a fifty-ish impression, although nothing could be certain. His expression was one that could turn from a gentle smile to a spiteful glare in the blink of an eye, but at this time he didn't give off any sign of enthusiasm. His face looked rather hollow, with cold eyes and a blank stare towards Cedric Riley. As the seconds turned into minutes, it was only a matter of time before this man made a move. Keeping his expressionless manner, he raised from his chair in the corner of the room. The hardwood floor beneath him creaked under his weight. Incidentally, he looked almost malnourished, although age could easily explain his bony torso. Regardless, the creak in the floorboard scratched through the silent room as the older man crept towards the bed. As he moved closer with each step, his face turned into a grim, hostile sneer directed at the unbeknownst Cedric Riley. He rolled up the sleeves of his sweater in a greedy manner, revealing thin arms of papery skin. “You stole my life. Now I'm going to return the favor,” he announced darkly in a slow, dull tone of voice. He outstretched his arms towards his target and wrapped his thin fingers around Cedric's neck like a raven's claws on roadkill. His intent was murder by cutting off his helpless victim's oxygen. And he squeezed. He squeezed harder. He showed no sign of emotion. No sign of pity. Just the raw, hollow look of a vengeful murderer. “Prepare to die, Mr. Riley.” And then he was gone. I woke abruptly, sitting up from my soaked bedding with rapid successions of breath. My face was swamped with sweat; beads of the slimy body waste crawled down my face, neck, and upper torso. I looked around me out of instinct at my empty bedroom in Berkeley, California, readying my hands for combat. I spotted no abnormal activity, and no special guests lurked in the shadowy corners despite my best effort at finding one. I used the back of my hand to wipe my sticky hair away from my forehead, then kicked my comforter off the bed and got off. It was four o'clock in the morning, but time had become less important to me by then. Recollections of my nightmare cycled through my brain. I tried to keep the vivid details in my mind but it was like holding water in cupped hands – they trickled away as I tried to remember what had occurred. All that came to me was an old man trying to kill me, but I felt that I was missing something vital. I decided to give up and walked out of my room and into the kitchen, where I filled a glass full of water and drank it quickly. I refilled the cup and finished it just as fast as the first. The third cup I would make last. Sitting at the kitchen table, I couldn't help but think back to my nightmare. It felt different than the random insanity I had been experiencing over the past few weeks. Something about it made me seriously consider the events portrayed and the people involved. I was lacking any remembrance partly because it was a rather short endeavor into the corners of my imagination, but it made me think. It made me think long and hard. But regardless of how much I strained to put the facts together and form a theory, nothing was clicking. Not yet, anyway. My cellular buzzed in front of me. I flashed my eyes towards it; I didn't notice it there before. Besides, who calls at four o'clock in the morning? I opened it up and brought it to my ear. “Hello?” I greeted tiredly, stifling a yawn. “Hi,” returned a sweet voice from the other end of the line. I recognized it easily and smiled – it was Natalie. “Hey sweetheart,” I said joyfully, emptying the depressing thoughts from my mind. “How are you?” “I'm fine. I didn't expect you to answer so late ... I was going to leave a message.” “Don't worry about it, I was up anyway,” I said as I got up from my chair and slowly paced towards the window. “Well, in that case ...” she began, “When is your flight to London?” “Actually, about that ... Well, the match I'm in requires me to take a later flight that will only arrive that day,” I explained. “Oh. I just thought ...” “I know, I know ... it's the perfect opportunity to see each other ... but it just isn't going to work out,” I ended slowly. “That's fine. I'll see you when you get here ... Bye, Ced.” “Bye Nat,” I said, then added, “I love -” The other end of the line went dead. I closed my phone and set it on the table, adding a fitting sigh. The abrupt end to that conversation was the exclamation point on a truly awful night. Over the next few nights I was haunted by the same nightmare that ran like an old, repetitious horror film. On the third night, however, I experienced something different. The nightmare started the same: the old man sat in the corner, as creepy as ever, and made his move in the same manner that I was accustom to. This time around, in the dream, I awoke before he had the chance to grip my neck. After a good look at his face, I was aroused to reality. Sitting up in my bed, I breathed heavily and felt the sweat ooze down my back. But that image of the man's face – his pale, lifeless features – they blinded any vision I could hope for. And I could put a name to his face. 2 days later Pacing the hallway of an old apartment building in downtown Minot, North Dakota, I couldn't help but feel anxious. It was rather chilly in the bordering state, enough to put me into shivers in a thin zip-up sweater. Perhaps I had become grateful of the Californian climate and lost my Canadian-grown adaptation to cold weather, but my body temperature was the least of my worries. I ceased pacing and stared at the door in front of me: number twenty-one. I checked my watch nervously – 10:00 PM. My flight had only touched down a short two hours ago, although I had planned my route and destination priorly. Once inside, I had a general idea of what I was going to say. Rather, what I needed to say. It would be difficult, that was decided. Preparing something on the flight in had proven to be fruitless; my intent was to give morose news to someone uniformed. I felt like I had the job of a Doctor, walking into the waiting room and telling a mother that her son just died, but in a much more devastating manner. I was the Doctor who fucked up and killed the patient. And no, this isn't an episode of House. I approached the door and knocked softly. I felt the urge to walk away at that moment, but the words of Lucas Emerson held me in my position, with my hands pocketed while shifting my weight nervously. Somebody moved inside. I perked my attention. With each step came another pang of guilt, and another urge to say “fuck confrontation” and bolt like the coward I was becoming. There was a pause inside as the resident observed me from the peep-hole. I faked a smile. I shouldn't have. The door opened. In front of me was a woman of about twenty five years. She had long brown hair, deep green eyes and was very attractive. She acted as if she wasn't uncomfortable, but she knew different. “Hi ...” she began, as if trying to put a name to my face. I hesitated, then asked, “Can I come in?” She looked at me curiously. Deciding that I seemed trusting enough to allow in, she stepped back and opened the door. “Sure.” This was not a normal reaction. For a young lady to allow a stranger into her house was surprising – however, she came off as the type who would spend long days volunteering for shelter homes and cook shacks. I stepped inside and she closed the door behind me. The apartment in front of me was warm and inviting. A comfortable arrangement of couches to my right begged to be relaxed on; the kitchen to my left was clean and on the table was a tea-set; and in front of me was a fireplace with surrounding furniture - the perfect place to curl up to a novel. The apartment was set up in a very cozy manner, but what attracted me most was the picture that stared back at me from atop the fireplace. Francis Edward. Francis Edward: the old man trying to murder me in my nightmares; the man from Mead, Colorado who I had spent an entire day forcing information out of the CIA about prior to today; the man who mysteriously disappeared from his small hometown in 2004; the father of the young adult that had just invited me, a stranger, into her apartment without anticipating the devastating information I planned to give to her; the innocent tradesman who enjoyed fishing in the mountains in his spare time. Francis Edward: the man that I had killed. I stared long and hard at the picture. Flashbacks of the night that I had murdered crossed my mind. I winced. “Is something wrong?” the woman behind me asked. I swiveled around. “No ... nothing,” I lied. She had no idea. “You look cold ... Would you like a cup of tea?” she asked. I was almost taken aback by her kindness, which added to the surmounting guilt I felt at the moment. “No ... I'm fine thanks,” I lied again, trying not to return the kindness. That was the only way I could keep the ball in my court. However, in this case, there was no court. There was no mood I could set to forewarn this innocent young lady of the devastating news I was about to deliver. I turned back towards her, and flashed my eyes to the living room, where I anticipated another picture of this young lady and her father. I was right – there they were, father and daughter: smiles across their faces in a frame that sat on top of the entertainment unit. “There's something I came to tell you,” I said finally, avoiding eye contact as I had paced awkwardly around the apartment. She frowned, getting a negative vibe. I didn't blame her. “What is it?” she asked. “It's about your father.” Silence consumed the room. I kept my eyes from connecting with hers, but I knew her stare was locked on me. For a moment, time seemed to stop, and every second was critical in determining my next statement. Or hers. I couldn't decide on words. I hesitated, stuffing my hands in my pockets as horrible memories flashed in front of me. I risked catching her eye – her blank stare was intimidating. She meant business. “What do you know about my father.” It was a firm statement. Grammatically it was a question, but she spoke it with such strength and demand that it was a blatant statement. “He ..” I began, but paused. “WHAT IS IT?!” she screamed, breaking her blank expression and looking like she was on the verge of a nervous breakdown. Her attractive face was gone in the snap of fingers; I didn't dare catching her eye more often than we necessary. “TELL ME! ... Or I'll .. I'll ...” “YOUR FATHER DIDN'T KILL HIMSELF!” I yelled back finally. Time slowed down to a crawl again. My announcement left the room in an silence, and the tension could be cut with a knife. She dropped down to her knees. At first, I couldn't decide what her reaction was; she knew he was dead, but maybe, just maybe, she thought that her father showed more strength in his death than a suicide. Her hair swung in front of her eyes, masking her expression. “He was murdered,” I said slowly and softly, breaking the silence. “By me.” She dropped to her hands. On all fours, she looked like a hopeless animal. Her sobs of shock and devastation were emitted in such a whimper it was unarguably effective. The resurfacing death of her father from two years before was too much to handle all in one earful, and she showed it. I couldn't handle it. Like the coward I was, I walked slowly out of the apartment without another word. I closed the door behind me and walked briskly to the outdoors. I got outside and walked. I walked, and kept walking. I walked for what seemed like hours, but was really only fifteen minutes. I didn't have a destination, I kept walking ... walking away from what had possibly been the worst confrontation in my entire life. I got to a park and stumbled in through the gates as if all life had been drained of me. I felt like a monster. I dropped to my hands and knees near a collection of bushes and vomited. I remained there, breathing heavily for a good hour. I was clearly losing the mental strength that I had spent oh-so long fine-tuning during my time in the CIA; I was formerly under the impression it was never to leave me. The confrontation with Edward Francis' daughter had broken me; it left me in the form of a pathetic weakling. My emotions were uncontrolled. Everything material that I touched felt cold, just like my insides. I was a cold son of a bitch with the emotional power of a teenage girl. The CIA agent and professional wrestler in me had momentarily evaporated, and all that was left was a broken man with no name, a black heart and a hollow face. I didn't know what time it was when I made it to the park, nor did I know what time it was when I shook myself into sleep, but I managed to somehow. Navigating a car down a winding highway of the mountains of Colorado, my route strictly followed a vehicle that was distantly ahead of me. The license plate was permanently embedded in the front of my mind; I could recognize the 4x4 I followed from a distance with ease. It would take more than a brand new paint job to detract my keen eye for this automobile. I anticipated the turns it would make, the speeds it went, and whether or not it was going to slow down for a curve in the road or not. I noticed that it drifted to the right more often than to the left. Most importantly, I noticed the odd time it would brake on a straightaway when it approached a right hand turn. This made it easy to find it if I ever lost it; this vehicle was waiting to turn right, and bound to do so some time down the road. The destination was obvious; this man was looking for a turn to a creek or stream of some sort. I knew because I had watched him for weeks – his main hobby was fishing, and I observed him load his tackle box and outdoor gear into the back of his Jeep Ranger the day before his departure. I checked my watch, then took a brief look in the rear view. Taking chances wasn't an option - I couldn't be followed. My vehicle was non-existent to this stretch of highway, and this area in general, and for good reason. There was nothing holding me back; nothing to stop me in my bid to follow the Jeep until the end of the world if I had to. This old man was bound to turn sooner or later, though. His age suggested that going away from home was a good escape, but at the same time, going to far away would suggest youthful adventure. This oldie was mature and intelligent, he knew better than to go outside his element. But for every bit of knowledge he boasted I showed five times more. His familiarity with the nature was nothing compared to mine. One could say that I was out of my element, but that made me scoff. I was never out of my element. The world was my element, and I would take advantage of it to my will. I wasn't intimidated by the towering mountains that surrounded me; I invited their presence as a challenge. I invited every curve in the road, every rise and depression in the landscape as a test to my ability to anticipate where the vehicle in front of me was headed. Every time it came into view I smiled; I knew it was in my range. I knew it couldn't escape my range. There was another curve in the distance that the Jeep headed into. It disappeared behind the block of trees on the side of the mountain. I maintained my speed, consistent to the mile per hour, and eventually emerged on the other corner of the mountain. In the distance was a rising hill, and no vehicle in sight in front of me. I smiled again. I had him. Based on our speeds, and the distance from my vehicle to the hill, he still should have been in my sights. I slowed down to a stalking speed just before the first right turn. It was more of a trail, so I expected it to be short, but I wouldn't risk pulling over and parking my vehicle just yet. I turned onto the path and crawled down it, keeping my eyes peeled for any sight of a stream or running water. Finally, to my right, among the dense trees I spotted a bank. I knew this trail could potentially follow the stream for a long, long ways, but if I knew the man I pursued at all, it was that he would stay as close to the road as possible. As I came to a stop on the trail I angled my car so that it would completely block off the path and prevent any other potential fisherman from disturbing my cause. I got out of the car swiftly and closed the door silently, but not before pressing a button on my pager. This would ensure my pick-up. Dressed from head to toe in camouflage with a thin sweater and mesh pants, I withdrew my Silence and moved into the dense trees and stalked towards the stream. I moved briskly but quietly. The noise was minimal; disrupting the habitat at all would, to me, be a failure. Movement was an art. To remain invisible was the ultimate goal; invisible especially to the people around you, and trying to fool even the animals. Some senses that human have are feeble compared to those of animals, and therefore, it was a challenge to fool the creatures. I remained at a safe distance from the stream of water but also equidistant from the trail. I was satisfied with my choice of path, and continued to move towards my target. There was his car, parked just outside a small opening to the stream. And there he was, Francis Edward, standing on the edge of the stream dressed in fisherman's gear, right in the open, waiting for the line on his rod to tighten. I crouched among the trees and moved into a vantage point – there was no possible way he could see me. My body blended almost flawlessly into the backdrop. This man in my sights had a long, secretive history that didn't sit well with the CIA. I failed to absorb the entire story, but he had been involved in a highly-publicized murder of an FBI agent almost a decade and a half ago, among other highly illegal organized crime. At the time, he had evaded the FBI's valiant attempts at capturing him. That was his forte – he knew how to escape. Once he had done that, he had been smuggled into Mexico and had the hook-ups to gain a new identity. The whole process was said to have taken years, which would make sense. In 2004, the case had resurfaced, this time under the watch of the CIA due to some new information gained from a suspect who was in a spot of trouble in a different case. This suspect had offered his information on the old case in order to decrease his sentence – that's where I came in. Needless to say, the American government didn't want this to hit the press. By that time it had blown over and was buried in the past, and bringing it back to life would only put strain on the FBI and, in turn, the government. I steadied my gun, the only sign of life coming from my position was my short and limited breaths. The man in my sights had returned to America under a different name and a different face. My bosses assumed that his return was because of his daughter, who didn't know of the circumstances. All I was to do was carry out the mission. However, the higher ups weren't holding the gun. The power that be weren't taking the action to rid this legendary murderer from American soil. Ping. My silencer rang this short sound as the bullet shot from the barrel. Like threading a needle it went straight through a series of trees and was planted to the side of Edwards' head, a spot that I needed to hit. He was dead on the spot. I moved towards him quickly. I carefully picked up his rod with my gloved hands and packed it back in his tackle-box. I dropped my gun to a specific spot beside the fallen fisherman to put the blame on him. After a quick survey of the area, I was satisfied that the scene pointed directly to a suicide - secluded area, bullet to side of the head, no noticeable footprints due to my “special” shoes. No sign that he came here to fish despite his gear, which could have been a mislead for his loved ones. I picked the tackle-box off the ground and moved back down the trail to my car. I threw the box in the back, grabbed the canister of fuel that waited for me, took it out and closed the door. I doused the vehicle, and touched a lit match to it, and threw the canister on top. It erupted into flames. The area around it was too wet from a recent rain for any risk of spread. I walked back to the road, checking my watch. I arrived at the highway and checked it again. A jet black car slowed to a stop in front of me, which I had paged before going in for the kill. I jumped in. Mission accomplished. I awoke abruptly, face down in patch of dirt, shivering in the cold. At first I was unsure of where I was. I got to my feet and wiped the dirt off of the front of my sweater, then headed for the park gateway. I had no idea of the time. It was still dark ... it could have been four o'clock, three o'clock ... my guess was as good as the bum across the street. This reminded me of my match that was in less than two days – incidentally against a bum. I thought back to my dream. It was very real, and for good reason, because such events had actually occurred. I came to a bus stop and decided to go inside for no particular reason. I sat down on the uncomfortable bench and leaned against the glass window. Had that dream have continued past where I had awoken, the circle would be complete. See, after the mission had been executed, I found out that Francis Edward was the wrong guy. I thought back to what Lucas's advice of confronting my skeletons. There was a reason that I listened to him, something that I couldn't quite put my finger on. Sitting in that bus stop in the middle of the night, I wasn't so sure if what I had done was for better or for worst.
