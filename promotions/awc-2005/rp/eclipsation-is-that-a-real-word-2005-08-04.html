Eclipsation... Is that a real word?

4 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 4th 2005 A mentor… Have you got one? Michael Fitzpatrick-McCarthy, better known as AWC’s The Farmer does. His mentor and trainer is Chris Metal, of PCW “fame.” A literal translation of mentor is advisor. Someone that is there to advise you on all decisions, big or small, drawing on their own experiences to do so. But to many that have them, mentors mean so much more. For most, mentors are seen as a point of achievement. Mentors are seen as a level to be reached, achievement’s to be equalled and even, where possible eclipsed. But for Mickey Fitz, he is slowly beginning to realise that his mentor isn’t that great. Although Chris Metal will claim otherwise, the former PCWer never won any titles. He claims to have won some junior heavyweight title, but he has never won anything of worth. Next Tuesday, the 9th of August, The Farmer will compete for the Relentless Championship. So, in less than a week, The Farmer will have outclassed his mentor. So a career that will have only spanned four matches after the title match, the young Irishman could eclipse anything that Chris Metal has achieved. He couldn’t understand either. Sitting in a small Paris gym, sweat was rolling off the brow of The Farmer, he looked around. In his left arm was a small weight as he raised it up and down. His grey t-shirt was sweat stained, front and back but it was okay. They had booked out the whole place. As The Farmer was working his arms, Chris Metal was giving him a peep talk in preparation for Tuesday. And once again he was going on about being screwed out of the PCW Rising Star Title. C’mon, think about it? Who actually had a problem winning the Rising Star belt? Everyone that entered that fed won it. And if they didn’t, they were either destined for bigger things, a tag team or Pierre. Once more, Mickey Fitz was being forced to pay attention as Chris Metal lamented about his days of competing. Why he bothered Mickey never understood? He had heard the story every week they had trained. Each time, Chris somehow managed to twist the idea of the match to include a screw-job. It was as though no one in the world had been screwed except Chris Metal… Poor, poor Chris Metal. Again, Mickey’s mind drifted out of the Paris gym and to other things, centring on Chris Metal. “If I win on Tuesday,” thought Mickey, “not only will I have eclipsed his career, I won’t need him…” Mickey turned his face, to watch the Metal speech. The sound was mute, as Mickey looked forward the moment that he could fire Chris Metal. Chris Metal was a terrible coach. Plain and simple he sucked. The Farmer would rather be a creation of James von Doom than remain a student of Chris Metal any longer. In preparation for the Spanish Bullwhip Match, Chris chased The Farmer around with the Bullwhip, striking him down rather than teaching him to use the Whip to do damage. That was the reason it took him so long to put Kris Krimzon away. In the build-up to the match Amy Silveira, Chris Metal taught him a few moves. Something that Mickey had employed Chris to do. But in doing so, Chris had paid special consideration to the moves that would help him “get that little something extra.” They were the moves that helped The Farmer have a quick grope of the splendid figure of the young Miss Silveira, hence why he lost. And even though Chris Metal hadn’t helped him in preparation for his opening match against Drake Coleman and Gabriel, he could imagine how the training session would have gone. Mickey pictured Chris teaching him to wrestle with his eyes closed so that he wouldn’t have to look at the disfigured face of Drake Coleman. Chris Metal sucked as a coach. Even though a complete rookie, Mickey knew that not only did Chris Metal’s training methods not work, but he really had no clue how to any moves except his own move set, which is… well, basic. And then Mickey began to question Keith Chamberlain in all of this. Keith Chamberlain was the chaperon appointed to look after Michael Fitzpatrick-McCarthy when he had first arrived in the Atlantic Wrestling Club. Keith and Mickey had got along famously and even though Keith had been assigned to the secret newcomers to AWC, the pair talked regularly on the phone. But why had Keith introduced The Farmer to Chris Metal? Chris Metal is a joke. Surely one of AWC’s most respected chaperon’s would realise that Metal’s training techniques were about as useful as wet paper. Surely Keith knew enough about actual wrestling to know that Chris Metal was a poor coach. Or maybe Keith owed Chris Metal something. Or maybe Chris Metal knew something that Keith didn’t want the world to know. “Bro, are you listening to me?” Mickey was snapped out of his thoughts as Chris Metal stood with his hands on his hips, Prima-Donna style. Mickey, who had been unconsciously working the weight, looked with a grin and a nod. “Am I going to have to start again?” A tone of stress was the underlying current in Chris Metal’s tone as he spoke to his protégé. He was either pissed off that Mickey wasn’t paying attention or there was something greater, something bigger. Mickey shook his head. “Nah, it’s ‘right,” he shrugged, still working the weight in his left hand. Chris shrugged and paced off. His training t-shirt did not posses the sweat stains that the t-shirt of the Irishman was drenched in. “I’m hitting the showers,” snapped Coach Metal, as he shoved open the changing room door. He stopped over the threshold of the door and turned back to face Mickey. “Finish up with that weight, and then 10k on the treadmill,” he commanded. Mickey nodded, knowing that there were only a few more days of listening to him and then it was over. As he raised the weight one more time, The Farmer finished and releasing his grip a little placed the weight back in the groves that held it to the wall. Shaking out his arm, Mickey’s mind was still dwelling on the relationship between Keith and Chris. Surely, Keith hadn’t honestly recommended Chris Metal as a coach to The Farmer. Mickey’s mind tried to replay the dinner the three had had in San Jose. His pace started out slow on the treadmill as he tried to think of any indication of their relationship. The dinner was uneventful. Mickey was charming. Keith was helpful and Chris was Chris. Nope. He couldn’t remember any cause for concern. But soon it wouldn’t matter. Soon he would have eclipsed the accomplishments of Chris Metal. In just a few days, he would be Relentless Champion. In under a week, he would be by himself. A grin grew across his face, as he began to build his speed. Eire Og would be filled with Champions. And Chris Metal would be gone. But no matter what happened... It felt good to be going home.
