From There To Here Part 1 (Nightmares)

2 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It had been two weeks since Louise and John had decided to go their separate ways. Their lives torn apart by a new life forming in the womb of Louise. When did things ever get so bad? First came the shouting. The type of argument where it didn’t matter what the other was saying, it was more of a vent, a release. There was no possible way that anyone would even win the argument, that wasn’t the point of it. Then of course came the silence. Total and utter silence. If you have ever been in a room with somewhere, where not a single word has been said, you can begin to understand how bad this can be. You become aware of absolutely everything, the traffic on the road, the clicking of a kettle, regular things that you hear, but blot out, as they are around you everyday, then you notice the stranger things. Like a high-pitched distant hum, which accompanies electricity, your heartbeat suddenly becomes the single, loudest thing on the planet. With no way to stop it being so but talk. And yet, to look across the room, into the eyes of someone you once loved, someone you would give your life for at the drop of the hat. To look into their eyes, which appear to pierce your very soul, looking for an apology, some tender words, hell even another argument, but finding nothing. That’s the hardest thing of all. It had remained like that for about a week. Every time John felt something rising in me, the confidence to just tell her what I was thinking, to say that everything would be ok and that we could work through it, his eyes fell upon her stomach and the hate just flooded back, returning him once again to silence. Nothing was even said when she left. John had watched her silently from across the room, as her clothes and other worldly possessions were thrust into a suitcase, occasionally she would look up with pleading eyes. It was clear that she didn’t want to go, that this was wrenching at her heart as much as it was John. Either too proud or too stupid to say anything. It was not until she had left the house, her suitcase rolling silently behind her, before rattling along the cobbled footpath, away from his life for the unforeseeable future. That was when the nightmares began It wasn’t the first time he had, had them. Nightmares have a habit of affecting an individual a lot deeper than you first might think. Deep seated fears and worries, which only come to light when a certain situation arose. You see, nightmares harbour your deepest darkest fears, things that you would never dare talk about. Recurring nightmares offered something else though. Some believe that our dreams, our thoughts, are a glimpse into the future. John had suffered from this nightmare before, the visions, which tortured his mind for months after the death of his brother. It was then that Louise had saved him, he had reached the lowest of the low, unemployed, depressed, he had considered his options and death seemed like the most sensible approach, the best course of action. This time however the nightmares had changed. Changed to the extent where he did not want to sleep, he couldn’t, the images far too ghastly, worrying to begin to comprehend. It was unlike anything he had ever seen before, which had in time led to his insomnia. It was once said that when you suffer from insomnia, you’re never really awake, yet, never really asleep. Living in a state of consciousness where time itself seeps through you, it holds no power over you as hour upon hour is gained or lost. When all of this was put together, it had begun to affect John’s work. His training was now next to non-existent. His diet had fallen from a strictly regimented regime to finding whatever the hell was in the cupboards. Then there was the drinking, the sweet form of escapism that anything with an ABV over 40% offered. In the relative comfort of his own home this was somewhat acceptable. When he was drinking before after and during work, that’s when real problems arose. Revolution 71 had not gone to plan. John had always intended to name his newly acquired Alias title, that was always going to happen no matter what. What he hadn’t planned on was the anger that had come out during it. It’s amazing what half a bottle of vodka and a rapidly failing marriage will do for a bit of rage. Some of the other workers backstage had noticed that John had seemed a bit ‘off’, he wasn’t his usual chirpy, self, choosing to spend time alone as opposed to coercing. Eventually it was Blaine Blair who had found him slumped against the wall, humming loudly to himself, his eyes rolling around his sockets. Blaine hadn’t had a good night of it either, having to deal with the drunken figures of Deville and Nova on camera; he now had to deal with a drunken champion. Maybe it was the stresses of PRIME, that led previously levelled out men to drink, not so much PRIME itself, but the demands that it thrust upon a lifestyle, whatever way you looked at it, PRIME was the big time. There was no time for mistakes, whatever happened would only happen the once, and there would be no chance to do it again. “Are you drunk John? Christ not another one!” Blaine’s words were thrust from his throat with a mixture of worry and frustration, he was supposed to look after the wrestlers, yet he always seemed to pick up the flack, a typical, wrong place at the wrong time sort of guy. John didn’t even bother to reply and pulled himself up using Blaine, nearly falling face first into the wall opposite him, his plain white t-shirt, stained by a mixture of liquids, some of which gave off a pungent smell, closely related to the nauseating smell of vomit. This was not acceptable, especially not when he was scheduled to wrestle that night. On the advice of Blaine, John had gone to speak to Hin-See, to ask to be excused. Hin-See however, was not in the best of moods, no one quite knew why either, not the whole story anyway. There were rumblings, rumours going around backstage, but no one knew the whole story, the only thing to be certain of was, Taun Pham hadn’t been seen which was rare and Hin-See was pissed off, majorly. Nearly biting the head off Karina Wolfenden and flat out refusing John’s request to be excused. Stepping into the ring, then out again. Even this proved to be a challenge for John, but it was what he did. As he crept backstage, collected his Omega Championship and left the building. He was later informed that Ellis Easton had won the battle royal, this just made him sneer, a man whom he had made a mockery of was suddenly thrust to the main stage, where as he would be fodder for the new batch of recruits. He had made a claim to be a fighting champion that every week a defence would be made, in order to return some prosperity to the Omega/Alias title. Yet even in his drunken state, he knew that his mouth was writing cheques his body couldn’t cash. Though no one in PRIME knew it John’s condition had been deteriorating, somewhat rapidly since joining PRIME. His years of the extreme high life had taken its toll. Has back was brittle, his neck, hanging by sinew. Every time he had a medical the doctor always said the same thing. “If you land wrongly, you could die.” That was part of the fun though, part of the danger. Mr 50/50 a nickname given to him in his younger years, he’d leap off anything onto anyone, all to hear the crowds reaction. Now though a ten minute television match would leave his sore for days and with a wave of new guys eager to make their mark, he knew the game would have to be stepped up. Lasting impressions would need to be made. One final piece of information had been handed to John as he left the arena, a tentative card for the trip to Salt Lake City. Apparently it had been announced on the card, his drunkenness shielding him from the news. If he had heard there and then he would have tried to put a stop to it. After all, this wasn’t just any match. It was the first defence of the Omega Championship and boy, was it a challenge. John had turned towards the information guy; lets call him Frank to question the validity of the piece of paper in his hand. Surely there must have been a misprint, or someone’s idea of a very bad practical joke. After all his questions and qualms however, it seemed that the information he had been given was indeed correct. At Revolution 72 John Edwards would be squaring off against the Negasonic Lupine. Former Universal Champion. Karina Wolfenden. Which nicely leads us nicely up to now. John was walking through his house. Everything around him seemed distant and blurred, as though this wasn’t real somehow. The world around him twisted and turned as he continued his journey, eventually reaching the kitchen. Not a sound could be heard within the room, not even the dull hum that often accompanies electricity, just the blissful silence that becomes to feel uneasy. You can feel your heart beat quicken as your surroundings and awareness become heightened You know that something isn’t right here. Stepping further into the kitchen, towards the stove in the far corner of the room is a task, with every step it feels as though the floor itself is moving, slipping and disintegrating under foot. Upon reaching the stove he turns on the gas, igniting it with a soft blue flame which flickers and dances, despite there being no wind. It was at that point you know that someone is behind you, or something at least. Yet the fear stops him from turning around. When he entered the room he was alone, yet now he can feel the presence of someone else, directly behind him. Too scared to turn around, for fear of what he may see. A deep breath blows lightly against John’s neck, not sinister, just enough to let him know that it’s there, whatever it maybe. That he is not alone. The flame on the stove had blown out of its own accord; no amount of trying will put it back on. He can now feel the beads of sweat forming on his forehead, dripping independently onto the floor, which has taken on a substance like jelly. This was the part of the dream he hated the most, but it always ended here, all he had to do was turn around and it would be over. But the face he saw would always stay with him, even when he woke up, for a few brief moments it would stay in his eyes. He allowed himself to turn around, to face what he had faced countless times before. His brother staring back at him, his face melting away as he screamed in agony, till there was nothing left but scar tissue and bone, flames licking wildly out of the now empty eye-sockets. Instead however he was faced with a small figure crawling towards him. He allowed his eyes to gaze upon this creature, its legs and arms seemingly moving independently of his own body. It was the naked body of a child, is head looking towards the ground, its eyes fixed a few feet in front of it, the effort clear on its face, with every movement, every inch forwards, until it stops dead in its tracks and looks up at John. Cold lifeless eyes. Slowly its lips part, a row of gum, no teeth. ”Daddy…” Bolting up in his bed John gasps for breath. The flickering streetlight outside his bedroom window indicates that it’s still nighttime. Sat in silence John feels the beads of sweat trickle down his face, his sheets are sodden as the sweat has seeped out from his back onto them, or at least he hoped it was sweat. He ran his hand lightly through his head, his fingers drenched in sweat as he did so. His eyes never falling away from the streetlight, it was a focus point, something to keep him grounded. In months of nightmares it had only started to change recently. The small child that tormented him in his subconscious now, he knew that deep down it was only deep-seated worries about Louise and the child being created in her. He didn’t know if it was his, he didn’t want to know, he had convinced himself that he was right. That was all that mattered in his head. He allowed his feet to find the floor, the soft carpet engulfing his foot as he pulled himself slowly to a standing position. His first few steps were tentative, as most peoples are when they first wake up, to John however it hadn’t felt like he had slept. Walking blindly down the corridor he entered the bathroom, despite the darkness, he had walked the same walk countless times, he knew it like the back of his hand. The cold water splashed over John’s face, trickling down the scar underneath his right eye. The tap continued to trickle water down into the plughole, as John cupped his hands, gaining a small amount of water in his hands, splashing his face once again. He raised his head silently to observe himself in the mirror, bloodshot eyes stare back at him as his stomach twists and turns inside him. Without even looking at the time he knew that it was about four in the morning, it was the same every night. Beads of water dripped down from his chin onto his chest as he leaned backwards, clicking his back into position, letting out a content sigh as he did so. He was finding it difficult to not think about Louise, he had to lay off the booze or he would be fired that had been made very clear to him. Plus only a fool would go into the ring with Wolfenden half cut. He didn’t know why he had been given Karina in his first title defense, a match with Karina meant heavy bumps, essentially pain for the next fortnight, it also meant watching your back. Despite the fans constant applaud, despite the championships she may have claimed, she was still green in the ring. Much like John in his early days. For Karina it was all about the adrenaline, the thrill. She didn’t care for the business, it was simply something to feed her urges, she was a junkie. This could have been a punishment for both of them, both had crossed Hin-See last week; maybe this was an act of vengeance for him. Truth be told he didn’t expect to retain at Revolution, but he would do what he could. Others seemed to have everything go right for them in PRIME, yet every time he arrived at whichever arena was playing host to PRIME that week, he felt alone. Happily content to sit on his own until he was scheduled, but part of him wanted to be approached, he wanted to get into the fun and games backstage, he wanted to be included in poker games with everyone else, he didn’t want to be looked at as the loner, despite him being one. He made his way out of the bathroom, working his way through the darkness downstairs to his living room. A leather armchair faced opposite a wide screen television, a beer cooler next to the chair. Allowing himself to fall into the chair he flicked on the television, more for a source of background noise than anything else. Without thinking he allowed his hand to fall to the beer cooler, pulling out a can of beer. As he tears open the can he pauses for a moment. In a brief moment of clarity, John looks at the can and shakes his head, placing it carefully back in the cooler. A small smile on his face. There would be time for beer and that would be after Revolution, because win or lose, he’d certainly need it. Until then though, he would have to be clean. He would have to face this test sober as a judge. Even if that meant yet another sleepless night, with only trash on television for company As the flickering of infomercials, bombarded his senses he slowly felt himself drifting to sleep, he didn’t know how long this would last, but any sleep was a blessing. His last thought was of Louise, his wife and where she was, what exactly she was doing. Wondering if she was as lonely as him. Elsewhere, in a small flat, the John’s estranged wife, Louise is sat on the edge of her bed. The sort of bed that would stab you in the middle of the night, loose bed springs that rattle and creak with every single movement. Now though she was sat silently, her hands cradling her stomach a grimace on her face. The flat door was wide open, a cell phone placed on the bed next to her. An open sports bag rested at her feet, containing a number of nightgowns, she knew that something was wrong and that she was going somewhere. The pain in her stomach had begun about an hour ago, a little twinge at first, the sort that you may get from a bad burrito or eating too much in one sitting. However as time had passed, the pain had grown, stabbing pains in her stomach, there was one thought running around her head constantly, a thought which she tried to dismiss, but every bolt of pain made it real, she couldn’t deny it any longer. The baby was coming. 3 months early and the baby was coming, this wasn’t a stomach upset, these were contractions, but something else was wrong, the intense burning in her stomach told her that. She’d called for an ambulance, but now the pain was so intense she had no idea how she’d even get to the vehicle, every movement led to pain shooting all the way through her body, every single inch of her body burned. Falling backwards onto the bed she let out an ear-piercing scream. Reminiscent of the scene from Alien she clasps her stomach, the pain is immense, she can feel herself blacking out, as her eyes slowly begin to flicker, losing consciousness and awareness of the world around her. She manages to murmur a single word. “John…” With that her eyes roll backwards as she lies on the bed. An escape from the pain. A few moments later the paramedics rushed into the flat and saw Louise unconscious on the bed, what they noticed first though was the blood that had begun to seep through her trousers from her crotch. “We need to get her to hospital NOW”
