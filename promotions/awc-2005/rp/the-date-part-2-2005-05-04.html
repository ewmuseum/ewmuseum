The Date, Part 2

4 May 2005

Date: May 4th, 2005 The Night of “The Incident” Inside Alex’s SUV, Nighttime Bored with his interview, Alex Strider took it upon himself to take Pamela Saunders, reporter for New Man’s Athletics magazine, to his favorite downtown Pittsburgh Dance Club. He knew she would feel out of place there, it was clearly not her scene, but he didn’t care. In fact, he rather enjoyed the thought of mousey, unassuming Pamela following him around at his heel while he enjoyed the scene while dressed in his finest clothes. When he was in “Frais Viande”, Alex ruled the roost. And he looked the part. Dressed up in the highest fashion, Strider loved to make public appearances in his hometown. He loved to hate the masses that would drunkenly gawk and clap and cheer for him when he’d arrive at whatever the latest hot-spot in town was. Even if they didn’t know he was an established wrestling star, they knew he was the biggest deal they’d see that night. As if Pamela didn’t already feel out of place, Alex insisted that he drive her in his brand new Cadillac Escalade EXT. This was the best way to get noticed, Alex knew. To be the best, you had to live like the best, and of course, that meant letting everyone know you were living the best life. He casually mentioned how he had spent more money on his Escalade EXT to get the White Diamond exterior. He optioned for the Entertainment package with the overhead DVD player with infrared headphones. He decided to treat himself to the Select Edition Package, because it was only an extra five thousand dollars. That bought him such useful amenities as the sunroof, in-dash GPS system, and seven spoke chrome hubcaps. He’s still thinking about upgrading the hubcaps to platinum spinners, though. It was natural for him to get the matching Cadillac Leather Bomber by Danier. Of course, it was a steal at four hundred dollars. Pamela, of course, did her best to act interested as she listened to Alex coolly give his grocery list of extras and gadgets that he purchased because it clearly meant a lot to him. This level of attentiveness came easily to her, compliments of wanting to keep her fiancé, Kevin, happy. Pamela was finding that most men love talking about things that they find interesting that most everyone else could care less about. She made a mental note on a possible article to be written later: “Men’s Obsession with Themselves: Why do they force it on unwilling women and why do we put up with it? “ A second later she put it out of her mind, because she knew it wasn’t what her editors were looking for. “So, while I’m really excited over the idea of you showing me your favorite places around town, Alex,” she said, calmly trying to butter him up, “I was really hoping I might be able to dig a little deeper. I have some questions that some other wrestlers haven’t really wanted to talk about.” Pamela asked, trying to get back onto track. “And what kind of questions might those be?” Alex asked. “How much do you know about the future of sports stars? Like, what pro-football players get into after their sports career is over.” “I think a lot of them make lucrative careers owning car dealerships,” Alex surmised. “O.k., but what about the ones that can’t afford to buy dealerships? What do you think happens to them?” ”I don’t know. They sell life-insurance or save lost kittens stuck in trees full-time. Am I close?” Alex sarcastically answered.” ”According to a study performed by the Department of Health and Human Services in 1994, professional football players actually have a decreased mortality rate than the general populace…” ”Mortality rate? I thought we were talking about what careers they move into.” Alex said, glancing at Pamela while he maneuvered his Escalade through the downtown Pittsburgh traffic. “This is all something I think you should be concerned with. Just let me finish. The mortality rate for offensive and defensive linemen is increased by fifty percent, give or take a few percent. Now, as you may already assume, these are larger men, so they already had an increased risk of heart failure and heart disease…” “O.k. I think I see where you’re coming from. So you are doing a similar survey of professional wrestlers and their untimely deaths?” “I just wonder, do you think that professional wrestlers would succumb to the same problems as linemen? Now, in a lot of these cases I was looking at the men in question were found to be heavy steroid users…” Pamela tried showing Alex the draft sheet she had brought with her, but he waved her off with a brisk motion. Alex didn’t take his eyes off the road as he answered. ”So they got what they deserved then. Look, I don’t really know what kind of opinions I can give you on this subject other than the obvious. If pro-wrestlers die younger lives than anyone else, they most likely did something to deserve it, right? Like, steroids, for example. If they can’t build their muscle the right way, they’re going to blow their quads out from under them in the middle of a match. I guarantee it. I have no sympathy for drug users of any sort.” Alex said sternly. “Very valid opinions, Alex, thank you. Now, if you don’t mind, what do you think about the stories in the news, about the wrestlers who die strange deaths?” Pamela asked, trying to take the conversation down another road. “Define strange.” Alex asked dubiously. “Well,” Pamela said, referring back to her notes. “There’s Scaramonga the Snake Eater from OLW. He died while handling one of his pet rattle-snakes...” “Sounds to me like that was an occupational hazard.” Strider guessed nonchalantly. “Not exactly. He was bitten while engaging in some bazaar animal sex act with the snake’s rattle.” Pamela said, as professional as a schoolteacher. “Darwin would be proud,” Alex asked, not hiding his disgust. “But that’s a freak for you. A freak suffers a freak’s death. I’m still not convinced that wrestlers die before their time. At any rate, that won’t happen to me. I’ll die an old man in my castle in a small European country that I buy after becoming AWC Transatlantic Champion. And rest assured, I will be a tyrant, ruling with an iron fist. I will be feared by all and loved by the comeliest countesses in the land.” Pamela couldn’t help but frown, not exactly sharing his passion for a rich fantasy world. She was about to ask Strider about foul play’s role in killing off pro-wrestlers before their time, but he pulled up in front of ‘Frais Viande’ “We’re here. You can put your notes away for a little bit,” Alex said, putting on his black sunglasses. “Just enjoy the atmosphere. Try to have fun.” Strider stepped out of his ridiculously oversized SUV, handing his keys off to a ready valet. Flashbulbs popped and the crowd outside waiting to get in to the exclusive nightclub screamed as they began to recognize the ‘home-town hero’. Strider smiled wide and surveyed the crowd. Pamela watched from the fringe, like any good journalist would do. Without interfering; there wasn’t too much risk of being noticed. She just observed as Strider let himself fall into his element. The entrance was alight with the pop and flash of camera bulbs, and was deafening with the screams of those who wanted to be in the company of Alex Strider, for even just one night. And it would be a very eventful night, as luck would have it. The Morning After “The Incident” Inside Alex’s Apartment; Morning Alex Strider was coming down from his LSD trip, and he couldn’t be more relieved. For twelve hours, he had gone through hell. He conversed with demons. He left a fist sized hole in his bedroom wall because it made inappropriate advances toward him. He ran for and was elected president of the United States of America. Alex found his blanket and tried to keep himself warm as he sat alone on his couch. He tried to keep his mind still. Though he stopped hallucinating and experiencing conversations with kitchen appliances and bleeding walls, he was left with a feeling of violation. Not knowing what to do or where to turn, Strider was overcome with depression. The doorbell buzzed. Alex thought about not answering it, but decided to take a dare on the off chance that he was hallucinating the whole thing. Opening the door, he met eyes with Pamela. Strider frowned. “Hey? What kind of hello is that?” He shut the door without changing his expression. “What?” Pamela yelped out a little squeal. Ever the reporter, a thousand questions popped into her head. Why were Alex’s eyes so baggy? Why did he answer the door wrapped in his blanket? Was he playing games? Was the blonde from last night over at his apartment; is that what he was hiding? A year ago, Pamela would have felt bad thinking such questions. It wasn’t her right to know. She was intruding. She was guilty of being a pest. She should mind her own business and just walk away, and call Alex back at a more opportune time. She’d come with a note of apology and a cookie mug to show her sincerity. Pamela was a journalist now. It was her job now to ask the questions that mattered. She should get the story. Find out why things were the way they were. She knocked again, this time harder. “Alex! What’s wrong? Let me in, to talk to you. Alex?” She stood in silence in front of his large white door, biting her lip. Moments passed with no response. “Alex, you don’t look so good. Do you need help? I know we just met yesterday, but talking can help.” Pam paused. “Stupid. Why am I trying to appeal to his emotions? He’s not a woman,” she thought. She looked down at her shoes. They were two for twenty dollars at Payless and they were ratty from years of wear. How long had it been since she bought new shoes, she wondered. She was standing in front of a door to a $100,000 apartment wearing shoes that even a street person would throw away. She was demeaning herself for some frat-boy just so she could get his o.k. to send in her work. Pameala curled her toes in anger. “O.k., honestly, that’s fine, Alex. We don’t have to talk, but please open the door. I had the copy of the article; you asked to see it before I sent it in. I have it here for you, if you want to look at it you’ll have to let me in. I need to send it in an hour from now. That’s my deadline, so this is your chance now.” Pamela was through placating him. If she couldn’t reason with him, she’d appeal to his vanity. Silence. … Another empty moment passed, and she turned to leave. Slowly, the door to his apartment opened. Pamela was satisfied, though she did not show her smugness as she entered the doorway. Alex stood beside the door holding it open for her, holding his chic brown blanket draping his body like a toga. She walked in to the apartment. It was not what she expected, to say the least. It was freezing, for one. She was shocked to see the state of the apartment: end tables overturned, lamps ajar, a fresh hole in the wall from a strong fist. The windows were all open in the apartment which explained why it was so cold. It certainly would have had the character of Alex Strider, the theme seemed to be pristine, white and clean. The art on the wall was of very bold weather or landscapes. But the place was a disaster area, as if a wild party had been thrown for someone on his last night before going to jail for life. ”My God, Alex… what happened here? Was there a fight?” she wondered. Alex closed the door slowly. “No. There wasn’t a fight.” He crossed and sat on his couch apathetically. ”O.k. Whatever you say. Uhm, I can just leave you the article, if you’d like. I don’t know if you’re in much of a mood to read right now…” “How could you guess?” He deadpanned as he stared dead ahead at the forty inch plasma t.v. that wasn’t turned on. ”Well. All the same, my job’s done here in Pittsburgh, so I’ll be moving on to another spot soon enough. I’d like to thank you,” she said, as she continued to look around his palatial apartment that was in a state of emergency, “for letting me interview you. I wish you luck in the Atlantic Wrestling Club.” She half smiled, but couldn’t grab his gaze. ”Have you ever been betrayed, Pamela? Would it be imprudent of me to ask if you’d ever been violated in any small or large capacity?” Alex asked, almost rhetorically. “Well,” she said cautiously, “without getting into too much detail, I suppose all women have been betrayed, or violated in some way…” she began to think back to worse times: The casual groping of teenage boys in cramped high school hallways; The inappropriate comments of the old men when she was a waitress. She wasn’t a stranger to violation. Alex didn’t notice. “I’m so angry I could just…break something. So mad. It’s scary.” Alex shook his head slowly, dwelling on the treachery. “What happened, Alex? How were you betrayed?” Her eyes searched his face for any signs. For as cocky a bastard as he came off only the day before, she could tell something had happened that put him in his place. “Look, I’m not working in official capacity now, I’m just asking. You look like something is seriously wrong.” Alex was embarrassed. He didn’t want to talk about it. He could never talk about it. No one would understand, they’d only judge. Judge like he judged others. He would have none of that. “Just, I guess when you’re a public figure you’re not treated quite like you’re human, is all. It’s like I’m supposed to be a superhero. Or supervillain, whatever.” “You seemed to like being above human yesterday.” She coaxed. “You were the cock of the walk at Frais Viande.” “Yesterday I had a job.” He muttered. ”What, did something happen with AWC?” “No. Last time I checked it was still there, anyway. I just don’t think I’ll be welcomed there with open arms now.” ”O.k., that’s fair. Why not? You’re a hot prospect, right? I seriously doubt anything has changed so dramatically in less than twelve hours that you’ve lost your place in the Atlantic Wrestling Club.” “I’m damaged goods now. Tainted. I’ll…” his voice trailed off into faint murmurs. Pam began to feel genuine concern for Alex. She’d never seen someone take such a dramatic turn in such a short period of time. Well, other than Kevin, that is. He became a borderline manic-depressive right after she got her job in journalism. “So, you’re going to lose your job. Well, alright, you’re talented enough; you’ll just get another job wrestling somewhere else.” Pamela sat down on his large white leather sofa. ”No. No one will hire a drugged out hippie.” Alex said, half to himself as he pulled his hand over his face. “Not reliable.” “Drugged out hippie? Who are we talking about here? You’re the guy who’s never done drugs, never had a sip of wine, doesn’t even get a tattoo. Why would anyone think you did drugs?” Pam softened her, reaching out to him, she asked “Alex, are you using steroids?” He raised his head and cast a glaring eye at her. “Steroids? What? God, no. No. No steroids here.” Alex shook his weary head. “Just good old fashioned acid. LSD. Lysergic acid diethylamide. Apparently, I was tripping on it all night,” Alex revealed in a rare moment of weakness. “You tried acid? You must have really wanted to impress that girl last night…” “Wrong,” Alex interrupted sharply. “I did not do anything for her. No, I don’t care who I impress. I didn’t take acid for anyone. I was drugged, damn-it-all. I didn’t even know you could be drugged like this, but apparently she put a some hits of LSD on her tongue right before she mauled me with kisses me. She wanted to ruin me. I never even met the girl before, but she wanted me dead or something. I can’t explain.” Pamela’s jaw dropped, she couldn’t believe what she was hearing. “My God, Alex, that’s horrible,” she said. “What did you do? Did you fight the girl?” ”No, Pamela. That’s very weak. No, though I was furious, I just kicked her out of my apartment before the drugs really began to set in. I didn’t want her to be around me. I have no idea what she planned to do when I was completely indisposed.” “Well that was smart thinking, Alex,” she said earnestly. “Don’t worry, Alex, no one knows. She was probably just trying to… I don’t know. I have no idea why someone would drug another person. You’ve just had a really rough night. I am sure you’ll be fine though. I mean, I won’t tell anyone at AWC what you’ve told me, alright? This is completely off the record.” “Sure. But what if they do a ‘random’ drug test on me? They’ll smell my paranoia a mile away, if they’re smart at all,” Alex said, downcast. “Alex, look,” she said, being logical. “Their first event is more than a week away, no one knows that anything has happened here. They’re still working on details for the Pay Per View. They’re not going to be able to pay attention to things that they have no idea are happening, trust me. They’re busy making sure that the Zero To Hero goes off without a hitch. As long as you pick up yourself, get clean and shaved, you’ll be fine. You just have to…do whatever it is you do to prepare for a match. You’ll be fine. “I think you might be right, Pam. I mean, it’s my business, after all. I am the victim here, anyway. I shouldn’t feel like I did anything wrong, right?” ”No. That’s good. Most people in your position would think that they brought it on themselves…” she caught herself as she realized the irony of Alex’s position. It was only yesterday that he callously stated that Karma was what brought down athlete’s lives to ruin. Now he wasn’t in all that different of a position. “But anyway. Listen, should I make some coffee? You could probably use a cup.” “No. Caffeine, remember? I don’t drink coffee.” “Oh, right. Well, can I use your bathroom?” Alex nodded and pointed to the door near the exit. Pamela smiled and got up. Walking over to the door, she stumbled on some loose afghan rug that lay disheveled on the floor. Looking back to see if she’d been noticed, she sighed with relief that Alex was still too caught up in himself to notice her klutziness. She honestly felt bad for him. She felt he was just misguided and had closed himself off from a lot of what is real. “He’ll learn a valuable lesson from this. That’ll be good. He’ll change his behavior. Maybe loosen up a little bit,” she thought to herself as she watched him rise and stretch. She cocked her head as she followed the muscular curve to his naked back, all the way down to where it disappeared beneath the brown blanket. “Shame all the good looking ones know it. That’s how they’re all broken, too conceited to be any good.” She opened the door, lost sudden lecherous thought. Her scream was deafening. Alex turned his head immediately. He watched as Pamela fell back down hard on her ass. She was too horrified by what she saw to notice the pain of her tailbone busting against the hardwood floor, and all the blood in her face drained away. Alex ran over to her, almost losing his blanket in the process. “What? What did you see? Was it a rat? I better not have rats!” All Pamela could do in response was point in the bathroom. Alex turned and followed her finger. Sitting upright next to his bathtub was Sophia, the blonde who drugged him, legs akimbo. In her left arm there was a syringe dangling like a loose cigarette in a bum’s mouth. A long black trail of blood ran down her arm and pooled on the floor, staining her skirt. Her head was cocked back. She wasn’t breathing. “I thought you said she left!” Pamela stuttered as Alex ran forward, putting his fingers on her neck to check her vital signs. ”Call 911;” he said, grim faced and serious. “There’s no heartbeat.” View Alexander Strider 's Biography
