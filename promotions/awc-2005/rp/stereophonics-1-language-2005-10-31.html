Stereophonics #1: 'Language'

31 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 31st October 2005 So. So far, so gay. I suppose at least it served Newton’s Third Law well – for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. All those philosophical and sociological theories of balance, too – for every high a low, for every right a wrong. But the short-term euphoria of going to see Stereophonics at Valentine’s was nothing – NOTHING – compared to the potential long-term agony of What I Had Just Seen. Let’s skip over the anger, the heartache, the heartbreak of the few hours that followed – spent on a bench in an empty children’s playground in the rain – and jump to the evening. You’d have thought I’d already seen enough drama for one day, and my narrative could pass over the content of the concert itself; just a fun way to unwind and forget about things. You’d be wrong. 7th October 2005 18:16 Travelling in style. Leg up, over saddle, iPod in and off we go, the appropriately-titled “Pedalpusher” blasting into my brain as I psyched myself up for the concert. Activate sweet voice babe Let your world come out Get your head off the chopping block And get your ouija board out You got your rubber on a pencil In case you make a mistake I hope you got it all down babe I’d hate to think you’re a fake A fake? No way. I was real, honest, see-what-you-get Tim Shipley, and tonight I was going to see Kelly Jones bark out the best of Wales since... well... anything. You got it all this side up but you ain’t got it down Push the pedal now The dying howl, the kicking chords, and I pushed those pedals, baby, I pushed them hard. I was going to Valentine’s. I didn’t care about anyone or anything. I was going to Valentine’s and I was going to have a good time. At all costs. A mistake. And I should’ve known it. 7th October 2005 18:32 I arrived early, hoping to sell off my spare ticket before the prime touts came to mark their territory. But no sooner had I locked up my bike, slipping the combination-code chain through the railings a few streets away from tonight’s venue, and started to amble down the street with my hands in my pockets than I was accosted by a scary-looking black guy in a scarier-looking bomber jacket. “Yo, ya wanna see Stereo Funk at Valentine’s tonight? I got tix at good prices...” “No, thanks,” I muttered, and moseyed my way past with my head down. Perhaps selling wasn’t going to be quite so easy with such... intimidating competition. But, with a sniff, I realised that I at least knew the name of the band. That had to be worth something. It was raining. It was more than raining – it was absolutely pelting it down. And tonight, I was at the mercy of the elements. Not wanting to wear layers, having anticipated the atmosphere of warm sweat that would exfoliate my entire body on entry to the concert hall, I had risked it in a t-shirt and jeans knowing full well that the clouds were about to empty. My outlook on this was simple, yet perhaps admirable – I’d dry off sooner or later. Still, I didn’t want to cycle that journey back in the rain. I placed a hand against the white material that was sticking to my chest, outlining my improving pectorals nicely, and sighed. The cumulus wasn’t being kind. Catching sight of Valentine’s for the first time, I hurried over, eager to scout out the arrangements for tonight. It looked like little more than a nightclub to me – yet never having been clubbing in my life this was an ignorant assertion. Still, the neon “QUEUE HERE” sign evoked memories of a cheap BBC sitcom I’d vaguely paid attention to while getting stuck into my extra maths homework with my dad at my side. Getting frustrated by the recurrence of x, and struggling with eliminating the fractions, but my dad always there to a mutter a hint in my ear, never telling me outright but letting me figure it out for myself – at least, that’s what my ten-year-old self thought I was doing, though in actual fact the path he was subtly leading me down was so narrow I could hardly fail to come up with a wrong answer. Mr. Benson astonished as he handed me back another 100% – and this was the extra work, remember – “Aiee!” a foreigner exclaimed as I blindly stumbled into her. “Oh God – I'm so sorry – I wasn’t watching where I was going –” I blurted out as she hopped on one leg. Presumably I’d stepped on her foot. “Is there anything I can do to help...?” I felt useless, standing there, but then she shot out an arm to grab hold of my t-shirt to keep her balance as she used her other hand to burrow into her shoe and locate the source of the pain, and suddenly I had purpose. I gently took her wrist in my hand (mainly because her grip on the t-shirt was so tight I feared she would rip the material) and she looked up at me, vulnerable and frightened. I would never use those two adjectives to describe Tatyana Olgarov again. I looked carefully into her eyes, trying to give off an aura of cool competence. And miraculously, it seemed to work. She broke into a smile, and suddenly it all came together. The disjointed parts of herself formed a whole. A beautiful whole. I was intoxicated. “ïðîùàíî,” she breezed (no, I have no idea how she pronounced it; Babelfish really isn’t all that great), and I took this as a pardon – no worries – never mind – it’s OK. The literal meaning of what she said was “forgiven”, as I discovered much later; presumably its intent is identical to those three English phrases I suggested in the previous sentence. So there I was, standing with a beautiful woman, tall and slender and pretty, her stylish blonde hair hidden for the most part under a dark velvet hat, an expensive-looking jacket with a furry lining keeping her warm, and me gaping open-mouthed. I still had my hand on her wrist. What a nightmare! I withdrew it hurriedly, but rather than make the swift departure I expected she instead moved in a little, her glossy lips smiling provocatively, and intoned in clipped English: “Would you like to go to a bar?” 7th October 2005 18:59 So there I found myself, underage in Albany’s most expensive bar, as awkward on my stool as she was graceful, she poised like a cat, I like a rabbit. I wasn’t comfortable with this until her first question brought back memories that were too recent, too fresh, too hurtful. “So do you have a girlfriend?” The Russian accent made it smooth – normal for an icebreaker, even before we’d exchanged names. Formulating my answer wasn’t what was worrying me – in fact as soon as the words left her mouth I forgot all about where I was. Sammie consumed me in that instant, and I knew I needed to break away. I'm not too good at that. I tend to dwell. I tend to mull things over for long periods of time before I can even think about taking action. If asked to describe myself, my first impulse wouldn’t be to say “impulsive”. That sentence was meant to sound clever. But I digress. Back to the bar, and Tatyana Olgarov (though I didn’t know these names yet), and breaking away. Tonight – at the risk of sounding repetitious – was the night. The night to do anything I wanted without a thought as to the ramifications. Winning those tickets had been more than luck – it had been a sign. I needed tonight, and fate had brought me to a bar with Tatyana where I was soon to pay upwards of $5 for her rum and mixer. It was the start of a swift decline, irreversible once put in motion, and I knew it. But, regardless, I answered the question – “Not right now” – and ordered the drinks. 7th October 2005 19:17 “So what’s your name?” That’s right. Six shots down and I still hadn’t found out. She knew mine, but in my hazy state I couldn’t quite recall how she’d asked and then escaped without giving the reciprocate information. “Tatyana,” she told me crisply. Tatyana was a nice name... Hell, Fuckface would have seemed a nice name to me. I’d barely familiarised myself with the taste of alcohol in the past, and here I was, getting Totally Wasted, as they say in the business. What business? I didn’t even have the faintest idea what I was talking about. Still, she seemed to be enjoying it – laughing away – high-pitched, getting lots of people looking over, but she made it clear she was with me, and I liked that. And I liked her. And her face was swimming and her eyes were leaking and there were three, no four of her, but it didn’t matter. I wanted her. Ever dance with the devil, baby? I knew right then that I wanted her. Make my day Who wouldn’t have wanted her? Do you feel lucky? She was young, beautiful, blonde. Slender but not fragile. Slim but not skinny. Vulnerable... yet in control. Too much control. Tomorrow’s another day But sprawled over the bar with my head in my hands and my wallet open in front of me I couldn’t recognise this. I could barely recognise myself. Can you walk on water maybe? Turn water into wine? I wanted her. Can I buy you a drink there lady? I ordered her a double and stared into her eyes, trying to look intimate, intense, involved. Can you tell me another lie? Lies, lies, all of it, I'm sure – and I should have known at the time. I was stupid. Drunk. Stupid drunk. Stupid to have got drunk. But I wanted her. Be my devil angel Be my shooting star Be my devil angel Be my shooting star And Kelly Jones shouted into my head, And suddenly I just wanted to go to bed, But I heard the sound of real progress And realised I could actually have this. She leaned in, her breath hot and fruity – I’d never take home this kind of booty. All I got then was a simple kiss But I was hooked, because this was bliss. She would be my devil angel. She would be my shooting star. But that would have to wait, because suddenly I knew what to do with my spare ticket, and even though I could hardly stand straight I knew I wanted to see the support bands. The mind works in funny ways.
