And suddenly, his conscience comes into play...

18 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 18th July 2005 “Do you want to get dinner?” smiled Keith as he entered the room. He was dressed in a sky blue shirt and khaki shorts with his white socks pulled up and his brown scandals flicking against his heel. Think the butler from Joe Millionaire, but looking a little bit older and more agile. That’s Keith. Mickey was dressed in blue flower patterned shorts and a white t-shirt. Now by flower patterned I mean blue shorts with like big white flowers, like you would wear to Hawaii. They aren’t pansy shorts or anything like Rainbow Flag would be wearing. And The Farmer was wearing sandals without socks. Keith watched him as he turned from the suitcase. He had helped him to get a wardrobe and they suited him. Mickey nodded. The Irishman didn’t say much, but Keith Chamberlain, the chaperon assigned to look after The Farmer didn’t mind. The job was only for a few weeks to get Mickey settled into the wrestling life and to make sure he was enjoying everything. Keith was one of the more experienced chaperons working in the wrestling industry. He knew what to do. Bring the wrestler around during the first few shows. Hook him up with a coach. Make sure he got to the hotels okay. Make sure he was eating properly and attend to any grievances that they might have. To be honest, Mickey was the easiest assignment that Keith had had in years. All the kids that were coming into wrestling nowadays were egotistical and loud, but Mickey was down to earth. He enjoyed a drink but knew his limits, especially with a rigid training regime on his hands. He was quiet and mature He took in everything Keith told him and paid great attention to his words of wisdom. He didn’t fuck any women. Keith knew that a man with Mickey’s great looks and Irish accent could have his pick of most women but Mickey didn’t get involved as most were out to make some money either through tabloids or illegitimate children. Mickey was a model professional. Even in the three weeks the pair had spent together Keith could see that Mickey was setting himself up for longevity of life. The pair were friends. They talked about stuff. Mickey told stories of Ireland. Keith told stories of some of the wrestler he knew and their antics. Each time something risky was brought Mickey seemed to be turned off. Chamberlain and McCarthy left the hotel and started down the street. Keith turned to look at The Farmer with a grin on his face. “This is my first trip to San Jose,” beamed Keith. The chaperon had always spent his time in America and never got to go on tours with wrestling promotions but seeing as Mickey was new and the AWC tour of the Caribbean had began just after Solarized, Keith was brought along. Mickey nodded. He hadn’t spoken all day. He was never the most talkative and that Keith accepted but on most days the pair were able to have a cohesive conversation. Keith directed the path they were walking as he was taking Mickey to a restaurant recommended by a wrestler to Keith. They crossed the busy San Jose road as Mickey remained silent and Keith remained sceptical. Mickey looked forward, walking just off the shoulder of Keith so as to not end up going in the wrong direction. Keith watched forward as well, deciding it was best not to intrude on Mickey’s thoughts and assuming he would open up about it at dinner. The silence between the pair remained as they walked down the street. Around them engines roared, people talked and sirens were heard in the background. It wasn’t an awkward silence between the pair as the background noise washed away any tension that may have bee in the air. Mickey’s eyes were locked forward. Even when Keith’s attention was griped by a voluptuous women walking past the pair, Mickey seemed oblivious and continued to march forward. The pair again reached a road crossing and broke to a halt. “It’s very warm…” sighed Keith as he used the back of his hand to wipe his forehead. His balding head was glistening with sweat as he wiped some onto his hand. Mickey looked down in his direction and groaned positively. Meaning he groaned so as to agree with Keith. And that was it. As the Costa Rican cars zoomed past and seemingly quite a popular road, Keith Chamberlain and his assignment, oh I’m making this sound sort of James Bond-ie, Michael Fitzpatrick-McCarthy stood in silence. Red cars, blue cars, green cars, orange, white, black and brown cars. They were all here, but still no speech from Mickey, who after groaning at Keith returned his attention to facing forward. Keith looked up. That wasn’t an out and out attempt to get Mickey talking but more of a chance to get a little light conversation going before he attempted to delve… and it hadn’t worked. Keith studied the silent man that was Mickey as he watched the cars pass. Keith turned attention from Mickey, who he realised wasn’t going to say anything and watched the cars. The stream of cars had finished and he stepped into the road. Suddenly an arm reached around him and pulled him back onto the pavement. Almost at the same time a red car sped past in the lane closest to Keith. “Ah be Jesus… I don’t want ye dying on me…” smiled Mickey as he dragged Keith from his near certain collision with the car. Mickey released his chaperon after a few seconds as he felt he was far enough away from the road. Keith looked up, a little startled, at Mickey and nodded a nod of grateful delight. Mickey nodded back with a smile and the tension emotion that seemed to have been filling Mickey’s face was gone. Keith steadied himself, patting down a ruffled shirt, breathed in deeply and then checked the road again. This time he stepped off the sidewalk and crossed the road successfully, closely followed by The Farmer over his shoulder. The pair continued walking down the street of San Jose. Keith looked up at Mickey ready to thank hi for his quick thinking to be greeted with the intense expression, returned to his face. Keith sighed inside as he faced down the street again. He was now more worried about Mickey. He had lost his immersed air only seconds previous and it had quickly returned. Something must be clearly eating away at him. Keith abruptly stopped as he looked up at a little sign hanging on the wall of the building. Mickey was slower to react. He had been looking straight forward and was surprised by Keith’s halt, so ended up bumping him slightly. Keith shrugged it off as he looked at the sign. “I think this is it…” suggested Keith. His voice sounded unsure. He looked over his shoulder at Mickey who shrugged. “I’ll go inside to check…” finished Keith and he disappeared in the narrow door. The Farmer was left to stand outside on his own. He looked grumpy and unwelcoming. No the greatest of ways to meet his future coach and Keith was aware of that. He needed Mickey to be cheerful when the coach would arrive for their dinner. Keith emerged from the door with a grin. “This is the place, but Chris isn’t here yet…” This was the first indication Mickey had been given as to the identity of his coach. Chris. Mickey nodded and followed Keith in the door. The Farmer was silent as he and Keith were lead to a table set out for three at the back of the very plush restaurant. The restaurant was dark with a red glow to everything. There were no males waiting on tables and even the bar staff were female. Maybe it was just fluke for this shift. A very pretty waitress soon followed the departure of the restaurant from the table. “May I take your order?” she enquired delicately. “No, no…” smiled Keith, “the third member of our party is not here yet…” The young girl nodded. “Drinks?” she smiled after a second. Mickey and Keith locked eyes. Mickey nodded. “I’ll give a glass of water,” asked Keith as he returned his attention to the girl. Mickey, whose attention was caught by the girl but not for her beauty raised his hand in the air, with his two main fingers outstretched. “Whisky… and a whisky…” Think George Clooney when he sits down with Julia Roberts in Ocean’s Eleven. I don’t think I explained The Farmer’s actions very well there but he made the action of a double measure of whisky and then a third measure of whisky, just like Clooney does when seated in Andy Garcia’s seat. Understand? The girl didn’t bother to jot the requests down and immediately walked away with a nod and a smile. Keith’s attention was straight away on Mickey. Fresh! Was tomorrow night and Mickey was drinking, typical Irish piss-head. “What’s wrong?” enquired Keith. Mickey shook his head. “Don’t shake your head… I need you in a good mood to impress your potential coach…” “Potential?” questioned Mickey, “For fuck sake! It’s not a dun deal!” “Nope…” Keith bowed his head. “Fuck me!” cursed Mickey as he sat back in chair. “Tell me what’s wrong and maybe we can fix…” Keith broke off his idea as the waitress returned with their drinks. “Maybe we can fix it…” finished Keith. “I can’t hit a woman…” … … There was silence between the two men. Never had Keith met a wrestler that was unwilling to slap a woman around inside the ring. This guy has a conscience. Keith shrugged and then looked shocked. “Shit!” “Wha’?” “He’s here… Pretend to be happy and we’ll work on a plan later s that you beat the girl…” smiled Keith, turning from Mickey to beam a grand smile at the man coming towards the table. Mickey nodded as he turned to watch the man walking towards the table. He was smartly dressed in a pin stripe suit and blue shirt. His brown hair was slicked back. He was well-built, clearly a former wrestler. Mickey recognised him immediately. Walking towards him, his potential future coach… A man that Mickey knew from his days of watching wrestling on television… The man that would hopefully teach Mickey everything, including how to hit a woman without feeling bad… He sat down opposite The Farmer. “Michael Fitzpatrick-McCarthy,” introduced Keith, “I would like you to meet Mr. Chris Metal.”
