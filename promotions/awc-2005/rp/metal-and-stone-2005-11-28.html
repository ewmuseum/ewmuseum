Metal And Stone

28 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28/11/05 Metal and Stone You can run, Paddy O’Shea, but you can’t hide… Paddy, who had been lying within an almost subterranean slumber only two seconds before, bolted upwards into a sitting position, from underneath the deceitful safeguard that was his duvets. He was panting heavily, his eyes as wide as saucers. A nightmare. Again. Years before, when he was but a mere child and his father would be running rampant around the caravan filled to the brim with booze, he would seek the sanctuary of his bed. It was his refuge, his haven, the sacredness of which lingered in his mind, long after he’d left from underneath the protective and equally disparaging wing of his Father. But the garrison he’d built from pillows and blankets had proven fraudulent because as it stood, boasting its strength, it knew that when Danny O’Shea brought down that iron fist that had seemed so gargantuan at the time, the walls would fall. Then it would be exposed as but a paltry contradiction; it would avow fortification and security against outside forces yet when push came to shove, its defence would wither away almost graciously. Beaten and dragged away, the fort would cackle at his gullibility that had convinced him he had nothing to fear. It was all but linen and cloth convincing others it was metal and stone. For a long time, he’d asked himself, what was to say he wasn’t the same? Was his success in the AWC a fluke? Was he punching above his weight? Then Shelly came along, and all those doubts disappeared. These duvets that covered his almost completely naked body were provoking him now though. For weeks he’d been unable to sleep, haunted by dreams of past hurt. Things he couldn’t put his finger on. Things that maybe were better forgotten. As he lay in the warmth and comfort of his bed, it would whisper into his ear, sleep tight my child, you have nothing to fear. But it couldn’t defend him, could it? Maybe is was the reprimand for his dissolute life, one filled with criminality and larceny, but he just couldn’t comprehend why God would penalize him now, after a new leaf had been turned, when he was at last, being the man he’d always wanted to be. A message was trying to be sent. It seemed clear to Paddy. Your existence is plastic, everything you have is artifical, these nightmares are only my way of reminding you. Paddy found himself sneering inside, his inner eye looking skywards. He found himself leering at Heaven, letting the chap upstairs know that he wouldn’t grate him down now, things were too good for that to ensue. And these duvets wouldn’t be a metaphor for his life either. He wouldn’t bend or break, he just couldn’t. He was contented, maybe for the first time in his existence. He had a daughter, a girlfriend, everything the social order had said a gypsy in America couldn’t have. If he was exception to the rule, so be it, he would be an ambiguity for all on show. But they wouldn’t take it away from him, nor would God. His person, both physical and mental, was walls and these walls were steadfast and strong; they were stone and metal and they would protect his interests. These interests couldn’t be lost or he too would crumple from the bereavement. They were authentic not plastic and he had to believe that… Because, aye need it more than ever… Already, he’d forgotten about the nightmare that had woken him up. He never could recall the details. Despite this, he had an inconclusive premise that it was recurring. Every night he’d awake, sporadic images of the past all but subsiding into the elapsed, every night he’d awake doused in sweat, so thickly spread over his body that the bed sheets would be drenched almost through. Fear that everything he had could so easily be taken away from him was his chief worry. But it wasn’t the only thing. Sleep was difficult. Alcohol helped, usually had him like a log until morning but he knew he couldn’t go down that path. Do that and he would soon just be a clone of his Father who had oft times crushed forts, the sanctity of his family being one. No, alcoholism was just another route to the destruction of his earthly possessions. So instead of falling into that pitfall, he’d try and weather the storm a little longer, many these nightmares would go away and he could actually sleep for more than an hour at a time. Trying to suppress the taste of bitterness in his move, Paddy arose from his bed. He wouldn’t get to sleep now, like many people, he never could with worry on his mind. Still tired, the yellowing sleep highlighting his eyelashes, Paddy stumbled towards his bedroom door, his legs being deprived of a proper recharge in weeks. He pushed the plastic door opened with an awkward shoulder barge and found himself in the dim haze of darkness always associated with dawn. Six O’clock read the clock on the wall. Although he could vaguely see the outline of larger objects in the room such as the tiny kitchen network that also happened to be in the same room as his living room, he was blind to smaller object around the floor and he was forced to walk whilst stewarding any loose toys at his feet. His daughter’s toys. Well, sort of his daughter anyway. It had been a few months now since Paddy O’Shea had inadvertently discovered the small whicker basket in Central Park. It was cold then, he remembers clearly how it had nipped at his hardened skin. He couldn’t leave the girl there, he didn’t have the heart to, so he took her to his hotel room, his intention to hand her over to social services the following day. That night he got news his only childhood friend, May had died. Maybe it was the savvy skin or the deep brown eyes, maybe it was the way she laughed or the way she gripped his index finger with her full hand; all he knew was this little girl, so young and innocent, was May. It was her. The unquestionable bond between the pair forbid him from doubting it. And so he had taken her for his daughter. Maybe not legally at first, after all, the law and Paddy were like water and oil, but he had realised he soon had to. It was time to stop being careless, incompetence would lead to loss, it was time to grow up. So he’d spoken to his lawyer who’d took on the burden of this extremely difficult process of adoption through such unusual circumstances. But he had to do this right, any slip ups and May was gone like dust in the wind. He’d lost too many people in his life for that to happen, especially when he’d gotten a chance to get his best friend back. Paddy struggled towards a seat, patting it lightly with the palm of his hand making sure it was clear of toys/newspapers/baby puke. Once assured, he sat down with a sigh. Listening now, he heard the melodic twitter of some early birds pierce the air. Already he was noticing how quickly lightness was overwhelming the dark. He was able to see the outline of his fingers as he reflected on another blessing that had come to pass. What’s your name? Paddy O’Shea What’s you status? Number one contender t’ the Transtalantic Title What’s your goal? T’ secure the previously stated title. Can you do it? Ye better believe it buddy. Sooner rather than later, tha’ shiny number is goin’ t’ be around me waist. Lavelle’s strong but the fans need someone t’ take them higher. They need someone like me. They need someone who represents the flaws in everyones life but also the possibility o’ overcomin’ them. Can you do it? YES! Really? YES! Well then, show the world that. Hitchin’ A Ride The names James Lafferty but I’m just plain old Jim to my acquaintances. I’m what the kind American public has categorized me as, a drifter. I go from place to place, coupled down to nowhere, living off the odds and ends I can find. It’s a life, a lonely life, but at least it’s an existence. It wasn’t always this way though, not five years ago, I owned a small corner edifice in Boston, selling second hands books. It didn’t pay well, I mean, how could it in an age when kids are too bothered with television and video games, the joy of literature was never going to prevail. The business did however make just about enough to stay afloat. Sometimes I ate, oft times I went hungry for a day or two until I could pull a little profit together to get a decent meal. It wasn’t idyllic but it was better than what was there before. As a kid, I grew up with an Army Lieutenant for a Father. He was good, very good. I never seen him in action obviously but he did have the medals to show he was something unique. Medals for bravery, leadership, hell, I could go all day telling you the things he achieved hoss. One thing he didn’t ever thrive in though, was Fatherhood. I was a frail boy, picked on at school a lot of the time. I liked reading so I was automatically an outcast to other schoolkids and especially to my Father. “Books ain’t gonna keep you alive in a life an’ death situation boy!” His rasping voice still nags me to this day. In the 70’s when all the High School kids were protesting against the war, I was supporting and my crew cut enforced by my Father made me stick out like a sore thumb. Uncompromising, unrelenting. That was my Father. He was the OC and I was the private. If he wanted me to walk on my hands from that day forth, in his mind, there would be no questions asked. Turns out, he wasn’t a great husband either. He was as uptight as a rod and it drove my poor mother to drugs. I figure she started on some acid, probably from some of her friends from the factory. Sure you could pin subsequent events on them, but my mother needed an outlet. Without it she wouldn’t have lasted as long as she did. Anyway, unbeknownst to me, she was getting on the hard shit. I’m talking needles here kids. As a boy, even I could see her becoming gaunt, persistently scratching herself – all the signs I learned to associate with a junkie. She was unhealthy and my Father should have seen it. He was smart. He should have seen it. But he didn’t. Whether he blocked it out because he just didn’t have the capacity to deal with it or because he legitimately didn’t see it, my mother gradually drifted away, the monkey on her back getting bigger and bigger. Then one day, the day my Father was due back from the army, I walked into the bathroom and my life was tuned on its head. Mom? Mom? Mo- I recall looking at the tub with its blue shower curtain bunched at the far end with the steel rod as I forgot how to finish the word ‘mom’. I stared at the tub, my face completely sombre and inert. A few seconds later I would scream. I would scream so god damn loud that my next neighbour Mrs Bentley would think someone was being killed and she would call the cops. But for just those two seconds, I stood silent; my hands clasped in front of me, my incorruptibility initially blinding me from what was in front of me. My eyes were huge but they were bulging now as I tried to comprehend what I was seeing. A scream was forming but it was too big to escape. Much, much too big. The light was on in the bathroom, it was one of those fancy beam lights, the long ones that lit up the whole room. Whether I wanted to or not, I was seeing everything. In the tub lay my mother, the water was tranquil save for the occasional plink as a water droplet gathered at the lip of the faucet, growing fatter and fatter, before dropping into the pink mess below. Didn’t I mention it was pink? Well yeah, it was. My mother lay with her back propped against the stern of the bath tub, her head rolled back so far that her stock-still eyes were piercing mine. Because of her orientation, her mouth hung open. Looking slightly over her chin, I saw the red split adorning her neck, a red mess had formed from this and spread over her breasts. Then I screamed. You know, Mrs Bentley was right when she supposed somebody must have killed someone next door. She was right. My Mother was killed. And it was my Father who did it, because just like me, he herded her to the edge. She had tried to kill herself easily with drugs, no pain, let’s just trip the fuck out. Finally realising it wasn’t getting her anywhere and dreading the thought of my Father returning, she panicked and slit her throat. He had that effect on people. It was my Father. Things were never the same after that between us. Our already dilapidated affiliation became just about non-existent, save for an intermittent grunt to commune something. I suppose he blamed me. He would, the selfish bastard, he never did understand this was all him. It was foreseeable therefore that I left home when I was fifteen. I’ve never looked back since. There were hard times for a while. Eventually I accumulated enough money from working at a crappy food outlet to get a lease on a place and so ‘Jim’s Jumble of Books’ was born to the world. As I previously said, business was bad but just about enough to keep the business sustainable. That was until, five years ago, I fell into the same pitfall as my mother. Drugs. Heroin mostly. Anxiety dictated my life and when a friend of a friend pledged he could take all that away with a simple needle, I practically thrust the crook of my elbow in his face. I started hard, I suppose I had to improve on my Mother’s legacy if you can see the sick humour in that. I don’t remember a lot what happened during those times. Within a month of getting hooked on the shit, I had spent all the revenue I could muster, and then when that went, I turned to my rent money. Then the money I was going to use on my flat. Four months later and I had nothing and I was on the streets. That monkey has gotten smaller but it’s still there. You can’t ever get rid of that sumbitch hoss. Though, like so many others (me included) you won’t learn from my mistake, will you? So now, I’m a drifter, just like what every boy wants to grow up to me. I had nowhere to go, no prospects, no chances, and no nothing. For a long time I had no hope either. Hope equals disenchantment. Then I met a man that gave me that kick in the ass that started my motor again. It was towards the end of November when I met him. I was stowing away in the carriage compartment of the Florida to New York locomotive (or whatever fancy term they use these days). Things had changed for hobos like me. I had been on the train since it had made a delivery stop at Birmingham, and sat amidst a small room full of heavy wooden boxes, all restrained with ratchet straps, all sitting on top of respective crates. It was lonely, always was on these train rides. I had been sat on the putrid wooden floor for what seemed like an eternity, my ass feeling more and more dead to the world as time passed by. I presume we must have been in the state of New Jersey when he got on. Being the coward I was, I hid between a few boxes as the interloper hopped on. He’d sneaked on, you could tell by the way he moved over the surface and I alleged already he was just another drifter. Then the train took off, and I contemplated my next move. I couldn’t see the man, only hear his heavy breathing and intermittently as he cleared his throat. I thought about my next move. Contradictory to popular belief, bums just don’t fight randomly, but they are protective of what they got. I’m sure you’d appreciate this too if just about all you had you wore on your back. After a few minutes, I realised the ridiculous situation I was in, and scampered out. “Hello?” I looked around the crate at a man that could have been me, hell he was sitting at the exact same spot I’d been in for three hours, his back propped up against the rear of the compartment. He could have been me because of the clothes he wore. They were tattered and discoloured. He wore khaki combats and a frayed black jacket over a white vest. On his head he wore a black leather hat that covered the top of his head and half of his dishevelled shoulder length hair. The man was sleeping now; my nervous greeting had fallen on deaf ears. I crawled forward now, noticing at least four days growth of facial hair. I edged further, now noticing a duffel bag beside him, probably containing his possessions. A fleeting moment of greed passed and I tried again. “Hello?” This time, there was movement. Initially the man simply murmured in his sleep, then his eyes opened and he looked up drowsily. The train was at top speed now, and I was amazed at how quickly he had fallen asleep. In his semi-conscious daze he noticed me and he was instantaneously on his feet. “Who are ye? Wha’ ye want?” Irish. God, if I knew that, I wouldn’t have woken him up. Still, I couldn’t really judge, not at this time in my life anyway. I got to my feet, taking a step back and holding my hands up as the man’s eyes narrowed and darted left and right. “No one friend, just getting a ride just like you.” The man’s eyes darted left and right again and I noticed his balled fists straightening and relaxing. He edged backwards and slid down the wall, falling back into sitting position with a thud. “Aye could use the company aye suppose. Wha’ be yer name partner?” My…name? God, it had been so long since someone asked me that. Well, my name is…er…it’s…. “…Jim…and you are?” “Paddy. Paddy O’Shea. Just travelling up t’ the next city an’ then aye’m off in the plane.” I thought for a second. The stowaway. In a plane? I was legitimately curious as to what he meant and so I pressed him, my nerves still tingling from the possible danger of the man, despite his friendly appearance. “A plane? You’re going to stowaway in a plane?” Paddy chuckled at the man whilst fiddling in the inside of his jacket for something. “Nah. Too hard, aye’ll just have t’ pay fer it.” This strange character then pulled a bottle of whiskey from his jacket and unscrewed the lid. He took a gulp as I looked on in slight puzzlement. Nothing to mull over, don’t agitate him, I found myself saying. Don’t let your guard down. “Ye want a swig?” He must have seen the naked lust in my eyes, because I reached forward almost at once on his invitation and snatched the bottle from his hands. I took a swallow then another, then passed it back quickly, avoiding eye contact. The man said nothing however. Instead, he handed me a cigarette, placed one in his own mouth and teased the tip of it with a zippo lighter before tossing it to me. “Thanks,” I garbled, now fiddling with the lighter with a shaky hand. The cigarette end glowed in front of my face as I inhaled deeply, then I handed the lighter back, before taking a seat near him but not quite within grabbing distance. “So…what do you do?” I found myself saying. Instantly I regretted it. He’s stowing away on a train you idiot, he doesn’t have a job. Still, it had slipped out, an innocent line of conversation. He didn’t seem affronted either, quite the contrary, he seemed fervent to talk as a smile slowly slid across his face. “Aye wrestle.” “Ye wrestle?” “Yeah…down in AWC.” “Never heard of it. Then again, I haven’t seen television in quite a while. Are you any good?” Once again I scolded myself for asking such stupid questions. Are you good? What the fuck kind of question was that to ask? I rocked to the right as the train slid over a minor obstruction then snuggled back into my spot, my eyes focused on the cool ones of Paddy who was looking through an upper window into the midday sky. “Aye think so. Number one contender t’ the Transatlantic title. Aye’ve been there before, a few months ago but aye wasn’t ready. Aye had t’ be given a lesson before aye got where aye am now.” “What was that?” Paddy chuckled as he inhaled his cigarette one more time, the glowing ash standing out in the dim surroundings. “Aye had t’ learn t’ take a beating t’ me body-“ Paddy tapped the side of his temple whilst looking at me unswervingly in the eye. “An’ up here.” Paddy kept staring for a few moments, his lightning blue eyes boring into the back of my head then he mercifully looked away. I was about to open my mouth to ask another stupid fanboy question but saved me the embarrassment. “Hate…tha’ was his name. Fer four months he tortured me, destroyed everythin’ aye was. He broke down every barrier aye had, every emotion in me capacity was felt. Aye was stripped bear…have ye ever been beaten so badly tha’ ye have no foundation to build up from again? Tha’ was the way aye was. Aye had t’ change completely. The Paddy O’Shea tha’ entered AWC six months ago is a very different man than the one who sits before ye. Hate…he took everything from me. Kidnapped me father, burned me home, made me bleed to the point tha’ aye thought aye was dead already. Then it turned out it was all Mike Wade, he was behind it all. Aye felt meself crumplin’ inwards, aye was a nothin’. Aye was in a deep hole.” Paddy conceded to the knowledge and took a drag of his cigarette again, the paper slowly burning away to tobacco, burning away to ash. Here is this man telling me everything about him and I’ve only known him two minutes. Yet I wanted to know more. “What did you do?” Paddy sniggered and blew smoke upwards. “Aye did wha’ aye had t’ do. Aye beat Hate an’ aye destroyed Mike Wade. Right now, he’s probably saunterin’ home from Mexico. Aye turned it around you could say.” “H-how could you do it?” Paddy passed for a second thinking, I could almost watch the mechanism in his face turning. Then a word formed on his lips. “Hope.” I sat and thought for a while. Hope. Oft times before, I’d considered it a liability. But then again, maybe I was too afraid to give it a chance. Paddy clasped me on the shoulder, I didn’t wriggling away from his grip or his stare. “An’ ye can get out o’ this too.” I looked into his eyes again, they were telling me ‘believe me’, then I dropped my gaze again and he sat back in his neutral position. Time passed slowly those few hours as we chatted of nothing and everything all at once. Going back home he said, had to find a partner for some high-fluting wrestling match. Mostly flimflam to be honest. Hope stuck in my mind though. He was right. Hope…I was so far gone, there was nothing else left that could help me. Hope. Like stone and metal it would support my rear as I moved forward. As we parted ways, I shook the man’s hand. There was something inside him that would never be extinguished; I could see it in his eyes. I said good luck to him and we parted. Now I’m looking forward not back. It’s time to make something of myself. Paddy O’Shea did it. So can I. END
