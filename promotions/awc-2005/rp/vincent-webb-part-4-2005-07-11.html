Vincent Webb Part 4

11 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/11/05 The next morning, the suns rays shine through the old and worn out curtains on the hotel’s windows, waking me rudely. I open my eyes, staring at the ceiling. My first chance to get a real night’s sleep and the hotel has to be low-rate and moderately priced. I got ripped off by an Asian lady… I yawn as I roll over to check the clock. The clock is now flashing “12:00”. Oh God, the clock is broke now too?! I get up out of bed and shiver from the iciness of the air. I grab my watch off of the counter where the television should be and I check what time it is. Six thirty-seven in the morning. I shiver once more as I put the cold steel around my wrist which has red imprints from being slept on. I pop my wrists and knuckles, angry from the fact that the bed I’ve slept on is harder than a rock and the covers are as thin as air. I toss on my single, now smelly, pair of jeans and my now torn up shirt. I step out into the hallway and I hear a loud screech which frightens me, making me slightly jump. Around the corner, I see the Asian lady, who ripped me off, looking at terrified, and then relieved. “Sorry sir,” the woman says, “you scared me. I’m not used to having guests here.” I want to laugh, but I’d rather not seem rude, nor awaken Alice who is only a few feet away. I hope she slept well… “Oh,” the woman continues, “our continental breakfast is ready. It’s in the lobby. Odd, you’d think I’d smell breakfast if it is only a few feet away. Oh well. Alice’s door pops open and the woman screams again. Alice steps out, her eyes narrow from the change of light exposure. The woman sighs, “Sorry ma’am…” I laugh to myself as she walks away, pushing a cart that I had not seen. Inside of the cart, I see a small piece of metal, barely sticking out of the sheets. Hmm… “Hey,” I hear Alice say. I turn to her. “How are you doing?” I smile. God, I love her voice… “I’m doing great, thanks. Did you sleep well?” “I’m afraid not. These beds are so stiff, it’s impossible to get comfortable. How’d you fare?” she asks. “It was alright. Extremely cold, though,” I say, laughing. “Yeah, I noticed that too. Weird, huh?” she says. “Yeah, yeah, weird,” I say… not weird, suspicious. “You should go back to sleep. Want me to bring you some breakfast?” “Sure,” Alice says, smiling. “Talk to you in a bit I guess.” “See you,” I say as she shuts to door. I stand there to assure that Alice has laid back to bed, and I rush back into the room, flipping the light on. I quickly rush back to the air conditioner and check the temperature. It is set at a normal, warm temperature… I check the window. The window is unlocked, letting the cool breeze of the outside world into my, our, rooms. The lady… I quickly run out of the room, following the hotel manager. “Ma’am!” I yell. She turns around, obviously frightened. It all comes together. “Hello, ma’am. Can you tell me where the breakfast is again?” I ask in a nice and polite voice. I hear a sigh of relief. Before the lady can answer, I quickly grab the metal piece, a gun, from the basket she is carrying in my left hand and cuff her mouth shut with my right. I hate this part… I whisper so only she can hear. “Look lady, I know that you got Webb’s men planted outside there. You tell me right now or I’ll give you another small hole in your head,” I say, referring to her narrow eyes. “Okay, okay,” the woman whispers as I release her, “just please, don’t shoot…” I nod and lower the gun. “One is walking in front of the door. The other is behind the counter. Just please, don’t shoot,” she says, as a tear begins to fall down her cheek. I can tell she wasn’t lying. I let her go. “If I hear one sound from you, you’re dead. Is that clear?” I say forcefully. She nods slowly, still weeping in uncontrollable fear. I hold the gun in my hand. A sub-machine gun, silenced at the end. Damn, Vincent must want this to go down quick and quiet. Sparing no cost. What have I done to him? I chuckle to myself. Nothing compared to what I will do to him… I carefully step near the corner, seeing no one at the desk. I look out the window. Sure enough is a large man, his hand inside his jacket, clenched around something. Probably a gun, of course. I look at the counter. Still bare. I quickly turn and fire two silenced shots, the first one putting a hole in the glass about two inches wide, the second rocketing through the hole and into the now alert guard’s throat. The sound panics the few standing outside on the sidewalks, but they just ignore it, not knowing what had happened. I hear the cracking of concrete behind me as dust falls on my shoulder from the wall. I turn back to the counter, where a man in Webb soldier attire, a pistol pointed at me. I fire, aiming for his shoulder, but the bullet hits something metal as I hear the “ting” of metal-to-metal crashing. I hear the man groan as he drops the pistol. That works. He reaches for the gun, which is in my view, and I fire two shots, scaring him away. He pulls his arm back. I walk up to him with pure calamity before shoving him to the ground on his back. I put my left knee on his left shoulder and my right knee on his chest. The gun in my hand is pointed directly at his temple. “DO IT!” the man yells, catching me off guard. “Kill me right now.” “On two conditions. One: tell me where your boss is. Two: tell him that you killed me. I want out of this shit, you got me?” I yell angrily. I hate this shit! “Ha, my boss is out there on the sidewalk dying. But if you want me to talk to Webb, I can do that,” the man says. “Why don’t you just end my life? Finish me off?” “I’m not that kind of guy,” I say, standing up with the gun still pointed at the man’s skull. I kick the man hard in the ribs, smiling. “But I am known to kick ass when needed.” The man rolls over, clenching his stomach. “I’m going to go inside that room,” I say, pointing to Alice’s room, “and when I come back, you and your friend better be out of here. Otherwise, I WILL end it for you. Am I clear enough for you?” The man nods as he gets himself to his feet. I walk towards Alice’s door, never taking my eyes off the man. I knock on the door. The door is almost instantly opened. Alice stands near the door, her hair all wet. She smiles nonetheless. “Hey, Steven. You want to come in?” she asks. “Sure, why not?” I say, stepping into the room. I then realize that Alice is wearing nothing but a bra and a towel around her waist. I look away, not wanting to seem like a pervert. “Sorry to bother you.” “Don’t worry,” Alice says, laughing. She drops the towel, revealing her short, really short, jean shorts she had been wearing yesterday. I smile. I love those legs… “Did you get breakfast?” she asks, her voice perky. “No, it was all gone,” I say, lying. I don’t want to lie to her, but it is for her own good. “Oh. I didn’t think that anyone would be here in this hotel,” she says humorously. I sigh, looking down. “Neither did I…” She looks at me, probably shocked and confused. “What do you mean ‘neither did I? Were they here? Did they find us?” “No, no they did not find us, alright? Don’t worry.” I only lie to because I don’t want to scare her. “Steven, what is that in your pocket?” she says, suspiciously. I close my eyes. Shit… I pull it out. She gasps in shock. “How did you get a gun!?” “Okay, okay, they were here. But they are both finished, alright? They can’t hurt you or me now,” I say in an attempt to reassure her. “But they were HERE! They FOUND us! How did they, how COULD they, find us?” she yells. “Shit,” I mutter to myself. “We forgot to put on fake plates for one thing…”
