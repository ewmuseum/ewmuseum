They'll Never Hurt You Like I Do: #1

23 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 23, 2006 1. Stop me if you've heard this one. Guy walks into a bar, rolls his leather off his shoulders and hands it to his brother, the short little fuck coming in just a half-step behind him. It's not so much a bar and more of a roadhouse, actually, a sawdust on the floor kind of place, like you'd expect to see Sam Elliot any second. Tractor trailer parking out back, no light beer, something like that. So, Guy walks into a roadhouse, sheds his leather and hands it to his brother, the brother could be ten kinds of dangerous if he was in the mood, but he's not the one with the faint shimmer of crazy in his eyes, he's not the one rolling up his sleeves as he makes his way to the jukebox. Guy takes his wallet out, fishes out a tenspot, feeds it in and the thing spits it out. He smoothes it on the glass, tries it facing the other way; the thing eats it and tells him he's got twenty plays for his money, so he scrolls through the tracks for about a minute or five and then he punches in the same number twenty times. The dudes in the roadhouse stop finding it funny around about the third, fourth run through, and the guy's just leaning back on the juke, smoking a cigarette with his left hand and working the heavy hunk of silver on his right middle finger with his thumb. Guy grins at his brother, the brother's at the bar with a bottle of MGD at his lips, and when Kenny sings again the best that you can hope for is to die in your sleep, they both cock their heads and smile. Chairs scrape and boots scuff, and that grin gets wider; Guy crooks his fingers, c'mon, c'mon. Who wants some? The brother takes another long pull off his bottle, says loud and clear: I got fifty on him. Never throw the first punch, and never let 'em get your back too far away from the wall, and never let 'em get you tired early, you've heard this one, right? A gambler never counts his money when he's sitting at the table, and a fighter never counts his take until the neon's faded far down the road. What? The punchline? I forget. I think it had something to do with a monkey. 2. Dean is eighteen and a half and the knot of men in the parking lot are yelling and shoving and Kip's fourteen and three-quarters and they're not supposed to be here. “You're a pretty little fucker, ain't ya?” the guy says and Dean knuckles his nose, rocks forward and back on the balls of his feet. “Shame to mess up that face,” the guy says, and Dean spits into the dirt and says, “We gonna dance all night or are we gonna fuck?” 3. --you you're you're gonna get killed one of these days you know those truckers half of em are on speed and stuff one of these days you're gonna go down and not get up again and I can't I I I can't Dean I can't watch that I can't do this anymore it's not just cuz Dad and and I don't I mean I just don't anymore we don't need the money that bad anyway and-- --shuddup Kip, have you ever seen me go down huh I don't wanna talk about this it's not your decision anyway and-- --one of these days I'm outta here Dean I I swear to God-- --you don't have the balls-- --... watch me. 4. Dean got his shoulders from his father but his father tells him he got his fists from his mama's uncle and maybe someday if we're ever in El Paso again we can go by the gym and see his gloves and his title belt, hanging there on the wall. Dad doesn't like it but Dad doesn't stop him because on a good night, a good night in a bad place, he can bring in twenty percent of a five grand take and that means he can pay himself for the stitches in that groove some gorilla took out of his right cheekbone. Kip got his mouth from his father and he doesn't need fists because he can sew, man, he can sew so good. Kip's words are bright steel and silver, and Dean will spend the next couple of years stumbling around holding his guts in with one hand while he swings away blindly with the other. 5. On the tenth anniversary of his mother's death, Dean walked into a dank tattoo parlor in West Houston, fake ID in hand and a wad of Jacksons in his pocket. The guy didn't give a shit how old Dean was once he put his money down, and he walked back out a half hour later with his mother's name in neat script over his heart. Dean thought Dad would be be pissed, but he only flexed his jaw and nodded, and Dean thought Kip would understand, but he only crumpled up his face and shrugged. 6. Twenty year old punk kid thinks he's Philo Beddoe or some goddamn thing and he's got this brother, this baby-faced short drink of water and it's a goddamn good thing the brother never steps in, not that he has to, 'cause that cocky little fucker, damn if he don't back it up. Two of 'em, never far apart, and god help us all if they ever stopped arguing long enough to fight back to back. God help us. 7. Dean had Kip with him this time, Kip dragging his feet and tripping over his bootlaces and blinking behind his too-long bangs. They'd agreed on it the night before, small matching pentagrams between their shoulder blades 'cause like Dad always said, you had to protect your blind side. But the dude at this place didn't buy that Kip was eighteen, even though Kip was twenty-one and even with the driver's license in Kip's wallet, and there wasn't any amount of money gonna convince him to ink the kid. Kip shrugged, told Dean to go ahead. I'll get mine next year, he promised, and he held Dean's hand while Dean breathed in the smell of disinfectant and chair vinyl, while Dean felt blood and sweat trickle down his spine, and drowsed to the hum of the needle. Kip didn't say anything, just rubbed his thumb back and forth over Dean's knuckles, back and forth and he would squeeze back when the needle stung and Dean's fingers flexed in spite of himself. When they got back Dad sent Kip out for milk and cigarettes and made sure he was down the block before he cuffed Dean upside the head. He said, Why the hell'd you drag your brother along? and Dean said, He was gonna get one too, and Dad went very still and said, I see. And Dean said, Listen, they wouldn't do it, I didn't… I didn't blemish your fuckin' baby boy, so you can just get off my case, and Dad's fist was about a half inch from Dean's cheek before Dean even finished drawing his next breath. The space between Dad's knuckles and Dean's skin throbbed and scorched and Dean's heart beat very slowly, and Dean opened his mouth to say he was sorry but Dad shook his head and dropped his hand. Neither of them ever mentioned it, after. 8. First it was just a little comedown after a fight, you know. Dad would be off welding or some shit like that, and Dean would be shaking and vibrating with adrenaline and it made sense, sort of, or at least it felt good. First Dean threw a punch and then Kip swung a weak punch. First it was Kip trying to help and trying to please, and then it was Dean trying to hold and trying to keep. Then it was nothing, until it was again. 9. Then it all went bad one night when Dean took flesh from beneath Kip's left eye. 10. --Dean I swear to God if you don't get out of my way-- --what are you going to do Kip huh you can't just leave now Dad's sick you know that and-- --this is my only chance to get out of here, Ms. Adkison told me I'm gettin' real good-- --come on Kip-- --no this is my only chance and I'm going to take it and you're not going to stop me Dad's not going to stop me and I'm going to do something for myself for a change-- --what are you talking about Kip, what-- --for the past twenty-three years of my life I've been taking care of you and Dad Dean I didn't even get to go to college because I had to stay with ya'll and it's not fair I just want to leave-- --you haven't been taking care of-- --yeah I have Dean every night you come home with some new cut or bruise or whatever and guess who has to tape it up huh, me. now get out of my way-- 11. From there on out Dean bought every magazine that Kip was ever mentioned in, even if it was just a small tidbit of information about Kip that he already knew. 12. Twenty-first anniversary, now, Kip is off in Hollywood and Dad is half-past dead, and Dean is at the bottom of a bottle of Jack Daniels, trying to decide how to mark the occasion. He's decides on a Solomon's Knot, a subtler charm than the pentagram. At twenty-five he'd gone for the obvious instead of the arcane, at twenty-five he'd been pretty sure that by twenty-nine he'd already be rotting, he'd have gone out in a blaze of glory, or at the very least a blaze. At twenty-nine he knows better. At twenty-nine he is pretty sure he needs a little something extra to keep him out of the ditch, because the end is going to probably be brutal and unexpected and not even a little bit glorious. A little something extra, because his brother isn't around to watch his back, and maybe he never had been, not really. The guy doesn't want to do the work when Dean is obviously drunk, and they go a couple rounds about it, Dean insisting he is goddamn well able to give consent and the guy begging to differ, so in the end Dean empties his wallet out on the counter, puts down three times what the tat is worth and that is the end of the negotiation. He has the knot put just below the pentagram, only larger; the darkwork burns and the outline stings, and even with the better part of a fifth of whiskey dancing in his gut he's about to spit blood by the time the thing is done. And this time there is nobody to hold his hand, and this time there is nobody to smear on the Bacitracin where he can't quite reach, and this time he doesn't tell his father, and he doesn't go home 'til he's as sober as he can get. 13. Later that night, Dad groaning and coughing in his sleep, Dean grabs the house phone and Kip picks up after the fifth ring with an irritated What?, because now he's a celebrity and he's too good for the casual hi's and hey's and hello's. Dean says, “Did you get it?” There is a pause, and Dean can hear Kip's television being turned off. “Dean. You ask me the same question every year, and the answer is always the same. Why don't you just --” “Why not?” “I'm not like you.” “This isn't about me. This is about Mom, and --” “I don't want to be like you.” Ten seconds later, Dean says, “Dad is getting worse. I just thought you should know, and -- well. Bye.” He hangs up the phone before Kip finishes drawing his next breath, and the fresh ink under Kip's skin itches. 14. Stop me if you've heard this one. Guy and his baby-faced short drink of water little brother, well, they need each other, a lot, but they both got too big a'balls to admit it to each other and even when their Dad's on his goddamned death bed, the two fucks are too obstinate to mend their relationship. Guy is twenty-nine years old, still lives with his pa, blindly throws his fists in fights around the Dallas area, this is his way of trying to pay for Dad's healthcare, because he doesn't have much else other than his mama's uncle's fists. Baby-faced short drink of water little brother is twenty-five and lives in Hollywood, charms the magazines with his bright steel and silver words and sends a $200 check home every month for Dad while simultaneously working on a sitcom and an indie film and training for wresting and -- and Guy doesn't know that his baby brother's been training for wrestling on and off for the past year and a half, out of all the magazine clippings he's collected none of them have mentioned that, and -- and he'd be pretty pissed off if he ever found out, but that story's for another time, because -- because this is the story about how we got to the now, on the twenty-first anniversity of Caroline Brown's death, and what shaped the Brown family's dynamic the way it did. 15. This is the beginning of an era.
