Trials and the Consequence - The Path

31 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 31/10/06 I probably should have put on some clothes. The biting cold of the evening hit me as I stepped away from my home, clad in nothing but my boxers and began the slow walk up the hill to Father Pearson. Where everything had begun, so too would the new rebirth and changes be amounted. There was nothing that would get in my way of reaching the pinnacle and speaking with Pearson. He had to know the truth of the situation, and I needed to know exactly how deep he was in this whole affair. I thought quickly and decisively about the possibilities. Could there be somebody waiting for me at the church along with Father Pearson? Would there be someone who would be waiting to finish the job that whomever my captors were started? Or would the priest be what he was supposed to be; a caring and forgiving man who was willing to help in any fashion that he could? I was going to find that out, that was for certain. If I got there that is. Cuts seeped from my chest and back where they had whipped me, bruises and welts forming wherever blood had halted from flowing. It felt like I was a walking abomination, almost like a zombie in the fashion that I was slowly shuffling from one place to another, on autopilot and ignoring everything that was around me. Because people were staring, they were taking it all in with their eyes no matter how amazed they were. I only knew this because for some strange reason it was like I was watching myself walking from the bottom of that hill up to the top. There was never any indication that I couldn’t watch myself but likewise I never really had a feeling like I understood why I could do this. The soles of my feet stung like they never had before, shredding themselves on the cold hard asphalt beneath me but it was something that I didn’t care to think about. Injuries at this point were happening but something that I took in stride, continuing on up the way and figuring out how and what I was going to say to Father Pearson. Occasionally somebody would join me on this slow trek, either cajoling or jeering me, mocking how I had gone off the deep end. On the other hand some of them merely nodded and let me move on up, sometimes offering a prayer or thanks to the Lord that I was still alive. At all times I was being consoled and treated like a pariah at once. Surreal doesn’t even begin to cut it. Cars would pass me by, some of the drivers taking their time to spit or holler in my general direction without any though, idiotically getting their useless banter in so they could feel part of the moment. They weren’t though, they never could be because this was a personal journey from the start. This was something I was doing on my own. I fell for the first time, but it wouldn’t stop me. The whole affair with Drakewerx, if it was Drakewerx, puzzled me. They had taken me in, tortured me, punished me, and then simply let me go. They aspired to some sort of god complex whoever these people were that was for sure but they ditched me because they didn’t want to do the dirty work of finishing me off in the end. It bothered me, like I still had some sort of part to play in their twisted games like I was important to them. I didn’t want any of that crap being laid down upon me but it seemed to happen all the time now. What’s more is the involvement of both Father Pearson and Sylvia. Up until that night I had assumed that Sylvia was nothing more than a woman who was looking for a career. On that point I had been right, but I was missing a crucial piece of the puzzle; that of her involvement with another employer. Then too you had her supposed relationship with Father Pearson that was intriguing and quizzical at once. At no point had I intimated that she was involved with him until it was too late so naturally I now wanted to know the answers. I fell for the second time but it would not let me halt. There were questions I needed to pose to Pearson, questions that needed informative and revealing answers if I was to figure out where to head next. There were also bargains and deals to be made which would have ramifications from here on out. For one thing, I was going to have to bury the hatchet with Pearson, hoping that we would strive together on a new course of action, whatever that may be. Unfortunately I didn’t even know what that course was yet. A searing gout of pain flew from the bottom of my heel upwards, connecting all along the nerves as it went and practically paralyzing me. I fell for the last time and as I did so I looked up to find that I had arrived. I was at the church of Kingston, the place which I had called my home for some time now, a church that had never welcomed me irrespective of how much I contributed to the community. Behind me I could see a crowd gathering to watch my actions. As I stood up and looked to the church I knew one thing. Today I was going to be welcomed, whether Father Pearson liked it or not.
