Going Home (Part 1 of 2)

Date: 25/6/05 From the graves of patriot men and women, spring LIVING NATIONS - Padraig Pearse d.3rd May 1916 God Bless Padraig Pearse 1. Reminiscing Paddy gazed out at an outstanding view that brought it all back… Childhood, happy times, his father. Not words Paddy would usually associate together to form a sentence because of his sometimes frustrating upbringing but there was a time that that was different that Paddy would cherish for the rest of his life. A time when his father embraced him as his loving son. Embraced him with the type of ferocity that seemed to make up for the countless years of neglect or mistreatment as an outlet valve for his father’s anger at life in general. This was the very reason that Paddy didn’t hate his father. He didn’t like his father personally but he loved him if that makes sense. And the occasion that he now remembers (inspired by the beach on which he’s beside) has reminded him of this…that his father does mean everything to him It was a peninsula far out along the coast road. It was shaped like an apple that had been eaten into on both sides. The beach was long and curving that stretched for perhaps a mile long. From the distance it looked golden and unearthly, almost like a talisman from the sun. Paddy walked across the sand dunes wearing an uncharacteristic suit. It consisted of a black and pinstriped jacket and matching trousers and underneath was a soft blue shirt (which was worn open collar). His shoes were polished to a tee and as he plodded over the sands dunes that were scarcely covered in coarse green grass, tiny fragments of sand began to stick to the them due to the moistness of their surface. As he gazed at it, contented despite the relentless beating of the sun that felt like repeated hammer blows to the back, he wondered at the meaning of them…tiny particles, sands that were once rock and have endured the test of time. What would happen when all the rock was sand? Paddy quickly pushed this thought away. He was a clever and sharp man. His lack of formal education left him with some literacy problems perhaps but he picked everything else along the way in the journey of life. This little paradox could be left to later; right now he had more pressing issues such as facing the family. Paddy’s line of vision swooped to the right and he gazed at the beaches peripheral. The long headland at the sea end of the beach consisted of solid great boulders that had resisted the pounding of the centuries. They were scarred and battered yes, but they still stood proudly protecting the sand of the beaches. This is where we sat Paddy was staring directly at a rounded out boulder. A closer inspection revealed the moss the rock had collected. Just like it had been seventeen years prior, such is the fondness of that day that Paddy was able to lodge the most minuet details into his long term memory. We changed behind those reeds Paddy was now looking at a nearby cluster of long grass protruding from the sand. And we picnicked down beside the beach … Oh…t’feel tha’ type o’ feelin’ again… 2. 14th June 1989 He was eight years old at the time when they went to the seaside. It was and still is called Lisfannon beach, a cosy little setting on the West Coast of Ireland that stared towards America. Paddy sometimes now wondered if the fond memories had inspired his movement to America, but who knows what the extent of the sub-conscious’ influence is Danny O’Shea had brought three of his children with him – Patrick, Owen and Aoife. Three men and a little lady. Patrick was the oldest at eight years old with the other two at six years (who were twins though not identical). Another two of Danny’s offspring were at the caravan with their mother with another three yet to be born to her. Danny watched the three run hand in hand as soon as they entered the beach over the protruding dunes that smoothed their way along his line of vision. Danny chose this beach because there wasn’t anyone here as they usually went to the adjacent beach to sunbath, picnic or swim. His father had taken him here as a youth and now he felt it was only right the pass on this minor element of the O’Shea dynasty to his children. Then Aoife stopped suddenly and picked something from the ground and came running back towards him “Oh, oh, oh!” she screamed with delight, clapping her hands in joy. The large grin that spread across her face and the tiny row of teeth that protruded instantly melted his heart. She was breathless; her eyes shining with her arms outstretched revealing a cloister shell. “Look daddy – it’s pretty” “Tha’ it be pet. Go see wha’ else ye can find” Danny replied with a warm smile “Okay daddy!” And with an excited little scream she was off again with her arms spread, her hair flying in the gentle cooling (and much appreciated) breeze. Danny was wearing an old plain red cap which had it’s peak folded into a point that shaded his handsome and strong face. He was carrying a pull string bag over his shoulder that he checked every so often to ensure was still pulled closed. Force of habit he would think. He walked over to the rocks closest to him and in a spot where he could watch his children who were now chasing seagulls frantically. “Kiddies, comere a minute t’yer daddy!” Danny called down. It was a shout that was soft unlike his usual barking at his children. He wished they were old enough and he wished he was man enough to tell his children he did love them and his shouting was only his way of coping with life with them being able to understand. That was really no excuse and he knew it but he wished he had that at least for a comfort of some sort “Do ye’s want t’go swimming?” “YES, YES DADDY” They eerily called in perfect unison. So alike and close he thought “But we have no suits daddy”, young Paddy remarked cautiously as he swayed from side to side with his arms behind his back “Don’ worry kiddies, aye brought-“ Danny pulled opened the bag and allowed the contents to fall out “…this” What lay on the warm sand was a small pile of swimwear containing three pairs of swim togs and a one-piece bathing suit. Freshly bought too for this occasion, which was unlike Danny who found himself financially challenged. But maybe just this one time he would give them something to really smile about. “Yahooo!!” Owen called. He always was the cheeky one that only added to his overall charm Owen seemed to be determined to change right where he stood until- “Wha’ are ye doin’ ye eejit! Change o’er here incase someone sees ye!” Paddy remarked with a cute scowl The three hoddled off in a line behind the rushes with Paddy in charge. Danny always admired the little chap’s maturity but was sometimes concerned he was growing too quick too fast. By God, with the way life was going, he would do anything to be under his father’s wing again. It was tough being strong for any family in times of crisis never mind a family of seven that was still growing. Danny himself changed too where he stood. The beach was empty anyway. He pulled on a pair of swimming trunks and ran into the sea. It was cool enough so he swam out into the deep and dove. When he resurfaced and spewed the salt water from his mouth, allowing the sensation of it running over his face, he saw his children running over to the shores edge. Owen and Aoife were waving at him with slightly worried grins but Paddy had darted on past them and directly into the water. “Daddy. Help me swim like ye!” Aoife called as Owen looked on paddling. By this stage Paddy was running through seaweed with a delighted cheer Danny swam back to them with a soothing smile on his face “Come on” he said holding out his hand to Aoife, as he knelt waist deep in the water. She came to him with all the trepidation of a child in the water for the first time. He had to coax her by repeatedly trying to calm her anxiety with “Come on, aye’ve got ye”. He saw the conflicting fear yet anticipation and sense of adventure in her shocking blue eyes. Eventually she caught his hand and he gripped her waist and held her vertically “Now kick yer feet and move yer arms” She screwed up her face and began thrusting her feet, screaming when her face bobbed under water so he placed a hand on her chin to protect her as his fatherly instincts instructed him. Then he helped Owen too. He was little braver now that he seen how it was done. He eventually became bold enough that he felt like he could perhaps do it himself. After being submerged on two occasions, he gave up with a spluttered cough. So Danny showed them how to look for cockles in the shallow water, finding six before the two could identify between themselves the tell tale holes that would lead to them being dug up from the sand. Danny then turned to Paddy who had taken to swimming like a fish to water. He paddled like a dog for a while until he built up the confidence to attempt a poorly constructed breaststroke that ultimately led to his head falling beneath the water. Danny wadded out to him and put a caring hand on his shoulder. “Good boy son, now yer getting’ it. Jus’ keep at it” Paddy looked up at him beaming “Aye can swim like you daddy!” “Ye sure can son…ye know that…aye…euh…love ye?” Danny was surprised as he felt his cheeks flushing when he considered his obvious embarrassment at addressing a child in this way. Paddy only looked quizzingly before smiling and going back to more motivated swimming attempts After frolicking in the changing blue and green water for a while, Danny walked up to the rock, which had served as their HQ for the last half hour. He thought of his outraged protective feelings for his feelings and questioned himself over why he hadn’t done this before. “We better eat” Danny murmured to himself. “Kiddies! Come here t’yer daddy! Look wha’ aye’ve got!” The three sprinted up with tongues hanging from their mouth. They waddled up like penguins Danny thought, not taking any time to bask in the glorious sunlight. Danny pulled a small blue lunchbox from the pull string bag and walked down towards the beach to meet them. When they met, they all knelt in the sand in unison revealing how close the family unit of the O’Shea’s was despite outside perception. Danny opened it to reveal cooked chicken, lemonade, a packet of crackers, cheese and chocolate. He broke the chicken into portions. “Here ye’s go”. He placed a piece of chicken into each of the enthusiastic outstretched hands. Before watching it being bitten into. They didn’t each much however as they were obviously anxious to get back to the sea promptly. Danny simply nibbled at the food he had brought and then after, packing the remains, he lay back in the sand. It didn’t irritate his body or even feel very coarse. Instead, it simply radiated the heat energy it was continually gathering from the sun and emitting it through him. It was satisfyingly overwhelming. The children played at the beach for a few hours before Danny called them to go home. Their devastated faces broke Danny’s heart to the extent that he gave them another ten minutes. His eventually managed to get the reluctant children leaving after threatening to leave them behind in jest. As Paddy trailed behind the rest and as he exited the beach, he took one final look at the beach. It was holy to him, even now as he remembers these events of that day. Almost like heaven on earth or your closed possible alternative 3. Almost There Paddy smiled as he ran the memories through his head again. He was driving now; along a country road in an old Toyota Carina, a car his cousin from Dublin had lent him for his two day return to home. It was one day since his father had phoned him for the first time since he’d left for America. “Hello…son?” He asked warily, almost expecting his son’s fist to punch him through the receiver of the phone he was holding to his ear. “Da! Aye…be glad ye called. Where are ye callin’ fro’?” Paddy had responded in unconcealed welcome “From yer Aunt Sheila’s…if ye need me in future ye can just leave a message to her phone aye suppose. Look Paddy, aye just want to apologise” Paddy found his face contorting into confusion. “Wha’ ye mean apologise. Fer wha’? “Well, ye know aye doubted ye when ye left fer America. Ye know aye told ye tha’ ye were a fool. Well, aye didn’ mean it.” “Oh…” Paddy responded as he allowed this new feeling of confusion wash over him. His father did not apologise, twenty-four years on Gods green earth had taught him that. “Yer not a fool. Infact yer a lot smarter than aye’ll ever be. And strong too. In yer head aye mean. Yer a credit t’me and yer mother and after aye hear ye’re doing so well at wrestling, aye realised aye just felt aye had t’tell ye…ye were right and aye was wrong.” Danny hadn’t held anything back right there when he said that and Paddy finally seen his father like he’d seen him in 1989. It took a while but it was always better late than never. Paddy remembered how they chatted openly for a while on trivial matters. Paddy was pleased to finally find a more friendly side to his father that he could grow to like as well as love. Then Danny asked him to come home to see the relatives. Danny revealed he’d arranged plans for the family to meet at a local hall in his hometown for the night after. That event was now going to be tonight after Paddy flew over night. Paddy had of course agreed enthusiastically After driving for a while at a comfortable speed down the small road, Paddy began to notice the small details of the poorly conditioned car. The windscreen was dirty with dried water stains arching over the glass. The dash had gathered a layer of dust that was yet to be removed. And the heat was pounding upon him now. There was no conditioning in the car and even with every window down, he felt the full force of the sun sweltering him. He predicted he was still fifteen miles away from his town. Fifteen miles before he got to gaze on perhaps a rejuvenated father. The thought didn’t seem overly hopeful to Paddy at this present time. He was looking forward to meeting his siblings too and Aoife and Owen in particular whom he could perhaps discuss the beach day’s events with. Yet despite this feeling of pending joy, his newfound career was always on his mind. Sometimes swaying to the depths of his thoughts, sometimes at his minds forefront. Sunday’s Fresh!Burst… Emerald Isle vs. The Full House… The Alliance League’s Final always on his mind… After Tuesday’s Fresh and Emerald Isle’s second consecutive win since their debut almost two weeks ago, the Alliance title belts was ever closer to being around the waists of Crimson O’Malec and Paddy O’Shea. In less than a week after Fresh!Burst would be Paddy’s debut PPV – Solarised. Paddy knew how much that event meant to the combatants of AWC. Many hadn’t been at the first PPV, Zero To Hero and now the newcomers were anxious to prove they could perform at the big stage. Paddy of course was no different. A singles match was on the agenda for him followed by a possible ladder match for the Alliance titles…but to get that opportunity, Emerald Isle would have to notch up top (or next the best) number of points in the Alliance league. And being top of the Alliance league table at the moment, Emerald Isle are bound to be confident. However, Paddy knew to keep his feet on the ground and before he even got to Solarised, his battle with The Full House had to be dealt with. Paddy had found them interesting characters from what he seen on the Fresh! shows. Hilarious at times and he had respect for them as true combatants following their win over Krimzon Karnage Inc. But that didn’t take away from the fact that Paddy of course put his desire for the belts before his respect for them. The combination of Paddy’s brawler techniques and Crimsons high flying innovative style was something The Full House could not deal with, Paddy was sure of it. Emerald Isle brought this unlikely pairing of styles together. They would be brutal yet devastating from all angles. And that was also a guarantee. The team of Emerald Isle could never be stronger - in the environment of America, their feelings of insecurity brought them together into a strong unit that was something he didn’t believe The Full House could emulate. They would strike, like every night, with unrelenting ferocity until the belts were around their waste. Paddy now only felt sorry that The Full House was standing in their way. Like a barrier obstructing a raging bull, it would be knocked down… END
