The Saga Of The Kinslayer: Part 1

27 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 27, 2006 The winds in the suburbs of New York were quite chilling this time of year. The houses were all quite and dark at this hour, except for one of them. On this house lived a priest. Not just any priest though, the leader of the Opus Dei in New York City. He woke up every morning inside his million dollar house and everyday he drove his fifty-thousand dollar car to the city to tell everyone else that they were sinners. He was not righteous by any means. That was easy to tell by the box in the little closet full of drugs and cash. It was also easy to tell by the expensive hookers that he paid for every week. This night, it happened to be one of those nights. The time where those vows of priesthood are hidden in a little box until the act is done and the money paid. The priest had just finished putting his clothes back on and the hooker was fixing herself up so that she could continue making her living. The priest lied back on the bed after putting the money on the counter. She took it and left through the door. When the priest finally got to relaxing he heard the scream of the hooker loud and close. He moved quickly and as he tried to get out the door he was pushed back by the hooker. She was sobbing uncontrollably and she was scared for her life. The priest had yet to see what had scared the woman so badly, but he would soon come face to face with him. The huge mass of body came through the door. The priest’s eyes widened with fear and the hooker could do nothing but sob uncontrollably. The priest got on his knees and began to pray. “Padre nuestro que estas en los cielos, santificado sea tu nombre, venga a nosotros tu reino, hágase tu voluntad así en la tierra como en el cielo, danos hoy nuestro pan de cada día, perdona nuestras ofensas, así como también nosotros perdonamos a los que nos ofenden, no nos dejes caer en tentación y líbranos de todo mal, Amén.” Dagoth Kinslayer could do nothing more than laugh. He had been there before, in front of priests staring at their deaths, but none of them had been as fearful as this one. None of them had that peculiar smell that gave it away. The same thing wolves could smell when they were about to finish of their prey. The priest concentrated even more on his prayers, but he knew that they were to be in vain. “Padre De La Torre?” The priest heard the gravity of the situation on the stranger’s voice. Was this a devil spawn or does God send people like this to eradicate the sinners inside of his church? “Sí. I am Padre De La Torre.” Dagoth took his sword out of its sheath and pointed it towards the priest. The ruby incrusted into the horse-shaped hilt glowed with the light almost the same way Dagoth’s eye glowed. “Do you know why I am here, Father?” “You are a spawn of the devil here to threaten His children! I will not fall for ‘his’ tricks!” Dagoth roared a chilling laughter that the priest and the hooker felt on their bones. It was nothing like they had ever heard and nothing they had ever seen. To them they felt like they had been transported to a long forgone era. They were not that far off. “You priests with your righteousness. You know the real reason I am here and she knows it as well. You have been a very bad priest, Father, and I am here to collect your soul.” The priest gave him a look of sheer terror as he remembered a deal he made long ago. His life had gone awry, he had failed on his business venture, he had failed in being a husband and he had failed at being a father. He wandered the streets aimlessly trying to figure out what had made such a promising young and happy man such a failure. He wondered why God had taken it out on him. One day, as he wandered through the streets he met a stranger. He had kind eyes and helped him with some food that day. He said his name was Heilil and that he was a Watcher. That didn’t mean anything to the priest, until now. They spoke about the man’s fall of grace and he remembered Heilil talking about his own fall and his subsequent plans to go back up. “What would you do to get yourself back up?” The stranger asked him. “I would sell my soul to the devil,” the priest had answered without knowing who he was talking to. “Shake my hand and you have a deal,” Heilil told the priest. They shook hands then. Heilil kind eyes disappeared and a he could see something different about him. He didn’t pay mind though. He was living on the streets and he needed a new life. “Here is some money for you to survive. Here is what you must do. You will join the church and many years from now, when I really need you, I will come to collect.” The man walked away after giving the priest the money but before he left, the priest could hear the words. “Don’t forget.” The mysterious man disappeared. Now that he remembered and knew what was going on, he couldn’t help but feel sorry for himself. He had not known what he had done, but he had still done it and no matter what his service for God had been, his soul had already been branded. “Heilil?” “No. I am not this Heilil, I am just another like you.” Dagoth sliced the blade of his sword horizontally at the kneeling priest. The body stood motionless for a second before the blood started to pour from neck. A few moments later the head fell to the floor followed by the body, making a crimson pool right besides the hooker. She screamed. She could not believe her eyes. A man had been decapitated in front of her. She tried to run, but Dagoth grabbed her by the hair and pulled her close to him. His huge arm enveloped her as he whispered in her ear. “Do something you can be proud of.” He let her go. She turned around and saw his face one last time. She saw something in his eyes that she had not seen that whole time. A hint of good was still deep down inside. There was still hope for the monster. She turned around and ran towards the door. Before she could reach the front door, the figure of a man manifested itself in front of her. The dark cloak covered him completely but the red glow of his eyes was unmistakable. He raised a hand and a fireball shot from it, completely pulverizing the hooker. The figured laughed maniacally. “Don’t you just love their look when they are about to die? It is priceless, Dagoth. You should learn to enjoy it for you will be seeing it for a long time. You are immortal now; you should not be concerned with the matters of mere mortals.” Dagoth turned away from Ur, but he could not turn away from his own deal with the devil. He would always be his pawn in this chess game between him and God. “When are you going to tell me Attila’s whereabouts? You have been promising him to me for years and I am tired of waiting.” “I told you where he is. You need to find him yourself. I can’t do everything for you.” Dagoth couldn’t take it anymore. The presence of Ur made him uneasy. He knew he could not trust the man, but he also knew that there was nowhere else he could go to. He was trapped. He opened the door and left the house. Ur stayed behind looking deep inside him, trying to hear what Dagoth was thinking. He had learned to hide his thoughts though. “Good. You are learning something from these menial tasks. Soon you will be more powerful than the Watchers put together.” At the same time he said that, another figure appeared besides Ur. “I don’t know what you see in him. I am perfectly capable of leading your armies against the Archangel and God. Remember, I was his throne’s caretaker. I know the weakness to defeating him.” Ur smiled under his cloak. It was amusing seeing how much the great angel Heilel Sheten had fallen. He once was the powerful leader of The Watchers and now third in line. Dagoth would become more powerful than Heilel could ever wish to become. “Let me remind you of your failure all those years ago. We were so close of thwarting His plans, but you had to be so hasty and ruined everything. This war would have been over long ago.” Heilel turned towards Ur and grabbed the cloak where the neck should have been, but when he grabbed Ur had already disappeared and reappeared behind him. “The Nephilim failed, it was not me, Azazel or whatever it is you call yourself these days.” Ur’s eyes flared red once again as his anger level kept rising. Hearing Heilel whine about whose fault it was kept getting on his nerves. “It doesn’t matter now, with Dagoth on our ranks, we will finally succeed. It is our time to be in power of the Heavens. It is his time to come down here and see for himself what he has created. It is time for him to see his failed experiment first hand.” Ur disappeared. Heilel was left alone to wonder just how long it would be until God sent his own messengers to this so called savior of the Hells. He must be waiting until he is vulnerable to strike. “Araqiel, follow him. He will surely send one of the seven to him soon.” The fallen angel left the house to follow Dagoth. Heilel disappeared as soon as the other demon had left. ----- Dagoth spent most of his time alone. It was the middle of the night and he was sitting on a bench in a park cleaning the blade of his sword. He carefully analyzed his situation. He had no way of releasing himself from Ur’s grip except by doing whatever he wanted. He was a slave and nothing more, but before the world ended and humanity was destroyed he would have his revenge. He would go up to the man that ordered his family killed and he would slice him into pieces. He could still smell the sweet scent of her hair. He could still see her beautiful face every single time he closed his eyes. His family had been everything to him. There were some nights where he would dream about them as they used to be, happy, and then all of a sudden they would all have no heads. It was painful, but he would wake up every night and he would continue of this quest. It had been a little hard adjusting to the technology of this century, but Ur had transferred a lot of knowledge from his host’s mind to Dagoth and that had helped him. If it wasn’t for that, he would have no idea what a car or a television was. The thing Dagoth hated the most about this era was the loss of honor. It was lacking from the everyday lives of people. They didn’t live like he had, where you had to watch your back every time. They don’t go through each day as if it their last and that is the reason for their overall mind-numbing lives. They don’t experience the thrill of holding a sword in your hand and escaping death by just a few inches. Those things are now lost. The kings of old were always greedy. They wanted more lands and more power, but not the common man. The everyday man just wanted to survive that day and be able to feed his family. That is all that Dagoth wanted, that and the honor of being the General of one of the most ruthless armies in history. These days though, everyone wanted to be their own emperor. He only wanted glory for Attila, more than he cared for himself. He knew his place in history, but Attila couldn’t stand to see someone shine brighter than himself, even when that person didn’t intend it. Thus Attila betrayed him. It still made his blood boil to know that they were best friends once. He knew something now though; he understood that he could not trust anyone, neither the most evil demon nor the purest angel. He could not even trust his senses. His own eyes could betray him. Life was never an easy thing to go through but if he wanted to finish what he started, he had to keep going. He stood up, morning was already upon him and he didn’t want to be in the park when the people started to pour in. Not because he feared them but because he didn’t want to bring attention to himself, not when a man had been decapitated with a sword not far from that same spot. Not that the cops could do anything, but he didn’t want to kill any more innocent people if they didn’t help him get to his goal. As he walked towards his apartment, he could feel eyes on him, and not just one set. He had learned not long ago to sense when someone was following him, watching him and those senses were sending him signals that it was happening right at that moment. He looked back and with the corner of his eye he saw it. It was only a shadow, but there was no one there, so it could only be one of Ur’s minions. Dagoth took a left into an alley and waited patiently for whoever was behind him to arrive to that spot. As he saw the shadow suddenly appear, he grabbed at it, only for a figure to appear, it extended its black wings and shot a fireball into Dagoth’s chest. Dagoth flew back and hit his back into a dumpster. He cracked his neck and unsheathed his sword. “Who are you, demon?” The demon tried to fly away but Dagoth threw a dagger at him and struck him on the leg. The silver blood started to pour down to the ground. People were starting to fill the streets and Dagoth was fighting an invisible demon. Only a few people can actually see demons and angels, so it wasn’t Dagoth’s worry, but someone could still get hurt. The demon shot at Dagoth again but he was able to dodge the attack. Before he could charge, a beautiful voice could be heard. The words were like music to his ears. “Leave, demon, in the name of God!” The demon shrieked and disappeared in a puff of smoke. The beautiful angel landed gracefully in front of Dagoth. He hid his wings and normal street clothes appeared on him. His face was like nothing he had never seen. “Walk with me, Kinslayer.” They started to walk towards the apartment side by side. They were silent for a while until they arrived at the apartment. No words were said in all that time. When Dagoth began to walk towards the door of the building, the wings of the angel reappeared. “He just wants you to know that you do have a choice. You are not alone Dagoth, never have been and never will. Keep in touch.” “Who are you?” “Uriel, the Archangel Uriel. I am one of the Seven. Call on me anytime you wish, but remember that we don’t have much time… war is brewing and it will be here soon, very soon.” The angel disappeared in a bright light. He could still picture his face. He wondered something every time he met an angel. Where was his wife? Back in their time the Huns were pagan and they didn’t believe in this God. Was his wife in heaven? His daughters? He decided that the next time he saw one of them he would ask. Life had just gotten a whole lot more interesting for Dagoth Kinslayer.
