Chapter 1: Always

13 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1.13.06 “Darcy, give me one good reason why I shouldn’t fire you here and now…” These were probably the last words Darcy Markson wanted to hear. Jack MacKempf, his boss at the Tomorrow Institute had not been a supporter of Darcy taking a sabbatical to rejoin the wrestling world, and had voiced as much to Darcy on several occasions. However, he was now especially displeased to learn that Darcy’s “wrestling vacation” as Jack had referred to it was going to be for a lot longer than expected. “Come on, Jack…what do you want me to do?” Darcy responded, propping his feet up on a nearby desk as he conversed with his employer over the telephone in his Cincinnati hotel room. Despite his extensive run in wrestling all those years prior, and all the traveling that went along with it, somehow Darcy had never been to Cincinnati. As he gazed out the window at the city skyline, he recalled what his good friend Johnny Hawk told him when he passed through the quiet Ohio metropolis a few years ago: “It’s like any other Midwestern city…not a bad place to live, but I can’t imagine anybody having the conversation ‘You know? I’ve had enough of this two-bit town. I’ve got to see some action. Pack up everybody, we’re going to CINCINNATI!’ ” Darcy smiled, silently echoing the sentiments, but his boss drew him back in. “You assured me that it wasn’t going to take this long…I seem to recall you saying you expected to be back before the new year.” “Well, obviously it’s taking a little longer than I expected,” Darcy said in kind, clearly annoyed that he was being forced to have this conversation. It was times like these that Darcy was thankful he didn’t take a mindless, white-collar job; taking that kind of tone with a superior as high up as MacKempf would have cost him his job in the blink of an eye. But at the Tomorrow Institute, the cause that they all worked for was far more important than any inter-office squabbling. Darcy did his job well, and knew his place was secure despite Jack’s threats. “I’m sorry, but I’m going to need more time.” “Like I said, Darcy…give me one good reason – ” “Because you could use the publicity, Jack. It’s sad to admit, but the words of celebrities carry a lot more weight than some Joe Schmuck, even if he has some great ideas. How do you think Jesse Ventura got to be governor? How do you think Arnold pulled it off?” Silence was all Darcy heard on the other line. He was right, and Jack knew it. “I’m sorry Jack, but this is something I really have to do. It looks like it might be awhile before I’m back. But just work with me, and I can find a way to make this work to both of our benefits.” More silence, before Jack finally responded. “….Okay.” “Okay?” “Yes! Fine! But you’re off the payroll.” Darcy laughed. “You’re a wise man, Jack. Don’t let anybody ever push you around.” With that, the sound of the disconnecting *click* signified that Jack was finished discussing the matter. Just as well, Darcy thought to himself as he hung up. He had more important things to worry about. Aimz. Somehow she had not matched Darcy’s enthusiasm about the two facing off, one on one, to finally settle a score that desperately needed settling after so many years. Somehow he had underestimated the fact that she might be reluctant to square off against a guy she had spent the last four years trying to forget. What were her words, exactly? 'I think you're full of shit, that's what I think. I think you want to use the celebrity I earned myself. You're eeking in because you probably flipped open a magazine or went on the internet and noticed 'Oh, damn... Amy's a big name now…you did nothing but throw my life into a pit, so why should I let you take advantage of my name just because you think you're going to launch some spectacular comeback?” Darcy let out a sigh. She had a point: Four years had passed. Their classic rivalry had been forgotten about. She had spent the time since making a name for herself and doing a damn good job of it; Darcy had vanished from the scene almost entirely, save for an extremely brief comeback in 2002. They no longer stood on equal ground – Aimz stood to gain nothing by putting her reputation on the line against a man whose most notable wrestling accomplishment in the past few years was putting world-class jobber Danny Oshun through a table in an OSW training-facility matchup that nobody saw. Add in the fact that his return out of nowhere could have already caused a deep emotional wound, and even Darcy could now see why she had been so hesitant. As epic as the saga of Aimz and Darcy Crisis had been back in the old Pier Six Wrestling, it paled in comparison to what happened behind the scenes between Amy Campbell and Darcy Markson. He knew that if he was to have any success in appealing to Amy for the Aimz/Crisis match, he would have to get her to somehow bury the hatchet on their torrid past – at least briefly – and reach her on a strictly professional basis. And to do that, he was going to have to prove in front of everybody that he could still compete. This had led to Darcy secretly approaching AWC management following Winter Warfare and asking to be put on the first Fresh! card of the new year. Darcy’s thoughts now turned to Jason Warr, the man he was scheduled to face in his first real match in years. He didn’t know a whole lot about Warr; apparently he and Teresa Tomes had just come over from HSW – whatever THAT stood for. Ironically enough (or perhaps purposely, knowing the Entertainment Managers’ flair for drama), Crisis would be facing Warr, while their rivals, Aimz and Tomes, would be facing each other in a Four Way Fury match later in the evening. Things definitely stand a chance of getting muddled, Darcy noted. Yet Darcy was not terribly worried. As the news hype for the Fresh! Card had noted, both Crisis and Warr were looking to begin illustrious runs in the AWC with a victory. But there was definitely a lot more on the line for Darcy. “Warr may want to put me away in his debut,” Darcy said aloud, to no one in particular. “He may want it badly. But I NEED it. I HAVE to have it. If I don’t win, how will Aimz ever take me seriously?” Of course, there was still the matter of convincing Amy that the match had to take place, Darcy thought to himself, deciding it weird to speak aloud in a room devoid of human presence other than his own. But first things first – he needed an impressive showing against Warr, or everything he worked for would be all for naught. While many might fold under that kind of pressure, these sorts of situations were the kind Darcy lived for. After all, he thought, when your options are win or go home, what else can you do but win? Darcy smiled, almost feeling sorry for Warr…certainly this would be no walk in the park for him, but there was no way Warr had any idea that his adversary had all his chips in the pot on this one, and that he might very well have to kill Darcy Crisis if he had any hope of a victory. Still, Darcy needed a game plan, and a drink. Fortunately, he knew where to get one of them. Fumbling for his room key in his wallet, Darcy lost his grip and the wallet fell to the ground, falling open on some of the pictures and other random crap he kept inside. He rarely paid those things any mind – they mostly consisted of family photos, business cards and the like. But as fate would have it, Darcy caught a glimpse of a picture he didn’t even know he had…a picture of Amy. He stared intently into her brown and blue eyes; A trait 1 in 200,000 possesses, he could almost hear her saying aloud, as she proudly had on numerous occasions. Remembering what was on the other side of the picture, Darcy flipped it to see the words she had scrawled on the back – the words to an old Saliva song that Amy told him ran through her head every time she thought of him. “I love you…I hate you…I can’t live without you…” A flood of memories came back…none of which Darcy was willing to sort through at the moment. While he knew that leaving Amy all those years ago was the right choice, he had always regretted causing her such pain, and hoped that when all of this was over the two would be able to reconcile. But for the moment, he had to focus on Jason Warr – and the road to his match with Aimz. Darcy picked up his wallet and left the room, in search of the hotel bar. I’ve got a lot of work ahead of me, he thought to himself.
