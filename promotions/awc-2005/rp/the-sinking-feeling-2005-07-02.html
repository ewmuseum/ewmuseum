The Sinking Feeling

2 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/2/05 An introduction? You should have already forgotten me by now. Or at least that’s what I’m used to. I expect that, after hearing this, you’ll forget who I am and just say “Drake who?” The thing is, though, I’m not very forgettable. If I was in a crowd, a blind beggar could spot me out. Maybe it’s the bandages that cover every square millimeter of my body, maybe its my shy gestures, I don’t know. But its not that you CAN’T remember me…its that you just don’t want to. Right now, this is when any sensible human being starts the “Oh, I could never forget a guy like you!” Save it. I know what happens. You have that conversation, that interaction, and after you become freaked out, you put me in the back of your head where I join the rest of your lost memories…well, don’t worry, I’m used to it by now. I suppose, though, that maybe you want to hear the tale of Drake Coleman, that maybe you want to see how my AWC adventure will unfold, if my little sanity I have left will make it out alive…but, wanting to know all that will mean that you’ll NEED to know my past. So, where do I start?... When I was younger, I joined the army and, right around that time, we were in Vietnam. For two years, I was over there serving my country, until one day we were ambushed and an explosion and God knows what. Long story short, I got burns all over my body and, for year, I was stuck in a bed recovering. Through out these years I began to lose memory of my past, my beautiful youth escaping me like water through a strainer, with only the dregs left. I was going crazy, speaking to myself, having hallucinations…nothing pretty. So, after years I was finally let out…but things were different. I was stronger, faster, more capable of reading attacks. Not knowing where to go, I booked it to Asia and learned the martial arts and becoming quite the fighter. So good that I never lost one match in the underground death leagues…though I suppose that’s obvious. I mean, if I lost, I wouldn’t be here today, would I? After missing my country, though, I decided to venture back and I went into the big city of Boston, Massachusetts…but things angered me, upset me. My country had betrayed me. No one recognized me, people shied away from me. In Asia I had respect from looking and fighting like a beast…but here, nothing. So I traveled the country, trying to get money and being shunned like a bum. After years of starving and nothingness, I decided that the only way I could help myself was to inflict the pain and misery I was feeling into others through fighting. Professional fighting took years to get into, and the only one that really earned you a living was the too-tame boxing. So, I learned how to wrestle, and to be honest, I became quite good. Forever, though, I am the outcast, the shadow of my former self. Forever, I am The Bonfire, not Drake Coleman. I am a mummy, a body of burns and bandages that reflects a former me. As many people say, though, what is done is done, and we have to move on. So here I am, standing in the bathroom of a seedy motel room, staring at the mirror. Staring back at myself is that man, that man I am not. Every day I brought myself to the mirror to remind me of me, to show me that this is what you have become! It was saddening, but… *KNOCK! KNOCK! KNOCK!* I jumped as the door startled me, turning my head quickly and glaring towards the direction of the room. “What?” I screamed out with hostility, as if whoever was at the door was causing a true burden for me. “A letter for you, Mr. Coleman!” The boy’s squeaky voice reeked with puberty, his nervous tone most likely unintentional. I stormed to the door and opened it up, ripping the letter out of his hand before I could glance at him and then slammed the door. I had to stop, though. What was I doing? I had never been this angry over something so petty, something so foolish like this. Usually, I coyly make my way to the door and he hands me the letter. What was doing this to me? Was I that engulfed in myself that I was boiling inside? I needed a breath of fresh air, fast. Stepping outside, I leaned against the railing of the building, staring at the tacky pool below me. Most every light was off, being it was 3 am, with just barely lit ones on. This motel was…well…it wasn’t the greatest of places, but it was something I could afford. The air was nice, though, as each breath completed me. As I stood there staring into nothingness with my straight face, a little girl walked up and stood next to me, staring at me. I could see her out of the corner of my eye, but I made no reaction towards her at all, making pretend she wasn’t there. “Hey mister,” she said, “What are you doing?” Her voice was sweet and innocent to match her pink night gown that covered her whole body up. She was about 2 feet, which made her about 3 and a half, maybe 3 and ¾, feet smaller than I. She had cute little pig tails and a cherubic face with the baby fat still on it. “Nothing.” I said solemnly and coldly. I still didn’t want to talk to her, because kids made the worst reactions. They’re the ones who always cried when they saw me covered in bandages, or the ones who always pointed and laughed. I really don’t need that. “Well,” she said, speaking her mind, “Then what darned thing are you staring at?” She wasn’t going to give up at all, I could tell. She must’ve escaped her room, her mom or her dad or whoever it was asleep and, looking around, I was the only one here. “The stars, maybe?” It was dark outside, with the sky black and cold with specks of light dotted about. They were stars, but not the kind you see in the old westerns. It was as if someone was like “Oh yeah, crap, forgot to put those there!” and just dotted some crappy things in the sky. It was still something, though. “My brother said that the stars were made when God farts.” Ah, the innocence. What she said would make any other person laugh…but I was in no mood to laugh. I don’t know what was becoming of me, I was less sullen and miserable and more angry than I have ever been. And now this little girl is softening me up to…to… I still hadn’t said a thing yet, and now she was standing there, staring at the stars with me. We both stood there for at least ten minutes before another word was uttered. “Whats your name, mister?” She had a faint little smile on her face, still seeming to not be able to understand what it was like to communicate with the people who “look weird”. There was no approaching the words carefully, because she didn’t even care that I looked the way I looked. It was as if I was living in the world before Vietnam, as if these horrendous burns had never even covered my body. “I’m…I’m Drake.” I was stuttering, as if I had never spoken before in my life. It was as if I was surprised I was even able to speak, as if I’d only spoken some other language before. “Whats yours?” I managed to force out, confused at this. “I’m…” “HONEY! GET OVER HERE!” Simultaneously the little girl and I turned our heads towards the noise. It was a large, southern lady, her clothes obviously way to small to fit her “petite” figure. She raced over, her fat wobbling her back and forth. With one foul swoop she picked up the little girl, who commenced crying immensely. “MOMMY, I WAS JUST…” “Darling, I don’t care what you were ‘just’ doing, you do not talk to strangers, especially weird looking ones like that. He could’ve been a rapist or a murderer or something…did you see him?...” The words hit me like flying daggers, cutting my soul. This thing that was so pure and innocent was now being soiled. It was as if I was witnessing a being become impure and evil, as if I could see the goodness being sucked from her. She was becoming less and less, that beautiful little girl turning into a horrible stupid bitch. It wasn’t really scary though…it was more frightening. In this little girl I saw me. I saw myself being engulfed in the flames and losing my former identity. I saw everything that was me become a mummy-like man, devoid of everything but depression and anger. If anything, this was symbolism, that maybe there was some higher force that was more real than the fire, more harming and dangerous to my well being. Maybe, just maybe, the fire wasn’t the cause of my transformation… But this was not the time to be going into this, especially not at…oh…3 am in the morning. Right as I turned to go into my room, I remembered that letter that I had barely glanced at. Once I got to my bed I picked it up and read who it was from…”AWC Headquarters.” Finally, something here…maybe about my first match? I ripped it open to see what was inside. A simple card. Oh, look, the first match: The Farmer vs Gabriel vs Drake Coleman Newcomers' Three Way Fury Match Referee: Joseph Reid A three way match? The Farmer? Gabriel? I was put asleep by this…I’d been in far worse first off. I’d been taken to the depths of whole upon introductions…and now this is what I was up against. But who were these people? Maybe they were the best wrestlers ever to hit North America. Maybe they were proof that God loved human beings. Regardless, my head was tired and sore…I needed sleep. I needed something. -----([([([END FEED])])])----- ------------------------------- I’m just a symptom of the moral decay that’s rotting at the heart of the country. --The The
