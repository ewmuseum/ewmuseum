The Long and Winding Road: Chapter Four - Memories/Musings

6 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6th June 2005 It’d been a long time since I’d been to this place. Far too long. Even when I was living in New York I didn’t come here nearly as often as I should’ve done. Paying the fare I exited the cab, clutching a bouquet of roses to my chest, and stared at the imposing iron gates. Why am I here? I thought to myself. What am I trying to prove? There’s nothing here but skeletons of the past…a past I’ve done my best to escape from…a past filled with nothing but pain and misery. I tried to will my body into motion but it point-blank refused. TURN BACK, it was saying but I rejected its cries. After what seemed like an age I took a deep breath and pushed the gate. It swung open with a grating creak. With trepidation I walked through. Immediately I was transported into another world – a paradise of green in the middle of NYC. All around me people were walking, some in twos, some alone. They all looked so sad. They walked with their heads down, intently studying the ground. Trying my best to ignore them, I made a beeline for my destination. It’s been so long I wonder if I even remember where it is? I spent a few feverish minutes searching the urban oasis before I found what I was looking for. Here Lies Ffion Garrett Beloved Father and Husband Jane Garrett Beloved Mother and Wife Frances Garrett Beloved Daughter and Sister Beautiful Angels, Now At Rest I knelt before the memorial, placing the roses (Frances’s favourite) on the soil, blinking back tears. It’s all my fault. All of it. They’d still be here today if it weren’t for me and my stupid desire to be something. Why couldn’t I be happy with what I had? What has chasing my crazy notions brought me? Nothing. Nothing of value, anyway. No sense of self-worth, no sense of accomplishment…everything’s been a waste. A waste of life – mine and everyone else’s. “I’m sorry,” I said, choking. “If I could turn back time and make things different I would, but that’s just not an option. I miss you guys every single day. Not a day goes by when you’re not in my thoughts and prayers. Forgive me.” I winced as a jolt of pain rushed along my spinal column. Despite coming away with the victory Krimzon had messed me up pretty bad on the last Fresh!, to the point I was still really recovering from the match. Of course, the AWC trainers strapped me up, gave me pills and sent me on my way as they always do. After all, they’re paid to deal with the physical, not to worry about the abstract. Things haven’t quite gone the way I planned them, I thought to myself. It’s like a disease. Everywhere I go, everything I do I let people down. I tried not to focus on that, instead pressed on to my next destination, and a meeting with an old friend. --- The bar was busy for a Monday morning, but not much else had changed since I was last here. The sawdust on the floor was still mixed with congealed blood and vomit and the same smell of cheap cigars and bourbon hung in the air. It didn’t take me long to spot my quarry, cunningly camouflaged as he was in a dayglo orange hat with the slogan “I LOVE NY!” stamped across the brim. “Ohayo,” I said. “Take that thing off.” Eiji Ohayo looked up at me and smiled. “Hi, Kurisu-san.” It had been years since Eiji’d been in New York. His dad, Yuki, and I had fought each other in some of Japan’s most notorious deathmatches. After the Den of Death’s closure I became so close to Yuki’s family that I practically became Eiji’s surrogate father, though now at age 30 he was really no longer in need of my fatherhood. He was trying to make his name as a wrestler in Japan with some success, under the Lawman gimmick. “So, what brings you back to New York?” I asked. “You! I saw you on the AWC PPV, Zero To Hero – man that was CRAP! You’re not kicking ass like you used to!” “Yeah. I guess not.” “So I decided to come and visit. I felt as if I should, since it’s been so long…” By this point I wasn’t listening anymore. Something wasn’t right about the tenor of his voice, but that wasn’t really what was bugging me. The truth was I’d been in a daze since the last TV taping. I didn’t know what was happening to me. It was like a little bit of me was dying as each second went by – as if unseen forces were poisoning me through the air – and there wasn’t a damned thing I could do about it, no-one I could tell. No-one would believe me if I did. I wouldn’t blame them. My cellphone started ringing, bringing me out of my trance. “Sorry, Eiji, I’ve gotta take this,” I said, looking apologetic. “Pact.” “Pact, baaaaaby!” squealed a familiar voice. I barely suppressed a groan. “Alexi, hi!” (my agent, I mouthed to Eiji, rolling my eyes). “I just got round to watching last week’s tape, that’s ratings my man, I’m telling ya your star’s on the rise!” “Sure, whatever. What do you want?” “Just to let you know your next booking – it’s AWC Fresh!, this Tuesday…” “Yeah, against who?” “You’re gonna LOVE this. I spent AGES negotiating it with the new GM. This Thursday night, you’re taking on Kris Krimzon, Crimson O’Malec…and Christopher Ashton in a Weapon Of Choice match for the Relentless Championship!” I barely stopped myself jumping out of my seat, “You’re serious?” I gasped. “Deadly.” “Alexi…sometimes you’re worth your weight in gold. I’ll speak to you soon,” I said, hanging up. “Good news?” Eiji asked. “You could say that,” I practically laughed. Fresh! would be the beginning of the my climb to the top of the ladder in AWC – and I already knew exactly how I was going to do it. “Eiji, it’s time to go old school…” --- Later that day, overwhelmed by the nostalgia that my meeting with Eiji had conjured up, I trawled through a career’s worth of keepsakes. Ah, memories! I thought to myself, pulling the crate out from under my bed. It'd been too long since I'd taken a look at these things. Alexi’s news had awakened something within me I hadn’t felt for a long, long time – passion. For too long I’d let my career take a back seat – I’d even considered retirement. No longer. I was completely focussed on the task at hand. Contained in this box was, quite literally, my legacy to the business – a collection of my tapes, writings, diaries and photographs dating right back to my debut. I blew a heavy layer of dust off of the lid and feverishly prised it off. First item I laid eyes on – a photograph of me delivering an exploder suplex to some jobber onto a bed of lightbulbs. The date on the back: 10/10/76. Nearly thirty years ago. I'm getting too old for this crap. But then again, I know I'm not. I live for this. Extreme is my life – it's all I've ever been, all I've ever known. Digging deeper I found more memories. Videotape of the raid on the Den of Death. DVD of my Best of Five Series against Kenta Kobayashi from last year's TKO Breakout. Photo album from the XLL reunion show. All good times. Then I came to what I was looking for. The black leather cover was crusty with dried blood, some of which had seeped into the creamy white pages within. Anxiously I opened the volume and was greeted by a smell that had been foreign to me for many, many years. Blood. Sweat. Damp. Decay. My cell. I turned to the title page – PACT: DIARIES, 1974-75. A smile crept across my face as I flicked through the journal. These were the best days of my life. The worst days too. There were times when I wished I were dead but the pain just kept on comin’. Some of the best friends I ever had died in that ring, some by my own hand. It’s not something I’m proud of, but at the same time it’s not like I had a choice. I was a prisoner. I felt my lower lip start to quiver at the thought of the friends I’d lost. I lit a cigarette to steady my nerves. My experiences in the Den of Death shaped the course of my entire life. I became a deathmatch legend. I wrestled thousands of matches all over Japan against a hundred different opponents, and what have I got to show for it? Nothing. Scars (not just the physical ones) and emptiness. But I know if I were given the chance to live my life over again I wouldn’t change a damned thing. I love being the best at what I do – even if what I do isn’t pretty. I leafed through the diary some more, basking in nostalgia, and came to an entry I’d all but forgotten about. I felt a lump in my throat as I read it back to myself. Jan. 17, 1975 Yuki and I had an insane match last night. I won after piledriving him onto some C4. Burnt my legs up pretty good. Yuki came off worse though. Half of his scalp was just blown away by the initial impact. As usual, no-one came out to help, no stretchers were brought down to the ring, no medics jumped in and tried to help him. This is for real. Here, deathmatches end, usually, in death. Not if I can help it. I picked Yuki up and ran down into the holding area, talking to him all the while. “Yuki, are you okay?” “Yuki, say something.” “Yuki, stay with me boy, I ain’t lettin’ you get off that easy.” “Don’t leave me on my own you bastard!” I tore strips off my shirt to bandage his wounds with, but nothing was working. He just kept on bleeding. All the time I was thinking, “No, I can’t let him die, I won’t let him die.” What good is any of this doing? Who does it provide entertainment for? These gladiatorial battles, life against life…it’s sick. It’s depraved. Life’s being bought and sold as a commodity, like it’s something that can be owned by someone other than the person it occupies. I wish I’d never got caught up in it. And yet…nothing could ever beat it. Not even war felt as good as this. Man on man. One on one. Me against you. Til only one of us is left standing. Victory is the ultimate rush. I love it. But victory comes with a price. I held Yuki in my arms as he died, rocking him gently, kissing his forehead, whispering in his ear that he was going to a better place. His last words were “anata wa yurushi desu” – I forgive you. All I could do was hold him close to me and cry. I’ve lost the only friend I have here, the only person that’s kept me going. When I couldn’t see a way out it was Yuki that spurred me on, Yuki that told me to never give up hope. But what for? I’ve lost him now. I killed him. I’ve destroyed the only family I have left. I won’t let Yuki be forgotten. He will live on through me. From this day forth, I fight in his name. My victory will be his victory. My defeat will be his defeat. My shame, his shame. I’ll do him proud. I’ll fulfil his dream of getting out of here and when I do I’ll make damn sure I take his spirit with me... Blinking back tears I closed the book. Yuki… I looked down to the tattoo across my right wrist – the kana yu, ki, o, ha, yo. Yuki Ohayo. I’d forgotten what all this was for – it was for him. I kept going for him. I'd lost sight of that somewhere – exactly where isn’t important. I vowed there and then to never let it happen again. I’d deprived Eiji of a dad – the least I could do was help him preserve his father’s memory. Replacing the lid on the box, I pushed it under the bed. With a sigh, I left the room, went to the kitchen and picked up a beer. I flicked the TV on to FSN to catch the AWC recap show. They had a nice feature on the Relentless Championship match, including my opponents’ promos. These guys are all men after my own heart, I thought to myself. They have goals in mind, they set out to achieve them, they’ll do anything within their power to get that belt. Deep in thought I watched the rest of the promos. Ashton…I’ve been looking forward to getting my hands on you. Oh yes. I haven’t forgotten what you did a few weeks back, and I won’t forget. You and I have unfinished business, and I’m coming to collect my debt, in the form of a scream. A deep, guttural scream. And once I have it I’ll take the Relentless Championship, bring it to a man who is truly deserving. It’s my destiny and duty, and I won’t let anybody stop me. View Pact 's Biography
