Call it Karma

6 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 6, 06 My weary state weighing down on me like a bag of bricks, I strolled through downtown Berkeley without a destination. Free of all thought, I boasted an over-confident pose and exaggerated posture, which deceived those who noticed my presence. I looked like a man with a purpose, when realistically I had none at all. Unless, of course, my attempt at cleansing my torso with repeated breaths of fresh air is considered a purpose. This was not the first time I trekked through the streets without intent, it was now almost routine; a required contribution to my being able to sleep. The last few weeks had been rough on me, and even with unrecognized sacrifice, I had made little or no progress in my AWC. The credibility I gained from barely defeating Vince Jones was quickly revoked after being embarrassed by the superior Garbage Bag Johnny. My involvement in the drama developing in backstage was also miniscule; each week I wrestled, then my antics to follow were limited to my lonesome dressing room. Gaining this ridiculous reputation as the guy with potential but no follow-through was becoming a nag in the back of my mind 24 hours of each day, and perhaps the main reason why I would aimlessly stroll through the streets and ignore the sleeping factor. And the fact that Natalie had left for London again, even after our memorable adventure, was also a burden I was unable to deal with. I missed her. What am I looking for? Sympathy? No, I wouldn't go as far to say that. It's my own fault for gaining this unrecognizable reputation, and even in the process, gained no notice from anybody. I used to be the self-proclaimed underrated wrestler of AWC, but now I'm just a weak mid-carder who got into the main event of a Fresh!special based on failure in a celebrated event. And now I resort to current ramblings that are frighteningly similar to last week's 'AgentNotebook'; I am not one to be repetitive. With time ticking by slowly and my body running on very little sleep, the sound of music caught my ear from across the street. Casually glancing over, I saw a neon sign basking light over the street forming the words 'Albatross Pub'. I wasn't one for bar surfing, but I egregiously directed myself towards the open door and stepped into the building. It was a jazzy atmosphere, with warm, hazy air and the jazzy background music solidifying it. People lounged in the chairs pointed towards the stage, enjoying drinks, light conversation and good music. A few lingerers were talking and laughing near the bar to the side of the pub, and I headed for it eagerly. I wasn't one for drinking, but thought it might take the edge off. Ironically, I didn't know why I had that edge, it was just one of those present, uncertain feelings. I wish I could describe it betterly, but my oblivious state wanted differently. I sat down at the bar with a sigh. A voice piped in, 'Had a long day?'. I popped my head up to see the bartender standing in front of me. I was surprised at his friendly nature; normally in this type of 'joint' they got right to the point of asking you how you wanted to spend your money on overpriced alcohol. 'Yeah ...' I began, then relayed, 'Well, it shouldn't have been.' He nodded. 'Had a few of those myself recently. Something to drink?' 'Vodka on the rocks, with a splash of gin.' He efficiently poured the drink and set it in front of me. I gave him a few bills and thanked him, then took a long sip of the drink. I wasn't much of a drinker; usually I had too much to worry about to get into it. But I enjoyed it once in a while, and I guess it was one of those times. I absorbed the casual environment willingly while sipping away at my Vodka and Gin, once in a while peeking over my shoulder to observe the stage which had an appealing set of talented musicians. I felt a finger tap on my shoulder and almost jumped out of my seat in surprise, the contents of the glass I was holding splashing onto my hand. Hearing the apologetic, stuttering voice of a woman to my left I looked over as I reached for a napkin to wipe the drink that spilt on my hand. 'Oh my god, I'm sorry ... I didn't mean to ... I just ...' 'It's okay,' I replied, setting the soaked napkin down for the bartender to toss in the garbage. 'I wasn't very attentive anyways,' I continued, then took a good look at her. 'Do I know you?' She blushed. 'I was about to ask you the same question ...' A small grin flashed across my face as I propped my elbows on the table. 'That's funny. Alot of people say that.' She frowned. 'And why's that?' 'I ask myself the same question every day ...' 'And why's that?' she repeated curiously. 'Because I wouldn't even say that I know myself anymore,' I admitted, then finished off my drink and set it in front of me. I sighed and looked at the woman curiously, but I was unable to put a name to the face I recognized. 'Oh! I do know you!' she claimed excitedly. 'Sean Aspinall!' My face fell into an unexpressed state of awe. It was uncommon for me to run into strangers in bars that knew me by that abandoned name. 'Yeah, that's me. Well ... was,' I said. She raised her eyebrow subtly. 'What does that mean?' 'My name is Cedric Riley now ... I'm sorry ... but how do you know me?' I questioned, even though I was convinced of the answer already. 'You went to Berkeley University. We dated. My name is Alex,' she stated without hesitation and lacking any real build-up. Taking a good look at her, I felt upset with myself for not recognizing her earlier; I had thought she knew me from the wrestling ring, so it was a considerably nice surprise that she knew me personally. I searched for something to say as I looked at her dumbfounded, then looked away uncertainly. 'Here ... I'll buy you a drink,' I decided, then beckoned for the bartender who came over. A few Vodkas, Martinees, and Shooters later, Alex and I were both feeling the effects of intoxication as the hours approached closing time; we had a lot of fun reminiscing our University years, recapping what we both claimed to be the best times of our lives. A lot of stories that escaped my memory resurfaced, and it was somewhat pleasing to know that I formerly lead a somewhat normal life; well, as normal of a life as a University student can garner. The drinks continued to be poured as the band left the stage and people left the bar in search of different territory, but there were still a few scragglers on the late Monday night. I felt surprisingly good under the influence of alcohol even though I had previously avoided it. 'So, you never told me what you did after University,' Alex changed the subject as we finished a good laugh about a prank we played on an old professor. I did a double take but recovered quickly by taking another sip of my drink. After a long sigh I tried to respond, 'I ... I went into athletic therapy.' It was the best I could think of. I didn't exactly want to tell an old girlfriend that I careered in a sports entertainment that involved butting naked chests with other men; it was more attractive to tell her that I joined the CIA, but that was confidential. 'Interesting,' she replied, sounding unconvinced, but didn't push the subject. 'And what are you doing?' I returned the question. 'I'm a journalist, Sean ... I thought you knew that!' I cringed. 'It's Cedric, remember?' She frowned. 'Oh, right ... why-' I interrupted her quickly. 'Don't ask. It's ... a long story.' That was an understatement. It was both a nightmare and a very elongated period of my life that I wanted to leave in the past, although that seemingly was an impossible expectation. 'Okay.' An awkward silence followed. We both began at the same time with something along the lines of, 'Listen ... I ...' followed by another short silence. Alex gathered her purse and got up from her stool. 'I should go. I have a long day ahead of me tomorrow - I have a deadline to meet by next week ... for an article ...' she said hurringly. 'Of course, of course,' I said, getting up from my stool after throwing a few more bills on the table as a tip. 'May I be snoopy and ask what it's on?' She blushed. 'Oh ... it's nothing ...' I shrugged. 'Okay ...' 'You'll probably think it's stupid.' I rolled my eyes as I thought back to my pathetic lie about being an athletic therapist. I laughed. 'I highly doubt that. Trust me.' 'It's on professional wrestling.' I thought the silence preceding that statement was awkward. This was beyond weird. I stood there with a blank look on my face as if my secret were uncovered, and she stood their with the same attitude as if she were ashamed to be writing about the proclaimed sports entertainment. 'Go ahead ... laugh,' she egged me on, shyly looking away. 'No, I wouldn't do that ...' 'Good, cause I'm having enough trouble as it is,' she admitted. 'Oh?' I wondered curiously, as if tempting her to further the subject. 'Nobody in the 'business', as I call it, is cooperating really well with me. It'd be nice if I could find somebody who could give me that first step that I need to kickstart this assignment. It's tough enough as it is.' Standing there, half-intoxicated, almost mesmerized by the fact that I was having this unexpected conversation with one of my oldest friends, I couldn't help but smile. 'I think I know just the guy.' I wish I knew then my attempt at being the nice guy would be an unforgiving mistake.
