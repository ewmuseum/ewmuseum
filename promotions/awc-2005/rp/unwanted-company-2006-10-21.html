Unwanted Company

21 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-21 A black hard-shell guitar case was propped in a corner of room number 5. A black Stetson was carefully placed on the small dresser beside a 20-year old television. A larger-than-average figure sat on the bed as he slipped on a pair of snakeskin boots. He wasn’t surprised his numerous phone calls last night were unanswered. He hadn’t expected her to pick up. He also knew by now, she had read the letter from her brother and probably wondered how it got all the way here in Spain when actually; Eric Tomas is in the states. He knew the answers and soon she would know too. The man stared at his reflection through the mirror. He carefully placed the black Stetson cowboy hat over his head and ran his hand over his blonde goatee. After tucking his black t-shirt into his Wrangler Jeans, he gave his reflection a satisfied wink and headed for the door. She didn’t notice the Ford F150 parked next to her rental car when she returned the night before. She wondered how a letter from her brother reached her. The envelope wasn’t stamped or addressed. It simply had her name handwritten in ink. When she caught a glimpse of the caller’s name on her cell before the battery died, she had her suspicions. The knock at the door came early. Teresa rolled over under the thin covers and stared at the door. The knock came a second time and with groggy effort, she swung her long legs over the edge of the bed and stood up. Her bare feet stumbled half asleep to the door and unlocked it. “Oh, it’s you.” Teresa left the door open and stumbled back to the bed and sat down. She grabbed her pack of smokes and offered her visitor one. The visitor feigned disappointment and took a cancer stick. After lighting up, he released a steam of smoke and stared at Teresa for a moment. “I thought you’d be happy to see me, or at least surprised anyway....Wait, when’d you start smoking?” The Redneck Princess shrugged and took a second drag of her morning smoke. She was wearing green plaid sleep pants and a worn out grey t-shirt. Her sandy blonde hair was in a bad need of a brush. She looked up at the man standing before her in the black Stetson hat. He stood a good 6 foot and built like a football player. Memories of a time almost forgotten flooded into her mind and she shook her head as she stubbed her cigarette into the ash tray. “Give me a few and we’ll talk over breakfast. You just don’t travel across the ocean and knock on my hotel door for no reason.” “No wonder you’re starting to look like hell. You ain’t eating a thing.” Jack shoveled in another forkful of gravy and biscuits into his mouth. He and Teresa sat across each other at a McDonald’s. Teresa sipped on a cup of hot black coffee. An Egg McMuffin remained untouched in front of her. “I see all those biscuits you’ve been eating are finally catching up to you. So, how many you put on? 10? 20?” Jack licked gravy off the corner of his mouth and looked from his plate to his former manager, then grinned. “Cute one Tomas; at least you still have that fine ass.” “Why you come here Jack? Seriously.” The Tope Rope Temptress sat back in her chair and crossed her arms over her small chest. She watched Jack finish his breakfast and gulp down a glass of orange juice before giving her his attention. “I saw the magazine.” “So has everyone else. What’s your point?” Jack removed his black Stetson from his head, then placed it back on his head as cowboys sometimes do when a little nervous. He tries his best to give Teresa a sincere look. “I want back in Teresa.” “What?” Teresa raised an eyebrow as she reached for her coffee. Years ago, she had managed this man in an indy fed. For moths Jack “The Ace” Slayter and the foxy Teresa Tomas was an item. But they were much younger and barely getting their feet wet. It didn’t take her but a second to shake her head. “No Jack.” “No?” “No, you’d never make it. Hell, I’m barely dragging my feet. Besides, the wrestling world already has Duke Williams. The last thing it needs is a wannabe running about.” Jack dropped his fork into the plastic plate in front of him and pushed it aside with his hand. He’s heard about Teresa’s previous friendship with this Duke and quite frankly, the name left a bad taste in his mouth for the old man. His leaned his broad chest across the table and pointed a hard finger at Teresa. “Now you listen, I sported my good looks long before I even heard of that wrinkled-assed has been. I didn’t come all this way just to go home empty-handed.” Jack paused to take a deep breath. He reached for Teresa’s hand and softened his voice. “Listen Teresa, I miss you. I miss us.” His hand squeezed hers. She tried to jerk it free, but he refused to let her out of his grip. Their eyes met, his pleaded for a reunion. Hers held annoyance. “You’re full of shit Jack. You don’t care about no one but yourself. I know you like a book.” Teresa paused and finally yanked her hands out of his grip. Jack sat back in his chair and brought his cup of orange juice to his lips. “So you know that I was the one that slipped that note from Eric under your door?” Jack smirked as he finished his juice. He knew Teresa almost as well as she knew him. She was a smart woman, but didn’t always use her brain. He just didn’t always have the brain to use. “I had my suspicions, especially seeing that you called my cell phone all night.” Teresa took her tray and walked to the trash bin. Jack followed leaving his trash at the table. Once outside, Teresa dug into her jeans for a smoke this time not bothering to offer Jack one. She leaned her back against the building as she exhaled the first drag. Jack fished into his own pocket and pulled out a pack of Camels. “You’re not curious how I got hold of your brother’s note?” “I don’t care Jack. Just go back home. Leave me alone. OK? What we had died years ago, personal and professional.” The line at the McDonald’s drive-thru was becoming longer mid-morning approached. Soon it would be lunch hour and the whole place would be packed. Teresa tossed her cigarette butt to the side and headed for her rental car. Jack did the same and followed. He wasn’t about to walk the 3 miles to the hotel. “I visited your mama the other day...” Jack said as he stepped into the small car. Teresa said nothing. She got in the driver seat and slammed the door. She hadn’t visited her mother since she attacked her nurse. Because of that incident, she won’t be able to go back. She said nothing as she pulled the car into reversed and backed out of the parking lot. Jack continued as he fumbled through the stations on the radio. “Your brother was there visiting. He’s doing pretty well by the way.” Teresa kept her silence as she made a left turn at a traffic light. Over the small speakers, Trace Adkins sang ‘Rough and Ready’. Enjoying the tune, Jack slightly nodded his head to the beat. “Anyway, me and him were talking ya know, and the topic of you came up. I told him I was heading out this way and he asked me to give it to you.” They arrived at the hotel parking lot. Teresa killed the engine and looked to Jack. Her face held no emotion. ”That’s nice. Thank you for the note. Now go home.” Jack shook his head as Teresa climbed out of the car. He watched her enter her room and smirked to himself. She wasn’t getting rid of him that easy.
