Time for a revelation (part two)

7 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 7, 2006 1:24 PM Unknown Location First-Person (Kris Krimzon) Perspective What the hell is going on? is all that is raced through my mind for the time I had been walking around the town I’d been living in. It took me half an hour and I’ve come to one conclusion: I’ve become everything I never wanted to be. Slowly over the years I’ve morphed from being young and carefree to being older and so concerned about every little problem that happens to me. Every possible direction or conclusion I reached sends me back to the true cause of all these emotions: the book. None of this would ever have struck me if it had not been for that book. Maybe it’s my father’s fault? Had he not written it, I would’ve just accepted the lie. But is that what I wanted? Then something hits me. It wasn’t a novel, it wasn’t a fiction book. It was a real book that affected me the most. Maybe because it’s more personal? My mind was going way to fast to keep up with and it had been making me sick to my stomach. Physically sick to my stomach. I stop quickly, now sweating, and sweating really badly. Everything suddenly began gray as I felt my legs seemingly sweep up from under me. I know that I hit the ground, but I felt no pain or displeasure. As soon as I realized what was going on, I pulled out my phone from my pocket and used all the energy that was left inside me to dial the one number that has never failed to fix any problem I had. 1:25 PM AJ’s Apartment First-Person (A.J.) Perspective I had been asleep for a maximum of three hours when I heard my cell phone ring by the counter next to me. Usually, I’d roll over and go back to bed. Something in my mind told me to answer it. I reached out my arm and grabbed the phone. I looked at the name that called: “Kris”. I opened it and held it to my ear. “What’s up bro?” I said. No response. “Hello?” Still no response. “Kris?” I sighed, hung up, and dialed Kris’s home number. It rung a few times before Kris’s girl answered. “Hello?” she said. “Hey Tam, is Kris there?” I asked. “No… he stormed out about a half hour ago,” Tamara said. “Why?” Hmm... “He… just called. What do you mean ‘stormed’ out?” I asked. “He just left without a word. Something’s weird about him lately,” she said, now sounding worried. “Have you talked to him recently?” “Weird? How so?” “Before he left… he said something about being tired of his life. I don’t know, he just left.” “I got to go find him,” I said quickly, rolling and sitting up on my bed. “Should I come along?” “No,” I said quickly. “There’s no point to really.” There was, but I had a feeling that Kris didn’t want to see Tamara right now. If he did, he would have called her. “Well, I suppose. If you find him, please call,” she said. “Okay. G’bye,” I said. I hung up the phone quickly. I rushed out of my bed, tossing on clothes sloppily, and ran out the door. I dived into my car, turned the ignition, and began my search. 2:15 PM Unknown Location First-Person (A.J.) Perspective It’s been over a half hour and there’ve been no signs of Kris. All my calls to his cell phone were unanswered. I began to panic. For the first time in a long time, a tear slid town my cheek. Kris had been my best friend, yes, best friend, for years now. If I ever had anything to get out, I could confine it in him. If I needed a joke, he was there for me. I honestly could never picture life without him. Something about this situation seemed strange. Something told me that there was something wrong, that Kris was in trouble. It killed me I couldn’t help. I sighed. I was almost ready to give up hope. I turned down an alley aimlessly. My headlights displayed a shadow. A large shadow, in fact. It hit me like a tidal wave. I hopped out of my car instantly and ran as quickly as I could to Kris. He was laying on the ground, the cell phone opened in his palm. I lifted up his head with my hand and looked at his face. No harm had been done. His eyes were shut though. “Kris?” I said quietly. “KRIS?!” I began shouting his name. The tears were pouring down my face. If this is a joke, I’m gonna beat him. I felt something on my hand. I set down his head slowly and looked at it. It was red. My eyes widened in disbelief. “KRIS!!” I shouted at the top of my lungs. No reaction whatsoever. I pocketed his phone and pulled his limp body onto my shoulders. “Don’t be dead, Kris… don’t be dead. Please God, don’t let him be dead.” I carried him to my car, opened the backseat, and laid him down face first. I closed the door, hopped in the front seat, and began to drive to the hospital. I opened Kris’s phone that I had in my pocket and called his house. 2:16 PM Krimzon’s Household First-Person (Tamara) Perspective I sat down, biting my nails, waiting for A.J. or Kris to call. The phone rang and I dived for it instantly. The caller I.D. read ‘Kris’s Cell.’ Oh thank God. I answered it quickly. “Kris, where have you been!?” I yelled. “No,” the other end replied sharply. It wasn’t Kris. “It’s A.J. Meet me at the hospital.” “WHAT? What’s going on!?” “JUST GET TO THE HOSPITAL.” he yelled. I’ve never heard him like that. I heard the click on the other end and headed for the car. 2:23 PM Hospital First-Person (Tamara) Perspective I ran into the hospital stepped up to the main desk. “Where is Kris Krimzon?” I said sharply to the attendant. She looked at me confused. “Relative?” “He’s my HUSBAND!” I shouted. She was taken aback by the screaming, but I didn’t care. “Now TELL me where he is!” “Room 156, ma’am. Sorry,” she said. I ran down the nearest hallway to room 156. The door was open so I stepped in. I saw AJ in the corner. His face was in his hands and I could hear his muffled sobs. “AJ?” I said quietly. He looked up quickly and began to wipe away the tears. His eyes were red, as well as the area around them. “Hey,” is all he said. “What’s going on? Is Kris okay?” I asked quickly. “He’s fine, Tam,” he said. “Then why are you crying?” “I’m just… thinking.” “You know you can tell me anything,” I said. He looked with the most serious face I’ve ever seen in my life. “No… I can’t. I want to, but I can’t. It’s one of those things you can only tell one person. I just… I can’t tell you.” I was shocked into silence. “I don’t mean to hurt you…” “I want to see Kris.” He looked at me. “You got arms. There’s a curtain. Just move it.” I was surprised. I shrugged and pulled the curtains to the side. There Kris lied. His head was bandaged up and he had small scrapes on his arms. His eyes were shut and I feared for the worst. I shut the curtains as quickly as I could. AJ looked at me again. “I did the same thing.” “What’s wrong with him?” “He’s better off than he looks. Trust me.” “That doesn’t answer the question.” AJ sighed. “I found him in an alley. He must’ve fell back and hit his head.” “You said he called you?” “He did. The phone was open when I got to him.” I sat down and prayed. There was nothing more I could do. Unknown Time Hospital First-Person (Kris Krimzon) Perspective There I was, age fifteen without a Goddamned care in the world. It was about midnight, and I was sitting in my room listening to music on my stereo. The music wasn’t necessarily ‘blasted’, but it was loud enough to hear and quiet enough to let my parents sleep. My door opened and in walked my brother. He was one of the ‘popular’ kids, but at the same time he was considered a punk. Every weekend, he’d have a little party in the basement and I’d sneak down and steal a few beers. Not much, just enough to get me drunk. Which, at the time, was very little. “Hey,” he said simply. “We’re hanging out downstairs if you want to come down.” “Alright,” I said. I didn’t have any intention of coming down. “No drinking though,” he said. “Okay,” I said as he left the room. I started drinking seven months ago. That night, I specifically remember five beers and fifteen shots. I loved it. Hanging out with people I loved and totally out of my fucking mind. I love New Years. At the time, it was the only time I got to drink. After that night, I didn’t have a drink for about four or so months, excluding two beers I stole out of a cooler. Then, I started drinking for real. One night, a buddy and I went downstairs to watch them fuck around and play some video games. I had three beers that night, all stolen from underneath his oblivious nose. That’s when I realized just how oblivious he is – and I saw opportunity. This is, of course, after a different incident that got me in trouble with the police and solidified my reputation as a ‘troublemaker.’ Another month went by, no alcohol. Then it came time for the big fiesta. My brother and his friends had a little party outside, where everyone camped out. At least, almost everyone camped out. That night, I had five beers before eight o’clock in the evening. I had five more beers afterwards, as well as a little whiskey and rum. That probably was one of the most amazing nights of my life. By this time, I had met a girl. She was amazing. Stunningly beautiful, smart, witty, and every time I was around her I felt so open. Every time I heard her name, my heart skipped a beat. I was completely and utterly in love with her. We were dating, but I knew she had no idea just how much I loved her. One guy, Scott, who was at that party was completely alcohol free. He had never had a beer in his life. He was the kind of kid you wished you could have yourself. He seemed like a poster-child of good behavior. That night, he guzzled down two beers and a shot of whiskey. I watched him drink this with pain in my heart. Over the last six months, I grew addicted to alcohol and I knew it. I didn’t think it was that big of deal. I simply reassured myself I liked it because of the taste. I knew I was lying. That night, my brother passed out on the front lawn. His friends carried him into the house and onto his bed. I saw him laying there and almost began crying. His squinted eyes were completely bloodshot and I could tell he was barely ‘there’ mentally. His friends sat by him and I knew then that he had the greatest friends in the world, that they truly and deeply cared for him, and that they would never let him get too out of control. While I watched my brother lay there incoherently mumbling, Scott stepped in. He was absolutely horrified. He was worried for his life. He had no idea whatsoever how bad I felt for him. I actually prayed to God that night, wishing Scott the best. I could tell by the look in his eyes that he was a good man. He deserved the best in life. The week afterwards, I decided to drink some more. The week after that, some more. The morning afterwards, I felt horrible. I honestly believed my temples were going to burst open. But I didn’t care. This was one week ago. The days leading up to today seemed like Hell. That week, I’d gone through some problems. The solution that I had come up with: alcohol. It had worked for the prior six months, it would work now. I longed for a beer, whiskey, rum… anything I could get my hands on. I found nothing. …and I was ANGRY. I was mad I couldn’t find the solution wanted. I was mad that none of my problems were solving themselves. I was mad I was growing up! Then, today had come. I flipped off my stereo after one song and headed downstairs. There, I saw a circle of my brother’s friends, all gathered around cards. One of them, Mark, shouted my name. “Grab a beer, buddy!” he said. “No,” my brother said strictly. “He’s not drinking tonight.” “Just one beer,” I said. “Even dad let me have one beer.” He sighed. “Okay, one beer.” I pulled out the beer and sat down in the circle, joining in on the game. A few minutes later, I finished the beer. I tossed it away and stood up. “What’s up?” Mark said. “I finished my beer, I’m just going to watch now,” I said. I was a man of my word. “No, no,” he said encouragingly. “Sit down, man, I got ya.” I sat down as Mark stood up and walked to the cooler. He opened it, grabbed a beer, and tossed it to me. Gratefully, I accepted it, opened it, and drank it down. I stood up, tossed the can away, but this time headed to the cooler myself. My brother stood right beside it, so I figured if he didn’t want me to drink he’d stop me. He didn’t. I had another beer. Soon afterwards, I went to grab another beer from the cooler. My brother slammed the cooler down and glared at me. “No more.” “Come on man, just one,” I said. “No. No more. Have a soda.” I sighed, but I respected the hell out of him. I did as he told me. Moments after I began drinking my soda, my brother, Mark, and a few others left the room. I saw opportunity and grabbed another beer. I finished it in under fifteen seconds. I tossed the can away and began to finish my soda. I headed upstairs and outside where my brother had gone. Where he was stood only him and a very close friend, Bret, who knew our family well. “Kris,” my brother started, “I need to talk to you.” “Okay,” I said as normal as possible. “The reason I’m not letting you drink is because you’re only fifteen and alcoholism runs in our family,” he said. The last five words hit me like a bombshell. He went on like it was nothing. “Wait, wait,” I said, cutting him off. “Dad is an alcoholic?” Bret laughed. He then looked at me and noticed I was not joking one bit. He stopped almost instantly. “Yeah,” he said. “I don’t want you becoming addicted to this stuff, man. You’re only fifteen.” Quickly, I downed the rest of my soda. I tossed it away and walked away. I unzipped my zipper, urinated, and ran inside. I stepped into my room, shut the door, and broke down in tears. Slowly, I opened my eyes. I didn’t move my body at all, but I looked around the room. This flashback shit is getting old, real quick. It took me a little while, but I figured out where I was – a hospital. I didn’t know how I got there nor why, but I was. It didn’t take long for me to feel the pain in my head. All I saw was a curtain, but I heard their voices. AJ and Tamara were here and both of their voices sounded choked up. What the hell have I gotten myself into?
