The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch. 15

16 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 16 August The Lavelle Chronicles, CH. 15 On the way home I stopped at the twenty-four-hour Muzo’s on 10th Avenue and bought a six-pack, some general groceries and some dog biscuits for Shelby. I didn’t expect to be getting much sleep tonight between my worries for Lois and the multi-agency confab the following morning. On the way home, I continued to recap on my meeting with Vinnie, and the thoughts of Ranger stalking me, put me on an edge. I didn’t think my meeting with Vinnie would’ve gone down well with Ranger - considering the fact he was a man on a mission. I constantly checked behind me for a tail, a recurring car. At the top of Western Avenue, just before you turn into Summer Plaza, there is a junction. I stopped at the junction, looked in my rear view mirror. I checked the turnings for left and right. I stayed longer than usual, allowing the green, yellow and red light to pass. I put up with the occasional cursing and cacophony of horns and drove calmly down the road and turned left into Summer Plaza. I felt relieved as I zipped up my jacket and pulled the brown grocery bag up to my chest. The day had been an adventurous one. I had gone back to see Vinnie after ten years. I was double crossing the NYPD agents, and I now had a dog and a gun. So much could happen in one day - I would only believe that now. Shelby was running around the apartment as I entered. She was jumping up and down, her little tail wagging in all different directions. I put the groceries on the kitchen counter, took off my jacket and locked the door. Shelby, by this stage, was now on her back, waiting for a tummy rub. I knelt next to her and stroked her stomach. “Hello Shelb’s. Did you have a good day?” I was talking to dog; well it was keeping me sane, so it must work. Shelby quickly scooted around the apartment, making some playful growling noises as she chased a small squeaky toy. I flicked on the answering machine and opened a tub of peanut butter and grabbed a spoon from the nearby press. I dove my way into the peanut butter, licking the spoon whilest listening to the random messages from various people. I had two messages from Tony Rush; stating our meeting was to be at nine in the morning. I had another from Vinnie, telling me it was good to see me again and to remember my promise. By this point I was laughing. The third and final message was a man breathing heavily, a car engine starting and some horn noises. I deleted all three and nodded the third one off. When I got into the bedroom, after taking a shower, making something to eat and having a can of beer, Shelby was nestled into Lois’s pillow. To my astonishment she was snoring. I smiled and got into my side of the bed. “You miss her too!” I whispered and turned off the light. I woke up tossing and turning in my sleep. Thoughts of Lois, Marvin Harrington and Ranger had flooded my dreams. I think the problem was that I knew very little on anything anymore. So many hours had passed and I was still swimming blind folded. I decided against going back to sleep and rose from underneath the covers at seven. I was cranky, irritable and bedraggled; unlike Shelby, who was sleeping peacefully. I hoped into the shower and dressed in baggy jeans and a New York Yankees t-shirt. I moved into the kitchen and started the coffee while I poured myself some cereal. The brown envelope that Vinnie had given me the day before sat on the counter, untouched. I filled my blue mug with steaming hot coffee and ate some cornflakes. I stared out the window, gazing at the early morning rush hour traffic that had just started. I realised, standing here in my creased clothing, alone, that I needed to quicken the pace or I could lose Lois. I ripped open the envelope flap and pulled out the first few things. There were mug shots of Jackie and Hailey. A few old bars that I assumed he went to - mostly strip clubs along 1st Street to 10th Avenue. I flipped a large blue sheet over and I was greeted with a creased map of Albany. A white arrow was pointing toward the old docks on Albany's river, along with some abandoned warehouses. I took a quick guess that Ranger had probably been to these places, has probably seen these photos and like me, is stuck in a man hole. Shelby emerged from the bedroom, stretched, let out a loud yawn and moved to her bowl for some water. I said my good morning and continued with the file. I gazed at an old phone bill and recognized a call made to the Harbrook directory. It immediately hit me that he was probably looking for Lilly, well he found her eventually. I was awoken from my brief relapse by the knocking on my front door. Shelby wagged her tail and moved toward the door. I looked through the peephole and saw the large head of Tony Rush. I unlocked the door and pulled it open. Tony Rush entered, wearing a black tight shirt, jeans and the usual gun holster. His jacket was slung over his shoulder. 'How ya doin'?' he asked placing a mug on the table and taking some coffee. 'Okay,' I was lying. I quickly slipped the cover files from Vinnie into the small bin. Luckily Tony Rush didn't notice. 'I just passed some old tubby guy in the parking lot. He was messing with your car. Said he was a repairman. How'd you bust your car?' Tony Rush suddenly saw the shock in my face. 'I didn't!' I grabbed my Beretta, loaded it and ran downstairs whilest listening to Mr. Hopkins favourite programme “ER”; blaring out of the halls, on the way down. Tony Rush was in hot pursuit; his Glock in front of him. Shelby stayed in the apartment, gazing out of the door. I leapt over four steps and almost went flying into the exit door at the back of the apartment. My car alarm began to ring as the 'tubby man' smashed the driver’s window. Tony Rush pulled out his FBI badge and yelled the usual statements before apprehension. The 'tubby man' didn't reply, instead he managed to pop the drivers door and was seated in the car. 'HEY ....' I was yelling now. My neighbours all had their heads out of their windows, eager for some gossip. The 'tubby man' still didn't acknowledge us and Tony Rush shot into the air, a warning shot he called it. The man didn't flinch. The engine started and he hit first gear. It was then a large flash of light erupted, followed by a loud explosion and some searing heat ripped across my face. I was sent backwards. After a few moments the ringing stopped and my face was about ninety degrees and over. I gazed at my car, in all its tiny pieces. The man unfortunately had been blown to bits. Tony Rush was gazing at the mess, he was shocked. For some strange reason I knew I was lucky and didn't feel so sorry for the man in the car. I realised now that it was Ranger who had called earlier - his breathing and the horns - he was in the traffic at the junction the night before. He must've planted the bomb. Within minutes, Rivers and Rider were on the scene. By this time I had explained everything to Tony Rush - Ranger, my meeting with the two hookers and my leads from my cousin Vinnie. He had made it clear not to tell Rivers and Rider anything, since they would have me thrown in jail. I was a liable asset and I couldn’t be found dead. Rush looked around at all of the agents and forensic teams and smiled. 'Guess we'll be having the meeting 'ere,' he laughed. I hushed him and returned my glare at Rivers and Rider. Rivers was dressed in a dark navy tuxedo and a white short sleeved shirt. His blue sunglasses gleamed at me as they reflected the suns light. I was blinded for a few moments until his gaze was adverted to the forensic team. Rider was taking notes, and she was dressed in casual three-quarter length jeans, a string top and a utility belt - holding handcuffs, her gun and her badge. “Pierce, we need to talk about your safety,” Rivers said. “I think I’ll be fine, Rivers,” I was too worried about the fact Ranger was stalking me. “I don’t care too much for what you think.” “Hey, back off Rivers,” Tony Rush stood in front of me. “That’s not how you handle case victims or liable assets. I should report you for insolence.” Rivers nodded and moved away. Rider approached and Tony paid her some attention. “Who’s the JANE DOE?” Tony asked, looking around at the media that had now surrounded the scene. Western Avenue had never seen so much action since the blaze, and that was something I didn’t want to repeat. “A Norman Dorsey, he used to be in division, but was sacked for rape. He now works for a bonds company. The usual, you know, catching the so called ‘bad guys’ and bringing them to court.” My mind snapped back to reality at the mention Norman Dorsey. “Em, what bonds company did he work for?” Rider looked at me with a quizzical look, her eyebrows bent. She leafed through a few pages and stopped. “A, Vinnie Reed. Never heard of him. Anyway, we’ll look into this. I believe Harrington has probably sent this man after you. I just don’t think he expected the bomb to go off so soon.” Tony Rush couldn’t stop smiling at the naivety of Rivers and Rider. He soon turned to face me and laughed. “What?” “Nice eyebrows,” he moved through the crowd and back to his car. “I need to get something. You head up to your apartment, avoid the media and I’ll see in a few moments.” “MR. LAVELLE, NEW YORK POST … A FEW WORDS PLEASE,” a tall man leaned over the police tape and stuck his microphone in my face. “I have nothing to say,” I continued to move away from the reporters and brushed passed a few discourteous neighbours. The lobby was crowded with neighbours, journalists and some apartment security guards. I brushed passed the police tape line and entered. Making my way to the elevators, I thought taking the stairs might be a bit of risk, with all the tittle-tattle neighbours waiting for today’s daily news. “WAIT, MR. LAVELLE. PLEASE. JUST A FEW QUESTIONS.” I turned around and saw the man from outside standing in front of me. “Listen, I’m tired. I don’t have much to tell you. I don’t know what to tell you.” “Does this have anything to do with the murder of Lilly Connelly, the kidnapping of Lois Wood and Harrington’s escape from prison?” It was then I paused, I had frozen. I was stuck for words - this news journalist for the New York Post had just simplified my whole situation into three names. Everything that had happened was because of my involvement with Lilly Connelly. I now regretted ever meeting her. “Please, I have nothing to say.” The man backed off and floor-by-floor the elevator climbed slowly until I had reached my apartment. My neighbours all retreated to their apartments. The hall was quiet. Too quiet. Mr. Hopkins had even turned down his TV, which was unusual for a man with two hearing aids. I moved into my room and found the window smashed. Shelby was hiding under the leather sofa, shaking. I moved in closer and felt a roaring pain shoot down the back of my neck. I fell to the floor. After a few moments, I pulled out my gun and turned around, now standing I had two options, run like mad or confront this mystery person. When I turned around I was greeted by another punch, this time to the face. I wobbled backwards and took another one. “Now, do you want to tell me what you are doing ruining my shot at the money.” I recognised the man immediately. “Don Ranger?” “Got it in one, kid,” he knelt down and picked up my Beretta. “Nice piece for an amateur.” “… That’s what you think,” I was trying to remain calm and cocky. “Quit the games, kid. I know all about you. Read your file. Now, I’ll ask you again, why are you ruining my chances at the money?” Normally I would have wet my pants by now, but something in me sparked a light of hope and I wasn’t that scared of this tall man, dressed in all black with enough artillery to wipe out a small army. I rose to my feet, again, and decided honesty was the best policy! “To save my girlfriend.” “Bullshit!” he exclaimed and smiled at Shelby, who was still shivering under the sofa. “Not much of a guard dog.” “Did you hurt her?” “Just a kick … it’s just a mutt,” he laughed and continued pointing the gun at me. “Now, tell me the real reason you’re after my money. Oh, and by the way, did you like my present?” By present he meant his attempt to kill me. “You mean the bomb. Well, I didn’t get it. Somebody else did.” “I kinda guessed that by now. Poor Norman, he was slowing me down.” “Wait, you set the bomb and then made him start the car. A little sick, don’t you think!” I was hoping to keep him distracted long enough for Tony Rush to come and help me with this little mess-up. “You catch on quick! Now, if you don’t drop this pursuit, I will kill you.” “I can’t drop it. He has my girlfriend and I want to rescue her, the damn police aren’t doing a thing.” “Ah yes, cops, useless things aren’t they? I don’t care too much for this bird. So drop the case, or face the fate of Norman.” It was then I moved, I turned my back on him and made my way for the door. A loud pop was heard; I then felt a shooting pain soar down my leg. I crashed into the coat rack and fell to the floor. Tony Rush came running in the door, a stun gun in hand. He shot Ranger three times and a surge of electricity surged through Ranger’s body. A few chattering noises were heard and then a load thud as Ranger hit the floor. Tony Rush shoved the stun gun into his back pocket and cuffed Ranger. “Where ya hit?” “He shot me in the ASS! A SWAT man had an open shot and he shot me in the ASS,” I couldn’t stand up, my left cheek had gone numb. Tony Rush was bursting into fits of laughter at the fact my ass had been shot. “It isn’t that funny, he got me right in the left cheek. Hurts like hell!” “Did it get … ya know?” “Oh crap, I hope not,” I rooted around, made a quick count and sighed with relief. “No,” I laughed to myself, but the sudden shooting of pain soon replaced the humour. Shelby came out from under the chair and approached me, nuzzling me with her nose. I picked her up and hugged her and felt around for any broken bones. “You right limpy? Time to go you to the hospital!” Tony Rush said as he looked at me. “Division will take care of sleeping beauty.” Ranger was still out flat, some saliva gathered around his lips. Rush removed all of Ranger’s weapons and placed them into my old grocery bag and labelled it: “EVIDENCE”. He turned around and lifted up Shelby. “Right so limpy. Let’s go,” he laughed and walked out the door with Shelby. “Funny, Rush. Real funny. RUSH! I CAN’T WALK … damn,” using the coat rack, well what was left of it. I hoisted myself up and stared at the red patch that was now forming around my ass. I began to limp out to the hall and saw division coming up to collect ‘sleeping beauty’. Two hours and seventeen stitches later; I found myself seated on a blow-up ring, on the passenger side of Rush’s rented Toyota, with Shelby sleeping on my lap. My blue jeans were now covered in a dark, dry, reddish colour - coagulated blood, with a bandage on the left cheek. Entering the hospital was something of an embarrassment. From the moment I entered I was turning heads and hearing laughter, but I failed to see the humour about my pain. I was now in a more subdued state as the morphine slowly wore off. My mind was still focused on Ranger’s surprise visit and the fact that I now needed a new car and new eyebrows - since they got singed in the explosion and well, a few days of bed rest and the feeling in my left cheek to come back. “It could have been worse,” Tony said as he braked at a red light. “Uh-Huh!” “Don’t be so glum; you’ll be able to walk properly in another twenty-four hours or thereabouts. That’s what the doctor said,” he shoved the car into first and watched as his back tailpipe and exhaust collapsed and remained on the road behind him. “Crap,” he looked around and decided against going back to get it - since we were on a freeway. “Funny Rush, real funny. You know, next time you’re in pain or shot in the ass, allow me the time to gloat at your misfortune.” “I’m only kidding with you. So, in all seriousness, how’d ya feel?” “Numb …” the response wasn’t poetic, but only one word could define my state of my mind and ass. Rush burst into laughter and pulled off the freeway into Johnson Road and out into Western Boulevard. “So, you want to discuss these leads?” he asked, pulling into a junction. “First, before we do that. What of Ranger?” “Sleeping beauty is waking up … right about now … in a steel four-by-four cell with some nice criminal charges against him. This man is in a lot of trouble!” he laughed and pulled to a complete stop at the drive-by for Mc Donald’s. “You’re not serious, are you?” “Course, love this crap. Besides, I can’t eat this junk with the misses at home,” he smiled. Tony Rush rolled down the window and ordered a long list - from a big-Mac, to a Mac-flurry, to a large coke and fries. The list was endless and by the time I got a word-in-edgeways we had already moved passed the counter to the check-in. Rush pulled in the large McDonalds bag and handed me a small coke and a chicken wing for Shelby, which wasn’t the healthiest thing to be feeding a dog, but she hadn’t been fed in hours. “You don’t talk much about your wife!” it was something I wanted to press on with. “Oh, now you’re interested. When we first met, you accused me of cheating.” “Yeah, em, about that, sorry,” I was being truthful and the accusation wasn’t one of my best moments, but I was blinded by confusion, guilt and anger after the death of Dr. Philips, the doorman and Lois’s kidnapping. “Heh, don’t worry about it. I guess you had reason to be suspicious,” Rush pulled out of McDonalds and drove back out onto the junction, with the large bag between his legs. “Well, what now?” “What’d ya mean?” Tony said. “Well, I need a new car and well, Ranger isn’t going to be too happy that we busted him and sent him to prison.” “He can’t do anythin’ behind bars. A new car? You got much money?” “I should have some left in the bank from my last few shows,” I pulled out my mobile and searched the phonebook for the bank manager’s number. “Shows?” “I wrestle!” it was a quick, short and a simple two word answer, which sparked a lot of enthusiasm from Rush. “Serious? That’s awesome. Like those guys on Prime and such?” “Yeah …” “So, when is your next event?” “This week, I have to get to a flight on Thursday morning,” I realised work probably shouldn’t intervene in the situations of kidnapping and murder, but I had a job to do, and if I didn’t do it, well, I wouldn’t have a stable income. “Erm, here ya go.” Rush pulled the Toyota into the Summer Plaza parking lot. The debris from my cars explosion had been removed. All the journalists and cameramen were gone, everybody was gone bar two Insurance agents. I opened the door and Shelby jumped off my lap. Rush rooted around the glove pocket and pulled out my silver Beretta. He handed it to me as I was struggling to stand up. I pulled the blow-up ring close to my chest, to avoid been scene in public and shoved the Beretta into the front of my jeans. “Listen, I’ll be back in about three hours … I’ve got a meeting with Rivers and Rider. Should be loads of fun!” We said our goodbyes and Rush pulled out of the parking lot, leaving a long trail of black car fumes behind him. I made my way up the stairs with Shelby always two steps ahead of me. I checked the upper-floor and then opened my door, pulling my Beretta in front of me. Shelby’s hackles went up and she sniffed around, but then relaxed. I followed in after her and locked the door, double checking each lock. I turned on the kettle, pulled open the jar of coffee and put three spoons into a black and cream coloured cup. Within minutes I was sipping down some hot coffee and walking into the bedroom. I pulled off my wrecked jeans, examining the blood stained hole in the left pocket. I then removed my boxers, followed by my t-shirt. I turned on the powered shower and hoped in, letting the water run down my face, wiping away the dirt and blood from earlier on. After turning off the running water, I threw on some baggy-worn jeans, a t-shirt and a black jumper. The weather in Albany was slowly deteriorating as the summer was drawing to an end and autumn and winter slowly approached. Shelby was tucked onto the bed with one of her toys, squeaking away. I pulled on a pair of white socks and decided to ring Joey. Joey Leone was an old friend of Vinnie’s who owned an auto shop and also sold cars. Some of the cars he would get would be top of the line BMW’s, Mercedes and Porsches, but then again, sometimes he would get cars like a V12 Ford Mustang or a Barracuda and these were the all American Muscle cars that most would opt for. I picked up my mobile and dialled Joey’s number. After three rings and some inference flickering; Joey answered in his deep husky New Yorker voice. “Yo,” he answered. “Hey, it’s Pierce. Can I ask you a couple of things?” “Whoa, dude, long time no speak. How ya been? How’s Vin?” “Well, I’m fine and Vin, well he’s the same.” “Still tormented by the duck?” Joey laughed into the phone. “Yeah,” I was smiling too. That was something that would be inscribed on Vinnie’s gravestone. “Anyway, Pierce, what can I do ya for?” Joey asked. I could imagine him in a grey jump-suit, covered in motor oil, with a towel over one shoulder. “I need a new car, my last one, it was sort of vandalized.” “Yeah, that happens a lot ‘ere. Wait, I thought ya lived in Harrisburg.” “I moved to Albany a couple months ago.” “Cool, ya like it?” “It’s good,” this was a lie of course, but I didn’t feel like going into great amounts of detail. “What car ya lookin’ for?” “You got a Ford Mustang?” “Whoa, top o’ the line. Yeah, I got four in. All refitted. Brand new … well sort of. I have two V12’s and two V8’s. Which one ya interested in?” “One of the V12’s. I’ll be over soon Joe; you open all hours of the day?” “I’ll be closing for my dinner break at 8:00 pm, but that’s about it,” Joey said into the phone. “Right, I’ll be right over.” I closed the phone, picked up my house keys, cleaned Shelby’s bowls and refilled them. I shut the door behind me and placed my Beretta under my jumper and into the small of back. My ass was still killing me, but the walk would do me good as long as it didn’t pull out some of the stitches. It was 7:15 by the time I arrived at Joey’s garage. The only thing I had learnt from my walk here was the stench of Albany’s streets and the amount of unemployed laundering the streets seemed to growing. Joey was seated behind a large steel desk, working on what looked like a metal intestine. I pushed open the glass doors and was immediately greeted by two men dressed in suits - sales men. “I’m not looking. I’m here to see your boss,” I smiled as the two men backed off. I walked up passed a Porsche. I was immediately drawn to a black Barracuda with a flame spray up the sides. It was something you would only rarely see on the streets, but it was enough to just look at it. Joey pulled off his glasses, leaving a rim of black around his eyes. “How ya doin’?” he asked. “I’m doing pretty good …” I paused for a moment, hoping he wouldn’t see through my act. “So, where are the cars?” “This way,” Joey came around the steel table and lead me down the glass building and out into a garage. There in front of me was a royal blue metallic Ford Mustang, with six cylinders pointing out of the hood, the sign on the window said: “SOLD”. Next to that was a black one, with red interior. I decided on paying the full price and Joey handed me the keys. “Treat her well. She’s a real beauty,” Joey smiled as I filled out a check and slid it across the counter. “Don’t worry, I won’t let this one get blown-up … erm, eh, I mean, erm.” “You’re funny, Pierce. Just stop playing games,” Joey laughed and handed me a receipt. “She’s got a full tank, so, enjoy.” I took the receipt and pushed it into my back pocket and took the keys for the black Ford Mustang. I pulled the Ford Mustang into Fowler Avenue and found a small parking space just outside Vinnie Reed’s bonds company. I decided it was time to pay him a visit, especially since one of his bounty hunters was dead and the other in lock-up. He had some explaining to do. I turned off the ignition, checked on my Beretta and then locked the car behind me. Edith Richards was stomping around the office, slamming drawers and cussing when I walked in. “Your cousin is a vile pig,” she said closing a filing cabinet. “What did he do now?” I said exhaustedly. “We had a new secretary, who knew the alphabet! Anyway, she was bent over at file D, and well Vin decided to pop in and play hide the salami as he called it,” Edith said as she pulling up two large bundles of files. “And this secretary?” “Ran out of the office screaming,” Edith said as she sat down and lit herself a cigarette. “Said we should give her pay-check to charity!” “Vin has always been that way,” I laughed. “It’s about time we got him neutered,” Edith smiled in between large gulps of nicotine. “Is he around?” “Should be in his office, sulkin’,” she said and returned to labelling and filing. “Thanks.” I moved up the hall and towards Vinnie’s office. Vinnie was slumped in his chair, a bottle whiskey on the side with an empty glass. His toupee was askew on his forehead and he had a 5-day shadowing around his chin. “Vinnie, we need to talk,” I said. I walked into the room and closed the door behind me. “Pierce? What you doin’ ‘ere?” he asked. His eyes were bloodshot. “I need to tell you something about Dorsey and Ranger,” I sat down. “What about em?” he looked around nervously. “Well Norman Dorsey is dead; a car bomb explosion. And Ranger shot me in the ass and he is now in a four-by-four cell facing life - including the other crimes he committed, mostly murder.” “Shit! I knew this was one of those shit hit’s the fans situations.” “Anyway, you should be expecting a visit from a Detective Tim Rivers and a Detective Casey Rider.” “Oh, crap … em, what’d I do to deserve this?” he asked as he looked out of the window, through the cracks in the Venetian blinds. “Vin, listen to me. You can’t tell them about me visiting here, or getting information on Harrington. Otherwise, I’ll be in prison until this is all resolved and both Lois and Harrington are dead.” “Whoa, this is pretty messed up ain’t it,” he poured some more whiskey. “Yeah,” I stood up. “Oh, and Vin, you’ll need to clean yourself up before you go home to the misses, you look a mess.” “Nah, I’ll just tell her the truth.” “What? Vin, she’ll divorce you,” I couldn’t believe it. Something in Vinnie wanted him to tell the truth, or maybe not. “Not that truth. I’ll just say it was a hard day at the office,” he laughed. “Wha’ ya take me for, a fool? I wouldn’t give-up my wife.” “Right, well, goodbye.” I closed the door to the office, said goodbye to an extremely livid Edith Richards and climbed onto the blow-up ring on the driver’s seat in my Ford Mustang. I reversed out of the lot and pulled out of Fowler Avenue. I arrived home by 9:00 pm. It was getting darker as the evenings came to an end and nigh time soon took its course. I locked my new car, pulled out the blow-up ring and limped my way into the hall of the apartment block. We now had a new doorman, Rodger, he wore a Kevlar vest; just encase another lunatic decided to brandish a weapon on the doorman of our apartment block. Mrs. Winchester was seated on her wooden rocking chair at the end of the hall looking out at the night’s sky. She gave her usual hello and asked on the progress of finding Lois, I gave the same, tedious answer: “Nothing new yet,” it was something I hoped would change. I pushed the door open and found Tony Rush making himself at home. “I could have shot you,” I said throwing my keys into a bowl. “But, you didn’t,” he smiled. Tony Rush sat adjacent from me on the leather sofa, four files in front of him, all labelled FBI and NYPD. He had a cool beer in one hand and in the other a biro. He leant back and continued to scribble down some things. “Anything new?” “No, just some long and boring meetings on Norman Dorsey. Believe me, Rivers and Rider won’t find Lois in time. We need to quicken the pace.” “I thought we were doing that!” I stated. Right now, at this moment in time, we have nothing that can give us a whereabouts on Harrington and your girlfriend. All we have is one dead man, a bounty-hunter, two escorts and two really bad leading agents from the NYPD,” he laughed at this comment. I rose to my feet, opened up the notebook and slid all the files on Harrington across the dining room table to where Rush was seated. He looked at me and then searched through the photos, notes, bank records and phone bills. A man our narcotics unit are trying to get.” “Well, at least that helps your narcotics unit, but how does it help Lois?” “It doesn’t,” Tony frowned. “I’m trying Pierce, really I am. I said I would help, and I will.” Tony sat down in a slumped position and looked blankly at the photos. The phone broke the silence and we both tensed. You catch on quick kid … it is a pity you didn’t take my offer of work.” “I wouldn’t want it,” I was now a littler nervous, but at the same time great amounts of anger surged through my body. “Well, I hear from a little birdie it was your 23rd birthday not too long ago,” he was laughing. “How’s your birthday been? You want a present?” “I, erm, em …” “Listen, your girlfriend is not in the best of conditions … very weak. Probably not a good idea to keep this ransom going on for any longer.” “Just tell me when and where,” I was now worried at the mention of her deteriorating condition. “She’s a right little army brat this one … gives me a headache … I shouldn’t have kidnapped her!” Harrington was changing the subject now, but I was smiling at the thought of Lois’s humour and wittiness still growing strong. “Harrington … just tell me when and where!” “I’ll call back with that information … don‘t rush me kid, or your little girlfriend here will end up like that friend of yours, Lilly Connelly,” the phone went dead. Harrington had hung-up. Tony Rush was smirking. “What?” “It means Lois is still alive,” he said with a grin. “It gives us hope.” “I have a plane to catch,” I remember then my job for the AWC. “What time is your flight, and where are you going?” “London, England. My flight is at 10:00 am. I need to pack my bags and get some sleep.” “I’ll stay here and keep a close eye on the phone and Shelby; you want to leave an away message?” “Better not, it means somebody might make a surprised visit.” “True, but I’ll be here,” Tony stated and sat comfortably in front of the TV with Shelby resting her head on his lap. “I guess you are right,” I pulled the answering machine up to my mouth and left an away message for any incoming calls. I was finished soon after and replayed the message from Harrington. This ordeal was going to pay the price of one life - Tony knew that too. We just hadn’t devised a good enough plan yet. I sat in solace and stared at the nights sky darkening as the sun had set. “Who Looks Outside, Dreams” “Who Looks Inside, Awakens”
