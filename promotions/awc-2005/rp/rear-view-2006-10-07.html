Rear View

7 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Oct 07/06 'Johnny, you need me.' The brassy voice announces pleadingly, distorted by the speaker phone buzz. 'I don't need anybody, Midas.' Johnny returns with a monotone displeasure. 'I don't need a manager, I don't need a driver, I don't need a publicist, public repesentative, or agent. I need you to be my accountant, that's it.' 'You're right, Johnny, you don't need me for anything. If you want to keep throwing your career away.' Johnny snarls at the receiver nestled safely on the seat next to his. 'I've been keeping yo books for two years, through the good times and the bad, and I have never seen you throw a fight like that since Trey schooled your ass out of Anarch Agencies.' 'You trying to make me hang up?' 'It was a Frontier Title shot Johnny, you had the ref giving you a quick count and you practically let that cracker throw you around!' 'What choice did I have? Really, Midas, you've been my accountant for two years and how many times have you seen me cheat to win? How many times have you seen me accept a four on one handicap match? The publicity, the fame, it's all bullshit, what's real is my honor, man. That was a lesson I learned real hard and twice they've tried to make me compromise that.' 'Who's this 'They'?' 'The Empire, the joke running the show David Harber.' 'Listen man if you don't want to take the opportunities given to you when they're presented then I think you're in the wrong business. Look around you, dude, everybody is being supported in one way or another by somebody else. Josh has Paddy, Gabriel has Harber, Paddy has Josh. Y'know that whole deal with them two is kinda creepy.' 'You're tellin me.' Johnny chuckles. 'Darcy has Aimz. You've been there better than two months and what have you got?' 'Three clean wins.' 'Hollow man, you've got no heat, no feud, no angle. You've got no game plan.' 'There's always a game plan.' 'Then somebodies missing it because you are sinking fast. You can't throw title fights, you want to be honorable then you give him a rematch with a clean ref you don't just pretend you're half retarded for three minutes!' 'What are you trying to make me say, Midas?' 'I'm saying let me make some choices, let me talk to some people. You know I'm the only person on your contact list?' 'It was either you or my lawyer.' Johnny shrugs though Midas can't see him. 'I've been getting calls from GCW about giving you a comeback, FUSE sent me a rep in person with an entire guideline on where they saw your image over the next six months. Ultimate Revolution-' 'Is that even Primetime Central?' 'No it's independant, but they're willing to offer you a twenty thousand dollar signing bonus. That's twenty thousand dollars man! Draven Masters is in contract negotiations with the place. You were a hot commodity before last night is the point.' 'That's the point?' 'I'll be surprised if I could negotiate a debut feud with any of these places now. This business is image based man and if I were you, I'd start asking myself if I was serious about being a wrestler. You're not a gladiator, there's not much room for morals or honor in sports entertainment. In this business how well you can fight takes second place to how good you look winning.' 'So you wanna be my manager then?' Johnny says abruptly. There's dead air for a minute. 'You're serious?' 'I'll be in Berlin by morning, you take some of my money you've been watching and fly out here to meet me.' 'But. But I can get so much more done here in LA.' 'I didn't say you were promoted just yet, Midas. If you want the job, text me your arrival time and I'll pick you up.' 'But. But what's in Berlin?' 'That's for you to decide.' Johnny pushes the button on his phone and glances at the scenery outside the confines of the car. His eyes meet themselves in the rear view mirror. It's not easy running away from who I am. Knowing where I come from doesn't explain where I've been. Or is it 'Knowing where I come from doesn't explain the things I've done'? I suppose it doesn't matter anymore, it's become a matter of who they think I am. My father wasn't always heartless, he used to be a good man living in a gentle time. My mother died of a sickness he never explained to me, I was only six years old. He sent me away to an expensive school, I used to think it was because I reminded him of her. Of what he'd lost. I learned much later it was because my father was not a good man and his time was far more bloody than gentle. And my mother was not sick, she was murdered. My father invited me to join him in Spain, but somewhere along the line I was taken. Held for a ransom my father wouldn't pay. I was tortured and starved for six months, beaten for names and secrets I didn't have to give them. I wanted death, more than anything I wanted to escape the life God had force fed me. I begged for a razor to slit my wrists, I pleaded for a rope to hang myself. I argued and fought in hopes of bitting a bullet that never came. When I stopped caring about the pain, gave up deciphering between life and death. Stopped saying 'I didn't know' and started saying 'I'll never tell.'. That's when my father came and took me away, he never told me why. When I asked he gave me a brochure for an exlusive hunting trip in Brazil. The trip included a cruise of the coast, a few nights in a luxury hotel and a twenty four hour hunt of the most cunning prey in the world. Humans. I got my first kill that weekend. My father called it cutting my teeth. I realized somewhere, hunkering down in a bush and watching a desperate man forage for a weapon to defend himself. Things started falling into place. My mother's death, the all boys boarding school, never a letter or package from home. I was being bred. My emotional ties were severed, I was losing respect for human life. I killed him with my knife. They gave me a rifle but I remember thinking that took the fun out of it. My father was paying for me to enjoy myself. It was my reward for pain and suffering in a cell, eating cardboard flavored oatmeal and drinking rain water. I was stripped of everything human and rebuilt into a killer. My father got me a job in Iraq, I was apprenticed to a man named Mohammed Ashamedi though it was forbidden to speak his name in front of others. Mohammed was a master of his trade, an artisan perhaps, my father said that he was the best. That the skills I earned in service now would benefit me later. Mohammed was a master of extracting the truth from those proficient at keeping it. The years after Desert Storm in the middle east were rife with opportunities to practice this skill, I was at first used as an interpreter for captured french and english speaking spies. I left before my final lessons, but my father found me as he always will. My training was almost done, he told me in Berlin. Standing before my mothers grave, he told me that I was ready to be his heir. He explained to me that my mother died because of who she loved, she was as innocent as I was. He told me he didn't want me to know that kind of loss, he wanted to protect me from that pain. He said that what happened in Spain lasted longer than he ever wanted it to, he said that my determination was too great. That even when I broke, I was still strong. He taught me how to kill, he taught me how to torture a man for the truth. Then he told me my final lesson was greater than them all, because it would keep me safe. The airport in Berlin, Germany buzzes with activity as Johnny leans against a wall and watches the escalator bring down departing passengers. He's wearing his dark blue jeans and denim jacket but nowhere on his face is the smile so many have grown accustomed to. Midas appears at the top and Johnny wonders if he's ever seen the proud looking, broad shouldered black man in anything but a suit. This would not be that day, Midas descends slowely and Johnny cantors towards him. 'Johnny Lexicon.' Midas smiles and steps up with an extended hand. His other hand running over his stubble covered scalp. 'Midas T. Gold.' Johnny slaps the hand and they pound knuckles. 'Alright, I'm here. Are you going to tell me what this is about.' Midas questions, his eyebrows raising. 'That's what you're supposed to be telling me.' Johnny motions Midas to follow him towards the exit. 'Where are we going?' 'I have to stop by the cemetary while you tell me all about how you're going to make a great manager for me and my sinking career.' 'That's just business talk Johnny.' 'Hey I have no hard feelings, but let's just get one thing straight. I would rather end an undefeated singles streak with a humiliating loss than win because some dick in a suit told the ref to make it happen. Fuck Darcy Crisis and The Empire both.' 'Fair enough. And this is why you need me, I'll set up a meeting with Harber and Wade, start talking strategy. I know this girl in LA who could successfuly market a bag of crap, I shit you not.' 'And if Harber won't listen?' The two of them settle into their seats, smelling of a rental car deodorizer and a long night of driving. 'Then we go somewhere that will.' 'EH! Wrong.' Johnny buzzes, wheeling out of his stall. 'I chose AWC on my own because this is where I want to be, you want to manage me than that's where you'll do it.' 'FUSE has been more than willing to work with me on almost every point. They're offering the debut feud with a guaranteed title shot in the first two months. They're giving us creative control of the gimmick.' 'I have creative control now, and I had my title shot in the first two months.' 'But where's the feud? Who's David?' 'Huh?' 'Exactly, nobody would fuckin care if it wasn't for Goliath!' 'Alright, you've made your point. But look, I've got a chance to go for another title shot' 'You really want to go from challenging for the Frontier title to winning a reinstated chump belt?' 'Beggers, choosers.' 'That's right, we're choosers Mister Lexicon.' 'I like that.' 'That we're choosers?' 'No, Mister Lexicon.' Johnny steers out of an intersection. 'I know I'm good, I know I should have used the avantage and taken Darcy's belt. But I don't need it, he needs it now more than I do. A good Livewire title run could be good.' 'You be the wrestler and let me be the manager. Did you realize you spend three fourths of what you make simply on hotels, rental cars, and plane tickets? The rest goes to food, equipment, and soon alimony payments. I really wish you'd take her to court for this divorce, she committed adultry.' 'She can have whatever she wants.' Johnny answers dryly and changes the subject. 'What about promotional efforts? Magazines, t-shirts, action figures.' 'We can set you up a meeting with a producer before the pay per view, we'll talk image, angle, gimmick. I know this guy Garret-' 'Garret Wade?' 'Yeah the guy's been behind the scenes for decades, really knows how to work with the audience.' Midas is so into his pitch he doesn't see the half veiled scowl cross Johnny's face. 'I'll set up a meeting with Harber, if they want you half as bad as you want to be there then they'll negotiate. More than half of your expenses should be covered on a company budget, cross atlantic flights aren't cheap. That'll leave you with enough freed up funds to pay me to set up a marketing campaign to make you more money.' 'You're an accountant at heart.' 'The world runs on money my man.' Midas explains with a smile. 'And how much are you expecting to make off me?' Johnny gives a side-long glance with a challenge in his tone. 'Twelve percent of whatever I put my hands on.' He wiggles his fingers. 'I could get a manager with experience for ten.' 'Ah yes but you're not just getting a manager, this twelve percent comes with a package deal. We're talking accountant, manager, and PR all wrapped up into one golden touched brotha. You've got almost five years professional experience, more natural talent than half these happy jacks can shake a stick at, with a face that begs for a camera. You're ready to break out. This is your time! I just wanna be there to show you how. I just wanna make sure nobody screws you around like they have been the last couple weeks. This is a shark eat shark business, Johnny, you can't always watch your own back.' Johnny chuckles, glancing quickly into the rear-view mirror. Learning how to pick a lock was easier than I had expected, or maybe I took after my father. The man could crack a safe by touch alone. I'm not that good, but six weeks after a broken thumb no hand-cuff's in the world can hold me. He taught me how to bend bars using only the instruments commonly applied to holding me; taking doors off their hinges and scaling the side of buildings without rope. The tricks were easy, Marcus told me often I was a natural. A prodigy to the family business. He taught me how to escape while teaching me how to break in. Every man's life has a price tag and only those willing to pay the highest prices came to Marcus Van Mill. We had a reputation for being unstoppable. We weren't always clean, but we never missed a target. When I was ready, or when Marcus said I was ready, I started meeting people. People I didn't want to meet, hearing plans I didn't want to hear. Marcus wanted a territory, he wanted more people working for him, wanted to set up an operation in Paris. He called me his general, the heir to the empire he was building. I told him I didn't want to be a general, he told me it was my destiny. He offered me anything in the world, he said anything at all. I wanted revenge. I wanted to know who killed my mother. 'We're here.' Johnny says, parking the car abruptly on the shoulder of a deserted stretch of road. 'We're here? Where's here?' Midas looks around at the sparse forest and rolling hills of nothing but a town in the far distance. And a burnt out old house off a grass covered dirt road. 'This is where I was born.' Johnny climbs out the passenger side door. Midas watches Johnny walk around the hood, looking at the charred ruins made gloomy by the grey ceiling of clouds. Finally he opens his door and joins Johnny in the cool fall climate, buttoning up the front of his blazer. 'You weren't born here.' Johnny barks with laughter. 'Oh no?' He challenges, looking over before he starts to walk up the path. 'You told me you were born in Calgary.' Midas says, hurrying to catch up. 'Everybody has a story, whether it's a true one or not.' They come to a stop a few dozen feet from the house, a light drizzle starting to fall from the heavens. 'My mother died in this fire.' Johnny explains, looking to Midas. 'She was murdered here.' He continues after a pause. 'And I wanted revenge. I spent my entire life searching for it. Preparing myself to get it.' Midas doesn't answer, reading the reverance on every inch of Johnny's face, his eyes no longer looking at the house. Instead looking into the past. 'I found and killed two men for what they did, but not before I got them to tell me who hired them. He was a booky, a thug like the other two but he spilled his guts in half the time. I killed seven people looking for the man who wanted my mother dead. When I found him.' Johnny seems to snap out of it, looking over at Midas paying rapt attention to every word. 'What happened?' Midas provokes after an extended silence. 'I couldn't do it.' Johnny turns away from the house. 'The man who went through so much trouble to hide his involvement, the man who paid to make it all happen.' Midas moves to follow Johnny back to the car, a few steps behind. 'But why? After all those people and coming so close, why couldn't you do it?' Johnny stops beside his car and looks back at Midas. 'His name is Marcus Van Mill, and he's untouchable.' Johnny climbs in and Midas hurries to follow suit. Johnny's gripping the steering wheel tight when Midas closes his door and looks over at him. 'He wanted me to find out. He gave me all the tools to find the truth.' Johnny looks over, meeting Midas' eyes evenly. 'I can't even tell him that I know what he did.' 'Why?' Midas asks quietly. 'Because I'll kill him.' Johnny starts the car. 'And that's exactly what he wants.' He checks the rear-view mirror, his eyes hesitating on the face he sees reflected before he drops it into drive and looks only to the road ahead.
