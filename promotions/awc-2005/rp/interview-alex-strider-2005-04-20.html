Interview: Alex Strider

20 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 20th, 2005 When asked to describe himself, Alexander Strider responds with: “I’m just your average son of a blue-collar steelworker, trying to live out the American dream the only way I know how: though hard work and honest living.” Like every time he answers one of my questions, he winks at me. Just telling me what he thinks I want to hear. We’ve met here at a local café near his home because it’s where he wanted. I just have to ask the questions and formulate an article. Send it up to the editors. Then they’ll see “That’s a start, but doesn’t quite make a feature article. You’re going to have to give me more,” I tell him. “hat or I’ll have to fill my article with even more exaggerations than would even be acceptable for a story on professional wrestlers.” “O.k., I wouldn’t want to make things too difficult for you. After all, we just met.” He winks, blue eyes twinkling. “Well, I think when pressed, anyone answering your questions first off identifies themselves with what they do. I don’t like the generality of that That doesn’t address “why” .For example… some men end up being a salesman. Zookeepers. Automatic car-washer repairmen, whatever. They live their life, and they wake up every morning, and that is what they are, it‘s what they do, all at the same time. But WHY? Why would someone choose to work at Sears selling appliances? Why would someone want to clean up horse shit with a shovel, for God‘s sake?” “Why?” “Because most people are too scared to think about what might happen if they actually succeeded, so they don’t bother trying. They let themselves serve. They lull themselves into thinking that they’re good at what they do, so they don’t need to try harder. That’s what I couldn’t get a handle on. No one is society is truly, really, self-actualized. They are all servicing something else. Serfs working the farmland for a duke they’ll never meet. A duke that determines how much of the produce they broke their back to make will actually come back to feed their children. I told myself that if that’s how society works, then I’m for damn sure going to become one of the people who are in control.” Alex pauses as he raises his cooling cup of coffee to his mouth. “So you’ve become a professional wrestler not because it’s your lot in life, but because you don’t want to be average?” Alex frowns at me. Looking up at the noon sky, he replaces his sunglasses. “No. I can’t help not being average. The way I look at it, if I had been interested in politics, I’d be the President of the United States. If I wanted to be a scientist, I would be the man to develop the next best doomsday device. Do you understand what I’m saying? All of that would be great, but it wouldn’t allow me the pleasure that I get from wrestling.” This was why I wasn‘t sure I wanted to take this assignemtn: I wasn‘t sure I could handle this much machismo packed into so short a time. “O.k., so what does wrestling offer you that being President of the largest economic power in the world wouldn’t?” I ask. “The president isn’t really in control, is he? He shares the power with the Congress and the Supreme Court. No, that’s not really what I would want to be after, sharing power. Because when it comes to the wrestling world, I am in control. Everything I’ve wanted up to this point, I’ve been able to get. That’s what I am after. When it comes to wrestling, down to every last detail, I’m in charge.” “Alex, I don’t understand. I mean, it’s a competitive sport. You’re not wrestling yourself, you’re wrestling someone else entirely. You can’t possibly control everything.” Alexander rolls back in his chair, staring at the blue sky above us from behind his black sunglasses with an irritated frown. “I don’t expect you to understand. You’re just a reporter. You only scratch the surface when you ask questions, you can only infer so much, anyway.” I bite my tongue, hoping that he doesn’t notice as I shift in my seat. “But trust me when I say… I have more control of what happens to me than you realize. I can get inside the mind of my opponent. I can work myself into their nightmares. When it comes to wrestling, I’ve honed my body to the very point of perfection. The very second I get into the ring, I refuse to relinquish control. The match moves at the speed that pleases me. I will hurt my opponent as much as I want, before I decide to end it. My opponent doesn’t fit into the equation, because it’s not about who I’m fighting. It’s about the violence. It’s about getting the crowd to froth at the mouth with anticipation. It’s about breaking someone else’s spirit.” “O.k., but if your ambition is so great.. Wouldn’t you want to be a commissioner, or owner of a league? Wouldn’t that symbolize your aspirations that much more?” “Hey, owners, commissioners, referees. They’re all the same to me. Bureaucrats. Use red tape to keep things tidy and under wraps. Sure, the owner makes money off of the crowds that I draw in. But it’s symbiotic. The owner brings me the opportunity to do what I do best on a global stage where I’m celebrated for breaking someone else’s face. I don’t mind letting someone get a little rich off of what I do. The Great White shark can’t help that the remora fish hang around to eat their scraps. “ So far, I’m learning more than I think I’d like to know about Mr. Alex Strider. “Listen, this place is boring me. Pamela, wasn’t it? What do you say, Pamela, why don’t we take the rest of this interview somewhere else? I know just the spot.” I have to let him have his way, even though I hate it. He knows that I’m not done interviewing him, so I can’t help but play along. I bite my lip, because even though I hate it, I can’t help but find a little bit of truth in what he says. Am I just a sheep bowing to the will of someone else? … … No. And by the time I’m able to get my story together, he’ll see just how wrong he is. View Alexander Strider 's Biography
