The Turnaround

13 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13th October, 2006 The Democratic Party Headquarters based in New Jersey was a place I had seldom visited in the last few years. My political career was fast-paced and constant; there were no opportunities to drop in since my breakthrough onto the local scene in 2003. Yet as I walked through the corridors I knew so well, the faces that watched me were familiar. I waved to the secretary as I passed by the front desk. I needed no direction from her. I had only one course of action if I ever wanted to see my father again, and that was the tough option – retirement. Upon hearing the threat from Dean Fisher, I was crestfallen. In truth, my father meant the world to me and I would do anything to ensure his safety, even end my career. I wasn’t even twenty-five years old yet; I had plenty of time to revive my career. That notion was by no means a comfort for me as I pressed six in the elevator shaft. I lived in the here and now, not in the future. My excuse for ending my career had to be a valid one, and so I opted for the mental illness approach. It meant that there was no physical harm done to me, and therefore it would be easier to prove. However, persuading my doctor to write a medical certificate for this illness hadn’t been easy. I couldn’t explain my situation with Fisher to him, so I had to tell a white lie or two. I reached into the breast pocket of my suede coat to assure myself the certificate was still there, and stepped out of the elevator on the sixth floor. The sixth floor of the headquarters housed what I liked to call “the brain boxes” of the party. It comprised of men and women who possessed great intelligence insofar as politics was concerned. Each one had the aura of someone special and unique. I made the short walk between the partitions that separated the offices on the floor to the office of my superior, Jonathan Murray. Murray reacted with surprise as he saw me enter his office, despite the fact I had called him this morning to confirm my appointment with him. He was a down to earth kind of person with a heart of gold. His weapon of choice, of course, was politics. “Wesley, did you check out the papers today? They’re saying we’re down in the polls overall!” Murray exclaimed, slapping the paper with the back of his hand as I took a seat without being asked. “Nope, can’t say I have, sir,” I replied, my voice telling Murray that now wasn’t the time for pleasantries or conversations regarding the papers. I was here on business. Once I showed him the certificate, I would collect my resignation form and leave the building, but in my gut I knew it wouldn’t be for the last time. “So, you said on the phone this was a matter of urgency, Wesley. What exactly is it?” “Unfortunately, sir, I have a mental illness that my doctor tells me will prevent me from working at my full capacity. Therefore, for the foreseeable future, I have to stay out of work. The bad news is that he doesn’t know if or when I’ll recover from this, so I’ve been asked to effectively… retire, sir.” The news hit Murray hard, harder than I imagined it would. One of the brightest sparks of his regime in charge, yours truly, had just asked to retire from the party. Murray composed himself and began to pace around his office, a habitual occurrence if he was stressed or unsure. I could tell questions were on his lips. “Wesley, this is definitely unexpected. Is there any way we could help you recover from the illness quickly so you wouldn’t have to retire?” “I doubt it, sir. It could be permanent. I have a medical certificate here to prove it, by the way,” I said, handing him the certificate. He took it and a look of pure defeat crossed his face. I knew he had bought my cover story. “Fair enough, Wesley,” Murray conceded after re-reading the medical certificate several times. “If you have to leave, then so be it.” I shook his hand and we had an amicable farewell. There were no hard feelings between Murray and I. I was simply disappointed in myself for allowing myself to succumb to Fisher’s desires and actually retire from the job I loved so much. I walked down the corridor by the partitions again with many eyes watching me, all of them undoubtedly believing they had seen me for the last time. I was on the walk of shame, and it was a walk I would not want to repeat. Phil Allen wiped the sweat from his brow as he turned off the freeway and onto the street he had come to know so well over the past few weeks. New Jersey had slowly transformed into a home away from home for Allen. Just a few days earlier, he visited his wife in San Diego, who was three months pregnant now with his unborn child. He had already racked his brain about how he would manage once the child was born, considering the commitments he had in wrestling. Since his tag team partner Wesley Reno had been ordered to retire by Dean Fisher, Allen’s life had changed significantly. The void left by Reno was filled by a small cards club based on the street Allen was currently driving on. He swerved right into the car park which faced onto the back of the building where the club gathered every Tuesday night. Tonight was Poker night, and Allen looked forward each week to pitting his wits again his fellow players. If he wasn’t training for an upcoming match, he was involved in card-playing. Since signing up for the cards club, Allen noted the attendance was consistently good. Tonight was different, for some reason. Allen entered the building and surveyed the room. There were at most five people scattered about the place, most of them practicing card tricks. Allen recognised one of the men as his acquaintance Roger Degger, a part-time construction worker from Boston. Degger spotted Allen at the entrance and waved him over to his table, where he sat alone with a rucksack beside him. Degger was dressed in his construction clothes tonight, having come straight from work, Allen assumed. “Not too active tonight, is it?” Allen commented, surveying the deserted club as he took a seat opposite Degger. “Not tonight, Phil. How are you doing, buddy? Still kicking the bad guy’s asses?” Allen knew that when Degger referred to the so-called “bad guys”, he meant the professional wrestlers Allen worked with on a regular basis. Degger knew little of the complexities the wrestling business presented. Allen chose to keep him in the dark on his career for the most part, sharing a friendly story now and then to discourage him from probing further into his affairs. “Can I show you something, Phil? Come on around the back and I’ll show it to you,” Degger said, sitting up and walking towards the exit door at the back of the club. Allen followed, albeit slightly reluctantly. The back alley was filled with men Allen recognised as members of the cards club, all of them smoking what appeared to be joints. The men exhaled in unison and laughed amongst themselves at nothing in particular. Allen, unsettled, shifted to the wall and watched Degger remove a joint from his rucksack. He handed it to Allen like a birthday present. At first, the feelings of anxiety in Allen were strong, but as he held the joint in his hand, those feelings all but disappeared. All at once they were replaced with eagerness to try something new and different. Allen had been the lapdog of too many people in recent years. As Degger grinned encouragingly at him, Allen knew what he had to do. “Is it done yet, Mr. Fisher?” the man asked impatiently, the Spanish lilt in his voice obvious. “I’m pleased to say it is, sir,” Fisher replied, confidence oozing through his words. “Good work. It’s time to set up the meeting to return his father to him. Let him have his pathetic wish.” “Are you certain that’s the right course of action?” “When have I ever been wrong before, Mr. Fisher? Tell me that,” the man asked, the tone in his voice leaving no room for argument from Fisher. “He’ll remain unharmed, sir. Then the plan can proceed.” “Everything will go according to plan if you follow my instructions, Mr. Fisher,” the man concluded, hanging up the phone after uttering them. He leaned back in his chair and lit up a Cuban cigar, relaxing to the melodies from the country of his birth. Dean Fisher slowly placed the phone back on its receiver and spun majestically in his leather chair, turning towards Joseph Lynch standing menacingly at the door. Lynch was dressed in a black jumper and jeans, accentuating his muscles. The small, cramped room was still as untidy as before. There was no semblance of organisation where Fisher was based – its purpose was purely to orchestrate terror. “Joe, where’s Lilly? She was meant to be here over an hour ago.” Lynch shrugged. “I haven’t seen her. She was feeling a little under the weather last night, as a matter of fact. You were at a conference. She couldn’t wait any longer and decided to head home.” “I understand,” Fisher said, more to himself than to Lynch. His mind wandered, worry creeping over him as to his girlfriend’s whereabouts. “Is there anything at all I can do, sir?” Lynch pondered. “No. I’ll leave her be,” Fisher replied, his thoughts fixated on Baines. She was a liability for him if she fell into the wrong hands. Fisher hoped she wouldn’t get into trouble, or all of his good work would go up in smoke. After resigning from the job I loved for so many years, I no longer had the inclination to go for dinner as planned. Instead I went straight home and began playing my guitar. Music always served to relax me when times were tough, and times couldn’t get any tougher than they were right now. Admittedly, I was only an amateur at the guitar, but I tried my best. The night was cool and windy. A sudden knock on my front door alarmed me. As I approached my front door, I couldn’t imagine who would be calling at this hour. I envisaged a private press officer outside, but when I looked through the lens, I saw the woman I once loved look back at me, almost as if she knew I was looking at her. I froze on the spot. Lilly Baines was at my door. “Wes, let me in,” she shouted, the raging wind drowning her voice out. I couldn’t simply trust her, but something inside me told me she was here with good intentions. It was her demeanour – there wasn’t the callousness she had developed in recent times while dating Fisher. It was replaced with a tenderness that I admired in her. I opened the door slowly and nodded for her to come in. She obliged, and placed her jacket on the hook at the bottom of the stairs. I stood and examined her facial expression as she turned to me, her face red with a combination of embarrassment and cold. “What are you doing here, Lilly?” “I’m here because… I’m here because I want to help you, Wes. I know what Dean has put you through, and frankly I want out of his plans and his life.” Skeptical by her sudden turnaround, I wasn’t about to commit to anything until I had concrete evidence that she was truly against Fisher. I had no real method to do this, so I decided to humour her. “What do you need me for?” I asked, folding my arms across my chest in a defensive gesture. “Wes, you’ve been messed around by Fisher long enough. I can offer you top secret information about him and his organisation if and only if you can promise me protection from him. If you do, I can tell you where he is and you can be reunited with your father.” Her deal sounded like a good one, but I detected a flaw or two. “I was told my father would be returned once I resigned. Why would I need to help you stay clear of Fisher if he’ll be returned to me anyway?” “He won’t be returned, Wes! Don’t be so gullible. Fisher never had any intention of returning him to you. If you want to see him again, you have to trust me!” “So, if I keep you safe, you’ll give me Fisher’s whereabouts?” I asked, stepping towards her in a threatening way as I did so. “That’s right,” she responded, a mixture of panic and anxiety inherent in her voice. “You’ve got yourself a deal, Ms. Baines, but I guarantee you that I will keep you on a tight leash. If you try anything that compromises my position, I will not hesitate to hurt you.” My threat was simple and effective; she knew her place in this operation. I decided I would give Phil Allen a call and once I learned of Fisher’s whereabouts, we would go there together to find him. As skeptical as I was about Baines’ intentions, I could use her to my advantage if anything went wrong, in the form of having leverage over Fisher if he tried anything. Baines began explaining where Fisher was, and I listened intently. Allen couldn’t do it. The joint in his hand felt like poison as he positioned it between his fingers. He threw it in fury to the ground and stomped it into the wet pavement, berating himself for even considering using such a drug. What would his wife think of him for this? Allen didn’t even want to contemplate it. He would be letting himself and everyone he knew down. Degger smoked his joint and leaned his head back against the wall, seemingly enjoying its intoxicating fumes. The phone on Allen’s belt began to chirp, and he picked it up as he made his way back into the cards club to escape the fumes outside. The fresh air felt good in Allen’s lungs as he opened his phone. “Phil, it looks like things have finally turned our way,” Reno began, his voice filled with optimism for the first time in weeks. Reno went on to explain that Lilly Baines had come to his home and, on the condition that she remained safely away from Dean Fisher, she would give Reno information about Fisher’s whereabouts. Allen could hardly believe what he was hearing. He processed it all in his mind and formulated a response. “That’s great news, Wes. Keep a close eye on the girl, though. She could be a problem.” “She won’t be, Phil, and I’ll make sure of it,” Reno replied calmly. “Where do we go to find Fisher?” “He’s been under our noses the whole time, right here in New Jersey. Meet me at Frontier Street in ten minutes, and I’ll lead you from there.” “I’ll be there, Wes,” Allen said, closing his phone as he left the cards club, a wave of surprise and regret overcoming him simultaneously. Dean Fisher’s reclining chair creaked as he laid back on it to relax his aching muscles. Joseph Lynch stood at the door, now smoking a cigar, the butt of previous cigars scattered around the dull, grey concrete floor. Fisher took a long sip from his cup of coffee, and turned to Lynch with something on his mind. “Joseph, we’ve done a great job here over the past couple of weeks. I’d just like to congratulate you on a job well done, my friend.” “It’s been a pleasure, Mr. Fisher,” Lynch replied, removing the cigar from between his lips as he did so. Just as Fisher was about to take another sip from his coffee, a high-pitched shout from down the hallway shocked him into action. Lynch turned to the door and in one motion opened it and lashed himself out into the corridor. Lynch removed his pistol from his holster and turned to Fisher. “Mr. Fisher, shall I investigate?” “Yes! Go, Joseph!” Fisher exclaimed in an authoritative manner. Fisher could only see the door at the end of the corridor burst open and Lynch going down at the hands of an uppercut to the jaw. The man who knocked Lynch out was none other than Phil Allen. Allen stepped into the corridor, closely followed by Wesley Reno. Fisher’s mind flooded with questions as to how they found a way in and discovered where he was. None of that mattered now, though. Fisher, for the first time in a long time, felt fear. Reno moved slowly up the hallway to Fisher’s untidy office, gently moving Phil Allen aside as he did so. There was purpose in his step. Reno relished the moment as he walked up to Fisher’s desk and stared him in the eyes. Fury flowed through his veins. “I’ve been waiting for this, Dean,” Reno said through clenched teeth. Allen shut the door behind them and the room went dark. It was Fisher versus Reno, no holds barred.
