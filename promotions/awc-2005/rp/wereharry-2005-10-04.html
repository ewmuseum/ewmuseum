WereHarry

4 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/10/05 Harry played rugger at school and never really knew how to behave around girls. He was boisterous and came across bashful rather than rugged as he hoped. His all-boys educational environment meant he matured late and even to this day makes inappropriate jokes that his trendy counterparts laugh hysterically at and cover up for in certain company, “Harry isn’t racist, why Dwayne here’s our breadbin” the term they were looking for was ‘bredrin’ and one which Dwayne, a doctor never used. Sarah went to an all-girl school and was by far the prettiest girl amongst the beaus in her class. Some will tell you that to say all-girl schools breed horny, well-educated teenagers is merely a stereotype. This is not to say it’s untrue, and Sarah Sunningdale was not only the smartest and prettiest girl around, she was also a minx. Now as a trendy twenty-something, Harry was aware of her reputation when he met her at the exclusive champagne unveiling of the new collection at the Saatchi gallery; John’s father (John senior) has a friend who know a bloke... Harry smelt her perfume before he saw she’d crept up behind him as he stood gazing at Damien Hirst’s shark, “You’ve been standing here for ages, what’s so great about this that it’s had you in rapture for so long?” She was pale and eloquent, her fragrance was like incense although it was this with a combination of champagne and cigarette smoke that completed her unique scent that set off her frail beauty; which was nice but Harry had to say something about art, quickly, he’d been gaping at her for too long now, “It reminds me of my childhood” she placed a hand on her chin in a show of interest, but Harry knew he was well out of his depths, “I had a goldfish, I accidentally killed it a few times” He had the girl in hysterics. Sarah thought Harry was hilarious as they wandered the gallery halls, him offering opinions of the latest works of the world’s top contemporary artists, her assuming he was satirising art critics and was infact passionate about art. As the two stood in front of a Dirk Skreber piece Harry contemplated holding Sarah’s hand in a juvenile romantic gesture as he explained how the piece is like a Hornby train set he once had, “but it caught on fire and we had to get a new carpet” John’s hand met with Harry’s shoulder, Harry thought he was well in, “Harry we’ve gotta go or we’ll be late, Skydome tonight, takes a while for Dwayne to drive up” Walking to the car Harry explained to Sarah that Skydome was a club. Sarah must by no accounts find out that Harry makes his money through professional wrestling, she was the most popular girl in school, and this kind of social status doesn’t disappear when one finishes education. She was so desirable on a social level, she was hot, she was actually pretty funny, and she has a degree in fine art. What on earth she saw in Harry, the rugby wrestling and beer machine god only knows, but she saw something and Harry needed to keep it that way. John knew Harry’s game. John was also Harry’s best friend since they squashed frogs on Mercedes windows as eight-year-olds, and as such did anything conversationally to help. The girls were already at the venue and wouldn’t hinder Harry’s chances of pulling. Dwayne opened the rear doors and Harry, Sarah and John were ticked off a list and lead down a corridor. “I don’t hear any music” Harry’s cheeks rouged, “This is the VIP section, it’s soundproofed off so we can have whatever music we want” interjected John, his delivery made the blatant lie seem believable. The girls knew who Sarah was and bitched about her the entire evening, but smiled at her from a distance. Thirty percent of any female TTS’s life is taken up doing this. John also stayed out of Harry and Sarah’s way and stretched off. Harry convinced Sarah that John’s stretching was a dance and everyone would be doing it, it’s part of the pseudo-chic-post-latte-modernism movement. This was also the explanation as to why the VIP room of the club was little more than drab cider blocks. Harry felt like he’d only just sat down before an hour had passed and John said “Harry we’ve to go” again. “Where, why do you have to go Harry?” “I just do” “That’s ridiculous, there must be a reason” “Come on Harry” begged John, Mr. Scruff’s ‘Get A Move On’ had already began to echo around the building “Turn away Sarah” “What?” “Don’t look at me!” almost afraid Sarah obliged, turning her head, but turning back as Harry finished taking his suit off to reveal his singlet “You’re, you’re, a...a... wrestler!?!” “Noooooooooooooooooooo” Harry howled. Harry never saw Sarah Sunningdale again, he hasn’t been able to go to the Saatchi Gallery since the incident.
