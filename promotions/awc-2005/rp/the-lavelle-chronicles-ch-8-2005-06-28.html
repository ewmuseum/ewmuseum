The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch. 8

28 June 2005

Date: 28th June 2005 The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch. 8 “And so the story continues --” Albany Medical Centre, NY Part 1 : Aftermath The hospital smelt of that overpowering odour of mixed chlorine and floor cleaner. I sat still on the bed waiting for my medical card to come back. I had been seated on the same bed for over 24 hours by now. My head was spinning from the fumes and the sudden burst of fresh oxygen into my lungs. My back had been stitched up, bar the bad news that I made need a skin-graft to replace the damaged skin from the fire. I had been thinking a lot about Lilly and how I had let her go. I blamed myself now for her death - I should have been able to help both her and Lois. I shouldn’t have left. Lilly had become extremely close to me and Rob over the last few weeks and now she was gone; all because of Harrington. Why am I doing this to myself? I couldn’t even answer that. A psychologist would say that I was blaming myself for something I couldn’t have stopped. What would they know? The hours ticked on by tediously. The rush of white suits flickered past my door endlessly, like the sun rising and falling in such a fast motion that it created a pure beam of white light. I felt I was being watched now. Maybe that was the lack of sleep and fumes talking. “Pierce, you’re up,” Rob said as he made his way into the room with two cups of coffee. His clothes still had black soot on them from the fire’s smoke and ash. His face was cleaned to heel the cuts and gashes from the debris that fell. “Yeah, thanks for the coffee,” I turned around to face him. All I was wearing were my black boxer shorts and my white sports socks which were now stained with dried blood from Lois’s cut stomach. I worried a lot about her over the passing hours. I knew I would have to see her once I had my medical card and clothing back. “You gave me quite a fright, Pierce,” Rob said as he leaned back on the chair. *Rob had been quiet all morning as he sat in the waiting room, awaiting the opportunity to see both of his friends. Rob had begun to contemplate why his best friend had gone back for Lois and not Lilly. He didn’t like thinking like this, as Lilly was dead, but he soon felt the answer was very evident under the circumstances that his best friend still might love Lois.* “I gave myself the biggest fright of all, Rob,” I couldn’t sit still anymore. I couldn’t lie down because my back was still stinging and the tingling in my lower back hadn’t gone. Instead it had travelled to both of my legs, leaving a “pins and needles” effect. Quiet filled the room between both me and Rob. He hadn’t spoken in a while, rather thought to himself. I couldn’t even imagine what he was thinking about at this moment in time, because all I could think about now was, Lois. She had been out cold when I last saw her and since then I had heard no news from anyone. “Is Lois alright?” I asked. Rob now stared at me. He had finished his coffee and he looked right through me as though I had answered a question he had been fighting with all morning. I felt slightly uncomfortable when around him lately. My own best friend had become distant and more inward with his own emotions. Perhaps I had pushed people away from me. “…you’re awake, that’s brilliant,” a man dressed in a white coat approached. He was a short, stump, of a man with very broad shoulders. His face was covered in stubble. His hair was shaved almost down to the skin and his glasses perched delicately over the brim of his nose as he held a brown folder in hand. “Yes, that’s what people keep saying,” I smiled at my sudden inconvenient joke. “I’m Doctor Edwards,” he reached out his hand and shook mine. “I have been going over your file for the last hour and you will need a slight skin-graft along your lower back. You burnt away a lot of that skin in your escape.” In a gentle fashion he held my waist and turned me around so that my back was facing him. He pulled over a small stool on wheels and slid over to the bed. He gently began to undress the medical dressing which was wrapped around my waist at least six times. Before I knew it, he was at the last piece and that’s when it began to hurt. My skin was pussy, or what was left of it. Plasma was oozing around the damaged skin, trying to join with the platelets to form a sort of mesh to stop blood from escaping and to stop pathogens from entering my exposed dermis (inner-layer of the epidermis). I cringed at the last part when he began to put a cool compress against it. “Man, this is sick,” Rob said. He began to move in closer for a better view. “How’d he rip away all of that flesh?” Rob asked the Doctor. “Simple really; when the floor collapsed it sent a lot of shrapnel and timber down to the people below. Now when Pierce was standing and felt a slight tingling, he may have thought it was a slight burn but in reality he pulled off some skin. His nerves weren’t able to allow him to feel a lot of that pain, as it would have been quite sudden. That is until he hit the escape door with his back.” Doctor Edwards began applying skin-grafts to the damaged area. “It looks extremely painful,” Rob looked away for a second. “All that trouble to save Lois?” He stared at me. This time though it was a caring and considerate smile. As though he had realised how much pain I was in and how much I risked in saving our friend. “Em, Doc,” I looked around slowly. I tried not to move my waist. “Yes,” Doctor Edwards said while keeping one eye on the skin-graft. “My legs have gone numb, my lower back in tingling like hell,” I couldn’t have put it in a more eloquent manner. “When did this start happening?” the Doctor was now concerned. “The legs have only started to go numb. My back has been killing me all day,” I was worried now myself. If I had damaged anything I would be unable to move for at least a while. The doctor laughed gently and scanned through the nurse’s notes. “What’s so funny?” Rob asked. Taking the words right out of my mouth. “It’s just the medicine reacting to your bodies elements. You are under a heavy dose of pain killers to ease the stinging along the lower back. The tingling sensation you are feeling is a common side affect amongst most burn victims,” the Doctor returned to what he was doing. I had a sudden wave of relief fall over me as I heard the words come out of his mouth. My heart had stopped racing and my mind wasn’t as misty as earlier. Perhaps the coffee boost was waking my system up. Robert had become as much of a brother to me as a best friend. But, as I sat here waiting for the Doctor to finish the skin-graft, I felt extremely disloyal to Robert because of the things I thought about Lois. How I held her hand and how I stared into her eyes in that moment when I thought I was going to die myself. “All done,” the Doctor stood up. “You’ll have to change the bandage very regularly. Last thing you want is an infection among everything else. You’re extremely lucky, Pierce. And from what I heard in the staff room, you’re also a hero. Well done.” The Doctor shook my hand and left. “I’m going to need some clothing, Rob,” I smiled with relief that the events of the last day were almost over. “Sure, only we don’t have a home anymore,” Rob looked down. Running his hands through his stubble and under his chin. “I still have some money left, I’ll head down to the mall and pick you up some new gear, you take care of yourself, hero.” Rob placed his hand on my unscarred shoulder and smiled. He then exited the room and made his way down the hall. Realisation (Lois) Lois remained still and silent as four nurses crowded around her unconscious body; checking vital signs, breathing equipment and her health status. Lois had been out cold for a day now, allowing her body to take in the new oxygen supplied by the ventilator, while her body also removed the toxins that entered during the fire. Lois was awake, even though her eyes were closed. She refused to open them with all the nurses in the room. All she could think about was how Pierce had risked everything when he came bursting into her bedroom. Her hands twitched uncontrollably as the fresh oxygenated blood moved to all of her muscles, causing a slight spasm, each time it did so. She knew she was on the mend and safe because of Pierce and she was extremely grateful. Rob on the other hand seemed like somebody completely different to her, since she had arrived in Albany. Not once had he ever checked up on her when she was sick. He never made her coffee in the morning, all of this was done by Pierce and she smiled. She knew what she felt for Pierce was something she would never feel for Rob. She sighed a little, realising that Pierce liked Lilly a lot more than he liked her, realising this made a small clear tear emerged from her shut eyelid. “She’s crying,” one of the nurses announced. “Poor girl, she’s been through a lot,” the other said. “That man is very lucky, he needed a skin-graft,” the male nurse said. While wiping away Lois’s tear. Man? Lois realised they were talking about Pierce. Who else would they be talking about? She wouldn’t know until she could speak without the tube in her throat. She coughed gently as they began to pull it out. This action caused a sudden acid reflux and the feeling of nausea, as acids approached up through her trachea and onto her tongue. She had nothing else to do, but swallow the vile acids. “All done here,” a female nurse said. She propped Lois’s head up onto a cushion. The four nurses left the room and Lois in silence. Lois finally opened her eyes and stared at the empty room, the equipment, machinery and the tubes protruding from her arms and chest. She worried about Pierce, now. Well since the doctor said he was lucky. It seemed even though she was awake, she was still dreaming. The last thing Lois could remember was putting on a string top when the window around her shattered and the room burst into flames. She could remember Pierce touching her bare flesh as he wrapped a bandage around her waist and how much she tingled with that touch and how he hoisted her into the air. She was out cold after that. She decided she needed to see him and in one motion she clicked the buzzer for the nurse. “Is everything okay?” a nurse ran into the room. She swung her stethoscope over her neck. The third degree “Pierce Lavelle?” a tall man said. He stood before me wearing a dark blue suit with a black shirt. His NYPD badge was hitched onto his black belt and his gun remained close to his hip, under his right arm. His coat was in hand as he waited for my reply. “Yeah, I’m Pierce Lavelle. You are?” I was confused by his appearance. “Detective Tim Rivers. This is my partner, Casey Rider,” he pointed to a slim Mexican woman, with brown eyes and long brown hair, tied up in a bun behind her neck. She wore a tight woman’s string top and a pair of jeans. “To whom do I owe the pleasure of your company, Detectives?” I continued to look at both. Not removing my glare. The last time two police officers had approached me was back in my rebel days in College. I had been out drinking and died the swimming pool green, when two well built guards approached me and brought me downtown to sit out my alcoholic manner. “Mr. Lavelle, we are here to talk about the fire.” Casey Rider approached me gently. I removed my look from Rivers and stared Rider in the face. She was a very beautiful woman, who obviously cared a lot about what she did. Her look held a manner of experience and wisdom over time, only a few cops ever held this look. “Why do you want to ask me about the fire?” I remembered then that Harrington had a certain involvement in the death of Lilly. I wanted nothing more than to rat him out but I feared for the safety of Lois, Rebecca, Rob and my mom. “We are asking everyone, Pierce,” Rivers stepped forward. “Just protocol,” he said. “Protocol?” I didn’t get what the two cops wanted. Casey took the floor. “Just a few quick questions on what you remember. That is all, Pierce,” she said while pulling out a pen and paper. “Sure. It won’t be of much help to you,” I looked out the window and saw that it was raining for the first time in a while, here in Albany. “We need to know about Lilly Connelly?” Rivers said directly. “It is extremely important that you tell the truth, Pierce,” he said. “Lilly was a very good friend of mine, she came to Albany with me two days ago. She died in the fire.” Just talking about Lilly brought tears to my eyes. I hadn’t had time to grieve over losing her, none of us had. Robert knew, but neither Gavin nor Lois did. I hadn’t even found out if she was having a funeral. “What was she doing in Boston?” Rider asked. “Investigating the death of her family,” I looked at Rider, but refused to look at Rivers - who didn’t have a great approach in interviewing victims. “We know this Pierce,” Rivers said rudely. “No need to get testy on me,” I looked at him. “I didn’t know that you knew this,” I couldn’t smile, cry or get angry. My body had become so worn out over the last few hours; I needed rest. “Sorry about my partner,” Rider smiled. “He can be a little edgy sometimes,” she smiled again. “Now, about Lilly and Boston. We knew she went their, we had a long discussion with a man named Tony Rush. You know him?” “No. But, Lilly called him the day of the fire though. We knew we were in danger,” I coughed lightly. My mouth still tasted of ash and smoke. “Pierce, we need to know about the man behind the murders of Lilly Connelly, Jack Dawson and Jeffrey Brunt,” Rivers stood up. “Jack Dawson and Jeffery Brunt? Were they the two firemen that died?” I remembered the floor collapsing and two firemen falling to their deaths. I was left helpless, unable to save them, only I and Lois were left in the building after that, everyone else that remained, were dead. “Yes, both were good friends of mine,” Rivers turned away and put back on his jacket. “I don't think I can help you, Detectives,” I was lying. “Pierce, don’t push me,” Rivers protested against my secret. “Settle down, Tim,” Casey Rider said. “Pierce, who is behind all the gang-land killings?” “I don‘t know, why would I know?” I looked away. I feared for my safety and also the ones around me that I loved dearly. Detective Tim Rivers’s mobile began to chirp amongst Rider’s interview. He turned swiftly and answered. “Yes…okay…thanks…I’ll get a team together..... we’ll meet you in Boston…,” Rivers closed his mobile and turned to Casey Rider. “We don’t need to continue this interview,” Tim said. “We are joining the FBI in a swat engagement. It seems somebody told them everything, we are shipping out, now.” Rider and Rivers stood up and opened the door. Before leaving, Rider turned to face me. “Thanks for your help. By the way, the New York Post is in the waiting room. They want to interview you for their newspaper. You are a city hero,” she smiled and left. Everything today had come as a sudden shock. Why and who told them everything about Lilly Connelly and the Harrington ordeal? I couldn’t answer that myself. I had seen Tony Rush that night arrive in an FBI helicopter but leave very quickly. He hadn’t stayed long and it made me that little bit suspicious - I kept thinking maybe he liked Lilly. She mentioned him a lot and from my quick glance he was quite a young man. Perhaps he left to grieve just like me. Nightmares! I was living in one now. When you lose somebody close everybody deals with it differently. Me, I haven’t even started to cry, the shock hasn’t even set in. The realisation that I wouldn’t ever see her again was creeping up slowly. I sat up and pulled on a pair of tracksuit bottoms that the nurse had gotten for me from the supply room. The consequences of the fire would take their toll, but for now, I needed to see Lois. Part 2 : Two wrongs don’t make a right! Harrington paced around his large mansion, drinking an Irish whiskey and smoking a Cuban cigar. He took two deep drags and blew the smoke into the whiskey glass. It swirled around the large oval shaped glass and soon evaporated into nothing. Marvin took a deep slug from the whiskey and then turned to face Reno, Romeo and Antonio. All three men sat proudly on the long stretched sofa. “I'm very proud of the three of you,” he said. Marvin began pacing the room once more and swirling his whiskey. “We have dealt with that little mistake, finally.” “Yes boss,” Reno said. “She’s dead. We made sure of it,” Antonio said. “I know idiots. I saw the news,” Marvin chuckled. “You also killed two firemen. Nice touch, boys,” Marvin pulled out his stopwatch and checked the time. It was already 8 pm and his meeting with the consulate would be beginning soon. “Boss, where's Isabel?” Antonio asked. “Upstairs. She's crying over Pete,” Marvin placed his glass onto the smooth glass table. Marvin neither cared nor wanted to hear anything that his wife had to say; in regards to their son’s brutal murder. Marvin strode proudly around the house all day as though he didn't care that he had murdered his own flesh and blood. Romeo, Reno and Antonio couldn't understand why their boss would kill flesh and blood like that. But, they knew better than to question his motives. “If you’ll excuse me, I have some very important business to attend to.” Marvin began to open the door when a loud burst of machine gun fire is heard breaking throughout the house. Four men come hurdling down the hallway with semi-automatics in hand, saying something, a word of warning before all four men are shot down in cold blood. Marvin’s eyes widen at the realisation of the amount of FBI and NYPD swat teams that have entered his house. “We must run,” Marvin yells. “It’s too late,” Reno yells back over the gunfire. Marvin pulls Reno in front of him as a load of bullets enter the room. Killing Antonio, Romeo and Reno - who shielded Marvin’s body. Marvin reaches for his shotgun, still holding a dead Reno in front of him and begins firing shotgun blasts everywhere, randomly. “On the ground,” two swat men with bullet proof vest dive into the room, firing their semi-automatics. “What do you want with me?” Marvin yelled. Now cowering behind his large desk; shivering like a scared kitten. “Marvin Harrington, you’re under arrest for the murders of Jonathan, Ben, Jane and Lilly Connelly. You are also under arrest for the murder of your son Pete Harrington and the two firemen in New York.” Marvin laughed, hearing the names of all his victims. “You forgot four more men,” he laughed. “Down on the floor, you sick bastard.” Five men entered with Isabel Harrington and the Godfather, who was in handcuffs. The two men turned to approach Marvin but stopped when they realised two more dead men behind the large sofa. Antonio and Romeo lay out flat on their backs; their arms spread wide and their pistols still in their belts. “Give it up, Marvin,” Isabel said while being handcuffed. “We have him.” Marvin was pulled up by two very large swat guards who now had the murderous mafia leader in handcuffs. Marvin spits at his wife on the way out of the room and continues laughing at some of the dead cops and FBI agents. “You should have known better, pigs.” Marvin grunts to himself as he’s hoisted into a police truck to be taken to a federal judge for trial. Part 3 : True Love Speaks. Lois sat still in her bed smiling continuously at me as I sat at the end of the bed. “How did it go with the New York Post?” she asked. I knew she really did want to know, she always cared about me and what was going on in my life. “It went well; they’re going to print the story tonight. Apparently they have a lead in Boston that they’ll also be printing. I’m just glad it’s over.” Should I tell Lois about Lilly dying? It was a question I had struggled with since I began my long walk from the burn-ward to intensive care. I couldn’t imagine how I would say it either; I now knew how the doctors felt when they had to approach a family to inform them that their loved one had died in surgery. “I wish I could work for the New York Post, one day.” “I think you’ll make a brilliant reporter, you have a knack for it.” “You think so?” she smiled again. “I know so,” I leaned forward a little more and put my hand upon hers, tracing the line of the drip around her hand. “How are you feeling?” “Now that you’re here, much better.” Lois clasped her hand in mine and looked at me with a caring smile that I hadn’t seen in a long time. “Thanks for saving my life, Pierce,” Lois sat up just a little more and adjusted the pillow. “Thanks for wrapping your shirt around my cut stomach; they said if it wasn’t for you I could’ve needed a blood transfusion.” “I’m just glad you’re okay.” Lois hadn’t mentioned Robert or the issue the other morning. She just stared at my bandage around my waist and the small amount of stitches along my back. “What about you?” she asked. “What do you mean?” “The cuts and the bruises and also your skin-graft. I heard the nurses talking,” she had a tear in her eye as she stared out the window. “I’m okay; it doesn’t hurt, if that’s what you mean.” She was crying now. I knew she had been crushed by the events of today. Waking up in a hospital without remembering half of what had happened to you. Not knowing whether the person you care so deeply about is dead or alive. I moved in closer and embraced her in a hug, I felt a slight tingling sensation along my arms and neck, I knew it wasn’t the medicine working; it was the touch of Lois against my skin once more. “I thought you died, nobody would tell me anything. The nurse didn’t even know who you were. They said I had come in alone.” “You didn’t come in alone. I was with you the whole time. I never let go of your hand. I promised you when we were in the crumbling bedroom that I would keep you safe. That’s what I did.” She removed herself from the hug and leaned back. “Pierce, me and Rob broke up a week ago. That’s why he was distant in the morning. He thinks I’m in love with you.” “Are you?” I was hoping for an answer, instead I just got silence. She looked out the window and back at me again. “Yes,” she stared me in the eyes now. Her blue eyes glistened. The bruising under her collarbone had gone down and she could smile happily again. “I know it’s not the right time as you like Lilly a lot, but I needed to tell you how I feel.” That was it. I couldn’t lie to her anymore. I wanted to tell her everything and anything there was to tell. How everyday I thought of our first kiss shared in the tree house in Rob’s back garden. How when I stared into her eyes the day she arrived in Albany I felt like holding her hand and kissing her, but I had to tell her that Lilly died in the fire. “Lilly died, Lois. When the fire first broke out, she was with me, but then the firemen burst into the room grabbing Rob and Lilly. So I left them and came for you. When I came back out with you, they were all gone. But, then the fifth floor collapsed and the two men that died were the two men that had Lilly. I don’t think she made it, Lois.” Lois had a sudden shocked expression on her face. She had expressed an emotion to me that she would never express; Lois had opened up and instead of me replying with my true feelings. I did the opposite, I told her about Lilly, which did spoil the mood. “I didn’t know; I feel stupid now.” Rob entered the room with two bags of shopping. One from “GAP” and the other from “Tommy Hilfiger”. He smiled at me and Lois and sat at the small guest chair in the corner. It ruined my plans for me to confess my feelings. “Hey guys, hope I’m not interrupting.” Rob smiled and looked at mine and Lois’s hands clasped together. “I’m terribly sorry that Lilly died, Pierce. I knew she meant a lot to you,” Lois looked at Rob again and then looked away. “We were just friends. To be honest with you, I think she liked somebody else.” “I got you guys some clothing. Just some tracksuit bottoms and jumpers. I have to head back to Harrisburg. My parents are coming back from Florida and I need to let them know that I am alright. Are you two going to be alright?” Rob looked at me. “I’ll be fine, Rob. Besides I can’t leave this hospital for two more days yet.” “Neither can I.” Lois replied and looked once more at Robert, who refused to make eye contact with her. “Well with that settled, I’ll head over to Thompson College as well. Lois, I’ll get your transfer papers so that you can go to Harbrook, if you want?” Rob now made eye contact with her. Both Lois and Rob agreed that no matter what, they would still be friends. Even if Robert still liked Lois a lot he knew where she stood and who she cared about. “Thanks, Rob,” Lois smiled. “Would you let my parents know that I’m okay, as well?” “No problem.” I stood up and made my way over to the bags. “Which ones mine?” I smiled at Rob. “I don’t think a woman’s bra is really my taste.” “Do you two mind not going through my underwear?” Lois was laughing. “Sure,” I laughed. “Careful Pierce, that’s as close as you’ll ever get to Lois’s underwear,” Rob took them from me and shoved them back into the “GAP” bag and handed me the “Tommy Hilfiger” bag. I placed it on the bed and hugged Rob. “Thanks man, you have been a great help to me,” I stood up and realised that Lois was laughing hysterically. “Nice underwear, Pierce.” Lois was holding a pair of black boxers with a yellow duck on the middle part. “Thanks, they’re my favourite pair,” I laughed as well. “Well I’ve gotta go, the train will be leaving soon. I’ll ring you guys as soon as I get to Harrisburg.” “Take care,” Lois said watching Robert leave the room. “Can I have them back now, before the whole nursing staff sees my underwear?” Lois continued laughing and put the underwear into the bag again. “So what now?” she looked at me. I knew she wanted an answered to her confessed feelings and I was going to give her one. Lois only rarely spoke about her emotions; she hadn't let many get close to her. Growing up with four brothers, removed some of her feministic qualities. She was a lot like a tom-boy, but now she is different. The Lois before me opened herself up and told me her true feelings; I needed to do the same... “I love you too, Lois.” Part 4 : Just Desserts Tony Rush paced up and down the steel cell. Marvin stood across from him, dressed in an orange jumpsuit with his prison number on the back. Marvin laughed menacingly as Tony stopped and stared the murderer in the face. “Tell me-” Tony Rush yelled. “Tell you what?” Marvin smiled. “You need to be more specific, Detective.” “What was it that Jonathan Connelly had?” Tony Rush looked at Marvin now. Marvin had shut up. Tony smiled and continued with his interview on the “Connelly’s”. Marvin of course would not mention anything until his lawyer was in the room, but he liked to watch the Detectives squirm. “Before you even ask. This is a federal judge, you’re not entitled to a lawyer. Your acts were focused on by the FBI and secret service. No public lawyer can defend you. Your case is as good as done,” a young woman Detective from behind Tony Rush mentioned. Marvin sat down on his small concrete bed. His cell had nothing more than a bed hoisted against the wall with two chains, a small toilet and a sink. Nothing else remained in the room. He had no windows or daylight. Some would become paranoid and claustrophobic in these situations, but Marvin accepted his fate with pride. “You wish to know why Jonathan Connelly was so important?” Marvin looked at Tony. “Yes,” was all Tony could reply with. “Three years ago, my father discovered a small chemical facility on the outskirts of Massachusetts. Anyway, this Jonathan Connelly was a photographer, a landscape photographer and by accident he happened to take a view photos of the dodgy dealings that were going on.” “Why did you have him killed then? Why was he so important to you?” “Patience, Detective,” he smiled. “You see, the owners of this plant were our sworn enemies, the Corallines. In Mr. Connelly’s photo I saw one of my own men discussing something with a Coralline and exchanging notes. I needed to find Jonathan and question him on what else he knew. Unfortunately, the wrong message was sent and he was killed. That was an accident. There you have it, Detective. His death and the whole families death was an accident, something needed to be corrected after that mess-up. So we lied to have the rest of the family killed. Couldn’t have the Harrington family looking bad, could we?” Marvin smiled, but Tony Rush didn’t see the humour. “Well, Marvin. You will be facing a life sentence without parole. However, your father won’t be so lucky. I am afraid he’ll be facing the death penalty for the murders he committed in Texas and Oklahoma.” Tony Rush asked for the cell door to be opened. He began a slow and steady walk outside. The door closed behind him. Locking Marvin in the small cell, like a caged animal. “We have him, Tony,” the woman behind him said. “That we do. It’s the judges turn now. Everything is out of our hands, finally.” Both Tony Rush and the woman left the penitentiary and headed out toward their black jeep.
