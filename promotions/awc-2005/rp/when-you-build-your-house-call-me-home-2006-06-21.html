When you build your house, call me home...

21 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6.21.06 Dear God!!! What is this? Aimz just leveled Captain Suleimon with that chair after convincing damn near everybody that she had her sights set on Darcy Crisis! Crisis smiles yet again, and drops to apply an STF to the down-and-out Captain Suleimon. With no choice, referee Richie Travis drops to ask Suleimon the next question in their Trivial Punishment contest. Richie Travis: Captain Suleimon… name the largest of the original Thirteen Colonies that ratified the U.S. Constitution. Suleimon gives no answer. Richie Travis: Captain… you have five seconds to give an answer. Richie Travis then begins his five count. At the last possible moment, Suleimon musters a response. Captain Suleimon: I… ungh… Suleimon’s eyes roll back towards the ceiling as he loses consciousness. Travis announces the count of five, and signals for the bell to be rung. The timekeeper obliges, and this one is over! James Brunt: The winner… DARCY CRISIS!! As “Open Your Eyes” hits, Darcy and Aimz celebrate with a victorious hug in the center of the ring. The fans aren’t quite sure what to make of the situation after witnessing the detestable Aimz assist their beloved Darcinator with a dubious victory over the equally detestable Captain Suleimon. Aimz and Darcy, however, look absolutely thrilled with the outcome as they back up the ramp, with Aimz holding Darcy’s arm triumphantly high… *** The night of March 24, 2006 must surely be remembered by AWC devotees for a great number of things…the only question is what ranks as most important. Some might remember the night best for what you have just seen…the besting of Captain Suleimon by Darcy Crisis in perhaps the most unique of wrestling affairs ever seen – the Trivial Punishment match. Others likely remember this night as the fateful night that Adam Dick, through nefarious means, upended Jack Murphy and claimed the TransAtlantic Championship for himself, officially crowning the “Royal Court” that has since asserted itself as the top dogs of AWC in recent weeks. Still others might remember the night for Jeremy J’s heroic trip to the catering table, where he dared to devour not one, not two…but a previously thought impossible FOUR giant krullers in a bold attempt to wreak havoc on his metabolism. But Darcy Markson remembered the night of the past March 24 very well, and for an entirely different reason altogether – for it was the last time his arm was raised in victory in an AWC-sanctioned contest. Life could be described as no less than tumultuous since Darcy’s first pay-per-view contest in nearly five years. Due to inner squabbling and a long-harbored grudge held against him by the Tomorrow Institute’s new CEO, Darcy lost his spokesman’s deal as well as any future role Darcy had had in mind for the company he so desperately wanted to help succeed. The Institute played a major role in “rescuing” Darcy from the Brazilian rainforest and helping him return to his home in California, and furthermore, recent years had seen Darcy’s passion grow from merely cracking skulls in the squared circle to a newfound love for the wildlife that surrounded him everywhere, and a responsibility to protect it from human beings who were clearly growing too big for their own environment. He had hoped to combine these two loves of his life until he was finally ready to retire, where he would be able to segue into working for the Institute and their cause full-time. However, the ego of David Jameson forced Darcy to make a choice between the two…a choice that at first had seemed quite difficult, but would turn out to be reasonably effortless – he chose destiny. Darcy also dodged a major bullet in somehow getting Amy to rethink her proposal of marriage to him. That bombshell she dropped on him a few weeks ago in Rio threatened to send his blood pressure into uncharted territories, but somehow Darcy found himself able to defuse the situation and walk away no worse for wear, other than a girlfriend with slightly hurt feelings. However, with all this and more on his plate, a toll had certainly been taken on Darcy’s concentration, which more than likely contributed to his recent losing streak. Fortunately, Darcy’s veteran craftiness and raw intensity kept every one of those matches close – he’d gone wire to wire with some of AWC’s best in his latest losing affairs. But while intensity and craftiness might be enough for the likes of Mike Carman and Jason Warr, it would take substantially more focus to take down the likes of Chainz and Ellis Nash – and he’d had a recent string of consecutive bells ringing in favor of his opponents to help remind him. But it seemed as if Darcy was finally about to get out of the woods after a seemingly endless odyssey through uncharted wilderness. His challenge laid down for Garbage Bag Johnny, AWC’s resident vagrant and most importantly, a thorn in Darcy’s side the size of a Louisville Slugger, had been accepted. Moreover, it was to be a ONE-on-ONE event, unlike the recent clusterfucks Darcy kept finding himself a part of, matches that Darcy found himself directly affected by men with generic redneck names like “Kip” and “Duke” – names that didn’t quite ring a bell for Darcy prior to these affairs and would likely disappear just as quickly. And most importantly of all, a victory for Darcy would earn him another shot at the TransAtlantic Championship – something he’d been aching for ever since Adam Dick deftly maneuvered him out of the title picture with an impromptu matchup that saw an unprepared and outmatched Darcy Crisis come out on the losing end. Indeed, this was the sort of opportunity of which Darcy had made a career of thriving upon; high stakes, a marquee matchup, and no one else with a stake in the decision. It would simply be a contest of Darcy’s talent against the Garbage Man’s. And with the previous distractions that had hinged on Darcy’s recent performance now squared away, and no new ones making themselves known, it appeared that the S.S. Crisis was finally due for some smooth sailing. So naturally we can assume that the waters that lied between now and Divide and Conquer would be anything but smooth. *** Finally, an afternoon at home. In California. To himself. For once. Ever since Darcy signed with AWC back in December, the job combined with his work for the Tomorrow Institute to make his life increasingly more demanding…and further away from home than he particularly cared for. The some-200 days on the road that came with the territory of any wrestling promotion offering a decent income was grueling enough on its own; however, Darcy carried the added burden of what amounted to a full-time job of its own with the Institute. In addition to serving as a celebrity spokesman of sorts, Darcy also fulfilled other duties for the firm, most notably his role as Media Relations Coordinator. This entailed not only serving as the Institute’s prominent on-camera face, but also directing much of the off-camera work as well: monitoring press cycles, scheduling interviews, maintaining partnerships with influential contacts and formulating strategies to round up public support through the media. The travel was considerably less, but it meant that free time was often hard to come by. But Darcy had now been relieved of said responsibilities. Without a doubt, it came as a blow to lose his job with the Institute, as their ceaseless quest to, as they put it…preserve life had certainly become a passion of his own. But now that he felt as if he finally had the time to breathe for the first time in months, there was no more continuing to lie to himself that he didn’t need the break. He couldn’t remember the last time he’d had a decent night’s sleep. He hadn’t returned a single phone call from anyone within fifty miles of East Bay in God only knows how long. Perhaps the most telling clue of all was the odor of Darcy’s living accommodations. Even with the ocean breeze consistently bombarding his loft that overlooked the bay area, Darcy found himself unable to rid the loft of that “new apartment smell” – and he was fast approaching the one year anniversary since he’d signed the lease on the place. Not only did Darcy find free time hard to come by; he’d been having a similar problem with time to himself. However, this predicament had been rectified as well. Gone were the relentless hounding of reporters and Institute higher-ups. The constant barrage of requests for advice and confirmation were vanishing like flies in winter. The call log on his cell phone was an excellent reminder for him. He’d been off the payroll for nearly three weeks, and the number of calls he received so far over the weekend dropped from a staggering ten to fifteen, which he’d come to expect as of late, to a svelte two: one from his mother, asking if he’d be around for Father’s Day, and the other an uneventful wrong number. And with the combination of Darcy having visited his parents for Father’s Day the day before, Amy out of the house all afternoon and no immediate plans to see anyone else, Darcy found himself alone, in his home, for the first time in a long time. So how to occupy himself? The possibilities appeared endless. His guitar was overdue to be recovered from abandonment; Darcy couldn’t remember so much as touching it since he serenaded Amy on Valentine’s Day. The entire DVD set of the subversively hilarious sock puppets Sifl & Olly had recently arrived in the mail, still remaining in the package for Darcy’s perusal. The World Cup awaited Darcy on the television if he so chose…granted, it would certainly turn out to be the invariable thumping of Saudi Arabia by Ukraine, but soccer was rapidly earning a more respectable role in Darcy’s love of athletic competition. But these were all merely idle pursuits. In the back of his mind, Darcy knew along that there was something he’d put off doing for far too long. And with no certainty as to the next time he’d have such a large block of time to himself again, he knew it would be foolhardy not to capitalize on the current opportunity. It was time for Darcy to receive some much needed guidance. Darcy had learned how to accept guidance from what local ayahuasqueros in Brazil called “the great spirits” in their ceremonies that celebrated the union between the two realms of existence, Life and Death. These ceremonies centered on the consumption of a bitter, hallucinogenic drink known as the “vine of souls.” Fortunately, Darcy kept a jar of the drink in an otherwise seldom used mini-fridge that he kept tucked in his laundry closet. There, the tannins within the liquid that had the same chemical makeup as bathroom soap could settle out from the rest of the liquid, preventing what could potentially surface in terrible nausea and vomiting. The Brazilian yage warriors did not bother with the separation of the soap from the tea, believing it to be in fact beneficial to wash out their insides with soap and sacred tea. Darcy, however, who had grown up in a society that seemed to frown upon purposely throwing up, understood that he would have to make changes to the formula. After all, he’d get nothing out of the experience if he couldn’t hold the tea long enough in his body to take advantage of its extraordinary contents. To the outsider, it would appear that the ceremony Darcy was about to prepare for was simply the abuse of a rare, but ultimately harmful hallucinogenic drug. Darcy himself was a bit skeptical when first exposed to the tea by the natives with whom he resided in the rainforest, but he came to see the drink as less a drug than it was a tool with almost religious implications. The yage warriors that consumed this drink they called yage indeed had formed an entire religion upon this very ceremony, and in participating in the ceremony Darcy saw for himself things that could not be explained by mere science, and events such as people being healed, souls being cleansed and faith in the divine restored tenfold over what it had been before. Darcy himself now regarded yage as sacred, but was still wary of making his use of it known to the public. He frowned on those who flaunted their drug use…especially the Royal Court. Particularly Adam Dick and Ellis Nash, who were not above stoning their IQs into oblivion on camera for all - including young, impressionable children – to see. Even though he saw nothing wrong with what he did, Darcy kept his use of the tea hidden from everyone. Even Amy was not aware that he still partook in the tea, with Darcy having only hinted that he’d experimented with the drink on a few occasions while living with the Brazilian rainforest natives. If nothing else, the last thing Darcy wanted was for his secret to leak out to the wrong people, and the press having a field day with headlines like “Darcy the Drug Addict!” There was also the unwanted matter of police possibly electing to get involved. Yage was considered a Schedule I hallucinogen, illegal to possess under U.S. law. While it had recently been ruled legal for use by religious congregations in nearby New Mexico, and Darcy was fully prepared for a legal battle should the law attempt to take his yage away, he still preferred to avoid any sort of battle over the matter at all, if possible. He didn’t enjoy keeping something that had given him so much a secret from his family and friends, but the old adage proved true yet again: Better safe than sorry. Or severely irritated. With the jar now in hand, Darcy returned to the front door of his apartment, and locked it to ease any paranoia of someone dropping in unannounced. This was not Darcy’s first attempt in preparing his special recipe for the tea, and by now he’d had it down to a science. Within minutes, the brew was prepared. First, Darcy strained out the undesirable tannins with an old “AYATOLLA ASSAHOLA” T-shirt barring the visage of Ayatollah Khomeni that Darcy’s dad gave him a few years ago. While it could be now consumed in this state, Darcy elected to place the remaining liquid into a porcelain pot on the stove to boil as much of it away as possible. As he waited for the tea to boil Darcy put some Syrian Rue into some empty gel caps. The powdery seeds that hailed from Syria functioned as monoamine oxidase inhibitors, or MAOIs. These inhibitors were charged with the task of preventing the MAOs located within the body to “steal” the hallucinogenic properties of the tea for their own purposes before they could reach the brain. The gel caps were to prevent Darcy from tasting the rue, which in his mind tasted even more bitter than the astringent tea. He consumed about three grams of the rue, which would require about thirty minutes to take effect. Darcy used this time to prepare everything exactly to his preferred specifications. He headed for his bedroom, the room in his loft that allowed in the least amount of light from the East Bay sun. Darcy turned down the blinds to get the room as dark as he possibly could. The only light Darcy would need for this endeavor would come by candlelight, which he achieved by lighting a few candles that also happened to give off the aroma of lilacs. Both Amy and Johnny, the two that frequented his East Bay abode the most, gave Darcy flack for his strange penchant for scented candles. Darcy sheepishly shrugged to their queries about the candles, allowing them to believe that it was simply a strange taste of his…in truth, Darcy didn’t care much for candles, and used them pretty much only in his yage ceremonies. The lilac scented variety was just to throw them off his trail. With the tea now boiled down and cooled, Darcy carefully poured out the precise amount with the aid of a turkey baster. Even with the amount of experience he’d had with yage that only a handful of white men could match, he was still quite frightened by the prospect of taking too much of the stuff. He settled on an amount containing around ten milligrams of the tea’s special chemical properties. Once he’d seen that enough time had elapsed for the MAOI to have taken effect, Darcy drank his carefully prepared tea. It tasted more foul than anything one could possibly imagine, and he had to fight his gag reflex more than once before finally getting the tea to travel down his throat. If Darcy had any reservations about the journey he was about to undertake, there was no going back now. Darcy idled over to his bedroom, where he planned on remaining firmly for the next several hours. Using one of the nearby candles, Darcy lit a cigar and puffed the smoke about the room’s confines. Darcy was vehemently against smoking, but cigars served a special purpose in yage ceremonies. Apparently tobacco was also regarded as sacred by the yage warriors, who smoked it during the tea-drinking ritual to ward off evil spirits who might attempt to take control of their souls while they were under the tea’s spell. This came as a surprise to Darcy, as it must to anyone else who trusts the validity of science and their finding that tobacco does more harm to the body than good. But Darcy was far more wary of being possessed by an evil spirit than he was of tobacco’s carcinogenic properties, and decided that partaking in a cigar solely during this ritual was not only acceptable, but probably the safer route as well. After a few minutes the yage began to take effect on Darcy. When he closed his eyes his “vision” was overtaken by brightly colored patterns of red, green and blue, patterns that he could not describe with the least bit of justice no matter how many times he’d seen them. They appeared to resemble etchings one might find on the inside of an ancient Aztec temple, in a language lost to all but those who once possessed the native tongue. These patterns were truly a spectacle to be seen, and they began to shine brighter and wave about in front Darcy, even pervading his vision when he opened his eyes. One might very well be prone to get lost in these early visions, but Darcy had had enough experience with the sacred tea to know that these visions were merely the tip of the iceberg. Rather than attempting to focus on any one of the flowing patterns or discern their meaning, Darcy allowed his thoughts to flow freely while waiting for the next stage of the ritual. As his thoughts often did when allowed to flow freely, they turned to Amy…specifically, what had transpired in Rio several weeks ago. He was unable to shake the mental picture of just how hurt she looked when he begged her to retract her asking of him to marry her. It wasn’t that Darcy had a problem with marriage…despite the abysmal fifty percent of marriages that ended in divorce in his native United States, Darcy had seen marriage work in countless places. His parents had been married for over thirty years, and both sets of his grandparents were pushing fifty years themselves. Nor did Darcy doubt his love for Amy, nor her of his. The two had first met when she was a lost, impassioned 18 year old, and he a hotheaded, narrow-minded man just barely old enough to legally purchase alcohol. He knew all along that their first attempt at a relationship could only end in disaster, and his walking out on her - not to mention the rest of the civilized world, as he was in desperate need of soul searching after his traumatic back injury pulled him from the wrestling ranks – was in fact the best scenario he could have hoped for, despite the screaming, sobbing and abundance of broken beer bottles that accompanied his exit. But things were different now. For one, they were both older, and had gained much wisdom in the years they’d spent without each other. They were both very different people – Darcy saw that there was more to life than simply occupying the spotlight of a champion. For her part, Amy had toned down her fondness of public indecency and lewd conduct, which added up to far less time in the county lockup. They were both calmer, more collected in their thoughts and above all else, able to understand how the other worked, and how to best serve each other’s interests without outright bending their wills. To put it simply; where before it had been an impossibility, Darcy Markson and Amy Campbell were actually able to be with each other. They didn’t always get along, and spent a lot more time at odds than Darcy particularly cared for. But it was love. And it worked. For now. This was the key to Darcy’s tremendous reservations about marrying Amy. He had no problem with marriage, nor did he doubt his love for Amy. Rather, it was his qualms about fate…in particular, how fate had a way of cruelly shaping Darcy’s life. Like any American boy, Darcy had been brought up to believe that anything was possible, that he could do anything he wanted so long as he had the courage to follow his heart. For many years he’d taken that belief to heart, always daring to dream of the possibilities that awaited him. But a series of consecutive, monumental failures led Darcy to believe that fate worked just a little bit differently for him… The disastrous breakup with Jane. Dropping out of college. A career in professional wrestling that brought him to highs he never believed he could reach before the rug was cruelly pulled out from under him, permanently injuring his back and forcing him into early retirement. And most recently, his firing from the Tomorrow Institute by David Jameson. All this added up to a new belief that governed Darcy’s life in recent years, a belief that hinged on two very simple, yet unyielding rules: 1) Nothing lasts forever, no matter how good it might seem. 2) Any time Darcy doubted the validity of Rule #1, a cruel twist of fate would unfold to keep him well reminded. And this was the problem. Darcy had always believed that marriage was the ultimate symbol, the ultimate statement that the love between two people would last forever. By agreeing to marry Amy, which he could confess to considering on more than one occasion, it would be as if he was directly tempting fate to intervene and take away one more thing he held near and dear to his very being. Darcy had let Amy get away once, and was determined not to make the same mistake again…even if his reasoning seemed a bit ironic in refusing to be at her side until death forced them apart. Any further thoughts he had on the matter were quickly forced away when Darcy felt himself gag. It was time for what the Brazilian natives liked to call la purga…literally, “the purge.” He’d done the best he could to remove the properties of the tea that caused the terrible vomiting, but there was no avoiding it altogether. In fact, as the natives suggested in their willingness to have their insides cleansed by the soap-like tannins, la purga was considered to be a necessary part of the ceremony. With no time to spare, Darcy made it to his bathroom just in time to violently expel the contents of his stomach into the (fortunately) already open toilet. While he had avoided the taste of the Syrian Rue on the way down, there was no avoiding the taste of it now that it had come back up and into his mouth. Darcy silently cursed to himself as he attempted to expel every bit of the rue from his pallet along with the equally foul-tasting brew. It took several minutes for the task to be complete, by which time Darcy was now a heaving mess crumpled on the bathroom tile. But he could feel the Vine of the Soul splitting his soul from his psyche, removing it from the physical confines of the body lying immobile near the toilet and taking it to a new place altogether. Just as the yage warriors had always claimed, it was only after la purga that his journey could truly begin. *** It should be noted that the spirits one encounter on their yage trips determine much of what the journeyer will see. As such, it is by and large a rule that no two trips will be alike. Sometimes the spirits want you to be formless, eternal, and with all the knowledge of a newborn infant so that they might impart as much of their wisdom as possible. Other times they brought you as you, because their message applied to you and only you, yours to interpret and apply as you saw fit. And these were only two extreme ends of the spectrum – as there were a seemingly endless amount of realms that a yage warrior might be taken to on the sacred journey. Deep beneath an ocean, atop a majestic mountain, floating through the cosmos…none of these were beyond what this miraculous brew were capable of providing. For this reason, there was most certainly an equal amount of forms the warrior could take on to best adapt. At this instant, Darcy guessed that he had been brought in toward the latter end of the spectrum. He knew exactly who he was; how he’d arrived here; where he was to go; and what he was meant to do when he got there. My name is Darcy Markson. I’m from East Bay, California, on Planet Fucking Earth. I took ten milligrams of dimethyltryptamene. I’m here for guidance, and I imagine it’s around here somewhere… But first, he took in a moment to gather in the tapestry that lied before him. It was almost as if he was staring into a photo negative of an immense canyon that a tourist had neglected to develop. The sky above him glowed a crimson hue that seemed to stretch on from the canyon to the horizon peering out from the distance. A large setting sun, still glowing as it hung on for its last stages of lighting the scene burned a bright, blazing blue. Beneath Darcy’s feet lied the oddly turquoise colored earth, stretching out from under him into immense jagged towers that seemed to form a winding path not far from where he stood. He only wished he had the time to revel in the odd, yet strangely beautiful landscape of the realm to which he’d been taken, but there was no time to lose. Coiling the strength of his hind legs, Darcy took off into a full-speed dash down the path through the canyon. It was exhilarating to move so fast using only the power of his legs, but after a time it proved to be rather toiling. Darcy’s long tongue now escaped his jaw, joining forces with his lungs as he began panting. This was not only in response to the increased oxygen needed now that he was bolting through the canyon path, but it also helped cool the skin beneath his luscious fur coat. The canyon proved to be especially winding, including a few break-neck turns that were practically aching to see Darcy crash into them at full-speed. But even though this was the first time Darcy could remember turning into a four-legged creature, he handled each of the twists along the path without so much as breaking stride. The physics of this realm appeared to be altered by a slightly less powerful version of gravity, aiding him in his high-speed scurry by giving him the ability to leap off the walls of some of the bends in the path that angled a little sharper. It seemed as if it would take forever, but Darcy finally arrived at the end of the path, pouring out from the canyon into a strikingly radiant meadow, draped by pink-colored blades of grass. Extending from this blanket of grass that strongly resembled the color of Molly Ringwald’s dress in Pretty in Pink came flowers that bore all the colors of the rainbow…presumably both Planet Earth’s and the realm Darcy currently occupied, which elected to utilize this negatively-charged color scheme. Slowing his speed to a more appropriate trot, which somehow seemed to suit the majesty of this pasture he had stumbled upon, Darcy crept up the slowly loping meadow toward his destination. On reaching the top of the small hill he could now see it in full view. A breathtakingly gigantic rock structure towered in the distance. The structure took on the shape of a domed European soccer stadium…that is, if the stadium were designed to seat what looked like would be a million spectators, intending to watch a sport that took up ten times the space of a football pitch. The other key difference was a large buttress that extended out from the dome, raising up into the now darkened sky. The color scheme around here might be a little different, Darcy thought to himself as he stared up into the vastness above him, but it looks like the absence of color is black no matter where you go... This accompanying rock structure stretched toward the heavens as if to beckon the twin cerulean moons now adorning the sky with an outstretched arm toward the dome in the evening twilight. Darcy obviously had never seen such a sight in his life, but he instantly knew exactly where he had arrived. The Dome of the Ancients. He didn’t really understand why, but he also knew there was no way he could help himself. Rearing back, Darcy let out a thunderous howl toward the twin moons that bore down their light all around him the color of beautiful sapphires. Pausing only to breathe, Darcy re-directed his howl toward the dome, again not knowing exactly why. Perhaps he was finally overcome by the splendor of the scenery he had experienced so far. Or perhaps it was because the majesty of the Dome of the Ancients demanded it of him, as a sort of reverence. Or perhaps still it was because Darcy could not in good taste abandon the opportunity to let out such a howl when it would likely never again be within his capacity to do so. The cause remained a mystery…but the action definitely served a purpose. “WHO DISTURBS MY SLUMBER?” beckoned an ear-splitting voice from the vicinity of the dome. Overcome by the sound, Darcy crouched to cover his ears with his front paws. Within moments a great wolf-like creature that seemed to teem with power appeared from behind the dome, surveying the scene around him just as Darcy had done only moments prior. The wolf looked around for awhile before spotting Darcy lying nearby, afraid to even look at him. “WAS IT YOU, BOY?” the wolf bellowed in his direction. Still afraid to look up, Darcy only nodded meekly in response. The wolf, who appeared to tower in stature compared to Darcy’s own canine form, gave him a prolonged, intimidating stare. Several long moments passed before the larger canine let loose a low laugh from his belly. “Relax, young one. It was merely a joke, uttered the wolf in a still-booming voice. Those who come here seeking wisdom from me, they’re always so tense. I was just trying to lighten things up. I didn’t mean to frighten you. “I…I h-hope the ‘dis-disturbing your slumber’ part was a joke, too. I can see it’s getting late,” Darcy stammered, finally mustering the courage to perk up and face the elder wolf. “You did not. In fact, I’ve been expecting you…” Darcy somehow already knew this, but was curious to know how the wolf knew he was coming. And what he was doing as a wolf in the first place. He meant to press on, but was distracted by a nagging itch on his hind leg. He stretched back to gnaw the irritation away. He got a bit too carried away, however, and was interrupted by the larger wolf clearing its throat. “Sorry,” Darcy sheepishly said, turning back to face him. “First day as a wolf.” “Quite alright. Believe it or not, as beautiful as this place might seem, it seems to have a knack for attracting unwanted pests. Anyway, to the point. You came here seeking guidance… “Yes,” Darcy responded. “You see, my girl-” “SILENCE! I DO NOT CARE ABOUT YOUR WOMAN PROBLEMS!” the wolf bellowed. His voice was so loud, Darcy could have sworn he heard a crack of thunder accompany the wolf’s thunderous command. Once again, Darcy found himself recoiled in fright. “I’ve had my eye on that… venomous companion of yours for some time now. And to be honest, your guess is probably as good as mine. No, my boy, your guidance comes in the form of a warning. “Warning? What warning?” Darcy inquired. He was certainly disappointed that he would gain no insight on his situation, but any warning from a mystical wolf-god from another dimension was a warning worth heeding. “You will soon be greeted by a visitor. One you hold very dear to your heart. “Who?” Darcy asked, just before his jaw dropped in both shock and glee. “Am I going to see Clipper again?” Clipper was the name of Darcy’s childhood dog, who unfortunately had to be put to sleep after his insatiable desire for food led him into the trash can where he met his bitter end in the form of chocolate, which must have been as tasty as it was lethal. He died when Darcy was a freshman in college, and if he would ever get the chance to see him again, it would most assuredly be thanks to the Vine of Souls. The wolf shook its head. “Not here. When you return to your home. Which, come to think of it, should be any minute now. I would get yourself cleaned up when you return, because your visitor shouldn’t be long. There were only so many guesses as to who it could be, but Darcy was still puzzled. Who could it possibly be that a yage spirit would find it important enough to warn him of their arrival? “Would you mind at least telling me who it is?” Darcy asked after several seconds. “This I cannot say, the wolf responded. As the words escaped his lips, Darcy could now begin to feel that he was not long for this realm. The sky appeared to bend in on itself directly above the dome’s buttress, and it looked as if the deep red clouds that adorned the sky were converging towards it. Time was running out, and he did not intend to waste it. “You mean you know when they’re going to show up, but you can’t tell me who it is?” “YOU’RE LUCKY TO KNOW ANYTHING AT ALL!” the wolf roared. He had to, because the wind had picked up to create an ear-splitting howl of its own. The clouds that were approaching the point above the dome had now been sucked in and vanished. Darcy didn’t know what the hell it was, but it was sure acting like a black hole. The world in which he and the spirit wolf occupied was caving in fast. “BUT KNOW THIS!” the wolf continued, attempting to shout over the piercing wind. “THE SITUATION IS CRUCIAL! HOW YOU HANDLE THE VISITOR’S ARRIVAL WILL GREATLY INFLUENCE THE REST OF YOUR DAYS! YOUR VERY FATE DEPENDS ON IT!” “THAT’S A LITTLE VAGUE!” Darcy shouted in kind, as the ground beneath the two began to buckle and crack. Darcy’s jaw gaped in astonishment as the entire dome was lifted from the ground and thrust into the gravitational pull that was mercilessly sucking everything Darcy could see into itself. Soon the entire ground on which he and the spirit wolf were standing too was thrust into the air as they spiraled toward certain doom. “I’VE TOLD YOU ALL I CAN! the wolf shouted to Darcy with his final words. “MAY THE SPIRIT OF GOOD FORTUNE BE WITH YOU!” With that, the two flew into the black hole. There was a blinding white light that engulfed his entire field of vision… …and Darcy’s eyes shot open, catching the small glimmer of light that peeked from underneath the doorway. He found himself once again lying on the decorative tile of his bathroom floor, in nearly pitch-black darkness. Slowly Darcy pushed himself to his knees, and simultaneously feeling all ten of his fingers make contact with the cold surface of the floor reminded Darcy that he had been returned to his proper species of homo sapiens sapiens.* Still able to taste bits of the bitter rue still in his mouth, Darcy’s first act in his triumphant return to humanity was to brush his teeth. He’d been at it for all of thirty seconds when he heard something banging against his front door… The visitor! Darcy thought. He spit out the toothpaste and rinsed his pallet under the flowing faucet as quickly as he could. With no way to know just how long the visitor had been there knocking, he swiftly clasped the doorknob and with a quick twist, thrust the door open, intending to meet his guest as quickly as possible. But just as he was about to exit he stopped himself, instead deciding that several seconds were in order to make sure he at least looked presentable. Darcy flipped on the light switch, and when his eyes adjusted to the invasion of bright light that had overtaken the room, Darcy was disappointed to see the damage done. While he at least looked relatively clean, it appeared that Darcy had vomited so violently that blood vessels in his face had burst, adding a glut of ugly red freckle-sized dots to his complexion. With luck, his visitor would buy the excuse that Darcy was recovering from a late-night bender, but a glimpse of the clock that reflected off of the closet door mirror reading “EE:S” led him to believe that his explanation might not go over as well as he was hoping for. Nevertheless, his several seconds were up, and Darcy pressed on to answer the door and greet the visitor. Somebody who knows where I live, Darcy thought to himself, considering that he hadn’t been living here all that long. That narrows down the list quite a bit. But when Darcy opened the door, the face he stared into immediately made him aware that this assessment was wrong. Dead wrong. Jane. Jane Goddamn Tits Ass Bullshit Motherfucking Connors. The spirit wolf’s prediction had been spot on. Darcy’s visitor had arrived shortly after he returned from his trip. And this visitor was someone Darcy held close to his heart. Once, that is. But the wolf had neglected to tell him that this “near and dear” visitor was also the same person who, many years ago, not only was the most important person in Darcy’s life and occupied the largest part of his heart, also cleaved said heart right in two, inflicting an emotional scar so crushing that he thought he would never recover. Darcy was left utterly defenseless as the painful memories came crashing back to him: His former roommate Sammy crumpled on the ground, with blood trickling out of his nose…Jane staring at him with tears ready to pour out of her beautiful, unforgivable eyes, making only the noises of choked up sobs…the song “Is this Love?” by Whitesnake softly playing in the background as Darcy rushed out of the apartment to which he would never return, a song that to this day Darcy still shudders to hear…and the most painful vision of all, that of the beautiful California February sunset that awaited him upon his exit from the apartment. Darcy could never remember seeing something that looked so gray… Jane Connors. CHRIST! Had Jane arrived at any other moment in time, the door would have been unceremoniously slammed in her face. There was no turning a blind eye to what she’d done to him, a deed so unforgivable that it altered the very course of his life like nothing ever had, or ever possibly could. As far as he was concerned, Jane Connors’ time in his life had come and gone, and it was all he could do to avoid slamming the door in her face anyway. But a tiny voice in the back of Darcy’s mind saved him from going through with it. The tiny voice was actually a much quieter version of the spirit wolf’s bellowing words: How you handle the visitor’s arrival will greatly influence the rest of your days… Normally, Darcy found it quite difficult to speak or think properly following a yage ceremony. But fortunately for Darcy, in this situation, there were only so many words necessary for a girl who by all rights deserved a door in the face. Due only to the wolf’s warning, Darcy elected to give her those words rather than the door. “What…in the BLUE FUCK…do YOU want?!” It should be mentioned that Jane’s gaze was directed toward the ground in front of Darcy’s feet, rather than into his eyes. Clearly Jane was just as troubled to be at Darcy’s doorway as Darcy was to receive her. In truth, there were an innumerable amount of words the two had for each other, as each knew deep down inside that things never should have turned out as fucked up as they did. And that they both deserved to part ways on much better terms. And that if their past selves from their teenage years were to see this meeting now, dictated on terms of bitterness and anger over love and kindness, they likely would have broken down in tears right there in front of them. But there was no going back on what happened, and this was no time to search for the words Jane knew she owed Darcy…half-knowing he wouldn’t give her the opportunity to speak them anyway.
