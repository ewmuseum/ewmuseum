Darcy gets a website!

12 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9.12.06 “I get my own website?” There was a slight pause after the words escaped Darcy Markson’s lips, before the voice on the other end of the phone gave their reply. “…Yessir, You sure do!” the male voice responded. His voice carried a charming snap that seemed to suggest that he possibly moonlighted as one of those used car salesman shouting about low, LOW, LOW! Prices on a ’92 Ford Festiva. “What’s the occasion?” Darcy of course already knew the answer to this question, and the shit-eating grin on his face reflected it as he held his hand over the receiver to hide his chortled laughter. The man took the bait. “Why, it’s in your contract, champ! Practically everybody’s contract, in fact. Once you win the Frontier Title, you get your very own website!” Yes! Darcy pumped his fist, thinking to himself that he would probably never grow tired of his new nickname. “Right, that’s right. Far out. Do you need me to do anything?” “Not at this very moment, champ,” the man responded, eliciting another grin from Darcy. “I’ll get back to you with the details and fine print, and all that fun stuff later. Someone will probably be in touch fairly soon to set up your bio. Mostly, I just wanted to give you a heads up…and to see if you wouldn’t mind writing a little something that we can throw on the main page?” His voice picked up at the end, suggesting that it was a request requiring Darcy’s approval…and a request that might prove difficult to grant. “Write something? I’m no writer,” Darcy answered, after a pause. The man laughed. “I’m not looking for a book here, chief. Just a few words straight out of your mouth should be enough to start pulling in those hits. Maybe you could write about what it felt like to win that Frontier Title of yours?” Darcy stroked his chin, pondering the request bestowed upon him. “I’ll see what I can come up with, brother. But no promises.” “Fair enough. And if you have any questions, you can reach me at this number…” The man dictated his special seven-digit code, prompting Darcy to scramble for a pen and nearby notepad. He wasn’t in time to catch it the first go-around, but luckily Darcy was able to take it down when the man, unprompted, elected to repeat himself. “Talk to you soon,” the faux-car salesman finished. But just before he could hang up, Darcy interjected with a query. “Wait! I have a question.” “What’s that?” “You said everybody who wins the Frontier Title gets their own website?” Darcy asked, scratching his head. “That’s right.” “Then…how come I never saw mikeyoreilly.com?” A brief moment passed before the two men shared a great laugh. “Take it easy, Darcy,” the man said, just before Darcy heard the click of his receiver. Damn. What the hell am I going to write about? Darcy plopped down on the flower-covered bedspread of his sister’s guest room. The upbeat site manager’s suggestion seemed good enough…but as he told the man before, Darcy wasn’t much of a writer. And he could scarcely find the words to describe what it felt like to capture his first title in half a decade. It was easy for him to get caught up in his job, and in the process forget to be a fan of the sport he had made his trade. Hearing the bell ring for his triumph, and finally cradling the ten-pound golden strap against his chest was the best reminder he could have imagined. It was the sort of moment he could cherish equally as a competitor, as well as a fan…the kind that a man could write a 7,000 word treatise, and still feel like he had failed miserably in being able to portray just what it felt like. Then again, Darcy did have quite a bit on his mind. And he’d been told that writing was somewhat therapeutic And, the website guy was counting on him. AND, other than ambling about town for another two or three hours before Marie returned from work to see him off, he had nothing better to do. Darcy silently cursed himself for not dragging along his laptop in his hasty retreat from California. He left the room in search of pen and paper, of which he was able to locate both on a nearby coffee table resting between Marie’s couch and television. Removing the top sheet covered with old Scrabble! scores, Darcy set to work. *** From the front page of www.darcycrisis.com… Sunday, September 3 2:33 PM I promised myself I wouldn’t do this. I promised myself that I wouldn’t turn into a big blubbery gush of emotion over this. But even now, even with only a few days to reflect on what has just happened to me, I can already tell that Friday, September 1, 2006 will be a night that I won’t ever be able to forget, no matter how hard I try. My job is different from any other job. I’ve worked in several different fields in my ever-increasing stay on this planet, so I can tell you this from personal experience. Most desk jobs don’t carry with them the risk of getting your brains scrambled via folding chair, or being jumped from behind by carnivorous zombies. Indeed, there’s nothing quite like my job…but it’s still a job. Just like my days with the Tomorrow Institute, there are days that I just don’t want to get out of bed. Days where I’m too sick, too hurt, too strung out, or whatever the hell else have you. But no matter what field you work in, a professional does his job even when he doesn’t feel like it. I’m a professional wrestler, and I have to earn my paycheck just like everybody else, and you can sure as hell bet my ass will get canned just as fast as a seventeen-year-old caught getting stoned in the McDonald’s break room if I don’t show up to work ready to perform to the best of my abilities. But in all my efforts to keep perspective, I sometimes forget just how great this job is. I get the chance to do something most people can only dream about. I’ve always endeavored to remember that while my job is important to me, and is certainly a big part of who I am…it doesn’t define me. I love this business, but I don’t eat, sleep and drink it. Not anymore, anyway. It can be pure hell, working in the entertainment business. Even more so when you’re an athlete, where you know practically from the very beginning that your days are numbered. Every one of us knows deep down inside that there are only so many days that we can do this before our bodies just can’t keep up, and every day that passes means we’re one day closer to the end. I didn’t always know this. I used to be 23, I used to think I was invincible, and I thought the dream would never end. A lot of people, myself included, thought the end came for me when I broke my back. It was heartbreaking to go out like that. I knew in the back of my mind that I’d have to go out eventually…but not like this. Not when I was still, in most respects, just a kid. Not when a fledgling California wrestling company was about to pin its hopes on its new World’s Champion. It was one of the most painful lessons in my life that I’d ever been charged with learning, that some things are simply out of my control, and that sometimes…well, sometimes s**t happens. It took me years to come to grips with this. Back then, wrestling did define me, and being cooped up in my home, unable to complete the most mundane daily routines, let alone step into a wrestling ring made me feel like part of me had died on the operating table. That was far and away the most difficult period of my life. I was angry all the time, bitter and despondent. I chased away almost everyone who tried to support me in my ordeal, as I was far too irritable all the time to make for decent company. In the end, I eventually decided that the only way I could possibly make it through this, and make it through the rest of my life entirely was to get away from it all. While I was convinced that there was no longer any happiness to be found in the life I was living…I still lived in an awful big world. It was possible that the inner peace that had abandoned me when I had been so cruelly stripped away from my life’s calling lied somewhere else on this blue-green globe. I’m telling you this because I want to make it clear that it was me who gave up on everybody else…not the other way around. I broke a lot of hearts when I packed up and disappeared to the other side of the planet without any real semblance of a warning. They may not have believed that I would one day be able to wrestle again, but these people – and they know who they are – couldn’t bear to see me like that, and tried every day to help me turn around. And I couldn’t bear it that they couldn’t bear it, so getting away from it all seemed like the only real path to follow. How exactly did I get from there to here? That’s another story, for another day. How my body found the decency to heal itself over five long, painful years is still beyond me. The fact of the matter is that I lost track of the nights I lied in bed for hours on end, praying for a second chance to do the only thing that ever brought me peace. (It seems odd that a man can find peace as he’s getting bludgeoned half to death in front of twenty thousand people, but I’ve learned not to question it). But one day…one day my prayers were answered. There’s been a lot of big moments for me since then. My comeback match, back in December was one of them. My first pay-per-view match in five years was another. But last night…last night trumps them all. It should be noted that I didn’t grow up a wrestling fan. My parents never particularly cared for it. Nor did any of my friends, or anyone I even knew for that matter. It still feels like an accident that a man named Edward “Impact” Osbourne convinced a down-on-his-luck recent college dropout to take a thirty-six hour bus ride to the uncharted depths of Quebec, simply because I looked like a man that he could mold into the squared circle’s new megastar. But since that time, I’ve taken to this sport the way an adopted child takes to their new family. A little uneasy at first, but before you know it you love them like you’ve known them all your life. Now, looking back on a career that began nearly ten years ago, I don’t know how I ever got along without my new family. There have been so many people I’ve met for only a few seconds, nothing beyond a few words and a quick exchange. There are countless others I have yet to meet, and some I might never meet at all. But without the unyielding support I’ve received over the years from all of these people, there’s no way that a former cripple pushing thirty could have ever come back to the sport he loved. But there are also the ones who deserve mention, as well as thanks. First, the incomparable Tony Little and Billy Mays, who were professional as could be and helped take my match to levels I couldn’t possibly have imagined. There’s Anton Assault, the former champ who pushed me to my limits and gave me one hell of a fight. I truly feel like our match stole the show on the Jersey end of Coast to Coast, and you can’t have a match like that when the guy in the other corner is a slouch. I’d like to thank my parents, who never stopped loving their son, even after he flushed fifty grand in tuition down the toilet. My boy Johnny and my sister Marie, who have been far and away my biggest fans since the day I laced up my first pair of boots. Last, but not least…I have to thank the fans of my latest adopted home, the AWC. Most of you didn’t have a clue who I was when I showed up last December to confront Amy. A lot of people were ready to write me off right off the bat. I was called washed-up, I was called pathetic, and I was even told that I shouldn’t tarnish the reputation I’d earned with a feeble attempt at a comeback. But you guys took me in, you threw me on your backs, and you gave me the strength to rekindle the old flame of Darcination. I couldn’t be prouder to represent you as the Frontier Champion, and as long as you continue to cheer me on, I promise that you will see me give no less than 1000% of my effort, night in and night out, to defend the title you helped me win All the best, Darcy Crisis AWC Frontier Champion. *** “What’d you think?” Darcy asked Marie, judging by her glance upwards at him that she’d finished perusing it. Marie frowned, and Darcy had a feeling that he know why. Her sentiments would prove him right. “You’ve left somebody out,” she said to him, quietly. “Yeah, I thought it kind of came off a little mushy, too. But sometimes, you just gotta write from the heart, you know?” Darcy rambled, pretending as if he hadn’t heard her. “Stop it, Darcy.” Marie said, flipping the pad of paper back at him. “You know what I’m talking about. At least you wrote this down on paper instead of e-mailing it off. There’s still time to change it.” “Actually…it’s already up there. I got a little excited about having my own website, and I called the guy back and they had me read it to a speed-typer. I didn’t even know they had those either, but that guy was good. Either that, or they already wrote up something better for me and I was just being humored,” Darcy said. “WHAT? Are you serious?” Marie inquired. Darcy was taken aback at her sudden incredulousness. “Uhh…yeah. I read over it, it seemed to sum up how I’m thinking. Is there something wrong with that?” “You really already wrote that, and you didn’t even take the time to mention Amy? Not even a passing remark?” Marie said, in more of a scolding tone than an ordinary query. “Not that it’s any of your business, but no,” Darcy fired back. Realizing a second later that that line wouldn’t work on a sister who could read him like a book, he offered more, in a voice barely above a whisper. “There’s no way I could put how much she’s meant to me.” Seeing that Darcy at least wasn’t being an outright jerk, Marie relented, moving from her seat in the nearby chair to the couch he occupied. “Well you can’t say nothing, Darcy. I know you two are on the outs, and I know I’d be a fool to throw my opinion about it at you when you’re just going to make up your mind on your own, anyway. But I’ve known Amy almost as long as you have. If you’re going to thank the ones who have been with you since the very beginning…you can’t leave her out.” Darcy sighed, rubbing his temples. He rarely won arguments against his quick-witted sibling. “I’ll talk to the site manager tomorrow,” he said, after a moment. “I hope they can afford to pay Speedy Type-zalez overtime…” *** From the front page of www.darcycrisis.com… Monday, September 4 4:41 PM Addendum. There is one person I left off the list of those deserving thanks yesterday. It’s because this person is deserving of far more than ordinary gratitude. Someone who is worthy of way, WAY more than the petty words I could come up with. She’s someone who I’ve known for many years, both as a bitter enemy, as my friend, and as my other half. Without her, I never would have been inspired enough to stand tall with her in Pier Six. If not for her, I never would have found the courage to make a return to wrestling. But once I realized that without the monumental part she’s played in my life, you couldn’t have read the thanks I bestowed on everybody else. So she deserves at least something. Maybe even more than I know how to give her. In the time I’ve spent with one Amy Campbell, we’ve driven each other to practically every emotion on the spectrum. I’ve laughed. I’ve cried. I’ve thrown s**t. And I’ve an entire night awake trying to figure out how to play a Fleetwood Mac song, just so she could have fun the rest of the day asking me if I was supposed to be Stevie or Christy. But I never once forgot how much she loved me, not once. And I hope she knows I always felt the same way.
