Born Down in a Dead Man's Town

15 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15/8/06 May 2006 “Born down in a dead man’s town” -Bruce Springsteen 1. Prologue The tale of how Patrick O’Shea gave up on life and threw a great big old fuck you to the world began on the night of the fifteenth of May, here - in Sandy Row, Belfast. This story begins with a mere photograph; battered and torn and fading to yellow at the edges, it encased a hundred thousand words all in a white bordered frame, all memories that would soon fade away as it fell gracefully from a bedroom window above. It was raining hard that May night, so hard that the grass below bowed before it’s malicious onslaught. The wind whipped and wailed and blew the rain in every direction so that no shade gave shelter. There are no havens here. This is Belfast where even the elements strive to put you down and every bastard on the street has a bone to pick. Right now for instance, the pelting rain and the shrieking wind were disguising the screams being emitted from number 46, the former residence of a certain photograph. And how the earth did shake. Falling into a gutter, swollen with rain, the photograph began to make waves, swimming along its makeshift canal. It bobbed under the water momentarily, listed then righted itself again as it dived through treacherous whirlpools and then soared like a free bird down the empty road towards the traffic lights. Like the carless tarmac below it, the lights were all eerily black. “Born down in a dead man’s town” as Springsteen would have chanted. The photograph paid no heed. Red, amber, green meant nothing now - it had abandoned it’s home so dear and so was now off to pastures new. It was A cowboy, footloose and fancy free, galloping over open plains on it’s trusty steed. It would provide memories elsewhere if God permitted. In the simple photograph was the image of an infant not more than six months old. Innocent and clad in white, little Joshua was giggling forever more with chubby hands lying delicately on his rosy cheeks. How ironic now that this giggle was matched by the screams of women and child, and of gun shots not twenty feet away in its former residence. Maybe if the photograph had ears, it would have sobbed instead as it was tossed from a second floor window, screamed as the masked men tore through the house like bulls enraged. But it did not, and so it went on giggling, never knowing that as it entered Turner’s Cross, the last scream had resounded leaving only silence. Never knowing that little number 46 on Sandy’s Row was now as lifeless as it. The little photograph clashed with a kerb stone and steered furiously left. It continued to bounce off the pavement as it soared alongside it. For awhile it ran neck and neck with an old beer can that floated with it’s silver rim pointing upwards; then, at another T-junction still not too far away from little 46, the beer can was swept right, while the photograph went straight ahead, right into a row of cars. The boat dipped and swayed, never allowing itself to sink, as it’s journey came abruptly to an end. After all it’s perseverance, the laughing babe’s image found itself trapped under the lip of the tire that belonged to a black Mondeo, half mounting the kerb. Funny how dreams can fade so fast. Or at least it should have ended there. After only a moment, the sound of feet splashing in the water could be heard. Staring upwards to the sky, the baby seen three blackened men tear the doors of the Mondeo open feverishly, throw a number of guns into the back, and then peel off the black balaclava masks that they wore. How curious the photograph would surely have though. And then a pair of eyes was on it, and its image, on it, the little child. Staring directly at each other, the photograph seen a broken face that carried tears in his eyes. It was not accustomed to this. Why cry on a night like this? The broken face did not answer and instead a scarred hand reached down and groped at the photograph taking it into the air. Those desperate eyes looked at his once again with such pain and the same question was asked - why cry on a night like this? In the moment between then and when he was in the pocket of the now unmasked man, the photograph’s question was answered. In the midnight sky was the sight of an inferno rising from a house not far away. Little number 46 it soon realized. There was already sounds of sirens but it did not hear - it didn’t need to hear Tucked away safely in a darkened pocket, the little photograph made only one wish. That it would never see again. 2. An Uninvited Guest Patrick wasn’t the only one hearing voices. It was on the day he was admitted to hospital following an unsuccessful suicide, that they first came; and in great torrents they blew hither. Initially, Henry Robinson thought they were coming from the vent of his little padded cell. He had a reckoning there were ghosts in those shafts and why not? The voices he was hearing belonged to ghosts… His wife, his son, his father, his brother. Surprisingly soothing, they whispered, their tongues flicking the inside of his ear. Hello Henry, would you mind picking up a few onions for dinner. “Sure Honey” Gurgle-gurgle-gurgle “Hehe…that’s my boy…little Josh. Say da-da for me. Comon now son.” Hello son. You wouldn’t be going mad now would you? “Get a grip Dad. Of course not, my tickers still ticking.” Why all the padding bro…who‘re they protecting you from? “Piss off John. Infact, all of you stop hiding, I don’t want to have a conversation with a wall!” “Shut the fuck up in there you whackjob, I’m trying to read the paper” This statement from his lovely “friend” Collins was followed by a quick rapping on the door. Henry could feel his face contorting into disgust as he imagined his swollen belly protruding out through his white jacket and over his white trousers. Guards here at Gransha Institution for the mentally insane always used that same rap. Shut the fuck up or I’ll be rapping like this on your head with a tidy little club I just so happen to carry about. “Yes sir, Mr Collins, sir,” Henry replied with a tone as pleasant as he could muster. Even if he was deranged, he still knew he would stick that cunt if he got half a yard. Collons grunted from behind the door to show his satisfaction then the sound of footsteps echoed his departure. The institution wasn’t quite what Henry had expected when he sought justice. After all, it wasn’t Henry Robinson who infiltrated little number 46, murdered his wife and son and left him for dead. Oh yeah, don’t forget burning the house to ashes with them all inside; killing all evidence the way only a professional would know how. How Henry got out is still a blur to him. He was shot eight times, hell, even the assailants thought he was dead. He should have been dead. But after waiting until they left, he dragged himself from the kitchen out into his backyard with his whole life blazing in an inferno all around him. And there was more than bullet wounds and burns that had to heal. In the subsequent days when he was recovering in hospital, he battled desperately to come to terms with the loss of his family but to no avail. One can not simply expect to digest such grief of that magnitude. And so, following hours on end of blood curdling screaming, he was shipped off here, to Gransha. He was physically on the mend but now there was the long arduous process of bringing the man back to reality. Make him grief for his loss and get on with life. And these voices simply won’t do for this to work. Why did you let us die Henry? It was his wife again but instead of the upbeat tone she usually carried, there was now only a voice void of any happiness. For the first time in a long time, Henry was speechless. He didn’t know what to say…that he was sorry? Sorry he pulled himself to safety without tending to his family? That he was a coward with two obliterated kneecaps? Sorry, he had been in the UVF for most of his young life and this assassination attempt was more of an eventuality than an improbability? Sorry, they had to pay for his crimes as a hitman for a paramilitary? That finally the target man was being targeted? What could you say? And so, Henry remained silent. Maybe you didn’t care…maybe you did. There was a pause. But you can prove yourself…you can show all of us you still care Henry. “I…” Kill them all, Henry, kill them all, kill them all, kill them all The voice followed him down a whirlpool as he swirled in and out of reality. His wifes words became haggard as her chants became screams. Kill them all, kill them, kill them all. In the moments that followed, Henry Robinson wished he had burned with his wife and child. It wasn’t right that HE was the one to escape death. Yet now, he made a promise to himself. The only promise he ever needed to keep anymore. He would kill them all. And then, maybe, he’d have peace. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Henry Robinson lay awake. It wasn’t the first time since his detainment and it wouldn’t be his last. Screams in the night were the main cause, but the guards were quite apt at finding ways of solving this. Other times it was because of various bruising that was once again at the work of those cheerful guards. But tonight, for the first time, Henry lay awake thinking of his family. They were dead and it was about time he got past that, he knew this to be true. Yet he couldn’t let go, not when (kill them all, kill them all) he had something to do first. At 3.05pm, in his lonely padded room, the lock in the door clicked open. Now be a good husband Henry and run along and do me proud Henry Robinson got up and subsequently escaped from Gransha mental institution. Observing all this from the corridor outside his cell and then from his car out in the parking lot, a shaped figure could nothing but smile. It was all working out nicely. 3. A choice It was a blur as it unfolded all around him. Paddy O’Shea, sitting behind a bush outside the house of his target, pulled his mask down over his face and flicked the safety off his small short. To his left, he watched his two comrades do the same. He did not know their names and they did not know his. This was good - all he wanted was one job to tide him over until he got back on his feet, until he was back wrestling. Names meant links, links meant jobs. This would be the final one and he had made great pains to assure this. With a nod, Paddy rose to his feet and without hesitation, marched over to the house. They told him little of the target, other than he was a vicious bastard, a UVF gangster. That made the fact he was to assassinate him a little easier to swallow. There was a rumble of thunder across the oil slicked sky - very unusual for mid May. Indicating to the other two to follow him, Paddy scuttled around to the back, leaping a small wall and landing onto a patio. There were French doors there to welcome his arrival while the house inside remained as dead as the night. A shower broke at last - this was good. Drumming hard onto the pink tiles of the roof, he smiled as he realized no one would hear a thing. All was good. Goody goody gumdrops. He entered with minimal effort and disturbance. From his left, he could hear fuzzy waffle emitting from a television. He eased his gun out and led the way to a door to his left. Henry Robinson and his wife, who were listening to a song from their hifi erected on a shelf near the window, never heard a thing until it was too late. There was a mere glimpse of three darkened men at the doorway before Henry felt a pain that screamed the whole way up and down his arm. His wife had barely time to scream before two soft plops, drowned out by the music silenced her. Barely conscious, Henry Robinson seen she had caught one in the eye. And all the while Franz Ferdinand, were shouting over and over again Gonna burn this city, burn this city Another volley of shots was released and this time, Henry got one in the neck and two in the chest. He felt his body faltering as his tongue became heavy in his mouth. He could say nothing as he slumped to what the men suspected was death. They swiveled and turned to leave the room. This fire is out of control. I’m gonna burn this city, burn this city The emotionless Paddy couldn’t help but wonder at the irony. “Not quite AWC now is it?” one of the men said. It took Paddy surprise: obviously the no names, no information guarantee had been voided behind his back. No time to bring it up now though: not until he was safe and clear from this. One of the men stayed at the bottom of the stairs while the other two gave a quick check. It was here that the world came down on Paddy O’Shea’s life turned on him. It was here he realized things would never be the same for him. The first thing he seen was- (kill them all, kill them all, kill them all) a babies cot, stained red with blood. The Mickey Mouse doing the polka night light made it look almost angelic. Paddy hoped for a second that perhaps he didn’t know what happened and the fact a babies corpse lay before him wasn’t his fault. But he knew. The spray of bullets had pierced the ceiling, ripped through the wood and tore through flesh. The little boy’s open eyes extenuated every point he was thinking. Why did you kill me? “I..” Why? I didn’t do anything to do you “I’m sorry…” Why? “I’m SORRY PLEASE!” He fell onto his knees as tears stung his face. “I’M SORRY, I’M SORRY PLEASE PLEASE!” ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ “I’M SORRY, I’M SORRY” “Take it easy Patrick, please!” Paddy bolted upright on the bed on which he lay. His voice was hoarse and he was shaking like a leaf. He wasn’t in the babies bedroom anymore. Nor was he overlooking a cot. He didn’t wear black, he wasn’t holding a gun. And he had been merely reliving a dark point of his life. To call it a dream would have been too kind. “Bad dream eh? I can understand that.” The voice came to his left. As he blinked his eyes in acknowledgement of his new surroundings he realized where he was and who in fact that husky voice belonged to. He was in a hospital room (the stench of bleach underlined this fact) and he was in company of a man he hadn’t met before in person, someone he didn’t know the name of. He only knew he was Q and his purpose was to acquire Paddy for a mission he had assured himself he so desperately needed to feed himself. Q was a gangster, or at least worked for a gangster and had paid Paddy handsomely for taking care of little number 46. He was the sandman that were bringing him these bad dreams so to speak. “How are you feeling Paddy?” How did he feel? He was a child killer and he had been put in that situation by the asker of the question. What did he think? Was Paddy happy, joyous that such an honour had been bestowed on him? Paddy glanced at the criss crossing scars on his wrists. The snaking stitching frightened him into politeness. “Aye’m fine”. Q smiled. Clad in a smart black suit by an Italian designer Paddy couldn’t even pronounce, he was the personification of calmness. His pepper and salt colored hair suggested his age to be at least 50, his curved, smiling lips suggested he cared about Paddy’s well being. His piercing, electric blue eyes suggested he was a liar. “Good. I’m glad to hear that.” Liar. “I don’t usually come and visit our employees, especially when they assure me our links are terminated…but I felt I had a moral obligation. I know about the child. I know about your pain. And I know I put you in that situation.” Q coughed. “And for that I’m sincerely sorry. We should have informed you of the full details” Paddy felt a temporary twang of relief inside him. It meant he wasn’t wholly to blame for that nights incidents. Then again, it was only temporary, for he soon realized, it was he who squeezed the trigger. “Despite that Paddy, I want you to work for us again. You have a great talent for these operations - I take it your time in the IRA was what refined your skill. I want you to come work for us because I believe you are the type of person to drive our company forward in the future. Of course, your wage packet and responsibilities will reflect this.” The word no began to rise to Paddy’s lips. “I can see you already wish to decline. That’s fine. I know that you will soon have a new contract in AWC doing what you love doing and any money we can offer you will only be a petty bonus to that. I can see you think that by taking this job you only risk your freedom as well as your career for the sake of an extra few pound we can offer you” Q leaned forward so that Paddy felt his hot breath on his nose. “But you haven’t considered one other thing…” Q leaned around now, so his forked tongue flicked in Paddy’s ear. “Your family isn’t as valuable to us as you” Paddy lunged as the sneering voice rattled inside his head. So this would be how it would be. He must work for them or his family are history. “You bastard…aye’ll kill ye. AYE’LL FECKIN’ KILL YE!” With wires coming out of his skin and his body a frail mass of bones, Paddy made another valiant attempt to swipe at Q but his strength was gone. He fell onto his side coughing as Q chuckled on, now edging backwards to the door. “Don’t think of it like that Paddy. After all, you look after us and we’ll look after you. And your interests. For instance, we happen to know that the man you were SUPPOSED to kill is now off his leash and if he so happened to find out where you were, you’re in no state at the minute to defend yourself. Bit of advice, sign yourself out now. And I mean now. “ Paddy felt tears welling up in his eyes. It was happening again. He was caught in another vengeful web. Another pawn for another prick. Just like AWC, just like Hate, just like Q. He closed his eyes and wished once more that he was infact dead. “Oh yeah Patrick, keep those eyes open. There are ways we can hurt people beyond the grave. Au Revoir P!” With a chuckle, Q exited the small hospital room. Paddy was shaking now, stifling tears and phlem. He turned onto his side and seen what he should have left behind all those nights ago. The picture of a little baby. A picture of a dead innocent baby. “Everything aye touch withers…” he mumbled. And then Paddy O’Shea made a choice. 4. Henry’s Day Out Henry spent that day hiding - hiding in hedges that bordered middle class houses, hiding behind dumpsters in desolate alleyways. Sometimes he would sleep and sometimes he would watch. He made a game of watching the police cruisers slide past out of his peripheral vision. Like hunting dogs, tracking the fox so feverishly. It dawned on him then he was now the fox. And it made him tremble with anticipation. They had locked him up because he couldn’t bear the thought of himself living, not when he had just let his family die. They had locked him up for that. No worries though, he’d gut the bastards that took his family from him and THEN they’d would have a reason to lock him up. But this was one fox the hunt wouldn’t catch up with. But he needed a name, he’d need a lead otherwise he was chasing the faded memory of three masked men. He needed something, anything, to get him started. On this day that Paddy O’Shea lay recovering in a hospital room and Henry Robinson bickered with the rabid voices, something extraordinary happened. Henry stopped looking for the lead that would bring him closer to fulfillment. Because it came to him. As he slowly ventured to the verge of the road, wary of police but yet so desperate for food, he felt something wrap around his leg as the wind whistled in his ears. A newspaper page. Look at the paper dear And so he did. “AWC Superstar’s Suicide Attempt,” he whispered, his voice trembling as something clicked on inside of his head. AWC…you remember what one of the men said Henry. One of the men was in AWC… “Irish born superstar, Paddy O’Shea is recovering in BELFAST’S Royal Victoria Hospital following a botched suicide attempt and-” He trailed off. He didn’t need to hear anymore. Henry Robinson stuck out his thumb on a road not so far from Belfast. That’s a boy Henry. Go make me proud Henry smiled. The ghosts were on his side now. After awhile, some fool came along and picked up and Henry was reintroduced to driving. 5. Henry Go Loco The shape stepped forward into the night crested hospital room and the moonlight fell onto the face of Henry Robinson. The drive had been comfortable, almost relaxing. It reminded him of the trips he would take his wife and Josh on. Reminiscing brought forth misery and misery bred anger. So, with a bloodied corpse lying in the boot of the car, Henry stopped at a fishing shop he spotted and bought himself a nice tidy little gutting knife. My, he loved to fish. The knife had jostled in his pocket the whole way here. It was part of Henry now and he was determined to make it part of this AWC superstar. The knife knew all about revenge and it was only too eager to assist. Yet now, as Henry Robinson looked into an empty room, the knife, like Henry, felt a twang of enragment. He was gone…gone, gone, gone. Bedsheet pulled back, empty dressing table. Paddy O’Shea had flown the coop. “Maybe he had voices of his own…” The light made Henry look eerie. His skin had an unhealthy tallow hue; sagging; the cheeks had become hanging jowls that were speckled with stubble, almost as much white in that stubble as black. Wavy lines, were engraved onto the shelf of his forehead, above bushy eyebrows that were now pointing downwards in crushing defeat. His eyes remained bloodshot and thoughtless. They were the eyes of a man being pushed into a premature age, a man perhaps 35 going on 60, carrying all the hate he had mustered over the years. “Bastard…” Don’t fret my love…look at the chart, we may have an address Henry did so. He stepped right into the moonlight so color unfolded slowly over his body then he picked up a chart at the bottom of the bed. “Paddy O’Shea…current residence…New York…” Henry patted the gutting knife he carried in his inside pocket. “I’ve always wanted to see New York.” Yes dear, very good. Now don’t forget to pack enough undies now! Henry nodded and slipped out of the room again, into the darkness. All was good. He had a name, he had an address. And pretty soon, he’d have a gutted pykie on a stick. Configure your epitaph. 6. Conclusion And so we arrive here, back in August 2006, back in AWC. We arrive back to a time where Paddy’s doing what he loves to do: wrestle. Every week he waits for that phone call, every week fearing he’ll be called out to another hit. Every week, wondering if his family have a gun to their temple. And that’s why wrestling is the escape, because for twenty minutes, Paddy O’Shea can throw fuck you to the world and forget all his troubles. In his savageness, he is human again, devoid of emotion or sympathy. Garbage Bag Johnny is a drugged up fool, too busy leeching off society to matter. Paddy has defeated him before. Red Rock is a joker who thinks life’s a big joke. That’s exactly why he’s picking scabs down at the Relentless division. Paddy has beat him before too. He’s beat Ellis Nash, the girl who wanted to be Dick. He’s won the Alliance Title before the Furious Fists of God even knew what a suplex was. And all the while Aimz and Darcy Crisis fuck in a corner. He’s 35 years old. There’s no one in the world that loves him anymore. His bones are weak and his spirit is breaking. But if there’s one thing he does know it’s this. He can beat people like these. He has stomped through the Uber Judges and the Frisco’s of the world. Whether it was gtt5 six months ago or PCW six years ago. These kids don’t know misery like Paddy O’Shea knows misery. And for those twenty minutes, when Paddy O’Shea becomes free. They’re going to take all his pain away from him. TBC Paddy’s back dickhead.
