The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch. 14

15 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15th August Prologue: A quick re-cap of Ch. 13 - if you haven’t read it. In Ch. 13, Pierce is struggling to keep things going around with his life. Marvin Harrington (Mafia boss and the man responsible for the deaths of Lilly Connelly and many more, escapes from prison and with the help from certain people, manages to get to Albany and tracks down Pierce, some unfinished business.) Instead of finding Pierce, Harrington comes across Dr. Philips (the doctor hired to look at Lois, who has a concussion) Harrington kills Dr. Philips and the doorman. He kidnaps Lois. Pierce arrives home, finds the place in tatters. Bureau arrives; bring Pierce to the station to help him and set-up a case. Forensic teams analyse the apartment. Now is the start of Ch.14. ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch. 14 {Pilot} The bureau had been in my house. That was expected. But, they had gone about it in such a subtle manner. The room had been cleaned. The living room table had been polished. My college assignments lay in a neat pile - just like I left them. The bullet holes in the door had been taped over. A large amount of carpet had been removed, along with the bed covers and duvet. I smiled and looked around the apartment. I knew I would have to have a long talk with my next-door neighbours - I would have Mrs. Winchester begging for some gossip, that much I knew. I moved into the kitchen, threw the car keys onto the counter and stared at my murky reflection in the dining-room table. I decided I should get some sleep before I plan my next move. My mind consciously moved onto the worries of Lois, and whether she really was okay. Harrington had yet to ring me, and make a ransom. But, as the hours rolled by, I became impatient and less thrilled by the work of those around me. I grabbed a bottle of water and made my way onto the veranda. The chair’s cushion still had some rain on it, so I turned it over and sat down, throwing my legs up onto the table. It was chilly out, but I still had my jacket on. I placed the bottle of water onto the arm of the chair, and shoved my hands into the pocket of my jacket. I still hadn’t managed to clean, shave and change my clothes. It felt good to be at home and just a slight bit more comfortable, after a long night in the silver cube. I looked around and stared unknowably at a picture frame, which seemed to be empty. I rose from the comfort of the chair on the decking and made my way into the dimly lit living room area. My apartment had an emptiness to it - with Lois gone. I turned the frame over and read out what was on the back: “Lois and Pierce at Rob’s 22nd Birthday party”. The FBI and NYPD detectives had removed a picture of me and Lois for use, without my knowing. I grunted and put the frame back down, making a mental note to ask for the photo back as soon as I could get in contact with either Rivers or Rider. I replayed River’s attempt to provoke me into doing nothing, how he pushed me against the Plexiglas window, and his threats that if I interfered in the case of finding Lois, I could go behind bars. I was what cops called, a liable asset! I was the only hope for Lois’s safe return and that scared me the most, because I knew if this didn’t work out. I might not see her again, or worse, we could both be dead. The chill in the air slowly drafted its way through the patio doors and into the living-room. I shut the patio, leaving my bottle of water outside, I wasn’t really that thirsty. It was coming up to 10:00 am now. Lois had been missing for around 11 hours. I removed my jacket and slung it over the coat rack and pulled off my dirt and blood stained t-shirt and shoved it into the bin, along with my jeans, runners and socks. I hoped into the shower, and remained under the droplets of running water for 30 minutes. Thinking and pondering what my best chance was. I flicked the power off and grabbed the nearest towel on the rack; it was Lois’s blue towel. I smiled at the memory, and wrapped myself in it. After I had thrown on a pair of boxers, a black wife beater and some grey baggy tracksuit bottoms, I sat on the coverless double bed and leaned back. Closing my eyes for a few moments, I began to drift to sleep. By now the police had built a room of evidence, photos, documents and interviews, but still didn’t have a single lead on Lois. I was in the dark a lot of the time, and Tony Rush began to move out of the ring of investigators and FBI. A loud knock came from my door. I rolled off the bed and looked at the clock. It was 1:00 pm. I had fallen asleep, and I was surprised that somebody would be calling. I rose from the carpet less floor and made my way across the hall, noting to turn off the lights. The person behind the door continued knocking. And a familiar voice said my name. I hitched the bolt and pulled the door across. “Rise and Shine, Pierce,” Tony Rush stood behind the door, still dressed in his blue suit, with a six pack and some breakfast. “Can I come in? My hotel is on Lincoln Avenue and it’s a long way from here.” I unhitched and the bolt and opened the door wide to let him passed. He sighed when he saw the room, and placed the doughnuts and beer onto the counter. He smiled at the sight of the pictures on the walls and removed his jacket. “Well, you got off lucky!” “Huh?” “The last time I did a forensic investigation, they left the place in a mess. So, how’d you sleep?” he smiled and popped a can. “I got about four hours.” “Well, it’s something. You’ve got some colour in your cheeks again, and you don’t look worse for wear. Have a beer!” he threw me a can and gulped down his own one. “Why are you here, Rush?” I flipped the lid of my can. I knew it was early and not the best time to be consuming alcohol, but in Moscow it was passed 6:00 pm, so I thought, what the heck and downed some of my own can. “Please, cut the formalities and call me Tony,” he smiled. “OK,” I returned the smile and moved over to the beanie bag on the floor. “I’m here because I don’t like the way Rivers and Rider are taking this, and because I’m not a New York agent, I don’t get a say,” he pulled out a packet of Marlborough cigarettes. “What do you propose we do?” I looked at him, finally relieved that somebody wanted to get off their ass and do something to help me. “Well, I want to follow some of my own leads, but I think if we work together we can find - … wait!” Tony became extremely sketchy and looked around. He moved to the socket and using his pocket knife, undid the bolts and pulled out two small microphones. He put his fingers on his lips and moved to the phone. Using his pocket knife, he opened the back and pulled out a receiver, he then opened the veranda and dropped the two microphones and receptors into the waste shoot. “What was that for?” “Bugs,” he said. “Bugs?” “From the FBI, so they can monitor who enters and leaves your house,” he said. “So, how are you with guns?” “Wha -?” I practically choked on the remainder of my beer. “You mean an actual weapon right?” Tony nodded, and smiled as I was still struggling to speak, whilest choking at the same time. “I’ve never used one,” I replied and took another gulp of beer to wash down the tiny bit of vomit that managed to make its way up my trachea and into the back of my throat. “Okay, well then, I want you to meet me at Pino’s shooting range on Bought Street. You know where it is?” I nodded, still unsure as to why he wanted me at Pino’s. “What is this for?” “Self-defence, Pierce. Just encase Harrington sends in the cavalry. Also, just encase you have to go in alone with Harrington,” he looked at me, and placed his half-empty beer can on the counter. “Listen, I have to go. I’m going to check on some leads and then I’ll meet you at Pino’s at four thirty. Is that alright?” I said my goodbyes to Tony Rush and closed the door behind him. I returned to the kitchen, removed the beer cans and feasted on one doughnut. My appetite was returning, a good note to an extremely strange twenty four hours. I walked back into the bedroom and looked at the bed. Lois’s two rings were still on the bedside table. I picked one up, and remembered when I gave it to her. It was a Claddagh ring. I bought it in a jeweller’s shop, which specialised in Irish jewellery. I bought it for her when she turned twenty. I placed the Claddagh ring on a small silver chain, and tide it onto my neck. Tony Rush was right - we needed to get to work. I pulled up Lois’s laptop, plugged it into the phone socket and began to access the internet. I thought about what a reporter would do, or better yet, what Lois would do, and then it hit me. I started traces on news articles based on those in alliance with Harrington. I needed to find something, just a tiny bit of information that could direct me to Harrington. My google search pulled up two names - cohorts, well escorts that worked for Harrington on a business trip when he was here in Albany. Both women worked the streets on 10th Avenue. I pulled off a small sheet of paper and wrote down the names of the two women - Jackie and Hailey. I threw on a pair of sports socks and runners, along with a black hooded jumper and grabbed my car keys. I knew Rush would understand if I started without him. I raced out of the door, passed my neighbours, and whilest yelling “SORRY” I leapt down the stairs and into the car park. {Investigating} It was almost two thirty when I drove into 10th Avenue. I decided to park the car at the grocery shop, and bought a can of pop before heading down the street to find these two women. I went around the block twice, until two women came out a small bar, smoke followed behind them. One woman was black, with a short leather mini-skirt and a velvet boob tube. The other was a short white woman, with a load of red lipstick, a short black miniskirt and a purple top. They moved slowly across the street, avoiding traffic and stopped near to a metal fence and pulled out some cigarettes. I threw the can into the dumpster and approached the two women. “Jackie? Hailey?” I asked, making sure I put some distance between us; just encase they lunged at me or they weren’t the two women. “Yeah, who wants to know?” the black woman moved forward and clicked her fingers at me. “I’m Pierce Lavelle. I need to ask you a few questions about Marvin Harrington”. “Uh-huh, well hun, we don’t give away information,” the black woman spoke up once more. “Now move or you’ll ruin our chances at some customers”. I looked around the street and noted the heat from the sun and the temperature around the place and looked back at the two women. “I don’t think you’ll be getting much action today!” I stated bluntly. “And thank god for that,” the small white one spoke up. “I’ll pay you for the information”. I was becoming desperate by this time; if only I was a cop and had a badge, I might be able to make some sense with these two, but I decided once more against calling Tony and decided to take the friendly approach. “How much?” the black woman moved forward again, her nose almost touching mine. I coughed to hide my nerves and moved my head. “Two hundred dollars.” “You got a deal hun!” the black woman stepped back. “The names Jackie and this here’s Hailey.” I paid them a hundred each and they said they would prefer to stand at their corner. I hadn’t gotten much information for my moneys worth. But, they mentioned a list of places that Harrington used to stop at while he was on business in Albany. Hailey made no effort to get a word in edgeways and I took it that she was the shy, timid one, and Jackie was the obnoxious and feisty one. I wrote the list of places down and made my long trek back to the jeep. All the while contemplating on Lois’s condition and my lost two hundred dollars. I looked around, feeling a slight paranoid suspicion that someone was following me. I drove out onto 3rd street and took a left onto 2nd and then a right onto 1st Street. The traffic light glowed red. I was becoming extremely warm, despite the powered air conditioning fans. I removed my jumper and flung it onto the backseat. It was then I heard a yelp. I turned around and saw a small puppy, a Daxhund. I pulled off the road and stopped at the curb. The puppy began to wag its tail and its tongue hung out of its mouth, as its beady eyes looked at me. I reached in and saw the note: Name: Shelby. Owner(s): Lois Wood and Pierce Lavelle. DOB: 9th June 2005. I smiled at the small puppy and realised now that Lois had spent the missing money on a dog - which looked strangely like the dog she had as a child. I stroked its back and placed it on the passenger seat. “NO! Don’t do that … not the interior!” I lifted Shelby up and watched her eyes gleam as she relieved herself all over the leather interior. I laughed and looked at the interior, praising the gods that nothing important was on the seat and decided to stop by a pet store and get some necessaries for the dog - and get the dog checked out by the vet. {Target Practice} The dog store owner was extremely helpful, and handed me a bag, filled with puppy treats, toys and puppy food. I threw the bag into the boot and pulled Shelby out, allowing her to run around the gravel, barking and sniffing. The vet had been forthcoming with his ridiculing of Lois and me for leaving the puppy in the car. I nodded it off, lied through my teeth and thanked the Vet. I had become extremely attached to the long-haired Daxhund. After she had done her business, I slipped her into the small bed I had bought that I had placed in the foot space of the passenger seat. She began to sleep quietly as I pulled out of the dog store parking space and drove back up 1st street and took a left into Bought Street and stopped outside Pino’s. Tony Rush was seated on the hood of his car, his sun glasses on. I looked once more at Shelby and muttered a few words to her, before closing the door and winding down the window down a little for some air for her. Tony Rush leapt off his car and looked at me. “What took you so long?” “Dog trouble!” “That’s why you stink of crap!” “Lois bought a dog. I found her in the back of the car. I didn’t notice her until now.” “You’ll probably have to take it to a vet,” Tony looked in the window at Shelby as she slept quietly. “I did that,” I looked around at Pino’s. It was a shooting range and an ammunition store. It was surprising the amount of Americans that now moved around with guns in their bags, bedside tables and cars. Tony Rush moved forward and opened the door, the bell clanged behind us. “First things first, we’re going to get you a weapon,” he said as he moved to the counter. “GREAT~!” I was now one of the growing populations with guns. A small tubby man, with a truckers hat that read “HOOTERS” came from behind the back. He was chewing tobacco as he and Rush talked. I gazed at the long list of weapons and turned my attention to the vests and other means of weapondary protection. “Here,” Tony put a silver gun and a box of ammo into my hand. “That is a berretta, and that is its ammo. You’ll be practicing here. Oh, and Tucker! He’ll need a bullet proof vest.” The man called Tucker, with the trucker hat, nodded and measured my waist and upper body and then pulled down a vest and handed it to Rush, who shoved it into a large bag, along with some extra ammo. Rush and I loaded our weapons - which was surprisingly simple. I shoved on a pair of headphones and took aim. Shooting the stomach, the head and the arm of the cardboard man. Tony Rush got all of his bullets in the bulls-eye on the head. He patted me on the back. “Not bad for a first attempt,” he laughed. “I guess so,” I slipped the silver berretta into the small of my back, against my waistband and followed Rush out into the parking lot. Shelby was on the driver’s seat, her small head poking out of the window. “Somebody’s happy to see you,” Tony joked as he opened his car door. “Em, Pierce. Listen, you’ll need to train here everyday. I found some leads and I’ll stop by to see you later. But, for now, just act as though nothing is going on. And, whatever you do, don’t tell Rivers and Rider.” Rush put the rented Toyota into reverse and headed up for his Hotel on Lincoln. Smoke billowed out of the exhaust pipe. I made my way back to the car and by this stage Shelby had peed once more with excitement. I picked her up and smiled. “No wonder Lois chose you. You have a character, and you like to ruin my interior.” I put some newspaper over the stain and reversed out of the lot. {An old reunion} After taking Shelby on a long walk, filling her bowls with food and water, I locked the apartment door, leaving Shelby to the comforts of a warm bed and a flat. I ran down the stairs and decided it was time to call the one person I knew that could help me with this situation. My second cousin, Vinnie Reed. He was a bonds agent by day and a pimp and crack investigator by night. I took the car into Fowler Avenue and sat patiently; devising a plan to get the information out of my cousin. He was never one to spill the beans. Which explained why his wife was still with him; despite his antics after hours. I switched off the ignition, pulled up the hood on my rain coat and made my way across the tarmac road and into Vinnie’s bonds office. His secretary Edith Richards was seated at her desk, filing her long nails with her legs crossed as she chewed loudly on some chewing gum. “You got an appointment?” she asked as she blew on her nails. “I’m here to see Vinnie. Tell him it’s Pierce Lavelle. He’ll know who I am.” She put down her nail file reluctantly and buzzed Vinnie. Vinnie Reed came out of his office and looked at me. He had changed since we last spoke, almost ten years ago. But, from random talk at family conventions I found out what he had been up to. He was a lot heavier now, more grey hair and some stubble around the chin. “Pierce, it’s good to see ya’,” he patted me on the back and led me into his office. “What can I do you for?” Same old Vinnie, I thought to myself as I gazed around his office. His appearance had changed, but his speech and interior décor hadn’t. “I need to know more on Marvin Harrington,” I replied, gazing at a famous boxer on the wall of his many pictures. “Who? Pierce, I run a bonds company, not a secret service,” he looked a little agitated. “Vinnie, I know damn well what it is you do here.” “Uh Huh,” he looked intrigued now, just waiting for my reply. “Criminals put a bond or bail into your company. Then, when they fail to appear in court you have men that go out and get them and bring them into court.” “Well, you’re right, mostly. They’re not ‘men’; they prefer the term bounty hunters. And, Pierce, I won’t be involving you in my business. Our mother’s would have me hung, drawn and quartered.” “Vinnie, our mothers don’t need to know. Now, I’ll ask you once again. Can I have the information you have on Harrington. I know he posted bonds and bail with your company.” Vinnie leaned back in his chair and laughed, he looked around his room and nodded to Edith, who left and returned to her small secretarial desk. “Vinnie … don’t make me tell Gabriel about the duck!” Vinnie went a crimson red and slammed his head off of his locker in frustration. “I’ll never live tha’ down,” he mumbled whilest rubbing his hand on his head. “Fine, you win. But, if I may say something. Why are you so desperate to catch Marvin?” “He has Lois.” “The chubby fat girl from Williamsburg Estates.” “Yes and no. She’s not chubby, in fact, she’s skinny, she’s my girlfriend and he’s kidnapped her to get to me.” Vinnie shook his head, and whirled his chair around until he stopped at a lower drawer and pulled out a file, labelled “RANGER”. “What’s wrong?” I looked at the file and then back at Vinnie. “Pierce, why didn’t you come to me sooner?” his head was still shaking. “Listen, Vin. Spill it; I don’t have a lot of time. I have a meeting with an FBI agent in a few minutes, and I can’t let him know I came here.” “Christ, the FBI! This is one those shit’s hit the fan situations, ‘en it?” “Yes Vin!” “Okay, listen, we might have a problem. I put my best guy of retrieving Marvin Harrington. His name is Don Ranger. He used to be part of the highly trained SWAT teams that we hear so much about, but he got kicked out for drugs. Anyway, to cut a long story short. I hired him for his expertise and now, now he is after Harrington with all of the best weapons. Pierce, what I am trying to say is; if Lois is a liability to his receiving forty grand, then he will kill her.” “God Vin, why’d you hire a man like that? Call Ranger off.” “I can’t,” he looked at me and closed Ranger’s file. “Why?” “When a Bounty hunter goes after his prey, so to speak, we severe all ties until he comes back for more information or with the prize,” Vinnie slid a brown A4 sized envelope across the table. “This is everything I have managed to get on Harrington. I hope you’re quicker than Ranger.” “Well, I have begun to ask some questions on various ex-affiliates of Harrington’s.” “You what?” Vinnie yelled, no realising the intensity of his own voice. “Damn, that means he’ll be watching you too.” “Great. My day keeps getting better and better.” I stood up and put the file under my rain coat and made my way to the door. “Em, Pierce -” Vinnie stood up and looked at me. “Yeah?” “You won’t tell Gabriel about the duck, will you?” he had a puppy-dog look in his eyes. He was truly frightened about having a pet control service knocking on his door. “No, but a word of warning. Next time you’re drunk, keep your pleasures to yourself.” “Duly noted,” he smiled and sat down relieved. I closed the door behind me, and became slightly nervous at the thought of Ranger stalking me too. Edith looked up from her table and winked at me; I waved goodbye and ran across the street, dodging the large puddles. I threw the document onto the passenger seat, along with some newspaper and put the car into gear. This situation was becoming increasingly confusing. I sat in the car, listening to the rain pitter-patter against the windows, my breathing slowed to a more relaxed rate as I gazed at Vinnie’s Bonds Office.
