Not your ordinary Muslim

14 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02.14.06 Muslims are stereotyped as being finicky and whiny, folks who complain about the slightest thing offending their religious sensibilities. They won't shut up about being offended, even in the face of hypocrisy, at least that's how the stereotype goes. There are a lot of things that prove the stereotype right. In Saudi Arabia, they burn Bibles regularly; hell, being in possession of the Christian holy book is a felony. However, urine splashes on the Qu'ran, and those same Bible-burners call for a jihad. Muslims are the ones who are organizing world-wide protests over some Danish cartoons that depict their prophet in an unkind light. Hell, Muslims even tried to get piggy banks banned in England because the pig is an unclean animal to their religion. Catholics bitch about Jesus Christ Superstar and are told to shut up and enjoy the art. Muslims though... well, they're not told to shut up, probably because of a small sect of them who will blow you up when you least expect it for not conforming to them. Think this is a passage from a right-wing conservative paper yet? Well if you do, you're wrong. This is all part of the thought-process of one Tariq Abdul Wahad Suleimon. See, the Captain fancies himself as a man of action, someone who doesn't take crap from anyone. Which is why it looks so bad the way he's reacted to Red Rock's comments at the Christmas Party. Even though he's not Arab and even though he's not even the most devout Muslim on his block let alone in the world, Suleimon is still looked upon as another whiny Muslim. This isn't Captain Suleimon. He's not the kind of person to fly off the handle and rant all the time instead of doing something, especially in a manner as trivial as a simple comment, which no one still knows about. But perception is reality. Still, Suleimon knows that he's handcuffed. Harber and Volkeyva haven't given him a whole lot of opportunities to face Red Rock head-on, and yet continued, covert assaults like the one perpetrated at Freshburst would lend people to think Suleimon was a terrorist. Tariq Suleimon is no terrorist. In fact, the Sultan of Smackdown hates terrorists. He hates what Islam has become in the minds of the West. He longs for the time when Muslims controlled the world and acted as they wanted to instead of skulking in the shadows and striking at civilians. He longs for the Ottoman Empire. But for now, he must wait and bide his time... ...and endure the label of being just another whiny Muslim. Bloodlust... what a fitting name for an event. Captain Suleimon certainly has a bloodlust, a lust for the blood of Red Rock. And thusly, he readies his caché of weapons to be placed in the Ottoman Torture Chamber, loading them into his U-Haul truck with the aid of Dogan and Hayvan, his traveling 'entourage' if you can call them that. 'The Bracks?' 'Check...' Hayvan carried the tongue-holding device used on Red Rock at Freshburst into the van. 'Pillory?' 'Check...' Dogan rolled it in. 'Dude,' Hayvan said. 'You're pretty hardcore about all of this.' 'I have to be though.' Suleimon furrowed his brow. 'If not, I run the risk of not being taken seriously.' 'Who cares?' said Dogan. 'Not many people take Mike Wade seriously, and he's a Triple Champion. Far worse fates than not being taken seriously.' 'You're mistaken.' His voice was cold. 'People do take Mr. Wade seriously. Maybe not outside of the ring, but inside of the ring, where things count... he is a force to be reckoned with. To understand why I need to be 'hardcore' is to understand the need to be taken seriously 'I am very much tired of seeing the Ottomans being regarded as a footnote in history. I should very well be 'hardcore' if I want to make sure that hooligans like Red Rock will respect me and what I stand for.' 'Dude,' Dogan spoke up. 'Dude, the empire is dead, man. Turkey's moved on. Can't you accept that? The world is moving on. There are no more empires...' 'The United States is no empire?' 'Well... umm...' 'Exactly my friend. You fail to find the words to counter me.' 'What's the big deal then... why do you have to prove anything? Why can't you just be happy with the way Turkey is?' 'Because I'm not happy with it. I can't be happy with a country that is a puppet to the United States. I want a country that looks out for itself and its own interests in the Middle East... not someone else's.' 'So why do you wrestle?' Suleimon looked at Dogan quizzically. 'Answer me. Why do you wrestle, put on a costume and go out and compete and kill yourself for people who hate you instead of staying at home and making a difference in Parliament, or by leading a movement or something like that.' 'Oh, you're a pathetic idealist. You can't change anything in Parliament or on the streets. People don't care enough about politics anyway. They do care about wrestling.' 'Oh yeah? If so, then why have ratings been down?' 'ThHey're coming back up, but that's regardless to my point. Wrestling's always been a cyclical business. People watch. They just won't admit to it. 'But back to my point, they care enough that people like myself are much larger role models than folks like Sezer or even George Bush or Tony Blair... if I can show people that the Ottoman is a breed to be feared, they will fear me. And maybe they won't stereotype the Turks as the filthy coward Arabs who hide in ratholes and attack people when they aren't looking. My ancestors never met a fight they didn't like until they were castrated by Britain.' Suleimon once again looked at them coldly. Dogan said nothing... in his mind, his friend made a point. Dogan wouldn't be silenced. 'Seriously,' he said. 'You believe that? You believe that by killing yourself in a torture chamber, you can get people to respect you? Fuck that man, you know the reason why you're in this is because over something Red Rock said. You're always telling me these tightwads who protest over the cartoons, they give all us Muslims a bad name, but you're doing the same thing.' 'Oh but am I? Tell me, am I calling for Red Rock to be beheaded?' Silence. 'Am I calling for Big Ben to be blown to pieces for the glory of Allah?' More silence. 'Am I... 'Look, I get the point.' 'Clearly you don't, or else you wouldn't get so fidgety. My point is, would Osama bin Laden challenge the United States to outright war ever? No. And he is the man all these ignorant Westerners relate anyone of our skin color to. 'When I come out and destroy Red Rock, I will not only gain a personal victory, a victory gained by making sure that no one can insult me and think they can only get away with it with hearing incessant nagging... '...but I will make people realize that I'm not a stereotype.' Dogan shook his head but said nothing. Once again, like with the fencing incident, he knew he couldn't get his friend out of the frenzy he was in. Suleimon wasn't done. 'Red Rock's blood will be validation of that. He will be the first hero of the West to fall. And he will do so horriffically. There can be no other way...' Yes, Suleimon's bloodlust was real.
