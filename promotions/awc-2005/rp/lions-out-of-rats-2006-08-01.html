Lions Out of Rats

1 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/1/06 Paris. 1998. Antony sat in his apartment, leaning back in the high-backed black leather chair that he had bought not two days past. He was just eighteen now, having graduated school and in Paris ostensibly for college. He had already made up his mind that he wasn't going, though. The university had placed several calls, but Anton could always hang the phone up. Eventually they'd give up on him altogether. He crossed his ankles and yawned mightily, stretching his arms. The phone rang. Antony blinked, brushing his hand over his face. Well, speak—or, rather, think—of the devil! It must be the university calling for something or other. ''Why haven't you shown up?'' ''We're going to cut you from the enrollment list.'' ''The matriculation fee is nonrefundable.'' He wondered what they would come up with this time. Still, it couldn't hurt to answer. It might do for a laugh. He snatched up the phone and pressed it to his ear. ''Hello?'' He spoke French; it was the lingua franca of the country, so he expected his caller to return in kind. ''Antony,'' came an authoritative voice. Antony recognized it quickly and he sat up straight, clutching his forehead now. ''Father?'' Antony was speaking Igbo now. His father wouldn’t accept anything less. A million possibilities about why his father had called whirled through Antony's head and none of them were good. ''How is Paris?'' Antony's father asked. ''Fine. I'm enjoying it.'' ''Good, good.'' There was a slight pause. Antony thought he knew what was coming next. He tensed for it as if it was a physical attack. A fatal blow, even. ''So you have been relaxing, then? Seeing the city?'' Antony almost collapsed as the chill of relief took hold. He grabbed onto the arm of the chair with one hand. ''Yes, I've been looking about,'' Antony said. ''It's a nice city, I'll like it here.'' ''You have seen the Eiffel Tower?'' ''Yes, it's beautiful up close,'' Anton lied. The Eiffel Tower had been the absolute last thing on his mind. ''You know, the last time you were in Paris, you were a baby,'' his father informed him. Just like he had when he was about to leave. Just like he had when he had gotten into the university. Just like he had several times before this, whenever the feeling struck him. Antony gave a little chuckle that he knew his father expected. ''Yes, it's good to be back,'' Anton said, letting the word ''home'' die on his lips. His father would have surely struck up a whole tirade about how Nigeria was his home if he had said something as stupid as that. His father guffawed for a bit on the other side of the phone. Soon enough, that died down and Antony searched for words that could send his father off. Before he could think of something to say, though, his father broke in on his thoughts. ''Antony, the university has called me.'' Shit. ''There are classes going on now and you are spending your time talking to me.'' ''Well, you called me.'' Bad move. Antony knew it was a bad move. He rolled his eyes preemptively. ''Faugh!'' his father spat from his end. Antony pulled the phone away from his ear. ''Do not be a… a… a smart-aleck with me! Why are you not at the university?'' ''Because… I don't know,'' Antony offered feebly. What the hell was wrong with the university, anyway? Why did they insist on torturing him by calling his father? He hadn't realized he was ten years old again for his parents to be called at every turn. There was no way he could be wanted that badly as to warrant whatever detective bullshit they were pulling. ''You don't know?'' His father laughed contemptuously. ''You don't know. Listen, Antony, I am paying money for you to go to school! Not for you to sit around like some… some layabout!'' ''I'm not being a layabout!'' Antony countered heatedly. His mouth hung open and he searched for something else to say, but really, he was being a layabout. His father didn't need to know that, though. ''So why are you not in the university right now? What are you doing? Are you doing hard labor in the apartment I have paid for?'' ''Stop it,'' Antony said sullenly. He pulled himself out of his seat, beginning to pace a bit as he cradled his chin in his free hand. ''You are accusing me of—'' ''Not going to school,'' his father finished. ''Why should I!?'' Antony blurted. ''What will they do for me? Make me take classes I do not want to take, that is what. I can just read and—'' ''And do what? My friend—'' Antony's father was fond of saying ''my friend'' rapidly when he was beginning an admonishment. ''—do not talk like I am a… a… a fool! You will enroll, do you understand me? You will go to the university and issue them a... an apology!'' ''What have they done for me!?'' ''They have accepted you!'' His father made a disgusted click of the tongue. ''Listen, Antony, I am not… discussing this with you. This is not a discussion! You enroll in the university and I do not want to receive another call like this again!'' ''Father, I—'' ''My friend, do you understand me? I will not tolerate this!'' Antony knew he was defeated. His father was a powerful man in character and influence. He knew that his father was concerned for him, but perhaps more evident was the fact that his father was concerned for himself. If his son didn't go to school, Nigeria's former ambassador to France might lose face. ''… alright, father. I will go to the university. I will attend.'' Antony didn't need to make his voice sound dejected. He did not like to be defeated like this, pushed into lying. He didn't like lying to his father when it was something serious like this, but he was cornered. He had to say something to appease the man. He had to get him off his back. He hated himself for it. As he said his goodbyes and hung the phone up, he vowed solemnly never to do it again. Even if it got him killed, he wouldn't be pushed like that again. He would stand his ground. Marseilles. 2000. Leopold 'Jackal' Eifler threw a rapid kick to Anton's side, knocking him backwards into the cage. Eifler moved forward, raising black gloved fists. The man in the blood-red trunks and wearing his his blond hair in a neat buzzcut was moving in for the kill. He was known as a striker in Franco-German Fights, one of the best that the organization had known in recent years. Of course, there was a reason that he was the group's Light Heavyweight champion. The man's girlfriend held onto the belt outside the cage, watching her man corner Anton and begin to initiate the finish. Anton had trained intensely to become what he was now, to get to the place he was. He was stronger than he had been two years ago, faster, more focused. He was a true fighter. Yet, he knew that some part of all this training was due to an intense desire to never be thrown into a situation he didn't like, to be able to fight his way out whenever he needed. To avoid being in a place where the only option he had was bad. To escape being cornered. And here he was, once again, cornered. Leo Eifler took a sharp step forward. He had a mad glare as he clenched his fist tightly, ready to pound Anton's face into putty. Anton grit his teeth. The arm flung out and Anton drifted to the side. His mind whirled into overdrive. Slam. His fist shot into Eifler's stomach. The crowd roared in delight as Eifler buckled. Slam. A shotei into Eifler's chest. The Jackal reared backwards, unbalanced. His girlfriend, a pretty girl with blonde hair that had nearly the sheen of Eifler's own, watched with wide eyes. The crowd was rising up to their feet. Slam. A roundhouse heel kick to Eifler's temple. The Light Heavyweight champion was taken off his feet and collapsed to the mat. Anton pounced on Eiflers back and began to drill him in the back of the head with punches and forearm drops. Soon enough, he felt himself being pulled away and the bell being rung. There was a new champion. In the space of six seconds, the match had swung around full-circle. He had been cornered like a rat, yet he had come out roaring like a lion. His vow had been upheld. He felt more satisfaction from that than he did from watching the belt be ripped away from Leo's girlfriend—she put up an outstanding fight—and handed to him. Los Angeles. 2006. ''I can't.'' ''Why not?'' Amber planted her fists on her hips. She had only come because Anton had accompanied her, because he was there to protect her. And perhaps because Luis was so badly incapacitated that he couldn't rise an inch. Making his way down to stop the fight between Anton and Nneka had reversed all the progress he had made in the hospital. Somehow, she believed that somehow his spirit could reach out and choke her or some such silliness. ''Did you see him?'' Anton asked. ''Look at him. He can't… I can't let him lie like that.'' ''You can, but you just don't want to,'' Amber said angrily. She took a few steps towards him, pressing herself up against him and taking his face in her hands. ''Don't you see, this is your chance. You can be free of him. Nneka will watch over him.'' ''And how will Nneka pay for his support?'' Anton asked. That was also one of the things she had asked him to stop doing. ''Things will work themselves out,'' Amber told him. It was perhaps the falsest attempt at sincere concern that Anton had ever personally witnessed. Amber's smile was only wicked. ''No.'' Anton put his hands on her shoulders and pushed her away to create a little space between them. She couldn't trick him. He knew she wanted to see Luis expire, wanted to see him cold and being lowered into the earth. She wanted to see the day when Anton would be hers and hers alone. ''Anton, the hospital won't let him die,'' Amber said. ''You can be sure of that. They'll do whatever they can to save him, even if it is without money. Someone will pay it off. He'll be fine. We should go somewhere, go to New York, something. Leave here while Luis isn't tying you down.'' ''No, Amber,'' Anton repeated. Amber frowned at him and turned around, facing the wall. ''What if… what if I leave you, then?'' Amber asked sullenly. She lifted her head, letting her hair hang freely over her back. ''What about that? What if I leave you here alone, skip off to New York myself where you'll never find me again. What if it's me or Luis?'' Cornered. An alarm rang in his ears, the sound of it blinding him for a bit. He closed his eyes and took a deep breath. ''Then I will help you pack,'' Anton told her solemnly. She turned around, shocked. He opened his eyes to see the woman gaping at him. He set his jaw. ''I am not leaving him to die. So if that is your decision, then I will see you off as best I can. I cannot stop you.'' Amber blinked a few times, her incredulity never fading. ''Oh no, boy, you're not going to get rid of me that easily,'' she said finally, regaining her composure. She moved into him, pressing her finger into his chest. He didn't move, simply looking down at her. ''You're not going to send me off like that. I was just testing you. You picked a stupid choice, but…'' She frowned at him. ''Well, you're sleeping on the couch at the very least. And I won't visit you out there, either. How do you feel about that?'' In truth, Anton felt giddy inside. Cornered, always cornered, but always standing his ground. Always keeping his vow. This time, for once, he hadn't lost anything for it. But what about the next time? He couldn't continue a run of luck like this. Sometime he would have to lose. Anton was oddly at peace with that.
