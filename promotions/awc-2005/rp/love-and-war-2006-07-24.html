Love and War

24 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-24 To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven: A time to be born and a time to die; A time to plant, and a time to luck up that which is planted; A time to kill and a time to heal; A time to break down and a time to build up; A time to weep and a time to laugh; A time to mourn and a time to dance; A time to cast away stones and a time to gather; A time to embrace, and a time to refrain from embracing; A time to get, and a time to lose; A time to keep, and a time to cast away; A time to rend and a time to sew; A time to keep silence and a time to speak; A time to love, and a time to hate; A time of war and a time of peace.Ecclesiastes 3:1-8, KJV, Holy Bible For some people, they were words to a song from a decade most of us never lived. At the time they seemed fitting, sometimes comforting to a nation, or possibly a world forced to adapt to a changing society. Being one that did not live through that era, I cannot put forth suitable words that would bring to life the emotional turmoil and confusion suffered by those people that did survive. For Teresa Jane Tomas, that passage of scripture was simply that; words in a holy book her mother read daily. The words within, she lived by and struggled to teach her children the same. She even had the eight verses posted on her refrigerator so anyone that approached would be reminded everything has a purpose. Teresa simply didn’t believe that. ----------------------------------------- It was thirty years ago. American soldiers were coming home from the controversial war called Vietnam. One of those soldiers was Teresa’s father, Private Steven Dwayne Tomas. There were no parades or welcome home ceremonies waiting these young men as there were following WWII. Instead, they came home unwanted and hated. The airport was practically empty the afternoon of April 1975. A young Edna Tomas waited patiently as her husband stepped off the plane and entered the terminal. Dressed in a pair of bell-bottom jeans and a baseball tee, she wondered if Steven still looked the same. Two long hours had passed and a small group of soldiers passed through the door. Edna stood as she watched the young men descend from the plane. They all looked scared, tired, and broken despite their freshly pressed uniforms and clean shaven faces. When she spotted Steven, she did not hesitate to rush to him. It had been almost ten years since she felt his embrace. To feel him hold her once again would prove this day wasn’t a dream. When their bodies met, Steven held her tight. She held tighter soaking the chest of his uniform in her tears. Private Tomas remained alert as his wife clung to him. They could take the soldier from the war, but couldn’t take the war away from the soldier. -------------------- An empty pack of Winston Lights was carelessly thrown across the bed of a Moscow hotel room. Teresa sat cross-legged finishing the last cigarette and drowning the nicotine with Jack Daniel’s Whiskey. Many thoughts traced through her troubled mind; Eric fighting in Iraq, Mama loosing her ability to think on her own, and the one man that holds her heart has left again with it beating in his grasp. Another issue had only added to the headache that had already begun to form; Tiara Belle Russell. The words spoken from Tiara Russell two weeks past still haunted Teresa. She had told Teresa her father isn’t the man she knew him to be. What the hell did she mean by that? What did she do to the woman to make her hate her so much? The longer her mind lingered on these questions, more she thought of the man she always known as her father. Teresa knew very little of the man. Despite Mama’s objections, Steven Tomas enlisted into the U.S. Navy when Eric was only a babe. He had died when she was 13. When he was home, she remembered him as always anxious, nervous, and short tempered. He had never laid a hand on Mama or his children. Teresa figured it was the reason he hardly was home. Mama Tomas always told her he died on his ship, but Teresa never recalled seeing any medals or recognition of any honors. It was after Captain Tomas’s death that Mama took her and Eric to Tennessee and the Tomas Farm. There was only one way Teresa knew to find some answers. It was a long shot, but she had to try. Leaving the bottle of whiskey on a side table, she slid off the hotel bed and rummaged through her luggage until she found her cell phone. The phone rang twice. A female voice answered that wasn’t Mama. “Hello?” The voice was soft. Teresa was about to ask questions, but remembered the woman was the nurse Eric hired to look after Mama. “Yes, this is Teresa. I would like to talk to Mama please.” Teresa winced. She hated the fact that a stranger answered her phone, the same stranger was living in her home, watching her T.V., eating her food, bathing in her tub, sleeping in her bed and basically taking over. Soon, they’ll be calling Mrs. Tomas their Mama and Teresa would be a page in the old woman’s past that she’ll soon forget. Damn you Eric! “Of course. Mama is just outside in her flower garden. She is such a sweet person Miss Tomas. The children have taken to her as if she was their own grandmother.” The woman reminded Teresa of one of those better-than-thou hypocrites that would visit Mama and take her to church on Sunday mornings. What were worse than their self-righteous attitudes were their fake-nice voices. Before Teresa could say anything else to the woman, Mama’s voice came through the line. “Teresa?” “Mama, how are you doing?” Teresa grabbed her bottle of booze as she fought the tears swelling in her eyes. “Oh, Honey, I’m doing just fine. The family you and Eric sent me is really nice. The two little ones remind me so much of you and Eric.” Silence came between mother and daughter, but only for a moment. Teresa could hear the father talking to his wife, what of, she couldn’t tell. A children’s program was playing over the television in the background. “Teresa, are you ok dear?” “Yes Mama. Mama, can I ask you something?” Teresa was hesitant but she had to ask. If she didn’t, she wouldn’t be able to sleep that night. “Why of course. What is it?” “It’s about Daddy. Umm… Did he really die on his ship?” “What ship?” A lump was forced down Teresa’s esophagus. What Ship? Please, not now. All she wanted was some answers and the poor woman starts memory lapsing. Teresa took a long swig of courage in a bottle and pressed the matter further. “Mama, you told me Daddy died on his ship. He was a Navy Captain. Remember?” Frustration and confusion settled in the old woman’s voice. Teresa instantly regretted making the phone call, but it was too late. “Teresa Hon, your father was never in the Navy. Where did you get such a crazy idea like that?” “Then how did Daddy die?” This was another question Teresa regretted to ask and soon realized this conversation was a waste of time. “I told you Teresa! He died on his ship. How many times do I have to tell you that? Please, your father was a good honorable man. Don’t question anything about him again.” Teresa placed a hand on her face. It was soaked with her silent tears. She heard the phone exchange hands and the woman’s voice returned to the line. “Your mother needs to rest. You’ve made her upset. I’m sorry.” The line went dead. The bitch cut me off. My mother is loosing her mind. Eric is fighting a war. Scott ran off again.. Teresa flipped the cell phone shut and threw it across the room. It bounced off the wall and fell onto the floor in three pieces. “DAMN YOU GOD!! If you even exist. What else are you gonna take from me?!”
