I Love You, pt. 1: She's Dying

2 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/4/06 The days were empty, to say the least. Tim Martin traversed, if I want to try and sound smarter, I say traversed, so he traversed down the road, lonely, looking at his feet as they stepped and stepped and stepped, each one slapping the ground and leaving his only invisible footprint with the microscopic dust that was laid across the concrete. Each step left a microscopic print that only he knew about, his own little secret to distract him from the current state of depression he had sunk in. So he stepped and stepped and stepped, creating a name for himself in the world of the invisible. The child of his life, the son of S-Laughter, was now gone. Tim and Liam left the child on the step of an orphanage, for you lazy fuckers who didn’t read the last one there was a little summary for you, and Tim and Liam turned their back on him. While they knew it was the best thing to do, they both slipped into depression. While Liam was swallowed in the TV all day long, shows like Family Feud and Passions constantly keeping his attention, Tim could only traverse, me trying to sound smart again, and think about the child that could never be. He would walk around the city, staring at his feet as he bumped and humped through the crowd. Often, someone would collide right into him, provoking an “oomph” or some other kind of sound a person makes when they hit a much bigger object. Sometimes you could hear some type of verbal feedback, such as “Watch where the fuck you’re going, you stupid son of a bitch!”, but usually it was just Tim, lost in the silence. Today was like all the other days he would traverse, me trying to sound smart again, with Tim just walking with no mindset. The only real thing he needed to do while he was out was buy cigarettes, but that’s nothing. It’s funny how it’s something he needs. The cause of the addiction becomes overemphasized and cigarettes become a need. But that’s the funny thing about a depression, that the littlest thing makes the biggest different. One puff of a Marlboro red can make you that much more relaxed, can keep your mind that much more on track. It can make you say to yourself “Well, I guess it was all for the good” rather than “FUCK WHY THE FUCK DID WE LET OUR SON FUCKING GO NO!”, which is really a bigger difference than you could possibly imagine, though I imagine that if you imagine that those two aren’t that different you need a better imagination, imagine that? Eventually, Tim memorized the sidewalks as he traversed, me trying to sound fucking smart again. He could tell where he was. Parker Street was usually very dirty, like trash and shit all over the place; you could tell that the place was ignored, one of those neighborhoods where the neighbors probably weren’t all that neighborly at all, which really isn’t a neighborly way to act in a neighborhood. Long Avenue had a distinct pattern of cracks and crevices: a long, thin crack, a short thin crevice, a long, thin crack, a short thin crevice, a long, thin crack, a short thin crevice, a long, thin crack, a short thin crevice, a long, thin crack, a short thin crevice, a long, thin crack, a short thin crevice, and so on until you finally made it to Smith Road, which was just straight concrete with granite curbs, almost beautiful in the sense of how simple it was. All these things made walking for Tim easy, as he didn’t have to look up while he traversed, me trying to look smart, and could find his way around. So now when Tim noticed the sidewalk was speckled as it was a dirty type of concrete, he knew he was traversing, me trying to look smart, on Wyland Way, which was the home to the local 7-11, which is only local if you actually live close to it. Tim didn’t really live close to it, but it was local to him at the moment, so he gave it that homely touch by mentally describing it as local as he made the microscopic imprints in the microscopic dirt with every step he took. When he finally approached the 7-11, Tim looked up. You see, a place like a 7-11 in a city like Boston is not the type of thing you want to walk into with your head down. It’s like trying to see if a gun is loaded or not by looking down the barrel—probably not smart. So even if you are in the most foul of fucking moods, you need to look the fuck up and look the fuck around. You need to make sure that the man behind the register will be able to handle his shit if he gets held up. You need to make sure that the Larrys in front and behind you aren’t going to blow your fucking head off. Your fucking Mother could’ve been raped by Adolf Hitler and you still have no excuse for walking into a place like a 7-11 in a city like Boston. I guess if you went to one of the more “public” place, like near the train stations and actually dead in the center of Boston, where all the tourists go and the people who like to go into the city so they can say “YEAH I’VE BEEN THERE IT’S AWESOME” and don’t look at Boston in the whole, as in all the other cities that surround it, the shit holes. So when you walk into a place like 7-11 in a place like the non-touristy parts of Boston, keep your fucking head up. While Tim’s head was up, he noticed the fucking place was empty. Not one fucking person was inside. As this fact seems insignificant at first, as there are many times that a store can be absolutely empty and more often than not it just means no one is in there, it was odd that there were so many people out front. Gang bangers, drug dealers, all that bullshit, they choose the fucking 7-11. This thought process, of how they fucking choose the 7-11, fascinated Tim to a point where it sufficiently distracted him enough to not think about all those things he was thinking about as he traversed, me trying to sound fucking smart again and again and again. I mean, there are a million places for these types of low lifes to “hang out”, and yet they choose a fucking convenience store. When Tim approaches the counter, he feels it necessary to address this. “Pack of Marlboros.” The cashier grabs them. “However much cigarettes cost, Sam L. doesn’t really smoke cigarettes at all so he can’t accurately display a price, which is the only way he would want to write this dialogue.” Tim hands him however much it happened to be. “Hey, why the fuck do you let those hoodlums stay out front?” The cashier shrugs and goes back to looking not amused. “Don’t you know they’re selling drugs and shit?” He shrugs, and is subsequently not amused. Shaking his head, Tim grabs the cigarettes and walks out the door. Not on purpose, he gives one of the hoodlums the Eye. Not the stinky eye, and not really the evil eye either. It’s more like a “I don’t really approve of you being here, but you’re of no real importance of me and the possibility of me never seeing you again is greater than it is not, so hopefully by doing this facial gesture you will reconsider your social values and change your lifestyle, attempting to do something else as I am in no mood to try to get you to change yourself” type of look. That type of look, though, doesn’t serve its purpose, but it does get the attention of the hoodlum. “Man, the fuck are you?” Tim stops. Violence has gotten him nowhere recently. In fact, it has only broken his heart and disrupted all his beliefs. But there is something about hoodlums, something about drug dealers, that just really gets his fucking shit. Something about hardwork contrasting just standing on the corner or hanging out while you sell drugs really digs at Tim in a bad fucking way. You always hear about the struggle and the streets and all that, but so much of it is bullshit. Sometimes drugs do seem like the only fucking way, but traverse, me trying to sound smart and it become awkward in the sentence, into the places where you’re gaining your money from. See the lives you’ve distorted. See the families you’ve torn apart. See the human nature of it all being sucked away as you sling your o, dro, and if you’re pimping, bitches and hoes. So, yeah, Tim has a real fucking problem with the hoodlums. So Tim turns with a face more serious than mother fucking Serious Sam. “I should be asking you the same question. What the fuck are you doing here, selling drugs?” “Man, you keep talking like that, and you won’t be talking.” “Why the fuck here? Why not the fuck in the alleyways or any other place, away from where my loved ones could be?” “Man, it’s all the same, every day. Why change?” “What the fuck do you mean?” “Why change where I’m at? Shit ain’t gonna change. My poverty ain’t gonna change. My sorrow ain’t gonna change. And every day, if it’s the same thing, shit won’t change.” Wow. Deep shit. Tim was a little taken back. “Then why don’t you break out of it?” “I’m too far in, man. I gotta stay true.” And the hoodlum turned, knowing his shit was spoken in good form, displaying an alarming sense of alertness for a rat like himself. In return, Tim turned too. But that’s the thing about his shit now, is that that kind of stuff didn’t really leave an effect or make a difference. It just kind of…went with shit, I guess. You see, Tim is just like that fucking kid in that he’s been in it too long. He thought that walking could clear his head, but rather it just made shit more complicated. So now there he was, stuck in his own rut, with everything repeating. And when shit is the same, over and over, it’ll never fucking change. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ “Name something that you could find in a woman’s purse.” “Condoms!” And the laughter. The crowd always dies for that type of shit, the outrageous, ZANNNNNYYYY answers, the things you would never say because you’re watching Family Feud at 3 O’Clock on UPN or WB or whatever second-rate channel it happens to be on. Maybe it’s on syndicate NBC, but NBC is going down the shitter anyways. Then again, so is most of that type of programming. But that’s not what I’m here to rant about. It’s the laughter at the stupid stuff that any woman would actually say if she had the ovaries to fucking say it. And, no, I don’t mean “balls”, because there is no reason to masculinize bravery or guts, as it were, when it’s not necessary. I can just as easily say ovaries and hopefully have it have the same meaning. As the crowd in the studio show laughs and that stupid guy from “Home Improvement” who is the host of Family Feud tries to be funny with some type of smarmy remark in an improvising sort of way, Liam sits in the chair, dead of emotion. He stares blankly, blinking sporadically and not even moving a hair. His eyes are sullen with sadness, a sadness that is less active than Tim’s. You see, Liam’s sadness has brought him to a point of sloth, a point of no activity. Liam is kind of like a blob, now, except this blob is unreasonably fit and could probably break your jaw with one punch. But this blob is more blob than anything else as he watches condoms inevitably draw up the “X” on the board. Stupid bitch, doesn’t she know that the Family Feud people don’t survey whores? But that’s not important, because the blob is a blob. This is a man that used to draw fear just by fucking walk. By moving his legs, your could would run away and hide because he was more of a man than you, and more likely than not could beast your fucking ass in a second. This is a man that throws men his size around like rag dolls, using superior strength and fortitude to take them down. Now, though, he is just a blob. This blob’s sadness is like his overactive brother’s in the sense that he truly misses that child. He never wanted to let it go, he never wanted to say good by, but it had to be done. And now he wallows (I think I already used this word earlier in the piece. That annoys the fuck out of me, because it makes me feel like I’m just repeating words that sound good. It could turn out that I didn’t actually use it at all. Should I check? Nah, fuck it, if I used it, oh well) in the thoughts of what could’ve been, what could be, and all that. Sad shit, really, how letting go of something he held for not that long could take so much out of him, really rip the fucking life force out of his body with one hard tug. The blob’s only train of thought is not anything that has to do with the fact that the Hermanson family won the 10,000 or however much it was, so small a number that each family member is going to receive, like, $3 from it, but rather the child and what could’ve been. S-Laughter, he said raise him, he said make him the young man, the young proud man, but the blob couldn’t even do THAT right. He killed an angel and gave away that child, banishing him to who the fuck knows. But that doesn’t matter. Dr. Phil is on. Shit can’t get any worse. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ The door creaked open, much later than the previous scene occurred, the darkness creeping from outside the windows into the house indicating the true time. Tim peaked in see that none of the living room lights were on. Fucking weird, he though, Liam is still usually watching TV by the time I return. Instead, Liam’s room light is on, and Liam’s voice can be heard over the air. Tim is no intruder, though, so he turns on the living room light. What a fucking pig sty. Empty soda cans, fucking chip crumbs, wrappers from food Liam started on and never really finished. I guess he was gaining a little weight, but no way was Liam turning into an actual pig! This shit was getting gross. “Liam, you need to come clean the fucking living room!” It was like he was speaking to a 12 year old or something, and staying true to that stereotype, no response was offered by the brother of Timothy Martin. Instead, the same voice continued to float softly over the air, sounding like Liam was on the phone with someone. Who was unknown, but it was someone. But Tim was no intruder, so he left the 12 year old to speak. As he waited for Liam to finish up with whatever he was doing, Tim walked into the kitchen and turned THOSE lights on too. It was like the place was a fucking…I don’t know what. I guess it seemed like they were those type of people who left all their lights on at night, maybe in order to simulate a daytime atmosphere or something, I don’t know really. But it’s fucking weird to leave all your lights on. Anyways, Tim turned the kitchen lights on, making the Martins look like fucking weirdos. Instead of fixing that horrible situation, he moved over to the fridge and pulled out a beer. Keystone Light… “Are we fucking poor? Why the fuck do we have Keystone Light?” Again, though, Tim was ignored as he called out for something. Tim wasn’t one to intrude, though, so he sat down and cracked open the beer, taking a sip. Fucking gross, Keystone Light is fucking piss water, the shit is absolutely gross. Nothing can really ruin a mood like Keystone Light. So instead of trying to wind down from a day or sorrow, he instead gets wound up by this sorry excuse for beer. Fucking grosssss, man. “Ok, thanks, bye.” The phone could be heard being place back on the thing where you put the phone, followed by the rustling of the blob as he got off the bed. Slowly, the door crept open and there was Liam, in all his glory. Yeah, he gained a little bit of fat on his previously spotless body, but it wasn’t that we’re now currently noticing, but rather the fact that those white eyes of his were now red and distraught, as if he had just been bawling them the fuck out. Tim placed his beer down softly and got up to go comfort his brother. “Liam, what’s the matter? Is it the child?” “No…Mandy…she’s fucking dying…” “Mandy…Mandy Hardings?” ~~~~~~~~~~~~ There were the Bizwrite Chronicles, the stories about the woman Tim wanted to pursue even though she was dating a black man, but this was different. Mandy Hardings was the love of Liam’s life before Liam became too involved. Too involved with work, too involved with religion. “But Mandy…” “But nothing, Liam! While you’re leading your ‘Eagles’ or whatever the fuck we are…” “Mandy…” “I’m just here, waiting for you! You know I love you, Liam, why can’t you accept that?” “I do, but…” “Liam, I’m leaving you…” “Don’t!” “But you broke my heart. I don’t think…no, I know I can never love another man like you…” “And I can never love another woman like you…” “You never loved me! Not how I loved you!” And that was their final relationship as a couple. But from then on (that was about 5 or 6 years ago), Liam has maintained correspondence and friendship. She’s living in Portsmouth, New Hampshire, and ever once in a while she’ll come down to Boston to visit or Liam will go there. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ “Oh God, what?” “AIDs.” “AIDs? How?” “She was pretty hardcore into drugs after she left me, man, and now…now they have her…” And he cried, cried like a baby as he dug his face into my shoulder. There I was, so selfish in my own problems that I can’t even consider this. I mean, really, this came out of left field…but it was good, y’know? “You can still see her…” “First the baby and now this!...” I mean, it’s horrible that she’s dying…but it’s like something new. A change. For me, here I am with a brother who isn’t concerned with a child so much anymore but is rather enveloped in something else. Another death. And the fucking cycle continues. “This isn’t your fault, man…” “I know, but…I loved her…” And I knew it, too. I knew my brother fucking loved her, and she loved him. But it couldn’t work out. And now, the only thing he loved other than me was fucking getting killed. Sure, this came out of fucking left field for sure, but that’s how life is sometimes. You’re just sitting around and you get news like this. It’s not planned, it’s not expected, but it’s fucking devastating. And you have to adapt, you have to drop whatever you’re doing to appeal to it. “Then go out and see her.” He paused on my shoulder, pulling away and looking at me. “Really?” “Yeah man. It’ll get your mind off…you know…and it’ll give me some time to think about some shit here. It’ll be good for the both of us.” “Ok, man, thanks. I’ll call her tomorrow.” “No. Call her tonight.” I liked Mandy. Really, I did. I can’t say I still do now, I never see her, but when she was around, I sure did like her. But that doesn’t mean I’m not trying to shove Liam out the door. I need a change, I need a life sans-Liam for a while, something to break it all up. And if he can find some kind of difference, maybe I can. “Allright…I’ll call her right now.” ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ *RINNNNNNNNG. RINNNNNNNNNNNG.* “…Hello?” “Mandy?” “Yes, who’s this?” “It’s Liam. Liam Martin.” “Liam Martin, like I don’t know who you are, how are you doing, it’s been weeks since I’ve heard from you!” “I just heard…” Silence. “I want to see you, Mandy.” “Liam, I don’t think…” “No, Mandy, I need to see you.” Silence. “Liam, please…” “When can I come up?” “No, Liam…” “That’s it, then, I’m coming up now.” “Liam, I…” “Please, I know what you’re thinking. You’re thinking that I’ll think less of you because you might look decrepit…” “No! Who the fuck do you…” “Don’t try and big up yourself, please…just let me come.” Silence. “Well, if you are going to come, come soon. We have some talking to do.” “Yeah.” Silence. “How’s your brother?” “He’s good, he’d good.” “He going to handle the construction company while you’re gone?” “Tim pretty much does it by himself anyways. I mostly work for him.” “Yeah, Tim was always pretty bright.” “Yeah.” Silence. “Mandy…” “I know, Liam. We’ll talk about it when you get here. When will you be leaving?” “Tomorrow afternoon, I guess.” “No, tomorrow’s not good for me. I have a session.” “A session?” “Yeah…don’t ask…it’s about…” “Ok…I got you. Then how about the next day?” “The next day sounds lovely.” “I might stay for a while.” “No, I can’t afford that…” “I have money. I want to help you.” “Just come visit, Liam!” “Ok…we’ll talk about it when I get up there.” “Ok…bye Liam.” “Bye Mandy.” *Click* ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ As I heard the conversation unfold, I could almost see the glimmer in Liam’s eye continually be cast and defused as Mandy constantly set off and defused Liam’s heart. Liam needed to get away as much as I needed him to get away as well. “I’m leaving tomorrow.” I just nodded. And I knew he wasn’t going to be back for a while, and I accepted it. And I feared it. I feared the monotony that haunted me after we gave away the child, I feared everything that was going to come. But I wasn’t going to let my selfish way get in front of Liam. Instead, I had to find my own thing to keep my sanity. I’ll need to fill the days, to say the least.
