Step off the plane, I'm walking down your street again...

5 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: The past week Stepping off the plane and touching the asphalt, he felt a sensation go through his body. He looked back over his shoulder at the metal steps leading up to the plane. Around him, tourists are taking in the view. They are embarking on a two week trip around the Atlantic coast of America. For one man on the plane, he is saying goodbye to his old life. For him, he is walking towards what can save not only his lively hood but also the well being of his extended family and future generations. Michael Fitzpatrick-McCarthy crossed the asphalt of the LaGuardia Airport taking in the American sky, feeling the American wind, breathing in the American air. Essentially it was all the same as back home. He was looking up in the direction. The wind brushed through his beard in the same manner as it did at home. The oxygen kept him alive just the same. But it just felt... different. He looked around, taking it in. Some of the tourists brushed past the tall burly man and as he tentatively walked into the terminal building. Standing at the baggage claim conveyor belt, he stared a hole in a piece of card he flicked between his fingers. He tossed it around and around. Studying it as though it was ready to change and morph into something greater, something he was afraid of missing. The card has a name and a phone number. The only bits of information he had on the Atlantic Wrestling Club. AWC as it liked to be called was in the midst of an Irish invasion and Michael was and is the latest arrival to the Irish army. The card flipped around again and he read the phone number once more. He knew it by memory. He was able to quote the number if anyone had of asked for it. But he felt some peace with holding the card. The card was his sanctity. The card told Michael all he need to know. The small white card was the assurance to Michael that he made the right decision. Rather than taking over the farm from his father next year, Michael decided to give to his father for the next few years. Mickey Fitz had a plan to safe guard his future. He had a plan to make his millions and then quit. He had a plan and just like Daniel Craig in Layer Cake, he was going to stick to it, right up to the point of being shot. Hell, if he met Siena Miller along the way, it wouldn’t be a bad thing… Would it? Michael Fitzpatrick-McCarthy got the AWC job by knowing AAS. Alistair Sheridan’s father was good friends with the Fitzpatrick-McCarthy and Mickey got the trial wit AWC through them. The card continued to be revolved in his fingers. He was making his debut on Wednesday night. Things will be fine he thought as he stood there. Payment would follow after a televised match and hotels were being paid for until Mickey could settle himself. The conveyor belt alarm went off and the mechanics clicked begrudgingly into motion. Immediately a swarm of people swelled towards the mouth of the belt, eagerly wishing to leave the airport and move forward. Michael was hesitant. He stood back, eyeing the card but aware the conveyor belt was working. He looked around. The teenage kids of some families were doing the running while the parents guarded the luggage trolleys. “Shiite!” thought Mickey. He needed a luggage trolley. Strolling cautiously towards the trolley, no one thought anything of the big man. His red hair and matching thick beard were stand outs but in the buzzing metropolis of New York he was a needle in a haystack. More and more bags slid past our Irish traveller as he stood, left arm resting on the trolley, left hand twirling a single piece of card. He didn’t know why he was twirling it. Maybe out of a sense of fear. He was scared that if he put it in his pocket, he would never find it again, only to realise any hopes of becoming a star and saving his family were gone. Mickey Fitz-McCarthy looked at the card and then noticed his bags sliding towards his direction. He looked around. He was the only person in the conveyor belt area. And his bags were the only bags left. How long had he been standing thinking of home, thinking of saving it? He reached out and in his broad arms scoped up his two bags and placed them roughly on the trolley. He looked up at the signs and eyeballed arrivals. Slowly, he began to push the trolley as he proceeded towards the arrivals gate and his lift to Solarized. He stopped. This was it. There would be no turning back from this point. This was the big time. This would mean some kind of celebrity status, especially at home. This was his last chance to back out. To never appear. Mickey looked over his shoulder. He knew that literally, he couldn’t get home this way, but his actions were more of a metaphor. He looked around again and studied the exit door, knowing that the AWC staff member was the other side of the door. He paused for another second. Wrestling… His new occupation went through his mind. The video that he was shown by his family before leaving Ireland ran through his mind. The bumps. The falls. The scars. The blood. The injuries. The drops. The commitment. The possible permanent damage… And there it hit him. The magnitude of what he was about to do, hit him straight between the eyes. One false step and he would pay for it for the rest of his life. He wanted to have his kids. He wanted to get married first, but he wanted kids. Mickey Fitz thought again, picturing a golden sunshine shining down on his farm as he played with his children. He wanted that. And with wrestling… He wasn’t guaranteed it. But without wrestling… He wasn’t guaranteed a farm. There he stood a loner Irish man in a New York airport. A few of the staff had noticed him and where wondering what he was doing. Into his pocket, Mickey rummaged again. He produced two pieces of paper. The name and phone number to make sure he arrived okay, and a photograph. A small photograph of three people. His father. His mother. Him. … I can’t describe his emotion. He has left home for the first time, guess his emotions. … A single tear fell slowly down the cheek of The Farmer as he was soon to be called. He studied the picture and looked “over his shoulder” once again. Wiping his cheek, Mickey folded the photograph and put the two pieces of paper back in his trousers. He looked around, down at the suitcases and then at the exit. He is here, he realises. He is ready, he thinks. He can do this. Michael Fitzpatrick-McCarthy took a step forward and headed towards the exit out of the arrivals area. He had no expectations of who he would meet. He was told that someone would meet him at the arrivals gate. Well it had two ideas. Either a really attractive blonde model. Or a huge wrestler. They are the only two types of people involved in sports-entertainment. Right? Stepping though the sliding door, Mickey looked around trying to spot his blonde beauty here to pick him up, but instead he was greeted by George Cassidy, the commentator for AWC. Cassidy smiled and approached the large Irish brawler. “Good morning, Mr. McCarthy,” calmly greeted Cassidy as Mickey approached him. The Farmer was more interested in trying to spot a blonde beauty hiding in the wings, ready to pounce out and be his other taking him to the arena. Mickey felt slight dejected when it dawned on him, no blonde girl was scheduled to appear, but still he felt upbeat. The wrestling world was soon to meet the farmer. “Top o’ the morn t’ye” smiled Mickey as he shook George Cassidy’s hand. The Farmer has joined AWC. Lock up your sheep.
