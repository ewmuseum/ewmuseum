sometimes i don't mind

31 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10.31.06 Onward, we press, to our great nation's capital! Yes, yes, I know. Washington D.C. Is the capital of the United States, not Spokane, Washington. I was perfectly aware of this. Most Americans with at least some degree of education are aware of this, as well. The geniuses at Delta Airlines, however, are not. I can't really say I can blame them. Two reasons for this: One, the top-notch squad barely making minimum wage protecting us all from those dastardly terrorists probably had a great deal of difficulty with their schooling, or else they would have chosen a more lucrative and far less mind-numbing profession. And two (and perhaps this is why they had so much trouble in school), the Powers That Be In Charge of Naming Things elected to make it easy on geography and alleviate confusion by naming as many things as possible after Mr. George Washington. Our first president's name can be found just about everywhere; the dear general has his own city, his own state, his own bridges...you name it. America's first commander-in-chief even has his own street named after him in nearly every town numbering more than ten thousand souls. You can't spin your arms in America without smacking them into something named Washington. Yes, we sure do love to immortalize our founding fathers a great deal, but Honest George easily trumps them all. Still, this was a mistake that would easily cost me several hundred dollars. And if a potential four-figure blow to my wallet wasn't enough, this egregious error would leave me stranded on the exact opposite side of the country from where I'd intended. An extra day that I could have spent resting up in a Spokane suite fit for a prince would now have to be used on another trans-continental flight that hopefully THIS time could get me to where I'd like to go, if the fates and the geniuses in charge of such things were gracious enough to make it happen. Okay, so maybe I'm getting a little riled up now. But the alternative would see my face turning a remarkable shade of red as I verbally ripped a new asshole for the poor guy standing behind the Delta counter. For reasons which I'm about to disclose, I just couldn't muster enough anger out of myself to go through with it. And really, if I'd bothered at any point to read the printed destination on my ticket, I probably could have avoided this predicament altogether. And maybe that's just it – maybe, deep down inside, I was hoping to get caught in such a quandary. You see, Washington D.C. has the unique feature, offered to both citizens and visitors alike, of reducing the I.Q. of an individual who spends too much time within the city's confines to that of Jell-O brand chocolate pudding. This can best be seen by the example of every budding politician who campaigns mightily that they will boldly travel from their grassroots homes to Washington to invoke some change into the dismal political climate. Those same politicians, now representatives endowed with the power and responsibility to serve their constituents, can be found three weeks later drooling on themselves and having great difficulty putting on a sport coat without assistance from several aides. In fact, our dear President Bush has already set a record for most vacation days spent by a two-term president...and he still has two years left on his second term to go. But really, can you blame him? The man is clearly hurting for brain cells as it is – a fact that even the most diehard members of his political party can attest. It might look bad in the eyes of the general public, but I feel a lot safer if he's cutting timber in Crawford, Texas, when the alternative would undoubtedly lead to his picking up the red World War 3 phone and attempting to order a pizza. If anyone could stand to benefit from a little brain-numbing, however, it'd be yours truly. After all, I've got a lot on my mind these days. A part-time deal with AWC that was supposed to culminate with one last match against my wrestling arch-nemesis had blossomed into a full-time involvement with the club, one in which I'm expected to carry the banner by running myself rugged through double-duty shows and brutal title-defense gauntlets. And speaking of brutal...I still can't believe it. I actually gave Amy the ring. Christ...I'm gonna wake up one of these days and there's gonna be a Mrs. Markson next to me. And it's gonna....it's gonna be...Amy. Ahhhhh, who am I kidding...I'm not okay with this little D.C. detour because I'm in need of a little brain-numbing. I'm okay with it for one reason, and one reason alone. The chance to pay a visit on an old friend. *** Listen...you can do this, Darcy said to himself, doing his darndest to work up the nerve. It's not like she even has to- He practically leapt straight through the windshield when the MIDI version of Bon Jovi's “Bad Medicine” suddenly echoed through his vehicle's interior. The woman either could read minds from thousands of miles away...or she simply had impeccable timing. He briefly considered letting the call go unanswered as Mr. Jovi was about to sing the chorus. But he knew it was no use – when Amy Campbell wanted something, she had no problem whatsoever with dialing and re-dialing as long as it took until her intended party gave in and picked up....and he would be much better served taking the first one, if he was to have any hope in deflecting the worry bomb that was primed to detonate at the center of her cerebrum once she found out where he was. Christ...Darcy muttered under his breath before snapping open his phone, making the audio connection complete. “Yeah?” he answered, hoping that he came off like he sounded busy. ''Part of the whole engagement thing means that you shouldn't just run off on me. That, and my mom is driving me CRAZY with this whole wedding th--- wait a minute, are you doing that thing I do when I'm pretending to be occupied so whoever's on the phone leaves me alone?'' Damn! Darcy winced silently. He really should have known better than to use her own trick against her. Luckily, he had the rest of his life to figure out a more effective strategy... “Well those of us who DIDN'T get our asses beaten into sick leave still have to make the scheduled shows, sweetheart,” he said aloud for her, sounding a little too much like Harrison Ford toward the end than he meant to. “I don't know what to tell you about Mommy Dearest, but I can tell you that the OTHER part of the whole engagement thing is that you're not supposed to spend so much time tracking my movements like you're making an Animal Planet documentary.” ''Lookie here, pal - I'm alone in East Bay, bored as hell, the phone won't stop ringing, my leg is still healing because of someone I might currently be talking to and on top of it all? It seems that everyone thinks I'm pregnant because of the sudden engagement followed by my disappearance from TV, so spare me it's bad that I'm desperately seeking a little bit of your time,'' she sighed but sounded happy all the same, as opposed to her usual snarl, ''By the way, if I have no reason to be 'tracking' you... why does the call display show your phone as roaming in Washington?'' Darcy's eyes shot open, which he could only thank his lucky stars that Amy couldn't see...then again, if her phone could tell her where he was, it didn't seem a far cry to believe she had a camera planted on him somewhere. Suddenly it made sense that her surprise birthday gift for him – a brand new cellphone – had come at such a high price. I really should have asked more questions, he noted, realizing that somehow the service plan enabled her to know where he was as long as he had a working phone. He silently longed for the glory days before GPS, before cellphones...even before pagers, where people could actually get out and away without any Snooping Sallies getting too wise to their movements as he gave his reply. “The geniuses at the airlines apparently don't know the difference between Washington D.C. and Washington State. Basically I'm stuck here for awhile. If you want to give them a piece of your mind on my behalf, go right ahead...but I don't really have it in me to bitch out a guy making $6.50 an hour.” ''The plane magically got routed to a completely different part of the country, huh? Sounds fair, considering the fact that you don't know anyone who works for a politician...'' There was a brief pause. Of course, Darcy did know somebody who worked in Washington – the very person whose house he was currently parked outside of. And he knew that Amy knew, especially by how biting her remark had been. There was no sense lying, nor was there any in beating around the bush. “I do,” Darcy said, without a hint of emotion in his voice. “I'm sitting outside her house, in fact.” This time, there was a long pause on the other side of the phone, and finally a shaky voice spoke up with more truth, ''Darcy... I can't do this again. You're gone already. I honestly can't take this...'' “Look,” Darcy interjected immediately, hoping to inject some sense into this conversation. “I'm stuck here for tonight. They aren't sending any flights out to Washington state until like six in the morning. I'm just going in to say hi- I don't know anybody in this town, I want to see if she's doing okay, and if you have any faith in me in the slightest, you'll trust me.” ''And if the first words out of your mouth aren't 'Amy and I are engaged and I love her waaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaay more than I ever even thought I liked you', what am I allowed to do to your penis and/or house?'' Darcy smiled. She might be insane, but only because she loved him. Thinking of it in this manner often made the “pleasure” of defusing these sorts of situations a bit more bearable. “I'm going to try to think of way to say as much without sounding like I'm trying to piss her off,” he said. “But in the event that I don't, you should go with the cheese grater. I just cleaned it and everything.” ''You know that if you even dream of cheating on me in any way, shape or form... you'll die slowly, while I force you to gaze longingly at a nude sumo.'' she sighed, ''And please, call me every hour tonight... I don't think I can stand being alone with myself for too long knowing where you are. Also, I have her address... so if you were wondering, you literally have absolutely no room to breathe if you decide not to be nice about this whole thing.'' Darcy sighed. This was definitely going to take some getting used to. “I'm not going to call you every hour,” Darcy told her softly. “But I'll call you when I leave, and I'll call you in the morning. Listen, if you can't stand it, just look at the gi-normous rock on your finger and remember that I follow through on...well, MOST of my commitments.” ''... You know I'm only putting up with this because you're the only human being who can get me to wear a girly ass ring. Have fun NOT banging your ex, jerkbox... and remember who's got the best body around here.'' The resulting click he heard on the other line ensured that, as always, Amy got the last word. But he was fine with that...a small price to pay for getting your way. “Crisis a-vert-ed!” he said to himself as he tossed his phone into the empty passenger seat, in a very self-congratulatory tone. “...now for the hard part.” The small set of stone steps leading up to her front door suddenly seemed like they were miles away. He kept repeating to himself every fact in favor of his dropping in on her: She'll be glad to see you........... She seemed genuinely sad that we parted so soon after we buried the hatchet............ You already won the argument with Amy, she's gonna let you see her...what was the point of that if you're not going to follow up? Hey, don't forget that she had the balls to drop in on ME...and that's when she knew the first words out of my mouth would chew right through her. You're telling me you can't face her in a FAR less volatile situation? All good points indeed. But they did nothing to shake the extra hundred pounds of burden that had attached themselves to his ankles, making them heavy has lead when he pondered the thought of actually going through with it. The thought of Jane re-entering the Darcy Markson saga in a recurring role after she'd been so unceremoniously and whole-heartedly cast aside was still too new. Too foreign for his brain to objectively analyze. Above all, it was too weird. Shaking his head, he re-inserted the key into the ignition, ready to abandon the plan altogether. It was just then that the legendary Voice of Reason, evidently held up in the notorious D.C. traffic until that very moment, finally arrived on the scene to add the last and most important reason for Darcy to stop the car that instant. Look, brain! V.R. loudly exclaimed within the confines of Darcy's head. Here is the honest-to-God situation: You're engaged, which means you can't spend the night out looking for tail. You don't know anybody here, which means there isn't really anything to do. So if you don't be a man and walk up to that door, the only other option for the evening is getting slammed in your hotel room by yourself while watching Rush Hour 2 because HBO only plays pure-grade bullshit. And when that happens, you're going to be bitching at me in approximately three hours time as to why I allowed this to happen. I hope we can skip out on that, but at the very least I thought I'd give you a heads up. Darcy shrugged his shoulders. It was difficult to argue against that kind of logic, and he definitely did not intend to. Now knowing the correct course of action, he looked to remove the keys once more from the ignition... ...but there was no ignition. No car, even. Darcy did a quick about-face to recalculate his bearings, and after a second he realized that V.R.'s logic had been so overwhelmingly convincing that while he'd been listening, his legs had taken it upon themselves simply to take his word for it and mosey on up to Jane's front door without waiting for the end of the speech. Suddenly, with the thought of being forced to watch Chris Tucker do his best Chris Rock impression all evening, it didn't seem so hard after all. And he didn't even have to ring the doorbell. Just as he'd had his index finger outstretched to do the deed, the door swung open form the inside. “I figured I'd spare you from standing there any longer like you looked lost,” Jane said, flashing the smile that had captivated Darcy for so many years. “Won't you come in?” *** Where the devil is he? He'd been waiting in the airport for three days now. He didn't have high hopes to catch him on the red-eye out of Toronto, but he kept watch all day at the airport just in case. With no luck, he waited all the next day, as well...still, no dice. All day, all afternoon, and all evening on the third day, he waited in the concourse, sure that this time he'd catch the famous Darcy Crisis coming off the plane, ready to track his every move into the greater Spokane area. But it didn't happen. And it didn't make sense. Markson always came into town for a show exactly two days beforehand. It never failed. Evidently he'd decided this was the perfect allotted amount of time to arrive, settle in, and work off the jet lag before getting into the ring. This unexpected alteration in his routine was quite unlike his nature. His mind raced to reach a viable conclusion. Was he delayed? Re-routed? Surely, he couldn't be so stupid as to fly to the wrong Washington... He shook his head. Nevermind, he thought. This put an unfortunate delay in the plan he'd hoped to hatch...but it was inconsequential. He'd waited this long – another week couldn't hurt. If he didn't spring the trap in Spokane, it could easily be reset in Phoenix. Or Las Vegas. Or Eugene. Patience was key. I can't jump the gun...anything less than perfection is unacceptable. He can thank his lucky stars that he might live another week. But his date with destiny is inevitable. Sooner or later, he will unwittingly fall into my trap. Oh yes...revenge will be MINE. *** “...and that's the long and short of it,” Darcy said after a sip of the kiwi pear-flavored tea that had finally cooled enough for him to drink. “Basically, after enough punches to the face I figured out she was the one.” “I've heard some crazy engagement stories,” Jane said, as she returned from behind a corner with a tea cup of her own. “But yours definitely takes the cake. I've never heard of anybody who started the night hellbent on escaping their irrational relationship just to turn around and decide to stay in it the rest of their lives...” Jane took a sip as well, despite the fact that she'd prepared it well after she served Darcy's, where it certainly must have been scalding. Darcy never understood people who loved to consume beverages at temperatures that, if placed in his own mouth, would surely eradicate every taste bud the boiling liquid made contact with. Jane was one of those people...and sure enough, for that reason and others, it was true that he never really understood her. “That's love for you,” Darcy said, shrugging his shoulders. “A series of irrational decisions. Most of the time, they turn out to be wrong...until the one time you turn around and realize it was right. Completely irrational, but completely still, the absolute correct choice.” At that point, Darcy couldn't help but break into a sheepish grin. He couldn't deny the fact that in a small way, he felt good about the fact that he'd just waltzed into the house of his ex-girlfriend and told her he was engaged to somebody else...the true love of his life. After being left high and dry like that by Jane all those years ago, it was almost vindicating, in a way. It wasn't the reason for the sheepish grin, however. The idea of marriage scared him out of his wits – this was impossible to deny. But he was excited, too. He'd made the leap. As crazy as it sounded, Amy really was Miss Right. The mythical creature he'd always had such difficulty in accepting the existence of as a young, eager lad had finally shown herself...and he'd been brave enough to ensnare her. As countless people had, over the scope of thousands upon thousands of civilizations the world over, throughout practically the entire course of human history, Darcy Markson had found his life partner. It was as if he was turning the handle of a doorknob that had previously been locked to him every time he thought he was ready to pass through. This time it wasn't. And on the other side lied a sacred rite of passage, the next essential step in human existence. It was a joy that could only be approached by the inevitable date somewhere down the road where a night of heavy drinking to the point where the necessary precautions were neglected and he accidentally knocked Amy up. But while the thought of that still solely scared the living daylights out of him, he could still partake in the comforting joy of journeying into adulthood with Amy at his side. He didn't get the chance to enjoy it for very long, however, before Jane jerked him back to reality. “Now that I have you here...” she said, placing her tea down onto the saucer, “...would you mind helping me with something?” Darcy froze, only able to move his eyes as he stared at her intently. A moment passed as the color seemed to drain from his face. “Depends on what it is,” he told her. “If it's anything like how you needed help last time, you can go ahead and count me out. I've had enough adventures like that to last me through old age.” Jane rolled her eyes. “If helping me move a recliner down into the basement is too much of an adventure for you, I'm sure I can find somebody a bit braver to take care of it.” Darcy smirked. Seizing the opportunity, Jane dove in for the kill. “What do you say...think you're up to the challenge?” “Ha-HA,” Darcy said, sarcastically applauding her comedic stylings. “Any particular reason why you're moving your one chair into the basement? I have to say, it really looks like it ties the room together.” “It does,” Jane admitted. “But not nearly as well as the La-Z-Boy 550 Cardinal I just ordered will. My particular reason is that it's supposed to come tomorrow, and it'll be a lot harder to get this chair downstairs if we have a bigger, far superior recliner to dodge in the process. Any other questions, detective?” Darcy shook his head. “So biting, Jane. You'll catch more flies with honey, you know.” Jane smiled. “Never really worked well with you, tiger. Grab an end.” Darcy moved into position behind the chair, firmly gripping it to get a good grasp of his weight. Expecting it to way a ton, he was surprised to find that it adjusted rather easily when he rotated it slightly to the left. “Are you kidding? You're really not strong enough to handle this?” he asked, attempting to get his weight behind the condemned recliner. “It's heavier than you think, Darcy...” Jane warned him. “Don't you get any ideas about lifting that thing yourself.” “Yeah, right...” Darcy responded, choosing to ignore her attempt at dissuasion. “I'll have this thing downstairs in no time. “And then I can make fun of your weak ass.” Sucking in the necessary amount of oxygen, Darcy summoned the strength to lift the recliner by his lonesome. It was fairly large, and most of the difficulty in lifting it came from the awkward way he'd been forced to carry it. But it was certainly light enough for him to the job, and after a few steps he moved purposefully toward the descending staircase. “See? Nothing to it- Just then, it felt like someone had shoved an ice pick into the exact center of his spinal cord. It hurt so excruciatingly, he expected that this sort of injury should have been accompanied by a loud pop...or at the very least, the feeling of something tearing within him. But he felt neither; only pain. Of the white-hot variety. He dropped the recliner, which crashed to the floor in a sickening thud. Jane could only stand there with a mortified look on her face, covering her mouth with her hands as Darcy stumbled toward the thankfully nearby couch, clenching his teeth so tight he thought they might crack under the pressure. “Jesus Christ! Are you OK?” Jane finally asked, recovering from the sight of his injury to rush to his aid. “FUCK! SONOFABITCH, FUCK GOD DAMMIT FUUUUUUCKK!!!” came his harsh reply, not aimed at her or anyone in particular. If he could think straight, he would probably have at least found a small chuckle at how severe pain can quickly reduce a man's vocabulary to the infamous Seven Dirty Words. Breathing now as if he'd just run a marathon, he desperately tried to regain some semblance of composure. “Should I call 911? Are you going to be alright?” Jane asked, her voice suddenly frantic. Darcy nodded vigorously. The worst of the pain had by this point subsided, at least enough for him to speak coherently. “FUCK! I'm sorry. I'll be okay. It's my damned back. I'm an idiot.” “Are you sure?” Darcy nodded again. “Just give me a minute,” he asked. “I should be okay, I just put too much stress on it. Happens every once in a while.” “Probably coincides with the times you try to do your macho man impression,” Jane said, deciding he'd recovered enough that she could chastise him. “I TOLD you it was heavy.” “It's NOT heavy, and you still suck for not being able to lift it,” Darcy replied, having none of it. “I could have done it myself if it didn't feel like somebody stabbed me in the back.” “Well give yourself a minute or two before you get back up,” Jane cautioned him once more, leaning closer to examine his predicament. “That looked dreadful.” “Really, I'm fine,” Darcy said, turning to look at her. “I feel better already. I swear, it's like my back will give out on me for seconds at a time, and the next minute I'm tip-top all over again. Besides, I've taken up enough of your time, and I just realized I left my phone in my car. Amy is going to kill me if I don't call her...” Using her shoulder for support, Darcy attempted to lift himself upright...but it was no use. Another sharp stabbing pain plagued him, and his ever-afflicted back locked up on him all over again. Painfully, he re-collapsed back onto the couch. “.........maybe not,” Darcy said, with an expression that seemed to suggest he was more disappointed than pain-wracked. “You park your ass right there, and don't even THINK about moving, Mister” Jane said, changing her tone from warning to outright scolding. “Do you need an ice pad? heat pad? Something in the middle?” Darcy sighed. “Don't worry about it Jane, honest. I've had every doctor with an ounce of credibility look at my back, and they can't make heads or tails of it. They didn't even think it would ever heal to the extent that it did. They're afraid to touch it, and I'm even more afraid to let them. But like I said, every once in awhile I have to deal with mini-episodes of my back going out on me.” “That's terrible,” Jane responded, her eyes doing nothing to hide the pity she knew he didn't ask for, yet still couldn't help but convey. “How long does it put you out for?” “Hard to say,” he said, wishing he could shrug his shoulders. “Minutes. Hours. Days, sometimes.” “You're welcome to that couch as long as you like, Darse,” she said, using his old high-school nickname that only she and Johnny still clung to. “I had half a mind to lounge around the rest of Sunday like a hermit anyway.” “That's not necessary,” he told her. “I've got a red-eye to catch in the morning, whether I'm still in excruciating pain or not. I can't miss the show.” “You're going to have a MATCH after this? Are you INSANE?” Darcy weakly held up two fingers. “Two matches.” Jane's jaw gaped open. When she finally clenched it shut, she expressed her sentiments of his decision. “Now I KNOW you're insane.” “Would you stop worrying so much, Janey?” Darcy asked, dropping a nickname of his own. “This sort of thing is inevitable in my line of work. If they want to run me dogged through a goddamned title gauntlet to prove my worth as a champion of this business, I've got to answer the call. I'll wrestle until this thing gives out on me once and for all. Now, either you can sit here and call me an idiot, of which I am perfectly aware, or you can be a dear and fetch me my phone before Amy gives herself a brain hemorrhage.” Jane elicited a deep sigh before rising to comply with the latter request. She made it all the way to the door before being forced to turn around and look back at him, unable to help the commentary she went on to offer. “I'll never understand this passion of yours,” she said, her hands placed squarely on her hips. “It's completely irrational.” Darcy smirked. “I know...but that's love.”
