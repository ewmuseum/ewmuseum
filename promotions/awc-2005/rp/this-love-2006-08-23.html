Date: 08/23/06 Amber swung around the right turn, once again thankful that she wasn't driving a stick. Night hung all around her, sprinkled with stars throughout. Her foot pressed down a little harder on the throttle; the scenery whipped by that much faster in her peripheral vision. The edges of her red-painted lips were quirked in a little smile, intimate, only really noticed by herself. Driving, she knew contentment. The speed made her muscles almost flaccid, cleared her mind. But her heart was still in the grip of orange stress. For that, however, she came equipped. Creigh was a con man. Amber was sure of it. He sat in his little room all day and night, all sober—he wasn't a junkie, or at least he claimed not to be, even though he sold what must be tons of the black stuff—and waited for her to come. Waited for his newest little cash flow. She paid for it, too, which had to be the saddest part. She thought she might be missing a hundred bucks, just on this little excursion. It was too much for a little relief. Every time, she needed more and more and more. It sickened her a little bit. Supply and demand was a natural law, though, and she needed this. She didn't know how she'd continue to deal without it. Her little balloon of smack was nestled between her breasts, just under her black top, and she reached up to touch the lump. Having it nestled against her skin, separated only by that thin veil of plastic, was almost reassuring, a black tar mother stroking her hair and telling her it would be alright. And it would be alright. Amber was sure of it. Anton was finally coming around. Luis was in the hospital, unable to sink his claws into Anton further. They would be free soon enough. Free of Luis and Nneka and this damned place. And then she could be free of this little release she depended on. Not yet, though. For now, she had to continue driving, and then when she wasn't driving, only the junk could relax her muscles. Nneka slipped into the house, shoving the key that Anton had given her so long ago into the back pocket of her jeans. She closed the door silently behind her and then started towards her brother's room, hands in pockets. She hadn't been able to see him for so long. It was almost as if he was avoiding her actively; every time she wanted to see him, he wasn't there. Or he was busy. She pursed her lips; he was busy too much. She stepped to his door and knocked before slipping her hand back into her pocket. ''Amber?'' a powerful voice called from behind the door. Nneka dipped her head forward a bit, sighing. ''No,'' Nneka said in Igbo. ''It's me.'' There was a short pause before a ''Come in.'' Nneka twisted the doorknob and pulled the door open, peering into the room. The ceiling light and fan were on, showering the bedroom with incandescence and breeze. Anton was sprawled out on his bed, hands folded over his bare chest. Nneka pushed the door closed behind her. ''Hello, Anton.'' ''Hello, Nneka.'' He didn't lift his head to look at her. Nneka frowned as she leaned back against the door, trying to appear casual. ''You've been well?'' Nneka asked. Her brother seemed so vulnerable like this. She knew that if she tried anything, he could be up with a lion's quickness, but still… seeing him prone, face up, almost as if waiting on death. ''I have,'' Anton said. The reassurance brought forth a sigh of relief. ''Have you?'' ''Yes.'' Nneka shifted. She'd come here for a reason, of course, but she almost didn't want to talk about it. Just speaking to her brother again was a welcome change. ''Have you seen the city?'' Anton asked. He raised his head just a bit to offer Nneka an inquisitive look. Nneka shook her head. ''I've been… at the hospital most of the time,'' she confessed. Anton nodded, not saying anything; what she meant was understood. Nneka rubbed her arm as Anton's head fell back to his pillow. She had to say something, though. Keeping quiet forever wouldn't help her brother. She had come to do that more than she had come for the pleasure of speaking to her older sibling. ''Anton, Amber is—'' ''She is what?'' Anton's voice had an edge to it now, a razor wire that seemed to be pushed against Nneka's throat, daring movement. Nneka sputtered soundlessly before shaking her head and trying to get back on her feet. ''You know what I think about her, Anton. She is bad! She is bad and—'' ''Don't. Speak.'' Anton growled. He sat up suddenly, crossing his legs under him and grasping his knees. His eyes branded themselves in hers. ''I tell you not to talk about her and I mean it. Be quiet.'' Nneka didn't follow his command voluntarily. It was just that all the air had been sucked out of her lungs. Across the hall from Anton's room was Amber's haven, the bathroom. She slipped inside soundlessly, locking the door behind her. Her position was with her back against the door, sitting on the ground. She set her purse on the ground and fished the balloon out from between her breasts, settling it on the ground in front of her. She gave the balloon a little smile, going just shy of personifying the thing before she reached into her purse. It was quite a beautiful little chunk, though. She slipped a bottle of rubbing alcohol and her silvery spoon out of the purse. Carefully, she cleaned off the spoon and set it on her knee. She opened the balloon and took out her beautiful bit of tar, setting it in the spoon. She admired it for a bit longer before getting out her syringe, cleaning that off as well. She squirted some water into the spoon and then fished inside her purse, getting her lighter out. She toyed with the lighter a bit, looking over the pink plastic thing. Amber sucked a bit of water into the syringe and set it on the ground next to her. Preparing was hell. She didn't like it. If only she had Creigh here to help her do that part of it, get it all ready and then she could just shoot up. Of course, Creigh would like to get a piece of her body in return. She wasn't going to give that up to a creep like him. Sometimes, though… she made a disgusted sound, looking back at her spoon. ''Luis is doing fine,'' Nneka said to answer Anton's query. Anton nodded. He couldn't hide being more than a little concerned for his manager; no matter how much Amber wanted the two to part ways, Luis was still Anton's manager. For the moment, Anton was obligated to be concerned. ''He's been getting his strength back?'' ''He has.'' Nneka smiled. ''He doesn't want to eat the food, though. He says it tastes like shit.'' Anton shrugged. ''It probably does.'' He'd been in a hospital before, in France. They served you just enough to get by. He had gotten the distinct feeling that, if they could, they would have left him for dead. It wasn't exactly comforting. ''Luis can't get to the bathroom, though,'' Nneka confided. Anton arched an eyebrow. ''The doctor won't let him. And the first time they wanted to insert the catheter… he fought the nurse. He said… ah…'' Nneka screwed up her face as she tried to remember. ''He said 'Nothing goes in my penis, you whore!' or something. It was funny.'' Anton gave her a smile; he hadn't found the story funny, but he imagined it more humorous in person. Nneka hid her mouth behind her hand as she laughed. ''So, uhm, how is Amber?'' Nneka asked. Anton blinked before his stare flatlined, all emotion gone. Nneka met his eyes. ''She's doing well?'' She had to know that Anton wasn't going to give her an answer. Nneka could be so stupid. He gave her a direct… an order! He had told her that there would be no conversation about Amber. He had said it as plain as his skin was dark. And yet she wouldn't listen. Nneka looked around curiously, folding her arms over her chest. ''Where… where is she?'' ''That is none of your business, Nneka,'' Anton snapped. ''Quiet yourself.'' Anton didn't know where Amber was, either; he would very much like to know. That didn't give Nneka the right to ask, though. He had specifically denied her that. She was a good girl. He knew that. Sometimes, though… he curled his upper lip as silence reigned. Amber lifted the spoon up to just above eye level so she could watch the flame as she held the lighter under the spoon, heating the mixture to dissolve her chunk. The flame danced and slapped noiselessly at the bottom of the metal spoon. She imagined the shallow curve turning red, but she knew it wasn't. Her arms began to grow tense as she held the spoon and lighter in position, not letting them move an inch. Doing smack made your hands become steadier; she wasn't going to waste any of her investment on the floor. A little bit of release took a lot of work, she reflected, but it was worth it. It was all worth it. Thoughts of Luis and Nneka bubbled to the front of her brain, but she pushed them away. It would defeat the purpose, really. It would make this all the less beautiful, all the more stressful. She didn't need that. She wanted to relax. Before she knew it, she held a syringe that was full enough with the liquid that she sighed with satisfaction. Her little chunk had grown up. The needle pushed into the skin and the heroin flowed into the vein. Anton watched his sister a while longer. He wondered just what was running through her head. Hopefully, she'd abandoned all hope of getting him to talk about Amber. She was not going to try and get him to crack. He wouldn't. ''I've been training hard,'' Nneka said timidly. Anton arched an eyebrow at her. ''When I'm not at the hospital, I'm at the gym. I've been getting better.'' ''Do you use the heavy bag a lot?'' Anton asked. At least this was a pleasant topic, something he could help her on. He did like to be helpful when he could. ''Yes,'' Nneka said. ''I've been—'' ''Don't,'' Anton told her. ''Do more push-ups, curl-ups, that sort. Run.'' ''I do run!'' Nneka said defensively. Anton smirked. ''I know,'' he said. ''Run more. You need stamina.'' Nneka nodded slowly, slumping a little more against the wall. She did need the stamina; from what Anton had witnessed of her, she tired out far too quickly. She was a good fighter, but she would never last against an opponent in a mixed martial arts competition. Nneka looked up at him and they locked eyes for a long while. Anton gritted his teeth. He knew. ''Anton, we need to talk about Amber. We do.'' Anton snorted contemptuously. ''Why should we?'' ''Because, Anton!'' Nneka cried. ''Because she's bad for you! Because she's… she's evil! Luis knows! I know! Deep inside, you know, too! You know she's going to ruin you!'' ''No,'' Anton said slowly. ''I don't know.'' ''You do!'' Nneka took a step forward. Anton bared his teeth at his sister. ''Nneka, do not put words into my mouth! None of that is true and YOU! You are the one who knows that!'' ''Listen to yourself!'' Nneka shouted. ''Listen to you, the big Lion! None of this happened before Amber! When she came, everything got—'' ''Shut up!'' Anton roared. Nneka was plucking at his heartstrings, tearing them from serenity and touching against the flames of anger. He balled his fists in his lap, but he didn't rise. He wouldn't give her that satisfaction. ''Do not speak of her again.'' ''Do not try to shush me, brother! You aren't papa!'' ''Stop that!'' Anton pushed his hands down on the edge of the bed. She always brought that up. Always! His heartstrings were in danger of charring. Nneka yanked the door open and slammed it behind her, leaving with one biting Parthian shot, an arrow for which Anton had no shield and no response: ''Do you even know what she's doing right now?'' Amber was feeling good. Everything had melted away into a sea of black relaxation. Her head lolled, her mouth half-open. And it was all for Anton. It was all because she needed to stay by his side. She loved him to death.
