This is where you saw my naked soul... (parts 3 and 4)

12 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6.12.06 DISCLAIMER: Last week’s title should have read “This is where you saw my naked soul (Parts 1 and 2. This week is the conclusion, parts 3 and 4. Not that this matters to anybody but me. Part 3: Running on the beach… When last we left our heroes… “I- I’m not sure what to say,” Darcy said, faced with perhaps with what might be the most difficult decision of his life. Had David Jameson suddenly done a 180 and elected to work with Darcy rather than against him? It seemed too good to believe. He'd been adamant with the Tomorrow Institute’s new executive officer that he wished to remain wrestling as long as he could, but he'd also admitted that he didn't intend to spend his entire life turning proverbial tricks for wrestling promoters, who in fact so closely resembled pimps that Darcy didn't care to think about it very often. Deep down, he wanted to help people and the planet as much as he could, and this seemed like his best ticket to do so. “You’ve certainly presented me with a very tempting offer…” Upon those words, Darcy could have sworn he heard someone scamper down the hall. At the time, he thought nothing of it. So here he was. Suddenly, the question that always seemed to have such an easy solution, even though the answer had changed several times throughout his life, was now proving to be a lot more difficult than he’d ever expected. Darcy loved wrestling. Loved it. He found himself despising the business aspect of it more often than not, but he loved to perform. He loved to compete. In attempting to answer his question of staying to appreciate love versus leaving it behind in pursuit of a dream career, Darcy had always thought the love would be for another person, the girl with the key to his heart. And maybe it was part of that, since his five plus years in the business had been spent around Amy, the girl with the key to his. But more accurately, the love Darcy was forced to choose either for or against was on behalf of his adopted sport. It was then that Darcy remembered his vision. The vision that haunted him every night he spent in the confines of the rainforest not ten miles from the Rio Palace he currently occupied, and the very vision that would eventually lead him out of the wilderness and back into life between the ropes. At first, the revelation featured only one entity – some kind of striking, overwhelmingly magnificent goddess beckoning to him in a language he did not understand. The language somehow touched his very soul, calming the years of overstress he’d put on his beleaguered body. But it also revealed to him the hole in his heart that would prevent his reaching true peace without being filled. Darcy would never in a thousand years forget how beautiful the deity was. Her body was molded from pure perfection, glowing all at once every color of the visual spectrum. Her face, however, appeared almost as if it came from two separate beings now fused into one, living a combined yet somehow dual existence. One on side the face glowed radiant, with hair of fire emanating past her fair features as the eye adorning her side glowed with passion and vivacity. The other side had no hair at all, and coolly glowed a pale blue. It was equally beautiful, but somehow more hardened – as if it was the face of a tested warrior. He saw her in return visions, with each journey to her presence harder than the last. He was forced to slay dozens of bloodthirsty warriors in the process, never earning the deity’s company until he’d conquered her best challengers. He soon came to interpret this deity as the dual embodiment for the love so coldly removed from his heart, and for the life to which he was destined to return. The warriors he’d battled were symbolic of the challenges he would have to face – from competitors in the ring, to his superiors at the Institute practically begging him away, to his own body likely working against him in his press to return to full, viable strength. Darcy sighed a sigh of relief. The choice wasn’t so hard at all. He had no regrets of his following his vision, and there was no sense in turning back now, no matter how wonderful Jameson’s offer might seem. Love had won out after all. “I’m sorry,” Darcy finally said to Jameson after what must have seemed to him like an eternity. “But I thought I made myself clear on this matter. I want to help the Institute. I care about my world. But as long as I’ve got a working spinal column, my place is elsewhere.” Jameson wasn’t going to go down without a fight. “I don’t get it, Markson,” he started, calling Darcy by his surname as he often did when he began to get angry. “How can you say you care about your world when you won’t even give it half the time of day you do with this wrestling business crap? What is this bizarre love affair you have?” Darcy stood up, his own anger that he’d been attempting to quell now quickly rising to match Jameson’s. “Listen up, Mister Jameson. Let’s get something straight: I do NOT love the wrestling business. I hate it, more than anything. I can’t even stretch my legs without some kind of little monster zombie creep chewing on my ankle, or walk down to the curtain without getting stopped by a homeless guy chasing after his drug-smuggling duck. It’s as stupid as it is outright bizarre, and I don’t know how the hell I stand it. Darcy paused. The grandfather clock counting the seconds in Jameson’s office echoed loudly in the brief silence. “But it’s my home. It’s where I have to be. And it’s the only way I’m ever going to be truly happy,” Darcy said, now calm again. “You must have known that before you flew me out here, so this has essentially been a colossal waste of time.” Now Jameson was shaking his head. “No, Darcy…I made you this offer specially, in person, because it’s going to be the last of it’s kind.” Uh oh. Darcy could sense where this was going. He offered no reply, instead waiting for Jameson to continue. “If you haven’t noticed, this…all of this,” he said, gesturing around the room with his hand, “belongs to me now. I’m the one in charge. People aren’t going to be listening to anyone’s visions around here for the Institute but mine. And part of my new vision for the Institute is responsible spending and allocation of resources. And THAT, my friend, includes cutting ties with so-called ‘spokesmen’ who aren’t exactly pulling their weight.” So that’s what this is about, Darcy finally gathered. He sure didn’t waste any time throwing his newfound influence around… “I certainly hope you’ll reconsider,” Darcy coolly replied. “I thought this company stood for bigger things than a battle between two egos on opposite sides.” “Others might be willing to play the waiting game with you, Markson…but not me. You’re either on board now, or not at all.” “Then it was a pleasure working for you, Jameson…for all of the thirty minutes that it was. But if you’ll excuse me, there’s a girl waiting outside who’s probably had just about enough of you as I have.” With that, Darcy excused himself from Jameson’s office without so much as a handshake, officially ending his tenure with a company that he had done so much for, and it for him. It had been his contact with Jim at the Institute that he was able to return to his home in the United States after living for so long in the Brazilian rainforest, with no money to speak of and practically no other contacts with the outside world whatsoever. For his part Darcy had tone tireless work for the Institute, lobbying hard in Congress for Institute goals and pressuring lawmakers into having his voice heard. It had once seemed like a match made in heaven, Darcy and the institute…that is, until destiny opened the door for him to return to the squared circle, in the very company that featured the object of his star-crossed love that he could ignore no longer. As he reached for the door Darcy turned to look back one last time at the man so eager to remove Darcy from his life. With a smirk, he flashed him a peace sign…backwards, of course, in traditional British vulgarity. Without waiting to see if Jameson caught the backhanded farewell, Darcy exited the office. “What a gi-normous waste of time. I totally apologize,” Darcy said to….Amy’s chair. Where the hell is she? he wondered silently. Then it hit him: The sound I heard earlier. Somebody taking off down the hall…right as Jameson presented me with his offer. She must have overheard… Frantically, Darcy moved down the hall back to the main office area. Quickly scanning the vicinity, he soon spied the intern who had led the both of them to Jameson’s office. “Hey kid!” Darcy shouted from a distance. “Did you see where that red-haired girl went?” The intern merely shrugged. *** He’d searched all day. He’d figured that not too many tattoo-laden, pissed-off diminutive redheads were traversing the city that day, but his luck didn’t seem to be agreeing with him. His first stop was obviously the hotel; her bags were still there, so Darcy surmised that she had to be around someplace. But his offering of said description to the various restaurant owners, shopkeepers and police officers all turned up fruitless. While still determined, Darcy knew that ironically, searching for Amy while the Brazilian sun still adorned the sky would probably be useless. She’s probably working hard to keep me off her trail, he surmised. I’d better wait until nightfall, when her behavior will be a bit more predictable… When night fell, Darcy began searching the clubs, judging that Amy would likely be on a quest for booze by now. However, his search through the Janeiro nightlife was proving equally as ineffective. And with the lines outside the clubs becoming longer and longer with each establishment he checked, Darcy was losing hope, and fast. Maybe she really doesn’t want to be found at all, he thought, pessimistically, a term that he liked to call “pessimised.” Whether or not he was looking to regroup for his investigation or simply to clear his head, Darcy headed for a nearby beach. While not nearly as packed as it had been earlier in the afternoon, when it had certainly reached the brim of capacity, a respectable number of patrons were still enjoying the shoreline under the starlit sky. Darcy walked to where the sand and water connected as one, allowing his bare feet and the bottom of his cargo pants to be submerged in the salt water. It was the precise moment when he had given up all hope entirely that fate, in apparent triumph, was ready to reveal her. She was some distance away, but a nearby bonfire organized by some beachgoers brightened the area, and the glow of the flames bounced radiantly off of her fiery mane. Without the slightest hesitation Darcy took off, sprinting headlong in her direction. When he arrived close enough he found his assessment to be correct; while Amy Campbell might look like any other smoking-hot, 5’5” bikini-clad redhead from behind, the printed dragon curving around the back of her neck gave her away. “Amy!” Darcy gasped, attempting to catch his breath as he turned her around. She certainly looked radiant, with the light of the flames making her stand out from the rest of everything around him. But the look on her face, what looked to be in the manner of a sinister scowl, quickly chased any thoughts of her beauty from his mind. 'Jesus Christ,' Darcy said, his breath now returned to him. 'I've been looking for you all day. Where in God's name have you been?' 'What,' she shrugged bitterly, ''You expected me to hang around that hippie hole all day while Jameson gave you a verbal butt-fucking to take some assignment that I'm already sure isn't actually worth your time?' Amy tossed her cup of beer into the fire, eliciting howls from the festive locals perched about twenty feet away. Apparently the situation looked like some kind of sexy party to drunken Brazilians. To Darcy, it looked more like his girlfriend had just turned away alcohol so she stayed in a clear enough headspace to properly chew him out. 'Not exactly...but I also didn't expect you to ignore my calls and ditch me all night either,' he replied. He certainly wasn't too keen on the hole she was trying to stare into him. 'I assume you're pissed off at me, that much I gathered. Would you mind telling me why?' She snarled, and led him even farther away from the small crowd - more toward the water, a seemingly perfect place to wash a body away... but aside from that, 'I'm not as stupid as your fuckhead boss thinks. He wouldn't have flown us here, and you wouldn't have taken me unless you were buttering me up for something I wasn't gonna want to hear. If you thought I was hanging around the lunch room in that office, you're retarded - I was pretty fucking obviously 'overhearing' your talk with Jameson.' The sea brushing her toes and reminding her of home didn't sway her temper at all, 'So go ahead, Darcy - this is where you do some oh-so-sweet and dramatic speech that tells me you have something you 'just have' to do, you know, for yourself. Self discovery and blahblahblah... just hit me with it now - I know you took the job, and I know no news is gonna be good.' Darcy squinted. He now had a pretty good idea when Amy had disappeared from the Rio palace. Somehow he sensed that easing her newfound anger by telling her what she probably wanted to hear wouldn't be enough. But still, he had to try. 'Actually, if you had stuck around long enough, you would have heard me politely decline his 'oh-so-generous' offer. Then you would have heard him chew me out, terminate my spokesman services and essentially put an end to this dual life I've been leading. Normally this is where I'd get steamed for you not giving me the least bit of credit...but you know what? I've already had my ass chewed out once today, so let's go. If you want to scream at me, fucking do it. But DON’T make the same mistake Jameson did by wasting my time.' 'I doubt you actually did that. And why are we here in the FIRST place, huh? Because that moron couldn't take a simple phone call. I just hauled ass to this spider-infested hive ON A PLANE for nothing. I've got jet lag, I've got a headache, I've... fuck - whatever,' she grimaced, waving him off and digging her toes into the sand, 'I don't have many words left up my sleeve... so tell me what I need to be doing right now. Appreciating, right? Being sooooo perky about standing on a beach in Rio, even despite the fact that I feel like shit?' 'Honest to God, Amy. I told him no deal. He fired me. I didn't know why he brought me- sorry, US out here in the first place, but I can't imagine we'll be returning anytime soon. And on the subject of what I want you to do, I think I can call it even if you would just calm the fuck down. But now that I think about it, I'm getting the idea that this is about something completely different. You wanna come clean, or what?' Darcy was spot-on. He knew that 'You don't want to know' look on her face. There was something going on between those little ears, and she wasn't gonna keep it in for long. She raised her eyebrow as if to ask if he felt ready to hear her side of things, and as Darcy stood adamant, she turned away for a moment to collect her temper. 'Alright,' she said flatly, 'Marry me.' Part 4: Smiling ‘cause you’re next to me… Ummmmmmmmmmmmmmmmmmmmmmmm………… He didn’t think it was true. He really, truly didn’t want to believe it. But as the last two of Amy’s words escaped her lips, Darcy could have sworn he saw a several hundred megaton nuclear weapon fall from the sky, striking the ocean and tearing the entire beach apart with fire, eradicating everything but the two of them in a mammoth explosion. KA-FUCKING-BOOM! “Marry you? MARRY YOU?! Have you…are you…what the fuck?!” 'Well... that was the worst 'yes' I've ever heard.' If the road to sanity could be measured, Darcy was several inches from merging with Lunacy Boulevard, where he would surely be creamed by an oncoming semi. To compensate, he began speaking approximately 110,000 times faster than he could currently think. ”Yes? No! NO! I mean- I- Amy, I love you, please don’t get me wrong, but- N-NO! No way. Jesus, God…Seriously, I love you, but we CAN’T get married.” 'Oookay...' she sighed, looking completely unreadable for once in her life, 'Why?' The awkwardly abrupt and blunt part of her personality that he loved so much was working HARD against him. Darcy took a deep breath, perhaps the deepest of his life that didn’t involve the massive yawns that came with his sophomore year university economics class. His composure only slightly regained, he exclaimed his sentiments. “I don’t know…maybe because we’ve been together for like three, maybe four months…something which amazes me, by the way, because I had at least one of us figured for spending at least that much time in a coma due to injuries inflicted by the other… I imagine it might have something to do with me picking you up from JAIL a couple weeks ago. All I know for sure is that I’m still feeling this out. Still scared shitless that something is going to go terribly wrong, and the other shoe I’ve been waiting for is going to drop. Hard. We haven’t been tested, Amy.” Darcy began to get the sense that he was rambling, perhaps on the point of babbling incoherently. “Does any of this make sense to you?” She sighed again, 'You idiot. You don't get it, do you? This relationship is a living, breathing test! It has been, you jackass. It's been five years of non-stop tests, and look at where we are. We passed, asshat!' Darcy’s heart rate quickened from that of a Formula 500 race car to that of a 747. She’s…she’s not going to take no for an answer! he thought, panicking. “Amy, please, I’m begging you…you’ve got to remember, this time last year I lived in the fucking wilderness that we could literally walk to from here. I don’t- I don’t know that I even belong here. I mean, I’ve learned a lot…I know how I feel about you now. But I’m just not sure. And I can’t do something like that if I’m not sure…” The look in Amy’s eyes nearly broke his heart and two. He’d known her nearly half a decade now, and had bared witness to practically every emotion she was capable of feeling. But it wasn’t until now that Darcy finally saw the look that she bared on her face. The despondency in her eyes suggested that she was hurt like never before, like her very soul had been crushed by what he’d asked of her. “I’m so sorry,” Darcy said as he rushed to embrace her. At first Amy made no move in response, but slowly she returned his embrace with a soft one of her own. “You’re still not off the hook for this giant waste of time,” Amy said, with her head buried into Darcy’s chest. “You still owe me something for this.” “Anything,” Darcy replied, hoping to God it wasn’t a baby. *** I made good on the promise that night. Amy wanted to go to some trance club she’d passed by earlier in the day. Apparently the theme for the evening was some sort of girly-ass trance. Not exactly my cup of tea, but Amy insisted that if I took her there then we’d be even for what would forever be known between us as “the Rio incident.” This wasn’t the highest quality of establishments; what Amy had actually seen was an abandoned building with a poster advertising the collaboration of local DJs later in the evening. Pills were handed out at the door, which Amy eagerly consumed. I did too, although a bit more reluctantly I would think; I’d had a bad experience with MDMA that came from a questionable source, which later turned out to be laced with a few unadvertised undesirables that kept me awake and paranoid for practically three days straight. However, the multitude of people who already had (and would soon be joined by others) taken the pills led me to believe they were on the level. And I’d be lying if I said the smiley face beaming up at me didn’t ease my worries just a little. It turned out to be a wonderful idea. Being in that state allows you to dance to anything – ANYTHING. 50 Cent, Madonna, Brahms, Union Underground, you name it. Or in this case, Euro-trash tricked out trance. Amy and I danced our asses off in a sea of people, under flashing lights that must have been every color man was capable of producing. The night itself wasn’t terribly remarkable, except for one moment that stood out above the rest. Somehow, as the night was winding down, it seemed like everything had slowed down. Like I said, there were people everywhere, but somehow they all blended into a darkened background, and it felt like all the lights converged on the spot that Amy and I had occupied. The night bathed Amy’s face in such a way that I nearly had a heart attack; with her vibrant hair flowing freely with every movement, and the beams of light sparkling off of her eyes, she exactly bared the visage of the goddess I’d encountered in my visions. I don’t know what it meant- I still don’t. It’s almost impossible to put into words. But I know I’ll never forget the words of the song that accompanied the scene. I can’t remember who wrote the song, nor the no-name DJ who mixed it, only the unforgettable, haunting lyrics. This is where you saw my naked soul… In Janeiro…
