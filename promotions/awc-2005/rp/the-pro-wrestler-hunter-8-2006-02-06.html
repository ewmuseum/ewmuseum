The Pro Wrestler Hunter #8

6 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/6/06 Flames of Fear Megumi groaned when The Green Grappler locked his motel room door and strutted out into the moonlight. “Wonderful night isn’t it?” he said with a quirky smile. The chirping crickets seemed to agree with him. She had hoped that her quick run to get some ice for her sore back would have gone unseen. But the man in the green mask was always popping up in odd places or at the omst opportune times it seemed, for him. “It was,” Megumi said. “Now, is that the way to speak to the man who pulled your butt from the proverbial fire last week?” He put his hands on his hips in a comical way, tilting his head, but Megumi wouldn’t bite. Her stoic features raised an eyebrow behind the green mask. “Number one, I had no need for saving, especially from the likes of you,” she said with a snort. “The Furious Fists tried to put me through a flaming table but Liam ended up getting burned. Number two, if you ever come near me with that rocket launcher again, you will quickly find it deposited somewhere between your---“ “Please, please, no vulgarity to harm my virgin ears. And besides… that was a mere bag o’ shells!” he announced. Before Megumi could discern his intent, he rubbed his hands together like a magician trying to distract his mark, and then pantomimed, wiggling his fingers in the air. There was a spark. “FIRE-AH!” WHOOSH! A huge fireball erupted from his fingertips and lit up the motel parking lot. Megumi was so startled that she flung back and slammed into a parked car behind her, setting off the alarm. WHEET! WHEET! WHEE-OOO! WHEE-OO! The Grappler nodded with a triumphant smile. “Startling, isn’t i---WHOA!” In one fluid motion Megumi grabbed the man by his t-shirt and spun him around, slamming him onto the hood of the car. She looked him dead in the eyes, her hot breath so close it made him sweat. “Play one of your tricks again, masked fool, and I will tear your twitchy fingers off one by one and jam them up your nose, do you understand me?” “But why?” he argued with a reasonable tone. “It’s my trademark, my gimmick. The fans love my wonderful fireworks, my fatefully timed detonations, and my sparkling personality if I do say so myself.” Just then a voice yelled out right as the car alarm stopped. “Hey, get off my car and get a room you two!” Megumi looked up to see a man standing in his shorts looking at them from his motel room. She pulled Grapps off the hood of the car and turned toward and walked toward the ice machine. How could she explain to this frivolous dandy her aversion to fire? He hadn’t been there that night; he hadn’t heard the screams of his friend, or smelled their burning flesh as they died in a fire that refused to consume her. As she formulated a half-truth, Megumi pushed away the memory of the night her dojo was set aflame and four young girls died. With great effort she kept her voice calm, her words neutral. “I do not need tricks or devices to win matches, nor do I need them to win the fans admiration.” She talked as she filled a bucket with ice. The crickets that had just stopped chirping due to the sudden noise began their night song again. “All I want is to win and the more distractions I have to deal with the more possibilities your little tricks are going to end up costing me rather then assisting me.” “Tell you what,” he said with a smile as he walked up to her, “add a few more ice cubes in that bucket for my aching jaw and perhaps I might keep my tricks up my sleeve for now. Agreed?” The Breakdown Megumi filled a handkerchief from her briefcase with ice and tied it into a neat bundle. She turned to hand it to The Green Grappler and get him to leave her alone, only to watch as he closed the door to her motel room. “Your ice is ready. Do you mind leaving me alone now?” She asked with a tone of impatience in her voice. “Well, yes. I do, if you don’t mind.” He smiled at her and sat down on the corner of her bed. “I am going to sit here on this bed and try to lower the swelling on my face while you tell me exactly what is going on here.” “I told you,” she stressed. “I want to be left alone. I am tired.” “Look,” he said to the back of her head as she turned away from him again, “once you tell me why we are driving around the Midwest in a red pick up truck we stole from a couple of bible-thumping grit-suckers, obviously on the run from someone or something, it would be nice to know what is going on.” She was cold as the cubes of frozen water she dutifully wrapped in a bathroom towel. “There is nothing going on.” “Really? How about the gym? When that fat Englishman in the wheelchair was screaming for your head, was that nothing? Since I am along for the ride, so to speak, don’t you think I should have some kind of idea what we are up against?” Why not? Megumi thought. Maybe the truth would frighten him into holding his tongue. She sank down in the bed drawing her knees up tight against her chest, resting her back against the headboard and a bag if ice. “All right, then, here it is. Since I seem to be stuck with you for the next few weeks until this “Celestial Fury” tag team runs its’ course in the eyes of the front office….” “Wheeee! And won’t THAT be a ton of fun!” Grapps yelled and bounced on the bed. “Have you heard of The Pro Wrestler Hunter?” she said bluntly. CRASH! The Green Grappler toppled off the bed. Megumi didn’t flinch, getting used to his foolish antics. He slowly peeked his head out from behind the mattress. “Ghastly business,” he said with a shudder, and looked to his left and to his right. His eyes then widened as he crept slowly back onto the corner of the bed. “Suffering succotash! I’m not sure I like where this is going. You’re saying that the Pro Wrestler Hunter is after you?” “You’re sharper then you appear,” she said dryly. “Thank you, but how do you know? About the hunter, I mean.” Megumi shrugged, trying to appear matter-of-fact. “For some time now, I’ve been followed everywhere I go. Several of my friends have been crippled. I was usually nearby when it happened.” “Oh, my dear. How awful for you.” The genuine warmth and concern in the masked man’s voice temporarily disconcerted Megumi. Her eyes flew to the ceiling, and she stared fixedly into the nothingness. At the moment anything was better then meeting The Green Grappler’s kind, green eyes. She had put this man’s life and career in danger, and fool as he might be, he’d done nothing to deserve the treatment she’d dealt him. “I regret involving you in this,” she murmured. “Believe me, I had not planned to bring you this far.” “So far, no problem,” he replied, cheerfully accepting her apology. “Anyway, it’s a rare honor for such a humble masked man such as myself to be on the trail of the Pro Wrestler Hunter. From what I have heard, he has been selectively attacking wrestlers who have been employed by CYDONIA Incorporated, yes?” “How do you know that?” she asked, curiously. CYDONIA Inc. was a well known agency, but how did he know everyone attacked had been a CYDONIA employee? “No,” she said slowly. “I’m no CYDONIA wrestler.” “Oh? Then why is the Pro Wrestler Hunter after you?” She didn’t know how to explain this, and in time if he found out she was lying it wouldn’t do her much good. “I work for them on occasion.” “Ah, I see. And what is it that you do?” Grapps drawled, eyeing her and waggling his eyebrow in a broad parody of a leer. Megumi glared at him, and he grinned in return. The fool enjoyed baiting her! She realized suddenly. It was a game. He wasn’t being lascivious, but boyishly mischievous. All of hr irritation with The Green Grappler flooded back, pushing aside the guilt of a moment before. An unworthy but irresistible impulse urged her to make him squirm a bit. “I’m a shooter,” she said in a threatening voice. A droll expression crossed Grapp’s face. “Do tell. And I hear Norton & bridges are about to go platinum.” Megumi grinned despite herself. “Remember, appearances can be deceiving. In some cases,” she added with a touch of sarcasm. Her dig went right over his head. “No, no. It’s not that I don’t buy you as a shooter. Although I imagine you’re prettier then most. It’s just that I haven’t seen you toting around a guitar case or anything like that…” “What?” “Like in ‘Desperado’, right? He had that suitcase full of guns. BAM! BAM! He came back to settle the score with someone. Anyone. EVERYONE! POW! POW!” The look on Grappler’s face made Butterfly think the man was a dunce or a simpleton. “No, CYDONIA does not employ snipers, you idiot.” Megumi shook her head and buried herself deeper into the pillow behind her. “I am a shooter. I enforce rules and agreements, collect debts, and when necessary, break bones. I am trained in submission style wrestling, or ‘hooks’. It is not often a woman is taught the art of hooking and in fact, I am sorely lacking in many areas of the style. It is almost a forgotten art form. Therefore, ‘shooter’ will do just fine.” “You are an enforcer for CYDONIA Inc.? Your style in the ring doesn’t seem to be shoot oriented, my dear.” He cocked an eyebrow her way. “Don’t be fooled, Grappler.” Megumi said with confidence. “Oh, believe me. I’m not messing with you for all the arm tassels and sequined robes in the world, my dear. The way you handled that giant oaf in the ring this week showed me enough.” He nodded. “But no wonder the Pro Wrestler Hunter is after you.” She raised an eyebrow in inquiry, and he grinned. “You know, for taking away his business. Perhaps you took his place in the organization. Out with the old, in with the new, that sort of thing. Maybe he doesn’t like you because you’re a girl! Some men, not me of course…” “Of course,” she said with a smile she couldn’t help. “Some men might not take kindly to a mere girl taking their spot as an enforcer. Perhaps now he has become nothing more then a bouncer or a security guard and wants to pay you back.” “It is not as though he had much of a choice,” she said with a sigh of resignation. “Because you’re just that DARN good, is that it?” He said it with a grin. “No,” she answered, not looking up at him, but studying the full sleeve tattoo on her left arm. “Because being a shooter… was my destiny.” “Ooooh. Do tell.” He clasped his chin under his palms and looked at her, batting his eyelashes. “I…” she caught herself. What was it about this man that opened her up and made her spill intimate secrets? She grabbed a hold of herself and rose from the bed. “I think it’s time to go. Good night.”
