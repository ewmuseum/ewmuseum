The Lavelle Chronicles, ch2

8 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11th of April Harrisburg, PA to Albany, NY The Bus Trip. The following extract (s) take place between Monday 4:00pm and Wednesday 11:00 am. Awake but day-dreaming, a common reoccurrence in my life. In College, everyday, I would sit down and dream about my next guitar riff. Or that I was Jimmy Hendrix, up on stage and playing from my heart and soul to a crowd that genuinely liked 'real' music. Then again, I could never be that good. Not without my guitar; I could picture Johnny Whelan, my replacement, damaging my fender. My fender!!! The words hung in the air, for just a few seconds. I wondered why I didn't go back and just take it with me. I regret not doing that now. Home was now a huge sky; it was flat fields of poor land fringed with hawthorn. It was birds in flight and the slight echo from the bus' large engine. It was the columns of the small, yet not so beautiful midges like smoke in summer dusk. Then again, a bus wasn't my home; I was still looking, wherever it may be. The bus drove on by, leaving shadows and dust behind in the long tedious road. I didn't know now what I should've known. Things that mother should've said, but didn't. I felt alone in the world, as though everything I once held had gone. Although, Rob's snoring seemed to reassure that somebody was looking out for me. At least he was getting sleep. I wondered what was next in my life. I already had quite a story to tell; maybe one day, I'll even write about it. Something I am sure that I would do one day. You know; when you're old and grey, and don't have much time left; but you feel you want to leave something of yourself behind. That's when I'll write my story. My life's story, the autobiography of Pierce Lavelle - I like it! The journey to Albany was long and, needless to say, boring. The bad news was that my mini-disc had run out of batteries half way across Pennsylvania, and it was in the middle of a great song: 'Stairway to Heaven' by Led Zeppelin. I wondered how long it would take to get there, to my new home. I remembered Rebecca's voice on the phone call - happy and innocent. Mine, however, was broken and hollow. I could recall the time I first listened to Led Zeppelin; I was 16 and my father had thrown his shoe at me. I know how it sounds - stupid! Yes, of course it is. But hey, when you're depressed, anything sounds good. I was the rocker, the Goth, the pierced kid from a posh street. I didn't know why then, but it seems to throw a shoe at someone is a dishonour in the Muslim religion, which made sense. I was a disinherited 16 year old kid. Anyway, I sat in my room, with my stereo blaring, Mrs. Carmichael yelling from next door: 'Turn that racket off, you hooligan!'. My radio flickered and Thin Lizzy came on, my mind listened; for the first time in a long time, it was listening, not nodding, or shaking, or ignoring. It listened. My life seemed to change; you know when you have those moments, the ones that act as a path of self-discovery, a moment of clarity. A new alignment in your life. The world stops spinning, and in that brief moment, your life flashes before your eyes. For me it felt like that, partly because I was falling unconscious; the heel from the shoe had clipped me on the side of the head, which has always left a scar. Every so often, I would touch the scar gently, to remind myself of that moment. The moment that made me realise who I was… for most of that time I was one of them… but now, now I was an 'I'. Rob was still very much asleep when we came to our next pit-stop. I could smell the sweet smell of the pine forest not too far away. The scent that is all too familiar in these parts. The gas station looked friendly, bar the odd walkabout. I sat up and shook Rob to wake him from his slumber: 'Rise and shine, sleepy head, morning's brewing and so is coffee,' I smiled and continued on my long hike down the double-decker bus. Of course I exaggerated a little there; but when you haven't slept in 24 hours, your muscles tend to become stiff and your awareness, not so aware. The coffee smelt delicious as I opened up the Styrofoam lid. Rob sat quietly staring at his physics book. He had hopes to one day become a medical physicist; I guess maybe that chance had gone. But, his marks said otherwise. I knew he could get into Harbrook, no problem. I, on the other hand, still didn't know what I wanted. At this moment I still couldn't tell my left from my right. My mind was hazy and my thoughts weren't logical. Nothing clicked. 'Coffee boy, I need a little help,' Rob said, looking at me. 'What is it?' I asked, putting down my treasured cup of coffee. 'What in the gods name in this funny looking contraption. I know I do physics, but I can't quite grasp what this machine is,' Rob said, handing me the book. I laughed to myself. Rob, the brainiest guy in 101 physics was having trouble with a sphygmomanometer. 'This Rob, is a device which is used to measure your blood pressure. Your pulse is measured by an ECG, Electrocardiogram.' I smiled to myself, so it appeared I had some idea of what the professors were teaching in class. 'What's the name of this contraption then?' Rob asked, taking back his book. 'Sphygmomanometer,' I turned around, picked up my coffee and went in to buy some new batteries. I knew Rob couldn't pronounce it, or spell it, for that matter; but, it was funny to see him try. I grabbed a pack of double (A) batteries, hoping I had enough money as I approached the counter. The man behind it stared at me, noticing my scruffy hair, my creased clothing and of course the stubble on my chin. Needless to say, he didn't know that I hadn't slept or showered in at least 2 days. So I guess I did look like a hobo from off the street, a walkabout. 'That'll be $4.00, please!' The man behind the counter said. 'Four dollars!' I looked at the back of the box. 'This reads $2.50c.' I knew he was trying to rip me off, but luckily for me I could count! 'I'm afraid that's wrong; now pay or get out,' he said, with a cocky grin. I smiled, and seeing his manager approaching, I threw the box onto the counter and yelled with my highest voice 'RIP OFF' and left. I whistled as I did so; I don't know why, but I felt proud. Standing up to bullies is great. Although he's now probably fired. 'What was that about?' Rob asked. 'Doesn't matter; come on, we will miss the bus,' I said, taking one last gulp from my coffee cup hurriedly. I couldn't quite understand why our bus route hadn't stopped in any of the passing hotels. Although, I didn't want to ask, just in case I would be thrown off, leaving me to hitch hike. Rebecca had called me that afternoon, asking about my whereabouts, and when I would be back. I remembered her words. Funny thing is Mrs. Green, Roberts mom said something about Rob and I, leaving Pennsylvania and going somewhere else. Of course, if my parents knew by now, there would be a search party. Was it the fact Rebecca found out, by herself? Or, only my mother knew. Something I had said a couple of days ago spiked something. It triggered an old memory, that lay deep beneath my mothers frail surface. Now I wonder, whether this time she will finally leave him. 'All passengers! We are reaching Albany, New York. Please make sure you have your passport or identification cards with you. There will be a long delay of routine checks before you lucky folk can jet off to the New York life.' I laughed silently. 'You lucky ones'; except there wasn't anything lucky for me. I was glad the bus trip had ended and the long journey had come to a halt. I think if it had gone on longer, I would have become somewhat philosophical - a scary thought indeed. Rob had always said that if you begin to talk to yourself, inside your head, then you are truly going mad. Of course he usually consulted the mirror in the men's bathroom for advice. I believe I live in my mind; a lot of us do nowadays. I mean, we spend half of our lives studying, thinking and developing our thoughts. Then when we talk to ourselves we are known as 'going insane'. 'Pierce, you have got to stop living in that mind of yours. Wake up!' Rob said, packing away his luggage. The bus had stopped. In front of me was a large sign reading 'WELCOME TO ALBANY!' in block capitals. This was it - my new home. 'I'm awake,' I said, pulling open the luggage hatch to retrieve my luggage. 'Good, because here come the inspection police.' Rob smiled and then spoke once more. 'You know this reminds me of that trip, remember, as freshmen? We all had to line up for the Dean and her inspection police as Pete called them for some sort of drug check. Although, all they found was a bag of sugar for some Home Economics class. A little ironic.' I stood up and routed through my rain coat and found my passport and bus ticket. I just wanted to get off the bus and to have a warm shower; no chance of that happening now. 'Passport or identification cards, please!' One of the policemen said, as he clambered onto the bus. You know when you read about accidents, bus crashes, car crashes and bomb explosions? Well, this was one of those moments. Although, not so dramatic. Two guys up from Rob and I, was a man; a small tanned Mexican, who had stowed away on the bus, without anybodys' realisation. As soon as the policeman approached, he freaked out. Waving his arms in the air, and dancing around like a monkey loose in a field of bananas, he tried to make a beeline for the window but, was caught in the middle of his monkey escape! 'Now that was funny,' A passenger said, watching as the second policeman handcuffed monkey boy and brought him to the police car. I wasn't laughing, though; it was funny, but I wasn't laughing. Finally, Rob and I had our passports checked and we were allowed to leave the hellacious bus, which was my home for 2 and a half days. Albany was very cold. The light showers of rain danced along the pavement as Rob and I, stood under the bus shelter, looking for anything; a hotel or a youth hostile. Anything for us to stay in overnight, until we get our bearings. Rob hailed a taxi and asked for the nearest hotel. The following takes place between Monday, 8:00 pm and 9:00pm The large block capital yellow letters read: 'The Mason Hotel'. I looked around and spotted the well dressed doorman; he was dressed in a shinny velvet waistcoat and black pinstriped trousers. His shoes, shinned to perfection. His top hat acted like a mirror as I stood against it; it was at that moment that I saw my face. My light brown hair was not spiked for the first time in a while. I was growing a beard on my face and my clothes were terribly creased. Rob was having the same problems, only his head was shaved. The doorman looked at me. I knew then we were at a five star hotel. He moved the door reluctantly to let us passed. 'Hello, how may I help you?' the receptionist asked. 'Yeah, we'd like to book a room, two single beds. For two nights,' Rob said, looking through his wallet for his bank card. 'And what name would you like to put it under?' The receptionist asked, refusing to look at me. 'Green. Robert Green,' Rob said, sliding his bank card across. Just then in Rob's very words, it reminded me of James Bond, as he would approach a lady in a film; Bond, James Bond. Shaken, not stirred. My thoughts were interrupted by Rob's voice. 'How much?' '$2000 dollars for the two nights.' The receptionist said, scanning his bank card. The receipt began to print and she slid our room key across the marbled counter. 'Enjoy your stay at the Mason hotel.' I took the key and followed Rob to the elevator. 'I think she likes you!' I laughed. 'Funny, very funny, Pierce. But she's around 50.' Rob pushed the key for flour 6. Our room was '320'. 'Okay, no more jokes. I promise.' I watched the numbers count down. 'Are you going to ring Lois?' I asked. Lois was the love of Rob's life. We met her in our Freshmen year in Thompson college. She was a small sweet woman, with all the womanly features. It was 'coincidence' that Rob and Lois happened to be in Physics 101 together. That's where it all began. The first date was funny; Rob was scared, unsure of what to do or say. So I devised a plan; I would sit in the booth behind him, then I would whisper a response to each question. Of course, Lois found out and wasn't impressed. It was then Rob decided to become a man, he'd grown-up over dinner! He raced after her, and told her everything. They are together 2 years to this day. 'Why would I ring Lois?' he asked, stepping into the elevator. 'Your two year anniversary, don't you remember? Women are very sentimental people. Haven't I taught you anything?' I laughed as the doors closed just before a large Texan man tried to enter the lift. 'Damn it, and what makes it worse is, I'm here in New York. She is going to kill me!' Rob said. The same ol' Rob. Worrying about the little things in life. 'Ask her to come out and meet us, tell her to get a flight, and we'll collect her. Or tell her to wait until we have an apartment out here. Now stop worrying, I need to contact my grandfather in Bel-Air. He might be able to give us one of his showroom properties here.' Rob was stunned. 'Showroom properties? Man, they are made for the rich and dare I say famous. They are penthouses. You have to get us that showroom,' Rob said, acting like a small boy in a sweet shop. He had cheered up. I had accomplished my goal for this evening. Bed, I thought to myself. Bed, a soft warm bed. Something I hadn't had the luxury of in the last two nights. Soon I would become an insomniac. 'Room 320,' Rob said. He unlocked the door. The room was huge. Both bedrooms were separate; like the upstairs of a house, each one with it's own en suite bathroom. The kitchen wasn't great; but, we only needed room service. I ran into my room and collapsed on the bed. Peace at last. The following takes place between, Monday 10:00pm and 10:15 pm I couldn't quite put my finger on what had happened in the brief moment I went to sleep, and Rob's whimpering in the en suite bathroom. I knew the phone call with Lois hadn't gone so well; but as the saying goes: 'There's plenty more fish in the sea'. Only how many fish must you catch, until you have the right one? What if they become extinct? The TV flickered periodically in the background as I slid around and picked up the phone, hoping for a free place to stay. I dialled the Bel-Air area code and then punched in my grandfathers number. The phone rang. The TV's light flickered behind me creating a pantomime out of the shadows bouncing off the walls. 'Hello, Charles. This is Pierce, I would like to speak to my grandfather.' I kept it plain and simple. Charles, of course, was my grandfathers butler. He was quite a stuck up man, but he was excellent at his job. 'I'm sorry, but your grandfather is too busy. He's away in Bermuda with his new gold-digger for a bride.' Charles said, obviously not happy with my grandfather's taste in women. Sometimes I felt that was also a cursed gene among the men of the Lavelle family. Although, sometimes I think my grandfather wanted to be very much like Hugh Heffner. 'Thanks,' with that I hung up. Our plans for the penthouse had flown the coop, so to speak. I knew the blonde, skinny 28-year-old would be living there soon enough. I sat up and turned back on the sound of the TV. I needed to think of money, something I hadn't thought about in a while. I wondered what it was I could do! Maybe work for a retailer, or something like any normal college student would do. A video shop, music shop or waiter. I didn't know. So many choices and I still hadn't chosen what it was I wanted to do when I get the right amount of money. I ran my hand along my chin as I was thinking; it was then I could feel the sharp bristles of my beard. I knew I needed a shower; I had gotten some sleep, but it was a shower and shave I really needed. The following takes place between, Tuesday 8:00 am and 9:30 am. Rob and I decided after we were refreshed to go and check out the local Pizza Hut on Western Avenue. They had job applications for full time workers needed. Hey, any money is good enough for a man in my situation. Good thing was the Pizza Hut was near to the Harbrook college, which suited both myself and Rob perfectly. Open days, I said to myself constantly. That would be the time I would step into a college. Then I would decide on my future. 'Here it is,' Rob said, staring at the Pizza Hut. I lifted my hand in front of my face, to read my scribbled handle writing: 1767 Western Avenue, PIZZA HUT Phone 1223 PIZZA HUT. I looked at the pavement to check the address. We were here. Rob opened the door and I followed. The place smelt gorgeous. My stomach began to talk to me; 'FEED ME', I could hear it yelling, amongst the gurgles of acid. If I didn't eat, it would eat itself. After all, in the last three days, all I had to eat was a doughnut and some leftover biscuits from Rob. 'Excuse me,' Rob said to one of the waiters behind the counter. 'Yes,' he replied. 'We are here to apply for the full time waiters needed.' Rob stared at the sign as he said this. 'Well, welcome aboard,' the waiter replied, handing me and Rob an apron. 'What!' I said, a little astonished that it was so easy to get a job. 'We need help; the college students come here after hours, and we are run off our feet. By the way, call me Gavin. You start now. You are paid $7.50 an hour, plus tips. So get to work!' Gavin said. I laughed to myself and wrapped the apron around my black 'Metallica' shirt. I pulled a pen and pad out of the apron and approached the nearest table to me. 'How can I serve you?' I said. This was great; my first job ever. Now look at me, 18 years of my life, I never had a teenage life. Now I was working a job, got expelled from college and living my own life, in New York. Who would have ever guessed! The following takes place between, Tuesday 7:30 pm and 8:00 pm. I wasn't used to 11 hour shifts; they became tiresome. Rob, Gavin and I were left to clean up after the last customer had left. I began to put the chairs onto the table when a group of girls from Harbrook dorms entered. I stopped what I was doing and waited for Gavin to make a move. 'Ladies!' he said, something I wasn't expecting. The sign said closed, but Gavin's mouth said 'Come in.' Rob pulled my sleeve and we stepped away from the herd of women. 'What's going on? Can we leave yet?' whispered Rob. 'Ladies, I feel a make-over coming on,' Gavin said, dancing around a girl later known as Sandra. 'He's gay!' I said, out loud of course. Another cursed gene in the Lavelle gene pool. 'Yes, yes I am…' Gavin said, tip toeing around the counter top. 'I'm sorry, I didn't mean it in that way,' I said, trying to regain some respect. 'Oh, well when you say it that way. Which is your favourite colour….red or pink?' Gavin showed me two different lip-sticks. 'What?!' I didn't know what to say; Rob was behind me laughing of course. 'Come on girls, I think these honey's need a make-over,' Gavin said, pushing me onto a seat. I must say I was quite comfortable with all the lady attention, but not so much the make-up and air brushes. 'Well, I guess I'll be leaving then,' Rob said, heading to the door. 'Oh no, you are the cutie, I'm making you over honey,' Gavin said, seating Rob down. Now this was embarrassing. When they had finished their torture, Rob and I retreated to the men's bathroom, our sanctuary away from the girls and, well, the 'girl'. I looked at myself in the mirror and it reminded me of a time when I was younger and I saw a certain clown; since then I've never been able to go to the circus. My eyes were a pale blue colour above the eyelid. My lips a bright red and my cheeks were….'PINK'. Pink, the one colour I hated, I despised. I can't believe I look like this; but Rob, now that was a sight. 'Well, at least we went down together,' I said, washing the make-up off. 'You got it easy,' Rob said, looking at his fake eyelashes. 'I look like a drag queen.' 'I guess that's why he hired us, eh, cutie!' I laughed when I said this. Rob was blushing, but I couldn't tell from the pink cheeks he already had on. 'So what now?' he asked. 'What do you mean?' I said. 'Should we quit?' Rob said, wiping off the lip-stick. 'Hell no, I like all this womanly attention,' I said, drying my hands. My face was back to normal, at last. 'That's easy for you to say,' Rob said. 'I get manly attention, you know?' 'Yep, I like Gavin, though, and he likes you,' I laughed. 'Come on, we'll be late. Cold Case is starting,' Rob said. Myself and Rob headed out of the bathroom and back into the main lounge. The girls were all seated on the long couches, singing and drinking. It was then I spotted her; my heart felt as though it had skipped a beat. She walked in the door just as I entered the room, her books in hand. Gavin ran over and hugged her, of course. 'Lilly, I would like you to meet my two new friends,' Gavin said, bringing Lilly over to us. 'This is Pierce and Rob.' My heart almost jumped out of my mouth, my palms sweaty. I hadn't felt this way about a girl in a while. She wasn't an eye catcher, but she was something. I stood still watching Rob introduce himself. Lilly, I said to myself. Only I couldn't get the words out... The following takes place between, Tuesday 10:00 pm and 11:00 pm In the space of two hours, I had learnt so much about Lilly. Everything about her fascinated me. Lilly went to Harbrook College, where she was studying 'History of Art', a subject I would now like to study. She was 21 and had spent most of her life here in Albany. Gavin had said a lot about her for me; I was far too shy to approach her. I guess in a way, I was grateful that Gavin spotted my insecurity before I did something stupid. Rob left an hour before me; he didn't want to stay around any longer than he had to. I knew he missed Lois; who wouldn't. But I couldn't leave, not until I got the nerves to ask her out. 'Stop being so shy,' Gavin said, drinking down a vodka and coke. 'I'm not shy; it's just I've never felt this way about someone,' I replied while staring at Lilly. 'You mean Lilly?' inquired Gavin. 'Yeah, she's amazing,' I said, now opening a new bottle of Sprite. 'Well, I'm sorry to tell you Pierce, but she's with someone,' Gavin replied. I had only known about this girl for an hour, and hearing that broke my heart. It was as though somebody had smashed a very delicate mirror; and the pieces slowly began to crumble. I knew then I had to just give up. He was probably the 'hunk' of the college. I had no chance. Without a goodbye to any of the girls I packed my things and left the 'Pizza Hut'. I knew I couldn't change fate. Ah well, nothing a coffee couldn't fix. I looked at my watch and realised I had so many hours to kill before my next shift started. Rob would be sitting in our hotel room, watching movies, over and over, to kill his depression and feelings for Lois. In a way, I felt responsible for the break-up. After all, he did come with me to New York; he didn't stay for her. I couldn't think about that now. Not after my own heart had been crushed. Some say, that in times like these the best you can do is move on; but I never even got the chance to talk to her. Maybe I could become her friend, or just an acquaintance. 'Excuse me?' A voice said behind me. I turned around and there she was. Lilly was talking to me. I was quite surprised. She had followed me out of the Pizza Hut, down the long street toward the college. 'Yes,' I replied. I could feel my hands start to shake. I had never been so nervous. 'I saw you leaving. You seemed upset; is everything okay?' she asked. Somebody actually genuinely wanted to know if I was okay. I couldn't tell her everything, could I? 'I'm fine. So, you walking back to your dorm?' I asked, my first question. I waited for her to reply. 'No, I'm waiting for my boyfriend. Would you like to wait with me?' she asked. Her 'boyfriend'. It was official. I had been dumped, before I had even started to talk to her. Lilly would be a pleasure to wait for, one day. 'I'm afraid I can't,' I said. Lilly didn't reply to me as I continued my way back to the 'Mason Hotel'. I just hoped one day, I could hold Lilly. Like this 'boyfriend' of hers. Meanwhile, I knew I would have to rescue Rob, from the insecurity of his own thoughts, otherwise he might start talking to the mirror once more. The following takes place between, Tuesday 11:00 pm and 11:30 pm When I arrived home, Rob was seated on the large sofa, watching a 'Legally Blonde' run through. I could tell it was that time of year. Already both myself and Rob had gone from being big shots in school, having a band and girls, to having nothing. I began to feel that this whole running away from my past life was a large mistake. But, then again, maybe it wasn't so bad. 'Have fun?' Rob asked, turning his head to face me. 'No, not really. You?' I asked. 'No!' Rob said, pulling out a box of Kleenex tissues. I could remember a time as kids, myself and Rob had wandered off in the large park just outside the estate. We decided we would hang around with the big kids, so we did. Rob hoped onto his BMX bike and began cycling, only he didn't see the trash cans in front of him. Before long he went head first over the bike and landed on the gravel. I remember then, Rob's tears and the box of tissues his mom had brought down. Rob had always been a little more sensitive than most men - some thought he might even be gay. But I knew he wasn't. I knew the tears were for Lois, not for 'Legally Blonde'. 'Didn't go so well with Lois, did it?' I asked, now seated beside him. Changing the movie to something which had action, blood and loads of violence. What we arrived at was 'The Punisher'. 'No, I think Lois will break-up with me. What I worry about is George Scott-Lewis,' Rob said, removing the box of tissues. George Scott-Lewis was on the Thompson College water polo team. He had always had a thing for Lois, ever since Rob and Lois had been together. George made it his business to try and break them up. Although, his plans never worked. Lois hated the men on the water polo team; partly because as a Freshman, they chucked her into the pool with her class assignment as a college prank. Rob was there to fend them off, which only brought Lois and Rob closer together. 'I hate to mention this now, but if we don't find a place by tomorrow, we will be sleeping in the dumpsters,' I said, remembering we had only booked the hotel for two nights. And our time was up. I worried now what was in store for me. The following takes place between, Wednesday 8:00 am and 10:00 am The next morning, I had received a phone call from my grandfather. He had returned from Bermuda that night; unfortunately he couldn't give me the penthouse apartment, because his new wife wanted to rent it out to her friends. The best he could do was give me a small two bedroom apartment near western avenue; which as a matter of fact suited me fine. I knew then that I would only be a few minutes away from work and a few minutes away from Lilly. Rob had taken the news extremely well. I think the movie marathon had cheered him up, because when I woke up, he was packed and dressed, waiting for the move out. I knew he would have to call Lois soon to fix things, something I felt he had to do. 'Okay, I have the address,' I said, handing Rob the piece of paper. 1890 Western Avenue Apartment 12 Room Key is at front desk. Furniture is already there, from Grandfather. S.H. Lavelle 'Nice little message,' Rob said, taking the page. 'Yeah. So are you ready?' I asked, zipping up my duffle bag. Rob and I still had quite a lot of money to spend between us. Rob decided he would buy a ticket for Lois so she could come over. I thought it was a good idea. 'As soon as we are settled you may as well ring Lois,' I said. The elevator doors now closed. The hotel would soon just be another memory in the life of Pierce Lavelle, me. I hoped in some ways that I would see Lilly once more on my way to work; but they were only dreams. 'So this place, what's it like?' Rob asked. 'I don't know, I've never been here,' I replied. On the way to the apartment, Rob spotted a flyer floating in the wind. The street was covered in them. I then noticed Rob's face. He became fairly hyper, an emotion of his which usually held good or bad things. He approached me with the flyer; soon I became a little hyper myself. It was my chance at something big. I turned the flyer over and was greeted by a tropical sunset background: Atlantic Wrestling Club Applications open. 8:00 till 11:00 interviews for wrestlers Park Avenue, 145 street. 'You should go try out,' Rob stated. 'Yeah,' I folded the flyer up and put it in my back pocket. I only hoped for a chance at something as big as a wrestling career. Hopefully my college wrestling awards would win me a place in this AWC company. The following takes place between, Wednesday 10:00 am and 10:35 am The apartment was brand new. I couldn't believe it. The apartment block was inhabited by some very wealthy people. The car park was filled, from top to bottom, with Mustangs, BMW's, Mercedes and Escalades. I felt a little out of place as myself and Rob arrived. When we reached room '12' we were greeted by a large oak door. This place was too good to be true. The door opened quickly. On the left wall was a high-tech security system. In the middle of the room was a large 'L' shaped, leather sofa and a TV. I knew then that my grandfather had gone to a lot of trouble to make me feel at home. I suppose I knew why. My grandfather and father don't speak anymore. My grandfather used to be a famous surgeon but soon gave up the job for the glamour and showbiz of cosmetic surgery. My grandfather hated the way his son would treat me and my mother. He left when I was 11 years old. Since then I have only spoken to him when my father was out of the house. If I was caught, I would've had the shoe thrown at me, which was a common punishment in my house. My grandfather soon moved to Bel-Air when I was around 17. Since then, he and his wife have lived there. I was very grateful for the card which had been dealt to me. 'Whoa,' Rob said. 'What is it?' I asked, a little stunned by Rob's sudden yelling of 'whoa'. 'We have our own double beds,' Rob said. 'What? Are you going mad, Rob?' I was confused now, because Rob never got excited over double beds. It was only until I saw the two different rooms. Rob's was designed like an aquarium, He had a fish tank at the end of his bed and a large circular double bed with tropical bed covers. Blue neon lined the corners of the ceiling. I was taken back at first. I worked in Pizza Hut and I lived in a house like this… 'Everything okay?' Rob asked. 'Yeah,' I replied, still in shock at everything that has been done for me by my grandfather. 'You should check out your room!' Rob stated, collapsing onto his bed. He picked up the phone. 'I'm going to ring Lois; I'm going to tell her everything.' I left Rob alone in his room, and crossed the long hallway until I came to my room. I had the larger bedroom. My bed was square. The usual, but my bed sheets had a large cross on the them. My wall was lined with two guitars. I was shocked. I pulled one of the guitars down and began to play. It has been a long time my old friend, I said to myself as I continued with my playing. It was then I saw the time. It was 9:30pm already. Time was flying by, I knew I had to run to the AWC meeting room. I yelled goodbye to Rob and ran out of the fancy apartment with my own key. I quickly hailed a taxi and made my way to the AWC meetings, for the interview. Maybe even a new job. This could be my chance, my new start… View Pierce Lavelle 's Biography
