Life After Death

24 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 24, 2006 May 23, 2006 I’m confused. I don’t understand why Meredith keeps looking at me the way she does. I don’t remember her ever staring at me like this before. It’s really very strange, but I assume it has something to do with me being, as she says, sexy. My knowledge of such things is limited to books I’ve read on sex and relationships. Somehow, I do not think they will assist me in handling these new feelings. Sometimes I miss being an emotional void. At least, in those days, I didn’t have to worry about hurting someone else’s feelings. I didn’t care. I wonder why I care now. In other news, I’ve been researching the accident. It seems that many stories have been told in an effort to keep what happened to me a secret. In order to get to the bottom of this, I’m going to have to seek out those that started these rumors and question them. Perhaps with force, but hopefully such things will not be needed. I simply want to know the truth. I want to know why it was I that was selected for Project Prodigy. Hell, I’d like to know how I even know about Project Prodigy in the first place. I suspect it has something to do with nightmares I keep having. The answers will come soon enough. For now? Dinner. * * * * * * * * * * * “Mine!” “No, it’s mine! Let it go!” “Mooooooooooooom!” Michael and Michelle, the twins, are in the process of fighting over something once again. Sprawled out on the living room floor, they tug on different ends of the video game controller, pulling and tugging with great vigor. Obviously, somebody didn’t get their turn or something. Meredith, bless her heart, has just come in the door. She’s in the process of snatching her coat off, when she hears the ruckus coming from the family room. She rolls her eyes some, tosses her coat onto the rack and breaks off into a jog into the living room. “What’s wrong?” she asks, as if she didn’t know. Michael’s the first one to talk. He’s always the first one to talk. “Mom! It’s my turn! She totally died and now she won’t give up the controller!” Michelle, as always, has a different story, “Nuh-uh! Mom! Listen, this is what happened! I was playing first and he was over there playing something else and then I won and I accidentally pressed the wrong button and died because I didn’t know there was a hole right there and it’s not fair because I didn’t know so it’s still my turn!” Michelle always talks the most. Always. Meredith smirks and holds her hand out. “Give it here.” Michael and Michelle both go into pout mode, “But Moooooom!” Meredith narrows her eyes and clears her throat. She shouldn’t have to ask more than once. Hell, she shouldn’t have to ask. Michael sighs and lets go of the cord, thus allowing Michelle to slap the controller into her mother’s hand. “The solution to this is simple, kids.” Meredith says with a small smile, stepping towards the television and the gaming system on the floor in front of it. “It’s my turn!” She cackles playfully and unpauses the game, mashing away at the buttons! Michael looks at Michelle. Michelle looks at Michael. They both fall into giggles and attack their mother for all their little eight year old bodies are worth. It isn’t long before the three of them are rolling all over the floor, tickling and laughing with each other. This is, definitely, what they call a Kodak moment. Although, in this house, it happens to often that you really don’t need a camera to capture it. Lincoln stands in the archway of the dining room. Yes, he’s wearing an apron, but he’s manly enough to make it work. His arms are crossed over his chest and he merely observes the happiness of a family that loves each other very much. Unfortunately, though, he’s never really had the chance to experience such a life. Only must recently has he even been subjected to a loving and nurturing family environment. He finds the whole thing quite weird, actually. “Dinner.” he says, with absolutely no emotion in his tone. Though, he’s not mad or anything. He’s just... Lincoln. Without even waiting for the family to finish their antics, he turns on his heels and heads into the dining room. Meredith knocks her children off her with a playful shove and gets back up to her feet. “You two go wash your hands.” she says, patting them and sending them off to the nearest washroom. Meanwhile, she heads over to the stairs and smacks the wall. “Stephanie!! Dinner!” With that done, she high tails it into the dining room, kicking her shoes the rest of the way off to see what’s cooking. “Need a hand with anything?” Lincoln’s standing over a piping hot full course meal, checking and double checking his sauce. He shakes his head, “No. Just sit down. I’ll serve.” Meredith frowns some, “You always serve.” Lincoln nods. Meredith sighs and moves into her seat at the head of the table. “Lincoln. I didn’t invite you to live here so you could be a slave to us. I invited you here because I care about you.” “Understood, Meredith. However, with my lack of income, I feel it is my duty to fulfill my share of ‘pulling the weight’, by providing and keeping your house running like a well oiled machine.” Lincoln’s response is dry and rehearsed. Meredith raises an eyebrow. “Have you been watching reruns of Who’s The Boss again?” Lincoln looks up from the sauce and starts pulling his apron off, offering her the slightest of smiles. Of course, they don’t get to share a moment of peace since Stephanie, the teenager, appears in the room. How does one know? The constant yapping that’s coming from her mouth as she retells some story of what happened in school today to whomever that is on the phone. Meredith rolls her eyes and watches as Steph makes her way to her seat. Timing it perfectly (because she’s a Mom and she can do that), she snatches the phone from Steph’s grip and utters, “She’ll call you back.” before hanging up on whomever that was. Stephanie. Stares. “Mom!” “No phone at the dinner table. You know that.” Stephanie sighs overdramatically and throws herself into her chair. While she’s having her fit, the twins come racing into the dining room and everyone manages to get themselves seated without too much trouble. Hands are held to form a circle around the food and heads go down as Lincoln says grace... * * * * * * * * * * “Stephanie! Help your brother and sister get ready for bed!” Meredith yells through the house, as she dumps some dishes into the sink. “Augh! MOM! I’m on the phone!” Stephanie’s voice rings back in that ever so irritated tone. But twenty bucks says she helps anyway. Meredith rolls her eyes and grabs a dish towel to dry her hands. That’s when she notices that the patio door is open. Sighing softly, she tosses the towel onto her shoulder and steps out into the night. “Link?” Lincoln’s standing outside, right on the patio. He doesn’t move. Hardly even acknowledges her. In fact, he’s merely standing there (perfect posture and all) staring up at the night sky. Almost as if he’s trying to read the stars or something. Hell, knowing him, he could probably do it. “So many questions, Meredith. So many.” Lincoln whispers, with just a hint of frustration in his voice. “When am I going to get some answers to them?” His question comes out more as a plea than an request or an order. It’s obvious he’s tired of not knowing who he is. Not knowing where he’s going. Not knowing why any of this happened in the first place. Meredith’s eyes soften. She hates to see him in such emotional pain. She honestly just wants him to be okay. She walks up behind him and places a hand on his shoulder. “I don’t know, Link. I don’t know.” She hesitates for a brief moment, but then she’s on her tiptoes and planting a comforting kiss on his cheek. Her lips linger for a moment, as does her hand on his shoulder, but that’s it. She turns and heads back inside, leaving him to his thoughts. Lincoln doesn’t move. He just continues to stare at the stars. 'It's time to leave.' he whispers softly to himself. Fade.
