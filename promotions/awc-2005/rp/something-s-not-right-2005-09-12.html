Something's Not Right...

12 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/9/05 1. Ashes To Ashes…. The jagged thorns of the single red rose are being embedded into the palm of his hand. Yet he holds it tighter. Of course it should hurt as he feels the thin stream of blood meandering in and out on the surface of his palm. But he’s beyond pain now. One of the reasons he kept everything upstairs is now gone and he’s got nothing left. Nothing. Paddy O’Shea dressed in his best suit is standing beside the grave of his only real friend trying to cry. But he’s got nothing left to well up in his eyes so now he must stay silent and still. He got his suit pressed yesterday, the first time in five years. Paddy knows May would have scowled him for going to such measures on her account but she’s not here anymore so that doesn’t matter. What does matter is that he is here as she is laid to rest. The fact he’s the only one here now means that much more. Paddy wipes the back of his hand over his reddened cheeks, stained by the tears that have long since dried. His hand passes over his clean-shaven face. Another mark of respect. Another thing May would outwardly scowl about but secretly appreciate it. Paddy runs a hand through his combed hair and finally has the courage to open his eyes. The simple mahogany coffin now hovers above the open grave. Its cheap design is apparent. It almost looks like chipboard for gods’ sake Paddy thinks. The coffin she had long since ordered and reserved for herself. Simple and honest, the story of May’s life. The priest who has been reciting the rites of the burial for the last ten minutes to a congregation of one now turns to Paddy and nods. Paddy knows exactly what to do of course. He’s had uncles and friends killed off by some disease or another or being shot dead in the arms of their Northern comrades. Paddy drops the rose onto the coffin reluctantly. And then tightens that fist into a grip. Just like his mind, his body is completely numb, without direction or thought. “Ashes to ashes, dust to dust…” the priest mutters solemnly yet Paddy has already tuned out again. May, the women who had helped him become a man in the way his father couldn’t have. The woman who had set him principles to stick by and entrenched in him the determination to stand by them. And secretly, the only woman he’d ever loved…was gone forever. A hand gently clasping his shoulder pulls him from his state. “Paddy…?” The anxious priest, Father McClenaghan has seen the burial of an individual countless times before yet somehow he can sense this is different Paddy looks around slowly with a devastated face frozen in time, his eyes screaming desperation. “Aye’ll be fine Father…thank ye…aye just need a minute alone please.” The priest releases his grip with a heavy heart then walks away leaving Paddy alone as the coffin is lowered into the abyss below. He suddenly remembers every single good deed she ever did for him. Protecting him when in need. His guardian angel. Paddy suddenly feels the strength in his legs give and he has to quickly grasp hold of a nearby tree for fear of falling. Yet the time seems right so he falls onto his knees, sinking slightly in the soggy mud. “May,” Paddy stutters slowly and painfully. “Thank ye. There’s nothin’ much else aye can think o’ sayin’ – aye’ve suddenly gone all feckered as she would say. Paddy smiles as he remembers May’s witty tongue and fighting spirit despite her frailty. He body deteriorated with age yes but she always was resilient and she always kept her wonderful mind. “Ye taught me t’ demand equality an’ how t’ fight prejudice. Ye undid the entire hurt aye suffered before aye met ye. But most o’ all, ye taught me how t’ do be a man. An’ fer tha’, aye will always remember ye and love ye.” Suddenly a light breeze touches Paddy’s neck before moving on. He trembles from the cold but somehow he knows, someone’s sending him a message. And for the first time in what seems like an eternity he feels the ends of the lips curl upwards ever so slightly. Feeling the strength return to him, Paddy slowly stands up, using his left hand to pry himself from the ground, letting it moisten form the morning dew. Now Paddy turns to leave. Step by step he edges away. He sees the encouraging blue sky patched with white. He sees the sun, hampered by another passing cloud. Then he spots a flash of silver from the peripheral off his eye. Turning in that direction intrigued he sees hanging from the tree over looking Mays’ grave, a chain and pendant. Without thinking a snatches it and traces the engravements with his index finger. The design of the Romanian flower, underneath it a phrase he can’t understand. Yet another thing that causes him great pain. But what is important is the fact that he knows who this belonged to. How did May’s favourite chain get here? 2. Rodger Evans This office needs a feckin’ makeover Two days have passes since the funeral processions and Paddy has finally allowed himself to begin the mourning process. Don’t stand around and wait, May would have said, or life will fly on past you. Now Paddy is in his lawyers’ office to go through the usual post-death proceedings. Yet every time Paddy is in this compressed office, he can’t help but help depressed. The slate coloured walls, the Victorian style furniture. It’s difficult for him to cope here but his lawyers’ a friendly guy. Now if only this fecker would hurry up Paddy is lying back in the brown leather, swivel chair contemplating his future. In his arms is a baby. The same baby he found abandoned on the day May died. The baby he said he would pass onto social services. The same baby that won’t let him do this by some emotional magnetism. The same baby girl he has named May. But that’s natural of course. May gurgles at him and Paddy coos back. It’s nice to be responsible for a change he thinks, devoting his life for the happiness for another. And if he keeps moving along from place to place with the AWC, who will know? It’s not like he can apply to adopt the child, his criminal record denies him this. So for now, Paddy and May are one in the same. But from now on he’s got to be extra vigilant, the threat of danger is looming ever so darker. My home Paddy’s mind flashes back to his home. The mobile home he’d spent his entire AWC tenure in. All the memorabilia, the T-shirts, the programmes, the awards – everything that told him that for once in his godforsaken life he was acting like a man and doing a decent trade. But not only that, his pictures, his possessions and trinkets. His entire personality that has moulded him into what he is burnt to a crisp. And once his name flashes once again before his eyes – Hate. That bastard. He will pay. Paddy can feel the anger being etched across his face but this quickly subsides when he sees Mays frightened little face. So delicate and vulnerable. So dependent on him. The time to be angry will come. Now is not that time however. Suddenly the door swings open with authority and a tall olive skinned man enters the room, his dazzling smile the reciprocal of Paddy’s mood. His black, greased back hair looks almost as dodgy as the knocked off navy pinstriped suit he’s wearing but Paddy has never really taken this into account, because of the fact he himself is somewhat lacking in the fashion department. “Paddy! My old boy!” The man almost stumbles over himself as he rushes to embrace Paddy. However he stops after seeing the baby in his arms. “What’s with the kid?” “Aye’m just baby sittin’ fer me cousin Rodg, don’ worry about it.” A grin suddenly appears across Rodgs’ face. All of a sudden the Cheshire cat from Alice in Wonderland pops into his head. “Baby sitting? Yeah right!” Let me introduce you to Rodger Evans, Paddy’s lawyer situated in New York who has travelled out to Ireland to sort out May’s will. He initially was only his lawyer because of the cheapness of his services. He remained his lawyer because he reminded Paddy of himself. Despite this however, Paddy knew fine rightly this man would sell his granny if a deal looked good. “Soooo…the will then. So you got everything huh? Good show old chap. Pity the old bat- “Watch it,” interrupted Paddy with a lot more bite then Rodger could handle. “Woman, sorry. Pity she didn’t have a lot but oh well. The house is yours of course. Not worth a lot but it’s yours. Any valuables of course, I’d get around there and claim it quick. If you know what I mean!” Rodger winks after saying this, clearly not getting the jist of Paddy’s mood. Paddy however ignores this. He couldn’t change Rodger even if he tried. “Fine, fine. Aye’ll head over in a while. In the meantime, aye want ye t’ keep an eye out fer a man or group called The Hand” “The Hand?” Rodger questions. High up his brow, a bead of sweat emerges, “Just trust me on’ tha’. Right, aye’m off.” “Bye now” Rodger waits to Paddy closes the door before he pulls out a tissue and wipes his brow. For a minute there, Rodger thought Paddy was playing baton. The Hand As clear as day, planted on the wall…a handprint of blood, plastered on the wall. A note scribbled underneath it…”come back to me” Paddy had entered the house along if only to reminisce when he’d seen it. Entering into May’s once warm room he’d seen it. What the hell had happened? The police had been in this house to recover May’s body, they would have seen this if it was there before. Yet now, the Hand. The same messenger as before was gleaming at him. Somebody was sending a message. When Paddy had seen it he had stumbled backwards and knocked into a locker causing it to topple, it’s contents falling everywhere. Yet one of the contents caught his eye in his dazed state. A used needle now replace back into its bottle. The bottle reading “Potassium Sulphite”…in other words poison. May had had a heart attack yes… But somebody had induced it… Somebody had murdered May. Paddy suddenly felt his eyes being dragged back to the handprint. The Hand…the answer was before his eyes.
