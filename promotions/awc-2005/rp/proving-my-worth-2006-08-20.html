Proving my Worth

20 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-20-06 She felt like a woman for the first time in her life. For the first time, she saw the world and everything around her through eyes that had matured. These feelings came to her after having her special date with the man she loved, Jack Parker. He took her to a special place with the most beautiful scenery she’d seen in years, and things changed after that. Losing her virginity to her love made her feel like she was worth something, made her feel empowered, and for the first time since living in Boston, made her feel at home. It was never Kiyoko’s intention to find a boyfriend after moving from Tokyo to Boston; it simply happened. It occurred to her that she was destined to meet Jack Parker on that beautiful spring day. She thought of him as someone who wanted to kidnap her, do what wanted to her and probably killed her when he was done. Jack Parker wasn’t like those perverted child molesters. He had the look of one, but his soft, gentle personality broke that image. He gave her the love and care she was looking for since she was a child. Sure, her parents loved her and cherished her, but she wanted someone else besides her parents to take notice of her. Kiyoko’s parents were always against her when it came to dating - her father, Hige especially - and it made her feel like she was never going to find love. Then Jack Parker came along, and all of that changed. Kiyoko Norazu found the love she meant to look for when she grew older, but found it at the age of seventeen, despite her parents’ strict orders. Fortunately for Kiyoko, Hige and Kaori Norazu had no idea that their only child was seeing someone behind their back, and hadn’t hold them about it. Kiyoko was afraid to tell them about it, so it remained a secret between Jack and herself. Jack wouldn’t introduce himself to his parents and Kiyoko wouldn’t mention that she found herself a boyfriend, especially someone who was seven years old and American. Kiyoko knew that her father would drive himself up a wall if he ever found out Jack Parker. Hige was a man of tradition, and when Kiyoko reached a certain age, she must find herself a Japanese man to marry. Hige made it emphatically clear that there wasn’t going to be any interracial relationships and no “crossbreeding,” as he once put it in a discuss he had with Kiyoko when she was only thirteen. He also told her in their discussion that if he ever found out that his daughter was in a relationship with someone who wasn’t Japanese, he would denounce her from his family and life immediately. Crossbreeding and interracial relationships did not run in the Norazu family, and he’d see to it that it didn’t happen anytime soon. Two months had passed since Kiyoko met and fell in love with Jack Parker. Summer made its introduction in Boston, school was released for the year and Kiyoko was up in her room, lying back on her bed, knees raised up to balance a book on her stomach she was reading. The novel was In Cold Blood, written by Truman Capote, about the horrific slayings of the Clutter family in Holcomb, Kansas on November 14, 1959. Jack had suggested the book to Kiyoko, telling her that it was his all-time favorite piece of literature he’d ever read. That part was true; Jack enjoyed the terrifying depiction of Capote’s story of the brutal slayings done by Richard Hickcock and Perry Smith. Kiyoko had wrinkled her nose when she heard the title, sounded like some sort of horror novel to her. She remembered asking him if it was a scary book and he said no, but the detail in which the author described can be a bit scary. Other than that, it’s a bona fide page-turner. Jack was right about the book being a page-turner. Truman Capote’s writing style fascinated Kiyoko, extremely intrigued her and she wondered if she could ever write a book this good. When she borrowed Jack’s copy of In Cold Blood, she discovered that she had nearly read seven chapters. She was so enthralled with the story that she didn’t realize how far she actually gotten. Some parts made her wince, felt her stomach turn at some of the graphic details, but she recovered and didn’t slam the book shut to save herself from more graphic material. She heard her name being called from downstairs, telling her that it was time for dinner. She groped for her bookmark, placed it in the middle of the two pages she was reading and underlined the next paragraph she was about to read with a pencil. She knew that marking lines in Jack’s book wouldn’t bother him; he had the same thing. She’d noticed multiple lines underneath paragraphs that hadn’t been erased properly. Some of them were recent, and others looked like they’ve been there for years. She closed her book, placed it on the nightstand and left her bedroom. The delicious smell of Japanese cuisine made Kiyoko smile and her mouth water as she downstairs to have her mother’s home meals. Hige Norazu threw his glass against the wall, smashing it into pieces after he heard Kiyoko’s inadvertent news. Kiyoko made the mistake by answering her mother’s question (even though it was a joke; Kyoko and Kaori always joked about how Kiyoko had so many boyfriends in her life), and it was the biggest mistake she’d ever done. She wished she could take back those words and said “Of course not, Mother,” and gave her a conspiratorial wink, continuing with their joke (making Kiyoko’s father oblivious to it ), and continued with their lovely dinner. No, that wasn’t the fairytale answer she had in mind. The words “Yes, Mother, I have a boyfriend, and his name is Jack Parker” inadvertently slipped out of her lips, and she could not catch them in time. There was an uncomfortable silence hovering over the Norazu family, as Hige stared at his daughter with anger and shock. He had an inkling for the longest time that his daughter had found someone and didn’t have the gall to tell him. His suspicions grew when he discovered that Kiyoko’s mood had improved after the school year ended, all giddy and giggly like some tartly schoolgirl, instead of her normal introvert, loner self, and with Kiyoko’s announcement at the dinner table, his suspicious were true. Hige’s life - hell, his entire world - had fallen apart. Kiyoko had gone back on his orders and now he’s looking at the girl who has betrayed his trust and desecrated the Norazu name. He raised to his feet, his eyes remained locked on his daughter, but he couldn’t bring himself to say anything to her. Kaori watched her husband carefully; when he got mad, he’ll take his anger out physically, and she knew that she’s going to take his anger out on the man called Jack Parker. She slowly raised to her feet, ready to calm her husband down, but his arm shot up, showing her his palm, which told her Sit back down, Kaori! I’m not going to do anything...yet. Kaori lowered herself uneasily into her chair, keeping her eyes on her infuriated husband. Hige was no small man, he stood six feet, maybe six feet, one inch, and weighed about two hundred and twenty, maybe two hundred and thirty pounds. (In other words, he’s larger than your average Japanese man, standing merely five feet, seven inches tall, and weighed about one hundred and seventy pounds.) All of his muscles had tightened, his clenched fists made his forearms and biceps bulge. (The short sleeves on his shirt were on the urge to split in Incredible Hulk fashion.) Veins from his neck and forehead were bulging and throbbing, and if looks could kill, he would’ve dropped Kiyoko where she sat. Hige felt his blood pressure skyrocket, but he no longer cared. His doctor reminded him several times to keep his temper in check, or he’ll be a risk of a heart attack, a stroke, or a brain aneurysm. He would take his medications as his doctor ordered, but they didn’t regulate his blood pressure like he wanted, so he got his medications increased, and there were still no improvements. With a moment like this he understood why - it was his daughter. His daughter had been doing this to him for years, and now she’s at the climax of her putting her father into an early grave. The silence wore on for what seemed to be an eternity for Kiyoko. She knew that her father was infuriated with her, and it was only time until he would do something mean to her. In her mind’s eye, she can see Hige yelling all sorts of obscenities at her in Japanese, covering her with mental lacerations. Then he would degrade her, calling her every single name in the book, breaking her mentally and emotionally. And then, he would deal the final blow - denouncing her from the family, giving her thirty days to pack up her belongs and get the hell out of his life. Kiyoko squirmed in her chair, her eyes were wide and brimming with tears. It occurred to her that Hige was making her suffer before he dealt out his punishment. She wished that her father would punish her already and get it over with, but that wasn’t how Hige did things. Kaori was feeling as uncomfortable as her daughter, wanting this madness to end. She, too, had strict beliefs about Kiyoko dating, but they weren’t as overwhelming as Hige’s strict beliefs. Kaori was less lenient, didn’t matter to her who she dated, but it all mattered to Hige, as if he was the ringmaster in the dating department. Hige was always like that and he’ll remain that way. Finally - and much to Kiyoko and Kaori’s surprise - Hige dropped into his chair, appearing that some of his anger had lifted. Oh no, his anger hadn’t depleted one bit, by God; both Kiyoko and Kaori could feel it seeping from his pores as if he was emanating a pheromone. His eyes had cleared a bit, but they were still fiery and angry, filled with a hatred neither Kiyoko nor Kaori had ever seen before. He let out a sigh, shaking his head and finally - finally - the silence had been broken as Hige had this to say: “I cannot believe this... I cannot believe that my own daughter would do such a heinous...such a heinous crime!” Hige paused for a moment, glancing over at his wife for a moment. He could see the pain in her face, the concern in her eyes and she was visibly shaken. It didn’t occur to him that after he had banished Kiyoko from his life, his wife told him that he went from beet red to ash white in a matter of thirty (or was it ninety?) seconds. She thought he was going to have a heart attack after the color drained from his body. He was visibly shaking now, his anger was taking over his body, but when he spoke, his voice was clear, steady and surprisingly composed. “You know...damn well if I found out that you were...dating behind my back, you are going to be punished. You know very well that I do not condone you going against my rules when it came to dating. You have crossed the line, Kiyoko. You have betrayed my trust, you have betrayed the Norazu name, but most of all, you have stabbed this family in the back!” This made Kiyoko whimper after her father exploded the final words in the last sentence. She looked over at her mother for help, but that would be no use. Kaori learned the hard way for many years that she couldn’t overrule Hige’s decisions, so she stopped trying getting through to him. Hige’s word was gospel; it would remain as such until the day he dies. “What do you have to say for yourself, little girl?” Kiyoko heard her father exclaim. She didn’t know what to do or what to say. What she knew was that she wanted the floor to swallow her whole, to take her away from all of this, but unfortunately, something like that would never happen. She also knew that whatever she would say wouldn’t appease the fact she’d been seeing someone behind her father’s back, much to her father’s dismay. She knew she was defeated, so she might as well accept her defeat and move on with her life. “I...I love him, Papa,” she replied in a small, weak voice. “I loved him ever since I laid my eyes on him two months ago.” “You love him?” Hige snapped, his eyes were literally turning red. “You love him?! How can a stupid, naive little girl like you fall in love with someone you only met TWO MONTHS AGO?!” He abruptly stopped, shaking his head as if he didn’t want to know the answer. He scoffed a bit, then his eyes bore deeper into his daughter’s. “If you love him so much, why don’t go and marry him?” His tone was mocking her now, and his eyes were taunting her to do something - anything - that’ll further her inferiority against her father. Hige’s mockery had snapped something inside Kiyoko that made her almost as furious as her father. She stomped to her feet, staring back at her father with the same, angry hardness in her father’s eyes. Kaori was looking back and forth from her husband and daughter, as if trying to keep up with an intense game of tennis. “You know what?!” Kiyoko hollered. “Maybe I should, Papa. Since I disappointed you and this family, I might as well marry my true love - who is seven years older AND AMERICAN - and have a bunch of ‘crossbreed’ children that you hate so much! How does that sound to you, Papa?!” Kaori Norazu’s eyes were bulging out of her head after hearing what her daughter had said to her daughter. She thought she was going to fall out of her chair, crying hysterically on the floor, but she discovered that she was holding on without any problems. Hige Norazu, on the other hand, hadn’t expected his daughter say something - or anything at all - like that. He was taken aback by her love and passion that had blinded her and, deep inside his heart, Hige accepted that fiery passion, but didn’t accept she was going to marry an American who was seven years older than her. Hige leaned back in his chair, feeling exhausted. He closed his eyes for a moment, knowing that what he was about to do next was going to hurt him - and everyone else. He knew that what he was going to do was going to kill a part of him inside, but it must be done. He also knew in time his decision would be the right one. “If that is what you are going to do, Kiyoko, then I am not going to stop you... Because from this day forth, you are no longer apart of this family.” He paused for a moment to see if his words brandished any sort of reaction from Kiyoko. There was none, but he could hear his wife softly weeping to herself, knowing that she wasn’t to see her baby girl again. He resumed, keeping his voice clear and steady. “Since you think you are ready to get marry, that tells me you are on the right step towards womanhood. I wish I could say that I was proud of you for taking that step forward and mean it, but I cannot. What you did cannot be forgiven. Do you understand, Kiyoko?” Kiyoko slowly nodded her head, replying to her father with a “Hai.” He let out a sigh, then nodded. The deed had been done. “Then there is nothing else to be discussed. Since you are going to get married, you are going to live with your new husband. You have thirty days to pack up all your belongings and leave this house. After that you and I will no longer have any contact. That also means you no longer have any contact with Kaori.” Still, no reaction from Kiyoko. “You may be dismissed from this table.” Without hesitation, Kiyoko got up from her hair by knocking it over, ran upstairs to her bedroom and slammed the door. After hearing that door slam, Hige knew that he lost his daughter forever and he could no longer have her back. Things were slowly returning to normal in the O’Reilly household. Some issues still needed to be worked out, but that’ll all come in time. All that mattered to me was that Stacy and I are on good terms. We no longer fought like we used to, my children were talking to me again, and Meryl and I had bury the hatchet with some issues we had ever since her mother and I started having our problems. It’s good to know that my family is in support of me again. If I didn’t have that, I wouldn’t know if I could continue living. My family is my life and without life, there’s no reason to live. If Stacy and I ever gotten a divorce, I could’ve gone on being single, I wouldn’t have a problem with that, but Stacy ever took my kids away from me that would shatter me. That’s what would happen if things went sour: take something that hurt me the most. Something like that won’t happen in this family, never in this lifetime. When I came home from Anapa Beach two weeks ago after winning the Frontier title, my boys were ecstatic to see a real life title belt up close and personal. My youngest son, Ryan, wanted to wear the title around his waist, thought it would be just awesome to see what it was like to be a champion, and that resulted him fall forward on his face, due to the heaviness of the title. I broke out laughing when he recovered, turned to a beet red after experiencing his embarrassment in front of his older brother and father. “It’s too heavy,” he’d said, trying to take off the belt, but couldn’t do it. I could see that he was on the verge of crying, so I helped him with the belt and slipped it off his waist. His first thoughts about how awesome it would be a champion had changed dramatically. The seven-year-old didn’t think it was so awesome after all. Now it was ten-year-old Trevor’s turn to marvel at the title. After what he saw happen to his younger brother, he had the inclination that the same thing was going to happen to him. But that would be silly; he was much bigger and stronger than his little brother, that something like falling forward wearing the title belt around his waist would happen to him. Before he jumped into anything haphazardly, he asked me to place the belt in his arms, so he can test how much the belt weighed. I did what he asked, draping the belt across his arms and he almost fell forward, being taken by surprise by the sudden heaviness of the title, and recovered gracefully. He noticed in his peripherals that Ryan rolled his eyes in jealousy. That made Trevor triumphant. Trevor asked me how much the title weighed; I said it was about fifteen pounds. Professional wrestling title belts weigh on average between ten to twenty pounds, I told him, depending what title you were holding. Normally, most World title belts weigh as much as twenty pounds, and Trevor looked up and me in awe. Steeling himself, he slowly put the belt around his waist. I helped by clasping the belt together. I held onto him until he felt ready. When he said he was, I let go and backed away. Trevor swayed a little, but recovered. He looked down at the belt and smiled. He liked the fact he was wearing my Frontier title around his waist. I heard Ryan utter a sound of jealous and disgust, and stomped out of the living room. I couldn’t help but chuckle at Ryan’s comical way of jealousy. As I stand here, I couldn’t help to think to myself why I allowed my career to take me away from my wife and kids. The way I’ve been acting ever since getting married to Stacy, it seemed that my career was the most important thing in my life, and my family was only going to get in the way of my success. It was foolish of me to think that, but I did. That’s why I didn’t want my family to go with me when I was touring on either tour of AWC. They would get in my way, they would get hurt, and they would hinder my success. The only thing I was doing to myself was making myself suffer. Now I no longer think of my family as someone who hindered my success. They are my drive, my dedication and my determination to stop at nothing to become, and with them helping me win the Frontier title, my success was slowly coming to fruition. Unfortunately for me, things like this aren’t always set in stone. Since I’m booked to defend my Frontier title against Anton Assault, the man I won the from, my family and myself would have more stress. They don’t mind that I wrestle in order to support them, but they don’t like the fact that I beat myself up week in and week out. This reminded me of the time when Stacy and I first got together, I’d told her that I was a bare-knuckle boxer. She didn’t like the fact that I used my fists to make money, but it was the only thing I was good at to make great money. She also didn’t like the fact that I would get my ass handed to me on some nights where I was hardly recognizable. One fight a few years back, I came home from a fight and Stacy screamed in horror when she saw that my face was an unrecognizable hamburger meat. She pleaded me to stop boxing, but I couldn’t do that. I needed to support myself and Stacy, and I couldn’t - and I didn’t - want to do anything else. Eventually I would retire in the summer of 1998 after the death of Ian McLeod, only to come back a year and a half later, and eventually retired for good in 2000. Now, I’m a professional wrestler and for most of the last six years I’ve been wrestling, there were times Stacy pleaded with me to quit wrestling. It was like going through the bare-knuckle boxing incident all over again with this woman that I get completely fed up with it. I would storm out of the house, drink beer until I pass out and return the next morning hung over and pissed off at the world. It still amazes me to this very day that Stacy has put up with my shit during our twelve years together, and she’s doing a damn fine job putting up with my shit during our two months of marriage. It takes a headstrong woman to put up with my shit; Stacy McFarland is the only woman to fit in that category. I’m very fortunately to have a family as loving as Stacy, Meryl and my two boys, Trevor and Ryan. Because without them, well... you know the rest of the story.
