Summer's Son #4: 'My Laptop'

10 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10th September 2005 6th September 2005 23:57 I fell through the door of my hotel room and didn’t see anything. Not the pretentious maroon carpet or the mirror framed in gold leaf or the teak dresser covered in scratches from being moved around by careless staff. Not the king-size bed or the small desk or the bedside lamp whose 60W bulb needed replacing. Just a mask. And my laptop computer, whole and then in pieces, whole and then in pieces, as it broke again and again in slow motion. And I fell through the door of my hotel room and didn’t hear anything. Not the incessant whirr of the air conditioning or the ungracious flow of water through the pipes or the buzz of the dimmer switch. Not the crunch of peanuts into the carpet under my feet or the tinkle of keys as I tossed them onto the desk or the crackle of a sore throat as I swallowed. Just a laugh. And that mocking voice – “I lied earlier. I'm sorry. I didn't really delete all your schoolwork. It’s all still in here.” And the sickening crack of computer on skull and I’d known no more and I was backstage and I was in the medical room and I was lying down and I felt like shit and I remembered nothing except my laptop “Where is it? What happened?” I’d yelled, ignoring the choking lashes of blood spluttering up into my mouth, ignoring my aching head and my burning throat and the cuts and bruises and the glass and the doctors and nurses and my aching head and whatever they were doing to me and the plastic sheet, the green plastic sheet on which I lay and my aching head and my burning throat and the cuts and bruises and MY LAPTOP “Where is it? What happened?” “Calm down!” Jin Osaka had exclaimed, her kind demeanour coming out in a time of crisis as it always did; nice, reliable Nurse Osaka, but not what I wanted, nor what I needed, I didn’t need anything except my laptop “Where is it? What happened?” My aching head, my aching head and the sleeping pills kicked in – my legs gave way – I fell onto the bed, the king-size bed, the warm, soft bed, and I was out like a light. 7th September 2005 11:17 “Oh, fuck.” I swallowed hard, but it wasn’t food in my throat. It was my throat in my throat. Gingerly, I brought my fingers up to my neck, then wished I hadn’t. Just a little swollen. I didn’t even understand why. Apparently, after you’ve been knocked out, a sore throat is perfectly normal, as well as constantly feeling dehydrated, migraines, and muscular aches. Heh. Nice understatements. I forced myself out of bed and tottered over to the large mirror above the desk, which served no purpose for me other than somewhere to comfortably open up my laptop and “Oh, fuck.” My laptop! Now it all flooded back to me. The usual argument with The Illustrious Face-Eater had gone further, and I’d even started the fight. With a headbutt. I’d never headbutted anyone before in my life. I didn’t know what had got into me. It was like last night I’d been an entirely different person, and now I was facing the consequences of that. Had I been a drinker, I would have made the analogy that this was the hangover from last night’s inebriation. But I wasn’t a drinker. Remember? 20th November 2004 20:24 I could hear the kick of the bass from down the hall. Grinning nervously, I made my way up the corridor, pausing to look wistfully at the door opposite Jake’s before knocking. This was the reason I was so reluctant to go to Jake’s, the reason he always had to come to mine, I realised. It wasn’t that I didn’t like Jake, it wasn’t that I didn’t want to visit him – it was that I didn’t want to confront my memories of what had happened with Sam. She had the room opposite Jake… “Hey Tim!” I looked around to see Jake beaming at me the other side of the door-frame. I forced a quick smile and walked in, Jake shutting the door behind me. “Glad ya could make it, dawg,” he shouted over the volume of the canned excrement that was filtering though the sound system. I didn’t see the point of Usher… He handed me a beer, which I took with a smile. I had no intention of drinking it – the Ringmaster pay-per-view was coming up this week, and I would be taking part in a gruelling six-man elimination match for Brian Wes’ Classic title, sure to test my stamina right to the limit and beyond. This was what I feared… most wrestlers in Core were finely honed athletes, who had maintained superb physical condition for years and years. I, on the other hand, was not. I was a college student far out of my depth! I’d always kept myself in what I considered ‘good shape’, but there was a world of difference between good shape for a normal person and good shape for a wrestler. The placing of a cold metal object in my hand brought me back to reality. With a sudden breaking of sweat, I realised I didn’t have a clue how to use a bottle-opener. “Thanks…” I offered in the vague direction of Jake, who had now turned away to talk to one of the many stick-thin fake-blondes that were in attendance. I turned my back to the party and took a few steps to the wall, where, with shaking hands, I put the bottle-opener to the lid of the bottle and jerked it around in a random fashion; unsurprisingly, to no avail. I jiggled it around further, still with no joy, and had actually begun considering using my knowledge of Mechanics to work out exactly how to open it when the lid suddenly popped off. Beer slopped all over my hand and the floor; I had in my frustration simply thrust the metal implement upwards violently and by a stroke of luck managed to open the bottle. Unfortunately, my hand was now covered in the golden liquid. I reluctantly wiped my hands on my brand-new dark grey O’Neill trousers, which I’d bought the day before along with the plain black t-shirt I was wearing and some other clothes ‘suitable for public consumption’. 7th September 2005 11:20 I grinned. No, I wasn’t a drinker. The grin left my face as I realised how painful it was to hold it. No way was I wrestling this week – “Oh, fuck.” Yes. Yes, I was wrestling this week. Dread filled me from the bottom up as I remembered the booking that had already been made for this week’s show, AWC’s return to the West Atlantic in Fresno, California (rather bizarrely, as California was far more Pacific than Atlantic, but I didn't book the shows). I wasn’t quite ready to go back to America yet; I hadn’t even had the opportunity to visit my hometown of Milton Keynes properly. But that was the least of my worries – the greatest of which was the Face-Eater. This was what he’d reduced me to. In one hastily scheduled match – initiated by yours truly, I might add – he’d reduced me to this state; I could barely move without feeling racking pains shoot through me. I didn’t even want to imagine what he’d manage in less than a week’s time, in a match he would actually have prepared for. But so would you... Ah, that’s rich. Me? Prepare? In this condition? It would be a big ask simply to make it onto the transatlantic flight, judging by how I felt. Peering closer into the mirror (it was difficult to see properly with swelling around my right eye) I acknowledged that I looked like the victim of a car accident. Perhaps perversely, it wasn’t my condition that I was bothered about at this moment. The Illustrious Face-Eater had destroyed my laptop, and in doing so, had destroyed my life. Harbrook. My summer project. Photos. AIM logs. My history – gone. The Sammy log... what Face-Eater had read out was, for the most part, constructed from his own imagination, but the beginning had been real. The Sammy log was there – and now it was gone. My record of everything that had ever happened between the two of us – that, of an evening, I would sit down and read, and grimace and smile and marvel at, variously, my ineptitude, stupidity and occasional wit. GONE. “Oh, fuck.” It hadn’t been the best of weeks.
