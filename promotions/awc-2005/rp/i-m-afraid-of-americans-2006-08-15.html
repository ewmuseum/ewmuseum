I'm Afraid of Americans

15 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/15/06 The year in the academy after Tariq's incident in the ring with Habib dragged on especially long for the young man. Very soon after he clocked the raving theological ideologue in the head with a chair, he gained the close, watchful attention of Rahman, but not in a bad way, well, at least not in a scornful way. Tariq thought it bad at times though, if only because he never felt he had room to breathe. His day never started earlier than it had. Even grizzled old wrestling instructors needed adequate amounts of sleep. However, during the training of the day, Rahman rode Tariq extra hard, pushing him to limits that no one had ever thought possible. When someone like Ozlem or Khan made a mistake, the problem was idenitified and they earned an informative talking-to from Rahman or one of his underlings in only the gruff way that they felt necessary. But if Tariq made a mistake? He was drilled on it. Every time he slipped, he had to repeat what he had done wrong dozens of times. One time, he was practicing jumping moves, leapfrogs to be specific. Tariq was the best in the school at leapfrogs, and at generally anything that involved a vertical leap, a dive or anything that most people would call suicidal. They were practicing against Khan this time, a mountain of a man, very tall. Even with his immense height, Tariq was able to leap him with no problem. Each of the wrestlers participating in the drill went. Some aced it, some were caught and summarily dropped with a spinebuster, Khan's reward for having to be the grunt in this drill. Then, it was Tariq's turn. He exhaled and started on his run. Unbeknownst to him though, there was a small puddle of moisture in the ring; apparently Hedonyet the Butcher was also a heavy sweater. Just as luck would have it, Tariq stepped right into the puddle of sweat, slipped and fell legs first right into the clutch of Khan, who took his spoils in the form of a power bomb. 'Oh yeah!' the big man exclaimed. 'That's what I'm talking about!' You see, Khan never got out that much. Any time he got to land a bomb of any kind was a good day for him. But enough digression. Tariq lay on his back, dazed and a little shocked that the most basic of moves he had done thousands and thousands of times before had given out on him. He had no time to recover or even catch his breath. 'TARIQ!' Rahman had rushed into the ring and was standing over him before he could even attempt to lift a muscle off the mat. 'Tariq! You did it wrong! You must do it again!' 'Allah in Heaven,' Tariq thought to himself, 'I didn't do it wrong. The mouth-breather who calls himself a butcher can't control his damned glands.' 'Get up! Do it again! How can you expect to make it if you're not perfect?' Once again, Tariq kept his thoughts to himself, figuring that the words 'Shove it up your ass, you don't expect anyone else to be perfect' to be appropriate verbiage at the time. So he struggled to his feet and rubbed his back and neck. Khan really packed a punch with his power moves. 'What are you waiting for! Do it!' Tariq sighed and bounced back off the ropes. No slippage, perfect leap, perfect dismount. 'Good... do it again.' Again? That was perfect. He sighed again, bounce, leap, dismount. 'Good... do it again.' What the hell. Wash, rinse... 'DO it again.' Repeat. Rahman made Tariq repeat the leapfrog another fifteen times before he was satisfied. At the end of that day, Tariq stewed like a good pot of goulash. It didn't help that he had to spend another two hours with Rahman at the end of the day learning English. Yes, every day after training, Tariq would sit in Rahman's stuffy room for two hours while his bilingual instructor would teach him how to speak 'la lingua pura.' On most days, this was something Tariq thought more of a burden than a blessing. He was already physically tired from being worked harder and faster than the other pupils. Some days, he was mentally tired too, having taken the slings and insults from Rahman. He didn't need to spend another two hours expending brain energy on things he really didn't have the passion for. His history books, tomes he enjoyed reading, that took minimal energy because they were more acts of leisure than burden, had been replaced by English readers. He had been involuntarily immersed in the English language so much that he even began to dream in the tongue. The exhaustion had begun to take its toll on Tariq. It didn't manifest itself in slippage in his work or even his study. From an early age, his father figures, Farouk, Mehmet... even the Imam, taught him that it was immoral not to be excellent at what he did. It did begin to take form of resentment against Rahman. Where there used to be an almost amicably warm relationship between the two turned into something icy and standoffish. Tariq would almost do well in spite of Rahman's browbeating. The more Tariq would rebel, the harder Rahman came down. Still though, for all the resentment that Tariq felt in his heart, deep down, he knew what Rahman was doing was working. After a few months, he stopped making mistakes altogether. In nine months, he was fluent enough in English to be able to read Time magazine, cover to cover. The rigorous training had made Tariq almost a machine, retaining only enough humanity to keep a healthy wall of resentment between himself and his mentor and to keep himself from succumbing to the subtle prods from his teacher to give in and use the chair at whim, like he had on Habib. Of course, it's easy to be robotic when you barely sleep. Then came that day, exactly one year to the day after Tariq left Habib in a crumpled heap on the canvas. Rahman was in the ring when everyone started filing into the training area, telling everyone to stand in front of him at ringside. When everyone had gotten there, he cleared his throat. 'Gentlemen, I have an announcement to make. Starting next week, I shall not be your teacher anymore.' A surprised collective gasp fell over the students. 'I will be going to America, and so will one of you. You see, I have procured a spot in a local independent wrestling promotion for one of you, one of you who has stood out in the last year or so since I've been training you. Tariq Suleimon, stand up, because you shall be coming with me.' Tariq shook out of his state of near-catatonia from the announcement. It didn't come as a total shock to him. One of his lone hopes throughout the browbeating Rahman was laying on him the past year was that he was doing it in hopes of getting him ready to go to America and wrestle with the best in the world. Still, he felt the knots untie in his stomach and energy rush to his brain in mass amounts for the first time since the Imam was still around. All the other students congratulated Tariq. Even though he was prone to throw an eye poke here and a back rake there in the ring, they knew he was only doing it to escape tight situations; it wasn't anything personal. For the kid who was ridden like a mule for the last year, this was a deserved reward. Tariq slept soundly that night for the first time in over a year. It was his first time on an airplane. Naturally, Tariq was very nervous and excited at the same time. Acting like someone half his age, he kept fidgeting and staring out the window. Rahman didn't mind this. He knew that everything was so new for Tariq, that he'd never been away from Turkey before. He was also concerned for his student, mainly because he'd never been away from Turkey before. The plane touched down in Philadelphia International Airport. Tariq was amazed at all the people bustling about there, of all different skin hues and nationalities, styles of dress. When they took a cab into the city, he was amazed even more at the skyscrapers, the hustle and the bustle... while Istanbul had those things too, it was totally different than what was going on in Philadelphia. Finally, they arrived at their destination, the corner of 10th Street and Oregon Avenue. They were to stay in a second floor apartment. Small quarters, but neither man had the kind of money for anything else. Rahman footed the bill initially, hoping that Tariq could at least help out later on down the line when he was getting his purses. That night, over a meal of Ramen noodles and Acme brand soda, Rahman decided to begin addressing his concerns to Tariq. 'You know, America is very different than Turkey.' 'I know that. It seems different already.' 'No, that's not what I mean. I meant the people here. They're very different than those you find at home.' 'How so?' Tariq replied, humoring his master. 'Well, most of them, if not all of them will be predisposed to root against you in the ring.' 'What? How do you know that?' 'Because they're Americans, Tariq. Americans are notorious for their hatred of anything Middle Eastern. They're all so bigoted towards our people, it's sickening sometimes. They make such a stink about the racism against blacks and Jews, but they turn a blind eye to us and the way we're treated. To them, we're terrorists or cab drivers or convenience store clerks. It doesn't matter if we're really Turkish and have nothing to do with those fanatics down in Arabia or Palestine, we're the same as those suicide bombers to them. When that freak tried to blow up the World Trade Center in '93, that gave these Americans more fuel to hate us. It's an uphill battle for you to be liked.' 'Preposterious.' Tariq shrugged it off with the glibness and naivete [sic] of a 12 year old ready to try and jump his mountain bike off a ramp over a ravine. 'Not everyone is like that here. You're generalizing.' 'Really, Tariq? How much time have you spent outside Turkey?' Tariq gulped. He had no answer. 'That's right. You've never been here before. I have. I've seen it firsthand. Not just in the wrestling ring, but in all walks of life. I've experienced it myself.' 'Well... you still can't speak for everyone. Especially not here. Mehmet told me once that Philadelphia has the smartest wrestling fans around. He says they see through all the stuff on top and appreciate good wrestling.' 'Oh, because Mehmet, who's also never been out of Turkey, said so, it has to be true. Trust me on this one, you're not going to be well-received.' Tariq shook his head and took a sip of his soda. As he took the can away from his lips and wiped mouth, he began to say something. 'What is it to you anyway... are you just trying to keep my spirits down so my ego doesn't get too big?' 'No. I'm concerned for you, that you're coming over here with one vision in your mind of what success is when the real path to it is paved on a different road.' Tariq looked at him quizzically. 'I bet you were so excited because you thought you'd be this conquering hero, that people would love you because you do things that not many other people do. It's not going to be that way.' Tariq was about to say something, but Rahman cut him off. 'Do you know why I've been pushing you to embrace your darker side the last year? Do you?' 'No... I really don't. I thought you were just a natural dick.' Tariq flinched, but Rahman only smiled, appreciating the comment. 'No, that's not it. I've been telling you that you need to do whatever it takes to win and get ahead, and that kind of behavior... that will get you ahead in this business.' Tariq was confused. He never equated success with cheating. Mehmet had always told him that those who cheated and broke rules were the bad wrestlers. 'There's no way for you to get ahead as a fan favorite. Even if you beat everyone in your path by following the rules and trying to suck up to the fans, it will get you nowhere. Promoters will sabotage you. The fans will still boo you no matter what. Other wrestlers won't talk to you. They don't want to see a Turkish hero defeat their American standards, and they certainly won't buy you if you try to be one of the 'good guys.' The only way that you'll have any staying power is if you play the villain.' Tariq was dumbfounded at what he was hearing, remaining silent. 'If you play the villain, you'll give the promoters a reason to keep you around, whether you win or lose. When you win, you make the fans hate you more, and it gives the promoters a reason to keep you around. When you keep winning matches, and one of their slovenly American heroes by some act of Allah defeats you, it makes them money. You're self-renewable because fans will automatically hate you because of your race. 'And in order for you to fully capitalize on that, you need to play the role to a tee. Gouge eyes, hit groins, and yes, use weapons.' Tariq was utterly shocked at what he was hearing. 'You... you can't be serious. It's not like that. I'll change... I'll change the world! You'll see. You'll... you'll see!' 'I admire your youthful exuberance, Tariq. But that's the truth. Tomorrow we go to the arena, New Alhambra Arena on Swanson and Ritner Streets. That's where you get to show your wares to your new boss. You have one of two choices to make. You can either follow my lead and be an anti-hero intentionally, or you can go your route and fail.' 'Okay, we'll see.' They continued to eat in silence. The next day, they showed up at the arena, more commonly known as the ECW Arena to us layfolk. There they met James Hopkins, Liberty Bell Wrestling's owner and head talent guy. Hopkins was a portly man, graying hair though not balding. He had a full beard too. 'Rahman!' 'James... we meet again.' 'Indeed we do. I trust this young man is the one from all those tapes you've been sending me.' 'Yes. James, meet Tariq Suleimon. Tariq, James Hopkins.' Tariq shook his hand. 'It's a pleasure to make your acquaintance.' 'Oh my, so polite, and such a good grasp on the English language. They teach you that as a second language over there in Istanbul?' 'Oh, no. Rahman taught me.' 'Well I'll be, Rocky. If he can wrestle as good as he speaks, then we're going to have a very long relationship.' James turned to Tariq again. 'Hey, why don't you get into your ring gear and show me what you got. If it's any good, I think I'll let you have a run on our next show.' Next show... Tariq couldn't believe it. He had a shot to wrestle for an American indie. Most people might not have thought that anything special, but to Tariq, it was everything. He was coming close to making it. He rushed to the back and hurried out, dressed in his tights and boots. 'Peters, in there with the new kid,' Hopkins barked. From ringside, a blond, mop-topped kid hopped into the ring. Barry Peters was only 24 years old, but he was a seasoned indie wrestler by all standards. He'd been in feds from his home area to Calgary and Portland, all the way across Canada to Ontario and around New England. Tariq hopped in and right away, they grappled. Peters got the upperhand first with a hammerlock, but Tariq quickly countered into a snapmare. Back and forth the match went until Tariq found an opening on an Irish whip. He caught Peters with his head down and nailed him with a jumping calf kick. Peters reeled back, but Tariq was relentless. He sent Peters out of the ring with a dropkick. As Peters was getting up, Tariq started running towards the ropes. He leapt, taking the breath out of every wrestler's mouth there who was watching... suicide dive. The move was fairly common by this time, but most of the guys there had never seen a newbie pull it out of his ass during a practice match. As Tariq crashed onto Peters, leaving them both in a crumpled heap, the guys who were there got up on their feet and started clapping. James, on the other hand, had seen enough. 'Alright, alright, no need to kill each other during a practice match.' Tariq and Peters both got up, holding various body parts. James came right over to them. 'Good job from the both of you. I'm very impressed, Tariq. I think there's definitely a spot for you on our show Saturday night. See you there, kid.' As James walked away, Tariq began to beam like a flashlight in a darkened room. His elation burst like a bubble as he felt a hand on his shoulder. He turned around... it was Barry Peters. Tariq flinched slightly, expecting to catch hell for doing such a daring move during a practice run. However, the look on Peters' face was amiable. 'Hey, I like your style, man. Barry Peters.' He extended his hand. Tariq was hesitant at first, but he shook after a few seconds of awkward staring. 'Tariq... Tariq Abdul Wahad Suleimon. Pleased to meet you.' 'Same here man. I was really impressed. You got some style.' 'Thanks... you're pretty good yourself.' 'Thanks dude. I think we're gonna get along just fine here.' Tariq and Peters walked along the ring. In the background, Rahman looked on with a mixed look on his face. He was half-smiling because he felt happy for his student. He was going to make it. He was half-groaning because he felt his student was making a huge mistake not taking his advice. Saturday night was crisp and chilly, the kind of mid-autumn weather Tariq would encounter at home. Still, he wasn't cold at all. The anticipation of being in his first wrestling match stateside kept him warm. When he and Rahman came to the arena, James met them right at the wrestlers' entrance. 'Hey kid! You ready to wrestle tonight?' 'Of course I am. I've been ready my whole life.' 'Good, because you're on first. Go get ready, the show starts in two hours.' Tariq couldn't contain his excitement. He shook James' hand vigorously before heading to the locker room area. The whole day, Rahman said nary a word to Tariq, and it was no different that night either. Tariq didn't seem to notice though; he was so wrapped up in the excitement, nothing seemed to matter to him except his first match. And finally it had arrived. Already in the ring was Tariq's opponent, a local schlub, about 240 pounds with a body fat index of somewhere close to 120 percent. He moseyed to the ring with no fanfare... clearly, he was one of LBW's stock jobbers. Tariq made his way to behind the curtain. He was alone; Rahman elected not to accompany him to the ring, instead opting for sitting in the front row. Finally, music played on the PA. It was 'Istanbul (Not Constantinople)' by They Might Be Giants. Tariq took his cue and exited the back. As soon as he came out and the crowd saw what he looked like... BOOOOO!! The crowd gave him a good, old fashioned Philadelphia greeting. 'Oh...' Tariq thoguth to himself, somewhat discouraged. 'They'll warm up to me when they see what I'm all about.' Tariq hopped into the ring and saw the slovenly man standing in front of him. 'This would be easy' he thought as the bell rung. They locked up, and Tariq quickly hit him with a body slam. As he bent down to put in a chinlock, the crowd booed some more. Some in the front started chanting 'Boooring! Boooring!' 'Mehmet warned me about this,' Tariq thought. Fans didn't like chinlocks, or restholds in general. 'Gotta show 'em what I'm made of.' Tariq shot straight up and went right over to the ropes. The slovenly jobber stood up and as soon as he got his vertical base, Tariq leapt from the top and hit him with a missile dropkick. BOOOO!! Tariq was befuddled. That was a dropkick from the top. Great high risk move. Why were they still booing him? 'Guess I gotta show 'em more.' And that he did. Suicide dive, the same move that wowed the wrestlers a few days ago. Spinning heel kick. Flying body splash from the top. Moonsault. All of them met with boos. 'Shit. I have to do something big,' Tariq thought. 'Have to win their trust with something they've never seen before.' He headed to the top again, with the jobber prone on the canvas. He got to the top, back facing the ring. Then, with one fluid motion, he leapt back, tucking forward, splaying his arms out, twisting his body... it was a move that no one had seen before... a backflip somersault into a senton splash, a reverse of a popular move among the indie circuit. It was beautiful. James was marking out from his seat. Rahman couldn't believe his eyes, seeing that Tariq never busted out that move in any training sessions. A few wrestling geeks around the arena were slack-jawed. The impact was hard, the jobber was out. Tariq covered and the ref counted three. Tariq got up, just basking in his moment. He had won his first match with a move that no one had ever done before... yet all he could hear... BOOOOOO!!! They were still booing him. The fans... the nerve of them... they booed him like he was Troy Aikman throwing a touchdown pass to Michael Irvin. Tariq was beside himself. He couldn't believe it. He turned around and spotted Rahman in the crowd, shaking his head and sighing. Tariq fixed his gaze on his trainer, and as Rahman saw him, he mouthed four words to him. 'I told you so.' Fast forward to another scene, well past that time and that place. It's the America West Arena in Phoenix, where the Atlantic Wrestling Council's Fresh!West tour is having a house show. Captain Suleimon is on the receiving end of some pretty hard knife edge chops from Paddy O'Shea. WHOOO! The crowd loves to pay homage to Ric Flair when anyone chops someone's chest into a red raw state. After three chops, Paddy takes Suleimon's arm and whips him off the ropes. On the rebound, the Captain catches Paddy off-guard with a spinning heel kick. Taking quick advantage, Suleimon runs to the corner and leaps on the top rope. Paddy recovers but as soon as he turns around... BOOM! Missile dropkick! Suleimon connects with the high impact move. The Sultan of Smackdown gets up, looks around the arena. Every single person in attendance is booing him with all their heart. The Captain basks in it. It's exactly what he wants to hear from these American mouth breathers.
