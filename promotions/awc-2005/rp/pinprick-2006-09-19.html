Date: September 19, 2006 the living man says - there is no use in hoping; the dying man says - there is nothing more you can take; the man with secret lips against his neck says - there is a way out but i cannot find it. and this is not a matter of desire, only one of unsalvageable fantasy and stale touches kept stored in the basement for much too long. ** When Howie had fired you, the way you looked at him was something like a betrayal, something that said: I thought you would be better than this, I thought you could be better than this. What he didn't understand was that there was nothing for you to be better for - there was no reason for you to change and there was nothing for you once you left the double-doors of the building. That job was your life. PD was all you had, and maybe you would have been a better woman if only you had the guarantee of something other than those four walls. It was selfish, maybe, to think that you ought to get something in return, but you've learned to keep your cards close. Maybe you would have been a better woman, but there was no family and there were no kids and there was no house waiting for you to change into something good. There was nothing. Now, blue envelope in hand, child dead and still a bad woman, you look at Howie and say something like: What is this? and Howie says something like: A case and you say: But, why? and he says: Because, and you can't even remember how to be angry anymore, and you want say: Please let me have this one thing, this one thing and I won't ask for anything else, at least not for a while, and I want to be a good woman, I want to change, but you think that might be asking too much so you say: Why? and all Howie says is: Welcome back, Ellis and you want to ask Why, Why me? but don't, because now you might have something again. ** the cathedral bells rang four times at three. it was that sort of day: my eyes drier than the dust that billowed upwards with each scuff of foot and skipping step, skin heavy with the weight of late summer and the pressure exerted by two months of uselessness and weak limbs. you rested your face against the windowsill, eyelashes and cheekbones exaggerated by the angle at which you looked outwards. your mouth was hidden. i could taste something like nostalgia and olives in the thick, ancient air. ** Truth is, you need to learn how to not care. You need to learn how to survive. You almost manage a smile and you almost manage to steady your hands when you pour coffee at a gas station on the way home and you almost manage not to trip over your own feet on the way out. You almost manage a lot of things; you don't know why you're doing this, why you handed over a little piece of yourself to someone who always put other people and places and things above you, why you can't stop, why you took your job back, why you didn't take two Midol instead of one because your cramps are fucking killing you. In your car, drinking coffee with too much milk, you see your fingers twitch against the steering wheel. You think of how easy it would be to twist it and slam your car into a tree, a wall, an ocean. You almost do it, but you're a coward. You've always been a coward. You spend the night in your car and fall asleep with your cheek against the window, parked outside of your own apartment, cell phone turned off, hands freezing, salt and cigarette and coffee in your mouth. You don't think you've ever been this scared. The last thing you remember before you fall asleep is that you don't have anyone now. Thing is: you've always bounced back, each time a little less, but you'd done it because you'd had to, because you needed it more than anything else. You can't even try this time. You don't know any jokes for this. You don't know the routine. All you can see in your mind are the turned backs of people you used to know and faces in shadow and your own useless hands. You've got no one but everybody's got you and you tell yourself that you'd better start learning pretty fucking fast. ** the water boiled long before us and when the steam escaped deep into our pipes, frosting the lines of our throats, we may not have meant it but we did not stop the water from overflowing no, no, you see: we are not breaking up we are breaking down ** You go to Dad's fifty-ninth birthday party. By this point, you haven't worn a dress in months. You used to keep count of the days it had been since you slipped out of that last Dior, three weeks 4 days three weeks 5 days three weeks 6 days, because that was the only thing stopping you from getting out of your bed in the mornings and choosing a dress out of reflex. A lot of things have changed - the fact that you haven't had more than two joints per day in a week being one of them. You're trying to change. You've learned how to cook, and it's nice, even if the saleswoman at Crate&Barrel had laughed at you when you asked her what the point of having more than one pan was. You're still not sure that you get it, but you can make a mean stir fry and boil pasta without incident. Well, most of the time. Baked ziti had been the first thing you tried before you realized you were being overly ambitious and lit Ally's sleeve on fire, and it's new in a good way to do something that you're shit at. This time, you don't bring champagne. You don't really buy extravagant things anymore. You thought of getting him a lifelong subscription to Sports America, but didn't. When you ring the doorbell and walk in you see the surprised looks, but it's a good sort of surprise, and it's nice to know that they don't resent you after it's all been done and said. You shake hands with your uncle, compliment your mother's hair. Dad doesn't show up until a few minutes later. The cake's cut and the candles are blown and everything's warm, comfortable. You wonder if this is what it feels like. Belonging somewhere. Dad smiles at you and the wrinkles crinkle up at the corner of his eyes. They only make him look nicer, not old. He tries to get you to hold your sister's baby, but you're nervous around them; you're always scared shitless that you're going to drop the baby by accident and get sued by Kendall, because the only times you've held a baby before was when and visiting your fellow cops and their pretty, sharp-nailed wives, and when -- -- you don't like to think about the other time. When you get ready to leave that night, Kendall says I love you and Ally says I love you and Jon says something gay and Mom smiles and says that you're welcome anytime and Dad nods but he's also a little drunk. You say, yeah, of course, but you're not sure that you will - they're your family and they're your parents, but it's not your place. You're still alone, really, but it's not the same sort as before. You're not there yet but you think that, maybe, maybe you'll be alright. You can get there, at least. It's not so bad. ** i saw the flat, flat land and the curved sky, and i swear i could hear them saying in the syllables of the long-gone: we will take this back someday, we will take back what is ours, we will destroy everything you have made and loved ** Home, and all of a sudden you want to wash all your make up off and walk around the house wearing nothing but an over-sized t-shirt from high school. All your old friends had signed it and drawn hearts and smiles and maybe that's why you've kept it, because some of them are gone now and some of them have lied now and some of them have broken your heart now. Masochist, Adam might say, but you didn't know him back then and you're glad for it. It would have meant fewer nights kissing gorgeous boys and more being in something close to love. You don't think that those girls in boring books are quite so stupid now. Sometimes you wish you had never met Adam, but sometimes you're glad, because it -- This is the secret kind of hurt and you keep it close, it feels like something that belongs to you, only, this heartbreak. It doesn't matter that countless people have scratched up and dropped their hearts from the sky, this is yours and you used it to make him regret things, you used it to make him leave. You're selfish about what you have and it doesn't bother you one bit. One thing you've always known: you are not smart. You're stupid. There's no reason for apologizing. You like skirts and shoes and earrings, you watch The Golden Girls obsessively and read Cosmopolitan, you have long legs and a cup C and the boys always watch you when you dance. You know a lot of things, subconsciously, you just don't think about them because there's no point in analyzing and dissecting and all those things that are a waste of time. Stuff happens and figuring out why it happens doesn't make anything easier because you just get wrinkles and become irritable and dissatisfied with life. What you like to do is operate in the here and now. When you want something, you go after it. You just don't care, because you're angry and it's a feeling that won't come out of the back of your throat; you can't throw up this chemical because it's diffused into your blood and feels like something sharp to prick your heart with. You are Ellis Nash, and you've painted yourself with a fresh coat of shiny glossy varnish and you're beautiful; you've filled up your cracks and chipped angles with something plastic and vaguely like lies. They can't hurt you anymore than what's already been done because you're filled up to the brim and old and stupid and you don't give a fuck. You are Ellis Nash, and nobody can hurt you except for the people that matter. Half of them are dead. That's your mantra. That's your goddamned slogan. You can't protect anyone because you can't even protect yourself. You've got power, but not the kind that matters, not the kind that can change anything. Your kid is dead. Your father has never liked you. You've got scars on your shoulder blades and scars in your mouth and a police badge in your back pocket. This is something you understand. You fall asleep on the couch to a rerun of The Golden Girls, hands folded in your lap and an empty styrofoam cup on the endtable beside you. ** our relationship was interrupted last tuesday at dinner when the tablecloth fell over our knees and we lost sight of where we were; the kitchen lights dimmed but we sensed by the sound of our cups rusting that we still had time to drink ** You don't really know why you end up standing on his doorstep the next day, early evening, but you figure it's because you might need him more than anything else right now and if you were the type to do so, you would be shuffling your feet and fidgeting with the edges of your sleeves like you were out on some fucking date. Instead, you glare at the wooden door, which seems so diminutively harmless but is the two inches separating you from some sort of mental peace, from him. The door opens sudden enough so that you don't notice until you do, which is when your shoulders twitch a little and you almost blush, ruining whatever semblance of bravado you had managed to summon up over countless meters of pavement and grassy lawn. You don't remember ringing the doorbell. Adam is looking at you and that sense of injustice resurfaces, more so than ever, and you seemed to have sudden developed an asphyxiating stutter. Your tongue sits heavy in your mouth. The tag on the back of your shirt itches and you resist the urge to scratch the base of your neck. You try to smile, but it ends up feeling almost like a sneer, so you stop and twist your fingers together behind your back. He steps backwards. You step forward. He's still looking at you and you don't know what to do, because he's quiet and you don't know how to be the one to fix things in a situation like this either. You feel almost wronged that you've been shoved into the position of the apologist, the blundering child. You're not used to this. The almost-anger is back, annoyed and increasing in velocity beneath your skin. The edges of your mouth feel dry and your fingers numb, so you step forward again. You almost trip on the edge of the doorway and you steady your hand against his shoulder and you have to get up on tiptoe to kiss him, so you do, so you do kiss him. There, in front of the universe and everyone, or maybe just the lone bird on his roof and a richly tinted sky and him. Faintly, you realize that your toes hurt from trying to reach that taller height, so you shrink down a little and mumble, sorry. Sorry for the bad kiss and sorry for whatever I did and sorry for. Well. Having to be sorry in the first place. It's too late to be sure. There's this moment where you think he might shut the door in your face and it's a little humiliating but not as much as you thought. You're set on fixing things and you'll stand there and knock on his door for as long as it's needed. You didn't realize that you cared so much until now. A slow breath leaves your lungs when he steps outside, instead, and pulls you by the arm with him. He smiles, bright, and doesn't move his hand from your arm. You think, maybe it wasn't what you did, maybe it was what you didn't. He says, I don't get you at all, and you smile and say, I don't really understand you either. For someone so apparently simple, Adam is kind of hard to figure out. You know that his speech is blustery and confident, that he smokes too much pot, that he likes action movies. You think, he likes me too. He says, you're kind of slow on the uptake for a prodigy, and you hit him on the arm but you're smiling a little so he kisses you. It's not bad this time. You think you can get used to it. This is your life, and it's fine with you. You don't know if you're fine with it, but you think you might be on your way to becoming a good woman, maybe, finally. ** smile like we found the secret to life.
