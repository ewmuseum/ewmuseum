The Web, ch. 1: Ignition

30 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30th October, 2006 The twang. It just had to be there, didn’t it? For the past three years it had been a constant reminder that one more awkward landing, one more botched move, could end everything he’s got. Everything he’d built up in his five year career could be laid to rest in a split second. It was indeed a threat, and at times, a serious weight on his mind. But Andy M. was used to it. The pain wasn’t there all the time, there have been many occasions where Andy hadn’t felt the twang in his back for so long that he figured it must have been gone for good. But oh no, as soon as Andy built his hopes up, it reared its ugly head. His memory of the night that he first seriously damaged his back was hazy at best; it was over three years ago, after all. Andy could remember being tossed from the top of a steel cell by his opponent, but almost everything after the moment his broken body hit the floor below with a sickening thud was black. It just wasn’t there. But he sure as hell remembered the countless months it had cruelly erased from his career. At the time, part of Andy wanted to bring about the demise of James “Kannon” Rande – the man who threw him from the cell – but he soon realised that Rande wasn’t to blame. Despite being an incredibly dangerous manoeuvre, it was a fluke injury. There was more chance of him walking away from the incident on his own accord than him leaving in the back of an ambulance. But that’s just how things pan out. Shit happens. Even if it meant regular appointments with a physician for the rest of his career, Andy M. would soldier onwards; he would push his body to its limits night after night for the love of professional wrestling. Perhaps he was foolish to continue with the threat of paralysis constantly hanging over his head like a sharpened dagger, but it was worth it. ------------ On a bright, warm Floridian morning, Andy M.’s personalised Volkswagen hippie van, the “Andymobile,” pulled off the highway and into the sparse parking lot outside his local Doctor’s surgery. The vehicle was one that would stick out like a sore thumb in any car park; its sides were splattered with red, green, blue, yellow, purple, orange, almost every colour you could think of, with none dominant. It also had the word “Andymobile!” crudely sprayed on either side in white – anyone else would have this thing re-sprayed on site, but the Scottish King of Cool wouldn’t have it any other way. Andy stopped the Andymobile in the closest space he could find next to the building that wasn’t reserved for the handicapped, before killing the engine and Poison’s “Nothin’ But A Good Time,” which had been blaring from the VW’s speakers, with a quick twist of the ignition key. Stepping out of the vehicle on this beautiful day, Murrr found himself glad that he had remembered to wear his mirrored aviator shades, as without them the sun reflecting mercilessly off the perfect white surface of the clinic would surely be causing some serious discomfort to his eyes at the moment. For the past then months, following his most recent injury flare-up, he had regularly visited his long-time physician, Dr. John Keaton, in this very building, and it had almost become his second home. As he paced slowly towards the automatic doors leading inside the surgery, he was hoping that this would be the last time he would have to visit Dr. Keaton for a while. As the automatic sliding doors gently swept open, Andy stepped into the waiting room, instantly met with a sudden burst of cool air from the air conditioning system; the fact that he had just been outside in the baking sunshine initially making what was in fact a pleasantly cool waiting room seem like a harsh British winter’s evening. The waiting room was pretty extravagantly decorated – all marble fittings, glass coffee tables and comfortable-looking grey leather couches – but retained a sense of tastefulness throughout. Sunlight poured in through the windows, bouncing off of the white walls making the room very bright indeed. Apart from Andy, the only other people in the room were an aging maintenance man, currently doing the rounds with a watering can making sure the many pot plants in the room are kept healthy, and a bright-looking, attractive young receptionist. Clearly no more than twenty-two years of age, the blond sat behind her desk at the end of the room resting her head on one of her hands, terminally bored. She didn’t notice Murrr walking towards her until he finally reached the desk and cleared his throat. Immediately she perked up, fearing that she’d been caught out slacking by one of her superiors. Instead, she breathed a sigh of relief when she saw the Scottish King of Cool standing tall above her, slipping his shades into the pocket of his fading black jeans. “Good morning Julia, hard at work as usual I see?” Andy said in his familiarly warm, mid-range voice, tinged with the faintest Scottish accent. A smile grew across Andy’s face as he spoke to Julia; the fact that she and Andy were apparently on first name terms testament to the amount of times that the Scottish King of Cool had visited the clinic in recent months. “Very funny Andy,” she responded jovially. “How are you doing today?” she enquired, seeming generally interested in what Murrr had to say. “Ehhh, alright, I can’t complain. Of course, the notorious ‘S-C-H-N-I-T has got family over at the moment and that’s kind of doing my head in, crazy green fucker. But hey, who am I to deny the Grob some quality time with his folks? He hasn’t seen ‘em in like twenty thousand years or some shit…” Andy’s vocal meanderings were only met with a baffled look and a chuckle from Julia, who had come to expect this kind of dialogue from the Scottish King of Cool in recent months. Clearly though, she had no idea what Andy was talking about. “Anyway, is the Keatonmachine ready to see me yet?” Andy required, referring of course to Dr. John Keaton, his physician. “Or are you going to make me sit and wait on those crazy leather contraptions you call ‘chairs,’ hmmm?” Smiling again, Julia glanced down at the appointment list which conveniently sat on the top of her desk. “Yeah, he should be free right now; you know where to go…” “Thanks kid!” Andy enthusiastically jabbered with a thumbs-up and a toothy grin. “And remember! Stay in school! Otherwise you’ll end up doing drugs, and die!” After tapping Julia in a faux-patronising manner on the shoulder, Andy made the short journey across the waiting room towards the doctors’ offices; more specifically, the one with the golden plaque reading “Dr. John Keaton” attached to the door. Andy raised his fist to knock on the door, but before his intents were interrupted as the muffled voice of Dr. Keaton came from the other side of the door – “Come in, Andy!” Expecting the imminent arrival of Murrr, Keaton must have seen his shadow under the crack of the door. Soon enough Andy had stepped into the familiar climes of the good doctor’s office. A tidy and thorough man, Keaton always kept the office in top condition and the place looked absolutely spotless. The first thing that Andy noticed was the bed that he had spent many hours upon recently; almost every time he visited the clinic he ended up receiving treatment on this dread, uncomfortable piece of furniture. The room was also almost as bright as the waiting room despite the fact that the sun was shining on the other side of the building. Dr. Keaton sat behind his clinically tidy desk, smiling at the sight of the entering Scottish King of Cool. He was dressed smartly in a short-sleeved white shirt and simple black slacks, and his combed-back black hair revealed a badly receding hairline, sunlight reflecting off of the areas of his head that his hair had already retreated from. John Keaton had been Andy’s doctor since 2003; when Andy first incurred his career-threatening injury, and since then they had struck up a healthy friendship. Keaton, of course, had major doubts over Andy’s constant decisions to keep returning to professional wrestling after constant injury scares, but had almost given up hope of trying to talk Andy out of his dangerous career choice. But then again, he was a good doctor, and good doctors always look out for what they perceive their patients’ best interests to be. “Good afternoon Mr. M., how’s it going?” Keaton enquired in a moderate Southern drawl. He stood up to greet Andy, the two exchanging a firm handshake over the doctor’s desk. “Well I haven’t fallen off the top of any steel cells recently, so that I can be thankful for…” Andy replied. “I guess that’s a bonus,” Keaton chuckled to himself. Almost immediately he pointed towards the bed, “let’s get this troublesome back of yours checked out a little bit first, see how we’re doing…” With a nod Andy pulled his sleeveless Guns ‘n Roses t-shirt over his head, laying it gently over the back of the chair on his side of the doctor’s desk. He took a couple of steps towards the bed and laid face down upon it – he knew the routine like the back of his head. No sooner had Andy laid down than Keaton had drawn a curtain around the bed, and began to kneed clenched fists stiffly into the Scottish King of Cool’s spine. “Okay, how’s this? Painful?” asked Keaton. “Nope, just exceedingly uncomfortable…” This was an uncharacteristic lie. Andy felt a familiarly faint pain in the small of his fact. “Have you felt anything when you’ve been exercising recently? Any ‘twangs’ whilst jogging, etc…” “Nope. Not a thing.” Lies. “Hmmmmm, okay,” began the doctor, pleasantly surprised that (to the best of his knowledge) Andy had not been having any troubles with his back recently. “So, nothing at all really then?” “I haven’t felt anything in weeks, Doc.” Lies, all of it lies. Alright, so maybe the pain wasn’t as prominent as it was, say, 8 months ago, but it was still there. It would never go away, and it took Andy by surprised that given Keaton’s expertise as a physician he was falling for everything that Andy was saying. But Andy had to lead him on, he had to get back in the ring – since the dissolution of his marriage a few years ago wrestling had become one of the main driving forces behind Andrew Hugh M.’s existence. Andy M. was driven. Keaton began to hold off with the massages, “Alright, everything’s looking good, you can get up now,” he instructed, as he pulled the curtain back following the brief examination of Andy’s back. The Scottish King of Cool was quick to slide from the bed and pull his sleeveless shirt back on. Needing no instruction, he took a seat in the padded black leather chair which sat in front of Dr. Keaton’s desk, knowing that the doctor would soon do the same. “This is great news Andy,” Keaton began, “excellent progress. We’ve been at this a long time and it looks like everything is finally falling back into place. If you’re back is as stable at the moment as you say it is, you’re well on your way…” the doctor stated. This was exactly what Andy M. wanted to hear. What followed, however, was the total opposite – “However, I would strongly advise you against getting back into wrestling. Just think about how many times this has happened to you, and what it could do to you in the future…” Too late, Doc. Andy was already back in training. “I can’t promise anything Doc…” Andy muttered truthfully. He knew that he couldn’t keep his intentions from Keaton; the chance of the doctor finding out through the media, given AWC’s constantly growing stature, was simply too big. Keaton’s lips let out a disappointment sigh, but he still managed the slightest of smiles. He knew how much wrestling meant to Andy, and that it would take a heck of a lot for anyone to take it away from him. He began to wonder if Andy was being totally truthful when he told him that he wasn’t feeling any pain, but decided against disputing it with the Scottish King of cool. “Alright Andy, have it your way…” ------------ The doctor’s appointment was a pretty unpleasant experience for Andy M. He didn’t like- nay, despised lying to John Keaton – a man who he now viewed as a friend first and a physician second – but he had his reasons. He couldn’t wait to be back in the ring, back in the spotlight, back in front of the bright likes with thousands of fans chanting his name. It was what he lived for. And as the Scottish King of Cool drove the Andymobile through the extravagant, purple gates leading into Andyville – his private residence – thoughts of his imminent comeback brought a smile to his face. Murrr drove the Andymobile along the lengthy gravel driveway which ran through the middle of a grove of trees, ultimately leading to his sprawling almost mansion-like home of four years. The grand, colonial-style building was purchased by Andy relatively early in his career in a poor state. Originally intending for it to become a home for his young family, Murrr planned to eventually renovate the place and move in with his now ex-wife Jane and young son Marvin. Unfortunately, things don’t always work out the way you’d planned. Andy slammed the Andymobile door behind him and stepped back out into the hot Florida sunshine. He paused for a moment to enjoy the beautiful day before eventually climbing up onto the porch, turning the gold doorknob, which had been warmed up by the sun, and entering into the grand hallway. However, no sooner had Andy stepped into the hallway than he heard a familiar, croaky voice echoing from the kitchen doorway at the far end of the hall. “GAEL! GOE LONGE!” shouted everyone’s favourite illiterate, unpleasant slimey green alien – Grobschnit (or Grobby, to his friends). Andy was taken aback by the sight of his younger brother, Cayle, dashing towards him at full speed, his torso decked out in full-on American football attire; hefty shoulder pads and a red Tampa Bay Buccaneers jersey. The notorious ‘S-C-H-N-I-T was dressed in the exact same attire, and Andy could only watch as Grobby hurled a football in his direction. Dodging out of the way, so as to avoid contact with his oncoming brother, Murrr watched as Cayle quickly turned and caught the ball with both hands, before throwing his arms in the air in celebration. “Booyah, bitch! How’s that for some catching skills!” Cayle yelped in jubilation, exchanging a high five with the oncoming Grobschnit. Cayle was always something of a tearaway, compared to Andy. He had looked to follow his older brother into the professional wrestling business, but the money eventually went to his head, and he ended up serving 18 months jail time in 2002-2003 for a combination of dangerous driving and driving whilst under the influence of alcohol. After completing his jail time, Cayle moved in with his elder brother, and hasdhad a large number of dead-end jobs since then. To the outsider, he was a layabout, a leech if you will. “Leet, dood, leet,” Grobschnit barked. Grobschnit, on the other hand, was an intergalactic “man” of mystery. All we really know about him is that he’s a short, green alien who speaks terrible English and somehow possesses the ridiculous ability of teleportation. He’s also the crunkest dawg in the hood, but that’s another story… “A-hem!” Andy suddenly croaked, grabbing the attention of his brother and his intergalactic homeboy immediately. Cayle jumped, quite surprised to see his brother standing there – obviously he had a gotten a little bit carried away with the football, and didn’t see Andy coming in the door. “Oh, shit, Andy… where the hell did you come from?” he asked, somewhat out of breath. “Just got back, biatch…” Andy took a glance at both Cayle and Grobschnit – particularly at their ridiculously big shoulder pads. “Football in the house, what’s the need man? We’ve got tonnes off room out back…” Grobschnit remained silent, as Cayle searched the back of his mind looking for a good enough excuse. “Eh, I dunno… it’s just more, fun, I suppose…” he replied rather desperately. “Anyway you cripple-ass bitch! How the hell was your appointment with the doctor!” He asked, swiftly changing the subject, much to the relief of the G-R-O-B. Andy raised an eyebrow. “Cripple?” he asked, “I’m not cripple, you cheeky little fucker! Shut it mate, or I may be forced to rudebox you, and indeed, your whole crew..” “But Andy, man, I’m bringing sexy back! If you rudebox me, who’s going to bring sexy back, hmmm?” Justin Timberlake>Robbie Williams. The Scottish King of Cool paused for a moment to consider this fact, but before he could get some serious thinking done, there was a sudden knock at the door from nowhere. Through the frosted glass Andy could see the silhouette of two figures – one tall, one short. “Eh? Who the hell is that, and where the hell did they come from?” Andy through out loud. With a shrug, Cayle turned towards the door and pulled it open. His facial expression sank at the sight of the face that met him on the other side of the door, before he could speak however an articulate female voice that was instantly recognisable to Andy asked “Cayle, where is Andy? It’s quite important…” The younger M. brother sighed and turned towards Andy. “Andy, Satan’s here… watch out! I think she’s going to cast a spell on us, like a curse or some shit…” Stepping up to the door, Andy was met by the sight of his ex-wife, Jane, who was holding the hand of their nine year old son Marvin. Jane was a startlingly beautiful woman, with deep hazel eyes, a crystal-clear complexion, and a figure to die for. Her slightly longer than shoulder-length hair was tied back, and she wore a tight, black blouse, the top two buttons undone, as well as a pair of dark grey trousers. “Help me out Andy, please,” she started, “can you take care of Marvin today, just until I get back from work? Somebody’s called in sick, and I don’t want to leave him on the house all alone…” Andy smiled as he glanced down at his son – every time Andy laid eyes upon him it never ceased to amaze him at how quickly he was growing. Marvin, in truth, looked much like you’d expect a nine year-old Andy M. to look, only with glasses. There was a smile across his face – he was excited to see his father. “Like you even need to ask…” Andy nodded, as Jane let go of Marvin’s hand. Andy’s son walked towards the Scottish King of Cool gleefully. “Thanks Andy, this is a great help,” acknowledged Andy’s ex-wife, who, after years of bickering and fighting, Andy was finally back on speaking terms with. They were now at a level where they could stand each others company for about five minutes… hey, it was progress! “Don’t mention it,” Andy replied as he knelt down to hug his son. “Well, I’ve got to hurry, but I’ll be back to pick him up again when I’m finished work… thanks again…” Jane said with a wave, before hastily retreating. A sneering Cayle closed the door behind her – he still couldn’t stand the sight of her. “Hey kid, how are you doing? Want to go throw stuff at Grobschnit?” Andy asked Marvin, as Grobber gulped. “Heck yea!” responded Marvin, before exchanging a high five with his father. “O noes!” yelped Grobschnit, darting off down the corridor suddenly, Marvin giving chase. Andy knew today he should be hitting the gym, building up his fitness and stamina, but fuck it – spending time with his son was far too valuable. There was always tomorrow, right? Fin.
