Anthology of Interest 2:

2 October 2006

Date: Oct 02, 2006 The pain inside of his head, berrating his brain and clogging senses, makes him weary of allowing his eyes to open. A sticky film the substance of cobwebs coats his tongue, tasting of metal and feces. The first thing he does is try to move, failing that he panics. Eyes open with despair, kinks and settled aches in muscles suddenly flare to life with a virulent vengeance. A dim orange light hangs lonely overhead, casting an eerie tinge to water stained brown walls. The concrete floor is cold to his bare feet and covered in a slimy moisture. Lightly tanned skin, stretched taunt over anxiously twitching muscles, breaks into nervous goose bumps and cold sweats. He casts his head about in only his boxers to protect him, arms secured by handcuffs behind his back and his feet tied firmly to the legs of a sturdy wooden chair. 'What.' He croaks, a dried throat constricting his words. 'Where am I?' But only his voice fills the empty room. Long blond hair mats to his forehead and neck; heated anger rising like a dangerous fever. He glares at the door before him, heavy and iron, mocking him with false security. He twists his arms. The door opens suddenly and casts a brilliant, blinding white light into the dank cell. Johnny averts his gaze, blessed refuge from the assault on his eyes, blinking his way through the pain he seeks something, anything to tell him what the fuck going on. 'Who are.' Johnny coughs, lightly at first. But coughing as a cough tends to do, it grows worse and harsh. Heavy boots beat a steady cadence upon the floor, the figure moving with enigmatic purpose for the bound and helpless Johnny. 'Who are you?' Johnny yells, his shoulders and arms tensing, bulging as he grows angrier. 'Drink this.' The voice is tainted by a robotic whir, unfamiliar and cold. A cup is pressed to his lips moments before warm; tepid water fills his mouth. Finding himself drinking thankfully Johnny nudges it away with a snarl. 'Who are you and what the FUCK am I doing here?' Johnny's chair skids as he screams vehemently. The figure straightens, still dark and out of focus as he backs towards the door. 'Wait.' Johnny begins, sliding himself another inch. 'No no, wait!' The door closes with a loud bang that echoes inside of the prisoner's ears. 'Fuck!' Blinking his eyes to return a clarity that seems so far from clear he wishes it foggy again. 'What did you give me.' His head lawls to one side as he starts twisting his arms. Whistling a light hearted tune the figure, an older man with stunning blue eyes, dumps the rest of the glass of water on the floor. He pulls red, scarred hands from black leather gloves and drops them too on the ground. Turning from the iron door he passes several more just like it on both sides of the tight hallway, illuminated by bright flourescent tubes every few feet. At the end of the hallway he passes through a barricaded gate, stopping he shuts and closes the gate behind himself turning not one but three dead-bolts into place. Lifting his chin he pulls the velcro of a strap from his neck and leaves it on a small shelf, clicking a button on the small voice box. He brushes off his black sweater before mounting the first in a long set of stairs, climbing each step he pauses only to groom his similarily black slacks and smooth back thinning brown hair. At the top of the stairs is a third door made of simple wood with a plain brass knob, the portal opens and the whistling jailor steps through and closes the door behind him, the tiny knob lock clicks. His happy melody drifts about a rather stylish loft, a black leather couch and tv by a large bay window hidden by heavy dark curtains. An open dining room and kitchen area themed black and stainless steel with a wide island counter-top sitting by itself. Rounding this island the man clicks a knob on his stove and watches as the multitude of blue flames dance into existence and cackle with laughter. He grabs a steel tea kettle from another burner and steps to his sink, filling it with tap water before setting it over the flames. He opens a cupboard and sets out a tea cup on a saucer, and then another beside it. He takes his time, retrieving a small tin from a higher shelf and setting out a pair of tea bags. Hunkering down the whistling stops; producing a tray he moves to the fridge and extracts a creamer. Next he moves to a pantry shelf and grabs a bowl of sugar cubes before finally opening a drawer and taking out a pair of tea spoons. The simple wooden door splinters and groans, there's a moment of brief silence before it crashes to the floor with a bang. Johnny steps out in his boxers, the handcuffs hanging from his left wrist and his face dark with murder. The man pulls back a black sleeve and checks his watch. 'Took you longer than I'd expected. You know I left a wire with the voice box for you to pick that door instead of breaking it down.' The voice is tempered by age and wisdom but still strong. 'It's been a long time since I've been locked in a dungeon and drugged!' Johnny comes forward but his momentum halts on unsteady feet, anger turning into the determination to stay upright. 'The difference between life and death could be in stealth. Grab a stool.' 'The fuck did you give me?' Johnny hits the seat and props his head in his hand. 'LSD in the water.' The old man shrugs as the kettle starts to steam up and he hears it shout. 'Five years ago it would have been poison.' 'Five years ago I'd have caught you at the stairs. What is this about?' The old man grins a grin full of menace and secrets. He pours the water and sets the kettle aside without saying a word, he takes out a bottle and pops the cap. Tapping out two little white pills onto Johnny's saucer. 'Take those, they'll clear your head. Then you can tell me how you did it.' 'Did what?' 'Got out of my dungeon.' Johnny frowns and tilts his fog filled head. 'Why?' 'So I can make it better.' There's that grin again, so very familiar. So much like Johnny's own. 'Marcus.' The old man stops and there's eye contact between them. 'Five years ago I'd have never woken up for the bullet in my brain. Why now? Why not plug me in my hotel room?' 'Because you were my protege.' The answer is quick, rehearsed, like he's waited a very long time to answer that very question. 'Because I taught you everything I know, and you taught me the one thing that I didn't. I played God for many years William, and after so long I forgot that I was indeed only playing. You knew that I would come for you, it is my head that should have a bullet in it. But you realized you're not God, William.' Eye contact is broken. 'And neither am I.' Johnny sets his tea aside. 'That's not my name anymore.' 'No.' Marcus sucks in a deep breath and watches the steam roll out of his cup. 'No I know, Johnny. You've put my life behind you and you've moved on. But before we're done I have to ask you a question that has been bugging me.' Johnny pours more hot water into his cup. 'I know what you can do, hell I taught you most of it. Nobody too this day has topped the Swanson hit.' 'Really?' Johnny smiles setting the kettle aside. 'That was a gorgeous job.' The smile fades slowely as the memories, as memories do, wander astray into unsettling territory. 'Gorgeous? It was fucking brilliant my boy. Motion sensors, infrared scanners, round the clock guard, twelve of the nastiest damn mutts I ever saw and to top it off the bastard limey had a vault for a door and a nine mill under his bloody pillow. Cookie?' 'Sure. You wanna know how I did it?' 'The Swanson?' 'Yeah.' 'I think every wanna be hit man in europe wants to know how you pulled that one off.' 'I had his wife do it.' Johnny bites into his cookie, stirring his tea absently. 'Wait, you had his wife do it?' 'With his gun.' Marcus pushes his saucer aside, eyes bright and his lips on the verge of laughter. 'How did you do that?' Johnny shrugs. 'He didn't like fore play maybe. I told her how to make it look like a struggle, there was really no other way on a forty eight hour contract. To be honest, she brought it up first.' 'Bloody brilliant.' After a moment of staring at Johnny, Marcus moves his tea cup and saucer to the sink. 'You had a question to ask me.' Johnny asks, watching. Marcus keeps his back turned, asking over his shoulder. 'Why wrestling?' The corners of Johnny's mouth curl into a smile as he sips his tea. Marcus turns around to look at what's become of his pupil, his prized apprentice. 'I can kill a man before he even realizes he's dead.' Johnn'y baritone rolls on a somber note. 'In that life there was no room for fighting chances. If we fucked up, somebody died and alot of times that somebody should have been us. I don't want any more lives on my hands and there is no room in my closet for any more skeletons.' 'But why wrestling? Why not mixed martial arts where you belong?' 'Five years ago you had a man you thought to be me shot.' Marcus lets his eyes fall away as Johnny leans forward to emphasize his statement. 'But he wasn't me. He had a family, a girlfriend who had his daughter and parents that loved him. He was in China teaching english for the summer, saving money to launch his wrestling career. He could have been my fucking twin but he had dreams and direction. All I had was a filthy soul and alot of secrets. It should have been me.' For the first time in many years there was the twinkle of a tear in Johnny's eyes. 'So you take his name, his family, his child, and his dream?' Marcus smartly stays out of arms reach. 'You fuck his girlfriend too?' 'Who are you to judge me? I did what I had to do. Virtue before vengeance and honor before glory, you taught me that, remember? I'm honoring his sacrifice to me.' Johnny stands, the LSD counter acted and it's effects starting to thin, though the world around him doesn't seem as it should. Or maybe, it's how it is and he's just never seen it until now. 'I remember, I also said loyalty before greed, or did you forget that part when you left me taped to a chair and vanished?' Marcus plants both hands firmly on the island counter top, Johnny blinking and trying to focus. 'If you had let me go I wouldn't have had to do it like that. You had me too deeply invested, you were using me as a crutch. I started hearing secrets I didn't want to hear, seeing faces and learning the names of people I wanted nothing to do with. And burning down that house because some old man shot one of your street trash thugs?' 'Examples needed to be made, how were we to exercise our authority in a neighborhood that refused to pay their dues? We had plans, William, plans that we put into motion together.' Marcus' facade of calm cracks at the edges. 'Plans that very nearly fell apart when you buggered off.' 'There were children in the that house.' Johnny spits the words with venom. Marcus looks to his scarred hands, fingers curling into fists. 'I saved your life.' He growls. 'You showed me where to sign over my soul.' Johnny hisses in response, his powerful shoulders tensing. 'Don't give me that bull shit, nobody made you pull the fucking trigger my boy! You seemed quite happy to do that all on your own for two fucking years!' 'If you're not going to kill me then why the fuck am I here? You could have stopped by my room to have tea and talk about old times you know.' Marcus takes away the tray and kettle as Johnny's words linger. His expression unchanging. 'You're going to kill me.' Johnny squares his shoulders back and narrows his eyes, a statement of rhetoric devise and no question about it. 'How did you escape my dungeon?' Marcus asks quietly, keeping himself distracted with tidying up. Johnny sighs audibly. 'Dislocated my thumb to get the cuffs off, broke the chair and used the nail to get the hinges out, rope and the gloves with a chair leg to bend the bars at the bottom of the stairs. Reached through to open the dead bolts.' Pauses as Marcus turns around to face the island and Johnny standing against the opposite side. Marcus steps up to the island again, Johnny gives him a weak smile. 'Just like you knew I would. Just like you taught me, just like you set it up. Otherwise you would have tied my hands and cuffed my feet, instead.' They stare at each other, their eyes the same vivid blue. 'Picking the lock on this crappy door seemed almost ironic. By the way are we still in London?' Johnny asks. Marcus licks his lips and again checks his watch. 'No. We're in Paris.' 'Damn.' Johnny rubs his neck. 'What day is it.' 'It is just after four am monday morning.' Marcus' voice takes a snappy turn. 'I was out for two days? No wonder I'm so fuckin hungry. Listen I'm supposed to meet my wife just outside of town tomorrow, I wanna surprise her by being early.' 'Johnny.' Marcus interrupts. 'You won't make that meeting.' Marcus' hand drifts under the counter while his gaze never leaves Johnny's face; his expressions and body language. Trying to read his eyes. His own cold, long ago left soulless by the life style he chose. Johnny swallows hard, watching that arm reach. 'You never should have became a wrestler, never should have crossed the atlantic. You should have stayed hidden with all your secrets, I'm not the only one who wants you dead you know.' 'I guess I shouldn't hang out too long then huh?' Johnny offers a lopsided grin. Marcus freezes as his hand finds: An empty holster. 'I took an acting course few years ago, thought it might help my career.' The grin grows wider. Johnny raises the weapon between them, a black twenty two with a silencer; grinning a grin full of menace and secrets. The handcuffs clinking and dangling from his wrist. 'You never should have come for me alone. And yes, I've taken his name, family, child, dream, and I did fuck his girlfriend. But I also took up his debt of vengeance.' Johnny pauses to read Marcus' eyes now. He sees a look he's seen a hundred times but somehow manages to be unique for each person holding it. The look of denial and worry as they stare down the advent of death; facing their past bit by bit under the judgement of a barrel. 'You wanted to read my face when you held this gun. You could have had a thousand different guys do it just to get on your good side but you saved this one huh? You weren't just waiting for the right time to strike, you wanted me to be on the verge, you wanted me to have something to lose. The Frontier title, Marcus? Should have given me a few more months, I've got plans, you could have got me going for the Transatlantic.' Johnny steps out from behind the counter, his face twisted by the demons of his past. 'You wanted this one to be special, I'll bet you even told your guys to get lost for a couple days. Where you gonna torture me? Or were you really just gonna shoot me here in the kitchen?' 'I was thinking behind the shed.' Marcus says in a sullen tone. 'Just imagine, they still talk about the Swanson hit and that was just a weekend in fucking Venice. This could be legendary.' Marcus narrows his eyes. 'You shoulda done me that day in the park, Gus. I made my choice. I'm not a hitman anymore. I'm a wrestler now and I'm going to wrestle until theres nobody left to beat and no belts left to win or I'm broken and dead.' When Johnny walks out the front door he's wearing a pair of black slacks and italian leather loafers. A tidy blazer just a little too tight in the shoulders, back, and arms and a white shirt left unbuttoned down his chest. Lifting a keychain he aims it straight ahead and clicks. There's a beep. Johnny smiles. He descends the front stairs to the narrow street, the lazy neighborhood quiet in the dark; wee hours of the morn. 'Thank you, Marcus.' His heels clicking against the pavement approaching a smokey grey Rolls Royce Phantom parked right out front as if showcased beneath the glow of a street lamp. Marcus opens his eyes and blinks about in confusion, an ache settling into his left temple. He glances about with a sudden panic as he realize his hands to be firmly tied to the arms of his computer chair now void of wheels. Second to register are the cuffs on his ankles and a raging anger boiling out of his mouth in a furious scream that slowely ebbs away into sobs. Bouncing around impotently against the water stained walls, and a haughty iron door. -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- The sun finds a hotel room early the next morning, sneaking around the edges of a drawn curtain. Dust mites dance and frollick along the beams of warmth casting their light over an empty, toussled bed and discarded clothes. Jeans and underwear, sweaters and boots alike. A small puppy, a golden retriever, chews laboriously on the heel of a black pump. The bathroom door opens and Talaina, a towel around her chest and another in her hair, makes her way over to a suitecase in the corner, the creature bouncing along in her wake. 'Oui mon cheri. How about another before you go.' 'I have to be at the airport in an hour.' Talaina says without turning around, doubling over and letting her hair hang near the floor as she attacks it savagely with her towel. 'Who is this?' The masculine voice sounds clearer, a lightly built man with an overly hairy chest tightens his towel around his waist. His eyes drawn towards the door, his eye brows knotted together in concern. The puppy barks and bounds towards a pair of italian loafers. Talaina drops the towel from her hair and straightens her back, a look of confusion and worry crossing her pretty features. 'I thought I'd break my no drinking policy and celebrate.' Johnny says, holding up a bottle of wine, staring at his wife. 'But it looks like I missed the party.' 'Who are you?' The man says, bravely putting himself between the hulk and his wife. Johnny turns his gaze on him, and he smiles. A hundred lives on his hands. A hundred souls in his bag. A monster catching his wife cheating smiles. Uncertainty sweeps the man, and suddenly a piece of stray doesn't mean so much. Suddenly Talaina doesn't look so good; her ass not so round and her hair not so blond. Suddenly that smile doesn't look as much like a smile as a sneer. 'Johnny.' Talaina breaks the silence like a hammer through glass. 'Let me guess, you were lonely, drunk, confused and it's not what it looks like. Right?' 'He's your boyfriend?' Talaina's eyes fall to the floor, the frenchman gathering his clothes in all possible hast. 'Tabarnac.' He curses under Johnny's glare, quiet and composed, watching him scramble for an escape. 'I love you.' Talaina steps closer, tears in her eyes, but Johnny backs away. 'I believe you.' Johnny says quietly. 'But I don't love you.' He doesn't look away, he doesn't cringe and he doesn't falter. 'Johnny.' Talaina tries again, her shoulders sobbing as the tears start to fall. 'How can you? Where am I?' She stutters and gives up for the weeping. 'I'll send you home on wednesday.' He puts a hand on her shoulder. She falls sobbing into his chest, her heart wracked by despair and loss. Instead of pushing her away, instead of being angry, Johnny holds her. He hugs her as she cries every guilt-ridden tear. He realizes how little he actually knew her, and how little he ever thought of truly loving her. He hugs her because he knows this isn't her fault, that it's his own. The visions of Darcy and Aimz running rampant through his mind. The industry destroys relationships. It only allows you to be happy for so long before the people are tired of it, when the drama and the novelty wears off. They actually did love each other, their relationship wasn't born on a drunken night in Moscow, sacred vows said in half slurred words. They didn't get together out of desperation for companionship. But Darcy might deserve Aimz, and she might deserve him. They might have been destined to a long and happy life together if they were in any other business but this one. He hugs her because he knows he didn't deserve her anyway. But they're not in any other business. They're in this business, and the Frontier title has nothing to do with the state of anybodies love life. The moment is far from lost in Johnny's mind and it's not prideful bravado but cold certainty in his heart. Darcy is not defending his love, he's defending his belt, and if he holds back he is going to lose it. Johnny only got this chance because he was smarter than three other people, now comes the time he just has to be better than one. He lets her go because she never truly had a place in his life of spotlights and rivalries. 'I'll give you enough money to get settled.' He kisses her forehead and steps away. Marcus would come for her if he stayed around anyways, he wants anything that makes Johnny happy. She'll at least be safe in some small town in Russia, marry some field worker and sleep with the black smith. Her affairs could never be his concern, he's invested himself too deeply to allow a heart to fog his mind. He walks away because he doesn't want to ever end up like Darcy. There's goals in this business, and there's priorities. You mix either of those and you're bound to lose, in Darcy's case it may very well be more than just his girlfriend. 'This is twice now I should kill you, Dad. There won't be a third time. I may have to pretend to be something that I'm not, but I like it a whole lot more than who I was. That may make me ignorant, that doesn't make me stupid. You tell everybody who wants me dead that their secrets are safe, their names are forgotten and their daughters yet innocent. William Van-Mill is dead, he's been dead for almost five years, but Johnny Lexicon will kill you if I ever see you again.' He leaves her behind because to a man who has been God in the eyes of many, praise is worth more than love.
