The Toxicity of Suleimon's Mind

8 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/8/06 WAKE UP! Get a brush and put a little makeup! Usually, the CD player in Suleimon's apartment was quiet. If it was playing something, it was traditional Turkish music or something of that ilk. Music was only used to soothe the warlike Turk when he wanted to sleep or get rid of a headache gotten by listening to someone prattle on in disagreement with him. In a way, he used his CD player to get away from Dogan's adversarial bantering. Hide the scars to fade away the shakeup! Why'd you leave the keys upon the table! Here you go create another fable!! Not today. Hide the scars to fade away the shakeup! YOU WANTED TO! Why'd you leave the keys upon the table! YOU WANTED TO! Here you go create another fable!! YOU WANTED TO! Deep in concentration, Suleimon lost himself in Sun Tzu's The Art of War. It's a book he had read fifty times if he's read it once, but the Captain was an obsessive being. He longed to memorize the pages... war to him, it was more a way of life than it was just the continuation of diplomacy through other means. While war, bloodshed, violence, fighting, dischord... while those things were unsettling to the common man, they excited, gratified... even soothed Suleimon. This current walk-through of Sun Tzu's strategical opus was well-needed, after having been thrashed by the Transatlantic and Frontier Champions... yet... 'Dude, you're so five years ago on that... they put out three albums since then.' Hayvan was still bemused at his best friend's choice of music that he bought at the mall today. 'I know, I know, but you know they don't sell this shit in Turkey. I had to get my own copy,' Dogan said with a smile. The wry look on his face belied the innocence he projected. Insolent fools. Suleimon was going to get up, but he was too engrossed in his book. Besides, they were going to be there all night to discuss match strategies. But then... 'THEY'RE GOING TO BUILD A PRIISSSSON THEY'RE GOING TO BUILD A PRISSSSONNN FOR YOU AND ME TO LIVE IN ANOTHER PRISON SYSTEM ANOTHER PRISSSSONNN SYSTEM FOR YOU AND ME TO LIVE IN!!' The noise pollution... and the worst kind. He could almost feel the headache coming back. He threw down his book and stormed into the living area of his flat. With a look that could melt the continent of Antarctica, he scowled at Dogan before he stormed over to his CD player. Oh I don't think you trust in my... self-righteous suicide He popped the top without even pressing stop on the console... I cry... when angels deserve to die Grabbing the CD out of the console, Suleimon, now with a wry look on his face himself momentarily, lifted it up so that it was in plain view of the man who had just bought it and... SNAP! DIIIIIIIEEEEEEEEEEEEEEE!!! 'What the hell did you do that for!' Hayvan screamed. Dogan was too shocked for words. Or at least he acted as such. Deep down inside, Hayvan knew that Dogan bought the CD just to antagonize Suleimon. But Dogan did nothing but stare, slack-jawed, at the audacity of his cousin. WAKE UP! Get a brush and put a little make up! Hide the scars to fade away the shakeup! Why'd you leave the keys upon the table! Here you go create another fable!! Two weeks ago, there was a story on the news. System of a Down was going to join a group of protesters out in front of the Turkish Embassy in Washington. They wanted to put pressure on both the Turkish government and on Congress to recognize the Turkish genocide on the Armenian people in the early 20th Century. That issue is one of the band's pets, and they're a band known for their social activism. And it was an issue very near and dear to their hearts, seeing as though the entire band is of Armenian descent. Hide the scars to fade away the shakeup! YOU WANTED TO! Why'd you leave the keys upon the table! YOU WANTED TO! Here you go create another fable!! YOU WANTED TO! In front of the television, Suleimon was having one of those conversations with the TV where he didn't get any responses but the dry monotone of the newscaster. 'Pathetic whiners!' Sheesh, if an ethnic group that suffered one million lost in a genocide are whiners, I'd hate to what he'd label the Jews. 'Always prattling on about the past. The past is done! Move on!' He's clearly getting agitated. His voice strained, eyes bulging. 'Stupid... your people were weak! If they didn't want to be killed, they should have risen up! They should have been strong!' In his tirade, he didn't notice that someone had slipped unnoticed and unseen into the foyer, silently eavesdropping on his one-sided rant at the television. 'Stupid musicians... always meddling where they are not wanted! They think because they're famous they can villify a strong Empire for doing what was necessary! The past is the past! Your people were doomed from the start!' The person in the shadows shook his head. Suleimon looked as if his tirade had tired him. He slumped back in his chair, brow beaded in sweat. 'And I don't know why we keep denying it... we should celebrate it. It was the last time we were strong.' At that, the man in the shadows shook his head. Oh I don't think you trust in my self-righteous suicide... I cry when angels deserve to die in my self-righteous suicide... And so, we're back to the flat in the present time. Suleimon, with the CD broken in half, one half in each of his hands. Hayvan, with a look of disbelief and anger on his face, and Dogan, the look of surprise wearing off of his face, concern coming onto it. FATHER! Suleimon threw the CD onto the ground, spat on it. FATHER! He sneered, looking at his cousin in utter disgust, as if this were the last straw in his history of antagonism. FATHER! 'You...' Suleimon could barely get the words out of his mouth. 'You, you're a... you're a traitor to your country!' FATHER! Father into your hands, I commend my spirit Father into your hands, why have you forsaken me, in your eyes forsaken me... 'You sympathize with these rats! Rats who don't deserve the air they breathe!' In your thoughts forsaken me... Hayvan was about ready to go apoplectic. Dogan nudged him, making sure he kept quiet. And so Dogan opened his mouth... calmly, yet with concern... 'Why do you show no pity for the innocent?' In your heart forsaken... Suleimon's eyes bugged out like he were Marty Feldman, only without the lazy right eye. 'Innocent? HA! They were not innocent!' ...me... 'Not so innnocent?' Now Dogan was starting to get indignant. 'Not so innocent? The Ottoman Empire butchered these people for no reason other than they weren't Turks! What are they guilty of aside from the blood that flows through their veins?!' Suleimon furrowed his brow and sneered. 'They're guilty of being weak. They're guilty of not fighting back, of sitting on their behinds and not even putting up a fight. They sit in their gossamer blanket of blame and self-pity, and instead of taking the world by the testes, they whine and cry and have their Genocide Remembrance Days. They should call them Regret Days.' Hayvan heaved a little in his mouth, then swallowed. 'Dude, you know I agree with you most of the time... but I can't fuckin' stomach this shit. I'm out.' Hayvan exited the room with haste, heading towards the bathroom. Only Dogan remained. 'Regret Days... you know, that's low, even for you.' 'No, it is not low. It is correct. In this world, you either sit back and whine while people walk all over you... or you stand up and you fight back. That is why I feel no sympathy for any of these Armenians. They didn't take what they wanted... they didn't take their vengeance. 'I, however, will.' Dogan looked quizzically at Suleimon. 'I will take my vengeance against all who've wronged me... and I will start by putting three other men through tables.' The words sunk into Dogan's head the Titanic into the icy waters of the North Atlantic. He too started to feel sick, seeing the warped mind of one Tariq Abdul Wahad Suleimon at work, seeing him rationalize even the most horrifying events into his sickening worldview. Yet, he knew that this dementia would keep him going. Suleimon looked Dogan right in the eyes. 'Now if you'll excuse me, I must finish my reading.' Before heading back into his bedroom, Suleimon stomped on the CD for good measure. Dogan shook his head, knowing that his cousin was a dangerous, dangerous man, not just physically, but in his ideology as well.
