Stereophonics #4: 'Other?'

11 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11th November 2005 Tim Shipley, then – getting drunk, having sex and being beaten the hell out of. About as likely as Tyler Nelson ever showing up in a wrestling arena again... oh, forget that. But you get the point. I just didn’t have nights like this. And as I stumbled out of Valentine’s, falling through the side door into a dark Albany side street, I knew I didn’t ever want to have a night like this again. 7th October 2005 21:31 My head swam as I stood stock-still on the stone steps, peering into the gloom. I couldn’t get my bearings. I could hear and taste the pulse of my own blood. Suddenly a crescendo, and a car cruised past, clean and crisp and clinical, gleaming in the half light, sending a lashing of storm water arcing through the air. I stepped back, spitting bitterly into the gutter and subsequently wiping my mouth clear of the bloody phlegm that was sticking to my sore lips. My t-shirt, still wet from the earlier bout of rain, resignedly began again to absorb the cascade of precipitation tumbling from the omnipresent bank of clouds. There was no point sticking around. Resolving to find my bike and power the ride back to Harbrook, I squinted through the rain, ascertaining which was the quickest way round to the front of the venue (I knew the way from there) – but I’d never work it out in this weather. Every direction looked the same to me. Sticking close to the imposing railings, I jogged around the side of the building, getting thoroughly drenched in the process. There was the entrance, and as I passed by I could hear the band still in full flow, working their way through the album’s closer “Feel”. I sniffed, instantly regretting it as I took in a cold raindrop and instinctively swallowed it. Its unpleasant taste infected my already burning throat as it slipped and slid its way down. Oesophagus, I remembered with a smile from my high school Biology – the food pipe, in layman’s terms. Peristalsis: how the muscles within me forced it down. Splashing my way across the road, I wondered why my legs felt so weak. What a stupid thing to wonder. The alcohol? The sex? The beating I’d had? Take your pick. With a sudden wave of nausea, I switched my thoughts to whether I’d ever see Tatyana again. I hoped not. It had all been too surreal... it was easier without. And there was still the nagging question... had it all been a set-up? Maybe I hadn’t ever told her my name; maybe that had been a slip on her part. The seduction, the sex, the kiss in the crowd – all orchestrated, all for my downfall. The big man could have been anyone. Her boyfriend, her pimp, her business partner... But why? What had I got to offer? What could they have wanted out of me? It was a matter for another day. I didn’t want to think about it. Besides, I didn’t have time to think about it, because my jaw had just dropped open. I was staring at my bicycle chain. My bike wasn’t with it. Oh, too much, too much... I collapsed to the pavement and just sat there, my head in my hands. I couldn’t take any more. The shallow stream of cold runoff I was sitting in penetrated right through my boxers in seconds, but I didn’t even pay attention. I couldn’t take any more. “Skunk,” someone breathed just above me. I looked around wildly, eventually locating the shaven head of a young black male looking nonchalantly in another direction as he chewed his gum. Chew, chew, chew. “No, thanks,” I said carefully, and pulling myself to my feet, I shook out my trousers before hastily moving on. I didn’t know where I was going, but I felt that hanging around drug dealers probably wasn’t the best idea after the night I’d had so far. A look around, and – chew, chew, chew – he moved on. I’d never been offered drugs before. Technically, what had just occurred wasn’t even an offer in itself; the single word “skunk” wasn’t a call to action, wasn’t a question, wasn’t an instruction or an option. I guessed it was for legal reasons: rather than offering me his wares he’d simply stated their name. There was probably a loophole that way, should he ever be caught. I heard some footsteps, sloshing towards me from in front. I couldn’t see anything through the driving rain. I hoped it wasn’t someone I knew. I was soaked to the skin. My t-shirt was now completely transparent, though that wasn’t all bad; no one expected such a richly muscled chest on that geek Tim Shipley. The personification of the footsteps strode past, brushing against me purposefully as he muttered “X” in an undertone. I sighed. Another dealer. What had this world come to? “You low, right?” An accusing voice. He was back. Circling me, it seemed. I shuddered, though this might have been more to do with the wet clothing pressed against my skin. And then it was my head; I winced; more pain from the migraine. “You could do wid a pick-me-up.” A Caribbean accent with a warm, chocolatey quality to it, undercut by a purpose of ice. Horribly persuasive, though, to a boy, a man who’s been through as much as I had in one evening. “I... I don’t want...” “Why not, man? De government don’t tell you shit. You can buy smokes in de shops, you know? De smokes fuck you up big-style, chief. De smokes are bad. Addiction, you know? Dis shit... X... it just make you happy.” I didn’t know what was coming over me, but one half of me was sorely tempted. It was almost schizophrenia. A minute ago I’d been so set against it, and that conservative, sensible side still remained, but there was someone, something inside of me reaching out, overriding my wills and my wishes and blinding my brain with short-term desire. I want to be happy! it screamed out at me. Ecstasy can kill, my rational side responded. But what can go wrong? You’ve been well and truly fucked over tonight. It can hardly get worse! was the pitch from daring me. And, sadly, daring me controlled my right hand, which reached into my pocket to grab my wallet. “How much,” I muttered, and I left that question mark off that on purpose, because that was how I said it. “Twenty,” he told me, and trying not to show my surprise, I dug into my wallet. (Later, I would find that this dealer was playing on my inexperience; the street price for a single MDMA tablet was actually closer to ten dollars at the time.) I withdrew a ten-dollar-bill and a five-dollar-bill. “I’ve got the rest in coins...” I mumbled, but when I looked up a moment later, he’d disappeared. “What –” I whirled round, but the man was nowhere to be seen. “Shit,” I said, but secretly I was glad, as I hastily bundled the money back into my wallet and shoved it into my pocket. I turned back around once more and walked straight into a barrel-like chest. Oh, shit. “Tim?” How do they all know my name?! “Tim, whaddaya doin’?” But the voice wasn’t aggressive, it was inquisitive. And presently I realised I knew it anyway. “Jake,” I breathed gratefully, stepping away and visually confirming that it indeed was my friend from college standing before me. “Whaddaya doin’?” he repeated, and now he didn’t sound too happy. “Were you buyin’ – have you been –” “It’s not like that,” I quickly blurted out, but I looked away when his piercing eyes met mine. “I know there’s somethin’ you ain’t sayin’, Tim; ya look like shit,” Jake Scott told me bluntly. “Alright, alright, I was buying,” I sighed. “But don’t say a word until you know the whole story.” He looked me up and down. “I think that can wait.” There was a pause. “Why –” I began suddenly, then stopped. “What?” Jake prompted gently through the rain. “What?” “Why did he – just go? I mean – it was a good thing that he did – I didn’t really want to buy anything – but – he just left as I was about to pay him...” Why was I asking this? I was coming across as desperate... or I was coming across as a user... but to my surprise, Jake understood what I was getting at. He even had an answer for me. “How much money did ya have? Just enough, right?” I nodded, dumbfounded by his psychic powers. But he smiled genially and continued, keeping his voice loud so I could hear him over the din of the rain hitting the road. “Those dudes have no interest in somebody who can only just pay up the first time. Ya know how they make their money? Ya know how they buy big homes and build up bank accounts and send their kids to the most expensive schools? Addiction. They make their money on pushin’ up their prices, or sellin’ you dud – they’re your only supply, see. You’re reliant. There’s no other way. The more ya use, the less elastic your demand –” I broke into a smile. “Your Economics in action, Jake?” Jake had switched the focus of his studies from Sports Science (read: bullshit) to Economics-related courses over the summer, partly through my guidance. “Damn straight,” he said, smiling widely. Then there was silence, and this wasn’t too comfortable. We both knew I’d had an eventful night. He didn’t want to ask. I didn’t want to tell. But as the rain poured and the cold penetrated and the head ached and the body burned, there was something else I did want. “Jake, how are you getting back to college?” 7th October 2005 23:21 I rolled over one more time. This side of the pillow was too hot. Far too hot. I switched. But I’d already used this side. Giving up, I rose from my sweaty non-slumber and switched on the light, my eyes closed tight. The cones and rods had five seconds to adjust before I forced my lids open and stood up straight, looking around and trying to think what the hell to do. I hurt. Everything hurt. I felt sicker than sick, but most of my insides I’d already flushed down the third toilet from the right across the corridor. I’d had sex, though... but was that good or bad? Tatyana... Tatyana... it probably wasn’t even her real name. Something glinted in the harsh light: a CD case. Picking it out, I smiled at the coincidence on seeing what it was. Language Sex Violence Other?, of course. On impulse, I flicked out the CD tray and put Stereophonics’ latest in. I knew which track I wanted. I knew which track would help. I needed thinking time, time for myself, time to concentrate on nothing but what had gone on and what it might mean. I needed to relive, reflect, rethink the past twelve hours. “Rewind”. It’s your time, it’s your day That’s how it was meant to be. A day to myself; a day to do whatever the hell I wanted to do. A day to let loose – but I’d slackened far further than “loose” on the scale... on whatever scale measured it. I was ashamed of myself, but I wasn’t sorry. Everyone needs a day like this. And everyone needs a time like this after it. It’s never too late to change lanes Perhaps I’d been searching for an identity that just wasn’t there. Sex, drugs and rock ‘n’ roll – not Tim Shipley. Another life, another universe, and maybe I could have ploughed that furrow from the beginning. But tonight had made me realise that I quite liked what I had. How’s your life? How’s your place? Was it where you wanted your head to lay? It was hectic, it was strenuous and it was stressful, but it was my life. I hadn’t the time or the need for what I’d done tonight. Drinking was fun, but at what cost? Sex had been amazing, but what were the consequences? And, although any good statistician such as myself would throw the hypothesis out of the window right away for being based on too small a sample size, I was ready to draw a correlation between alcohol, girls and getting beaten up from my own experience. But wait: you can breathe You can see what I see Don’t waste your time you can’t make back I had a lot. There was no doubting that. I had a place at one of the most eminent universities in the world. I had a job that thousands would risk their lives to get a chance at – the stuff of dreams. I had both my parents – just – and a comfortable financial situation. I had shelter and food and water, democracy and stability, human rights, sanitation and healthcare and electricity. What if I’d been born in Sierra Leone? Natural disadvantage. I wouldn’t be here today. If you could rewind your time would you change your life? The question to beat all questions infiltrated my ears, my hi-fi slowly releasing the stifled sonics of Stereophonics. Would I change my life? I wouldn’t. I couldn’t. You learn from experience. Take that away and you’re nothing. Life is what you make of it. Go back and change something and the whole world shifts around you. A new universe. Something else. Something that isn’t real, something that shouldn’t be. Friday 7th October was one of the most important days in my life. I knew I would never forget it. I still haven’t. Do you like you? Now I knew.
