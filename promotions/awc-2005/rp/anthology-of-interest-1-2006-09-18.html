Anthology of Interest 1

18 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sept 18/06 'Oh my god!' Her voice reaches a crescendo born of fear and panic. 'Somebody help!' Before falling away into sobs. The wails are the first sound he hears before opening his eyes. Johnny. Heat assaults him from the left, laying on his back he blinks and lifts his arms. Still working. He sits up and looks around, the gravity of the situation slow to break through the fog. Somebody runs past him. Johnny pushes to his feet, a small fire burns not far. The orange glow glances off a peice of metal, twisted and black. 'What.' Senses begin to find their way merry into his consciousness. There were alot of people on that bus, a girl with her baby, on route to Glasgow. Johnny pats himself down, he didn't feel hurt, few bruises and a headache. Blood on his forehead. Blood on his hands. 'Help me!' She screams again. Somewhere. It's dark, small fires show the crash, there must have been an explosion. Johnny uses trees to guide himself towards the loudest screamer, a girl in a plaid skirt and a dirty blue vest. She's familiar, clutching a blanket to her chest and crying out for help, oblivious to the people already trying to help her. Red hair orange in the glow of fires, half tied back in a pony tail the rest disheveled or burnt. That's when Johnny sees the bus, the nose is crushed and the back end aimed up into darkness. His face contorts in pain, hand on his temple. -------------------------------------------------- Unable to see anything out the window except his reflection, and without the overhead lights on unable to read. Johnny shifts his head against his pillow, restless, he casts his glance about. She shuffles past his seat near the back of the bus, quietly trying to settle a similarily restless babe. 'I'm sorry.' She apologizes, a tired smile. Weary eyes and disheveled red hair. A pothole suddenly rocks the bus and she's nearly thrown off balance. A few cries of protest and surprise erupt nearby, Johnny hops to his feet. 'Are you alright?' He asks. 'I'm fine. Thank you.' She repeats with a shy smile. Head down and retreating to her seat Johnny steps after her. 'Excuse me.' Overweight and balding Johnny has to take his seat to let the man get past. He disappears into the bath room, taking his feet again Johnny spies that red hair ahead. His balance is suddenly rocked, something bright flashes from the front of the bus, a scream. He's suddenly weightless, contact with the ceiling. Darkness. -------------------------------------------------- 'Ugh.' Johnny opens his eyes again, the woman is on her knees and crying. People are running, calling out to each other, but all he can hear is a buzzing. Incessant. Annoying. Johnny jumps a small fire as somebody crawls through a shattered window and falls. About ten feet, he should be alright. He bleeding. Overweight and balding, the man's shirt is soaked in dirty water. He's breathing, Johnny gathers his arms and drags him back. Away from the bus, away from the fires that spread furthur every moment. There's a pop in Johnny's ears, a wave of pain causes him to again clutch at his temples. The nearly unconscious man falls to the ground with a groan. 'There's somebody else in there.' A man shouts, fearful of approaching the wreckage. The buzzing is slow to fade as Johnny runs back towards the bus. His steps dig in and he stops, a sudden eruption of flame nearly knocking him from his feet. There's no going back in, Johnny back pedals away. The woman's sobs grow louder, her screams more fierce. Breath coming short and fast Johnny looks around, most of the people seem to be alright. They're all watching the bus burn, listening to the man inside scream. Only Johnny is looking as another familiar face fades in from the shadows. The mystique of the blaze gives the man's features a malevolent curve Johnny would never forget. Moving the crowd he realizes he has no proof, not a shred of evidence, but he knew this was Jordan's fault. He was here, and that was proof enough. 'You weren't on that bus.' Johnny growls. Jordan White gives a half hearted smile, his eyes lit by the flames. 'I can't believe this cell doesn't work out here.' Jordan holds the glowing screen to Johnny. 'I don't know what. I don't know how. I don't know why. I know this is your fault.' Johnny's tone is low but it draws Jordan's gaze away from the fire. Biting his tongue and flexing his jaw Johnny glares only a moment longer. He turns to everybody and sucks in a breath. 'Anybody with a working phone?' He yells. He's answered by mutters and sobs, confusion as a couple dozen points of light appear. It takes a few moments before it is concluded nobody has reception here. 'The hills must be blocking the signal.' Johnny says. 'Somebody should go fer help then.' An older man says, hugging his wife close. 'That's right, the closest town can't be more than a few miles away.' A young local, half his face covered in tribal tattoos. 'The road was curving up, maybe somebody can get a signal up there.' A young girl, maybe fifteen. Eyes wide and scared, like a deer in the headlights. 'No. I know these lands, there's nothing at the top of that hill but the long road down the other side.' An american, holding his right hand against his chest. A cut tracing his face from chin to just under his left eye. 'But back, the way we came. There's a few farmers, a town. Should be able to get help there.' 'I'm goin'.' Jordan steps fully from the darkness. Johnny frowns, speechless as a few people nod and form thanks of appreciation. 'I'll go with you.' Stepping up. Jordan shoots Johnny with a spiteful glare. 'It looks like most everybody is alright.' The tattooed local comments. 'Except that poor bloke, his leg's a right mess. And that lady what can't find her baby. And that fella burned up in the bus.' Eyes lower. 'Alright, just have everybody sit tight and we'll go for help.' 'If you're comin, come the fuck on.' Jordan snaps and starts picking through a pile of wreckage. 'What are you looking for?' Johnny starts but stops when Jordan throws him a bottle of water and then clicks on a flashlight. 'Right. Good idea.' Jordan snickers and walks off into the darkness, Johnny dodges a tree to catch up. 'How did you know where to find this?' ''Saw it while you were busy playing leader. Now, uh, think we should probably get outta here.' -------------------------------------------------- A plume of smoke cascades against the windsheild. One hand flicks an ash out the open window and the other guides the steering wheel around a long curve. The cherry glows as he inhales another. One after the other, again and again. 'That was a bit of classic America with the Beach Boys, Good Vibrations. Next up we've-' 'The fuck?' Jordan taps at his radio as the speakers are filled with static. Every station. AM and FM. Defeated he clicks it off and puts his eyes back to the road. A quick flick sends his smoke into the cool night air and he fishes in the pockets of his brown jacket. A blue glow comes to life in his palm, a quick glance shows no bars. No reception. A dead zone. The wheel jerks out of his hand hard after a sudden and violent pop. A tire must be flat, his foot slams hard onto the break pedal. The phone clatters to the passenger side floor but Jordan's full attention is on the guard rail, he hits it but it doesn't give. He's stopped. He breaths again. 'Shit.' He mutters first before hitting his steering wheel with both hands. 'Shit!' Undoing his seat belt he leans over and reaches into the darkness, looking for his dropped cell phone. 'Perfect.' He says, looking at the little screen as he settles back into his seat. A glimmer captures attention in the rear view mirror. The moment is curious, shattered by the quick reactions of instinct. Jordan bails and rolls away as the bus slams into his car and goes over the edge. -------------------------------------------------- 'You'd think a road they would send a bus on would have a little more traffic.' 'Scotland.' Jordan snaps sarcastically, the pair of them walking along the shoulder of the down-hill road. 'That's a good point. What exactly are you doing in Scotland?' 'Really had to piss.' 'So you just happened to be walking along when a bus goes over the edge of a cliff? A bus I happen to be on?' Johnny's voice raises, falling back a step. Jordan spins around and aims the flashlight at Johnny. 'You ain't that fuckin' pretty, man..' Jordan sneers and shakes his head. 'Keep walkin.' He turns and storms away. Johnny raises the collar on his jean jacket and walks a little faster. 'You didn't answer my question.' Jordan doesn't respond as Johnny steps up beside him. The silence that hangs is long, both men are wearing boots and their heavy foot steps seem to be the only noise in the night. Tranquility. 'I do' not know what happened.' Jordan finally says. 'My radio died. My phone died. My tire died. Impaled, nasty. Bad? Yeah. Worse? Some local fucker rolls up behind me, lights all bright. Look, man. We both got off pretty smooth, what with still breathin' and all.' A sidelong glance. Johnny puts his hands in his pockets. 'Don't believe me?' Jordan asks. 'I believe you.' Johnny almost smiles. 'Just thinking that must be why you volunteered to go for help. You can actually feel guilt.' 'I don't know what this guilt thing is. I just really have to piss, and somewhere in this little shit town's gotta have a clean pissroom. Dig?' Johnny actually chuckles. 'Look.' Jordan interrupts, looking back. 'Headlights.' 'Good call Holmes.' Johnny's thumb extends into the beam expectantly. The rumble comes on louder, and it passes by without hesitation stirring only the chilled night air. 'Was that a tow truck?' Johnny asks. 'Probably. There's a scrapped rental at the bottom of a hill. Fuck's the surprise there?' Jordan lights a smoke. 'That was dissapointing.' Johnny lowers his head and marches onward after his smaller companion. 'What a night. I've got to be in Glasgow by Tuesday.' Jordan doesn't respond. 'How are things with you?' 'How long are you gonna keep this talky talky shit up?' 'Hard to say.' 'Then I'm fine. Everything is fine.' Johnny falls into step beside Jordan as a moment of tense silence hangs, both men watching their feet. 'You must have come through here on your drive.' Johnny can't keep quiet. 'I don't remember it.' 'How do you not remember wether you did or did not drive through this town?' 'Fuck am I supposed to tell one Scottish hick town from another?' Jordan's look is becoming more serious. 'Hey what's that?' Jordan swings the flashlight in the direction Johnny points. 'Houses. Scary beasts, they are.' Jordan produces, changing the direction of his walk, Johnny keeps a step behind. 'Not seeing anybody.' Jordan comments. 'Street lights all dead.' His beam of light illuminates a dead lamp post. 'There has to be somebody here.' Johnny walks past. 'Or at least a working phone.' 'Have fun with that, Fred.' 'What?' Johnny pulls up short. 'No, you have to help me look. You check that house over there, I'll go look around that generator, maybe there's a power switch or something. Power these lights.' Jordan's not listening though. 'There's definitely somebody here.' Johnny follows the beam of Jordan's flash light to a beat up tow truck. Tracks in the dirt appear fresh. 'Alright.' Jordan flashes the light in Johnny's eyes. 'You check the house, I'll look for a power switch. And, yeah, twenty bucks says it's Don Knotts and we're about to have one hell of a mystery. Fred.' Johnny lets out a deep breath, eye brows knotted in a frown. 'Whatever.' He shrugs in defeat and brushes past Jordan. His eyes have grown accustomed to the dark, but it's still hard to see past the extended deck to the door. Johnny ascends the stairs, a creak with every step, he looks back to see Jordan wandering off towards the tall tower in the center of the town. His flash light bobbing and dancing in tune with his stride. Johnny turns back to the deck, beseiged by shadow he plunges head first into the unknown without hesitation. A weak screen door comes open on squeaky hinges, holes and disrepair rendering it useless. Johnny tries the handle. -------------------------------------------------- Jordan approaches an old chain link fence, the gate hanging open. His light finds a cut chain a few feet away, steps growing cautious he passes the portal. The tower stands on four legs and stretches high into the air, a small shack rests to his left. To his right a box, and a pair of little blinking lights going back and forth. Back and forth. Jordan walks on towards the hut, his boots scraping dirt with every step. The hut is little over six feet high, the door a sliding peice of aluminum already half wide. Jordan's light plays into the inky black beyond, approaching slowely. He moves the light from his right to his left, grabbing hold of the door. Something moves suddenly. Jordan pulls his hand back and stops. Silence. 'The fuck?' His light doesn't waver, steadily he reaches around to the back of his pants. It sounds again, fast, it crashes through. -------------------------------------------------- The door locked Johnny hops the north side of the deck and moves around to the back. Passing an empty dog house he squints and peers through the shadows. A chain catches his toe and he nearly takes a spill, regaining his balance he leans on the side of the house and kicks his foot free. Johnny emerges from the shadows and into the moon dappled back yard. At least a dozen cars fill the space, parked haphazard and crooked. Approaching the first, a rusted out beetle. Flat tire. The next the same. He looks through a window, cupping his hands to the side of his face and squinting. The glove box hangs open and its contents spilled across the seat. 'What the hell.' Johnny frowns and moves to the next car, cupping his hands and leaning close. A trio of gun-shots pierce the night calm. Johnny snaps his head around. 'Jordan.' Dashing around the hood of a car he breaks into a run and almost slides around the side of the house. He pumps his legs fast but forgets. He hits the ground hard, his toe caught on a chain. -------------------------------------------------- Jordan's breath comes fast but he sneers in distaste. The corpse of a german shepard before him, the smoking gun in his grip. 'The fuck am I even looking for. What the fuck is this.' Shaking his head Jordan reaches again for the door. Something moves, Jordan's heart stops, an axe emerges from the shadow and nearly scores a hit. Jordan scrambles back a step and levels his gun. He fires once, through the alluminum door. He fires again, hitting the far wall of the hut. He holds his final shot. The door bursts open, Jordan squeezes the trigger and scores a hit. The axe comes around unphased, Jordan falls to his back, the flash light falling out of his hand. The axe descends as a pair of mighty legs straddle the earth, Jordan rolls to his knees, the axe hits the ground and comes up in a sweep for his ribs. Jordan's faster though, scrambling to his feet he runs for the gate. A pair of headlights sends him leaping to the side, the tow truck crashes through the gate. The figure dodges the light and Johnny crashes into a small box with a big antenna and two blinking lights. Back and forth. Seated on the right instead of the left Johnny kicks the door open. 'Get in!' He yells at Jordan who's only too quick to comply, Johnny throwing it in reverse. The truck is moving before Jordan is seated. There's a smash and the right headlight goes out, Johnny steers the truck backwards through a second portion of the fence. He spins it about as fast as he can, holding the clutch down as he jams it into second and spins the tires. Jordan slides across the bench seat and into Lexicon, scrambling back to his side once they straighten out. Jordan pulls the seat belt across his lap and clicks it in. 'What did you do?' Johnny yells. Jordan doesn't respond, fumbling with his empty gun and a handful of bullets from his pocket. 'What the hell did you do?' Johnny repeats. 'I shot a dog.' Jordan snaps back. And they both pause. Jordan's phone gives off a faint melody from his inside pocket. Retrieving it quickly he flips it open and looks at the glowing blue screen. It reads: 3 new messages. Full reception. 'What is it?' Johnny asks. Jordan reaches forward and clicks the radio on. Animal I Have Become by Three Days Grace. 'Hey. Reception.' Jordan says. 'That means those people should be able to call for help.' Johnny says. 'Meaning we don't have to go back there.' 'We?' Johnny says with a grin. Jordan doesn't respond. 'We always have worked well together.' Jordan looks over at him with a tight expression. He shrugs with a laugh, leaning back to get comfortable. Johnny smiles.
