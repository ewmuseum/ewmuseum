Date: 17/10/05 The view of the lower east side of New York City at sunset from the building’s rooftop was completely striking in its vibrant radiance. The sky was painted with a plethora of reds, oranges and yellows, all out into astounding perspective by a well-placed cloud here and there. It is the views of sheer natural beauty such as this that remind me of my own origins. It reminds me that I am not natural – I was created, artificially, in a lab at the bequest of my current employer, Cornelius Stakiri. I remember my birth well. Five Years Ago: A Stakiri Lab. Location Undisclosed. I opened my eyes for the first time in my life and stepped out into the world that existed outside of the cocoon I’d grown up in. Normal people would’ve used that as metaphor for something. Normal people would now explain what that metaphor actually meant and what was next for them. Unfortunately, in my case it isn’t a metaphor. I was given life and was subsequently ‘developed’ in the cocoon. The technical term, I guess, for what I am is ‘Artificial Life’. I was grown in the cocoon and had a veritable plethora of nutrients, vitamins and a selection of other interesting substances pumped into my body at regular intervals. The scientist may have created the cocoon, but they don’t know – can’t know - what it is like to be trapped inside. They don’t know that I can hear everything they say, they can’t know that it is so boring being trapped inside the cocoon for twenty four hours a day that I hang off every word that leaves their lips within a close approximation. The scientist known as Karl approached me first with a large blanket, wrapping it around my naked, wet body with gentle ease. The implanted memories told me it was somewhat of a taboo for a grown woman to be naked in public, but also told me it is generally acceptable for small children to be seen this way. I wonder; which am I? Before these thoughts could be examined any further, Karl ushered me into the next room. The room was oval in nature, with chairs all directed toward a stage and a large flat screen monitor that dominated the entire room effortlessly. It was here where I met Cornelius Stakiri for the first time; my creator, my father. Two weeks ago: Lower East Side of New York City. Evening. By the time I’d finished reminiscing about my rather sheltered and diminutive past, the sun had gone down completely. It will be time soon. If my information is correct – and it always is – the target should be leaving the restaurant shortly. Men of habit are always the easiest to track down and Paulo Dinucci is definitely a man of habit. He always ate at the same restaurant every Thursday night from eight to nine at night. It is eight fifty-nine now. I readied my throwing star and set the explosive fusion trigger at the side of it. Perhaps the greatest skill of any assassin to make a hit that leaves the targets family, friends and associates guessing on who and what caused the sudden death. That is my speciality – unless, of course, Mr Stakiri wants the targets family, friend and associates to know precisely who was responsible for his or her death. Sometimes it benefited my father to allow this to happen; it provided him with a powerful negotiating position in most cases. In other cases he just did it in vindictive cruelty. It pleased him to have insignificant people, who cannot possibly harm him, to know of his involvement. It is one of my father’s traits that I cannot understand and strongly dislike, but I do it all the same. I do pretty much whatever my father says. He is my father, after all. The big hand of my watch flickered onto the twelve and like clockwork Dinucci exited the restaurant with his usual cocky exuberance. His car is parked directly in front of the building and within a few strides he’s already there, jamming his key in the lock attempting to open the door. I took delicate aim with my throwing star, waiting for Dinucci to finally climb into his car and close the door behind him. With both confidence and grace intertwined into one, the throwing star cut into the cars gas tank and was promptly followed by a rather large explosion. I couldn’t but smile as the flames flicker higher into the night air. It reminded me of more innocent times, just after I was allowed out of the cocoon and my training began. I recalled those times with great reverence. Five Years Ago: Training Room. Location Undisclosed. “Now,” my father began, with a broad smile painted across his face. “Your instincts and memories have already been put into place. Whilst in the field your instincts should be able to point you toward your extra abilities with relative ease and the personality provided will allow you to improvise at any given moment…” I raised my hand and he paused mid-sentence. He smiled politely. “Yes, dear.” “Whose memories do I have?” I asked him. The question seemed to shock him and his smile quickly evaporated. “Another Subject’s.” “What other subject?” I persevered. “It was another woman. She died, but her memories were stored…” He explained. “Am I a subject?” “Yes,” he replied, losing his patience. “You’re going to kill people for me.” “But I don’t want to kill people,” I sobbed. “Ah,” He said, his smiled returning now. “But you will…” Two Weeks Ago: Stakiri Building. Downtown New York. I entered his office without even knocking. My father said he allowed me, and me alone, to do that, but I just think that’s because nobody else has ever had the nerve to try it before me. He glanced up from the mounds of paperwork on his desk and smiled. “I trust your little job went well?” I sat down in the luxurious leather chair opposite him. “Of course.” “Excellent,” He praised. “Anything go wrong?” I glared at him, letting him know that I was slightly hurt by his thoughtless query. “Of course not. I threw the star with perfect aim – it couldn’t have gone better.” My father held his hands up, defensively. “Okay,” He said, “I’m only asking.” “Anything else lined up?” I asked him, eager to move onto the next target. My father smiled. “You’re enjoying this now, aren’t you?” “Yes,” I told him, “love it.” “I told you, you would.” “Yes father,” I agreed, feeling slightly saddened inside. It felt as though I’d been manipulated, from those innocent times before the training commenced to now, but I the thought evaporated into the air as quickly as it was formulated. That didn’t matter now. What’s important now is; I’m making daddy happy and that’s all I need. “You did.” Present Day: A Stakiri Lab. Location Undisclosed. Alexa strode into her father’s office, extremely animated indeed. The doors were already strewn wide open, but despite that fact he was holding a rather delicate business meeting with a number of his elderly shareholders. They were probably here to congratulate him on another rise in share prices, or something else equally as dull. Upon seeing her enter the office, her father stood and made his way around the desk to embrace her with open arms. “Gentlemen,” her father – Cornelius Stakiri – said, “you all know my rather stunning young daughter, Alexa.” Alexa shot a meaningless smile at all of her father’s friends and they greeted her with a variety grumbles and grunts. Cornelius took Alexa aside, to the corner of the room. “As you can see,” he whispered, “I’m in a meeting here. Can this wait?” Alexa shot him her best ‘angry and I’d better get what I want’ look and crossed her arms in defiance. “No. This can’t wait.” Cornelius sighed and turned back to his shareholders. “I’ve got a bit of an emergency here, gentleman. Can we reconvene this meeting for another date?” One the older men, nearest to Cornelius stood and patted him on the back. “No matter, Cornelius. We were just about done here anyhow.” Cornelius smiled, as they all slowly filtered out of the room, quickly finishing whatever beverage they happened to have at the time. Cornelius closed the massive oak doors behind them as they left, then made his back to his seat. He lit a rather large cigar and then sat down. “Precisely why couldn’t this have waited until the morning?” Cornelius asked, lounging back in his chair. “You know why!” Alexa screamed; her arms still firmly crossed. “It’s a dangerous business, Alexa…” Cornelius trailed off. “More dangerous than the business you have me do?” Alexa questioned, knowing too well that she had her father on that account. Cornelius his head. “Alexa, becoming a professional wrestler will grant you too much exposure. Think of it; being on national television every week! Being pay per view every month! You’re putting yourself at unnecessary risk!” “No,” Alexa bellowed. “You’re more worried about yourself and where this will leave you. I don’t intend on putting my other occupation at any kind of risk.” “How in the hell can you say that to me? Wrestling’s watch by millions all over the country! One eye witness spots you and the police will be knocking down our door in a heartbeat,” Cornelius explained. Alexa delicately placed both hands on the desk in front of her and leaned forward. “Do you really think I’m that stupid? When I am doing my other work, I do intend to where a mask, dad!” “I realise that, Alexa! But it’s still a risk and I don’t think it’s a risk worth taking!” Cornerlius promptly responded. “Well I do!” Cornelius smiled. “You think I don’t know what this is about, but I do you know.” “What this is about? What are you talking about?” Alexa asked. “It’s about him isn’t it? Your little friend I sent to train you last week,” Cornelius replied. “Who? Jonathon Winters?” Alexa asked, acting slightly shocked at what her father was prosing. However, she knew as well as he did that Cornelius knew her all too well. “Yes. Ever since that training exercise you’ve buzzing about him non-stop for an entire week. And now that you’ve discovered what he does in his spare time, you want to copy him,” Cornelius explained. Alexa removed her arms from her father’s desk and folder her arms once again. “So what if you’re right. I still want to do it!” Cornelius grinned and nodded. “How could I ever say no to my beautiful baby girl. But just be careful of this Jonathon Winters, he’s a cold one and you don’t want to be getting involved with him.” Alexa silently turned to walk away, rolling her eyes at her father’s overbearing nature, but still quite happy that she’d managed to get her own way.
