The Legacy of a Feudal King

29 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29/10/2006 Like an ancient feudal lord, Jonathan Alexander Spencer Kae III sat in his satin-lined chair, eyes peering out of the large windows onto the suburban grassland. His left hand hung low over the side of the chair, while his right hand cautiously swirled a couple of drams of malt whisky over a layer of ice, in a small crystal glass. His brows were furrowed, and creased skin looking heavily taxed by time and vigour. At thirty-six, Jonny Kae wasn’t getting any younger, and he knew it. The partial onlooker knew it. And as of last week, the wrestling world knew it. In the ring, Jonny Kae had been unable to get the job done. Against an up and comer and a soon-to-retire icon of the wrestling world, he’d fallen flat on his face. Some might say that’s just the way it was scripted, but his expression said something different. The glass lifted effortlessly in his large hand to his waiting lips, letting a slight dab of the liquid loose into his throat. The lightning outside complimented the scene perfectly – overbearing lord, decadently lounging in the glamour of his kingdom. But Jonny Kae’s thoughts were far from majestic. Failure seemed to be a word common to Kae’s English dictionary these days. What happened to the man who’d won 10 world titles? What happened to the man who’d helped forge the wrestling world as it was today? No record books held the name Jonny Kae. No memories lingered over his exploits. Was he just supposed to curl up and die? Forgotten on the winds of ambushed legacies? He turned his head to the ground. The expensive red carpet seemed to provide some solace for the beating pain that laid siege to his head. He had money, lots of money. He had everything he ever wanted. For a time, he was most content. But things hadn’t been the same. They say Ignorance is bliss. They are, most probably, right. Before he considered himself in a different light, he was happy. But when his conscience had come knocking at the door, the movie reel of his life became a savage slap in the face. Now, languishing in his memories, Jonny had to face up to the fact that he might never be in the same place that he was some five years ago. He would never feel as complete, as perfect. It was all an illusion, yes, but then ignorance was bliss. In two thousand and six, things just didn’t seem as damn good. *********************************************************** Jonny Kae had played the first hand in his new battle at the door of politics. He’d refused an invitation from Charles Sandon, his nemesis in the running for the Illinois Governorship, to come down to his house just outside Chicago. Kae had, instead, persuaded Mr. Sandon to make the journey to his lavish mansion on the coast of Lake Michigan. It was the first political battle movement, establishing a power game, and a hierarchy. At this juncture, Kae was assuming the lead power position, and Mr. Sandon lapping at his heels. Jolene ran into the room, holding a fire, and wearing those sexy spectacles she tormented Jonny’s soul with. Jolene: “He’s here Mr. Kae. Charles Sandon has arrived.” Kae peered at her from his seat at the head of his conference table. Jonny Kae: “Please Jolene, I would hope we had been through enough together for you to call me Jonny. It’s hard enough I have to endure your plastic smiles and shadowed grimaces. At least can we be on first name basis? It would set my heart at rest a great deal.” She hesitated, but nodded after due thought. Jonny Kae: “Good. Now, Jolene, could you please tell Jo and Rams to come in here?” She peered at him questioningly. Jolene: “But, sir, do you think that would be appropriate?” Jonny Kae: “And no more sirs either! Yes, I think it’s appropriate. You deal with what you know, and let me worry about everything else!” Jolene held back a grimace at his words. She knew he was right about her shadowed grimaces. She personally held a paradox of feelings towards the man who was her boss. On the one side, she loved him, as a friend, and perhaps once as something more. But on the other, she despised him. She’d seen what Jonny Kae could do, and it made her physically ill. Jolene: “Yes Jonny. Right away.” Jolene left, leaving Jonny on his own for a moment. He buried his head in his hands, regretting how harshly he’d treated her. He knew no other way to do it. After a brief moment, he got to his feet. He walked over to the mini bar set up in the room, and rearranged the drinks. From his research, he knew Mr. Sandon to be partial towards Southern Comfort and Dark Rum. Personally, Jonny hated the idea of Southern Comfort corrupting his drinks collection, but there were more important things at stake. He’d also known that Mr. Sandon was a lover of Vivaldi, and so he’d perused his own collections to find a particular Cello Concerto he found most divine. The soft music played as he switched the on button from the remote control, and fiddled with the drinks, to make the Watsons and Southern Comfort most visible. He then jogged over to his black chair, at the head of the table, and turned to the giant window. The loud speaker came through, announcing Mr. Sandon’s arrival. Kae didn’t answer. It repeated the call. Jonny Kae: “Ok, send him in.” First rule of engagement…make them fight the battle on your territory. Second rule of engagement…make them wait. The combination of unfamiliarity and restlessness was a powerful one. The side door to the room opened, and Jo and Rams came in. The two large men, Jo from Manchester, England, and Rams from Brooklyn, New York, had been a mainstay with Kae Enterprises since 2002. The two had made a brief appearance in his wrestling career in the VWF, and were his most loyal employees. For that reason, they were his bodyguards. He’d nicknamed them Cannon and Mortar for their wrestling appearance, and the names had stuck. Kae quickly motioned for them to flank him on the table, standing in respective corners of the room. Third rule of engagement…make the enemy feel uneasy. Antagonise his peripheral vision. The main doors to the room opened, and Jolene came scurrying in. Jolene: “Mr. Kae…may I present Mr. Charles Sandon.” Kae was about to turn round and chastise her for using his surname again, but stopped himself, realising it was vastly inappropriate in front of the guest he so desperately sought to undermine. Charles Sandon entered, and with him came a large air of authority. Jonny Kae: “Thank you Jolene. That will be all.” Jonny sat in his chair, waiting for Sandon to make the first gesture. He wasn’t about to greet his guest. Another rule of engagement. Charles Sandon: “Mr. Kae?” Music to his ears. Jonny Kae turned round slowly on his chair, to take in the figure of Mr. Sandon for the first time. He wore a long black coat, down to below his knees. Under that, he wore a very fine grey pin stripe suit. His tie was yellow, made of silk, and his shirt blue, also silk. His hair was neatly combed back. This man had the arrogance of style, and posture of a gentleman. There was style, yes. Maybe this would be harder than he’d expected. Accompanying Sandon came two men. So, he brought his own ammunition? Though the two were smaller than Mortar and Cannon, they were none the less imposing. Jonny Kae: “Good afternoon Mr. Sandon. Welcome to my humble abode.” Charles looked at him with narrowed eyes. He wasn’t here for games. He took off his coat, and handed it to the man on his left, who nodded and took a step back. The large doors to the room closed, as Jolene disappeared to another facet of Kae Enterprises business. Charles Sandon: “You know Mr. Kae, I was rather disappointed when you refused my invitation to Central Chicago. I may not have as lavish a home as this, but I was extending to you my courtesy as a personal guest.” He walked slowly around the table, letting his finger trail across the mahogany sideboard near the window. The man made sure not to lock eyes with Jonny Kae. Kae felt the anger within him slowly rise, as he watched this man play his fingers over his furniture. Jonny Kae: “I must apologise Mr. Sandon. You have to understand I’m a very busy man. Besides, I thought this would be a more fitting environment.” Kae got to his feet, moving over to the window near Sandon. He gestured with his hand towards the window, and looked at Charles. The smaller man looked out onto the expanse of Lake Michigan. Charles Sandon: “Very impressive. But tell me Mr. Kae. Are you planning to win over Illinois’ voters with a mildly good view?” Kae furrowed his brow. He walked over to the mini bar, letting himself chuckle deliberately. Jonny Kae: “Of course not. If the people were that fickle, I would be the president by now. No, Mr. Sandon. I intend to give them much, much more than that.” Charles turned to Kae, his hands in his pockets. The two sets of bodyguards were wearily eyeing each other, playing the game of eyes. Charles Sandon: “Why don’t you tell me what you do intend to give them?” Kae smiled his plastic smile at Charles. He stopped at the drink mini bar, and gestured for Sandon to sit down. He did. Jonny Kae: “Please. Must we be so formal? May I offer you a Southern Comfort? Or a Dark Rum? Perhaps a whisky?” Charles looked unimpressed. Charles Sandon: “Whisky please. And I would appreciate if you didn’t look into my personal affairs.” Jonny grimaced. He had been too forward. But, at times like these, he purposefully lost his tact. It was uncontrollable, but he was too shrouded in his own arrogance to care. Jonny Kae: “Very well. Malt? Scotch? Bourbon?” Charles nodded his head towards the bar. Charles Sandon: “Malt if you don’t mind. On the rocks.” Kae smiled back at him. Jonny Kae: “Naturally.” He handed the drink to Sandon, who took an immediate sip of it before placing it on the expensive table without courtesy or regard. Jonny Kae: “No, Mr. Sandon. What I intend to offer the people of Illinois is a fresh start; a bright future; and my own personal expertise.” Sandon shot a glare at Kae. Charles Sandon: “And what expertise might they be sir? Taking from the poor to give to the rich? Wrestling? Oh yes, I’ve read up about you too Jonathan.” Kae smiled as he crossed the table to the window, leaning against the radiator there. Jonny Kae: “Well, if you must know, I…” Charles interrupted him. Charles Sandon: “Murder?” Kae lost his balance momentarily. He almost slipped away from the radiator. He first shot a stare at Mortar, then Cannon, then back at Charles Sandon. Jonny Kae: “What the hell are you talking about?” Charles stood up, downing the malt. Charles Sandon: “I’m sure the people of Illinois would be most interested to know what dark histories lie behind their Republican candidate. This is Politics Mr. Kae. Cobwebs are expensive to cleanse.” Kae looked uneasy. Jonny Kae: “I’ve never even contemplated such an act! Your facts are wrong.” Charles smiled for the first time since entering the room. Charles Sandon: “Am I? Facts are easily bought in this game Mr. Kae. Between you and me, I have nothing concrete, but a lot of consequential and circumstantial evidence. I’m personal friends with the District Attorney. I’m sure we’d be able to put something together quite easily, given your…past.” Kae felt the fury building further. This hadn’t been what he had expected. He’d expected a small man from a small town. He’d gotten the small man, but the small town didn’t fit. This guy was playing ball, and playing fast. Time for the saving pitch. Jonny Kae: “How bold of you. I assure you, Mr. Sandon, you’re not the only one with friends in high places. Why, with the click of my fingers I could…but then threats are so boring, aren’t they? Why don’t you quote me a little price…we’ll call it a friendly competitive gesture…to make this a level playing field?” Charles shook his head, pouring himself another drink. Charles Sandon: “Unfortunately for you, Mr Kae, I don’t deal in the currency of George Washington. The people of Illinois deserve someone who has this State’s interests at heart, rather than his own. They certainly don’t need a megalomaniac like you.” Jonny took a step towards Sandon. His bodyguards flanked him, as if to protect their master. Cannon and Mortar reciprocated. The tension was tremendous. Jonny Kae: “Oh, you have me all wrong Charles. I would make this State great. I would make it prosperous. I bring a lot more to the table than you could imagine.” Charles turned his back on Kae. He looked back. Charles Sandon: “Well, I’m afraid Mr Kae, that you won’t get the chance. If you run against me, you’ll lose, and come off worse for it. I know how you operate. I’ve read the articles, watched the news. I know about the underworld culture you bring to the table, whether you mean to or not. If it’s a fight you’re looking for, you’ll get one. And listen to me very carefully Jonny, very carefully indeed. My political war machine doesn’t take prisoners. We don’t make deals. It’s outright victory, for better or worse.” Charles Sandon put on his coat, and made for the door, leaving Kae uneasily standing with his whisky. Charles Sandon: “I’m going now. Thank you for your hospitality. I’m sure you have a lot to think about. Have a nice day.” With that, the two bodyguards opened the door, and the three walked out. Jolene came running in, with a bemused expression on her face. Jonny Kae shrugged to her and downed the rest of his whisky. This guy was good. This might be harder than he had anticipated. *********************************************************** This was his personal space. His personal room. It was where Jonny Kae came to think, to be alone. Only he was ever allowed to enter. Sometimes it had stayed empty for months, and sometimes it was occupied for hours every day. This was one of the latter times. With all the worries currently on Kae’s mind, he now turned to that which was maybe keeping him sane. Wrestling. The AWC start hadn’t been as smooth as predicted, as so many things currently in his life hadn’t been. Kae was in prime physical condition, but he had been a little rusty. He wasn’t the man he had been some five years ago. Age was catching up. But he still had a lot to give…just needed to hone those skills. Seymour Almasy and Johnny Lexicon had proven too much for Kae last week, with his fabled partner BOT. However, this week it was Andy M., new recruit to the cause, and fresh meat for the ego. A win here would certainly revitalise his career. HSW had always been a hunting ground for Jonny Kae, but curiously he’d never hit the main title. He was touted as a legend over there, but had never held the top strap. He was, perhaps, the biggest name on their roster, but had never been the front man of the promotion. Superstars like Devon Jacobs, Deszaras, Taurus Capone, The Mainstreamer and Vain had been there, but Kae was certainly better than those men. In AWC, he was not going to make the same mistake. This was a new pond, and he was a new fish. A shame for Andy M. that he had to go up against such a juggernaut at this juncture in his career. A loss when returning was always hard to bare. But then, he simply didn’t care. Afterall, Andy was Scottish, and he didn’t care much for Scots. Jonny Kae: “So, we dance the dance once again. But this time, a whole host of different partners. Last weeks endeavours were unproductive, so that puts even more Ernst on this week.” Kae talked to himself, supplying a monologue to an imaginary camera. Jonny Kae: “Andy may have his own agenda, winning the pathetic cheers of the crowd, using the Empire and such as his method. But, I assure you, I’m no ‘Kintu’. I’ve never had to inject myself with serum to look this good. I’ve never had to take damn orders to get the job done. I give orders.” He pulled his hand up to his chin, holding it there, a godly pose, as his eyes danced over the garden someone under his pay had carefully crafted for him. Jonny Kae: “I don’t know what Andy will expect. Maybe he’ll prepare. Maybe he’ll think this a walkover. Honestly I don’t care. There’s only one outcome here. The referee only raises one arm. Only one man makes the three count. And at the end of the night, James Blunt will only announce one name as the winner. All four of these events point to the name Jonny Kae.” He closed his eyes, as if imagining his name bellowed by the purple suit wearing bald phenom. Jonny Kae: “I’ve won titles across the world. From Japan to England, from Russia to America. It doesn’t matter. This is the now, this is the future. I’m not going to let my name fizzle out in obscurity, become an anonymous voice in the history of wrestling. It’s bad enough I’m not recognised any more when I walk down the street. Worse still if I’m chastised for not being capable of my trade.” He fingers nervously scraped on the expensive material of the chair, as his eyes reopened. Jonny Kae: “Business, politics, wrestling, all three facets of my life hold similar elements. Building a respectable name. But respect is built in many ways…reliability, ambition, consistency and fear among them. Dominating an opponent, whether it be financially, verbally, or physically. But most importantly, each require the kind of confidence that no man who is not fully secure with himself can possess. I may have walked slippery roads, been on questionable journeys in the past; but I’ve come out of it all a greater man.” He sighed for effect. Jonny Kae: “When I step into that ring, though, politics and Business are left on the ramp way, much as the ring announcer plays witness till his part in the ballet is required. When I step into that ring, my entire thought process is concentrated on one thing…victory, however it might be achieved. When I step into that ring, I feel whole, complete, full, destined to make my mark on the history of yet another industry, but one that plays true testament to my character, will and ambition.” Kae perked up, as if invigorated by some flood of ethereal light from an invisible source. Jonny Kae: “Andy M., forget what you’ve heard, forget what you may think. When I step into that ring, there are no apologies. You’re going to be in the ring at the wrong time, and unfortunately for you, with Jonny Kae. If only everything were so easy…” Kae slumped back into his chair. His eyes drifted across the horizon. His hand draped over the side, other hand holding the glass of whisky. He was, as a feudal lord in ancient times, brooding over his conquests, and anticipating his future. No, this legacy would not die.
