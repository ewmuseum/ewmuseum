Fallen Whispers

18 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Fallen Whispers: Chapter 2 CONTINUATION OF CHAPTER 1 – RETURN ROLEPLAY District Attorney Court, Los Angeles ‘How does the defendant plead?’ Judge Harris requested, rising onto his podium, gripping the mallets handle. ‘Not guilty, your honour,’ Lois responded, standing to address her remark. With the permission of the judge, Lois returned to her seat. Taking a deep breath as she waited for the final moment of this trial - The day when it would all seem nothing but a bad dream was the hope of Pierce and it wouldn't be long before many nightmares would cross his path. Staring toward the jury he clenched his knuckles’ under the table, feeling his nails dig into his palm. The nerves creeping silently up on him as his stomach began doing summersaults. ‘Have the jury reached a verdict?’ Judge Harris turned his attention from attorney Lois Wood towards the Jury, now seated and tired. Their previous, light-hearted and witty selves depleted through exertion, over-work, long hours and lack of sleep. His surprise lay in the Priests’, their eyes barely able to remain open. A Priest rose, Father Lockmann, a well respected Catholic with a profound interest in the Los Angeles political system. His tinted glasses - to shade his eyes - glistened against the suns ray, his pale white hand gripping the hand-rail for a sturdy leverage, feeling pressure with the camera's glaring unto his albino features. ‘Yes, we have your honour.’ Father Lockmann responded, his German-American accent accentuating through the high ceiling court house. Judge Harris nodded his head, turning toward his right and gazing at his Bailiff, issuing orders for the outcome of the verdict. ‘On the count of illegal drug use…’ Father Lockmann although used to his voice being heard in mass felt an increasing wave of pressure and nerves as he listened to the silence enveloping him. ‘We the jury fined the defendant, not guilty,’ Father Lockmann said, regaining his composure. The Judge clasped the mallet's handle and slammed it onto the wooden circle, a smile across his face. Pierce unclasped his fists, feeling the blood rush back into his fingers and palm, his pale knuckles fading back to a redness they had once been. Tony Rush smiled from the audience area, clapping his hands silently with a smile across his face. ‘Pierce Lavelle, you have been found ‘Not Guilty’…Court dismissed...’ Judge Harris announced to the relief of the Jury as he marched off back into his lavish office. Lois stood-up a glint in her eyes as she gripped her notes into her arms and turned to the media scavenging for coverage and a report. Pierce saw her eager smile to gain the 'big-shot'. He nodded with approval. ‘Go on. They are good for something, right?’ he said and shook her hand. ‘Thanks Pierce. I appreciate this,’ she leant up, placing a hand on his shoulder as she kisses his cheek. ‘They’re all yours.’ She turned to leave, but stopped. ‘How about a drink to celebrate, later I mean?’ Nodding with approval and saying the time he watched her fade through the crowd, her hair glistened against the beams of the cameras. Turning toward the bailiff he smiled and went through the exit. Back Entrance to the D.A. Court The evening's sunshine set gallantly along the horizon, taunting the moon on its journey. His eyes glazed briefly over the shadow filled back alley, before lighting-up a cigarette. The tip hung delicately between his clasped lips as he loosened his tie and turned toward the last remaining minutes of daylight. A chill crept lightly up his back along the beads of sweat. Taking a puff from the cigarette, he blew out and chucked the remaining burning tobacco onto the concrete where it became a smushed mess beneath his black polished shoes. He couldn't quite grasp enough nerve to ever fully finish a packet of cigarettes, but they did sooth his frayed nerves and migraines that had become consistent over the trying past few months. He gazed at his black divers watch and realised he had about an hour and a half before Lois planned to meet up with him. Walking briskly into the open, he pulled out his car keys and set his mind on a direction. A passing figure looked toward him briefly before disappearing in the vague mist of bottle-necked traffic. Pierce opened the door of his rental car and slid into the heated interior. 'Always a pleasure LA' he muttered to himself looking at his melted chocolate bar on the dashboard. He now needed another snack to quench the rumbling acid in his stomach. Gripping the clutch he set his gears to reverse and moved out into the vast vacuum of cars, his taillights flickered on as night now swept dominantly overhead. He still had forty minutes before the bonds agents closed; he knew he'd make it. Santa Maria, Los Angeles Vinnie Marino Lavelle was seated eloquently behind his desk sipping a brandy as he looked over the various bails made recently by his wide expanse of clients. The last year had been good to him, with his deal from Harrington and the two million now endorsed into the company he was no longer swimming in mud with the pigs. Jessica Coral sat behind her desk, twiddling her thumbs as she chewed vehemently on nicorette gum. Pulling out a nail-file she began to file her long red nails, batting her eye-lashes every so often in the mirror looking toward Vinnie who sat behind his desk, his toupee askew and the bottle of brandy empty beside him. Snorting she turned to the phone and pressed the receiver. 'Hello...' she answered with a heavy New Yorker accent, still chewing on the gum. ...Nothing. She hung up and turned to the man now entering through the door. The small bell chiming upon entry. He shut his mobile and turned the latch on the door. His eyes gazed toward her briefly as he removed his tie and placed it into his trouser pocket. 'Evenin' Jess,' he said. 'My cousin in?' he questioned, but stated the obvious as he pointed to the door. Jessica was shocked to see Pierce Lavelle standing before her. She hadn't seen him since the Harrington incident in Albany, but knew Vinnie owed a lot considering the fact Lavelle stopped Harrington and allowed Vinnie to cash his two million in bail bonds. Jessica placed her nail-file onto a stack of documents and nodded her head. Pierce gripped the small bagel which sat on a tray next to a heated coffee and took a bite, smiling toward her as she folded her arms in disgust, looking toward her only snack before she heads home to an empty fridge. Pierce entered with a sturdy posture to his frame, his muscles tense. This wasn't a time for formalities or greetings. Vinnie was nose deep in bail bonds when Pierce slammed the door for dramatic effect. Vinnie didn't flinch or look up. 'Jessica, I'm not in the mood to…Fuck - - -' Vinnie didn't have time to finish his sentence; his neck was gripped with brute force as he was flung against the wall, the blue eyes of his cousin staring back at him. 'You're with your secretary now?' Pierce stated the obvious, once again, nodding his head to a picture of Gabrielle - Vinnie's wife. 'P, Pierce?’ Vinnie stuttered. Pierce maintaining his grasp on his cousin’s neck. ‘Jesus you dumb fuck, what ya playing at?' Vinnie muttered, out of breath. Pierce drove his fingers in harder, cutting the airways off completely. 'Nice to see you too cous',' he said and released his grip. Vinnie collapsed back into his chair, his toupee falling onto the floor. Pierce seated himself on Vinnie's desk and picked up the empty bottle of Brandy. He knew his cousin was a drinker and had reason to be - gambling, smuggling, brothels’ and employment of criminals - all fronted by his bonds company. 'I need your help...' Pierce started off by saying. Vinnie squirmed slightly. 'Nah, you’re bad for business. Chase away my clien - - ' he tried to respond, but Pierce stood up in a threatening manner. 'I'm not asking, Vin,' He ordered. ‘I did you a favour. You owe me, Vin…’ Vinnie nodded, feeling a sense of earnest and respect to his cousin’s new found courage. 'What can I do for ya?' he asked with a husky voice still regaining his breath. The smell of brandy wafted up Pierce's nose. 'I'm looking for information on anybody that runs the Prometheus company that's here in LA and don't say you don't know - -' What the fuck does he want that for? Vinnie thought to himself as he pulled out a packet of cigarettes, his hands twitching uncontrollably as he put one into his mouth and lit it up, regaining the nerves and filling his lungs with poison. 'I know of one man. My mates were talkin' 'bout him. His names Vlad - No, Vladimir somethin’ or other...’ Vinnie snorted whilst choking on some excess smoke. ‘His last name, Vin…’ ‘Ully – No – Ulrich. That's it. Vladimir Ulrich. How he got a Visa is beyond me…’ Vinnie commentated while he lit up another cigarette. ‘Anyway, from what I hear, he's frontin' the operation now. He goes to La Vie, a joint downtown...' Vinnie pulled out a notepad and wrote the restaurant name down. He handed it to Pierce along with the name and phone number of a client that knew directions to the place. 'That it?' Pierce asked. 'I swear that's all I know.' Pierce nodded and began to leave, as he opened the door he stopped and turned once more to Vinnie, their eyes meeting for a split second. Vinnie uneasy, the cigarette balanced on his lower lip as he heard his cousin’s words. 'You might want to shower before you head back to Gab; you stink of cheap perfume...' Van Nuys Airport, Los Angeles The dust settled lightly along the cooled tarmac of Van Nuys airport, a jet lay prepped for take-off, its turbines winding-up as its jet propulsion engines worked on acceleration, but it had yet to move. A black limo sat parked silently in the shadows, its tinted windows allowed the moons rays to glisten its reflection. “You sure it can be done?” the man’s crisp Russian voice questioned the dark figure shaded in the back of the limo. “Have I ever let you down?” he replied, answering a question with a question. The man with the Russian accent edged forward, his grey eyes foreshadowing distrust. Vladimir Ulrich couldn’t fathom the exact nature of this business call, but they had a problem that needed removal. “You have two main targets,” Vladimir stated handing a small brown envelope across the floor of the limo. “What about casualties?” the voice replied. “Do what needs to be done, but remove the two I’ve requested. After you’ve done the job, I will contact you once more with a payment,” Vladimir reclined into his chair, swirling a Vodka and lime in his hand. “You sure your source can be trusted?” the voice questioned. The door opened, Michael Vaughn extended his robust figure in through the darkness, four eyes peering onto him, unwelcome. “We gotta leave! You’ve got a guest arriving at La Vie,” Vaughn commented, looking toward the Hitman. “Very well,” Vladimir responded to Michael, sitting himself up he knocked back the rest of his Vodka and turned to the Hitman. “You have your orders; I’ll be in touch…” Route 110, Downtown, Los Angeles Rush had said his goodbyes to Pierce before exiting the court room. His suit flapped violently in the dancing wind, a cool breeze welcoming the night as he slid into his Sedan. Ramsfield sat in the back, agitated, thirsty and tired, but Rush didn’t listen or respond to the corrupt cops complaints. He had a simple task at hand – transfer Ramsfield downtown. ‘Take it easy, Ramsfield,’ Rush said, checking his rearview mirror as he exited onto the freeway, making his way downtown. ‘Are the handcuffs really necessary?’ Ramsfield asked, sitting awkwardly in the back seat, his wrists sore and craving for blood. Rush ignored him and pulled out a CD. ‘How’d you feel about Bob Marley?’ Rush asked slipping his CD into the stereo and turning left along Route 110. ‘Guess he’s alright. Not big into reggae,’ Ramsfield said, his eyes wandering along the passing cars. Bad Boys by Bob Marley cruised through the speakers of the Sedan, Rush singing along with the best Caribbean accent he could muster, his fingers patting against the steering wheel as he kept the car at the speed limit, keeping plenty of distance between him and the car in front. ‘You trying to make us out to be complete morons?’ Ramsfield said over the steel drums. ‘What?’ Rush replied, turning the stereo system up a notch louder. Ignoring Ramsfield’s comments, he shut the slider between them and placed his sunglasses on. ‘Asshole,’ Ramsfield stuttered, stretching his legs slightly as pins-and-needles returned to his feet and hands, his ass now numb with the uncomfortable position he was in. It wasn’t so much the comfort, or lack there of, it was the embarrassment of the brown stain excreted on his frontal region that aggravated him. ‘Bad Boys…Bad Boys…’ Rush sang along placing one hand on the GPS system and typing in his next coordinates. In his rearview mirror he saw a white van approaching fast, the windows tinted. Thinking nothing of it as it overtook him. ‘Turn left on boulevard…’ the machine ordered. ‘Left is the way to go,’ Rush said with sarcasm longing for the days of maps and a simple directional system. He hated the modern technology more than most officers. The white van skidded left and came to a screeching halt, its tires skidding a moment, leaving a trailing black line of scorched tire before the clutch managed to stop it dead in its tracks. Rush saw it just in time and came to a sliding halt, his car now blocking the rest of the oncoming traffic. ‘What the fuck?’ Rush demanded, turning off the stereo. Ramsfield opened his eyes and looked at the slanted stop made by Rush, they were exactly 180 degrees to the van in front of them. ‘What’s going on?’ Ramsfield asked. ‘Fucked if I know,’ Rush exclaimed, pulling out his gun and opening his door slightly. Suddenly the back of the vans doors opened and men carrying machine guns, dressed in all black and balaclavas emerged, taking stance. ‘Oh Fuck!!!’ Rush dived onto the passenger seated, reaching for the door handle. ‘N, No…O, Oh God…’ Ramsfield cried as the guns opened fire. La Vie, Downtown, Los Angeles Pierce drove along Downtown with the direction’s Vinnie’s corrupt friend had given to him. His eyes meeting briefly with a long queue forming outside a club, the club was bustling with life, a long line of guests stood in the harsh wind as he drove his car along the road, slowly, his eyes scouting the two bodyguards that surrounded the entrance. He waited a while and watched the line. People gathered shuffling their feet, sipping on bottles of alcohol and talking to random people to keep their spirits alive. He noticed a small group of people disappear around the corner. Seeming suspicious, he got out of the car and locked it. Taking out another cigarette to remove his nerves he plodded through the small puddles of rain and gazed around the corner, watching the people enter a large door, guarded by two men dressed in lavish suits. A small sign glistened above them – LA VIE. ‘Shit...’ he thought. Gathering his nerve he slicked back his hair and removed the cigarette before heading to the door. The door men gazed toward him, the train bustling overhead muffling his words. Checking his ID they seemed surprised. This is the place. He knew it, their eyes showed it – suspicion. They opened the door and let him passed. The bar was simple, lavish and spacious. It had a European feel to its interior décor, sort of French Colonial. He moved swiftly across the red velvet carpet until his eyes met a table. A large man with gelled back black hair sat smoking a cigar, surrounded by bodyguards. He wore a black pin-stripped suit with a red interior lining and a black shirt. Chest hair peeping out over his opened top button as his rotund stomach flopped over the edge of the table. Pierce moved toward him, a large hand threw against his stomach, winding him on impact. Pierce coughed in pain and then looked up. The man gazed toward him placing his newspaper down. ‘You look a lot bigger on TV, Mr. Lavelle,’ his voice was deep, Russian decent. ‘Vladimir Ulrich?’ he questioned. He felt his momentum shift as he was shoved into a seat. ‘You are either stupid or desperate, which is it?’ Vladimir questioned blowing smoke into Pierce’s face. Pierce hardened - his jaw tightening - his muscles tensing. His reasons for being here flashed before him as a bead of sweat trickled from his hair line and down his cheek. He ignored it. ‘I want to be left alone...’ The request issued no response, Vladimir’s eyes remained passive and his facial expression droopy – as though he were bored or waiting for a good enough excuse to leave. ‘…In return I’ll leave Los Angeles and will never speak of Prometheus.‘ Vladimir laughed and cursed in Russian. ‘Stupid! You didn’t think much about this before making this arrangement, did you? What about your lovely lady friend in the DA office, your FBI friend or your family?’ Pierce didn’t respond, he looked around feeling the threatening eyes of Vladimir burn through him as he began to shake his legs with nerves. ‘I never go into business without knowing who I’m dealin’ with. Nothing would give me greater pleasure than ending your life, Mr. Lavelle,’ Vladimir grinned towards him and allowed his cigar to hang on his lips as he took one more swallow. ‘But we’ve already tried that, haven’t we?’ He held a smug grin, laughing a little as he placed his cigar into an ashtray. Pierce felt a grip tighten around his neck. Suddenly the table cloth went flying as he was reefed into the air, another shot to his sternum with a hardened fist. He bit his tongue as the air became scarce in his lungs. Left. Right. Shots to the face as another held his arms back, firmly. His eyes remained open, his jaw tensing as his cheek muscles flexed. They had finished. It was a warning, but Vladimir had threatened his friends and family. ‘See you around, Mr. Lavelle…’ Vladimir said mockingly as he watched Pierce being hurled out of the door by the bodyguards. He rolled along the dirty gravel, his knees bouncing off the curb as he collapsed into a heap against a gathering of empty barrels. Wiping his lip with the end of his sleeve he gazed at his scuffed trousers and his bloodied elbow and stood up weakly. A small man sat huddled in the corner, looking toward him. His black eyes casting the guilt he already felt. ‘Should’ve worn a suit…’ his ruffled voice spoke to Pierce’s back. Route 110, Downtown, Los Angeles The freeway was lined with dents and holes, the radiator of the Sedan leaking fumes into the night’s sky, the white van vanished from the path as a scene began to circle around the crime that had been committed. Ramsfield sat spluttering his own blood against the shattered glass on his lap, his eyes sapped of all life as he looked faintly toward the stars twinkling above. The heated pain in his legs, chest and arms had vanished, a cool liquid current ran over him, a dripping noise on the floor below as he coughed more blood out of his lungs – punctured. He was dying slowly. This is my justice… he thought to himself as he closed his eyes. His heart stopping. Rush sat in a pool of blood, collapsed on the other side of the Sedan, the pool not only his own, but Ramsfield’s. He waited as he listened to the screams and bullets and then…nothing. As quickly as they had come, they had vanished. A pain clung to his chest; a bullet had entered and threatened his consciousness with lack of blood. Another dripping noise became privy to his ears as his exasperated body looked toward the fuel tank, fluid trickling from its bowel to the floor below, dancing down toward the fumes of the burst oil tanker, which had become caught in the cross-fire. A spark. Ignition. A racing plume of heated flame. His eyes wide with terror. Hilton Hotel, Brentwood, Los Angeles He arrived back at the hotel to silence, emptiness and a hushed tone as he walked with a slight limp into the foyer. A group of tourists gazed toward his ruffled shirt, grazed knees and bruised lip. Ignoring him they moved with their backs against the wall, their eyes casting onto him with a single thought - junkie. He knew he looked awful, he gathered it from the slight beating he had received, but when he reached reception, his tired eyes met his reflection in the polished marble. Turning aghast from his poor demeanor he rang the bell. The kind receptionist who he had dealt with on many occasion since living in Los Angeles emerged holding a warm brew, her eyes reading an article in a newspaper. When she saw the ruffled clothes she held the receiver for security but recognized the face. 'What happened to you?' she asked, care and worry in her eyes. 'Fell down some steps.' 'Do you want to see a doctor?' 'Just my key, thanks...' She slid the key across the marble top, Pierce extended his grazed hand and gripped it firmly. He turned to leave, but stopped, slipping around on his heel he moved his body so he could see her through the mirror. Their eyes met. 'Any chance you could send a first aid kit up to my room?' Pierce asked and with her nod he left. He took a bath, a good long soak before emerging out of the tub and changing into some clean white boxers. The green first aid box lay dangerously on top of the granite sink. Wiping away the condensation he looked at the damage in the mirror. His cheek bone was bruised. His lip cut. His temple swollen and his elbow had a nasty gash. Pulling out a packet of antiseptic wipes, he cleaned the gash and ignored the rest of his minor injuries. Now straddling into a pair of jeans he checked his watch - the clock face broken when he was hurled onto the ground. Lying on his bed he closed his eyes and hoped the headache now pounding within his mind would fade. He was awoken from his pounding with another pounding. ‘I’m coming.’ He rose off the bed and pulled on a white t-shirt. Slipping across the wooden floor he gripped the brass handle and opened the door. ‘Hey, skippy,’ Lois entered carrying a six pack of beer dressed in casual jeans and a t-shirt, her hair hanging loosely around her shoulders, tossed a little by the wind. He ducked his head behind the door and shut it. Lois turned around, placing the beer onto the table and turned, arms folded, toward him. ‘What the hell happened to you?’ ‘Fell down the stairs!’ ‘You’re lying. Your lips move, but your eyes close.’ Lois moved forward, placing her soft hands on his face, tracing the swollen muscle along his cheek and the high bump on his temple, their eyes met and she parted, her hands lingering a moment. ‘I’m fine, really.’ He muttered, turning to the kitchen and opening up a beer. ‘And I suppose you won’t tell me how it happened,’ she said while taking a beer from him. She knew all too well he wouldn’t but it was worth a shot. He ignored her comment and placed a little Van Morrison on his CD player while escorting Lois out onto the balcony. Brown Eyed Girl by Van Morrison tinkled nonchalantly in the background. ‘How’d the media coverage go?’ he asked over the rim of his beer. ‘Extremely well, big hit apparently.’ Her reply was short and enthusiastic, her eyes lingering on the Los Angeles sky-line before turning back to Pierce; she rooted a moment through her jacket before flinging it onto the balcony railing. ‘Her name’s Ana Lopez-Covas. She’s a good friend of mine and if you ever decide you want somebody to handle your story…’ ‘Lo, I don’t want to go public about it. I just want to move on.’ ‘I know, but I said if you ever,’ she said with a sly smile and slipped the card into his front trouser pocket. Silence emitted from the both of them as they sipped quietly on their beers and listened to the bustling tone from below them. Lois moved slightly toward him, her eyes never drifting from the sky. ‘I gave-up you know,’ she said. ‘On?’ ‘Us. I gave-up hoping you’d come back or there would be an us…’ She turned to him now, placing her beer onto the balcony rail. Her hips grazed his as she moved closer. He stepped back, pulling his beer up to his mouth and taking a sip. ‘I gave up hoping, but I never stopped loving you…’ She looked at him now, a pain in her eyes as he moved closer, linking his arm around her waist. Their eyes meeting briefly as she hooked her arm gently behind his head; their lips touching delicately. Lois stopped, moving away from Pierce. Gripping the jacket firmly against her chest. 'I have to go. I have an early flight back to Harrisburg.' He dipped his eyes, looking sorrowful into the beer as he watched her leaving through the sliding doors. Lois crossed the landing, her legs moving but her mind yearning to stay and finish the kiss. She paused a moment, glancing back toward the balcony where she had left him, but didn't move. When she turned around she noticed the door was ajar. Her eyes swept with caution along the room. Shadows moved briefly from the tossing curtains placed against the open windows. Suddenly a cold hand grappled her neck, his black eyes gazing dominantly at hers. His fingers sliding down her neck and along her chest till she saw what he was holding. His face was emotionless. His eyes held no remorse. The glint of the gun whistled in the silence. 'Got a message for you...' He spoke with a husky tone. A quick flash illuminated his face. A searing pain ripped through her skin knocking her backwards, she screamed holding her stomach. The gun shot was unmistakable; Pierce swung knocking over her beer bottle as he raced into the darkened hall. His eyes watched her body fall limp onto the ground. He moved with a quick pace but felt a strong pain tear passed his lower calf; he collapsed onto the ground crashing into the coffee table, a small vase shattering around him. He screamed in pain as he turned over, feeling the bullet grazing passed his lower leg. 'P...Pierce,' she called but faintly. Her eyes fluttering in the darkened room. His scream scared her to the possibility he too had been shot. 'Lois, hold on.' He crawled, but stopped. He watched the eyes in the shadows. Black and clear, void of any emotion. The figure moved out of the shadows and was soon gone; he had vanished as quickly as he had arrived. Ignoring the searing pain and using the sofa for leverage Pierce climbed up and managed to lunge his body forward, but his leg gave way. He collided next to Lois. She was shivering, her hands placed on the warm liquid soaking through her cotton t-shirt. He moved his leg around and laid her head on it. Holding her hand he applied pressure to her wound. He could see tears creeping down her cheeks, the moons glow flowing against the salted water. 'I - ' Her words fell into the darkness, her eyes fluttering. He watched as she shook within his grasp. Her hand gripped firmly against his t-shirt, clutching on. Her green eyes faded, blood trickling through his fingers as he tried to apply more pressure, but her grip loosened, her breathing fading... 'Lois? Lois?' The darkness engulphed him; his cry muffling his screams for help … Mercy Hospital, Harrisburg, PA She lay peacefully on the bed, a white linen sheet wrapped gently around her delicate curves and slender figure. Her hands draped without any resistance by her sides, a small drip trickling from her hand to a satchel extended above her head. The humble and assuring tone of a simple beep alerted him to the fact she was still alive, her heart beating. He sat next to her, his eyes worn, blood-shot and droopy. It had been weeks since that night and he hadn't left her side - the guilt he felt became immense as the nightmares followed. Holding her soft hand he stroked her thumb with his and spoke gently to her, hoping that she would wake up and all would be okay, but nothing. 'I'm so sorry...' he muffled through a cry. The door opened slightly with the wind from the opened window, the heat of another Los Angeles Summer day blasting through the thin walls of her cubicle. He had waited long nights and days for any movement but had yet to see a flicker. Pierce knew he had been lucky, the bullet passing across him without any serious damage, but the doctors hadn't been forthcoming with her news. Lois's mother and brothers had gathered around to hear the news, all throttling the blame toward Pierce, his mother his only defender. 'Lois?' his voice spoke but quiet and refined. 'You have to wake-up...’ 'What do you mean...' Thomas shouted toward the doctor. 'Your sister, she's unconscious, she lost a lot of blood...' the doctor squirmed watching Thomas hurl a set of magazines towards him. 'Will she wake-up?' Susan stepped forward, Lois's mother held her jacket - the blood still present on the items the hospital handed over. 'I think so...' he stammered a response. All the while, Pierce sat in the steel, cold wheelchair, his eyes sore with tears and the wallop he had received from Thomas. His mother sat carefully by his side, her hands shivering with worry and fear. Thomas moved forward, holding his mother's weak frame as he helped her back to the sofa. Justin, Lois's younger brother sat by a man Pierce didn't recognise, tears ran through his auburn skin, his brown eyes hung with sorrow as he rested his elbows on his spread legs. 'How long till she wakes up?' Justin asked with quivered voice. 'Weeks, months...There is no way to know,' Dr. Goodman moved backwards, feeling the heated gaze from Thomas. 'Can I see her,' the unfamiliar man spoke with a strong Southern American accent, holding a small ring box in his hand, tossing it over and over to calm the nerves. Pierce met the men's eyes, but the man looked away, ashamed to be gazing at the man – Pierce - that had caused his fiancée serious heartache and damage. Susan looked toward the unfamiliar man and spoke with a tenderness that Pierce had never received. 'David, it'll be okay. I promise...' Thomas suddenly shifted, sensing Pierce's eyes on him. 'What you looking at? You son of a bitch...This is all your fault...' Thomas lunged, gripping Pierce's neck and hurdling the wheelchair backwards into the wall. Pierce's mother screamed for Thomas to stop, but nobody stopped him. Thomas's eyes burned though Pierce's, his reflection a mere shadow in the flames that burned brightly in his grey eyes. 'You stay away from my sister...' Thomas said, releasing his grip. 'It's all your fault...' ‘I didn’t mean to… I, I’m sorry… ’ Pierce stuttered, uncontrolled tears came tearing down his cheeks, his hands quivering as they clutched onto the wheels. ‘Pierce, we should go,’ his mother spoke with a tender tone and wheeled him away. Lois hadn’t moved, even though he hoped for a flicker, still nothing. His mind had wandered back to that night with the doctor and the news he had dreaded. He couldn’t erase Thomas’s eyes – the hate and anger – coursed through his dreams. The unexpected shock he had found out was the unfamiliar man, known as David Haller, Lois’s fiancé. ‘Lo, please wake up…’ he stammered another plea through a quivered tone. The door opened with a single touch, the handle moving as footsteps emerged. He turned around, standing with his leg still slightly limp as he hovelled forward toward his mother. ‘I think its time we go. You need to go to Europe, it’s for the best,’ she placed a hand on her sons cheek as a tear trickled down her own. She didn’t want to say goodbye to her son, having seen the pain in his heart. ‘You’re right,’ He turned to look once more toward Lois. ‘Just be careful, okay. And please…look into getting some training.’ ‘Alright mom. I’ll look into it. Take care of Lois…’ Bending down he hugged his mother, kissing her cheek gently he hovelled to the door. ‘Goodbye Mom.’ His eyes glanced down the corridor where Thomas slept, David was outside the room holding a coffee and reading a newspaper, their eyes never met. The pounding rain shuttled along the glistening tarmac as he stood under the porch, his eyes gazing once more into the hospital foyer, hoping for some resolve, but nothing came. Hailing a cab, he hovelled against the rain, sliding into the backseat…his eyes remained down, heavy and his mind wishing to…forget.
