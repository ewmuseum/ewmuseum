The Journey Home

4 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4th of August 2006 The flight was a long and arduous one ahead, he most certainly wasn’t looking forward to it, and in his eyes it seemed an unfair trade. Two years of his life contained in a cell, two years of his existence spent locked up for twenty three hours of every day, two years of his life and for what? A crime he had no recollection of carrying out? An act so heinous he surely did not possess the enmeshed enmity it required to carry out… hence the name change. Those that raised him would surely repent over his supposed sin. For all they knew, Umaga Reihana was either dead, or lost to the sands of time. A drifter. Some would call his act extreme, but to lose the respect of those who brought you up to a Fijian orphan was well, a fate worse than death. He wouldn’t allow it. Not after coming this far. He’d traded it all for a round the world flight to America: the land of opportunity, the brave and the free. Nonsense he thought. Last time he had visited the states he had their finest cowering in his hands, all in awe of the power of the Fijian Lion, the Fijian Animal. Two years traded for an eternity on a plane. If this was freedom, he’d rather spend his life in captivity. They’d spoken very little to him about why he was released and where he was heading. Perhaps they didn’t know? Though, the conjugated diction that sprung forth from The Suit’s pallet every so often was enough to suggest he would be told on a need to know basis, and well, currently, he didn’t have to know a thing. Ignorance is bliss, so the adage went – it wasn’t bliss, but it wasn’t far off. He’d never experienced freedom. Honestly. Not one experience comes to mind. Ever since birth, and being raised on the African plains, he’d never experienced true freedom. Everything was done in patterns, everything had a purpose, and no piece of energy was wasted. Every year he had spent on this earth was the same: every second, of every minute, of every hour, of every day, of every week, of every month, of every year the same. All spent in preparation of something. Preparing for combat, preparing to hunt, preparing for safari, preparing for his first fight… it seemed as if he was doomed to exist in a circle of purpose. The thing was, he’d never known anything different, but routine. Routine was what kept him going – it certainly wasn’t the stimulating conversation on the inside. Sitting around him were the highest concentration of misfits and degenerates walking the green pastures of Fijian soil: conversing was not the highlight of the day suffice. He’d grown accustom to the isolation, to the loneliness and everything besides. This freedom lark was just a shock to the system… was it? Everything seemed like a dreamy day, it had come too soon. He needed to be bled back into society. Slowly. Easy does it. Things like this just didn’t happen to guys like him. His thoughts were intermingled, peculiar, his mind painting pictures of residual imagery too complicated to explain… his most demanding thought? I wonder if they still do that Red Devil drink. They’d rushed me out of there pretty quickly. I mean, after breaking a warden’s nose, and well, a few others beside I was glad to be out of there. They seemed to have some influence. Who were these men? Army Men? Or was it the power of the almighty dollar that had given me the reigns back on my life? I found myself staring at Gatsby. On closer inspection he hardly looked like a military man at all. In fact, I would say he looked more like a suit than the Suit himself. The hypnotic lyrics of The Grouch & Eligh filled my ears. Every word caressing a new idea… He’d been given a new name and other such measures would need to be taken for his transformation to be complete. The Suit’s eyes were fixated on the back of Afeaki’s skull, ‘the hair has to go.’ His thoughts on the situation were clandestine at the minute; he knew Afeaki would most certainly not condone trimming his pride and joy, the last vestige of his heritage that clung to the walls of modern society in Fiji like a discarded condom. “Excuse me…” the Suit’s voice grumbled into action… No response. “I said, excuse me.” Again. Nothing. The Suit reached forward pulling the white earplug from the ear of Afeaki. In a flash, the Fijian’s vice like grip was tight around the wrist of the suit. “Don’t… ever… do that again.” Each word slipped monotonously from the mouth of Afeaki. He looked towards the now dormant Gatsby, “Look Suit, what is it you want?” The Suit managed to free his wrist from the clutch of Afeaki… how the hell on earth would he react to us cutting his hair? “Mr. Afeaki, it has come to my attention that certain elements of your appearance still remain, well, how shall I put it. Traceable.” GA turned to the Suit, “What are you driving at?” His voice croaked a little, “your hair…” “What about my hair mang?” “It needs to er… it needs to… go.” Silence. Absolute nothingness. For those precious seconds, sound did not exist, it was a concept foreign to all those in the plane. The Suit looked despondently on… not knowing whether the next noise he would hear were the bones in his wrist breaking, or just this… the harrowing silence was doing him no good. “Well…” the words were forced. Here, one row in front of him a 6’3”/285lbs monster sat capable of breaking every bone in his body, and more than this, someone capable of enjoying every second. He wanted to yell at him… berate his existence. His better judgement stopped him. Clever so and so… “Just let me sleep…” Gabriel pushed the Suit back down into his seat… replaced his ear plugs and slipped off into dream land. His thoughts drifted back to that night… that night when everything changed. They always did. It was one of those recurring dreams, but not the good type. It was his version of the naked in class one, only, with more costly repercussions. The truth was… you couldn’t handle the truth! Well… he didn’t know it either. He was never told. It was like one of those childhood books, “You’ve been a bad boy, so we’re going to imprison you because of it.” Except this wasn’t a story. This wasn’t a fairy tale. This was real life. It wasn’t even one of those damn dreams. The night was, well… wild to put it mildly. He’d just been announced as the number one contender for the title after a victory of hellish proportions. He’d tweaked his rotator cuff… not the greatest injury to have. In a bar surrounded by company, nothing seemed out of place… nothing at all. Then that was it. It wasn’t the tequila. He could stomach that until the cows came home, went to bed and dreamt their sweet dreams. It wasn’t the Magner’s… Irish Cider was potent, but hell, his gut had seen worse. And that’s just it. No recollection. Not a drop. It was painful. Every night spent alone in that cell. Sitting. Waiting. Wishing… just like the song said for just one more flashback, just one more piece of information. Just one piece… anything salient. Nothing was coming. It was if… as if… as if he’d been drugged. Or something… but no one came near him all night, he was sure of it… totally sure. The feint drone of a bee awoke him… A bee. Try some hair clippers. They wouldn’t? They couldn’t? They did. “That my friend… was… a big… mistake.” Lights out for the suit: it would be a sweeter dream for him. He turned to Gatsby… some explanation. No, not about the night… the fucking cretin had just shaved his hair off. “What the fuck… mang, I was sleeping…the sona munga…” The Suit’s jaw looked as if it had been broken. Possibly in three places… his limp body hung over the seat like a dead Lion’s carcass. A dead Lion’s carcass. Was he calling him out? Things were about to change suddenly in his life; always look to the future they said. In all fairness, he never listened to they… they could kiss his Fijian ass. He ran his hands over his freshly shaven head. Maybe it wasn’t so bad… I mean, a new life, a new name, a new beginning – maybe it all demanded some extreme changes. This extreme? He was like Samson. His hair was his strength. His everything… well, that and the 285lbs of lean muscles his thick frame supported. He could do without the locks… the mane… It was irking him. No. Not the hair. That night. “I’m going to sleep… you touch anywhere else… and I will asphyxiate you.” I didn’t mean it. But they didn’t know that. Well, the Suit certainly didn’t. As I embraced the cousin of death I allowed my mind to rest, just for once. Big things were ahead, perhaps bigger than those he’d left behind. It would most certainly be a journey. And that journey… is one you’re all invited along on… enjoy, fuckers. GA sweet dreams.
