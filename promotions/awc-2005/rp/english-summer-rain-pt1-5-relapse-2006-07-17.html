English Summer Rain pt1.5: Relapse

17 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17.07.06 From a tiny window, Lacuna tried to watch the rain falling. She wanted to be absent minded, figuring pictures from the patterns and using the pattering as a backdrop to think away her predicament, but she was concentrating too hard. It wasn’t a problem she had suffered from before she came off the heroin, but now it was all she could do. Everything just seemed so bland; so forced. It was all she could to do tear her mind away from it for a second when she heard the buzzing of a wasp. After a few seconds of purposely scanning the room, she spotted a large dot banging itself angrily from wall to window and back again, flicks of dust spluttering from where the collisions occurred. It wasn’t a fear that gripped her, more a wave of disgust. Bloodlust may have been a better word to describe it, because more than anything, Lacuna wanted to see it dead. In a small box Sorrow had left in the corner of the room were a few things, namely deodorants and body sprays which he thought she might have found useful. Included in this was a bug killer spray that she had never found herself wanting to use before. Now, though, the compulsion was strong. She jumped nimbly to her feet and knocked the lid excitedly from the box, shuffling around and eventually settling on the can she required, the sleek, blue killing machine. It was at the window, which she had closed earlier when the scent of storm began to flow in. She had often wondered how long it would take for an insect to evolve far enough to understand the concept of a window, but now she secretly blessed their lack of this particular gift. She crept, almost stealthily, to a position behind the wasp and raised the can of spray, closing her eyes in preparation for the kill. Just then, she heard the roar of Sorrow’s Jaguar from the drive and she began to turn a familiar shade of green. Lacuna had never bothered to take a driving lesson, having in the past always had someone to take her from a to b with stops at c, d and e for ice cream and snacks. However, she had always maintained a love of cars and in particular the Jaguar XJ8. It was all she had ever wanted, to sit behind the wheel and let the big cat roar her away somewhere. Old, stylish and quintessentially British, but beautiful and she knew Sorrow had brought it to wind her up. But back to the task in hand, which seemed easier without the chance of Sorrow bumping and barging his was into the room. He wouldn’t see her kill. The wasp hadn’t moved much, besides making small spatter-marks on the neglected window. She crept back into her wasp-eradicating position, raised the can and sprayed a jet of white liquid onto the yellow and black posterior. Instantly it fell from its position and landed on its back, wings beating in a flapping, wet sound on the windowsill. She sprayed again, this time at its exposed underbelly, and watched its legs kick. They didn’t stop; pitifully throwing away the last of its energy is some vain attempt and bringing itself back onto an even keel. Suddenly, Lacuna was hit by a wave of nausea and she threw herself into the corner of the room, spewing her guts violently and loudly into a waste paper basket. Still the wasp kicked its legs, hopeless and downtrodden but not dead. It didn’t die for some time, as Lacuna recovered control of her stomach and returned to morbidly watching the demise of the insect. Minutes passed, maybe five or ten, before she could draw her eyes from the death of the insect and close her own. Her thoughts rampaged and she didn’t know why. She had reacted so forcefully to something that mattered so little, yet a morbid intrigue had kept her tied so tight to a trivial insect. Still, it was a trivial insect that had done nothing to her. She looked up again and it was still kicking. It wasn’t going to give up, so she gave it another coat of white death powder until the whole body was a frosted white reminiscent of a decorative Christmas log, the one that the robin usually perches on top of. Still it kicked. She was sick again, disgusted at herself for what she had done and the reaction it had brought from her. The pain arose mostly from the fact she couldn’t understand what she had done. She reasoned with herself for a long time, and with each glance up checked that the legs were still kicking, until the eventually stopped. A stained-white insect frozen in a statue of death and eventual decay, a monument to what she had achieved that day. She continued to not understand what she was feeling until she fell asleep, and the matter ceased to exist. __ Lacuna was by no means an idiot. She knew what was going on around her, and even through the toughest days of her comedown she had maintained a strong grasp of what was happening and what wasn’t. She had always been a pragmatist, and she knew hallucinations and dreams would not hurt her. The subconscious was powerful, but not as powerful as Lacuna Firenze Devries. But, upon awaking, she was being taunted by her own imagination. She could smell her antidote, the same thing she had been waiting to smell every day since she came off the drugs. “He’s cooking up, the stupid old fool is cooking up” She jumped nimbly to her feet and strode lightly to the door, making as little noise as she could in case he sensed her interest, and placed her nose to the crack in the door. It was faint, but she was sure of it. But, was she? Before her face she saw the line again, and urged herself not to cross it. This sort of thing had been happening far too often recently, and she threw herself back to the bed, sighing loudly and cursing everything she could see. Through Lacuna’s eyes, there was no end to the torture. Once the sickness, muscle cramps and incontinence had passed she thought she would be done with it, but her mind was much stronger than even she had bargained for. Over the past few weeks she had learned that she could conjure almost any sensation if she thought about it hard enough. The smell was nothing in comparison, just a simple trick of the mind. Her subconscious knew she what she wanted more than anything in the world and brought it to life, rubbing it once more under her nose. Instead of allowing it to get to her, she rolled over and listened to the light patter of the rain on the window. It was still coming down, but it was still too light to really entertain her for any length of time. The smell returned to her nostrils and she muffled a scream with a fluffy white pillow, biting down until her gums began to bleed. The smell was old, having probably been created a while ago now but having taken its time in dispersing throughout the house. She reasoned that if her mind were to create a smell then it would be fresh, pure and ready to use. Maybe it was a moral to a story she had taken a full part in, maybe it was telling her that she had reached the end of her purgatory. No, this was real and she didn’t understand it. The smell was legitimate and she could not doubt it any longer. She crept softly back to the door, reaching for the lock and twisting it as gently as she could manage. It unlocked with a click and she unlatched the thin wooden door and stepped into the darkened hallway. The smell was stronger, and it solidified her confused thoughts into a solid state of mind. Intrigue and a sense of danger forced her onwards, and she noticed a sense of purpose that had been lacking for far too long. There was a light on in the room at the back of the house; the one Sorrow would sit in when he was thinking too hard about something. Usually, she imagined, that it was her and she was hit by a sharp pang of guilt deep in her stomach. What had she driven him to now? He had been so good to her, cleaned her up and pointed her in the direction of finding her feet, whether she wanted to or not. She approached cautiously, taking a deep breath and inhaling as much of the sweet smell of heated heroin as she could. The urge ran strong, but she resisted and instead gasped in shock as she saw Sorrow flat out on his back, snoring like an ox, a needle on the floor by his hand and a small trickle of blood running from his right bicep. Shock forced her to sit down and do a double take of the situation. Sorrow had cooked up, shot himself with heroin and collapsed into a deep sleep, his mind no doubt as full of the shit as a first timer could take. He would have been sensible, not using much, but he would have enjoyed the sensation, the ecstasy as the shit found its way into the bloodstream. This much she could tell by the grin that his face adopted in sleep, and by the fact that on the floor was a small ball of silver foil, half opened and leaving a trail of fine brown powder. Lacuna’s eyes sparked and she caught a gasp in her throat. She’d hit the jackpot. By the time he came round, he wouldn’t know how much he had used; he’d be busy emptying his stomach somewhere and shivering with withdrawal. Admittedly, it was his first time, but there would be a sickness. The sickness always followed. But as she leant towards the small ball, Sorrow’s pocket erupted into tune and Lacuna visibly jumped. Out of habit, she hooked his mobile phone from his pocket, answering it if only to shut it up. Out of new found intrigue, she hoisted it to her ear and listened intently, his business had always been kept a deep secret to her, and he was becoming much more of an enigma as of late. “Leave a message after the tone. Beep.” She didn’t know why, but she had found herself saying it. She kept her breathing at a minimum, something she had been able to do from an early age, and continued to listen intently. “Angel, it’s your new best friend, the man with the plan and the means to get to heaven and back. Just wanted to touch base and see how the shit went down. I left you some extra in the bag, for the come down. I hope you didn’t use the whole bundle at once, because you’ll need a whole lot more than the extra I threw in.” He took a breath, Lacuna still holding the phone to her ear intently. “Anyway, hope you enjoyed yourself. That stuff was as pure as the driven snow-“ “Yeah, maybe after you’d bloody pissed in it you lowlife piece of shit. What the hell did you give the poor guy?” She couldn’t hold it in. She had always despised dealers and all they stood for, but this Australian guy was taking the piss. She could smell it was cut with something, and probably too much to be safe. Dave was taken aback. “Look missy, I don’t know who you are and what you’re doing with this phone but that wasn’t a nice trick to play on a poor old fella. I can guarantee you that was good stuff. I don’t mess a mate around.” “Bollocks” She hung up, having left enough hints for the time being. Back to the matter in hand. Lacuna reached down, dipping a finger into the brown powder and rubbing it across her gums. It wasn’t pure, but it wasn’t terrible. She looked at Sorrow, helpless, snoring but still beaming like a Cheshire cat, and held the ball of heroin in her hand. The other hand tore at the brown paper bag, finding the comedown materials the Aussie had mentioned. They glinted in the light, and with that one look, months were undone. She slipped them into her pocket and began to tidy the small room. This is the story of the first relapse.
