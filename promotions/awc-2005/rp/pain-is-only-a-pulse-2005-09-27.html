Pain is only a pulse.

27 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: this is the end of what we learn. Leaving Eddy’s gym today, now that I’ve spoken with him, I feel totally assured. Before I had talked to him, my entire psyche had been overwhelmed by this sense of guilt or sorrow or regret; all feelings you’d normally have for someone when you can’t see them in-the-flesh. It caused a certain restlessness within me, one I could never recognize in a specific form. It was just there, lingering. But that was before I talked to him. After I read his book, but before he explained a few things to me. And once he sat down with me, making everything clear, I was more than positive that Eddy was perfectly safe. I had been delusional; worried about nothing. Our talk had actually given me enough confidence to walk with once I left. It was the type of confidence that makes you stand out in a crowd. The type of confidence that makes other people start raising eyebrows. The type of confidence that people don’t respect in a small town like this. Eddy was safe, that much was sure. He wasn’t about to be victim to the politics of the city’s stupid game—he was certain his position among Josiah and the Mutineer was certain to remain firm for some reason or another. Some reasons he had no problem explaining to me. Then again I’m pretty sure that there’s more reasons than he’d care to explain. Moving on, it must be awesome to hold such prestige in a town like this, which can’t really be surprising when 30% of the town’s income comes from your gym (forget however the hell it was funded). The twisted bastard’s Politicult needs his business to keep their totalitarian district going, and Eddy doesn’t mind feeding off of them. It’s probably why they were there when I first arrived. Indeed, Eddy Estrada seems to have it all going for him, despite being in a mildly bleak situation. In retrospect, he’s so well off that I shouldn’t even be worried about him. I’m kicking myself, because Eddy’s well being is trivial when comparing to my own safety. I’m a firm believer in the term ‘Survival of the Fittest.’ I guess I should be worried about myself, and why my head is tied off from the visual aspect and deprived of all things, “fresh-air”. My first instinct was to rip this bag from off of my head. Never mind how it got there, I’m the claustrophobic type—only dealing with the problems in front of you/around your neck and trying to steal your oxygen. Don’t deal with the “why’s” right now, man. Just rip the fucking bag off your head and we’ll deal with the rest later. Shit, it’s zip-tied. So, try to break the zip-tie. Who am I kidding? I can’t rip a fucking zip-tie! Well I have to try. Being derived of the rest of the world showed me the things that my eyes took for granted. Instantaneous, I could see the things that I probably wouldn’t have noticed. The fresh tar on the street next to me was especially putrid, and could only be found amusing by those who take a liking to the more odd smells; like sulfur and ass. There is an incessant tap occurring behind me, but still further from the Mutineer’s voice. Immediately the picture was more vivid in my brain, than what my eyes could ever show me. It was a prime situation, though. The Mutineer stood at the head of his followers; everyone of them, all alert and all impatient and all willing to act out their leader’s will. It was hard to tell how many where there, being aggrandized in my brain it seemed as if a sea of idiots followed this man like an army. All of them brainwashed by some stupid cause they’ll never really believe in but will continue to pursue because it seems easier. Footsteps, now. They come from behind where the Mutineer speaks, but indicate that they don’t belong to the same person who continues the inane routine of tapping his feet. At least I was correct in assuming there was more than one of him. And of course I was correct to assume he’d have someone else do the dirtier deeds for him. Those perfectionist-types never get their hands dirty, which is a source of their unhappiness when everyone fails to meet their expectations. It really didn’t though, and I’m guessing all the Mutineer had to do was nod. I could have probably seen his eye twitch underneath that mask, but when he turned to the man who stood over me with his foot in my back, I’m sure all it took was for Mutineer to wave his hand, flick his wrist, or tilt his head for whatever action followed. His toe touches down to my spine, his foot fully on my back, and I feel a hand grip my wrists. Uh oh. They pull up, injecting pain in equal distributions up and down every nerve-ending in my fucking body. The way I think, I’m paralyzed. Listen, man, I begin thinking. I begin panicking, and saying things in my head. Things that I should say aloud but they don’t hear it. Please just listen to me, I’m thinking. And right now I think all I want to see is the sun; not this brightness that pierces through the seams of this bag over my head. I don’t want continue gasping this warm and stale breath that makes my mouth feel like a pressure cooker; I just want to breathe crisp, fresh air and look at the trees surrounding me. I just want to know where I am. I mean, I know where I am. But I want to have that feeling of safety. So I stammer out the worst excuse imaginable. “I’m new, man, please.” I didn’t tell them about the book, which probably wouldn’t be the best time but had the thought come up I probably would have jumped at any chance to get out of this situation.
