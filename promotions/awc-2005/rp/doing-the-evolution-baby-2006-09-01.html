Doing the Evolution (Baby)

1 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/9/06 Doing the Evolution (Baby) 5th Avenue, Bailer‘s Street, New York, NY 2.03pm 30th August The morning after. Whilst basking awkwardly in the pulsating grasp of a hangover, many find the morning after to be filled with regret. With salve encrusted eyes, bonded by interlocking eyelashes, struggling to prise themselves open and allow oh so glorious light to flood into one’s corneas, only hazy flashes of the previous nights events may be recalled and so they find the easiest possible shield is to cover all bases and assume they did something wrong. One is sure they offended someone along the way to mass intoxication. The forge of one’s gruelling recovery is tempered only by the notion that it sure as hell could have been a lot worse. Not for one man. He did not realise it but his night of forceful freedom of mind, the night that was supposed to guarantee the spreading of the wings of introspection, was one despicable ordeal after another; yet despite the vulgarity that correlated with said events like a humid air encasing all in a breathless mausoleum, he could not quite recall what exactly happened. When Pat Whatshisface, the negative personality of Paddy O’Shea, came to town and took over a certain amiable Irishman’s body, Paddy O’Shea had been docile - but he had been left with a vague comprehension of what perhaps happened. This conception was liquid to him…if he did have the resolution to his nebulous questions, they became fluid before his eyes and slipped between his fingers. His mind simply would not allow him to partake in his sub conscious memories. With great strain of thought, he briefly remembered an overpowering feeling of lust; he had no image to correlate. He recalled the feeling of nausea; while his body was standing at rest, the sharp tang of vomit lingered at the back of his throat. Then for a split second he was sure he could envisage a naked, bloodied woman; a sock in her mouth that was dripping with her isolated screams, felt almost etched onto the insight wall of his skull, like a twisted perception of one man’s view on the world. And then the feeling had gone. If Paddy O’Shea had had at any point, a notion that he had had a tenant in his body, that his split personality had emerged the previous night; if he knew that this side had befriended the ever malicious Henry Robinson who sought to kill Paddy O’Shea and who had kindly provided the fists in a vicious rape the previous night; if he had knew any of this, he knew it no longer. Unbeknownst to Paddy and even his alter ego Pat Whatshisface, the seeds had been sown for the return of the later. In times of great misery, would he return to deal with grief in a way that would make Paddy grimace? It would not be long before Patrick O’Shea would realise his life A.P. (After [the actions of] Pat) was bathed in relentless desolation. However, for now, and for the first time in quite a long time, he felt rejuvenated despite a sleuth of even though’s. Even though one - he was still a child killer. The burning image of the crimson baby, forever asleep in his cot/coffin, had mitigated following “the-night-you-know-who-came-to-play”. For reasons unbeknownst to Paddy, his culpability had been, at least for now, alleviated. Even though two - he had found his hotel room a sepulchre of bedecked blood, a decoration that covered the answers that lay beneath. He obviously drew connections from this and the black spot in his reminiscence (i.e. “the-night-you-know-who-came-to-play”); yet, even though he found himself once again with deficient rejoinders, he put his mind to rest by purchasing a bottle of bleach. He would cleanse the room and his mind of wariness. Even though three - He forgot the shopping bag that contained the bottle of bleach in a taxi. Even though four - The police called him to the station today, to help with enquiries relating to a Henry Robinson. A resounding whose he had greeted their inquiry, ignorance, a thorny fabrication; Paddy O’Shea of course did know the name…this certain man had left a note along with the mess in his hotel room but to be honest that was all. His claims of complete innocence were held with tightened grasp and they released him unscathed save for a chilling account that should have shaken him to the core. Apparently, this Henry had been identified from CCTV as the killer of two airport security staff in Belfast International and one at New York State. Alarmingly, they had thought Paddy could help them because Henry Robinson had also been traced to a hospital camera from Belfast’s Victoria Hospital that had been surveying a certain ward that a certain Paddy O’Shea had been recovering from a botched suicide. This was mere hours before the incident at the airport. So even though there was four even though’s, Patrick O’Shea was refreshed. The kind police officer had given him a mug shot of this Henry Robinson in case he approached Paddy; despite the white bordered image displaying a tattooed, bald thug, he did not grow anxious. He was sure the name of Henry Robinson would never come up again. The sun nearing its zenith now inspects the Earth below and smiles, sending scorching rays to all within its vista. Paddy O’Shea bows his head to the king of the sky, shielding his eyes with his perched trucker cap from the pulsating light, as he exits the police station, allowing time for the dilation of his pupils. Skipping down the steps now, he filters through the traffic of the sidewalk and skips down the path having now decided it is a fine day to walk back to the solace of his hotel room. Despite the unanswered questions and the suspicious stain of blood, he had decided that when he gave up his mind that last night, he had made the right choice. Things were better now; for a start he was winning again. His guilt had been pacified for his previous life’s actions and a new Paddy had been born. It was as if he knew that he only needed to look at the rest of the world to know he was a good person. And he could think of wrestling again. Once again, his worries would be limited to the double figures of his win-lose record. Chainz had underestimated him and so had proved stale. With an unperturbed mind, the gypsy’s hand had slain the beast with great vengeance and furious anger. He had fallen hard, his incredulous screams resounding throughout the Belgrade arena and Paddy knew in that instant that Mike Sloan would not simply accept defeat by a man of such brittle frame. However, that was for another day. Now, as Chainz undoubtedly shirked off to mastermind a damning revenge, Patrick O’Shea felt like dancing. Not because it was bred from joy or bliss, but because every aching muscle and bruised extremity demanded it of him; because he had sweated long and hard to steward a promotion; because of every bump and bruise he endured so that he could scream out with wheezing lungs “I’M THE BEST!” Defeating Mike Sloan may be a small step towards this goal but this countdown trophy and its secret prize was a talisman; he would realise this favoured fate sooner than he expected. Coast to Coast was another step. Though it pained him to be filler for the promotion’s finest hour, he knew it was justified - his lengthy time on the sidelines though by no means pleasant did not compare to the strenuous hours invested by his fellow wrestler’s in his absence. He may still have a faint tinge of disgust for AWC and all it contains but he appreciated that basking in the glory of one’s accomplishments was a process that stipulated hard labour. Still…he felt it a slap in the face that Pierce Lavelle, despite sharing the same vantage point of inactivity for as long as he, had escaped the arduous climb back to the top by becoming Transatlantic Champion within a month of his return. It seemed that Dave Harber was keen to slap the belt on him as long as he was a member to the federation, and that the promise of equal opportunity for all in the contract of every AWC wrestler was as hollow as Lavelle’s head. Dave Harber was a shyster, and if not him, whoever pulled his strings. No, he would not think about it any longer…he wished to preserve the upbeat spirit he now carried. Harber, Lavelle and every other con in AWC could wait; right now, he had full attentiveness on the trials and tribulations directly in front of him - an East versus West match that contained the very essence of both sides of AWC. The experience of Red Rock and himself, the crushing talent of Ellis Nash and Gabriel Afeaki, the stability of Mikey O’Reilly and Colby Korver, the new blood of Johnny Lexicon and Josh Marquez. Josh Marquez. Josh Marquez. He was the crevice in the plan. He knew his duty to himself and he knew his duty to the fans…but even so, he could not turn his back on the desires of the one man who actually cared enough for him to stand by him as he struggled to his feet. Paddy was washed-up, out of shape and demoralised. Traumatised by the impending distaste of malfunction, it was Marquez who grabbed him forcefully by the scruff and told him, not to try his best, but win the fucker every goddamn time. At the expense of his own training regime, he demanded gruelling gym time from Paddy to the point that euphoric belief in himself was received in exchange for the incapacitation of muscle. Despite his brashness and the insensitive aura that followed him, Paddy believed because Josh Marquez made him believe. Showing his gratitude for returning such a rarity as self-assurance could never really be expressed he decided; but he could try. Perhaps passing up the chance of winning the Relentless belt for him could show him. After all, any deficiency Josh had in the ring was ultimately because Paddy had occupied so much of his time… Perhaps. He hated the situation he was in now where he knew, despite having him on his team, Josh Marquez was no ally. He hated that if it came to it, pinning Red Rock and winning the belt would be a betrayal of Josh Marquez who had sacrificed his ambitions for him. He hated this goddamn set-up. Give him Ellis Nash one-on-one and he would deliver onto her the pure misery that HE was bestowed with for the past few months while she gleefully chased after Adam Dick with the mind of a drugged up teeny bopper. Give him Mikey O’Reilly one-on-one and he would soon teach him what bare-knuckle boxing was. Who the hell was he? Some prick he did not know despite his insistent claims…he who used the name of O’Shea to grasp an AWC contract. He had beat O’Shea on his return, ring rust still encasing him in a cocoon. Let him try once more, now that the machine had been oiled and primed. Give him Red Rock, Lexicon; hell, give him GA and Korver too. Just give them to him one-on-one and he would prove that wrestling is about will; then he would show the bastards what a will was. Give him anyone, ANYONE one-on-one and he would show that the defeat of the Uber Judge was no fluke. He’d show, why it was Paddy O’Shea who had been Fresh!man this week. Give him Pierce Lavelle one-on-one…just one more time, and he would show the arrogant little prick that sob stories about Prometheus, drive-bys and girlfriends he had lost were a day at the races compared to the gauntlet he would set out. You can’t say that, he’s a good guy! Better re-eat your regurgitation! Ermmmmmm, no. Just don’t give him the one thing he doesn’t want to know the answer too. Don’t let him know if it is he who can stop Josh Marquez in his tracks. And futhermo- “Spare a lil, sir?” Paddy’s head jerks to the right so promptly, he thinks he must surely have had a Linda Blair moment; he grinds to a halt holding his neck. Expecting to peer down and see his heels, he instead finds his view dominated by a black youth, no more than eighteen, perched against the doorway of a liquor store. Smiling, displaying a gold tooth like the incomplete painting of a picket fence, he blinks doe-eyes and repeats himself “spare a lil, sir?” Kid was going to piss the money up against the liquor store wall for sure but who was he to judge? He knew all too well how a little bit of juice could get you through the day; suddenly you weren’t trekking a virgin mountaintop, rather gliding down a transverse slope on a bike. Dropping a few loose coins into the partly crushed Styrofoam coffee cup (contorted like his hopes no doubt), Paddy manages a smile for the lad and moves away. “Thank you, sir! Have a good day!” the boy calls after him. Unlike the bustling traffic roaring on the adjacent two lanes, the boy’s apparent gratitude is not synthetic, and Paddy grins. “You shouldn’t encourage them,” a woman walking to his right bemoans, a frown maladroitly hung from her voluminous old face. Her not-so-wanted estimation is met with a shrug. “It’s hard fer me t’ say no t’ anyone in tha’ state.” With a scoff, the dogmatic bitch hurries on and around the corner. Paddy’s gaze drifts from the street corner back onto the road ahead and in the distance the glow of the “Hyndman Hotel” sign, erected tactfully from the fourth floor of the building comes into view.. No more murders, no more enquiries, no more stress. It was time to return to normality Paddy Meets Henry Hyndman Hotel Lobby, New York, NY 2.45pm 30th August He really shouldn’t have got so comfortable. His peaceful state of mind had lasted, what, 43 minutes? Then, as he entered into the hotel lobby, it was ripped from him, as shouts as powerful as the words of Jesus himself resonated up and down the walls and spiralling into his earlobes. “Wha’ the FUCK ye mean aye can’t get int’ room 407!” Well maybe not that powerful. But there was something worthy of side note. 407’s my room Uneasiness stirred in Paddy’s eyes and they drifted to the hotel reception table off to the left. There was a plethora of people strewn about in the lobby, all transfixed on the scene being played in front of their eyes. “I’m s-sorry sir but you w-would n-need to know the occupier. And there‘s n-no P-Pat Whatshisface the-there.” The middle-aged receptionist, though stout in build and resolute in face, was finding his nerve as slippery as soap as he struggled desperately to keep a grasp of it. For the intruder in question? Paddy did not need to even pull the mugshot from his pocket; he recognised the tattoo’s on the back of his neck and the side of his face and knew already Henry Robinson was his name. A killer…and somehow his friend? “Aye can’t deal with this…” Paddy whispered feeling it all fall apart. He knew it now…he knew the deceptive truth - he had followed his confidence without comprehension, basked in the power he carried through vagueness. He wasn’t Paddy O’Shea, AWC wrestler and all round nice person…he was Paddy O’Shea, murderer and charlatan, associate of scumbags. The man was looking for him. He couldn’t take it, he couldn’t take it. Release me. “Aye…can’t” RELEASE ME With hesitation, he bit his bottom lip…then he paced quickly to the elevator on his right. Paddy O’Shea couldn’t deal with this. But maybe the other side…the one who’d cleansed him of his sin could help. Maybe the one who’d landed him in this fresh batch of shit could. Pat Whatshisface began packing his bags as he eagerly awaited his return beneath the surface. The Reminder Hyndman Hotel, New York, NY 2.49pm 30th August He DEFINITELY shouldn’t have gotten so comfortable. His heart was beating as if it never had, the thudding so deep in his skull that it pulsed in fraught eyes. He had scrambled out of the elevator like an egg and broke into a full sprint down the hallway to his room. Stretching his body to its thinnest, he felt muscles sear with incapacitation, but he did not care; there was a killer downstairs that claimed to be friend and who would certainly be making his way up now one way or another. He did not need this shit, not today, not when he had relinquished the hood of grief he had adorned now for so long. Then, as he hammered through his hotel, room door with safari like ferocity things got just that little worse. “Hello Patrick.” Paddy lurched backwards, tripping over the doorframe and falling onto his ass. He looked like a mischievous child shying away from the belt his Father had produced. “No…aye…ye can’t” “Time to go Paddy”. The voice was soft; it caressed the syllables of his name. In contrast, the two gorilla’s that emerged from his shadow didn’t look quite so mild; grabbing an arm each they hoisted the gypsy to his feet whilst Paddy tried to break the shock and offer some form of tussle. “B-but aye-” “Shush Paddy…you must be silent…we’ve got work to do.” With a stern nod, the man gesticulated for the two henchmen to take Paddy away. They complied. The man followed their exit and daintily closed the door behind him. This demure pretence was in stark contrast to the true persona of the man… something only to be seen through his actions. Setting Paddy up in an assassination attempt he had in fact sanctioned, blackmailing into continuing the work by bargaining off the life’s of his family…and now, invading his succour and dragging him off to partake in another vicious ploy; he was as false as the light of the moon. Q had much for Paddy to do, and he would not rest until his trials were complete. TBC
