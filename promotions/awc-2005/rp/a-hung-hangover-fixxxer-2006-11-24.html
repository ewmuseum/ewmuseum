A Hung Hangover Fixxxer.

24 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: november 24/06 Dolls of voodoo all stuck with pins One for each of us and our sins So you lay us in a line Push your pins, they make us humble Only you can tell in time If we fall or merely stumble But tell me Can you heal what father's done? Or fix this hole in a mother's son? Can you heal the broken worlds within? Can you strip away so we may start again? GETTING INTO IT: I'll hurt myself just to watch you suffer. That's the idea behind my special lesson for today. I'm going to get absolutely wasted in an effort to make Darcy feel as much pain as I do when I see him overworking his back for the sake of a win or two. On an unrelated note, I'd like to state that juries are anonymous groups, each individually protected from those who they'll judge. The flaw of the system, though, is that you instantly know who is and isn't the jury when they leave the courthouse. It's pretty clear to the kids standing across the street that the completely eclectic and predominantly overdressed group of people looking inconvenienced by having to take time out of their days by being there are probably NOT the defendants or the lawyers. When you see a tattooed redhead standing next to a fat Asian dude with a housewife on the other side, all being ushered out of the court at the same time... they're jurors. That's because in each side of ANY case, there's little variation. The accused will be whoever they are, while the accusing are usually from another race, gender or general walk of life. The jury is simple to spot, because the governments today try to pick out a variety of people so as not to appear or have their cases judged with bias. Today, I'm downtown with a practical melting pot of people. Most of them are family men and women, have jobs that pay almost the same as mine and probably come from rich or snobby families - I know this because none of them knew who the hell I was. I get to be the street rat of the group - the reason the courts can go 'Our jury was fair and featured people from all walks of life'. I look like I'm a hooker, a stripper, a criminal or just a bored suburban girl when in reality, I'm none of the above. I'm in Canada, 'back home', because I was selected for jury duty. Our towns are generally too small to weed out people who might know the people involved in the case, so it didn't really matter to the courts that I knew both the accused and the victim plus their families, neighbors, lawn gnomes etc. It also didn't matter that I currently hold residence in America, land of inferior cola products and oil wars. That's how desperate small towns are for juries. I tried skipping it, but it's impossible to get around things when the Canadian government wants them done. It didn't matter what prior engagements I had, jury duty took presidence. Of course, the pressure wasn't at all helped by the fact that my mom used to babysit the victim in the case. He was dead now, so I guess my presence didn't really matter much... except in the court of morals, and that wasn't a place where I stood very high. So, in an attempt to bring about home town justice and square away my half-acre in Heaven, I was trying to help judge a case that I was already too tangled in. The moment you walk home to a small town, everyone knows you're there -- it doesn't matter if you're the cashier at the grocery store or the mayor -- everyone's got their eye on you. Being on television certainly doesn't help matters. Now every single person who might NOT have known my grimey past does, and they know that they were all there to see me lie and cover my mistakes as a teen. It's not fun, and that's why I decided to drag Darcy along with me. It'll give him something to think about outside of a wrestling ring. Plus, broken backs don't swell much in cold like we have up here. Tell me Can you heal what father's done? Or cut this rope and let us run? Just when all seems fine And I'm pain free You jab another pin Jab another pin in me... PART Two: One good reason that I live in the US is because the liquor there is usually a lot weaker than it is in Canada. But now I'm home, and I went to the liquor store the moment my eyes opened this morning... Cuervo, Sauza... Fireball... For anyone who doesn't know, those are the names of two cheap brands of tequila and one gorgeous combination of cinnamon and whiskey that I've yet to find in California. Saying those three brands would be the same as standing in front of a child uttering 'Hershey, Willy Wonka and Bubblegum' over and over. You can't mess with my candy when I get my hands on it, and that was something Darcy had always been well aware of. The key in the equation, however, was the fact that I'd considerably moderated my 'candy'/alcohol intake over the last year of my life. Why? All you need to do is pull up a photo or video of me a year and a half ago, and it would be blatantly obvious why I stopped a lot of the things I was doing at the time. Five years ago, I was a drunk and Darcy Markson was a cripple. Darcy 'Crisis', as he likes to call himself, had ceased existing after being thrown off of the top of a cage. Darcy's spine was hanging on by the same thread that held his sanity and, had it not been for extensive rehab and surgeries, his lower body motion would have gone the way of the dinosaur. His persistant need for excellence is what kicked his ass, not the throw off of the cage... and he was the only one who didn't see that fact. He couldn't look at himself for what he was, because he wasn't happy with who he saw if there wasn't gold around his waist and a block-long lineup for autographs. Fast forward five years later. I'm getting drunk, and Darcy Crisis is on his way to being buried again while Darcy Markson can only poke his head through the cloud of ego every once in a while to say 'Hi'. Both of those people, trapped in one body, are on their way to agony and trials that will more than likely result in less luck than Darcy had half a decade ago. I've been watching every positive about his career turn him down further into the spiral of extremism that always consumes him and, while I struggled to stay away from the booze after it destroyed me all those years ago, I was seeing him do the equivalent of me drinking a quart a night just by working out and training his suplexing. You see, everyone has destructive behavioral patterns -- obsessive tics, if you will -- that are never quite quenched. That's why they're patterns... your head tricks you to hit the next obstacle for the high you desire, but you never seem to be able to get the ultimate fix. Most people's patterns are in gambling, alcoholism, drug use and other general addictions, while a number of people face all sorts of different patterns... some might even use wrestling to attain the 'high' that comes alongside money, fame and the ability to punch someone out without being in court the next day. When it comes to dealing with people who suffer from the later types of behavior disruptions, you usually need to utilize less orthodox methods to bring the problem to their attention before they hurt themselves... again. This is where my plan comes in. One of my destructive patterns is the endless pursuit of being deemed correct or right in a situation. Another pattern for me is the use of alcohol and drugs to attain a physical high that I always end up paying for with illness and a lack of funds in my wallet the next day. Tonight, I'm combining the two in a sort of case study on Darcy and his own problems. I'll get drunk to make him think I'm repeating a horrible pattern. He'll feel the pain that you exist through when you're watching a loved one hurt themselves, and then he'll piece it all together and come to the realization that I'm watching him do the same type of thing, only with a sport instead of a bottle. At least, that's what I'm going for. My vice of choice for this experiment will be alcohol, mainly due to the fact that I'm not quite stubborn enough to restart my cocaine habit just for the sake of getting a decent 'I Told You So' in. He'd been freezing his ass off all day, so the moment that my parents went off visiting one of my brothers for the night, Darcy had the heat cranked as high as he could stand it. Poor thing is too used to sweating. When he came into the room, I didn't hesitate before offering a drink. This wasn't to get him drunk, but the bitter taste of the tequila he'd end up pouring into his mouth would make him look twice at whoever had given it to him. And in this case, he'd be looking at me... so it might spark him to wonder why I had a lineup of bottles in front of me. That's when he threw me for a loop. Instead of realizing what the hell was going on, he let himself verbally fall directly into this little trap I've set. He claimed that he was worried about his match, something about 'Worried about the guys I can't take on' in response to a joke about my dad kicking his ass. I, of course, couldn't help but respond with my worry. ''Do you ever think about what happened in Pier Six?'' was the first wake-up call, and I was using my annoying, high-pitched nagging voice, ''This is ten times as bad, Darcy... and your spine honestly isn't getting my vote of confidence.'' See, my current problem with Darcy Markson is the fact that he's repeating what nearly crippled him five years ago. My specific issue with what happened back then was the fact that not only was he in pain, but he was also single. That's really not an issue I'd care to contend with again. Call me selfish, but I've got a right to want to keep him able to walk... and lay down in my bed at night. Naturally, he argued with some random tirade about how great and successful he's been in his wrestling efforts so far. Of course, he left out the critical word 'Luck'. Also natural was my response that effectively just pointed a giant neon 'SHE'S TRYING TO TELL YOU SOMETHING' sign at me. Now, the tequila's starting to take hold but if I'm at all correct, I asked a bitchy question and followed it up with, ''Maybe you should put down your wrestling tapes and start researching the torture of seeing someone you love repeat destructive past behaviors just to get a buzz that'll only make them suffer.'' Again, there's a giant neon sign pointed at my silly dual-coloured eyes. Things are starting to get a little blurry. Well, it's not really my vision that's being messed with -- it's the sensation of balance. When I moved my head to one side just to raise an eyebrow at Darcy's cocky response, I nearly doubled over in dizziness. That's the easiest way to diagnose the problem that's keeping me from properly reading his emotional response to my concern - it's called drunk...itisismsomethingorother. I'm starting to realize that this plan isn't working as well as I'd hoped, just around the time that my hazy little eyes watch Darcy walk away. I honestly have absolutely no clue what he just finished saying to me, if anything at all. I do know that I'm going to be hungover tomorrow morning no matter what I do at this point, so I think I'll have a few more before I pass out in bed for a good night of sleep before the 40 Ounce Flu sets in. Mirror, mirror upon the wall Break the spell or become the doll I see you sharpening the pins So the holes will remind us We're just the toys in the hands of another And in time, the needles turn from shine to rust... PART Three: ''You a'right, Amy?'' A tiny man beside me was peeping in my ear. I hate court rooms, and I might actually hate hangovers a little more. He knows who I am because he's the father of a kid my mom used to look after when I was still a toddler. I'm not sure he expected me to grow up like this, but back then I doubt he saw himself twenty years down the line with a pot belly, no hair and a bad back -- which I only know because he sits just like Darcy does. I only know that because I've been bored enough to watch the finer points of my jury-mates' existance. Why? Well, over the last ten hours, and even during an hour-long recess, we got to hear about a young man being beat up at a party. A high school chum of mine got his ass handed to him when he showed up to a party he apparently wasn't welcome at. He walked away from the beating and got jumped again at the end of the driveway to the house party. That's not all... a few days ago, he was found in a bog only 1.2 kilometres from the party. Clearly, he wasn't alive, or I wouldn't be sitting in this god damned jury box. Here's the kicker: Autopsy determined that he died from 'Exposure'. Saying someone died from exposure doesn't mean anything except that the guy was outside and it was chilly. They don't know if he was drunk at the time, which I think is completely ridiculous -- if you can tell that the pristine 19 year old man died of the sniffles, I'm pretty sure you can find out what his blood alcohol content was upon death. But that's one of many little details that the police have decided not to fill us in on, despite the fact that we're here deciding the fate of four guys who could be up for manslaughter - that's a step down from murder - charges and spend half their lives in prison. There's another layer to this cake. Remember that small town babble I was spewing earlier? It's true. Everyone in this room knows everyone else to varying degrees. I happen to know both the young man who died and the guys whose lives I'm currently juggling around with some fat guys and ladies who smell like they thought they could be held in contempt of court for not wearing eight gallons perfume. But I'm ahead of myself. I still haven't answered Chubby beside me. ''Fine. Why?'' Apparently he's smart enough to know that looking at photographs of the scene on the night that my former classmate croaked wouldn't make me especially perky. Or maybe he just noticed that I smell like a brewery that got doused in a heaping dose of ASS. Either way, I completely ignore his answer and, when it looks like he's done yapping, I smile one of those 'Thanks for asking, but I didn't hear a word you said' smiles and turn my feigned attention back to the front of the room, where a Crown lawyer is telling us all about frostbite and blahblahblahblahblahblahblah... I don't think I'm the only one pretending to pay attention. Everyone within a hundred miles has read the paper, listened to the radio or watched the news and, as human nature dictates, they've drawn their own conclusions that probably can't be moved by anything short of the victim's reincarnation. I've got my opinions, and so do the eleven rednecks around me. The lawyers look happy to be prosecuting something other than a child custody or assault case, so the entire jury is throwing them a bone by pretending that their words are actually going to sway the conclusions we walked in here with. While they babble, I'm just planning what to do when I get home. My parents are still off visiting my brother Kevin, his wife and my niece, so I've got free roam again tonight. That means I can get drunk in front of Darcy again, but this time avoid getting TOO drunk so I can handle myself and really press my point to him, unlike I did last night. He drove me to the courthouse this morning, but seemed to avoid mentioning anything other than the case that I technically shouldn't even have told him about. By law, I'm supposed to keep hush and not even tell people what kind of case I've been called to jury over. Of course, there's that small-town style again... nobody in this room will keep their mouths shut, and our families, friends, neighbors... perhaps even the squirrels who raid our bird feeders will know the gritty details of exactly what was said in this court room today. At least, our versions of the gritty details... since we aren't exactly paying attention to the morons trying to change what we all think. Quite clearly, they think like I do. They're all smiling at me, letting me know we're all on the same page despite the talking suits in front of us. And what do I think? Chris died because he was drunk, got knocked around a bit and passed out wasted in the woods. I've had it happen to me a trillion times, only without the whole 'death' part. In fact, I'm gonna tell Darcy the whole story the very moment we get out of here... maybe he'll link the part where he gets drunk, then dies to my recent efforts. Then again, after last night's clues falling on deaf ears and blind eyes, I'm not so sure Mister 'Crisis' (As I'm told to call him this week) is going to be connecting any dots. The theme of this whole trip is death, booze and nagging. I booze it up while nagging him not to die in the ring just to stay famous and be called the best. Now I've found myself jotting down little notes of key points I should bring up tonight when I try to convince him to smarten the hell up... but that really doesn't help the case I'm supposed to be working on. And it's very clear to me when I turn my attention back to the more current case that something went wrong somewhere along the line, because the first thing I hear is one of the jurors telling a law intern that we all think the boys should be charged for manslaughter. Given the fact that I don't believe they voluntarily or involuntarily killed Chris, it's clear that I MUST have nodded my head at the wrong time when I wasn't paying attention. And now, while the judge takes a few minutes off, we're ushered into a lovely little jury room where I'm about to tear their heads off. We're given an 'indefinite' period of time to deliberate, despite all apparently previously agreeing to a verdict. Of course, the first words out of my mouth are, ''How can you all disagree with me?!'' ''They assaulted him and then he was found dead in the bog on the same property, it's not hard to connect the dots,'' said a lovely woman who looked something like Condoleeza Rice and used to work at the mall pretzel booth, which made me wonder how she could afford such a nice pantsuit, ''Plus, you already gave it the go ahead. We all agree, and you can't just hold everyone up because you think it might have happened a different way.'' ''I don't think anything happened in a different way.'' ... And I think that's what they found so hard to swallow. Maybe it makes them feel some significance to condemn these guys, or maybe they just want a good story to discuss over the dinner table while the news is on the livingroom TV. I continued in my own defense, even though it was obvious that they were all ready to take my head off for delaying their supper hour, ''I think he got beat up, like a lot of people do. The statement we have said there was no significant damage. That means he wasn't walking around with a concussion or any brain damage. He was, however, drunk off his ass from everything I've heard when I've been standing in the line at the liquor store. It's November, how long do you think you'd survive if you were wasted and wandering around a marsh, then passed out?'' ''They beat him!'' I have four lives in my hands, and it's hard to carry all of them when I'm also weighed down by the need to give my friend's family some sort of closure. On top of that, I'm being dogpiled by a bunch of stuffy jurors who all know my mom to sentence the previously mentioned 'killers'. On top of THAT, I'm the only one who even has a hope of stopping the love of my life before he ends up in a wheelchair for the rest of HIS life. God, vacations suck! ''That's the easy answer, the one that'll keep you guys sleeping soundly when you go home early today. It's not the truth. Deep down, every one of you knows that there's nobody specific to blame here... and there's no way in hell I'm walking out of this room to pester my fiancee a few hours early if it costs four young jerkoffs twenty years of their lives. I know as much as the next idiot that the four guys being accused today are somewhat responsible for Chris' death. So is whoever drove him to the party, and whoever told him ABOUT the party, and whoever held the party. I'm sure it's not hard to see what I'm getting at, guys. Technically, you can bring these young men up on manslaughter charges, but that's only going to ruin the lives of them and their families. And for a technicality? I'm not especially fond of completely trashing families just to satisfy the minds of a couple of jurors who don't want to waste watching their maid cook supper just to fight this out for the sakes of others. You might be able to see their moms in the grocery store and not flinch knowing you sent the woman's baby to prison, but I've sadly grown a heart.'' Some of them look like they might agree, but just want to get the hell out of here. Others are too stuffy for me to correctly read. Either way, one thing's clear to me... and I let it be known to them. I was fighting this, no matter how long it kept me locked in a little room with the same people. Maybe it's because I've grown up, or maybe I just want to show Darcy that there are things to dedicate your time to that are more valuable than wrestling superstardom. ''I'm not changing my stance unless you convince me. That means we aren't leaving this room until someone moves me. Trust me, it's not an easy thing to do.'' Yeah, I'm pissed that he died and even angrier that these guys were dumb enough to so quickly resort to violence... but I'm a pro wrestler, so what the hell can I say? I honestly believe that this death is actually accidental. He passed out in the woods. He wasn't buried, placed somewhere... he stumbled somewhere, and there's no way to tell if it was because he was drunk or because he got punched in the face earlier that night. I believe that's the truth -- that there's actually no one to blame. There're a lot of 'This lead to him being in that place at that time', but no proverbial triggerman. He fell asleep and he died because he was cold and wet and hammered. At this point, I just need to convince eleven of my neighbors to think the same way. ... And thus, here comes the story of how I landlocked eleven jurors for an extra seven hours of argument just so I can eventually make them say that you shouldn't wander drunk to a party without your coat on. Jab it. Here come the pins... PART Four: Getting home that night, I was exhausted. Luckily, I drove the whole way knowing that there was a flask of tequila in my trunk that would not only help get my mind off of the death of my high school pal, but drive a point home to Darcy that would hopefully keep him from completely building his own coffin. But now that the jury duty was almost over, and the weight was lifted off of my shoulders thanks to my ability to argue anyone into the ground, I could lend all of my mental faculties towards convincing Darcy to take it easy. That, and get soooo wasted! I'm confident that I didn't just convince the jurors to agree with me, but I made them believe the truth in their hearts. It sounds so warm and fuzzy, and really makes for a nice bed to lay any of my possible guilt on. I stuck by my beliefs for once... too bad it wasn't something I could tout to my co-workers or the internet forums who think I'm the Devil. After I made my initial argument, almost everyone instantly switched sides. They made it seem easy... but that's not what took so long. By the time I was done babbling, there were only three people left who didn't see things my way. Surprisingly, the black lady who initially argued wasn't one of them. The first guy to shoot me down was a local farm market owner. He sells me apples when I'm home. Hard to convince, but I knew he'd eventually come around. Being a stuffy old man, and one who's worked on a farm his entire life, he sees things as very black and white -- hard workers prevail in the end, and with McDonald's keeping employment rates in low-end jobs high, the punk kids of this generation are just misguided and completely corrupted by lazy parents and the lack of a good, hard job pushing grandpa's apple cart twice a day. Too bad for him that I'm a walking stereotype, too, and I'm a lot less likely to put up with that wise old man act. He was absolutely convinced that the guys beat Chris up and put his body where it was found. That's where he differed from everyone else -- he thought they'd gone above and beyond what the reports said. Clearly, they didn't... but this old putz wants to imagine a group of n'er-do-wells up to old school shenanigans. It was slow, but I convinced him to throw out his moronic notion and slowly examine, then accept the fact that I'm experienced when it comes to dealing with young men. I've been cheated on a trillion times, and I know full well that young men are notoriously bad at covering up their mistakes. They aren't, however, ALL that stupid... and four boys putting their heads together could have easily formulated a plan more advanced than 'Toss him over there where everyone can see!', especially since their hides would have been on the line. I'll get back to that in a minute, though... the second person who tried telling me I was wrong was my old high school math teacher. He's the definition of 'Asshole', and hadn't said a word to me since I'd entered the court earlier in the morning. Even my mother loathes the man, and she's essentially the least-hating human being on the planet. Of course, I never passed his class... and I'm mathematically handicapped. That, and I spray-bombed his classroom windows during summerschool with some not-so-nice phrases that described his unique style of teaching. Essentially, he thinks I'm dumb as a board and will disagree just to spite him. On the last part, he's entirely right... most of the time. This is a little above me hassling a teacher because I'm a rebel, have a nose ring or whatever he thinks - we're here because Chris is dead, and I remember where he always sat in math class. He was at the front of the class, and so were his grades... that's why our old teacher wants so badly to blame someone, but I quickly let him and everybody else know that I had it figured out. Almost everyone was too emotionally tied to this, and it was leading to a lot of selfish points of view that would end up condemning the wrong people if they were carried out. So, that convinced him to rethink his position. The third... an unimportant ex-boyfriend of mine. Funny how all of the women agreed with me after only a few seconds of talking, but these three men persist. It's not like a man to actually carry out an argument, but it IS like me to win one anyway. They could all be convinced, and here's how: 'One' knows how cold it was that night, because he would've been out locking up his cows and checking for pesky little apple thiefs. Two knows what temperature the body is, and when told the temperature that night he can easily deduce how quickly hypothermia would have set in. Three... well, he'll basically listen to anything I say, so long as he thinks another guy agrees. A lot of truth and smart remarks - That's how you fix a hung jury. I won them over by getting ahead of them and predetermining all of their little emotional conditions. I knew their histories to a degree and could figure out which part of my argument would move them the most. I won the argument, and then convinced them to agree that I won... if that makes any sense. So the idea, in essence, is to win before your opponents can hurt themselves or others. And now I've got an argument ahead of me that's more difficult than convincing eleven locals that they're wrong; I get to try convincing Darcy Crisis to lay low on the whole 'wrestling' thing when he's in the ring this week. But I think I just discovered a method that doesn't require tequila... If I get into the Triangles match and push myself, I can beat him before he has a chance to get hurt by someone who really wants to win. Snapping Teresa Tomas' drunk ass in half won't take much time or energy, so I should be spry and ready to rumble when the main event comes around... that is, if I can get into it. I guess I'll pour that tequila out and think a little, because this is one issue that might actually be made better if I wrestle my way out of it sober. Amusingly enough, if people knew that I spent time doing things like jury duty, my 'fans' might realize that Darcy and I are fundamentally different right now. I'm not a wrestler who happens to do other things, I'm a person who happens to wrestle. I also want what's best for (most) people, I just tend to use guerilla tactics to make their lives a little better. Wow. You really can't write this stuff. Blood for face Sweat for dirt Three X's for the stone To break this curse A ritual's due I believe I'm not alone. Shell of shotgun Pint of gin Numb us up to shield the pins Renew our faith Which way we can... To fall in love with life again. (/Fixxer. Metallica, ReLoad, 1997. [detail withheld])
