Chronicle One: The Offer

11 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/11/05 [Acclaim video game testing center: Anaheim, California, USA. Patrick Mapleleaf does not care much for visiting the United States, especially the land of make-believe known as California. However, sometimes when these things happen, you have to seize the opportunities that are presented to you and take them. Let us go back in time for just a moment here. Let us rewind two weeks. We are in Toronto, Canada, where Patrick Mapleleaf is inside of his condo located in the outskirts of the city. Mapleleaf is lounging in his imported Italian recliner, sipping a Canadian beer and watching his CFL football team, the Toronto Agronauts. The condo is painted in a pale gray color, and has expensive art on the wall…painted of course by Canadian artists. The hardwood floor reflects the hanging lights above. The crunching and cracking of skulls is heard through the loud surround sound in Patrick’s living room. He takes another sip of beer and nearly spits out the contents.] Mapleleaf: Oh, come on! You call that a block?! Come on boys, I could do better than that! [The door opens up, and Mapleleaf’s roommate, Cedric, walks in. Cedric is wearing a white disco suit and white boots with water and little fish in the bottom of them. His high afro covers his head, making him look like a Q-Tip. This African-Canadian looks to be in high spirits as always, wearing his black aviator sunglasses and whistling “That’s the way I like it” by K.C. and the Sunshine band. He takes the remote control away from Mapleleaf and changes the channel to VH1 70’s gold. “Stayin’ Alive” by the BeeGees is playing, and Cedric begins to swivel his hips.] Mapleleaf: Hey, I was watching that you, crowfooted snappletus! Cedric: Hey, hey, now baby…it’s cool, it’s cool. Dance to da music, that’s what I always say. Mapleleaf: The dang Agronauts were just…oh fine, keep it on. Cedric: Brotha, I’m gonna get you hooked on disco music if it’s the last thing I do. Mapleleaf: Cedric, what country were you born in? Cedric: Uganda. Mapleleaf: Okay…but where did you grow up? Cedric: I grew up some in Britain, a little in a small grotto in France. Mapleleaf: Where did you spend…the last…how long have you been here? Cedric: Three weeks, brotha man. Mapleleaf: So that makes you a Canadian. Grab a cold Molson and start acting like one. [Cedric grabs an opened Molson bottle from the living room table. He takes a sip, makes a disgusted face, and spits out the contents.] Cedric: Yo, baby, daddy o’! This stuff is warm and it tastes salty! Mapleleaf: Sorry, that’s my pee bottle. Cedric: You take a pee pee in a beer bottle?! Mapleleaf: They have not yet made a toilet suitable for my grade-A, Canadian made, supercharged wang, so I do my business here instead. Cedric: What about number 2? Mapleleaf: I do that in your clothes hamper. Cedric: Sweet baby Jesus and a half! [There is a knock at the door. Cedric and Mapleleaf just look at each other.] Cedric: Ain’t ya gonna get it? Mapleleaf: We’ll let Alice get it. Alice! [Alice from the Brady Bunch comes stumbling into the living room. She looks to be almost one hundred years old, and is still wearing the blue and white outfit from the TV show. She has a large, 200 pound safe changed to her, which makes her movements slow.] Alice: Please, Mr. Mapleleaf, you have to let me retire! At least let me change out of these clothes…they smell! Mapleleaf: Never! I won you at that police auction fair and square! The Bradys…a drug selling operation. I would have never thought! [Alice drags herself to the door. She opens is, and a man with shiny black hair, wearing a black business suit is waiting. She lets him in. Mapleleaf stands up. Let me just add in at this time that he is hardly suited to entertain company, wearing nothing but his white and red striped boxers with Canadian flags all around the elastic band.] Man: Uhh…hello, Mr. Mapleleaf. Mapleleaf: Please, call me Mr. Mapleleaf. Man: I did. Mapleleaf: Don’t you sass me, boy! I can have you bumped right down to…a lower version…of whatever you are. Cedric: Yo, Mappes. You ain’t dressed to entertain company, especially a man like this. Mapleleaf: Right you are, Cedders. This man deserves me to be dressed in a way that suits him. [Mapleleaf takes off the boxers and is now buck naked.] Mapleleaf: Okay, let’s talk. Sit down. [The man sits down on the couch next to Cedric.] Mapleleaf: Alice, go make us some dodo bird sandwiches with some t-rex egg salad. Alice: Sir, both of those animals are extinct— Mapleleaf: DO IT! Man: My name is Alan Haxley. I am the President of development at Acclaim gaming. We would like to make a Patrick Mapleleaf videogame. Mapleleaf: Me? A videogame? Haxley: Yes sir. Ever since Super Mario mysteriously disappeared, we have been trying to find a new poster boy. Mapleleaf: Yes…disappeared. [There are knocks coming from the closet by the entrance to the apartment.] Voice: MAMA MIA! Let’a me out! I need to rescue the princess! Haxley: What was that?! Mapleleaf: Uhh…nothing! Haxley: What do you say? [We will stop this portion of the story here, for it is irrelevant. Don’t worry, you’re not missing much…a discussion on world politics, the space mission, and rubber novelty poop. Mapleleaf is currently, as of this very second, inside of a lab in Acclaim studios. Alan Haxley is there with him, with a stack of discs and a Playstation 2 in front of him. Cedric is also there, sitting on the opposite side by another Playstation, bee-bopping and scatting to Dance Dance Revolution, the disco edition. Alice the maid is next to all of them, scrubbing the floor and now chained to a 500 pound safe (for giving Patrick lip and not making those sandwiches.)] Haxley: Here is the first demo disc, Mr. Mapleleaf. You are a superhero, trying to save the world from space aliens. [He puts the disc in. Patrick Mapleleaf is shown in the game, flying across a screen above tall buildings. He is wearing a pink and black suit, and his long blonde hair is flying in the wind. A space alien shoots at him. The demo loses a life and the game sets back to where it started.] Mapleleaf: Freeze! What is this “losing lives” crap? Everyone knows Patrick Mapleleaf can’t be killed! Haxley: But the whole point— Mapleleaf: Realistic! Yes, I agree, which is why I am telling you that this won’t fly. Patrick Mapleleaf will not go down because of lasers, or machine guns, or— Cedric: Wait. Didn’t you screech and hide on the couch when mice were in the condo? Mapleleaf: I heard them plotting! They wanted to kill me and take my money! And what is with the pink suit? Patrick Mapleleaf is not a fruit booty! I wear red and white, beeotch to the neeotch! Haxley: Fine. Something a little more calm. Remember Tetris? Mapleleaf: Yeah! Did you know I can get a high score of 99999999 on it? That’s because I’m sweeter than you. True story. Haxley: Well, check this out. [Haxley turns the PS2 back on. A screen appears that says Patris. The game begins. It is exactly like the early 90’s Nintendo classic Tetris. Shapes fall, and the object of the game is to form lines out of those falling shapes. However, instead of shapes falling, little Patrick Mapleleaf heads fall from the screen. The demo rearranges the little heads so that they form lines so that the number of lines can be built up. In the background, the music playing is “Oh, Canada,” the Canadian national anthem. Patrick reaches over and shuts off the machine.] Cedric: What now? Mapleleaf: What the holy crimson hell were those things falling from the screen something to be? Haxley: They were supposed to be models of your head, Mr. Mapleleaf. Mapleleaf: Oh really? Haxley: Yes. Mapleleaf: Truly? Haxley: Mmhmm. Mapleleaf: This a fact? Haxley: Indeed. Mapleleaf: She sells sea shells down by the sea shore? Haxley: Whaa? Mapleleaf: That did not even closely resemble my head. They looked like floating lumps of crap! I saw something closely resembling those shapes the last time I ate at any of the low-class, non-Canadian dining establishments! Haxley: Fine. I have one more. [Haxley puts in a disc.] Haxley: Now, this one is different altogether. This is an educational game. Mapleleaf: NEXT! Haxley: No, listen. It is an important thing to teach. Cedric: How to get down? Haxley: No. Alice: Laws against illegal servitude? Mapleleaf: Pipe down, or else I’ll take away your sheets! Alice: You did already. Mapleleaf: Fine, your mattress. Alice: Did that too. Mapleleaf: Box spring. Alice: Gone. Mapleleaf: I’ll make you sleep on a bed of spikes, and the spikes will be on fire, and it will be one of those HOT fires! Alice: … Mapleleaf: Yeah, that’s what I thought…shut up! Now then, what is important enough for ME to educate? Haxley: Canadian language and culture. Mapleleaf: Sold. Haxley: But I haven’t even shown you the disc yet. Mapleleaf: Anyone and everyone knows that anything with the Canadian logo on it is high quality. While America can whore itself away, sticking that eyesore of a red, white, and blue jumble of feces that looks like something a fourth grader threw together for his mommy on any product, Canada is different. I will not allow this product to come out unless I know it is of top quality. Also, I want to be able to hand-select those that buy it. I don’t think anyone should or deserves to look at my disc if they are of below average intelligence. This constitutes 96% of society, so it looks like you won’t be making much on it after I take my cut. [And so it was…Mapleleaf is a man of many hats. He is a wrestler, a Canadian advocate, an educator, a supreme human being, and a video game dude. We will look at the actual production of the game in our next chronicle. However, this chronicle has not been concluded as of yet. We now join Patrick Mapleleaf and Cedric on Patrick’s private jet, which is headed straight back for Toronto. The negotiations of the deal have not been set in stone yet, but they will likely get Mapleleaf out of that God awful condo (God awful by his standards…you low-lifes would be lucky to get a place that is the size of the bathroom of that condo.) Mapleleaf and Cedric are discussing various topics of intellectual and aesthetic interest as the plane makes its way just across the Canadian border.] Mapleleaf: --and that’s how I got crabs the third time. Cedric: Lovely. Hey, where’s Alice? Mapleleaf: Oh, she’s out cleaning the left wing. We had a smudge and I didn’t want to have to look at that during the flight. Cedric: This baby ready to go back to Ireland? Mapleleaf: My GAWD, don’t remind me. No, we won’t be going back any time soon. It’s off to California for Fresh this week for my DEBUT! [Debut song sequence loading…loading…loading…failed loading sequence.] Cedric: Were you lying when you said there were monsters in the streets of Ireland? Mapleleaf: Actually, it turns out that’s just their women. ZIIING! Cedric: So why didn’t you just stay in Anaheim and drive to Fresno? Mapleleaf: Cedric, my friend…some things in life you just don’t understand. Why are we here? Why is the earth round? Why does the sun shine, the birds chirp? Life is full of complexities, and we can only hope to someday find the answers are we hurl ourselves aimlessly through this crazy cosmos that we call life. Cedric: You got caught driving drunk again and they took your license, didn’t they? Mapleleaf: Yes…but I won’t let that deter me. I have a special surprise planned for the fans in California. Cedric: Do you? A victory? Mapleleaf: That’s not gonna be a surprise to anyone, nut-munch. A fetal fourway? Please. Cedric: Did you just say fetal? Mapleleaf: No, I said fatal. Cedric: But— Mapleleafs: If if’s and buts were candy and nuts, it would be Christmas everyday. No, I have something that the fans in California will truly appreciate. Not only that…you’re gonna help me do it. Cedric: Oh? How? [Mapleleaf whispers into Cedric’s ear. He starts to grin and shakes his head.] Cedric: You truly are evil. [Out]
