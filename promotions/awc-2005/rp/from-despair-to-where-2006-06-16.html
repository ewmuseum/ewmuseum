From despair to where?

16 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 16/6/06 Her eyes jutted open, and instantly she sprang to attention and took in her surroundings. They hadn’t noticeably changed; although the thick, grimy black of the room made telling difficult. A small crack of light penetrated from the blackout blinds, and lit the black like a beam from the heavens, illuminated in the steam and stench. The floor was cold and wet against her bare skin, and wholly unwelcome was its touch. The stench was unbearable. Her self-imposed prison, a chamber of healing, the only problem was that this journey was meant for two. It had been eight days since she locked herself in the room, idly tossing the key back underneath the door and waiting for the others to join her. Eight days since she piled her clothes neatly in the corner and collapsed naked upon the concrete floor and waited for the healing. Eight days had passed; and still no word from the others. They’d told her cold turkey was the only cure, and that was all well and good until the sickness took a firm hold, assaulting her dreams and tearing at her from inside. A day and a half off the junk and she began to feel ill; a feeling she knew and was expecting, but was easily sorted with another quick fix, just one for the road. The problem was that there was no let down option. At first she ate as a way of taking her mind from the growing dread, and latterly because that was all she was able to do, but it wasn’t long before she had little else to live off. Three days gone and she had eaten all her supplies. Then it was that she truly felt the meaning of hell. Her body convulsed, and each touch to her skin felt like a prod to an open wound. Her bones felt like glass, her stomach like a washing machine and her head swam with everything she had come to know as torture. For two whole days she screamed for mercy, roaring until her voice failed completely into a horse croak and she sat retching in the corner. “End this, please, end this. End me.” Nobody came. Her fingers bled from scratching a rut in the thick wooden door, and now blood mingled with the sweat and filth that coated her skin, defiling all that she had once stood for. All she had once stood for, the morals that had held her so strong, all that she once had was lost. Now the people she would have spat at with scorn were the only people that could save her life. “One last shot, please, anything.” But worse were the hallucinations, sporadic at first until they seamlessly merged with reality, leaving her transfixed and horrified, screaming at her four walls and clawing at her eyes. She’d held her mother tightly in her arms, her mother who had been dead long years, the rotting corpse whispering in a foreign language she no longer understood. She’d been buried alive, and for one brief moment she’d sensed salvation. They’d come at last, smiling and joking with clean, comforting faces. But they had prodded her, jabbed her and screamed at her, and with every word it made sense; it was her fault. It was all her fault. Her mum was her fault; the failure she had become was her fault. Every jibe they threw made more and more hideous sense. It was all her fault. She would awake, clutching herself in a pile of filth in a dark room or picking at a scab on her forearm. The scabs and scars were not healing; they were becoming infected in the squalid conditions, adding to the fever and deliriousness. As it all unfolded, one thought gained momentum and strength above all others. She would welcome death with open arms, almost praying for it. The name of a song she had liked crossed her mind now and again, ‘Thoughts of a dieing atheist’. For a moment she knew there was no salvation, no afterlife and nobody looking over her. The long dark oblivion waited, and she willed it on. There was nothing for her outside; she had spent the remnants of her money on a final fix and the rent of unit in the middle of nowhere, and she now felt so ill that freedom did not even become an option. Her limbs refused to move and she could feel herself wasting. What was once a model physique, strong and toned beyond belief, was now just another waste, a statistic to a group she did not want to be a part of. Haunted by the darkness and dirt in day and in dream, she did not pay attention when the door opened, and she was tossed across the strong shoulder of a man she had once disregarded and would now come to rely on with every sinew of her body. __ “Why the bloody hell do you do this to yourself?” he muttered, taking a handful of ice cold tap water and splashing it haphazardly over his tired, worn face. The face in the mirror was not his; it can’t have been. He looked old, red bloodshot eyes surrounded by vacuous hollows, wrinkled crows feet nestling in the corners. Not in a long time had he felt so desolate. Slowly dragging himself from the mirror, he forced himself to piss. It seemed like a great effort now; he’d been pacing back and forth since he brought her in and he couldn’t help but feel that he’d let them both down. Some of the things she’d said, draped across his shoulder like a rag doll, they were truly haunting. Mutterings in a tone so horse it resembled some sort of apocalyptic death rattle. He’d seen a lot of things, maybe more than his years belied, but never anything that had hit him so hard. She’d cried when he first placed hands on her, naked and covered in her own filth. He didn’t speak, wrapping her in a vomit stained coat and carrying her out of the disgusting unit. He didn’t need to ask where she needed to go, it was more than obvious. Jabbing a huge finger at the numbers on his mobile phone, he called a taxi and threw her inside. The driver was not too impressed. “She leaves a mark and you’re paying for that, mate, that’s for sure.” He’d nodded and left the driver rambling to himself. It wasn’t worth the hassle. Five minutes later, he climbed out of the cab, stuffed a crumpled message and a twenty-pound note into the hand of the young woman an, instructing the driver what to do and where he could shove his attitude. He couldn’t go in with her. Instantly he would have come in for question, and the giant frame of Angelus Sorrow was not a hard one to recognise. Six foot eight of pure muscle tends to draw attention, and too much for his liking. Carrying in the figure of a woman who was all but dead would compromise both Sorrow and a lot of people that an upstanding citizen would not be talking to. Taking one more furtive glance in the mirror, Sorrow steadied himself and with a sudden gust of courage he stormed out and into the bleached, eye searing white of the hospital corridors. The smell hit him instantly, the artificial cleanliness and the sole reason he wouldn’t usually step foot in a NHS hospital if his life depended on it. His work had its privileges, both the wrestling world and his favourite brand of undercurrent, and private treatment was much more to his taste, but now he didn’t have a choice. He had to see her and she had to be all right. Guilt was not an emotion that Mr. Sorrow was used to. It wasn’t his fault that she was in this state, but he knew that he should have acted sooner. There was a period where he couldn’t find her, where he had used everything he had at his disposal but still came up short. There the guilt began to grow. If she died, it would be his fault and he couldn’t allow himself to live with that. Slowly he approached the desk, his head spinning and mind working overtime. It took a while before he realised the receptionist had been trying to attract his attention, her fat little arms having in his face and her gaping mouth spitting out words. He instantly disliked her, she almost reminded him of an owl. Piercing eyes and sharp claws. Not what he needed right now. “I’m here to visit my cousin,” he murmured nervously, trying to focus his eyes and senses. A ‘thank fuck for that’ look of relief splashed across the features of the receptionist, and her fat little face contorted into something construed as slightly less of a glare. “Can I take a name?” she replied, matter-of-factly and rolling her eyes down to the computer screen. “Lacuna Devries” __ The fresh, cool air breezed across her naked form and the sun burned into her back. She was saved and now she felt the flapping on her back, bright white feathers forcing her into the sky. Up and up she flew, leaving the green and yellow fields behind like a patchwork blanket as she shot towards the burning yellow sun. For some reason the sensation of flying was not new. It just seemed natural, almost as if she belonged. The flapping was rhythmic, calming and relaxing to her weary mind. But then came the burning, a searing, violent pain that tore at her every cell. Her skin peeled, the pure white of her outstretched wings became a singed black. The smell was hideous. Her arms, face and torso were alight and she was falling, unable to hold altitude through the pain. “Her temperature is up” Now the ground was closing, but the burning was getting worse. She screamed but no noise left her lips and now she could see it clearly, the gaping tear in the ground, the black chasm that felt out for her, tearing her back down. She twisted and turned, thrashing her arms and wings, screaming as the darkness enveloped her, wrapping itself around her like a second skin. There was a light at the bottom, burning and searing again and then a crash, and then she woke up. For a while she lay, staring blankly up at the bright lights descending from the ceiling before beginning to take in the new surroundings. She was still burning up, but she was clean and wrapped in a white bed sheet. Around her were a troop of men and women in green, like something out of a horror movie she seemed strapped down, attached to machines she didn’t recognise and surround by murmuring voices she couldn’t quite make out. She rolled her hazel eyes away from the strange people and suddenly she saw someone she recognised, and collapsed happily into a dreamless sleep. __ Waiting with baited breath on the other side of the glass was Sorrow, his eyes watering with the effort of retaining his constant vigil. Then he saw her smile, and the final piece of the puzzle clicked. A weight lifted from his shoulders, and instantly he felt alive. He felt that she felt alive, and that was what made the difference. Now he just had to fix her, and he would do it the only way he knew how. There was no need for repenting, or for setting her up with an Ikea-furnished two bed in the suburbs, she just had to find her feet and he would help her if it killed them both. He’d been through too much now to turn back, having felt emotions he only knew through others. The churning was fading now, but it still had its hook-like claws gripping just below the surface. The pain had hit him hardest when the receptionist heard the name. The look of shock on her disgusting little face hit him deep. “The woman who used to wrestle? The rich one? I wonder what she’s in for” she had wondered aloud. “Can you just tell me where she is?” Sorrow snapped back in retort. Then the owl woman’s eyes lit up as she saw the case notes on the computer and she smelt an anonymous newspaper tip coming along. Famous British export wrestler falls foul to heroin addiction. Sorrow despised this. He hated everything about her now, and he hated even more what Lacuna had done to her reputation, but she would get it back and he would make her. He would fix her; fix her by forcing her headfirst back into a wrestling ring. Luckily a gym owner was paying him protection money. The underworld always had its privileges.
