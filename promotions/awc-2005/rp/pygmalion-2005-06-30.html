Pygmalion

30 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-30-05 Alex Strider pointed the giant hand-cannon directly between the eyes of the man with short black hair. “Did you really think you’d get away with it? Did you really think you could send your goons after me and I wouldn’t track you down, Darius?” With each word, Alex edged the six-inch barrel closer to the man’s forehead. The man furrowed his brow nervously, and seemed to shrink under the pressure of standing in front of an angry professional wrestler who was holding a .44 Magnum handgun. ”Well, whatever you were thinking, you can just forget about it. I’m the one in control now, asshole! If I wanted too, I could split your head open; it’d be like going through a cabbage, you understand? At this range, it’d make for a great pictorial on rotten.com, that’s for sure. Have you ever seen any of the death pics they’ve got there? It’s fascinating to think about how one moment, somebody is a living, breathing, thinking person, and then one second later… BAM! Uncooked Hamburger and meat sauce! Simply fascinating.” Alex continued the torture of his prey, allowing himself to tap into his feelings of frustration and anger to intimidate the younger man. “I’d love to see what color your brain really is. I’m willing to bet it’s charcoal black, you evil son-of-a-bitch. I’ll give you seven-to-one odds, whaddayasay? You game?” Alex smiled, a crazed gleam in his eye shone out. The dark haired man took his gaze off of the gigantic Colt Anaconda .44 magnum that hung in the air, suspending from Alex’s hand merely inches from his head. “Alex…” Pamela Saunders said softly, putting a hand on his shoulder, trying to get his attention. Alex did not break his attention from the man. “You think she can help you? You think anyone can help you? You’re wrong. Dead wrong. Nothing can save you now!” Alex cocked the hammer with his thumb, the double action revolver was just begging to have its trigger squeezed. “Say goodnight, Gracie.” “ALEX! THAT’S ENOUGH!” Pamela shouted at the top of her lungs, having had her fill of the twisted game. She pulled at Strider’s extended arm, pulling the gun away from the man. Alex turned to Pamela, frowning as he said, “What now?” “Can you cut it out? Sometimes I really wonder about you. You’re kind of sick, you know?” Embarrassed, Pamela put her hand over her eyes, shielding her face from the few other patron’s in “Chuck Sr.’s Guns and Ammo” who turned their attention to the register when she screamed. The dark haired man breathed in a deep sigh, and bent down behind the counter. After the attack Alex “suffered” in Pittsburgh, he decided that the best course of action to prevent further bodily harm for him would be to arm himself. Alex had never shot a gun before, but he decided he’d thought about it long enough, and it was time to buy one. ‘Thinking about it long enough’ in this case meant he decided that was what he was going to do before he’d even woken up that morning. “I’m just having some fun, Pam! Look, he didn’t mind, did you mind, Chuck?” Chuck Jr. reappeared a second later, holding the box for the Colt Alex was still holding. ”No, sir. It was an honor for me to pretend to be your intended victim, Mr. Strider,” Chuck Jr. deadpanned. Alex concerted, ”See, Pam? Junior liked it!” Pam was blushing. She’d never been around guns, and they certainly made her nervous. Never mind Alex’s flippant nature, which only served to make her feel more on edge, like anything could happen. Pam was used to only having bad things happen. “Besides, you know this isn’t loaded, fer-Christ-sakes.” Alex goofily waved his hand at her, bringing the four pound gun to aim at his temple, and squeezing the trigger three times. Making a crazy pained face, Alex stuck his tongue out at Pam, gurgling. Pamela looked away, trying to keep down the laughter. Turning back to Chuck, Alex places the Colt Anaconda .44 back in the box. ”Have you seen enough then, Alex? Maybe we can go talk now, right?” Pamela said, as she swung herself toward the entrance on her crutches. “Yeah, just one second. O.k. Chuck, since you’re such a good sport, and your Dad’s the owner of this place, I’ll take it.” “What? Are you crazy? You said you were going to think about it! You don’t need a gun, Alex!” Pamela said, turning around on her crutches. “I said I was going to look. I looked. This is what I want! Have you seen anything so powerful? It’s huge! I don’t even need to load this, this will make somebody shit themselves by sight alone! That’s how powerful this is.” Alex smiled knowingly at Pamela, before turning to Chuck, continuing as if he hadn’t been talking to her at all, “I’ll need two boxes of whatever bullets I’ll be putting in this thing. And a holster, and some kind of a case for my truck. And do you have an optional scope for it? I’ve seen those before.” Chuck Jr. just nodded silently and walked away, grabbing out the most expensive accessories to add to the sale. Alex smiled back at Pamela, giving her an innocent look even though he was as giddy as a boy on Christmas who found out he was really going to get that Daisy Red Ryder BB Gun he’d wanted. “Alex, I wish you’d think about this just a little bit harder. You may have been attacked, but you handled yourself pretty well, right? Why would you want to add the possibility of killing someone?” Pamela reasoned. “Pam, I don’t need to hear about ‘killing somebody’ from someone who could have died when she tripped herself down stairs, alright? You need to worry less about what I’ll be shooting at, and more about your foot placement, ‘kay?” Alex smiled in his usual over-the-top manic depressive manner. “I want that cowboy hat, too,” Alex said, pointing at a jet-black Johnny Cash special. Alex silently motioned to Chuck Jr. to grab him a second gun to complete his brace of six shooters. Chuck Jr. nodded and grabbed a second box. Looking back toward Pamela, Alex placed a hand on Chuck’s shoulder, and pointed to a smaller snub nosed pistol. Chuck unlocked the case without a word, and Alex leaned back on the counter, staring at Pamela. She looked helpless as she teetered along with her crutches, forcing a frown to plant itself on his face. Alex wasn’t used to this feeling; empathizing for someone else. Concern for another’s wellbeing; Pity; these were both new sensations. Not understanding the way he felt, the only emotion he was used to crept inside, made him warm, and he welcomed it. Alex replaced his pity for Pamela with anger and resentment. She was weak, and injured herself, he thought. Seeing her injured forced him to admit that she was in danger. If he was being targeted by his brother, then his brother knew about her. His brother knew who she was, where she lived; where she worked, who she lived with. What kind of car she drove. It’s her fault for sticking around, and he’d be left paying for the funeral, just like he was with Sophia Riley. Alex gripped the counter until all the blood drained away from his fingers. His frown turned into a deep scowl, and his mind was filled with everything that made his life difficult. All the people who wanted to take from him. His brother. Pamela. Pearl Harber. The Riley’s outrageous lawsuit. All Alex had ever done was live his life the way he wanted. Why was that such a crime? Why was he always left cleaning up messes he wasn’t responsible for? No matter how he tried to rationalize it, who he was really angry with was himself. It was his fault Pam might be in danger now, thanks to the choices he’d made himself. She was just an innocent. A weakling. A woman. Just a girl, really. Alex drank in what he saw while he watched her from afar. She was stick thin, almost sickly. She didn’t dress well at all. For today’s outing, she was wearing huge roach stomping boots, even though it was summer and they made her legs look like licorice lollipops. Her choice of dress was a motley gray skirt and frilly sundress top. Her straight brown hair pulled back out of her eyes made her look like one of those hippy girls who sit around protesting animal cruelty in their dorm lobby, reaching no one of consequence with their futile bellyaching. She couldn’t weigh much, at all, and would obviously be helpless if Darius wanted her hurt. No, there wasn’t anything immediately remarkable about Pamela, but when Alex looked at her, he could almost see something in her. Her profile was strong, almost cute. Her chin was soft, but her face was symmetrical. Her skin was clear and white like an alabaster statue. Maybe if she tried contacts, instead of those untrendy Lisa Loeb cat glasses? “Yeah, colored contacts…” mused Alex. Perhaps another day he’d talk to her about the way she dressed, when he had the time. Right now, there were more pressing matters. It was his responsibility to make sure she’d be safe from harm that was meant for him. “Will that be cash or credit, Mr. Strider?” Chuck Jr. inquired dispassionately from behind the counter. Alex tossed a plastic card on the counter and wiped away the sneer on his face. ******** Walking back to his truck with his newest toys, the silence was palpable. Pamela could tell something was wrong, but didn’t want to mention anything. Avoidance was her tool…if Alex had something he wanted to talk about, he’d bring it up, she thought. And on top of it all, she felt she had a some right in being upset as well. He’d bought himself not one, but two guns. “Unnecessary,” she thought to herself. “He’s going to get himself killed.” “Pam,” Alex finally broke the silence as he approached his Cadillac Escalade EXT, the look of concerned annoyance still plain for any to see, “we need to talk about something.” “O.k.” she answered back, no emotion. ‘It’s always impossible to read her’, he thought to himself. “I bought these guns for two reasons. The first reason was that they make me cooler than I was before. I know, hard to believe, right? I mean, I am the most attractive pro-wrestler this side of the Mississippi River, and the Transatlantic Champ to boot, so why would a gun make me cooler? I don’t rightfully know,” Alex said, beginning to realize he was rambling, and for the first time in a long while got self conscious about it. Clearing his throat, he continued, “That’s just the quiet mystique of a Double Action handgun.” “That makes sense,” she replied quietly, without making eye contact. “My point is, that I have a better idea of what is going on right now, Pam,” he said, hating himself every second for making himself vulnerable to her. “I think I’m beginning to understand more now, too, Alex.” Alex nodded. “If you talked to her best friend at Pitt, you already know that Sophia dated my brother, Darius. I didn’t know that when I picked her up, I just found that out recently.” The wind whipped through Alex and Pam’s hair, making it obvious as Pam avoided Alex’s gaze. She looked around the street, listening patiently as Alex spilled his guts. ”Right now, my brother’s got some kind of boner out for wanting to ruin my life, Pamela. Darius is jealous because he thinks I left him holding the colostomy bag when it came to taking care of our father. I guess I did, I don’t know. The fact is, when I went off to become a professional wrestler, I did some things that I’m not so proud of. One of them was turn my back on my family. It’s catching up to me now.” Pamela nodded, finally meeting his eyes. Her heart tugged, as much as she didn’t want it too. She should be treating this like a big break, finally getting inside the labyrinth that was Alex’s mind. But she was more concerned with him. Now she was starting to understand why he was such a bastard. He was wounded emotionally, just like she was. She wanted to take care of him. She felt pity; empathy. “But why would Darius want to kill you? I mean, it’s one thing to be pissed off because he had to take care of your father when you left, but…” “There’s more too it, we should talk about it somewhere else. Let’s get off the street. We’ll talk about the rest of this in New York.” Alex flashed his security system and unlocked the doors, He held the door open for Pamela, held her crutches for her like a true gentleman. Letting himself in, Alex put the cargo in the back. Climbing back in the car, Alex didn’t notice the ratty Dodge Neon parked down the block start up it’s engine. As Alex drove away, he didn’t see in his rearview mirror as the Neon pulled into traffic and started mirroring his moves one by one. ******** ”Alex, where are we going?” Pamela asked, slightly annoyed. ”It’s a secret,” he said coyly, a smile on his face so wide it couldn’t possibly be for real. The two were walking down a random street in New York City, New York. Several days before Alex’s second title defense at Madison Square Garden, but the two hadn’t talked about his inferno match at all. They were busy going over the varied details of his life. Alex revealed that his father was a very, very wealthy man. His family had been in Pittsburgh smelting steel for a long time where they built their fortune. Alex disappointed his aging father when he decided to forgo college for a life of professional wrestling, and in doing so cut himself out of the family fortune. But Darius was left, the only son to a dying father with no real mother to speak of, save for a blonde trophy wife that was exactly the same age as Alex. It was clear to both Alex and Pam that Darius was using his father’s fortune to take care of his vengeance, even to the point of hiring weak thugs to attack Alex from out of nowhere. Alex knew that was less about hurting him then, but more about keeping the pressure on him. Darius wanted to ruin Alex’s life. Darius was the key to unlocking Sophia Riley’s death by overdose. ”Saks Fifth Avenue.” Alex said, waving a hand towards the giant New York Icon of a store. “Entre-vous.” Holding the door open for her, Pam smiled sheepishly. Kevin never held doors open for her, even as a friend. “What are we doing here, Alex? You have to buy something for one of your backstage groupies?” Pamela said with a smile. “You could say that. Or you could say I’m doing something nice for a friend. Pamela, meet Paul. Paul’s going to be your personal shopper for the day. Paul, Pamela isn’t to leave the store until she’s found a new outfit for going out on the town tonight. Let’s get started, shall we?” Paul was standing, one hand on hip, his jaw dropped wide open at the sight of Pamela. You could tell from the look on his face he wasn’t thrilled with what he was working with. Paul himself had short red hair, thing string bean arms, and even if you didn’t know what he did for a living, you could still tell he was gay from forty paces. “We’ve got our work cut out for us today, don’t we?” Paul disappointedly said as he looked over Pamela, not able to hide his pain. Pamela started to voice out her protest, but Paul grabbed her by the arm, saying “You’re very lucky! He’s so handsome! I’m going to take great care of you.” “Alex, what is going on?” Pamela called out as she was being dragged away towards the latest fashion by her fey captor. “Just have fun. We’re going out tonight, so trust Paul, ‘kay? You’ve got a hair appointment in an hour…don’t let her be late, P!” ******** Paul had her try on innumerable dresses. She tried to protest at first, but it turned out that Paul was one of those homosexuals that you don’t fuck with. He had a job, and it was to sell her expensive and gorgeous dresses, shoes, and then get her to her hair appointment. Paul could be a bitch, but he told Pam when she looked good, and when she didn’t. Pam actually found herself having fun. She’d never tried this many dresses on before, not even for her high school Prom. It was a whirlwind of extravagance. It was as if Alex was still trying to show off, but at the same time, this was something kind of special. If Alex couldn’t open himself up emotionally, this must be how he shows his appreciation, she concluded. ”Fantastic!” Paul said, clapping his hands together. This gown is sooooo you!” Paul almost squeeled with delight when Pam stepped out of the change room wearing the Kay Unger sleeveless jersey dress. It was black with a crisscrossed fabric design and came up to her knee, showing off her thin legs. It made her feel fabulous. Her small breasts stood at attention, daring anyone to notice her. She blushed as she looked at herself in the mirror. “Here, take these, put these on…OH MY GOD!” Paul handed Pamela two gorgeous crystal hanging earrings. “These look SO GOOD on you! Where did that ugly little bookworm go? She’s dead! Long live Queen Pamela! Beautiful!” Pamela chuckled to herself. Paul carefully hung the earrings in place, then stepped back to take a look at his masterpiece. He clapped his hands across his face. “New shoes, new dress, new earrings! You’re a new woman! Purrrrr!” She got the same treatment at the hair salon. It was like she was going on a vacation, but the hectic pace of schedule made her so dizzy. Her hair was washed, cut, curled. They put make-up on her to hide the bags under her eyes. Her hair cut into a bob, it framed her face like it was a Da Vinci fresco, fresh and young. Gone was the one length Super-Cut pony-tail. She had dimension, depth. She was reborn a new woman, all the wear washed away. The tough, beaten exterior built up from years of inattentiveness to her own looks cleared by the skilled hands of beauticians. She could be beautiful. Pamela looked in the mirror and didn’t recognize herself. She didn’t understand any of it, but she was kind of high from the excitement and the pampering. She’d never had any of it before, and she didn’t want to think about how in one day, she’d be back to her old self. She wanted to be a princess forever. It was dangerous thinking, because in her heart she knew it would never last like this. Alex opend the cab door as it pulled up to the Atelier, NYC’s finest French restaurant. ”You look amazing.” Alex said, holding his hand out so she could step up from the cab. ”You know, I am a little offended that you had to spend over a thousand dollars on me before you’d be seen in public with me.” Pamela said, playing with her hair as she stepped up over the curb. “Nonsense. I just wanted to do something nice for you, since you’ve had to follow me all around the Coast for the last two months. Besides, I owe it to you. If it weren’t for me, you would never have seen a dead body. Don’t think anything of it, other than a gesture of friendship, o.k.?” Alex said sincerely. “Alex Strider have a friend? Take that into account with my actually feeling like a woman, and I think it’s safe to say that hell just might have frozen over.” Pamela deadpanned. Alex chuckled lightly, holding the door open for her. “After you, my lady. We’ve got a lot to discuss tonight.”
