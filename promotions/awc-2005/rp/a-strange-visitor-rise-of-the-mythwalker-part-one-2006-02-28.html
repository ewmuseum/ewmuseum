A Strange Visitor (Rise Of The Mythwalker Part One)

28 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-28-06 Another gray day in Washington. Everything is dull and gray to Jiro Sennosuke. The gray just piles up on itself, the hours, the minutes and the days. The gray is drowning everything, even itself. Even himself. Lying stiff on his cheap secondhand futon, Jiro Sennosuke is waiting for something to happen. Something OTHER than a Fresh! booking, where he’ll have to try and cover up Joey’s weaknesses. Something OTHER than Joey, who’d barge in and start talking about something insignificant, like how much pot it would take to make a rhinocerous high. Something OTHER than how Kensuke Suzahara or Jason Core at the school would talk down to him, like he didn’t know… Something…something that felt real. Like when he beat the shit out of Chainz. Or, helped beat the shit out of Chainz. But let’s be honest, Jiro knew he could do it without Joey’s help. I mean, come on. He hits like a woman. Or something like when Jiro managed to eke out a victory in a triple threat match, where Teresa Tomas wrestled her finest, but Jiro still managed to steal a victory. His first in a professional match. EVER. Two moments, since he came to America to wrestle. Two moments that made him feel something. It’d be nice to know that other people had felt like this before. The gray outside sky gave in under the weight of water, and it started raining. To be fair, it didn’t really affect Jiro, he wasn’t going to leave his apartment anyways. Jiro, actually, doesn’t typically leave his apartment. Period. He goes to the gym, to work out, because he has to. Other than that, he orders food in. Or he’ll occasionally go out to someplace because Joey or Allison or Kyle makes him feel like he has to. But Joey’s gone for a while. Joey said something about a relative in Jacksonville, and that he’d meet him at the show in Philadelphia. And when Jiro doesn’t see Joey, he doesn’t see Allison Nash. So Jiro Sennosuke was alone, ordering take out, and watching American television. Not because he really wants to, but partially because he doesn’t know what else to do, and partially because he’s not a successful wrestler. And the third reason is that it’s easy to slip into old habits. But we’ll get to that some other time. Jiro Sennosuke waits and waits…hoping that something will happen. Something that will inject life and energy into the wasteland of his soul. The funny thing about the universe is that sometimes…you actually can get what you want. knock-knock-knock There was a knock at the door, and Jiro’s heart threatened to jump out of his chest. knock-knock Three people, and a host of delivery boys knocked at Jiro’s door. But none of the people were in Seattle. Joey was in Jacksonville, Kyle was still in Japan, and Allison Nash was…somewhere else. She didn’t live in Seattle. The voice that came from outside Jiro Sennosuke’s door was deep, rich, and righteous. “Are you nervous? Jiro?” Jiro did not recognize the voice, and stood up, eyeing the door warily. The doorknob started to turn. The words tumbled out of Jiro’s mouth. “…away. Go away.” The doorknob finished turning, and slowly the door swung open, revealing a medium-sized man in a brown trenchcoat. “No thanks. We need to have a chat.” As the man entered the fluorescent-lit apartment, Jiro looked him from the top of his head. He had the strangest hair, as it appeared to be dyed a silvery-gray, and fell around his face, wet, but short. Kind of like a willow tree. The face was young, and didn’t quite match the hair. It took a second for Jiro to recognize the person, as the hair was, well, new. “D-Dan? Dan Ari…Ari…” The young man shook his head and chuckled a little. “Arianhrod. Don’t worry. You’ll get it soon enough.” Jiro, less worried now that he at least knew his visitor, slumped into a sitting position on his futon. “Where…why…” Jiro took a deep breath, as there was too much going on his head. He settled on a single question. “What are you doing here?” Danny didn’t smile, didn’t react at all…just locked eyes with Jiro. Never blinking. “Jiro. Seriously. What are YOU doing here?” Confused, Jiro looked around him, suddenly unsure if his surroundings would change, or if this was an odd dream sequence. This was his apartment…right? But everything was the same as it had been since he moved in, save the strange man who was now removing his trenchcoat and shutting the door behind him. Though Jiro’s eyes shifted and swirled about him, Danny Arianhrod was the picture of calm, collected cool. He pulled the trenchcoat off, and underneath it was a simple white wifebeater, and simple blue jeans. Everything about Danny’s body was simple, save his muscular arms, which were covered in tattoos. “Anoooo…” Jiro slipped back into his old tongue briefly. “…those tattoos…” Danny held up an arm, and eyed it for a moment. “What about them?” Jiro was slow to respond, and Danny took the initiative, sitting on the floor in a lotus position, and rubbing at his upper right arm absently. “Yes, they’re new. And yes, I know Jason Core and Kenichi Kazuyo have been looking for me. Don’t tell them I came to see you. I went for a walk, and did some soul-searching. The tattoos are part of that. I found myself.” Jiro finally had something to say to this, and was about to speak up, but Danny’s eyes gripped his and held them for a moment, as if to say ‘wait your turn.’ “I found myself, Jiro…and I think I can help you find yourself too. I’ve seen you wrestle, not just when I wrestled you in HSW, but in AWC. I sensed something in you that was something like what was in me. The caged tiger. The Titans in Tartarus. The archetypal beast.” Jiro just half-nodded, slowly. “Jiro, I found The Way. And The Way found me.” Sitting before him was a tattooed man with silver hair, something that seemed seriously out of place in his apartment. But there was something about his demeanor. His calm. His glance. Something that made Jiro want to know how he held himself like this. “Um, Danny…what is ‘The Way’?” Danny Arianhrod shrugged slightly. “Well, are you sure you want to know?” Jiro nodded, hesitantly at first, then with more conviction. As he did this, Danny stood up, and went to put his coat back on. “I’m sorry, Jiro Sennosuke. But, I don’t believe you do.” Without thinking, Jiro jumped to his feet, and shouted. “No!” Danny Arianhrod stopped for a moment, and waited for Jiro to say more. “Give me something, anything. Something that will make me feel something.” Danny looked as if he was thinking about something for a moment. “Fine. Answer me this question, Jiro, and I’ll tell you something about The Way. Why is your finishing move in AWC called ‘Kusanagi-no-Tsurugi’?” Greatly surprised by the question, Jiro’s eyes widened. He stuttered for a moment, then spoke. “For a while I lived with my grandfather. He made swords.” Danny spoke quickly. “Did you make swords?” Jiro inspected his feet. “No. Not really. I helped, once in a while.” Danny looked on with amusement. “The same way that Joey Six helps you wrestle?” Stunned, Jiro could say only one thing. He looked to the side, shamed. “Uh…well…yes.” “So why Kusanagi? Why the grasscutter?” Jiro gave it a moment’s thought, before the words rushed out of his mouth. “Because it’s the greatest weapon in my country’s history. Because with it, I should be able to win.” Danny shook his head. “But do you know anything about it? Do you know that it’s wielder, Susanowa, was the personification of evil, the god of storms and snakes? Do you know that you may be brave, but you are also wild and impetuous? Are you Susanowa, Jiro Sennosuke? Do you know?” Taken aback, there was nothing Jiro could do but stammer out a response. “I don’t know.” Danny Arianhrod lifted a hand, proceeded over to Jiro Sennosuke, and placed it gently on his shoulder. To Jiro Sennosuke, the world opened up. The rain came down harder, and Jiro felt the hand on his shoulder was similar to the feeling he’d had when he hit people, and won matches in AWC. It felt right. “Don’t worry, Jiro. I have a plan, my son, and I will show you The Way.” And the two men, one with silver hair, and one with onyx, talked through the night, as the storm of Seattle raged around them. And from that day forward, Jiro Sennosuke was not quite the same as he had once been.
