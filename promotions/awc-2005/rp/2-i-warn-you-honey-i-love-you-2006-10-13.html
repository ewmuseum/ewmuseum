2: i warn you, honey i love you

13 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 13, 2006 Eleanor, Forgetfulness comes softly, tiptoeing on the edges of a bad drink; even now, I can't quite say that I no longer know how to pluck out old momentary photographs of your hands, the insides of your elbows, the birthmark on your neck, the creases behind your knees and roots of your brain. These snapshots are hurried, developed under the wide-eyed snapping of your lids, drawn from sideways observation. Sometimes, I meet faces that have already been scarred upside down deep in the retina. Sometimes, I think about nights spent on hotel balconies, smoking cigarettes and blaring the stereo so loud, laughing into the grey air stained with neon smiles, jumping on hoods of cars in the parking lot, running through the corridors and kissing like a crime: spine pressed against the staircase rail, shoulders falling just a little over; feet pressed through the gaps between the metal bars, unyielding. But thoughts are nothing but connections from one neuron to the next, chemical transmitters hiding nothing in their infuriating alphabetic code. * Spirit, I write this to you on a post-it. It has come to my attention that you can only squeeze so much on a thin piece of paper as neon green as something really neon green. If I could, I would trap your stupid hands and stupid legs, but I've run out of space. Fatass. You stole my scarves on winter days, kept them in your wool coat pockets like secrets saved to open when we're older. You always said we'd be together. I lined my heart with heavy lipstick. You transmuted faint riches into something dense, heavy between the ribs. Your arms under the fake golden haze of light bulbs in the bedroom made me think of those days with the glorious feeling of sun on skin and eyelashes against cheeks. We took those times, those argosies of quick smiles and thin hands, and we lined picture frames with them; hung them up on the living room wall like victories in repose. We're still young, you would say. I thought you were tired of being young, I said back. You've forgotten that we're still young, you said. Maybe I don't want to be young, said I. Stupid in a melodramatic way, gelid words redolent of silent movies, but I held on to you and we never decided who was right. I've never been young without you and I've never been old with you. I'll keep that close, tie it to my ankle and paint it on my nails. I hate you all the same. -- Ellis, (you've never been young in the first place, said I. no, you don't remember how to. no. i don't know how to measure it in years, only laughs and places and hands held and boxes of chocolate slipped beneath the door. is there a lesson to be learned, or just a memory to keep?) The hotel manager says hi when he passes me on the streets, by the way. On some nights when I'm sure you won't call -- an on and off thing and I don't ask you for excuses, Ellis, you don't tell me that your work is keeping you or you have to go home to your husband because they are assumed, these marital transgressions -- we occasionally go out drinking. If he's really drunk, he asks me how you're doing. I smile and nod my head and say that you're fine and that your husband is beautiful and that your house is beautiful and that your life is beautiful and that I'm fine and that I don't miss you at all. He pretends that, bitter and mirthless, I am telling the truth. I know you don't approve but he is a decent lay, and he doesn't ask me to sleep on the left side of the bed only. He likes to kick towels into the corners of the bathrooms and sometimes on Fridays he smokes in bed with me. We practice flicking our ash and I always laugh when he starts cursing because we're burning holes into the sheets. I know you miss me, Ellis. I know you know I know you know Adam will never be me. Simple math. -- Spirit, I hope you feel bad that I used to lie and tell people we were friends when in truth behind closed doors you and I would fuck so hard we would wake up and forget what day of the week it was. I hope it's hard for you to tell the hotel manager what my name is. He's pretty, at least. He has the kind of body I know you know I know you like. His teeth are a little horsey and his ass is hairy, but you never did ask my opinion. ** Ellis, Must you be bitter, better half? The other day I was thinking about that time you and I went to visit Uncle Will in Manhasset. Remember? You told everyone I was your cousin, but everyone knew what I was. I was thinking that it might be nice to go again sometime, just you and me. If you remember, if you still have it in you, you could say all those things you told me when we went there for the first time; you could tell me that you love me and that you will always love me, that even when you get married, even when there is someone else, even when it gets hard and I treat you like shit, even when love gets all Bukowski on our asses and the thing you want to do the absolute most in the whole entire world is to drown me in the bathtub, even when we go back and at parties we pretend that this is the first time in a long time, like we are acquaintances, and I have to say awful polite things like, Oh, you went to Manhasset? How nice! I've never been -- even then you will love me. And maybe if you say all of this a million times in my ear as softly as you would take the breath from the lips of a baby, softly because I am dying all the same, if you say that, leaning over the table, your hand just brushing mine, maybe, just maybe, it would be as if nothing ever happened, as if you have indeed always loved me, and I would take your hand in mine, kiss it, tell you again that there is nothing, nothing -- no words -- nothing. And indeed, there are none. -- Spirit, What the fuck was that? A love song? ** Ellis, Perhaps. -- Spirit, Fag. ** Ellis, Alright. Jump to this, then: psuedo-romantic car rides in Albany. I'd taken you there once, remember?, had said, I want you to know what it's like to drink cheap champagne in the evenings, sitting on park benches, watching lights flicker through the Dunn Memorial Bridge, had said, I want you to know what it's like to be young and in love and believing it like you only can in a city meant for lovers. Funny how Albany steals love for itself, dips soft fingers into apartment windows and taps ashes onto hearts with drops of water slipping down the eaves, so much so that we both fell in love with that city, that feeling; that pink-tinted morning and green afternoon, instead of person. You went to smoke on the street, toes kissing the grey sidewalk lightly, small. I took my ridiculous dreams and believed, even with my empty wallet and counter full of bills, even with my sleepy eyes and lost backpack, lost, lost like your feet have always been, misplaced between the stairs and thrown away. I believe. Someday, you'll have to thank me for that. -- Spirit, Whatever. ** Ellis, I know you cling to bits of the old days. I know you hoard them away like a beggar. I know you nibble on them. I know you are always hungry. I know on good days you hum broken notes through the bleached cracks between your teeth, colliding and bumbling and sticking to the roof of your mouth, shingled with nothing but an odd sort of dryness. I know on the bad days you light a joint and purposely burn holes into your bedsheets. I know everything. -- Spirit, No. I will bury you on the edge of Staten Island. The fish will eat the gold off your stomach and your hair will snag in waters accustomed to the fingers of small children with wandering shoes. ** Ellis, I'll say, in the language of slack arms and snapped necks: it's not your fault. The freckles on your spine will jump with goosebumps; you'll fiddle with your voice box and throw words into the water like stones. They'll sink beside me. Your eyes will still say no, bleached into blindness by thin exhausted oil and half-heard whale songs. It's not your fault. -- Spirit, Just as you cannot love goddesses, I loved you. There are some lessons that can only be learned the hard way. I do not love you anymore. ** Ellis, And if I live a thousand years and bring the seas to the streets of New York City, if I go to infinity and feel the beating of a heart in my palm, I will not let you go. You have left your fingerprints and I cannot rub them away. With blood on my hands and danger like razor blades pressed against my wrists, you would kiss me behind domed buildings and curtains of rough linen. You would kiss me, but never on the mouth, and never with open eyes. -- Spirit, I can't do this. You have spun my heart with the merry-go-rounds of your heels. If I love you, my wrist will not fit into the circle of your forefinger and thumb; my pulse will pound like the ocean's waves and your tides will not be enough to smooth away the jagged edges. Your smile is like sun on water, breath like dew on mountains -- but you have drifted away with the winds and the weathervane of our heart is forever twirling, cupid's arrow pointing towards the ground. I can't. ** Ellis, It’s all a matter of predestination: you were meant to be a bitch, I was meant to believe. -- Spirit, A bitch? Listen, I sold my dreams for you. There is no bribing of the gods these days, no easy sacrifices; your arrows were the hot fire that ravaged my veins and broke through the sunrises to leave me panting, dragon-breathed and dying, never to love anyone but you. You left me burning, the last wick of a dying flame. You walked away with my heart like nectarine flesh in your hands, pulsing and hot. I have burned for no one else since and I will not burn again. I will wash out your memory with three weeks of dirty clothes. I will lose these long days to soap bubbles which, popped, will disperse as dandelion seeds, as fibers of wind. When it rains I will give the remnants of your expressions to the puddles and to the broken rain. I will lose you, wring out the last vestiges of feeling, the last gossamer threads of spiderweb hope, the last longing that leaves wrinkles in my clothes. ** Ellis, You could be standing at the edge of the world, head bowed to the rain, hands clasped; you could be the last shadow that falls and shapes the edges with the electricity of your eyes, you could carve out the world in your own colors and there I'll be, fumbling in wet pockets for cold keys and coming up empty. I'll be between one locked door and another because your eyes are beyond me, they're looking out on a world that has turned its back and has frozen itself and has tripped on its edges like shoelaces and tipped the scales. There are no worries like locked doors and broken arguments at the end of the world at the end of the week at the end of the day at the end of an impossibility I would strain on forever to keep. There is no me to a you who looks down to the pavement and sees shapes in the rain, a you who looks for someone else in mirrors and shop windows, a you who has buried yourself in unexpected expectations from a me you never expected. I want nothing more than to say goodnight to these city lights glittering in sight of the corner of my eye, where I stand in the corner of yours. This is me, wrapped in the reality of you, drawing breath in the lonely position where you stand, waiting for the rain to wash away your sharp edges and to drain away the colors of your figure, to paint you in anonymity, to pound a name into the cement to be washed away and bled between the streets of Cortland the same way it is in your dreams. You could be standing at the edge of the world, looking down into a cradle of civilization you never tasted, waiting for my frozen fingers to give up on the door the way you would be waiting for my weary soul to give up on you. I want nothing more than to say goodnight to these city lights that wink at me and remind me of a world that you could shed like snakeskin and wave out with a smile, only I am the city you are blinking away, and I am the one brought down by the rain and I am the one facing an empty door in an empty night with an empty smile. There is a hushed goodnight to the city, to the storm, to the conversations I have abandoned as lost. To me. You could be standing at the edge of the world, sharpening the words I am waiting for you to speak, only when you turn I have only eyes for the rain and see no shapes between, no sodden figure with your back like a door you have lost the keys to. I sit on the steps and let the rain wash you away. -- Spirit, I am not your perfection. I am not a doll for you to dress or to kiss or to haunt with empty promises and false words and lofty passion. I am not here for your convenience; your whimsy does not dictate the movement of my feet and you will not hold my plans in place. I don't rely on your touch or your mouth or your fingertips and all the sentences that you don't tell me and all the things you don't say. I am not defined by the motion of your emotions; you will not scatter my peace like clothes, like that that burns, flames, dies. I am not yours anymore. I'm done. -- el. ** Ellis, I will always be in your memories of lace afternoons, fragile and half-empty, always unfulfilling, a parody of relief. I see you through windowpanes and the broken glass of old conversations, the background noise that washes over me the way my gazes wash over you. I think I may be content with these moments like coins, warmed in reminiscing, more background clanging to pull my reverent silences awake. even so, I think I will watch you through the transparence of these crowds. Hope loses luminescence at the next stop. As much as you try, you can't forget. Won't. You'll come back to me soon. “You’ve been having dreams, yes?” Yes. “Of?” I don’t know. Post-its. Neon green post-its. “Post-its?” Yeah. Neon green post-its. “Just neon green post-its, then?” Of Albany. Manhasset. Logan. “Logan?” Logan. “Who is Logan?” “Ellis?” My fiance. Ex-fiance. My spirit.
