Running Along the Mobius Strip (Part 1)

4 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 4th, 2006 I was very familiar to the concept of waking up in strange places with no recollection of how I had gotten there in the first place. I had woken up before in ditches along the sides of roads. I had woken up in the back of a moving pickup truck once, halfway across the country. I even woke up one time, buck naked, inside of the walk-in freezer at Red Lobster, but then again, you do some crazy things when you’re on mescaline. How I had gotten here, though, and more importantly, how I would extricate myself were the two immediately burning questions that ran through my mind as soon as I found myself in this particular predicament. I feared that I had gone blind, as I perceived no change in pitch black shading whether my eyes were open or closed. After repeatedly opening and closing my eyes for short intervals, I began to question too, which one was which, and only then did I notice the otherwise indistinguishable changes in saturation that arise from blinking. When keeping my eyes open, I became sooner irritated by the eventual dryness, alerting me to consciously blink in rapid shudders until my eyelids became heavy and sore. I felt the ground beneath me in a circle the diameter of my wingspan, and it felt uniformly grainy. I had ample space in which to move, so I hunched down using my arms for balance as I teetered around with bent knees, inching around the textured floor until I felt a wall in front of me. The wall kept the same granular texture as the ground beneath me, and I followed it around in a perfect square forty paces along each side starting from one corner. I walked it twice, to be certain, still in the same arched position with one hand dragging along the walls and the other feeling out forwardly for each corner. I slowly tried to straighten myself into a standing position, reaching upwards, but I found myself to be inconveniently impeded by the same texture above me. I returned my hand to the wall, softly scratching to get the substance between my fingernails. It caked between the fingertip and the nail, and I scraped my nail across my teeth and tasted the dry bitter flavor of dirt. “Shit!” I responded, trying as best I could to spit out the scrapings that stuck in my mouth. The one word outburst bounced off of the walls and spread throughout the room, cracking and fizzling before dissipating back to silence; a quiet stillness that felt even shallower than before as if the room was imploding into its own emptiness. I scratched more at the dirt wall and felt how tightly packed the walls actually were. My hands flew up and down, frantically, trying to tear the beginnings of a tunnel into the wall, but I still felt as if I was making very little progress. My arms got tired quickly due to the solid tightness of the dirt, and mentally, I grew hastily frustrated at the futility of my tunneling endeavors. I sat with my feet pushed forward against the perpendicular edge of the cube where one wall met the ground, contemplating how to escape a chamber without any apparent entrance or exit. It was too early, at this point, to pursue the consideration of submitting to my state of helplessness, and I tried to rationalize my present predicament by blaming it on some hallucinogen I might have been on at the time. I figured that if I waited a while and tried to calm myself down, my mind would clear up and I would find that I wasn’t entrapped at all other than inside my own overactive imagination. I reached into my pocket and pulled out a pack of cigarettes and a lighter. I fumbled to light the cigarette in the dark, but the spark of light provided a brief and sobering reconfirmation of my dilemma. The room lit, and my pupils shrank with the sudden change of light, revealing that I was in fact trapped within the confines of a solid dirt room. “Mother fucker,” I muttered to myself, the automatic vulgarities being the only words worth speaking as well as the only ones that felt logical to mouth aloud. Without reason to move, now, I continued to sit and smoke, until I was forced to move as the whole room shifted so that my back was now against the ground, as gravity would have it, and my feet were pointed upwards. After unintentionally facing upwards as a result of the shifting of my chamber, I was forced to immediately shield my eyes as a square hatch opened, staining the room temporarily white while I shielded my eyes. As they slowly adjusted to the brightness, I could make out the silhouetted shape of a head, watching me, but it seemed only a shadow in the contrast as I cowered in the dim corner of my space. Then, the hatch was shut again, and the room turned further, as I fell awkwardly to the new ground with the ceiling becoming low once more and the peripheral area around me again allowing me to move in all of my lateral directions. And even though I could have moved, I sat still, calculating, expecting the whole room to shift again in one direction or another that could send me crashing against any wall. But the room didn’t move, simultaneously increasing my hope and paranoia that it would. I wondered what exactly I had done and who I had pissed off to be imprisoned in such a vengeful manner. I wrung out my memory for any clue to help justify my being confined in this rotating sliver of space; I wrung it until it was dry, but I only found random, blurry scenes in between inexplicable gaps. *** The world turned beneath my feet as if I were balancing on it as it floated in the middle of some endless ocean. The city lights and neon signs shined vividly though the streets were empty and the lights inside the rows of stores were off. I walked along the edge of the road, tripping over misjudged distances, hurling myself forward with an uncertain perseverance. There was a strange urgency to my movements all the while, though I had no concrete mission other than remaining in constant motion. I was being propelled by the slow momentum of my rigid stumbling, my body hunched forward against the heavy mass of air that conjoined in front of whichever direction I faced. I stopped walking for a moment a few blocks back, and the peculiar distribution of gravity pasted me backwards to the ground, forcing me to laugh uncontrollably while immobile on my back near the curb. But the laughing subsided, and I was able to roll from side to side until I flipped myself onto my stomach, where I was able to push myself back to my feet after a considerable amount of struggle. Returning to my feet, though, I was facing the direction from which I came, and I found turning around to be an obstacle. I kept walking instead, passing the point I started from and continuing in the opposite direction. “I’m going the wrong way!” I shouted while laughing euphorically and taking exaggerated steps, carefully one foot at a time. I was laughing too hard to do anything but angle myself slightly, and I eventually veered into the middle of the road. I saw a late night city bus coming towards me, but once I noticed it, there wasn’t enough time for me to make it across the road at my current pace and trajectory. I shot my palm forward in a jerking motion, unable to bend my elbow at the joint. The bus honked a loud, prolonged belch as the driver slammed on the breaks, stopping dangerously close to my palm, which jutted me out of my vegetative trance march. The driver opened the door as I semi-circled my way towards the door, meeting the driver in the bus doorway. He was a baggy-eyed, burly gentleman, middle aged with thinning grey hair arranged in a sharp, boxy cut. He wore a goatee to shade the pudgy roundness of his chin and spoke with the buzz of caffeinated coffee in his breath and a quickly beating tempo in his furious flurry of irate admonishment. “Jesus Fucking Christ, you’re going to get yourself killed out there, you fucking asshole!” he spat out, with a choppy emphasis on every audibly capitalized syllable. I slid underneath his arm, oblivious to the actual shock and timbre of his words as he perched his hand against the side of the doorway. He tried to stop me, pinning my body against the side of the folded glass, “What the hell do you think you’re doing?” I slid my frame through his bulk and laid down on the first row of seats, staring up at the lights and advertisements on the ceiling, though the latter were all just rounded strips of paper with shaking, dyslexic text and vaguely familiar insignias. “Take me to the end of the line, governor! It is imperative that I depart from town post haste,” I nonsensically rambled, unable to filter the stream of gibberish that confusedly balled through a hole acidly bored in the separation between my mind and my mouth. The bus driver then tried to pry me off of the row of seats I was sprawled across, but it was to no avail. My dead weight just flopped to the ground until he bent over and hoisted me to my feet underneath my armpits. I felt my feet unresistingly drag across the ground as he held me and pushed me through the aisle and out the back door of the bus. I landed in the grassy tufts over the curb, unaware that the bus hadn’t moved since I had been on it as it sped away, leaving me roadside in its wake. “My gratitude for the expedient transport, good sir,” I decreed, waving my worn to flimsy cap in the air at the bus. I stood up, dusting off my knees, and with a cigarette that had unnoticeably stopped burning hanging from between my lips, I put a hand over my brow and peered off, searching for the dimmest direction before continuing my jaggedly rhythmic trek away from the sleeping neon through to the crooked bends that led to nowhere in particular that seemed so necessary to reach in my state of mind at the time. *** The dirt room hadn’t turned in an excruciatingly long time, and combing my memory left painful bristles in my brain where the holes in my recollection flamed through like burnt film. I had since crawled the length of the room while horizontal so many times that I had lost count. I was curious about the frightening light with the distant opening and the blank observer. Nothing had changed about my confinement, save that I discovered worms and other insects burrowing around in the dirt, but even they were hopeless to find escape. I had never seen more than a specific number of worms or flies or spiders at once, although I had taken into account the eventual fluctuation of their populations on account of breeding habits and the inevitability of death. It was in the counting of these buzzing and squirming things that I decided I would have to subside on their meats and juices, but I vowed to try my best to let my deprived stomach writhe inside of me rather than deplete my food source by eating living bugs. Living bugs could still breed, and I considered my self-deprivation to be a great investment. Once the worms and flies and spiders had birthed a healthy swarm of offspring, I would be able to indulge my appetite more comfortably. For now, I waited for worms to curl up and die. I zeroed in on the sound of frantic buzzing, waiting for it to stop. I checked webs obsessively and oftentimes beat the spiders to their prey. If I got desperate, I picked scabs and ate my own dead skin or chewed off fingernails and toenails. I chewed what little I could peel off for as long as I could, well beyond the loss of my own salty flavor, subsiding on fractions of nutrients that threaded their ways into my saliva as I chewed. The death of an earthworm was cause for celebration. Some of the worms here were long or fat, and my wrinkling, withering stomach had started shrinking to the point where a sizeable worm would satisfy me for quite some time. In the meantime, when I wasn’t chewing, I monitored my own droppings as the flies buzzed around them. I oversaw my agricultural project with great pride, and I felt the flies would grow strong, healthy, and most importantly virile and fertile from my waste. I noticed that over a span of time, they began to increase in number. I felt a modicum of success, as I counted seven flies all gathered around my latest dehydrated, spongy dropping. And then, when I was lulled into my false sense of pride, the dirt enclosing tipped back vertically, and I crashed to the bottom of the tall space, forty paces from the only way I’d seen in or out. I froze in place as the hatch above opened, the shadowy head craned in the same position underneath a shower of light. I cowered in fear, forgetting about escape and solely worried about the microcosm I was guarding over, playing God to a scope of simple insects, too small to see me as a whole entity and too primitive to deify me, unable to grasp the knowledge of life beyond instinct. I think, perhaps, that is what I envied about them. I scampered and scratched at the walls at arms length on both sides after finally shaking myself from the shock of unexpected movement, but it was too late. The light faded into slivers again before the thinnest visible line of illumination disappeared beneath the echoic clatter of randomly cycling inescapability; a merciless sentence handed down from a faceless judge who towers over me in a bath of brilliant light. The room turned back over to the other side horizontally, leaving once again a shaken man questioning his crime and silently appealing his literally sealed fate. *** I huddled behind some foliage, shielding myself from the flashing lights and blaring sirens of emergency vehicles rushing down the road. I feared detection, though as it seemed, the fire engines, ambulances, and police cars that barreled down the road in a parade of frenzied relief. After the procession had passed, I peered out from behind a bush. Though mildly intrigued by the prospects of excitement that lay in one direction, I continued down the initial direction I had decided upon walking earlier. The sun was just appearing over the rotating earth as I rolled it underneath my feet until for no apparent reason; I turned in the opposite direction again and rolled the earth back away from the sun into darkness. I walked back and forth, rolling the horizon up and down, hiding and uncovering the sun. I was intrigued by this dizzy pacing, and the changes in the tint of tree leaves and such left me lightheaded so that I once again had to sit down to collect my thoughts. Balancing on the edge of daybreak were the punctually clockwork people, gliding along their deepening niches grooved into the earth from the repetition of their routines. Men in dress shirts and slacks walked dogs before work. Women in track pants and sports bras jogged to inspirational music that fuzzed out indistinguishably from their headphones. They were all exactly on schedule, not noticing the friction being worn away from their daily grooves borne into the crust as it dripped away drop by drop through the tiny pores in the pavement. I unfortunately committed the grievous error of interfering with the natural course of these routines as they were the engines that powered up the sunrise. A man pulled on a leash as his terrier approached me with a curious, sniffing investigation. I rose immediately as the dog jerked back against his will and looked at the man on the other end of the leash. “Excuse me, sir! Would you be a sport and tell me which direction will bring me to sunset? I have some important matters to attend to, and it would be favorable for me to travel under the guise of nightfall.” The man backed up upon my incomprehensible request, although it seemed fairly straightforward before I had put it into words. I felt the fissures in the earth grind apart beneath me, instigating a seasickness that churned inside of me as he hurried forward to reset his interrupted routine. I promptly fell to my hands and knees with my equilibrium disturbed, and I vomited violently, mudded brown, in the dewy grass while the warm half sun heated my insides to a feverish degree. I shakily returned to my feet, stumbling back and forth unpredictably as a jogger tried unsuccessfully to avoid me. Upon impact, I fell back to the ground, turning my head as my stomach jerked and I heaved choked attempts to regurgitate the sunken emptiness from the pit of my stomach. The sun and the earth continued to perform their torturous voodoos upon me as I lay exposed and shamed under the inconveniently visible light of day. I edged across the roadside, the accidental agitator of multiple stealth pursuers who were, as I lay there wrenching in gastric agony, closing off the narrowing chances I had of reaching a safe place where I could rid myself of these overwhelmingly evidential stains that stuck to me, lingering in my stenches and stigmatizing me with the mark of involvement in vague, hazy events so remarkably unfathomable that even I had to immediately destroy them from my knowledge. Whether it was to absolve any possible guilt or to preserve the architecture of reality (or even my own sanity), it was especially important that I, above everyone else, wiped my mind clean of something that had just transpired. It was even more important that I escaped without stumbling across something or someone to remind me of what I had already forgotten. *** The mirrored horizontality of the dirt room put me in a foreign mood from my ambitious optimism, and I temporarily abandoned my project to stimulate the insect population. I stared blankly into the darkness, conserving the fluid in my lighter for the moment. My eyes, anyway, had adjusted to the lack of light, and the room was shaped so simply that I could navigate my way around it with muscle memory. I waxed over the possibility that I might never see another human face as long as I lived aside from the head shaped shadow that monitored me from time to time. I felt a strange sense of Robinson Crusoe’s moderating affliction, fearing both the absence of human interaction and the threat of encounter under such circumstance. Crusoe, however, was a fantastic figment, created to resolve his author’s tremendous debt. I was created for no such purpose, as desperate as that purpose was. I was crafted by circumstance, not god-sent in the slightest, but a random drifter, a mixture of chromosomes and genetic coding. My disposition was in no way metaphorical. These walls around me were solid and real, but still I tried to extract some meaning from them being around me. In Crusoe’s predicament, he oftentimes felt both blessed and cursed with his solitude on the island. He felt like servant and master, only surviving by the grace of god, and the supple provisions that the island afforded him, but still holding dominion over its borders. I felt my situation was far more concrete and powerless, supervised but not servile. I had oftentimes considered the inherent flaws in the idea of some omnipotent being. All the stories of some pre-existential creator who ordered a world full of such restriction, such lack of control, and such a possibility for unchecked expansion. There were too many incomplete intricacies that tectonically smashed into each other, jutting out in metaphysical protrusions that melted back in willow shaped overhangs, hardened into drooping tufts by slow gravity. The ugly randomness shaded the world into a convoluted darkness of conscience. I had heard many theories that aimed to prove the simplest explanation was the most likely to be correct. To me, these uniform walls coffining me into a seamless enclosure where nothing existed beyond and the edges painted a tangible finality seemed more provable than a culture of innumerable conjurations of shape and color that supposedly existed outside. Perhaps the latter was just a complex dream I had dreamt before snapping back to the realization that such a blueprint would be unfeasible as well as undesirable. Such an imposing structure could only operate on a balance of decay as the insides rotted at an incomparable pace; slowly choking the slowly expanding outer walls that struggled to escape the exponentially self-consuming corrosion built upon itself and pressed the ballooning skin until it burst. The outside faded like an aged memory, and I soon doubted the existence of something so intricate when this containment was wholly present. Even more questionable than the existence of god was my own existence. Man must be constantly reminded and reconfirmed of his own image, and I had little need for such a reminder now. I found it mattered little whether I existed in any part of this present, tilting finiteness. I found consolation in the sticky feelers that crawled around on bugged legs. I wished I could become one. *** As I continued on, walking in one direction or another, I spat the resurfacing taste of my own tainted stomach acids out of my mouth. There was a mutual discomfort in the glances I stared with strangers as they passed. I began to amuse myself by making sudden movements towards them, sending passers by into quickening hustles in the opposite direction. The earth treaded faster under their feet, carrying me in the opposite direction with fast rolling bursts. The sunlight kept no secrets, and I felt a building sense of paranoia concerning my identity. I was determined to find out who I was before anyone else did, because truth be told, I had somewhat forgotten everything about my identity in some surreal mishap that I reassured myself was commonplace in the confusing arena surrounding me. There comes a point when you realize that among billions of fucking people, it becomes a bit blurry at times to recognize which one you are. For now, I felt the safest course of action was to develop a suitable guise to camouflage my dangerous vulnerability. I searched around, scanning my immediate area for any image that I could mold myself into effortlessly. Having been sequestered from the normal functions of society for so long, the people moving around me bore little resemblance. I was unkemptly hairy with a beard that spun a wild mane around my face, and I walked around barefoot, dirty skin uncovered by a tattered brown bathrobe. The people around me had hair cropped close to their heads with big shaven faces, tinted pinkish. To transform myself into a passable member of this crowd would take a grooming beyond my capabilities. My disguise, however, appeared to me in the form of a stained glass beacon, shining down on me in a prism of vivid color as the sun’s light brought out its beaconed beckoning in an extra-dimensional vivacity. The image crested the top of a monolithic stone edifice, crowned by the outstretched arms of a perpendicular intersection. The colored glass centered on a man bearing a striking resemblance to me, too similar to be coincidental. His arms were laterally outstretched, and they narrowed towards his robed frame. His long, thorny, brown hair flowed into a beard as he paralleled the symbol above his image- must have been some sort of receptive device like an antenna or something. I stopped a man who walked by, trying to pay as little attention to me as he could. “Excuse me, sir…” I started. “I’m sorry, I don’t have any spare change,” he replied, trying to pull himself away. “I was just wondering if you could tell me the name of that man,” I begged while pointing a finger at the stained glass image. The gentleman eyed me with lowered eyebrows, as if he was questioning the integrity of my inquiry. “I have to go,” he resisted. “I’m on my way to work.” “Please!” I desperately goaded, now grabbing his tie with both hands so he couldn’t escape. “Let go of my tie!” he demanded, trying to walk away, but I held on for dear life, dragging my feet into the cement sidewalk as he pulled his upper body. “Jesus Christ!” he exclaimed, accidentally answering my question while trying to bat me away with his briefcase. Still, I held onto his tie, tugging him towards me with a miraculous, unrelenting strength. He submissively put his hands up in the air and said “alright” five times in a row. “That’s Jesus Christ, okay?” “Jesus Christ? What kind of nonsense name is that? I think you’re pulling my leg.” “I swear, I’m telling the truth,” he defended, his eyes wide with fear. “Will you please let go of me?” He was breathing heavily. I released my grip on his tie, and he fell backwards, his briefcase snapping open and papers spilling out onto the sidewalk. He hurriedly put the papers back into the case, now unorganized, before getting up and jogging away. I rubbed my bearded chin and thought to myself that this Jesus Christ guy, whoever he was, must have been important to have his picture painted on such a large building. The perpendicular antenna must have been some sort of satellite receiver as there was no other practical purpose that came to mind for its position atop the building. That must have been his house; no- my house. I bet that satellite thing must have received hundreds of television channels- even the ones with the naked girls- with crystal clear reception. Now all I had to do was devise a seamless plot to steal his identity and start using him as a cover until I had more accurate information. I let the name sink in. “Hello, my name is Jesus Christ.” I repeated to myself, practicing my intermitted identity. “Would you care to join me in my mansion to view satellite pornography?” I approached the large oak doors of this soon to be seized structure and knocked twice, readying myself for an unexpected pounce. Nobody answered, so I crept into the unlocked doors and looked around, until I was approached by a white-collared man dressed in black. This must have been Jesus’ butler. After all, he had to be filthy, stinking rich to afford such a state-of-the-art satellite system. I stood there, expecting anything. “May I help you?” I scratched my head, trying to stimulate my mind to work faster. “Uh, is Jesus Christ in?” “Jesus is always in,” his butler responded smiling and extended his arms. I extended mine in response, thinking that this must have been some sort of common gesture. I also smiled in response, thinking that if I could get alone with Jesus, it’d be quite simple to brain him with a blunt object, steal his attire, and then bind and gag him. I’d put him in the back of a closet where he wouldn’t be found for quite some time, and the switch would go off without a hitch. The beauty of my plan came from its simplicity. “Um, where is he?” I asked in all seriousness, though the butler gave me a look questioning my intentions. I was found out! As I was deciding whether to spring at the butler with a blinding whirlwind of fists and teeth or turn about face and run off, he quelled my fears. “He’s in your heart.” This was too easy. I could pass as Jesus Christ with a minimal required amount of maiming. I repeated under my breath, “Hello, my name is Jesus Christ. Would you care to join me in my mansion to view satellite pornography?”
