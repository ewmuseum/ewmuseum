Date: 4th July Fallen Whispers – Chapter 1 ‘Hide and Seek’ Imogen Heap Prometheus - ‘a Sinister End’ – November 2005. The wind ripped through the sky, as leaves scattered onto the many police cars delved beneath the maple trees. The havoc enveloping the oncoming storm was ironic to the situation within the large hanger, a simple whisper, the sound of a barrel moving and then, gun fire and with that, panic coursed through the police officers veins, each one hunkered by their bullet proof doors, brandishing their guns and vests. Each man, alone, aware and silent as they prepared for battle. Adam Dick was on his knees, buried heavily into the saw dust covered concrete bottom, the rough, cold touch seeping through his ripped jeans as his eyes gazed into the small black, never ending hole of the gun barrel, another click, the next bullet now awaiting the ignition. The words couldn’t fathom the inevitable and yet things seemed as clear as day for Pierce, the opaque shadow that once glazed his mind, slowly became transient to the evidence knelt before him. ‘Don’t kill me. Please, don’t kill me,’ Adam Dick said through muffled cries. ‘You fucked up my life,’ Pierce’s lips cursed through tensed muscles, his hand sweaty as he held the gun firmly in a dead mans clasp. ‘I…never…’ Adam Dick paused a moment, his blurry eyes looking toward the barrel and then listening to the cop cars, he decided to continue the mind games, despite the inevitability of death. ‘You got what you deserved, asshole,’ Adam Dick said with determination in his voice, his words crisp and hollow. Pierce swung with his arm turning sideways as the gun handle connected to the side of Adam’s face, his mouth opening on impact as spit accompanied the impact of the gun handle. Adam Dick choked on his own blood, his tongue mauling the freshly slit gum as a tooth trickled from his chin till it landed softly on the dust covered floor. ‘Why’d you do it?’ Pierce demanded, tensing his grip, his eyes glistening with anger. ‘Why’d you have me drugged, have my emotions messed with and ruin my life?’ Pierce pressed even further now, fed-up with his life, he pushed the barrel into Adam Dick’s forehead; the smell of sizzling flesh simmered up their noses as its hot nozzle gripped and pinched his skin. ‘For the title…Please don’t kill me…’ Adam Dick pleaded, feeling completely degraded on the floor in tears. The doors burst open, the darkened room covered in blues and reds, each man swarming around Pierce, their guns in a defensive posture, a losing battle had now been lost for Pierce, he’d resorted to the man on the floor. Lowering his weapon, he was shoved onto the ground and handcuffed and he knew, the ‘truth would never matter’. Los Angeles – Present Day – June 22nd ‘Man in the box’ Alice in Chains - song Pierce awoke from his sleep, his eyes gazing into the abyss as his mind fluttered through his dream, the same nightmare he’d experienced each night for the last year, insomnia was slowly kicking in. As he rolled over, he could feel the humidity of the Los Angeles summer and it felt foreign as he felt his skin, drenched in sweat. The bed covers clung to his chest as his eyes darted across the room, the silence nauseating, his ears still ringing from the small alarm clock, its monotonous beeping tone coursing through his middle ear as his mind focused on the bleared green numbers – 5:00 am. Rising from the soggy, wet covers, he swung his legs out and over the corner of the bed. The cool brisk air conditioning kicked in, sending chills over the sweat beads the lined his chest and back. Running his hands through his sleeked brown hair, he took a few deep breaths, the same routine he had done on countless times, but this was no ordinary day, it was the day of his appeal to the DA office; if he won, he could return to the sport he loved and if he didn’t, the same stark realisation swept over him like a black cloud – ‘The truth will never matter’. Now rising from the bed, his bottoms prying from his sweaty body, he flicked on his CD player and cocked his neck to the side, stretching. As he plodded across the tiled floor his mind awoke to the evocative stylings of ‘Man in the box’ by Alice in Chains. The power jet came to life, sending a cascade of water down his body, his forehead balanced on the cold tiled wall, feeling the water course over his body, almost therapeutic he could hear the faint words of his CD player in the background, his body turning against the flow of water as his eyes fixed on the steamy glaze on the glass before him. ‘For the title…’ Adam Dick’s words continually embedded in the back of his mind, searing his cerebrum with a pain, a pain that had surfaced over the AWC for a while now, with ‘King’ Dick in reign and he didn’t deserve it, Pierce thought of many ways in which he would seek revenge, one being the title. Pierce allowed the water tank to drain of hot water until the cold chilled water trickled in a sizzle off his skin, evaporating on touch. As he stepped into the cooled bathroom, the condensation bleared each mirror and window in the small room. The phone billowed in the background, resounding against the silence and drawing Pierce away from his washed appearance, his hand gently wiping the mirror, only to greet a man; he didn't even recognise. Wrapping a towel around his waist, he toddled into his main living area, his wet feet leaving an impression on the wooden floor as he answered the phone. 'Hello?' his voice spoke with a deep, crisp dry mouth. 'Hey, hot stuff, where can I get some coffee in this damn city?' Lois's voice spoke through the receiver, a shrill in her high pitched tone. 'Lo? It's five in the morning...' Pierce said, his eyebrows creasing with confusion. 'I know it's killing me. Did I wake you?' 'No, I just had a shower,' Pierce replied, tightening the knot in his towel as he dried his hair. 'Oh, quell my beating heart,' Lois replied through a giggle. 'Lois, how many cups of coffee have you had?' Ramsfield gulped with nerves and removed his shirt from inside his trousers in desperate hopes of covering his shame, but his plan held no avail to the situation he was in. ‘Do tell, Ms. Wood,’ Judge Harris leant forward, nodding for Lois to banter a little. The state’s accusations against my client have all been based on false pretences and tampered evidence…’ Lois straightened her suit jacket and turned to the jury. ‘…I could bore you with a rambling, nonchalant lawyer speech, but there is no point wasting anybodies time when my client is innocent and I have the evidence to prove it, if you will allow me to call my witness, your honour,’ she said with a glint and turned sharply on her black heels. Judge Harris leant forward, placing his hand over the microphone, both Wood and Jones joined him at his podium and the court room had fallen silent. Pierce’s mind was running out each logical scenario, worst case he thought, he could go to prison. It was a fact he had learnt to accept, but not without anger and hurt for those involved. ‘Ms. Wood, what is this?’ Harris asked, his eyes gazing toward her over the brim of his glasses. ‘We have spent months building a case and now you are going to stop it with a single client, please,’ Jones said, folding his arms in a huff. Lois’s eyes rolled and she once more turned toward Harris, who like her, saw the immaturity in Jones, a young man with perhaps an ounce of talent to add to an already receding justice system. ‘I am aware the court does not need to be humoured, your honour, but I believe this to be the breakthrough we have all been looking for. The client is under arrest by the FBI in light of charges relating to my own client,’ Lois said affirmably and shook her head in the direction of Tony Rush. Harris followed her nod and saw the FBI agent’s golden badge glisten behind the mahogany banister. ‘Alright, you may proceed, Ms. Wood,’ Harris said and removed his hand off the microphone. Jones grunted and returned to his seat. Lois smiled and called Detective Ramsfield to the stand. Ramsfield stood with difficulty, the handcuffs pinching his skin. In an instance, the media all jumped to life, their cameras glaring a bright light against Ramsfield’s back as he shuffled ashamed toward the stand. Each journalist braced themselves with a biro and jotter, they weren’t going to miss a word. Lois pointed to the stand and Ramsfield sat down, placing his hand on a bible and swore his oath. Truth… A word foreign to Ramsfield, but he swore as he looked into the camera’s that he would redeem what little dignity he had left and fix the mess he helped create. ‘Detective, please state your name once more for the jury members,’ Judge Harris announced, leaning in his chair toward the witness. ‘Martin Ramsfield, LAPD Narcotic division,’ Ramsfield stuttered, his hand gripping his trouser leg with nerves. ‘Detective Ramsfield, do you recognise the witness?’ Lois asked, starting with the plain and obvious. ‘Yes, I do,’ Ramsfield nodded, preferring not to look at Pierce as an immense wave of guilt swept against his heart and mind. ‘You were the detective assigned to charging and booking my client, correct?’ Lois continued. Each question the jury members clung on the edge of their seats, hoping for the ‘big bang’, the resolve. ‘Yes I was, at the time I was placed in charge of detaining those involved in large narcotic operations,’ Ramsfield replied, taking steady breaths, the camera’s lights glaring an unnerving heat against his skin. ‘Is it true that you had little evidence to charge my client with?’ Lois asked, looking for her door in to get the truth out and to finally get a good enough media covered case with a big bang, it would be her window of opportunity and it would also be her clients. ‘At the time, all we had to go on was possession of a firearm with intent to harm,’ Ramsfield commentated. ‘What changed?’ Lois smiled, turning to her table and pulling out a folder. ‘We had a witness come forward with…’ Ramsfield was stopped, Lois pulled out a photo of a young woman, showed it to the jury members and then toward Ramsfield. ‘This is Kelli Stoner, recognise her?’ Lois asked, a smirk balanced discretely along her red lips. ‘Yes, she rang the station, said a man was trying to kill somebody,’ Ramsfield replied with slight recognition of a night that had transpired almost a year ago and remained very much a blur. ‘She rang to save my client, to protect my client. She gave you evidence that incriminated another man, yet you chose to ignore it, isn’t that right?’ Lois jumped, she had her window. ‘I – eh…’ Ramsfield stuttered, his time for the truth was coming quicker than he had planned. ‘You tampered with evidence, didn’t you? You needed a scapegoat for the biggest crime syndicate in Los Angeles, isn’t that right? And you had my client charged with possession of a fire arm, who better to charge and make a show of, than a wounded man…’ Lois slammed her file down for dramatic pause and waited as Ramsfield stewed in his own juices. ‘It was never like that, you have it all wrong…’ Ramsfield yelled. The court rose with talking, their voices erupting into suspicions, the media in hype with the coverage and their breakthroughs. ‘ORDER,’ Judge Harris yelled and the room fell silent. Pierce was on the edge of his seat, his eyes transfixed on the picture of Kelli Stoner, her eyes still held the care he had seen when he first met her, but the lies and set-up she had conspired with Dick had left a pain of hate running through his mind. ‘I have it wrong? Really,’ Lois smiled and pulled out a page with writing on it, she showed it to the jury members and court as exhibit B, the written confession by Kelli Stoner. ‘…Pierce Lavelle was framed and set-up, he was used by the company as a test subject with intention on killing him. He was, in no way, involved....’ Lois read a small paragraph from the page. ‘…What exactly have I got wrong?’ she demanded with resonance, her eyes glaring toward Ramsfield, who was beyond savour now. ‘I never meant for it to go this far. They said they would deal with him…’ Ramsfield muttered into the microphone. ‘Who is they?’ Lois asked. ‘All I had to do was forge evidence and make him the bad guy and they said they’d deal with the rest, they said it would be over and I wouldn’t be caught…I never meant for it to go this far…I framed your client, for them…’ Ramsfield said, tears streaming down his rosy coloured cheeks. A loud gasp came from the court room, the jury members shocked. Jones shuffled in his seat, closing his suitcase. The case had been wrapped up and he’d lost his big entrance. Pierce, was taken aback by the situation, now looking in awe at Lois’s determination and fierce presence within the court room. ‘Order in the court room, order,’ Harris demanded once again. ‘That’ll be all, your honour,’ Lois said and sat down. ‘Your honour, the state pulls out of this case,’ Jones stood up and pleaded with Judge Harris for withdrawal, he wasn’t going to make a fool of himself. ‘Jury members may leave to discuss a verdict, court will resume when the jury have reached a verdict,’ Judge Harris ordered, rising from his seat he strutted down toward his cabin and shut the door. Pierce watched the jury members leave, the court room began to recede, each person leaving for the time allocated, in hopes of getting to the lavatory and in the case of the media, he knew the journalists would all be trying to call their Editors in hopes of being the first to get the big story – LAPD COPS RUN BY DRUG LORDS. As the court room empties, one man seated alone in the back of the court room stands up, the glimmer of a Rolex shining against the last of the sunshine as he pulls out his cell phone. The media converge on the marble steps, waiting for Ramsfield to emerge and to bombard him with questions and claims. The man moves forward, his body shifting through the large crowds. ‘Yes, we have a problem…’ he says and disappears into the shadows. His eyes remaining fixed on one man and one woman.
