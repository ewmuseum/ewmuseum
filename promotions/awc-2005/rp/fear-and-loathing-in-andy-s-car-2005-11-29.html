Fear and Loathing in Andy's Car

29 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Today We begin outside a gas station on a surprisingly bright and sunny day, with not a cloud in sight to mar the picture-perfect blue sky. The only thing to break the barren wasteland which surrounded the dusty road piercing through it was the gas station. It was a pretty run down place; paint was flaking from the pumps, the windows were covered with a caked-on coating of grime which suggested they hadn’t been cleaned in a long time, and the roof built to shield travellers from the sun whilst filling up their cars was full of holes. One thing was instantly recognisable about this place; the vehicle to end all vehicles. Faster than a Ferrari, more eye-catching than a Porsche, cooler than the Batmobile… oh yes, it’s the Andymobile! Andy’s legendary form of transport – a multi-coloured Volkswagen hippy van with the words “Andy Rules!” spray painted on the side in white – sat gleaming in the sunshine, unattended at the moment. Andy soon emerged from the gas station, the sun reflecting against the gel in his jet black hair. Today he was wearing a black Avail t-shirt, a pair of well-worn black jeans and a pair of black Aviator shades, protecting his eyes from the sunlight. Andy looked as tremendously awesome as ever, grinning cheesily from ear to ear, as per usual. “Come along now, my old chipatties! We have no time to waste!” Andy announced, glancing briefly back into the shop. “We must beat the Evil Baron of Doom to the drop zone, otherwise there will be hell to pay!” Following Andy closely was his posse; his brother Cayle, butler Alvaro, homeboy Grobschnit, and his prized pet Bolivian fire-breathing badger, Snowball, who was being carried in a travel case, usually meant for a cat, by Grobschnit. Cayle, who was sipping on a can of Pepsi, was wearing a white vest and a pair of beige cargo pants, along with a dark red coloured bandanna. “Andy, what the fuck are you talking about this time?” he enquired between sips of the soft drink. Andy shrugged, “I don’t know, man, just trying to brighten up an otherwise dull journey a little.” Alvaro, the Portuguese butler not seen in a while, was, as usual, decked out in a perfect tuxedo. His black hair was slick back over his tanned head, and his moustache was as immaculately trimmed as ever. Grobschnit, meanwhile, was dressed like a homey from the hood, fo’ sho’. He wore one a baseball cap at a ridiculous angle over the top of a bandana, and his white t-shirt was so baggy it almost reached his knees – and don’t even get me started on the damn jeans. “’Ey Andy, how long ees dees escapade going to take us?” asked Alvaro in his twangy Portuguese accent. “I can’t get no sleep in dee back of dee van with dees green theeng…” “Hay fagit! Joo beta schuttz upp b4 izz kik u r azz!” snapped Grobschnit, clearly not amused at being referred to as a “green theeng,” whatever that may be. “Settle down chaps,” Andy began, defusing the situation, “I’m sure it wont be long now; I’m gonna unleash the fury on this mother!” as Andy finished another typically nonsensical babble, he pulled open the driver’s door of the Andymobile, and slipped inside. His crew were soon to follow; Cayle in the front passenger’s seat, Alvaro and Grobschnit in the back, Snowball’s carry case nestled between man and beast. As his passengers belted up, Andy rather carelessly planted his finger down on the acceleration pedal, and sped out onto the dusty road without a care; narrowly avoiding hitting an oncoming care – the other driver responded with a good, long burst of his horn. “Jeesh, what’s that sucker’s problem! YEEEHAAW!” Andy yelled almost maniacally, as he increased the speed at which the Andymobile was travelling. Cayle, somewhat riled by the near-miss moments ago, spoke up; “JESUS! Andy, are you trying to kill us or something!?!” “Of course not you crazy bastard! Lets roll!” “Wurd lyfe, dis iz crazee Andyphonics,” piped up Grobschnit. It didn’t take long at all for Cayle to finish off his can of Coke, and when he had done so, he casually tossed the can out of the Andymobile’s window. “That’s one for the rats, heh,” he muttered. “Hey Andy, you could have made this sucker a little more comfortable… I mean, I’ve only been sitting here for a couple of hours, and already I’ve lost all feeling below my waist…” However, Cayle’s words fell on deaf ears, as Andy had all attention focused on the road ahead; or specifically, something he saw at the side of the road ahead. Squinting through the sunlight, Andy thought he could make out the sight of a figure in the distance, growing larger and larger as the Andymobile approached it. “’Ey Andy, what are you loo-“ Alvaro piped up, noticing Andy’s squinting. However, Andy raised a finger to his lips signally for Alvaro to shut up. As the figure before them became more apparent, it was clear to all men in the car that yes, what they saw before them was a dude, and that he was carrying a suitcase. Cayle immediately scowled at this, recognising that type of person that stood before them… “Shit no, a fucking hitch-hiker!” he yelped. “A hitch-hiker! Quality! Lets scare the shit out of the fucker!” Andy proposed with a grin, the figure was no more than fifty yards from them now. The hitch-hiker, sure enough, turned around and stuck out a thumb, just in time for Andy to pull up beside him. “Oh great…” Cayle quietly muttered, with a roll of the eyes, as Andy rolled down his window, and began to converse with the hitch-hiker. “You looking for a lift, boy… hmmmm?” Andy enquired, glancing at the guy over the top of his shades. The guy standing before him certainly wasn’t all that pretty; tall, thin and with long, blond, already receding hair. His teeth were yellow and crooked, and his glasses were at least a centimetre thick. He wore a plain white t-shirt that was dirtied a little bit by the desert dust, and a pair of tattered blue jeans. The nice collection of dust on the back of his suitcase also suggested that it had been too much for him to carry, and at one point at least, he had started to drag it along the ground. “Oh boy! Am I glad you showed up!” the hitch-hiker yelped in a voice that could, quite clearly, shatter glass. “I’ve been trying to hitch a lift all day, where are you guys headed?” Andy recoiled a little, somewhat less keen to give this guy a lift now. Quickly he searched the back of his mind, trying to think of something that would drive the guy away; “Fuckin’ Liverpool,” he replied. “Shit,” Andy thought to himself, “that wasn’t very scary…” “Oh cool! Well, I can get a bus from there, I guess!” ”What the heck? Doesn’t he know that Liverpool is in England!?!” Andy thought. ”Better think of other ways to drive this boy away…” he figured, before beginning; “I must warn you, kid, you’ve gotta be a very, very brave man to roll with the Scottish King of Cool and his posse. You see, we live life on the edge… don’t think about the consequences of our actions, and all that. The journey will be perilous, and we shall face numerous, life-threatening battles along the way… besides, can you handle the awesomeness of these bad boys?” Andy asked, pointed towards Alvaro, Snowball, and Grobschnit. “We’ve got Alvaro, the craziest foreign motherfucker in the world! He knows at least 68 different forms of kung-fu, or something along those lines…” Alvaro shook his head, “What dee hell are you on about, boss?” he enquired, only to be ignored by Andy. “We’ve also got Grobschnit, perhaps you know him? He’s currently number 1 on the FBI’s most wanted list… and man, I’m not even gonna tell you why – you wouldn’t be able to sleep for weeks!” The hitch-hiker glanced at da ‘Schnit, who tipped his hat, and said “Wurd bitsh.” “And finally, Snowball, who despite her cute name and unassuming demeanour, is quite the badass. Say hello to the nice man, Snowball…” Snowball responded with a tiny jet of flame, which passed through Alvaro’s opened window. Andy nodded with satisfaction, before removing his shades, slipping them into his pocket, and raising an eyebrow. “So, boy, the question is, are you man enough to roll with the S.K.C., hmmmm?” After a few moments of not-so-careful consideration, the hitch-hiker grinned; “Sure! Let’s do this!” Andy immediately thought ”Shit, didn’t work. Oh well, gotta give him a lift now…” Andy leaned over to face his butler in the back seats. “Alvaro, budge up, sit with Snowball on your lap. It might be a little cramped, but whatever you do, make sure that motherfucker doesn’t start a BBQ up in here, alright?” As if to question Andy’s logic in giving this guy a lift, Alvaro, none the less, did what was asked of him, and budged up. Pulling his small case behind him, the hitch-hiker plonked his arse down on the seat, and buckled up. Alvaro, a man of class and taste, turned his nose up immediately; especially at the stench of the kid, kind of like a cross between chicken shit and dried sweat. Grobschnit too was less than impressed, and kept his eyes fixated on the hitch-hiking, growling. “ALRIGHT MOTHERFUCKERS! LETS GO, GO, GO! MWAHAHAHA!” Andy cackled, in attempt to terrify his new passenger. Much like he did at the gas station, Andy launched the Andymobile forward at an incredible pace, almost causing the hitch-hiker to throw up. The presence of the new passenger seemed to have almost entirely killed all conversation between the group, none of whom were impressed by Andy’s decision. The hitch-hiker, now a little shaken up, noticed Grobschnit sitting, staring, snarling, and now foaming at the mouth. He gingerly raised a hand up, and waved… “Uh, hi there… how are you doing?” asked the hitch-hiker. “Fux u, fagit!” Grobschnit sneered in response, before resuming the whole “big scary monster” routine. “I tell you what, boy, you got some major cojones coming on a ride in the Andymobile!” Andy announced. “We’re gonna hunt us down some hillbillies, yehaaw! And once we’ve done that, we’ll cook ‘em on a spit-rost and gobble those fuckers up! Cannibalism, baby, it’s the future!” Suddenly, as Andy ceased speaking, the car’s engine began to splutter; ironically enough, considering Andy’s talk of death. A puzzled look came across Andy’s face, Cayle too looked slightly surprised. ”What the hell?” Andy questioned, ”I just put gas in this bitch.” Andy thumped the top of the dashboard in frustration, even though he knew fine that it wouldn’t make any sort of a difference whatsoever. The engine kept spluttering; the needles on the dashboard by now were going crazy. Slowly, the car began to lose speed. “We can’t stop here! This is bat country!” Andy wailed. “What the fuck are you talking about?” asked Cayle. “I don’t know man, I just don’t know…” Eventually the Andymobile drew to a complete halt on the desolate highway, a thin trickle of smoke rising up from underneath the bonnet. Andy punched the steering wheel, as Cayle sighed in frustration. “NO! MY BEAUTIFUL CAR!” Andy shouted, before looking over his shoulder. However, before he could open his mouth to hell at the hitch-hiker, the guy had already bolted out of his seat and out of the car. Andy shrugged, “Oh well, at least that’s one problem dealt with…” “Oh yea, but how about this problem we’ve got right here! We’re freaking stuck…” Cayle panicked, annoyed that his brother didn’t really seem to realise what had just happened. “Oh shit, yea, I didn’t think about that...” “Ah fook, dees ees not good…” Alvaro pointed out, stating what was already blatantly obvious. He undid his seatbelt and slid back across to where he was sitting before; he didn’t really care for being cooped up that close to Grobschnit at the best of times. Even Snowball looked concerned – the Bolivian badger was pawing at it’s cage, letting off tiny little flame blasts. Andy instinctively delved back into his pocket, this time pulling out his mobile phone. However, a quick glance at the screen confirmed his fears; no reception this far out in the desert. The Scottish King of Cool sighed with exasperation, “Well, looks like we’ll have to play the waiting game… we’re never going to get any phone signal out here. Don’t worry though, sheep loving brotha’s, I’ve brought Scrabble!” It took somewhat less than Andy had expected for someone to show up; around about 45 minutes to his estimation. Indeed, with Snowball still occupying the backseats, Andy, Cayle, Grobber and Alvaro stood at the side of the road, halfway through a game of Scrabble – internationally renowned game of old people and cardigan-wearers everywhere – on a ridiculously rickety looking travel table that Andy always carried with him in the Andymobile. Some people bring a first aid kid, maybe a spare tire… Andy? A travel table and a game of Scrabble. That’s just how he rolls, baby. A police highway patrol cruiser pulled up behind the van, although it wasn’t until the officer slammed his door shut behind him that the M. posse noticed. He was quite a short man, and not especially well-built. He also looked to be middle-aged and his face sported a thick, but well-groomed, sandy brown moustache. Wearing a beige police uniform and a black tie, the officer’s badge glimmered in the sunlight, and he had a notebook in his hand, ready to jot any details down. “Afternoon, sirs, having a spot of bother?” he asked in a thick, Southern accent, stepping up to the group. “Oh, thank goodness!” Andy said, clearly relieved. Either that or he was just keen to leave the game of Scrabble that his illiterate alien homeboy had been beating him so badly in. “Can you help us out, officer; we sort of broke down, and stuff.” “So I see,” muttered the highway patrolman. “Interesting vehicle you’ve got there,” he stated, glancing at the Technicolor delight that is the Andymobile. “Thanks, I painted it myself…” “See, thing is, I met a boy a couple of miles back running from his life. Said some guys in a ridiculously coloured hippy van attempting to kill him… you wouldn’t happen to know anything about that, would you sir?” the cop asked suspiciously. “What?!” Andy’s jaw dropped. “Oh no, you’ve got it all wrong…” Andy began, his bored friends continuing the game of Scrabble without him, “We gave that crazy sucker a lift, and I swear, he pulled a damn SUPER SOAKER on me! I was terrified, so I stopped the car… he quickly legged it, and when I tried to drive away, the damn thing wouldn’t start… watch out for that guy, he’s not the type of person you want on the loose, man…” The officer raised an eyebrow. “Super Soaker, you say?” “Word. Crazy bastard…” “I see, I see…” the policeman said as he slid his notebook into his pocket. He didn’t know who was the bigger psycho; Andy, or the kid he had just come across in the middle of the desert. He figured he’d throw any suspicion out the window… “Lets get a mechanic out here then…” Thank Jebus for that.
