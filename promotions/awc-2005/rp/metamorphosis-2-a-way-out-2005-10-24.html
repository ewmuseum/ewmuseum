Metamorphosis #2 - A Way Out

24 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 24th October, 2005 July 14th, 2005 Los Angeles, California Location: Unknown *** Knucks, an organisation which prided itself on its tradition of ruthless killings and inexplicable disappearances of key Los Angeles personalities, had become a thorn in my side since my capture by them not too long ago. It was a little over three and a half months, in fact, since I had felt content. Each day I felt an underlying feeling of utter resignation; was there any way out of this prison I found myself in? My personal mental and physical torture continued like clockwork without fail every waking moment. Sometimes I had the pleasure of being treated to a half decent dinner. Mashed potatoes and beans was usually the best on the menu, but I had no say in what I was fed. I was a basic animal, deprived of all but my own thoughts and feelings. My only company was myself, sadly. There was the occasional Knucks staff member visiting me, informing me of the latest trends in the organisation’s plans as if I had an interest in such things. In response, I would look down at the steel-topped table before me, almost in a sleep-like state. The staff were less than impressed, but it pleased me to score a psychological win over them. I shivered as my room became cold, much like the staff member who at that instant walked in my small, rectangular room. He stared at me with a deadpan look in his green eyes. It hit me what was happening at that instant. The mission I had been assigned was about to begin. Thoughts, doubts rushed into my unstable mind. The man signalled for two security guards to enter to remove my cuffs. They roughly did so, and I almost had the inclination to head butt them both, but I opted not to. I no longer had the mental strength to combat men in such a manner. If Knucks had succeeded in anything in my time here, it was the destruction of my spirit. The men pushed me up the hallway, and I could feel the blood flow returning to my legs after a prolonged period of sitting down. I disliked being idle for such long periods, but Knucks had a plan, and they never failed to execute it. A blindfold was placed over my eyes, and all went dark. It was not dissimilar to being in my room during the day - nothing but darkness and silence. The men mumbled to each other, though I could not make out what was being said. Suddenly I was shoved onto a hard surface. I assumed I was in a vehicle of some sort when I heard a loud bang. I backed away from the door I had entered through and found a corner and something to hold onto. The car started abruptly, and my “mission” had begun. *** Although I had no idea whatsoever where I was being taken, or what exactly this mission at hand was, I had a feeling it would not be pleasant in the least. The vehicle would periodically bump up and down, creating an uncomfortable sick feeling in my gut. The air was thin in the back of this vehicle, and I opted to stay put rather than attempt to find a way out, a task which would most likely prove futile. Finally, the journey ended, although I almost wished it had gone on a bit longer. I had no desire to do any missions for anyone; not if they involved what I thought they would. The doors flew open with a crash. The two men who had tossed me in removed me from the vehicle and I was roughly wrestled onto the ground, which was cold concrete. The blindfold was removed, and my eyes adjusted to the conditions around me. I appeared to be in a back alley of some street, most likely in Los Angeles. The security guards, before I could make any move, attached a tracker to my ankle. It would keep tabs on my position, presumably. I couldn’t help but gulp as my mission was delivered to me verbally: “You’re here, punk,” the brash security guard blurted, “So now your little mission begins. Bet you can’t wait, huh?” The sarcasm wasn’t appreciated, and I simply chuckled at him. “You ought to be shakin’ in your boots,” the second, less aesthetically pleasing guard said, “After this, there’s no turning back from Knucks. You’ll be one of us. People always try to resist, you know, but it has never worked once they’re come up against this challenge.” This time there was no chuckle from me. Not even a smile. Instead, an imaginary fist punched me squarely in the stomach, winding me momentarily after the horrifying prediction I had just heard. Was there really no way back after this mission? What could it possibly involve that would rip one’s own will away from them? “Cut the crap,” I said defiantly, “Just spit it out. What do I have to do?” The two grinned devilishly at each other, as if they had heard the same question asked a million times before, each time savouring their chance to answer. In a well-practised move, they folded their arms and their deep-set eyes stared blankly at me. “You see that little apartment over there? You gotta go over there and kill the young woman who lives there. She’s been meddling with Knucks, trying to find us out, for the past few years. She’s gotta die!” The guard finished his sentence almost as if it was a pleasurable and rewarding thing to end someone’s life. Certainly, I was absolutely opposed to such a mission, but what other choice did I have? I didn’t want to return to that awful room again. The darkness played on my mind every day as it was. Anymore time in there would surely destroy what was left of my sanity. I decided I would go into the apartment, but would not kill the woman. Then again, wouldn’t the guards have expected that? In short, I was screwed. A decision had to be made. *** I crept slowly around the back of the apartment, just adjacent to the back door. I was bathed in darkness at the bottom of the house’s tiny back yard. Thankfully, there were no animals to prevent my entry. As I moved towards the door, I had an overriding sense a guilt coursing through my veins. This wasn’t me. This wasn’t Tony Aliso. There was a clatter of something from next door, and I listened intently as a man cursed his cat which purred at him softly as he berated its behaviour. That was life in L.A., though. The motto was to stand up for yourself. Everyone else came second. I continued my path up the tiny garden and finally reached the back door. Made of wood, it resembled many of the doors I had been accustomed to knocking on back in my L.A.P.D. days. This time was vastly different, though. I pondered my position. I couldn’t just make a run for it and hope the tracker device would malfunction, could I? It was a dead cert risk, but I maintained my composure and with a deep breath decided to try and remove the tracker device. First of all, I needed something to knock it off with. A hit on the device would require considerable force to even make it budge, such was its tightness around my ankle. Then I spotted the garden shovel which had been left by the owner of the apartment. It lay there as if it was meant to be there just for me at this moment. My journey towards the shovel was interrupted by a light being switched on in the kitchen of the house, which conveniently overlooked the garden. I shot down to the grass and covered my head with haste. Then I saw her in the window, filling up a glass of water, most likely to take to bed with her. A dark-complexioned woman with short coffee-brown hair, she dazzled me with her beauty despite my compromising position. If I had had even the slightest inclination to enter the house before now, that had all disappeared. The Aliso policy was never to harm a woman. The security guards were still waiting for me a few doors down from the apartment, I assumed. This mission, as they called it, apparently had to go ahead. I saw no point in wasting time, so when she had switched the light off, I made my way towards the shovel. I wedged the top of the shovel between my lower leg and the tiny space between it and the device. I applied some serious pressure, but it refused to break. I cursed silently to myself as I realised I had no other option but to break and enter. To complete the mission… “Looking awfully frightened, kid,” said a gruff, manly voice from behind me. Instantly turning, I found the voice to be coming from a tall man of approximately six foot. Dressed in a smart suit, the man looked ready to attend a prom. His eyes were his most attractive feature, glimmering like brown gems under the pale moonlight’s glow. “Don’t be afraid,” he said, assuredly, “It’s going to be alright.” “Wh - what’s going to be alright?” I retorted, though I could not mask the fear in my quivering voice. “The name’s Peter Robinson,” the man said, smiling, “And who might you be?” Taken aback by the sudden appearance of this man, I couldn’t speak for a moment. I took in everything around me, desperately hoping the woman wouldn’t return to the kitchen. I shook the man’s hand like a gentleman and he accepted it. “I’m Tony…” I replied, forgetting to add my last name. “Tony?” He trailed off, obviously expecting a last name. “Aliso.” He reacted with a mixture of shock and excitement. “You must be kidding me! You’re the real Tony Aliso? Wow, I’ve been waiting to meet you for a long time. I’m a big fan of your work. Oh, one thing… do you mind if I ask you what that steel thing on your ankle is? It’s slightly unsettling.” Robinson’s enquiry was concerning my tracker device belonging to Knucks. I prayed that I could get out of this place without harm, and that Robinson could help me to this end. I was clutching at straws with this hope, but I got lucky with the next thing Robinson said. “You look a little ravaged, kid. You wanna come with me? Come on, I’ll find you somewhere to stay.” Risk or no risk, I decided to place my trust in this stranger. I had only known him for less than two minutes, true, but he was a world better than any mission or return to mindless punishment with Knucks. I nodded to Robinson, and he placed his arm around my shoulder as we headed around the side entrance of the apartment to the front. The security guards’ vehicle was no longer there. Curious, I felt like investigating, but opted to ask questions later in this instance.
