Summer's Son #2: 'Leaving Today'

15 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15th August 2005 30th July 10:13 AWC’s European tour came at a good time. Despite my need to delay departure by one week, so missing an edition of Fresh!, to go to Core Wrestling PRIDE and settle things with Matthew Lanier (not quite the conclusion I had hoped, as it turned out; I was just as angry with the noncommittal ways of that uncaring bastard as I ever had been), now really was a good time. Ever since my chance meeting with Pierce Lavelle had propelled me back into wrestling I’d been kicking my heels at Harbrook. My summer project lay untouched for over two weeks; the key question ‘Is music by Coldplay aimed at those with lower-than-average intelligence quotients?’ was gathering dust in the back of my mind as more immediate, inspiring and stimulating thoughts took its place. Like “When do I get to use The Eternal Hope again?”. Like “When will Pierce and I get to follow up our victory over Emerald Isle in another Duo Tag match?”. My mind now jumped straight from one Fresh! to the next, leaving no room in between for life outside wrestling – life as I had known it. Which really hadn’t been that good. When you’re spending your summer vacation doing a project which might or might not gain you unneeded extra credit, and still living in your crummy college room to do so, you know something’s gone wrong. Harbrook couldn’t have been better about letting me stay, to be honest. They’d let me have the room I’d occupied this entire year, and told me that I could keep it for next year too, if I liked. They’d done all they could to keep everything running as usual for me – obviously, catering was out of the question during the vacation, but they’d given me access to a properly-equipped kitchen close to my block, far larger than the claustrophobic pantry provided for all students during term-time that I’d never actually bothered to use – and actually learning to cook and cook well had been a valuable use of my otherwise free time. After a couple of weeks of lonely nothingness, I’d made the startling realisation that Chris Martin, the lead singer of one of my favourite bands, Coldplay, never used a word of more than one syllable if he could help it. And so, my summer project was born, and I made enthusiastic haste in gathering a list of bands with a sufficient number of songs to examine and analyse. What better way to spend your time than picking apart songs that you love and realising that you have to be a good deal more intelligent to gather any meaning from even such mainstream UK rock as Muse (Ignorance pulls, apostasy and apathy still rules) than the minor-chord-dominated epic power of Coldplay? It really was quite interesting, and filled my empty hours with something I could really get my teeth into. And then wrestling happened. Now, I could all too quickly consign the latest statistical test to be applied to a cross-section of The Coral’s works to the “tomorrow, if I can be bothered” pile. I realised I would far rather analyse the subtle differences between the wrestling styles of The Educator and Alexander Strider than count once more the total number of three-syllable words on a Snow Patrol album. As many people say, most things are fit for one purpose, and when a product tries to embrace too many aspects of life, that’s where it fails. I agreed with this hypothesis: the point of having a mobile phone that could create, transmit and access pornography wirelessly had, strangely, eluded me. And music was the same. Music is for listening to: keep it for that. Still, though, I couldn’t bring myself to fully execute the right-click:delete sequence on the folder entitled Summer Project on my hard disk. Someday, I might look back and want to pick up where I left off... someday. But not now. Not today. Because today I was leaving. I fired my hi-fi into life and lay back on my narrow bed with my hands behind my head, finally satisfied. I hadn’t managed to pack everything, of course, but I had all I needed: my laptop, my iPod, clothes and toiletries... really, what else could a man want? I’m leaving today Too much time’s already wasted I know I can’t stay So I’ve made my reservations I couldn’t deny that my sudden aversion to academic work depressed me rather a lot. I had envisioned handing a completed project to the lecturer with whom I shared a special bond, Sir Richard Blakey, on the first day of the new term and seeing the beaming smile on his face as I told him what I’d spent my summer doing. Statistics wasn’t his favourite mathematical discipline, but I knew that he’d make time to pore over any original work of mine, desperate to find something worthy of publication and help me onto that first rung of the ladder of academia. I’m closing the door To this old empty house of sorrows They’re only sorrows until tomorrow I’m leaving today I’m leaving today So it was with a heavy heart that I decided to take no college-related books or equipment with me to Europe. I could do with a break, I told myself, to focus my mind and restore my faith in learning, and its means and its purposes. I only hoped that, come September 19th, I would want to commit myself to the new Harbrook term. There would be a couple of weeks of splitting my efforts, like I had been in Core – my AWC contract lasted exactly three months, ending on September 30th – but after that it’d be back to school, back to lectures, back to hours of work on complicated equations with no real solutions... and no real rewards. Dare I say... back to the grind... no, I daren’t. Things I’ll never know All the words I wanted to say Ghost at the window And things will never be the same I sat up suddenly. I hadn’t even told my parents I was going to Europe... I’m closing the door To this old empty house of sorrows They’re only sorrows until tomorrow I’m leaving today I’m leaving to – I switched off The Coral. I grabbed the phone. I punched in the numbers. I swallowed. “Hello?” I heard a voice... a tinny little voice... no more than a whisper. My spirits took a dive as I realised my father must have taken another bad turn, to reduce my mother to this scared little girl once more. I was going to feel very, very guilty...
