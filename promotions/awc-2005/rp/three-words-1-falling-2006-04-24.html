Three Words [1] - Falling

24 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 24, 2006 Before we dive into this story, there are a few bases that need to be covered. When it comes to the business of wrestling, Cedric Riley was not young at that time of his life; being 28 years old among a flock of superstars in their early twenties, he knew his experience and maturity should be one of his strengths, but they hadn’t shown a significant difference in his performance thus far. He didn’t ignore the truth, though, as he was also experiencing similar circumstances in his life outside of wrestling. When a career becomes your job, this kind of realization calls for a change. So, when it is realized that Cedric has intentions of marriage with Natalie, it shouldn’t come as a surprise. But it is, simply because we haven’t quite learned that side of this ex-agent. We’ve painted a good picture for him, and it involves fighting crime, wrestling, and dealing with national affairs. The fact that he is also a father is usually pushed aside. It is a little on the juvenile side for us to ignore the fact that he is living in the real world. Sometimes people get confused between the wrestling business and real life, and can’t find a decisive medium. He had found it simply by being enveloped by love, as clichéd as that sounds; it’s the ultimate truth. Cedric and Natalie were in San Francisco, in a hotel near the pier, and both of them were half-dressed in formal attire, rushing to elegantly dishevel their hair and decorate themselves in accessories. Natalie saw it fit for a “celebration” on behalf of Cedric’s acceptance into the Zero 2 Hero tournament that would send him to Manchester in less than a week, so the couple was bound for dinner on a cruise ship that warm, spring evening. It was unfortunate that they were pressed for time, and even more so for Cedric, who was struggling to put on a tie. He always had difficulty with those damn things, but nevertheless stood in front of the mirror in a very concentrative manner. He wanted to wear it because he liked it; the blue material matched nicely with his shirt. The bottom line is that his fingers just did not have the skill and finesse to weave the cloth in the proper manner to create the desired end result. On the verge of giving up his cause, Cedric heard the sound of the blow dryer through the small opening of the door to the bathroom. He looked down at his failed attempt to successfully fasten his tie, then over to the corner of the room where his open suitcase sat, tempting him to dig through its contents and find the zipper tie he had conveniently brought with him. This was the opportune moment; it was now or never. With swift efficiency he pulled the tie off his neck and opted for the zip-tie in his suitcase, which was around his neck effortlessly in less than a minute. Checking himself in the mirror, he decided that if the tie was convincing enough for him, it would be for Natalie as well. He grabbed his Swiss Navy watch from the dresser and strapped it onto his wrist, impressed with the ten dollar piece that he had bought nearly three years ago and lasted this long. He doused himself with a touch of cologne as a final touch just as Natalie came storming out of the washroom in a keen search for her purse. Cedric looked at her and a small smile flickered across his face. He couldn’t help but be incredibly grateful, more so lucky, for finding such a perfect woman to be the love of his life. Her beauty, grace, sophistication, and astuteness all contributed to create this image of a flawless woman that he never could have expected being with even in the most creative corners of his imagination. She had this dreamy effect on him - an uncanny ability to change his mood with the snap of her fingers. Cedric had never claimed to be a romantic guru, but ever since he met Natalie had had decided that if he was ever to fall in love in the midst of his unpredictable life, it would be with her. Over the past two years that had spawned into a remarkable relationship that involved a child, but the thought of marriage had never butted into their everyday conversation. Perhaps through all the trials and tribulations of the agency among other problems prevented this, Cedric didn’t know, but he knew that the time would be soon. At one time, the only thing that stopped him from proposing to her was the fact that she was only twenty-three years old. He had gotten past that since, and realized that her age made her attributes seem even more remarkable. She was mature beyond her years and maybe even Cedric’s, and he knew that well; she was the only one that kept him rational. Cedric had always been cautious of the three magical words that sometimes bond people for a lifetime or destroy them all together, but if he had been waiting for the opportune moment, then tonight was the night. But he didn’t think he had been waiting for the right moment, more so the right time. There’s a fine line between the two, but nonetheless, he was intent on making the gesture tonight; the cruise ship would provide a romantic setting if that’s what he wanted. “Ced, we’re going to be late!” She said. Cedric looked at his watch, which read five minutes ‘till 8:00, at which time their reservations were for. “Sorry, I couldn’t help but be distracted by that dress you’re wearing. You look beautiful,” Cedric complimented, placing his hand gently on her side. She caught his eye with an innocent smile, but Cedric knew her patience was limited; they didn’t have time to delay their departure even longer. He grabbed his wallet and keys off the dresser and opened the door for Natalie. Cedric didn’t claim to be a skilled driver, but he was good enough to dodge traffic and get to the dock in a hair more than five minutes. He parked the Audi in the parking lot and breathed a sigh of relief at the sight of the ship still loading passengers. They hustled out of the vehicle and to the ramp that bridged the boat and the dock. A man that looked to be in his mid-thirties stood there in a suit and bowtie, a gentle smile on his face that pleasantly observed the late passengers. “Running a little late, are we?” He joked to Natalie and Cedric, who hustled up the ramp to board the ship. “Not too late, I hope?” Cedric asked optimistically, apparently too distracted by their current situation to notice the man’s humor. “Of course not,” the usher said, surprised. “Names, please?” “A table for two – Cedric Riley and Natalie Garner,” Natalie responded politely, fixing her hair in a fashion as to suggest she thought it somehow became messy on the trek there. Her significant other noticed this and smiled at how cute this looked, to him, at the very least. The usher looked through his book, but frowned afterwards. Cedric noticed. “What’s wrong? Are we not on there? I brought my receipt …” he said, beginning to look through his jacket pockets. “No, the reservations are here, but we’ll have to put you at a table for four. If, of course, that’s tolerable,” he explained. “Well—” Cedric began, keeping in mind his intentions for the night. “It’s fine,” Natalie said plainly. The usher smiled. “Right this way.” Cedric decided that arguing his case would be useless, for Natalie had no idea of his intentions. He hoped silently that they would be the only two at the table and followed obediently, anxiously feeling that if he was going to tell Natalie how he really felt, then it would be better if they were alone. The dining room of the immense vessel reminded Cedric of something that he seen in Titanic, but proportionally smaller. It was a very formal dinner, with tables set everywhere and filled with people that chattered anxiously while waiting for the ship to set course. A host led the couple to an open table for four, where they thanked him and took their seats. “Isn’t it nice to finally do something like this?” Natalie said cheerfully. “Yeah, I’ve needed an escape from reality for a while,” he replied, gazing through the tables. “It is true that we don’t get out enough. We should do this more often,” she suggested, reaching across the table and clutching his hand. They exchanged glances and smiles as if they both felt different than the rich people that surrounded them. “Maybe we can get away soon. We could go on a vacation … somewhere,” he said, careful not to suggest a honeymoon. Not yet, anyway. Natalie laughed. “I don’t know, hun. I’ve been away from Seth for too long now, I hope that Mother has taken good care of him.” “I’m sure she has, so don’t worry about it. Enjoy yourself for once.” Natalie shot him a bemused look. “You should probably listen to your own advice, might learn something.” “Let’s save that for a later day,” Cedric opted as a waitress conveniently showed up, offering them wine and a menu. They accepted both. Cedric flipped the menu open and skimmed through the contents, furrowing his brow. Natalie looked at him curiously, noticing the confused look on his face. “Let me guess, you don’t know how to read French.” He looked up at her. “Of course I do,” he lied. She laughed. “Wait … you’re telling me that you were in the agency for over a year and you don’t know French?” “I know some, okay?” He explained, trying to make out some of the words on the menu. “… Enough to read a menu?” He gave up and closed the menu. “Why did we come to a French restaurant anyway?” “Cedric, I toldyou they would be serving French food,” she reminded. “Well, good thing I forgot, or you would’ve had to drag me here. Sorry to state the obvious, but who in their right mind would want to eat snails?” “You do know I’m half-French, right?” She asked wonderingly, as if she felt insulted. “Yes, sweetheart. I’m sorry.” “Don’t be. After your meal tonight, I’ll have you pleading me to make ‘snails’ every night,” she predicted confidently and continued to read through the menu. Cedric smiled at her and took a sip of the wine. He didn’t like it, but thought that it was the stylish thing to do in this setting. He couldn’t help but fidget with his spoon, trying to imagine what saying “I love you” would be like. He was still uncertain as to when he would do it, and hoped the time would present itself. Things were headed in a good direction so far – the light conversation, the passionate mood. The orchestral music that came from the corner of the area felt perfect for a gesture like the one he planned. He felt happy with the circumstances, something that relieved him as opposed to nearly missing the launch, which gave him anxiety. Yes, everything was going just fine. Cedric made an impulse decision and took Natalie by her hand as she put the menu down, about to say something. The words spoke themselves. “Nat, there’s something that I’ve wanted to say to you for a very long time now. Something I’ve waiting to say for months … ever since I met you … but I haven’t gotten the chance.” Natalie looked at him intently. “What is it?” “I need you to know that I …” A shouting man beside their table interrupted him abruptly. He turned around and muttered, “What now.” A couple was arguing with a waiter. The waiter was trying to exercise patience. “We’re very sorry, sir, but the lobby is already occupied. We’re going to have to seat you here.” “Ridiculous!” The man was saying. “I pay good money to get first-class dining and end up here!” “Sir, I assure you that the service down here is nearly as good.” “I imagine it is, but I’m not paying for nearlyas good of service, am I? No!” The woman butted in quietly. “Lenard, honey, just sit down. You’re making a scene.” Cedric tried to ignore them, and turned to Natalie. “So, as I was saying …” he began loudly, trying to drown out the voices behind them. The woman involved in the argument suddenly motioned towards their table. “Lenard, it’s Natalie and Charles!” Cedric was the first one to snap his head towards them upon hearing them address him as Charles, the name he had recently abandoned. Natalie did the same. “Bloody hell,” said the man named Lenard. He turned to the waiter and said, “We’ll be just grand sitting at this table hear, now why don’t you take our order?” Cedric shot Natalie a look as if to say who the hell are these people? Natalie couldn’t respond as she was greeting the woman. “Kathy … how wonderful to see you!” She greeted a hint of surprise and indecision in her voice. Cedric knew that voice was an indication that she probably didn’t want to be joined by this couple. The names Lenard and Kathy together were familiar to him. Cedric dug into his memory and recalled a dinner at Natalie’s parents house in England a long time ago where he had met them. He hadn’t seen them since, and didn’t remember what their connection was to Natalie’s family. He guiltily didn’t want them to have dinner with them simply because of his intentions to tell Natalie how he felt. He wanted alone time with Natalie, and apparently fate was preventing him from getting it. “It’s so nice to see you two again,” said Kathy, taking a seat beside Natalie. “What have you two been doing? I’m sure you still have marvelous careers at that … what was it?” “The Virginian Financial Advisors,” Cedric reminded her. He had that name permanently engraved in the front of his mind – it was the career that everyone got who didn’t need to know they worked at the agency. It was a requirement. “Yes, of course. So, how is that going?” Natalie shrugged. “Ever since we’ve had the baby, we’ve moved on. Cedric is now actually a--” “A sports agent,” Cedric finished, then took a sip of wine. Natalie gave him a confused look. “It’s a very good business right now.” “Bloody hell, that’d be the life, wouldn’t it?” Lenard finally entered the conversation. “Following sports for a living!” “Yeah, I guess you could say that,” Cedric admitted, sticking to his false story. He could almost feel the glare coming from Natalie without looking at her – he found himself denying being in the wrestling business simply out of habit; he was familiar with doing it all the time in the CIA, but he felt like he needed to anyway. “And on a side note, there’s something I need to say.” He paused for the effect, as the table was silent, letting everyone know that he was serious, then looked at Natalie and continued, “For so long, I’ve wanted to tell you the truth. I’ve wanted to look at you and just straight out tell you how I feel.” Kathy gasped. “He’s gay!” Cedric dropped his head in dismay. “No, Mrs., I’m not.” “Well, just spit it out already. Everyone’s listening.” Cedric looked around anxiously and noticed that everyone had turned their attention to the table following Kathy’s assumption, and simply went ahead with confidence. “Fine, I don’t care anymore. I don’t care if the whole world hears me. Natalie Garner, I love…” Abruptly the doors to the dining hall slammed open loudly and everybody turned their attention to six men dressed from head to toe in black who fired two gunshots into the air. The head of the group had a man who looked like the Captain in a headlock and threw him relentlessly to the floor, following with an evil laugh while firing another two shots into the air. The Masked Man looked at the frightened crowd menacingly. “Ladies and gentleman … boys and girls … this is a hold up. This ship is under our command, and you’re all held captive - by us. Everyone here, consider yourself a hostage. Congratulations to everyone.” The entire dining hall filled with cries of shock, panic, and terror. Cedric could only sit in his chair. He put his hands into his face and muttered to himself, “Of course.”
