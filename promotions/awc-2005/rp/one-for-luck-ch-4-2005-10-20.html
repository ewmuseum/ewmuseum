One for Luck, Ch. 4

20 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 20th October One for Luck | Chapter 4 (Prometheus Serum: Section 2) 'All deception in a course of life is indeed nothing else but a lie reduced to practice, and falsehood passing from words into things.' -Robert Southey City of Angels The plane came to a screeching hault amongst the tarmac of the Los Angeles International airport. The atmosphere was one of calmness and happiness. Each and every passenger dying to get up out of their seats and into the scorching sun shine; for whatever reason. I took a deep breath and waited as the plane taxied into its lot. Kelli had gripped my hand extremely tight as the plane came down toward the ground, and even though it had stopped, she still held on tight. 'Eh, Kelli!' I smiled. “Can I have my hand back?” I asked. She looked down at her hand on mine and quickly removed it. I pulled it up and cracked each knuckle. 'Sorry,' she said. 'I didn't mind the holding my hand part, just the lack of circulation,' I joked. The plane door slid open and we were greeted by a gush of fresh air. I unbuckled my seat belt and opened the upper compartment and pulled down Kelli’s bag and mine. The crowd of people rushed passed me, knocking me into Kelli. The two of us remained close together, struggling to remain upright. The crowd eventually passed and Kelli immediately jumped backward and away from me, took her bag and charged toward the door. I grabbed my own bag and ran after her. “HEY!” I yelled up the runway. Kelli continued walking, completely ignoring me. I struggled to hold my bag with my bandaged arm. The amount of people began to disperse and Kelli and I were now in a speedy dash along the conveyer belt toward luggage collection. I eventually caught up to her and took hold of her arm, softly and turned her to me. “Is everything okay?” I asked. “Yeah, fine,” she murmured and turned to the luggage bay door. I shrugged my shoulders and watched the long line of bags come out of the door; one by one they were collected. Kelli’s bag, however, wasn’t on the belt. I pulled mine onto my back, tightened my shoulder strap and followed Kelli to the lost and found section. She was in a severe panic; something of importance had been in her bag. “How can I help you?” a lady in lost and found said politely. Her head peeking over the large blue and white counter. “Yes, my bag wasn’t on the same flight as me,” Kelli said. She stood on her tip-toes to stare at the woman. I could hear the worry in her voice. “I can’t help you with that,” the lady said. “WHAT?” Kelli squealed. I stood in front of a now frustrated Kelli and an enraged desk clerk. “Perhaps you could take her bag number and when it arrives, deliver it to the hotel,” I said. “Yes, that would be possible,” the lady sat into her seat and took Kelli’s ticket. “When the bag arrives, I shall ask for it to be delivered to the hotel.” I moved Kelli away and thanked the desk clerk. “You don’t always have to help me out,” she muttered. “I can handle myself,” she said and stuck close to me. “I can see that, but I didn’t want two huge police men escorting us out of the airport,” I laughed. “I see your point,” she giggled and moved to the taxi depot. I placed my bag in the trunk, along with Kelli’s handbag. “Hilton Hotel, in Westwood, just off route 405, thanks,” I said and slid in next to Kelli, who was now gazing out the window, arms folded. I could tell she was in a defensive posture and didn’t inquire about anything. The car trip was one of delight, well for me. I got to see a side to LA I thought didn’t exist as we had to pass by Beverly Hills - my grandfathers home. Kelli laughed when I mentioned who my new grandmother was, she found it hilarious and it seemed to cheer her up, despite my bad history with accepting it. Before too long we had arrived at the Hilton and the taxi man unloaded our bags and the bus-boy loaded them onto a trolley and sent them to our room. “So, the Hilton, very expensive,” Kelli said. We walked up the marble steps and toward reception. “Well, the company I work for is paying for the room, so it’s good for me, bad for their bank balance,” I smiled and collected the key to our room. “Your wrestling company is paying for this trip?” Kelli questioned under a whisper. She was too busy staring at a couple, probably extremely rich, who were contemplating whether to buy some auctioned diamonds. I moved hurriedly passed the stand. “Don’t even ask!” I laughed and pressed the elevator button. “You said it would be they who’d suffer,” she said and hopped into the elevator. “You don’t like wrestlers, do you?” I said. The doors shut closed and Kelli and I were now in a small cube, travelling four floors up. “No!” she said. “I’ll change that,” I smiled and watched the doors open. I stepped out of the doors and was suddenly greeted by a shooting pain down the back of my head. “Pierce!” Kelli screamed. I stumbled forward and received another blow to the face, by a tall man, dressed in all black with a sleeveless shirt and a large dragon tattoo on his extremely muscular arms. I stumbled backward again and crashed into a breakfast tray. “Leave him alone,” Kelli yelled and hit the guy on the back of the head with her high heel. She was immediately struck to the face by the large mans elbow. I shot up, grabbed a serving tray and smashed him in the face. It had barely any effect. He grabbed my throat and shoved me against the wall, squeezing tight on my trachea. “You’re after GOONE!” He whispered in my ear. His breath heavily intoxicated with liquor, the smell pungent. “You won’t make it,” he murmured again and squeezed tighter. By this stage I was coughing and gasping for air. I lowered my knee and swiftly jammed it into his crotch area. He cringed and if you have ever seen a bulldogs face, smiling, it looked a little like that. He fell backwards and I speared him with all of my strength into the elevator and with the help of Kelli, left just as the doors closed on him. “Are you mad?” she questioned. “You could have done some serious damage,” she said. Her lip was bleeding. I stood up and felt a serious pain shoot from my damage collarbone down the arm. I had broken my sling. She noticed my facial expression. “Shit, you did hurt yourself.” “I’m fine,” I said and grabbed my shoulder. She opened the door and helped me in. The bags had been placed in the hall and I sat on the small chair. She ran steadily into the kitchen on one high heel and emerged seconds later with an ice pack. Sitting in between my legs she opened my shirt and placed the cool compress on my damaged collarbone. I shivered but ignored the cold. Some emotion in me was changing; Kelli looked a lot different in the last couple of days I had been with her. She was smiling, cheerful and without worry, well, sometimes. I placed my hand on her lip and for a few moments our eyes locked, she shot up and looked at her lip. “It’s cut!” I said, realising what she had assumed I was going to do. “Oh!” she squeaked and ran into the kitchen. Was I falling for Kelli Stoner? This was a question that I believed I now knew the answer to. I sighed and held the ice pack on my shoulder, staring at myself in the large wall to ceiling mirror. G-O-O-N-E? The scene opens in a large warehouse, somewhere in Los Angeles, location pretty much unknown. The large man comes charging in the doors, fuming. He walks passed a couple of men in white coats, tending to a white powder. Suddenly two large bay doors open and we are greeted by a huge factory inside the warehouse, with about forty to fifty men and woman in white suits, tending to various machines, with the product coming out in tablet form. The large man skims passed a trolley and pounds on a small door. “GOONE!” he yells over the sound of mechanics. The door opens and we are greeted by a man, dressed in a purple suit with a red shirt. His hair combed sleekly back, with a lot of gel. He removes his purple shades and stares at the man. “Calm down, Ranger,” he says calmly. His voice husky. “Calm down!” The man named Ranger steps forward. “Boss, the man, he managed to over power me.” The man nicknamed GOONE laughs. “That’s new,” he says. Ranger shakes his head, slightly embarrassed. “What now?” “Well, let’s wait and see, shall we. I have a delivery that needs to be made. Call Burg and Endo.” Ranger storms off passed the fumes and white suits. GOONE shuts his door, leaving a large echo reverberating around the room. Succumb to defiance My shoulder began to ease down in pain. I moved to the bedroom and removed my old shirt and placed on another one. Kelli had made up her own version of a sling out of a robe from a dressing gown. I placed it over my arm and found Kelli, leant against the doorway, glass of cola in her hand. “Where you off to?” she enquired. “I need to go and check something,” I said and grabbed my leather jacket. She stepped forward and looked at me. “You are not fit to go anywhere,” she claimed. “I’m fine, really,” I said and moved forward, but Kelli was blocking me. “Pierce, I am not staying here by myself,” she said and placed the glass of cola onto the bedside table. I slouched in posture, looked up at her and smiled. “Fine you can come, but any more cut lips and somebody will think you are being beaten.” “Deal,” she said and grabbed her denim jacket. “Good,” I laughed and looked through the eye-hole, just to make sure I didn’t have any unwelcome visitors. She stood behind me, her head peering over my shoulders. “Is anyone there?” she asked. “My lips still purple and he broke my expensive shoe!” she muttered. “He? Last I recall, you slammed your high-heel into his head,” I said concentrating on the eye-hole and the people passing by. “That was to help you!” she replied, defiant. “Okay, I’ll buy you some new high-heels,” I laughed and opened the door. “Pierce?” she said, still standing in the hotel room. “All clear,” I said - and to be honest, extremely relieved. “Great!” she said enthusiastically and shut the door behind her, handing me the key. “So, where we off to?” she asked and linked her arm around mine. “I need to see somebody; if you don’t mind coming for the ride?” I said and pushed the button for the elevator door. “Not at all,” she said. Once more the elevator engulphed us and we moved, quietly, silently, motionlessly down four flights, without breaking a single sweat. Once the door opened I jumped. Two men had their backs to the door, dressed in posh suits, they stood aside and I slid by, carefully. I had a sudden image of Romeo and Reno from back in Albany, sitting on the car in their posh clothes hurling a Molotov-cocktail at my room. I shivered a little and continued walking. “So, how are we supposed to get to this place of yours? Encase you haven’t noticed, we don’t have any wheels,” she said. She put her hands in her pockets and swayed on her heels. “Don’t want to break those!” I smiled. She looked down at her red high-heels and smiled. “Nah, these were cheap,” she stood back shocked at the car that pulled up next to us. It was a 2005 royal blue Lincoln Navigator. The tall valet clambered down from the height of the four-by-four and handed me the keys. “Here you are, Mr. Lavelle,” he said. He nodded his head and went back to work. Kelli stood dumbfounded. Mouth gaping and if it wasn’t for the cold season, I was sure that a fly would soon land and she’d choke. I beeped the doors open and climbed into the driver’s seat. “You coming?” I asked. “Eh-yeah-sure,” She climbed in and put on her seatbelt. “Wrestling?” “No, this would be my grandfathers apology present,” I smiled and turned on the engine and drove out of Westwood and onto route 405. “Ha!” she exclaimed. Mysteries unfolding I pulled off route 405 and into Culver City, driving quietly through until I reached my destination in Winston Hills. Adam Master's manager’s office was located in this address. The building was a two story building, it had a fire escape running up the side, two large dumpsters to the side and the main door read 'Managerial assistance and retail advice'. Kelli stopped the radio and looked through the jeep window. 'What is up with wrestlers?' She slumped into the seat and undid her turnbuckle. I turned off the engine and parked the car in the 24/7 across the street. The traffic was now in full swing with rush hour. I stood patiently at the curb, waiting for a clearing in the evasive traffic. Kelli stood arms folded gazing at the building. 'You don't have to come,' I yelled over the sound of horns. Before she could answer, I saw the clearing we needed, grabbed her arm and pelted across the large street and toward the office block. The door was still open, meaning the manager was still in. I slid it open and was immediately greeted by the smell of fresh carpet. I plodded up the stairs, with Kelli in hot-pursuit and knocked on the plain glass door. A movement was seen inside. The white writing soon became visible, 'James Lawler'. The door swung open and I was greeted by a man, probably mid thirties, dressed in a pair of black suit trousers, blue shirt and red tie. 'Yeah?' he said. 'It's Pierce Lavelle?' I said, quizzically. 'I know who you are,' he retorted. 'What do you want?' he asked and held open the door. 'You rang me in Harrisburg. You said Adam Masters had gone missing with related links to Prometheus,' I said - slight curiosity now tilted my brow. 'What? I-don't-recall that,' he sat into his office chair and spun to face us. 'I never called you,' he replied. 'I think you did,' I said. He sat forward, arms on the table, spreading around his opened laptop. 'No, I never called you,' he said, again. 'What?' I asked. 'Adam's not missing, just in rehabilitation at the moment,' he said. 'I see,' This situation had now dealt me a new card, only I didn't have the complete deck. I needed more information and by the looks of eight empty coffee cups and a sketchy manager, I wasn't going to get them here. 'Thanks for your time,' I said and left. Kelli followed quickly behind me. 'What's wrong,' she said. 'I was played,' I mumbled and found my way back to the car. Kelli had brought back two coffees and two doughnuts and sat in the passenger seat. I sat in the car, engine turned off, my eyes fixed on the top left window, James Lawler’s office. The light was now on, as night had floated over the cities skyliners. “Why are we still here?” she asked, blowing on her hot coffee. “Stake-out,” I replied. “Fun!” she said, sarcastically and sat back into the seat. We remained silent for the next couple of minutes, she looked at me every now and then, and inside I was doing a conga-line, she had a sort of defiant nature on her part, she reminded me a lot of Lois, but was in a way, far superior. Her personality was strong, her will, well, wilful. “Can you turn on the engine, it’s starting to freeze in here,” she said and sat up. I pulled off my leather jacket and placed it delicately over her shoulders. I smiled and was about to say something when I saw the shadow move in James Lawler’s office. I undid my seatbelt, opened the car and told her I would be back in a few minutes. I raced across the pause in traffic to the side of the building. Gazing at my splinted arm, I removed the make-shift bandage and stretched it, ignoring the excruciating pain that followed. I looked at the fire escape, pulled a beer box across and launched myself up onto the rung of the ladder, gripping onto the iron bars. I knew his window was open; it was just a matter of agility and balance. Crossing lines Kelli sat patiently in the car, twiddling her thumbs watching Pierce from a distance. Her heart pounding as he launched onto the fire escape. She felt the need to run out of the car and help him, but knew it was best to stay put. Her silence was broken by the unnecessary buzzing of her phone. She removed it from her jeans and sighed. “Hello!” she said, her facial expression dipping into sadness. “Hey, sweet lips,” the voice said into the phone. “What do you want, Adam?” she replied. “Information, Kelli. Don’t forget who you are working for,” he yelled. “Okay! Okay!” she said, dipping her head against the dash board. “Good, because you break this deal and Zach goes free,” he said. “Fine, what do you want to know?” she said. “Awe, you like him, don’t you!” he laughed, menacingly. “I-eh-just get on with it Adam,” she said, wiping what looked like a warm tear running down her cheek. “I see. Well, what have you found out, or should I say, what has Pierce found out?” Adam asked into the phone. “He knows somebody who wasn’t Adam Master’s manager rang him. He’s closing in quickly Adam, he’ll find out, you know he will,” she said. A smile across her face. “Don’t sound so pleased. What do you think would happen if I told him you were working for me?” he said. A tapping sound echoed through the phone. Kelli remained silent - she knew exactly what would happen, the very man that had been nice to her, would most likely leave her. “Now, listen to me. I want you to seduce him, play on his emotions. Do what needs to be done. I need to know everything …” the phone went dead. Kelli sat back and tears came flooding down her cheeks. She wiped them away and wound down the window. Mysteries Unfolding: Continued I had climbed the first few rungs of the fire escape and remained just underneath the window sill of Lawler’s office. The door slammed shut and I peered my head over the sill, the room was empty, desolate and deserted. I stood on the sill and hoisted up the window, my momentum soon came unbalanced and I crashed into the room, hitting my head on the soft furnishing of the carpet. I lay still for a second, hoping I hadn’t been heard. After a couple of moments, the coast seemed clear and I stood up. The first thing I noticed was his laptop had been left on his email account. One email struck me: “GOONE”. It was the same name that had been read to me, by the stalker. I clicked the email and read the contents. “HANCOOK PARK. 1:00 AM.” That was all the email read. I then realised that Lawler had gone there, the clock read 12:35 pm. A sound woke me from my sleep and just as quickly as I had entered, I climbed back out of the window and down the fire escape once more. My arm was now in a lot of pain, but I ignored it, to the best I could and ran back across the street. I opened the door and Kelli jumped. Her eyes were a bloodshot red. “You alright?” I asked. She had been crying. “Yeah …” she said and turned to put on her seatbelt. “You’ve been crying,” I said. “No, just a reaction to the coffee burning my lip. I’m fine.” I turned the car into first and pulled onto the main road and drove down toward Korea Town. Hancook Park I pulled out of Korea Town and onto route 10. I had filled Kelli in on what I had seen, but she remained stern with what she wanted. I knew there was no telling her otherwise. I parked a couple of minutes away from Hancook Park and got out. “Eh, we’re walking?” she asked. “Yeah, I don’t want to draw attention. Something is going down,” I said. I made a stealthy walk into Hancook Park and dragged Kelli behind me. “Pier-” Kelli said, but didn’t get to finish her sentence. I heard voices in the distance and pulled Kelli down with me behind a couple of bushes. I peered through a gap in the leaves. Rain was beginning to pour down and Kelli lifted my leather jacket over her head. One man was stood in the middle of a basketball court, hands in pockets. The voices I had heard, turned out to be four very large men, all dressed from head to toe in suits. They approached him. “Did you come alone?” one of the voices asked. “Yeah,” the other voice quivered. “Good,” a deep voice replied. “I told you to keep an eye on possible targets,” another voice said. “I - did - but it’s …” The man quivered again. “Its what?” a voice said strongly and came in between one of the large men. Kelli kept behind me, the bushes acted as a perfect shelter. “It’s … hard …” the voice replied. “The young men could die from the drug,” he said again. “Die! Oh, I see, Burg … deal with him,” the husky voice demanded. Within seconds, the shinny end of gun appeared beneath the spot light. Rain was pouring down and blurring my vision, but the unmistakeable sound of a gun shot crackling was enough to put me on high alert. I dipped Kelli lower and watched the man fall to the ground, lying in a puddle and gasping. The man with the gun finished him off, one shot to the head. I moved slightly and took a closer look at the man on the ground, it was James Lawler. “Did you hear that?” one of the men said. Shit, I thought to myself as I cracked a twig. Kelli’s eyes told the full truth; we had been discovered. I jumped up into gear, grabbed Kelli and ran. Gun shots fired at us, but missed. I rushed out of the park, with the men in hot pursuit. “Catch them you idiots …” a voice yelled in the distance. The rain was now pelting down; making it hard for Kelli to grip the road. I pulled her into a dark alley; two large dumpsters stood next to one another. I pushed her in first and followed, standing face to face with her. Her back against the wall and my front to hers. Rain could reach us, but there was enough shadow to shelter us from the men. Footsteps ran by, but the echoing of sirens soon dispersed the search and the men ran off, scared of being caught for first degree murder. I remained against Kelli, my breathing synchronising to hers. She looked at me, relief in her face. The crackling of thunder roared through the sky. I moved back and checked the alley. It seemed like they had run away. I pulled Kelli by the hand and led her back to the car. We had arrived safely and gasped with relief. My heart was pounding and my skin had come over with Goosebumps from the rain and cold weather. Kelli slid into the car and sighed. I sat in next to her and put the engine on. “What now?” she asked. “We are getting out of here,” I said and screeched out of Hancook Park and back onto route 10 toward our hotel. Evading Moonlight I stepped into the doorway of the hotel room, and left the light off. Rain dripped off my head and onto the carpet. I was soaked from head-to-toe. The only light source was the moons evading glow against the droplets of rain, trickling down the window, it created a sort of ripple effect over the bed covers. Kelli came up behind me and shut the door. Tears running down both cheeks. Her hair had turned black with the rain, her clothing soaked and her heels ruined. My leather jacket had been scratched and ruined from the dodging into the alley. I turned to face Kelli and removed my bandage from my arm; all of a sudden Kelli embraced me and rested her head on my chest, tears pouring down both cheeks. She could hardly breathe with hysterics. “Kelli, it’s okay. We’re safe,” I said and held her. From having a little sister, I knew how to listen to the women species; when they cried, they needed reassurance. When they wanted a hug, they wanted to be held, so I remained silent, and embraced her. Kelli’s tears added more moisture to my already soaked shirt, my body was now freezing and I could feel Kelli shivering. “Kelli, you should have a shower, dry up. Everything will be okay,” I let go gently, but Kelli’s eyes locked with mine and for a split moment I saw the pureness in her. She leant up and kissed me. I pulled back, unsure whether it was right, but she pulled me down into an even deeper kiss and I accepted it. I placed my arms around her and pulled her in. I was letting her lead me, partly because I was unsure whether it was right for me to be with Kelli, in her state. She removed my jacket that was perched on her shoulders, all the while remaining in a tight lip lock. I continued to hold her and kiss her, something in me was eager to let go of the nice guy attitude. Before I knew what was happening my shirt was off and my bruised shoulder was glowing against the moonlight. Kelli stared at me, her blue eyes casting a glow over my chest. She ran a finger down my stomach and traced the line of my jean rim. My mind was racing, my emotions bouncing from side to side in my mind. Was this right? She touched me again, barely grazing my hand and I knew the answer… I removed her jacket and pulled her into a kiss, each kiss led us closer and closer to the edge of the bed. I had finally removed her blouse. She stood shivering against me, her bra completely soaked. Kelli hugged me and struggled removing my belt, I turned her around and lay her gently on the bed and climbed on top, remaining in a kissing embrace. I removed her bra and remained close to her. Kelli looked at me and removed my belt; I did the same for her belt buckle. The storm had begun to brew as thunder flashed across the sky, creating a bright light over the room, a light that soon faded. The light startled me but I felt no fear. I knew now in that instance that this was right. She quivered underneath as if she just felt the same thing, connected on every level imaginable. Kelli had turned me over, bringing the bed cover with her, she removed my jeans and I was left, flat on my back in my boxers, I had managed to remove her ¾ length trousers and ran my hand up her back, the Goosebumps lining her spine. She leant down and kissed me heavily this time. “Kelli?” I whispered. “Ssshhh-” she whispered and came in closer. My heart was pounding a mile a minute. I was falling for Kelli and each touch made me realise the truth … she lay opposite me and slid under the covers. I followed and lay next to her. A strand of loose hair danced over her face, I removed it delicately with my finger and ran it gently behind her ear and tracing her jaw line, to her chin and lip and kissed her, passionately. We lay in a tight embrace, together in a pool of light spilling in through the window. I looked into her eyes. Those piercing blue eyes were desperate, but something told me she was sure, almost pleading. I kissed her gently on the lips; never breaking eye contact then took her into my arms as the world seemed to darken all around us.
