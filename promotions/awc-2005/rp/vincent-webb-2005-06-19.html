Vincent Webb

19 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/19/05 Steven Xandrous As I sit alone in the darkness of my mind, I begin to glisten. Why, I do not know. I am not nervous, I am not fearful. I simply just am. The sweat drops off my brow as my thoughts deepen, as I attempt to contemplate what exactly I am aiming to accomplish in my blind madness. Why am I here? What have I done with my life, and why is it so important that I continue it? To make others suffer. My purpose is to assure that no others around me achieve theirs. My purpose is one that I am not particularly proud or certain about. I do not understand why I must do what I do, but it runs in my blood. My bloodline, yet another topic that others do not really find all that interesting, but I find not only interesting, but also confusing. My bloodline has manipulated me into who I am. I am a cold-hearted being. I do not know how I became what I am, but I am. My father was a sick son-of-a-bitch. Sadly, I have no recollection or memory of him because I lost all of that the day that someone did his or her biddings upon my body and mind. My mother was a blessed soul, however, doing all she could to protect me from that man. She did all she could do to make sure that he never got his demented hands on me, and for that, I owe her. I owe her my life because she risked hers. She risked and she lost. For that, I owe her. As my thoughts crumple and dispose, I am warped into the realism of my life. I look around, seeing dark streets buzzing with swarms of people. Men, women, children, teenagers, all of different races and nationalities. I stand up, realizing that I am sitting on a dirty and weakened bench. I wipe my bottom off, cleaning myself of dirt. As I look to my right, I see a telephone booth. The phone begins to ring, and I walk towards it. I step inside and answer the phone, not knowing how these actions came forth. “Hello?” I say gravely. “Steven? This is Steven Xandrous, correct?” the other end, a male, says. “Yes, you are correct. Who is this, might I ask?” I inquire. How can people assume I know who they are? “This is Vincent Webb. I know you have no idea who I am or how I ‘magically’ got a hold of you, but just bare with me.” Who the hell is this person?! “For the second part, let’s just say that I happen to have friends in high places… just like your father used to. Before you killed him, of course.” “Who the hell is this?” I ask, anger rising in my voice. “Calm down, son. You are with family. I’m your uncle, Steven.” Holy shit… he was not lying when he said that. “Of course, on your mother’s side. I don’t consider your father too much of your family.” “How did you--” I begin to say, before my “uncle” cuts me off. “Know? As I have said, Steven, I have friends in very high places. The man that arrested you, the cop, is a great friend of mine. Why do you think you got off without a murder trial?” Lucky me… “I also have a great sum of money, which I think you’ll be glad to know that you will be inheriting when I pass on.” “What makes you think I even want your money? And how the hell can I know if you’re really my uncle?” I say, skeptical. “Look, if you would like me to prove it, turn around and go into the building. The door is directly behind you,” the man says. “Steven, if you want to know what you’ve been through, you should come.” “What are you talking about? I know everything about myself,” I said, lying. Maybe he will catch the hint and fuck off. “Steven, have you not been listening? I know you have amnesia, my son. I’m surprised you even know your name yet,” the man on the other line says, chuckling in the deep voice of his. “You think that it’s funny? Why don’t you go to hell?” I say loudly into the phone. I slam the receiver down hard. People around me turn to look at me, some angry and some that find it humorous. I ignore those beings, and I turn around. I look at the building, examining its every detail. The man wasn’t lying; the door he mentioned is directly in front of me. Who the hell is that guy, and what does he want with me? Do I really have anything to offer to him? Is it a trap? Ignore it, I decide. I’m going to forget that the call had ever happened, and I’m just going to go to that piece of shit hotel that I call a home. Vincent Webb I hang the phone up, wondering what had just happened. Why does this kid refuse to accept the truth of the deal, that his life is a smoldering piece of shit? I could help this kid, you know. I could make him rich, so he doesn’t need to wrestle in that little wrestling organization he’s in. I hear a large slam. My door has been shut, meaning that someone has something personal to tell me. I spin my chair around to see one of my bodyguards, Hewitt, standing a few feet away from me, standing in perfect stance and dressed in perfect attire. “Sir,” he says, “we’ve located where mister Xandrous currently lives.” “And?” I ask. “He lives in an apartment building on the other side of town. Quiet a small place, might I add,” he says, a smirk on his face. What an ass… “I want five men down there in five minutes. He’ll be there shortly afterwards. Leave on my orders. I have a parcel for you to deliver for me…” I say before shooing Hewitt off. As he leaves, I turn around to my desk and pull out a piece of paper and pen… Steven Xandrous As I near my “home”, I begin to notice something… different. There are a few more cars around, and they are not the shitty ones like usually. They are nice, beautiful actually, black, spit-clean. The car I’d love to have… Actually, I’d go for a car period. It’s not as though I have enough to afford one anyways. Besides, Lord knows if I can drive a car anyways. I get about fifteen or so meters from my apartment when I notice a man inside of one of the black cars. I only saw a glimpse, but I know that it was his head sticking out the window, staring and watching me. I check my watch, seeing that the time is only about six thirty. Way early for residents to consider a gang fight. I begin to walk towards the vehicle, and as I do, three of the doors pop open, and three men step out. All of them are wearing the same clothes: a black three-piece suit with a ‘V’ stitched near the heart. Vincent Webb… What a cocky little bastard. “You got a problem, boy?” one of the men yell. “Yeah I got a problem. Some jackass is claiming to be my father and is having little jackasses tail me,” I say, letting my ego roll. I step forward, as does the vocal man. As we become chest-to-chest range, I stare him dead in the eyes, my hatred running vividly. “You know what I find funny, you piece of shit?” “What’s that, youngster?” the man says, grinning. I laugh to myself, knowing the man doesn’t expect a thing. “Apparently Vincent hasn’t told you much about me.” I bring my hand, which was curled into a fist, and connect it right in the jaw of the man. The man grabs his jaw in pain and then comes back with punches of his own. All of which were easily avoided. You’d think a man claiming to be my uncle would send better ammunition. I hit him in the gut with another punch and as he leans forward, he meets my knee to his nose. He stumbles back, his hand on his nose. He holds it there briefly and then pulls it down, a trickle of blood running from his nose. He charges at me at full force, throwing wild punches, obviously off focus. The punches come slower and slower as his adrenaline slows, and as he throws another punch, I duck and go behind him and thrust kick down hard on the back of his knee, forcing him to crumple onto the ground. As he is on one knee, I bring the heel of my foot upward and connect with the side of his head, centimeters from his temple. The man falls over motionless and the other men quickly back off. They put their hands in the air in retreat, seemingly afraid of me. I nod, laughing inside my mind. What punks… I leave the battleground and walk towards my hotel. I take the elevator, too exhausted to use the stairs. As the elevator reaches my level, I step out; hurriedly walk to my room number. I reach in my pocket for a key and open the door. I instantly see something on my eating table. I walk towards it, and I see a note lying on top of a box. I pick up the note, and begin to silently read it. Dear Steven, I know that you don’t believe me when I say this, but this is your uncle. I want you to come and meet with me, alright? No pressure at all. This is really for your sake, my son. Make sure to open the box. If you want to call me, just hit redial on your phone. PS: I guess I will be needing a new bodyguard, huh? Below the note, in perfect calligraphy, is his name. I smile at the last sentence. He knows me after all… I set the note aside and open the box. Inside is numerous bundles of what seems to be one-hundred dollar bills, and setting on its side is a book. I look at it, confused, and open it. Pictures… Pictures, many of them, all in perfect aligned order and they all even have captions of what they are! This man was telling the truth… he IS my uncle… No, he cannot be. All the men in my family are nothing but cold hearted, murdering sons of bitches. Unless… I flip through the pages, astounded at what I am witnessing. There are pictures of my mother, my unfortunately biological father, and others in my family. I flip back to the start, this time to look at the pictures more carefully. The first one is of a child. Somehow, this child looks very familiar to me, as though I have seen him before. Have I? Maybe on the street? I look at the caption to see whom the picture is of, and it reads four words: “Steven – Age Four.” I fall back in my chair, astonished. How is that picture of me? How did this man get it if we are not really related? I flip through the pages quickly, wanting to see more. Something catches my eye, so I flip back a page. The eye catcher was a man. The man is quite large, might I add. Not in a masculine way, either. I look to the caption and I realized why the picture caught my eye. “Devon – Steven’s fourth birthday.” Stenciled in underneath, in the same handwriting as the note, reads “Your Father”. Then I realized something. That was the man in the vision. The woman in the photos was the same in the vision. The child in the photo was the same as in the vision. I was that child. That man was my father, and I murdered him. Astonished, I stare at the picture. My new life has been severed extremely, and the main link to my past has been shattered. Unless… No, this man MUST be lying. How can this be? If this Vincent Webb man truly is my uncle, how can he be so… normal? That man told me about my family. He said that all the men in my life were liars and murderers. But whom should I trust? A man with a gun, or a man who has just given at least ten thousand dollars and my entire life… back? I meticulously and slowly reach for the telephone, lying a few inches from the box, and in perfectly straight alignment. I push redial and but the phone to my ear. Vincent Webb I bring my feet up onto the top of my desk, relaxing, waiting for the call from Steven. He should be calling any moment now… As I wait, I pull open the drawer at the bottom of my desk and pull out a booklet perfectly identical to the one I had given to Steven. I flip it over, glancing at the pictures. Steven, I assume, must be bewildered at the sight. Little does he know that – The door of my office swings open quickly as two men carrying one man rush in. Hewitt and two other lesser guards, Alex and Martin. Hewitt and Alex set Martin against the wall as he clutches his stomach. At least it was not Hewitt himself… “Sir, Steven decimated Martin,” Hewitt says, as though it was a surprise. “Of course he did!” I say, almost laughing. “Didn’t I say this kid is extremely dangerous, and not only that, but this kid is fueled! He will crumple a brick wall with all that adrenaline pumping in him. Not only that, but the kid is on his way. Lead him to the basement, to the obstacle course. Make sure that all the lights are shined on one spot a few feet from the door and away from everything.” “What? What are you planning?” Hewitt says. I simply grin. “To your NEPHEW?” I laugh. How could he possibly know? Sure enough, the phone rings. I quickly answer it. I have been waiting for this… Steven Xandrous “Steven?” says the other line. “Damn right. Now what the hell do you want?” I say, trying to hide my belief in his identity. “Steven, I want to meet you. I need to meet you,” he says. My jaw drops literally to the floor and my eyes widen. I swallow the saliva brewing on my tongue. “What makes you think I believe you, Vincent?” I say, spitting out his name bitterly. “Come on, Steven. I know that you believe me. Just look at those pictures, son, and you will realize that I am who I say I am. That is assuming you’ve seen the pictures?” he says. He is probably grinning to himself… “Look,” I say after engulfing in my thoughts, “I’ll meet with you, alright?” “Brilliant,” the other line says. “When would you like to do this?” “Thirty minutes. I am going to take a shower, and I will be right over. Tell your friend congrats, he made me sweat,” I say, chuckling. Vincent Webb The line ends, as Steven hangs up the phone. What a nice conversation… “Hewitt!” I say, turning around. Hewitt snaps to attention. “You have fifteen minutes. I want all of them, and I mean ALL of them, equipped with the best, or worst for him, ammunition possible. Got it?” Hewitt nods and runs off. What a kiss ass… He does not get that much pay. Steven Xandrous Ha, a shower. Like that piece of shit could make me sweat! I walk to the door, throwing my cheap-ass jacket on and throw two packets of hundreds in my pocket. I step outside and shut the door quietly, due to neighbors. Personally, I would slam that damn door shut. I run to the elevator and quickly hit the main floor level. I impatiently tread the floor, waiting for it to reach the floor. Finally, it does and I quickly jog out of the elevator and out of the building. I look to my left and to my right and I run to my right, dodging numerous people until I get to my destination. The destination that is Bill’s Pistol and Rifle Store. Who knows? --To Be Continued--
