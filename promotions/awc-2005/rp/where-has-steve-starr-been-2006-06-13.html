Where has Steve Starr been?

13 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06-13-06 Three short years ago, Steve Starr was on the verge of becoming just that. “Nike was calling me up with a huge multimillion dollar contract, and I was getting ready to become as big as Michael Jordan.” But life on the professional wrestling track just wasn’t cutting it for Steve. There was the hectic schedule that he had to comply with. There was the fact that he could never schedule a vacation due to always having to be on call. There was the fact that he couldn’t go anywhere in public due to constant pressure from fans. And oh yeah, he also had to deal with getting hit in the face with any imaginable objects. As a man who prides himself on being “in it for the money”, it’s hard to conceive that Steve Starr actually walked away from the Cash-Cow that was PRIME wrestling. PRIME was certainly picking up steam as a premiere federation, and Steve Starr was one of the bright young guns on the circuit. At just the ripe age of 25, Steve Starr had already claimed the federation’s Intense Championship, which by definition was awarded to a superstar who knew what it took to make the fans saw ‘wow’. Inside of the squared circle, Steve was no stranger to hitting the big moves that got the crowd talking. His repertoire includes a dazzling array of aerial maneuvers that normal men simply do not attempt. Steve calls it the will to thrill. “If everyone could do some of the things I do in the ring, then there wouldn’t be as much of a need to pay me. But since not everyone enjoys climbing a turnbuckle and throwing their body into potential danger, there will always be a place for Steve Starr to get a job.” The media labeled Steve Starr an idiot. He’d reached a new low after turning away from deals that would have him set for life. After all, Steve professed that money was the only reason that he ever wrestles. “When I decided to quit wrestling, my agent informed that I was making career suicide. He predicted that I would live in infamy with some of society’s biggest idiots” Steve recalls. What Steve did from 2003 until now enabled him to stay in tip top form. Those in the know remember Steve Starr as an all-state Point Guard on the basketball team back in his high-school days in Kansas City, Missouri. Quick as a cat, smooth as butter, Steve Starr had a very accomplished high-school basketball career. After receiving a scholarship to play basketball from the University of Missouri, Starr had his mind set on playing professional basketball. “I saw myself going up to Mizzou, perfecting my game and hoping to make the jump to the NBA by the end of my sophomore year. Never did I expect myself to not even make it to a game.” Due to what Steve labels a bad decision, he never even made it to the team’s opening game. He was kicked out of school when police executed a search warrant revealing an ounce of marijuana in his dorm room. Steve was subsequently arrested, kicked out of school and the team, and found himself at the last place he wanted to be. Home. “When I came back home, everyone was like damn Steve, I thought you was gonna be a Tiger.” Steve reflects, recalling his allegiance to the school’s sacred mascot. Obviously embarrassed by the situation, Steve used what he calls one of his best assets, his mouthpiece when answering questions on his schooling. “I told people everything from I was having a baby and I needed to be back home to support my new family to that I decided to enroll in the local community college to stay at home before transferring out. It didn’t take long for people to learn the truth, which happened to be that I was dealing drugs. You know the saying that everyone is broke in college? Well I was beyond it. Busting my ass for the basketball team while I could have been working a job like my other peers, I turned to the ways of the street.” Back home, Steve was given an ultimatum by his parents. He was either going to work, or go to school. Despite doing well in school throughout his life, Steve was in no hurry to go back to school. So he got a job. “My aunt was a long-time Post Master for the city before retiring 2 years ago, and she used her pull to get me a job. Yep, Steve Starr was a mailman.” Despite failing horribly at the job, Steve was introduced to local wrestling promoter Hank Collins. Hank represented small independent promotions as well as small independent wrestlers. He was known throughout the city for producing top-quality entertainment despite what many labeled a lack of talent. “Hank recognized me from my work on the court, and he said he was very fond of my athletic ability. He introduced me to the idea of actually turning professional wrestling into a career, which is something I’d never even thought of.” Steve’s experience as an acclaimed paid immediate dividends as he trained as a wrestler. He took the ring with great passion, and was very eager to learn. He wowed people with his natural athletic ability, and his knack for nailing the high-flying moves that got the masses talking. A local citizen recalls… “I saw Steve Starr at his very first event in May of 97. It was obvious that he was very green in the ring, but I was just so impressed by his natural ability. If he gets around the right people, he can make something of himself in this business.” Naturally the general praise throughout the community led Steve Starr to fully devote himself to becoming a professional wrestler. “Honestly I’d found a new reason to live.” Steve states glancing through a few of his early photos as a wrestler. “Getting dismissed from the university had to be the most humbling experience of my life. I’d been prepped for a steady career of basketball and before I could even realize a dream of playing college ball, it was snatched away.” Honestly what relevance does any of this have? For the last 3 years, Steve Starr has been in Italy. He hasn’t been involved with any of the mob bosses despite the tabloid press; he has been participating on the Mac Aciv Talib Italian League Basketball Team. The pay was no where near the likes of what he was bagging in PRIME, but it did allow him to rediscover himself. “I’m a risk-taker. It’s not uncommon to see Steve Starr take part in activities what most would label dangerous. Going over to Italy to play pro-ball allowed me to reconnect with a lost part of my life. The truth of the matter was this, I wasn’t ready to give up playing the sport I grew up loving.” Ideally Steve Starr would have loved to remain over in Italy. But then it happened. As owners throughout the league saw popularity decrease, the subsequent pay-cut followed. While Steve really wasn’t playing the game for the money, he obviously could have made much more wrestling. It was the love of the game that attracted him, but he certainly wasn’t going to continue to play if he had to do so, on a budget. “I’m Steve Starr, and this is my Universe! I’ll be damned if I let some Italians hustle me out of my cash. I may not speak Italian, but I know money. Those greasy bastards thought they were going to pull a fast one on the old Yankee. To hell with that, I got the hell out of there like that” Steve says as he snaps his finger for emphasis. Leaving Italy Steve says wasn’t as tough as he once expected it to be. His family and those close to him tried repeatedly to get him back to the States. “Steve come back and wrestle, they would say. Steve, are you seriously going to waste your time trying to live a boyhood dream. Steve, we need money Steve.” Steve Starr was never going to be one to be told what to do. Absolutely not. Only when he and he alone believed the time was right to return, then he’d return. Upon leaving Italy Steve didn’t plan on turning to wrestling right away. Then he got a call from his agent. “Big news Steve!” “Give it to me straight Jason,” Steve says. Jason Sharpe has continued to represent Steve Starr even though he has been inactive in the States. No one really cared about a former wrestler trying to make a career out of basketball. That would be almost as stupid as leaving a promotion after becoming the top dog because you want to play pro football. “Despite your recent absence from anything interesting Steve, I was able to convince a company that you should be their poster child.” Jason was truly a sleaze-ball. Steve watched Jason cut a lot of bad deals and do work under the table. Luckily for Jason he never tried any of that with Steve or else he would have been sucking food through a straw for a while. Interested, Steve rushed over to Jason’s house as he refused to reveal to Steve over the cell phone. Instead he preferred to show Steve. Upon arriving to Jason’s house, Steve was definitely disappointed. “You’ve got to be kidding me” Steve stresses. “Well Steve what do you expect you haven’t exactly been competing actively in a promotion for nearly 3 years. Did you really expect Nike to be knocking on the door again? No, maybe you thought Adidas would settle for you. Get real Steve.” It was a tough pill to swallow, but Jason was absolutely right. Steve Starr once was a recognizable figure in society, as he sometimes would dress in disguise to go out. Now he could walk down the street and be lucky for anyone to even remember who he is. He’d get the occasional greeting from a weird individual who looked as if all he did was enthrall himself with wrestling. Steve’s big deal turned out to be a creative marketing tool; it just really didn’t fly straight with Steve. Jason had negotiated a deal with Johnson and Meade, a company that made children’s toys. Not a very large company by any means, but you could find their products in your local Wal-Mart and such. The idea was for the company to sell those Glow and The Dark Stars that you could hang on your ceiling. So when you would turn off the light they’d give off a neon color. They hadn’t settled on a name, but they were really leaning toward… Drum Roll Please… Steve’s Starr’s. “Listen really Jason” Steve began. “The idea is very cute and all. But I’m Steve Starr, and I don’t want anyone considering me cute but women. Do you know what this could do to my reputation?” “Think about it Steve. Glow and the Dark Stars are cool! Kids love these; I have a set for my girls at home. And listen, the stars wouldn’t just be any old stars. They’d be personalized with your initials.” Whoopty-doo Steve thought. Whoopty-damn doo. So you could see the predicament Steve found himself in. It was either… get a job and try to assimilate into the regular culture, or turn back to the profession he left in the dirt. You can imagine the smile on Jason’s face when he found Steve in a gym, training. “Does this mean what I think it means?” Jason asked; face beaming with joy like a child on Christmas. While Jason would never admit it, without Steve he was nothing. He had no other high-profile clients. The only reason Steve associated with him was a favor to Steve’s cousin, who at one time was her fiancée. That went through the toilet, but Steve and Jason actually formed a bond when Steve’s career started to boom a few years back. “Yep” Steve said in a whisper for dramatic effect. Steve was actually in terrific shape, maybe even better since playing all of that basketball did wonders for his conditioning. He never was much of a physical specimen, but he wasn’t fat or anything like that either. Jason worked like a crack-fiend trying to score in his mission to get Steve Starr in another high profile promotion. Preferably, Steve would have liked to return to a place where he was most comfortable. The only two promotions he was ever contractually bound to were the North American Wrestling Federation(NAWF), and PRIME! Sadly NAWF had closed, and neither Starr nor Sharpe had the testicles to even make the call to PRIME. PRIME had evolved into something so huge, Starr was intimidated. He didn’t necessarily leave on bad terms, he just did it his way. The search wouldn’t take long as a suitor would present itself in the form of a club. “Atlantic Wrestling Club, what is this some type of joke?” an uninformed Steve touts with not just a hint of disrespect, he was being downright ignorant. “Steve it’s not a club, similar to the Boys and Girls Club you remodeled in your hometown. Club is just the name used to represent how competitive things are there. You have to be among the elite to even be considered, and well of course Steve Starr is better then average.” Better then average eh? Steve Starr wouldn’t have to wait very long to prove himself.
