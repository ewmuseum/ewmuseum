Trials and the Consequence - The Invitation

18 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 18/07/06 A simple fax was all that sat in the house, most likely from some connection Jessica had made during her frequent travels far and wide. What it told me and said to me were two different things. Of course. It read: ‘This Saturday come to the Alternate Lifestyle Choice fair in the Hammerstein Ballroom! Experience the opportunity to try something different!’ Of course. Well, if the current situation in Kingston wasn’t going so great, I guess the opportunity to find a hope somewhere else was the only thing I could do. Scrawled on the bottom of the fax was clearly Jess’ writing. Give them my name when you get there, I’ve already got you a booth. You need to sign in on the Friday. Well, guess I didn’t have a choice. I turned to look for some things to bring…and it all was ettled nicely on the desk behind me. She really does outdo herself sometimes…I don’t know what I’d do without her. There was a mixture of the promos that we’d made, my gym clothes, plus a nice little titbit hidden away. It told me there’d be a ring at the Ballroom. Makes sense when it’s the heart of ECW, it’s bound to have a ring nearby to use. I guess the only problem was if there’d be anyone else looking to use it. Well, I’d find out. It was Friday morning and I had to be there by six at the latest to have any shot of getting a spot. Best get going. *** 15th July, Hammerstein Ballroom - Registration A hubbub I believe is the accurate term to describe the Hammerstein right now, a hubbub suits it perfectly. The cornucopia of alternative hit you like a wave of lost souls, searching for the one thing that would bring them peace and tranquillity. I don’t quite know how I fit into all of this but I trust Jess’ instincts and I went with it. If your average, plain citizen would walk into this hall they’d be hard pressed not to feel like the isolated ones here. Minority is majority here and extreme doesn’t even cover half of it. Some of the people looked to be representing all of the minorities at once, wearing their associations proudly and prominently. Take for example a skinhead punk. In one go he could legitimately be: a tattooist, a piercer, a punk, a neo-nazi, a sleazy pornographer, the lead singer/bassist (he wasn’t good enough looking to be a guitarist) of a punk and/or ska band, or finally, an activist for the rights of animals or some such. You know the funny thing? I was still wrong. He was a professional snowboarder. Ah you win some you lose some. Regardless in all of this time in the queue to register (you didn’t think I was simply wandering around did you? Bah not nearly enough description to justify that), I was now finally next. What follows is the abridged dialogue between myself and the staff member who was oh so happy to be taking names for the event. ‘Name and lifestyle choice.’ ‘Jack Murphy, professional wrestler.’ ‘Anything potentially illegal or abusive you may have intentions on doing?’ ‘No?’ ‘Booth 23, between the suspensions and the tattooists from Zulu Tattoos.’ Seems pretty normal for a conversation. That is because I left out the curse words, frequent hacking cough from his predilection for smoking, liberal grabbing of his crotch followed by a sniff and the caressing of his nipples anytime a girl came within fifty feet of him. Make no mistake; they didn’t come any closer. And somewhere inside my head my conscience was saying “maybe Jessica didn’t think this one through so much…” but I let it pass for now. We’ll get back to that one I’m sure. The walk around the ballroom was pleasant though it’s definitely going to be just that little bit sweatier and heavier in the air tomorrow morning once everyone arrives and gets their feet wet. To do a little background, the ballroom is pre-war and shows off the elegance and class of that time. Sweeping balconies, grandiose twisting classical architecture and exuberant gold trimming give it that elegant time period which is so clashed with the wasteland of Eliot’s time period. But let’s not forget Prufrock’s dingy dirty town. The floors are faded, jaded and brown. No amount of cleaning and maintenance can stop the lingering plumes of shady under comings from the past. Much like myself in a way. I won’t forget that past, and neither will my detractors. Like Father Pearson. His tirade in front of the discussion group over a week ago was only the beginning of an assault on the fortifications. It was a skirmish if you will. With the opening salvo a success, the sounding of the call to all those around in the community was heard, a score rounding themselves up by the banner of the church. I had few friends in the community as it stood and it wasn’t going to get any better. The crowds outside the gym had become larger and louder, making it almost impossible for me to make it in there. I had to for the sake of face. I don’t go and it looks bad, really, really bad. There are other things surfacing but this would be enough to satisfy Fr. Pearson for the moment. He’ll have more to come I’m sure. In no time at all I found the cramped booth. Once I sat down behind the desk it became clear that space had been taken by the suspension guys and the tattooists. I couldn’t do anything about it though, plainly because both of them were there. ‘Alright mate, good to have you. What you promoting?’ What looked like the main tattooist had approached me from behind his desk and stuck out his carved up hand. I was pretty sure he was tanned, but with the amount of tattoos covering his body, I could be wrong. The twang of the accent belied he was from the Southern Hemisphere, but exactly where I wasn’t sure. Regardless he seemed nice enough and I responded to the outlying hand with my own and we shook firmly and smiling to each other. ‘Ah I’m a wrestler, hoping to nab a couple of recruits for the gym. You looking for apprentices too?’ ‘Nah, not me. Some of the others maybe but I’m here mostly just to maybe do a couple of tattoos and spread my work. If you’re up for it I can arrange one for yourself if you’d like?’ ‘Maybe, but I’m pretty covered as it stands.’ ‘Really? Can I see mate?’ ‘Sure, hold on.’ I dropped the bag I had with me and whipped off the top I had on. Such a wrestling thing, because put it this way, I wrestle basically naked for the majority of the time and I wander around backstage topless a lot too. It’s not like I’m ashamed or afraid of how my body looks. So, it all came naturally to me. ‘Nice…though I do have a question about the one on your shoulder; why?’ ‘I figured you’d notice that one somehow. To be honest…I don’t know. I can’t remember getting it or where, or when. It’s a total mystery to me. Why, does it remind you of anything?’ His face crinkled up and he frowned. Something was off and he saw that I knew it but he wasn’t about to tell me why. ‘I…it looks familiar but I couldn’t tell you from where. What I can tell you is that it’s biblical definitely.’ ‘Why do you say that?’ ‘Well, it’s got the inscription for whatever it is. I can’t make the entire thing out though. It’s warped and fuzzy.’ ‘Yeah…I know.’ ‘Well mate it’s interesting anyway. I’m going to head for my hotel and I’ll see you in the morning. You should go too.’ That’s probably true, it was getting late. I stayed to fix up the booth properly, and once that was done, I went to the hotel for rest and relaxation. I didn’t realise how much I’d need it. *** 16th July - Aftermath ‘I don’t want to talk about it Jessica.’ ‘Was it helpful.’ ‘Sure, sure.’ ‘Are you alright?’ ‘I’m fine. I’m going to bed, I’ll see you in the morning ok?’ ‘Ok. Oh there’s some post for you on the table.’ A trickle of a tear was swept away quickly before she could notice. I shuddered at the thought of what happened earlier. Not now I told myself, not now. I flicked through the letters and one distinctly plain one came to the forefront for a simple reason. It was from Father Pearson. I opened it and all that it held became clear. He wanted to meet, give me the opportunity to tell my side of the story. Now I know I have to cover up what happened today. It wasn’t even a question anymore.
