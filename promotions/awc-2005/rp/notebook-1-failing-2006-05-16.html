Notebook [1] - Failing

16 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 16, 2006 In this stage of Atlantic Wrestling that proves to be more good than bad, it is important to know that some careers flourish, while some spiral downward while the roster floods with competitive additions. I'm still unsure how I managed to find my way into a bit of a slump around the most important time of the year, but I try to convince myself that I just missed the target of fullfilling a dream. I can't fight destiny; it's the year of the Garbage Bag. But it wasn't for a lack of trying. But disposing of any more praise for our Z2H Champion, I'm sick of working my ass off and coming up with nothing. It seems like everytime I reach my peak of any career, others around me are also soaring to their highest potential, which is enough to slightly edge me out. Anton Assault proved this little theory to be correct by out-smarting and obviously out-wrestling me. It's been the same ending to every story my entire life, and I'm sick of making pathetic excuses. Why is recognition so hard to come by for you, you ask? I don't know, I ask myself the same question. Is there something I can be doing better? Am I a little rusty in the ring after two years of absence? More excuses. When a man was seconds away from swiping my life away from me like a handful of times before, he delivered something considering my wrestling career; sent a message that I've been waiting for ever since I took my try at the Atlantic Wrestling Club: that I spend too much effort struggling to cover up my past and deal with skeletons in my closet rather than focus on wrestling. And almost ironically, that was the only talk of Zero 2 Hero I experienced leading up to the event. In a state of panic, I defended myself and tooted my own horn, claiming that I had high hopes of success in the tournament. Had I have realized how truthful his statement was, I'm not sure what my reaction would have been. How do you agree with someone who's trying to kill you? You don't. You fight for your life and find any little flaw in his approach to prolong your survival that extra second to get the edge. That's my problem, right there - when it comes to wrestling matches, I'm not fighting for my life. I'm not simply trying to find a way to stay alive, I'm trying to succeed, or rather, should be. It's getting a grasp on the how-to methods that I'm finding difficult. The difference between wanting to accomplish something and succeeding in it is considerate, and bridging the gap is something I’ve been striving to do. I’m just not there yet. I try and claim that I’m waiting for the right opportunity, but that’s just lying through my teeth. Zero 2 Hero was on a silver platter, and what did I do with it? Throw it away into the garbage can that the man who won the tournament lives in. Now that, my friends, is something to be ashamed of.
