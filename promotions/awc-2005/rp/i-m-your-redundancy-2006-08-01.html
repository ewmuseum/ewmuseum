I'm Your Redundancy

1 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/8/06 Paddy on Holiday “Aye’m going t’ die today”. The hotel bathroom seemed to agree. With it’s curtains drawn together, only a hazy stream of light filtered through the fabric. These sort of things were always better in the dark. The walls nodded, indeed he was going to die today, and this would be his coffin; the same walls that were slowly closing in on him now. Squeezing the air from his lungs. It would be a fine way to die; pity it wouldn’t be quick enough for him. Patrick O’Shea picked up his razor blade. Applying it to his cheek he closed his eyes and slowly cut diagonally downwards. Although a temporary solice, the pain he felt as the blood meandered down his face would never compare to what was wedged into his brain. Everytime he thought of that infants body, riddled with gunshot wounds, a twang of excruciating pain rose up in his chest, threatening to burst right through the skin. And he had the abominable effrontery to feel sorry for himself. Christ, throw me into the fires of hell and let me burn in my pity. Flicking the blade from the razor, Patrick closed his eyes. This would make it easier he thought. The world would be a better place without him he also thought. I hope it hurts like all hell he thought once more. He had considered writing a note, maybe to ease the sleeping of the few people in his world that he cared about it. But what was the point? They would have questions but how could he give them answers. To people who watched him fight, he was the mild-mannered gypsy making a living for himself. But that was merely a gimmick. Ham it up he had been instructed, drive a caravan around the place, drink a lot and everything’s happy as Larry. But for God sake, never tell them about the war. What war they will say. And like the suicide note, you will have no answers to their questions. Nothing they can listen to anyway; people have an intriguing knack for creating their own stories in their head after they’ve heard only heard a shred of background. If they applied it to paper, everyone would be a best selling author. There would be no mention of his days as an innocent boy, then a vengeful boy, a rioter, a brigadier, a hitman, a Commander…there would only be the happy go lucky life of a gypsy and let’s everyone make daisy chains and fuck under the oak tree. So there would be no note. Let people keep that image of him. If nothing else, his passing would be made more bearable for his family if his secrets went down with him. A strange thought came to him then. He thought of a film he once seen where all the guy barked about was not looking at your image, but through your image and into yourself. Donny Darko, wasn’t it? And he’d gone up to crazy, paranoid, insomniac heaven in the end. Yeah, Patrick wanted to see into himself and this razor blade sure was going to help take down the walls. Patrick O’Shea, of County Galway, Ireland, raised the blade to the square of his jaw. Ear to ear he mused, then the gaping wound would be his eternal smile. Time to get that big old eraser out and rub out this smear of bravado that is his life. And then…finally, he could sleep. Brazenly, he pierced his skin, already contented at the thought of his rotten carcass becoming an ornament in this locked bathroom. This bathroom where the walls blocked out any inhibition from the what-comes-nexts. Au Revoir. Ring-ring…ring-ring Patrick merely paused as his cellphone buzzed on the toilet seat. His eyes still gripped to his image in the mirror. He hesitated then slowly lowered the blade. A call from God? Whoa there son, think about what your doing there! Well fuck you God, for giving me a gun in one hand and a conscience in the other. My god died along time ago… Yet, through his defiance, he knew he would answer this call. Ring-ring…ring-ring “Aye’m going t’ die today,” Patrick repeated then he broke his gaze and lifted his phone with a trembling hand. “Hello?” A smoke encrusted voice replied after it unmistakably exhaled over the line. “Hello Patrick.” The voice was instantly recognizable. It was perhaps the one voice in the world he didn’t want to hear right now. “Hello Q”. That wasn’t his real name, Patrick didn’t know that information, but he insisted on being called that. He had no personality; to Paddy, he was merely a voice with a letter for filing purposes. “I’ve been told our little operation was well handled. I want to thank you for your services. The child…forget about that. It wasn’t strictly necessary but nevertheless, it has no effect.” Patrick bit his lip. Forget about that. “Now…I knew you took that task into your hands when you were out of work and I also know you have recently been re-contracted to your former company. I wish you all the best in your field - I know how passionate you are about it. However, I would like you to know, we’d be more than pleased to retain your services. You pick and choose your jobs as we put them in front of you. There are no obligations, only the standard fee for each task and you work as much as you want.” He paused then- “Afterall, it’s not everyday we come across someone of your…talent for the vocation.” The next few seconds are something of a blur. To sum up, the phone became part of the wall, a piece of modern art if you will. Then Paddy, with a scream, began his own artistry as he sculpted himself into something more desirable. He slashed one wrist then the next and slumped down, allowing the blood to soak onto and through his jeans. A few minutes later he would be unconscious. Another few and his heart would stop beating. But thank Christ for busybody maids. I’m Your Redundancy
