Proving a Point

30 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/30/06 27 January 2006 I have always assumed that Western wrestlers were boorish and crude, but when I came back to my locker room in Lexington to find that Red Rock had defecated in my kit bag, every single one of my so-called 'stereotypical views' were assured. As if what he had said to me wasn't indignacious enough... Tariq Abdul Wahad Suleimon has never once played a video game in his entire life. Part of that has to do with the fact that his impoverished childhood in the slums of Istanbul precluded such luxuries. Of course, the militant Ottoman thing has a lot to do with it too. That's probably the reason he'd give. Some wrestlers, however, live and die by video games. Take Seymour Almasy, for example. Guy's crazy, absolutely crazy for RPGs. Mentioning things about the Royal City of Airyglyph, or Midgar's lower level, Lindblum Castle, and mentioning folks like Frog or Locke, Quistis, Magus, Quina and all those other queer characters. He's a guy that would make the Captain rip his own eyes out, what with the cosplaying and the frilly Japanese stuff... too homoerotic for his tastes. And when he saw the bio sheet for one Sebastian Archer, well, there wasn't a whole lot there. But there were a few things that stuck out... well not to Suleimon, because he doesn't play video games. But to his cousin and travelling partner Dogan, the one who got hooked on video games when he came stateside, well, a few things stuck out. 'Theme song: Kuja's Theme from Final Fantasy IX' 'Set up move: Limit Break' Suleimon was taking on Seymour Almasy-lite. Once Dogan told Suleimon all of that, well Suleimon naturally started to get a little arrogant. 'He should be an easy target. Flabby, weak, probably spends his waking hours pretending he's traipsing around mythical cities... he shall be made into an example.' It was almost like a talking point from his search and destroy mission. 'Geez,' said Dogan. 'You don't have to be captain militant all the time. Don't you know that these games are like the best simulations you can get for some situations. I mean, the US government uses Rainbow Six to train their mili...' 'And that's why they have a weak military. Their only power is in size and numbers. They are lucky that the Ottoman Empire no longer has the army to stand to them, or else they'd fall.' Indoctrinated much? 'Whatever you say man,' Dogan replied, with the sound of resignation in his voice. It's almost as if he's had this conversation with Suleimon time and time again. Or at least a conversation like it. 'I'm just saying, I don't think you should diss someone just because they like video games. Seriously, we don't even know that much about him. The report we got from Harber was barebones. For all we know, he could be one of those young lions who came up through the indies or something.' 'Bah, I shall hear no talk of overestimation for this feeble loser.' 'Y'know what...' Some life started to creep back into Dogan's voice. 'How about we have a little game, a mock swordfight... I'll get a kid who plays like Zelda or some other samurai game, and you get a kid who's athletic, has taken some fencing classes, but has never really thrown down in a real situation. Let's settle this once and for all.' Suleimon furrowed his brow and stroked the stubble on his chin. 'You're on. But like always, cousin, I am going to make you look foolish.' Some time later, both Suleimon and Dogan returned with a pre-teen of their choice. Dogan's pre-teen was a white male... Jimmy Spencer, the son of one of Dogan's friends in the business. Suleimon got his second cousin Jamal to step in. Both of the kids were given plastic broom handles, and were also instructed not to be too rough. The last thing either one of them needed was to have the kids' parents on them about teaching children violence. Suleimon got in the middle of them. 'ASSUME POSITIONS!' Jimmy held his broomstick with both hands, much in the same way that Link holds the Biggoron's Sword in Ocarina of Time. A big Zelda fan he was, even those Capcom games for GBA, the ones that didn't feel like Zelda games, but were part of the series nonetheless. Jamal took a fencer's stance, naturaly because Jamal had six years of fencing experience under his belt already. Suleimon looked in approval at the stances and then shouted 'EN GARDE~!' The two children charged at each other. Jamal lunged in with a fencing stab, but Jimmy swatted the broomstick away with wide, flailing Biggoron swing. In another fluid motion, Jimmy pulled his sword back and tapped Jamal in the left arm. 'BREAK!' Dogan yelled. The two children backed off from each other. 'So, what was that all about, cous?' If Dogan's 'I told you so' attitude was water, we'd be looking at the Pacific Ocean right now. 'Lucky break. Lucky, that's all. Positions again, lads.' The boys assumed their positions. Same positions for both. 'EN GARDE~!' This time, Jimmy took the offense first. Thrust, parry by Jamal. Jamal came with a thrust of his own, but Jimmy parried. With the deftness of a cat, Jamal snapped his broomstick down, looking for a sweep of Jimmy's legs. But Jimmy backflipped, much like Link does in the games, landed on his feet, and gaining his bearings, took his broomstick and smacked Jamal upside the head. Jamal dropped his broomstick and held his head. 'BREAK!' 'Once may be a fluke, cousin, but two times... I think I might be onto something here.' 'Silence! Two does not signify a trend!' Suleimon would not be proven wrong. His pride would not let his doctrines seem false. Even if being proven wrong would help him in the long run, he wouldn't allow himself to seem weak, even if it was to his cousin, someone who genuinely wanted to see him succeed in his mission, someone who didn't want his cousin to lose to someone in his first match in the company. 'Are you sure you want to keep doing this, cous? Shaheeda is going to be pissed if Jamal goes home with welts all over his body.' Suleimon's eyes started to boil over with rage. 'NO! He still has fight left in him. Don't you, Jamal?' Jamal nodded. Dogan sighed. 'Fine.' Suleimon back in the middle, and both of the pre-teens went back to their positions. 'EN GARDE~!' Jamal went on the offensive, swinging with an overhead chop that Jimmy immediately parried. Jimmy held the parry in place as Jamal didn't do anything to move his stick. THat's because Jamal had other ideas... PUNT~! Jimmy dropped his broomstick and fell to the ground, clutching his groin. Jamal went over and thwacked Jimmy while he was on the ground. He went for another shot before Dogan stepped in. 'What the hell!' he screamed. Dogan grabbed Jamal's broomstick. Suleimon just smiled and nodded. 'Jamal, you run home now. You ought to be ashamed of yourself.' Dogan turned to his cousin, who was smirking at him. 'And why are you so happy? That was a cheap shot! Are you teaching him that? That's bad sportsmanship!' Suleimon shook his head. 'No, that was doing what was necessary to win. Something you don't learn from a video game. I told you. Video games breed weakness. But you and your little friend had to find out the hard way.' Dogan sighed. 'You're something else, y'know that?' 'I am a warrior. And I have proven to be right.' Dogan shook his head as he helped Jimmy Spencer to his feet. Still though, he couldn't have helped but feeling like he was played by his cousin a little.
