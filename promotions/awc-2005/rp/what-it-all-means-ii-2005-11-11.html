What it All Means.....II

11 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/11/05 Of all the symbols in the world, the symbol of the cross (for western society anyway) is the most recognisable. Anyone originating from the western side of things has some basis in Christianity be it Protestantism, Methodist, Mormon, Baptist or Roman Catholicism. No matter where you go, it is impossible to escape the clutches of the symbol of Christ. It radiates his belief that in suffering for us all he can provide for us, give us the chance to be just like him and love our neighbours and all the commandments with it. As countless comedians have pointed out, why Jesus would want to see something that gave him so much pain is one thing but one cannot deny the sheer power of the symbol. It is unmistakeable, it is a one of kind object that does not have multiple purposes, no multiple representations or bearers of the object, it is synonymous with Jesus. Everybody in the world pretty much has an understanding of what it represents to so many people. It is the ultimate symbol. This was something that Fr. Troy was at pains to tell us on that cold winter’s day before Christmas holidays truly began. Fr. Troy was one of the most offensive Christian Brothers that has ever travelled the path of the priest and he made no attempts to conceal his vehement hatred of children. He indulged in many practices, drinking and smoking were two particular vices, but nothing got him going like humiliating and insulting a child in front of his peers, taking extreme delight in watching a boy squirm as everyone was hushed around the room, praying silently that they never had to endure such torment from the ashes of hell ever in their lives. It wasn’t an exaggeration to say that these boys would grow up and never feel a more shameful moment than that of Fr. Troy’s lashings with the language of God and the brittle crack of leather. That day was going to be one of those days for me it seemed. But, I’m getting ahead of myself. So we were sitting in the small, dimly lit classroom of 4B, a little bit of snow drifting by the windows now and then and it’s the 22nd of December in 1978, one of the coldest winters in recent memory. My classmates are huddling as close as they can together, trying to get as much body heat going between them for the sake of everyone, because as usual the heaters just aren’t working. We’re just back from the break of the day too and the coats we had on are now sitting down at the back, a gooey puddle sitting on the floor, slowly melting all into one. Like a giant coat monster. The classroom itself was pretty plain by the 70’s standards and had no frills beyond the one, necessary object that was placed in every classroom in every school in Ireland. The cross, right at the front of the class and hanging above the teacher’s desk. Some of the kids liked to say that the cross was there to watch over the teachers that entered into the classroom and make sure they did their job but even then I had a very different opinion. I knew it was a reinforcement of the power the priests had. They were the servants of God and commanded respect and compliance from whomever they saw fit to teach, nothing else would suffice. With a whack of the door Fr. Troy entered the room and all the boys stiffened at their desks, rigid bodies frozen by the control Troy had over them. In one awkward regimented move they all sprung up (myself included) and stood, the first mark of respect once a teacher (and especially a Christian Brother) entered the room. Not even stopping he immediately blessed himself and began. ‘In nomen of Abbas , Filius quod Sanctus Phasmatis…’ There’s nothing like Latin to scare the populace even more. People will always be afraid when they can’t understand what somebody is saying because they’ll fear they’re talking about them. The Catholic Church knew this precisely, and despite the Vatican Council in the 60’s which finally eradicated Latin mass, the Brothers still employed the subtle intimidation tactic to ease their prey into their own direction, bringing them closer for the slaughter. The prayer was mumbled by all the class and then Troy regrettably dismissed us to slump back into our desks. The clock above him read two in the afternoon. Only an hour and then the sweetness of release from school was real for another two weeks. I just couldn’t wait. Fr. Troy had other ideas of course. He was a stocky man, looking like the Friar from Robin Hood except with bushy red hair covering his head. The plump barrel of a chest was replaced by a more rotund wide general waist size and his dumpy legs waddled from side to side, not at all like a normal human being would walk. Then again, class 4B had thought he was from hell itself, so readily was he willing to talk of the punishments we would all receive down there. ‘Today class we are going to discuss the reasons behind why…’ I wasn’t even listening. Instead my head was propped up by my hands, staring directly out the window to my left, fantasising about chucking as much snow over Fr. Troy as I felt was possible. I remember it so clearly because of the following…. ‘Murphy! Stop dreaming of bliss and the heavens! Don’t you know you’ve determined yourself for damnation?’ It was just the kind of bizarre comment that half of us didn’t get at all. Troy relished this kind of puzzlement by the class, his supposed high-brow comments going way over our heads. Basically he was an asshole who resided in using big words to confuse and befuddle us. But I was listening now, no two ways about it. If I was caught daydreaming again…. ‘What did I tell you Murphy!’ The pull of pleasure had taken hold and I’d slipped back into my listlessness watching of the snow. Troy had immediately picked up on this and in his mind I was meat ready for the taking. Ready to be toasted over the fires he cultivated and enriched with his vitriolic diatribe. After the event, my peers told me that he’d asked me more than once to answer a question about the importance of the cross in Christianity and I had stared blankly out the window, not a word escaping my lips. Then I just gulped. Troy flashed into action, almost slithering down the aisle between the desks like something catching fire, lapping at each object in turn until he’s spread himself across the distance. He was upon me and grabbed my shirt collar, rising me up out of the desk as he always did when confronted with someone so ignorant and disrespectful to him. ‘Murphy, get up to the top of the class right now!’ He shoved me and I scuttled along up to the desk and stood facing the class, my head bobbing violently in the panic. Troy was possessed and his eyes lit like matches with anger. He began to address me. ‘There is nothing that I find more disrespectful than a boy who has closed his mind to the teaching of God and you have just done that Jack. Explain to me exactly why you did this? There better be a damn good reason or you shall rot in eternal infestation in the mud of hell!’ I don’t know why I said, I couldn’t tell you then, or now. I just did. ‘I was dreaming Father.’ The matches went ablaze and torched. They consumed Fr. Troy and he was both enraged and shocked at the same time. Normally it was customary for the boy to give in and say he was distracted or had heard him wrong but the casual tones that I elicited from my voice made him all the more infuriated. He grabbed at the leather hanging by his desk and raked me across the face, something even unheard of in his class. He repeated it several times, speaking to me whilst doing so. ‘You have shamed both myself and you in front of God right here and now. You have disgraced yourself under the cross and should be subjected to that same punishment our Lord so nobly took to spare us all. You have deemed yourself unfit to be a servant of God and should be cast from on high just like Lucifer himself was. You are…nothing….but….a…servant…of…HELL!’ Each gap in the last sentence was made by the violent thwacking noise of the leather beating my body in various places. As soon as he stopped I crumpled against the desk, not willing to falter and fall, not willing to give him the satisfaction of seeing me down on my knees. I tried instead to stand and regain my footing. He looked at me in disgust and kicked me in the stomach, leaving me lying against the desk. ‘You will stay afterwards and clean both yourself and the entire classroom for your repugnant behaviour. You sicken me and you will not go until I say so. The rest of you may go.’ Only then did I see everyone was wide mouthed and in shock. They couldn’t even begin to comprehend what had just happened before their very eyes. If they thought their own sufferings were torment, they now had a bit of perspective. There is much more to the story, however the pain and aching have blocked it all out. Suffice to say that was not the end of my pain. Christmas didn’t exist after that very day and it never has. The 22nd of December has been marked as my own personal day. My emancipation from Christianity. What does this lesson teach me though? A symbol isn’t just something that will remind people of the bearer and their deeds it has an even more powerful message to bestow upon those who look upon it. It inspires. Just like in the classroom, Fr. Troy used the power and inspiration instilled in the cross from his own knowledge to drive him to the edge…and subsequently over it. Without such a driving force, none of that would have happened…and I….I may not have been so changed. But the importance of this fact is not lost on me. As Triangles ventures ever closer I can see the importance that can be invested into the Triangles structure itself. Not only that though, but the Transatlantic Title. Just think of the power it can hold over people. What people will do for a big shiny belt… Triangles awaits us all…it awaits its crowning as a symbol for all to see… And one that I will claim… For myself.
