Lighting had always fascinated me. Electricity, within the mind, is a transporting device. Messages travel in precise flashes from the mind to the body and from the charged sky to the earth and its receptors. All those crazy prophetic mother fuckers from back in the day must have been struck by lightning. Electricity was never meant for man to control, and now that mankind does control it, we have completely misinterpreted it. We channel God through circuits to power all of our material devices. He makes our toast. He lights our lamps. He transmits pornographic images through a vast web of wires at a nearly instantaneous velocity. But that was never what Ben Franklin had in mind when he supposedly tied a key to a kite string and flew it in a fucking thunderstorm. He wanted to steal the secrets of the cosmos, risking death. His life was worth the cost of a peek inside the Godhead. But maybe shit backfired. Maybe Ben Franklin didn’t know what he was getting into when he decided to try to fuck with lightning. Maybe he condemned us to an existence where we worship the ionic streams that fuel our cell phone chargers and coffee makers and our TiVo…our precious fucking TiVo. So anyway, there I was, sitting in the kitchen, smoking a cigarette, and waiting for my Lender’s egg bagels to pop. I was high off my balls, staring down into a half-empty container of cream cheese, when I noticed the dull operational whir of the toaster, a buzzing hum of prolonged contemplation that popped and burst with a springing clatter that interrupted the message before any actual words could be tongued. As I spread the cream cheese on the bagel, the buzzing sound remained. I lowered my ear towards the toaster, expecting the murmur to swell in volume and voices to vibrate out with some clear announcement. Instead, the volume remained constant, and I realized that it had penetrated my brain, reverberating there like flies were nesting in my brain canals. I had always thought it was an important quality to possess to always be prepared for the worst possible situation. The first step in preparation is to assess any and every possibility that might arise in a particular situation. The toaster was inactive, but the sounds of activity still remained inside me, dividing from their cluster echo until I could pinpoint several distinct pitches, each pulsating to peaks at varying frequencies. The sounds reproduced and started growing as I focused in on them and nothing else. They sang their constant, random sound, and I felt it solidify inside, taking form as matter; living, organic matter that had to eat, digest, and shit. It was a very transforming process; one that would rearrange my own composition in perhaps even the tiniest of ways so that I could decode some meaning from the snoring chords that swished and shook me. God had transmitted his seed into my head, fertilizing tiny sonic messiahs inside. Upon arriving at this conclusion, the sound of larvae chewing grew intensely, and my head was bursting with sharp, jutting pains. I stabbed back, driving the butter knife into the toaster’s slit. But for a brief moment, I had absorbed the knowledge that only a god could possess. I saw past the images to the blank canvas upon which they were painted. I stood up and walked to the sink, turning the cold water tap and letting the water run over my hand. I let it run for a while, knowing that my hand would only hurt again once I turned off the tap. And sure enough it did, but I couldn’t run my hand under the tap forever. My hand would prune severely. So I walked over to the couch in the next room and sat down. I rested my feet on the coffee table in front of me and pulled out a cigarette. I clutched it between my teeth and lit it with my palm, which was still smoldering to a numbness. After being nearly electrocuted by the toaster stunt, life had returned almost overbearingly back towards being mundane. When I was sitting on the couch with a burnt hand, I could really only think about my burnt hand. Everything else was regular and boring. Smoke, or at least the aura of smoke, loomed about in the rooms. A thousand different unpleasant smells merged into one haunting vapor. The olfactory sense of a nicotine addict is weak, though, and I was used to it, or I didn’t care. I ran the water, and it washed over the pain. It freed my mind from concentrating on my burnt palm, and my thoughts strayed back to Ben Franklin and the white flash I experienced earlier. There had to be some connection between my experience with electricity and Mr. Franklin’s. But I was caught with only a small glimpse of this blank flash. My methods were more indirect. The electricity I had channeled was initially powered by some energy plant off somewhere through a series of wires into my toaster, and the electricity finally passed through me by means of butter knife. Ben’s approach was far more direct, channeling lightning directly from the great above into a key and then through his own body. I sat down and thought about this key, obsessing over the beautiful symbolism Franklin employed to sacrifice his own being for the chance to let omniscience strike its way into his being. It was brilliant. I needed to do it again. In just one time, I had developed this strange mental addiction to electroshock. I needed to hear the message again. …and again, until I could hear it clearly and know what to do.
