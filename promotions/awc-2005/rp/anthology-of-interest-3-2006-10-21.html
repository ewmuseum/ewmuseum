Date: Sat Oct, 21 'Johnny.' 'Uhm?' 'Johnny? What's wrong?' 'I uh. I don't know. This-' 'Ugh, are you serious?' 'It's just, c'mon!' 'This is perfect.' A dainty hand presses against Johnny's bare chest. With a disappointed sigh the lithe brunette pulls herself off of the hotel bed; taking a white sheet with her and wrapping it above a pair of pert breasts. 'This has never happened before.' Johnny pleads sincerely, leaning back on the mattress and pulling a blanket up to his waist. 'Whatever.' She shakes her head and dissappears into the bathroom. A hand comes across his features, wiping a layer of sweat away and blinking tired eyes. 'What's wrong man?' He asks himself, knowing full well the answer plagueing the back of his conscious mind. The match was taking on a malevolence of its own, a heart beat that even now he could hear in the pocket of his discarded jeans. The words, every one, cackle and mocke him, taunting him to read the line up over and over. 'You have my number?' Johnny looks up as she emerges from the bathroom. He doesn't even know her name, just the fact that he could crack and egg on her ass. 'No.' He responds mildly. 'Good.' She crosses for the door without looking at him. 'Hey c'mon.' She laughs and leaves without another word and Johnny falls back into his languid position. He thinks to himself and lifts the blanket, looking down at his limp little friend and shaking his head dissatisfied. 'You gotta make up your mind.' He chastises with snicker the moment before there's a rap on his door. 'Yeah!' Johnny beckons and the handle lock beeps before Lucious opens the door and pokes his head in. 'Lucy.' Johnny says. 'That was kinda fast man, you alright?' Johnny chuckles. A chuckle full of frustration that is just not happy being a chuckle, bubbling and growing into laughter. 'Everything's great!' Johnny snaps suddenly. 'A-alright. I'll just. I'll just watch my post.' 'Lucy!' Johnny swings his legs over the side of his bed and sits up to look at his bodyguard. Slight of frame with a hawk nose and self conscious eyes looking nervous beneath the steady bead of Johnny's gaze. 'Don't tell anybody about this.' The wrestler's tone drops a few notes into serious. 'You're the boss.' Lucy complies, lowering his eyes dejectedly. 'You take my dog for a walk yet?' Lucy looks worried. 'No.' 'Good, I'll do it.' Johnny says. 'I'll go with you.' A hand drops to his belt where a bottle of mace is holstered. 'Don't worry about it.' Johnny stands, the blanket falling away. Lucy looks away but the Livewire champion speaks on unabashedly. 'I'm not a fucking child, Lucy.' Johnny pauses and takes a deep breath. 'Just, go find Midas and make sure he's not too drunk.' Forcing his tone calm. Lucious nods obediently and closes the door behind himself. ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Toronto had taken a decidedly crisp chill in the wee early hours, but Johnny didn't seem to notice the plumes of breath cascading from his lips. He didn't seem to care he hadn't brought a jacket, in fact his expression reads that his mind far from this sidewalk. His gaze is not directed to the small golden puppy trucking along happily, stopping occasionally to lend his nose to good use and finally to do his business. Johnny ceases his march to comply with the diminutive creatures needs. His eyes cast themselves out into the anonymous, the faceless cars and personless people. Empty figures moving from one pre-destined point in their pathetic lives to the next, following the rest of the sheep while the dog circles and barks, nipping at their heels for stepping out of line. A bus travels by, the dimly lit seats empty except for the passing, faded image of a sleeping drunk. Smiling from the side of the bus is Rollins, grinning from ear to ear because it is that season once again. 'For the sixth year in a row anybody who wants to be somebody is ranting and raving about GTT. Like it actually means something besides the measure of your luck in a months time.' Johnny explains to Titan, the pup at his heels. 'Rich 'The Renegade' Rollins went forty eight and zero last year, this year he's out in the first round. You would think the man who goes forty eight to nothing would at least make it past the first round, but he didn't. In fact this isn't the first time previous GTT winners won't even see bracket finals. It boils down to logistics and probability, all the skill in the world isn't going to help a hundred and fifty pounder suplex a heavyweight. Neither is it going to stop him from getting his achilles heel of an opponent in the first round.' Titan parades by his owners side, ears perked and glancing up as though he just might be listening. 'Not to mention the lack of integrity of those behind the scenes, on a yearly basis rules are changed, edited, and revised. This is a tournament that promotes the finding of the best wrestler this year, but the method of finding this 'best' seems to be a matter of prefference and opinion. But by all means let's do this their way, and by doing it their way I could potentially have to say a god damned fucking duck is the best wrestler this year? Or a guy who pretends to be an alien from another planet and pays fuck heads to pretend like he's controlling them with his mind. What the hell is this crap? Is this some kind of joke? There's always been a certain level of theatrics in this business, angles and gimmicks, but this is making me want to puke. Where's the honor? Where's the integrity? I see a test of endurance, skill, and might degenerating into a damned beauty pageant where people are being hailed for their photogenics instead of their contribution to the sport. This isn't Vegas, people, this isn't a side show carnival. This is professional wrestling, you're supposed to be professional wrestlers, act like it.' His voice raises, a passerby takes notice as Johnny continues to meander down the sidewalk, talking with his hands. 'Every week I step in front of a sea of faces, in front of a million impressionable young minds across the globe. When they see me walk out they see a man, I'm not promoted by my hair, or my eyes. I'm not reknown for my smile or my sexy thighs, seductive mic banter or cheesy gimmick. I'm known as a good wrestler, I'm the guy that will pin you before you're done shaking your bon bon for the fuckin camera.' He offers a wink to a pair of blondes on their way home from the bar. 'This isn't a tournament, people!' He calls in their wake and turns himself back to the path ahead. 'It's time to wise up and face the facts as they truly are. This is no more a contest of talent than Paris Hilton is a virgin, this is over a hundred men and women arguing and hissing for ratings. Not to mention the fact that a half dozen people who couldn't do it themselves are judging those who are trying. Tell me if this sounds a little off to you, but they don't put a man who can barely two step at the table for a dance competition. They don't put a tone deaf girl who can't even warble in the shower as a judge on American Idol. So why, then, is there undecorated washed up wrestlers volunteering to judge wrestling matches? The concept is as ludicrous as it is true.' He pauses, noticing a few people have been listening. 'This was never about finding the best wrestler of the year, and it's always been about who the big guys can pimp out the most. Who's gonna jump the most hoops and learn the most tricks. Who's got the prettiest hair and puts on the craziest make-up.' Johnny points an accusing finger at Doctor Curiosity, who's looking malevolent behind the glass of an appliance shop. 'Don't get me confused with a hater, this is a generalization of everything that's wrong with this tournament. But beneath these mistakes and flaws is a beautiful ideal, and there are good wrestlers who take up the challenge. The system is flawed, it will always be flawed and can never be any different. And that ladies and gentlemen is the point, we are flawed, every single one of us has a vice, hang up, or qualm. But still we insist on pursuing this plastic dream of perfection, every year we're forced to stare down an ever improving mountain of talent. Every year they come in younger, stronger, smarter, and faster but not one is perfect. Not a single one is best, none are better than everybody. But everybody is better than somebody.' He pauses for dramatic effect, hobos and drunks gathered around and shuffeling along behind as Johnny continues his paces. 'It all comes down to luck.' He proclaims, spinning on his audience with a feverish look in his eye. 'And luck is very much like a fickle lady, you see. She will be good to you, she will be kind and generous and loving. Until, that is, you want her be.' 'If you know so much.' An invisible voice objects. 'Then why aren't you in the tournament.?' Johnny's grin grows wider before he proclaims, 'Because!' With arms spread wide. 'I don't believe in luck.' There's a discontented murmur. 'What?' Hands are waved, with a final shake of his head Johnny moves to leave. 'I've got one more question.' The mysterious voice beckons. Johnny knew the voice the first time, he doesn't need to turn and confirm but he does. Eyes as vivid, as blue, and as cold as his own meet him through the slight veil of darkness. Marcus Van Mill stands directly across from his estranged son with an unreadable mask for an expression. 'Don't keep me here all night old man.' Johnny says without disrespect, settling his shoulders and his tone. 'Besides, you're supposed to be dead.' 'I know.' Marcus raises his hands and steps closer to Johnny. 'I know. I just wanted to see you again before.' He sucks in a breath. 'I just wanted to say thank you.' 'Marcus.' 'Damnit Johnny.' He interrupts his son, closing the last of the distance between them. 'You haven't called me Dad since you were six years old.' Johnny doesn't respond with lips tight and hands shaking. 'I've put you through so much. Somewhere along the line I started seeing you as this little version of myself that I could train and program. But you've never been like me, not one little bit.' Marcus' eyes, for the first time Johnny can ever remember, look on the verge of tears. 'I just, wanted to say thank you.' Grabbing his larger sons shoulders tightly. 'And I'm proud of you. I'm proud of what you're doing with your life, proud that you're not like me.' Marcus wraps his arms around Johnny before the wrestler can extricate himself. 'All my life I wanted to be as you saw me.' Johnny says quietly. 'It wasn't until I saw you in the mirror that I realized I was empty. If you have a question, ask it.' Marcus nods slowely, clearing his throat with a trembling fist. Johnny sees him for what he is, a stubborn old man well past his prime. 'Why didn't you just tell me you wanted to wrestle? You never complained, you never told me you didn't want that life.' There's an uncomfortable silence. A long silence where father and son stare at each other within the circle of an overhead lamp. The silence stretches. Far into the realms of awkward. And is broken. 'I didn't know.' He answers. And now it's Johnny's turn to step forward, enveloping the stubborn old man in his powerful arms and whispering in his ear. When he's done he kisses the old man's head and for the final time lets go. For the final time Johnny turns away and for the last time Marcus sees his son's back. And he breaks. Falling to his knees Marcus buries his face into calloused hands, shoulders wracked with sobs. But Johnny doesn't look back, his own words ringing in his ears. His feet beat the sidewalk faster, carrying him farther and farther away. ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Johnny pushes through the doors of his hotel, his skin covered in goosebumps and his expression: Settled. He's walking past the elevators when a flash of blond snatches at his peripheral vision, stopping he swivels himself to catch a second glance. 'Eva!' He calls out, his voice inadvertently eager. But as the golden tresses of lavish hair turn it is not Eva looking back at him with eyes he will never forget. 'Excuse me?' She questions, propping a hand on her hip, expression melting from annoyed to intrigued. 'I thought you were someone else.' Johnny apologizes. She considers. 'I can be whoever you want.' Lips glisten and eyes shimmer with allure. Johnny smiles. 'Hang on let me ask the boss.' Johnny grabs his groin, moving it this way and that as the blond frowns. 'Sorry, he's not feeling up to it.' Wink. 'Johnny!' Midas' unmistakable bark resounds throughout the lobby. Before Johnny can turn away the blond grabs his groin herself. 'You sure about that honey?' 'Get your hands off my client!' Midas interposes the situation and the woman releases her grip as requested. She growls playfully at a mildly interested Johnny, and turns away. 'Hussy.' Johnny turns the look at Midas. 'Don't look at me like that.' The manager crosses his arms, glancing at Johnny. 'You fuckin cock blocked me.' Incredulous. 'I'm protecting you!' Defensive. 'You dunno what she's got.' 'Oh I don't know.' Head tilts as Johnny peers after her. 'I'd say thirty six, twenty four, thirty two and oh, that's a natural blond.' 'Down boy.' 'Oh don't worry, that's covered.' 'Something wrong?' 'Should something be wrong?' 'Just potentially the biggest match of your career thus far.' Midas reasons. 'Oh right, you mean the tag match?' 'Be serious with me for a second, I know you. I know it ain't like that, I just want to know you're ready.' 'Midas man, I'm fine.' 'Don't bullshit me man, I want in the loop.' 'What loop?' Johnny stops in the hallway and turns his friend about face. Midas though is already far too eager to do so. 'This loop that nobody but you seems to be in on. It's like there's this joke, and only you get the punchline.' 'I've heard that somewhere.' Johnny frowns and starts to ponder. 'Stop it!' 'Alright. What do you want me to say, Midas? That I'm an obsessive perfectionist who can't spend more than an hour without dissecting every possible outcome for my next match? Or that I would rather spend my nights watching old footage than meeting girls? Or do you want to know about how I feel so much fucking pressure I can't concentrate long enough to get a woody.' Midas tilts his head. Johnny looks at the maid. 'Forget it man.' Midas waves a hand. 'No, you don't seem to get the picture yet. I'm not the best.' Johnny pokes himself in the chest and Midas looks horrified by the admission. 'I'm not.' Shake of the head. 'In fact, I'm not even close. I'm twenty nine years old and I've had back surgery twice and every single week somebody younger shows up. Somebody full of piss and vinegar, their dreams still being broadcast in full fuckin color-vision while I'm stuck on black and white. There's kids almost ten years younger than me doing double what I am, but all I can do is keep fighting. All that I can do is keep going out there, week after week, and give them whatever little I have left to give and hope to God that when it's all gone I've got something, anything, to show for it.' He levels Midas with a snarl. 'And don't for a second think I'm ready to throw away my last chances sleeping with slutty girls and walking the dog.' Wait. 'I've been there, I've been young and cocky and I've ate every single word I ever spewed. I realized a long time ago that there is alot of hard work involved in making it anywhere in this business. There's no such thing as loss or failure, there's only calculated concessions because it doesn't matter what I say to who. It just has to be right.' 'My dear Father. You have stolen from me my mother, my childhood and my innocence. You've dipped my hands into the blood of many and let me believe that I was a God. Everything I've done for the past five years has been born from the spite I hold for you. The killer that lingers inside my heart will always hunger, I will always be what you made of me. I cannot escape the past, but still I fight what I became. I will never see you again, I have given you the death you wanted. I give what you always wanted to deny me. Freedom. The world believes you are dead, this is my final gift to you, take it and go. But know that I will always hate you. I will always walk away from you. I will always know it's not in you to apologize but. I forgive you. Dad. '
