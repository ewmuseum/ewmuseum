I'll teach you a lesson for keeping secrets from me.

5 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: now Somewhere between the guy with a hard-on for theatrics giving his dramatic speech on the fallacy of American Democracy, and the part where I almost get killed for being in the wrong place at the wrong time, my mind began to wonder. You’re probably saying “your mind wonders incessantly.” You’re also probably saying “What the fuck is going on, you confused the shit out of me in your last roleplay!” Maybe that’s why I couldn’t see the positive side of communism. Not the second part; I meant the part about my wondering mind. To live in a situation where you need to stay focused on a common goal for, like, a fucking year. Honestly, I hate that shit. I hate hard work. I’m all about finding some odd talent and exploiting it for profit. Sometimes, I even have to exploit other people to survive. It’s always worked in the past, me getting by. And I really don’t have to do jack shit. So while I’m sitting in front of some psycho with a star on his head, I’m ignoring every reason he’s screaming at me. Every little detail that can benefit the greater good of society, they’re already refuted by any illogical detail that sounds suitable to me. I’m going to have to pay attention, because the only way to deter an activist is through acknowledging their views, respecting their views, and calmly disagreeing with them. For some reason, though, I have a feeling that declining this group of small-town radicals would likely end in my testicles being removed from my scrotum. So… this is going to take a little bit of care. And now this is the section that I like to call HISTORY time. The guy standing in the corner, sparking up a cigarette in front of a no-smoking sign with such tenacity, his name is Josiah. Josiah used to have a stage name, or a ring-name, called Pointless. He used to fight in this controversial cage-promotion—the Asylum—where he ran bag-work for the owner Joe Campbell and his chain of basement-fighting-leagues/bars known as Smilthy’s. You see, Josiah would travel from town to town with the promotion, fighting in the weekly event known simply as The Show. He held a title or two, none of them very memorable, but he was a fresh and interesting face amidst the Villam Ender bullshit. In each town of the events, and in subsequent small towns between, Josiah would visit these local clubs and check on their operations, tending to specific and situational needs of Joe’s in the area. It was in one of these towns where Josiah would “hear his calling.” There were these rumors about Pointless, you see. There were reports of Josiah murdering various moguls in religious society on a sort-of Mission-from-God style of spree. Then there were reports that people had actually killed Josiah, yet they would see him alive no less than six hours later. All of them vague, all of them outlandish, none of them really believable. But I guess I’ll tell you what I think: I think that Josiah’s a delusional amnesiac who defines his presence on earth by acting out the will of God. That’s how he met Joe; doing dirty work that Joe happened to need done. Rumor has it that Josiah didn’t even know who he was; that he woke up one day from eternity and found himself in someone’s body. That he was an Appointed Angel, picked specifically for this task from God of “destroying the Anti-Christ.” I don’t believe any of it. Not for one second am I going to be frightened by a religious kook. My thoughts are that he went crazy, reinvented himself, things went better and now he’s leaving the craziness behind. He’s found a purpose now, a real purpose; not some bullshit crusade he convinced himself was true. Instead, his purpose is someone else’s… I guess you could say that about his earlier lifestyle, but at least here he has made some progress. Now how Josiah met the guy giving his speech, the Mutineer, is sort of strange too. While the Mutineer was getting his political cogs in motion, Josiah was cleansing this city of whatever crime it had. Apparently on a “mission from God,” Josiah hit the city with brute force in an effort to transform it in to Neo-Babylon; or some cultish-religious haven for hypocritical Christians. Serial-killing Christians. But when Josiah found that killing criminals one at a time wasn’t going to save this city, he began to get desperate. That’s where the Mutineer enters, already making a name for himself. He first made his name by cutting off some politicians balls, I heard. Then he started a small riot in the north end of the city that spread to the city counsel building, who was then having a meeting. I don’t really like to talk about what happened after, but let’s just say that mob-mentality will make even the most humane people do the most ungodly things. On one hand, we have the Robin Hood of the streets, and on the other we have Robin Hood of politics. The two combinded…. No, combound. SHIT! What is the past-tense form of combine?! Uh, let’s just say that the two joined forces, and in turn, their appeal grew. Josiah needed a mask for his brand of justice, while the Mutineer needed the will and drive to take his vision to the top. It was a group of vigilantes, using their own justice on the streets, then taking that justice to routine. The people leeched on, as all masses do. This small town was easy to dominate. And if I had my way, I wouldn’t have come here. Only my “girlfriend” “insisted” that I come here, specifically here, because of the man in the ring. The man who seemingly hasn’t lived in order in the small town of Gary. The man who is not playing by the rules that the Mutineer and Josiah’s cabinet have established. That man was Edward Estrada. Deft. Never held a title, always turned a head. Estrada was the type of guy who worked hard and expected nothing in return, but in his own right deserved all the attention. Some say he’s crazy, too. Shit, hold on guys. I’ve got to go take a smoke-break. (pause for five minutes, or enough time for the Face-Eater to get totally ripped) Alright. So uh, Eddy Estrada. Yeah, he wrestled for Action! Wrestling for a few months, alongside the likes of guys like Coral Avalon, Daniel Phillips, and shit, I forget. Uh… KSZ was there! Oh, and Jeff Garvin, and fucking Rook Black. And Duel! Yeah, now I remember; Deft got in to Action! because Duel trained him. He stayed there until it closed down, I think. Yeah, it closed. So Eddy goes to the Squared Circle, and starts to do shitty there. And then he begins to date this girl, Elizabeth Pleh, who is from Action! Wrestling as well. Fuck, I fucked up the story. What I meant to say was that while Deft was in A!W, there was a problem with some form of a glass ceiling. A lot of wrestlers left when LLB, a former Champion in the fed, returned and wanted to change things to how they were. Instead of Action! showcasing a lot of the new talent, he wanted to promote the seat-fillers. Tensions mounted, and people left. Deft was a part of the exodus, and subsequently, here is where his story gets fucked up. He met Liz in Action!, right? Well, things didn’t get fucked up until he tried to kill himself… at the same time that Elizabeth Pleh, P.R. chick for tSC, decided to hang herself. Her struggle distracted Eddy from his process, so he decided to check out this hidden commotion. When he found Liz hanging in a closet with her belt, struggling for a breath, he slashed the belt with the same knife he was about to use to slash his wrists. And thus, Love was born. And so now we get back to the story, because this is about the time where the Mutineer finishes his speech, and again all eyes are on me. ”What do you think, boy?” Josiah said, exhaling his courtesy-cancer right in my face. So I look up at the Mutineer, because he’s a tall son of a bitch, and I start to tap my toe. “Well that was mighty impressive and all, but haven’t any of you guys looked at all those countries out there who’ve actually tried your system and failed miserably?” Josiah began to speak but was cut off. Again the Mutineer’s voice came clearly, though his mouth could not be seen behind the black satin mask. ”We have not adopted our own interpretations from the book which we stand for; The Communist Manifesto. The ideals of Karl Marx and the words of Friedrich Engels are the only guide we have to establishing a righteous society, and so it should be. There are human flaws imbedded within our guidelines that will restrict stern policy, nor will they ignite a conflict of morals.” Hmm, he lost me, like, at interpreting things. ”We are the purest form of lifestyle there is to live and once the American Public watches Utopia in practice, it will not be long until we live in an age with barcodes on our necks.” I couldn’t really see it, and I couldn’t tell because all of his buddies stood with a straight face… but for some reason, whenever I would look at the Mutineer, I could only see the outline of a generic smile, placed directly underneath that mask. For some reason, it was hard to believe that someone was actually getting off on communism. Haven’t they ever seen the sun? “And who can argue with that, eh?” I say with a ruffle in my voice, trying to ease the tension. If the Mutineer was smiling before, the movement in the mask definitely showed that he wasn’t anymore. ”What business do you have here, boy?” Josiah inquired, once again stepping close to me. ”I just wanted to speak with Edward Estrada about wrestling classes. That’s all.” Everyone began to laugh, aside from the Mutineer. Josiah and his few goons, they were all cackles. ”Oh, so you’ve got ‘legit’ business, now—huh Eddy?” Josiah asks, before shaking his head in disbelief. “You know, I don’t even want to hear about it. We’ll see you next week, Ed, same time. You’ve got a quota.” As the group began to leave, the Mutineer leading and Josiah tailing the rest, Josiah could only chuckle as he walked down the hall. He still thought of my notion as funny. ”Oh, and Eddy, if you’re really going to train this kid… be sure to tell him about when you called yourself ‘Filth’.” Eddy’s eyes got wide, and Josiah disappeared out the door. Now, I considered myself pretty knowledgeable about wrestling. Not on the WWE side of things, but where I figure obscurity is prominent. Never before had I heard of Eddy Estrada wrestling under the name, Filth. And considering his story of ‘BEING’ Filth, I really wish I never had.
