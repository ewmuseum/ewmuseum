The Good Shepherd: Begginings

26 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 26, 2006 ['Prelude'] A month ago, the corrupt Haitian Government launched an operation, closing down schools and forcing children into early labor. For years, citizens of National Palace, Haiti have been able to go to their Arch Bishop in order to escape the madness of their government…but for years, their Arch Bishop was the heralded Francois Sacar the III. One year ago, however, Sacar III took a fatal death allowing his son, Francois Sacar IVth to take over the throne of Arch Bishop of the Tabernacle of Christ. Now, as tragedy looms, Sacar IVth must find a way to assist the people who have fallen to his feet for help…somehow. It's been over a month, and the children of Haiti are still being held captive under the corrupt wing of the Haitian government. It was over a month ago that Haitian officials called for the closure of several schools, disbanding a mass percentage of the student population while also forcing children between the ages of 10 and 15 years old into early labor. The United Nations was reported this morning as to having passed a resolution that will enable the immediate investigation of this clause, but citizens are still engulfed with terror. Rain pelted the stained windows, bearing a muffle of noise that only added to the melodramatic mood in which had taken over this Tabernacle as well as this town. The only source of illumination came from a spotlight that shone directly upon me as I knelt before the altar. My father had endured this position many of times, and I often wondered why, but with his impulsive death came an opportunity for me to step inside of his shoes…it was only then that I could really understand his motives for approaching the altar. He approached it for a spiritual motivation that neither me nor my mother could give him. He approached it for guidance in which elders of the church could not provide. He approached it for escape from the madness that came with being Arch Bishop of the Tabernacle of Christ. Now one year later, I find myself approaching for the very same reasons. As soon as the Haitian government initiated there forcing of Haiti children into early labor, the people of the church fell to my feet pleading for help…help that I, alone, could not provide them. So now I am positioned here, kneeled before the altar, seeking my own dose of help from the most faithful of sources…my father himself. “Francois…” I paused for a moment to gather myself. “I mean…Father. Your people, the very people whom you’ve lead for twenty plus years, have been dealt a hand of afflict. The corrupt Haitian government, the likes of which you took on during your entire tenure, has arisen once more to strike fear into the hearts of our people. They have taken on the role of King Herod and attacked our children.” I paused once more, as I felt slight waves of wind swifting through the air. “I feel your presence father, and I welcome it, for it is the very thing that I will need in order to assist me in this mission in which I’ve been chosen to partake. During this period of afflict, your people have come to me in order to grant them salvation. I, father, lack the proper essentials to properly aid them…but you…you have those very essentials and more equipped within your arsenal. It is for that reason that I ask you to lend me them long enough for me to save the people of Haiti and their children, thus saving them from the Government’s wicked grasp. Father, I…” A roar of thunder followed closely by a strike of lightening ensued. The wind picked up greatly, this time issuing howling sounds that echoed throughout the tabernacle. I felt a chill running through my veins as the intensity continued to amplify. I, however, continued on. “I feel your presence father, and I fully embrace it.” The howls turned into full-scale screeches. “I welcome your presence!” Thunder and Lightening took a strangle hold over the atmosphere. “Yes! Fill me with your presence!” The grueling winds nearly swept me off of my feet. “Grant me wi-…” “SILENCE!” Everything stopped. At the very command, the howling ceased with thunder and lightening following it. My father had spoken…and now I had to adhere. Nothing else mattered at this moment, only the embracement between the two of us. “Francois. My passing made you the heir to my throne…a throne in which you took against your very will. You disrespected not only my legacy, but also my father’s legacy, and even his father. You could have easily yielded the throne to someone who would truly appreciate it, but instead you took it on in order to save your own self-embarrassment. Now, when tragedy strikes, you come seeking me for help? You choose the easy way out instead of facing your problems like the true man you claim to be.” “But father I…” “SILENCE! When I speak, your talking ceases. I am your father, and as your father is it my duty to guide you…but I will not deliver you from this situation, for that is a task in which you must complete on your own. Instead I will give you one tool in which you will need in order to deliver yourself. Look beside you.” I wanted to respond, but without saying a word I did as I was told. Beside me sat a brown and black leopard-wrestling mask, the same in which my father wrestled under before becoming a Bishop. Upon first glance, memories of those times began to occur. I was too young to experience them, but my mother always told me of those times. She told me of the times in which she would sit at home; crying to herself because she’d thought my father had gotten killed inside of the ring. Times in which she would go for years without talking to him, simply because he was away. Contemplating began to run through my mind of just how this mask would help me in my quest. “It is obvious that now, as you stare into the eyes of that treasured leopard’s mask, that you are clueless as to how it will assist you…the answer, however, will soon come. You cannot let me down…promise me, you won’t let me down…” “But wait, father…I need your-…” And then he was gone. “Help.” His departure left me standing at the Altar in bewilderment as I stared into the open eyes of the mask. It was then that it hit me. I managed to take a swing back into memory lane, revisiting the time in which my father taught me the art of martial arts. It was a skill that I practiced daily, always seeking to master. Taking the throne of priest halted my expedition for this mastering, but yet I still had it in me… And now it was time to put it to use. A huge 32-man Grand King Wrestling tournament was fast approaching and it cost a couple of grands to get in. From my understanding, my father wanted me to enter this tournament, win, take the money and pay off the “Early Labor” clause, which would set the children free from the Government’s grasp. What I didn’t understand, however, was exactly how I was going to go about getting the money. Through an act of desperation, I paced forward into the financing department of the Tabernacle. Only five trusted people were allowed to enter this department, but my master key of the entire church allowed me access. I opened the door and crept inside, taking the time re-think my motives. As the leopard’s mask continued to stare at me with unforgiving vigor, however, I came to the conclusion that I was indeed doing the right thing. It was this very conclusion that led me to take the several grand and quickly make my exit. Before stepping back into the harsh weather conditions outside, I slipped the mask on. It was time to retire the role of Francois Sacar IVth, the Bishop… And take on that of Francois Sacar IVth… The Fighter. Pelts of blood gashed through my nostrils, staining the canvas below as a sharp pain pierced me through the side. My ribs took on a sense of disfigurement, presenting me with a pain that disabled me to even push myself up onto a vertical base. Slowly, my eyes peeled open, countering the bruises which had taken form upon them. Darkness ensued as I struggled for a sense of visibility only to find yet another set of five knuckles planted into the bridge of my already frail nose. The crack upon impact echoed throughout the arena, drowning out the ravage roars of the gathered masses in attendance. Shaking began to succumb over my bones as I felt a sense of death fast approaching. It was then that he covered my lifeless body. The referee's hand slapped the canvas once, then twice, and in an instant's second a pause came. It was during this pause that a scan of previous events occurred. Flashes of blood flooding the streets, flowing from the souls of innocent Haiti children. Flashes of corrupt government officials being hoisted over a country torn by deceit. Flashes of faithful believers assembled at my feet, praying for salvation from the madness that had been brought upon them and their children. All of these flashes served as my motivation for continuing on with this impossible fight. But even as my soul strived to fight on, my body wouldn't allow it. The third slap of the referee's hand brought forth a roar from the crowd. Darkness formed as my opponent was announced the winner. This mission in which I'd been set out to endure had been met with failure. A tear slowly crept down the side of my face, tickling my cheek as it rolled to the canvas below. I'd let my people down, and even moreso...my father. I blacked out. “EXILE!” A loud, exuberant roar sounded at the command. I trembled fearfully as a solid boom occurred at my front door, growing far more intense with every passing second. Each blow lifted the door farther off of it's hinges until it'd completely fallen down, providing a passageway for the army of bandits whom all held one common purpose in mine...Destroy I, Francois Sacar IVth. They were commonly given the name of “Black Bandits” and served as a group of radical religious personas whom sought to regulate certain aspects of the church with an iron fist. They had never been officially sworn in to take on such a duty, but instead ordained themselves. Despite there lack of authenticity, followers of the church took them in as a valid force that would serve as a method of obtaining order in the otherwise orderless church. In my eyes, however, they were nothing more than a group of thugs whom boasted a religious that contradicted everything they truly stood for. Treason is what they aligned the mission under, stating that I'd gone against church bi-laws and taken large sums of money without the permission of financial trustees. In a sense they were right, but I knew my motives. Those very believers had fallen at my feet, begging that I save their children from the corrupt strangle hold that the Haitian government had placed on them, and when I finally set out to do so...I'm treated as such. I couldn't stand for it. They charged in full speed, surveying the surroundings in search of the man that the people of Haiti wanted dead or alive. They wouldn't find me, however, as I'd escaped through the back door and set off on a treacherous journey through the woods, running non-stop for an hour at best. All I wanted to do was get away...get away from the publicity, away from the death threats, away from a position in which I didn't even want to inherit in the first place. Away from National Palace, Haiti. I had not the time to gather any of my belongings. The only items that accompanied me on my journey out of Haiti were my father's Leopard Mask in which I'd stuff into my pocket, a torn sheet, and a Five Dollar bill. I took the time to rest in the middle of nowhere, seeking to replenish energy in my otherwise battered body. I'd manage to escape the Black Bandits, but they weren't going to tell the people of Haiti that. They Couldn't. If they'd told the people of Haiti that they'd failed on their mission, they would immediately turned their backs on the Bandits. Therefore, with me nowhere to be found...they spread the word. Francois Sacar IVth had been killed by the Black Bandits. ['Epilogue'] As I reached the border to cross over into the U.S., the guard approached me. “What is your name?” he asked. “…Machaiveli St. Romani.” I wonder if they’ll laugh when I am missed. - Tupac Shakur
