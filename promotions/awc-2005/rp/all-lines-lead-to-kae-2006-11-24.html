All lines lead to Kae...

24 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 24/11/2006 The alarm on the digital clock blared into existence, destroying the calm before the storm. What storm you ask? Well, that would ruin the surprise. You’re just gonna have to read on. A hole no bigger than a keyhole parted the curtains of the elaborately gauche room, but sent in more than a keyhole’s volume of light. It seemed to engulf an entire chest of drawers with its piercing gaze. Like a torch, you know? Expanding light. I never did Physics past sixteen, and even then, I’ll be damned if I was paying much attention. Needless to say, point is made, it was morning. Jonathan Alexander Spencer Kae, the fifteenth in a line going back to the sixteenth century – though the name then wasn’t Kae, it was something far more vexing to the naked eye…many syllables – stirred restlessly in his bed. The fine quilted duvet that covered him, gave little respite to the conglomerate tycoon; the corporate hegemon; the egotistical wrestler. The dream he’d had obviously disturbed him, but that wasn’t of any importance at the moment. What was important was that incessant howling of the digital bitch screaming beside his ear. He should have servants to do this kinda thing. Pay them enough, and someone surely would. What kinda job would they have? ‘Professional Alarm Dude’? Kae smiled at the thought, but not long enough to make it a proper smile. The howling had to stop. Crack! That was the end of that howling. No. Still howling! “Bastard!” Kae’s words leapt from the bed like a thundering accusation. Funny how we speak to inanimate objects as if they were persons sometimes, isn’t it? Especially in times of distress. But this electronic oppressor of easy listening was far from inanimate. Who on earth would have designed such an annoying tone? Someone who wanted to piss of Jonny Kae, that’s who! He slammed the alarm clock again. This time silence. He must have hit the right button in his rage. Finally, the eyes of the great man opened, letting the world flood his senses with thousands of pixels of image. No wonder blind men were often far more intelligent. Or Deaf men for that matter. All the incessant bullshit we need to listen to day in day out could be replaced by logical thought and deduction. Men like Beethoven, inspired by the extra twenty percent of their brain function freed from listening to damned digital howling! Time to get up. He removed the armour of the duvet from his half naked frame. Holding his head, the realisation that he’d gotten little sleep hit Jonny Kae like a wet cloth to the privates. He sat up, peering around the room, his hair all dishevelled. He had never been a morning person, but then he never really had to be, except at school. What had that dream been about now? Funny how one can’t remember things like that. You can watch an entire movie and only remember one line of it, even ten minutes afterwards. That’s because it’s a crap movie. Must have been a crap dream. Kae got to his feet, stepping over to the chair sitting at an angle to the desk that loomed over it. He’d sat there last night, going over all this stuff that seemed to swamp the mahogany desk, until the early hours. Sleep had been forced upon him, he hadn’t sought it. Probably why he felt like shit this morning. Kae lifted the robe off of the back of the chair, and floated it over his body, arms fitting into the grooves nicely. He wondered for a second what kind of fashion designs had been prevalent in the preceding centuries, and how the populous’ had eaten them up. What looked silly today was quite cool, even though the word hadn’t been adapted for its present meaning, back in the ‘olden days’. Perhaps it would be the same a few decades from now? Jeans, t-shirts with logos printed shamelessly upon their faces, clothes ripped purposefully to give them a worn look. Imagine what the objective thinker from another time might think of the fashionable ‘emo’ look? I’ll stick to my suits thank you very much! They never go out of fashion…except in the sixties. Jonny tied the robe around his midsection, and made for the paper that Demus regularly slid under his door at six in the morning. All that was missing was the coffee… * Knock! Knock! * Ah…Problem solved. Kae opened the door, paper slung under his arm. There was the slight figure of Demus, his chauffer and attendant. He loved how Demus was up and ready for war every morning. He wondered if the small African-American ever really got any sleep? Maybe he dreamt of cleaning, driving and bringing cups of coffee to brilliant men. Maybe, on the other hand, he dreamt of wilder pleasures – perhaps crudely carnal in nature. Kae looked unimpressed at Demus at the thought of his fantasies. It struck him as somewhat inappropriate that this man should have any fantasies at all. Demus returned the stare, with a somewhat puzzled gesture. Demus: “Sir? What is the matter?” God, the man could use apostrophes in his sentences if he wanted! Kae wasn’t an English Exam Invigilator! He again thought of Demus and his Carnal fantasies… Jonny Kae: “You sick, sick man!” Kae slammed the door in the face of his butler, chauffer and coffee-bringer – all in one! Briefly, Kae wondered what the politically correct way to address Demus’ culture in the near future would be? Would the ‘correct police’ slam on his door, with accusations of the use of the word ‘black’? Would ‘African-American’ be outlawed? Perhaps they would have to address all nations as colourless? That would be mightily complicated! But Demus was Demus. It made no different if he was colourless. Afterall, his carnal pleasures were sickening! Or so Kae imagined. Instead of fantasising, Demus simply turned his back on the door, shrugged, and went back to attending the manor in whatever duties were necessary. But, of course, Jonny wouldn’t know that, would he? Unless of course he was omnipotent, which would be awesome, but a little unrealistic – kinda like having a 2500 year old vampire as a character. But then, we’re all masters of our own incredulous machinations. Kae nobly walked back towards his desk, papers and photographs strewn everywhere. He sat on the chair, crossing his left leg over his right, and taking a sip of the coffee. Perfect. Demus really made this stuff well. He should give him a raise. ‘Chuckle’. The coffee mug was relegated to the desk, and the paper took precedence. He opened it, and there, on the third page, the face of that Italian bastard! Big letters headed the article – “MOB ENFORCER RUN DOWN” It was such a shame that in Chicago, such news as this didn’t make the headlines. It sent a shudder down Kae’s back to imagine that there was such worse events to occupy the top reporters than the apparent murder of two of the mob’s key hitmen this side of Las Vegas. He didn’t care for those headlines today. He just cared about the article in question. Kae skimmed it, his reading spectacles somewhere discarded on the floor, and he didn’t have the occasion nor the bother to dive for them at this juncture. Something struck a chord with the business icon, and he re-read the paragraph, this time out-loud, as if to let the impact sink in. Jonny Kae: “Mr. Lucini, thirty three, was a known accomplice of the West Coast gangster Vincent Camberloni, who was shot, and subsequently died, last week outside the Italian ‘De Palmer’ restaurant. In what many speculators are calling a ‘vigilante mission’ against the Italian Crime syndicate, Troy Lucini and Vincent Camberloni were killed within a week of one another. Two witnesses identified a man at the wheel of the Mercedes, wearing a pair of sunglasses and a head of dark brown hair, and one elderly woman managed to give the car license plate to the police. Last night, the owner of the vehicle, Phillipe De Causser, was taken in for questioning by the Chicago Police Department.” Kae stared motionlessly at the words on the page for a moment, before closing the paper and slamming it down on the desk. “Fucking Idiot!” The calm was officially over… ___________________________________________________________ ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Jonny had heard from one of his contacts in the Chicago PD that Phillipe had cracked quite easily under the pressure of two rather large – sideways large, I mean these guys were really fat! – detectives, with little motivation for getting a confession. Of course, the amount of money that Kae Enterprises ploughed into the local economy meant he would be exempt from any serious investigation even though his name had come up, but his political enemies could use this information as leverage. Kae’s only real hope was that Charles Sandon didn’t have a contact within the Chicago PD, which, of course, he knew was ludicrous. Politicians and Corruption are wedded together much as the carriage is wed to the horse – the former is never far behind the latter. Kae slammed his fist down onto the glass table, nearly breaking it into shards of penetrating transparent spears. He tried to focus, but there was little he could do. Within the course of five minutes, he’d made his mind up. He would have to call a press conference. He would pre-empt any action by Charles Sandon. He would play his hand, and see what else there was to come of it. Jonny Kae: “Jolene! Jolene!!” The slight frame of the woman came bursting through the doorway. Kae marvelled in her presence for a moment, making sure to take ample note of her imposing cleavage, before continuing. Jonny Kae: “We need to arrange a press conference woman! Quick!” Jolene was about to run off, but she stopped, and peered at Kae. Jolene: “But, where Jonny?” He loved that she called him by his first name now. Jonny Kae: “Well…” He bustled through his thoughts for a moment…furiously trying to maintain his calm. Jonny Kae: “…Anywhere!” Jolene nodded, before quickly disappearing into the adjacent operations room. Kae sat, he sighed, he thought of yet another scheme to try and repair the damage caused by this episode. ___________________________________________________________ ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ The press had gathered eagerly to the announcement that financial mogul, aspiring politician, and multi-billionaire Jonathan Alexander Spencer Kae was giving a conference. Press from the United Kingdom had charged across the pond, in their dignified masses, like a flock of proper geese, observing the etiquette bequeathed unto them from prior generations of migrating birds. Canadian reporters, a little less inclined to know much about the international tycoon, had slid down over the boarder. Mexican reporters had come for the expenses paid trip. And then there were the bitches…those fastidious women who had gone into journalism with their weapons of immorality and a the kind of face that just looks like it could suck a … you get the picture. They sat in the hall, a quickly reconstructed gymnasium in the ever busy city of Chicago. Kae sat, as a mighty warlord over his optimistic populous, looking down on the mass of grey suits and neutral colours. Why is it they always wear neutral colours? Why can’t journalists sometimes be more like John Snow? Red Socks with a black suit? Or that other guy with the multi coloured ties that he obviously must let his wife choose for him? The flashes of cameras glittered the scene, before Jolene, the strong willed and unobtrusive Jolene, entered the room. She stood beside Jonny Kae, leaning down into the microphone, perhaps purposefully revealing the edge of her cleavage to the massed onlookers, a fantastic strategy with these battle lines drawn, and the siege of the Kae fortress about to be broken. Confusion always haunts the confused. Or maybe she just forgot to do up that button…who cares, it looks good. Jolene: “Ladies and Gentlemen, thank you for coming here to Chicago on such short notice. Mr. Kae has a brief statement to make concerning the events of the past few days, and the issue of his name in connection to the deaths of the individuals in question.” Jolene smiled for the inevitable pictures, and took her rightful place beside her hegemonic master. Kae stood to his feet, his crisp suit buttoned up, and a tie across his collar, an unusual adornment for this particular international superstar. Kae stood at the podium, and coughed, to free his voice. Jonny Kae: “Over the past few days, a travesty of modernism has rocked our world media.” The audience groaned. They had been denied their humble Kae, who they could chew up and spit out and get back to their jobs. It was the typical Jonny Kae start. Like a conquering emperor, or an American president, he spoke as if on some moral high ground, a perch he neither merited nor, many thought, wanted. Jonny Kae: “Certain accusations have been levied at myself which are both unfounded and unwarranted, to do with the deaths of one Mr. Lucini and one Mr. Camberloni.” The pictures started to flair from the population in attendance, an undeniable evidence of their frustration. Jonny Kae: “These two men have been linked to the murder of my own Mother, the courageous and awe inspiring Samantha Kae. I must stress that we, in the Kae Administration, have made no such connections between these men and my late Mother. It is mere speculation to create such a web of lies, distrust and deceit that have supposedly structured these past days’ events.” Administration? The arrogance of this man to compare his own corporate conglomerate to an American leading regime was blinding. The boos that should emanate from a wrestling audience under these circumstances, were instead replaced by the vigorous clicking of cameras. Jonny Kae: “Furthermore, to the man, the despicable individual who has made such claims, I implore him to find some shred of moral integrity, over fabricating intelligent untruths and proliferating lies. He has shown the very dynamic fabric of what his profession entails, that of killing without regard, and he has shown himself to be unworthy of the leniency this great country is bound to afford him. To the families of Mr. Camberloni and Mr. Lucini, I extend my deepest sympathies, and I assure them that I, nor any associate close to me, had anything to do with their unfortunate, and untimely deaths. Thank You.” Kae stood at the podium, reeking of the very lies, deceit and distrust that he’d condemned only moments earlier. From the audience, a hundred hands flew up (exaggeration there, but hell, it’s my damn roleplay!) Jolene looked at Kae, who nodded. She then pointed out a particularly bland looking individual in a black suit jacket and grey trousers in the middle of the room. Reporter 1: “Mr. Kae. Thank you for your statement to clear up the cloud of uncertainty surrounding these affairs. However, I would like to ask you, what is your position on recent allegations afforded by your opponent, Mr. Sandon, that your past is tarnished by dealings with various organised crime syndicates?” Kae leaned forward. Jonny Kae: “I would like to assure you, your piers, and the American people, that I have never collaborated with, and fully condemn, all forms of organised crime. Mr. Sandon is presenting the weakness of his position by trying to create weaknesses in mine. Perhaps if he would care to take a closer look, he might realise that not every enigmatic entrepreneur is corrupted by criminal intent, a handshake not an underworld instruction, and a corporate gesture not an outlaw crime.” A brief chuckle sped across the audience, as another bunch of hands filled the air. This time a young woman was chosen. Reporter 2: “Mr. Kae. The recent confession of the apparent murderer of Mr. Camberloni and Mr. Lucini clearly implicates you as the benefactor of the assault. He also claims that there were other targets. The police have promised to release a statement within 24 hours. Should that statement look unfavourable on you, would you consider your political position untenable? And therefore, would you relinquish your position as Republican candidate for the state of Illinois?” Kae’s face down turned for a brief moment. But his grin returned soon thereafter. Jonny Kae: “As I have said, there is no foundation in these accusations as of yet. I have nothing further to add to that. As far as my ‘political position’? I am fully committed to seeing out my campaign, and becoming a beacon for truth, honesty, and the will of the great American people, as a placating voice and advising opinion to the current administration and thereafter.” A third reporter raised his hand on the nod from Jolene, and was abruptly chosen before any others had the chance to do so. Reporter 3: “Mr Kae. If you do, indeed, succeed in gaining the position of Governor of Illinois, what would your policies be vis-à-vis crime of this sort?” Kae, at the obviously drafted question, slammed his fist on the podium. It was becoming quietly obvious that this had been pre-staged. The many journalists who had come from far and wide with the view to ask questions, had made their journey in vain. The questions had been decided, and the questioners chosen, long before the conference was announced. Not Kae was playing on the momentum caused by this publicity, in an unprecedented way. Jonny Kae: “I assure you, and the great Illinois people, that I, if elected, will not stand for this kind of slap in the face of liberal democracy. Frances Fukayama was right to call this the end of history, but he was wrong in not foreseeing a new dawn. I guarantee that I will rid our streets of the vermin, not just that which is controlled by politics, money, and nationality, but the petty crimes on our streets every day. I propose a hard line, to create prosperity where there was oppression, to give peace where there was commotion, to create a state free of sin, of plagues caused by the evil hearts of lesser men, and the fear with which such prospects strangle society.” Kae nodded to the audience, and he and Jolene stepped back. They posed for a few photos, and disappeared off into the darkness behind the curtains. Kae’s campaign hard line had been decided indirectly by his own actions on a completely different matter. He had managed to turn a potentially incriminating affair, with disastrous consequences, into a political momentum with which to propel him further towards his goal. And then, of course, there was the AWC…oh yeah, he had a match.
