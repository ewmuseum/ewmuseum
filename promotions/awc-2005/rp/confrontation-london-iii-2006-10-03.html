Confrontation - London (iii)

3 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Oct 3/06 The dull roar of the engine that powered the double-decker bus I was aboard quieted to a purr as it slowed to its next stop. A handful of people queued in the aisle to make their exit, and I slid in. The line crawled through the narrow aisle and exited the bus. I stepped onto the street and inhaled a breath of cool London air, grateful to be free from the stuffy double-decker. I spotted a bench to my right, and headed towards it. A few stragglers awaiting public transport took desperate drags of their recently-lit cigarettes. They rushed past me, leaving the bench temporarily vacant. I quickly took advantage of the free seat. Trying to find a comfortable position, I silently wondered what what was occurring at Fresh; something about Tuesday, September twenty-sixth didn't quite fulfill the exclamation mark that AWC's weekly show provided, hence the grammatical mishap. Admittedly it was a personal vendetta, as only a half hour before I was outlawed from the London venue in result of my loss to Garbage Bag Johnny. Granted, I came awfully close to stealing a win from the Transatlantic Champion, something I felt rightfully proud about. If it wasn't for an illegally used jetpack, or j4tpack, or j5pack ... I would've been treated to a luxury dressing room. Instead, I was left on this outdoor bench, swarmed in second-hand smoke. I silently cursed myself for letting off my guard ... for allowing the perfect opportunity to win the match slip through my fingers. I was becoming soft, which I blamed on my dreadful encounter a week before. Or, it was a temporary excuse that I could use at will. However, the intent had worked – my former mental strain had almost completely ceased. I was quite grateful to empty my conscience of that overwhelming guilt, and I looked forward to freeing myself of the confrontation completely. I didn't expect it to leave me fully, but instead of being racked at the front of my mind, it could be shoved to the back. I could deal with that; being in London made it even easier to cope. I liked London. It was an interesting setting with a rich history, both good and bad, and an satisfactory escape from my home in America. Berkeley was becoming less like home and more like a place to call home; they say home is where the heart is, and mine was most certainly in London. My girlfriend, Natalie Porter, was due to pick me up soon. I was anxious to see her again – it felt like so long ago we were together. My emotions were complicated and incomplete, something that I anticipated she would cure. I felt cold, and more than just in an emotional manner – the cool breeze easily penetrated my thin sweater. Running around London on a wild goose chase of an arena that didn't exist kept the blood flowing, and I was feeling the difference now. My phone buzzed in my pocket. I perked excitedly, deterring my absent focus from the pedestrians around me to my cellular, which I removed from my sweater. I opened it, hoping it was Natalie. “Hello?” “Cedric!, mate, how are you?” rang an exuberant voice from the other end of the line. The expression on my face was a surprised one – I didn't expect a call from Lucas. “Err .. I'm good ... I didn't expect to hear from you so soon ...” I returned. “Ah, I figured as much. Listen, what's your schedule like for this week?” he asked, interested. “Well .. I'm kind of in England at the moment.” “London?” “Yeah.” “Bloody hell! That's perfect!” he announced enthusiastically. I frowned. “I'm not quite following, Lucas.” “I'm at my London vacationing home!” He confirmed, then paused. “Well, how about another meeting?” “Er ... well ... when would you be thinking?” “Why not right now?” I sighed. “Listen, Lucas, I'm kind of busy at the moment.” “No you're not .. I just saw your match ... you got the boot from the arena tonight!” I cringed; he was right. “Right, but I'm supposed to be spending tonight with Natalie ...” There was a pause in the conversation. Lucas replied, “Well ... I suppose we can meet another time. It is rather important, but for the meantime, it can wait. I'll be keeping in touch, Cedric.” “Thanks, Lucas ...” I replied instinctively, and closed my phone without further adieu. I thought about his farewell and frowned; what could he have been referring to as significant? I couldn't help but wonder; it was out of human instinct that I was curious for his information. I put my phone back into my pocket, and glanced both directions down the street in front of me. The sun was about to set, and I started to wonder if Natalie had confused my stop with another. I suggested that I simply take a taxi to her house, but she insisted that she pick me up at this particular bus stop. She didn't specify why, and as the obvious male in the couple, I stuck to my role and didn't ask questions. I couldn't help but worry about the time, though. Maybe I was being paranoid. After all, I had only waited fifteen minutes. And at an incidental time, a blue car pulled up in front of me and I spotted my significant other manning the steering wheel. I got up quickly and strolled towards the car, jumping in as if to avoid the wandering eyes of the pedestrians waiting for the next bus to arrive. I wasn't exactly comfortable with strangers in a foreign territory, and the neighborhood didn't exactly spell friendly. Needless to say, I was glad to get out of there. Natalie quickly navigated the vehicle out of the bus lane and back into the flow of traffic. “So how did your match go?” she asked, initiating conversation. “Not great. I'm here, aren't I?” I said half-jokingly, and in the meantime, insulting my own performance. She smiled, then said adoringly, “Well, I like it better that way. Leaves us time for a night out for dinner.” I did a double take. “Wait ... what?” I paused, gawking at her. “Dinner? Tonight? Out?” She detracted her view from the road in front and looked at me, amused. “You heard right.” I held my bemused stare for a moment, effectively, then fell back into my seat and glanced admissibly out the window at the passing vehicles. “But look what I'm wearing!” I said, indicating my rather casual attire which hadn't been changed since working up a sweat in a losing cause. “C'mon, honey ... did you really think I would make arrangements unprepared?” She challenged, offering me a puzzling look that I couldn't quite stomach. “Um ... I guess not?” I said, unsure of what kind of response to give. “Well, for your information, I'm too organized to let something like this fool me,” she confirmed, putting her eyes back on the path in front of her. “I'm sorry, sweetie ... I should have never doubted you,” I offered sincerely, trying not to go overboard with the heartwarming voice. She remained unpleased with my attitude. I was convinced she was putting on an act, but I didn't dare make a point of it. Instead, I decided to go along; again, I was was dubbed the male in the relationship, and I intended to follow through on that and choke on my short leash. “So what restaurant are we going to? The Keg? Olive Garden?” I asked, aimlessly listing North American franchises. Natalie giggled. “No, silly ... we're in England, in case you didn't notice.” “Right ... so where are we going?” “The Dorset ... it's a fairly nice place, so it's a good thing I brought you something respectable to wear,” she said. I was tempted to throw in 'I look good regardless', but bit my tongue, and merely nodded in agreement. We continued to move through the city, which was a fairly entertaining drive. I had been to London plenty of times but never fully appreciated the scenery. It certainly had some to offer, but I couldn't put names to anything, and constantly prompted Natalie to name well-known landmarks. “Here we are,” Natalie announced as she veered the car into the right lane and turned into a parking lot. She slid the vehicle into a convenient spot near the doors, put it into park, and turned off the ignition. “Now get in the back and change. Our reservations were for ten minutes ago.” I weaseled into the back seat and started removing my clothes, which gave off a rather pungent smell that filled the car. “Jeez, you didn't even shower after your match?” Natalie choked. She grabbed her purse and opened the car door and got out. After five minutes of twisting and pulling my limbs and the clothes intended for them, I got out of the car. I stood there in dress pants and a sharp black sweater that I was fairly impressed by; my girlfriend had a good eye for fashion. And did she ever know how to dress herself – I almost gawked at her beauty as she stood before me glowingly. Her black dress fit perfectly to her body and was appropriate for the occasion, and her short blonde hair was arranged attractively. She moved elegantly towards me and in an unpredictable move took to messing around with my hair as she pleased. I trusted her judgment. I took her arm in mine and showed a smile which she returned. We walked into the restaurant and were seated immediately to our table. It was a nice setting, although not the fanciest, it was what one could expect out of a formal restaurant – a nicely arranged and decorated interior that set the mood appropriately for dining. Sitting at our table, we skimmed through menus as our waitress filled our wine glasses with champagne. She informed us she would return soon to take our orders, and we thanked her. I casually sipped from my glass, moving my eyes from the menu. I glanced at Natalie as I set the glass down, and noticed her looking at me curiously. “Something wrong?” I wondered, looking casually at the menu. “I think you should ask yourself that,” Natalie commented. I moved my eyes from the menu to meet hers. “I don't think I understand ...” I began. “Nevermind,” she said, then turned her attention away from me and took a mouthful from her wine glass. “What is it?” I asked, rather irked at her observation. Natalie slowly swallowed the champagne, keeping her eyes on me. She slowly set her glass down, as if to prolong her response so should could mull it over. “You look different, Cedric. And you sound different. Everytime we've talked on the phone over the last month you ... oh, I don't know what I'm trying to say.” She took another sip from her glass of champagne. I frowned, and tried to formulate a response, but the words just weren't coming to me. Natalie asked, “Is there something going on that I should know about?” “If you're asking me if I'm having an affair, then no,” I stated quickly. “I wasn't,” she returned calmly. I sighed. “Listen, Natalie ... I've been going through some things that I wanted to keep quiet ... how can I say this ... I've been experiencing some ... mental issues,” I said, then paused, leaving my announcement open for interpretation. She didn't say anything imminently, so I continued, “But I think I've handled it well by myself, so you shouldn't worry.” She soaked in the information. Her facial expression didn't seem to indicate any immediate reaction, so I remained unsure of what type of effect I had. Natalie sighed and placed her hands gently in front of her, formulating a response that I wasn't convincingly ready for. “Well ... what can I say? You should have told me, Cedric. That's why-” And as my eyes met hers, and the moment was set to take a turn for the better or the worst, an unexpected guest became present at the most inconvenient time. “Cedric! Why, I didn't expect to see you here!” said a man who strolled towards us. I looked at Lucas Emerson with a somewhat cold look, which I tried to transform into an inviting smile and in turn, alter my mood towards his unmannerly self-invitation to our table. “Good evening, Lucas,” I greeted, forcing manners. He beamed with enthusiasm as he approached our table with an outstretched hand. I grasped it, showing a weak smile. Lucas pulled out the spare chair at the table and got comfortable under his own discretion. He nodded at Natalie, saying, “Good evening, miss,” in a very polite manner. I looked from my English friend to my girlfriend. She had a smile that indicated she appreciated the chivalry, but at the same time, she favored privacy. “I don't mean to be rude, Lucas, but Natalie and I were just ordering here,” I said, looking back at my menu and avoiding my friend's eyes. “Oh, well .. don't mind me then. Just thought I'd drop in and say hi,” Lucas said abrutply, about to get up from his chair. Natalie looked at Lucas, and then to me. “Actually, I have to go to the ladies room. You two can talk,” she announced, “Excuse me.” She got up from her chair and walked briskly away from the table. I watched uncertainly; was she trying to punish me? Was she that upset I didn't tell her about my mental struggles? Women were so unpredictable and stubborn, I was unsure of what to think at that point. “I won't lie, Cedric ... this leaves me good opportunity to have that talk we chatted about,” piped Lucas. I turned my gaze towards him. “Y'know, Lucas, I don't know if this is the best time ...” “Bloody hell, it'll only take a minute,” he explained. I sighed. “Fine ... what is it? Make it fast.” Without my approval Lucas grabbed my glass of champagne and leaned back in his chair. He took a mouthful from the cup and swallowed, then let out a subtle breath of refreshment. He followed this by casually saying, “I need you for a job.” I crossed my arms and leaned on the table, looking at him unbelievingly. I held my stare for a few effective moments as silence followed the bomb-shell he had just dropped. Lucas remained unaffected, keeping his professional and powerful manner. He crossed his legs and folded his hands on his lap, then surveyed me with interest. An interest of business, it seemed, nothing more. This was upsetting: Lucas was a very close friend of mine, and now he expected our relationship to take a ninety degree turn. A few tense moments later, I retreated into a slouch in my seat and looked away from him. I sighed, then looked back at him. His attitude remained unchanged. “You can't be serious,” I challenged finally. Lucas chuckled. “Do I look like I'm kidding?” He was right in that sense, but that wasn't enough for me to believe him, and I was intent on making that clear. “I don't believe this,” I said, adding a short laugh. I grabbed my glass from in front of him and drained the remaining contents. “It will be a one-time thing, Cedric. It is crucial, and I hope you understand the merit of that coming from a man of my importance,” Lucas stated firmly. Another moment of silence followed. I contributed nothing to the conversation; there was nothing I could say. There was so much holding me back from accepting such an offer, and at the same time, I didn't want to disappoint Lucas. “I hope you realize what you're asking me,” I said finally. “I think I do,” Lucas returned with a smile. “Outside of the agency affairs,” I explained, then paused. After a moment of thought, I said, “There's no way Natalie will let me do this.” “And why does she have to know?” Lucas challenged. He didn't allow me to respond, and chimed in with, “That is why the agency is in place, Cedric. To protect your secrets. I thought you knew that.” “Lucas .. I don't know what to say. You're one of my best friends. And as obliged as I feel to do this, what Natalie and I went through before was too overwhelming. I can't make us vulnerable again,” I explained. “That's fair enough,” said Lucas. He got up from his chair, and paused one last time. “But remember, Cedric, you're still in my debt. That one you still owe me might not ever come up again, and I hope that means something to you. Not to mention, my power within the CIA is immense, and I'm not afraid to use it. I hope you take that into consideration. Good day.” And he strolled away, satisfied with getting the last word and leaving me tongue-tied. I sat there, speechless, like the defeated man I was. Natalie returned just as Lucas had left. I felt like it wasn't coincidental; Lucas Emerson was not known to make random decisions, even when small, and probably ended the conversation upon spotting her leaving the ladies room. And for good reason ... I wasn't sure if I was in favor of this decision or not. “You were gone for a while,” I said as she sat down across from me. “Well, I thought I'd give you two time to talk,” she said. “What was it about?” “Oh ... y'know ... nothing really. Just catching up,” I lied, shaking it off as if nothing had happened. The waitress finally came back. I had lost my appetite.
