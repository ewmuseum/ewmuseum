While the night slept...

23 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Title: 'While the night slept' For: Fresh!East (Tomas and Lavelle vs. PWC) Huntington Beach, California Two weeks ago Huntington Beach now lay silent, the breeze rustling the sand along the shore as the night sky drew overhead, the waves calmly jolting back and forth, the froth of foam curling along its colliding path with the shore, eroding the edges and soon receding. Its sandy shore, bizarrely void of any human activity as silence coursed through its beach chairs and umbrellas. His eyes fixed on the moon’s glow shimmering against the deep blue ocean surface, waves tossing and turning in the distance, a vague flicker of life beneath the deep blue vast essence and with a blink it had vanished. Pacing quietly, dressed in Hawaiian shorts, a white wife-beater and flip flops Pierce drove his hands into his pockets and stood alone on the sandy surface, breathing in the sea air, as the chilled air coursed through his body, a refreshing, tranquil feeling fluttering with a patter against his muscles as the hairs on his arms stood on end. The grit of sand splashed between the ball of his foot and his flip flop as he moved with a melodic pace toward the crashing foam frothed mouth of the ocean, a smile of serenity lined his lips. His dreams had been ripped from him abruptly, but tonight as he found himself gazing into the immeasurable world before him, he felt the dreams returning. The hope became a substantial feeling within him as he won, once again, the title. His hope was set in motion tonight...The pain wasn’t evident…The hurt dissipated in those fleeting moments… A heavy sigh emerged from his thin pinkish lips, a clog in his throat as he sat on the cooled surface beneath him, his toes burying themselves within the sand, curling around the ends of the flip flops as he watched the waves casually back and forth, moving with a monotonous movement, but simple, pure and refined. And for the first time in a long time, he thought… He thought about many things, his dodging of death on one two many occasions, his short stint with the court system and his win tonight. His blues eyes gleamed with immense satisfaction at the prospect of never facing the pain and anguish he had been faced with the last few months and for as long as he could remember, he no longer felt lost. He watched the chattering of seagulls fluttering overhead, their shadows circling over the ocean, swooning against the waves. He watched them with interest and saw how they worked in pairs, each more agile than the next, swooping and raising, tossing and turning, whilst keeping a fluid motion and structure. He rose onto his feet, patted the sand from the back of his shorts and legs and turned toward the sparkle of lights from the hotel. He did have one person to share this with, one person that remained in his thoughts day in and day out. As he strode against the thick, soft sand along the outer rim of the beach, a shaking feeling, all too familiar, rustled him from his moment of clarity. “And reality bites me in the ass…” He gazed back toward the serene scene behind him and took deep steady breaths, beads of sweat lining his hairline. A small plastic bottle touched his finger tips and he hardened his grasp turning on the heels of flip flops toward the ocean. ‘I can’t do this anymore; I can’t hide and run from my problems’ he thought looking at the medicine container. “No more…” he yelled into the abyss. He gripped the medicine container and flung it into the enigmatic world of the ocean, a small pop as it splashed into the tossing waves. “I won’t run anymore…” He turned to the hotel, tonight was a night of change, overcoming the demons and facing the angels. He walked up to the hotel, entering the silent foyer, sand sliding off the bottom of his flip flops to the marbled floor below. Little lines of bread crumbs followed him from the door to the elevator. “Lovely night, isn’t it?” A small man brushed past Pierce, his t-shirt and shorts wizened with sweat patches as he flings a small gym towel around his neck and heads toward the hotel’s spa. “Yeah, it’s amazing,” Pierce replied to nobody but himself, a smile of contentment across his face as he slid into the elevator and gazed at himself in the glass mirror. His eyes radiating against the harsh overhead light as the doors pinged open and he was alone in the hall. “Just go up to the door and knock, simple” He thought standing outside her door, as though he were a stalker, waiting in silence. His eyes caught a small designer pot filled with fake white and red roses, he scurried over, checking both directions and stole a small white rose. The ease of romantic seduction or the begging man looking for an apology, he looked once more out the window to the night’s sky and placed a hand on the door, knocking discretely. A flurry of movement, a humble murmur and the click of the latch, the door handle moving and his heart kicked up a couple notches. Eyes lingering on the moving handle. His palms clammy with sweat; the rose clutched delicately behind his back. The door opened and she stood before him dressed in the hotel dressing gown, the crook of the top, open loosely, adhering more to his imagination as he looked at her bare skin; a moment’s glance at the cleavage before turning his attention to her sweet smile and gentle blue eyes. “I’m here to apologise!” he said with a grin and moved forward, extending the white rose. Sarah took it from his hand, their skin touching discretely as she looked toward him. His hands gently rested by his sides, his hair tossed by the wind and sand on his shorts. “Come on in.” Sarah held the door open and Pierce walked in, looking in awe at the room she had booked for them. Sarah turned and saw the designer pot of roses, one white rose missing; a smirk crossed her lips as she clutched the fake rose to her chest – ‘thought that matters’ - shutting the door behind her. Pierce stood in the hall, hands in his pockets, eyes on the carpet fibres slicing between his toes. “I’m sorry, for everything that’s happened recently, I never meant to hurt you…” Sarah beamed; placing a hand on his shoulder she kissed him gently on the cheek and moved into the living area, the television playing but on low volume. Following her into the bedroom he felt a tingle run up his back, a steady pitter-patter of his heart against his rib cage, the churning of nerves as she turned to him, offering him something to drink, which he accepted politely, his throat parched. “Apology accepted,” she said sweetly kissing him gently on the lips as she strolled into the bathroom, the door open ajar. He couldn’t help but smile every time he was with her, the perky humour, the witty lines and the humorous attitude, made him love her. He stopped. The glass lingering on his lips. ‘I love her’ he thought, frozen almost in time. “You alright? You’ve gone pale!” She stood before him, changed into shorts and a t-shirt, her blonde hair hanging loose around her shoulders, still dripping from her shower. He stopped a moment, remembering the moment of clarity on the beach where everything became as clear as day for him. The stark realisation that everything would be alright. “There’s some stuff I need to tell you,” he spoke quietly. “OK, I’m all ears,” she looked toward him, waiting to see his facial expression change, but it didn’t. “Not here, maybe over dinner. You want to get something to eat?” he asked, placing the glass down and standing up. “Ever the romantic, but in case you haven’t noticed Sherlock Holmes, everything’s closed.” His eyebrows rose as he turned his focus to the clock on the wall, it was midnight. ‘So it is’ he thought and looked back toward her, now seating herself comfortably on the bed next to him, her hands gently rested by her side, inches from his. “I got it.” “Got what?” she said looking toward him, a large smile across his face. “Walk along the beach?” he asked, raising one eyebrow with a gleam of crystal blue in his eyes. “You just went for one -” she said, stating the obvious and pointing to a small mound of sand now by his feet. He reached into his pockets and pulled them inside out, small speckles of sand dropped to the floor. “Give a guy a break. I’m trying here, you always wanted a moonlit walk on the beach…” he said winking and stood up, pointing his hand to the clock. She leant back into the comforts of the bed, an eager smile across her face. ‘How’d I get a guy that likes moonlit walks on a beach?’ she thought, as she felt her heart thumping energetically, her lips suddenly dry and a tingle along her legs as he ran a finger gently along her thigh, he tickled her sides and stood up quickly as the smack of a sharp back hand almost hit him, his agility the only thing keeping her from smacking him across the shoulder. His eyes remained fixated on her luscious legs, slowly glancing up toward her midsection, a smile of glee discretely placed along her lips. “You want to go for a walk on the beach, now?” Pierce smiled, placing his hands into his pockets. “There’s a lot I want to tell you…” Rome, Italy The Cicerone Hotel sat in Via Cavour, overlooking the famous forum as the sun set gallantly along the horizon, trickles of light fluttering through the gargantuan columns, flickering shades of darkness against the walls of the conference room. The room was lit with a golden glow from the setting sun, a radiance emanating from the men circled around the table and a porter of Italy’s finest red wine was available for the men to drink. The scent of garlic lay rampant in the air as they chowed down on delicious Italian cuisine. The many men had an air of importance to them as they sat still, mumbling amongst each other, dressed impeccably in lavish tuxedo’s. One confident man, seated at the top of the long, polished table, stood up suddenly before the men gathered before him. He was a well-built gentleman of Italian descent, though his features betrayed a strong confidence inherent in him, despite his rather unorthodox appearance. The room fell silent in an instant, the men in attendance realising the severity of the matter they were all here to address. “Gentlemen, it is a pleasure to have you all here this evening. Now that we have enjoyed the refreshments sufficiently, it is time to get down to business.” The word ‘business’ slid off his tongue with impeccable ease. There was a respect in the room for the man at the top of the table, but more crucially for the subject matter of the meeting, which drew silence from all in the conference room. The man moved forward, sipping the last of his red wine, smacking his tongue against his lips, enjoying the smooth texture as it washed around his mouth. “Recently, we tragically lost a very dear compatriot and fellow businessman.” He started, making eye contact with everyone, asserting his authority – making it seem he wasn’t a man to be crossed. “You all know this man to be John Howard Lavelle. With his recent departure, he left his business and our shares in stocks in the hands of his assistant. However, this is not what is important; we now need to address his heir.” A chatter of disgruntled men arose through the fleeting silence, all in accordance as they registered the topic at hand. Sipping their wine and smoking their cigars, their eyes lingering on the man standing at the head of the table. “Gentleman, we all have opinions on this delicate matter, but as it stands…” he paused, the fine scent of Cuban cigar smoke wafting up his nose. “…without John Lavelle we’re losing sufficient funds, that were imperative to our cause here in Europe through the temporary owner.” Another man began to address the council of men; his grey hair gelled back along his olive skin. His voice was refined, polished and clean. “As Don (Sir) Ciccone has said, gentlemen, we’re losing money and stock while we maintain a stronghold within the American economy,” his fine English accent almost foreign to his now Continental looks. “John Lavelle spoke of his grandson, his heir, his protégée. It is he that should be leading the business and rectifying the situation…” “How?” a dark man moved forward, a South African accent propelling from his thick pink lips. “How you ask my dear friend. John was training his grandson through medicine, martial arts and politics all with one grand design, to take his place as owner, but we’ve run into a minor snag within this exceptional plan.” He stopped pacing, eyes remaining on the men before him. The South African nodding his head as he took a large intake of his Cuban cigar before blowing out small rings of smoke, that fluttered aimlessly toward the air conditioning unit. The Italian, Don Ciccone, inched forward, curious to discuss matters at hand. “Unfortunately, John never managed to finish his training or explain his grand design to his grandson and now, through unforeseen circumstances, his grandson no longer resides in Los Angeles, but has been on the move since our mishap with Prometheus.” “But the boy proved efficient with that, Mr. Fleming,” a rotund German moved forward in his seat, snorting as he spoke with a heavy Bavarian accent, taking large gulps of wine. “He excelled with that challenge, but that set us back, as he is now fearful and unwell. Hardly fit to take over a multimillion dollar company.” “What do you suggest we do?” a smooth talking, slicked back Frenchman spoke, the stem of his wine glass perched between two fingers. Don Ciccone now addressed the council, remaining seated as Fleming joined his side, he pointed to a man, that had stood anonymous in the corner of the room, oblivious to those seated at the table, completely forgetting about his presence. “Where there is a will there’s a way, gentleman. John left us a ‘will’ in which he gave explicit instructions for one man. I would like you all to meet Mirudo Montoya. A very influential man in Spain and Portugal,” Don Ciccone waved his hand toward the shadowed figure, stood in silence. The man moved forward, a lean muscular tone to his tanned skin and olive green eyes. Grey wisps of hair sliding through the sides of his finally combed jet black hair, a tight muscular jaw recently shaved as he walked with a confident stride to his posture, back straight, eyes straight, chin held high in his lavish black suit and deep blue shirt. The English man, Fleming, now turned to Montoya moving slowly from the darkened corner into the golden glow of the Roman sunset, its intermittent light glinting against his tanned skin. “Mr. Montoya is a long-time friend of John’s and has been a very decisive business partner for this council in Spain. Now, Mr. Montoya, would you care to explain to my fellow alumni why it is you are here.” Mirudo turned carefully, his green eyes set along the line of gentlemen before him. Each man more important in his own way, offering more than sufficient funds to this ‘council’ he found himself being brought back to on each occasion they deemed adequate. “John Lavelle was a dear friend of mine and I am his grandson’s godfather. In his will, John asked me to continue his grandson’s training,” Mirudo spoke softly, a deep Spanish lilt to his English tongue. “What makes you think you can do it? We’ve been working this plan for months since Mr. Lavelle’s passing and the boy is proving difficult to ascertain,” the German reclined into his chair, once more taking a large gulp of Italian wine. “I don’t. The young man has been through a lot, I’ve been keeping an eye on him and from everything I’ve seen, he is not ready to join the council,” Mirudo said the words justly and with defiance the council weren’t used to from an outsider. “John signed his grandson up to this, trained him for years, you do not decide what is best for him.” Fleming spoke the words with caution, looking toward Montoya, knowing full well the man was a master of martial arts. “My decision is a mere opinion. I will follow John’s wishes and look after the boy, but he will not come to this council willingly. Too much has happened to sway his faith,” Mirudo paused taking note of the German and Frenchman’s reactions. “A minor glitch in the great scheme of things, Montoya,” Don Ciccone remained standing, swirling a refilled glass of wine around his hand. Montoya was referring to the Prometheus problem which led to many a fault in John’s plan but could not be rectified as he had passed on. “You will carry out your orders, as instructed.” “Very well, as you so wish.” Montoya nodded his head, hands clasped as he inched back from the council. Eyes low to the ground, a contrast to his earlier swagger and eagerness. The crisp Italian air flew through the small back alley of the Via Cavour, the suns rays barely distinguishable behind the large cylindrical columns of the once great forum. A small scooter zoomed past, a young Italian boy eagerly riding on the bike, a clove of garlic in his hand. The familiar scent of Italian cuisine and car fumes lay rampant in the heated evening as Montoya stretched his back, pulling on the tight muscles to relax. The smell of cigar and wine in his clothing as he moved along the back alley toward the main street, over looking the remnants of the forum as his eyes shot along the Romanesque roofs till the familiar Saint Statues stood in the distance, like emblazoned warriors in the evening’s sky. The Vatican was only moments from him. A black Fiat Siena sleeked along the cobbled stones of the Piazza Adriana, coming to a halt along the bollards against Piazza Cavour. Montoya gazed toward the black Fiat Siena, hanging his head with shame as he wavered through the bollards. A tall man emerged from the car, dressed in black suit trousers and a white linen shirt. His grey hair shaved neatly along his pale skin. “Sorry for being late, Sir. I assume it didn’t go too well, Master Montoya?” the man’s refined English accent an odd noise to the Italian’s quietly passing through the Piazza. “Unfortunately Conrad, it seems we have no choice.” Montoya slipped into the back seat of the Siena and watched Conrad shut the door before returning to the driver’s seat and kicking the engine into first gear, turning out of the Piazza Adriana and heading toward the hotel. “I believe my godson is going to be in Russia.” “Russia, Sir?” Conrad asked through the visor, eyes firmly on the road, swerving passed the Italian motorists. “Got any warm clothing, Conrad? I’m afraid this will be a cold trip.” Montoya smiled, pearly whites gleaming against the passing headlights as he watched the magnificent city of Rome carefully pass by him. Cuba He stood shakily in the cold night air; hand gripped firmly on the handle of his colt magnum. His stony gaze burning into the murky abyss before him as the odd twinkling star became incandescent to the ominous sense that fluttered through the stormy ocean waters, the rustling sand blowing up against his expensive cream suit trousers. The wind rustled his shirt, the tails flapping discretely as his watched calmly into the distance. The delightful murmur of an engine became apparent as his eyes scaled the vast expanse. The engine roared against their eardrums, their hands gripped the railing beside them as the carbon bottom sprinted over the waves, tossing it every so often into the air. Despite the calm, collect nature of his usual bravado he felt the gurgling of nausea rise within him, the margarita seeping from his bowels. Rush looked on, checking his black sports watch every so often, the corner of his eye every so often gazing back toward the beach entrance – for fear of being followed. The speed boat lurched onto the sandy shore, the driver killing the engine as he slipped off his belt and waited. Five men exited the back of the boat. Rush found it hard to distinguish features in the dark light, but knew the Gucci suits and Rolex watches were far beyond FBI or LAPD allowances. A tall olive skinned man with wry black hair and grey eyes limped forward, the sharp point of his cane delving into the sand for support. “You got the money?” Rush asked, hands clasped in front of him, the colt magnum now tucked carefully into the small of his back. The olive skinned man smirked and moved forward, enough for Rush to see into his eyes. “You get your money when you complete the mission.” He spoke bluntly. Rush nodded his head in recognition of his buyers demands, the four men stood behind him each mirroring Rush’s posture. To an untrained eye it would seem uncanny, but to Rush he knew they all had munitions of some sort on their hip, waist or back. A simple switch of one hand and he would be ‘sleeping with the fishes’. “Have you got the instructions I asked for?” Rush questioned, his eyes trained on the Jeep parked under the shrubbery. Bates and Vandenberg were seated in the car, both listening in on the tantalizing conversation at hand; their partners voice steely and dry. The olive skinned man nodded, known only to Rush as – ‘Broker’. One of his men moved forward and withdrew a manila file from the inside of his suit coat, the glint of a sawn-off shotgun staring at Rush as he accepted the file, a quiver in his adams apple. “Do we have an accord?” the man nicknamed the Broker requested the only answer Rush could give him. “You’ve got yourself a deal.” Russia For now she took it easy, sipping the hot coffee balancing precariously on the duvet while she let her fingers delicately graze the keypad of her laptop, eyes fluttering with each new line she wrote, carefully skimming the text. She couldn’t focus on anything at the moment, although she sat comfortably in the best hotel, she felt empty inside, the same silence that she felt on one-two-many a night. The clock ticked monotonously in the background behind her, the televisions light flickering shadows onto the wall in front of her, the simple aspects she’d become accustomed to, to make her feel homely. The quiet night in Huntington Beach had bedazzled Sarah with more than sufficient information on many a shadowed door. As she looked back to the long walk on the beach, everything had become opaque, the familiar cliché – as clear as day – and yet she hadn’t spoken a word since his truth had trickled into her ears. She remained frozen, paralyzed by many a parody and choice. ‘This isn’t easy for me’ he had said to her that night, and at first, Sarah believed she would get the – we’re better as friends – speech, but what soon spread through his lips almost made her wish for her prior fear, it would have been more simple. As the screen flickered aimlessly in front of her, she sighed and turned over, slamming the back of her head into the pillow and rubbing her tired eyes with the ball of her fist, the slight tremor of a headache forming. ‘I don’t know what to say’ she had spoken softly whilst climbing back up onto the shore. Her words lingered between them and somehow still did. He had disappeared for a walk and remained anonymous still. Checking the clock as she opened her eyes she lifted herself from the comfort of the bed and slipped on her shoes, dressed in casual jeans, a tight long sleeve top and a white zip jumper. Sarah strolled through the hall, eyes focused on her feet as her hands were shoved into her pockets. Her breathing light and slow as she moved with a methodical pace, the storm outside eerie to her ears as thunder darted back and forth, the rolling dark clouds overheard unsettling her as she gazed out of the window, refusing to use the elevator. Her mind wandered once more to everything that was said on the beach. She had learnt so much about him that night when he’d sat her down on the sand and told her everything. She knew about his withdrawal and the drug use, the issues back in Harrisburg with his parents and even more irritating was the topic of Lois Wood, she found herself jealous of the woman that had caused Pierce pain. Although she was angry at him for all the secrets he’d held back, she couldn’t help but feel happy as well at the thought she was the reason he wanted to change. In the small bar at the hotel he found himself gazing into a glass of vodka and lime, swirling the ice cubes within as his hand gripped the small glass. The more he looked into the alcoholic beverage he felt the thirst dissipate, the need to run from his problems became less apparent as he shoved aside, watching the bar tender remove it in disgust and toss it into the sink behind her. “This seat taken?” Turning in his seat he saw the gentle blue eyes and beautiful smile gazing toward him, she smiled and sat down as she ordered water. “I know it’s a lot to take in but I just hope -” Sarah stopped him, her hand on his cheek as she leant in and kissed him gently on the lips and smiled, their eyes lingering on one another. “I’m proud of you, Pierce.” “You are?” he asked curiously staring in wonder at the change in her attitude. “Course,” she smiled and sipped her water and turned toward the bar. “In that case, how’d you feel about dinner?” he asked a smiled brimming along his lips. “Sounds brilliant, I’m starved…” Russia The silver BMW laid in patient silence in the back alley, its headlights turned off as the darkness engulphed it. The wind rattled against the window as the dark eyes watched the young man and woman cross the street. The man in the back stretched forward, his black suit becoming tangled in the seatbelt as he undid it and looked out the window toward the empty street. Pierce held the door open for her as she streaked through and shook off the droplets of rain, her hair dripping as she emerged into the foyer of the hotel. The receptionist looked toward Pierce, disgust in his eyes as he watched Pierce enter the foyer with his shirt and jeans drenched in water, the squelching sound in his shoes adding to the receptionist’s frustrations. Behind them a man emerged, impeccably dressed with dark olive skin and Hispanic features as he waltzed by the soaking young couple toward the reception desk. As they climbed into the elevator, he could feel a large smile brim across his face, the sweet tingling of nerves running up his back as he held her soft hand in his. The gentle touch of her silk skin a comfort to him as the doors closed. She stood in silence, feeling no need to talk. She had spent an hour with him and felt immense joy and satisfaction with the dinner she had eaten as she held his hand in hers. The doors pinged open, neither noticing the tall man cross by them dressed in black trousers, a shirt and a cardigan. His grey hair and pale skin unfamiliar to the usual brand of guests within the hotel. “Thanks for the dinner.” She said pulling out her door’s card key while struggling to hold the handle. She opened the door, entering into a darkened room. “My pleasure,” Pierce said with a sweet smile, standing in the doorway as he shivered slightly. Sarah switched on the lights and turned back toward him, Pierce being polite and mannerly said a sweet goodbye, kissing her gently on the lips before heading back down the hall. In the distance the man with cardigan moved forward, following Pierce as he walked down the long corridor toward his own room. The sweet sense of happiness fluttered through Pierce’s beating heart as he held his key card outstretched and held his door handle. “Mr. Lavelle.” A voice asked from the distance, almost faint to his hearing, he turned toward the dark figure in the hall behind him. Approaching him now, Pierce saw the man’s caring old eyes, his wry grey hair finely combed backward and his wrinkled skin. The refined English accent adding a touch of prosperity to the man’s clothing sense. “And who might you be?” Pierce asked, the card key lingering in his hand. “I’m merely a messenger, Sir.” The voice was English, but it was refined and well spoken. The word ‘Sir’ unfamiliar to Pierce’s ear. “Message from who?” Pierce asked quizzically. “Whom, Sir.” Pierce turned around, placed his card key into its slot and opened the door. He had had his fair share of mysterious meetings with men more secretive, lucrative and discreet and each one more dangerous than the other. His trust had begun to wane. “Please, Sir, I need you to come with me,” the Englishman pleaded, but his pleas fell on deaf ears. “I’m sorry, but I don’t know you and forgive me for my lack of manners, but I don’t trust strangers.” “I know you, Sir. Well, actually, I knew your grandfather,” the Englishman said with a blunt simplicity, his wrinkled pale hands slipping into his trouser pockets. Pierce sighed, turned back toward the man and shut his door, the two alone in the hall. “What message?” “If you’ll come with me, Sir, all will be explained.” “Can I get a change of clothing?” Pierce asked, wringing the ends of his shirt. “Afraid not, Sir, this is important.” The Englishman walked off toward the elevator, Pierce grunted and looked down the hall. “Great, so I’ll get pneumonia.” They drove in silence in a silver BMW, the polite Englishman behind the steering wheel his eyes focused on the deserted streets they drove, with nothing but the moons glow to accompany them. Pierce sat in the back, twiddling his thumbs as his eyelids gently began to resist the urge to stay awake and slowly closed. The sound of the air conditioning unit almost therapeutic as his breathing lightened, the cold almost imaginary, but he stopped, opening his eyes and tapping his knees. “Where are we going?” he asked, conversation being the key to quenching his desire of sleep. “The pier, Sir,” he replied, taking a sharp left as the window wipers dealt with the splashing of rain. “This man you want me to meet, who is he?” “You’ll see, Sir.” The car sleeked down a small pass and into the cobbled stones of the pier, the lights still lighting the harbour as boats lay abandoned in the darkened shroud of the gloomy sky. The Englishman pulled the car to a stop next to a small bench covered by a large shelter, a lowly man seated under its shelter, eyes embedded with the darkness around him. Pierce turned to the care and warmth in the Englishman’s eyes. “You may go now…” Pierce nodded and opened the car door, immediately hit by the rain as his eyes blurred momentarily with the harsh cold and salt-water. The man stood up, dressed lavishly as his eyes met those of Pierce’s, his sleeked black hair with grey lines and his olive skin shimmering against the moon’s overcastting glow. “Pierce Lavelle,” the man spoke with a thick Spanish accent. Pierce nodded, turning back toward the car, the Englishman stayed. “I see you’ve met, Conrad.” The man pointed to the Englishman in the car, the familiar light stylings of Mozart now playing from the cars stereo system. “He your butler?” “He prefers the term confidant.” “And who might you be?” “My name is Mirudo Montoya and we have a lot to discuss…”
