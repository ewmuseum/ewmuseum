Date: 30/1/06 Twisted Logic “I’m Your Villain” ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ “Take a seat…”. It wasn’t a request, his tone of voice assured Dr Bamford he had better do what he said. “So…wha’ can aye do t’ help ye?” Bamford looked up at his patient, Paddy O’Shea, confused. However his hazy expression was reciprocated in the Irishman’s eyes. Only thing was, Paddy’s eyes were misty and not in the safe way; his eyes screamed twisted logic, when right and wrong are intertwined so subtly that one would deem it possible to distinguish the two. “Paddy…it was you who phoned me.” Paddy blinked, gazing expressionless for a few seconds as the cogs generated an output. Then he nodded. Whether that nod indicated he understood was another thing. Bamford gazed around his surroundings now, trying to figure why exactly he agreed to come here to this shitty hovel in this shitty part of town in shitty New York. The place felt dead. Blackening walls save the yellow foam that protruded from gaping holes, the faded linoleum floor that was more gone than there, showing the unforgiving concrete, exposed like a open sore. And here he, a respectable Psychologist, sat as his screwball patient ran around, looking for a chair. Mental depressants….God he had seen so many in his time, but Jesus this guy was as far gone as you could go. He hadn’t been taking his medication, that was obvious and maybe the fact he didn’t reinforce it’s importance to Paddy had played on his conscience which in turn brought him here. Maybe but uncharacteristic…Bamford was more concerned with making a quick dollar with minimal work whilst pruning his ego, rather than going out of his way to actually care for patients. And so he asked himself once again Why the hell did I come here “Got me a chair Doc,” a voice came from beyond the open doorway that led into the bedroom. The voice resounded around the bare apartment alone and Bamford now realized how quiet it was outside…for such a busy city, the silence was unnatural. So no one hears you… Paddy’s shadow became cast on the wall and it served to introduce his reemergence, chair in hand. It was an old dining table chair, a match to the one he sat in. Paddy placed the chair beside his and sat down with a smile, plopping his hands onto his lap theatrically. He gave a shrill whistle and the sound of patting footsteps was now heard and from the same doorway, a black dog came. Bamford couldn’t distinguish what breed it was exactly. He seen Labrador in it but the jaw made him also think Spaniel. It moved towards Paddy and sat down between his spread legs. Only on closer examination could Bamford see the meandering scar that ran along one side of the poor mutt’s face and the gaping bald patches that corseted down his back. “Aye call him Rebel,” Paddy muttered as if to an empty room, as he petted the dog softly. “Found him on the street, thought aye’d take him in because he was in a bad state. Ye know, care fer him, someone in need who wants help.” Paddy’s eye’s now darted up to meet Bamford but he looked away quickly - the dog was as much a mongrel as the man but it was the latter who carried enough madness to contort what intelligence he had, and he was a danger. “Aye’ve got a problem Doc.” Bamford took his gaze up from the floor so he was looking into the unmoving eyes of the patient. In this lifeless apartment…Bamford realized those eyes were the deadest things in here, spiraling blackness that threatened to bring forth a crippling scream. Feeling his hands chattering his knee caps, Bamford cleared his throat and forced out the easiest thing he could say. “O-oh?” ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ “Why won’t she phone me?” Paddy whispered. Sitting in the desolate bar in the wee hours of Sunday morning was something he wasn’t a stranger too, being alone save the bartender likewise. But being sober in this scenario. It was just surreal. With only a half filled pint as company, Paddy mopes in a dark corner of the bar, the fireplace is beside his table see and the warmth is both welcome and comforting. “Maybe she doesn’t love me anymore…this is a hint perhaps”. Paddy muttered again, running a finger tip around the rim of his glass. Well, you always have me the pint cooed back. And this made Paddy smile, even if it was plastic. In Paddy’s head, this voice that seemed to come from the occasional lifeless object, was feminine. Whether this was Paddy’s way of compensating for those lacking there of, he didn’t know, hell, he was too depressed to get onto any other question apart from (Why won’t she call me?) It didn’t matter. What mattered was he was spiraling out of control all because he wouldn’t take his medication - although, not exactly his fault as the charming voice persuaded him, they would only make him worse. Who needs her anyway? Me and you, we can go anywhere, do anything. You can fight Paddy’s eyes lit up. Oh yes, I can tell you like to fight. Who is it you‘re fighting this week hunny? “Patrick Mapleleaf,” Paddy replied, now feeling better. Another Patrick! Darling name! Are you going to hurt him Paddy Paddy squeezed the edge of the table in a steady, quick rhythm. Squeeze…squeeze…squeeze. Thick blue veins wriggled up the white underside of his slim forearm like a snake. As he gazed, completely fixed on the opposite wall, he knew how strange it was, here having a conversation with no one What about me?! Oh sorry, yes, you of course… Well, having a conversation, almost with himself. But even that was strange, I mean voice needed a mouth to make noise…and well, it was just damn strange. Thing was, when you’re so down and depressed, your self esteem so low you begin to plan how you’ll end it all, it’s then that you might accept your madness and accept the voices. Not as real entities, but that they exist only in your head and you are in fact an outsider, irreparable and so must be cordoned off from normal, fully functional society. “Aye…shouldn’t be talkin’ t’ voices…it’s just not right.” Don’t say that! I am real, I want to help you! Paddy clasped his head with his hands, allowing his nails to dig into his flesh as he dragged them downwards. And then the blame game started. “People…people have done this to me…they’re makin’ me crazy…who…who did this?” Darling, it isn’t so hard to see. Just think… And he thought. He had trouble remembering things these days, his head was too busy being crazy and shit to store any information. But a few names did pop up. “Well…aye remember being at the wrestling arena. No wait, aye was in hospital…an’ aye remember Mike Wade and Shelley…and he threw her out of the room.” But she won’t call you…Mike Wade seems like a nice person for being mean to mean people. Right? Paddy blinked. What was he doing again? “Right…an’ aye remember getting’ out an’ seein’ me Da…but he’s family, he wouldn’t hurt me…” So who does that leave? “…well…aye went t’ the other Doctor…Psychologist fella…an’…” Who does it leave Paddy? Paddy suddenly jumped up from his seat and dived his hands into his pockets, fumbling about desperately. Then he pulled out a small plastic tube with a number of tablets in it. “Aye…he gave me these. These tablets…he’s been makin’ me go MAD! It‘s him!” Paddy was hyperventilating now. He looked once more at the tube (not caring to notice it was actually FULL) and then threw it into the fire. Speaking of which, a voice decided to reenter to stoke a completely different kind of fire. And who did this to you? ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ “Bamford…” Bamford positively quivered now as he seen the famished stare in O’Shea’s eyes. He was one of the three little pigs, sat beside the big bad wolf. He cleared a blockage in his throat and then- “Paddy…listen…those tablets were to help you…I mean, are you sure you used them?” Paddy chuckled and began stroking the dog again. Well, it wasn’t really stroking, it was more grasping the mutt’s neck and releasing. It was strange how he worked it, his fingers moving into the hairy mass and out again, in and out, in and out. He never stopped, never dropping a beat. “Aye bet ye didn’t think aye would kno’, ye thought aye was just some stupid Irish gypsy…no worries. Aye kno’ yer type, ye don’t like t’ be hit, right? Ye wear yer shirt an’ ties, and use yer big words…but when it comes to talking.” Paddy raised a fist. “Real talking, yer as equipped as a bag of shit.” Bamford said nothing. It seemed to be the wisest choice. “See, if ye had have jumped me an’ hit me, aye wouldn’t have minded. Happens t’ me every week in AWC. Aye’m small and weigh next t’ nothin’ so aye’m a soft target. Aye try t’ be smart, aye try t’ fight back…but they always grind me down. Just because aye’m me…so if ye had have jumped the parade, aye wouldn’t have cared…but yer tryin’ t’ fuck with me head…an’ all aye really care about now is finding’ out why…” Bamford went on saying nothing, trying to keep a poker face. There was a dismaying tingle coursing up his spine now while his heart picked up speed like a train tearing through the countryside. He heard the dog yelp and looked down. Paddy was still squeezing-releasing the dog’s neck, squeezing it so damn hard so vein were fat with blood. “Paddy…the dog…you’re hurting it.” Paddy squeezed again, this time harder and the dog yelped once again. “Paddy, please…stop it now so we can talk!” Paddy squeezed again right up until the point Bamford thought for sure Paddy would release and the dog would fall lifeless. He could see his nails digging into the dog’s neck now… “PADDY!” That snapped him out of it, but not in a good way. He lifted the dog off the ground and threw it across the room. It slammed off the wall and scuttled off quickly, whimpering. Incredibly, Paddy sat back down smiling. “Aye wasn’ goin’ t’ hurt him…just scare him.” Bamford seemed to breath a sigh of relief. That didn’t last long. Paddy plopped his hand down on Bamford’s crotch and squeezed it. “Pa…Pa…Padd-” “Shuuuuuuush,” Paddy whispered soothingly. He smiled at Bamford who issued a hoarse little howl. This wasn’t no fun. Don’t hurt me…Please don’t hurt me. That’s what Bamford wanted to say, but he couldn’t catch his breath for long enough to string a word together never mind a sentence. Paddy leaned in close so that Bamford could smell the combination of beer, cigarettes and piss on him. Then he curled his fingers around his testicles. Bamford howled once more, Paddy squeezed hard until he shut up. “Yer gonnae tell me why…ye kno’ why yer gonnae tell me?” Bamford eyes were gaping as he shook his head numbly, his skin sticky with sweat. Paddy squeezed harder and now Bamford knew why, as a fresh wave of pain rolled from his crotch to his stomach and threatened to come out his mouth. “These tablets of yers…they hurt me. Sent me mad see, so much tha’ me girlfriend can’ even bear t’ look at me. So much tha’ when me friends come t’ see in the hospital, aye keep thinking’ they’re attackin’ me…like tha’ Mike Wade fellow…he’s a friend o’ mine, we were in Eire Óg together. And ye took tha‘ away from me, didn‘t ye?” Bamford didn’t answer, paralyzed to the point that he couldn’t even indicate with his head. Paddy made an inarticulate growling sound and squeezed his testicles yet further. And now the pain did come up his throat as he tasted acidy vomit in his mouth, that stinged the back of his mouth. As he screamed in a high pitched voice, he realized that was the least of his worries. Still Paddy was calm, as he stared into the far wall. “Aye should kill ye right now. Who does this to someone they don’ kno’? How could ye? Yer tablets, yer evil!” Paddy, pulled Bamford around by the scrotum so he was facing him. He was staring right into his eyes now. Paddy smiled a toothy smile, and Bamford felt like screaming all over again. “No’ ye answer me…if ye lie, aye’ll feckin’ squeeze yer balls till they pop…” Paddy applied more pressure and now blots of darkness flashed over his vision as he willed himself to faint. This was one question he just couldn’t answer the way Paddy wanted him to. “Ye won’t lie t’ little old me…will ye?” “NO!” Bamford wept as Paddy squeezed once more ruthlessly. “WHY? AYE WANT T’ KNO’ WHY!” Bamford looked at Paddy and half laughing, half screaming shouted at him “I didn’t do anything! I wanted to help you!” As Bamford’s voice separated from his head, he slumped sideways in a faint. He was sheet white, to the point Paddy paused for a second to consider if he was dead. Then decided he could be bluffing and squeezed until the tendons on his arm stood out. “WHY! TELL ME NOW OR AYE’LL KILL YE!” Paddy looked at Bamford again then released his grip. Confused, he got to his feet and began to shake Bamford. “Aye don’t…aye didn’t want t’ hurt ye…just scare ye…” Then reality sunk in…he had just assaulted a doctor. They were going to lock him up in a screwball asylum and he’d never see Shelley again. Oh Jesus…oh crap…. Paddy picked up a coat, and darted for the door, leaving Bamford where he lay. He whistled for the dog and opened the door, letting the pair out. Just as he closed the door with a click, he looked back in, hyperventilating as he realized the shit he was in now. “Aye jus’ wanted t’ scare ye….please…oh shit.” Paddy closed the door with a click and ran off. Once again he was headed for the crippling lows. END
