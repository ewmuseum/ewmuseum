The Essence of My Obsession (2)

13 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10 - 13 - 06 Do you think we could make it all the way to the moon if we continued to drive. If we continued to drive, could we coast the distance to the sun with just one eye open and our lips locked together? Is it a believable theory that becoming one with our atmosphere, melting in the rays of sun light, is a tranquil inevitability? Is it possible that theorizing is one of the many acts of the Devil? I don't know. I don't really care, though it is on my mind, because Melody is beside me and I'm holding her hand. I could feel perspiration sliding down my back and my neck, and the air seemed to get thinner the more we drove into the darkness. I didn't know where I was going! I had no idea if there was a Super-8 looming around the lonesome trail we were on. I took this women with an intention and it seems as if that intention is under water now. I don't know why she's in the passenger seat, and I don't know why she's holding my hand. Michael: I need to tell you something. Melody: Oh no. You're not gay, are you? Michael: No? What would make you think that? Melody: I don't know. We haven't spoken since we left the bar. Michael: I've got a lot of things on my mind. I'm sorry I brought you out here, and I'm sorry I said there was a Super-8 motel around here. Right when I said that, I noticed a sharp glow peaking out from a forrest of trees. As we got closer, I saw an entranceway into a parking lot, and then a brown building, and then several doors stretching from the beginning of the building and to the end of it. I couldn't believe my eyes; it was actually a motel. The place was called Froggy's Cabana and it had a Hawaiian theme for decoration. Fake palm trees surrounded the external quarters of the motel, along with cute little ponds swarming with frogs, sand everywhere, and beach chairs overlooking the expressway Melody and I were on. With this miraculous sighting, I applied safety netting for my dangerous statement. Michael: ..But there's Froggy's Cabana. A marvelous 'R & R'! Melody: This place? It doesn't even look like it's open. Michael: It does seem a little half-lit. Do you want me to check it out? In a realistic world, you're not going to get anywhere with a lady if you expose aggression and anger at the plate. There has to be concern, feeling, and brittle emotions. You need to make her feel secure and warm, and she needs to understand that you're there for her in every way imaginable. This will open up the floodgates, like the arrival of four oceans, and she will feel as complete as ever. She'll belong to me, if only for one night, and she'll like it that way. I pulled into the dark parking lot and randomly picked a slot. There were two trailor-tractors parked in the back, along with a blue Sedan, all four of its tires blown out, and a topless, charred Jeep Liberty. I spotted a couple of kids actually sleeping inside the jeep, while a motel sits beside them. That had to mean something, right? I opened the door and noticed Melody unstrapping her seatbelt. Alarms, like school bells, sounded off inside my head. SILLYWORLD BREAKDOWN Michael: NO. Don't go anywhere! Melody: What?! Why? Michael: Why? Be-because I said so, Melody. Sit the FUCK down. Melody: Excuse me? Who do you think you're talking to, you're fucking child? Michael: I don't have any fucking children, but if I had some talking the way you do I'd lock them in a closet and swallow the fucking key. That's what I would do, Melody, you obnoxious little jezebel. You strut yourself, like you're queen of a new world, in that bar and barrel down alcohol like you're some hairy Irishman, but I know it's all fake. You can't handle the pressure of a nice guy approaching you and treating you out to a GOOD night. Simply because it's ADVENTUROUS, you manage to smoke out the negatives and shove them up my ass. How does it feel to know that you're shoving negative overtones up my sweet white ass, Melody? Melody: Fuck you, you freak! I could feel wires snapping undone in my neck as I yanked my head to the right. Michael: A freak? Yes? A FREAK?! SILLYWORLD BREAKDOWN The ghost of me propelled from within and covered Melody's entire body. I could hear a whistling-pitched scream ringing in my ear drums but noticed she was blankly staring at me, with her mouth completely closed. Her eyes had a somber hopelessness to them, like a fish whose got a hook caught in his throat. Deep down inside, in that mushy little fish brain, he or she knows that there time's coming. They also know that there's nothing that can be done. There's no emergency warning for that fish; when it's evident that the inevitable is upon it, it'll relax and allow death to have it's way with it. She looked as if she were hooked onto a similar hook, a hook that I casted out. I can hardly remember the days that I felt normal; where the simple things in life pleased me. My childhood is a failed memory, fading like a heroin addict, and I have absolutely no control of its tail spin. What was I doing with this lady in my car, anyway? Who the fuck am I, anyway? I didn't want to kill her. I had no intention of hurting this woman in any way. This wasn't an obsession for innocent bloodshed. No, this was an amazing attraction, wasn't it? I didn't want to kill her, did I? Shit, maybe I did. Okay, what if I wanted to kill her and didn't? That's not a crime, right? Right. It wasn't a crime, and that very thought was what kept things warm anf fuzzy this whole time. I didn't feel the coldness of my anxiety and depression in the bar because I knew that if I had felt the desire to commit to something like that, I knew that I really wouldn't ever do it. No, I find better things to do; things that won't get you in trouble. Melody was in a certain line of fire at the time, when I really wanted to be 'adventurous' for the night, and now it seems like I've lost interest in her. She's constantly chewing her nails, combing her bangs, applying lipstick touch-ups, and reading. When I met her in the bar, she was 'touching up' on her World War II history. While driving in my eddggy silverish green Geo Metro, she was flipping through pages of a giant poetry book. She's barely social, but her smile makes me want to pull the car over to the side and rip the clothes off of her back. I can't stand her personality, but I want to fuck her. Michael: You're coming in with me? Melody: I don't have to if you don't want me to. Michael: Well, you can. I just think the place is a little shady. Melody: It's just half-lit. Michael: That's where it's primarily shady, isn't it? Melody: No, not really. Shadiness doesn't have a primary division. I mean, it could be anywhere. It could be staring right in front of you, or it could be a couple houses away. You can never know, and experimenting is the only way we can. Michael: So.. you want to know, then? Melody: Sure. Michael: Fine, let's go. Both of our doors shut simultaneously, and we walk shoulder-to-shoulder to the entrance. I allow Melody to slide in, and then take a couple steps in when I see something bulging out from the wall. It's a giant Hawaiian holding a ukelele, and grinning like he just clamboxed a giant bong-pack in a crawlspace. The whole place is dressed down in hot, stylistic colors and the theme music seems to surround you from the moment you walk in. I semi-enjoyed this place, if only for their effort to entertain. This, however, was completely strange. It was out in the middle of nowhere, with barely anyone around. They must only get the people that get lost, or people that blow out a tire and have to walk to find service. In that sense, it's fitting, and even seemingly clean. So, now we're at this motel. I don't even want to be with this girl anymore, but I can't just dump her out in the middle of nowhere. I really don't want to stay at a themed motel with her, and it's because I know I'll have regrets afterwards. I don't even know if she likes me; she's really seemed moreso frightened than anything else. I haven't done a thing to frighten her, but that's what happens to uptight women. They start to feel like their 'little corner' is being violated, and that's when they completely close shop. It's as if they turn into a completely different person as that violation occurs, and it's hilarious when you honestly don't know how the violation developed in the first place. As we approached the front desk, a man, dressed in a frog suit, pops out from the back door. I could smell smoke, and then noticed a trail streaming out from the room. I took a whiff, only ciggarettes. Too bad, I probably would've asked him for some pot, if only to loosen this chick up. I couldn't believe how theoretical she got on me in the car. Unbelievable. Froggy: Welcome to da' Cabana bana! I'm FROGGY! Michael: Hi. Melody: The 'Froggy' that owns this place? Froggy: Yes! Michael: Wow. Melody: Awesome! What a creative idea, Mr. Froggy. Froggy: It's George Peckinbush. Mr. Peckinbush, but thank you for those kind words. Michael: Terrific. Can we get a room? We don't want a suite, or a heart-shaped bed. Just one queen-sized mattress and box spring, cable television.. Melody: Smoking, please? Michael: You smoke? Melody: Yeah, sorry. I could feel my testicles shrivel up. I hate women that smoked. I could just picture the yellow tar dangling off of her choppers. Michael: ..add an ashtray to the order, please. Froggy: Complimentary ashtrays in all rooms! Michael: Great! Thanks! What's the room number? Froggy: Twenty-one. Michael: Key? He handed me the key and I started to walk away. Under Froggy's breath, I heard him say something about me. Froggy:(applying sneaky under breath tactics)Rotten, obnoxious man.. Michael: Excuse me? Melody: He didn't say anything, let's go. She grabbed my arm. Do not, in any circumstance, grab my arm. I don't grab yours, do I? Michael: Remove your hand from my arm, please. He just called me a 'rotten something something man', Melody. That's not something to take lightly. Melody: Yes it is. Michael: What are you talking about? It's an insult. You must be thicker than a fucking coconut. She ignored that comment. I knew this because of how quickly she shot back her response. Melody: You know what? This is out of control. I don't know what your deal is, but I'm not going to stay with you in your motel room. Uh, what? Michael: What? Melody: Froggy, or whatever you want me to fucking call you, I need a separate room. If possible, neighboring rooms. I'm not trying to get away from you, but I don't want to be a part of whatever is going on in your hand. You deal with that in your motel room, and when you start making sense, come to my room. Number, frog? Froggy: Seventeen? Melody: That'll do. Michael: Are you kidding me? Melody: Key, frog? The frog-man then tossed her the Room-17 key and Melody walked off. I was amazed at what just happened, and I didn't know how I should've felt at that moment. To consolate, I smiled and headed over to my motel. I opened it and put one foot in the door before I slammed it back shut. No. I didn't come all this way, and I didn't approach this woman, for absolute dogshit. No way. This will not happen. She called me a 'freak' and defended someone, whom she knew even less than me, that called a 'rotten, obnoxious man'. To top all of that, she's a know-it-all, prissy BITCH. What was I thinking thinking that she had promise. There was never promise in her, and I was blinded by those lovely red locks. The hair is never consolation for a personality, and the color of that is a defecating shade of purplish brown black, delightfully entitled Blurpack! I turned around and started to walk towards Melody's room. My obsessive intentions were getting the best of me, and I could almost feel the steam pushing out through my ears. I crossed over a patch of grass, which divides the motel rooms from the motel station and noticed, through the open door, Froggy laying back with a newspaper in his hands. Michael: Hey there, George. Froggy: Sir? Michael: Oh, now I'm getting the 'sir' treatment? Just because a woman isn't by my side you immediately resort to the suspicion that there's going to be violence, right? Froggy: Excuse me? Please, no violence in the Cabana. Michael: Nope, there isn't going to be any violence. I have no intention of hurting you, Peckerbush. Froggy: It's 'Peckinbush'. Michael: I honestly don't give a fuck. Froggy: ..Okay. Michael: I'm sorry, it's just me being honest. Do you know my last name? No, you don't. In fact, you jotted down zero information for either one of us when we booked rooms. You musn't care to know my last name, so I don't care to know your last name. However, I found your 'last minute' comments rather impudent and unnecessary. Be a gentlemen, and say what you have to say in front of my face. You barely know me from a Goddamn hole in the wall, and you had the nerve to insult me because I'm having a bad day? Froggy: You're absolutely right. I noticed his edginess, and he realized that there were possibilities for a threat. His eyes were incredibly alert, and seemed to be on-guard for something random to happen. All of this was taking place, and he had no desire to settle his 'disapprovals'. I had the advantage, and I felt like milking it a bit. I moved in on and took it upon myself to walk into his booth. He backed himself into a corner and I stood at the at the end. I could literally hear him heaving for a sturdy wave of oxygen. Froggy: Listen, I said I'm sorry. I don't want to fight. I have to run a business. I looked around, but didn't feel like getting side-tracked by how terrible of a businessman he is. I just wanted to present a little bit of harmless intimidation. My arms were at my side, innocently lifeless, the whole time. Michael: I'm not going to fight you, George. Christ, I'm barely a fighter myself. Words of advice, though.. Froggy: I'm all ears. Michael: If you ever, and I mean ever, say something that's completely out-of-line, and directed at me, under your breath again, I'm going to break your neck. This is the movies, Frog. I will twist your neck until it literally snaps. Do not disrespect me as a human being. Froggy: I wasn't sir, I wasn't. Michael: Then we're square now. I turned around and walked out of the station. I started to walk down the second aisle of motels, and held back my laughter. I could just about picture Froggy urinating in his pants and crying to his mother that he was just threatened by some guy with no name. Room #15. Room #16. Room #17. I was here, and she was inside there. The girl of my dreams, or the girl of my nightmares? I suppose the way to truly find out is to go into this room. I wanted to kill her. 'Knock..Knock.. Melody: Yeah? Michael: It's Michael. Listen, I'm sorry about before. I just lose my head when I don't know my surroundings. Silence, if only for a moment. Melody: Do you want to come in? Michael: Yeah. Yeah, Melody. I'd like that. 2*
