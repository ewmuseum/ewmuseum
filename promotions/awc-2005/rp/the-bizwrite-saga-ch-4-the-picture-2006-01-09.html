The Bizwrite Saga, Ch 4: The Picture

9 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/9/06 When I was younger, I wasn’t a man for possessions. I was always told they were never things you needed, and that you should only keep what is necessary. The keys to life were a good working attitude a love for God, and a sense of brotherhood. These things were reinforced for what seemed like forever. Every man has something they need, though, in which you shouldn’t. For some people it’s that Ferrari, for others it’s that big screen TV. Even the holiest of holies like my self have to succumb. It’s a humanity that is inevitable. For me, this thing was my photo album. Mine, though, was different. You couldn’t find pictures of me having fun, goofing around with my buddies. I didn’t bother with any of that shit…life was for working and praying, not fucking around. My album was filled with a single picture. The picture was of a person that, while I was younger, I knew I’d marry her. I knew that, for the rest of my life, she’d be mine, without a doubt. Liz Bizwrite, first page center. Without fail I could look into my photo album and see that picture sitting there, her face smiling back at me. It was my bright star in my working life…it was as if we were already married, for Christ’s sake! I couldn’t live without it. She gave me the picture when I was 15 years old. When I wasn’t working or in school, I was with her, talking or flirting or whatever 15 year olds did. I remember the day when she gave it to me. “Tim,” she said, “Do you have any pictures of me?” I laughed, shaking my head. What would a girl be doing asking a foolish question like that? A boy, with a picture of a girl? What were we, fucking? “No!” I exclaimed, trying to hold back the laughter. I then looked at her face—a feeling of sadness struck her down, hard. I could see her staring at the pavement, looking like she was about to cry. “Um…er…why do you ask?” “Well,” she said, fighting the tears back at the notice of me seeming to care, “I just thought that, like, you know, me and you are wicked close, so it’d only make sense for you to have a picture of me.” “Wicked close? How so?” “Well, you know. I love you Tim.” Shocked. Love? She loved me? Isn’t that what married people do? Isn’t that reserved for a later date? What would God say? What wouldn’t I say? My God, would this be true? “Love me?” I asked, questioning her. “Oh, not like that, silly!” She slapped the side of my arm, deflating all the dreams I might’ve had. “You’re my best friend! I love you, Tim!” So that’s what that picture was to her. To her, it was her way of saying “Don’t forget me!” It was her way of keeping me in her life forever. For what, though? So later on I could bail her out? This picture, to her, was to insure that friends would become nothing more. That friends would be friends would be friends. She wanted nothing else—just friends. I wasn’t on the “fuck” ladder…just very high on the friend’s ladder. To me, though, the picture was something else. It was a life, an entity. I could find a soul in it, see something new every time I looked at it. Through that picture, I could live, breathe, be. That picture kept me running. She was stunning in it. Her parents must’ve had it professionally done or something, because it looked perfect. Her black her reflected the light and made it look like it’s own entity, each strand probably encased in its own case of shine. Her skin smooth and silky, a finger nail could scratch across it and cut it. Her smile white and perfect, each teeth looking exactly like it should. And those eyes…I could lose myself in them. Often I have, over and over again. The picture, ultimately, was perfect. She was my goddess, my Venus, the thing I lived for. There was no other woman I loved more, except maybe my mother, rest in peace. She was everything. Every day, I’d arrive home after working at the construction yard after school and walk into my room, collapsing onto my bed. Liam would always take a giant shit, so I was always left to myself. The long day would exhaust me, my body wanting to shut down. On the brink of passing out, though, I’d force myself to sit up and pull the album out from under my bed. When I peeled it open, there she was, smiling at me. That picture was the reminder that, yes, there is something after all this. When I retire to marry, everything will be better. Always, though, the realization would hit me like a sack of fucking bricks. Liz and I were, as she said, best friends, and nothing else. There would be no growing old on the rocking chairs in front of our house. In fact, more likely than not, there would be nothing more than friends until after she went off to college or where ever…then it was gone, nothing, nada. After a long time of coming home to see this picture, I would ignore the realization and just jump into the fantasy. It was my portal into a world that I couldn’t live in, my door to a place which I could never experience. After high school, my father was already dead for years so Liam and I just worked full time, living in my apartment. Liz did go off, she ran away…but I didn’t want to accept the fact, the fact that she went off with a n*gger. I wanted to live in my fantasy world, believing she was still here in my life. I removed the picture from the album and kept it with me, placed in my wallet next to the picture of my mother and my father, rest in peace. I threw the album out and went along with business. I didn’t need to look at the picture, either; knowing it was there was enough for me regardless. While Liz lived in her made up world out west, I lived a reckless life. Liam and I were the leaders of the Eagle’s Nest, the neighborhood clan based on our strong belief for the White Lord and a sense of family in community. When disputes occurred, we were looked upon for solutions. When we had to solve problems, we were looked upon for the answers. And when n*ggers and mics and all them were fucking around too close to the Nest…then we became the Furious Fists of God, striking down these fuckers. One day, I caught some young Eagles smoking reefer in the Wendy’s parking lot on 3rd and Yard. After some slapping around, I pulled the answer out from them: Which fucker sold you these? “It was an Eagle!” the main kid cried, writhing as I held him against the ground. *SMACK!* I hit him across the face, sick of his bullshit lies. “No Eagle would sell this n*gger shit! Now, who sold it to you? Tell me, damn it!” “Ok! It was Joe Herra! From Madison Ave.!” I paused for a moment, trying to remember who that was. I have seen a lot of drug dealers, and as long as they aren’t infesting our Nest, they can do whatever the hell they want and send themselves to hell all they’d like. “Who’s he?” “Um…small guy…always has, like, 3 guys with him. He has guns, Tim!” “Yeah, well, I have the White Lord, right?” Liam and I went back home and prepared for what we were going to do that night. We dressed in our black, pulling on the ski masks and the gloves as well. We became as black as night, almost appropriate: doing the Lord’s bidding against a n*gger, looking like one as well. We figured out the best way to get to Madison and then grabbed out weapons, a hand gun each, and headed our way there. Jumping from yard to yard to alley to street, we snuck our way to Madison Ave, stopping in a distraught alley before the street. Liam poked his head out to look for this Herra before we went out there to do our business. “Oh, I see him. Yeah, I see him allright.” “How many with him?” “Three fellas…” “No, Liam, don’t give them that name. Fellas is our word, not theirs.” He turned back into the alley, giving me a funny look. He shook his head with a puzzled grin. “Right now, you’re correcting what I call them? You kidding me, Tim?” “No, Liam, I am not. We’re the fellas, the men, the Lord’s people. They’re the rats, the meat…just hunting each other and killing each other. Nothing…just a bunch of fuckers afraid of a White Lord who hates ‘em.” “Well, whatever, are we gonna do this?” “Yeah, one second.” I pulled my wallet out and then slid out pictures of my mom and dad. I said “I love you” to both, and then pulled out a picture of Liz. I lost myself in her world for a moment, but then I realized I was killing a folk she loved. She betrayed me. This was my revenge. I put the pictures and placed the wallet back in my pocket, shooting Liam a nod. I walked out of the alley way and Liam followed me from behind, keeping his hand on the gun in his pants. I stuck my arms out to the side, trying to catch them off guard in a sense. I needed to make sure they didn’t shoot a fella like myself, walking towards them all in black. “Hey! What are you guys doing here?” “Who da fuck are you, man?” I kept at my pace, slowly making my way towards them. We were about 100 feet away now, getting closer and closer. I stayed calm while Liam remained ready to kill at any moment. “Don’t worry about that. The question is, what are you guys doing here? Don’t you know what this is?” “No, man, I don’t…inform me, n*gga, please!” I cringed when he associated me with one of…*cough* THEM…but I kept walking. I had to remain cool unless I wanted a body full of lead. “This is the Eagle’s Nest. And here at the Eagle’s Nest…” “Yeah, I kno what da fuck da Nest is about! All you racists, man! Hating a brother!” “Sure, whatever. That doesn’t change the fact that YOU are selling marijuana and who knows what else to my Eagles. Frankly, I don’t appreciate that.” Now, we were no more than 7 or 8 yards away, his cronies grinning and laughing at us while he stood there, pseudo-defiantly putting me off like I was nothing. “Man, suckas wanna buy, I’m selling, man. I suggest you walk da fuck away ‘less you want yo’ toe ta have a tag, y’know?” “I understand what you’re saying, but it won’t happen, Joe. At least not to me.” “The fuck you saying, man? And how you know my name?” “Don’t worry about that. Let me talk, all right? You’re lucky I didn’t send one of my Eagle Soldiers here…you’d be dead before knowing it, unlike now.” “The fuck?” “Just listen. I’m giving you two options. Option one is you go back to your hell holes and your ghettos and sell to the rest of the n*ggers.” “Number 2?” “You die.” He walked up to me, getting no farther than 3 feet. With a smile on his face, he flipped me off right to my face, his three cronies laughing. I wasn’t, though. “Ok, then…” *BAM!* In one swift motion, I pulled out my gun and put one in his skull, blowing him back. His cronies were quick too, though…or at least quick enough to get off a shot before Liam took them all down. They were all in a bloody heap now. But I was down, too. After Liam finished firing, he saw me collapsed and rushed over to me, hovering over me. I could see through the mask that he was concerned, but it left his face when I sat up. “Oh fuck.” I said, shaking my head. “What the fuck happened, Tim?” “Shit… I don’t know…my leg, though…” “Oh shit! You got shot!” I did…but there was no blood. Just a hole…leading into my pocket? I didn’t have time to question it right now, though, as the police was always eager to arrest a lover of the White Lord like myself. Liam lifted me and helped me through some alley ways until we were far enough to be safe. He laid me down, not wanting to damage the unknown, and immediately pulled my pants down. But there was no wound, nothing…the skin looked untouched. “What the fuck? Where is that damn bullet?” He then rummaged through my pockets and pulled out my wallet. Lodged into it was the bullet, sitting there like it belong. I opened the wallet up and pulled out the piece of metal, seeing what it went through. A few business cards, some money, and then… Destroyed. Ruined. Blown to bits by one of those damned n*ggers. ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- “Tim, I still don’t see why we’re visiting this whore.” I was stuck in a day dream, driving across the country. Remembering this…the picture…made me lose my mind. I woke out of my day-coma to look at Liam, gripping the wheel. “Just drive, Liam.”
