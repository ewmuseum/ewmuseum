Remembered

24 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/24/06 It was the first time in a week that Anton smiled. He caught his sister in his arms as she bounded out of the gate, squeezing her tightly. She was just like he had remembered her; lively, lithe, graceful, and she still sported that wild hair of hers. He hugged her close as they exchanged greetings in the middle of the airport like so many others that had been reunited with loved ones from Africa. The situation was not so dire as in some other parts of the world, but one thing that made Anton happy was family. He had been away for so long. It was a wonder that his sister—a university student—would on a whim decide to travel to Los Angeles. Luis Ferrara, dressed in a smart suit with a stylish hat of the same cream color, looked on the proceedings with interest. His eyes frequently darted to the long, brown legs that extended past Anton's little sister's denim minishorts. He gulped and adjusted his collar, looking away. A conflict of interest. He was going to break one way or another. He'd betray either his client or his lusts. Tipping his hat over his eyes, Luis strolled away for a bit. He needed a drink. Anton sat in the middle of the three, traveling back to the apartment. Nneka, Anton's sister, rattled off current happenings in excited Igbo. He was genuinely surprised to hear that she was studying in France rather than in Nigeria; she had been born in Lagos, not Paris, like he had. However, his family had enough money to pay for things like that. When her subject turned to her social life, however, Anton tuned out. He wanted to hear about his mother and father, but he would let her get to it rather than directing the conversation. After all, it had been seven years since the last time he'd even spoken to his sister—and that was over the phone—so he wanted to let her talk. Luis's eyes were focused outside the window. He tried to keep himself separate from Anton. His chin rest in his hand as he thought. What the hell was he going to do about all this? He thumbed inside his suit pocket, pressing his fingers against the small, tightly-wrapped pouch of white powder. He didn't know when he was going to use it. He carried it around with him as something of a good luck charm. After all, he didn't know when he was going to find a hooker willing to bend over for him. Anton looked up as Nneka shook him, asking him whether or not he wanted to know what was going on at home. ''Of course,'' Anton responded in Igbo. He adjusted himself in his seat so he could face his sister a bit more. ''What is going on?'' ''Everyone is well,'' Nneka responded proudly. ''Mama was sick some time ago, but she got better. The family has moved to Abuja, except for Charlie; he stayed in Lagos. He has a business now.'' Anton smiled. His youngest sibling had always wanted to be an entrepeneur. ''Oh? What does he do?'' Nneka laughed. ''He makes a fortune selling your fighting tapes,'' she responded. Anton's smile faded a bit. He didn't expect his brother to ride coattails, but he was a resourceful kid. Well, resourceful man, he supposed. ''He sells others, too, but those go the quickest. He runs a video store. Charlie's Good Times Video.'' Anton rolled his eyes; he had seen too much of the world to be wowed by the idiotic catch phrases and 'eye-grabbing' names Nigerian companies used. ''So everyone is doing well, then?'' Anton asked for clarification. Nneka nodded and Anton responded in kind. ''That's good.'' Anton relaxed a bit. He had half-expected to hear something terrible had happened to his mother or father, or even perhaps Charlie. Moving to Abuja was unexpected, but not bad. That was very well. He did not want Nneka's visit to be drowned out by his grief. Nneka sat across from Anton, looking at her brother intently. A black bag sat between them on the ground. She smiled at him, grabbing the bag by the strap and pulling it towards her. Anton glanced over at the television set; it was off and he didn't have the remote. He couldn't get up and get it, either. That would be rude. Slowly, he turned back to face his sister as she fished an object out from the bag. She held up a small necklace of beads, twisting it in her hands. Anton's upper lip curled and she glanced up at him, sighing. ''Mama says you should pray,'' Nneka said softly in Igbo. ''Because of the fighting you do. You should ask God to protect you.'' Anton looked at the rosary disdainfully, but he held out his hand to receive it. Nneka tossed it towards him and he caught it. He looked at it and sighed. ''She knows, Nneka,'' Anton said. ''I've left that way a long time ago. Didn't you tell her before she gave you this thing?'' Nneka nodded. ''She said I should take it anyway. She said maybe that it would remind you of the right path.'' Anton laughed and tossed the rosary towards the duffle bag. It hit the side and fell on the ground. ''I don't need to be reminded of the path I'm walking,'' Anton replied. Nneka smiled and reached back into the bag. Luis sat in his room, looking at the ceiling. He was still wearing his suit, but it was unbuttoned. He didn't know how everything was going to work out. Anton said she'd be staying for a few weeks, maybe a month. He didn't know if he wanted her that long. He snorted a bit and shook his head. Not women troubles, not in the traditional manner. He just didn't know if he could keep a handle on himself. He looked down, his eyes centering on the small pouch of cocaine settled on his chest. He snatched it up and sat up quickly, rummaging through his nightstand. He pulled out a small china plate and set it on his nightstand, plopping the pouch on top of it. He went to untie the pouch, but just as his fingers found the knot, he heard Anton's voice carrying through the wall. Not now. He frowned and slipped the china plate back into the drawer. Later. The coke wasn't running. Anton forced himself up, counting another push-up. His sister giggled like a schoolgirl—she was a university girl now, fully nineteen—as she sat on his back, counting along with him. He was up to fifty and still going. The weight was starting to get to him a bit, but he persevered. Fifty-one. Fifty-two. Fifty-three. She slapped the back of his head and Anton's arms quivered. ''Come on, strong man,'' Nneka mocked in Igbo. ''More! Fifty-five! Six! I thought you were a fighter!'' Anton didn't respond, gritting his teeth as he continued to do push-ups. She clapped as he kept going and he smiled evilly. After hitting sixty, he popped upwards, tossing her off of his back. She let up a little shriek as she tumbled to the ground, landing on her side. Anton looked down at her, smiling victoriously. She sat up, laughing. ''Come on, then,'' Anton challenged. ''Let me sit on your back while you do push-ups.'' He lunged for his sister and, again, she shrieked, this time as she deftly dodged him. He didn't try too hard as she ran laughing from him, leaving him to call 'helplessly' after her. She teased him from another room and he simply lay back, smiling. It had been too long since he had been a child, since he had been able to act like a child. Since he had had such juvenile fun. It was so pure and raw that somehow, carnal pleasures, combat, and drugs seemed tainted. Seeing it objectively, he knew it wasn't true. But at the moment, he was happy. He remembered his sister and she was just as she was then. He had not wanted to discover her changed. Night had fallen, and to reflect it, most of the lights were off. Napalm Death's debut album Scum blasted through the formidable stereo system, blastbeats and grinding riffs filling the air. The main room of the apartment held only one person: Luis Ferrara. He was not wearing his suit any longer, simply opting for a white wifebeater and loose jeans. He was hunched over, having pulled the table close to his seat. On top of the table rested a china plate and, next to it, was his pouch of cocaine. Carefully, he untied the pouch and dumped the contents out onto the plate. Lifting up an old, split credit card, Luis began to separate it into lines. His breathing was shallow, filled with anticipation for the rush. He lifted his trusty straw—an old coffee straw that Luis had been using for months now—and brought it to his nose. The first line went in quickly and Luis rubbed his nose, grinning. He always grinned like a fool after starting. He was too much a fan. He blinked and let it settle a bit, biting his tongue. He didn't even feel anything yet, but placebo led him to marvel about how good of a band Napalm Death was. He felt like getting up and two-stepping right here. He had cocaine to finish, though. He brought the straw to his nose and leaned down again. A shadow was thrown over him from the back as he snorted up the second line. As he rubbed his nose, he turned and looked who it was. As his eyes fell upon Nneka, dressed in the same tight-fitting black blouse and minishorts that she had been wearing when she first arrived, he gulped. Affecting a weak smile, he watched as she sat down next to the table, her eyes on the lines of white powder. Those legs. Luis could barely take his eyes away. ''What is it?'' Nneka asked in terse English. She looked up at him and Luis smiled. ''Coke,'' Luis responded. She frowned a bit, scooting closer. ''Cocaine,'' she said slowly, rolling the word around her tongue and trying to figure it out. Luis smiled. ''Yeah,'' Luis said. He extended the straw to her. ''Wanna try it?'' Nneka looked at him, her face impassive. He began to bring the straw back to him, but she reached up and slipped the straw away from him. Holding it, she put one end towards the next line and made to put her lips on the end, but Luis quickly corrected her. She glanced up at him and he pantomimed snorting. ''Go down the line.'' Nneka did it, stopping mid-way, but finding her place again and finishing. After she was done, she tossed the straw away and rubbed her nose furiously, as if trying to get something out. Luis laughed at her and turned the plate back to him, but she kicked his ankle. ''Give me again,'' she protested. ''Let me do it right.'' ''Fine, fine,'' Luis grumbled, passing the straw back off to her. She pressed the straw down to the cocaine and snorted again. Luis watched her intently. She was perfect. He loved that hair, too. Classy. Soon they were done, both relaxing. Luis looked at her, seeing her laid back so relaxed, like a regal cat. She stretched and opened her eyes. ''What is this?'' Nneka asked. ''It is so… violent.'' Luis blinked at her and then he figured out that she was talking about the music. ''Grindcore,'' Luis told her. Nneka nodded slowly, suddenly jittering. She sat up and looked at him, alert. ''My friends in Paris… they showed me something. He said it was the Slayer or something.'' Luis laughed, standing. So, she had heard some death? Luis had had to introduce Anton to metal. She was already well started. ''Come on,'' Luis said. ''A good use of the rush is two-stepping. I'll teach you how.'' Nneka took his hand and he pulled her up. She would be a good partner. He was used to two-stepping alone. Anton sat up, gripping his throat. Water. He felt like a camel in the desert. He climbed out of bed and stretched. It was still late. Luis was blasting Napalm Death, though; Anton recognized the sound. He wondered if Nneka was sleeping well. He didn't look over to where she was supposed to be resting, though, trusting her to be there. He walked towards the door and pulled it open, giving a slight yawn. Instantly, that yawn became lodged in his throat. His eyes widened and he stood, fixed in place. His sister gripped the coffee table. Her leg was propped up on the chair. Her blouse was tossed onto the opposite couch. Pink panties were wound around one ankle. Luis stood behind her, gripping her hips. She was screaming. Igbo. ''More. Harder.'' Screaming. Anton's face twisted in a scowl, but he didn't move. Water would wait. He silently closed the door and went back to his bed. Not to sleep. To wait. Anton re-emerged sometime later, stepping into the living room area. He stood in the middle of it, looking about. Luis sat on the couch, sleeping. A small dose for a small rush. That and the fucking had likely tired him out. The fucking. Anton gritted his teeth. His eyes swiveled towards the form on the ground. His sister lay curled up on the ground next to the chair. Naked. With a roar befitting his nickname, Anton gripped Luis by the neck and lifted him into the air, pressing him against the wall. The Venezuelan's eyes popped open quickly and he gripped Anton's wrist, the action of his two legs kicking bouncing his third about madly. A gurgle was the only thing that rose from Luis's lips. ''Why are you alive?'' Anton asked Luis in abrasive Spanish. ''I will tell you why. Because I let you live. Because I look out for you. If it were not for me, you would be gunned down by the Lopez brothers and tossed in a river in Venezuela. And this his how you repay me!? By fucking my—'' What made him stop was out of the corner of his eye. His sister had awoken. She looked at him fearfully. She was no longer naïve. She was no longer innocent. He had been foolish to think she hadn't changed. She would have still looked beautiful, though, but something was wrong; her wild hair was plagued by flecks of white. Anton slowly let Luis go and turned, stalking back to his bedroom. The image would be burned into his mind forever. He would deal with them in the morning.
