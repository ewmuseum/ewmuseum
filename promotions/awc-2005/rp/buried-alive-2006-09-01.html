Date: 09/01/06 'I feel sorry for people who don’t drink. When they wake up in the morning, that’s as good as they’re going to feel all day.' ---Frank Sinatra And for years that creed might as well have been emblazoned on Colby Korver’s very liver, so fervently did he adhere to its cause. With a litany of sexual conquests, failed courses, angry bar owners and battered patrons in his wake, the road to the Atlantic Wrestling Club was never straight nor narrow for Korver. But then again, what fun would that have been? 'Chance favors the prepared mind.' ---Louis Pasteur He’s spent enough time in the gym in the last month to earn a masters degree in physical education. Squats. Overhead presses. Lunges. Triceps extensions. Hammer Curls. And a hundred other exercises have honed and chiseled his body into peak condition. But it’s still not yet good enough. That belligerent drunk that “The Violence” Vince Jones threw off the pier on the Fresh!east Special hasn’t been seen in weeks. In his place labors a tireless machine with only one goal in sight--Coast To Coast. But beneath both these facades—-that of the fearless drinker and that of the relentless warrior—-exists another man still. But he hasn’t been heard from in years. 'The finest qualities of our nature, like the bloom on fruits, can be preserved only by the most delicate handling. Yet we do not treat ourselves nor one another thus tenderly.' ---Henry David Thoreau Korver has long since swept any memories of this persona from his recollection. That is, until tonight… Alone on the floor of his bedroom, mindlessly sifting through an old cardboard box of files recently delivered from the attic, rests one-eighth of the upcoming East vs. West pay-per-view tag match—-Colby Korver. But this isn’t the brash frat boy many of the Atlantic Wrestling Club’s twenty something demographic have grown so fond of, for in his place sits almost another being entirely. One, who with the pull of a paper, has found himself suddenly transported to a time not so very long ago, but seemingly forever away. -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- Colby Korver AP English 12 11/29/97 An Expressive Silence An expressive silence is a sleeping boy on his first day home from the hospital. As his parents proudly view the peaceful expression upon his sleeping face, they maintain joyful smiles upon their own. An expressive silence is a 6-year old who has just been caught in his first lie. As he sits in front of his parents, eyes watering from the embarrassment and the shame, he earnestly attempts to find some sort of reply to his parents’ valid accusation; yet his mouth can produce no sound, but for the mere rhythm of his frantic breathing. An expressive silence is the car ride home from a local arcade, after a 12-year old has just been involved in his first fight. Donning a disguise of foolish pride and ego, he nonetheless secretly yearns to bury his head in his mother’s shoulder and cry, for lack of a more satisfying alternative. But all he can manage during the somber trip home is a forlorn expression upon his face, and an air of silence with the car. An expressive silence if the 18-year old senior who has just been accepted into his college of choice, yet cannot manage even a smile, knowing this move will take him across the country from his girlfriend, who is only a high school sophomore. As he walks across the stage to accept his diploma, he should be thanking everyone who has helped him along the way, yet he cannot say a word; because the pain of knowing this his graduation symbolizes the end of their relationship has crippled his once-powerful voice. An expressive silence involves a 24-year old man who has just proposed to the girl he has always loved. As he looks into her eyes, he can’t help but guess her answer to his question. As she begins to cry, he mirrors her reaction, realizing they both shed tears of joy. An expressive silence is the 30-year old man, standing next to his 28-year old wife, in the doorway of their infant’s bedroom. For as he watches their infant son drift to sleep, he remains silent, knowing that silence if often the most expressive reaction of all. -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- And as Korver silently finishes the work he long ago authored, a single tear falls onto the page and almost instantaneously blurs out the word, “first.” Wiping this foreign substance from his eyes, Korver looks back to the page and realizes that with its disappearance, the bordering words have become even more relevant still. “his fight” And as this vulnerability disappeared back into the brazen exterior from whence it came, albeit much closer to the surface than it had been in years, Korver’s focus shifted back to the genuine reason for his resolve and ambition.
