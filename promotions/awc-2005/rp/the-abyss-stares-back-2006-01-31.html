The Abyss Stares Back

31 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1-31-06 Scene: Seattle, WA * * * * * There's a fortune-teller in downtown Seattle. She's an old woman, who claims to be Wiccan sometimes, claims to be a voodoo priestess others. It depends on who comes by, and what she would guess they'd like to hear. She lives in a run-down fifth story apartment, and from the only windows in the house, you can see a Starbucks, a Seattle's Best, and a small coffee shop called 'Bad Ass' coffee company. Her name is Mattie, and she hates coffee. Oh well. At any rate, she spends several hours a day sitting at a small table in front of her apartment, or in the winter months (which in Seattle, tends to run from September through April) she'll take up residence in a small head shop with a framed painting of Kurt Cobain on the wall down the street. For several hours she'd sit and wait, usually with only a few 'customers' who'd only drop a couple of bucks in her 'donation' jar. She didn't need the money. She had enough. She'd tell you it was from a past husband, but the truth was she'd head out to a Spokane Salish casino every two months and play blackjack. Mattie will tell your future using any number of different methods. The tea leaves weren't too popular (hey, it IS Seattle), but the tarot cards always were. The palm reading was another one, and sometimes she'd just stare at a person for about twenty minutes, and absorb all the future just from a look and some intuition. No one ever liked what Crazy Mattie had to say about them, and this was generally because she was always right. So on this rainy Seattle Sunday, Mattie sat in 'Smoke on the Water' and looked at the picture of Kurt Cobain, whom she didn't know but always thought he looked like a nice oung man, and would look nicer if he brushed his hair once in a while. The bell on the door clanged, and Mattie turned her attention towards it. The head shop clerk was in the back, and she'd truly prefer not to be accosted by some young hooligan who'd already gotten high for the afternoon. The two young men who entered the store were not exactly 'hooligans', though Joey Farnsworth did try his best to dress the part. A track suit in baby blue, a matching North Carolina Tarheels baseball cap ('They're a hockey team, right?') and a gaudy gold chain with the digits 'J6'. The young man behind him wore a black t-shirt with no writing, and blue jeans, with a black overcoat atop it. While Joey Six looked like some of the 'urban' element that Mattie always feared would mug her, the young Asian man actually looked like the dangerous one. 'Holy shit, Jiro...I'm gonna get some stinky bud here. I'm gonna have damn grass stains on my new suit, because I'm gonna be rollin' around in that shit.' Joey Six hopped around the store like a ferret in a shitty track suit. 'Whatevah.' With no cameras on him, Jiro Sennosuke didn't bother keeping his perfected faux-English, and was lazy with his words. The other Japanese at the dojo where Collision Course trained weren't much to talk with in his native tongue. Kenichi Kazuyo hated the Japanese language as much as he hated everything about his native culture, and Kensuke Suzahara...well, he just creeped Jiro out. Meanwhile, Joey Six was affecting a bad British accent. 'GOOD SIRS! I SAY, GOOD SIRS! PLEASE HOOK THIS BROTHER UP WITH TWO POUNDS OF YOUR FINEST MARI-JEW-ANA! FOR THE QUEEN, I SAY!' Crazy Mattie drew her eyes to Jiro. Black wavy hair, black clothes, black eyes...well, almost black, they were so dark. Black draws light in, and sure enough, it drew Mattie's eyes in as well. She really didn't want to do a reading on the young man, her intuition was good enough to know that she didn't want to read him unless she had to...but she couldn't help it. It was out of control. Images flashed through her mind, one after the other: A young Asian boy, holding a little red balloon, standing in the middle of an abandoned amusement park. Alone. Jiro Sennosuke, beaten, bloody in the face, face leaking blood all over a canvas mat. A woman, beautiful but dark, she was a liar. And she was cutting Jiro's hair, making him weak. A golden mask, that covered Jiro Sennosuke's face, and suffocated him. A blue-haired young white man, laying in a hospital bed, as Jiro held a knife above him. His face was contorted with pain. The other kid, that Joey Six, begging. Pleading. 'Stop hurting me...stop hurting me...with Sennosuke bearing down on him.' Then a thick, oily blackness. Crazy Mattie's head went light, and the next thing she knew...desk clerk was already out near the front counter, trying to explain to Joey that he had to leave, and that they had no marijuana to sell. Joey Six was indignant, and the young desk clerk wouldn't remove his hand from Joey, who whined. 'Stop hurting me...stop hurting me...you, serious, G. I'm gonna come back in here with the police if you don't sell me some muh-fucking ganja. I'm a PAYING CUSTOMER!' Crazy Mattie drew her eyes back to Jiro...who was staring a hole through her. 'JIRO! THESE FUCKERS ARE OPPRESSING ME!' Jiro ignored his tag team partner. He mouthed a few words silently to Crazy Mattie. 'Tell me what you saw.' Crazy Mattie shook her head, and buried her face in her hands. CRASH Joey Six knocked over a display of glass pipes, and then looked terrified. 'YO! JIRO! WE GOTTA BOUNCE!' Six pulled one of the pipes up, and held it like a weapon. 'NOBODY MOVE! I'VE GOT A BONG! YOU TRY TO STOP ME, AND THIS PIPE GETS IT!' Jiro just turned his attention back to Joey, and quickly slapped the pipe out of his hand, where it clattered to the floor, almost gently. He then addressed the clerk. 'Sorry about this. We'll be back later to pay for the damages.' As he finished his sentence, he turned back to Crazy Mattie. His words echoed in her head. As Jiro pulled his spastic tag partner out of the building, he took one last look at the old woman who could see into people, and left. The clerk half-heartedly straightened some of the pipes, and Crazy Mattie sighed to herself. This...was going to be interesting.
