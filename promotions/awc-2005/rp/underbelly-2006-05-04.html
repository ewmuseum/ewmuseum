Underbelly*

4 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Thursday, May 4th, 2006 Underbelly* 1. A ruling was put into order that states that asylums be debunked and completely renovated, in hopes for an integrity rush. For years now, asylums have been labeled as “dangerous” and “mysterious” because they were pushed back towards the middle of forests and out in the middle of nowhere, and rumors of mistreatment being administered to the patients. Most of the asylums are now supposed to be lenient state hospitals, mainly because the patients are suffering from diseases, alike those laying in general hospital beds. These patients, while dealing with their stay, are supposed to be given three meals, several strenuous classes/treatments, and an onslaught of attention each day in and each day out. As human beings, this is what we call a system. Inside this system, two or more people must carry out divided tasks that meet up. It’s as if two drivers are driving in two completely different lanes, but still heading to the same destination. One of these men is already in the right lane, and carrying out the earlier responsibilities, while the other guy is taking care of side projects that relate to what our other guy is doing. At the last mile or so in the drive, the other driver will merge over to the right lane and follow him, and the tasks will become a united operation. For things to work properly, everything has to connect. Unfortunately for the government, these rulings haven’t put a dent into these administration’s prior agendas. This crap was established years ago. I’ve been under the helm of Sequester Campgrounds, a mental facility, for two years now and nothing has changed. These men want nothing more than to drag you into a bathroom stall and make you their bitch. It’s an act that a lot of security guards play, but they never got me. I’ve spent most of my time in Sequester with the right people. In fact, the group I belong to is called “The Question”. We’re a pack of guys that nobody knows anything about. One of my main priorities, while staying here, was to keep below the radar for as long as possible. Not only that, I didn’t want people to know my real name, my age, my background, or even the fact that I’d been wrestling for a few years. If I was staying here for a while, I wanted people to either like me or take me with a grain of salt and ignore me. I, of course, had no desire to be a standout. I know a lot of them, and they’re still sleeping on their stomachs. Being that a lot of people had known me from previous appearances in other facilities, I was especially a target when I first got there. The whole layout of the facility is quite laughable, though. There’s fourteen rectangular log cabins spread out along 100 acres. Tall fencing, sharp barbed-wire, guard towers, and spotlights have all been put into play to convince those, willing to break free and try to escape, that security is tight as a knuckle. There’s yellow-tinted fortification surrounding the eight 15-room lodges, and tall security gating provided for staff and other administration buildings. The grass is mowed in diagonal formations and the way the birds sing and the sun shines, it seems like a retreat from the busy living city life. I sure was fooled once they guided me off the blue and white school bus. “Help comes to those who accept it.” Was painted in blotchy red bubble lettering on the side of it. It almost looked as if the bus had to stop in the ghetto for a couple of minutes, and serving as the opposite of what those may think, an intellectual gangster comes over and sheds comprehension. I turned around, without noticing that the sky had turned a miserable gray, and stared at the giant black gates. When I think about it now, they don’t seem as big, but they seemed infinite at first glance. There was a guard on each side of me, holding my arms like we were preparing our marriage, and I could feel all hope erase me. In my brain, numbers started to spin like wheels of vehemence, as the apprehension shot out of me like an inflated balloon with a giant pop. “Lucky tenth...” “What was that, Nic?” “Do you know why it’s raining, Paisley?” Edward Paisley, a fifty-eight years young security guard who’s seen me more times, in the last six years, than my mother and brother combined. He was the first guy to consider mental rehabilitation to my former psychiatrist, the late Dr. Jack Klingman. He was the only guy I didn’t care for seeing, because I knew I’d let him down. Last time he saw me, I was feasting off of success and somewhat healthy. Simply said, I was believable and inconceivable. Now he sees me, and I’m torn and tattered. I’m so fucked up in the head and he’s directly inside my fire. He didn’t respond to my question, and I kind of understood what he was doing. His job was to transport me to the facility, not converse with me about this and that and this. But I was lonely and, especially, looking for answers. “It’s raining because I’m being shipped to somewhere I’m not supposed to be. This isn’t the answer anymore. Why can’t you FUCKING PEOPLE understand that?!” “Nic…” Paisley sighed as a large green light, placed directly above the entrance door, turned on. This light signifies a danger-free entry through the quad and into the quarters. The level of such precautions varies by the level of danger such patient breaths. Paisley, and the other guard, started to move forward but I refused to go any further. “No, I’m sorry. I didn’t mean it to sound like I’m talking about you guys. It’s just that, I’m not supposed to be here.” I caught the attention of the guard opposite of Paisley. This was the first time I’ve ever seen him and he wasn’t a nice guy. He had the cross comparisons of a stocky Ron Jeremy and a taller Rick Steiner, but suited himself well, like the president of the UFC. He was wearing a couple gaudy gold rings on weird fingers (index and pinky) and shielded his eyes with reflective glasses. “If you weren’t supposed to be here, you wouldn’t be. Let’s go…” Relentlessly, I stalled, kicking my heels up as they tried to drag me in. I don’t think they understood me. It wasn’t like I wasn’t going to follow orders and go in. It was just that I felt there were a need and an opportunity to defend myself. I turned a cold shoulder on the unknown-named guard and started rant towards Paisley who, like a genuine person would, took it all in and nodded his head in understanding. Unfortunately enough, turning a cold shoulder to a stocky security guard, who’s already full of himself, was not in my best interests. He yanked me by the arm and turned me towards him. “Look at me. Are you looking at me, Nic?” I nodded. “Edward Paisley, who is a great guy who’s invited me to several of his Sunday football barbeques, is not the one in charge here. I’m the one in charge here. I understand that you’re not interested in coming here, and that’s fine, but your opinion doesn’t matter anymore. By order of a judge, you’ve been sentenced here. It’s time to suck it up, move along, and deal with it. Welcome to Sequester…” Like a puppy, who’s gone to someone in the house for friendly refuge after being disciplined by someone else in the house, I turned to Paisley in hopes that he’d clear his throat, cite his pro-me opinion, and punch stocky-guard’s lights out. That didn’t happen. Instead, Paisley released his grip from my arm and took a couple steps backwards before completely turning around and walking up the steps into the bus. Resurfacing from the back of the vehicle, Paisley exits the bus with a manila folder in his hands. Opening one half of the stocky-guards navy-blue wind jacket, he shoves the documents into the inside pocket. “Those are his records and similar statistics that’ll be required at the front hall. Do you need me to wait for a “clear”, or are you good to go?” “No, all set, Ed.” “Alright Ryan, you take care.” “If I don’t, the wife and kids will lynch me.” “ (Giggles and smirks) Yeah, the same…” And there he went. This was the second to last time I saw Paisley. I should’ve expected the lack of defense, for he didn’t work for me, he worked for the people that were tying me down. I don’t think his intentions were to see that I be brought down as painful as possible. I think he was doing his job like anybody would. I wonder how the operators of the electric chair felt after they toasted the life out of someone? I wonder how doctors feel when they’re injecting a prisoner with poison that’ll inevitably kill them? How about being a mortician, and having to deal with the despair of families dealing with a loss? Point is, there’s anguish and regret to each and every job out there. Whether you can identify it or not, is another thing. So, I let Paisley go on his way because there was no sense in making this harder on anyone. For once, I defended the guards instead of myself. “So, how we doing Nicholas? Ready?” I swallowed hard. The pulsation in my temples was so severe that I literally felt like I was going to black out. I was numb to what I was supposed to feel because I, simply put, didn’t know what that feeling was. Jesus, where had time gone? It’s amazing. I’ve been ridiculed and molded into a clown that everyone is afraid of, everyone except for these guards. Why is that? Where the fuck does such valor come from, ya’ know? Point is, I didn’t want this asshole thinking I was some clown with a joke. Before anything else could pop into my head, the revving of the school bus sounded off. Ryan waved towards Paisley, who did the same. The bus started to accelerate and off it went, down the rocky hill and through the man-made gap between the oceans of trees. This is where it all became so perceptible to me. The reception of my neuronal television was now at its highest definition. The bus was gone. I was in the middle of nowhere. The decree started. Before I knew it, Ryan had me by the collar, pushing me along with one of his knees in my back every four or five steps. My head was in such a position where I was allowed only to look up. At the time, I thought his knee was a loaded gun. Resisting the temptation to wish myself away from the moment, I closed my eyes and let the force against my back subside on its own. “Do you we have a problem here?” I didn’t answer. I didn’t know what he was talking about; I was too occupied to care. Tragically so, I made the wrong decision. Whole-handedly, he grabbed the back of my skull and slammed it into the wall. My face felt like it had combusted and the dust of what remained seemed to shine like electricity around me. I thought I was unconscious, until I heard the echo of a voice. “Get me straight, you fucking freak. You belong to me now, and for as long as I say. It is now your choice to either get help here or in prison. Help comes to those who accept it, understand?” “So does respect, and I don’t that coming out of you, Sir.” “I don’t need to respect anyone but myself. I’m here to keep these people in line.” “Now we’re people? Weren’t we freaks a couple seconds ago?” “The only freaks inside this here asylum are those that don’t cooperate. I decide when you leave this place, Nicholas, and because of that? A lot of people have had extension after extension after extension. Sammy C. is a sixty-three year old patient suffering from severe bi-polar. His release date was more than twelve years ago. He’s no longer healthy enough to be re-released into society. The ‘dangerous’ that do not cooperate do not achieve the bliss of an ending.” I finally opened my eyes, and to complete darkness. I thought I was dead. My ears were plugged and the echoing voice was nearly obsolete, illegible muffles and grounded resonance. Then, with a slight tingle in my Achilles heel, I was alive again. Like water running steadily out of clogged eardrums. I pulled my head away from the darkness and noticed that my head had gone through a thick wall of tile. No blood, but I felt the butter- knife-stabbing sensation going off like fireworks in the middle of my forehead. Groggily so, I accepted Ryan’s orders. I didn’t want to be thrown headfirst into another wall, at least for a while, and I figured I’d have my chance to get back at the motherfucker. “Understood… understood, okay? Understood.” And that was that. I didn’t talk to the bastard again that night. He played the act of a ‘guide’ and showed me around my quarters. There was some grass with a couple hot spots for sun on those beautiful days, a food hall that had legitimately good food, and a recreation center. On the outside of it lay basketball courts, a track for running and walking, and a tiny pool to get your feet wet. The inside was still a work in progress when I first arrived. The paint hadn’t even gone up yet, but there was a ping-pong table, a chess set, a small television that got six or seven channels, and a radio. At first glance, it looks like a retirement home. I now shake when I think about all of the corrupted mania going on in there. The things they would do to people, the shock therapy, the endless tirades that would shoot out of the administration’s mouths at all times of the day, and the questions. I’d never want to change the orchestration of my life, for I think it’ll connect one day. In a way, I think the better half of all the corruption and hypocrisy, which has scribbled over my life in vivid colors, will turn into something completely different when it’s all said and done. If not, it seems like a big waste. The housing projects are called “Gray Houses”, and it got that name because the external walls are built with hollow wooden logs that have bundles of steel rods in the middle. I have no answer other than what I’ve heard through rumors. Supposedly, the architect who thought up Sequester was heavily into acid, and did a lot of his designing while under the influence. Of course, these rumors are, in fact, coming from the brains of whacked-out-of-their-gourds Sequester patients. Therefore, who knows what’s true or false anymore? Point to all of this is, I belonged to Quarter-3-Gray House #4. Three others were occupying it before I arrived. Supposedly (again) a fourth was staying there until he cut the tip of his cock off because he thought it turned into a fish that started singing the intro-melody to Cosby. He had smuggled a plastic butter knife into his room with the intention of killing one of his roommates, but soon found out that his conscience was playing tricks on him. That said, I’m not sure if they reattached the head of his penis. I remember the three guys sitting in their beds, staring at me as I entered. It was as if I was walking in uncharted territories, or a gang neighborhood. I wasn’t sure what to make of it, and was too tired to make friends, so I dropped my toiletries and clothes and collapsed onto the bed. Whether they liked it or not, this was my home now too. Besides, I’m not afraid of anyone, and I’m sure they aren’t either. “Pssssshh, hey, Ryan!” I heard a nasally voice, followed by a couple footsteps. “What do you need, Carrot?” “Who’s that guy?” Ryan laughed. The room was so quiet that I could actually hear the gum sticking to his mouth as he chewed it. God, I didn’t like that guy. He came off as such a prick, such an obnoxious “it is what it is” prick. “I don’t even know, really. He’s got more than a few identities. You know what? You call him whatever you want, Carrot..” “Really?! You really mean that?!” “No, now get to fucking bed.” I heard Carrot gulp as he repositioned himself in his bed and pulled the sheets over his body. I still had my eyes closed and my back against him, but I knew he was staring at me. Supporting his head with his hand, supporting his elbow with a pillow, a smile that symbolizes outrageous interests in why I’m here. I know that feeling, because I was “Carrot” long before he was. In a whisper, he said something to me. “The way the light from the window above pierces the shadows crawling on your back. Lines that move in parallel fashion across you like roadways with walls on each side. Permeating you until that first roll onto your back. Shadowlines… yeah, I like the sound of that.” That was that. I let his psychotic observance fly over me like dust in the wind and fell asleep. I’d confront him tomorrow, or whenever my head was fully cleared. This always seems so new at first, but it never really is.
