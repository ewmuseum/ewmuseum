The Strangest Thing

20 June 2005

Date: 6-20-05 The Strangest Thing “It was the strangest thing I’d ever been witness too: Two girls relentlessly hitting each other, not caring about any kind of reprimand. The crowd that gathered, no one made any motion to stop the fight. No one even egged them on. Just bore witness to the brutality of two girls who hated each other, tearing at each other with fake nails and tiny fists…” Pamela stopped, musing over the violent image that had burnt itself onto her mind’s eye. ”So you didn’t do anything either? Just stood there and watched?” ”No. I just watched. I was stunned; frozen. I don’t really know either of the girls, and it happened so fast. I didn’t even think to try and separate them. It was like I was watching it on T.V., you know?” ”Not really. I’ve never watched any Women-In-Prison movies before,” said Kevin lazily as he leaned back in his chair to take a long drag off of his cigarette. Pamela Saunders sipped her cup of coffee. She found that Kevin was lightening up, not so quick to anger now that she had been able to work out of home for the last two weeks. He could keep an eye on her, and as long as she didn’t bring up Alex Strider’s name, he wouldn’t get angry. She was glad to be able to have a normal conversation with him again, like when they were just friends, before they decided to live together. Before they decided it would be a good idea to start dating. Before things changed. Kevin’s pants had soot on it from the shop where he worked. His face smudged from working on cars all day long, he hadn’t bothered to change or shower after he got off work. Pamela didn’t care. Appearances didn’t matter to her. What mattered to her was that he was there. That he came home, and he would be there for her to talk too. She just needed a person to listen, so that she could have a confidant. So she didn’t have to be scared and alone, thinking through her problems all by herself. “I would imagine it must have been hotter than you’re saying. You’re leaving something else out, right?” He asked, pulling his hands behind his head. “They started making out, right?” “No. I don’t know. They were so angry at each other, that’s all I can remember. That wasn’t even the weirdest part about it…one girl ripped out the other’s hoop earring right through her lobe! Blood dripped out so red, it was like a painting, or something. There wasn’t much, but it stood out against her white sweater. I swear to God, it was so surreal, you know? The injured just girl ran away, and the fight ended so abruptly. I was interviewing her, and she ran off. I didn’t follow. And what did everyone else do? They just calmly walked away like nothing had happened!” ”What? No one said anything about the girl’s ear? The campus police never showed?” ”No! It was over as fast as it began, and nothing was solved by it. The two girls hate each other. I did find out one thing though... The dead girl, Sophia Riley?” ”Yeah?” Kevin asked with waning interest. Pamela leaned in close as she readied herself to let loose the big secret, saying in a low whisper:”She used to sleep with Alex Strider’s brother, Darius.” ”Oh man! So he was hitting on his brother’s ex-girlfriend? I guess he was just trying to keep it in the family, eh?” Kevin smiled a sardonic smile, not even attempting to hide his contempt. Pamela lit up her own cigarette. She had tried quitting, because of the time she had been forced to spend with Alex she hadn’t been able to smoke anyway as he forbade it. But at home with Kevin, things were comfortable, easy. She could think, and smoking came naturally when she was around him. Everything that was toxic came naturally when she was near Kevin. Or everything that was natural was made toxic. Pamela spent days at home, making phone calls, collecting the police reports, and finding the trails to verify all the facts of the story she was building. Kevin seemed to appreciate having her around, and was doing his best to keep her happy. ”I am going to go to New York City for the next ‘Fresh’. I have to go over the newest revelations with him in person. It’s been great being here with you the last few weeks though. I’m going to miss you,” she said, exhaling blue and gray smoke. “What? Why can’t you just, I don’t know, talk to him over the phone?” Kevin asked, nervously edging himself forward in his kitchen chair. “Why do you have to go to New York City?” “Because,” she said, flicking the cherry off her cigarette into the ashtray, “Next week Alex’s civil trial begins. I have to meet with him to finalize the last loose bits of information. This is a huge break in the story; his brother having been involved with the deceased. I can’t continue to do a subject piece without getting feedback from the subject,” she said, matter-of-fact. Kevin’s face got a bit darker, and his Adam’s Apple bobbed up in his leathery neck as he swallowed down his anger for the moment. Pamela tried not to notice as Kevin stewed in his seat. She got just a little bit of satisfaction this time from his jealousy, enjoying his reaction. “He doesn’t have to worry about me, I’ve never cheated. If he only treated me better I’d know I never would,” she thought to herself. ******** Alex Strider stood outside the door that read “Gerald Garcia Esq.” The warm morning wind blew through his hair. It was only 8 A.M. but it was already a sunny warm eighty-five degrees and climbing. The weather didn’t matter to Alex, who hung his head low as he exhaled, gathering the strength to press onward and face the ordeal that lay ahead of him. As hands on as he was with almost every aspect of his life, dealing with the particulars of his ‘Wrongful Death’ lawsuit was something he had been procrastinating. He stepped into the air-conditioned, vacuous waiting room of his lawyer’s office. Alex had known Garcia for years; he helped him settle a contract dispute with wrestling federations in the past. This situation called for something a bit more concerting, and would require a fine hand. A ‘Wrongful Death’ Lawsuit is going to call in more than just the facts; Alex’s very character was going to be on trial. Very few things in life make Alex nervous, and for the first time in his life he was popping Tums tablets like some construction worker who lived on chili dogs and cheap beer. ”Alex! Hey man! Right on time as usual. Have a seat!” A short man appeared from an office door toward the back. The man motioned for Alex to come into the room. The short lawyer smiled wide at the professional wrestler, shaking his hand and motioning toward the seat in front of his cherry wooden desk. On the desk was the open file concerning Strider’s case. A familiar picture of Sophia Riley smiled back at Alex, upside-down. His stomach did flip flops, and bile shot up like a geyser, trying to make a jail-break through his esophagus. Alex swallowed down the bitter hotness. “You o.k. Alex? You look like you’ve seen a ghost.” Gerald Garcia had slick peppered gray and black hair pulled back into a long limp pony tail, with a matching large moustache hung under his hook nose for decoration. He dressed well, but gave off an air of insincerity that would immediately set off your bullshit detector to “Waist High”. Alex’s bullshit detector always seemed on the fritz the days he had to meet with Garcia. ”I’m fine. I just haven’t had much sleep this last week.” ”Too many women, eh, Alex? No time for sleep! You make me wish I was your age again! Ha!” Gerald said with a toothy grin. Alex was in no mood for smiles. ”I need this to be taken care of as quickly as possible. What are our chances of settling out of court?” Alex said sullenly. “Whoa! Alex, that’s a little fast, don’t you think? I think if we go to trial, we can get you out of this without having to spend one red cent…Well, other than my fees of course, but you know what I mean.” Gerald said with a stunted laugh. Strider frowned, which sobered Gerald’s mood quickly. ”I want this over with as soon as possible. I don’t want it to go to trial, Gerald. That would be a mess that I can’t afford to deal with, do you understand?” Gerald cleared his throat and paused. Clapping his hands, he nodded and smiled at Alex. “Crystal clear Alex. I’ll draft up something right away and see if the Riley’s will accept it.” Gerald smiled, and then raised a finger in argument, “But…if they don’t accept it, though, we do have to move forward with the idea that this is going to trial in only two week’s time. We have to consider what direction we would want your defense to move in. With your current line of work, being in the public eye, it will be considerably harder to make you look sympathetic in the face of the crying parents who are out one young hot blonde college student daughter…” ”Right. I don’t want to drag this out for them, really. I had nothing to do with her death, but I couldn’t imagine what her parents are going through. I am prepared to give them an award of two-hundred thousand dollars to settle.” Gerald nodded. “That is very generous of you, Alex. I know how much money that is to you. They’d be crazy not to accept it. The only problem is, when you’re dealing with parents of a deceased child,” he shook his head quietly, continuing after a moment, “It’s never really about the money. It’s about principle. Two hundred thousand dollars is a lot of money, believe me. That’s how much my second wife cost me in my divorce!” Gerald let the joke fall flat on the table. Receiving no reaction from Strider, he continued. “Alex… they’re suing you for twenty million dollars. Right now, their principle might have a higher price tag than you think.” Alex choked on his own spit when he heard out loud for the first time exactly how much his civil suit was going to cost. “TWENTY MILLION DOLLARS? TWENTY MILLION? Are you fucking KIDDING me?” Alex wanted to jump out of his seat and knock over Garcia’s expensive book shelf in rage, but the shock of hearing how much he could be indebted for filled his legs with lead; all the blood drained out of his face leaving him with a ghostly expression. ”You didn’t read the memo I had sent over to you three weeks ago? Oh no. I thought you knew!” Gerald said, embarrassed. “Alex, I’m sorry you’re only finding out now, but that’s the truth. They think that because you’re a professional wrestler that you think you’re above the law, thanks to pricks like Michael Jackson and O.J. Simpson. They want to hit you where they think they can hurt you the most…your pocket book.” Alex shook his head in disbelief, his eyes not focusing on anything as his mind ran circles. ”I’ve never had TWENTY MILLION DOLLARS, Gerald. That is a huge sum of money! I’m not Tom Cruise, for God’s sake! I wrestle in a God-Damned regional federation. What the hell makes them think I’m worth that much money? Haven’t they seen any of the AWC shows? The production value alone on those should make anyone sympathetic enough to send ME money. FUCK!” “Well, maybe they caught wind that you come from a long line of steel barons. Maybe they just want to make a statement. Who knows?' Alex's eyes narrowed, he tried to focus his anger, and the lawyer was the closest thing at hand. 'Now, calm down for a second, Alex. It’s not as bad as you think. We’re going to try to settle, o.k.? In the settlement I’ll use some language that should make them aware that this is a better deal. If that doesn’t work though, we’ll just have to put all our energy into the trial. We’ll have to paint a picture of Sophia Riley that’ll let the jury know that she’s not really worth the twenty million, o.k.? We’ve got enough evidence that you’re a victim here, too. The circumstances of her death were just a series of a few unfortunate events, and that you have no culpability in them. Trust me. We’re going to get you out of this o.k.” Alex nodded, though he did not really believe it. ”Great!” Exclamed Gerald. “So, first thing I need you to do, is buy yourself a nice blue suit, nothing flashy. Something soft, so you look sympathetic to the Riley’s plight. Secondly…I know you’ve got this whole ‘Bad-Ass’ thing going for you. While you’re on T.V. the next few weeks, and before you argue, hear me out. I know this one’s asking a lot, but…” Gerald sighed, smiling, as he tried to find the right words. Alex waited for the lawyer to get to the point with agitation. “… try not to do anything that would give the prosecution much to work with. I guess what I’m trying to say is, be less of a dick for a while, o.k.?” ”’Be less of a dick?’ That’s the advice that you’re giving me in your capacity of MY LAWYER? Are you serious?” “Afraid so, Alex. We need for you to be someone that the jury can identify with. The Riley’s will be sitting in court every day. Sophia’s mom will be a tear factory, I can promise you that. All the sobbing and handkerchiefs. It's terrible. Very sad, very sad.' Garcia caught himself, as Strider looked on incredilously at his lawyer. Grabbing some paperwork, Garcia closed a file nervously. 'No matter what is said in there, that’s an image that is incredibly powerful, and impossible to ignore. You have to be mature and dignified in order to make this work. Don’t worry, Alex. We’re going to get you through this.” Gerald smiled that insincere smile underneath his bushy gray moustache. Alex swallowed down more hot-bitterness, his bullshit-detector finally going off. ******** Later that night Pam and Kevin went out to the local country bar with several of Kevin’s friends from Dylewski’s, the garage where he worked. Kevin’s friends were bawdy and big. She always stayed towards the back of the group like a shadow. They often acted as if she wasn’t even there, so she overheard things she shouldn’t, the normal bar talk when guys think they’re alone: “That one there, she needs my dick in her,” one would say. “Somebody’s got to fuck that. Are you that somebody, Chuck? I think you are!” Another would chime in. “Holy shit, look at the tits on her!” “Her hair is so curly. I love it!” “No, it’s straight, man. You’re shitfaced!” “I ain’t talkin’ ‘bout the hair you can see, fat ass! SCORE!” Much hand slapping and high-fiving came out of intellectual conversations like this. Kevin rarely joined in on the lewd commentary, perhaps out of some small amount of respect he had for his fiancé, but it did not stop him from laughing at the things his friends would say about the women who’d commit no crimes deeper than merely walking by their festering testosterone filled kennel. Though she never questioned him to his face, she knew he would talk about the girls the same when she was not there. She lied to herself, saying it was all harmless. The reality of it was it was a block that she could not let herself get over, no matter how hard she tried to ignore it. She knew someday she’d have to deal with his behavior. But she prayed that he’d change before they’d get married. Just like she prayed that she’d be happy one day, like she used to be. After last call, and everyone had consumed enough alcohol to forget about how much they hated themselves, Kevin and Pam excused themselves. Kevin was feeling particularly randy, saying “The little miss wants to get back home for some tender loving,” as he held her tight from behind. Pamela could only roll her eyes. Everyone said they’re goodbyes, and Pam felt empty inside. Nights like this made it difficult to look at Kevin, because all she wanted to see was the man she got engaged too. Now, he was this jealous cretin who behaved like a hairy ape protecting his female from phantom violators. They did not talk much on the ride home, Pamela claimed herself the victim of a headache early on to stave off Kevin’s sexual advances. As they walked up the stairs to their apartment, Kevin started to talk nervously. “So, this AWC thing, you go to New York when again?” Kevin slurred, holding himself steady against the guardrail. ”Madison Square Garden on Tuesday. I’m leaving Monday morning,” she said flatly so as to not raise his incorrect suspicion. “Tuesday. Shit.” He ran his hand through his light brown curls, thick with bar smoke and grease from improper washing. “I bet you’re looking forward to it, huh?” He asked, leading her like a World War II sniper led a trooper. Pamela paused, catching on that he wanted to start a fight. “Well, it would be nice to get this all over with.” ”Get what over with? Our engagement?” Kevin asked quick and sharply, jumping to conclusions. Pamela cocked her head, no expression on her face, saying “No… the article on Alex Strider. I want to move on.' She was cold, uncaring. ”Uh huh. I bet.” Kevin stopped on the foyer, one flight from their floor. “I bet you want to move on. But the next week’ll be nice. You’ll be seeing Alex.” Kevin’s tone was dark and ominous. The alcohol had made him angry, and in his dementia, he was a scary drunk. Pamela was sick of being scared. “Yeah, I guess you could say I’ll be seeing a lot more of Alex.” Pamela cocked her chin, defiantly standing against him. Kevin turned around, anger, whiskey breathe and bloodshot eyes. He grabbed Pamela by the throat forcefully, his eyes narrow slits filled with the rage of every self-persecuting man who’d ever felt wronged in his mind. ”You fucking slut! I knew it!” Pamela pulled at his hand in fear, trying in vain to back away from him. Her eyes bugged out of her head as she feared for her life. Kevin, now not a man but a beast working solely on anger and alcohol, shoved her backwards. Pamela tumbled down the flight of stairs like a discarded rag doll, unloved and broken. Her only thought ‘survive’, she had no time to pray that she would not land wrong and end up a comatose vegetable like Terri Schiavo. Her tiny body hit the floor hard, feet first, her right ankle taking a sick angle against the floor. She cried out like a wounded kitten when she reached the bottom, not so much out of the pain of a badly sprained ankle, but more from the relief that she was alive. Then she cried from the sadness that she had gotten herself involved in such a mess. She cried, and the tears came readily. Because she’d pushed them down so deep for so long, they almost leapt out of her now that they'd finally been given an opportunity at freedom. Seeing her tears sobered Kevin. He walked down to her, and promised her she’d be alright. He said it was an accident. He was going to take her to the hospital right away, and she’d never have to hurt again. “If you only wouldn’t make me so mad…” he muttered in the typical way so that the victim is made to feel responsible for the violence she is forced to endure. She just cried, and he held her head to his chest. He let her tears wash away his guilt. His ignorance of her true feelings freed him from all responsibility. At the hospital, she told the nurse she fell. ******** ’You have to be mature and dignified in order to make this work’ Alex thought to himself as he walked through the cement parking garage trying to remember where he parked his EXT. The meeting with Gerald had not instilled the sense of calm he was hoping for when he went in there. Walking in a daze, all Alex could think about was the potential reality of being twenty million dollars in debt. Mature and dignified he could do, but this week he had a high-profile wrestling match. Against a woman. Yes, he was certain that beating up a woman and smacking her around would win him all the sympathy he needed to come out of the trial smelling like peach cobbler. He didn’t even sleep with Sophia Riley, but she was going to be the most expensive piece of ass he’d ever met. Alex was so lost in thought that he didn’t even hear the screeching wheels come up behind him. A dark vehicle slammed on the brakes as it just passed him, but Alex didn’t seem to notice. It wasn’t until three men in matching jumpsuits piled out of the sedan that he realized something was amiss. And the fact that they each had aluminum baseball bats in hand and were wearing black ski masks also helped Strider understand that something big was about to go down. Working on instinct, he worked himself into defensive posturing. The three men ran straight for him. ‘Stupid’, thought Alex. ‘Clumsy.’ Without so much as a yell, one of the assailants raised his aluminum bat high in the air. Not waiting for his two comrades, he rushed forward, aiming his bat high for Strider’s head in what would surely be a death-blow if it connected with his melon. Strider ducked low, his head narrowly missed being a grand-slam home run. Instinctively, Strider spin kicked at the first assailant as he ran past, sending him sprawling. His aluminum bat rang out against the concrete as it slipped from his hands as he tried to break his fall. Adrenaline pumped through Alex Strider like smack through an addict’s veins. He knew he was in danger like when he was being pursued on the highway, but this time was different. This time, he wasn’t trapped in a metal box flying down a road at 90 miles an hour. This time, he was in control. No matter that his opponents were armed, or that he was outnumbered 3 to 1…Alex had more than enough aggression to keep them in check. Dodging a second baseball bat, he let his masked opponent hit a concrete barrier. The sheer force of the masked man’s swing left a fracture in the barrier, but snapped bone and tendon in his hand thanks to the reverberation in the bat. He dropped the bat, crying out “Fuck!” Strider punched across the black cotton ski-mask with an uppercut, knocking him on his back. The third assailant had much better aim. Cracking Strider across his back, the aluminum bat found it’s mark. Strider dropped to one knee, the wind knocked cleanly from his lungs like a line-drive down the right side foul-line. The lucky assaulter smiled, and raised his bat for a final blow. Strider would not allow himself to be hit a second time. Rolling backwards, he donkey kicked the man with the bat square in the testicles. A loud “Oof!” and the bat fell out of his hands behind him. The man doubled over, clutching his manhood. ”You dirty motherfucker!” Strider thought, saliva spewing from his mouth as he axe-handle smashed a reinforced fist across the side of the man’s head, knocking him out. Like a wild animal, Strider looked around to see who else was still moving. Pointing out his target, Strider lunged after the man with the broken hand. He was trying to jog away, holding his crippled paw as he looked back over his shoulder. Strider ran full speed and brought him down like a cheetah taking down an antelope on the Serengeti. A few full forced punches to the man’s head, and a muttered promise to disembowel him if he kept fighting cowed his spirits. Strider dragged the man back toward the other two by one foot. Strider pulled the three men in a pile on top of each other. Not at all satisfied with simply disarming them, Strider muttered curses to himself as he gave each man another full forced punch to the face to remind them that it wasn’t over yet. Then Alex punched the man who he’d already knocked out hard across the jaw a second and third time for good measure. ”You assholes…are lucky…” Alex said, catching his breathe as he crouched over his fallen opponents, “you didn’t break my cell-phone.” Strider flipped his phone open. ”Because if you had… I wouldn’t be calling the police right now.” Alex dialed 911. “You better thank the officers when they get here, because without them, you wouldn’t be stopping by the hospital. You’d be going straight to the morgue, you stupid motherfucks.” Strider spat out some phlegm as he cleared his throat. It landed without protest in the left eye of one of the goons. ”Yes, operator? Hello. I was just assaulted at the Parking Garage on 221st Street. What’s that? Yeah.” Alex kicked one of the downed men in the taint. The man groaned out a guttural whine. “You’re going to need to send an ambulance.” ******** At the hospital, Strider gave his statement to an officer as a doctor wrapped gauze around his ribs. Strider sighed with relief when the doctor told him that though several were cracked, none were broken. Strider would have regretted not killing at least one of them if they’d actually managed to put him out of action. ”So, Alex,” the officer asked, “Why do you think these men attacked you?” Alex stopped, and ran his hand over the stubble on his face. ”Because my brother is trying to kill me.”
