Date: 06-02-05 It’s late, and he was going night-blind. The constant stream of headlights opposite him dulled his senses, allowing boredom to creep in like a late night prowler looking for an easy mark. The radio had been turned off because everything sounded the same that late at night, and it made him sick to his stomach to think about how much money entertainers made for doing such little work. Pop singers who get to rehearse and rehearse and rehearse and then sing out the same line a hundred different times before their overpaid producers finally find something they can mix till it sounds sugary sweet and devoid of any shred of soul; rock music that relied on the same three chord riff ripped off note, that refused all innovation because it wasn’t a part of the formula that the industry bigwigs decided would appeal to the largest audience. No personality, just bland imitations. Alex often compared himself to other entertainers. Nobody worked as hard as he did to earn their money, and he knew it. Every day he’s in a new town where he has to put his body on the line to entertain. None of that pussy bullshit that musicians got to deal with. The plush surroundings with the contracted venue “riders” that gave them amenities like the various bowls of M&M’s sorted by color. No dripping honey down your throat so you could hit those impossible high notes. Tens of millions of dollars handed out to star in a movie. Alex didn’t get those kind of breaks, and it made him mad to think that others did. No, Alex had to work out before each match. Break down the muscles so that they would grow, so he’d be strong enough to handle the punishment he had to suffer, week in and week out. Punish himself so he could endure. It all boiled down to discipline. Alex had to be smart with how he invested his money to make sure it would hold up. He wouldn’t be able to be a wrestler forever. One day, he’d take a fall wrong, or land on his neck and be up shit creek without a paddle. Forced early retirement was something Alex didn’t like to think about, but knew the possibility was all too real. He wasn’t going to end up penniless on the streets, drinking butane to forget the hell that his life had become. No. Alex was too smart for that, he put plenty of his savings into smart stocks and bonds, investing in his future. He knew how to balance living the life of a superstar with preparing for his future. That’s what he stood by: Have a plan for the worst possible outcome. He'd been driving since the moment Fresh! ended. As luck would have it, it was fortunate to get an extra week off, thanks to Harber’s mismanagement of setting the venue in Wilkes-Barre. “Finally”, Alex thought, “Harber’s fuck-up worked out for the better instead of fucking me over.” It would still be a busy time for him. He’d get to go back to Pittsburgh to report back on ‘the situation’. Having been cleared of all foul play didn’t take too much weight off of his shoulders. Alex still wanted to know who she was, and what she was doing with the drugs that killed her. She was no experienced heroin user, she was set up, and he wanted to get to the bottom of why she was dead. THUD… Alex was jerked forward in his seat, and before he could blurt out “What the fuck?” he was pushed hard from behind again. THUD… Another forceful bump came crushing from directly behind, like a great white shark trying to capsize a fishing boat. High-Beams flipped on behind him, shining directly into his rearview mirror. The sudden switch from blackness to 10 million candle lights blinded Alex, who was left turning his head to blink out the floating spots burnt into his eyes. THUD… All he could see in his rearview mirror was blackness surrounded by white hot high beams, some kind of a truck, ramming him from behind. No time to think, he reacted with pure instinct. He was jolted back to reality, all thoughts of the impending investigation out of his head now. Pushing the gas to the floor, he gunned his Caddy. He switched lanes, the truck following behind like a shadow, staying right on his bumper the whole time. Without so much as breathing the word “Asshole…,” Strider multitasks, checking his rearview mirror as he decides his next move. No amount of strategy on “The Mongooses” part could help him, his driving skills not being exemplary by any means. His shadowy foe slamming his truck again a third time, Strider came to the slow realization that someone just might be trying to kill him. Alex sped up, trying to take his truck to its very limit. This late at night, he had happened across few cars driving into Pennsylvania, which was fortunate, as the two large trucks sped along the highway in a death defying chase. Strider had to constantly check his rearview mirror before deciding what to do. A knot formed in his throat when he adjusted his eyes back on the road: he could see coming up in the distance was a diesel breathing eighteen-wheeler. Alex had to make a split second decision, as he was encroaching 90 MPH on the highway, and was quickly going to driving along side what could very well be a very messy crash into the side of a semi-tractor trailer. Alex applied the breaks, and slowed down, pulling out into the fast lane. Too late. His pursuer followed him, move for move, breaking with the same rate and following his movements almost exactly. What’s more; the challenging truck took the opportunity to push Strider’s truck from the side, engaging Alex in a perfect P.I.T. maneuver. If you’ve never ran from the police, chances are you haven’t been in a situation where someone was trying to run you off the road. The “Personal Intervention Technique” is often employed by police to avoid using deadly force. But at 90 miles an hour, it’s probably just as deadly as a simple ram and stop. Now, Alex had never been involved in any kind of a vehicular chase before, but he had seen enough of “World’s Wildest Police Video’s” to know that he was being forced off the road. There was nothing Alex could do, speeding along as he was. The enemy truck pushed his rear to the left, and Strider started a fishtail, speeding at 85 miles per hour. He completely lost control of his Escalade EXT. The world became a fast blur of darkness and blinding headlights that spun around like a cheap amusement park ride as his vehicle crossed over two lanes into the empty berm. Slamming on his brakes, Alex gritted his teeth, “Oh God, don’t let this flip over! I don’t want to get crushed like that guitarist from Metallica…” he silently prayed to a deity that he was quick to call on in dire situations, only to forget about when things are going well. Someone must have a funny sense of humor, because for all the bad Alex had done in his life, it wasn’t his fate to die just yet. Miraculously, his vehicle skidded to a stop, facing down the wrong way of the highway, halfway onto the berm. Alex looked around, he was only inches from the guard rail protecting him from a three-story drop into a ravine. Alex's head stopped spinning a split second later; long enough for him to catch one final glimpse of his attacker as he sped away. Whoever it was, was driving a very familiar vehicle: A diamond white Cadillac Escalade EXT. Thirty seconds later Alex remembered to breath again. ******** I hate making people cry. I feel so bad, asking her these questions about her best friend in the world. She’s already been through so much. Hearing about her best friend’s death through the news, the police questioning, the funeral…it was so much pain for someone so young. And now she’s being pestered by a reporter. I don’t know what she must think of me. And what’s worse, I’m here on campus; somewhere she should still feel safe, asking her personal questions about her dead best friend. I stopped by the cafeteria and got Jen a salad. I asked her to sit outside with me, and answer some questions about her friend. She agreed, because Jen and Sophia both liked to sit on the bench outside the dormitory and make fun of all the poorer kids who didn’t act like them. I guess a fair amount of guilt and regret was making things difficult for Jen to cope with Sophia’s death. “I’m terribly sorry, Jen,” I said meekly, and handed her a tissue. Her eyes were bloodshot and red. It had been a month since her friend died, but having to relive it every time someone comes by and asks questions can’t make the wounds heal any faster. ”I just don’t understand,' Jen said, cradling her cigarette inbetween her hands. 'Sophia was my best friend in the world. I would have known if she’d ever used heroin before. It just wasn’t like her.” She took a drag on her cigarette, the smoke rising in front of her teary eyes. Jen Dumas was a thin red-headed teenager, and she’d known Sophia Riley for years. They were both freshmen at Pitt, and they were both gorgeous young ladies. “Did the two of you go to 'Frais Viande' together often?” She nodded, ashing her cigarette. “We had fake I.D.’s. We could get in anywhere. She liked the luxury. She knew that was Alex Strider’s favorite hangout spot. She always wanted to meet him…” “How did she know that?” “Pittsburgh doesn’t have many local heroes. Word spread around school that was where you could find him.” I opened my notebook, ready to catch what information I could, “And how long had you two been waiting to meet him here?” “I don’t know,” she responded, pulling her red hair out of her eyes. “I guess we started going there a few months before. We liked it there, we felt like we belonged there, you know?” I nodded, but I really didn’t know. I never felt more out of place than when I was with Alex at the fancy dance-spot, 'Frais Viande'. It was full of attractive people drinking expensive drinks, wearing the flashiest clothing. The elite of society, showing off for each other, waving their tail feathers so they could find a mate for the night. Sophia and Jen were young, rich and privileged. Jen was probably so shaken up because she and Sophia imagined themselves to be invincible. “Well, did she talk to anyone else there? Anyone who was trying to help her meet Alex?” I asked, trying not to lead her. Jen pushed around her untouched Caesar salad with her fork. “I don’t know. I mean, we didn’t talk too much about the guys we’d meet there. Well, she wouldn’t talk to me about most of the guys she met, anyway. She was kind of protective of herself that way.” ”Why wouldn’t she tell you about the men she might have been involved with?” I wondered why Sophia wouldn’t talk to her own best friend about cute guys she’d meet. ”Well, sometimes Sophia would date guys who were already involved with someone. She didn’t like to be judged, I guess.” ”But you were her best friend, surely you didn’t judge her,” I said, edging out to her. ”No, but I would tell her if she was getting over her head with anyone. One time, she dated this guy I didn’t like. I heard he’d raped one of the seniors here at Pitt. I guess they broke up after a while, but she and I had a big falling out after that one.” “What was his name?” ”Look, it was last year. Part of the reason she got so mad at me was because I had never met him, and I had heard from a friend of a friend that he was trouble. She thought I was jealous. I feel kind of stupid about it now.” “You know, friends do look out for one another. You didn’t do anything wrong,” I said, placing my hand on her knee tenderly. She looked away and raised her eyebrows. ”Yeah, well I couldn’t stop her from going home with Alex Strider. I’m a great friend,” she said sarcastically, “I just never should have let her go to 'Frais Viande' by herself.” “I don’t know what the big deal is,” a woman's voice called out from behind us, “Some star-fucker slut O.D.’d on heroin? It was going to happen sooner or later. Just move on.” I turned around, the sun shining in my eyes. A brown haired, thin girl stood there, hands on her hips looking down at Jen and I. She was dressed like Jen, expensive clothes meant to show off her goods. ”You fucking bitch!” Jen said, standing up, flicking her cigarette away. “Oh, so now I’m the bitch? You and your friend Sophia are the real bitches here. If you ask me, she got what she deserved. That whore slept with my boyfriend last year. You think she was some perfect, preppy girl? She was nothing more than a star-fucker.” Jen stood up, and stepped over to Amanda, smacking her hard across the face. Amanda turned, her jaw left open wide like a fish stranded on dry land. She brought her hand to her face, her eyes tearing up. The look of shock wore off her face into a mask of anger and shame, and she lunged back at Jen, hands fumbling over her neck and shoulders. The next few moments moved in slow motion for me. I never liked watching girls fight. It makes me incredibly nervous; I started sweating right away. The girls rolled around outside, not caring as strangers stopped and stared. Jen got on top, ripping at Amanda's hair. I couldn’t move, no matter how much I wanted to scream stop, but I was frightened. Their reactions were so primal, not at all thinking through their actions or consequences. Expulsion, injury, neither Amanda nor Jen were concerned. They just wanted to hurt each other. The college kids that stopped by made no move to end the fight. They, like me, gawked on like the audience at the Coliseum. Jen let out several guttural growls, as she continued to smack hard at her enemy, pulling at Amanda’s hair. Jen's anger seethed through, relentlessly smacking and pulling. Feebly, Amanda reached up and desperately grasped at anything. She struck gold in Jen's large hoop earring, ripping it out through her earlobe. Jen screamed out in pain, raising her hand to her damaged ear. She stood up, and ran away without looking back, holding her bleeding ear the whole time. Amanda clutched the large gold hoop earring in her hand, shocked at the damage she had caused. She stood up, wiping the spatter of blood off her lip. She continued to hold the earring, now as a warrior clutching her prize. As she looked around, the look of shame disappeared from her face. ”Just who are you, Amanda?” I dared myself to speak. Amanda looked around, as the crowd began to disperse. She paused as she tried to catch her breath, calming her nerves down from the fight. “I’m just a girl who knows the real truth about Sophia Riley. She’s nothing more than a star-fucker,” she spit the words out of her mouth as she violently threw Jen’s hoop earring down on the ground. “She’s fucked a running back from the Steelers, a Chicago White Sox pitcher, hell, she even fucked the drummer from Our Lady Peace, and he's not even attractive. That chick got around. Trust. I’m not surprised she overdosed on drugs at all.” I countered, “She didn’t use drugs, Amanda. That’s what I’ve heard from almost everyone else.” “Well. I’ll have you know that the boyfriend she stole from me last year? Yeah, he was pretty big into them. That’s why I don’t miss him. When she was with him, she was definitely around drugs enough to know what the deal was. She probably thought she was strong enough to do anything she wanted. Well, the bitch was obviously very, very wrong.” I studied Amanda for a moment. Disheveled, she was shaking from the adrenaline rush. She looked like she could have been cousins with Sophia. What was with these women hating each other when they were so similar? I guess that’s kind of like a sibling rivalry that comes along. Territorialism at it’s worst; women fighting women over men. She was obviously a very bitter enemy of Sophia Riley’s. I had no idea what I could believe. She could be toying with me, like this was some kind of a game. “And what was your ex-boyfriends name?” I asked forcefully. I don't know what it was, but something about Amanda was making me angry. I wanted to find my backbone. I didn't want to be afraid anymore. She looked up at me, and frowned. Finally, she spoke: ”Darius. Darius Strider.
