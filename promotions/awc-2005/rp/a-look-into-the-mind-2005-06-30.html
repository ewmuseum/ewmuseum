A look into the mind...

30 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30th of June The following is a transcript of a discussion held between Crimson O’Malec and Dr. Jason Thurgood, his psychiatrist on June 28, 2005. Jason: How have you been lately, Crimson? It’s been awhile since we’ve had a good session. Crimson: Oh, I’m fine. Yeah, I believe it has been quite a while. I’ve been busy with this new wrestling career and all. Jason: I could imagine. And how is that going for you? Crimson: What the wrestling? It’s been going quite well actually. I’ve excelled better than I thought I would. I didn’t expect much starting out. Of course it had been years since I had even seen a wrestling ring. I had just started my third year in wrestling school when Jamie died. I was afraid I would have forgotten it all, but to my surprise, wrestling is sort of like riding a bike. Jason: So, the wrestling has caused no problems? Crimson: No, I had realized the possibility of getting into the wrestling ring causing me to have problems with my father’s death and all, but it has yet to be something I have had to face while in the ring. Jason: Do you believe that wrestling has caused any negative changes outside of the ring? Crimson: No, in fact I believe it has helped to free me from some of the troubles I’ve had. Jason: How so? Crimson: My flashbacks have decreased dramatically. It is no longer everyday that I struggle with the past. I have noticed a dramatic difference with the way I go through everyday life. Of course, there are reminders of things that I would rather not remember. However, these reminders do not hit me as hard as they used to. I really do believe wrestling has been good to me. Jason: What would you say is your general attitude towards wrestling? Crimson: I believe wrestling is a good thing. I believe wrestling is something that requires complete and total dedication to be something that you can excel in. I believe wrestling is an art form and is more than entertainment. Entertainment is grounds for censorship. Wrestling is not. Perhaps some of the aspects of wrestling and the wrestlers are not so beautiful, but that’s everywhere. Jason: So your censored parts of Ms. Nash’s um… I don’t know what you want to call it. Crimson: That was different. I complete understand censoring that on television. However Ms. Nash needed some extreme suggestions given to her. One of those being every man deserves to have his pride. Another being every man deserves to be able to have children. Jason: So what would you say is your relationship to Mr. Strider? Crimson: I don’t know really. Strider gets on my nerves. He sort of reminds me of gnats. Annoying as fuck, but you need them to go camping. Jason: How so? Crimson: Wrestling has its losers and winners. Wrestling has its headliners and pre-gamers. Wrestling has its bad guys and its good guys. Strider is just a little on the bad side. I think it’s kind of fun to screw with him every now and then. Jason: Would you say your accomplishments in AWC make you humble or egotistical? Crimson: Jason, you know me. I have never been egotistical. I think winning the Relentless title has made me proud, but I also see the sacrifices it took for me to get there and to stay there. Some days I regret winning the title and doing so well. It’s made me spend a lot of time in the hospital. It’s given me a lot of extra work. However, I also respect what it’s given me. Which is a platform for helping myself, a new view on life, and the ability to travel the world. Jason: How do you think that wrestling has given you a platform for helping yourself? Crimson: I can now look at my father’s death and not wish he had never been wrestler. I hated wrestling for the longest time. I couldn’t understand why my father would risk so much. I realize now that we O’Malec’s have a wrestling instinct like no other. I can’t explain it. It’s not a competitive person as much as I consider myself a deserving person. I know what I want and I go for it. Jason: So why do you want to wrestle? Crimson: I want to prove to myself that I can. It’s something that I was raised on. I think my appeal to wrestling comes from the honor that grows for yourself out of being a wrestler. Jason: What appeals to you with the relentless championship? Crimson: The relentless title is more than a championship for me. The relentless title proves to me that I can be as risky as my father. My father gave his life taking this sport to the next level. I think I atleast should give him the dignity. I think that I am letting my father’s spirit rest in peace and that I am finishing his project by innovating wrestling technique and taking each and every AWC event above and beyond the expectation of the spectators. Jason: How do you think your father felt about wrestling? Crimson: My Dad loved wrestling, it was probably his favorite thing in the world. He devoted his life to it. Jason: And what is devotion to you? Crimson: Devotion is the act of giving all of one’s able self for a goal. Devotion is a word many people do not understand. Devotion is something that many people do not give. Devotion gets the job done. Other words similar to devotion include dedication, commitment, allegiance, assiduousness, and most of all love. My dad loved the wrestling business. After family, wrestling was the most important thing to my dad. Jason: You feel you have this same devotion? Crimson: Absolutely. I’d give an arm and a leg for this business. In every match, I have that devotion. I want that pin, submission, knock out… Whatever it takes. It’s all about sacrifice. You get out of the match what you put into the match. Jason: Do you feel other people have this same devotion? Crimson: It is a rare occasion to see someone who is actually devoted to wrestling. It’s actually kind of sad. I’d like to see more people in the business who come in and give there all. There are way to many people who think wrestling is the way to a nice paycheck. Ninety percent of the wrestlers who do it for the money are horrible wrestlers. Only a select few of the money seekers are any good. The irony of it is that they are the ones making the most money. Then you have wrestlers who do it for the fame. These are the most despicable. One liners they do more talking then entertaining. Most of them are all talk. They talk about how great they are but their ass can’t cash the checks their mouths write. Jason: And your devotion is inspired by what? Crimson: My devotion is inspired by duty and return. To me it is about that dedication of self to do well. Also wrestling gives me more than popularity and a nice salary. Wrestling gives me a burning fire deep inside. Wrestling is inspirational to me. I would climb to the top of Mount Everest naked if I knew there was someone at the top whom wanted to wrestle me. Jason: So you want to wrestle everyone? Crimson: Oh, no. I’m not a violent person by nature. I just enjoy the action. I do find some satisfaction in taking some people back down to size. Some people start to be a little too confident of themselves and these are the people who need a little dose of reality. Jason: You think you are the person to give these people that dose of reality? Crimson: No, not exactly, but I know that some of them… I can give that dose of reality to them. Jason: Is there anything you dislike about wrestling? Crimson: Other than some peoples motives for getting into the business no. Jason: Is there anything you dislike about AWC? Crimson: I dislike the lack of respect I get sometimes. My size usually convinces people that I’m not a worthy opponent. This angers me. I do shut most of them up though, but like any other type of human being, some people don’t learn this way. I really wish some of the comments and jokes would stop, but it’s nothing I can’t handle. Jason: Why do you think these comments bother you so much? Crimson: For one, I know my dad had to deal with it. Back then, cruiserweights were not very popular. There actually weren’t too many cruiserweights at the time in the United States. Most of them were in Japan and Mexico. Secondly, I’ve never been to fond of people who judge a book by its cover. Jason: Are there any other assumptions made about you that bother you? Crimson: I really don’t like the fact that many people seem to guess that because I live here that I must be criminally insane. Jason: Do you think that is an unjust characterization? Crimson: Unfortunately, no. I just think people should give me or anyone else a chance before passing judgement. Jason: Don’t you think that moving out and starting your own life would keep this stereotype from being cast upon you? Crimson: I know it would, but I don’t want to leave. Jason: Why? Crimson: Rolling Creek is my home. I feel most comfortable here. My wrestling accomplishments are nothing compared to the things I have overcome within these walls. I have done so much with what I used to be since coming here. Jason: So why does the stereotype bother you? Crimson: It’s like what I’m doing now means nothing. Jason: What you are doing now, meaning what? Crimson: Like getting a wrestling career and trying to do things on my own isn’t enough for them. I think that becoming independent and slowly introducing myself back into society isn’t enough for them. I can’t stand that people think either once your insane always insane or that there should be just one easy fix. Jason: Who is them? Crimson: Society! Society seems to think that every patient and resident inside this building is a hopeless case. We live in a world where Zoloft and Saint John’s Wart are the answer. Well that’s absolute bull shit. Jason: So, insane people are correctable? Crimson: No! It shouldn’t be looked at that way. I don’t even like the word insane. There shouldn’t be a category for people who don’t conform to society’s expectations. Most people who become mentally unstable are people who are trying to self medicate. Let’s take D.I.D. for a second. People create multiple personalities to cope am I correct? Jason: Yes. Crimson: Then is it not societies fault for creating situations for which the human mind can not protect itself without having to remove itself even further from society? Jason: Interesting view. So do you think you were justified in silencing yourself for three years to get over the tragedies you witnessed in your own life? Crimson: Yes and no. Jason: How so? Crimson: I was justified when I went into myself. At the time I did not know that I was not to blame for the death of everyone close to me. I felt that if I allowed myself to get close to anyone they were automatically going to die. This was a perfectly normal self-actualization considering the stress I had been put under. Then, when I began to come out of my silencing times and began to have sessions with you and the other staff, I don’t think I was justified in silencing myself. I started to come to terms with what happened and my involvement in it, but I have every right as the next human being to be wrong in my assumptions. Jason: Would you consider yourself healed? Crimson: No healing. Jason: What do you think has kept you from being healed? Crimson: I still have times when I have troubles coping and I often times still find myself confused with the possibility of misconceptions. Jason: What do you have confusion about now? Crimson: Jessie. Jason: What confuses you about her? Crimson: I’m not sure how I feel about her. Jason: What feelings do you have towards her? Crimson: I have a huge variation of feelings for her. Hate, love, annoyance, attraction, everything in the book. Jason: Do you think you love her? Crimson: I’m not sure. I am afraid that I may only be transferring the love I had for her sister to her. Jason: Do you think Jessie is like Jamie? Crimson: Yes. Jason: Then what is the problem with transferring that love? Crimson: It’s not love for Jessie. I don’t have the history with her. I would always second guess the motivation. Jason: Do you think that the reason you don’t have a history with Jessie is because you never gave Jessie the chance? Crimson: Possibly. However, what if it becomes to painful? Jason: What if it doesn’t? What if it’s the obvious? Crimson: How will I ever know that my love is for her? Jason: Do you call her Jamie? Crimson: No. Jason: Are they times you mistake her for Jamie? Crimson: Not so much anymore. Jason: Would you consider it more of a remembrance of Jamie or is it more of forgetting Jamie died? Crimson: Remembrance. Jason: I’m not going to give you my opinion. I think you must come to your own conclusions, but I’ve given you some things to think about. Let’s move on. How are you finding life on the road? Crimson: Enjoyable. It will be my first time to New York City for the upcoming pay-per-view. Then we head off to Europe. Which for me is very exciting, I can not wait to return to Ireland. Jason: Which reminds me. Tell me more about this Paddy O’Shea. Crimson: Paddy is a nice guy. He’s oftentimes subjected to the same stereotype I am. Which is why I gave him a chance. He has his history as do I, but I don’t question it. I let him tell me what he wants to tell me. I let Paddy be Paddy which I don’t believe many people have ever done for him. I treat him like a normal human being. I don’t pass him off because he’s different. It’s sort of like Paddy and I have that in common. We aren’t exactly your perfect role models and maybe for that reason we aren’t accepted as easily by “common folk.” Paddy deserves a chance from everyone. Sometimes it bothers me that people use him for a good laugh. I am trying to help Paddy he’s a great guy and he deserves it. Jason: Do you see yourself in Paddy? Crimson: Absolutely, he’s a lost mind, just as I was. I think he just needs the guidance onto the right path. He’s a hell of a good wrestler too. Not as technical as I am, but he definitely has the dedication and the heart. His only flaw in dedication is his addiction to alcohol, but we all have our coping mechanisms. Jason: Can you tell me anything that’s going to happen at the Pay-per-view? Crimson: Other than I’m going to win the alliance titles and show the world that the sun is the best damn thing to ever happen to it? No… it’s all top secret. Jason: We’ll I think that about wraps up your hour today. Let’s see my hourly rate these days is… One hundred and twenty dollars I think… Or do you need me to convert that to pounds? Crimson: Nice try. This ones on you! Jason: Aren’t they all, aren’t they all…
