The Lavelle Chronicles, CH.10

6 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6th July The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch.10 Part 2: I could have expected something a little more appealing than something such as this, but then I remembered her face when she had first learnt the truth about Lilly Connelly and Marvin Harrington and I knew from that moment that I couldn't keep secrets from her. I had received a cold silence for an hour, but it soon passed as she asked me all the questions she wanted and I answered, with the full truth. I felt a lot more relieved sitting here and waiting for her to pay the doctor bills. I had thought a lot about Robert since Lois had told me of his sudden departure to Florida. I always pictured that he would return after a few days and be just fine with life. I guess maybe I was so self-involved with my own issues and reasons that I never saw nor listened to what was going on with Robert. I regret it now; I was his best friend and I wasn't there for him, instead I protected somebody else. I knew that there was one thing for sure I didn't feel wrong about doing or saying and that was being in love with Lois. I knew Robert had a problem with it. Lois had told me of his interference and also of her brother’s quick arrival. I felt some betrayal in a sense that I was seeing somebody Robert cared about too, I guess when I first came to the realisation of my feelings for Lois, I was in a sort of denial, in the sense I wouldn’t actually be able to accept the bad things that could come of it. “Pierce, is everything okay?” Lois stood next to me with a bill in hand. “I’m fine.” I had to lie there, I knew she would only worry about me and I couldn’t have that. It was a fresh start for both of us, so I knew what I had to do. Robert would have to be forgotten for now. I would let him get on with his life in Florida. “You ready?” I looked up and saw Lois smiling at me. “Couldn’t be more ready,” I jumped up off the cold plastic seat and linked my arm around Lois’s. “I hope this flat is nice looking.” “I’d say so, the name is appealing,” Lois looked at me now. “What is the name of our apartment block?” “Summer Plaza,” “Yeah, you’re right. It does sound appealing. Come on, Lavelle. It’s time to enjoy life now. I think after our little scene from “Scarface” it might be time to move on. I need some breakfast, you up for pancakes?” I laughed at Lois’s reference to ‘Scarface’. She too had become sucked into our movie marathon on a Saturday. She had an interesting take on what had happened to us two weeks ago. “There’s just something I need to do first,” “What’s that?” “Visit Lilly’s grave.” “But, that’s in Boston,” she looked at me, saddened by the mention of Lilly. “No - eh - not her real grave. The one the residents of Western set up where the old apartment block used to be. I don’t have time to fly to Boston. I wouldn’t even know where the grave is. It’s a big city.” Lois tired to put her arm over my shoulder, she struggled a little, she was much smaller than me, so she resorted to putting it around my waist. “I’ll come with you, if you like?” “I’d like that.” Letting go The old apartment block was now just gravel, with three name plates rested on top of it. Each one made of marble. The names of the three people that died were engraved in gold filigree, along with their birth date and death date. Lilly Connelly’s was in the middle of the two firemen. I knelt down, a white rose in hand. Lilly Connelly 19th April 1983 - 28th June 2005 Beloved daughter and sister A loving friend Rest in Peace… “I’m sorry Lilly….” I placed the rose on top of the small slab and stood-up. Lois was waiting for me and I smiled. I had said my goodbyes to Lilly for good now and I felt thankful somewhat that the events were now more or less forgotten. “Do you need a moment?” Lois asked gently. “No, I’m fine. I’d like to have that breakfast with you, if you still want to?” “Of course. But who’s paying?” She winked and held my hand. Her fingers clasping around mine. “Both of us, we’ll split it.” “I have just noticed something,” she dipped her head down. “Yeah, what’s that?” “This is the first time anyone’s taken me out to anything, since I arrived.” “Really? Rob never took you on a date.” She shook her head. “I hope you like blueberry pancakes,” I smiled. “I love you, Pierce.” “I love you too,” We began to leave the old apartment block, leaving the memories of disaster behind us as we headed over to “Grandmas home cooked breakfast” diner. The events were like an old memory left in the back of my mind. The morning always made things a lot clearer for me. I have begun to look at life a lot like a ‘pay as you go’, sometimes we play a little in life, but most of the time we pay a lot. Pain and suffering are the prices we all must pay, but then sometimes, just the odd little time; we get love and someone to care for. That’s when relief, heartache and panic end. I was glad to have found Lois. Summer Ave. “Summer Plaza” Apartment 234 Western Blvd. I paced up and down the main lobby of our new residence. Still digesting the four pancakes I was left to devour after Lois had run after someone that stole another woman’s purse. She had a direct impulse to help others, something she couldn’t help. I was just lucky that the thief didn’t have a knife. When Lois had eventually caught up with him, he was so scared of her obnoxious, army-brat attitude and also her karate, that he threw it back to her. Lois laughed the whole way back to the diner. Now I stood, alone, by reception in the Summer Plaza as Lois had paraded off with the manager in an attempt to settle on deal with a more appealing view. Our apartment looked out into the back alley, which was nice with its little serene garden, but our view was on an old, mouldy dumpster, it was something I didn’t mind, but if I wanted a new view, Lois was the girl to get it. “There you are sir,” the receptionist handed me a new key. I looked at it, quizzically. “Your wife has a way of getting things, doesn’t she? Well, your new flat room is the top floor penthouse, with the circular view of the city, enjoy.” “Eh - er - she’s not my wife,” I smiled. “You could have fooled me,” the receptionist pointed to Lois. Lois was still dressed in the same clothes as two nights ago, but this time she had a new backpack filled with newly bought attire from all her favourite womanly boutiques. “The truck out back contains all of the furniture, please show them where to go,” Lois yelled over to the fitting manager. “Lois, I think you should cool down for a second,” I whispered to Lois. “Why? I like playing this role…I’m just using the voice my dad used to use when he bossed his troops around. Funny thing is it works.” “This way,” the fitting manager showed a loud of men from the furniture store up the long stairs towards our flat. “Thanks and sorry about my out-burst,” Lois apologized to the fitting manager and he smiled. “Oh my god, Lois just apologized. That should hit the newsstands by tomorrow morning.” Lois punched my shoulder lightly and then held my hand. “Come on, Pierce. We have a flat to decorate,” she smiled and pushed the elevator button. “I wouldn’t want to argue with you, I have seen enough General Lois today!” She laughed and entered the elevator; I followed quickly and watched the doors closed. She turned to face me; she was smiling now and looked me in the eyes. I placed my hands on her hips and pulled her into a kiss, a passionate one. She gave me a look when the doors opened and I jumped off. “Quick reflexes,” she smiled. “Whoa, would you look at this flat!” “It’s pretty amazing, isn’t it?” “It’s lovely. I would never have pictured myself living in a penthouse in Albany with a boyfriend.” She looked around at all of the furniture and began rearranging the couch so that is faced the TV. The window offered a large view of the city, including my new College, Harbrook. I smiled when Lois called me her boyfriend, but hid it from her as I gazed at all of the College students seated on the green grass, cars passing by, oblivious to the future lawyers, doctors and journalists. “What are you looking at?” Lois came up from behind me. “Harbrook College. It looks so tiny from up here, a lot clearer actually.” “Speaking of College, shouldn’t you get on the phone and call Dean Winters at Thompson.” “Em…that’s probably not a good idea.” “Why not?” “She expelled me, remember?” Lois laughed and handed me the phone. “She can’t keep you from moving to a new college.” Lois once again was right. Lois made everything so much simpler and I loved her more and more as time went by. I placed my fingers over the buttons, but I couldn’t bring myself to dial the number. I decided I would head over to the supermarket first and then I would deal with Dean Winters. “I’m going to head over to the supermarket; pick up some food and beverages.” “Sure, leave me to do this then,” she looked at me and then pointed to the furniture and boxes. “If I moved anything, you would kill me. I know you’ll make it perfect.” “Yeah, you’re right,” she smiled. “I always am,” I pulled on my jacket. Lois smiled at my impression of her and began unloading some of the boxes containing sheets for the bed and pillows/ I kissed her and left with one bag for groceries. We hadn’t even unpacked the kitchen appliances yet. The cooker was working though, Lois had used it to light herself a cigarette, but as soon as she lit it, she put it out. I knew it brought back memories of the fire. I knew she would never smoke again and in some ways I thanked the fire for that. Pariah The shopping centre was jam packed after I left. I was just relieved to be heading home. Night time had come quickly today and I couldn’t wait to cook something. I opened the door to our apartment. The lights were all on and working. Pictures of me, Lois and Rob, along with both mine and Lois’s families were up on the cupboards and walls. The furniture was all arranged perfectly, the boxes piled up against the coat hangers. Lois emerged from inside the master bedroom, dressed in a new pair of jeans and a “Thompson College” jumper. Her hair was soaking wet. “Shower works,” she smiled. “I got some food,” I still had a look of awe on my face. “You like what I did with the place?” she approached me and took the bag of groceries and began putting them into the fridge and cupboard. “Yeah, it looks….really nice, Lois.” I hung my jacket up and sat down on the sofa, turned on the TV and NFL came on. “Finally, something relaxing…” my speech was interrupted by a cushion crashing into my face. Lois had thrown it at me. “Okay, now you have asked for it,” I jumped up, but she stopped me. “You have some boxes to bring down to the dumpster,” she pointed to the empty boxes which once contained all of our furnishings. I nodded my head and ambled my way over toward them. Lois laughed and changed the channel from football to something more her style, ‘The O.C’. “I’ll be right back,” “Pierce, I’ll order Pizza.” “Hold back on….” fitting a few boxes into one large “Pepperoni,” she laughed and looked at the “domino’s” menu “No…” “Mushrooms. I know Pierce - I - I have known you for six years,” she laughed. I smiled at Lois’s quick response to my pizza order. Grabbing as many boxes as I could carry, I began my long decent down the stairs toward the back parking lot. The back alley for the apartment block was one of tranquillity. There was a large fountain with a few benches surrounding it, a small pathway cobbled with small shells and stones. The grey, mouldy, dumpster seemed out of place, an outsider to this back alley. Not dwelling further on the appearance of the back alley; I flung the boxes one by one into the dumpster. I began to walk back toward the apartment when the last one crashed into the black abyss above me. The light to our room was still on and I knew Lois would be waiting. I turned on the heel of my shoe, listening to the shells crushing beneath it and made my way, with a fast paced stride back to our room. Unspoken words The word sounded alien in my mouth, as if it were spoken by somebody else. There was a slight hint of urgency in my own voice that I didn't even recognise. The simple 'hello' that I had spoken so willingly, lovingly and quietly into the phone had come out as almost desperation. But, the voice that came back was one I just didn't want to hear. 'Pierce Lavelle?' 'Yes, who may I ask is this?' I already knew who it was; I just needed to be sure that this wasn't some weird dream, like last time. 'This is Dean Winters, from Thompson College.' I sighed into the phone, my mouth became dry. I thought after I said my goodbyes to that College, I would never have to go back or listen to her again. Dean Winters certainly expressed the unwillingness to have me back in our last confrontation. 'How may I help you, Dean?' my tone was clearly sarcastic. 'Were you expecting somebody else to ring, Pierce Lavelle?' I could picture her seated at the dusty oak desk, my large file, opened out flat in front of her as she grumbled into the phone. 'Yes, but I can always call that person later. How may I help you?' 'You can't.' 'Excuse me?' 'I got a phone call from a Lois Wood. She is very persuasive Pierce, you are lucky. Well I have accepted her proposal and I am sending your transfer papers to your new address. I have also agreed to drop the charges on the count that we expelled you. If 'Harbrook' requires a background check, then I shall tell them nothing other than what a good student you were.' 'What about my expulsion?' 'What about it?' the Dean asked cautiously into the phone. 'Well - em - won't they ask about it?' I couldn't say anything other than this; I was still in shock that Lois had gone to all this trouble just to get my College life back on track. I was grateful. I thought she had ordered Pizza, which she did, but not until after she had made a mystery phone call, which turned out to be to Dean Winters. 'Pierce Lavelle, your expulsion is not another College's concern. If they ask I will agree to tell them that it was wrong of us and that we expelled the wrong student. Once you left Harrisburg, I did a background check on the people you mentioned that had set you up. Since you left there have been three times as many broken windows. Late night parties in the girls dorm rooms, the pool has been died green, red and orange. I realise it was wrong to fully blame you and your expulsion is being taken off your record. I am sorry I misjudged you, Pierce. I hope you prosper in your career in Medicine and your new life in Albany, good-day.' After a brief silence, the Dean hung-up. I felt relieved. She had found out the truth and my name was cleared. Lois had done this for me. There was no other explanation. I knew I could always count on her to get me out of some really bizarre situations. The pizza lay on the counter top. Lois had gone into the bedroom for some rest. I opened up the 'Domino's' box and pulled out one slice. Just as she promised, she held back on the pepperoni and there we no mushrooms, I smiled and then moved over to the open bedroom door. 'Thanks Lois,' I whispered while looking into the master bedroom as she slept, soundly, still dressed in all of her clothing. I ate the one slice and put the Pizza onto a plate and then into the oven. I wanted our first dinner to be together. So now the waiting game started. Instinct Lois remained seated in the bedroom as she listened to the commentary from an NFL game that Pierce Lavelle was listening to. She knew her previous phone call to the Dean had gone off without a hitch; as she heard Pierce’s thank you and smiled to herself. She realised she really was one for journalism and media, she had a way of getting things out of people and it emitted a sense of control over herself. She got up and stared at herself in the mirror. Her hair had become a ball of frizz since she hadn’t dried it after it was wet. She realised then that the pillow would most likely be soaking wet and would have to be changed. “Damn this hair,” She looked around for the hairdryer and resorted to using a hair-straightener instead. After a few minutes of intense burning of the hair, her appearance was back to normal. Her hair dried, straight and without frizz. She smiled and decided she would do some research on “Harbrook” and some journalism courses there. “This looks good,” In front of her was a “Harbrook” brochure she had bought on the open-day. Its page was open on News Journalism and Media relations. Underneath the modulus she realised the other course she could take with it, Law. “I couldn’t possibly be a lawyer?” she said to herself over and over. Scrolling down the page once again, checking the points needed for the course she wanted. “Or maybe I can?” It was something she had never intended on even glancing at, and now she found herself drawn to the three letter word as though it held some magnificent importance to her. The brochure of “Harbrook” remained open on the same page as she quickly ran her finger over the newly bought laptop. The blue coloured windows label appeared and she quickly hit “internet explorer”. Glancing down at the brochure she hurriedly typed the Harbrook internet address into the search engine and was immediately taken to the site. ‘Perhaps I am going above my limit’- she thought once as she hit Law and Criminal relations. Her job was now full of opportunities. Law - Criminal - Contract Law - Business Relations - Political Law News Journalism Media Relations Lois smiled and hit the first two choices as past for her modulus. “Why not,” she said again and began filling in the form on her bedside table. For name she struggled with. She didn’t know whether to use Lois Mahr-Wood, Lois Mahr or just Lois Wood. She realised Lois Wood would be the best name to have in regards to journalism and law. She filled in the rest of the sheet and logged off the laptop. Her decision was made, her choices set. She smiled and made her way into the living room. Calamity “I didn’t wake you, did I?” I smiled at Lois now seated next to me. “No,” she handed me her form for ‘Harbrook’. “Law?” I was so confused. “I thought you wanted to do journalism?” “I do, but look at it this way. I have a better chance in making some good money and making a difference by being a lawyer and solving problems. I am taking a small journalism course. This way I get the inside scoop into everything,” she took back the page and rested it on the small coffee table. “Attorney Lois Wood. Sounds good. You always have the best ideas when you need them,” I smiled and put my arm around her. “Do you want some pizza?” “I’d love some. Oh - em - isn’t it cold?” “I put it in the oven, I hope it isn’t burnt. I got a bit sidetracked with the game.” “And a beer!” she smiled and held up a bottle of beer. “I haven’t touched that, yet,” I smiled and pulled out the pizza with an oven glove. There were three knocks on the front door. Lois gracefully hoped over the back of the sofa and ran, barefooted, to the door at an amazing speed. Her face dropped as she opened the door. She stood back shocked. “Mr. Lavelle?” she was pushed aside as my dad stepped into the flat. “Lois,” he said as he brushed passed her and stared at me. “What are you doing here?” I looked at him, putting the pizza on the counter. “My father owns this residence, that’s how I knew you were here,” my father moved forward, removing his leather gloves and straightening his gold Rolex. “I didn’t ask how you knew I was here. I asked why you are here.” “No need to be rash with me,” he gazed at Lois. Lois stood back away from my father. She was always afraid of him, every time he came to her house to get me home, or when she used to be in my house with Robert. She always thought he had something against women, so she kept her distance. “I’ll talk to you how I want. You are just related to me by blood, I said it once before - I don’t have a father. Now if you don’t have any reason for being here, then get out.” “Hush, son. I have a reason. You should learn to respect your elders,” my father pulled out a thick document from the inside of his briefcase, he threw at me and I caught it with the oven glove. “What’s this?” I looked at a blank white page. “Turn it over and you’ll see. Now if you will excuse me, I have a plane to catch.” When I turned it over I was greeted by a large red word “DIVORCE”. I realised then that my father was going to divorce my mother, but not face to face. He gave me the document to give to her. “You son of bitch, you’re going to divorce mom, just after she has recovered from cancer. You really do have a cold heart,” I threw the document on the counter. “You are just going to walk away from her, without a goodbye. Despite everything you did to her, she loves you.” I ran forward and grabbed him by the shoulder. “Listen to me when I speak to you,” My father turned and flung his fist into my stomach. I took it with a pinch of salt and delivered my own punch to my father’s nose. He wobbled backward and it began to bleed. Lois dove in behind me and pulled me back. “Pierce, it’s not worth it,” she said. Lois looked at me and held my hand. “Good day son,” my father rose from his fettled position and wiped the blood from his nose. He left hurriedly down the stairs. Lois closed the door and turned to face me. She looked at the droplets of blood on the floor. “For a man with such a black heart, he sure does bleed a lot. I think you broke his nose,” she pulled down a cloth and began to wipe the blood off the tiled floor. “I’m sorry you had to see that,” I turned away and looked at the documents again. “I don’t mind Pierce. I never liked him anyway.” “That makes two of us,” I turned to face her. “What I am going to tell my mom and Rebecca?” Lois stopped wiping the floor and stood up. “We’ll figure something out,” she hugged me. My mind was a drift now. The man that had ruined my life, now planned on destroying two others and he had done it with such precision and chaos. I felt so stupid, because for one moment I was happy he was leaving this family. I had no idea on what to do next. I felt like chasing after the man that so boldly called me 'son' and beating him to death, but Lois kept reminding me that violence would never solve anything. 'Can you talk to you Rebecca; you connect with her a lot more than I do?' I looked at Lois now. 'Sure, I want to help with this. Your mom and sister are like my family too, I have known for sometime now.' 'Thanks Lois, I think I'd be lost without you.' 'Don't worry about it. We'll deal with this in the morning. Let’s enjoy the pizza and watch some television.' 'I guess so,' 'You can't help everyone, Pierce,' she looked at me and placed her hand on mine. 'You shouldn't beat yourself up when you can't help the odd person. You are extremely caring and selfless, your mother will be okay, and she is a strong woman. You just need to be there for her and I am sure you'll do that fine.' I looked down at the document once more and I realised, for once, that maybe this was a good thing for my mother. After trying for so many years to run away from him, he has run away from us. I felt some anxiety over the pending conversations that I new was to come with the lawyers…
