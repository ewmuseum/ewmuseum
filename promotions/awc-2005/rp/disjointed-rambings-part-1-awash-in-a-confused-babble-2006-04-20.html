Disjointed Rambings Part 1: Awash in a Confused Babble

20 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/20/2006 The insides of my eyelids must have been tattooed with scenes of sliding swarms, displaying the festering ugliness of not only humanity, but life in general. I couldn’t sleep. Every time I closed my eyes, I was hit with short bursts of visions: French peasants cupping spilt wine from cobblestone grooves in Dickens, greed teeming from hunger and need, flies coating dead robin corpses or shit. The buzzing was loud and incessant, and it pulsated with the throbbing of my own heart, beating too fast, beating like tiny insect wings. I felt that lump again, a thin legged crawl itching around in my throat. There were definitely spiders inside, weaving webs to catch my saliva to create a warm moist place in which to lay eggs and multiply- start a colony inside of me- to swallow what I have chewed before I could swallow it myself. But I couldn’t swallow. The more I thought about it the harder it became. So I laid there with a sponge dry tongue and a pounding drum heartbeat, wondering whether or not I was going to wake up if I fell asleep. This is more or less how things happened every night, and I’d wake up every morning, but that did little to console me. I had mixed feelings about dying. I think most people do. Most people make a pretty big deal about it, but it can’t be any worse than living. They say that when you die, your whole life flashes in front of your eyes. They say that because there’s no better euphemism than cliché. And that’s what I respect about death. With or without a god or an afterlife, death imposes a respectful, unconquerable awe on mankind. It’s the most unspeakable taboo. It still humbles man in an era where nothing is shocking. And a large part of me hopes that we’re all shocked by nothing. Eternity is a long fucking time to spend stuck in the same consciousness. Still, the part in me exists that houses the deepest sense of arrogance. Something deep inside me pushes towards the surface, echoing a contained yet resounding question. The soul, it exists, and it rearranges its individual parts in every combination, looking for some reasonable pattern or some patterned reason. I want to know why I exist. I want to know who I was, and if I was one constant being, a specific blotch of thoughts and emotions; an identity; a philosophy. Or was I more than just one? Perhaps the body is a vessel through which the force that separates the self-aware from the dormant may travel freely, cyclically. The human psyche changes ever so slightly as each sifting grain of sand falls through fate’s hourglass. Soul drips through body slowly and vitally like saline into the thirsty vein. Sand and water are part of the same global mechanism. Deserts and oceans expand on forever, all consuming, with mischievous murderous intent towards those they cradle. When enveloped by one, man desires the other, lest he drowns or dehydrates. I was gasping for both water and air. I was praying for wings. If I had wings, I’d fly right up to the fucking sun like Icarus. But I’d be invincible. Instead, I’m stuck with these worthless limbs that flail and kick and try to stay afloat. The legs slowly turn into dead weight, and I let the numbness seep up my torso, down through my shoulders and through my fingertips, shooting outwards like telekinetic rays. My mind grapples towards focus. Ignore the ticks and itches. Ignore the moving light, cutting through the shadows from the outside, peering through blinds, moving with every passing car. Ignore the loud, tiny noise that inflates with bloated boisterousness, frilling reptilian to shout away its perfect predator. Let the soul pierce the skin and perception’s horizon. The sleeping pills start to kick in. I remind myself of the nicotine patches that cover my naked, blanketed body. I hear that helps with lucidity, and it curbs my sleeping tobacco cravings. I tie my mind to my body with the patches. They let me know which me is there. They’re my stitches to the present; my reminder of exactly what moment I was leaving in case I got lost. Chances are, though, that I’ll die of a nicotine overdose while I’m sleeping, and I won’t even know about it. Everything is a fucking drug. The brain plugs and unplugs synaptic bonds in an ongoing process of becoming. The order and location of these attachments channels the soul to new conditions and realities. It’s a fact. I heard it from some scientists. I had gotten to thinking about dying, and nothing was flashing before my eyes. It’s funny. I feared that flash. Suppose that did happen. Suppose everything you’ve ever done reemerged at once. The flux of memory rupturing the membranous skin of the mind pressures and disperses into the permanence of uncertainty. It would be too late, and there would be nothing gained. It would pop like a balloon, but it would sound much worse than it would actually hurt. I needed to operate inside my mind, manually disconnecting and reconnecting the various outlets in my head. But I wasn’t without concern. I feared the damage that had already been done would be too great. My memory was a dysfunctional vehicle towards enlightenment. How many things had I forgotten or distorted? The truth exists somewhere outside of memory, undoubtedly, but then so must illusion. Maybe it was irreparable, maybe it’s pointless, but I have nowhere left to go. So I dug through, in search of a clue as to who I was, and why I was here because maybe it can help me to find out who I am and where I am going. That way, maybe I can change it to something better. I take a cigarette break from my present reality. Here I am, standing on the outside, soaked in the plasmatic goop after emerging from the mind’s womb, born a second time; naked and fearless. And ignorant. I pull out one hundred millimeters of sweet tobacco and light it up. I can take on anything. *** The arena of my memory was a blank, stormy gray. It had a certain offsetting lack of emotion; an incapability of remorse. It hung like a low hovering cloud over the familiar street with the brightly colored row of familiar houses swooning with life behind green trees with thick, brown bark. I remembered peeling the bark off of these trees as a child and holding a piece of something so big and amazing in my hands. I had heard that animals and trees traded carbon dioxide for oxygen, and it made me feel some connection to the trees. As I grew, the world got smaller, fell ordinary, and I had stopped caring. I realized how vicious and ominous that plant life actually was. Tree branches poked and scratched in the dark. Flowers spouted off allergens to ward us off. In uncivilized territory, you have to watch out for thorns and nettles, and in some cases, teeth. Plants have teeth now, and snap action jaws- and poisonous berries and other potential weapons of mass destruction. But here, I felt obligated to peel bark again. I wanted to pick the wooden scabs, trade flesh and blood like we were breathing into each other. There had to be trees inside me still. So I grabbed a loose piece of bark and pulled, and it revealed another bare edge. And I peeled this tree wide open until I saw organs, breathing and pumping like some self-perpetuating machine. The grass glowed bright green with glistening sweat beads of dew, newly awake under the hot sun. I knelt down in front of the tree, cigarette dangling from between my lips, and put my hands down in the dirt, massaging the ground, feeling its rich texture crumble and meld around my fingers. The earth’s pulse beat and subtly rippled beneath me. I could feel it give underneath my weight. It was taking me in. I remembered caterpillars, and how those ugly, slimy creatures would emerge one day with colorful wings. I used to step on them to watch their gummy entrails pus out in pools. I felt like a giant, trampling the masses underfoot. I felt innocent in my actions, that is, until I started feeling guilty about killing caterpillars. They have no agendas, and under the shadow of a child’s sneaker, they’re too slow and defenseless to do anything but succumb to imminent caterpillar doom. Happy bells churned out “The Entertainer,” breaking my chain of thoughts, and I looked up for the source of that familiar sound. An ice cream truck slowly floated down the street. It was a clumsy truck that ambled past me. I stood up and ran after it until it slowed to a halt. I was mesmerized in front of the colorful selection of frozen treats. There were ice cream sandwiches and Dreamsicles and Flinstones Push Pops and firecracker popsicles. The quilted bright menu extended on in vibrant patches as far as the eye could see. And then I was surrounded, engulfed in a sea of children all screaming and waving money. They demanded ice cream. They needed ice cream. Pavlov’s bells kept ringing out the inflammatorily chipper tune. “doo doo doo DOO doo DOO doo DOO! doo doo doo DOO doo doo DOO doo DOO doo!” And the flock of dog-headed children responded, snarling, foaming. The rabid fuckers started biting me. I pushed my way to retreat and escaped the howling horde. I started running, the patches of blaring, primary colors whirring behind me. The small squares on the side of the truck blended to boxy houses, rowed in shades of beiges and olive greens and suburban blues. I was afraid of rabies, briefly, until I realized that nobody got rabies nowadays. It was a dead form of entertainment, though little was more entertaining than the notion of becoming rabid and attacking strangers. It’s too socially unacceptable, but that’s not the way it always was. I came to a stop in front of a house, a two story, pitiful rectangle that was painted a withering shade of bright yellow. I watched from across the street as a small blonde kid batted a tennis ball as far as his bony, little arms could hit it. He dropped the bat, ran across the yard after the ball, and repeated the process. He was content following this same routine all day. He had pretended not to notice me. He was taught never to talk to strangers. I looked suspicious and dangerous, and I fit the profile perfectly of the type of man the child was warned to stay away from. I had never thought of myself as a stranger before, and the moniker felt somewhat descriptive at the time. The worst type of stranger, though, was always offering candy to children. They were disgruntled ex-taffy pullers, waging a war on the youth of America with their forbidden confectionaries, and apparently, they were everywhere. And the safety programs never told you this, but they’d only lure away little girls in cars with tinted windows. The proper way to go about kidnapping a boy was to let him come to you, get a little too close to your doors, and it was easy to lure them inside for warm biscuits. Little boys were just asking to be kidnapped. How they loved those warm biscuits. The question most of these kinds of strangers faced was how to get the boy near the lawn. Nothing seemed to work better in their minds than freshly mowed grass. In the right neighborhoods, at the right time of day, you could see them mowing. And sometimes the grass seemed too even and freshly trimmed, and people would start to wonder about the constant mowing. Maybe some of them dreamt about mowing. It was a strange addiction, to say the least, what with the fumes of gasoline and clippings, that mowing. I returned from thought after hearing a blunt pop. The tennis ball deflected off the tip of the bat and flew sideways, bouncing across the street and landing in front of me. The boy turned and looked at me. He was anxious. I was the dangerous stranger; the one he would grow up to be. I knelt down, picked up his tennis ball, and softly tossed it back to him. He hustled back to his spot at one end of the yard and started swinging away again. I turned from him. The fear in the child’s eyes pierced me. I held back tears as I continued walking north, past yards that smelled like freshly mowed lawn. I lit a cigarette, but before I could even take a drag, I broke down. I wanted to run back across the street and warn him. I wanted to show him what would happen when he did this to himself later. I wanted to tell him to keep wondering, to stay curious, and to never give up his idealism. But how? That was impossible, it was too easy. I couldn’t even figure out how I could have done that myself in hindsight. Part of me was still trying to figure it out. I regained myself and sat down where the sidewalk curved into a cul-de-sac. I puffed, nurtured, on my cigarette and watched the cars casually pass by. The nicotine began to kick in and numb the confusion. Everything spun around me, but I was here, and I knew I was here, because I could feel the cravings sucking most appeased. I became aware that I needed to find a way back into that naïve, bony frame. *** Now, I wasn’t a very religious man, and I never was one to pray for heaven or fear hell, but I believed that both existed in a metaphorical, metaphysical sense somewhere within. The knowledge of a philosophy creates the existence of that philosophy in the mind. Gods and devils exist in our understanding of their concepts. Hell does exist. The world as we know it is an illusion, simply the highest dimension we can interpret as human beings. Other dimensions exist below us, dimensions too basic for our minds to see, and other dimensions far more complex are out there as well. These dimensions are layered on top of one another, only creating what is real through the combination of layered illusion. We cannot see the real thing unless we can see it all at once, and we cannot see it all at once. No matter which way you turn, there’s always going to be something behind you. I began to peel my perception away, closing my eyes to the layer I fit within. I shaped hell. Hell is the darkest place you’ve ever been, and the devil is the worst person I’ve ever known. I was sitting on a cement ledge, watching people enter and exit a pizza place. The sky is so dark that there is no sky. The air in front of you is so unlit that you can see right through it. I had taken to asking each passerby for the time, though I rarely even got a reaction. Suddenly, a familiar voice rang out. It was mine, without the words echoing in my head. It was my voice coming out of someone else. “It’s 3:14.” Even in the pitch dark, he cast a shadow over me, and the shadow didn’t move, so I looked up at his face. A sinister smile peeked through a disheveled mess of beard. His long, gray overcoat stunk like it had been dug up out of a grave. He wore an eye patch over his left eye and a bandana on his head. Greasy strings of bluish black hair gathered in thrushes through folds in the bandana. And he was brandishing a big fucking knife. Ominous bass lines bounced in the distance inside of my head. He extended the hand that wasn’t bearing a knife, and I shook it. He sat down on the ledge next to me and pulled out a box of cigarettes. He smoked American Spirits. Black box. Perique Blend. He tapped on the bottom of the pack with the long, dirty nails of his bony fingers, and one cigarette flew up perfectly, just far enough to stick out of the box as far as possible without falling. He held the box up to me, and I slid the rest of the cigarette out and lit it. Who was I to turn down a Perique from the devil? “Thank you,” I said, trying to light the cigarette in the wind. “I presume I need no introduction?” he said, arching an eyebrow, two spots pulsed on his head where horns tried to push through skull and skin, like new teeth cutting through the gums. “So, what’ll it be this time?” “I want a fresh start.” “Come on, Johnny. You know I can’t do that. There’s nothing in it for me.” “What about Jenny Taylor?” “Who’s Jenny Taylor?” “I don’t know. She was from Suffolk. I bought her soul on ebay. It came with a certificate.” I was a collector. I had bought over three hundred souls on ebay. I filed them in a box, still in their original envelopes, in alphabetical order. I kept the box hidden. I paid almost fifty dollars for Jenny Taylor’s soul. She claimed to be a virgin. If it was true, I got myself a deal. Most souls are tarnished, jeopardized, and worthless. A pure soul is rare to find on the market. Those with untainted souls usually know that their souls are priceless. Once in a while, though, the market will get an unsuspected leak. Someone will make a mistake, and one will slip through the cracks. I was lucky to find it. “You say it came with a certificate? Show me.” I reached into my pocket and pulled out my wallet. I shuffled through various cigarette receipts and ticket stubs before pulling out a piece of paper folded five times. I pulled that out and unfolded the certificate. I gave it to the devil, and he examined its authenticity. “Legal and binding, I see.” He looked briefly pleased, but his face changed quickly. His arched eyebrows sunk back down and his right brow capped his piercing eye. “But why would I engage in a trade that had no profit for me? I already have your soul as it stands. If I take Jenny Taylor’s soul, I’ll have her soul, but you’ll be able to save yourself.” It was obvious that the devil already knew the answer to this, but he was just testing me; toying with me like a cat swatting at a wounded mouse. “I can only try to save myself. It’s likely that I’ll fail again. All I’m asking for is a fresh start.” The devil appeared to be thinking my offer over, but appearances can be deceiving. It was really his over. Nobody just came to him with requests; he delivered stacked deals to the desperately seeking. “You do realize that if I grant this to you, you will cease to exist as you know yourself,” he remarked, “instantaneously.” I nodded my head, not quite knowing where my soul would end up. My ears perked at his accenting of that last word. “And just to play my own advocate here, doesn’t it weigh at all on your conscience that a young woman whom you have never met is paying the price for your slim chance at salvation?” “The nonexistent have no consciousness.” I replied, trying to shrug off the entire issue. “I didn’t say that you’d cease to exist, you just wouldn’t exist as you know yourself.” “Then I’m willing to carry that burden.” I knew any hesitation would make matters worse for both him and me. “Hold your palm up to me.” I did so, and the devil grabbed my hand, digging his long nails deep into my palms so that my blood ran down his wrist, dripped off of his elbow, and pooled into a coagulation beneath it. And that’s all that was left of me. *** I sat in a tiny, square room across from a balding, middle-aged man who dressed like a bureaucrat. He had a bulbous frame that bubbled out his white button-down shirt. His blue tie was in need of ironing. The man wore black, thick rimmed glasses. He was reviewing some papers on a clipboard. I really didn’t know why I was here, just that I was dead. “Why don’t we start out with any questions you might have, Mr. Johnny?” he calmly stated, as if it were a question he was reading off of some standard procedure booklet. He hesitated a bit on the name, the only part of the question that ever changes. I thought long and hard, so many things were running through my mind. “Is it alright if I smoke in here?” “That’s perfectly fine. You can do whatever you wish so long as you observe two basic rules. First, do not try to affect the living.” “Okay, and second?” “Do not try to affect the dead.” I pulled out my pack of cigarettes, and thumbed through the pack of smokes, finally pinching in between two cigarettes and pulling out a joint. I always slid a couple of joints into my packs in the empty spaces, you know, in case a situation like this ever came up. “Is it alright if I smoke this?” I said, holding the joint up in front of me. “Whatever you’d like, Mr. Johnny.” I enthusiastically light up the joint, and puff away at it. In a short time, it burns to the part where the roll starts getting larger. I cough a little bit as the man watches me. Where are my manners? “You want a hit?” I say, as I hold the joint towards him, holding back laughter. But the mother fucker surprises me, and grabs the joint. He takes in a big hit and lets it out, and I start laughing out loud. The man doesn’t seem to be amused by my laughter, so I let it lull to a stop before the weed fully hits me, and I start to get philosophical and inquisitive, although slightly less eloquent. “So, uh, what’s with the two rules?” “Well, there not so much rules you can consciously break as they are like rules of physics on earth; concepts like gravity or the law of conservation of mass.” “The law of what, now?” “Never mind. It’s irrelevant now. Basically, it’s impossible for you to affect the living now. All you are is pure energy, and the living are blind to that. The things you create and destroy now are wholly within the imagination.” To illustrate his point, he reached across the table and pulled on my face. The skin stretched back as far as he could pull it, and when he let go, it rejoined my face, or at least what I did not understand at the time was only my perception of my face. “That’s pretty fucked up,” I said before I took a few more puffs from my joint. “Can I try?” He held out his arm, and I kneaded my fingers into his skin like dough. I pulled and stretched his skin, squeezed it thin; I watched the matter expand and contract before my eyes, like nothing I had ever seen before; nothing I had ever deemed possible. I slowly sank back into my chair, a sad wooden thing that somehow seemed to fit me; my shape and my appearance. “And what about the second rule?” I asked, leaning back on two unstable legs that strained and cracked under my weight like Atlas holding up the globe. The earth could balance on this chair, because unlike Atlas, it knew a secret. Earth is small, everything is small in relation to the mind released. It expands and spreads, pushing up, pushing out, and reaching for god. And then it occurred to me; I use far too many commas and semicolons. “The second rule? That’s impossible to break, too. You cannot disturb the dead. You are the dead.” “I don’t understand.” ”Everyone and everything that has died is you, and you are it. The dead are all one. You and I, we’re the same energy, just rearranged within the eternal imagination.” “Then what is this? Why can I see that you and I are separate.” “You can’t. Only the living can. And only words can describe what this scene looks like. Words are a tool of the living that is meaningless now. Words are letters, letters are symbols, symbols are random shapes. When you’re alive, random shapes are different, and they have meaning, but the shapesstarttobleedtogetherandeverythingisone.” He was right. We weren’t communicating through speaking. I could never hear voices in dreams, and I couldn’t hear them now. Everything he was thinking, I was really thinking, and vice versa. It’s all a bit complicated for explanation. Even more complex was the notion that while I was dead, I was also alive. Here I was, back in the stream of energy through which all life flows, but somewhere in that world of the living, my life was being played out again. The devil had given me a second chance, and even though the devil is manipulative, he never lies. *** Back in the confines of life, I had been going crazy, splitting hairs over this strange sense of déjà vu that I had been feeling- not at certain sparse intervals, but throughout my whole life up to this point. I had been living my life through a mirror, just a puppet of my reflection. I was paralyzed with a strange, hazy familiarity lurking behind every action, every observation, and every thought. And then one day, I felt a deep, dark edge approaching. At first, I was convinced that I was dying. I felt like any day could be my last, and I was traveling with so much momentum that I couldn’t stop if I tried. So I accepted it. I had no choice. My mind was awash with a blank, black wall. It obscured my focus, and I couldn’t see past it. Maybe there was no past it. I closed my eyes one night and saw nothing but deep black. This was it. This was goodbye. And yet, it wasn’t. I woke up the next morning with a peculiar clarity that felt more like emptiness hidden behind fog. The déjà vu was gone. The familiarity of the world around me was gone. I was cold and scared and shivering. I was naked and fearless. And ignorant. The ultimate emotional paradox boiled inside of me. I had reached a place where I was finally lost, uncertain, and unaware. I had reached the end and moved past it, and now I was on the other side. I rose, unashamed of my nudity and carefully traipsed my way through the trash covered floor of my room. I slowly opened the door and slid through, snakelike, to find that the house was empty. I peered out the windows and saw the quiet of the afternoon in its bareness as well. There were people, walking or driving, just passing by, but none of them seemed alive. They all seemed drawn by some predestinated path; a path that bound tight and pulled. I wanted to shout out, draw the fire from my lungs, puff out my chest and yell as loudly as I could. And yet another part of me wanted to kneel down into recluse and shield myself from everything around me; the foreign diorama that I had designed, now crumbling out of my control into pieces to small for me to hold. So I started to shout, but I only managed to belch out a fraction of a noise, very loud and brief, much as I imagined the way Germans would speak in every day conversation from opposite ends of a tiny room. And then I decided not to draw attention to myself. I covered my mouth, and muted the noise coming out. But it built up within again, and I took my hand off, and the sound shot out again. It was frank and harsh, like the sound a deer makes when you run over it with a truck. Deer don’t normally make sound, though. They’re relatively quiet animals, content with chewing grass and running around, unless they are being hit by a truck, in which case I would imagine they squeal a painful shrieking croak. I kept repeating this two step process of yelling and covering my mouth. I must have looked like an Indian, whooping out a war cry to invoke the powers of some ancient spirit or some bullshit like that. And then I wanted to shout out again, so I yelled loudly, over and over, until I was out of breath. It was a summoning. It would have been almost like a prayer had prayers not been so utterly useless. And then I knelt down into recluse. I closed my eyes and took myself to a safe place; an empty place with flowers. I sat in the sand, and its soothing warmth crawled up onto my naked skin. And I watched as a figure self-consciously wandered towards me through tulip fields. His face clarified through the distance as he neared, and it was me or it was god or it was my conscience. It was all three. He stood before me, wearing a ripped white shirt with an image of Jesus and the word “FLORIDA” in pink underneath. His greasy hair hung out in strands beneath a brimmed purple hat. He sat down in front of me, and pulled out a joint that we passed back and forth. He spoke. “Do you remember me?” he asked in a fragile voice, hinted with the attrition of his once confident guidance. “I’m not sure I remember much, but I know you.” I conveyed. This was all in thought, though mind you, because I had been renewed. Memory here would flow back to me. My mind was fertile for growth again, and ideas and concepts vaguely hovered in clouds above me. The man handed me a loose stick and motioned towards the sand. I tried to etch something in…it was the only thing I could remember. He read it aloud. “Words?” I had drawn the word “words” in the sand. I wasn’t yet fully conscious of what it meant or why I did it. But it felt somehow important to me. “I’m not sure what it means. I just think it has to do with something.” “Yes, your universal truth. The word is an atom, a particle that binds to itself to form a larger combination. With the right mixtures, you find truths.” “How do I find the right mixtures?” “Any mixtures find truths. Even opposite ones. The difficult part is that the combinations you build are the combinations that build you, and as you create and destroy, you change yourself. Here, follow me.” We both stood up and I walked behind him as he led me to a small, manmade pond in a clearing. I looked in the water to see my face. The top of my head was tangled, alive, and growing. It branched out in veined obtrusions. I was antlered, like some deformed moose. I asked what it meant. “This is what you look like through the mind’s eye. Right now, you look small and infantile. Your mind has been cleared, renewed. This reflection tells a lot about you.” “What does it tell?” Your name is Garbage Bag Johnny. You woke up today and immediately, you were confused. The first thing you saw was the white wall next to you. You turned your head and saw a mess of objects of different colors strewn about your floor. You had no opinion on whether or not you were at all dissatisfied with the squalor. There was no previous basis for any stance on cleanliness to be formed. You trudged through the mess. Instincts led you to be careful because your feet were bare and you were afraid to step on anything sharp. You exited your room, questioning your own identity, taking in the surroundings, adjusting to different intensities of light. Thoughts emerged as to the presence of others like you. Were you the only one? Were there others? Where were they, and how similar were they to you? You looked outside, saw similar beings. They were similar, but they weren’t the same. You felt detached from them, and they seemed untouched by life and incapable of will. Cells died. New cells replaced them. You felt alone, felt like yelling out to see if anyone could hear. But you felt threatened. You yelled anyway; yelled and then recoiled. You were partially frightened by your own noise and more wholly frightened by the threat of response. You shielded yourself. You came here. That is what I read, or in a sense, what was read to me. And there was more, it was too small to decipher between the tangling. It kept growing, too. It grew too fast for me to catch up. Then he explained this to me. “Everything you do, everything you say, and everything you think shapes you. That is how you grow. And you grow in rapid, quadratic equations until you grow large enough to connect and clash with everyone and everything. You become one with all that is alive.” “But I don’t understand. I felt so separate. Am I my own being? And if so, am I one constant being, or do I become someone else as I change? When cells die, and new ones replace them, am I slowly dying? Or slowly becoming someone else?” “That depends, Johnny. You are what you are defined as, and you can only be defined through appearances, thoughts, and words. And what makes words different? Is it their shapes? Their meanings?” “I don’t know.” “That’s fine. There is no correct answer. It’s all about the combinations you make with them, however contradictory they are. You are connected with everybody or nobody depending on how you define yourself. Eventually, you’ll become so colluded with thoughts and words that you’ll be indistinguishable from anyone or anything because it doesn’t matter. You’ll be swallowed whole, Johnny, and you’ll question whether you were ever anything to begin with.” “This is so fucking confusing.” “It has to be. If it weren’t this complex, the answers would be clear, and once they’re found, everything would stagnate. Life would halt to a slow buzz and then die with the dull, quiet, spurting noise of passing into stillness and immobility. Life is the configuration of its moving, growing, twisting pieces that ends when everything is in its place.” “So what do I do?” There was a long pause before he answered words he was once too grand and majestic to speak. “I don’t know.” I had expected some important instruction that would leave the fate of the world in my hands. “I don’t know” was about the last thing I wanted to hear, because he was supposed to be the guy with the answers. If the obvious answer wasn’t there, maybe I’d have to do the opposite. Maybe I’d have to come up with it on my own, mash combinations of words together and find something that rolls off the tongue pleasantly. I thought for a while. I sat down next to the pond and watched as my reflection branched out, meandering in chaotic patterns. It looked so ugly to me. And then the dichotomy became clearer to me. If the answer was ever found, the search for it would stop, but if the search for the answer stopped, the moving, growing, twisting pieces would stop moving, growing, and twisting. Either way, life would fall dormant. Maybe that was the point of it all- to die. It was a destination that could be achieved through one of two opposite ways, but the exponential growth of man confused the issue, making it equally more increasingly complex. Life sustains itself through momentum. It can’t be stopped or found. It can only grow. Grow: a simple word with misplaced positive connotations. *** The me that was dead had since exited the small, cube room, and I was swimming within myself. It seemed easy now to find the equality in particle composition between air and solid ground. I could balance my weight on either, sifting through, becoming and detaching through chemical reactions as a means of travel. The faces of the dead pressed against me, guided me down a straight path down a narrow, midnight black path. I could see them, semi-translucent pushing and bobbing. I felt their warmth against me as they guided me into a speck of bright white light and powdered me into it. Piece by piece I joined with the light, and once inside, saw it contained a prism of vivid colors, most that did not exist in life; too complex for the eye to interpret. The colors could speak. I heard murmuring. I heard the chatter of firing and crashing voices exploding all around me in a tumultuous competition for my attention. But I was already drawn, focused in on the peculiarity of a shady color, speckled with cynical experience, fired by the smell of its own blood, lupine, autumnesque. “There are loopholes, you know,” it told me, and I immediately felt like I was talking to some guy in an alley somewhere, trying to sell me social security numbers. “What do you mean?” “There are loopholes; ways back in. You can travel between the dead and the living. I’m sure you understand how life and death operate now.” “A little bit. You’re talking about multiple potentials? How each possible outcome exists in branches separate from one another?” “Exactly, but I’m not sure you understand how that structure is built. The design is not too dissimilar from the way the mind and the body are connected: electricity. Each split that separates one potential from another is separated by a microscopic synapse. When the energy that breathes life hits one of these holes, it splits and redistributes through multiple pathways. Men are born with only a limited amount of electric energy. It can be recharged, but all in all, the electrical signals weaken and run out, and that’s when man dies.” “But there is a pathway where I’m still alive, right?” I asked. I had gotten the gist of the brick of words, although the intricacies escaped me. If you pay attention to one thing too long, I had learned in life, someone could be right behind you, stealing the sandwich you were about to eat right from your pants pocket. Old habits die hard. “Yes, you chose to divert all your energy to one potential when you sold your soul to the devil. This is highly unnatural, so the synapse where this occurred is larger than most. It is one of the few large enough to drift through between both worlds. You could go back.” “Why would I want to?” “To trade places with the version of you that is living. You made a foolish decision, erasing yourself like that and starting new. That isn’t you in that body. It is him.” “Who?” “It’s the devil.” “But I thought God and the devil and all that bullshit didn’t exist here.” “They do, but not as themselves. Here, they are just like you and I and everything else around. In life, they are just figments inside of your mind, but very few people actually ever interact with them. In these rare cases, another potential of yourself that has died can find a loophole and either assist or exploit a different outcome of himself.” “So he’s the one walking around in my body?” I started to get angry. The bastard stole my skin and bones, and he was wearing them around like an expensive coat, albeit one that was in need of a dry cleaning. “Maybe. It doesn’t always work that way. And let’s not forget about Jenny Taylor.” “He could be in her body?” I diverted my contemplations to that notion, and how strange it would be to end up in a body of opposite gender. I figure if it was me, I’d eventually get bored of playing with my more circular anatomy. The woman’s figure is a lot rounder than a man’s. Walking, I’d imagine, would be a more awkward endeavor for a woman as halls and doorways are all rectangular. “Theoretically, yes. It’s unlikely, though. The procedure of transfer from one world to another would be even further complicated,” the color theorized. “Is there a chance he didn’t end up in either body?” I questioned after a brief stumble in thought. Sometimes, you had to throw in responses to fool people into thinking you were paying attention to their story, when really, you have no idea what the hell is going on, and you don’t want to start all over because it makes you look like a fucking idiot. “Yes.” “Then what would happen to the body? And where would his soul go?” But this was important, and his voice sounded weaker. He was probably getting to some sort of major point that I should have been focused on. “Then the…is…empty. He…go to…in between life…death.” I began losing his words beneath a confliction of my own thoughts. Only one thing was apparent. This color had drawn me in despite the calling of an indistinguishable amount of voices. I felt as if I had to pursue this. The prospect of living in an existence unaffected by the concept of time frightened me. There were no cigarette shops or gas stations around. “How will I know if he’s in the body? Wait, fuck! How will I know?” “…………………..” Something faint came out. It sounded like it was deflating.
