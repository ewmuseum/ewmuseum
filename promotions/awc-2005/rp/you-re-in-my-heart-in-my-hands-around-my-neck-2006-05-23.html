you're in my heart, in my hands, around my neck.

23 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 23, 2006 caroline brown knew as any other woman did that from the moment she discovered she was to be a mother, she’d been blessed with a gift that no one could ever take away from her. it didn’t matter what evils were hidden in the shadows -- that one gift would give her the strength to face the darkest of them all. when she held her first son, there were no words to describe how she felt. he was so tiny; a squirming too-pink bundle of screaming flesh and blood. the strands of blonde on his head were like silk. when he finally opened his eyes, they were a swirling blue. “hey, baby boy…” caroline whispered, a finger tracing his soft, rosy cheek, “…i’m your mommy.” if she hadn’t known better, caroline would’ve thought he understood her; maybe he had, but that didn’t matter. all that mattered was this one tiny child wrapped in the soft blue blanket in her arms and the fierce look of pride in her husband’s eyes when she looked up. dad crossed the room and settled on the edge of bed. “and this is your daddy.” caroline smiled at the tears glistening in her husband’s eyes as dad reached out, capturing the tiny clenched fist that had escaped the blanket. their son, she thought, her heart beating with far more joy than she ever thought possible. “he’s beautiful, carol,” said dad, voice tight with emotion. he leaned, brushed a kiss first on her cheek first, and then on the baby’s brow. “have you decided?” her eyes lit up as she met her husband’s gaze. “dean,” she replied, quietly, “…his name is dean.” - No matter how hard he tries, Kip can’t remember a time when he didn’t ache. He’s felt it for so long, just this constant thrum of pain below the surface of his skin. It’s a part of him. Sometimes it feels like it’s all he is. But staring down at Dean in his driveway, well, this feels like the end. He thinks the lack of pain means something. Sometimes, Kip thinks he doesn’t know his brother at all. -- kip’s eyes burn, but he’s damned if he’s going to sit here and cry like a girl. ** when caroline brown held her second son, there were still no words to describe how she felt. he would grow to be taller than his older brother, she thought, his body long and all legs and arms. he was quieter than dean and his head was covered with dark hair that curled in spots. his eyes never left her face. “he has your dimples.” caroline whispered softly as dad walked in, a four year old dean in the crook of one arm and a bundle of wild flowers in the other. caroline lifted her gaze from their son’s face. she shook her head. “flowers? why, sweetheart. you’re becoming a romantic in your old age.” dad had just smiled. “i’m not old yet, carol. and you always did bring out the best in me. i’m learning.” he said. he sat the flowers on the bedside table and dean down next to her. squriming, dean got comfortable and leaned in his nose bare inches from the baby’s. “is it a boy or a girl, mommy?” “meet your brother, dean,” caroline announced, laden with pride. little dean grinned at his mother and leaned in closer again. “he’s little, mommy. was i that little?” “smaller, baby. you were smaller.” dean gave her a childishly doubtful look before his lips curled into broad smile. his eyes lit up. “does he have a name, mommy?” caroline’s gaze lifted to where dad loomed over their family. “ask your daddy.” “daddy? what’s his name?” “kyson.' replied dad, voice rough with tears. “for your grandpop.” dean looked up at dad, nose crinkled. “that’s a big boy’s name, daddy.” he stated matter of factly. “he’s a little boy. i think his name should be…” he chewed his lip thoughtfully, “…kippy.” “hm. kippy it is, then,” chuckled dad. “hi, kippy. i’m your big brother. my name’s dean.” kip cooed softly. his dimples deepened. -- The pain makes Dean want to cry, but wanting to cry is better than actually crying because he can’t show any sign of weakness when he’s around Kip, so he bites his bottom lip and chews on it and swallows a sob. Kip’s face is all straight lines as he cleans Dean’s wounds, first with a washcloth dabbed with water and then with the needle and thread. Beneath his fingernails is Dean’s blood. Moonlight slices through the blinds, shadows breathing life against the walls. Dean, on the edge of the bed, winces and hisses through his teeth when Kip tugs on the thread a little too hard. “Stay still,” says Kip, tugging again. *** the night caroline brown’s world came crashing down was the night she knew what it meant to be a mother with her entire soul. there were a million little things she could have done differently, but there was no need for recriminations. what was done was done and there was no turning back. a mother held many griefs and fears. her grief was as bottomless as the deepest river. she would never see her boys grow to men, never witness their first crushes, their first broken hearts, the day they learned to drive, or the night they would attend their senior prom. she wouldn’t see them graduate and feel that pride as they left to attend college. there would be no joyful call when they found that one girl that would be theirs forever, no marriages and no grandchildren to spoil because that was what grandparents did. her fears were razor sharp. who would ease their fears in the darkness? would her husband be able to let her go and create a new life? she knew he loved her deeply and she wanted him to never blame himself, but she also knew her husband deeper than most. he was a soldier, a man who saw it as his responsibility to protect and provide. he would blame himself, on some level, but what she wondered mostly was: would he be able to lay aside his grief to give their babies the life they deserved? staring down at kip with his bright blue eyes, caroline knew there would be pain for him -- the pain of having never truly known his mother. the horror in her husband’s eyes and his terrified screams entangled with kip’s would be her last clear memory before the fire consumed her. save them, she screamed, as the darkness closed in. --- “Alright,” Kip says, cutting the excess thread. “You’re set.” “Thanks.” “Showed up on my doorstep bleedin’. There wasn’t much else I could do.” Dean watches as Kip replaces the roll of blue thread and the minuscule pair of scissors in the drawer beside the bed. Then, he gets off and heads somewhere towards the right. “You need som’en to sleep in?” “No,” starts Dean, dully, “I’ll just sleep in these bloody clothes.” Three and a half seconds. Kip throws a fresh pair of track pants at Dean. Then, he takes off his shirt and throws it, too. “Take the bed. I’ll go sleep in the guest room.” This time, it’s Kip who’s out of there before Dean is halfway through his next breath. **** back in conroe, there’s a grave for caroline brown, but no body beneath the soil. the quiet of the night had been shattered by glass and metal, and the sky had been swallowed by flames. ---- The next morning. Dean feels like he’s on his last rope, trying to figure out some way to make Kip come back with him, back to what was really Kip’s life, not press junkets or photoshoots or, for fuck’s sake, wrestling. This is just the bitterness, though, Dean figures. Kip would always go back to his life in Los Angeles with his press junkets and photoshoots and wrestling and pretty mansion with a pool outside and a big, nice mirror in the bathroom where Dean didn’t have to look at himself, all bloodied up and a murderer. The next morning and Dean is staring at Kip in the mirror, standing beside him. “Listen for a minute, just listen. I -- I already know what your answer is, but damn it, Kip, this is Dad. He needs us, man.” “Why bother?” asks Kip, dipping his razor beneath the running sink water. “You ain’t gon’ta get me to change my mind just because you say you want me to come back. Dad sure as hell made his stance on that possibility very clear last time I saw ’im.” “He was pissed off, Kip, pissed off and worried ‘cause he wouldn’t be able to protect you anymore.” “Worried? Ha! Right. He was just pissed off ‘cause he’d have one less soldier to command, Dean,' spat Kip, running the razor down his cheek, “and you know that’s the only reason.” “That ain’t true, Kip.” “Like hell it ain’t! That's all we were,” he says, “and that’s all you still are. I got out of that, and there is no way in hell that I am going back to that life.” This hits a nerve Dean doesn’t even know he has. In a flash he has Kip up against the wall, Kip’s razor clattering to the tile and Dean’s face a breath length away from his. “You listen to me! I’ve put up with your shit my entire life, Ky, but I’ve about had enough. This ain’t about no grudge. This is about Dad. Dead. Dying.” And Dean watches as the surprise fades to anger and just a small flicker of pain before he’s shoved back, the small of his back colliding with the sink counter. “You wouldn’t have had to to put up with my shit if you had just left me the fuck out of this, Dean, and you know it, so don’t even start with the bullshit. I'm ain’t gonna listen to it.” “Are you even fucking hearing yourself, Ky? How can you be like this? It’s our dad, man!” Kip pushes himself from the wall, and he really is trying not to cry when he says: “I don’t care.” * one time, in second grade, a teacher asked her students to make mother’s day cards. all of the children went on their merrily way; collecting construction paper, markers, scissors. there was only one child who didn’t. the teacher asked, “what’s wrong, sweetheart?” the boy frowned and said, “i don‘t have a mommy.” the teacher gasped, slightly, and leaned down, a little, placing a palm on the boy’s shoulder. “oh, sweetie, i’m so sorry. why don’t you make a card, anyway? make a card to someone you really care about.” the boy lit up. he said, “can i?” the teacher smiled and said, “of course you can! here, follow me.” she led the boy over to the rest of the kids, scrambling around to make the best mother’s day card ever. the boy picked out a sheet of orange construction paper and sat at his own little table in the corner. in blue Crayola marker, he scribbled: Dean, your wundful! Happy mothers day! luv kippy then he grabbed an orange marker and did his best to make a heart, but it looked like a jagged triangle instead. - At four o’clock Monday night, five and a half hours before Kip has to hop a plane to Czech Republic, he sits on the couch and watches BMW reruns on ABC! Family, nursing a bottle of Sprite. Dean approaches from behind. He clears his throat, but Kip ignores him. “Kip?” Kip’s eyes don’t leave the screen. “What?” “Uh. You gotta can opener or somethin’? I’m hungry.” “First drawer left of the stove.” Pause. “Alright. Thanks.” Dean begins to make his way back into his younger brother’s kitchen when Kip says, “You, uh. You want to come to Prague with me? Just tomorrow. We’d be back Wednesday night, Thursday morning. I guess.. I guess we can talk about Dad then.” It takes a minute for Dean to smile. “Yeah. Okay, yeah. Sure.” Kip nods. Scars are permanent and so is history, but that doesn’t mean you can’t try and learn from your mistakes. He’s at least willing. ** so if i were to return - all the old nonsense in coming home, finding skies the same overhead, under neath, the ponds still steeped in ice, the blackbirds, the empty cotton-sleeves of symbolism - and if i were to see you now (a little more gray in your hair, more shadows to screen your eyes) would there be anything left to say? not for me. write your life, as you have been. i have my midnights, no longer yours. it’s all right.
