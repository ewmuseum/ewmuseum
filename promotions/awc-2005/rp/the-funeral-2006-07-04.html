The Funeral

4 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-4-06 Alone. That is what it had now come too. That was what I had become. Alone. There was no one. There was no single thing. When this happens what does one do? What can one do when there is no one and nothing out there left in the world for him? Simple. He goes home, yet unfortunately for me on this day. Going home wouldn’t be the simple thing to do. You’ll ask yourself the question, “What now?” The low grey overcast seemed to accentuate the general mood of the day perfectly. Not that I had expected the mood to be any different though. Funerals were generally not a fun thing to be part of, especially in my case that day. I had questioned myself as to whether or not I should I attend. In the end I had felt I must if not to respect the dead, regardless of the feelings that might erupt from the source of my presence. There were some things a person just had to do. When a person dies it is the right and honorable thing to do by attending their funeral where their life is remembered. This is especially true when it is your fault that the person is gone. I had asked myself in the days before whether I was a murderer or not. I finally decided that I was not. Luckily the local District Attorney agreed. ‘A simple accident’ he had called it and nothing else. It might have been a simple accident to him, but to the teary eyes family I had seen in the front row it had been much more. It had bee so very much more to the now motherless son and daughter. It had been much more to the now former husband. Feelings of contempt were in their hearts at the moment, I was sure of it. Interwoven between the feelings of sorrow and regret there would the feelings towards me. None of them could be good. I had thought of all these things before I ever started to dress myself before the ceremony. It was just something that I had to do, something I must. I don’t know if I’ll ever know how much I hurt them. Knowing what I did to them still hurts me everyday. I sometimes just wish I could rewind and take back everything. Unfortunately none of us have that luxury and we must all live with the actions that we make, so is the way of the universe and it’s mighty laws. ‘I could make out the dim outline of the person in the opposing vehicle as we neared each other. The car sped past me, and I by it. Quickly I turned my head back to look at the car as it sped away down the long country road. There seemed to be nothing out of the ordinary in what I could see. Yet, the feeling inside remained as if something for coming or wrong beyond measure. A loud horn going off made me turn my head back so fast towards the road that I felt a sharp pain shoot up my neck. I saw the outline of a car hurdling at me. It seemed that in the wake of the car which had just passed me and my fascination to find something strange with that I had inadvertently drifted into the left lane. Which unfortunately for me was a very bad place for me to be at that time and place for the car that had used their horn to get m attention was only a few yards away. I tried to swerve to miss it, but to no avail. It seemed almost as if slow motion had been set for this moment as the next few seconds seemed to take a lifetime. I saw the two vehicles begin to collide. Then the strangest thing that had ever happened to me began at the very second. It seemed like time had stopped. I saw the collision beginning to take place but I wasn’t registering it.’ I had purchased a bouquet of flowers for the affair. It seemed to me that it would be right. I had to scour the last remnants of my pockets to come up with the money however. The unemployment checks had long since stopped and my small bank account was not nearing the single digits. It wouldn’t be long before that to would be long gone, just like everything else. I sat there and listened to the speakers of the event. They all seemed to mime each other: ‘She was a great woman; she really cared; she was a wonderful mother.’ Those were the many things said by the many speakers. I tried not to look towards the front row where I knew that a son was struggling not to cry and a daughter surely had the marks of tears dried out by the cool breeze that had began to move into the area. As the speakers droned on about the kind of woman she was and what kind of life she lived I began to think of the night barely a week ago, the night where the wreck wasn’t the main thing on my mind. The wreck of course had been something that I would never wish upon any other living soul. It wasn’t the fact that the wreck had happened, but more of the fact that when I finally came too and exited my small battered truck I came to find that my tuck for the most part was fine except for the dent in the front left side. I finally came too and after a couple of minutes of me trying to get my bearings I decided to make the journey outside of my truck to check on the occupant of the small red compact. My truck door creaked open as always and I took my first steps out onto the now muddy grass in the small ditch beside the road. I went to stand straight up but the quickly went tumbling down into the mud. My head had still not quite recovered from the beating it had just taken. I took a moment to let my mind rest and brought myself to my feet, not worrying about the fact that I was now covered in mud. It was now raining harder than ever and I sensed that the lightning spectacle that was dancing across the sky was only bound to get worse. I took a step up the ditch to try and get a better look at the destruction that I knew lie ahead. The darkness was making it hard for me to see but the remaining headlight of the compact was helping out as much as it could. I walked over to the driver side door from behind to make sure the driver was ok. I knocked on the window to no avail. My view inside was blocked from the fact that the windows were tented, so I went ahead and opened the door. I was amazed to find when I opened the door that no one was in the seat. My first thought was that maybe they got out. Maybe. It was then that I noticed the water pouring through the windshield through a huge bloody hole. I froze up at that moment because I feared what I may find at the front of the car. I slowly shut the driver’s door and proceeded to walk the short length of the car to the front. As I came around the front of it I saw what lie there. It was an image that would haunt my nightmares forever. I grimaced as the pain I had felt that night forced their way back to the front of things. It’s not everyday you see a lifeless body, especially one in the condition I did. Though death had become an almost to common thing in my life, that night and what I saw would still be burned into my memory for a while longer and I knew it. It was now time for me to pay my last respects to a woman I had never met in life. I can say that it was then that I began to stiffen. Not from fear or from some illness, but from the realization of what had happened. It had only just dawned on me that I had ended a life. In a twist of event where my life should have been taken, it was spared and hers was taken instead. I had to start to gasp for breathe their in the farthest back row. To me everything else was now starting to lose focus. At that moment I didn’t care about the fact that I had no money or the fact that I had no family or friends. It especially didn’t matter to me that in just one day I would once again make my way back to the AWC, now my home, now my place to show what I can do if I can do it. No, none of it seemed to matter to me at the moment. It just didn’t seem fair to me at the moment, me sitting there with my hands in my pockets and my heart in throat, alive and well. What crimes has she committed deserving such penance why couldn’t she have just been five more minutes later. She never even had a chance to cry for help. She died there on that cold wet road, alone. Alone. Just like me. The rows in front of me had emptied and it was now my turn as the last guest to walk up and my respects. Yet, as I stood there and came upon these many realizations I knew in my heart that I couldn’t walk the center aisle. There was no way that I would be able to pay those respects that I had come to offer. To face those left behind in the wake of it all. It just wasn’t fair. Just not fair. Why did life have to treat people in such ways? Why did her family have to suffer and I not. Why was I here and she not? It just wasn’t fair. It was then that a thought came into my mind. It should be fair. Then of all the things that could happen, a grin came upon my face. It would be fair. I would make it so. I stood up as the dark clouds now began to move into the area. Yes, I would make everything right. Though I knew this wouldn’t be easy. I took my turn finally and walked up the center aisle to the where the casket lied. I sat the pretty purple flowers that I had brought on the some how dampened brilliance of the oak finished casket. I took a moment to reflect on it all. How it was not fair. How it would become fair. I began to hear the whispers behind me. They were from the family I was sure. I could have sworn I heard the son say to his father ‘is that him dad?’ I still have no idea to this day what the father answered, though to me it still matter not. I turned slowly around to where the family was sitting and just stood there with my hands now back in my pockets and my heart once again in my throat. Warm tears began to rolls down my face. “I’m sorry” I sputtered. Not sure of what else to say. The father just acknowledged me with a nod of his head, clearly trying to be the male figure by not crying. Though I could make out the swelling eyes to which tears formed. “It’s just not fair.” I said in a low tone as I was forced down by the seeming less weight of the world. I went down to my knees as my tears began to come out in streams now. I looked up through my tears and saw that the son had began to cry now as well and that the daughter had her face down in her hands balling every drop her eyes would allow. It was then as the first few raindrops fell that I made my promise to them. The one for which I would reflect upon for the rest of my life. “I will make it right. It’s just no fair.” There was only one thing I could do to even the score of life. The thing most precious to them had been taken. So something of mine would have to be taken as well, and seeming as how I had no money or friends or loved ones that just left one person. Myself. ‘It’s just not fair’ Those were the words I had said as I crouched with my knees on the ground while the raindrops began to fall down to the remnant of the procession. That had been an hour ago. Since then the casket of the fallen mother had been lowered. The tear dried husband and children had long since left to go on with their life, and I had left to go and fulfill the promise I had made. It was raining once again as I began my trek down the slippery road to the place I called home. The long country road gave me plenty of opportunity to think, and that was most certainly what I was doing at that point in time. I had made a promise and I intended to keep it. I thought to never mind the consequences of my actions. Nothing else mattered to me at the moment. I had no friends. The only person who I had ever counted as family was dead, and I had no money. What else was there left to pay a debt with it not with the thing I had thought of. What else could even the score for a life ripped away from all that was good to it? That woman who had just been buried had plenty to live for. She had a family. She had a job. She had money and friends and plenty of people who loved her. On the other hand there was me, the person who survived that fateful accident. I was the person who was alive and well. I was the person who escaped with barely a scratch. I was the person whose small black truck had somehow managed only a dent in a collision with the force of 140 miles per hour. How, I still did not know. The only I knew for sure was that the woman who had it all and should have kept it all was gone. Yet, the person who had nothing at all to live for was still alive and kicking. I was the person who had no family, friends, or money. I was the person who had nothing to live for and she was the one who had EVERYTHING to live for. Why then had fate decided to let me live and take her away? It was just simply and undeniably unfair. It was also something that I planned on replacing. I had to. This was just something that had to be done. This wrong must be righted. It was something that would not go quietly into the night, but I would make sure it would be known. The score would be evened. Even at the price it would cost me. I had no choice. The price was simple. No other would work. There was only one thing I had to offer that could make up the loss of their loved one. There was only one thing I could give to the family whose mother and wife had been ripped away from them much too early. The simple yet ever so elegant price was in fact my life. My life would have to pay the debt where none other could suffice. It was truly as simple as that. I had nothing to live for so I cared not. I was all alone in this world and nothing could change that fact, or so it would seem for the moment I continued my trek down the long dreary country road as the rain continued its abysmal drenching of the area. I had my mission and I was sticking to it. I had only one this to do. It was to get and home and quite simply and unquestionably to end my life. I had a gun. It would suit nicely for this. I would leave a letter apologizing to the family and explain why I had done this. That it was for them that my blood was spilled. It was for them that my life had been ended as retribution for their loss. It would work. I clicked my windshield wipers to their fastest speed. The rain was coming down harder than ever now. I wished it would at least let up a little. Even though I knew it was stupid I decided to continue through the now blinding rain as it straighten even more so. I finally cursed under my breath and decided to stop on the side of the road. I slowly pulled to the side of the road and went a head and put on my emergency light lest some idiot driver end my life before I had the chance too. I put my truck into park and sat there in silence for a few minutes, trying to not think about the upcoming task at hand. After a moment I laughed at myself at the realization that the only thing I had going from me no longer mattered to me any more. The AWC would surely not miss the fact that I would simply be gone they could decide to stick a dead body into the ring. Now wouldn’t that be something to see. I began to peer out into the surrounding area and noticed something white to the right of my car. I couldn’t tell what it was so I moved to the right side of my truck to get a better view. It was only after a minute of focusing that I realized that I was in fact a white cross. Puzzled, I decided to further investigate. I totally scooted myself over to the side of my truck and opened the creaky door and was instantly drenched by the downpour. I didn’t care as to how wet I was. I would be dead in only a matter of time now, right? I crossed the rain streamed ditch in a single hop and lowered myself level with the cross which I could see was now adorned with rain drenched flowers. ‘To our loving mother and wife. May god rest her soul.’ And then further down it said “Born: Dec 13th 1963/ Died: July 4th 2006.” It was then that it hit me. This was the place This was the place where it had happened. It was this very spot where the woman whose funeral I had just attended had ceased to live I jerked my head towards across the road to the place where I knew I had been, to the place where it had been decided that I should live. Pain rushed through my mind as I began to relive the night. It had started out simple, just a normal drive into town, nothing more. Then it had gone wrong. ’ I took a step up the ditch to try and get a better look at the destruction that I knew lie ahead. The darkness was making it hard for me to see but the remaining headlight of the compact was helping out as much as it could. I walked over to the driver side door from behind to make sure the driver was ok. I knocked on the window to no avail. My view inside was blocked from the fact that the windows were tented, so I went ahead and opened the door. I was amazed to find when I opened the door that no one was in the seat. My first thought was that maybe they got out. Maybe. It was then that I noticed the water pouring through the windshield through a huge bloody hole. I froze up at that moment because I feared what I may find at the front of the car. I slowly shut the driver’s door and proceeded to walk the short length of the car to the front. As I came around the front of it I saw what lie there. It was an image that would haunt my nightmares forever.’ ’ When the memory had ended I found myself back on my knees where I had already been that night once before. It was then I was reminded of why I had to do this. Why I had to give up my life. To pay the debt that was owed. Yes, that would be the way it would be. It had to be. I smiled in a way I never had before, much like a maniac even though I was perfectly *sane*. It would be simple I thought once more. I would use the same gun that Richard used to kill himself to kill myself… The same gun my Richard used. The same gun the man who I had called father used… It was then that I remembered something else from that night. It was something he had said that I hadn’t thought of much till right then. Though it was something I should have though of long before. ’ You have much left to do. Yet you waste your time. You are better than what you believe yourself to be. You have a purpose in this life. Now you must figure it out. You must forget about me and everything I once stood for.” My purpose. What was my purpose I wondered? Why had Richard, dead for four months, appeared then? It was for my purpose. My destiny… My destiny. Fate, destiny intertwined and considered much the same thing. As I thought about there in the freezing cold rain it dawned on me. If it was fate and destiny for the message to be given to me on that night then it was also fate and destiny for that woman to be gone from this world as a result of that night. Yes, it now made sense now. It was all coming together. I had purpose in this world and I needed to find it. My purpose was to live on and I knew it. I would have to live on. I would live and repay my debt to the family anyways, not by sacrificing myself but by living for them and helping through their time of need. Yes that was it. I had figured that piece of the puzzle out. I had to live on, and I most assuredly had to win.
