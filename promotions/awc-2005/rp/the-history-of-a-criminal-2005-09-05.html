The History of a Criminal

5 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/05/05 August 31, 2005 Nottingham, England I could put away Jason Locke again. To everyone I knew, I was Jack Vance. It would stay that way. If I went out, though, I would have to keep my masquerade going. Atlantic Wrestling Club has some following in Europe, especially the United Kingdom. I was good at disguising before, I could do it now. Because I was in the United Kingdom for the last show at the National Ice Centre, all I had to do was drive over to Belfast and pick up a hotel room there. I got into my car, turned it on, and slipped a Sisters of Mercy CD, First and Last and Always, into the CD player. I was ready to go. I started from some hotel in England, I forgot the name of it. I was forced by AWC to go to last week's show, even though I didn't have a match. I thought I'd make the best of it, but there's no one but idiots in AWC. So still so dark all over Europe And I ride down the highway 101 By the side of the ocean headed for sunset For the kingdom come I laughed, as I still had youthful musical tastes still, and I am ninteen years of age. I thought I would already have had the boring musical tastes of my parents, well, what I remembered of my parents, but I won't go there. Maturity isn't automatic, I learned, but in some ways, I had matured. I had to mature, or I would be dead. Anyway, I started thinking again as I pulled out from an on-ramp onto the highway. It was raining outside, but some people in New Orleans would gladly trade their devastation for this dreary rain, so I wasn't going to complain about it. I also thought the rain looked cool. In front of me was swaths of black pavement, and very few cars in front of me. I dared to speed, because these cops over here don't really know about me, and I could bullshit them, anyway. The details of this trip aren't too terribly important. ---------- August 31, 2005 Belfast, Ireland I was in my hotel room. I didn't really feel like doing too much, and I had been driving all day, so I went to sleep. It wasn't like there was much else to do, as I don't really like watching television. I seemed to recall sleeping very easily, as I don't recall getting up after I had laid down. ---------- October 12, 1999 New York, New York I had remembered my mother's words very well: 'If you fail one more time, you will be punished.' I didn't like school too much, and my parents had always wanted me to succeed. My mother had always imagined me as her little surgeon, astronaut, or politician. I always imagined myself as an adventurer. Adventuring past the line of good taste. Adventuring past the lines of morals. My parents were devout Christians, but I had little room for religion. I was cold. Maybe too cold for my own good, but there's nothing I could do. Everything was peachy at this point, if I may borrow a word from someone else's vocabulary that I will never use. But things were going to sour. Today was the day, but I didn't know it yet. Unlike other people, I didn't have any sort of foreshadowing. It just was. I was at my house, a sad affair. It didn't have much in it. My room didn't have much of anything in it, only a TV, and a Playstation, and a couple of games. I didn't have a computer or a big stereo system, like some people I knew at school. A lack of material goods drives people out. The only difference is what they're driven for. Some are driven for adventure, some are driven for worldly possessions, and some are driven to piss as many people off as they can. Anyway, I was wearing a Metallica t-shirt and blue jeans. I was driven out because I didn't want to be cooped up in this house all day, and I don't really enjoy home life. As I was fifteen years of age, and didn't have my driver's license yet (and as an aside, the streets of New York are congested), I rode my bike over to my friend's house. He really wasn't that far away, but I liked my bicycle. It kept me out of the house, and it gave me an exhilarating feeling of speed that walking or running couldn't give. As lots of people walk on the sidewalks, I had to weave in and our of people's ways. I finally got to the his residence, hidden in a maze of tall buildings and alleys. I knocked on his door a couple of times, and he opened the door. Back then, I was about 5'11'; I had a bit of growing left to do. My friend, Mark Smith, was about 6'0' and skinnier than I was. To the uninitiated, he was about as interesting as his surname indicated. He was dressed in a black t-shirt and blue jeans. 'Hey,' I said. 'Hey Jack,' he replied back. 'You want to go to the arcade?' he asked. He liked to play, because he always beat me. He always beat me, because he had more practice. He was good at games such as Street Fighter and Mortal Kombat, and sometimes he didn't want to let me have a punch in edgewise. We went outside and grabbed our bikes and went to the local arcade. It wasn't too far, but today's trip seemed a lot longer. We were staying up close to each other, but something was wrong. 'You hear something?' I asked. Mark replied, 'Nah.' I thought I heard a very faint noise, like someone trying to tell a secret to another. 'Quick, the alley up ahead!' was the cry as we stopped, placed our bikes in front of a building, and made sure to be very careful. There are many horrible people in New York, and you better be careful, they say. Luckily, there were boxes stacked in front of us, so we had a very easy hiding place. There were two men standing in the alley. One man was white, he was wearing a leather jacket and black jeans. He didn't really look like he was interesting, but he had a suitcase of something. The other man was black, and he had a suitcase, too. He was wearing a football jersey, of some team I don't really know. His face looked worn, and he looked older, like he had been through a few battles. 'I have the money,' the black man said. 'Alright,' said the white man. 'Just to make sure, show what you have.' The white man opened his up to reveal several baggies of cocaine, and the other guy flipped his to reveal bundles of crisp one hundred dollar bills. 'We exchange at the same time. On the count of three.' 'Oh!' Mark blurts out, but I cover his mouth with my hand, and whispered. 'They're drug dealers! If they see us, they'll kill us! Be quiet!' 'One.' 'Two.' 'Three.' At the same time he counts three, the white man pulls out his gun and shoots a couple of rounds into the brain of the black guy as he prepares to give his suitcase to the white man. He makes sure the buyer is dead before taking both suitcases and running away. 'Poor guy,' I remarked to Mark. 'Yeah, but that's the risk you run when you play in the street.' I ran out to where the drug buyer's dead body was, and Mark tries to hold me back. 'You can't do that! You'll get evidence on him!' 'But you know he's got to have SOME money on him,' I explained, and shrugged him off. I examine his body, and look through his pockets. I find something, and fish it out. A wallet. Good job, I thought. You found something. 'That's great and all,' Mark said, 'but how are you gonna explain that to your parents?' 'I don't know,' I said. 'Maybe I'll say I found it on the street.' That's a good half-truth that wouldn't be investigated. I look through the wallet, and it has the normal stuff such as driver's license, social security card, etc. I noticed that the dead guy's name was Kevin Roberts. Holding up the driver's license card, I remarked, 'They're going to have an easy time identifying him.' It sure beats looking at Roberts' head with bullet holes in it. 'Come on, man, if you're gonna look through a dead guy's wallet, show me the money!' he said. And then, I pulled it out. Two hundred dollar bills, and two twenty dollar bills. 'That will be an easy split,' I indicated. I gave him one hundred and one twenty. He pocketed it. I checked his other pocket. I found something cold, hard, and in a L-shape. My worst fears were true. He was packing heat himself! And as soon as I knew it, I had tucked the gun into my own pocket. 'Oh shit,' Mark said, 'you've really done it now!' I looked at the gun. I wasn't really an expert when it came to guns. I only knew that they were good for killing things. I didn't know if it was 9mm, 10mm, or a .357. I did some fiddling with it, and I saw that it still had a live clip in it. I know, I had done it. For the first time in my life, I had power. If I didn't like anything, I could change it, with this gun. There were legal ramifications involved, but I didn't really care. I felt invincible. I guess someone must have heard me talking, as I saw a woman yell, 'He did it!', pointing at me. I couldn't get a good look at her, because I knew I didn't have much time. I guess she saw that I had a gun in my hand, and assumed I was the murderer. I didn't trust any legal system, so I had to do what I had to do. Mark just stood there silent as I ran. Ran where? I don't know. I had power, but I was running. The only thing I cared about, however, was the power.
