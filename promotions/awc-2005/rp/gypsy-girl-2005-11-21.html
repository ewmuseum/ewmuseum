Gypsy Girl

21 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 21 November, 2005 a. you’re like my favorite damn disease The harbinger comes to Ellis in a dream, right foot over left, left foot over air, stepping out of the future into an immaterial past. He comes to her in the middle of a subway, auto-gates jarring in the background, or like they are in a place with a hundred identical machines churning out separate fates. The harbinger reaches out a hand to take her bag. “Thanks,” Ellis say. A woman jostles past and steps on her feet. “Everything’s stopped hurting,” Harbinger says. “The past has no feeling.” Harbinger’s small enough to put in her hand but would burst her bones open if she was to make a fist. Harbinger sits beside her in a bookstore with the wood-panelled floor and a security guard by the door. Check out, check in. Check out, check in. “Check out this book,” Ellis tells Harbinger. E.E. Cummings. Harbinger doesn’t like his name. Cummings writes about women who loved Longfellow and believed in Christ, both dead. Harbinger says that Ellis should love Christ and believe in Longfellow regardless of the state they are in. Ellis says they should be in the same state we are. Harbinger says alive. Ellis, dead. Harbinger says, “The dead is the dead is the dead. The dead take the Emily and make her married and put her in a new story where the dead walk the earth. The babies walk the earth. The babies will be dead is the dead is the dead,” and Harbinger sings, “The dead is the dead is the dead.” Harbinger talks in circles that do not make sense, as if to tell Ellis what begins will end where it started. Later Ellis sits by the pool and dangles her legs in the water. Harbinger leaves like broken glass clunking into the sea. Clunk, clunk, clunk. Onomatopoiea pulls her eardrums taut then snap. The night is so quiet it could be death. Harbinger turns, trying to sleep. Screech, screech, screech. “Harbinger, be quiet!” Ellis shouts, but Harbinger doesn’t cease. Her legs feel cold, suddenly, a blast of Niagra air. Ellis turns quickly to see the water sucking a path down her legs to her feet; messy kisses. Ellis turns around’s around. The harbinger is nowhere to be found, quick as a receding tide. Their afternoon together is gone. (Ellis doesn’t think she cried.) Lights come on at two a.m. Ellis’ neighbor plays The Beatles on repeat, inconsiderate like incognito. Music blasts like wind. Niagra air, Niagra water, Vermont wood. Ellis shows him her room, it was good. The Beatles believe in reincarnation. ** October. The sky is in rose half bloom. Petals of night are unfurling and smelling like the dead. In the car, waiting, there is the tick-tock sound of the signal, like avoiding a clock and being haunted by its noise. It is like descending into a Picasso, falling into shapes and angles and sharp things. Ellis grows uncomfortable while waiting. Her fingers scratch at the dashboard. Chandler is turning the key in the lock. Outside, the sun pulls its way out of the window sill, casting furrowed shadows on the rumpled shirt, the loose tie, the shoes that he stole from his father, scuffed from abuse. The world is brightening, birds chirping, yellow and sort of flitting around his head, the type of story he would probably find in the beginning of a horror novel, before the disaster or morbid monsters arrive. A large insect flies into the window and tries to smash its way through the glass. Inside, the room is so long that he can’t even see the other end. Ellis looks at the steering wheel and thinks, in one second, she can go from 0 kmh-1 to 200 kmh-1. In one second, she can go from having everything to having nothing. In one second, she can actually forget Logan’s name. Switch camera angles, differentiate like black/white/gray film and Chandler is picking through the garbage on the floor, picking through the empty pizza boxes and the ripped-open-letter/bill/taxreport-left-in envelopes and now the rich scent of blood and urine and something else so nondescript is swimming the twenty-lap Olympic marathon up his nostrils. Oh, God, fuck fuck shit shit this is fucking disgusting. The body looks like a fallen mannequin in a department store, like a body without its limbs attached, like a body that doesn’t look like a body at all. Red pools beneath it and Chandler snickers a remark about the red sea to Ellis over the radio. It is not well received -- Ellis tells Chandler to shut the fuck up and get his fat ass out of there quickly because there is someone looking at her, someone is looking at her from the driver’s seat of a black Navigator. Ellis looks back, stares, sort of, lights a joint, takes a drag, shapes her lips, blows, watches a circle of haze, and welcomes the burn against the roof of her mouth. Later, Agent Landon will ask her why her eyes are so small, even after she has plastered on iced packets by her temples or quelled the onslaught of a new migraine. There is blood encrusted to the woman’s hair. Latex sliding over flesh, snapping with a resounding snapsnapcracklepop and Chandler examines the cut perpendicular to the lobe of her left ear, pushes the hair that looks like brown dreadlocks/worms away, swallows the tenacious urge of bile stringing from his gallbladder through his small instestines and up his esophagus because her eyes are not there when he looks down. Outside Ellis is taking her hands in a journey through her hair, making them curl out at the ends like the ends of a laugh, drawn out thick like salve. The man in the black Navigator is watching her, still, and Ellis taps stray ashes from the cylinder of marijuana outside the crest of her window. Inside the woman’s skull closes like an anagram, the oddly-shaped mix of words disguised as a mystery so confusing it becomes solving a query through myopia, through black fuzz. If Chandler takes off all her hair, he guesses that he could map out her mind onto paper which he would duplicate and fold into origami cranes, paper birds to bring good luck. Instead he grabs his radio and tells Ellis that he is coming, coming, too late too bad so damn sad, Ellis is already gone, so to speak. “Were you smoking?” Chandler asks when he slides into his seat. “I don’t smoke,” Ellis says, and coughs. “Smoking is bad.” Sixteen minutes later they sit in Manhattan traffic. Ellis lights up another one. “I thought you didn’t smoke,” Chandler says. “Bullfuckin’shit,” Ellis says. “Will you turn up the radio? Logan used to sing me this song.” Chandler turns it down, but Ellis can still hear lyrics about a wild and crazy gypsy girl. b. so in your broken home he broke all of your bones Agent Landon asks why Ellis’ eyes are so small. Ellis tells him her eyes are so small because she was getting high in the passenger seat of Chandler’s car while Chandler was looking at some dead lady’s hair and then asks him to get out of her way because she has to pee. Then, Landon asks her to take on a new case. “I already have a damn case,” Ellis says, tracing a fingernail around her nicotine patch. “Remember? The slashed wrists homicide bullshit?” “I’m giving that case to Laura,” Laura Carmichael, blondehairedblueeyed agent/retard Laura Carmichael from “IwenttoNYUohmygoshyouwenttoNYUtoo!!!!!?,” and Ellis snorts. “Are you insane?” “I'm sane.” “No, you’re not. Laura is a retard. Plus, I’ve already started. You can’t just boot me off of the case.” “Yes, I can.” “No. I’m already half-way done with the one I have. I’m not starting all over again. I’m not taking another case.” “Why not? It’s not like you have anything better to do.” Arms crossed, eyes narrowed. “What are you trying to insinuate?” “That you have nothing better to do.” “How do you know? I mean, hello. I’m a woman. I always have something else to do. I have a pedicure in two hours.” Landon sighs. “Ellis.” “And I’m in a real scuffle about what color to get them done,” Ellis continues, pointing her toes with a sigh, “I was thinking this clearish little tone they have, but that wouldn’t be too exciting. Actually, it’d be utterly boring. So, I’ve narrowed down my choices to either red or violet and God damnit, Landon, picking out nail polish is harder than you think it is.” Landon stares. “What?” Ellis throws her hands up. “See?” Landon blinks and sighs. “Ellis, just take the damn case.” “And what if I don’t?” “Well, I assume you like your job?” Scoff. “So?” “So you’ll take the case or you lose your job. That is that.” Ugh. ** “I hate my damn job,” Ellis sighs, flinging the back of her hand against her forehead. Nancy Wang nods, smiles, and massages Ellis’ heel. Ellis decides on red. When she steps outside, later, she wants to peel the sunset from its layers, put the globe in her fist, squash it like a circle of roe and destroy a life in return for everything that has happened to her. Instead, she hums the gypsy girl song. har·bin·ger (härbn-jr) n. One that indicates or foreshadows what is to come; a forerunner.
