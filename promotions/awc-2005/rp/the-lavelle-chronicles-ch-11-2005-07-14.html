Date: - - - - - The Lavelle Chronicles, Ch. 11 15th July 2005 10:00 am - 11:30 am Buzz Beep Beep Buzzzzzz I rolled over on my side, my arm still supporting a snoring Lois. My alarm buzzed at 10:00 am. It was a good start to a fresh day. I rolled over and allowed myself to wake up. My mind wandered to my dream about Lois and Harbrook. I then took a quick glance at Lois; her face still rested on my shoulder as she dreamt, softly, quietly, completely unaware that a new day had already begun. A small strand of hair danced lightly around her nose; using one finger, I gently stroked it back over and around her ear. My mind had been contemplating what to do about everything that had recently entered my life; college would soon be back to normal. Dean Winters had removed the expulsion from my record, thanks to Lois and her snooping around after the three that had framed me. My old band members, the new “Gods of War”. Robert had called a few nights ago to let Lois and I know that he was settling in. He had already started surfing lessons, getting a tan and had begun to drink smoothies. Lois joked around saying that he would eventually date some tanned bimbo, no brains and big boobs. Funny thing is Robert mentioned a woman he had taken an interest to, her name was Lucy Carter. When he mentioned what she looked like, Lois smiled, holding back her laugh. She was right. I didn't feel as guilty now - laying here next to Lois, because Robert had moved on. We had agreed to stay in touch and Lois and I would head down next week for a three day vacation. Grunt Lois nestled her head into my shoulder more, running her hand along my chest. Her eyes opened and she smiled. 'You're up early,' I beamed. 'Your alarm woke me,' she smiled back and leaned up and kissed me. 'I have an early start anyway; I have a job interview at 12:00. I think I'll need a shower, can't go looking like this.' I smiled as Lois got herself out of the tangled duvet; she was wearing a pair of baggy woman's white pyjama bottoms and a tight wife beater. I smiled as she skipped into the bathroom, almost tripping over the trailing duvet she had left behind. 'I'll start on the breakfast,' I yelled over the sound of rushing water. 'Sure... damn it’s cold...' Lois peered around the corner of the bathroom door, her hair only a tiny bit wet. Her naked shoulder offered a pleasant image for me to envision. 'You need some help?' I smiled. 'Don't be cheeky. Could you hand me the 'Head & Shoulders' shampoo, it isn't in here.' 'Em - sure - where is it?' 'Try under my towel,' she had disappeared into the bathroom again. I rose up from the comfort of the bed and lunged myself over the large ruffled duvet and onto the carpeted floor. Low and behold, underneath Lois's blue towel was the shampoo. I turned my head to find Lois standing in front of me, my mouth dropped... 'Em...' 'Thanks Pierce.” She smiled and went back into the bathroom. I swallowed nervously and went into the kitchen. Continuously smiling at the last image of Lois; it felt as though my face had been injected with botox. I could imagine Lois smiling and saying 'Keep dreaming, Pierce -'. She was a very independent woman when it came down to certain things and I had agreed to take it as slow as she wanted. I began to run the water when I heard a yelp come from Lois in the shower. 'SORRY...' I yelled back. I turned on the radio. 'White Shadows' - by Coldplay was playing and I cranked up the volume. I had begun to like the band Coldplay; my taste in music would change as frequently as the weather. I had similar tastes to most people. Indie-rock was the most preferred in our flat. Lois's favourite band was the Kaiser Chiefs. She had bought their album off e-bay and had listened to it over and over. I smiled every time she would play 'Oh My God', purely because it reminded me of home and living abroad. “Good morning and thanks for the cold rush!' Lois stood in front of me; dressed in a white shirt and black suit trousers. 'It woke me up at least.' 'Coffee is almost done,' I began to devour a small powdered donut as Lois came up behind me and hugged me. 'I love the smell of coffee and donuts in the morning. You know something, I really could get used to this.' She hugged me and with her free hand took a large chunk out of my donut. I smiled and started on a new one. Our conversation was interrupted by the loud hissing from the pot which meant our early morning brew was done. Lois was straight up, sucking the sugar coating off her fingers, she quickly tended to her coffee and then relaxed next to me at the counter. I began rereading the divorce document, again. “You okay?” she placed her hand on my shoulder. “I know I should be upset for my mom and Rebecca, but I am actually happy that he’ll finally be out of my life. I’m not being selfish in that sense, am I?” “You’re not selfish in believing that. You really shouldn’t worry so much about what other people will think. Relax and enjoy the early morning birds. Hey, I didn’t notice that we had a veranda.” “Oh yeah…I figured that out last night, just after you had conked out on the bed.” “Hey! I had been up all day and I was wrecked.” I kissed Lois tenderly and moved out onto the small decking at the top of the building. The wind blew a gentle breeze against my face as I stared down at all of the tiny dots that were people. “Good morning Albany…” “I don’t think they can here you,” Lois joked as she stood next to me. “You sure you don’t want to finish this donut? If not, I will…” she laughed and waved the powdered donut in front of me. With my free hand I grabbed the donut and put the remainder of it into my mouth. “What time is your appointment over at?” I looked out toward the sun; it was going to be a beautiful day today. “Probably around one,” “Is it alright if I meet you outside ‘Harbrook College’? I want to drop in and see Tim,” “No problem. I’ll meet you at the Harbrook coffee house then,” she smiled and kissed me on the forehead. “Good luck…” I kissed her back and watched her leave the flat. I was alone now and I decided it would be good to fix the place up a bit. Lois had left me a small note next to the empty cup of coffee: 'Thanks and I love you'. Lois had completely changed and I loved it; she was kind, caring and romantic at times. This was a side of her I had never seen before; not even when we first dated as teenagers. The image of Lois standing before me this morning continued to play in my mind. I smiled and picked up a photo of Lois, Rob and I at a 'Bon Jovi' concert. I couldn't stop laughing - I could remember the day as though it were yesterday. We had made our way through the crowd and up to the front. Robert had been singing along to every word, when suddenly a huge canister of beer was thrown over his head by some college prep guys. Robert turned around angrily and was greeted by Bon Jovi and he gave Robert, Lois and I a backstage pass each. One of the best concerts I had ever been to. The duvet was still a mess, so I decided I would tidy up the bed and bedroom and take a shower myself. I pulled open the lower cabinet and dragged out a pair of dark navy baggy jeans, with a green flame on the lower left leg. I couldn’t figure out which t-shirt to wear. Rustling through another cabinet, I accidentally pulled out a pair of Lois's lingerie and smiled. Not dwelling on the future image of her in it, I shoved it back into the drawer and pulled out a white wife beater and a dark navy shirt, with 'American Chopper' on the back. Lynx wax and some shower gel, along with clean clothing and underwear. I had definitely become tied down to one woman. I was actually taking a shower everyday, making sure I wore nice cologne and dressed nicely for her - dress to impress. I laughed as I saw myself in the mirror; I still had some lipstick on my cheek which I quickly scrubbed off. Lois’s saying this morning really did fit how I felt - “I could get used to this…” 12:00 pm - 1:00 pm 'Harbrook College' - land of the intelligent, prosperous and humorous students to walk amongst the streets of Albany and now, I was one of them. The small street corner paid a little 'hello' as I passed by, my trouser ends dragging along the pavement. I had never been to the residential areas of Harbrook, let alone inside the coffee house, so when I stood there, facing the large doors, I looked like a rabbit caught in headlights. I checked my watch and it was already 12:20 pm in an already blissful day. The bell rang and herds of students burst through the doors, in all shapes and sizes. I smiled as I watched each one separate into tiny groups; the nerds, the popular and good-looking, the jocks, the Goths and then the normal folk. The ones untarnished by the simple human divide that is so prudent in our adult lives. Back in Thompson College I believed it was self-explanatory to be part of a clique. Now being on the outside I see it as pointless, futile and unnecessary. If we will it and want it, everyone should be able to get along. Lois and I would be outsiders when we would start here in the few weeks, but I welcomed it. I didn’t see a reason to be shy around anyone here…not today. I had taken the gargantuan “Healy Hall” and moved down toward the small “Harbrook Coffee House”, which was stuffed to the gunnels with studious students, struggling to get a decent sized cup of coffee that would last them the day. I knew most of the people brushing past me, nudging my shoulder as they did so, didn’t even notice me and I smirked at their rudeness. Tim Shipley was seated by himself in the back corner of the coffee house, against a large window that gave a beautiful view of the lush, green, lawns of Harbrook College. He was sipping some water, whilest writing on an A4 pad. “Is this seat taken?” Tim Shipley put down his pen and smiled as he saw me. “No, go ahead, Pierce. It’s nice to have some company.” “How long will I have to wait for some coffee?” I said while taking off my shirt. The temperature in Albany had continued to rise, it was already 30’f and a stuffed College coffee house didn’t help with the muggy heat. Tim Shipley once again put down his pen and looked at the long queue of students at the coffee counter. “At least ten minutes… a lecture just ended and most likely everyone is dying for a recharge. I hate how it’s become fashionable to survive on coffee. Anyway, what brings you to the college?” “I know what you mean. I have lived on the stuff since my High School days. Oh - em - I’m waiting on Lois to get out of a job interview, so we agreed to meet here. Whilest I was here I thought I’d catch-up with you, see how you’re doing.” Tim Shipley looked at me and smiled, nodding his head. “Lois?” Tim had a quizzical look. I remembered now that I hadn‘t really mentioned much in regards to my life, friends and girlfriends. I was a mystery to my new found friend and tag partner. “My girlfriend, she’s really nice, once you get passed her front.” “Front?” Tim raised his eyebrow with a hint of a smile. “Sorry. I should have said façade. She’s an incredibly independent woman and had a father who was an army general, put a few hard bones into her.” “I see.” Tim Shipley took another gulp from his water bottle and then continued on with his work. I looked around again, feeling slightly uncomfortable being in college once again. “Thompson College” was nothing compared to “Harbrook”. We had small tiny dorms and this place has residential areas. We had one tiny main hall, this has many gargantuan halls. Everything seems to be twice as better as “Thompson” and it was unsettling. “I have never been to a place so stocked with students. My College was a private one and a lot smaller.” “You get used to it,” Tim rolled up his sleeve and turned over to a fresh page. “What are you working on?” “Oh, some assignments...” he seemed determined to be vague. “Isn’t it the summer vacation here?” “Yeah, well, sort of. But, I like to stay on top of things… I fell behind a while ago. And I‘m doing a separate project over the summer… I‘m not sure whether it‘ll count towards my degree, but I have to get it done before lectures start again in autumn; I can‘t let it interfere. Not this year.” “I know that feeling; I fell behind in one of my last assignments in Thompson. I was writing an assignment on the Excretory System and the processes involved. Completely forgot about it, I felt the punishment of my professor the next day. Come to think of it, I have a lot of assignments to finish-up for my interview with the new professor.” Tim once again stopped writing and decided on putting the A4 pad into his rucksack for the time being. The queue for coffee was beginning to disperse and a lot of students were more relaxed, at ease, and drinking caffeine. “What - er - happened to you in your last college?” Tim asked. “If you don’t mind my asking.” “No, it’s fine. I was expelled.” “Expelled? Why?” “I was framed for something I didn’t commit and I suffered the consequences. Plus, I spent all of my college tuition to spite my father. Just to let you know, I’m not rebellious or evil, it’s just I hate him and well he made me go to Thompson. But, thanks to Lois and her many ways; my record is clean and we are joining Harbrook.” Tim Shipley nodded his head as he took in the information about my past, each time his eyes would widen at the experiences I had in getting to where I am now. “I have known her for about six years now. She’d like to meet you.” He finished off his bottle of water and threw the empty one into the nearest waste bin. “What were you studying?” “Biology in general, but I did a variety of courses to improve my chances of getting into medicine.” “Sounds interesting, what does Lois study?” “She’s going to study Law with a small module of Journalism,” I smiled to myself just thinking about her. I checked my watch once more, it was already 12:45 pm and I knew Lois would be arriving soon; I couldn’t wait to find out how her job interview went. “If you want some coffee, I’d recommend you go now.” Tim pointed to a virtually empty line. Two people stood at the top of the coffee counter, pouring milk into their espressos and lattés. “You want anything?” “No… I’m fine,” Tim looked at his own watch and pulled out the A4 pad once again. I was up just in time; another bell rang and a second herd of students came pouring out of a different lecture room. I stood my grounds and waited for the two in front to finish decorating their coffee. “What can I get ya’?” a small lady in a red apron popped her head over the muffin rack and stared at me. “Two coffees…one black and one with milk and sugar.” “To go?” she asked as she began to pour the coffee. “No thanks…” I stared blankly at Shipley as he continued to write his assignment. I realised as I stood here, waiting for the small lady to finish my order, that I knew very little about my friend and tag partner, Tim Shipley. I could vaguely remember what he was like in CORE, but I knew exactly the kind of person he was. He was like me, just a lot shyer. “Here you are sir, two coffees - em - one with sugar and milk and one without, enjoy.” I paid the price for the two coffees and returned to the table I was last seated at. Tim Shipley leant back in the chair and sighed, putting his hands over his head and clasping them. “Not going too well?” “It’s finished, finally - well, the introduction.” Tim paused and finally relaxed. I stared at his ream of paper, all written on. “THAT is your introduction?” “I wanted to be thorough,” he said shortly. “That’s good then…” I stated. “Two cups of coffee?” “One for Lois, she didn’t get her usual amount this morning, so she’ll be looking for something to wake her up,” “Pierce, em, well, have you spoken to the Dean yet?” Tim asked pulling off some spare A4 sheets and sticking them into his ring binder. “I have a meeting next week, I just hope it goes well. Speaking of lady friends and dates, what about you?” Tim’s hands fell to the table. He looked around the room once again and unconsciously began messing around the pen in his hand. “What about me?” “Do you have anyone special?” Tim went strangely quiet, his eyes looking away from me. His hands twitching once again with the pen. His body language gave me a slight hint that he was a little nervous about this topic of conversation and unwilling to talk. So I sat back and smiled at the sound of a familiar voice. “Lavelle…” Lois approached me, her jacket in hand and a large smiled on her face. “Hey Lo, this is Tim.” Lois smiled and shook Tim’s hand; she then leant in and kissed my cheek. “Ah the gorgeous smell of freshly brewed Coffee…which one is mine?” I handed her the coffee with milk and sugar. Lois had once tasted just plain black coffee and it ended up spewed all over my mothers cream sofa. It was a panic that day. “How’d the interview go?” “That man hadn’t got a clue what he was talking about, so I set him straight.” I smiled. Lois had gone for an interview in the “New York Post”. She had brought in some of the news paper articles that she had written for Thompson and our previous high school. I knew whatever this man said or did probably got a mouthful. “He thought the whole point in journalism was being a photographer. I couldn’t stop laughing,” she took a sip. “What?” I pulled open the lid of my coffee. “Oh, it was a small, stubby, bald man. He approached me in the corridor after my interview and said journalists would be nowhere without a photographer and I laughed. Half the time the New York post news articles never have a picture in them.” “Sounds good…did you get the job?” She smiled and kissed me on the cheek once again. I took that as a ‘yes’ and left it at that. Tim Shipley was now even more uncomfortable than he had been when I had first sat down to talk to him. He rose up from his chair, putting his ring binder and A4 pad into his rucksack and fastening it. “Nice talking to you, Pierce. I - er - have to go, but… maybe we can catch up some other time.” “Sure, see ya,” I shook his hand. “Nice to meet you, Lois,” Tim threw his backpack over his shoulder and began to move around the table and out into the corridor. “Ditto…” she replied and waved goodbye. He said that each and every person has a limit. No matter what profession you do - whether you’re a cop, a lawyer, a journalist, a scientist or a bin man. Everyone has a limit. Break that limit or hit a nerve and eventually you’ll crack. I could see what he meant now. My limit had been reached subconsciously; I had cracked and my father paid the price with a broken nose. I, like Robert, wore my emotions on my sleeve. At certain points I could crack, it was something Lois mentioned I would need to restrain. She had learnt to take things in her stride, to ignore most of her emotions. I guess a loved one dying does that to a person. But now she had begun to change, her emotions were more evident around me and I felt somewhat safe when around her. I felt like the world couldn’t touch me and it was a feeling I had never held. We arrived at the apartment around 4:00 pm. The place felt tranquil, silent and cosy. Lois had flung her expensive suit jacket over the wooden chair by the door and headed toward the fridge for a beer. She had asked nicely whether I wanted one, but I refused. Now that I was here, I didn’t feel up to going to the hospital, not now and not ever. You know when you find things out or you have a certain problem, you just sweep it under the carpet and let it go. This was one of those situations. But, I didn’t have a carpet big enough for this problem. “You sure you don’t want a cold beer? I don’t want you to get dehydrated,” Lois said while pulling off the bottle cap. Really, I’m fine.” “Glad to hear it,” she smiled and moved over toward the CD player. The bedroom hadn’t been changed since I had left this morning. The duvet was still ruffled and both of our pyjamas lay on the floor in a heap. I smiled. This apartment gave a feeling of unconditional love. Back in Harrisburg when the temperature and atmosphere became unbearable; Lois, Rob and I would head down to the private pool and just swim for a few hours. Ignoring the world around us and the many rich folk that sat on inflatable chairs, drinking martini‘s. I was seated on the bed, dressed in nothing but my boxers. Lois came into the room smiling.
