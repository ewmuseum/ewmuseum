Distorted Reflections

Date: 4th of June Prologue -- A Bad Day -- Not a Wasted Day When I was fifteen years old, the 'crazy old lady next door' died. Sadly, not many people seemed to care. However, even at fifteen, I knew that I would forever miss her. Not that I even knew her name-- she was always the 'crazy old lady next door' to me, but everyday on my way home from school she would ask me how my day was. Knowing the consequential response, I usually mumbled fine continuing down the sidewalk as quickly as possible to avoid answering the inevitable. 'Why?' 'I don't know.' I would say. Her response: 'There you have it, another wasted day.' One day when I was in third grade, I made that familiar walk home from school past the “crazy old lady next door’s” house to my own house. It had been a really bad day at school and I was really angry at life in general. I turned the corner onto the sidewalk that lead down our street and past the old lady’s house and to my own. I found a rock on the sidewalk and tossed it at a bird’s in a tree. I hit the nest and it fell to the ground. The eggs within cracked and the potential chicks seeped into the ground. I was not normally a demented kid, but this made me feel good. I began kicking another rock down the sidewalk. When it fell down the curb and on to the street I grew so angry I picked up the rock and threw it at a car. I put a pretty bad dent in it, but I felt good. I had never felt good being destructive. This was a new feeling to me. I wanted to tear up everything. I felt like I couldn’t be any worse so why not? So I continued down the street stepping on flowers, kicking up dirt, and then I passed her house. “How was your day?” I stopped frozen. This is exactly what I wanted at that moment. Normally I wouldn’t say anything, but today I was different. I was hot. I was about to spill my heart, but instead started crying. “Awful. Just awful. I got into a fight with my best friend. I forgot we had a spelling and a math test today. I didn’t do my big English project and the teacher said that tomorrow was too late. We had hot dogs today for lunch and I hate hot dogs. I wasn’t allowed to play kick ball today at recess because I was picked last and the teams were uneven. So I had to go play on the playground with the girls. Then this girl named Jamie Marsh tried to kiss me. It’s the worse day of my life.” “Ah… It seems like you might have learned something.” She said somewhat sarcastically, somewhat seriously. “I doubt it.” I said in a matter of fact manner. “You think? I think that you learned who your friends are. You learned that procrastination is damnation. You learned what you like and don’t like and that sometimes you don’t always get what you like. Then you learned about life.” “Whatever.” I said. At that time, not exactly understanding what she meant. Present-day (Jessie) – Paper Pieces of a Ripped up Heart On days I really fail with Crimson, I go home and drag myself to my file cabinet. Inside are some of the things Jason has allowed me to take home of Crimson's creations from the center. Crimson writes poetry and makes some amazing paintings. Some of the paintings hang on my walls. They often make good conversation pieces. Most of them are very angry. My favorite is a graveyard all in shades of blue and gray. Whenever I bring someone to my house, they are often surprised by the dreary artwork. I love those paintings, they show real emotion, not your store bought pretty shit. I open the file cabinet and pull out a notebook. Jason has been a Godsend. He's always been there when I needed him. I remember the day I met him. I went to the center to drop off a new notebook and pen for Crimson. He asked a million questions; mostly things that normally someone would find annoying. At the time, I was living out of town. I didn’t really want to be that close to the memories. The last time I had visited I was told that Crimson had started writing. I saw his first poem and was brought to tears. I was taken back to the day my sister died. The notebook flops open to the poem that I want to see. By this time the notebook is very used to being open to this page. Jason asked a million questions; mostly things that normally someone would find annoying. I believe I would start to understand how mothers with deformed children react to people staring at the mall. Contrastingly though, I found comfort in the fact that someone began to care for Crimson the way that I do or possibly it was that this was the first time that someone other than myself found Crimson to be more than a demented mind. Jason's prognosis was not every other doctor's hopeless case. Jason believed that Crimson could be saved. Jason gave me the gift of confidence in what I was doing and actually thought that it would help himself. I find myself hugging the notebook I’ve gotten out. I think it was almost two years ago that Crimson started painting. It’s only been around a year since he began speaking. Other than the occasional calling out for Jamie or calling me Jamie when I walked in the door. The hardest thing I’ve ever found to do in life I had to do almost six times a year. Imagine how hard it would be to watch a man come out of his shell and believe an angel has come to take them home. Only to tell him you are not that angel, that that angel is dead, and you are only her sister trying to do what you know is right for her mourning husband. I pry the notebook from my fingers and lay it in my lap. I can not look down at the page; instead I begin crying. I’ve always wanted to not tell him I was Jessie and tell him that I was Jamie. I just wanted to see if I could break the spell that he was put under. I could never bring myself to do it. I always thought that it would tear him up even worse in the end. I wonder to this day if it would have brought him back to the real world for awhile. I know that sometimes the mere sight of me takes him back to the days when my sister was alive. I have to cherish the fact that he doesn’t completely turn me away for being the dagger in the heart I sometimes am. I know I could not be so strong. The page in my lap is already rippled and stained from tears. I close the book slowly not to break such a treasure. Some days I hate that he can’t get over it. Every now and then I get so pissed that he has to constantly bring it up. It was hard enough the first time. How many times had I wondered, in my lonely nights of solitude, if life would ever be the same? Mother and father, would they ever be with me again. Happy faces. I used to wish foolishly to die and be with them, in Heaven above, to know a little of the happiness we had shared when they were alive. How many times had I wandered in the darkness, longing, dreaming that I had someone at my side? How many times had I prayed to them in Heaven, to grant me just a moment of happiness? I buried those questions and the memory in the hole of my heart. I did not want to bring it up again. It took me so long to bury the losses of our lives. I could even envision a box. A box that I put down in the ground. A locked box that is dirty and useless and unmentionable. A box that I hated. Then one day I went to find Crimson. I don’t remember what drew me to him. The book closes and I drop it to the floor. I immediately go to catch it but I catch myself on my legs and hang on to myself for dear life. I begin to cry. Sobbing, moaning, sighing. The tears begin to soak my jeans. I feel the warm water wet my knees. I imagine myself drowning in my tears. It motivates me too pull myself together. I sit up and slump into my chair. I look up at the ceiling fan. It is motionless but the still air nevertheless dries my tears. I look down to see the book has fallen open to the page I cannot read. With thundering anger and flashes of light that break through overcomed darkness. I'd like to think that rain is an omen. It helps to remember that the rain always ends, and a rainbow sometimes appears. Yet sometimes the rainbow doesn’t appear and the rainbow is never really there to begin with anyway. The blind can feel the rain, but they never see the optical allusion of the colors. Flashback (Crimson) – The Curtain Falls Almost every medical facility is the same. The over use of white and green, for example, enough to make your average homosexual-- not just your Home Interior selling, Trading Spaces host 'wannabe'-- roll his eyes. Then there is the smell. You know, that mixture of new car, cleaning agents, antibiotics, and the sweat of over paid ''doctors.' And you would think, if only for a moment, that they would get more comfortable furniture. Something. not pleather, perhaps? Maybe one day you can go into a doctor's office sit on a real couch- one you sink into not stick on to. Then, you will look to the magazines on the stand next to you and instead of having to read the 'Cat in the Hat' again. There will be something worth reading-- an Auto magazine, a Sports illustrated not for kids, hell maybe even the swimsuit issue. Its awful enough that your sick, waiting for bad news, or waiting for the forty dollar check for donating some sperm. Awful enough that you shouldn't have to be uncomfortable too. Whenever I went into Junior year in high school, Jamie asked me to be in the school play. We were cleaning up the pool for the summer. We had taken our last swim for the year and were draining the pool when she asked me. 'Crimson...' She said with that pleading voice- you know the one, that I'm going to beg because I know you are going to say yes eventually. Every woman knows this too. Women have the power to make a man do almost anything. It's called the Vagina Clause- they have it - you don't - and there is no way in hell its going to get anywhere near you if you don't do this for me. 'Crimson, I need a favor will you be in 'Romeo and Juliet' with me at school?' I thought she was out of her mind. There was no way she was getting me into green tights and spouting out girly love lines. 'It's just that I know I will get Juliet, and Joey Meyers will get Romeo and I don't want that perverted scumbag touching me let alone kissing me.' Hold up, we aren't exactly dating but I will beat the living daylights out of this kid if... Okay so maybe I was too shy to confess my love... Yet. So instead I played stupid. 'So why do I have to be in this stupid play?' I knew even at sixteen that being in the school play was shattering to a guy’s reputation. Jamie was obviously a little uncomfortable. She sat down on the edge of the almost empty pool and said, “Well I Know l can talk Mrs. Witt into letting you be Romeo.' There grew an awkward silence. Being in the play was one thing… being The big guy was another, Jamie could see I was reluctant, so she did some persuading, 'If you were Romeo, then you could be the one to cuddle and kiss me on stage. We could even… practice.” Okay she had me at kiss. I mean who doesn’t want to be the guy kissing the best looking girl in your high school in front of everyone. It helped that she said we could practice kissing too. Not that we already hadn’t, I had kissed her at just about every opportunity I could. Yet we never really sat down just to start kissing, I imagine I could use some brushing up anyhow. That sure as hell sounded like fun to me. So we had our play rehearsals after school and we had our extra practicing in the garage kissing for hours. In that garage “practicing” for that play was also the place my young self ventured past the kissing line. I was never much into baseball, but I think it was something like second base. I think my cold hands took her by surprise. “You are not going to do that on stage are you? My mother will be there!” “Like it’s no different than my mother being right through that door in the kitchen making you and I something for dinner.” She shrugged and put my hands back where they were, and I liked it. It would take a driver’s license before I got any better at baseball. The last performance of “Romeo and Juliet” was a Saturday Night and the night of one of my Dad’s big matches. It was a cage match for the top title in the organization. I had never missed one of Dad’s match and having to be at the performance was really bugging me. Ms. Witt made arrangements for a TV to be put backstage for me to watch Dad’s match when I wasn’t on stage. Unfortunately Dad’s match just happened to start right when Act II Scene ii started. I pushed my way through to see EMTs putting my father on a stretcher. From that point everything is a little blurry I ran through the stage and out through the crowd. The next thing I remember is getting out of the taxicab and running into the hospital. To this day, I can’t stand waiting rooms. The hospital is a very dreary disgusting place to me. It is ironic that I found my only virtue in an insanity ward. The very medical type place I hated for years. I remember sitting in the waiting room pissed that I had nothing else to do but stare at the white ceiling tiles. No one there to tell me what was going on. My mother was down the hall waiting outside the emergency room. She was hopeless, I could do nothing, because I felt worse than she did. It wasn’t until two o’clock that morning that Jamie came walking through the door. “I’m so sorry. I prayed and prayed he would make it through. When I heard on the radio that he didn’t make it I was just…” “What? He’s dead? YOU FOUND OUT BEFORE ME!! YOU FOUND OUT BEFORE ME!” I had probably a hundred faces staring at me, many of them facing there own bad news. I ran out the door and all the way up the steps to the roof of the hospital. I sat on a park bench and stared out into the city. It was a couple minutes later that I felt a hand on my shoulder. I turned around to see Jamie crying. “The is nothing you can say to make this any better.” So she didn’t say anything. She sat beside me and let me cry on her shoulder. The wetness of my own tears on her dress for the final scene shocking me back into consciousness. “You finished the play? Who played Romeo? Did Joey kiss you?” I said through the sobs. “Actually. Ms. Witt kind of took over. She actually does a pretty good young man. And we didn’t kiss. Thank God.” We both laughed and Jamie just squeezed me a little closer. Present Day (Jason) – Reflections of Distorted Proportions. It seems like a normal day at the clinic, if you consider a day going around and talking to the legally insane normal. One of the new nurses runs to tell me that ZOZ1578B is no where to be found. I roll my eyes and walk past the nurse. I turn around to see that she is not following me. “You know we do let Crimson out of his cage.” The young nurse is not very happy with my sarcasm and she lets me know by turning her shoulder like any other attitudinal young lady fresh out of her two year degree. “Follow me, Miss Faherty.” I began making the walk towards the elevator. “Crimson lives here on his own account. At anytime he can sign a paper and walk right out of our facility and not come back. Crimson is a responsible and relatively sane individual. He finds comfort in living here, but he was not always so stable. It is not our responsibility as medical caregivers to worry about his exact doings and whereabouts. However, it is our responsibility to get to know him well enough that we know when something is wrong.” I push the button and wait for the elevator to open. This silence adds to the general effect of my speech. “Crimson is welcome to do as he pleases on these premises. He has my permission and as director of this community I think my orders are better followed. Crimson and the rest of our patients are to be treated as a human beings and if I hear any word that you have not done so, I will fire your ass, and you will loose your license faster than you can say, ‘I’m sorry.’ Do you understand, Miss Faherty?” She is too speechless to say anything. She just nods to let me know that my point is understood. The elevator dings and the door opens. We walk outside and down one of the dirt paths towards the creek. “Mr. O’Malec likes to come down to the creek and reflect on life. If he is gone for any lengthy period of time you come down here and see if he is here, and then check his normal hang outs. Sometimes he is in the cafeteria helping the dietary aides prepare the nights meal. Mr. O’Malec tries to earn his keep around here. Some days he will be helping the janitors, doing paper work at the front desk, or sometimes counseling some of my patients, and if you treat him like a friend that is no less than yourself, maybe he will help you to. There he is… sitting at the park bench. If you are more so concerned you may observe our conversation.” I walked the length of the path and washed my hands off in the creek. I stood in front of Crimson and just looked down creek. “I should have figured you’d be down here.” I said. “You know I like it down here. There’s nothing better than a good park bench. I have some of my foremost memories on park benches.” Crimson says. “Well, what exactly are we remembering today, Crimson?” I say bending down to turn over a stone to look for crawfish. “Nothing too spectacular, just my match last week. I fought a woman you know.” “Yes, I know. It took a good bit of talking to convince my wife that wrestling is something we can allow on our TV without our daughter suffering too badly.” I said, watching a turtle jump into the water after a minnow. I’m careful with my words, for I know that sometimes watching wrestling can do some damage to an unsuspecting child. Proof of that sitting on the park bench behind me. “Well, I wouldn’t exactly teach your daughter that it’s alright for a man to beat up on a woman.” Crimson said jokingly. “Oh I would never intend to, I showed her that it’s just proof that a woman can do exactly what a man can. Then you won, and my wife said I didn’t choose to good of an example.” “I’m not so happy I won. I mean, I am happy that I didn’t lose, but I really didn’t want to wrestle a woman. I made a couple mistakes anyhow. My ring dust is coming off, I’ll be back to where I was before long.” “You should be happy with your accomplishments, you gained a title match. You could be a champion before long.” I said trying to cheer him up. “Oh, I have a feeling I will be bringing home that little belt of gold in a couple days.” “That’s what I want to hear.” I am starting to realize the actual large change in Crimson. It wasn’t long ago that he was keeping me up at night wondering why this guy who looked normal and didn’t have any outward appearing differences was so withdrawn from the world. I always had some sort of longing to know what kept him ticking. I don’t really understand the reasons I was so obsessed with him. One day it dawned on me, he could have been one of the guys I go to the bar with. There was a guy next door type of feel to him. He is the type of person where you could swear you’ve met him before you are just not sure where or when. Except I knew I never met Crimson, but I felt like I could only imagine his life story. “Well if everything is alright down here, I might as well get back to doing what they pay me to do.” “Jase, hold up a minute. Can you call Jessie for me and see if she’s alright?” This took me by surprise, it was not something I expected. I motioned to Ms. Faherty to start walking back to her post. I looked over to Crimson and he seemed to be in a alright state of mind. It was only yesterday Jessie had asked me about asking Crimson to move in with her and out of Rolling Creek. I told her it wasn’t a bad idea, and she told me she planned on asking him this morning if she could gather up the nerve. I told her not to ask him unless she was ready for him to say no. Not that I thought he wouldn’t take her up on her offer, just that I knew Crimson thought of Rolling Creek as his home, and it was one of the only places he considered shelter. “Did she not stop by this morning, is that why you are worried?” I asked, the puzzling question staying inside. “No, it’s just she came here today and said she had something to ask me. I told her that I wanted to ask her something first. She said okay and I sat her down and asked her not to come to my match next week. I don’t think she took it to well.” Crimson said almost more so to the creek than to me. “Why did you ask her not to come to the match?” I said, wondering what exactly was going on. “I don’t want her to see what might happen. This is a weapons match and I know that sometimes wrestlers don’t always come out so pretty.” He said, assured that he was right in asking her not to come to the match. “Did you explain that to her?” “Not exactly, I couldn’t find the words. I was only trying to protect her.” He said. “Jessie is a grown woman, perhaps she wishes to keep her protection up to herself.” “I don’t know, I know she was upset, she didn’t even ask me what she was going to ask. She said it didn’t matter anymore.” “I don’t know, perhaps you should call her.” I said, knowing that she didn’t get to ask Crimson to move in with her. “Maybe, I don’t know, I think I’m just going to go fishing instead.” Some days we all fail Crimson. Other days, Crimson fails us. Today it seems it was both. View Crimson O'Malec 's Biography
