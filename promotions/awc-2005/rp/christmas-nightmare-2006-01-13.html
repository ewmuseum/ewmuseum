Date: 1/13/06 ***[ This was the rp I was supposed to write for the PPV before my comp died. I just needed to post it to further my plot and storyline for Hamada. It does not count toward the FRESH! card next week. ]*** Nightfall had come some time ago, and only the stars and a few gas powered street lamps guided the solitary pedestrian toward Kolnarena, Germany and Winter Warfare. The moon was high when the lonely wanderer finally entered the lobby of a small German hotel, taking a seat in the now quiet dining hall near the bar. She had walked for so long her calves hurt, yet she remembered so little about her walking tour of the German city. Her mind was elsewhere, stealing glimpses of imaginary shadows from the past in the reflections of store windows and peeking around street corners. Megumi Hamada would have preferred to stay awake, to keep moving, and to get her mind off of everything that had happened to her in the last week. But there was no question she needed the sleep, for tomorrow she was going to be tested like never before in her life. Since the early morning brought the sun streaming its' first rays through her window she had put many miles between herself and all the memories she would try and leave behind. It had been like that since leaving England. Amsterdam was barely a memory. She walked and walked, trying to make sense of it all. She tried to forget it all. She tried to figure out the best course of action she should take. But day after day, mile after mile, all her speed and stamina, walking to the border of exhaustion, she could not escape the guilt of the past. She spent a long time sitting there at a small round table up against the west wall. She rubbed her sore calves and stared straight out into the ethereal. Ghosts danced in her head, of deeds long forgotten and what could have been and should have been. On the night before the biggest match of her life, Megumi Hamada could not help but watch the macabre display of apparitions of defunct bodies and tortured spirits dance before her eyes in visions of lament and memories started from their grave. ************************** The days before Christmas at CYDONIA Inc. were always the social highlight of the year. The agency had its' annual 'Christmas BASH' every year. Professional wrestling had no off-season, but there was no mistake that Christmas was a time most in the business appreciated for the good times and good memories, the one night everyone could get together and have a party. And far be it for anyone in pro wrestling to miss the opportunity for a party. They came out in full force, the workers and the agents and everyone one from the mail room clerks on up. CYDONIA Inc. held a stable of sometimes up to one hundred wrestlers, not to mention lawyers, managers, and staff. This year the party was being held in New York City, home of the corporation’s offices. Everyone was there, and it was considered serious business for many who were looking for bookings and jobs, and to make new acquaintances and renew old friendships. Of course, there was always that competitive spirit that would boil over in a room full of testosterone and God knows what else. Some were there to upstage business competitors. Some were there to start rumors and listen to gossip. The younger crowd merely gathered to enjoy their new stardom and money, their hard bodies and harder lifestyles, all with high spirits. The gathering was always a lavish affair and this year was no different. Ablaze with the Christmas lights and a grand old tree, people danced and talked. The mood was merry as it should be at this time of year. People laughed and glasses clinked. The dancers spun around on the marble floors, their jewels glittering, their clothing sharp, and their exposed and oiled bodies shining. Heck, these were pro wrestlers, some of the vainest people in the world, and they came out in style. Other revelers enjoyed the buffet tables or helped themselves to the trays of delicious delicacies circulated by a small horde of hired hands. No expense had been spared; tonight everything was of the highest quality available to CYDONIA Inc., celebrating their eight year in pro wrestling business. Santa hats and mistletoe hung everywhere. Music filled the air. People talked about life and business and everything in between. In remote corners of the office some people were getting into a more comfortable Christmas spirit together. It was a night to be happy and enjoy your fellow man. It was a night that men and women who sacrificed their bodies day in and day out, would forget the bruises and the pains, and have a good time and enjoy their lives. But even in this sea of happiness and Christmas cheer, one man stood quietly alone, watching, thinking, his mind on a soul five thousand miles away. Johnny Olympus wondered just what kind of Christmas Megumi Hamada could be having. ******************************** Once she was warm and comfortably settled in, Megumi was able to shake off the spider webs of her mind and ask the hotel clerk if she might have something warm to drink. The old German lady was more then happy to go behind the bar and fix up a cup of hot cocoa for the young girl and bring it to her. Megumi settled in next to the hotels fireplace. She had walked like a nomad throughout the day, as much to escape her own thoughts as to elude anyone in possible pursuit. Now, in the quiet of the hotels emptiness, she could no longer avoid thinking about Tozen Fujita. After Tozen's body had been discovered, Megumi had allowed anger and hate to take over her emotions and she was able to block all things out of her mind. She was able to take on Jack Murphy, substituting for Fujita in that challenge match over a week ago, and pin the man even after her closest friend and confidant had been taken to the hospital. But now she couldn't get him out of her mind. She couldn't get him or the brand out from under her skin. The AWC front office knew she was using CYDONIA Inc. as her agency. They had asked her a few basic questions about the company, and about the brand that had been left on the back of Tozen's shoulder. But the AWC wasn't all that concerned for a wrestler not under contract, and they didn't know the inner workings of CYDONIA Inc. Megumi worked for them, that was all anyone knew. If you worked for CYDONIA you would know more, and the attack on Tozen would be another reminder that there was a mysterious assassin out there. A shooter, a hooker, whatever and whomever they were, there was a person hunting CYDONIA agents. Sighing, Megumi sipped at her cocoa. Everyone had shown great concern over Tozen's plight and how Megumi would feel. She hadn't made any real friends in AWC except the nurse, Jin Osaka, but everyone had been extra nice to her, trying to help her through this rough time. In truth, little did anyone know that Megumi and Tozen shared only friendship, and an adventure. But she had done nothing to squelch the idea that she and Tozen were lovers, mainly to discourage unwanted advances from other people. To her, Tozen's friendship was a rare and special gift, one that a girl from Japan with no family or friends treasured greatly. Megumi glanced down at the only ring on her left hand. It gleamed faintly in the firelight, and she spread her fingers to look at it more closely. It was a simple ring, just a silver band engraved with the Japanese character for love. Tozen had given it to her when they had first met, when a romance between them hadn't quickly evaporated into a strong friendship. She wore the ring all the time, but now it symbolized something more to her. Their friendship, their travels, their hardship, it was a reminder to her of everything they had shared. Dismayed at the unaccustomed sense of loneliness that plagued her, Megumi busied herself by moving her seat closer to the fire, grabbing a comforter from a couch nearby and snuggling up as close as the heat would allow her. The old German woman looked up from behind her glasses, setting her pen down in another large novel that would carry her through the night, and looked at the young girl with a sense of pity. Megumi stared into the fire, pulling the comforter as close to her body as she could, and tried to let the flames enter her other world and burn away the cobwebs of pain. ********************************* One corner of the party -a corner very close to the open bar- echoed with peal after peal of laughter. But such an oath wuld be an affront to her mother, who had known love, who had birthed her, and who had loved her father so much she was thrown out of the family. And anyway, in her case, thought Megumi, an oath like this would be redundant. She had no one in her life and wanted just that. No one to get hurt, no one to let down. She sipped some of her hot cocoa, then put the mug down on a small table, lacing her fingers beneath her head as she pondered the matter. Close relationships of any kind did not come easily to Megumi. She had followed the old fashioned traditions of the joshi way of life. And then there was her training. Her true job, her true self, that she could not let anyone know about. Armed with forgotten holds and crippling manuevers, hookers had become scarce as wrestling developed over the years into a sports entertainment medium. Rare and deadly, an unbeatable combination. And Megumi Hamada was convinced the hooker wasn't out there to destroy CYDONIA wrestlers. The hooker was out to detroy her. *********************** 'Well...' Max hesitated. Inside a German hotel, in the middle of the European continent, and she still felt that she was being watched. The was no sound but the crackling of the dying fire. There were no threatening shadows ready to pounce. There was no indication that someone was watching her, but Megumi felt a presence lurking just beyond the reach of the glowing ember's light. Outside a window, around the corner, right behind the door, something was there. Lurking. The comforter around her shoulders fell and she looked down at her tattoo. Whenever she felt that sense of danger, her tattoo would always itch. It was an old habit, a weird nervous tick she had developed after she had gotten her tattoo. But there was no itch, there was no goose bumps on her ink-stained shoulder. Megumi had learned early in her life that the ceremonial tattoo her mother had given her was something more then just ink and design. The traditional irezumi was something more to her, a living part of her that she could not explain. That it was a gift from her mother, drawn and inked by her over the course of the short years they had together probably had more to do with it then tradition or art. There was something spiritual about it, like a piece of her mother's spirit was in the design, her soul mixed in with the ink. Wen she felt danger was near, it would tingle. When she slept she sometimes saw it in her dreams, manifesting and metamorphing into beings and animals, telling her of the future, of the past, and her destiny. Since she so often traveled alone, lived alone, worked alone, the tattoo was like her friend in some ways. It was a piece of home. She was never alone, even though she was. Tonight however, there was no itch, there was no shiver down her arm. Like her surroundings, all was dark and silent. She was alone here. She didn't mind. She had her final match with Jack Murphy in the morning and she wanted nothing more then to relax and clear her thoughts. Why, then, did she have such a haunting feeling of eyes upon her?
