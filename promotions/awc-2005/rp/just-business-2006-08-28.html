Just Business...

28 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28th August Title: ‘Just Business…’ Show: Coast to Coast Tokyo, Japan Off the Coast of Yokosuka The small vessel lay shrouded in the hazy mist of the Pacific Ocean; a cool sea breeze rustled the black drapes covering the ships dock as it propelled at a steady pace. A group of tall, executive men from every orifice of the world struggled against the tossing waves in their Doc Martens and Armani suits, climbing down the steel steps to the large conference room. The churning spindles of a passing helicopter fluttered in the night’s sky, the waves beneath the black helicopter churned in circles under its air propulsion. The stealthy helicopter hovered momentarily in the shrouded mist, the pilot holding the stick in a set position. Tony Rush inched forward in his seat, dressed in a dark navy suit with a white shirt; he slipped on his decorated black sunglasses and clutched his briefcase. Out of the small window he could see the vessel he was supposed to retrieve his client’s purchase on. Seating himself back into the seat he felt the pull of the engine as the helicopter skimmed along the ocean surface before landing on the helipad located at the stern of the ship. Slipping off the headgear Rush watched three men emerge from the black canvass dancing over them, their eyes shaded by expensive glasses and headgear. ‘Evening, gentleman,’ Rush said in his best Japanese, his attempts at civility ignored. ‘Follow me,’ the other responded in a shrill high pitched tone, his Japanese accent masked by a murky voice. Rush followed the men to the main gully of the ship, its belly. Tall oak doors welcomed him to the ornate, decorative and lavish bar room and conference setting of the ships lower deck. Various men of different origins sat comfortably around the ship, tended to by beautiful looking women, their eyes remaining fixated on the little cleavage shown. ‘May I get you a drink?’ Rush turned to see a short, but slender Japanese woman standing before him. She was dressed in a decorative kimono with delicate patterns and gilded displays of flowers and ornate Japanese customs. Her long brunette hair tied gently behind her in a bun, balanced only by a chopstick. Her slender tanned hands rested on the small serving tray as he studied her body, scanning from the legs up, till their eyes met. ‘Whiskey on the rocks,’ he said with a smile and watched her leave, his eyes lingering on her momentarily. A tall man emerged from behind Rush, a stone chiselled jaw accompanied with icy grey eyes and a recently shined bald scalp. His muscular stature almost bulging through his expensive black suit and dark red shirt as he outstretched his hand, resting it on Rush’s shoulder. ‘What you fancy?’ the man asked with a husky, deep English voice. ‘Excuse me?’ The words almost clogged in his throat as he turned to the chiselled man in front of him, their eyes meeting briefly. The man laughed whole-heartedly before grasping Rush’s shoulder and urging him forward toward a spare desk. ‘I believe you have something for me?’ Rush nodded his head, feeling hot around the collar as the nerves built, the adrenaline surging. He gripped the suitcase and turned to the man in front of him. Rush studied him a moment, the Cuban had mentioned little about his business partner, except for the cryptic name of ‘Broker’. In the corner of his eye Rush could see Bates, seated comfortably with a small amount of German and Austrian business men all being tended to by beautiful looking women. ‘First I need to see the shipment,’ Rush ordered, shoving the briefcase against his thigh. The Broker laughed, his jaw muscles tensing as he chewed on some tobacco. ‘You will see your shipment, when I see the documents’ Two men moved forward, arms folded in a threatening manner. Outstaying his welcome, Rush produced the briefcase and slid it along the table toward Broker, who remained standing, eyes fixed on Rush. The silence was parted by the presence of the Japanese woman, she slipped Rush a small glass of Whiskey, their eyes meeting briefly. ‘Thanks,’ Rush said politely and watched her leave. The Broker and the many business men within the bar all moved into a private room, taking the briefcase and documents with them. Bates emerged reluctantly from the grasp of a Japanese women and moved quickly toward Rush. ‘What’s going on?’ Bates asked in a low German accent. ‘How should I know,’ Rush responded whilst keeping an eye on the exit. ‘This was supposed to be quick, something’s not right,’ Bates said as he pulled out his silencer, gripping its handle. Beads of sweat began to flurry along Bates’s hairline, his muscles tense as he watched the conference room door. Rush moved forward, slipping his hands into his pockets. There was a small creek in the floor as Rush and Bates shifted their momentums. The Japanese woman inched forward, sliding the door across as he moved with a sturdy posture along the floor, picking up Rush’s full glass of whiskey. ‘Hope everything with the drink was alright?’ she said while gripping the glass quickly before she walked off in the opposite direction. Rush raised an eyebrow with a question on his mind, but the doors to the room opened and the Broker emerged, surrounded by disgruntled business men and his own goons. Bates turned around an image of displeasure cruised along his eyebrows and lips. Broker was holding crumpled sheets of documents as he inched forward. ‘What’s this?’ he asked. ‘Sorry?’ Rush questioned, bellowing over the arguing customers. Broker hurled the documents into the air and moved forward, gripping Rush’s shirt, tightening his first around the collar, creasing it under his grasp as he shoved Rush back against the edge of a mahogany table. ‘You playing some dumb American joke?’ Broker asked, his fowl breath sliding up Rush’s nostrils. Rush cringed with the smell of vodka and cigars as his throat ached for air. ‘I had nothin’ to do with it. I was just supposed to -’ Rush paused hearing the pop of a silencer as he watched one of Broker’s goons fall to the ground, the glint of an Uzi crashing to the metal covered floor. Bates stood in a firing position, legs spread and arms outstretched as he turned to the other goon and unloaded two rounds into the man’s head. The popping noise broke the grip as Rush quickly twisted his body, delivering a sharp upper cut, knocking Broker backwards. Rush jolted, the rubber on his shoes gripping the metal surface beneath as he hurled himself toward the bar, launching himself over the bar as bullets ricocheted against the many beverages’, broken glass falling on top of him as he covered his face whilst pulling out a small magnum concealed in his crotch area. Shifting his weight to avoid the broken pieces of glass, Rush lifted his head slightly and unloaded two rounds from his magnum, the harsh shattering of metal against metal reverberated around the room. ‘Go to the exit!’ Screamed a person as they shuffled along the floor whilst holding one hand on their head for some sort of protection. Bates was huddled behind a turned over desk, his breathing a frenzy of panting and nerves. The clip on his silencer almost empty as he watched the Japanese girl woman he liked hurry toward the exit, realising her predicament, Bates jumped to his feet and fired the rest of his round toward the remaining goons, providing cover for he and the small pale Japanese girl. ‘Quick, this way,’ Bates said, holding her hand. He felt a tug and turned toward her, her long black hair flowing loose around her shoulders, as she lunged a jagged knife toward his gut. Bates felt a tearing pain as he fell to his knees, blood trickling from his mouth as she pulled the knife upwards and out. A sadistic grin lined her lips as she watched him fall to the floor, dead. Rush snuck out the back exit and began hurling his tired legs up the stairs toward the main deck. Wind and rain clattered against the ships deck as he scurried behind a metal crate, two bullets fluttered past. As Rush turned around he watched a figure float by, unloading rounds into the belly’s of the attackers before turning to Rush, who now placed the gun level with the mysterious figure. ‘Who are you?’ he asked. ‘You don’t remember me?’ The female moved forward, now dressed in black combats and a tight wifebeater, instant recognition with Rush as a smile brimmed across his face, their eyes met briefly before a line of bullets collided with the metal tank. He dragged her behind him as he pointed toward the helicopter, still stationed on the helipad. ‘Quick, hurry…’ she ordered in a direct tone. Without a seconds guess, Rush followed the Japanese woman who’d offered him a whiskey toward the helicopter, providing cover fire. Anapa, Russia One week ago, along the pier Mirudo Montoya was seated eloquently on the seat dressed in a lavish suit ensemble; a long trench coat draped to his ankles shielded him from the droplets of rain and the harsh wind. His eyes remained focused on the tossing waves along the pier. The wind gently rustled his black hair as Pierce turned around, shaking in his t-shirt and jeans, the fluttering of rain droplets dancing along his skin. ‘What are we doing here?’ Pierce asked through the chattering of teeth. ‘To tell you the truth,’ Montoya spoke with a crisp Spanish accent. ‘That’d be fine if you’d tell me who the hell you are.’ Pierce stood back a slight bit, jolted by the thunder clattering overhead. The rain became heavier as he retreated under the small porch of the seating area, shielded by the plastic roofing. The pitter-patter of rain drowned out his murmuring heart and frilled nerves. ‘My name is Mirudo Montoya.’ ‘We’ve established that,’ Pierce spoke bluntly as he watched the tossing waves against the ice coloured cobbles along the rivers edge. Their was a lasting silence between them, neither man willing to offer the first word of clarity or reason, both seated in silence, stubborn and unrelenting in their approach to decent conversation. Conrad remained seated in the silver BMW, the classical stylings of Mozart played in the distance as he shuffled through the newspaper, staring mainly at the pictures making neither heads-nor-tails of the writing. ‘It’s not that simple,’ Montoya said, dipping his head slightly. ‘You bring me out here, to the middle of fucking nowhere and now you are having difficulty with your conscience,’ Pierce responded with an angered tone, the chattering of teeth loosening his wording, almost a slur. ‘I was a friend and business partner of your grandfather’s,’ Montoya started off, wrapping the ends of his jacket tightly around him as he felt the chilled air dancing against his skin. ‘I promised him I would look out for you as your godfather.’ ‘You’re my godfather?’ Pierce said as more of a rhetorical statement than question. ‘There was never any record of it or acknowledgement of it,’ Montoya spoke the words with a slow, silent voice. ‘I was a secret confidant of your grandfather’s and it was his wish that I look after you,’ Montoya reiterated his prior speech. ‘I got that, but why after all this time would you approach me now, here?’ ‘Because the conciliate members have taken a keen interest in John’s protégée,’ Montoya stood up now, shoving his hands into his pockets after fixing a loose strand of hair that dance loosely above his eyes. Pierce turned toward the car, the warmth and lights, yearning to sit in the lavish leather seats but yet he found himself seated on a cold hard stone slab, weaning words from a man of faint recollection. ‘My grandfather’s protégée? Oh, you mean my father; he took over my grandfather’s business when he passed on. If you want to know about that, ask him.’ Montoya leaned forward slightly. ‘Your father was nothing more than a minor replacement, he is not the heir.’ ‘Who is the heir then?’ Pierce questioned, suddenly feeling nausea rise within his bowls. ‘You are,’ Montoya spoke it bluntly, his Spanish accent sounding eerie against the clash of thunder. ‘That’s not a good thing. The Council are an elite group of extremely powerful men and you have sparked their interest.’ ‘Why am I on their radar?’ Pierce asked, patting his knees subconsciously, nerves kicking in. Montoya withdrew a small manila file from under his trench coat and handed it to Pierce, a quizzical look drawn along his lips as he extended a shaky hand and received the document, their eyes never meeting. ‘Because your grandfather established you to be,’ were Montoya’s words as he handed Pierce the file, sighing with a deep breath as he seated back down, lifting the collar of his coat against the wind. The small manila file opened up to reveal small packages of photos, signatures and notes on him. The words and images embedding themselves into his mind, grafting his fading truth as he slammed the file shut and shook off the thoughts of distrust. ‘What do you mean? My grandfather would never use me,’ came a squeaky tone as Pierce spoke with little reassurance or faith in his own words. ‘Everything you’ve learnt and know was a result of testing and training to join these alumni. From your fourth grade martial arts training right up to Prometheus, it was all a test, a leap of faith.’ Pierce rose up onto his feet, grappling at Montoya’s coat creasing it under his grasp as his breath became heavy. Montoya looked toward him, his gaze never wavering. ‘That’s not true,’ Pierce muttered, pulling tighter in his grasp. Before he had time to react, Pierce felt Montoya’s body shift, his legs spread as a sharp kick floated to his side, followed by a swift jab to his chest. Gasping for air, he shuffled backward, winded. Holding his stomach he looked up amazed at the man, stability and style to his presence. ‘Why are you here?’ Pierce asked, pleaded almost. ‘When your grandfather found out about his condition, perhaps a sudden wave of conscience hit him, he called me in and asked me to protect you from the council. He wanted me to teach you how to defend yourself. I guess in the end, he never meant to involve you in the council or the Prometheus experiment.’ Montoya extended his hand to Lavelle, helping him back up and patting him on the back as a gesture of reassurance and to help with his winded stomach and gasps of air. Pierce straightened up and remembered the cryptic letter he had received after his grandfather’s death, the words: ‘forgive me’, never ringing true till now. ‘What do the council want with me now?’ Pierce asked, turning to Montoya. ‘To finish what John had started, to sculpt you into his successor.’ Montoya stepped back, holding his hand toward the car. ‘And what’s your job?’ Pierce asked, sheepishly, his eyes bloodshot read. Montoya turned around, stopping in his quick and sturdy pace to face his godson as the morning’s sunshine rose in the distance, the feint glint of orange glazed along his eyes. ‘To protect you from them,’ Montoya responded and pointed toward the car. ‘How do you plan to do that?’ Pierce asked, shoving his hands into his pockets. ‘Through a note in your grandfather’s safety deposit box, I’ve got the key.’ ‘Safety deposit box?’ Montoya routed around his jacket pocket before his fingers latched onto a small silver object. Pierce watched as Montoya branded a small silver key, emblazoned with the initials J.H.L. Handing the key and a small business card over to Lavelle, Montoya watched the man’s eyebrows raise with bewilderment, there was so much he still had to learn. ‘When I told you everything, I was to give you this. Your grandfather’s last will and testament.’ Montoya turned toward the silver BMW stationed at the curb. Conrad emerged with a scurry in his pace, opening the back door for Montoya before heading toward the front of the car and slipping into the driver’s seat. Pierce watched as the car’s engine revved up before it jolted down the street toward the city. Turning his eyes toward the many dilapidated houses and his hotel he began a brisk walk toward the hotel, his mind rattled with questions. The key and Montoya’s business card held firmly within his grasp. Tokyo, Japan Helicopter landing strip, 20 miles inland Rush powered the engine as the propellers jolted the helicopter in the night sky, the young woman seated next to him as she typed in coordinates and speaking with a fluent Japanese tongue, eyes trained heavily on the landscape beneath them. His hand shook with the strength of the gale force winds as he kept the helicopter straight, going against the current. ‘There it is,’ she said in English and pointed to the yellow H in a small corn field. Gently slowing the engine down, Rush managed to safely land the helicopter down keeping the propellers a good distance from the safety gate surrounding them, the young woman clambered off the seat into the back of the plane while she reloaded her Uzi and slid into a Kevlar vest. ‘Thank you,’ he said, pulling on a bandage to cover the small wound he’d received while escaping in the plane. She moved forward with a piece of tape, their noses touching delicately as she placed the tape around the self-made bandage to keep it from falling enough, emerging from the back of the plane she pulled out another vest for Rush. ‘Thank me later, we aren’t out of the woods yet, literally,’ she said with a smile, her green eyes dazzling Rush as he slid into the vest. ‘Who are you?’ he asked, tightening the clasp on his gun before sliding it into his belt. ‘Since you saved my life and all,’ he said with a smug grin, charming the lady in front of him. ‘My name is Emi Natsuko,’ she said and turned toward him. ‘That’s a really nice name.’ Emi Natsuko turned to Rush, her eyes meeting his. ‘You ruined my undercover work,’ she said sharply and undid the sliding door dropping into the long grown field, perfect cover. Rush slipped out of the plane and followed her, his eyes remaining fixated on her body. ‘You chose to save me, I never asked.’ ‘Code of honour,’ she said with a slight smile, seating herself on a large boulder and removing her GPS system. ‘Where are we going?’ he asked. ‘Tokyo,’ she replied, slipping the gun into the inside of her Kevlar vest. ‘We must move quickly, we have made some powerful enemies. I’m sorry about your friend, but she was a trained assassin.’ ‘She was the one that discovered you?’ Rush asked, acknowledging the fact Bates’s body now lay deceased onboard the large vessel in the pacific ocean, but his concern lay for Emi - the beautiful Japanese woman that had saved his life. ‘She was very good, I’ll give her that. Come, we must go.’ Emi jolted up, swinging the Uzi over her shoulder as she clambered through the long grass. Rush followed feeling relaxed as he marched next to her. ‘You still owe me that whiskey,’ Rush said with a cunning smile as he saw the outskirts of Tokyo’s skyline emerging. London, England 7:00 a.m. Present Day The streets of London were a flurry of traffic, bottled necked at each junction as the early morning traffic began to overflow. Buses and taxis’ began bellowing their horns but having no avail on the roadwork’s or builders blockading most of Piccadilly. A long black vintage Rolls Royce moved forward along the back streets of London toward Brown’s Hotel. The luxurious Hotel lay right in the heart of Mayfair, its Georgian features distinct amongst the modern building’s as the Rolls Royce pulled up outside, the morning sunshine glinting against its polished roof and flamboyant emblem. Mirudo Montoya sat in the back, dressed in a cream suit and royal blue shirt. His chiselled jaw recently shaved and his black hair gelled and sleeked back out of his face as he stared toward the Hotel, the next meeting grounds for the council. ‘Shall I come with you, Sir?’ Conrad asked, seated eloquently in the front seat. Montoya stretched forward, placing a reassuring hand on Conrad’s shoulder. ‘No need, I’ll make this quick.’ Brown’s Hotel is set in Mayfair, its exquisite and sophisticated English style interior, left Montoya in awe as he marvelled at the Georgian architecture and interior design of the hotel. The main foyer gave way to the marbled, well furnished conference room held in the back of the hotel and reserved for stopover business clients. ‘Kind of you to join us, Mr. Montoya.’ Came a well spoken, refined English voice as Montoya pried open the large oak doors. The room before him was modern, well lit and empty except for the familiar faces of Fleming and Ciccone. Shutting the door he turned around, his muscles tensed as he felt a hand on his shoulder. ‘Traffic was awful,’ Montoya spoke, his throat clenched. The hand behind him pushed him forward slightly. Fleming moved from the small bar area holding a chardonnay in one hand and dressed in a lavish white suit and black shirt. Ciccone remained seated, his overweight posterior making it awkward for movement, as he puffed on a cigar and yawned into the morning sunshine. ‘Drink?’ Fleming asked, extending his hand in a gesture toward the bar. ‘Don’t drink, but thanks,’ Montoya stood in the middle of the floor. Ciccone’s Italian features accentuated his love for wine and fine cuisine as he began to munch on a full English breakfast. Fleming sat down and slid a manila file toward Montoya. ‘How far are you with the boy?’ Fleming asked. Montoya leaned forward, his hands gripping the manila file as his eyes remained on Fleming and Ciccone, a force of habit as he didn’t trust or like the men before him. ‘You’ve damaged him,’ Montoya spoke bluntly. ‘Damaged him?’ Ciccone spoke, black pudding spluttering out of his mouth as he spoke, droplets of chewed pudding falling to the wooden desk beneath him. ‘His trust has always been thin with strangers,’ Fleming said with an assured tone. Montoya opened the manila file and saw a small composite document on Pierce Lavelle, each note a simple statement of the tests and theory’s the council had on him, more documents. A wave of pity and worry for the young man coursed through Montoya as he gritted his teeth together. ‘How much does he know?’ Ciccone asked, smoking his cigar. Montoya stayed silent a moment, his mind battling with the wavering truth of his morals, a profound interest in the boy now swayed him of his loyalties to the council. ‘Quite a bit, but his refusal to join is evident.’ ‘What do you mean he’s not going to join? We made him’ Fleming bellowed chucking his glass of chardonnay against the opposite wall, the liquid trickling down the white washed walls. ‘Prometheus and the truth of his grandfather has hurt the boy, he won’t join.’ ‘You lying bastard,’ Ciccone shot up, the chair flinging backwards, the table shaking under his weight. ‘You haven’t told him anything.’ Montoya stood back a moment, his eyes widening with expectation of what was to come. ‘This true, Montoya?’ Fleming asked, calming himself down. ‘Does it matter? I won’t let you use the boy.’ Montoya remained dominant and respective in his approach to the council leaders, his voice echoing against the walls of the room. Fleming moved forward, his eyes lingering on Montoya. ‘You are obligated to this Council, Montoya. If you don’t do as we say, then you will face the repercussions,’ Fleming’s eyes narrowed as his threatening tone oozed out of his lips, the well spoken English accent fading momentarily. ‘You will be punished for your insolence, Montoya.’ Ciccone waved his hand and the man behind Montoya moved forward, Montoya spread his legs apart just enough for him to spin sharply as he dodged the coming punch and threw a roundhouse kick toward the back of his attackers head. The man collided with the table before tumbling to the floor, his eyes rolling into the back of his head. Montoya straightened his suit jacket and turned back toward Fleming and Ciccone, each with a surprised look on their faces. Montoya realised he would need to go about protecting Pierce in his own way; the Council would be ruthless in their quest for their heir. ‘You will change the boy’s interest, Montoya,’ Fleming ordered. ‘As you wish,’ Montoya said with a hushed tone, his eyes foreboding a truth that remained hidden. Allied Bank, Los Angeles Quick stop-over The bank was a hive of business, people dashing through the swirling doors, dressed in the impeccable styles of most of the economic class within Los Angeles’s business district. Scurrying through the crowds, dressed in jeans and a black shirt, Pierce moved slowly, quietly, unnoticed by the frantic crowds. The Allied Bank was a cold, marble building, inlaid with high-tech security systems and excessive amounts of security guards. Its large bank was respectable as the best storage for the rich and famous that lined the hills of Beverly and Bel-Air. Immediately upon his arrival, Pierce withdrew the silver key and walked to the ‘customer service’ desk. A slim lady sat disgruntled in front of him, phone pressed into her ear as she argued on the other end, her cheeks a rosy red while she cranked up the air-conditioning unit. ‘I will not, there is no way I can give you a loan…’ Slamming down the receiver she discretely placed a loose strand of hair behind her head and looked toward Pierce, his eyes staring toward the key in his hand. ‘I need to see the deposit box for this key,’ Pierce said, extending his hand. The lady nodded her head, stood up and scurried along the floor toward a small stairs in which Pierce followed, leading to a large room of storage and private booths for customers to view their items. Slicing a key card into a socket she opened the door and moved toward a small box. ‘Here you are, Sir. Take your time and buzz me when you’re finished.’ She left the key attached to the opened door and moved out of the room. Placing his hands firmly around the box he moved to a small red velvet booth and slid the box onto the table. He sat in silence a moment, pondering the contents of the box. The lid lifted with the gentlest of touch. Inside were sheets of paper, photos, jewellery, keys and more documents. Lifting all the contents out of the box he stared at the photo of his grandfather and himself, placed it to the side and lifted the sheets, each addressed to him. THE COUNCIL INHERITANCE PROMETHEUS Placing all three documents into his back pocket he slid the rest of the contents back into the box, shut it and slid it into its socket whilst he buzzed the lady who returned swiftly. ‘Thank you,’ he muttered and headed up the stairs, the documents bunched into his back pocket. Moving toward the rental car he slipped into the driver’s seat and placed all three documents into his rucksack before heading out into the congested vacuum of rush hour. Pierce strode across the street, the rucksack slung over his shoulder as he approached the car in waiting, the street lamps bulb fluttered overhead till he stood in complete darkness, surrounded by the vast emptiness of the night, a void murky sky above him. ‘Now do you understand?’ a deep voice spoke, his eyes watching Pierce from the darkened car. Pierce looked toward the man, their eyes meeting briefly. Wiping the tears from his cheeks he felt a clog of pain well up in his throat as he nodded toward the man. Slight recognition coursing through the two as they seated themselves in the backseat, the car drifting in the night, a shadow, a blimp, a minor light before it vanished. Harrisburg, Pennsylvania 5:00 p.m. Williamsburg Estates The gates to Williamsburg Estates watched him with daunting eyes, ominous in his approach, the circles on the wrought iron gates casting a foreboding gaze toward him as he crossed the street. Dressed in a change on clothing, he rubbed the stubble along his cheeks and moved with a sturdy posture toward his home. The gates moved electrically as he scurried past and walked briskly against the evening’s sunshine toward his home, set delicately in the distance. The house in Williamsburg Estate was dark; it’s windows as empty as a dead man’s eyes. The house, or otherwise known as apartment to some, was an old style Californian Craftsman with a full front porch, to welcome the visitors and two dormer windows set on the long slope of the roof. He approached the cobbled stones with a stagger in his step, watching the small crack that still remained in the dented porch. ‘Welcome back.’ Pierce looked up to see Lois perched against the door frame dressed in jeans, a tight t-shirt and a red zip jumper, a large smile along her lips, completely healed from the incident in Los Angeles. The familiarity of his home allowing him to remove an ice pack from the freezer and head into the living area. Marie lay on the sofa, eyes closed whilst the television played in the background, the daily weather report. He turned toward his mother and saw the bruise now forming on her cheek. I promise. Just a business arrangement, its all settled.’ ‘Are you sure, because your father seemed desperate,’ Marie said bluntly, turning to see Rebecca now watching the television. ‘I told you to change the locks; he’s always been desperate and dangerous.’ Pierce folded his arms and stood up, turning toward the car parked outside the house. ‘You can’t always protect us, Pierce,’ Marie said as she watched her son walk toward the hall. Pierce opened the door and walked out into the street, the car had its engine running. The tinted windows covered the figure in a darkened shroud as Pierce squinted for recognition. Get in.’ Obliging to his father’s wishes, Pierce shuttled around the side of the car and slid into the passenger seat. The engine roared as Bradley hit it into first gear and then into second, charging down the street and out into the evening’s sunshine, Pierce seated in the passenger seat with the urge for revenge, but concealed his feelings as he watched the skyline flutter past.
