Tire Go Flat?

6 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
An ash-blue painted Chevy pick-up sat in the driveway of her mother’s home. Teresa stepped out of the front door of the old farmhouse with two fishing poles and a dark green tackle box. She carefully placed both in the bed of the step-side and turned back toward the house. She wore cut-off denim shorts, a light blue tank top and a pair of sneakers with no socks. Eric leaned against a wooden post on the front porch and watched his baby sister unimpressed. His hands were stuffed the front pockets of his dark green cargo pants. The grey t-shirt he wore read ‘ARMY’. “I don’t like this Teresa. You just up and leaving?” Teresa walks out with a minnow bucket and a stringer. She rolled her eyes as she passed her brother and tossed them into the bed of her new, well new to her, pick-up truck. Her court hearing went better than expected. Due to lack of evidence and the fact the only witnesses were her and Eric, the judge dropped the case and warned Teresa next time she fired a gun, it better be at a target or game and not a human. “Relax Eric. It’s over. I don’t owe anybody shit, except you of course. I mean, if you hadn’t done your investigation on Bob and worked with the FBI like you did, I’d probably be dead. If not now, it would’ve been a matter of time.” It was early morning, about 7am or so. The forecast for the day calls for sunny skies and warm temperatures throughout the day. It had rained the night before and is the perfect time to load up and head to the lake. Eric knows this. He’s not much of the fisherman. Football was always his sport of choice. He also knows his sister and when she has it in her mind to go fishing, no one will stop her. “I’m not going to always be around to save your ass ya know. Just a few weeks ago, some one snuck in your hotel room and tried to shoot you. What does that tell you?” “I’m not an idiot Eric. Someone wants me dead. It scares the shit out of me, but I’m not letting that stop me from doing what I want. What you expect me to do? Lock myself up in isolation? C’mon, I got the best birthday present ever Friday, freedom!” Eric releases a deep sigh. Teresa is as hard headed as they come. Even as a girl, not even their Uncle Tom’s mules were as stubborn as Teresa Tomas. As he watches her climb into her classic truck and back it up next to her fishing boat, he thinks to himself not even Satan himself could prevent her from fishing today. “What ya doin?” Teresa asked Eric when he placed two more fishing poles and a couple life jackets in the bed of her truck. He hands Teresa a camouflaged boonie hat and placed an orange and white University of Tennessee cap over his sun-blonde hair before sliding into the passenger seat. “I’m goin’ with you. Got a problem with that?” “I don’t reckon, but you’re baiting your own hooks and if by some miracle you catch any, you’re handling your own fish.” Behind a thicket of bushes across the road, a beige Ford Escort was hidden. The driver lowered his binoculars and sat back. He had followed Teresa home once he read about her head injury. Just like any wounded animal, they immediately seek a safe place either to die or heal. Unfortunately for Teresa, she will experience both for reasons that are none of his concern. All that concerns the man is his job and he has been watching her all morning. It would’ve been too easy to sneak into her bedroom window the night before and take Teresa out quickly and quietly with the use of a silencer, however, he was given specific instructions for a slow and painful death. This fishing trip would be perfect. “Aww hell!” A semi truck sped by on the highway. Teresa and Eric knelt by the left rear tire which was quite flat staring at it as if watching it long enough, the air would return. They were able to roll the truck to the side of the road. The morning hours were slipping nearly as fast, it seemed, as the speeding tractor trailer. Teresa stood up and kicked loose gravel from the side of the road as her cursing continued. Eric stood as well and dug in his pocket for his cell. “Shit.” “No signal?” “Nope, you sure you don’t have a spare?” “The truck didn’t come with one and I hadn’t got around to buying one yet.” Eric stared impatiently at his sister. She of all people would have the brains enough to carry a spare tire. “Great! What we gonna do now? We can’t just leave this boat on the side of the road like this. Someone’ll steal it. Another semi truck sped by leaving a whoosh of air that flew Teresa’s long hair off her shoulders. She grabbed a pack of Winston Light from the dash of the truck and dug a red lighter from her front pocket. After she exhaled her first puff, she grabbed the camouflaged boonie hat Eric had given her earlier and put it on. “Fine, you stay here with the damn boat. I’ll hitch a ride and buy a spare. Don’t need a lug wrench or a jack. They’re behind the seat.” Before Eric could oppose, Teresa already was walking away with her thumb in the air. Going after her would be useless. She would be no safer if she stayed and he went. Several yards along, a beige Ford Escort slows down and Eric watched as Teresa stepped inside the car and is driven away. He reached in the bed of the truck, and grabbed a beer from the cooler.
