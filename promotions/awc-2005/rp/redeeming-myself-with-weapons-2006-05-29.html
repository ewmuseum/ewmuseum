Redeeming Myself...with Weapons

29 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-29-06 Story I My Worst Nightmare I am back on that rainy day in late 1979. Everything is in black and white. Why am I here I can’t figure out. During this time, my brother Thomas and I would be heading to the delicatessen to pick up sandwiches for dinner, but no one is walking on the semi-torrent rainfall. I smell something in the air, an affluent smell, a smell I can’t pinpoint. The odorous stench is all around me, hanging in the air like a grotesque miasma. The stench is stronger now, filling my nostrils and perhaps seeping into my skin. The more I smell this stench, the more I’m familiar with it. It’s the same smell that has given me nightmares for numerous years... It’s the smell of blood. A gunshot booms overhead. It reverberates inside my head and shakes my body raw. I look around, trying to determine where the gunshot came from. There is nothing; no one is on the muddy road. I turn my head to the left to see a man dressed in black standing over a prone, motionless body of a child, perhaps eleven, twelve years of age. I watch in horror when I realize who the gunman is. The man pulls down his hood to reveal a bald head and green eyes. The man’s face is chiseled and handsome, and has a sinister demeanor. He places his gun in what appears to be a trench coat and looks over at me, smiling, actually smiling, and that’s when I can identify the gunman... It’s Chainz... Mike “Chainz” Sloan. His evil grin shakes me to the bone. He says something inaudible, but I can read his lips. He says “See you in Hell, O’Reilly,” and simply walks off, as if nothing happened. I run after Chainz, yelling at him. When I realize nothing is coming out of my mouth, it’s too late. I reach out to grasp Chainz, yelling my inaudible obscenities at him, but he is already gone. He disappears before I can get a hold of him, evaporating into evanescence. His diabolical laughter fills my head. Damn him. Damn him to the deepest pits of Hell. With Chainz’s damning laughter slowly dissipating, I walk over to the motionless body of the small boy. To my horror, I’m staring down at my brother Thomas, arms akimbo and legs splayed. His eyes are closed. There’s a bullet wound in his chest, oozing out blood from the wound. I drop to my knees, tears brimming in my eyes, as I look down at my dead brother. To my surprise, Thomas’ eyes snap open, and he turns his head to face me. I half fall, half leap backward, stunned at what I had just seen. This can’t be happening. My brother is dead. This must be some trick. Yeah, it’s a trick. My mind is playing tricks on me is all. I rub my eyes, telling myself that my brother is indeed dead and my mind is indeed fucking with me. I look down at my brother, half-expecting him to have his eyes closed and his head turned in the opposite direction. He would remain looking peaceful, even in death... Boy was I fucking wrong... Thomas is still looking up at me, eyes staring at me accusingly, as if it is my that he died. For several years I did blame myself for this death. If I hadn’t gone along with him, he may not have died. Now he’s looking up at me, accusing me with his eyes. His mouth can’t move, but I can hear his voice, his twelve-year-old voice... Why... Why did you let me die? My head starts to hurt. I get to my feet, clutching my head as it fills with unwanted memories... Images of Thomas being buried underneath his favorite tree, two hundred yards east of my childhood house. A small service, inviting family and friends; a small eulogy; eyes blaming me for his death... Why did you let him die, Michael?! WHY?! Images of my mother dead in the bathtub, throat and wrists slit with a plastic bag over her head. There was a smile on her face upon discovery, both peaceful, yet mocking, as if it’s trying to teach us a lesson... Images of my father going through his drinking binges; malicious assaults administered on me; my remaining sisters turning their back on me, not helping me in my time of need. Images of my attempted suicide, a dream that almost became a reality... I can’t escape from these images. Now they’re playing in distorted random, showing me what I had done. But I didn’t do anything wrong! None of this was my fault... Sounds of laughter fill my head again... It’s a familiar laughter, something I know all too well. Feeling a presence behind me, I slowly turn around. There he stands, Mike “Chainz” Sloan, looking down at me with a smug, evil look on his face. His laughter is stronger now, painful and deafening. I drop to my knees, clutching my head, wanting the suffering to stop... Chainz can fulfill my wish... He points a gun at my face. I stare inside barrel, staring into the void of inevitability. There is a flash in front of my face, accompanying by a loud BOOM! Everything goes black. As I die, I hear Chainz’s voice filling my head. He only has five words to tell me... See you in Hell, O’Reilly! I awoke with a jerk, sitting up ramrod straight. There was a cold sheet of sweat on my forehead and a cold chill running through by body. I looked around, half-expecting that I’m still on that muddy road back in 1979. Realizing that I’m in my bed with my fiance, I let out a sigh of relief. “It was all a dream,” I said out loud, then laid back down, trying to shake the dream that I had. I feel movement next to me. Stacy rolled over, looking over at me. “Are ye okay, Mike?” she asked. When she got a better look at me, her eyes widen with concern. “Mike, yer as white as a sheet, are ye all right?” I nodded, giving her a kiss on her cheek. “I just had a nightmare is all.” “What about?” “I don’t really remember now,” I lied, looking Stacy in the eye. “It’s not a big deal... really.” Stacy didn’t looked convinced. “Whatever dream ye had, it scared ye pretty good it looks like.” “It did. But it’s over now. Let’s go back to sleep.” Too tired to pursue the matter, Stacy gave me a kiss on the cheek and rolled over, going back to sleep. I laid awake, reliving the dream in fragments, and hearing Chainz’s damning laughter. When the laughter finally stopped, I slowly drifted into a fitful sleep. Story II Health Scare I need to get ready for this Weapons Match against Chainz. In two short days, I’ll be in another war with that delusional, psychopathic monster. The last time Chainz and I were in a match was nearly three months ago at Twilight of the Gods in a brutal, graphic Sadist Match. It was a match I had lost, getting Chainz out of his losing slump and on top of the AWC scene. All I get are multiple scars on my forehead, multiple scars all over my body, a grade three concussion and three days in the hospital. I go to my local gym, exercise like a madman, hit punching bags, wrestling training with Zsasz - the usual two-hour training regiment I have every morning. Zsasz puts me through hell like he does every morning for the past six years, sculpting me into a wrestling machine. He would insult me, belittle me and throw me off my game. He would use anything to get the upper hand on me, the way any of your opponents would while competing in the ring. Zsasz was no different. Zsasz would get into my head with underhanded tactics, exploiting weaknesses, and capitalizing on them. He would give me notes whilst kicking the shit out of me in the ring. Gotta love mentors who beat you up and then tell you about it. It’s degrading, but it works. I would surprise Zsasz with some dirty tactics of my own. He would look at me, bewildered, then applaud me. “You’ve been learning well,” he once said to me. With all the nonsense out of the way, we would have exhibition matches against one another. This would further hone my wrestling skills against those I would face in the ring. When I face Zsasz in an exhibition match, I’m lucky to actually walk about of the ring. His seventeen years of experience and his new “Sadistic Brutality” style does take a lot out on me. Zsasz’s Sadistic Brutality wrestling style consisted on an exquisite blend of power, grappling and submissions. Zsasz is also a master in psychological warfare, playing a flawless game of human chess against his victims. His underhanded trickery can also play a valuable role in the ring. I mean, with an I.Q. over two hundred, one needs to use his intelligence against the muscle-bound Neanderthals that face you in the ring. Zsasz’s sadism against weaker opponents really show in the ring, administering brutal beatings until Zsasz decides to finish said opponent off, hence the name “Sadistic Brutality.” With my reverie getting the best of me, I find myself laying flat on my back, looking up at the ceiling of the gym. My body is completely racked with pain as Zsasz stood over me, grinning from ear to ear. “I never thought I would enjoy dismantling a student who hardly lifted a finger in defense,” Zsasz said. He crouched down, helped me up and kept me on my feet. I was sore from head to toe. Having no memory of what happened in our exhibition match, I looked at Zsasz quizzically. “What happened? How did I do?” Zsasz scoffed at me, as if I was the biggest imbecile on the planet. “I brutalized you in this ring, and I relished it, I might add. How you did was that you did nothing. You didn’t lift a finger to do anything to me and didn’t lift a finger to defend yourself while I annihilated you.” He smiled, realizing why him manhandling me was so simple. “Your mind wondered, didn’t it?” I stood there, guilty as charged. I nodded, flushing a bit. “You were going through one of your reveries, weren’t you?” Again, I nodded, flushing deeper. I felt about two inches tall, being dominated by a giant, waiting to be squashed underneath his foot. Zsasz simply chuckled. “You get into the habit of doing that too much in our training. You should put an end to that habit. It isn’t smart when your mind wonders in a match. You’ll get destroyed in the ring, much like you were destroyed today and not realize it.” His gaze went over to a wall with a clock mounted on it. It read 10:18 am. “Our training time is up. Hit the showers and go home. I’ll see you bright and early tomorrow.” I gave him a nod and exited the ring. I went into the shower room, showered, dried off, put a fresh pair of clothes and went home. By the time I got home, I was in a state of tranquility. I was in a place where I could relax and not have a care in the world. I lay on the couch, flipping through the channels on the television as Stacy walked in, with a tall glass of iced tea in one hand and a sandwich in the other. I smiled, thanked her by giving her a kiss on the lips and sat up, eating my sandwich and drinking my iced tea. She sat down next to me; I can smell her perfume, which aroused me. Stacy knew it by the devilish smile on her face. Yeah, I was that obvious. “So, what do ye want t’ do after havin’ yer sandwich?” she asked, giving me a seductive wink. I tried to play dumb, but with the growing erection in my pants couldn’t be stopped. Having an “afternoon delight” of sorts always sounds like a great idea. “I’ll meet you upstairs in a couple of minutes, dear,” I replied. Stacy giggled like a schoolgirl, got up from the couch and left the living room. With my sandwich finished, I downed my iced tea and stood up. As I stood up, I felt a little dizzy and the whole room began to spin. Perhaps I stood up too quickly. Yeah, that was it, I stood up too quickly. I made my way toward the kitchen and the feeling hit me again. To accompany my dizziness, my stomach started to turn, as if I was going to throw up. My vision blurred. “What is going...” The next thing I know was that I collapsed on the floor, hearing the glass break as it shattered on the floor. I couldn’t move, I couldn’t talk... I couldn’t do anything. Was I going to die? As darkness swallowed me up, I heard Stacy running toward me, calling my name. “Your stress level is through the roof, Mike.” I’m sitting in a hospital room after going through a gambit of tests. I had no recollection how I got here or much of anything else for that matter. All I remember was that I collapsed at home, heard Stacy running toward me, calling my name and...that was it. Now I’m here, looking at Doctor Timothy Kennedy, who had a concern look on his face. When he told me that my stress level was through the roof, I looked at him quizzically. How could I be stressed out? I felt fine. I had some minor dizziness, stomach issues and blurred vision. That couldn’t be part of my stress, could it? Well, all of that caused me to collapse in my home, so that had to be the instigator of this whole debacle. “I’m surprised that you’re not dead, Mike. As much stress you have now, it could’ve killed you. Would you mind telling me how you got all this stress?” I couldn’t think of a reasonable explanation why I would be in such bad health. It didn’t really make much sense to me. “To be honest with you, Tim, I don’t have an idea why I would be under a lot of stress. I feel fine.” “Well, you’re not fine if you collapsed in your home an hour ago. Stacy filled me in on what could be the problem: your wrestling career, your wedding in a month and your rigorous training. Could any of those be indicators of your stress?” Now that he mentioned it, my wrestling career had been rather stressful lately. I’d been losing some big matches, didn’t get my Relentless title shot a few weeks back and now I’m in a Weapons Match against Chainz. Wedding plans had been stressing me out as well because this would be my second time getting married and Stacy’s first time. My last marriage failed miserably and I don’t want this to be a repeat performance. With those two things stressing me out, I go to the gym every morning to alleviate that stress by exercising and sharpening my skills in the ring. I never thought this stress would get the best of me. Today it evidently did. “Yeah, my career has been stressful, going out there on a weekly basis and perform can get the best of you. And with my wedding coming up next month, I guess that’s stressing me out too. Like I said, I felt fine until I collapsed in my home.” Timothy Kennedy gave me a nod. “Tell me about your training. What do you do every morning?” I explained to Tim what I did on my daily routine each morning. He arched his brows when I was finished. “Wow, that’s a lot to do in two hours,” he said. “I know, it relieves my stress and keeps me in shape.” “Obviously not enough,” Doctor Kennedy replied dryly. “And with your aggressive training schedule, you’ll end up with heart failure and die by the time you’re forty. You should back off on your training.” He paused for a moment, as if he remembered something. “And I advise that you don’t show up at the Czech Republic on the twenty-third for FRESH! I gave my doctor a double take. “May I ask why?” “In the shape that you’re in now, you’d die if you had your Weapons Match against Chainz. Push back your match until next week, indefinitely, I don’t care. The fact of the matter is that you’re not going to Prague in two days!” “Okay, I’ll take that under advisement. Besides, I could use a vacation. Is there anything I need to know before I go?” Doctor Kennedy shook his head. “Nope. You’re free to go. Just take it easy this week.” With that said, I leave the hospital. Stacy was waiting for me to take me home. Story III Forgive Me, Brother With Timothy Kennedy giving me the week off, I thought I would fly out to Belfast to pay my respects for my brother Tomas. The dream I had about him a few nights ago told me I had unresolved issues. Thomas had blamed me for his death, when I was too young to save him. I was only six years when it happened, and he was almost twelve. What was I to do, fight someone who was at least three times my age, three times my size and meet the same fate as my brother? I needed to tell my brother I’m sorry... I needed my brother to forgive me... The thought of flying out to Belfast to ask for my dead brother’s forgiveness does sound ridiculous, but it’s something I need to do. Can a person ask forgiveness from someone who’s been dead for twenty-seven years? It would be almost three months since the last time I was in Belfast. The last time I was there, I wasn’t to solve some unresolved issues with my father. Now it’s time to solve some unresolved issues with my brother. “Are ye sure tha’ ye need t’ go t’ Belfast?” Stacy asked, watching me pack a suitcase. “I don’t think Tim would approve.” “I have to go, Stace,” I replied. “I have more unresolved issues with my family. I need to go out there and confront my brother, Thomas.” Stacy looked at me in bewilderment. Confronting his brother Thomas? He’s been dead for nearly thirty years. “This is crazy, Michael. Ye can’t resolve anything with Thomas. He’s dead! What are ye gonna accomplish with all of this? Mike, I worry about you.” I finished packing my suitcase. “I don’t know,” I replied, closing my suitcase and snapping it shut. “Maybe if I see his tombstone I may feel better. All I know is that I need to do this. I’ll see you in a few days.” I turn to give her a kiss. “Don’t worry about me, Stacy, I’ll be fine.” Turning away from her, I pick up my suitcase and leave the bedroom. “I’ll call you when I get to Belfast,” I said, turning to face my fiance. I went downstairs and left the house. Helpless, Stacy McFarland watched me go. Tears filled her eyes as I loaded myself into the backseat of a taxi. It was a cold day in Belfast, Ireland, the capital of Northern Ireland. The sky was leaden with clouds; not a trace of sunshine could be seen. I’ve been rehearsing in my mind what I should say to my brother, wondering how I should approach this matter. The only option is to go to his tombstone, pay his respects, tell him how I felt and then leave. I walked two hundred yards east of my childhood home, towards Thomas’ favorite tree. The tree was on top of a hill where Thomas and I used to play, along with the rest of my brothers and sisters. We would invite friends to see who would roll down the hill the fastest, play King of the Hill, all sorts of silly games as children. Thomas once told me that if he ever died, he wanted to be buried underneath this tree. On that dreadful day back on 1979, he was killed. That night he was buried underneath that tree and had a small service the next morning. Thomas had gotten his wish, but his wish was fulfilled too soon. He died too young; he still had a life to live. Children weren’t supposed to die at eleven years of age. I stand over Thomas’ tombstone. It was weathered, worn and chipping away. The front of the tombstone was barely legible, but I could still make out what the script said... THOMAS R. FARREN OCT. 8 1967 - SEPT. 27 1979 HE WILL BE MISSED. I let out a sigh, wondering where to start. There were so many things to talk about, but where to start? What could there be to talk about? Fuck it; might as well wing it. “Hey, Tommy,” I said, crouching down in front of the tombstone, placing my hand on it. It felt cold to the touch; it sent chills up and down my spine. “How’s it goin’?” That was a stupid question, Mike. He’s been dead for twenty-seven years! That’s how it’s goin’. Letting out another sigh, I try again. “I’m sorry about everything that has happened to you, Tommy. But how dare you blaming me for your death twenty-seven years ago? It wasn’t my fault that you died!” I straightened myself up, pulling a pack of cigarettes out of my jacket pocket, took one out and lit up. I inhaled the cancerous smoke into my lungs then exhaled. “For the longest time, I did blame myself for your death. My parents blamed me, brothers and sisters blamed me - even my friends - our friends - blamed me. I’ve asked for forgiveness for twenty-seven years and no one would reply. No one would forgive me. Now I’ve been having these nightmares, about Mother killing herself, about you getting killed and about our father beating me within an inch of my life!” I took another drag of my cigarette, being overcome with a plethora of emotions - anger, sadness, regret, all sorts of emotions. “Perhaps I did deserve the beatings I got from my father, everyone accusing me of your death, but I don’t deserved getting blamed by you, even in death. I did not cause your death; some asshole with a gun caused your death. All I could...” I trailed off, choked up on tears. “All I could do was hold you in my arms and watched you die. When I came home, all hell broke loose. I never wanted to see you die. If it were up to me, I would take your place and let you live...” I started to cry, realizing how much I missed my brother. Out of the six brothers and sisters I have, Thomas was my favorite brother. Don’t get me wrong, I love all my siblings equally, but Thomas and I had a closer relationship, and we both got along extremely well. “Please forgive me, Tommy... Please...” Much to my surprise, the clouds parted and the sun began to shine. This must be a sign. I looked down at my brother’s tombstone, smiling brightly at it. “Thank you, Tommy.” With that said, I walked away from Thomas’ tombstone. After my visit with my brother, a heavy burden had been lifted off my shoulders.
