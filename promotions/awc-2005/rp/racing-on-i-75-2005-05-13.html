Racing on I-75

13 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 13th, 2005 I-75 In which Alex drives to Atlanta, while Pamela tries to sort out her own troubles back in Pittsburgh. *************** “Get out of the way, you stupid bastard! No, not you… I was talking to some idiot who’d driving in the other lane,” Alex said into his wireless Bluetooth cell-phone headset. “You were saying?” Alex Strider is a busy man with a lot on his mind. Perched in his diamond white Cadillac Escalade EXT driving north on I-75, Strider is racing as much literally as he is figuratively. Alex, though usually deft at multitasking, was having a bit of trouble maintaining a conversation on his cell phone as he sped along the busy highway at 90 Miles Per Hour in his gas guzzling SUV/TRUCK Hybrid, weaving in and out between the vacationers and semi trucks. It didn’t help any that he was switching repeatedly from drinking his bottled iced-tea, flipping from track to track on his 60 gigabyte I-Pod, while toying with his GPS system all at the same time. These are the things that are important to Alex. “Hold that thought,” Alex said as he rolled his window down. Sticking his head out the window as he peered down on a lime green station wagon with wood paneling, he screamed, “HEY! YOU STUPID OLD MAN! GET THE FUCK OUT OF THE FAST LANE! AWC TRANSATLANTIC CHAMPION ALEX STRIDER HERE! You’re holding me up!” Alex screamed down at the middle aged man carting his family back home to Ohio, displaying no regard for the impressionable children sitting in the backseat as they stared back, wide-eyed and open mouthed. Alex held up his championship belt for them to see, nodding to let them know he wasn't lying. The station wagon slowed down and allowed Strider to pass. “Okay, I’m back now… Hey, did you know it’s supposed to be a seven-hour drive from Kissimmee to Atlanta? That’s crazy, isn’t it?” Alex said as he referenced his in-dash DVD/GPS system. “I don’t know, Alex. I’ve never been south of West Virginia.” Pamela Saunders sighed on the other end of the phone conversation. She was having a bit of trouble on her own. While Strider drove, she sat in her cramped cubicle in the “New Man’s” Magazine office. She was dizzy from the smell; stale from cigar smoke and coffee, thanks to the cubicle’s former occupant. She was trying in vain to edit her interview, which was now overdue thanks to her involvement in the incident at Strider’s apartment the week before. Alex had called her back to ask her a few important questions pertaining to the case. “Look, Alex, I’d love to talk right now, but my boss is really breathing down my neck. I am still editing the article with your interview in it, and I have a new assignment I’m supposed to be researching. Can we catch up in a few hours?” “Nope, that’s no good for me. We’ll have to talk now, when I get into Atlanta I’ve got to get in touch with… some people. About some…things,” Alex said dubiously. ”Well, as long as you’ll be busy doing something…important,” Pamela said sarcastically as she continued typing on her computer. She heard Strider honking his horn repeatedly. “Is something wrong?” She asked, out of genuine concern, she had seen his reckless driving first hand. “No, everything’s fine. Oh, hey, what did you think of the Pay Per View?” Alex asked, taking another swig of iced-tea. ”I didn’t catch it all, actually. When you skipped out, they called me back down to the station to answer…more questions about you. Which was a lot of fun, thanks for asking,” Pamela said, her patience being tested as she become more and more aggravated at the small case of writer’s block preventing her from perfecting her article. “Yeah, I felt terrible that you had to be dragged into it. I’ll send you some flowers,” Alex said. Pamela had difficulty telling if he was being sincere or not. “But you did watch the Pay Per View, right?” “I fast forwarded to the parts you were in. What came of it?” “What came of it? What came of it?” Alex repeated as he changed lanes without signaling for the hundredth time that day, the protestation of horns from offended drivers growing louder every minute. “What I think you meant to say was: ‘Congratulations, Alex! Your matches were impressive, and you really deserved to become the AWC Transatlantic Champion.'” ”Congratulations Alex. Your matches were impressive,” she said sardonically, “when you weren’t running away from your opponent or spitting on defenseless referees.” “Ho Ho HO! You’ve got JOKES now! You know, they said they were making robots that would learn human behavior, but I didn’t think they’d have come out so soon, Pamela.” Alex paused for the laughter, but Pamela neither heard the joke, nor would she have found it funny if she were paying attention. “But All kidding aside now,” Alex said, the tone in his voice changing to a more serious level. “You’ve got to help me. You know I had nothing to do with that girl’s death.” “What do you want me to do, Alex? You didn’t help yourself when you ran away like that.” Pamela stood up, sliding her computer keyboard under her monitor. She was through trying, distraction winning out over responsibility. “Yeah, I got to experience that first hand. But the good news is, they got the toxicology report back, and my lawyer says that it’s only a matter of time before I’m cleared completely. They’ll rule out foul play soon enough.” “Sounds great, Alex. I guess you don’t really need my help after all.” ”Wrong. I know the legal stuff will work it’s way out, but you’re a reporter. I want you to help me figure out what happened. You’re an investigative reporter, I need you to investigate. Why did that girl drug me, why did she shoot up in my apartment, and why was I fingered as the fall guy?” “Maybe she fell into the wrong crowd, Alex, and she just wasn’t careful.” ”No. The cops told me her friends all said she’s never done drugs before. She wasn’t some crack-whore,” Alex said, taking another big swig of iced tea. “I don’t pick those up. No, she was set up. She was sent there to meet me, I know it. She was used to take me down, and I don’t like that.” “What do you want me to do, Alex?” “Well, it should be obvious that what I want you to do is...FUCK ME!” “WHAT?” Pamela gasped, too shocked to be offended. “God-Damn-It-All to Hell!! Fucking cunt shit fucker!” “Alex, if you’re going to be crude, I’m going to go…I don’t have the time or the…” As Alex was changing lanes again, still without signaling, he narrowly avoided causing an accident, finally realizing there was a vehicle in his blind spot after hearing the blowing horn of the sedan. Coming within inches of smashing into the sedan, Strider reacted faster than a boxer when the bell rings. He swerved to avoid contact, and he succeeded in dropping his iced tea into his lap, where the contents spilled out all over his crotch. Alex loosed a string of curse words as the tea continued to ”Damn it. I was almost hit by some ignorant shit head Sunday driver. I’ll call you back, Pam,” he said, cutting her off. “Then to top it off I just spilled tea on myself. Listen, see if you can’t meet me in Atlanta this week, we’ll talk there.” Alex ended the call abruptly, leaving Pam in the dark. She sighed, relieved that the conversation was over. He was becoming almost too much for her to handle. Finally finding a moment of peace in her busy day, Pamela leaned back and shut her eyes. She started thinking about what Alex had said. She might look into it if she found some spare time. She thought about her fiance, Kevin, and his recent mood turn. It seemed as if there was no pleasing him any longer. Once she was done with this story, she knew he’d go back to being his old self again. She opened up her ‘Word’ document again, and stared at what she had written. “Saunders! Get your butt in here!” she heard yelled from a nearby office. Pamela stood herself up, thinking “So much for editing,” as she walked towards Mr. Schreckengost’s office. *************** Alex was feeling quite the fool, his new khaki’s drenched in iced tea. He pulled into a rest stop after three minutes of cursing to himself in his escalade. Stepping out to get paper towels, he was nearly knocked back off his feet as a lime-green station wagon with wood paneling pulled in to the spot next to him, screaming like a banshee. The same wagon, with the same middle aged man behind the wheel. Like some sort of irate monkey, the father leaped out of his car, screaming unintelligibly at Alex. “Whoa, whoa, calm down old guy. You’ll give yourself a coronary. What’s the problem?” The man’s face was red all the way up to the crown of his scalp. He was bald, save for the horseshoe that “The problem is you were driving like a maniac out there! You could have killed somebody! What, do you think this is a game? Where’s the fire, pal? Think you’re so damn important because you drive a Cadillac!?” “Come on, pal, calm down,” Alex said, trying to bring the man’s voice down, looking from side to side to see if any bystanders were beginning to notice the altercation, “and besides, don’t let your kids hear you talk like that. You could scar them for life with that language, you know.” Alex smiled, trying his best to be charming. “WHAT? You don’t remember telling me to get the,” the man lowered his voice to a whisper, “to get the fuck out of the way?” Raising his voice again, “You damned hypocrite!” Alex cocked his head to the side, thinking. After searching for a moment; “No. But! I know what’ll make you happy: how would you and your family like an autograph?” Alex asked, smiling. He turned around and opened his car door, reaching inside for a black and white 8X10 of himself. “What? I want you to apologize for nearly running us off the road! I don’t want some stupid picture!” The man’s children looked on in fear from the back seat. Alex continued, not listening to the man’s plea. “Nonsense! Half price for you! Only five bucks because I can tell you’re having some money trouble, buddy.” Alex turned around, smiling, hoping that his discount would clear up the situation. “What the hell is wrong with you? Would you look at yourself? What is that, piss? You pissed yourself? You’re drunk off your ass! Jesus!” The man picked up his digital camera and snapped a pic of a slightly confused Strider holding a picture of himself in one hand, and his Championship belt slung over the opposite shoulder. “What? HEY! AWW! NO PICTURES! You paparazzi vulture!” Alex tried to cover his iced-tea drenched crotch with the Transatlantic Title, dropping his 8X10’s on the tarmac below. Too late to save face, as the man had already snapped off the pics he wanted and was heading back to his driver’s side door. ”Where did you say you worked? The AWC? We’ll see what they say about having drunk work for them!” the man said, waving his camera at Alex triumphantly. ”Oh, piss off! I’m not drunk, you moron! It’s iced tea!” Alex stopped for a beat as the man got back in his station wagon. “BESIDES,' Alex started to laugh, 'The JOKES ON YOU! They ALREADY THINK I'M A MURDERER!' he yelled out as the man pulled away, flipping Alex off as he turned around.' 'You sick bastard!' The man yelled, his tires screeching as he peeled away. He threw up his hands, saying, “You just can’t win with some people!” Alex looked on as the wind lazily blew around his remaining glossy photos. *************** Mr. Schreckengost was sitting in his office, which looked much smaller than it was because he was such a large man. “Shrek”, as he was called, long before anyone realized that if you painted him green he’d be a mirror image for the Dreamworks' ogre of the same name, was a bit of a tyrant. He had a large bald head, and giant teeth situated inside two craggy lips. Pamela timidly walked inside the office. He looked comical, his arms almost stretching across the width of his tiny office, small desk practically being lifted off the floor by his large legs. Pamela didn't find the situation funny, she was too concerned with doing her job right. ”Sit.” He groused. She did as he instructed. “Saunders, what is this shit?” ‘Shrek’ asked as he tossed a piece of paper over to her. Pamela picked up the sheet of paper. It was an associated news article on the arrest of Alex Strider, the fact that a dead woman was found on his property, and his subsequent winning of the Atlantic Wrestling Club’s highest honor, the Transatlantic Belt. ”Well, this all happened after I wrote this article, Mr. Schrenkengost…” ”You were sitting on a scoop, and we lost it. A DEAD woman in the guy’s apartment, the night after you interview him! And he’s still wrestling! Amazing!” Shrek was laughing and angry at the same time. “There’s more to this story, you know.” “There might be, but I’m almost done editing it, perhaps we could do an afterword?” “No. You’re going to go back down and stick with this guy, and write an article about it all. This is huge, this can get O.J. Simpson big. We’re not going to lose our inside line, here!” Pamela got flushed. Kevin did not want her to work near Alex anymore, let alone go down south to further investigate the story. “But…Mr. Schreckengost…You already gave me the story on the…” “Yeah, the waterskiing squirrel league, forget it. That’s small-time, Saunders. You could really write a great story here, if you buckle down and get inside that guy’s head. Figure out what’s wrong with him, how he’s still allowed to be champion and what-not. I don’t know, I don’t care about wrestling. We're looking for the human drama. Just get down there and do it. You’ve got a greyhound ticket waiting for you at the station. Go pack your things, you’re going to Atlanta.” “But…” ”And you're going to stick with him until you get the whole story. No buts. Go. Now.” Shrek looked away from her and back at his computer screen, waving her away with a dismissive hand. Pamela stood up, a bit shocked, and walked out. She just wasn’t going to be able to get away from Alex Strider and the AWC that easily. View Alexander Strider 's Biography
