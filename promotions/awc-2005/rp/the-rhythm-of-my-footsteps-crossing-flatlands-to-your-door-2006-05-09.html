the rhythm of my footsteps crossing flatlands to your door

9 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: one in the morning question: what do you remember? of that night? answer: i can still feel the burn of ashes and other things i don't really want to think about, in my nose and my skin and my eyes. the police sirens have become a constant buzz in the back of my head. the fire took days to burn out, flames a shade too bright, arching toward the sky in licks and swirls that began to look like arms. the crackling and heat simply curled away almost overnight, leaving not even the skeleton of our home behind. that isn't why my throat itches, though. * i. cancer is composed of waiting rooms, strung along with too-bright lights. regarding idle fingers that study the particular smoothness of the endless sterile walls, i wonder if heaven is so white-- the magazines linger listlessly, like bound articles of a forgotten life, expecting to be pored over and then forgotten, thumbed through with anxious fingers, and dashed with distorting drops of saline tears; their broken spines lay testament to the sharp hours that have not yet dulled for a little girl, whose legs hang limply off the edge of her seat: she wears her brother's mismatched hand-me-downs and a lost look drawn over her rounded features as if she's waiting for someone to tell her what's real. ** The truck doesn't know or understand very much at all. In fact, it shouldn't have been able to know or understand anything, but it woke up one day without knowing how and now it's slowly picking things up; learning things, feeling things, whatever. It knows that it's a truck (a 1976 Ford F150, although it's not certain whether that is important or not) and this is the first step in being sentient, although the truck doesn't know that. In fact, the truck will never likely know what sentient means, but you can't miss what you don't know and so the truck doesn't mourn (doesn't know how), it just accepts. It knows it's being driven. The truck doesn't know what driven means, but it thinks that driven (drivening? it wonders) = moving, and it's pretty much right. It knows it has wheels, and brakes, and windows, and a dad part, and a dean part, and it knows it has a kip part, although it doesn't know if the kip part was broken or if they just couldn't get a replacement in from somewhere so the dad and dean parts somehow managed without. It doesn't like managing without. It knows that the dad and dean parts sometimes need repairs, so they stop driving (drivening? it still wonders) at a 'bar' or a 'service station' and fix themselves. It's not sure how those parts manage, since it always needs someone else to do the repairs. It knows it likes the music from around the time it was made, maybe the music from a while after, but not the music they have now. At least, not so much. It's starting to remember names like Black Dog and Back in Black and Road to Nowhere and cakehole, although it's not certain what they all mean exactly. It thinks they are part numbers, or maybe labels or dates like it sometimes notices on itself. It knows that the dean part and the kip part are supposed to fit together somehow, but it seems like some alignment must be off, or maybe the metal's been stripped, or maybe there's a crack somewhere, but it knows that those parts must just be missing some important (mechanical or something like that, it thinks) pieces and when those pieces are recovered, they will stop somewhere. It knows that when they get their pieces shipped in, they will reassemble back together the way they were built. *** Back in Conroe, Dean’s seeing this girl, Claire, every other night. She’s sweet; fills out a dress real nice and has an easy smile. She tried to convince him, once, that they should go on a picnic. “Not really a daytime activities kinda guy, sweetheart,” he drawls, slipping his hands up under her tank top. It’s a lie. Sometimes, if it’s warm enough, he pulls the 150 up onto the verge and stretches out in the long grass, the sticky lick of it against his skin, insects settling into the cracks of his palms, blue sky curving away from him in a haze of rib bones. Sometimes, if it’s warm enough, he dozes off to the smell of petrol, warm rubber and the hum of tires on the road just a few meters away -- and sometimes, if it’s warm enough, he doesn’t dream at all. **** The truck doesn't know a lot of things, like the moment that the dean part starting talking to it. It doesn't know why the dean part never talked to it before, but now it seems normal. It doesn't know why it likes it when the dean part cleans things and moves things around, but after a while the car realizes that it doesn't know quite what the dean part is or why it does that is but it likes it anyways. It doesn't understand what feelings are yet, so it just accepts. ****** The road to Kip’s place stretches on forever and speaks to him in revolutions. The stereo blares rock into the flat silence, the needlepoint steeples and traffic-jammed centers, rolling into the hungry yawn of Highway 97. Eternity stops scaring him, because he stops caring. It’s all sleek and cyclical and he can’t get a hold on a damn thing. He goes on. * The truck hasn't ever actually seen anything, or tasted anything, or smelled anything. It doesn't know that there are things it could crash into on the side of the road. It doesn't have the proper modifications. The truck can hear things, though, because that's how it woke up, and it can feel things because, of course, everything feels things. The truck doesn't think that's unusual at all. The truck doesn't understand about seasons passing. It doesn't know, for example, how long it went without a kip part and it doesn't know how long the dad part's been gone. It thinks it might remember the sound of water hitting something called ocean, but the truck doesn't recall if that was before or after the second to last oil change, or if it ever happened at all. Time is irrelevant, really – only mileage counts. It hears music again, words strung together like gears saying things like I've felt the coldness of my winter, I never thought it would ever go ... It isn't very pretty, the car thinks, but it's kind of beautiful. ** ii. her daddy comes home with tubes that steal his soul, dripping into an absurd plastic bag. she watches wide-eyed as he withers, and knows the scent of cancer is as empty as murmured condolences stifled with sincerity. i see a photograph curled in her hands, and imagine he once smelled of musky mountain air, when they danced clumsy hula atop the world. *** When Kip wakes up, he thinks he’s still sleeping. There’s music. Kip can’t get his brain to work and connect the dots, can’t get past basic instinct and towards reasonable thought. He has to fight the overwhelming urge to go back to sleep – or was that unconsciousness? – while something else claws at the insides of his stomach, making nausea the only real thing he can feel. As more of the lyrics register in his brain, Kip realizes he knows the song. He might even have liked Bad Moon Rising at some point in his life. Now, though, it makes his entire skin crawl, intensifies the dread that rushing through his vessels. He swings his legs over the edge of the bed, pads across his bedroom. His feet nearly drown in the plush carpeting, white fuzz tickling the soles. Lazily, Kip rubs a hand over his head, causing his already-disheveled bedhair to haphazardly stick up. When he tugs up the curtains and throws open the window Dean is outside, the F150’s outside, and Kip’s really, really getting fucking sick and tired of Dean waking him up all the goddamn time. **** iii. the house is still with ready grief: her mom replaces the heady whorls of nicotine-flavored smoke for thick sobs she catches with her tongue; in far off lands her brother retreats, where sprites sit upon his shoulders and whisper the secrets of mortality; from somewhere inevitably her sister creeps in, with street-bruised feet and worry tangled in her wind-blown hair. his slow breath is not enough-- there is no peace in waiting. there is no peace at all
