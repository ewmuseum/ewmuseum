Destroying The Demons

13 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13/6/05 1. Thinking about thinking A man of the Russian intelligentsia once said that the wisest thing a man can do is to grab each opportunity presented to him like it was is last. And now, as the social misfit that is Paddy O’Shea searches himself for an answer to the predicament put before him, he now begins to understand that there was no predicament here at all. A weary Paddy O’Shea has been contemplating for the last half hour when the scene opens. Staring into a mirror, looking at himself. Questioning himself - is he really strong enough person to surmount to this challenge…to scale this mountain? Paddy rises to his feet and feels the fuzzy texture of the rug tickle between his toes. His back pops as he rises but not a word escapes his lips. He only stares with those tortured eyes; eyes that only want to close and sleep forever for he doesn’t know if he can take any more pain. Looking back at this moment in the future, Paddy would think that was the moment he realised they had got him truly because he didn’t even have the guts right now to achieve something he’d been seeking for all his life – to show the world he could do something. But something was holding him back. They were holding him back. They. Those cruel little bastards 2. They Paddy had grown up in a very strange household. Well perhaps home is a better word to describe his childhood lodgings because he had been member to a travelling family – a gypsy family if you are unfamiliar with that term. He is son to an alcoholic father and a loving mother – not untypical of the world we live in today. His overall outlook on his father has never changed. His father was loving and hateful, kind but harsh. This paradox never needed explaining to Paddy for when you’re taught from such an early age that shit is shit and don’t eat shit because its dirty and that’ll make your tummy sick son, you just didn’t do it. You just trusted your parents. They had made you through Gods glorious gift of sex and now they as the architects of that wonderful act were in the wise frame of mind to make completely genuine statements. So his father was a grumpy old cunt? So what, that’s just the way it had to be. This world was Gods little experiment; let the almighty have his fun Reflecting on it now (although he would not have admitted it at the time), Paddy would find it difficult to say his experience in that caravan was bad. He had brothers and sisters, and lots of them, and in a world where a gypsy is treated as a social outcast, they were the most goddamn important things a boy could come home to. There was Francis and Terry and Anne-Marie among others. Little annoying gobshites maybe but the building blocks of a strong family unit. Back in those days, home is where he would have spent all his time but that of course was an impossibility because of his fathers’ insistence that his children have at least basic education. He didn’t want any children of his being any proverbial retards. As a child in Ireland (or anywhere in the whole world) Paddy believed the very reciprocal of the truth. That anyone different from the norm in any way was disgusting and must be insulted on a regular basic and so the world goes round. In Ireland, as a youth, there were few blacks or any foreigners of any kind. The North was too busy with their own sectarianism anyway to be racist. But the south however was in a “peaceful state”…well, when something was out of the ordinary such as for example if you like to have a home with wheels, with nothing else to discriminate against, that was good enough for the “normal” people. Fathers would teach their sons to only talk to gypsies with their fists, mothers to teach their daughters not to marry a dirty pyky and gypsies could go fuck their sister for all they cared. The worst thing was that Paddy accepted this as truth. He didn’t like this but he supposed some people had to be born into wealth and social security and some had to be born to be marred on by them. For a long time Paddy got silence from people in his town (The children gypsies were considered the worst). No one would return his friendly hellos and friendly can I give you a hand with that? Only when he was older like his father would he be able to go to a bar where maybe a few others would be there. Outcasts just like him. Maybe he could even have friends. Well, maybe. With thoughts of possible friendship in the future, at that that time he thought silence was the worst atrocity in the world But when he went to school at the late age of ten, he realised sometimes it’s better to fade into the shadows and let the world pass you by than expose yourself. A mouse in a field that that spots potential food is always taking a risk when it lunges for it when the hawks are always watching. At school he was subjected to mental torture of the highest degree. He was insulted with every name under the rainbow and then some. He was a gypsy, why was he here for, he only stank up the classroom…and worst of all, he would never surmount to anything so why bother. This indoctrination of racism was hard to bear. The wise teachings of a father being passed onto a son. Liberalism could never survive in that world surely? That was the hardest pill to swallow. As a boy he’d envisaged Captain O’Shea, Astronaut O’Shea, hell, why not President O’Shea? Nobody would talk to him but that was okay, he could deal with that if he knew he’d gotten where he’d wanted to go The teacher couldn’t give a flying fuck either. Mrs. High-and-mighty had a special face saved for Paddy when he addressed her. It consisted of turning moving her eyes into her nostrils and looking down at him at the best possible way “Please Patrick, get to your seat. The mere sight of you is giving me a headache” And still they continued. As he pass from primary education to secondary, he realised that some things can’t change. Hateful children just can’t let go. School was getting harder to cope with and he felt walking down those classroom halls, was like being squeezed further and further in a vice. He spent most of his last few years of school life, in a local field, under a tree by day and yes mum and yes dad, I had a wonderful day at school. Yes, silence was better than he had envisaged. And his mother and father would never notice with their constant fucking. He really hated the world at that point. Downtrodden And those mind would never stop echoing those goddamn words You’re a failure…give up now and save yourself the embarrassment. You know it’s the right thing to do 3. A bird in misery Paddy can easily recall the day his fathers’ unprecedented decision was actually seen through. By the time Paddy was 19, he was four inches bigger than his father whose persistent chain smoking had stunted his growth. His father, Danny, had had bad health since Paddy could remember. He had an ulcer in his stomach by the time he was 35 leading to a rapid deterioration in his overall condition. He was aging beyond his years in front of Paddy’s very eyes like watching a caterpillar become a cocoon in a sped up version on television. He had lived with the hard yellow crust of skin crawling up his ankle gradually and even now, at 62, it was getting worse. Even the whites of his eyes were colouring like they were decaying in his head And yet, this withering corpse of a man was still able to unleash the full extent of his selfishness on his son for although his body was literally decomposing, his mind had always stayed true and his outlook towards his son had never softened. His decision was that he wanted Paddy out and he would give him a living to get going. But bare-knuckle boxing wasn’t exactly an ideal professional as I’m sure you will agree. Paddy didn’t believe or listen to the rantings of what he perceived as a cynical old bastard at first. Yes da, mmhhhmm, yes aye’ll box fer ye sure. And then aye’ll pull a salt shaker from me arse and flavour yer dinner shall aye? Danny had ranted quite a lot actually, right up until he was blue in the face. A boy must earn his keep, fend for himself, you’re 19, you can’t live here forever etc. etc. Oh go to hell would you, you dirty gobshite One day after the perpetual aggravation was getting a little out of hand and just to shut the crusty old bastard up, he’d gone with him to one of his “clubs”. Hugh’s was the name of it although it could easily have been named Hugh’s: a bar for the stereotypical misfits of society. Of course a name like that couldn’t actually fit on the board and of well, Hugh’s was snappy. Hugh’s basically consisted of a single, vermin infested room with a bar to one side. He’d heard stories about Hugh – about how he’d water the beer down to pisswater or add salt to drinks to make the consumers thirstier. Instead of an urban myth, these were mostly likely true. This was Athenry after all. Paddy had a lot of fun on first entry looking at the pick and mix of chairs – a few old dinner chairs, a couple of stools and a lovely set of garden furniture stole from only God knows where. They didn’t even take the umbrella from the table – how novel of them! The basic impression I’m trying to make is that Hugh’s was (and to my knowledge still is) not fit for an entourage of rats never mind a social club for men. It is however a perfect place for bare-knuckle boxing. Paddy had actually gone with his father for an inspection, unaware of his scheme – a match was already arranged between him and a local called Mickey O’Hagan. Now Paddy knew Mickey and he was actually something of a pleasant acquaintance compared with the vast majority of others. Far from friends but a shaky mutual link at the very least. Neither would have a problem fighting the other either though. Mickey would violate his own mother for a few bob and Paddy just wanted to hit something at that particular time of his life because of his fiery anger at that moment in time. He always had a good imagination and if he tried really hard, he could picture Mickey was his father and use him as an outlet valve. Paddy agreed to the fight after his father threatened him with eviction (“No yella son o’ mine be stayin under MY roof”). He was street wise but Athenry was more of a death-trap and he really wanted to keep his home. It wouldn’t be that bad he supposed. Mickey would probably beat him eventually because of his extra inch or two on Paddy, but Paddy would make it hard for him for even now he knew he could take a punch like a man and he sure as hell could give one. Paddy hadn’t even shed his top half when he felt two large hams grabbing at his neck. With his arms still inside the sleeves of his shirt, he’d been thrusted backwards and landed hard on that damn concrete floor. And he had bled. At first, he simply assumed his head had landed in a puddle and was now damp but a closer inspection with the touch with fingers and check technique, had revealed the bloody truth. He remembers trying to look up at the degenerate behind this crime only to see a quick glimpse of a toe of a boot being planted in his left eye. After that, all he could see were those crazy lights flashing before him. Reds and yellows…and my weren’t they oh so pretty? Numb from this shot to this face Paddy could only look up at the ceiling with his mouth gaping open, his eyes fluttering Gotta get up, gotta move -he’ll feckin kill ye if ye don’ Paddy sprung to his feet and staggered a little before pulling off his shirt. The pain that was once there was subsiding now though he could now see the swelling under his eye entering his peripheral of vision Better close it then ye silly gobshite So he did and then he turned almost in a trance and saw Mickey O’Hagan a few metres in front of him being held back by two burly men He was bigger than Paddy had predicted. Had it really been so long since he had seen him last – when he’d been mechanically digging through the contents of a skip searching for who knows what. For one thing O’Hagan had grown rapidly in his late teens and was an easy 6’3. He’d tanked out too and his shoulders were much much wider. Big strong arms too. And his eyes. My God. Those eyes were dead in their sockets. As Paddy stared into that face which was dripping pure, unprovoked hate and determination and firing arrows of detest at him, he got scared. But not for him. For Mickey. This business had done something to him. He knew Mickey was apt to do mostly anything for money but his laid back approach had always been his meal ticket, his claim to fame. Kids would kick him in the nuts for 50p and he’d make a comical dance about it. That was Mickey. This thing before was not. Aye’ll put that thing out of it’s misery if it be the last thing aye do “Let him go,” Paddy had said. He too was now focusing in, going to that zone where everything was quiet apart from you. Everything was slow except you. You were invincible and they weren’t even fit for the steam off your piss. “But son, look at yer feckin’ e-“ “LET HIM GO GODDAMNIT!” Paddy barked but never breaking contact with O’Hagan. His father had once broke a birds neck because its wing was broke. Paddy had cried for an hour after it, hating his perceived cruelty of his father until his father had said,” Aye had t’put it out of it’s misery, don’ ye understand boy?” No he hadn’t – he could have kept it in a box, fed it and let it go when it was healed couldn’t he have? Only now did he understand that not all injuries healed for an event could leave the mind with gaping open wounds too. So they let Mickey go and he had charged But everything was slow and to Paddy he was still a million miles away. Mickey wouldn’t arrive for days. Go left ye stupid bastard. PADDY GO LEFT! So Paddy did. So sidestepped left. But then he pirouetted around on his trailing foot anticlockwise and caught Mickey O’Hagan with his elbow on the bridge of the nose. He found it hard to believe he hadn’t had his nose broken before considering some of the stories he had heard before but sure enough, as his elbow penetrated deep into his face in those excruciatingly satisfying split seconds, he felt every damn bone in his face break including his feckin’ nose Under his force His force. He actually had some and those kids were wrong the whole time. Mickey O’Hagan is now dead. Not because of Paddy though. O’Hagan’s liver shit the bed after a three week maniacal streak when he had won £70 on the lottery. They said his taste for drink was so great after his marathon-drinking spree, that he had drunk aftershave when his money ran out. Following an escapade of consumption of every household liquid he had, Mickey realised he must have drunk bleach in the process just as it ripped the very lining of his liver apart. “Imagine that” was his last drunken thought. The thought was of course nonchalant. It seemed Paddy had perhaps saved him However, up until that fateful point, Mickey had suffered from brain damage. The shot to his face had apparently rattled his brain in the process leaving him as little more than a dribbling cabbage. But there were no regrets from Paddy Had t’put him out o’ his misery. Those bastards at Hugh’s had taken him too far. Money had taken him too far There wouldn’t be a next time. Instead of Danny O’Shea’s initial plan to kick Paddy out upon the “shamed” defeat, Danny had a celebration and actually bought Paddy a pint for the first time. Feck, it did taste like pishwater. Paddy continued to fight there for a while but knew he could never get too involved and never let money control him like it done Mickey. He did it for the smile on his fathers face who he was finally coming to love in the most bizarre way. But he decided there he had to be careful. For the next time it could be him being examined by his inconsolable mother in a morgue – face still crusted with the vomit of his own contents. But for now he’d just like to finish this pint for he’d found something he was good at… 4. The Call? The hint of a smile was now creeping over his face. An involuntary one that only comes about when you can’t stop laughing at something or when you realise what a twisted bastard you are in those cheesy old horror movies. It had been a good day. He was proud because his father was smiling at him and he couldn’t stop patting him on the back, because he had put a fallen bird out of commission and done the right thing…and because they were wrong all along and he was good at something Yes, he decided, he would do it. To uphold the honour his family and all that bullshit. He would prove that a gypsy wasn’t just a person to cast aside when society decides they are unworthy of societies rewards. He would gain respect for his family – everyone else would be social rejects instead. They would be the different ones not him So aye’ll ring him He was right, aye could adapt to that way of life, aye mean bare-knuckle boxing and wrestling goes hand in hand Right? The phone was already at his ear when the phone began to ring… Ring ring Click “So you’ve decided to take up my offer”, a smug voice is heard saying as the scene fades to black.
