The Real Battle of Britain

17 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17/8/05 The Battle of Britain was never going to be Paddy O’Shea’s favourite PPV but sometimes you have to wade through the muck to make a point. 1. The Real Battle Of Britain When the twentieth century arrived, Dublin was one of the world’s unhealthiest cities. Britain had sapped the country dry of almost all of their resources yet basically none of this capital was used to help rejuvenate the country to it’s once magnificent self. Slums were overcrowded and open sewers crept throughout the city. Why Britain did this isn’t as clear as it might look. Yes Britain wanted more resources for itself in their shameful give me more foreign policies but it’s also believed that these measures were introduced to starve and kill off a nation that were born and raised with Rebels blood. Whether this is true or not doesn’t matter. Because in 1916, men from throughout the island of Ireland fought side by side and changed things forever… In 1914, possible Home Rule for Ireland had been delayed because of the outbreak of the First World War. Irish nationalists however had seen schemes like these many times before, their governments deceit in matters such as this. Instead of wait to be disappointed once again, nationalist men formed themselves into a gallant band known as the Irish Volunteers and then latter the Irish Republican Brotherhood – the IRB (and latter still, the current days IRA) Along with persecuted workers in Dublin, the IRB marched into the city on Easter Monday 1916 and quickly took over the GPO in the city centre. From the steps of the building, the forces leader, Padraig Pearse proclaimed freedom for Ireland and an end to British tyranny. Supplied with only a few guns from Germany, the Rebels ultimately lost. However, the British’s cruelty awakened the spirit within every Irishman to make a stand and say we won’t accept this. The British shot innocent people for fun. A blind old man appeared at Eden Quay and was shot by a sniper as he crossed the road. Henry Olds of the St Johns Ambulance Brigade ran to help him. The British Sniper waited until the man had bandaged the old man and then shot both of them dead in a sick game of target practise. All over Dublin, innocent men, women and children were buried in their backyards as the British ran rampant through the corroding city of Dublin. The ultimate act of cruelty however was the deciding point. The rebels had held the building for almost a week before they were captured. The British then sentenced the surviving rebels to death and in the end many were shot dead. The outcry was at it’s loudest as people heard James Connelly drop dead from a firing squad – he was dying from bullet wounds and had to be propped up on a chair just to be executed. Men all over the country swore they would unite to take what had always been theirs. This is the history Paddy O’Shea has been born into and in many eyes including his own, nothings changed since 1916 – The North remains unfree and Nationalists are persecuted and beaten for wanting their freedom. To this day, men are dragged from their homes and battoned on streets, leaving mothers to clean blood from 16-year-old boys heads. Paddy has never forgotten his history and The Battle of Britain is only his own Easter Rising. No matter if he wins or loses, he will make an impact. He will fight for his families name – many members still live in the North, the origins of his family, and continue to fight against British rule. And especially, he will do this for his cousin… 2. Blind Loyalty …Sean Casey. Casey had served in the Derry Brigade of the Irish Republican Army. Essentially this was a paramilitary or terrorist organisation (depending on your outlook). Its sole aim was to gain self-determination for the people of Ireland. It mostly recruited young Roman Catholics who wanted to stamp out the discrimination of 1970s Northern Ireland. However the glamour of the uniform and appeal of companionship clouded many of these naïve boys’ views and subjected them to the demands of the IRA leadership. By 1990, Sean Casey had rose swiftly up the IRA hierarchy. He was in control of a flying squad consisting of ten men and had some input in the army council’s decisions. Paddy had always found him a remarkable man all his life. Not just for his patriotism but also because of his general view on life – attack was always the best defence. He remembered Sean once telling him about all the travesties that were occurring in the North. Measures such as gerrymandering were introduced to deny Catholics a vote in 1970s Northern Ulster among other things. And how had Sean fought against such blatant discrimination…by attack and through the IRA. Casey had also taught Paddy about using his brain. Being brave usually made you dead in his line of work. Rushing in carelessly left you always open to be stabbed in the back. In 1991, Sean Casey hatched a plan for his squad. It would be something above and beyond what they had ever done before and a handful of a job for twelve men. This plan entailed the complete destruction of an army barracks located near the centre of the city of Derry. The plan was extremely daring but Sean’s thorough method of thinking had ensured the plan would run as smooth as possible. Yes it would be dangerous but it would also be very possible. Two men would infiltrate the building dressed in night gear via an underground system. They would despatch of any personal situated around the communication centre and destroy of any way of getting information out. As soon as this was completed, the two men would contact the another five who would at this point enter the barracks through the same system. Four would be carrying the heavy bomb device while one other would be making sure the path ahead was clear. The bomb would then be simply planted as near the core of the building as possible and activated. They would have five minutes to get out. The major difficulty was the fact they had no blueprints of the building if even they did exist. The lack of informers on the inside of the army had made such measures difficult. There would be five left over. Led by Casey, the men would patrol all roads around the barracks to make sure there was no outside intervention Casey soon realised there was a further complication. One of the four men he would lead was as unreliable as they came. Unfortunately he learned that a little too late. Casey told every minuet detail to Paddy of what happened on that day. They had been moving down a lane known as the Goarty road when they had realised too late that they were being followed. They had been staying close to the hedges the whole way up the road, always with their eyes fixed straight ahead but never thinking to look back. They had been young at the time, green. Only two got to learn from their mistakes though. “Get your hands up…NOW!” They had all swung around to see the face of their enemy – four soldiers with their guns point directly at them. “Drops your rifles” He’ll never do it…oh shit…. Mickey, please fucking do it Sean had thought at the time Mickey was the hothead. Only really in their elite flying squad because Sean had insisted upon it. Mickey would never have spoken to him if he ever found out he had considered leaving him out. Mickey was the most passionate towards the cause and also the most blind. He thought he could dodge bullets in that uniform. And at that moment in time, Sean realised how much of a liability he really was. Mickey…do it…I’M ORDERING YOU Sean continued to think half expecting him to pick up his thoughts They put their guns down, except Mickey who just stood there, staring through the eyeholes he’d cut out of his balaclava, shaking. But not with fear but with adrenaline. The other four knew what was coming, hell the British knew what was coming; they’d seen it before. One of the soldiers made sure his rifles was cocked…then Mickey began to lower his weapon but- “Go to hell you fucking scum” “MICKEY NO!” But it was too late. Sean’s shouts of protest had fallen on deaf ears. Mickey had already begun firing taking down two soldiers before being despatched with a shot to the head that left a gaping hole through the black balaclava. A sight Sean could not easily forget. Whizz Two of his comrades went down at each side of Sean before they had a chance to pick up their guns as the remaining soldiers began to fire. One of them was in a photograph Sean carried in his wallet – Pete was his name. Levelheaded, quite a lot like him. Sean, ducked and rolled, whilst grabbing his rifle in the process. Before he’d even completed his roll, he’d begun firing whilst closing his eyes. He was scared shitless. His friends were dying beside him and within two seconds he would be to but- When Sean opened his eyes, he had looked up, expecting towering bodies – vertical, the way man is supposed to be. However, after adjusting to the new situation, he realised everyone was down. All nine people there were down. He had thought for a split second then that it wasn’t worth it – Killing wasn’t worth it. That was until the reality hit him. His friends could be dead – he knew Mickey was, he could have squeezed into the goddamn hole they left in his head. Anger began to build up, the mechanism by which armed struggle depends upon. Mickey slowly got to his feet, checking himself for bullet wounds – no, he was fine thank God. Then the peripheral of his vision was processed in his brain and he saw he was standing in blood, running downhill from the British soldiers to him. He scanned quickly through the mass of bodies – They were dead, their eyes still open, haunting him, staring at him You led us into this captain. You did Then a groan…someone was alive? Oxo, thank God, someone was alive. They had called him that for two years because he wouldn’t eat anything without gravy. Sean ran over to him despite him being only two feet away and fell on his knees hard. He didn’t mind the pain though; he was too busy for that now “They shot me here,” Oxo said and pointed to his intestines. The khaki colour of his jacket was stained scarlet red. Thank God the spewing blood was covering up that hole though, Sean didn’t think he could take looking at it without throwing up. “How bad is it Sean?” “It’s bad….” Sean said gravely. He was regaining his composure. He’d always had been the cool customer, no point in spoiling his reputation now. “Then leave me here. You need to go, they’ll be more of them in five minutes tops” Sean instead grabbed him into a fireman’s carry and stood up. The adrenaline was running from him now and his knees buckled for a second under the wright. “What are you doing you stupid bastard, fucking go now!” “Shut your mouth a minute” James didn’t open his mouth again for that whole journey back. He had dreamt about being the hero who had fallen in combat and told his friends to go on, he was brave enough. Right now however, he didn’t want to die for all the curry in China and simply gave in to Sean’s aid. Sean knew the British would be here any second. His friends had fallen but he didn’t have time to grieve, getting out of there was his top priority. He pushed open a gate and ran through a field. He would get back to a safe house through fields. It was the only safe way. Sean ran for fifteen minutes with James on his shoulders although it felt like hours. He kept feeling like the struggle wasn’t worth it, it couldn’t be. People were dying. Yes he heard about British and IRA volunteers alike fallen but not his friends…or not at his gun. They eventually did get to a safe house without a trace of the British. He heard about the British and his comrades being found the next day on the news in between fits of tears. He blamed himself for what happened, he was their leader and had allowed the enemy to sneak up on them. He had allowed the predator to become the prey and it hurt. He never blamed Mickey’s stupidity on that day. His death had led to his mind colouring his memory of him and he was installed in the annals of Sean’s brain forever as a hero. My God, the propaganda machine had worked on him. He never even blamed the IRA leadership’s poor judgement to send a completely amateur flying squad in for duty. Apart from that split second of remorse when he had seen the dead bodies, Sean never ever felt guilt over killing a British soldier again. It never occurred to him that these men were simply fulfilling obligations to their respected country and that there was respect for your enemy during war. James words only reinforced his new found personal blame: “You saved my life brother” A doctor who was a friend of the IRA had come as soon as they got to the safe house. He had operated quickly and without anaesthetic. James had a towel in his mouth at the time as they removed the bullet, trying to catch the pain with his teeth before it reached his brain and sent him mad “You saved my life brother” Sean had been sitting on the body when James had said it. James with a pale face and salve all around his eyes. Sean without a scratch. He was the captain, it just didn’t seem right. He took my hand Sean had come within an ace of leaping to his feet and running out of the house screaming. He knew what James wanted to say: You did this to us. His paranoid mind kept picking up on innocent things. The tone of his – he knew the truth. Something that wasn’t even there. James was genuinely grateful. He never let that blame leave him. He had killed his two best friends. He didn’t even have any friends after that. James had left after seeing the black core of freedom fighting and seeing beyond their claimed need for violence. He became a politician, determined to do things the right way. To represent the interests of his people in way where they could be catered for. Sean however only became more committed. Before he had been put in against his will by his father. He had felt no true love for their methods. He didn’t care about self-determination nor for the romantic aim of a United Ireland. The event where his friends were killed however had stained his life forever and he was determined to hurt any enemy in his path in an attempt to calm his anger. He just didn’t even care about a free country anymore. That was an old desire like a child remember how he’d wanted a tricycle for Christmas but laughs when he realises how stupid he would look on it now. 3. Important Lessons To Be Learned This is the family Paddy O’Shea has been born into. They have strong values and are anti-British. Paddy for a long time looked up to Sean as an inspiration. His stories of daring missions always left out the important lesson – people were dying on both sides on horrible pools of blood. Paddy was never as extreme as the some of his family members. His father for example was complete anti-British. Not only did he hate their rule of law and culture but he hated each and every person that bore the title Brit. It might have been expected that with influences like this and the cruelty of his cousin that Paddy O’Shea would be just of the same mould. This isn’t the case. Paddy O’Shea can make up his own mind and for a long while he’s decided that he doesn’t hate the British as many of his countrymen do. He supposes you can’t hate a person when you yourself are a person and only invisible lines dictate who to hold a grudge against. He will however never ever forgive British law for their tenure in Ireland. These people he has decided were governed by tyrants who would destroy other countries for the ‘glory’ of their own. This is why Paddy O’Shea is finding it difficult to come to terms with the Battle of Britain. He’s finding it difficult to compete in an event that has the secondary aim of celebrating the glory of Britain. He is not the blind man his cousin is nor is he a cold blooded killer. After the London bombing, Paddy cried. The world was going crazy and people fell all around him…so yes, he would forgive the British and every soldier that shot dead one of his countrymen, but he wouldn’t let them celebrate these ‘victories’. The Battle of Britain will have a different ending this time. I mean, for Gods sake, some of these fans will be cheering for him. There’s only one solution for him he can see. And that’s to win the Transatlantic title. To win the main event at a British event. An Irishman. How ironic, eh? He won’t hate the British. He will educate their ignorance to the fact that we are the people, and we shall overcome. Beware of the risen people, who have harried and held. Ye that have bullied and bribed END
