A brief interlude, Part 1: Troll Hunting

13 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11.13.06 “Come on, you long-bearded son of a bitch...let's dance.” With a sly grin creeping across his face, Darcy began circling the monster that lied before him. Actually, “monster” was probably an understatement...even as the creature stood snarling just a few feet from our hero, it was quite difficult to accept its “monster” status when his stature ceased imposing itself after a mere three and a half feet. Nevertheless, “monster” was the title that these fellows had had bestowed upon them from the landed country folk nearby, and nevertheless still, they looked like they could be somewhat dangerous if not treated with a certain degree of caution. Like the soldiers of the fabled Terminator cyborg army, they could not be bargained or reasoned with, and they certainly had no taste for pity or remorse. Fear, however, was a completely different story – and the seemingly unwieldy club they carried with them into battle, easily more than half its own height in length, helped them in inspiring it. But if this monster had any hope in inspiring a similar fear in Darcy, he would be impossibly pressed in his efforts. This wasn't his first encounter with the creature's brethren, nor would it likely be the last. Seemingly, behind every tree, and underneath every bridge crossing the various streams and gullies of Azmodane, one could expect to be accosted by these creatures that could only be described as “troll-like.” Thick, coarse beards shot out from their jaws toward the ground, almost as if they were attempting to take root in the earth beneath them. Combined with the long, tangled hair protruding from their scalps and aforementioned club, they looked quite like Captain Caveman of “Captain Caveman” fame – the adorable superhero who assisted a group of Charlie's Angels rip-offs by pulling anything from a submarine to a jaguar from his face fungus to save the day. This troll, however, had no magic beard with which to trounce to Darcy...perhaps the only thing that could possibly secure a victory against our hero. Nevertheless, he was no less determined than the good Neanderthal Captain, and he charged at Darcy with all his might, beating stick held high. Darcy was ready, however, and sidestepped the troll's initial attempt at a clubbing blow, dropping into a crouch as the club whizzed harmlessly past his ear with a foot or two to spare. The troll's momentum left him out of position to defend the counterattack, and Darcy was quick to capitalize on the smaller combatant's predicament by springing from his crouch and delivering a shoulder charge underneath the troll's jaw, easily hard enough to knock the consciousness from any normal human being. The blow to the jaw knocked the troll several feet backward, landing him square on his back. As the monster fell, a rather large, cream-colored number sprang from the ground beneath him, where it hovered for several seconds before vanishing into thin air. “27?! Crap on this!” Darcy exclaimed as he interpreted the mysterious appearing and disappearing numeral. He had yet to determine the origin or true significance of these numbers that suspiciously appeared every time he dealt a blow to one of these trolls, or for that matter any other creature in this monster-infested area he had the pleasure of doing battle with. What he had deduced, however, was that once the numbers that came with every blow to the troll added up to fifty or so, victory was assured. While some might find a blow that vanquished over half his adversary's health enough to hang his hat on, Darcy was disappointed that he hadn't dealt even more damage. After all, he'd been training relentlessly with Old Man Sage for practically the past two weeks straight, under the belief that the training would give him the strength and deadly accuracy to pinpoint that strength well enough to defeat the trolls with one fell blow. Evidently, this either was not the case, or he wasn't done hauling bags of sand and punching Sage's palms...and neither of the two scenarios left a pleasant taste in Darcy's mouth. He didn't have long to ponder his disappointment though, as the troll was soon back on his feet and ready for another charge. Dashing forward with remarkable quickness, he swung his mighty club once more, this time aiming for Darcy's knees. With no time to think Darcy leaped into the air, but the sweeping arc of the troll's swing managed to catch his left calf, sending him a crashing back to the grassy meadow surrounding him on all sides. Sure enough, Darcy looked up to see the number “12” come from the ground just like it had when he smashed the troll's jaw. He wasn't sure how many numbers he had himself, but he'd recently had his palm read by one of the local gypsies, who told him he probably numbered somewhere in the high eighties. While questioning the judgment of someone who couldn't come up with the precise number despite possessing “mystical” powers, at the same time he figured that it was probably a fair figure. Judging both by the effort it typically took Darcy to bring down one of these trolls, combined with the already throbbing pain in his injured calf, it stood reasonable to believe that six or seven “12” level club blows would probably do him in. On the defensive and clearly unable to land a blow that could match his earlier surprise shoulder block, Darcy had to settle for a sweep kick, bringing the increasingly angry troll down to meet him on the meadow. “10” jumped from beneath the monster. It went on like this for some time, with the two trading back and forth blows; some landing, some missing completely, some leaving some noticeable swelling, the kind that would probably take an ice pack and several afternoons' worth of heavy ale consumption in the shade before they healed. But experience and a healthy endurance advantage allowed Darcy to prevail in the end, the final blow coming in a palm smash into the bridge of his diminutive opponent's nose. He let loose one final snarl from his bruised lips before finally falling silent next to the similarly collapsed victor. A few moments passed as Darcy lied there in the meadow, victory music flowing epically through his head. One would be hard pressed to describe the music to someone who had never heard it, but those who inquire should at least take this information away: there was a steady drumming cadence, some congratulatory trumpets, and the tune itself was no less obnoxiously repetitive than it was triumphantly fitting. Eventually growing tired of basking in his defeat of the mighty troll, Darcy rolled over to claim his prize. The troll's weapon was of course his for the taking, but Darcy paid it little heed; other than what it might catch on the open market for the price of a single log of firewood, it served him no real purpose...and it wouldn't fit in his bag of loot, besides. He instead moved to dig deeper, within the thick, tangled web of the troll's facial hair, searching for the secret spot on every one of them where the true riches lied. Darcy would be lying if he attempted to feign indifference to the act of robbing a dead troll of his gold, but he'd become very good at talking himself out of the guilt. For starters, the troll clearly was no longer in need of hard currency, unless for some reason the afterlife so happened to resemble that of a town, complete with high-price market squares. And secondly, since the troll economy seemed to rely primarily on extorting gold from harmless people merely wishing to cross a bridge, it was difficult to feel guilty that he was stealing stolen gold from a dead troll. Thirdly, Darcy couldn't even fathom what a troll would need gold for. He'd never seen one in the markets, and had no idea whether trolls had anything to buy, or if they preferred just to collect the coins to return home and revel in their shininess. Fourthly, he hated trolls, and fifthly, his calf was really stinging by this point. Sixthly...and this was the key...it was a decent living. Having recently come of the age where he was expected to leave home and make something of himself, Darcy found it difficult to find his niche in the world. Options where he lived were severely limited, and the idea of toiling the fields for twelve hours a day as most people he knew did somehow never struck him as terribly appealing. He lacked both the natural charisma and complete lack of self-regard to make it in town as a merchant. He was too old to take on an apprenticeship in any of the major Azmodane trades. Becoming a sailor seemed like a slightly better idea, but Darcy had a strong distaste for scurvy afflictions. The only option that seemed to fit at this point was troll hunting. Typically the rewards were rich enough to at least keep his stomach full, and he even found a small solace in the notion that he was performing a community service. Citizens of the town and surrounding countryside had complained of troll abuse as far back as he could remember, and each of the little creatures Darcy brought down certainly did not go missed by the local folk...and they certainly didn't ask any questions about anyone who collected gold off of their corpses. Without another thought on the subject, Darcy obtained the small amount of gild the troll had on him and scooped it into his pouch. It wasn't much – either the troll was new at this business, or wasn't very good at it. Judging by the girth and age of the little monster, both of which seemed sizable for a troll, Darcy guessed that it was probably the latter. Electing to leave the fallen troll in peace, Darcy walked a way's away, coming to rest at the edge of a grassy knoll. The lack of trees in the near vicinity allowed the sun to beat down on Darcy's already overly-warmed frame, but he didn't mind. An ever-constant rain had kept both him and his troll-hunting enterprise confined indoors for the better part of the past three days, and he welcomed the all-too-rare opportunity to bathe underneath the afternoon sun. Removing the pouch from his side, Darcy examined the weight of the contents and planned his next move. He'd been at this since he'd awoke and had breakfast some five or six hours ago, and had barely made enough to pay for his next meal, leaving slightly enough to get himself moderately intoxicated later on at the tavern. Nothing to write home about, but nothing to be depressed about, either; he'd certainly had less-effective runs than these in the past year he'd spent battling countryside creatures and relieving them of their gild. Tonight he could eat and rest easy, and he could be content enough in that. But then what? The ever-burning question continued to bite at him, no matter how hard he tried to ignore it. Undoubtedly, no matter how his night ended, he'd wake up the next morning to do it all over again. Same for the day after that, and likely the day after that, too. But where would it end? The troll-robbing project worked well enough for the moment, but Darcy had no interest in continuing it for the rest of his days. And he would, too, if the sheer number of them had any say in the matter. The countryside was chock-full of trolls and other nasty creatures, so much so that one could not hope to pass through without running into a nasty, random encounter. Seldom did one travel through the area alone, lest they were armed to the teeth or utilizing a method of transportation that could leave any potential unpleasantries trailing in the dust. Even if he devoted the whole of his life to the extinguishing of rural danger, Darcy couldn't hope to come even close to resolving the matter. Nor did he have any interest in making an attempt at it. The nobility in securing a peaceful traveling area for his Azmodane brethren did not pique his fancy in the slightest. This probably had something to do with his lack of ties to his hometown itself. All his life, Darcy had been unwilling to accept the tranquil life of a toiling farmer who either lacked the time or the notion to look beyond the end of his plow to the world beyond. He wanted answers – he had no idea what they might be, nor was he even sure of the questions he sought the answers to – but every day he was haunted by the overwhelming feeling that he'd never find them prattling on in what the locals proudly exclaimed to be “The Golden Emerald Isle.” It seemed a silly name to Darcy, as he'd never seen a golden emerald in his life, but the combination of a glowing yellow sun, endless fields of green and a nearby shoreline were enough to convince the patrons otherwise. His distaste for the name grew on a daily basis, as did the sour taste of Azmodane itself. But where was there left to go? On all sides save for one, Azmodane was surrounded by the crashing waves of the Wyndemere Sea. Other than the short route to the south that led to the Isle of Verseus, Azmodane Townhip's sole trading partner, the Wyndemere waters were deemed too dangerous for any ships to venture across. It would likely take several decades before Darcy could climb high enough on the sail trade ladder to convince a ship and crew to deviate from the Verseus trade route for some high-seas adventures...and with those kinds of odds, Darcy probably would either get himself lost at sea by accident, or purposely throw himself overboard long before he ever got there. That only left the path to the north. Azmodane was technically a peninsula, but one would never guess it by the way the locals treated the land beyond the Forest of Infinite Peril And In All Likelihood Death, Or At Least Serious Injury. Usually called the Forest of Peril for short, it was a place that no Azmodanian dared tread. Those brave enough to even near it always returned with the same bone-chilling reports: the forest crawled with the most despicable looking creatures they'd ever laid eyes on...monsters that put the countryside menaces to shame. That, combined with the fact that no people or any other intelligent beings came out of the forest to make contact with them, settled the matter – traveling into the Forest of Peril was strictly forbidden by Azmodane Ordinance #267B. But ordinances be damned! He had no way of knowing for sure, but Darcy could feel in his gut that something grand lied beyond that forest. If he was brave enough to leave behind the comforting, sheltered life he'd known for the entirety of his existence and face the dangers of the Forest of Infinite Peril And In All Likelihood Death, Or At Least Serious Injury, the adventures and answers he'd been seeking for so long would await him on the other side. Ah, who am I kidding...Darcy thought, chucking a small stone lying beside him into the distance in self-defeat. He wouldn't last a day in the forest, if the rumors were true about the ferocity of the monsters that inhabited it. Just facing one of these trolls was usually enough to give him a run for his money. Facing two at the same time was asking for serious trouble. Any more than that...well, it sent shivers down his spine even to conceive of it. “BRREOW!” came a shriek from over the edge of the knoll, sounding as if a cat had been placed in a burlap sack and beaten against a tree. Darcy clamored up to the edge to investigate the mysterious noise...and in doing so, shivers came down his spine powerful enough to make him recoil. At the bottom of the other side lied a small troll, presumably a child, lying motionless next to the rock he'd thrown only moments earlier. Surrounded by the child-troll were four larger ones, who looked on at the fallen child in shock. That shock soon gave way to anger, however, when they turned to see Darcy frozen at the top of the knoll looking on. He couldn't tell whether they were kin or mere acquaintances of the unconscious troll, but that was the last thing on his mind as he saw them advance up the grassy plane, looking to avenge their fallen comrade. In fact, the only thing on Darcy's mind was the instinctual primordial response to these sorts of situations...fight, or flight. Darcy quickly discarded the latter; they might be small, but the little buggers were quick, and nowhere around him did he see a bastion of safety that he could reach before he would be cut down from behind. His odds with the first option weren't a whole lot better...but between choices where one gave a survival probability of zero, and the other slightly more than zero, the choice wasn't all that hard to make. Time to see if Old Man Sage's training was worth its weight in gild, Darcy thought, as he braced again for combat. *** It never failed. All she wanted was to drink herself under a table in peace. It wasn't a monumental request in the least ...but she'd gone about it all wrong, and she knew it. The few people she could tolerate long enough to share a drink with – friends, she believed they were called – had long since gone to sleep to rest up for another busy day of toiling the fields. As had her brother Sheldon, her usual drinking partner, at the behest of their ailing mother. She'd given up trying to control her insubordinate daughter years ago, but Old Reliable Sheldon could always be counted on to abandon her in favor of Mom's every request. Drinking alone became the sole remaining option. It wasn't all that bad – the Azmodane Tavern & Eatery provided the best atmosphere for consumption that she'd ever known. The drinks were good and cheap, the local musicians usually weren't all that bad, and the night almost always ended with drunken merriment...or at the very least, drunken tranquility. But drinking alone, especially for a young girl with cans as big as the cursed pair resting on her chest, only invited trouble. And even though she turned her face away, pretending as hard as she could not to notice, Trouble insisted on walking over in her direction. “Buy you a drink, beautiful?” came from the mouth of the disheveled, presumably human being. His hair came down in a mess of gray tangles, coming just to a stop near his lips, behind which the caucus of missing teeth had just defeated the remaining teeth for majority control of his gums. He'd almost be worth my pity...the young woman thought, if she didn't already have it on good authority from his gaping smile that he'd evidently never heard of a toothbrush. “Sure...” she said, not so much as even turning her eyes to look at him. “...if, after doing so, you immediately go back to the hole you came from.” Seemingly unperturbed at her harsh choice of words, he shook his head, still smiling. “No, no...that's not how the deal works, my dear! First, I buy you a drink, you return the favor with your company...and we get to know each other a little bit, eh? What do you say?” “Not interested...” she said, turning her gaze to the lune player in the corner as she placed a closed fist between her chin. “Leave, before there's trouble.” The greasy old coot remained undaunted as he let out a wheezy, somehow high-pitched chuckle. “Trouble's me middle name, m'lass!” he told her, either slipping into a Scottish-sounding accent or pretending to have one in a last-ditch attempt to impress her. “And I'd like to leave, but I'm afraid I just can't without-” He leaned in toward her face as he spoke, but never got the chance to finish his sentence. He was only a scant few inches from her lips when she put up his hand to block him...complete with the sudden, rapid unsheathing of razor-sharp claws extending from her fingertips. The man practically leaped out of his skin as he recoiled in horror. Amy Syddartha, who had come to be known by the locals as simply “The Cat,” suddenly sported a seductive smile as she peered at the frightened hobotrash between her waving claws. “Still fancy a go, m'love? Or do I need to put some scars where those nasty old wrinkles are setting in?” He offered no reply as he cowered back from whence he came, evidently allowing his exit to suffice as a reply. He more than likely would never bother her again, if the half score of men who had made a similar faux pas and never returned for a second pass served as an appropriate judge. But his disturbance had certainly left a mark. Several mouthfuls of ale remained in her second mug of the evening, leaving her well behind schedule in her quest to inebriate glory. But no sooner did she lift the mug to her lips did she find herself interrupted yet again – yet this time, she wasn't the only one. A collective gasp came from the tavern's patrons as a tall, dark-haired man who looked like he'd just gone several rounds with Mr. T (as Clubber Lang) stumbled through the swinging doors. It looked as if he was giving every ounce of effort he could muster just to hold himself upright, as he stood motionless for several moments before lurching forward and nearly collapsing before grabbing onto the edge of the bar. All eyes were on this unfortunate long-haired soul as the bartender, who happened to be stationed quite close to the spot where the beleaguered man stood. “You look like you need some help, son!” the barkeep exclaimed, staring at him with mortified eyes. The mysterious walking-injury nodded. “Twin bartenders, interesting concept!” he said aloud, bracing his arms to hold his head upright. “And yes good sirs, you can help me. I'll take either a doctor or a Pallual Ale, whichever you've got on tap.” With that, and a wink, his face pitched forward into a basket of pretzels and ceased to move.
