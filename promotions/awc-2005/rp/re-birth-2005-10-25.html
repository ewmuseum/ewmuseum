Re-birth.

25 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 25/10/2005 Frighted away the Dryads and the Fauns From rushes green, and brakes, and cowslip’d lawns, The ever smitten Hermes empty left His golden throne, bent warm on amorous theft: John Keats: Lamia, 1819 I struggle here, to come to terms with the negotiations of my past. A tear of blood, shed for a father, who let me dwindle into the night…like some forgotten ship to the unforgiving Aegean, or the Mediterranean. Who, but I, to feel his painful rebuttle, stinging across my cheek, like the force of a thin metallic whip unto my skin. Ah, why does life bequeath such sorrow, and then punish those who detest it with such a dark gift as this immortality? I am your child, fate, for I have no father… Even now, as I write my chronicles for all those who would take concern at them, I feel the err of my ways haunting me, forever hounding me, like a shadow attatched to my soul, made apparent by the burning sun. But alas, this is my time…my time for revelations. And last week, was it not the case that Alcaeus stepped into the light, full of the glory and eternal grace? Yes…John Edwards…a worthy mortal, lay beaten beneath my foot…and so Alcaeus is revealed to the world once more. Perhaps this will be the only way I can cleanse these sorrows, regrets? The look in my father’s eyes, when he cast me away into the night…to become what I became? It doesn’t matter now…for since that day, never have I set eyes on my father, except in his grave…tired, old, shrivelled…and as dead as I sit before you now…on my throne of dust and air. But, enough rememory. If I am to recount to you the years of my eternal struggle and journey through the pale ages of man…then I must put such sorrows behind me. For I could write entire novels on the consequences of such. This week, it would seem…I am given an opportunity. This, Atlantic Wrestling Club, that has become my uneasy home…in this alliance of myth, legend and achievement…have thrown a bone to me. I am to step into the ring, and face another seven competitors, full of the burden of youthful exhuberence. But is it such a burden? I, too, was once full of such promise. I, too, was once looking forward onto the river of time, with hope and dreams…but where are my dreams now? They abandon me to the stars… Anyway…enough. Allow me to continue my voyage through memory… *********************************** As I have already recounted, and do not wish to revisit, for perhaps the entirety of this chronicle of revelations, I left my father on less than amiable terms. He cast me out into the unforgiving night, with my pride in tatters, and my family in ruins. The pain of my Mother’s death, as if that were not so much for one man to bear…I also held the pain of my father’s dismissal. His rejection of everything he had once held so dear. The mighty Leonidas, now weak and battered by time and regret…lost me to the world and vampirism. Here, I shall reconvene my history, where it might be most prudent to do so. After I left my father, I travelled to Persia. There were small skirmishes here and there, as the Greeks held the Persians as bay, unable to advance, or retreat. There is little to note about this time…but for the fact that, while we were in camp, news reached us of the Spartan invasion of Athens. This shocked us terribly. I was past caring though. The pain of a forgotten father, and the guilt of my past deeds seemed to haunt me perpetually. But it was then, that something snapped. It was then, that I stopped being “Alcaeus, saviour of Greece”, and became “Alcaeus the merciless…the butcher of Greece.” I returned to Greece, a changed man. Too much time I had given myself, to think about these things…that they had swelled in my mind, and conspired to crush my humanity! My return was heralded by a bloody blade, cleansed with the death of fellow Greeks. It didn’t matter which side I was on…whoever had the highest offer, I would fight for. Athens managed to secure me first, and I rode into battle against the Spartans without remorse, pity, or apprehension. I no longer sought glory. No longer did I seek to be a martyr of history. It was now about blood…and revenge. Vengeance against a world that had stopped me from returning to my father. Greeks fell by the hundreds, as we rode through them in battle after battle. I care not to recount the details of those times, for they are as painful now to me, as the loss of my Mother. Only know, that this time was a time when my mind was lost. I was maddened by the lust for death. Blood, and decapitated flesh were my only allies, as my brethren fell like crows onto a land of death. Men became objects…and war became a game. And I always won… The year 443BC changed all that. Two years before Euripides made history, winning the Athenian prize for drama, I was hired by a wealthy nobleman and scholar from Athens, to return to Persia. He had lost his daughter to the hands of a travelling merchant. The merchant had come to Athens, wearing the gold and jewellery of the Persian Gentry, and the man had given him hospitality, in exchange for agreeable rates on his interesting wares. Incense from the east…furs from India…trinkets from China. They had collaborated in their dealings, and the man had left. But no sooner had he gone, the nobleman’s daughter had disappeared. There was no trace, not even a note. It was as if she had just vanished. I was told to go to Persia, and paid handsomely, with the promise of three times the amount upon her safe return…or the head of the vile creature had he done away with the most precious of creatures… I dutifully accepted this…another opportunity to slay…to kill. Even if he hadn’t taken the nobleman’s daughter, I would take his head, and return it for the gold. Her life meant nothing to me…I felt nothing for anyone…least of all myself. But I would do this man’s bidding, if only for the gold to buy myself into more blood and death. And so it was, that in 443BC, I once again travelled east, to the land of my fame and glory. However, this time, it wasn’t Alcaeus the soldier…it was Alcaeus the mercenary…for that’s what I was. On the western shores of Persia, I travelled village to village, asking for details of the travelling merchant, and soon realised I was catching up on the man’s trail. He had been seen in villages heading south along the coast. But no girl had been seen with him. Then, my plan became clear…butcher this fiend, and return his head to the nobleman in Athens…who’s name I did not know. Before long, I came upon a small settlement in the south. I asked an old man, with the promise of gold, had he seen this travelling merchant? Beard down past his neck, a blue hat, pointing to the sun. Wearing the decadence of the East, and the arrogance of the west over his body and legs…carrying carts of silver, and the strangest items one could set eyes on. The man had seen such a merchant only half a day before, and he had stayed in one of the inns. I smiled at this man’s help, and before he had a time to demand the gold I had promised, I was on my horse, and to the inn. There, they told me of his plans. He had been a man of the whitest skin…who had shied away from the sunlight, sleeping during the day, and coming to the bar in the evening. He always ordered a cup of hot brew, and a pitcher of wine…but never drunk either. He just let them sit there, and smelt the aromas. A strange creature this had been indeed, the inn-keeper and his guests agreed, and one night, when all were asleep, he took his leave. But never had he mentioned a girl, or played host to one…which they thought strange, seeing as he always had the offer from the local ladies. So, he had headed south, just this night past, and it was my chance to catch up to him. I bid the inn keeper farewell, and charged my horse into the south. The woods, as the inn keeper had promised, soon greeted me. He was travelling on foot, and so I would have made vast ground on him with the horse…so I decided he must not be too far into the woods. I unsheathed my sword, and tied my hair back with a band of silver. Then, I sent myself and my horse into the dark woods. Before long, there was a fire burning in the midst of the trees. I approached, and found what must be a merchant’s travelling sack, and cart. But where was he? There was no man in sight. I jumped off the horse, and searched the area, for any evidence of someone there. The fire must have been started, and kept going by someone, and he wouldn’t have just left his equipment sitting by its side. I began to survey the clearing, looking for any tracks of any kind…none. This man was a stealthy one. No matter, he must return some time to claim his wares…so just wait. So I did. Sitting by the fire…I waited…and waited…till night fell. The nocturnal beasts came out from their dwellings, and the woods filled with the bustling sound of a new ecosystem, thriving on the dark blanket and the stars that filled the sky. The fire’s light only gave me vision for about ten feet in front of myself, either way…but this didn’t bother me, as it would be the same for the man. He obviously had set the fire to be able to return to, when he had finished…but strangely, it burned perpetually, where most fires would have died down. I heard a twig snap…and quickly to my feet…attention! I scanned the area…could see or hear nothing. There had been a rustling, I was sure…and the cold now gripped me, as I turned away from the fire. “Gahhh!” I screamed as the knife pierced my side! How had I not seen him coming? Outwitted by a travelling merchant…I was losing my edge! I turned, swinging my blade, but nothing was there… The blood sprang from my wound, but it was not a fatal blow. Then, again, the knife pierced me, on the upper thigh. I let out a cry, as I fell to my knee, and swinging my blade again…no one there! “Come out, you coward! By the word of Zeus! Show yourself, and be judged by the jury of steel! I am here to claim your head…so this dance of time will inevitably run dry in my favour!” I shouted into the woods…hoping to entice my enemy out. But all I heard in reply, was a cackle…a laugh. “You, kneeling before my fire, with blood staining my ground? You are the one to present to me this judgement?” His voice was soft, smooth…and full of composure. “I play no games with foul creatures like you! I’ve heard of your nature! Slinking into the night…a nobleman’s hospitality not good enough for you? You must take his daughter?” My words came from anger, and vengeance… Then, silence…from the darkness, a figure stepped forward. He was dressed in blue, of the most immaculate style, laced with gold trimmings. He wore a blue hat, and his beard was curled to perfection, almost like it were sculpted. In the light of the fire, his skin shone…it glowed. Reminding me of the essence and texture of marble, as it cast its reflection into my eyes. He held a knife in his right hand, now stained with my blood. He peered at me, smiling, his mouth closed. And then chose to respond… “I did no such thing. That man’s daughter ran as freely into the night as an eagle soars the skies. He beat her Alcaeus. He beat her, and told her her dreams would amount to her demise…” How did he know my name? I’d never said it…and my fame wasn’t that great that it would travel along with an image so perfectly described so as to be evidently me. “Such things as reading minds, are but of my nature Anacletus. What would your father think of you now? Kneeling before a humble merchant…with only the thought of blood on your hands?” “Foul daemon! You dare to mock my family?!” I shouted in a rage, as my blade was sent forward. The merchant darted out of the way with ease, and I fell onto my belly. Without hesitation, he was upon me, on my back. His knife pressed against my skin, and his coarse breath upon my neck. “Many things I am Alcaeus…but I am no daemon. Though, perhaps I am damned. I do not mean offence here…just the clarity of what I see in your mind. You mourn the loss of your mother, and now you mourn that you cannot do so with your father. Life, it seems, has abandoned you. Perhaps we are not so different, you and I?” His words were like silk. Seductive, yet full of wisdom and clarity. I struggled against him, but it was no use. He had me down. A mere merchant, had taken down the mighty Alcaeus! “What…what words are these? The language of trickery? Surely, you are an enemy of the Gods! You are no man!” I knew it instantly. My words were true. He was no man…but what? A laugh came from this creature’s lips, as he tightened his grip on the back of my neck, reaching his mouth down to my ear. His lips grazed the skin on the edge of my ear, and I felt myself go numb. It was as if this man was seducing me…but not with anything in the realm of visual attraction… “Your skin, so soft. I can smell it in you, like a warm liquer. The blood, Alcaeus. It’s the very life force that links us to the perpetual dance. I see the pain within you. I see the remorse…the struggle. Give yourself to me. End it. Let me take you into this dark embrace…the light, Alcaeus…follow it.” I was in a trance. I couldn’t help but be guided by his words. My mind wandered, as if led by some unnatural force. My thoughts drifted to my childhood…as I felt a slight pain in my neck. The pain was easily submerged by the visions of playing in the Apollonian gardens, my father Leonidas throwing the discus, challenging me to better him. My Mother, the scent of Cammomile…as she waded through the gardens. My school…the soft skin of the few maidens I bedded during my military tutelage. My limbs were growing weak, the pain was still there, but overshadowed completely. There was a voice in my head… “Give in, Alcaeus. Tell me your life…tell me your beauty.” And I did. Memories of travelling…of Persia, of Athens. Memories of my sweet Mother, before I left her for the last time. Memories of the rose bushes, scattered along the streets, on the lead up to the military academy in Athens. It was all so succulent, and so selective. “Yes. Give it to me. Let me savour your journey from birth.” I was so enamoured by the sweet rememory. There was no bad…it was all good. I could smell the smell of wine. And I felt the sensation on my neck, making my body grow numb. My eyes glazed over…and I was playing in the garden. Ahead of me, my father. His arm outstretched, holding his hand out to me. Beckoning me to him, the discus he had thrown now in my hand. I followed. Behind my father…the light. Run, Alcaeus…to the light. Leonidas beckons you. My feet were so light, as I ran in between the flowers of our garden. My Mother’s laughing, as she swept along beside us, her hand drifting through the scented leaves. And the light…stronger now. My eyes were almost blinded by it. My father…smiling…forgiving me. He wanted me back… But then, suddenly, a spear swept through my father’s chest. Blood was sent forth, onto the ground. A thousand soldiers ran from the light, all drenched in blood. Each one, pale as death, scarred and mutilated. Then it hit me…these were my victims. The victims of mighty Alcaeus. They charged through the light, and the light moved further and further away. Horses were heard neighing, as the rode, soldiers clad in metal armour from the light. Men charged the dead soldiers, but they were thrown aside, and a battle commenced. Limbs were scattered in all directions…and the sweet scent of Cammomile turned to death…the stench of flesh. “No…No. Remember the sweet smell. Your Mother Alcaeus.” And there she was…in the middle of it all, but a sword briskly flew through the air, and sliced her head from her shoulders. The fighting ensued…and men were flung to the ground, lifeless corpses, with no history, no past…and now no future. And in the middle, Alcaeus! Dressed in the military uniform of Greece…flinging Persians this way and that. Men who had families…wives…children, destroyed in an instant by the vengeful blade of the saviour of Greece. I heard a loud gasp, and the pain returned. The pain on my neck. Slowly, the war seemed to evaporate, and I saw the ground beneath my face. My eyes came back, and I felt him on my back, pinning me to the floor. I yelled a roar I have never since yelled, nor before had I…and my entire body thrust upwards, flinging this creature off my back. I stood to my feet, my sword in hand, but I was weak…could barely stand. It didn’t matter…the strength of my will shone through…persevered. I turned, venom in my eyes, and caught the blood red eyes of the merchant. A trickle of red fell from his lips…and it hit me…I realised… The pain had been his teeth…on my neck. The sensations had been my blood flowing out of my neck into him. He had been taking my blood. “What vile creature robs a man of his blood? A thief in the night…taking the life of the daughter of a nobleman, and trying to take the blood of mighty Alcaeus?!” The merchant looked frightened, but furious. He stood to his feet, and wiped the blood from his lips. He was in a trance…had to have more. I swung my sword, he dodged, but my knee sprung right into his stomach, and he fell forward. He rolled away from my churning blade, and sprang to his feet. I charged him, roaring as I had done on the battlefields of Persia just a few years before…and my blade hit his, and his knife went flying into the air. I slammed my fist into his jaw, and he stumbled back, my blade being sent forth straight away, and it ran straight into his shoulder. With a loud cry, his body accepted my blade, and he swung his hand against my face, knocking me from my sword, to the ground. I looked up, and there he was, as if the sword hadn’t phased him, sticking out of his shoulder. He came at me, and flung me across the floor. I grabbed a stick with flame, and swung it at him. The flame touched his skin, and he let out another cry. He slapped my face again, but I rolled out of his grasp. I pulled a dagger from my boot, and threw it into his ribs. He was felled, but still came at me. His eyes were full of anger, and hunger. I had gotten away from his grasp. We continued our dance for what seemed like hours. No matter how many times my blade pierced his sides, and his body, he never fell. We fought into the night, neither one of us gaining the huge advantage. There was something unnatural about the way he moved, dodged my shots…but I fought for my life…and the feeling of self preservation drives a man. I learnt that from my father. I must have plunged my dagger into him nine or ten times, when finally, he fell back onto the floor. He was motionless, but I knew he wasn’t dead. Blood came from every hole I had made…but it was dark blood…as if dead. He was dead. But he wasn’t… He didn’t have the strength to move, but he still watched me…his eyes following me. I had beaten him, I knew it…so did he. But he lived, in whatever form of life this was. I slumped down next to him, and held his head in my hand, my dagger in my other. “What trickery is this? A man who can receive so many blows, and still lives? Are you cursed by the Gods? Promethius perhaps…doomed to live forever, only to be tortured? Immortality. Tell me now, before I end your existence!” I was furious with this creature. He had thought he could beat me…ME! Alcaeus! His lips could barely move, but his words didn’t come from his lips…but I heard them none the less. “Spare me…heal me…and I will give you this gift of immortality. It is no curse…and true, it is frowned upon by the Gods. But join me, and they will have no juristiction over you.” His offer came to me in thoughts, as well as words. I imagined myself as an immortal…walking the earth. I could experience every corner of the globe. I could live through war, battles, death…I could beat life itself! Oh, what glory that would be? Defeating life! And I wouldn’t have to be embraced by the victims of my blade. My brothers, who waited for me at the gates of Hades, arms outstretched, as they called my name. I would become more legend than Achilles himself! And the Gods would bow down to me! “Speak, foul beast. Tell me more…” He nodded, with all his strength. “Give me blood. Blood from your wrist. Let me take life force from you…rebuild myself. Then…and only then…can I bestow this gift upon you. But you must tell me now, if you really want it…” I nodded. Of course I did. Immortality would render me a life of greatness. “Very well. Give me your wrist. To my lips…” I did, and he pierced it. This time, there were no visions…no scents. Just the fact that this frail creature was drawing blood from me. It wasn’t accompanied by any sweet sensations…just the pain. I watched, as the life came back into his face. His veins became more prominent, and his eyes went red again. I felt myself go incredibly weak, and fell to my knees. I had to make him stop, but I was too weak…he had a tight grip now, and rose to his feet, holding my wrist. He sucked on the blood further, and longer…and my body went limp. I fell to the floor as my hand was released, and watched as he let out a laugh, and a slight roar. He stood over me, hands on his hips, shaking his head. He lay a boot into my ribs, and the pain was immense. Had he tricked me? “No, Alcaeus. I will give you this immortality you crave.” He smiled as he spoke the words. Then, kneeling down, he bit my neck, and began to drink again. What was he doing? He was supposed to give me immortality…not death! I felt the bloody leave, and found myself unable to breathe. He released his grip on my neck, and looked into my eyes. With his mind, he asked me “Do you really want this?” And I replied in my thoughts…YES…YES… He nodded, and bit his wrist, putting it to my mouth. I drank, greedily…taking the blood…the dead blood from his veins. It tasted so good…as if spiced with some foreign flavour. I felt nothing, but still drank. Finally, he pulled his wrist away, and pushed his foot into my chest. He pinned me to the ground, as I flailed wildly. I felt a sensation in my chest, and my stomach, and an immense pain. “Do not worry Alcaeus. You are dieing.” I felt my body relieve itself of waste…as my pants were soiled. The wetness on my backside felt horrible, but it didn’t matter. I was dieing! Fiend! He killed me! “It has to be this way…” Once my body had relieved all food and waste…it relaxed. Then, I felt my chest cramp, and my heart quickened. Until… My eyes closed on the world…I was dead. **************************** When I awoke…everything had new meaning. The trees, the contours of his face…the sounds…the smells. It was all so deliciously amplified. He lifted me to my feet, and sat me down next to the fire. He looked into my eyes, and winced. “Strange…I can no longer hear your thoughts. I’d heard legends that a master cannot hear the thoughts of his fledgling, but I never deemed them to be true. Regardless…welcome my son. You are a vampire…” With that, I took my first steps into a new world. I believed it to be a world of promise, but now as I look back…I did not free myself from the embrace of my victims. They laughed at me, as I eluded death for a much worse fate…the fate of the damned. Now, I have to live with my memories every night…feeling their pain…discord…and rememory of evils I have committed upon the world. But this is only the beginning. And so, I was born to vampirism… This week, I go into the AWC ring, to face another seven men. The winner of which will get the glory…something I sought in the ancient days…as a soldier, and a mercenary. I never found that nobleman’s daughter…but I wonder, is it her who calls to me at night? Is it her voice I hear, laughing, and telling me I’ve fallen? Her voice that promises me resolution? I don’t know…and fear I never shall And so the life of Alcaeus, twenty five hundred years of death…continues…
