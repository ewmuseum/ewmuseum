Under A Killing Moon | Kiyoshi

29 August 2005

Date: 08.29.05 Note: Anything in [ ] is written/spoken in Japanese. 08.20.05 | Kyoto, Japan | Afternoon [So, you've won after all.] Those were the only five words on the letter Jacob woke up to. He'd wished to sleep a little later, but his throbbing headache refused to let him return to his slumber. The five words were simple enough; so much that even Jacob- in his half-sleep trance and terrible amount of head pain- could comprehend what they meant. jacob hadn't won much of anything in his life, let alone a wrestling match. In fact, it was the first time Jacob ever did wrestle professionally. He took up Karate when he was a kid, sure, but aside from that and the times when he got into fights after school, jacob had little fighting experience. 'And even when I learned Karate it was only to be used in self defense... hmph.' However, given the circumstances that he'd been on the losing side of life since the strange disappearance of his sister... 'I had no choice but to win that match,' Jacob mumbled, to no one other than himself. Jacob had no idea he would have a shot at debuting in a wrestling company on a Pay-Per-View, no less in a match that was nearly the top billing of the event, yet even those thoughts paled in comparison to the fact that he had actually won that match. And the best irony of all out of that: 'Sheesh... I'm not even Welsh.' Well, at least that was a slight reason for Jacob to be happy. In the end, however, Jacob would have to keep in mind that while a wrestling win did a lot in boosting his confidence, but it did little in finding Adalia. It somewhat assisted Jacob's chances of remaining under the roof of the household he was staying in over in Kyoto. He was making money, and he'd at least be able to pay off his rent while he was staying. The only problem with that, was wondering if money payment was enough for the strange man he was staying with. Jacob had given his adopted sister a day to return home after finding out she was missing, before he decided it was time to do something about it. Adalia had told him of her real parents, and where they had lived, but Jacob would have been very lucky to find the girl's mother and father simply at home awaiting the arrival of their daughter. In fact, Jacob simply 'met' the girl's father by pure accident. 07.05.05 | Night The twentyone year old man was fortunate to have known slight Japanese upon reaching Kyoto. He was not a fluent speaker of the language by far, but Jacob knew just enough that he figured he'd be able to get by in the city; besides, although he only thought it for moral support rather than thinking it would be fact, deep in the back of his mind he hoped that his search would not have to go on very long. 'All I need to know is that she is okay.. for all I know, she could very well be back with her parents, doing just fine.. no problems at all. Yeah... I could just be worrying myself for no reason.' Unfortunately, Jacob also knew that it just couldn't be so simple. If it was, Adalia could have always went back to her parents whenever she wanted; it just did not make any sense. But the meeting with that mysterious man was even more bizarre. 07.11.05 | Near Midnight Jacob had stayed at a hotel for about a week when the phone call came to his room. ['There must be some mistake,'] Jacob muttered, insistent that someone had called asking for him. ['But sir, this man specifically asked for you.'] However, he couldn't exactly argue with that. ['Alright then, I'll take the call. Thank you.'] The talk lasted seconds, if that. Jacob had no time to reply to the words spoken to him, only enough to take them in and let them swirl around his head. 'Kyoto Imperial Park, one hour.' The way the man spoke, it was almost as though Jacob had a better chance at understanding him if he spoke in his native tongue, but he knew what the man stated anyway. Even if he didn't, he had no chance to say so, as the man abruptly hung up. Jacob stared at the phone helplessly, before his mind was able to tell him that there was no other alternative other than to hang up. That, and listen to the man's request. One hour later... The only thing that seemed to be in the park with Jacob were the ever-comforting shadows that he had grown used to over the years of his life. Most wouldn't consider them a comfort, sure, but when one couldn't be allowed into sunlight, you'd have to wonder how open a person would need to be to the darkness. Luckily for Jacob, he was used to it; and granted, there were places in Toms River that looked ten times scarier at night than they did in Japan. Not only that, but Jacob had grown to spot things in the dark that one normally wouldn't be able to notice; his eyes attuned very well to the night, indeed, and they were most definitely scouting out for the figure who was behind the voice on the phone. It didn't take very long before he was spotted. 'I can see you...' However, it wasn't Jacob who spotted someone- it was the other way around. However, before Jacob could notice that he was in someone's sights, that someone had already made themselves known; just not before sneaking up quite close to the unsuspecting man. 'I wouldn't have expected you to show up...' Jacob jumped, against his will to suppress his momentary jolt of fear gained from the voice behind him. He turned and faced the silhouette that stared back at him, eyes squinted hard in hopes that his uneasiness would be swiftly forgotten. Luckily for him, the man didn't seem to care- or notice. 'But then again, most Americans are quite foolish... and late.' The shadows refused to allow Jacob's brown eyes to give the man a face, but by the sound of the figure's voice, it surely must have been the man on the phone. Slowly but steadily, Jacob tried to relax himself. 'Well that's true... but I guess if you've done your research, you'd also know that most Americans aren't exactly the same.' Silence. Oh, how Jacob hated it, and he was thankful that it was soon patched up by a slow grumble of a laugh. It wasn't a laugh of pleasure, but still, it was better than nothing at all. Jacob couldn't stand it for a second. 'Quite pretentious,' the man answered back, little effort in restraining the sound of disapproval in his heavily accented voice. 'I guess I should have known that from someone who would go out of their way for someone they barely know.' Jacob tilted his head at the man's statement, before he quickly sneered. 'Likewise...' Again, silence. However, that time, it came from a verbal stalemate. 'Fair enough. However, you won't see me doing something as haphazardly as flying to another country for another human being.' It was very difficult for Jacob to express hatred toward another human being, but this man was definitely attempting to make it easy for him this time. 'The human being I'm doing this for is a part of my family,' Jacob battled back, giving up no ground to the man in front of him. They both appeared to be the same height, and Jacob wasn't going to bother questioning the figure's weight. It didn't really matter anyway, no matter how big the man was, Jacob would not back down from him. Jacob Moreno backed down from no one. No one. That said, Jacob was indeed worried about having to defend himself past vocal debate when the man began to slowly step forward, closing the already small gap between the two. 'I didn't hear you right?' Needless to say, however, the intimidation did not work. Jacob had seen more life threatening scenarios in his day, that was for sure. 'Yes you did.' And once more, there was silence. Jacob's body tensed; something was going to happen, and he could feel it. And when Jacob had a hunch like that, ninetynine percent of the time, he was right. Not another moment that Jacob called it in his head, the mysterious figure lunged in for a punch. Jacob's perception of the move allowed him to block it with ease, however what he wasn't expecting was how swift the silhouette's left hand would shoot out at him. Before he knew it, he was on the ground and holding his nose. It wasn't bleeding and it hadn't caused more than a moment of pain, but it was the surprise in the blow that knocked Jacob backwards. But again, that was not to say that Jacob was intimidated. To reiterate: Jacob Moreno backed down from no one. In fact, Jacob stood up and showed off his resilience quite well. If anything, the punch seemed to psyche him up. 'Your hand comes anywhere near me one more time, and I'll break it.' Jacob felt himself gain a bit of pride when he saw the man take a slight step back. However, the pride quickly deflated when he figured out that it wasn't from intimidation like he initially assumed. 'The last thing I came to do was fight another person,' the figure stated, his voice garnering the sudden change of annoyance to sadness. 'All I came here for was to warn you.' 'Warn me?' Jacob questioned instinctively. 'Don't stay in Kyoto much longer, if you don't want to get yourself hurt- or worse.' The man wasted no more time; he turned quickly, and walked away. Jacob had started after him, but something caused the young adult to stop in his tracks and look down at the ground. Another shadow made itself present, in the form of a fluttering piece of paper. In the moonlight, Jacob struggled to read the Japanese letters. [Are you ready to die?] 'Hey!' Jacob screamed. 'What the hell is this? Hey!' The Japanese man was quite put off by the young adult's persistence, and hoped that if he returned home without answering Jacob's cries, that it would be enough of a hint for Jacob to turn and leave. Unfortunately for the man, getting rid of Jacob Moreno simply was not that easy. Jacob followed the man home, and at that point, there was nothing more the figure could possibly do. 08.20.05 | Afternoon The five words on that first letter were quite different indeed from the ones he found himself staring at in the warm bed, but as comforting as the new letter was, it didn't change things. AWC was nothing more than an attention grabber; something Jacob hoped to take part in to unleash his anger about his situation in a legal manner, and train for the day when he possibly may have to use that anger far from legally, not to mention it was money in the pocket. However, that win at Battle of Britain meant more. It told that man that Jacob was not someone who was unknowingly stepping into a dangerous scenario in searching for the young girl. It was more than reckless endangerment; Adalia was not just a part of the family, but a good friend- and Jacob was always willing to give the shirt off his back for a friend. The only thing that troubled him, however, was that if he was so willing to risk his life for an adopted sister, why did it seem that her own father refused to show so much as half of that conviction? 'That doesn't matter. All I need to make sure of is that I continue making my conviction shine through. And the first thing I can do to make good on that is to keep winning.'
