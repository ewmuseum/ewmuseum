Men and Trucks

20 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It’s your typical summer day in middle Tennessee. The sun is hot and the humidity is hotter. Overcast skies are deceiving and the forecast of that late afternoon thunderstorm is inviting. It’s days before Teresa must leave her home country for the Divide and Conquer event. She’s got a four-way dance and she’s not leaving without her dancing shoes. Honestly, on this day, she’s just washing her new pick up that seen better days than she. Over the worn speakers, classic AC/DC rocks the front yard of the Tomas Farm. Her denim shorts, she cut just this morning and the oversized tank top invites a cool breeze against her skin underneath. After finishing washing the hood with her sponge, she pours the remaining bucket of soap water over the entire truck and grabbed the water hose lying on the ground beside her. Once she begins spraying the old Chevy, her cell phone rings. “Hello?.....I’m washing my truck….Yeah, I can be there give me half an hour.” After the truck is rinsed completely, Teresa coils the water hose beside the front porch and slips inside to change. Mama Tomas is in the living room dusting the coffee table with Windex. The Alzheimer’s appears to be taking over her mind little by little. Teresa watches feeling hopeless. The disease will only get worse as time goes by and it’s only time until a live in nurse will have to be hired or Mama Tomas would have to live in a nursing facility. Teresa will be damned if she gives her mama up to a bunch of strangers that wouldn’t know how to take care of a hose pet let alone a human being. “Hey Mama, I found a new can of Pledge and a clean rag for you.” Mama looks up at Teresa then down as realization fills her eyes. This realization soon is replaced by a mixture of frustration and sadness. The aging woman manages a smile. “Your mama’s mind ain’t what it used to sweetie. Thank you.” Teresa hands her mother the half empty can of wood polish and the old cleaning rag. She returns her mother a smile then embraces the woman with a warm hug and kisses her cheek. “I’m gonna be gone for a while Mama. My job is taking me out of the country again. I’ll be working back in the states real soon.” “Why couldn’t you have found a normal career, like a nurse or an accountant? This wrestling, it’s just too dangerous. It’s no place for a young lady.” Some things about a person never changes and with Mama Tomas, it’s her old fashioned values and morals she sternly believes and lives. Teresa knows and respects this in her mother, but there is a mutual understanding that with the change in time, certain values and morals change as well. “You know my place isn’t with a bunch of women gossiping about hair, nails, their weight, and their men. We’ve been over this before. You know my temper Mama. The ring is my outlet.” Teresa smiles and gives her mother a wink. “I can kick someone’s butt and not get arrested for it.” Mama Tomas smiles back and returns to her cleaning, now with the clean rag and Pledge. Teresa retreats to her upstairs room to shower and clean clothes. =========++++++====== “Where’d you get that thing” They are the first words that exit Scott’s lips once he steps out of his black Jeep and meets Teresa on the front porch of her farm house. He is dressed in a red tank top un-tucked and a pair of dark colored loose-fitting jeans. His long dark hair is pulled back in a loose ponytail, the way his almost always wear it. Their eyes meet for a few seconds until Scott clears his throat and turns his head. Teresa swallows a lump and leads him to his Jeep. “Got plans for him. When I’m done, that old thing will look better than the day it came off the assembly line.” Scott watches Teresa smile to herself and gently takes her hand. He can still remember how her face lit up the first time she watched her self-sponsored Monster Truck, Teaser sink its huge tires for the first time into the mud of a dirt track. This infatuation she has for her trucks he will never understand. “Come on. I got something I want to show you.” “What is it?” “Just get in.” =======++++======= Mid afternoon traffic is always bad, especially in the summer. The kids are out of school and driving their Fast and Furious wannabe cars and the first-shifters are leaving work while the second-shifters are clocking in. Teresa sat silently in the Jeep while some current metal band engulfs the speakers. She wonders what Scott is up to while puffing on her third cancer stick of the day. In the meantime, traffic is inching forward as they pass through various intersections of downtown. “You know those things will kill you Teresa. Why did you even start smoking in the first place?” “Don’t even start Scott.” “What? I just asked a damn question?” Teresa sighs and flicks the rest of the cigarette out the window. Since he came back into her life, they’ve done nothing but argue. The tension between them gets worse with each meeting and right now she doesn’t know whether she wants to choke him or burn a hole in her seat with her next cigarette. “Why did you even bother coming back? You obviously don’t want to see me.” “Is that so Teresa? I shouldn’t want to. Shall I remind you why?” “Scott, please.” “No, it’s you that screwed things up Tomas.” “That was three years ago” “And I should just forget what you did!?” A pause passes between the two. Eventually they are out of the downtown area and cruising down a two lane highway. When Teresa doesn’t respond, Scott turns off the music. He forces his voice to soften as he slows down to a Stop sign. “Listen, I know I wasn’t the best person to live with back then. After I got that title, it’s all I thought about, but still, HIM?” Teresa turned her head to stare out the window as they made a left turn. A used car dealership was just ahead. Everyone makes mistakes in their lives. This particular mistake is the root of everything. Guilt can tear a person apart. Since their last fed together shut down, she had turned to the booze and the nicotine to numb the pain and forget how she screwed up the best thing that has ever came into her life. To have him return to her live has only reopened the wound. “I wish Eric never called you to bail me out.” “Teresa….” “I’m not the woman you used to know.” “I understand that. I’m not the same either.” Scott parks his Jeep and notices a single tear race down Teresa’s cheek, a rarity on her part. He leans toward her and uses his large hand to wipe it away. Teresa doesn’t flinch when he doesn’t remove his hand from her face. Beneath her rough exterior is a woman and this man is the only man that could ever reach it. “I still love you. I shouldn’t but I do. Look at me Teresa.” Teresa lifts her eyelids to find Scott’s green eyes staring into her baby blues. Before she could respond, he gives her lips a soft kiss. When he pulls away and sits back in his seat, she realizes where they’re at. A sign hanging over the door of an old trailer reads Al’s Used Cars and Trucks. She gives Scott a confused look. He simply grins and points over the steering wheel. “Look” Parked two rows ahead is the whole reason for this trip. Teresa feels her heart drops to her gut and the argument her and Scott is forgotten. The Midnight Purple paint reflecting the mid-afternoon sun. Teresa swings the door open, jumps out of the Jeep and rushes to the step-side Chevy. She takes a moment to circle the pick-up before opening the door and climbing in. Scott meets her as she continues to examine all the details. It is the exact same as the night she lost it to Patrick Mapleleaf a few months ago. There’s not a scratch on the paint, the windows are still tinted, and the chrome bumpers are shimmering. Inside, the black leather bench seat has not a scratch, an eight ball is still on the end of the gear shift and the handcuffs remain dangling over the rearview. “How did you find it?” “By pure accident. Appears that Mapleleaf guy you lost it to got rid if it after he left AWC.” Scott tosses Teresa the keys. She catches them and slips one into the ignition. With a quick turn and press on the gas pedal, the motor revs just as it should. Teresa grins and gives Scott a wink. “Get in. We’re goin’ for a ride.”
