Crisis

14 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/14/06 Anton stood with his back against the wall in the small, cramped gym, his broad arms folded across his chest. The one-room gym—selected for price and not for the ''inspiring'' setting—was dimly lit, a single rickety dust-plagued lamp hanging in the center of the room. His dark brown eyes were focused towards an opposite corner, alighting on the dark frame of a person almost dancing and throwing rapid punches towards a swinging heavy bag. The chain creaked as it swayed, every movement creating a heightening sense of apprehension towards the roof's stability. Anton stepped forward, letting his arms fall to his sides. He was wearing a black tank-top and similarly colored short trunks the hugged his form tightly, accentuating his rippling muscles. His posture was all business as he started towards that slender form that thudded its fists against the sand-filled bag. As he neared, the image became clearer. He laid his hand on the person's shoulder and their action ceased. He pursed his lips and stepped in front. His eyes fell upon his sister, decked out in her yellow cropped tube top and short trunks as well as her protective headgear. She sported black knuckle gloves in her brother's style, attempting to toughen up her knuckles a bit for striking. She looked back up at Anton, her breath coming out in pants. Sweat rolled off her dark skin as she stood still, taking a breather for the moment. Anton reached behind him casually and stopped the bag from swaying unnecessarily. ''You've been doing well,'' Anton said slowly in Igbo, his eyes still on his sister. He turned finally, resting the back of his hand against the heavy bag. ''But you're also training with this thing wrong. You have to really hit it. Don't just tease it.'' Anton turned and moved around the heavy bag a bit, hunching his shoulders and bringing his fists up. Nneka stepped backwards, watching her older brother's form interestedly. Anton continued to step around the bag, his eyes focused on it as it continued to move slightly. Suddenly, he exploded at it with a rapid one-two combination, his fists driving into the bag and sending it shuddering backwards, swinging wildly and sending an alarming series of creaks through the room. Nneka's eyebrows raised as the thick thud! thud! filled the air, taken aback by the force. Anton took two steps back and raised his chin a bit, rubbing his wrists. ''Like that,'' Anton said softly. Nneka nodded and reached forward, gripping the heavy bag and managing to slow the thing down. Anton gestured for her to resume her training and Nneka tried to copy her brother, throwing her body weight into her punches a bit more now in the style that Anton had done it, but not quite getting it. Anton noticed all the little niceties in the way she hit, all the things she was doing wrong. Her feet were not squared enough, she was not putting enough force into the punch, her shoulder didn't move enough, etc. He didn't know exactly how he knew—whether it was because he was her brother and he noticed what she did more or if it was because he himself knew how to hit most effectively—but he could pick it out on the spot. He didn’t speak, though, preferring to let her continue uninhibited. It took Anton years of practice and concentration to master his art and he had done it young. He had been tireless. He had barely taken time to sleep, much less to have a social life. His eyes were always focused, from the beginning he started his path in mixed martial arts, on becoming the greatest fighter he could be. Every little detail of the sport he explored, every esoteric rock of form he took and assimilated, broke it down into its core parts and digested. It was all fodder for the here and now, for what he had become. All those years ago, when he had been a neophyte like his younger sister, when he had just been getting his feet wet, the pinnacle of his sport was all he was thinking about. The recognition of being the very best, all the trappings of stardom. Even in a sport like mixed martial arts that wasn't so well favored by the mainstream community, he would find a degree of fame that would satisfy all his hard work. He would achieve something that would reward his hard work and all the years he had spent ''paying dues.'' The problem was how much of a burden it became to be so good at something. No one wanted to simply appreciate the form. They wanted to cut it apart, analyze it, and when they found he was acceptable, they wanted to use him. He tired quickly of purists, but then, he tired quickly of flippant observers who identified fighters as ''the dark one'' or ''the one with the red hair.'' He didn't know what he longed for, but he wanted to escape the whole culture of shoot fighting, the whole circus act that it had become. Yet here he was, trapped in it. He was no longer fighting amongst the MMA crowd, but he was amongst a crowd that appreciated him even less, even though the draw was greater. He was amongst a group that wanted to see big slams, reversals, to see protracted fights between two ''iron men.'' They didn't want to see Anton's prowess, to see him knock someone down with a few punches to the jaw or dislocate their shoulder in a couple seconds. They didn't want to see fighting, to see war. They wanted a spectacle. Anton wasn't going to give them a spectacle. He found himself without the love for the entire setting that he had had at the beginning. All of that had been stripped away and all he had left was the drive. The drive to continue on, to become the best, to win match after match, not for anyone else's satisfaction but his own. He still trained relentlessly, still pushed himself over the edge to become better, to win. He didn't walk down the ramp every Tuesday night to entertain people, as much as that was his job description. He came to fight. If they didn't want a fight, then fuck them. ''Whadda fuck're you doin', mang?'' came a sleazy voice speaking in English. Anton craned his neck about to look towards the door, spotting Luis Ferrara slipping inside the gym. Ferrara wore an immaculate white zoot suit, letting his hair cascade down over the shoulders of his suit jacket. He looked from Anton to Nneka and then back at his client, stepping towards him angrily and poking him in the chest. ''You gotta match! Don't be messin' around wit' dis little girl over here and get to yer own trainin'!'' ''Fuck you,'' Anton said, turning back to Nneka. She had since stopped, her eyes rolling warily over the scene. He gestured for her to begin again, but just as her fists resumed driving into the bag, Luis stepped in front of Anton. ''Fuck me?'' Luis snapped. ''Listen, motherfucker, you hadda con – trac – tyoo – uhl – aw – bli – gay – shun, y'understand? You ain't jus' blowin' this shit off! You lost a good motherfuckin' shot at somethin' big in this comp'ny and you ain't doin' it again!'' ''Don't lecture me,'' Anton said grimly. ''Don't you think I know what happened? I was there. I know what I did and didn't do.'' ''An' I know what you is and isn't gonna do!'' Luis shot back. ''You is gonna start workin' your ass off and you isn't gonna waste time with your bimbo sister.'' Anton glared at Luis and took a menacing step forward, his fingers working. Luis knew better than to start talking shit about Anton's sister, but Luis also knew just how to dig into Anton's skin. Luis lifted his chin, his gaze meeting the big Nigerian's. ''Well? Get your ass in gear!'' Anton looked at Luis a bit longer and took a deep breath. A smirk flitted across the Venezuelan's face, but it was quickly shot down by a stiff gutshot from the Lion. Luis groaned as he keeled over, clutching his stomach protectively. He stumbled against a nearby wall and shut his eyes, drool leaking down from his lower lip. Nneka had stopped again and she was giving Anton a horrified look. Anton simply turned and walked out of the gym into the lobby room, seeking his clothes. Luis was good with money, it was true, but he wasn't good with reading into people. Luis didn't care. Luis was in it for the money and the money alone. That wasn't what Anton was in it for, so if Anton wanted to take his sweet time with training, he was going to. A skinny little Latino wasn't going to make decisions for him. Luis shouted after the Lion, but Anton simply shouldered his dufflebag and started out the door. He wasn't a mercenary, no matter what Luis might think. He was in this for himself. Anton sat cross-legged on the balcony of his high-rise apartment, letting the breeze wash over him. He wasn't someone who even dabbled in meditation, but he did like the calm surroundings. It helped him to unwind after the day, to collect his thoughts before he went to sleep. He took in a deep breath of the cool night air and exhaled it right back where it had come. It was calm enough out here, even with the intermittent chirping of crickets interrupting him. The sliding door behind him opened with a grinding sound and Anton looked up, scowling at the form of Luis Ferrara. Luis was dressed as he was, sans the jacket, his white wifebeater revealed. The Venezuelan stepped out onto the balcony and slipped his hands into his pockets in a contemplative fashion. He gave a short smirk and then glanced down at Anton. ''You wanna smoke?'' Luis said in Spanish after a few moments' hesitation. ''I don't smoke,'' Anton reminded Luis curtly. Luis snorted. ''Oh yeah.'' He extracted a cigarette from one pocket and a lighter from the other, carefully lighting the end of the cigarette. He inhaled deeply, closing his eyes as he took the fresh drag. He looked back down at Anton and laughed, nudging him with his foot. Anton resolved not to look up at his manager and Luis shook his head. ''What're you, a fuckin' crybaby? Get over your shit, motherfucker.'' ''Close your mouth,'' Anton snapped. ''Maybe you'll breathe less of my air and ruin less of my intelligence.'' ''I don't believe this!'' Luis chuckled. He leaned over the railing of the balcony, glancing down at the city below. ''I fuckin' tell his ass he should get to training and he gets all pissy! At me!'' He looked back at Anton again. ''You probably bruised a rib of mine, you bastard. I should be the one angry at you!'' Anton looked stonily up at Luis Ferrara. The man simply didn't understand, couldn't understand. Anton sighed and shook his head. ''You deserved it,'' Anton said simply. Luis turned around, leaning backwards against the rail and regarding Anton for a while. He finally stood up on his own two feet, dusting off his elbows and starting towards the open sliding door. ''Come inside, you fuck,'' Luis said. ''I got the AC on, so quit fuckin' wastin' it.'' Luis slipped into the door, leaving it open for Anton. Anton looked inside and snarled, slowly rising. It was pointless to be rebellious just for the sake of being rebellious. Anton knew more than to exercise silly gestures like that. Anton closed the sliding door behind him and blinked a bit at the incandescent lighting. Luis reached towards the coffee table and pulled up a small bag, setting it on the table. He leaned back in his chair and glanced over to the kitchen. Anton's gaze followed his manager's and he saw Nneka coming over with a small china saucer. Anton moved towards her and caught her by the arm before she could reach Luis, drawing her backwards. Anton's gaze fell disdainfully to the saucer before he looked back up at his wild-haired sister. ''What are you doing?'' he asked in Igbo through gritted teeth. Nneka wrenched her arm away from his grip, which was not held particularly strong this time, and tried to move past him. He blocked her, bearing down on his younger sibling. ''Coke,'' she replied rather condescendingly. ''What does it look like I'm about to do?'' ''Don't,'' Anton snapped. ''You have to train, or are you not worried about your progress anymore?'' ''I'm worried about ending up like you,'' Nneka growled. ''Quit getting in my way. Luis is waiting.'' Nneka deftly ducked under Anton's arm and picked up her pace, moving towards Luis and setting the plate gently down onto the table. They shared a kiss and Nneka perched on the Venezuelan's lap, forcing him to lean over to prepare the cocaine. Anton's upper lip curled. It was amazing how soon a woman would get over being called a bimbo. The problem was how much of a burden it became to be so good at something. People wanted to use him for something, and when they did it once, they wanted it again and again until they became dependent on it. People began to need him to do things for them. To fight their battles for them. To get them out of binds. Anton didn't want to do all these things, to get embroiled in everybody else's issues, but he was compelled to. He was good at what he did and he felt it would be wrong not to use his ability in service to people. So it was starkly clear to him when he saw that his sister needed help. She wouldn't last without him here, without some sort of support other than an idiot coke-addicted, two-stepping Venezuelan. She couldn't juggle everything she wanted to do, couldn't survive too long. So far she had remained alive even through being raped twice, but her luck would run out soon. Then she would need him and Anton… Anton had to be there. No one wanted to end up like Anton, but they all wanted him around. They saw the protection that he could give them, the security that being around him provided. No one wanted to sympathize with the big Nigerian, only to find what they could use for their own ends. Anton wasn't going to let it destroy him inside, though. He simply had to go on, determined. Relentless. Tireless. Eternal.
