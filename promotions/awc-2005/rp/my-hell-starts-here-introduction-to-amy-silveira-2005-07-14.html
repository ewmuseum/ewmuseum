My Hell Starts Here (Introduction to Amy Silveira)

14 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Thursday 14/07/05 Wrestling. I still couldn't believe it. Even though I'd been told my fate almost one-and-a-half months ago, it still hadn't quite registered into my head that an industry I'd never cared about in the slightest would be my future. I'd be sitting for an hour or so now, in my quite nicely done out bedroom, just thinking about the thought of having my head caved in for a living, and life in general. My parents had been successful for years, and we'd moved around Lisbon several times while I was there, the houses getting bigger, better and more braggable each time. But, nothing compared to this monster of a place in suburban Los Angeles. First of all, there was six bedrooms, which was two more than the biggest we'd had back home. Then there was the fact the garden was twice the size of the old one, complete with a nicely-sized swimming pool, and a patio sitting area where I'd, well, got bladdered a few times over the last few years. On top of that, the neighbourhood was full on minor celebrities, driving around in done-up Lexus, Mercs and Chevrolet's, instead of the cheap junk I'd been used to seeing in Portugal. The thing I loved most about the house, though, was the really cool bedroom I'd been given. I was always going to be sad to leave my friends at home, but I don't think my dad needed to promise me the 'bestest bedroom ever, and a bit more', as he'd put it. Of course, I wasn't complaining, because now I had everything a normal teenage girl could ever want in her bedroom. Apart from a nice looking lad, but then again, I doubt that even the old man could get me that.. But, anyway, I'd been thinking a lot lately. My bedroom had been the best place to do it, because lately I just couldn't get any quiet elsewhere. My dad just wanted me to talk wrestling non-stop, my mam wanted mother-daughter conversations about wrestling, and my sister Catarina, well, was being my sister Catarina. The problem was that, even though I wanted to avoid talking wrestling with my parents, I'd only go away and think about it some more on my own. It was some time in early June when my dad brought it up with me. I'd been enjoying myself, as any 19-year old would do, when all of a sudden we had a 'talk' about it. I couldn't believe what he was saying when it happened. Not only did I not think he'd want to do something like that to me, but it just came out of the blue so quickly that it hit me. The pain of it hitting me hasn't worn off since. My dad had always cared about me. In contrast to my sister, I'd pretty much been a daddy's girl, in that we'd had a close relationship from my early years to my mid-teens. He'd done a lot for me, helped me with a lot of problems, and been there for me whenever I needed him. If it wasn't for him, I wouldn't be sitting in a six-bedroom monster of a house, either, because it was his financial dealings and intelligence that had got us into a position of pretty good wealth. At the same time, looking back at it all, he'd been preparing me for this 'career' path since I was six or seven years old. He'd got me in to gymnastics, kickboxing and other things like that. I'd kept with both of them for years, and thinking back, maybe he even drove me forward into continuing with it. With kickboxing, for example, I'd been uncomfortable doing it at first, but he insisted I carried on, promising me presents and treats if I did. I'd never doubt his intelligence. As far as wrestling is concerned, my dad had always been a fan. I can remember being nine or ten years old, and sitting with him, my mam and my sister watching NTICW, PCW and whatever wrestling was on at the time. I'd never become a fan, but my dad was obsessed, and even though he'd never really gone into detail I'm well aware that he even tried to make the big time as a manager. And I guess that's where it all comes together. His passion for wrestling and desire to be a famous, well-respected manager was what ultimately made him want me to become a professional wrestler. I don't understand why he put himself first, but I daren't ask him. He's my father, and I care about him, so I didn't dare refuse. After all, he'd already got me a contract, so I kind of didn't have much choice in the matter. The fact I 'agreed' to his wishes doesn't mean I like them, though. Although I'm not letting anyone know, apart from my closest friends, I'm really scared of it all. As in, really scared. I've been a normal teenage girl for years - with friends, with a good life and with ambitions for the future. When I was younger I had the normal ambitions of being a vet, an actor, a singer, stuff like that - until recently I'd been planning to go in to journalism or something like that. Now I just feared the worst - becoming a ringside doll, seriously injury - even death. My father's interest in wrestling had, throughout the years, let me learn a few things about the industry. Believe me, I know its fake, but he's tried making a career out of it himself and he insists that it isn't always make-believe. There are serious injuries, and there are even places where the people fight for real. I've seen the blood, the gore, the pain - even when my mother covered up my eyes and my dad laughed, I've seen it. I didn't want that to happen to me, because I wanted to live a long, happy life. I feared the worst, without a doubt. And it was this fear that had led me to sit in my room, hoping to chill out, but really sweating inside and wishing it wasn't really going to happen. I'd done nothing but have wrestling on my mind recently. If it wasn't my dad 'preparing' me for 'becoming a star', or my mam being concerned, it was me doing normal things and having that at the back of my head constantly, nagging at me, and scaring me. Although it might be a bit 'fear the worst', the phrase that kept playing in my head had been 'I don't wanna die'. Today had been quite a boring day, compared to most of the days in my busy life. It had been warm outside, but my friends had headed out of town to do some shopping, and I just didn't feel up to it. I'd been left to sit in my room, watching the television for a bit, talking to some lads from back home who were now looking up to me after hearing the news of my new-found 'fame' on MSN Messenger, and then just listening to some music while I'd been thinking and chilling out. The Winamp playlist had finished about 15 minutes ago, but I'd not got up to restart it. Instead, I'd just lied down on my baby blue single bed, looking at the ceiling and beginning to feel a little more relaxed than usual. That wouldn't last long, though. As I drifted off into some thought about a lad called José from back in Lisbon, I got a casual knock on my bedroom door. I'd had a lock put on to my new door, so I could have some privacy if I wanted it. I stood up, shaking myself down after an hour or so on the bed, before unlocking the door and opening it to see disappointment. I'd been hoping for a hot American lad, and instead I got Hugo Cristiano Davi Silveira. Yeah, that's my dad. Whereas myself and my sister had apparently been made by the Lord into the epitome of perfect beauty, my dad looked like a complete embarrassment to the situation nowadays. Not only had he last nearly all of his once long, curly hair, but he had a curly, light brown moustache which made him look like some sort of Mexican cannabis smuggler. Just think Mario, only worse. He stood there with his typical mild grin, obviously planning to enter my room. I stepped to the side, and he entered, looking me up-and-down as he had been recently, and as I closed the door I noticed my sister being nosy and looking from a distance. I shut the door, not planning to get into a conversation with her, as recently she'd been being really bitchy. And that was due to the one good thing about wrestling - I got one over my sister, in that I'll be on television, and she won't. I could sense the jealousy. By the time I turned around, my dad had already sat down in my desk chair and started checking to see if I'd gone any 'inappropriate' e-mails. Funny, even though I'd turned 18 recently, he still felt it necessary to make sure I wasn't selling my body to people. He even said I shouldn't use my webcam with lads once. I just laughed at him. He'd always cared too much, I'd thought, making his throwing me into the ring with big, sweaty ogres otherwise known as male wrestlers quite out of character. Nevertheless, I'd now propped myself up on the end of my bed, leaning against the bottom of it whilst smiling at him. There'd been no love lost between the two of us, and even if there had, I wouldn't dare have showed it. After checking my e-mails he swung the desk chair around, looking at me, and smiling at me like he always had. Hugo: You know, darling, you really should let José go now. He lives across the other side of the Atlantic Ocean. My dad was referring to José Concasteras, a lad a year older than me who I'd been going out with just months before we left for Los Angeles. I didn't still like him, I just liked to flirt with him, but my dad couldn't accept that. Amy: Yeah, I know, but.. well.. Normally I'd defend him endlessly at this point. But lately, I just couldn't be bothered with it. He, as he always had, noticed this instantly. Hugo: Hey, Amélia.. is everything alright? Amy: Yeah, yeah.. I'm fine. Just been thinking a lot, I guess.. Amélia. That had been my birth name, and the name I'd been called for 13 years. When we moved to the US, people started calling me Amy, and it stuck so much that now the name is practically defunct. Except for with daddy, who things its a beautiful name. Which he would. Hugo: Ok, well I've just got word from Dave Harber that you'll be starting next week against The Farmer, which is great because we can take him down without a doubt. Amy: Ok.. if you say so.. I just couldn't have a conversation with him anymore. When it came to wrestling talk, I just agreed and let him carry on. Otherwise I'd get a lecture off him, and when he starts whinging, he starts whinging. Amy: So, I guess you'll want me to train endlessly for the next week? Hugo: No, no. I've got some money to make today, but we'll have to prepare, because we've got to impress the world next Tuesday. That was odd. Normally he'd have me running laps by now. Hugo: Anyway, I'm sure that you have lots of girlie things to do now. Hugo stood up, making his way over to me, and touching my shoulder and looking down with his concerned, yet happy, look. He'd been happy ever since he told me about my 'big new career'. Hugo: Are you sure your ok? He really does worry too much, though. I give him a cute smile, looking up at him, and answering in a way that he wouldn't ask again. Amy: Yeah. He slowly moved his hand off my shoulder, walking toward the door, and opening it. Just as he opened it, he turned around, making sure he could sneak in a bit more Hugo Silveiraness. Hugo: Love you. I didn't turn, I just smiled and laughed silently. He'd done that for as long as I could remember. He left the room, leaving the door open, and a few seconds over before I could head to the door to close it properly my sister poked her head through, wanting to sneak in some sarcasm. Catarina: 'Love you'. By the time I got to the door she was long gone. At least, with wrestling, I'd be able to say more people love me with her. Which was normally the other way around, given her boyfriend count. Of course, the fans had to like me first, because I feared they'd boo me. I wouldn't be able to handle that. Regardless, in five days time, my career would start. My life would never be the same again. My hell starts here.
