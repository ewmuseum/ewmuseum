Decisions

19 September 2005

Date: Today...duh. 19th Sept 05 If he was honest with himself, he hadn’t put a hundred percent effort into the bout. He could blame it on first show nerves, he could blame it on a dodgy burger the night before, and healthy eating had never been an option. That wouldn’t be true though, the simple fact of the matter was that John was scared. With every single bout that he wrestled his body took that little bit longer to recuperate, in four years his physical condition had deteriorated at a radical rate, long gone were the days when he would wrestle a show, then hit the town partying till all hours. Now he returned home and had a bath with healing salts. It was a rock and roll life. That’s for sure. His fear had dominated him for the best part of a year. It had been documented in various magazines and web blogs that his style had altered radically. No longer was he an eye catching, high flying crowd pleaser. Now he was just another face in the crowd, the same mundane, dull style of wrestling that had caused a recent slump in the industry. He longed for the years gone by, to become the man he once was, the man who with three other men destroyed the confines of a steel cage. The man who claimed the PTC Extreme Championship by diving off a forklift truck. Essentially, he wanted to be a winner once again. He wanted the glitz and glamour of championships, he wanted to be re-recognised, his name in lights, the works. Yet every single time he felt the push come along, every time he climbed into the ring, his instinct kicked in, told him to tone it down. Why try for a suicide plancha when a baseball slide will do. His own mind was censoring him. That’s what had happened at his Fresh debut. It hadn’t started well. He was expecting there to be questions about PRIME, whenever anyone leaves a company for another there is always a story to tell, a story that everyone has to know and generally it’s told, you do so to make friends with your new colleagues, to get them on side. There is nothing worse than being the new guy that nobody likes. But John simply didn’t care; he was always a very enclosed character. Choosing to spend time alone rather than be buddy buddy with every Tom, Dick and Harry on the roster. He knew how people saw him, the sly comments that were sent his way and in the past it would have got to him, he would have called them out or just attacked them for the hell of it, to prove a point or just for fun. Now though he would always simply bide his time, if something was meant to be then karma would make sure that it happened, or didn’t as the case may be. Life was too short to care about what others thought about you. His life would have been even shorter if he had, his own way. Even plunging a blade into his own chest, would not relinquish him from the pit of misery that this life held. After that his outlook on life had changed, whilst he didn’t go to the extremes of a tree-hugging hippy, there was a certain level of respect for life now. He wasn’t a believer that everyone in life had a plan set out for them, destiny was a pile of bollocks. Life is for living and as such we carve out our own destinies. John wanted to make his future here, but as it has been previously stated, he had developed a reputation, a reputation that was not favourable by any standards. When people thought of John ‘Moraless’ Edwards, one word came up frequently. Unreliable And on a certain level they were right. Numerous feds in less than four years in the sport had tarnished his reputation. Numerous press conferences had him stating why he would remain with federation X, for the rest of his career and that he will help federation X to develop into a world class product, by giving knock out performances week in and week out. The fans would cheer, the owners would smile and plan things around him, then he would leave. Simple as that, leaving the federation with problems. He didn’t do it intentionally, life just started to take a horrible twist every time he fell into a routine, every time things seemed to get boring. At Fresh he’d over heard a conversation, they were placing bets on how soon John would leave. Part of him wanted to join in, just for some quick and easy cash. In that one conversation he’d felt his self-esteem crushed on some level, the other events of the night led to trouble as well. The loss at Fresh hadn’t helped John’s self-esteem. For a long time after the match he sat alone in his dressing room, a towel draped over his head, a barrier away from the outside world. As he breathed in and out every inch of his body ached. Even though it was just a short bout, it had taken a lot out of him. His problems, which were deep rooted in his personal life, had drastically altered his training regime. His wife Louise now came first in every aspect of his life, every aspect apart from one. It was a small routine he had fallen into whilst in PRIME. Every night after a show he would go and drink. He would drink until the pain had been numbed and then he would take his painkillers, the pills the for just a few hours of everyday allowed him to pretend, that his neck may not snap at any given moment. He knew he was toying with his life, using this deadly cocktail, but after competing his neck was at it’s absolute worst, regularly the pain made him vomit, sick to his stomach. This was a release, not a suicide attempt. After the cocktail he felt alive, the drunkenness lowering his inhibitions, he finally felt as though he could relax, sat at the end of the bar, a bottle of cool beer in his hand and everything suddenly drifted away. No longer did he worry about his wife, who was more than likely at home sat in the dark with tears streaming down her face, silent tears which had not stopped flowing since the death of their unborn child. No longer did he worry about the scars over his body, the various blade and battle wounds, a tortured canvas of a body painting a picture. Instead he saw them as a message, each individual scar serving to aid a memory, with a story to tell. No longer did he worry about the fact that one move overshot, one stiff kick. Just one tiny mistake could not only end his career, but his life. This was the hardest thing to forget. Sat silently at the edge of the bar John had gone over the four way match at Fresh, trying to figure out exactly where or what went wrong, he had already come to the conclusion that he didn’t try hard enough. That was blatantly apparent, to anyone watching the match. It’s at times like these when a man begins to try and take point of his life. Where exactly he is coming from and where he is to go and in that brief moment, a little thought enters your head. Maybe you don’t have it anymore. “Tough break tonight man” The words were rough, the voice belonging to a man that sounded like he gargled with liquid sandpaper, if ever there was such a thing. John didn’t bother looking to see who it belonged to, in all honesty he didn’t care, also moving would probably mean falling off his stool and that would just look uncool. Instead he simply nodded in acknowledgement, hoping that this would be enough. Much to his dismay he hard the scraping of wood being dragged across wood as the stool next to him was pulled out, the man sitting down next to him. John considered three options, none of which ended with smiles and laughter fighting fleeing or fighting, yes there was two fightings in there but he was drunk and this was how his mind was working. ”I know what it’s like you know.” The man nodded towards the barman as he speak, signalling to bring over two drinks, the barman duly obliged and placed them both down in front of the stranger, who in turn passed one to John. He may not have known the man, but a free drink that you saw where it’s coming from is something else. ’Really’ thought John to himself, I’m sure you know exactly what it’s like to be walking time bomb. To be a cancer to everything around you even yourself, the world doesn’t need people like me and people like me certainly don’t need the world. That’s the problem with society today, everyone has something to say, looking for a way to work themselves into others lives in the interest of ‘helping’ them, when really their own lives are simply so dull. Voyeurism at its most truthful and blunt, none of this big brother crap. “One day you’re on top of the world, then bang. You’re a down and out drinking alone in a bar at two in the morning, just passing the time so that you don’t have to go back to the hell hole you call a home, scared of what you’ll find there.” The stranger took a large gulp from his drink and sighed deeply, John’s hostility dropped somewhat as he heard the tone in the mans voice change. That was a voice that had felt real pain and more than likely did know what he was going through. He wasn’t about to let him know that though. ”Sometimes it’s all I can do to wake up in the morning, to go to that god awful office day after day. I swear one of these day’s I’m going to flip and then there will be blood a flowing. Oh yes, I swear sometimes the only thing that keeps me level are these.” Below John’s eye line, just out of sight of the barman, a small packet of pills appeared in front of him. So that was his story huh a fucking peddler. They did that, preyed on the weak down and out, but that was when they didn’t know who they were. This dealer clearly knew exactly who John was, his line of work, which probably meant he knew his weaknesses too. You see, when you get involved in the wrestling business, you lose your life. For two years John was Moraless. He lost his identity, signing countless autographs as ‘Moraless’ eventually you begin to believe it yourself, you get entirely rapped up in the character, you become that person. He had seen it before, wrestlers whose gimmick meant worshipping Satan or something ridiculous, just to tell the story, but then they started to actually believe it, next thing you know, you’re invited to dinner and they try to sacrifice you, still worse things happen at sea. “Look pal, I’m not interested in the slightest, I’ve got my booze that’ll do me.” John mumbled as he became a lot more aware of the beer that had been given to him, taking care to place the bottle down carefully next to him on the bar. ”Fair enough, but hear me out. These aren’t your conventional drugs. I know your back-story and I know that you are on the way to an early grave, booze and painkillers man, that’s not right. You don’t let yourself go as far as you did anymore. I’m doing this as a fan not for the money. What you have there is a revolutionary new painkiller. Doctors can’t get their hands on it due to it being so damn effective the costs are sky rocketing, medicine is just like everything else a business. From a throaty man came the sort of salesman spiel that you expect to find from a used car salesman, trying to make commission. John had to admit he’d started to pay attention at the super effective pain killer line. The regular diazepam, which he was prescribed, now just simply didn’t cut it anymore, everyday becoming less and less effective. He picked up the pills and looked at the m, small little white pills that could offer him a life of normality. “Look…whatever you do with them doesn’t bother me. Use them, flush them whatever. I’m offering you a chance here…deep down you know that you want to take control of things. Fresh this week wasn’t you. Do something spectacular this week, make yourself noticed.” Before John could even reply the man had gone, disappeared into the shadows from which he came. The packet clasped in his hands. The journey home was an extremely quiet one, his mind wondering over these tiny miracle pills that were resting in his pocket, the pills that would make him pain free. If these pills were as good as promised, he would be able to refind his old form, become the man he once was. Opening the door to his new apartment he was greeted by the scene, which he had imagined only a few hours earlier, his wife alone in the dark crying. He put his arm around her, tried to comfort her, but he’d been trying to do that for over a month now, nothing he did seemed to matter. Nothing he did seemed to work. He left for the bathroom his hand in his pocket, clasping the bag of pills, which had been given to him. He intended to flush them and just amble on through life the way he had done for the past two years. Locking the door behind him he lifted the toilet seat and pulled the bag from his pocket, holding them above the water, his hand trembling slightly. Opportunities like this did come up all the time, but he needed to do it… “WHAT IS YOUR PROBLEM JOHN!” The voice screamed from the next room, Louise had suddenly found her voice again, her angry voice. Not just, you forgot to buy my special toothpaste angry voice but, I’m going to take out twenty-nine years of pain and angst out on you right now and if you say a word I will use your stomach as a sponge. So pretty angry. A series of fists and feet began to slam against the toilet door, John still clutching the pills stared at the door, a distinct look of fear in his eyes, its like being in a zombie movie knowing that they’re right behind the door ready to eat your flesh. “I’M HERE IN PAIN AND SUFFERING, OUR CHILD….AND YOU COME IN SMELLING OF LIQUOR, SO WHO WAS THE WOMAN YOU HOOKED UP WITH I BET SHE DIDN’T SPEND ALL NIGHT CRYING DID SHEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEE” Her voice extended into a wail which seemed to pierce John’s very ear drums. It took all his effort to not reply ‘I didn’t hook up with anyone, but I can guarantee wherever she is, she didn’t cry no.’ If he did that he knew his penis would be last seen floating over the skyline attached to a helium balloon. That’s when he realised. ”OH SURE IGNORE ME, YOU ALWAYS DO THAT ANYWAY. I’M GOING TO MY SISTERS!” She didn’t have a sister. That’s when he realised; he had lost control of his life. It was time to take control again. He took one of the small white tablets out of the packet and stared at it for a brief moment as the walls shook fiercely as Louise left to wherever she was going to. Then without another doubt, another thought he swallowed the pill. And it was euphoric, the pain disappeared completely. And as he sat on the toilet, the bag in his hands, he smiled…
