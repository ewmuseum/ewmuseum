It appears the president isn’t as honest as we once thought as breaking news is flashing across the screen about some scandal they are calling Water Gate. Nobody really cares as Viet Nam is escalating and images of napalm babies rot our minds and sour us to the world as a whole. Sitting at the far corner of the bar is a young kid of about twenty-two completely uninterested with Walter Cronkite, the war, or life. He is at the bottom of the barrel as his football career has ended due to a knee injury, college is going rough, and everything seems to becoming unhinged. He drowns his sorrows in pints of Blue Ribbon Beer while listening to the jukebox in the back ground cranking out the last Janis Joplin hit ‘Me and Bobby McGee’ as drugs took her a year ago in 1971. Being at the bottom isn’t always that bad, as you can always look up and see tomorrow; bright, consistent, and there. It’s always there as long as you allow it to be, by surviving today. A simple concept that’s easy to preach when you’re not at the bottom, yet hard to see from the shitty seats of a bad point in your life. A stranger of about forty sits next to the down and out college kid with big muscles, good dreams, and bad knees.
