The Big Apple

12 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12th August, 2006 Location: New York City Time: 9:35am New York City had grown to become my home away from home over the years. I frequented the city with my father as a teenager to attend political debates which showcased some of the most respected political minds in the country. As a result, I had integrated myself into New York life, almost as if I was a New Yorker, only without the distinctive accent. East Rutherford was, after all, only a five minute drive from Manhattan, my destination today. Dean Fisher had recently informed me of a case of enormous magnitude involving a middle-aged woman named Ruth Beckford, herself a New Yorker, who was accused of manslaughter on multiple occasions. The stories about how Beckford had mutilated her husband and children were sickening, to say the least. Within the last week, Beckford had fled her home just as a private investigator began to delve into the case more deeply. Now off the radar, she was the number one priority for the NYPD. Fisher and I sat across from each other in the back of the reasonably priced limousine which we had rented from a local rent-a-car establishment in New Jersey. It was mid-afternoon in the city that never slept, and true to its reputation, New York City was buzzing. Our main objective here today was simply to meet a reporter from the New York Times, who desperately wished to interview the both of us and possibly enlist our support in finding Ruth Beckford. Politicians had power, clout and influence, no matter where they traveled. Fisher and I planned to use our reputations to our advantage. Fisher’s phone began to chirp just as we pulled up to the restaurant on Amsterdam Avenue in Manhattan where our meeting with the reporter was scheduled. He answered it, flipping it open in a well-rehearsed move. I was unimpressed. “Hey baby, what’s up?” Fisher began, speaking to who I could only assume was one of his “lady friends”, as he so affectionately dubbed them. I sat with my arms folded across my chest, locking eyes with Fisher, who was chatting away to his woman friend. I tapped my watch pointedly, and a sudden realization came to Fisher as he noticed that we were five minutes late for the meeting. Fisher was strict about punctuation. “Damn, babe, I gotta jet. I’ll hit you up later on AIM, okay? Alright, bye.” He ended the call, slapping his cell phone shut, his mind elsewhere. I snapped him out of it with a friendly clap of my hands. “Come on Dean, I’ve never seen you so entranced with a woman before. Who is she?” There was a long silence as Fisher stared at me intently, rubbing his goatee with glee. He took a strange enjoyment from keeping his cards close to his chest on relationship matters. A knock on the tinted window of our limousine alarmed us both. I opened the door, inspecting the tall man outside as I did so. Reporters all looked the same, as far I was concerned. This particular gentleman was no different. His features were well-pronounced and his shoulder-length blonde hair hung loosely on his shoulders. He was dressed in a white shirt, brown jacket and black trouser pants, all of which made him appear very distinguished in the morning light. “Gents, it’s great to finally meet you. Come on inside and I’ll fill you in on your roles today,” the man said, curtly. Without a further word, the reporter hastily made his way across the sidewalk to the restaurant from where he had come. Fisher and I exchanged confused glances, both of us on the same wavelength for once regarding this rather unusual reporter. “Just go with it,” Fisher advised me, making his way out of our fancy transportation. “I plan to,” I replied. “We ought to step to it, Dean. Didn’t your mother ever teach you never to leave a reporter waiting? Oh, I almost forgot – your mother never taught you anything.” I nudged Fisher with a friendly elbow, feeling the time was right to lighten the mood with a joke. He simply kept walking towards the restaurant, an almost eerie presence about him. I sensed there was more to this meeting than what I saw it for at face value. I would have to play my cards carefully. The flight from JFK in New York to San Diego hadn’t been the most pleasurable experience of Phil Allen’s life thus far. Turbulence ruined the flight from start to finish, the winds over the country unusually strong on this summer’s afternoon. In stark contrast to the bumpy ride over by air, the drive to Allen’s fiancée’s home on Ocean View Boulevard was the epitome of smooth. The polite taxi driver, who incessantly babbled on the way over from the airport about nonsensical journeys he had taken to the Middle East to search for oil, pulled over as Allen pointed out the house. The taxi driver let out a prolonged whistle. “She’s a beauty, sir. How much did she cost?” Allen inspected his wallet for a few dollar bills, handing them to the quirky driver, who took them with glee. “Oh, this place? It’s not mine, buddy. It belongs to my fiancée. She and I are spending some time together this week. It’s been a while since we’ve seen each other.” The driver nodded to himself, understanding where Allen was coming from. He held the wheel with clammy hands, as Allen prepared to bid farewell to him. “Well, you have fun together, sir. And hey, when you’re on your way back to wherever it is you came from, ask for Larry.” Allen smirked, knowing the man before him now to be simply “Larry”. Allen nodded, shook Larry’s hand, and opened the door. The taxi sped off, its driver desperate to retell his Middle Eastern story once more, he imagined. Contemplating the taxi driver’s stories, Allen began to think about his own. Phil Allen had first met his current fiancée, Sarah Woods, over ten years ago. Allen had always had a way with the opposite sex, and after approaching this woman in a bar on one of his trademark drunken rampages, he had developed a bond with her. It was his first real experience of female companionship on such a regular basis, and it alarmed him at first. Allen refused to admit that he was falling in love with this woman, being the tough, emotionally distant person that he was. Sarah Woods was irresistible to Allen, so much so that the couple became engaged to be married last year. What irked Allen the most was Sarah’s apparent reluctance to go ahead with the wedding. Allen suspected her father was the main culprit in this particular matter, holding her back. Allen snapped out of his flashback, angered at himself for dwelling on such matters, and making himself appear like some kind of stalker standing in front of her home. Allen navigated the meandering pathway to the front door and knocked. Sarah Woods snapped the door open instantly, jumping into the arms of her husband-to-be. “Phil, you’re here at last! I thought your flight was delayed. I – I didn’t know if you’d…,” she trailed off, a cheeky smile appearing on her cute face. Allen kissed his fiancée passionately and led her inside, smelling the pancakes cooking in the kitchen. “You know me so well, sweetheart. Pancakes and everything. So tell me, what kind of crazy things do you have planned for this visit?” Sarah Woods sat down on her pink leather sofa beside Allen, a growing fear detectable in her eyes. It looked as if she was ready to burst into tears right then and there in front of Allen. “What’s going on, Sarah? What’s the matter?” Sarah Woods wiped a tear away from her eye, her mascara beginning to run. Then it dawned on Allen – these were veritable tears of joy. Before making any assumptions, Allen stepped onto the hallowed ground that was “the question”. It was the one piece of information that all men secretly dreaded. “Phil, I’m pregnant,” came the news from Sarah’s lips, the words smacking Allen like a wet backhand across the face. Allen sat in silence, gathering his thoughts. “Sarah… that’s amazing! When did you find out?” “I took the test just a few days after your last visit. It was positive. I checked it again this morning, you know, just to make sure I wasn’t imagining things. The result was the same, Phil,” she whispered excitedly. “You’re going to be a father.” Allen and his fiancé embraced, sharing this special moment with each other. As Allen smelled the aroma from his wife’s combed hair, he couldn’t help but wonder what Wesley Reno, his tag team partner, was up to in New York. This strange thought was quickly dismissed by Allen, who stared into the teary eyes of his fiancée. “So, what now, sweetheart?” Allen pondered. “What happens now? We do baby-shopping!” squealed Sarah with excitement. Allen’s fiancée had discovered his Achilles heel long ago – namely the wrath of shopping – and had capitalized on it on multiple occasions. Today was Allen’s ultimate test. It was him, a trolley of moderately priced baby goods, and a hysterical woman pointing out the next item to purchase. “Life just doesn’t get any better,” Allen mumbled sarcastically under his breath, a mixture of happiness and utter despair overtaking him simultaneously. Amsterdam Avenue in Manhattan, undoubtedly the busiest area of America’s capital, was bustling with activity as I watched the people walk by the restaurant window. I spotted businessmen frantically fumbling with their cappuccinos and briefcases, an element of New York life. There was no rest, no escape. Fisher interrupted my daydream abruptly, digging me with his fist in the arm. I sprung back to life. The reporter, who I had learned was named Jake Wilson, sat across from us, arms folded on the table. Wilson had a calm demeanor, which complemented his journalistic mannerisms. “So, Mister Reno, Mister Fisher, we have to get down to business,” said Wilson, clearing his throat as he unveiled a manila file from under his jacket. “This file contains all the information you’ll need about the Ruth Beckford case. Your task will be to recruit a team of detectives – three maximum – to investigate this case. I trust you are aware, gentlemen, that this is an extremely sensitive matter, for both your parties.” I sensed the threat in Wilson’s voice, although I was not convinced. Fisher nodded to Wilson, understanding the severity of the case at hand. “I’ll need you to head over to the 5th precinct to recruit these detectives within the next couple of hours. Are we clear?” “Perfectly,” I retorted, sending the cocky reporter the clear message that we would not be manipulated by him. “Right, I’ll leave this file with you two to sort out. It’s been a pleasure meeting with you.” Fisher and I both looked down at the yellow manila file before us on the table, slightly stained with Wilson’s pancakes’ maple syrup. I brushed the stain off with a napkin and placed it under my arm, ready to go. Wilson had already left the restaurant, heading up the crowded street into the mayhem. Fisher stopped me as I went to leave. “Where do you think you’re going, Wes? I thought you said we’d be working side by side on this thing. You can’t just up and leave like that!” Fisher was perturbed by my actions, but I simply stood there and shrugged my shoulders, nonplussed by his remonstrations. “I think I’ll be fine working this by myself, actually. I’ll catch up with you later. Maybe you should chat to your girlfriend more. She seems nice,” I said, a fake smile crossing my face. Fisher seethed with anger as I walked out of the restaurant, although I suspected for some reason that my political rival had something up his sleeve. Like an omniscient being, Fisher seemed less than forthcoming with his agenda. Fisher knew that Reno was suspicious, but this didn’t bother him. Reno had never been the perceptive type in the past, and Fisher didn’t expect this to change. He would give him credit, though; he had taken the case by the scruff of its neck. This was exactly what Fisher desired, unbeknownst to Reno. The wheels of Fisher’s plan were already in motion… Ducks n Daisies was a treasure trove of every baby related gift in the world. Allen navigated through the aisles of this deathtrap, all the while Sarah Woods picking out items which would suit their child. Granted, the child was only four weeks old, but his fiancée craved some early action in the baby department. Allen wiped beads of sweat from his forehead, the trolley he pushed already half full with paraphernalia such as rattles, bottles, a large duck toy stuffed with cotton and bizarrely, a book on “How to raise children for dummies”. Allen assumed correctly that this was for him. “Are you enjoying this, sweetheart?” Sarah Woods chirped, as she tossed another mound of baby goods into the trolley. “I really am, Sarah,” Allen replied, feigning his enjoyment of the trip. “Maybe we should take a break soon. You know, get some lunch or something?” His fiancée seemed not to notice his desperate cry for respite from this cruel fate, continuing with the same level of energy as she had started with. Allen knew it was a race against time – it was him or baby shopping. He had to devise a plan to escape. Allen wheeled the trolley forward just as his wife-to-be nabbed a sales assistant to consult him on new baby goods available in-store. He quietly moved away from the trolley into the clothes section of Ducks n Daisies, the department store sparing no expense when it came to clothes for toddlers. Allen spotted something in the corner of his eye. It was a tiny button-up suit babies wore, the only one of its kind in the store. The design was that of a duck and a daisy embroidered on the front, with the words “Quack, quack!” on the reverse side. Allen, never one to give in to his emotional side, was powerless before it in this instance. All the activity around him seemed worthless now, the realization that in eight months’ time he would be a father suddenly dawning on him. He picked up the small baby suit, spinning on his heel as he felt his fiancée’s hand touch his shoulder. “Are you finally accepting it, Phil?” she asked soothingly. “I’m starting to,” he answered, in a distinctly sheepish tone. “I think we’re gonna be alright, Sarah.” “How do you mean?” Allen looked deep into the eyes of his fiancé, his message now crystal clear. His ambition was simple – he would look after this child until the day he died. “We will be great parents,” came the response from Allen, who was more emotional now than Sarah had ever seen him. There was a prolonged moment of silence as Phil Allen and Sarah Woods both came to understand that this baby was more than just buying material goods. This baby was theirs, and theirs alone. It was a symbol of the love they had for each other. “Before this gets all cheesy, why don’t we head over to Wendy’s? I hear they’ve got a great offer for pregnant parents,” Sarah Woods said, smiling broadly as she wheeled the trolley along with her. “Watch it, now,” Allen joked, “you’re the pregnant one!” The couple walked towards the automatic doors of the department store, unaware that they had left the trolley of baby goods behind them. The doors opened with a start, and the two headed out, their futures before them. I had never been fond of extreme heights, and my New York hotel room couldn’t have been much higher up, sitting on the eighty-ninth floor. The view from the wide windows was spectacular nonetheless. I did my utmost to avert my eyes from the tiny dots that were people far below and instead concentrated on the skyline. Negotiations earlier in the day at the 5th precinct had gone very well, the detectives agreeing to place the Ruth Beckford case at the top of their agenda. Politicians’ influence, I suppose you could call it. If these detectives were mentioned in the same news article as myself or indeed Dean Fisher, they would stand a chance of enhancing their career prospects. Understandably, it was a case that detectives were instantly attracted to because of the political interest. This policy of the Democratic Party, namely to piggyback on certain high profile cases, was undertaken usually to promote a camaraderie between politicians and the public domain, more specifically areas like crime and employment. Crime had taken over as the primary area of concern many years ago, and the system had not changed since. This particular case was extremely valuable to all concerned, and would continue to be as such until Miss Beckford was put behind bars. Naturally, I believed I had the edge over Fisher and the Republicans, as Fisher had hardly tried to prevent me from leaving the restaurant earlier with the case file. Although it was unlike him, I assured myself continuously that there was no ulterior motive in Fisher’s behaviour. I still wasn’t convinced. As I concocted in my political mind all the scenarios and possible avenues open to Fisher, I was interrupted by the ringing of my cell phone. I reached into my luggage, removed the cell phone, and flicked it open. It was Phil Allen. “Phil, I’m surprised to hear from you. What’s up, man?” Allen did not reply at first, detecting the worried tone in my voice. “Wes, what’s the matter? It sounds like you’ve got something on your mind. Please don’t tell me it’s one of those political debates. Think about women for once – even men if that’s your thing!” I was taken aback by his comments, but knew at this stage that this was Phil Allen’s idea of humour. “Very funny, Phil,” I commented, unaffected by his remarks. “Tell me, what do you know about the case I’m working on?” “Not a lot, frankly,” came the reply. I pondered for a moment. Was it a good idea to ask my tag team partner to come to New York? It would be temporary, but with the importance attached to this case, there was no way I could fly to San Diego to train for our upcoming match. It was a long shot, I knew, but I decided to ask him. “Wes, you’ve gone all silent. Is everything alright?” “Oh yeah, I’m cool. Phil, I know this is kind of out of the blue, but would you be interested in coming to New York?” There was an awkward silence on the other end of the line, as I listened to my tag team partner and mentor breath a sigh of exasperation into the phone. “Wes, this isn’t really the best time. I’m here with Sarah, we’ve just found out she’s pregnant,” he said, sounding displeased with the invitation. “Wow, great news, Philly. The thing is, man, there is no other way we could prepare for this tag team tournament that’s coming up. I know I’ve competed in a diner match and all that, but tag team wrestling is your thing,” I said, putting across my point nicely. Now it was time for the death blow. I shied away from showering Allen with compliments usually, not wanting to expand his ego. This was an exception to the rule. “The truth is, Philly, I need you here to train me for this match. There’s no other way out of this.” Allen chuckled to himself, realizing how hard that must have been for me. He acquiesced, agreeing that he would meet me at JFK airport on Thursday morning. We said our goodbyes and I hung up. My outlook on the world of wrestling was changing quickly. A few weeks ago, the idea of a training session had me running a mile; now, it seemed to be more worthwhile. I was now ambitious about my wrestling career, and I was ready for the challenge before us. I felt a sudden adrenaline rush as I stared out the window of my hotel room. The sky truly was the limit for The Coalition.
