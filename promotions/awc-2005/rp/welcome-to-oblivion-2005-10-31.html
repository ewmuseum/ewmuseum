Welcome to Oblivion

31 October 2005

Date: 31/10/05 When something happens in Kingston, it doesn’t take long for the majority of the community to find out. This, coupled with the fact that Dan didn’t exactly hide my beating of Vico made it quite the news around the community. Especially with Father Dave Pearson. You see, the Kingston community is pretty much centred around the single pilgrim church that dates back to the very colonisation of America itself. That same church has withstood emancipations, famines, book burnings, holocausts and whatever other major event you’d care to mention. Throughout them all, the Kingston family has stuck by the St. Peter’s Memorial Church through thick and thin. Sure they’ll go through phases of having few people arriving, but something always swings the Catholicism right back into you when a major disaster or event occurs. There’s nothing like a fire and brimstone sermon to beat the tar out of you and make sure that you retreat to your prayer books. So naturally, when Pearson got wind of the way things went down in O’Grady’s gym last week, he made every effort to fit it in for his sermon for the following Sunday. Never one to let major events slide, around the time of September 11th, the first thing he was able to say was his proclamation of the absurdity and failure of morality in society. Nothing to do with how bad it was for the victims, or how the families felt. No, it was time to wake up and realise little Jimmy has been exposed to too much porn and isn’t being taught the right way. He brought this all upon us, not the government and their idiotic actions tabled under the title of ‘Diplomacy.’ Apparently (because I wasn’t there) Father Pearson spewed forth a vitriol of acidic bile towards the seedy side of Kingston, absolving Dan completely from the whole affair. He spared me no venom however. His attacks upon the incident led to a personal assault upon my own character, claiming me to be the very incarnation of the devil himself the way he was talking. I may as well grow a tail and sport some horns. He decreed from his pulpit that I was a lying poison, waiting to fill the youth with my corrupt views and how I would destroy all that was left of morality. Oh that was his second tactic by the way. As soon as the word morality left his lips he was inspired, he was empowered to lump everyone into the sermon then. He bound them to his words, accusing them of it being their fault for allowing me to return to the community, their fault for allowing such a heathen to spread his sinister ways through the flock… …of course it was neither her nor there the fact that he was the first to credit himself with my return, claiming I had come to find God. Oh no, once it’s a couple of weeks, it doesn’t count anymore to ol’ Father Pearson. This is what I got back from Dan, who was present at the service itself. He was sitting in front of me now, watching my reaction to the entire tirade, the parting of the Red Sea to Father Pearson. Dan tugged at his hair irritably while he waited, the red hair slowly fading to grey. We were sitting in his house on the porch, calmly watching the sun set over Ulster county. ‘So what do you think?’ His words seemed forced, like he knew he shouldn’t ask the question but at the same time, knowing that if he doesn’t ask he’d be screwed and I’d be in a bad mood for the rest of the week. He knew I had an important match coming up. Looking at him I only had one vision, one thing I could retort him with. ‘I’m thinking that I’m expecting the community with pitchforks and torches any moment now.’ A smile moved outwards from the centre of O’Grady’s wrinkled face and he clapped me over my shoulder, happy that I wasn’t in as dour a mood as he expected. I’m glad he took it that way. Because something was bothering me about it. And I couldn’t quite…nah scratch that I knew exactly what was bothering me… …And it had for quite a long time. For my short time in Ireland (while it has been my place of birth, I’ve haven’t lived there in twenty years), there was one overriding judgement laying over each and every person that was present in Ireland. For nigh on forever the Roman Catholic church had used its iron fist to control the population of Ireland through its decrees and statements. They chose to decide what was right and wrong under their eyes. This was paramount when I was growing up in the 70’s and while the revolution of the 60’s was being felt all across America and Europe, Ireland still was firmly under the grip of the Church, the elderly priests going backwards and trying to reinstate Latin masses. This is the same Catholic Church that banned its congregation from using contraceptives, because it was barring an act of God. This was the same Church that held its faith in a higher power but never took a stand publicly on the atrocities that were going on in Northern Ireland. They casually forgot about them, too scared to fight for the cause of their so-called people because they had the possibility of being shot by paramilitary actions. It never occurred to them that their flock was being gunned down on a regular basis anyway. This was the same Catholic Church that held sway in the schools around the country and had the licence to beat children with their hands, fists, belts, sticks, whatever they felt like if the children were being ‘impious.’ It was not a happy time for me and it was unsurprising to see how many people left, including myself, in the emigration of the 80’s as freedom in the USA beckoned. I remember my school though, St. Josephs of Newbridge, a national primary school that prided itself on modelling upstanding boys for the community. Let’s not forget that there were no co-educational schools in Ireland, oh no. That just wouldn’t be right, it could provoke pre-marital sex don’t you know? Anyway, our teacher for my last year in that school was Father Thomas Troy, a rough and unwelcome man who looked like a pug. You could tell that nothing had worked out in his life simply from his demeanour and that being a priest was his only option for a steady job. So he took his bitterness out on each and every one of the boys in his class for the slightest error that he could find each and every day. It was a cold winter’s day and we were looking forward to Christmas, the respite from school and the chance to muck about and forget. It was easy to see we were all anxious for class to finish so we could run home and not have to go back for a precious two weeks. Fr. Troy was having none of it mind you. He was determined to have his own Christmas present… ‘Jack, you still there?’ Dan broke me from my thoughts and brought me back to the present. ‘Yeah, I am.’ ‘I guess you didn’t hear me then. What are you going to do about it?’ ‘About what?’ ‘About Pearson and his ramblings. Are you going to address them in any way?’ I’d totally forgotten about his own brand of perversion I had been so caught up in the other one, but they were equally disgusting in their own ways. Well, maybe not equal. ‘Yeah, I’m going to go and talk to him right now. Best to get it over with and be done with it.’ ‘You want me to go with you?’ ‘No, it’s better that I do this alone.’ ‘Ok. Well, I’ll speak to you later then, I think I hear Jessica calling for dinner. You want some before you head off?’ ‘No, tell her thanks though.’ Good old Jessica (O’Grady’s daughter who was now about 27 or so) she’d never been snapped up by any of the boys and I guess I could relate to it. Who would want a gruff, rough and strict man such as O’Grady as their father in law? I could definitely relate to that. Jessica came bounding through the porch door and looked at us both, beaming with her clean white teeth shining out at us. It complemented her plain but charming face, big blue eyes still looking like she was twelve and her brown locks of hair trussed up in pigtails. She was dusting herself off in her apron and now looked like she was going to scold both of us. ‘Would you two old fogies get off your asses and come in for some tea. You know you’ll only catch your arthritis sitting out here this time of day!’ She flashed a wink at me, easier to do so because Dan was facing the opposite direction to her. I smiled and could see that Dan had fallen for it hook, line and sinker. ‘Who are you calling fogy? I’ll have you know…’ Just then he slipped slightly and looked like he was going to crack his hip off the wooden porch. Thankfully for his reflexes he got himself upright and looked quite red in the face at that. Jessica was holding her hand in front of her mouth so that she wouldn’t laugh. Sheepishly Dan continued. ‘Maybe I should be inside!’ Dan ambled past her and into the house, leaving the two of us alone on the porch. ‘Are you coming?’ ‘No, I’ve got some pressing business awaiting me in the church.’ ‘Ah, don’t be too hard on Father Pearson, you know what he’s like. Stop by for some food on your way back though, you look like you’re famished.’ ‘I will Miss O’Grady.’ ‘Oh don’t you start with your pleasantries!’ She smacked me with her tea towel and then giggled to herself before heading inside. It left me, all alone on the porch. Ah well, time to head to Sacred Heart. Thankfully it wasn’t too far away. The community being what it is, the church was placed in the very town centre of Kingston. It was a monstrosity amongst smaller, more elegant buildings and the church itself was basically one big phallus for everyone to stroke and get off for the pleasure of the Lord. Born out of a Puritan Catholic background, the church epitomised this following. It was shaped in the form of the cross, and everything was grey on the outside. No colour, no design, just one big block of stone to go from. It really was an eye catcher I can tell you. Standing in front of it however it did impress on one of its feats. It managed in all its glory to make you look wholly insignificant to the power of God. Sadly, (for them of course, I didn’t give a shit) God held no sway over me. All because of that time… ‘Hmmph, I never thought I’d see you standing before this.’ I pulled my head from staring at the sky and looked directly in front of me. I knew who it was from the thick Irish accent he still held. It was Father Pearson alright and he hadn’t changed one bit since I’d been gone. Imagine if you will a terrier’s face and then strip it bare of all hair, right down to its skin. Now, stretch it slightly outwards on either side and you’ve a good idea of Pearson’s face. To complement this (though I’d hardly call it a complement) were straw wires of hair hanging from his mostly bald head. To finish it all off he was wearing his monk’s robe, the name of it escapes me right now, and that’s basically what you need to know. ‘And I never thought you’d get any more pompous but life’s full of surprises isn’t it?’ ‘What do you want heathen?’ Man he didn’t waste time with the strong words. He’s on top form already. ‘I want you to butt out of my business, I don’t come in and ransack your choices in life so why should you?’ ‘Well by making your actions so public you’ve left yourself open to such criticism.’ ‘I’ll give you that but don’t be a pussy and avoid me then in all other avenues but your paltry church. Be a fucking man and face me like now.’ ‘I shall not hear you when you lower yourself to cursing on God’s patch. I see no reason why I am going behind your back. You choose not to come to mass, I have made no attempt to hide my feelings on the subject. It was there to be heard, no matter whether or not you wanted to be there.’ ‘Well, if you’re so confident, tell me now.’ Father Pearson wasn’t the smallest person in the world, but then again neither was I. As such he was a good five inches smaller in height. Nevertheless he was up to my challenge and stood proudly, channelling the phallic device behind him for his power. ‘No problem. I find your actions disgusting and repulsive. Your actions this past week in Kingston itself need not be explained for the repugnancy of them, but this isn’t the end of it. Your treatment of women, whether or not it is character based in this organisation that you work. I find it abhorrent that you get away with such antics. You play with people’s minds and get away with it because you hide behind a character, a façade. You are nothing but a shell, built to harbour feeble thoughts!’ The words echoed round the courtyard that surrounded the St. Peter’s Church and left a hollow ring in my ears. It was like he was speaking to my memories, speaking to the pain…. …but I relented, keeping them down. Instead I began to laugh, laugh at his unintentional irony. Pearson was irritated by this and let it be known. ‘Why are you laughing at me philistine? Come, show me your knowledge that you claim to have so. Indoctrinate in your beliefs and show me the light.’ ‘You don’t understand do you? You call me a manipulator when this is all the Catholic Church has done for two thousand years! Each week you give a sermon that is exactly the same in all but the words. It may be dressed up different but you speak the same meaning every time. It is built to brainwash and placate your flock. Nothing but submission is all you require. Blatant submission to something that is based on so much hearsay and ignorance to fact that I find it hard to believe that you still have so many followers in this day and age. There’s a reason you teach it from an early age, because that way you can catch the children before they can learn to talk. You abuse them and treat them to the wonder of God, doing how you feel like, whenever you feel like! I’m sick of it and you have lost this man!’ I abruptly turned and began to walk .Then he held me back with his words. ‘So much for courage in the face of your convictions. It’s good to see you still can’t face your demons.’ I wanted to him then, but I couldn’t, it would only prove to him of his perceptions. ‘So much for treat your neighbour as you would wish to be treated Father.’ I walked again and left him, left him standing before his rock. He shook his head behind me and his words came to me on the wind like a whisper. ‘You were never my neighbour.’ Maybe that was true…but blame Father Troy for that one…. And then I cried.
