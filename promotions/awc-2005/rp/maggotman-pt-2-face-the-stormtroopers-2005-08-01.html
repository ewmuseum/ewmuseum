Maggotman pt. 2: Face The Stormtroopers

1 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/8/05 ”One wise man once dreamt, every dream there was… KILL THAT MAN! KILL THAT MAN! KILL KILL KILL FUCK HIS CORPSE TOO!” With the screaming opening line, the band launches into another piece of apparently random instrument torture. Bach and Reeves inspect the scene from the back of the room, straying away from the flashing lights that flicker on and off in the ceiling like heartbeats. The floor in front of them is full of black-clad kids, all smashing into each other, all the while a band covered in bloody white jumpsuits pumps out every inch of noise they can from their chosen pieces of musical production. After 30 seconds, most of the kids stop, and start chanting ”TOO SLOW, TOO LONG!” at the band, who finish their song prematurely and launch straight into the next one. Bach shakes his head while Reeves flips through his notepad, apparently immune to the aural thrash around him. Finally, after what seems like hours to Bach, the band stops and the dancers spread out in the room. The light suddenly flicker into their full glory, almost cutting in their brightness, and the white-faced people scatter from the floor like cockroaches. Reeves taps Bach on the shoulder and the two start moving, crossing the well-lit floor and to the other side of the room, watched constantly by tens of eyes, calculating their purposes and expressing their hatred towards the two outsiders. Bach leans closer to Reeves, careful that his voice doesn’t rise above the general murmur that has taken over the general atmosphere in the damp cellar that now serves as a concert stage. Bach: ”Joe… How the hell can you stand that sort of stuff?” Reeves: ”Easily… My son plays the same kind of crap twentyfour-seven.” Bach looks at Reeves with a curious look on his face before continuing. Bach: ”You actually understand what they’re singing about?” Reeves: ”Hell no. It’s all the same to my ears. I just block it out.” Bach: ”Good.” Apparently satisfied that his partner isn’t going to join the fray when the next band comes up, the two detectives duck into the corridor leading to what can loosely termed backstage. A gloomy kid in white makeup backed up by two muscular skinheads stand up to block their way, but a flash of the badge sends the trio grudginly to the side. Bach and Reeves walk through the dusty corridor, checking into differnet rooms as they pass by. They find several youngsters passed on one of the floors, with needles still sticking out of their strapped arms and their eyes nearly bulging out of their heads, but the two pass by them without as much as dodging their twitching legs. They aren’t here for the druggies: both know that if they wanted to raid this place, they could haul pretty much everyone here away on one charge or another. They also hope that everyone here knows that too. Finally, one room yields what they came here looking for. With red pentagrams adorning the walls like so many twisted, angled eyes, a man sits in front of a full-length mirror. A white jumpsuit, stained with red and more pentagrams, lays on a bench next to him. The man is naked from waist up, clad in only a pair of black sweat pants. He is skinny yet muscular, with black hair coming down to his neck and covering his face as well. It is unclear whether he is meditating or staring at his image in the mirror: his eyes cannot be seen. Bach and Reeves inspect him and notice a bit of text sticking out from under his hair. The man apparently notices this, as he chuckles and then lifts the hair on the back of his head so that the detectives can see the word tattooed to the back of his neck. HATE. Bach and Reeves stare at the tattoo for a while until Bach steps aside and goes to inspect the room while Reeves steps forwards and pulls out his notepad, flipping through it until he finds a page full of text. Reeves: ”Trey Carmichael…” The half-naked man suddenly begins to laugh in a dry, mocking manner, and Bach immediately turns towards him to try and shut him up with an icy glare. The man doesn’t heed this, but still continues to laugh. Finally, he shuts up once Reeves pulls out his badge and flashes it in the mirror, but the man still keeps on staring in the mirror and does not turn towards the detectives. Clearly ticked off, Reeves adopts a more formal tone of voice, sounding cold as he mouths off his next sentence. Reeves: ”Mr. Carmichael, we are here to question you about…” Man: ”You got the wrong guy.” Reeves: ”Excuse me?” Man: ”I said you got the wrong guy, pigs. Azagtoth isn’t here.” Bach: ”Aza who? Stop playing with us, kid, or you’ll be going out of here with your hands cuffed!” The threat doesn’t seem to intimidate the man, as he just laughs and suddenly bends his arms behind his head. As neither Bach or Reeves move forward to arrest him, the man laughs again, mockingly, and pulls his arms back to his lap, never moving his head an inch. Man: ”I knew it. You haven’t got the guts or the reason to do it, pig. Save your little NYPD Blue routine for the weak, beat cop.” Reeves: ”*ahem* Let’s start again, shall we? I am Detective Reeves, and this is Detective Bach of the Chigago Police Department. We’re here to ask some questions from one Trey Carmichael, and…” Man: ”And I told you, you got the wrong guy. I ain’t Trey. The person you want is named Azagtoth.” Bach: ”Is… Azagtoth here?” Man: ”Hahaha! No, he’s not. He’s in Europe, pig. Good luck in trying to reach him there.” Bach and Reeves exchange glances: they were expecting the people to be uncooperative, and they’re really filling out the role quite well so far. Bach steps a bit closer to the man while Reeves flips open a fresh page in his notepad and pulls out a fountain pen, checking that he can still write it before nodding to Bach. Swiftly, Bach moves in and twists the man around by his neck, raising his face up towards his own. The man’s face is twisted in a manic grin, and his pupils are the size of needle pricks. Bach shakes his head as he realizes that the man’s brain must be addled with so many drugs that it’s a wonder he has managed to survive with the chemical coctail inside his head. Bach’s face hardens and he shakes the man a bit, noting down that the man has a similar ”HATE”-tattoo at the base of his neck. Bach: ”OK, punk. We’re going to ask you some questions, you’re going to answer them, and then we’ll be on our merry way. OK?” The man just laughs weakly and places his hand on Bach’s wrist. Bach instantly tightens his grip of the man’s neck, but the man makes no move to either release himself or relent the pressure. Instead, he taps Bach on his arm, as if egging him on to do it even harder. Man: ”Haha, pig… This is police brutality. Should I go to your department to show off my bruises tomorrow?” Bach: ”Try it, punk. I’d arrest you for resisting arrest faster than you can think.” Man: ”You could try… But you wouldn’t leave here alive.” Bach and Reeves stop to listen. In the sudden silence, they can hear someone on the other side of the wall in the hallway, and through the open door they can hear SOMETHING being drawn out. Something metallic. Something sharp. Slowly, Bach releases the man from his hold, his eyes fixed on the door. The man just laughs and whoever was standing there apparently walks away like nothing happened.Bach just shakes his head and then steps back, all the while looking down at the man in disgust. The tattooed man begins to slowly sway back and forth on his bench, looking up at Bach to ansewr his glare. Reeves: ”We’re investigating an assault case that happened a few days ago around this neighborhood. You know anything about it?” Man: ”Should I? I don’t follow the normal world. It’s not interesting enough for me. Not enough death.” Reeves: ”Will you answer some questions?” Man: ”Why should I? I’m not Azagtoth, and I have nothing to do with your case beyond a mild amusement over some weakling letting themselves be beaten.” Bach: ”You know anything about someone named ’Maggotman’?” The blunt question shuts the man up and makes him lose his smile as well. Bach looks victoriously over at Reeves, who is ready to write away at a moment’s notice. After a moment of awkward silence, the man takes a deep breath. Man: ”Yeah… Yeah, I know Maggotman. Isn’t that a song?” Bach: ”EXCUSE ME?” Man: ”Yeah, yeah, I’m sure it’s a song. It goes like… Wait a sec.” The man starts tapping the floor with his heel and starts doing what looks like a complex drum beat in the empty air. For a moment, only his arms swish here and there before he stops and smiles broadly at Bach, snapping his fingers as if realizing something new. Man: ”Yeah, now I remember. This rotten stench fills the air, I’m your loving darling, I’m your Maggotman… Heh, it’s a good song too.” Bach looks like he is ready to snap, and he draws his arm back as if preparing to hit the man. Reeves steps forward and raises his hand, and Bach puts his arm back down. The man sitting on the chair notices this, however, and starts laughing even harder, prompting Bach to stomp the floor and turn around, cursing out loud. The man laughs and then looks back at Reeves, smiling a pitying smile. Man: ”I feel for you if you have to work with this idiot. Police force’s finest, indeed. Now you… You seem like a reasonable man. I’ll talk to you, but out of pity if nothing else. Him, however… He has to go. He can wait for you outside. That’s the only way I’ll talk, and that’s me being extra nice to you.” Reeves gives Bach a helpless look, who returns it with a scoff. Bach looks down at the man, who’s still staring at Reeves, and then looks back at his partner who now looks more pleading than anything. Bach scoffs again, his eyes growing wide in disbelief. Bach: ”You’re SERIOUS? ARE YOU GODDAMN SERIOUS, JOE? What the hell is wrong with you? Let’s book this bastard, he’s got enough stuff in his system to put a whole gang of narcs behind the bars.” The man lets out a laugh and slightly turns his head, looking at Bach from the corner of his eye. Man: ”Funny you should mention that… I got prescription for everything I take. And if that’s not enough for you, I got a credible expert waiting to testify that I have a medical condition to match every bottle I own. You’d lose in the long run, my friend. Leave it be.” Shaking his head, Bach stares into nothingess for a minute before he spits on the floor and looks at Reeves. Bach: ”You sure you want to do this?” Reeves: ”Yeah, I’m sure. Just wait outside. I’ll be out soon.” Bach mutters something and walks out of the door, noting that the goth and his two skinhead helpers have laired next to the doorway, lounging around. Without anything being visible, Bach can just imagine the tire irons and shivs cradled by the hooligans. Without giving it a second thought, Bach walks away, leaving Reeves in the room with the man. Now, without any visible word or command, the door suddenly closes, leaving Reeves in silence. He slowly looks around, but the man laughs reassuringly and leans back on the nothingess behind him. Man: ”Relax, pig. It’s soundproof. We got the privacy you wanted now. Ask your questions and then you can go home.” Reeves finishes checking his surroundings and then neatly folds his notepad back into the pocket of his jacket. The man looks on with mild interest, until Reeves puills out a seat and sits right in front of him. Reeves: ”Answer me. What do you know about Hate?” The man instantly freezes and grabs the tattoo on his throat even before he can think clearly. Reeves looks on as the man begins squirming, before speaking in a low tone. Man: ”What… I thought this was about an assault…” Reeves: ”It is. It is about the assault and Azagtoth. But I know that Azagtoth is tied with the Cult of the Fifth Horseman… And so is Hate. Who is he?” Man: ”I… I…” Reeves: ”Look, you scumbag. Hate has my daughter. My daughter ran away from home months ago. Now I learned that she’s apparently tagging along with someone calling themselves Hate, and that isn’t healthy for my little girl. Who is he and where can I find him?” Man: ”I…” Reeves: ”ANSWE ME.” The man sits in stunned silence for a moment before he starts twisting his fingers and biting the backs of his hands. Only now Reeves seems to notice the fine scars that seem to emanate from beneath the black tattoo on the man’s neck. Reeves stares at the pale lines while the man begins to whimper and then closes his eyes. After a few deep breaths, the man is apparently calm enough to continue. Man: ”You… Want to know who Hate is. I’ll tell you who he is. Or WHAT he is.” The man suddenly opens his eyes and leans closer to Detective Reeves, his eyes as large as they can go and his face twisted into a feeble, weak smile. Man: ”You have children. You know when you switch off the light at night and the REAL night comes. The thing in the corner, the thing that makes your children wish that you’d rot in hell for not letting them stay up any later, THAT THING which eats away at your own children right under your own nose is Hate. Hate is the final fear of death: it’s the last thing you see before you know you’re going to die. It’s the paranoia of looking out of your window and seeing shady people on the street: Hate is the army of evil camping right behind your door.” Reeves stares at the man even more intense as he begins to gibber something unintelligent before setling on biting his hand and then repeating the same phrase over and over again, much like the girl at the hospital did. Reeves cannot help but feel that these two have been through the same kind of hell: both have been mutilated beyond sanity. Man: ”Cross your arms or the Devil will break your neck. Cross your arms or the Devil will break your neck. Cross your arms or the Devil will break your neck.” Reeves shakes his head and then grabs the man by the shoulders, shaking him violently. The man seems to focus, but only for a moment before drifting away again into his own worlds. Reeves shakes him again, his voice carrying an audible edge of anger in it as he yells into the man’s face. Reeves: ”I don’t give a shit! He’s got my girl and I want her back! Now tell me, where can I find them? I want BOTH of them, Hate and Azagtoth alike, hanging for whatever they’ve done! WHERE ARE THEY?” The man once again focuses on Reeves, and smiles again, as if trying to laugh but not having the strength to do so. Man: ”They’re in Europe… I told you. Find Hate, and you’ll find Azagtoth. They can’t be separated. And… Angelica is with them too. Reeves’ eyes flare open as he tries to keep the man awake, but slowly the tattooed weirdo slips into drug-induced stupor. Reeves: ”SHE IS? Where? Where is she!? Tell me! Where is she? ANSWER ME!” But the man is gone already. Slowly, not wanting to let go of the only real hint he has learned in a while, Reeves lets the man fall on the floor with a soft thud. He straightens out his jacket and then pushes open the door to find the corridor empty and deserted. Reeves spits on the floor and then begins to walk towards the stairs leading up to the street. Poor Bach, Reeves thinks. He thinks this is about the crime. But it’s not. It’s about revenge. Cleanse the Impure. Raise the Warcry.
