One for Luck, Ch.6

11 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11th of Nov. at 8:00 pm One for Luck, Chapter 6 (Prometheus: Section 4) Entwined in Pain I moved steadily up the ramp with McGrady on my shoulder, blood oozing out of his body and down my shirt. I could hear the footsteps, the calling of death - a buzzer, the sound of a roaring bullet and the yells of pain and screams. This was certainly an experience, I thought to myself as I hurdled toward a step, trying to keep McGrady on my shoulders. It was obvious that he was over-weight; my biggest fear was that he was dead. I managed to reach the hanger-bay door, but it was a cardboard cut-out that suddenly caught my attention. There was a life size cut out of a man, a man I had worked with, an enemy and now he was the spokesman for a murderous drug. Alex Strider was stood in front of me, a wide grin on his cardboard face holding a bottle of Promecil. I didn’t have time to linger as the men continued, guns blazing and echoes of ricocheting bullets alerted me to the exit. I hoisted McGrady into a rigid position and with a burst of energy attempted a run. I could see the men behind me through the corner of my eyes. My heart pounding, my stomach churning and my muscles aching. This was excruciating. Another shot went off! A sniper! Brilliant! I had time to exaggerate, but the sound of McGrady coughing in pain, with a splutter of blood, meant the sniper was aiming for the man on my back. I kicked into panic mode and saw the mound of earth, safety! I thought to myself. The loose earth gave way under my feet, with the dead-weight on my shoulders. I continued to push; eventually I hoisted McGrady off of my shoulder and rolled him over the side to safety. He was safe, I dove and in the right time too, another shot fired, but the bullet hit the mound of earth, earth blew into the air, an explosion of contact. I lay on my back, coughing. Dirt, grass and some muck had made its way into my mouth and down my throat. I didn’t dare look over the rim of the mound of earth, instead I focused on McGrady and applied pressure to as many of the bullet wounds I could. He had slipped out of consciousness. The Nissan pick-up came charging around the corner and I lay out flat. Kelli slipped out of the driver seat and arrived. I shot up and got her to help me with McGrady. “Pierce?” she said with panic, gazing at the blood drenched into my clothing. “Are you …?” she said, but couldn’t finish. “No, I’m okay… We have to get out of here. They’re right behind me …” I turned around and felt the handle of a gun hit the side of my face. I saw Kelli’s fear in her face, but soon blacked out. *************** My eyes fluttered. I saw a small light and became delusional. The small light was very like the tunnel toward heaven! Was I dead? This was something of pure delusion, as I found myself on a chair; my arms were bound with rope. I was wearing nothing but my jeans and boots and my lip had been split open. My head felt like a conga-line, every motion hurt. I fluttered my eyes once more and saw McGrady, standing up right, removing fake blood packs. I shook my head, I felt strange. My body was tingling, my mouth dry and even though I was semi-conscious my only worry was Kelli. Where was she? McGrady turned to me, hands on his hips. “I see you have decided to join us,” he mumbled. “Why?” I muttered. My voice low and my throat dry. “Why what?” McGrady turned to me, and placed is mucky size twelve boot on my knee. He seemed to be fine, he had never been shot, which left one conclusion. He was working with the organization behind Promecil, but if that’s true, then he purposefully, knowingly led me to my near death experience and now, I find I am sat here with him. “Why are you doing this?” I managed to splutter. I could feel my throat clogging up, gagging for water. “Pierce, you are more gullible than I thought. It was so easy to play you,” he laughed. “Your grandfather too. Did you enjoy the show?” he questioned. By show he meant his pretend death and the bullets that supposedly entered his body. “Where’s Kelli?” I asked. “Ah, the whore …” he said cockily and removed his shoe. I drove forward in anger, trying my best to hit him, but both my hands and legs were bound and all I had accomplished was nearly falling off the chair and hitting the concrete floor beneath me. He turned, wide eyed and smiled. “Calm down, lover-boy,” he said. “Your whore is safe. I gave her a little something to help her sleep.” McGrady moved the light and shone it in the corner, Kelli was in a heap, asleep on the concrete floor, and her arms were tied behind her back. “Where are we?” I asked. “If I told you, I’d have to kill you,” he said. “Isn’t that your plan anyway,” I retorted. He pulled a chair across and a jagged blade. “Perhaps!” he said, balancing the blade between his thumb and baby finger. “But where would be the fun in that?” he questioned, but not to me, more a rhetorical question to himself. A question and an answer, only he could answer. “Now, I can make this easy or hard. What way will you have it?” I sat frozen in my chair. There was a sudden memory flash of Lois, sat in the abandoned warehouse in Albany, tied and gagged to a chair, being tortured by Harrington, but this time the situation was in reverse and it was a different capital city. I didn’t answer and he smirked. “Have it your way,” he said and dragged the jagged blade along my arm, slicing it. It was a burning sensation and I gritted my teeth in my pain. I remained silent and he moved up, slicing my arm again, staying away from the major blood vessels, his intent was torture. “Come on!” he smirked. “Scream…” he said sadistically and grinned as he ran a third slice parallel to the other two. This one was deeper and a lot more painful, I winced and bit my bottom lip, I felt the blood oozing from my lip and lading on the tip of my tong. “O-K-” I said in pain. “Awe, I was beginning to enjoy the torture,” he said. He placed the bloodied knife onto a metal tray, covered in various syringes, knifes and saws. I didn’t want to ask what they were for. “What do you want?” I asked. “How much do you know?” he asked. “Huh? You know more than me. You were working with my grandfather, you saw his investigative laboratory,” I said, confused. “Ha, see, stupid! You don’t understand. Your grandfather was smart; he remained attentive to two men, the men he trusted most, but he was suspicious of me. Perhaps that is why I decided to play him and bring his biggest weakness into this,” he said with a grin and folded his arms. “What weakness?” I asked. Hopefully I could prolong this long enough for some sort of help to arrive. “You don’t know? It is you; you are his one weakness Pierce. You think it would be his money, or his whore for a wife, but it is you. The expelled college student, the rebel. Basically you used to be a mess, correct?” I wasn’t going to reply to something he already knew, so I remained starring at his green eyes. They had a black façade over them; he was wearing a cloak to hide his true self. John McGrady was two men, the man earlier was kind and brave and the man before me was a brute, abusive and sadistic. Perhaps this was the true John McGrady. “Okay, let’s skip the formalities and introductions. I want to know what you know about Omnikon, Promecil and GOONE,” he said and began dangling the knife again. “I don’t know much …” I said. It wasn’t good enough. He inched his chair forward and put the sharp point of the blade against my stomach. He began slowly to dig it in, pressure adding to more pressure, causing kinetic energy and a hell of a lot of pain. “Don’t play games, Pierce. I know you know all about James Lawler, who GOONE are and what you found in Omnikon,” he said and removed the blade. I looked down and saw a small puncture wound. He was going to torture me until somebody decided to come rescue me. I sounded like a damsel in distress now, and I wasn’t proud of it. “If you work for them, why are you asking me?” I said stupidly. My anger had always played the bigger, more dominant role over my sensibility and commonsense, they remained recessive when I was put into a corner. “You would think that, wouldn’t you?” he said and picked up a five inch needle and drove it into my thigh. I screamed in pain. I looked at my thigh, only the end of the needle was protruding from my jeans, the rest had pierced the muscle, with its slender point and thin metal casing. I swallowed and turned to him. He smiled and picked up a second and drove it through my other thigh. I screamed in pain again, this was excruciating and a tear rolled down my cheek. He leant back and watched me squirm with pain and shock. “A form of Russian torture isn’t it lovely. Now, where were we? Oh yes, you were going to tell me all you know …” he said and leant forward again and pressed record on a Dictaphone. I gulped and began to speak. “James Lawler was a manger for a wrestler that used to be in my company. He supplied his client with the drug Promecil and it gave him an adrenaline boost. The wrestler was found out and fired. James Lawler then used his younger athletes as test subjects for the drug, till one of them died. Then he backed out, I think that is why GOONE finished him off.” “Keep going,” he muttered. “That led me to Hancook Park, that is why I was there…I saw him being killed. Omnikon are the manufacturers of the drug. GOONE are the distributors for the street drug addicts and local sports men or women that need help winning … That’s it … that is all I know,” I said. I was beginning to feel queasy. Something was in those needles. I could feel darkness closing in around me. He stared at me and knew what was happening. “Ah, the affect of the drug is kicking in!” he smiled. “Drug?” I questioned. “Promecil in liquid form. You are the first to test it, and could be the last …” He laughed and stood up. “I best be off, enjoy your last couple of breaths. I just pity the lady when she wakes up and finds her only comfort … dead,” He put the Dictaphone in his pocket and walked to the exit. Suddenly the door burst open and six men came in, guns blazing. I heard the voice of my grandfather in the background. My head felt heavy. The needles were now somehow being removed. I saw the blurred vision of Kelli in front of me; she held one of the needles. My grandfather untied me and I fell into the arms of Kelli. “Hang on, kid …” my grandfather whispered and I fell into the darkness. A large blanket, waiting to sweep me away. *************** Darkness rushed by me. I could hear church bells echoing through the land. The tweeting of birds in flight, flocking and migrating from season to season. The grazing of leaves and trees against glass. The beeping of car horns. I felt like I was walking on air, light and breezy. My heart had stopped pounding, my throat moist and my eyes shut. The voice of AC/DC ran through my mind. I was either dead or falling to my death. The silent beeping of a machine rang in my ear. I opened my eyes, but became blinded by the sharp lights. I was on a table, being tended to by surgeons. A woman looked at me, a surgical suit on. She applied a plastic mouth piece and tube over my mouth and I felt the darkness soon float back around me, slipping me into a deep sleep. When I arose from the sleep, I felt very drossy and heavily medicated. My arm was in a bandage. The three slices cleaned and stitched. Kelli was sat on the bed, holding my hand. Tears in her eyes. My grandfather was opposite her, writing and on the mobile. I looked toward Kelli and she looked up, seeing I was awake, she hugged me. 'Ouch,' I said, remembering the stitches that seemed to be on my stomach too. She leant back and kissed my hand. 'How are you, kid?' my grandfather asked. He came over and looked at me. 'Tingly ...' I said. Kelli smiled and held my hand in a tight grip, tears still flooding down her cheeks. “How’d I get here?” I asked, with barely any recollection of what had happened to me. “I traced McGrady’s mobile. I knew he was up to something. When I arrived, my men apprehended him, untied Kelli, but we arrived too late. You had been injected twice with the Promecil drug. You have been in surgery for almost eight hours. They had to remove it from your system and mend you punctured muscle. We were told you had a 50% chance of coming through, the drug had already reached some of your major arteries, if it had reached your heart ….” “I would have died,” I said swallowing the upset I knew was welling up inside me. “Yes,” he said and patted my chest. “I’m glad you are still with us, kid,” he smiled and ran his hand along my forehead. The protective, fatherly touch of affection. I only wished my own father had been as caring as my grandfather. “What of McGrady?” I asked with worry. “He’s in jail, waiting for his punishment. He’ll be getting attempted murder, torture and kidnapping, along with first degree murder,” my grandfather said and shook his head. “First degree murder?” I asked. “He killed Dwight,” my grandfather said with a creased brow. “His epithelial’s were found on Dwight’s sports coat, poor kid didn’t know what hit him.” “I have a wrestling match on the weekend, am I good for release?” I asked. “Take one step at a time, kid,” he said and patted my head. “I need to make a call. I’m sure you both need to catch-up,” he said and winked at me before I left. The door closed and Kelli came down and kissed me, he tears tasted like salt. He sat, gazing at me and then at the bandages and stitches and looked away. “Hey, everything is okay now,” I said with a smile. “Not really,” she muttered. “Huh?” She straightened her posture, shook her head and came down to rest beside me on the very cramped small bed. She put her arm over my stomach and closed her eyes. “It doesn’t matter,” she said and kissed my cheek. “I’m so glad you are okay … I thought I had lost you … I thought you were …” I knew what she thought and I had felt like that before. I had experienced the same emotions with the death of Lilly (Supposed death of Lilly). I hugged her gently, ignoring the pain in my stomach and arm and looked at the night’s sky. “I’m fine, Kelli. It’s all over now …” I said and stroked her head. There were so many things that I wanted to tell her when McGrady was torturing me, but now that I was here, with a second chance, I didn’t know how to say it. I had never felt like this before, not with Lois, Selene or Lilly. This was different; something about my relationship with Kelli was more magical! A cheesy word, I guess, but true. “Pierce, when will this be over?” she asked. “I don’t know … hopefully soon …” I said and gulped with tears clogging at the back of my throat. I knew, deep down, Kelli was suffering from this experience in LA and I didn’t want to be a cause of that. She sat up and gazed at me, stroked my cheek and looked at the nights sky and then at her mobile. It was beeping and vibrating and on the table. She leant over and switched it off. I cared for her deeply and barely knew that much about her past. It was something she kept sealed and I could understand that, I had kept a lot of mine secret. I had yet to speak of Albany, Lois and my issue at home with my dysfunctional family. I wanted to tell her how I felt, but the words wouldn’t come out. “Pierce, I - I - I love you,” she said bashfully and then looked away. My heart jumped and the beeper began to beep faster. Now I was embarrassed. Not the right time to tell me you love me, especially when my blood pressure is linked to a machine. I saw Kelli smile at my increased heart rate. She had given me the courage to unlock the locked words. My mouth opened and I felt comfortable enough to confess all of my feelings to Kelli, but three simple words were the most important. “I love you too, Kelli …” I said and kissed her. ******************* The triangles pay-per view played in the back of my mind as I gazed out the double-glazed hospital windows. There were many things that posed a problem for me in my new situation, with regards to my title defence. The first and foremost was that my most hated foe, Alex Strider, had returned. This, under normal circumstances might agitate me a little, but I would have moved on. It was the fact that Alex Strider was now the spokesman behind Promecil. His cardboard face played in the back of my mind. It was Alex Strider all right, I would never forget his face, but he, out of everyone I had crossed in the AWC was my most hated and worst enemy to come across. He had been around with me since day one of the company opening and had beaten me at Zero to Hero. He then went on to rise in fame and fortune and soon turned toward me. Some say your anger is like a fuse, one drastic movement, or current and you blow. That was a way to describe Alex Strider’s innuendos toward me, but I eventually beat him at Solarized. I had been the one to end Alex Strider’s long run of victories and now what worried me was that he had been away for a while, a long time in fact and probably held a vengeance in the back of his mind toward me and the Transatlantic belt, but also Alex Strider probably knew I had been in Promecil and I have been snooping around. It was exactly secret, GOONE knew I was after them, but funnily enough haven’t really bothered to do anything. Could Alex Strider be involved in the inner workings of Omnikon and GOONE? It was something I knew I had to figure out and to that; I needed to get back to work. Kelli was asleep on the makeshift bed the nurse had fetched for her. She slept quietly, despite the amount of noise in the halls and opposite rooms. I turned over and removed the blankets, stretching my legs. My thigh muscles cramped and pulled with each movement I took, but despite the numbness and aching, it wasn’t as bad as I thought it would be. I tensed my forearm and felt each stitch tighten with each motion. Triangles would be possible, in light of my recent situation, I could still make it. John McGrady hadn’t killed me and I wasn’t too weak to wrestle. “Pierce? What are you doing standing?” Kelli asked, jumping of bed to my side. “Testing to see if I can walk,” I said. “Like hell you are! Sit back down … Can’t you see you could do more damage,” she said with determination written all over her face. “Kelli, I can’t. I need to go to Triangles, I need to compete,” I said. “Why?” she asked. “I must defend my title.” She stood aside, hands on hips and tilted her head in an ‘I don’t believe you’ manner. “Okay, I have something I need to follow up. Well, someone. He is involved with Promecil. I need to figure out what it is he is doing back,” I said and pulled on my jeans. “You mad?” she said. “Yeah,” I said back, stubborn as anything. She handed me a clean shirt, socks and shoes. “I won’t stop you,” she said and watched me dress. I was ready to tell her all of my feelings, not just the three words of utmost importance, but when my grandfather strolled in, whistling the national anthem of Harrisburg, I decided against it. Perhaps another time! Always another time! “What? OH NO YOU DON’T,” he said and gripped my shoulder. “I have to,” I said. “It’s about Promecil, one of the wrestlers that is in the company, now works for them. I need to go and keep an eye on him. Keep the rest of them safe,” I said. “Pierce, you just had a 100 mls pumped into your body full of that drug, not sixteen hours ago and now you want to wrestle and get straight back into action. Well, if that is what you want, I can’t stop you,” he smiled. My grandfather, lenient, now that was a new thing. Perhaps Susanne being married to him was paying off and not just to her back in Switzerland. I turned to the door and watches Kelli and my grandfather look on, both ready to leave as well. Nothing was ahead of me now, but problems and solutions. Things would have to brighten up for me, I was tired of being pulled into catastrophic life circumstances, for once I wanted a peaceful, normal life, but that would have to be on hold, at least until I ended this Omnikon and Promecil distribution and put a stop to Alex Strider.
