One for Luck, Ch. 5

9 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9th Nov. 2005 One for luck, Chapter 5 (Prometheus: Section 3) Aristotle once said “Love is composed of a single soul inhabiting two bodies” [Aside - Conspiracy] Pierce lay asleep, his breath quietly moving against the moons glow. Kelli awoke in his arms, the bed covers half off her body, she stared at Pierce’s face and a smile of happiness washed over her. She had spent the most passionate night of her life, with a tender man, a man that wasn’t playing mind games with her, or abusing her and now, for the first time that she actually had something good, she was being forced to ruin it. Without waking Pierce, she carefully removes his arm and slides out of bed, her naked skin reflecting against the glow of night. Luckily enough, they were in a high hotel room; otherwise she might have been seen. She slides into a dressing gown and stares at the clock on the table. It’s 3:30 am. She tip-toes into the kitchen area and opens the fridge, pulling out an Evian bottle of water and also a glass from the cabinet. After pouring a glass of water, she stares at her lip. The swelling has gone down, a small gash remains. All night, Kelli had put her phone on silent, but the screen read two missed calls and one new voice message. She lifted it up and dialled the voice message service; she hoped Pierce wouldn’t wake up. “You have one new message,” the voice machine started. “Received at 2:00 am. Kells, I know you're having fun... probably too much fun. Anyways, I need something from you, anything, actually. Call me back as soon as possible with what you've learned. I'd hate for things to get drastic, you know? I'm pretty sure Zach likes his new surroundings, but we can always bring him around again. Ta ta, toots.” The phone went dead and Kelli sat down on the stool, the mobile still in her hand. Adam was proving to be persistent and dangerous, she never intended falling for Pierce and now, deep down, she had. She couldn’t remember a time when the intercourse had actually meant something, but it did with Pierce. She knew Adam’s threats were not just words, because he was one to carry out actions and she feared what Zach would do, once he got out of prison. He had already hit her once and the scar on her shoulder wasn’t healing quickly. She felt a warm tear roll down her cheek. Deciding against calling Adam, she turned off her mobile, slipped it into her purse and removed the water that she had intended on drinking. As she walked into the bedroom, Pierce moved slightly in the bed, and she froze. She removed the dressing and let it drop onto the ground and slid back into the bed and into the embrace she had woken up in. The alarm clock broke the soothing silence of my deep sleep. The evanescent stylings of Stevie Wonder rumbled the sound waves to those lazy-bums still in bed. I rose from the bed covers and left “Superstition” playing. Kelli grumbled and pulled the pillow over her head. It was 8:00 am and the city was buzzing with life - a hive, busy to fend for themselves and collecting the daily pollen for honey. I pulled on a pair of faded jeans and a black wife-beater. I knew I had to get my act into gear in regards to the new situations that constantly reared their ugly heads in Los Angeles. I needed to tackle some things head on; investigation might open some new doors. “What time is it?” Kelli mumbled under my pillow. “8:00 am,” I replied. I had a large smile on my face. There was something claming about waking up to a beautiful woman every morning. “8:00 am! OH!” Kelli moaned and rolled over, pulling my pillow with her. I moved from the bed to the kitchen and brewed up some hot coffee - the caffeine boost that awoke everyone from the deep sleep. I placed two cups on the table and filled them. I moved into the bedroom and sat next to Kelli, the hot mug of coffee balancing in my hand. She rose with its sweet scent and gave me a peck on the lips. I turned around, clambered over her legs and the duvet and grabbed the phone book. “Who are you calling?” she gargled with hot coffee in her mouth. “An old friend. I just need to find an area code,” I said and pulled out my mobile. I found the Boston area code and dialled the number on my phone. After a few rings and the odd click from connectivity, the phone was answered. “Hello?” the soft voice on the other end asked. “Lilly, it’s Pierce. How are you? Is Tony there?” I asked. I never thought I would actually speak Lilly’s name again, but it felt good to know she was still around. “Pierce?” she questioned, still groggy from sleep. “I’m fine. Yeah Tony’s here, hold on.” I heard the exchanging of phones and then a very horse-voice answered the phone. “Yo!” he spoke. “Hey Tony,” I started off by saying. “Pierce, what’s up?” Tony asked happily. I heard the sound of a baby crying in the background and assumed immediately that Lilly had, had her child. “I need to ask a favour,” It was my best shot for an inside investigation. “Yup, shoot …” Tony said. His mind seemed too busy on the crying baby. “I need to look up some info on the murder of a James Lawler. He was killed in Los Angeles.” There was no reply for a few moments. I turned to Kelli, who had now creased her brow, constantly looking quizzical at me, but soon finished her coffee, kissed me on the cheek and went into the bathroom. I heard the shower head running and the vibration of the power shower charger against the tiled wall. “LA? What you doin’ in LA?” His Bostonian accent echoed through the phone. “Long story… Some of my own investigation work!” I smiled to myself as Kelli struggled with the collapsing shower curtain; my t-shirt she was wearing, was now drenched. “Alright, what’s the dude’s name?” Tony asked. “James Lawler,” I spoke it softly. “Okay, I’ll see what I can dig up, I’ll get back to you soon,” Tony said his goodbyes and hung-up the phone. I shut my mobile and leant back into the headrest. A new door had now granted me a window of opportunity. “Eh! Pierce! A little … help …” Kelli slipped to the floor with the broken shower curtain. “Ah, shit!” I smiled and jumped the bed toward her. I managed to switch off the shower and untangle Kelli from the shower curtain; she looked at me, drenched in my clothes. “Who are Lilly and Tony?” she asked, weary-eyed and soaking wet. “Some old friends,” I smiled and brushed aside some of her hair, that danced loosely around her face. **** The wind billowed through the trees of Hancook Park, I found myself standing in the exact same place that James Lawler was gunned down on. The night flashed through my memory constantly, each time looking for a new reason as to what happened and how I was found. I retraced my steps back toward where Kelli and I had hid. The bramble bush had been tampered with, probably the forensics - I just hoped they hadn’t found any evidence belonging to Kelli or I, well, what could they do? The park is used by everyone. I took a deep breath and knelt down on my hunkers and stared at the twigs lining the ground, not a single one broken, strange! It certainly was. I continued looking around because I believed I had cracked a twig, but maybe not. On the news report it said that James Lawler’s body was found thrown into one of the ditches, meaning the men must have moved it, after they got fed-up chasing Kelli and I, or before. I never counted how many were following us, didn’t have time to look. “A little early for browsing,” a voice spoke from behind me. I jumped up into a rigid position and turned to face the man. “Granddad?” I questioned, shocked. “Who else!” he said and smiled. He was dressed in his usual posh get-up. A brand new black suit, a red shirt and a grey trench coat. A brown briefcase swung by his hip. “Are you following me?” I asked. “Have been since you arrived,” he stated and sat down on the bench. “Why?” “Wanted to know what brought you to Los Angeles. I thought, perhaps, you had come on the off chance to visit your mother and me, but I am wrong, aren’t I?” he said and placed his briefcase onto his lap. “You tell me!” I said. I sat down and wondered whether he knew what I was doing snooping around a crime scene. “Fair enough! I can tell you haven’t lost your wit,” he said and popped the lid on his briefcase. “I need your help on something. Actually, it seems you need mine.” “What?” I asked. Now I was confused. My grandfather was speaking perfect English, but his change in direction and use of riddles, baffled me. “I know you are investigating a certain drug. That’s why I have come to bargain,” he said. “Okay, I’m listening,” I leant into the back of the bench and crossed my arms, starring at the many walkers and runners, that swiftly moved passed the crime scene. “I have a PI, named John McGrady. He was investigating this new drug for me. The first time I encountered it, was during one of my days in the surgery. A young man came in complaining about stomach pains. He paid money, so I didn’t ask questions. Anyway, I removed the pill that hadn’t dissolved in his stomach and just assumed it was a bad ecstasy tablet, it isn’t uncommon, but three days later, another man came in, round the same age, with the same problems. So, I sent the pills to be analysed. Turns out they’re not ecstasy, actually far worse. Enough of them could kill someone.” “I -eh -” Before I could question where the story was going, my grandfather continued with his tale of the how he came to be here with me. “Anyway, they aren’t using the correct enzyme to allow the pill to dissolve in the pepsin. Now, I knew it would only be a matter of time before that problem is figured out. So, I asked John to look into it. He found out it was called a diet pill and that both the young men were athletes, one a judo master, named Marshal Longley and the other, an NFL hopeful, named Nathan Dwight. John went out two days ago, to the club called Wenza, where the two men hang out, and he hasn’t been seen since. I was wondering if you could help.” I leant back and nodded. This was sort of coming together now. The drug that had been taken by Adam Masters, was now being branched to the young and minor athletes, and like my grandfather, I worried about the use of the correct enzyme being utilized, it would mean young men and women athletes, could die from it. “What do you need me to do?” I asked, willingly. “Here’s all the information. Once you have found him, come to my mansion. Please, don’t hesitate about it. I have all the information you will need there,” he handed me a brown envelope. “Be careful Pierce, promise me?” he asked. “I promise,” I stood up and watched him disappear into the distance toward the black stretch limo, from which he came. I shoved the brown envelope into my pocket and looked around Hancook Park. Whatever had happened that night, all the evidence was gone and in some lab, it would be up to Tony Rush to help me. The ball was now in his court on that detail, but I now had a new direction to look at, a bar called Wenza. **** We hopped the fence and plodded along the mucky trenches that led up to Wenza. Kelli cursed and mumbled in the background as her shoes sunk into the wet mud, acting like a sponge. “Yuck! Why couldn’t we use the sidewalk?” she questioned. I turned to the footpath and stared at the four men in suits. I saw the slight glimmer of a gun handle, gulped and turned back in the direction of the exit door to Wenza. “Glance over your shoulder and you’ll see why!” I retorted and opened the exit door. “What? They are four business men. Not mafia or drug lords …” she saw one of the guns and turned to me. “Okay, I see your point. I’ll shut up,” she said with a smile and entered into Wenza before me. The bar was alive with music and dancing. Smoke fumes from cigarettes and the smell of beer and vodka lay pungent just above the lights. I followed Kelli around the bar, passed a couple of drunkards and toward the backstage area. I knew Longley and Dwight had to be back there, most of the dealing was done backstage. Well at least that is what my grandfather’s file stated. “Pierce, eh, there’s a bodyguard …” Kelli leant back and held my hand. “It’ll be fine,” I whispered in her ear and clambered between two chairs and a couple of very drunk love-birds. The bodyguard stepped forward and placed a hand on my chest. “Hey!” I said. “You need to pass the test to get in,” he said and folded his arms. He was too large for me to jump and besides, there was a large biker gang in the corner, I wasn’t going to risk a bar brawl. “Oh great!” Kelli muttered under her breath and stepped behind me a little. “Okay,” I said. “What?” Kelli exclaimed. “You don’t even know what the -” “It’ll be fine, right?” I turned to the bouncer. He grinned and dipped his head to my height. I gathered he was around seven foot. “You’ll need to get enough grades off the judges,” he said. “Judges?” Kelli asked. The bodyguard pointed to the stage that a young man was playing on. Four other men sat in front of him and lifted up small white sheets with a number on them. “You get high enough and they give you a pass. Then you’re allowed into the backstage. Otherwise, you ain’t gettin’ in,” he spluttered the words at me. “Right,” I walked back and leant against the small guard rails next to the stage. “What now?” Kelli asked. “I’m going to try it out!” I said and dipped under the rails. “What? Pierce?” Kelli said, but didn’t follow. “Trust me,” I jumped onto the stage and toward the judges, gave them a fake name and moved toward the backing band. Kelli stood, arms folded, obviously embarrassed by my drastic move, perhaps I should have mentioned that I used to be a lead vocalist and guitarist in my old band in college. Oh well! Too late now. I stood under the spotlight, the electric guitar in my hand. I had yet to pick a song and I was now beginning to sweat with the heat of the lights that highlighted me to the many gazing eyes in the shadows. The judges sat, pens taping against their clip boards, and in that moment I knew what to play. I turned to the backing band and whispered the song “Born to be wild by Steppenwolf”. The drummer nodded and counted me in. I turned to the mic, it had been a while since I had done this, but I kicked in on the fourth beat and began to sing. The bikers seemed to be enjoying it, and a smile washed over my face, I did miss this. Especially in front of screaming college girls. Kelli was clapping in the background, probably relieved. Before I had reached the chorus the judges lifted their sheets and handed me a pass. I put the guitar back on its stand, jumped off stage and under the rails. I was applauded on the way down. The bodyguard let us passed and I heard the door shut behind us. “You never told me you could play?” I continued walking down the long corridor. “Yeah, sorry about that. Used to be in a band.” “Yeah? What type?” Kelli asked. Enthusiasm hinted in her words. “Mainly metal, rock and sometimes we would play some indie-rock,” I said and pulled out the photos of Longley and Dwight. I turned the corner and found Longley lying on a bench doing weights. I grabbed the collar of his sports coat and hoisted him up, slamming him into the concrete wall. “WHAT THE -?” he shouted and hit the wall with a thud. I immediately regretted jumping a judo master, as he whacked me in the jaw. I stumbled back and charged at him, connecting with his ribs and drove him further against the wall. He coughed. I straightened and thumped him in the face. “Where’s John McGrady?” I asked. “Who?” he replied, spitting out some blood. “Don’t mess me around. Where is he?” I asked again and punched him square in the nose. I felt my knuckle crack. “Okay, okay, the cop is down there …” Kelli smiled and pushed me to go down the hall. I ran as fast as I could, all the while crunching my fist. I hadn’t broken my hand. I pushed open the door and found John McGrady. He was bound and gagged to a wooden chair. I removed the gag and untied him; before I could have a proper introduction, Kelli screamed at the top of her lungs. My eyes widened with fear and I ran back. Kelli was being held by a man, dressed in an all black suit. He wasn’t armed. I ran forward and I was met with a swift punch to my stomach that winded me. Two men grabbed my arms and the other thumped me in the jaw. Blood flew out of my mouth and onto the floor. A man, wearing an all red suit and black shirt, with black glasses and a red trilby hat moved forward out of the shadows with a cane in his hand. He stood in front of me and tapped his cane on my face. He was smug, a gold tooth glinted at me, sort of reminded me of a bad Snoop-Dogg video. “You just keep arriving and messing with our business!” he muttered. He turned to Kelli in the arms of the large man and smiled, dragged his cane up into the air and ran it against his face. I spat some blood out, which knowing my bad luck just happened to land on his shinny black shoes. “Humph!” he said and pulled out a white handkerchief and wiped it clean. Not a speck of blood remaining. “You seem to be messing with my corporation a little too much,” he said. “Corporation?” I asked, playing the dumb fool. “Please, we are known as GOONE. You followed a James Lawler to Hancook Park. We saw you …” “You killed him?” I questioned. It was a dark night and with rain pouring down hard, I couldn’t get a visual on the attackers. “Who else? The mob? Ha, they’re long gone…We are the big guns now, Pierce,” he said and grunted. “How’d you know my na-?” Suddenly the man on my left crashes to the floor, glass shattering everywhere. I duck and watch John McGrady defend an incoming punch from the goon on my right. I duck with the incoming fruit bowl that lands smack on the goons head. The man holding Kelli tries for McGrady, but McGrady ducks and delivers a swift uppercut. The man in the red suit tries to make a beeline for the exit door; Kelli slyly sticks her foot out and watches the man crash to the floor. However, he is up with the help of the judo master Longley and the two dash. “Leave it,” McGrady shouts to me. “Well get em’ later,” he says and turns to me. “Bloody hell lad, next time you plan a rescue come prepared and never leave a man or woman behind. Remember those morals kid; it saves a lot of ass!” He said and bent down toward the three knocked out men. “I eh-” “Americans, all the bloody same,” McGrady snorts. He picks up one of the goons and searches around the inside pockets. He pulls out a mobile and smiles. “Stupid too.” Kelli turns to me and holds my hand. “Right, lets get out of ‘ere,” McGrady smiles and holds open the exit door. I follow him down the mucky trenches, back toward my Lincoln. Kelli remained close beside me, holding my hand the whole way, nerves trembling. My phone rumbled the silence and she jumped. I checked the caller ID, it was Tony Rush. Finally some answers, I thought. “Hello!” I stated with pause. “Hey man, didn’t catch you at a bad time, did I?” “Em, no, not at all. Find any info?” I asked. McGrady started the car and Kelli and I sat in the back, she fastened her seatbelt and nestled into my shoulder. “Well, thing is, I found out a lot!” Rush paused. “First off, Lawler’s death was not an accident. Professional hit, my friend. So, it led me to believe that Lawler was into something. So I checked around with the LA narcotics unit, the men that worked the case. Anyway, he was dealing a drug, not sure the name of it. Well he would slip it into his young client’s sports drinks, just before a match. They were test subjects, unknowingly. Anyway, one day the drug goes bad and a kid named Rubin Demorto dies from it, of course, nobody but Lawler knew why. Then a sudden guilt trip hit him, that’s what I’m thinking and the drug lords knocked him off …” “They’re named GOONE,” I finished his sentence. “Well, that’s it man. I’ll e-mail you the info on Demorto. Take care, Pierce,” Rush hung-up the phone and I shut mine. McGrady had turned off Santa Monica and was now heading to Bel-Air, the home of my grandfather. *** “You sure this is a good idea?” I asked, concerned for the safety of my grandfather. I check behind us for any tailing cars, but there were none. Kelli glanced at me and then at the fairly odd looking couple, stood on the doorstop, and by odd I mean my seventy-five year old grandfather and his twenty-nine year old wife. “Don’t worry, Pierce. Everything is hunky-dory!” McGrady laughed. “Huh?” “Never mind,” McGrady says and slides out of the car. My grandfather approaches and wraps his hand around my shoulder. “Good to see you,” he says and leads me down the hall. I watch as Kelli is kept behind by Susanne. McGrady and my grandfather take a fast paced walk down the corridor and down a flight of stairs into what looks like a crime lab. There are around eight PI’s, a crime analysist and a computer nerd in the corner. I stare, mouth open at the investigative work of my grandfather. “You weren’t kiddin’ about evidence, were you?” I asked my grandfather. I stand in front of a white slab, splayed with photos, addresses and notes. “Everything you need is here,” McGrady said and hands the mobile he found to the computer nerd. “See what you can get off this,” he mutters and wanders off into the crowd of PI’s. My grandfather pulls me aside, up the stairs and into a library of guns. GUNS! AGAIN! My last experience wasn’t memorable, if I remember correctly I got shot in the ass, by some crazed bounty-hunter. “Pierce, I am worried about you. You know that, right?” he said to me with worry in his grey eyes. They iced over time, especially after the death of his first wife, Gloria. She had been his childhood sweetheart and soon his first wife. She died from cancer of the lungs and it broke his heart, I used to think his eyes turned to ice. Piercing and did nothing but reflect. “I know grandfather, but why am I in the congress of pistols?” I smiled. “Protection,” he retorted and handed me a black 9mm Beretta and some ammo. A hundred rounds of 9mm bullets, lucky I wasn’t passing through customs on the way back. “Thanks, for the-em-gun!” I said and slipped it under my t-shirt into the small of my back and jeans. “You sure you are okay?” he asked, hand on my shoulder. “Yeah, I’m fine. I’d like to go home with Kelli, if that’s alright!” I responded. Before he answered, he led me down to the hall, where Kelli sat. She shot up and over toward me and hugged me. It was strange to see her this emotional, but I just assumed it was something feminine. McGrady approached us at the Lincoln. “Sorry about me sarcasm earlier. It’s just I get rattled by some bloody situations, you know!” he said in the easiest way he could. “Yeah, I do actually,” I said and climbed into the car. Kelli and I had remained in complete silence, all the way back to the Hilton; she remained gazing at her mobile and then out of the window. I pulled the car into the valet and handed the bus boy the keys. Kelli followed in hot pursuit and we soon arrived in the comfort of our hotel room. She plonked onto the bed and sighed lying down. “…Well that was an interesting day,” I said and turned on the computer. “Yeah,” she said and sat up. Rush had sent me the e-mail on Rubin Demorto. I copied it and pasted it to word pad for safe keeping and shut off the internet, in case of hackers. Okay, now I was paranoid. I turned around to find Kelli standing in front of me. “Eh, you sure all is we-” She leant in and kissed me, dragging me up and over to the bed. I sat up, but felt the pressure of Kelli forcing me into the bed covers. I rolled her over and returned the passion. She began lifting my t-shirt and soon found the handle of the Beretta, her eyes widened. “What the hell?” she questioned. “It’s a precaution,” I started off my explanation, but found McGrady standing in my living room. “What the hell?” I turned around shocked. “Sorry, got no answer,” he said. “So you broke our door down,” I asked. “Best bet!” he said with a smile and looked at Kelli. “I have a lead from the mobile. It’s a factory, called Omnikon, according to one of the text messages. Anyway, apparently it is dealing these drugs. I thought, perhaps you would want to take a look,” he said. “Shouldn’t we call the cops?” I asked. “You haven’t been in LA long, have you?” He didn’t let me reply. “Most of the cops are corrupt, that is why your father got eight PI’s from different places around the world. So you coming or what?” I looked at Kelli sitting on the bed, she shook her head for a yes and handed me back the gun. “Yeah, I’m in,” I said and loaded the gun. “I’m coming too,” Kelli jumped in. “Too risky,” McGrady started. “Risky! Pierce?” she said and turned to me. “Okay, fine, you can come, but stay safe,” I said and closed the broken door; the security officer’s stared at me with an annoyance in their faces. They now had to put a new lock and safety key back on the door. *** McGrady followed the satellite navigation system in his black pickup. Kelli and I remained silent on the drive across Los Angeles. We finally arrived at our destination. McGrady parked just near a large mound of earth, in a simple attempt to cloak his car and us from the various men and goons that lay beyond the mound. I pulled out my gun, closed the car door and began climbing the mound, laying flat on the top. McGrady rose to my left with a pair of binoculars and Kelli lay on my right. He placed them on his face and stared at the white fan that sat under the two spot lights. Four men surrounded the van, but I could barely make them out. McGrady grunted and handed me the binoculars. “Here, take a look,” he said and loaded his own 9mm pistol. I placed the binoculars on my head and saw the very man from Wenza. He was still dressed in the red suit and by the looks of things was collecting a shipment. The horror had already started; drugs were now going to be distributed to the population of Los Angeles. “Come on. I see an opening,” McGrady removed the binoculars and tugged my sleeve. Kelli turned to me and didn’t follow. I ignored her refusal and continued to follow McGrady down the mound into the dark and along the back fence. We clambered over a small fence and found an opening in the warehouse doors. McGrady pulled out a small mirror and placed it around the corner, he nodded and entered. “Wow, its huge,” I said in amazement at the size of the warehouse, along with the huge cargo load sat before me. Crates upon crates and more crates, all of the same drug. McGrady turned to the cameras and shot one. He then used his gun to shoot the lock on the large crate, the door flew open and small packets of white tablets trickled out, like a bag of sand opening. “Hey, did you hear that!” a voice said. “Shit,” McGrady said with a hush tone and dipped me behind one of the crates. “That loaded?” he asked. I nodded my head and switched it off from safety. McGrady turned his head around the corner and was immediately spotted by three men, with semi-automatics in their hands. The yelled “over there” and McGrady began defensive fire. “Come on,” McGrady yells and runs. I follow quickly, shooting off rounds behind me. A single gun shot goes off, it grazes my leg, leaving a burning feeling and pierces McGrady’s thigh. He falls down behind another crate and I follow. “Feck …” he mutters and applies pressure. I use up the rest of my rounds firing at the men coming after us, and by the sounds of coughing it seems I hit one. McGrady tugs my arm and I find two more in front of us, the barrels of the semi-automatics pointed at us. They grin, smug with themselves and my hands freeze, my limbs numb and my heart pounding. “Bollocks,” McGrady cursed. I looked up the dark hole that held a 9mm bullet, around forty rounds of them. Each one could kill me, at a point blank range. Suddenly McGrady raised his pistol and fired a round; it drove its way through the cranium of one of the men. The other fired two rounds, I closed my eyes. This was it, I was going to die. McGrady yelled in pain, he had been shot in the shoulder and arm. The man then fired two more rounds and shot McGrady in the other shoulder and stomach. McGrady smiles cockily at the man, terror in the other man face. McGrady fires and hit’s the man, dead-centre in the third eye. He falls like a dead weight into a heap. Footsteps come charging down the corridor. I gulped, put my gun into my jean belt and rose up. McGrady was now coughing up blood, along with severe bleeding in his chest, arms and legs. The crate is still wide open and a packet of the pills is upright, the name PROMECIL reads in big blue capitals. I grab it and shove it into my pocket. I turn to McGrady and see the pain in his face. I grab his arm. “Never leave a man behind,” I smile, but my smile is more fear and worry. I knew McGrady was dying. “Bollocks kid, go,” McGrady coughed and spluttered blood. I hoisted him into a fireman carry. “Bloody hell, ya feckin’ ejit,” he mutters and I begin a fast paced walk back out the door. Gun fire bursts through the air. Yells are heard as they find the two bodies, killed by McGrady.
