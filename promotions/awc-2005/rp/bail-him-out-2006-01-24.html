Bail Him Out!

24 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/24/06 Damien’s hand reached upward landing on his forehead and resting there. He was covering a gash on his forehead received from the previous nights bout with Damian. His eyes gazed into the mirror, pouring over his reflection. His usually spiky, jet-black hair was matted and his black and blue eyes and body reflected his mental state. After one match I’m beat? He removed his hand from his forehead and flicked the light switch. Exiting the bathroom of his hotel room he made his way over to the bed and flopped down on it. He took a second to take in his surroundings, because last night had been a blur. The hotel room was sparsely furnished, with a single bed sitting in the center. On the perimeter of the room sat a small television set that appeared to be the first one every invented, and a small coffee table that looked as if it had been made by small children in Andorra. Well, at least I’m put up nicely. …I must have started to doze off when I was awoken by the sound of my room’s telephone. I wiped the sleep from my eyes and answered. “Hello?” came an unsure voice on the other end. I could tell by the voice it was the front desk at the hotel because he called me before I went to sleep last night to ask if I needed any medical assistance or anything of that nature. These hotel people are the real considerate type. “Yeah, I’m here,” I responded “Or at least I am now that I have been woken up.” I decided to be a little fresh with him, hoping to discourage any other late night phone calls. “Well I’m sorry to disturb you sir, but you have a collect call coming in.” Strange, I thought, who could be calling collect at this time of night. “Should I put them through?” “Yeah, yeah.” The earpiece returned to emitting low-pitched fuzz until an operators voice came over the line. “Union County Police station, we have a call for you from Thomas Wilson, will you accept the charges?” What the fuck was my first thought, slowly followed by Jesus Christ. How the hell could my father end up in prison, he’s not that type of person. My early memories of my father are ones full of laughter and playfulness. He would take me to sporting events and wrestling tournaments. Anywhere he was going that a child was allowed, he’d take me. I never recalled him ever having a problem with alcohol or with any other sort of activity that would sit him in the county tank. “Yeah, sure, I’ll accept the charges.” The line went dead again and this time I wondered if it were going to come back on. The low buzz now grew to a frantic pitch of feedback until finally my fathers voice came over the line. It sounded old and tired, and I suddenly remember that recently we were not on the best of terms. The last time I spoke to him was after I was legally emancipated by the court system of New Jersey. What a fine system it is. After the hearing he came up to me, put a hand on my shoulder and told me to do what I had to do, but don’t come crying to him for help when my luck and money runs out. I told him he could go fuck himself for all I cared because when he can’t pay for the houses mortgage and I’m off on my own making some cash, he shouldn’t call me either. It was all a symptom of the love-hate relationship we had developed since I had entered high school. “Damien?” My fathers voice cracked over the line. It seemed a lot more worn an old then a remembered. A certain vigor was gone, but then again, why would he talk to me in an excited tone? I’m the stray lamb that left him and now he was calling that stray lamb from prison. Not exactly a great situation from his viewpoint, I’d assume. “Yeah Dad, seems like you fucked something up.” “Now’s not the fucking time, Damien!” The sudden anger in his voice gave me a start and I decided to lay off the sarcasm for the rest of our conversation. If this was a serious matter than the least I could do was hear him out. “What’s the problem Dad?” I asked, not so much worried, as curious as to the predicament he seemed to be in. “Me and your mother had something of a disagreement. We got into a screaming match and bolted out of the room. She was heading downstairs to her office when she slipped on a toy car that was on the step. She –.” I cut him off. “Why the hell was there a toy car on the steps?” “Well that was what the argument was about. We were cleaning out the garage and she found a couple of your old things, you know, jerseys from high school, pictures, some of your old toys. It hit her pretty hard, you know, the realization that you might never come home again. She got a little unnerved and temperamental.” “And?” I asked impatiently. “She was on edge, and we got into an argument about who was supposed to do the dishes that night. That fight escalated as to which one of our faults it was for driving you from home. I told her that if she hadn’t been as strict with you that you would have stayed. You were young, it was time to make mistakes and have fun being in high school. Hit hard by this fact she fled the room and ran downstairs to her office. I guess she put one of your old toy cars on the staircase to be put away later and forgot about it. She slipped and fell down the stairs, broke her damn arm too. A neighbor heard our argument and then her cry of pain and called the police. I called an ambulance. She’s in the hospital now, but this the woman from across the street claims to have heard her yelling and says I beat her. Mom was at the hospital, too drugged on painkillers to rebuke this accusation so I was taken to a holding cell until she was comes down from the medication enough to talk.” “You expect me to believe that bullshit? You hit mom, didn’t you?” I asked without emotion in my voice. I really didn’t give a shit. “It’s true, I swear Damien. You know your mother and I always got along. I would never put my hands on her…in that way.” He chuckled. Me and my dad had always made jokes about girls like that when I was younger. I looked pretty old for my age and would be approached by girls much older than me when I was out with him. We would joke about it on the rides home. I used to feel like he was my friend. “Alright, so still, I doubt you called me just to give me an update, what’s your message?” Here it comes; he’s going to ask his son for bail money. I was right. “How are you doing, they put your little wrestling show on the television in the prison. You put on quite an act out there!” He said with renewed excitement. Fuck. He didn’t ask. “Yeah, yeah, quite a show.” I glanced across the room into the mirror again. The swelling and bruises all over my body were a show all their own. “There’s one other thing I have to ask you though.” “Yeah, dad?” Oh hell yeah, here comes the bail question. “Do you think you could wire me some money to pay bail? I really can’t sit in here anymore.” Jackpot. I was right and he was wrong, even if he just admitted it subliminally. “Yeah, yeah, I got a nice little signing bonus that put some cash in my pocket. I’ll wire some down there in about an hour, you should be free in two.” Well, I guess my old friend Top Ramen will be coming out of the cupboard to hang out. “Thanks son. This really means a lot. As soon as your mother gets out of the hospital we’re going to come up and watch you wrestle. I’ll call you when I know the date.” He was excited now, thinking that we had somehow mended fences. I think we have a long way to go before that happens completely, but I’m sure it’ll happen in time. “Yeah dad, I’m going to go soak in an ice bath, I’ll talk to you later.” I hung up. For a second I almost contemplated not wiring down the money to him, and letting him sit there in jail for a little while, but I thought better of it and forced my self to get up and dress. Now clothed in my old Bernards High School Wrestling shirt and a pair of worn jeans that I probably had donned a couple times in high school, I grabbed my wallet and headed to the bank. Once reaching the street I stepped to the curb to hail a taxi, but stepped back, deciding that a walk would be good to get the blood flowing again. A lip would be nice too. I reached into my pocket and pulled out a fresh tin of Skoal Long Cut Apple that I had picked up at the arena the night before. The casing reeked of fresh apple tobacco. I stopped, opened it up and took a pinch. Placing it between my bottom lip and gum, I stood there for a moment letting the familiar sting of the particles of glass they said was inside cut my lip. The nicotine felt good and gave me a desperately needed start. I pulled out an empty water bottle and spit inside. As I walked in the direction of the bank, some kids walked by and snapped their fingers by flicking their wrists and hitting their index finger against their thumb and middle finger. The international sign of lip packers. It was a nice little fraternity to belong to and I enjoyed although most females found it utterly disgusting. This was true, it was a terrible habit to have, but one that I held onto since my first varsity wrestling match. It was like yesterday. “Hey Damien, come over here.” The deep voice of the Senior captain James McKeirnan boomed across the gym. I sauntered over to him. We had just had our first match of the season. I was victorious and had earned the respect of my teammates. Or at least I assumed I had. “You showed us a lot out there on the mat today, but there’s one more thing you have to do before you become a full member of this team.” He reached into his pocket and pulled out a blue and black tin. Opening it up revealed a very strong smelling substance that looked like some sort of candy. “Pinch it and put it between your bottom lip and gums.” I did as he said, the buzz I received was my first one ever. One I thoroughly enjoyed. After about an hour or so I took it out. All of them looked at me in approval. We were in the locker room now, just bullshitting, talking about girls and the like. I threw the remainder in the garbage and walked back to them. They all had enormous grins on their faces. Mckeirnan spoke up. “Do you feel a little funny, Damien?” He asked. I couldn’t see the humor in the question that he obviously did. Truth be told I did feel a little funny. Suddenly it him me. I was going to chuck. I chucked hard, for about twenty minutes. I emerged from the bathroom, pride in shambles and a sour taste in my mouth. I returned to the locker room to cheers, jeers, and laughter. Obviously someone played a joke. “You’re a member of the team now Damien.” McKeirnan said with a smile on his face. “What the fuck man, that shit made me sick.” “Well, we left out the minor detail that you were supposed to be spitting, not swallowing your spit. They did the same thing to me when I was a freshman, and when you’re a senior I expect you to do the same thing.” Those words echoed in my head When you’re a senior I expect you to do the same thing. I had dropped out and became emancipated after my senior year. My good mood ruined, I removed the lip and cut across the street to the bank. Well I thought to myself, time to bail my dad out of jail.
