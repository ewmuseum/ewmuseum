It was the California heat. That had to be it. The temperature change would've made her sick... that's why she was running the fever and feeling ready to pass out. But she was also staying at Darcy's, and they hadn't negotiated any sort of rent at that point, so she'd play the nice moocher card and buy some paltry amount of groceries to fill the void that had claimed the traveling bachelor's cupboards. Storming through the frozen foods aisle, she scoffed at the sugar content in American food. So much sweeter than Canada needed to be... it was a sad little irony that only Amy would have noticed. She really had too much free time on her hands, which was displayed by her taking five minutes to compare ingredients on popsicle boxes. The basket had all of Darcy's things - carrots, a lemon and lettuce - and more of Amy's things: Chips, mustard, processed meat, honey mustard, ramen noodles, more mustard and a box of Rainbow Chips Ahoy cookies. Apparently there was a mile-wide line between their eating habits... and their shopping habits... and their grooming habits... and their... Okay, so there were observations that Amy could find herself entirely oblivious to. It was the bliss of being 'the Canadian', sweltering in stupidity under the California sun. That stupidity helped mask the huge warning sign she should've seen when she got to the checkout. The woman in front of her was grossly overweight, wearing sweatpants and a faded black t-shirt that showed off her rolls - the three telltale signs of a pro wrestling fan. Of course, Amy never noticed until it was too late... ''Mah gawd, it's Aimz!'' the jellyroll proclaimed, leaning over to the pimply-faced cashier who looked like he could care less, ''D'you know who that girl is? She's a big deal out 'n around!'' He politely smiled and continued ringing through the grocery items. Amy was still way too tired, medicated and entirely oblivious to do much more than stare at the price that came up when the kid scanned a box of laundry detergent. ''Damn, that's a good price on Gain,'' she said to herself, ''But it IS American dollars...'' ''AIMZ! You! C'mere!'' The butterball of a woman promptly engulfed the tiny redhead in an embrace of sweaty breasts and elbows, causing Amy to gag and pull away. ''Uh, hey. Just, y'know, doing my shopping. Gotta get home, big rush and all.'' The cashier sighed when the woman turned around as opposed to paying the stated amount of her food. Amy grinned sympathetically at him and shrugged, soon looking back to the woman who seemed to insist on making a scene. ''Yer just great. You beat tha HELL outta those guys every week!'' Apparently the lady wasn't that avid a viewer, considering she'd so easily glossed over the fact that Amy hadn't been booked for at least two damned weeks, ''Thanks. Now, uhh... I've gotta... I don't feel so well. Sorry!'' She faked a stomach bug and rushed out of the store, leaving her groceries in the basket at the checkout. Screw Darcy's healthy snacks and her munchies, that bitch was just plain creepy. Now that was how to handle PR. At least, in the low-paid and socially unhealthy world of Aimz. Her Side Nothing really added the necessary anonymity to living. No matter where she was, people would recognize her... but why couldn't it be decent people? Sure, you couldn't say it as a professional, but everyone was thinking it - greasy fat guys/girls and annoying-as-hell little boys were the bane of any wrestler's existance, unless they happened to be a greasy fat guy. Everyone loves their fans, but there are few who can stand their fans. Aimz never claimed to be in the later category, and never would. Back in fWo, when she was with Brandon Roche - Brand Frontier, every little boy, girl and fat guy mattered. Every fan was to be appreciated to the fullest extent possible, and that was how she'd always known Brandon was completely full of shit. The Mighty Hero had believed in righting injustice and the world being a beautiful, safe and easy to swallow place and, no matter how much coke she'd ever done, Amy found that utopia impossible to even glance at. She kind of assumed that's why she'd spent every agonizing year of attempted accomplishment after 2001 wishing to be back around Darcy. Sure, he didn't have a crew cut and muscles out the wazoo, and he wasn't one of the biggest stars in wrestling - or anything else, for that matter. Hell, he wasn't even very good in bed if she remembered right, but Amy paid attention every single time he'd step into a fight. She didn't even mind watching him struggle to ponytail his barely long enough locks, or giving him advice on trimming that sketchy stubble beard. It had never been entirely clear whether or not Darcy knew exactly how good he usually looked, and Amy certainly wasn't ever going to be the one to tell him. Her pride had kept what would have been drooling stares snared down to quick glimpses when nobody else was around. They'd met when she was freshly eighteen and, back then, she definitely knew how good she looked. She used it to get her training, her fights and her recognition. After all, no man wanted to fight a little girl until she egged him on with a few bats of the eyelashes and a 'Come on, big guy, it's not gonna hurt.'. It sometimes felt like verbal hooking, and took a much larger step when she ended up in bed with the guy who ran her first legitimate federation. Manipulative bitch? Maybe, but that move could mostly be chalked up to youthful innocence. Within months, she grew tired of her success, cars and wrongly earned 'fame' and decided to make what was probably the most prolific move of her life. On television, she broke the character she'd been living for a year and tossed her boyfriend and all of his associates what could be summed up as one HUGE middle finger by taking the side of her longtime enemy, Darcy Crisis. Truthfully, having spent months seeing the guy shirtlessly preparing for matches, completely oblivious to his obvious sex appeal had helped cement the decision, but the initial move hadn't been for love. She'd wanted out of the life she was living, and he offered that exit. It was needless to say that her boyfriend at the time took every penny, right alongside her job and all of those pretty little cars... but once she'd gone back to living how Amy Campbell had lived, she couldn't regret the decision for so much as a minute. She barely even wanted to wrestle at the time, it had become such a dirty thought to her. Sadly, it wasn't such a bad idea to Darcy... and that lead to their eventual split. Why was she thinking about the past again? Amy was perched atop a drier in Darcy Markson's basement, watching him discard a shirt after smelling under his own arms and laughing as she'd seen him do so many times, in so many laundromats, so many years ago. His hair was ponytailed, just like it had been back then... it was a combination of nostalgia and just enough pectoral muscle to make even the staunchest lesbian sweat. She'd actually forgotten why she'd even followed him down, aside from making sure he didn't find too many thongs littered in amongst his boxers. Wouldn't want to give the guy the wrong idea, right? Who the hell was she kidding? Darcy seeing her underwear was probably the last negative thing on her mind. The flu she'd been riddled with was at the forefront, but a glance at his abs made her quickly forget about the sick fever as she started to think of another heat... one that needed to be stomped dead if she wanted to avoid rape charges. Attempt one at exercising her willpower, ''You, uhh... you're letting a little stubble grow back on your chest. It's... nice. Nice.'' Darcy raised an eyebrow and cocked his head her way, but chuckled. He nodded his head and simply went back to the task at hand - measuring laundry soap. If her noting growth of body hair without jumping him didn't strain her iron will, very little short of 'Oh hey, take your pants off and let me measure your cock... but I won't touch it!' would. Amy's brain was on overdrive, going through about twenty thousand scenarios from movies - most involving guns, which she could thank the fever for - but had to chuckle herself... Markson had no idea what she'd been thinking. ''So, Tide. Nice choice.'' He never had. ''I hear Gain's on sale over at the, uhh... you know, on the corner.'' It was nice. ''So... yeah.'' As far as she knew. Darcy looked at her and smiled again. ''Do you REALLY want to have a conversation about laundry detergent?'' ''I, uhh, it's helpful! I'm trying to assist you at washing your... stuff. Shut up,'' she pouted, ''And what's up with your little pose here, Mr. Housewife? Aren't you supposed to be tough and extreeeeeeme?'' ''You don't know tough and extreme until you've battled some of these stains,' came Darcy's reply, half-jokingly. ''I'd rather go up against a walking muscle factory twice my size than try to get mustard off my shirt any day of the week.'' Amy smirked, ''I should actually give some of my friends your number, Stainman. You probably deal with cumstains on a daily basis, and they could use tips.'' If you're wondering why the two have to repeatedly burn one another to get into any form of serious conversation, you aren't alone. You're also never going to get an answer. Darcy laughed, partly at Amy's mincing words and partly at his forthcoming response. ''Whatever... you love that shit. If you didn't, I wouldn't have to keep making it.'' ''You must be storing a boatload then, because it's pretty clear that neither of us have gotten near anything related to the subject for months,'' she snipped, folding a few shirts of his before starting up the stairway with one half of their laundry load, making sure Darcy would follow. She continued with a slight disdain in her tone, ''I still sleep in the guest room, and you've still got your five favorite ladies to keep you company nightly. Speaking of nights... I'm incredibly bored, and I've barely seen any of East Bay for the last five years. Ahem...'' Darcy followed her up the stairs, realizing that she had a point. As far as being a model ''boyfriend'' was concerned, he was far from... well, model. Unfortunately for the people that Darcy surrounded himself with, they always found out quickly that he was a man of many pursuits, and there was always something that kept his mind in the clouds. He was not oblivious to this, and what Amy saw in a man who time and time again failed to fully be there for her was a subject he would never fully grasp. 'Some questions are better left unanswered,' he mused in his head. ''You know I hate to keep you in that guest room,'' he grumbled, ''I wish I could spend more time with you, especially in the evening... but I don't get much time for sleep these days. Holding down TWO professions is tougher than I thought, but those bastards at the Institute are lost without me...'' ''You're the lost one. Whether it's at that god damned 'institute' or in a ring.'' Amy had dropped the clothes basket and turned on her heel to face Markson before she continued, ''Your passion for a profession belongs in wrestling, but you're tossing it away and accepting second best just because you don't feel like investing too much emotional stamina back into it.'' She scoffed, ''I haven't seen you truly passionate about anything since I've been back here, and that's a complete 180 from the man you once were. You might've been a dick, but you had a purpose and a vision. Now, you're just walking around hoping something nice gets tossed your way.'' ''I DO have a purpose and a vision, Amy...'' Darcy responded curtly. It was always simply a matter of time before Amy tried to bruise his ego, but it was never something he could get used to, ''That vision just doesn't happen to involve glory hounding anymore. I don't need it. I have a chance to help others now...'' ''That chance will always be there. You ALSO have a chance to see the world, compete and travel with... well... with someone you hopefully care about. Your career shouldn't amount to just saving my ass once a week, Darcy.'' ''What do you want from me, Amy? Are you asking for the Darcy Crisis of old to return? I'll admit, he was entertaining as hell to watch, but...'' Darcy exhaled sharply. This was a subject that often came up among people who knew him well, and it was never easy to discuss. ''...it never made me happy. Sure, I was good, and I KNEW I was good, but I was obnoxious, and a jerk, and ultimately unfulfilled. I've done a lot of soul searching these past few years, and if I've learned anything it's that I'll never get anywhere if all I care about is my own ego.'' The little redhead let all of her weight fall onto one of Darcy's leather sofas. Her back needed rest after carrying even such a tiny weight up the stairs, and her mind was much worse for wear with the subject at hand. She sighed deeply, ''I want you to be who you are, but passionate because that is who you are. The real Darcy Markson is a happy medium between the jerkoff I knew five years ago and the shell of a man I know now. I occasionally see it... like on Valentine's Day. That was ballsy. It was you. This 'I'm torn between humanitarian work and my life's first real passion' bullshit is way too emo, and way too dramatic. It's not who I see, and I know that who I see is a closer version to who you are than what you see when you look in the mirror. You need to decide on something. If you came back to wrestling just to see me again, I will make it my one goal to get you fired because, quite honestly, if you're gonna half-ass it like this... you don't deserve your job.'' Those last words ate at Darcy's soul. Amy knew just how to tear into the man, like a cold and calculating predator who had softened up her prey before delivering the final blow. But unlike the usual sort of Amy's attacks, this wasn't malicious. It wasn't even mean. She was right, and Darcy knew it. He was all too prone to getting caught up in his own life, always believing that he was doing the right thing. Up until now, this was no exception. But the girl he cared so deeply for was returning the favor by letting him know that he was not the man she had given up her life for. Not even close. Not even at all. ''Amy...'' Darcy finally responded. ''I don't know whether to laugh or cry. But you're right... this... this isn't me.'' The admission actually gave Amy chills. From her position on the couch, she looked up to make a gentle sort of eye contact with him, ''How can I help you find what is you?'' ''Putting up with my bullshit is quite enough,'' Darcy said, with a hint of despondance in his voice. ''But I think I'm going to have to figure this one out on my own. Up until thirty seconds ago my life made complete sense... this is going to take some time to mull over.'' Darcy then returned the eye contact. He could see that she truly did care about his well-being. And this was the sort of thing he lived for... at one time there had been so much fire in both of their souls, so much so that they often couldn't help but pour it into each other in the form of tearing each other apart. But once they survived those conflicts (and looking back, Darcy realized they always did), it always brought them closer together. This was no exception. ''If you really want to help... just don't relent on those world-famous swift kicks to the ass. Sometimes my head stays a little too long in the clouds, I guess.'' ''Can do,'' she smiled, turning to sit up on the sofa and look through one of the bay windows Darcy had left uncurtained, in true lazy fashion, ''While you think on it, I think I'm gonna go for one of those sunset walks on the beach alone, like you see in Dashboard Confessional videos.'' ''And you call me the emo one...'' Darcy said, rolling his eyes. ''But still, if you want to get a good look at East Bay, you can't beat the beaches. I'll take you out when you get back though. I need a break...'' He smiled, ''...and I need you there with me.'' Plus A Week He'd kept good on his promise to be more passionate, to be a better boyfriend than he'd ever been before. It was a slow but sure process that involved some amusingly cheesy walks on the beach, sunset barbeques and surprisingly little alcohol. That week, Darcy seemed to be collecting a better sense of himself while Amy mused over the fact that she was practically out of work unless she started trouble within AWC to get a throwaway match. Thankfully while he was finding himself, she was starting shit and finally getting booked. Sure, it was a match against another in a sea of drunken Irishmen with horribly corny accents, but it was a match and she needed it, even if it was crap. The chance to perform had reinvigorated her sense of worth, and put something other than the tight effect four years had on Darcy's abs in her mind. ''I really love it here, Darcy…'' Did she just say that? Couldn't it have been 'It fucking rules here', or 'This is a scenic place to bury a body'? Amy was going soft again and it seemed that her emo-varies were warming her usually cold heart. ''Really,'' Markson queried, ''You’re telling me you wouldn’t rather be candy-flipping at some rave to the stylings of DJ Queef?'' ''DJ Queef?! Is that some sort of joke, or is that the shit you listen to when I’m not around? Anyway…no, I really wouldn’t. A girl has to slow down sometime…'' For a fleeting moment, she was unwinding. Her head laid back slightly in his loose embrace, while both their eyes overlooked the beautiful and starry aftermath of the sunset Californians got to witness daily. It was something that Amy saw too little. She was always either inside at home, or at home in Canada. Sarcasm took over, ''Couldn’t leave the phone at home, Mister Romantic?'' ''Sorry babe, but I gotta take this.'' 'Of course you do,' she thought, 'Otherwise you might have to have a serious conversation that didn't involve me yelling at you... and how unnatural would that feel?' Instead of voicing her thoughts, though, Amy simply nodded, sighed and turned back toward the stars. The conversation was oddly short, and Darcy was back by her side within a minute. ''What the hell was that? You said like two words...'' ''Just Johnny, drunk-dialing as usual. Y'know.'' While she could tell that what he'd just said was a plain-faced lie, she trusted Darcy enough not to be fooling around behind her back - though he should've been, since most men would've had blue balls by now. It was probably some ridiculously business call from that institution he worked at on the side, hopefully confirming his resignation, something that'd please her far too much for him to just tell her about. If he left that institution, she technically 'won' their months-long argument that he couldn't hold down two jobs without his passion waning. At that point, she felt safe enough not to be her usual overly paranoid self. She felt safe enough to trust that whatever call he'd just lied through his teeth about, it was a pleasant surprise for her and not a horrible secret. It was weird what a little optimism did to Amy Campbell. ''So, this guy I'm facing this week...'' the subject quickly changed by her hand, making sure that Darcy didn't notice her observation, ''When you heard him telling his little life's story, did you notice the shit that just didn't make sense? In his big last fight, he worked and worked and blahblahlbah... but he considers the opponent moving as his tactical error? Then he says that the guy not only moved, but head-butted him IN THE ABDOMEN... then tossed an uppercut to his head?'' Darcy looked slightly confused by talk of work coming up during a rather well-planned evening, but he let Amy continue her rambling, and she did. ''... So basically, this guy was in a fight where someone hit a career-ending uppercut WITH HIS ELBOW while their face was in his stomach? How the hell do you head-butt someone in the stomach during a boxing match, even if it's this supposedly rough and tumble Irish bareknuckle boxing. O'Reilly smelled like booze last week, and now he just reeks of bullshit. He seriously needs to study the human anatomy before he makes up another story about his glory days gone by.'' ''You're paying way too much attention to the drunk bastard.'' Amy chuckled, ''Probably because I don't want his haggard-ass 'manager' delivering another challenge the next time I have a shower. That whole thing was just creepy, and totally unprofessional for a so-called career fighter. And what's this kid think he's doing, trying to out-drama me? Boo fucking hoo, he killed his best friend in an illegal fight that they both consented to. That's assisted suicide. Nothing accidental.'' He took her hand and squeezed a little, laughing, ''You realize you of all people are calling someone unprofessional and too dramatic?'' ''Eh...'' Yep. Aimz had been verbally defeated. There was only one thing left to do... ''Just shut your noise hole and enjoy the evening.''
