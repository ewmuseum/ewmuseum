Daydreaming The Color Of Blood

23 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 2005 She stands at the top of the concrete steps leading down to the cellar level at the side of the nondescript building. Behind her jacket-clad back the nighttime streets of Philadelphia spread into the horizon filled with flickering streetlamps and abandoned cars. Here and there lights streaming from the quickly degenerating buildings tells of either a drinking binge or a hopeless, lost cause of pumping their inhabitants’ veins full of whatever they have at hand and in the needle. She shivers in the night breeze and tugs at her coat, torn and patched, to keep herself warm. She is only 16, and has escaped the officials who wanted to take her back to her family just a couple of hours ago. She knows that there are two ways: down or back where she came from. With a final gulp of breath, she makes up her mind. Finally, she takes the first step down. Almost immediately she becomes aware of a noise she hadn’t heard moments ago: something that sounds like machinegun fire echoing from beyond the door at the bottom of the stairwell. The rapid fire repeats itself every 15 seconds or so, lasting from 10 to 30 seconds at a time before dying away again. She hesitates and ducks down against the building, playing with her shoulder-long brown hair. As she watches, a police patrol drives by, apparently in a hurry to get through the street without seeing anything too troubling. Breathing heavily, she takes another step down the stairs, then another until to her surprise she finds herself at the bottom. Leaning against the doorway, the machinegun rhythm is joined by what sounds like guitars and people screaming things, obscene things, and things better left unsaid by any man. She shivers both out of the vibrations coming through the door and out of the finality of her decision. From this door, there is no turning back. She suddenly feels sick and throws up: wiping her lips and then hands of the bile onto her worn jeans, she takes a deep breath to calm her shaking and opens the rusted metal door, slipping inside. As she takes in her surroundings, the mistake she made becomes immediately clear: the bang and clang of the door closing behind her is not even heard in the low cellar. Stretching out further than she would have imagined, the room is a chaotic mess of moving and writhing bodies. Skinheads, punks and leather-clad metalheads with painted faces gleefully throw themselves at each other and at the three musicians playing at the other end of the room between two towers of speakers. Led by a skinny man with a crewcut, the trio keeps on pounding out bursts of complete aural chaos that do not seem to make sense in the slightest. While the crewcut frontman and his backup guitarist scream their lungs out into the mics, literally inches away from the roaring crowd, the drummer looks to be in the midst of a fit of ecstasy and lunatic rage, flinging himself at his kit time and time again to produce the vein-invading pound the girl felt outside. No song is longer than 30 seconds: and no pause between songs is longer than 15 seconds. And the crowd never stops moving, not even once. The music itself seems to invade her head even when she screams and clasps her ears shut. The lyrics, mindless gibbering and screaming to her ears, seem to form their own horrific pictures in her mind. They are awful, leading her on a brief, dark trip to what these people are here for. She starts to recognize the songs, some which are played again and again to hammer the message into the minds of anyone within an earshot. The Sniper At The Fag Parade. 36 Counts Of Battery. Fuck Her Like You Paid For It. Prowler In Your Backyard. Screaming until her voice grows hoarse, the girl sits down in front of the door, too weak to open it and go back outside, back where no such intense hatred existed. She cries as a wave after wave of misogynism, anti-Semitism and militant solutions assault her conscious. She weeps, her shoulders heaving up and down, until finally she collapses in her own arms and feels like passing out. And suddenly, the grotesque theater of pain is over. When she hoists herself up from the floor, there are only a few people in the room, sitting on the floor and discussing this and that over a few beers. Where moments ago fists were flying, now there are only words spoken softly by tired young men who have expended all their bodies hold in them. Several bodies lie on the floor, some covered in vomit, and some covered in blood. The girl stands up, shyly, but it is apparent that nobody is interested in her. On the far wall, several graphic descriptions of acts of violence have been painted where the band was moments ago, but now everything seems to be oddly calm. The air feels like after a thunderstorm: calm after the show of rage. A door is open, leading into the building: more music streams through, but this time it is clearly from the radio, and to her relief it is nowhere near the level of what she was forced to witness… Moments ago? Or hours? She has no idea of how much time has passed. As her numb mind tries to go through what happened, she suddenly becomes aware of something she saw. A man, bald, his whole head painted red. Storming through the crowd without a shirt. The girl remembers seeing him always at the thick of the crowd, where the punches were the most numerous and the mayhem was the greatest. She remembers him repeatedly urging the band to go into even more extreme heights, one-upping even the singer’s electronics-powered show of vocal torture through sheer power. Her head suddenly feels like it has been cast into icy-cold water. She actually picked the right spot. She lived through the audiovisual carnage to know that her search is nearing its end. She has reached the source of all the answers, or so she believes. Finally, her future mentor is in her sights. Steadying herself against the wall, she crosses the bare concrete floor and slips into the open doorway. The wooden staircase smells of rotting insulation and old wood, but the pounding blood in her head makes her ignore it completely. Skipping up the steps quickly and easily, she comes to a dizzying set of corridors and rooms, all seemingly intermingling with each other in random patterns. Flakes of paint are peeling off the walls, and the old lightbulbs hang by their cords from the ceiling as she walks from room to room, carefully and lightly. Sleeping or passed out bodies populate most of the rooms, while others are filled with long-haired hippies wasting their bodies and minds away at their bongs. The raw, almost animal-like screams and moans emanating from one of the rooms force her to quickly go on; she knows what is happening in there all too well. A brief silhouette of her father flashes across her mind, and her body convulses involuntarily, forcing her to kneel down and close her eyes to concentrate on not throwing up again. Finally, her search reaches an end. At the end of the corridor, a barely open door lets in a few rays of electric light and low conversation. Walking silently, the girl reaches the door and presses her ear against it, breathing heavily out of fear and excitement. The voices on the other side sound exclusively male: the topics are on violence, revolution and intolerance. What’s the most effective way of beating someone up? Where can you find the best spots to pick a fight? Why black people are inferior to whites. Why capitalism should be crushed. Her mind lights up again, but this time with something else. She has finally found, by the sound of it, people who feel just as she does, who share her views cultivated in the turmoil of a destroyed childhood. Suddenly, the door is thrown clear open, and the girl stumbles forwards, yelping. Strong, muscular hands receive her in a crushing grip, nailing her where she stands. Scared, she looks up to a man well over six feet tall, with his head shaven and chin crossed with scars. The skinhead smiles wickedly at the intruder, and shakes her a bit to make her whimper. Several other skinheads sit on the floor or in moldy old chairs, backs pressed against the wall and faces turned towards the girl. Besides the skinheads, there is a ghoulish-looking young man in the room, all skin and bones and long hair, with his face painted heavily in white and black to resemble a dead corpse. And at the largest chair, at the middle of the circle, sits… The girl forgets her fear once she sees him: the same red paint, same muscular frame, same militant clothing. Unlike the rest of the men in the room, who are either laughing or cursing her, he sits in silence, eyeing her up and down. His eyes are not hateful; rather, his gaze is a calculating one, measuring her up from every angle and side. The girl falls oblivious to the words flying towards her, as she feels herself sink into those eyes, eyes that have seen many horrible things, and yet hint of things even worse to come. Suddenly, a violent shaking by the skinhead who is holding her awakens her back to reality, and her situation becomes apparent to her. Skinhead: “Fraulein, are you deaf? I asked you why were you snooping outside of our door? Have you ever heard of knocking, Fraulein? Look, if it’s crack you bitches want, I thought boss here established that you go look for that in the nigger quarters. None of that shit in here.” Girl: “Please, sir, I didn’t mean any harm, I just…” Skinhead: “Silence! You’ll speak when told so, mein Fraulein!” The skinhead whips her to the center of the room by her wrists and then backs to the door, closing it after making sure that no additional visitors await on the other side. The girl falls on her knees, suddenly aware of all the hatred directed towards her. She looks up and meets the gazes of the skinheads, looking at her like some subhuman being, while the ghoulish corpse eyes her with sheer lust and madness. As the young hopes of Aryan pride spit and insult her, the dark-haired youth at the side of the red-headed leader breathes faster and faster until he springs up from his chair and walks to the girl, looking at her with eyes that have not seen sleep in a long time. Roughly, violently, the ghoul grabs a handful of her hair and pulls back, making her scream and arch her back in an effort to relieve the pain, exposing her neck to the white face and white eyes. Ghoul: “Ooh… The One-Eyed One sure blesses us tonight with such a piece of meat. I wonder if she tastes better than she looks, a lanky whore like this…” The ghoul forces her upright by pulling her on her hair, making her plead for mercy as she stands up in agony. First drawing his tongue, then his finger across the girl’s throat, the ghoul brings his face and body close to the girl’s. Her senses are overwhelmed with disgust: disgust towards this bastard and these idiots mocking her. And then, out of the corner of her eye, she sees that the man she came looking for hasn’t still moved: he sits in his chair, still looking at her as if appraising her value, blue eyes darting in a red-painted face. Ghoul: “Do I scare you, girl? Are you afraid of a humble servant of the Great One-Eyed God? You do realize that your God matters not in here, whore? You are at my mercy, for me to do as I wish on you. I can do ANYTHING. And I have quite an imagination…” The skinny youth grabs a hitherto hidden piece of jewelry hanging around her neck, a simple golden cross on an elegant chain. With a snarl, he rips it straight off her neck, as the skinheads shout him encouragement and target further abuse at the girl. Her attention, however, is not at them: it is at the silent figure that has not moved since she entered the room. In his eyes, the girl finds that this is a mere test. A test whether or not she is worthy. Iron-hard conviction fills her essence and being, and she straightens her back in defiance to look straight into the ghoul boy’s face and growl at him in an almost animal-like way. Girl: “Whatever you do, you should do quick. You won’t get another chance.” Her assailant is taken aback, as the skinheads whoop and holler at their paintfaced friend for being zinged by this gutsy girl. A brief moment of physical weakness is replaced by intense hatred, as a bony hand lashes the girl against the face and then forces her back on her knees by roughly jerking her by the hair. In an instant, a hunting knife flashes into the ghoul’s hand: his white teeth are bared between the strands of raven-black hair, and his eyes are burning with the flames of hatred. The girl can feel the coldness of metal on her neck, so that every pulsating heartbeat feels like it is sending the skin on her throat closer to being cut. An evil, spiteful snarl answers her defying gaze, a gaze that cannot be wiped away with the threat of physical violence. Ghoul: “Arrogant, stupid whore! I’ll cut you up and offer your heart for the One-Eyed One! You will hang on Odin’s gallows by dawn!” ???: “Azagtoth!” Suddenly, all the noise and movement stops in the room. All that can be heard is the heavy, rasping breathing of the ghoul as he holds his knife to the girl’s throat with a shaking hand. The rest of the men are looking over to the last chair, and even the girl’s eyes turn into that direction. The man with the painted head is holding his right arm up, his palm pointed at the ghoul’s back. His face is stern, like a ruler apprehending a subject of his. As the girl turns her eyes back on the man holding her, she sees droplets of saliva at the corner of his mouth, but also sees something else: the unmistakable fear in his eyes. ???: “Azagtoth. That is enough. Now, put your toothpick away unless you wish to hang from gallows by dawn. The difference between yours and mine is that I only need rope and a window: you actually need a mythical being. Pathetic.” The ghoul trembles in place for a moment and then slowly draws his hand back, holstering his weapon. He takes a fearful look at the speaker and then suddenly spits into the girl’s face before releasing his hold on her hair and backing off. Again she finds herself in the center of all the attention, but this time the whole room is quiet. Men follow her every move while stealing glances at their leader, trying to reason out his confusing behavior. The man pays them no heed, instead staring at the girl with intensity. Despite her torn jeans and an old jacket, beneath which a homemade Adolf Hitler T-shirt sticks out, she feels terribly naked in front of his stare, and what moments ago gave her strength now takes that strength away. She turns her head towards the floor, too afraid to look at the powerful man in the eyes. ???: “Do you know who I am?” Slowly, the girl nods, and unseen to her a brief smile crosses his face. The others smile too, but not nearly as broadly as they do when he follows up with the next question. ???: “Speak my name then. Go on, don’t be scared. Just speak my name and assure these gentlemen that you are for real.” Suddenly overcome with a terrible feeling of shame, she feels the words lump in her throat. Tears begin to form in her eyes as she tries to force herself to say the simple request. Several of the skinheads in the room let out a chortle or two, obviously enjoying her humiliation. For her life she cannot understand what is going on: finally, she manages to blurt out the words, almost choking on a mix of fear and humiliation. Girl: “The Fifth… Fifth Horseman. Hate.” Several of the skinheads mockingly applaud her, while the ghoul known as Azagtoth looks at Hate, trying to figure out the man who commands his respect through fear and awe. Hate, however, has wiped the smile off his face, and now leans closer on his chair, his face taking on the look of an expressionless mask as he stares at the girl. Hate: “Well, that is most certainly correct. But chitchat aside. Look at me when I speak to you, girl. What is your name? Why are you here? Tell me, or I will not call Azagtoth off another time. I will let him have his way. Your future does not mean anything to me… YET. But it most certainly means something to you. Namely, it means whether or not you will get home to taste mom’s meat loaf tomorrow night.” Despite the thought-out way in which Hate speaks and the monotone in his voice, the girl cannot help but detect a hint of sarcasm and challenge in his voice. Slowly, she raises her head, first afraid but then swallowing her fear. Hate fills her whole vision, his face suddenly commands her all attention. Despite the weakness in her knees and stomach, she forces herself to answer, almost whispering into the air which is again heavy with silence. Girl: “I’m… Angelica, sir. I…” Hate: “Angelica? Oh, a tainting mark of the Judeo-Christian rot. How nice and sweet. Your father must think you as beautiful as the angels, those creatures of lies and pseudo-perfection. Do you ever think about that? That you are named after creatures that were created to make Mankind feel inferior and bad about itself? How would you react if I told you that millions have been driven to murder and suicide because they were not as perfect as your namesakes?” The brutal honesty and conviction in his words kills any objection or retort from Angelica. The girl stands, with her knees shaking uncontrollably, absolutely terrified of this man sitting in front of her. Her lower lip shakes as her mind is overridden with the thought that she is only a little girl, a girl of sixteen in a world that is far outside of her capabilities. These people are of a whole different caliber, she realizes: what she thinks or feels matters nothing to them. Recognizing her weakness, the skinheads move in again with laughing and jokes, but an angry wave of a hand from Hate shuts them up. Hate: “Enough! Incompetent fools. Even though she is named after a lie, and raised with lies piled on top of another, it does not mean that she is a lie herself. The fact that she stands in this very room right now testifies that there has to be something else underneath the surface: buried, yes, maybe even half-smothered by this disgusting upbringing that rears it ugly, beastly head. But no matter, programming was made to be deprogrammed. Lies are meant to be broken with truths. Angels and Angelicas are meant to be seduced by the so-called ‘devils’ of this world to show them the truth. The wings have to be cut. It hurts, but it is the only way.” The talk about cutting makes Angelica even more nervous, and she actually takes a step backwards towards the door. The feeling of something poking her in the spine stops her: and as she turns around, the smiling face of the skinhead who pulled her into this freezes her into place, as does the fist she backed into. She turns back to Hate, whom by now has gotten up from his chair and looms over her, making her feel insignificant and worthless to appear in front of him. Hate studies her with a pondering look on his face and then shakes his head, as if looking at something completely devoid of hope. Hate: “It is clear that you have heard something of me, Angelica, although I doubt it is anything too complimenting. More likely than not it is that inane blabber on the news or in the papers: those Judeo-Communist bastards will print anything brought to them that is suitably wrapped in hundred dollar bills. The thing is, even if you have heard of me, what forces you to come here? Are you expecting me to take you under my wing? Make you my disciple? Or do you just think that it would be ‘cool’ to meet me, so you can then return to your empty life and brag to your equally empty friends how you are ‘radical’ and have met Hate himself? Answer me. What do you want of me?” His words again suck her strength away, the straightforwardness makes her sick to her stomach. She lowers her gaze, but a displeased grunt from Hate brings her attention back to his face. An expecting look has taken over Hate’s face as he stands in front of Angelica and awaits a response. Finally, she coughs and again whispers, almost scared witless. Angelica: “No, I… I have nowhere to go, and… I think Hitler was a great man and that… That the government shouldn’t exist and that Jesus Christ was a homosexual and… I’ll do anything for you, sir! Anything!” Curious, Hate lifts his hand up and shuts the girl off. The skinheads are smiling, obviously amused by this scared young girl who tries to act like one of them. Behind Hate, Angelica can see Azagtoth smiling wickedly and tapping the handle of his knife, his mouth forming silent obscenities in the air directed towards her. Then, as Hate begins to speak, Angelica’s attention is back on him, like a death row victim listening to the sentence laid on them. Hate: “Ah. It seems that you do not know anything about me after all. Please, do not speak to me of the greatness of Hitler or the sexuality of Christ: those are arguments for the weaker men, for those who are as morally corrupt as those they claim to oppose. I am Hate: the root of Famine, Pestilence, War and Death. For does not a man deny his wife and children food out of hate? Is it not hate towards society that drives people to knowingly spread the HIV amongst their partners? What is War if not the culmination of hate between two nations? And hate is at the base of every death: every demise is the result of hate, one way or another. There are no deaths born out of love, only those borne out of anger and spite exist. And please, do not tell me that you will do anything for me. I am not a man to take such a comment lightly. If you suggest, if you even hint of total submission, I want it to be true. I want it to be real. I want you answering of your actions only to me. Not to the police, not to the courts of law, not to any Gods out there. Take Azagtoth here for an example. He once came to me and pledged himself to me. He prays to Odin but knows that his soul is truly mine. And he told me the exact same thing: that he would do anything for me, would do anything I asked him to do. So I asked him to burn down a church. The exact same church where he was falsely introduced to an unfriendly dogma through the spiritual attack known as baptism, to where he was further cast into the ethereal abyss through his confirmation, to where he finally came to terms with himself and denounced that liar called God. And if this emotional burden was not enough, the body of his father was also on display at the church on that night I ordered him to do it, left there from the wake before the funeral. A man could snap under such pressure, be left as a gibbering mess at the door from the sheer thought of the task. Azagtoth, tell Angelica what you did.” Horrified, Angelica turns her head towards the being known as Azagtoth, who flashes another vampiric smile at her before hissing his answer in pleasure. Azagtoth: “It burned for three hours. All they recovered from my father was a burnt finger.” Hate nods approvingly and then looks at Angelica, who again looks absolutely mortified. Hate looks at both sides of him, where the skinheads are shouting encouragement and cheers for Azagtoth and his dark deed. Azagtoth himself sits back in his chair, crossing his legs and smiling in great pleasure. Hate: “So you see, Angelica, I take this very seriously. I take people very seriously. If you want to be one of my people, you have to give yourself over to me. Give me everything you have. Whatever love you have for your parents, you give it to me. Whatever loyalty you have to your friends and mentors, you give it to me. Whatever blood ties, eternal friendships and even verbal bonds you have made, you turn them all over to me. I am the center of your universe: your very essence, your very soul is tied to me. Every single one of these men in this room has pledged themselves to me, and have not regretted it since. The question is, are you willing to do that? Will you give yourself to me? If you are serious about doing anything for me, if you seriously want to please me that bad, then by all means do it. Do anything, ANYTHING that proves your loyalty to me. Show me that your former life means nothing in comparison to the new one I can give you.” Slowly, Angelica lowers her head, her face filled with terror and tears. The words that Hate has spoken touch her deep and bring back memories, both happy and sad, healing and hurting. Her parents, her siblings, her schoolmates and teachers, the neighborhood kids, all spin and weave in her conscious, whispering and calling her to come back. To abandon this nonsense right now, turn around and march out of the door, back to where she belongs. And yet, ultimately, she can see that they all rotate around one face, slowly drifting towards it and getting absorbed. The face of Hate suddenly begins to grow in her mind’s eye, starting as small as the head of a pin and quickly growing as it assimilates more and more of the people she knows. As her favorite arts teacher sinks into the inky blackness that is the eye of Hate, a shiver goes through the body of Angelica and she slips her right arm out of the jacket. When her trio of girlfriends, one by one, fade and disappear into Hate’s impassive expression, she lets the jacket drop on the floor and lets her gaze follow it. As the memory of her brother drops, so does her homemade T-shirt, landing in a heap so that Adolf Hitler seems to be looking at the proceedings with his brow distorted into a sad expression of caring, looking ever so perverse on the face tied to mass murder. As Hate picks up the T-shirt and then pulls it over her head, forcing her back into it, she breaks out into uncontrollable sobs, hurt by the rejection of her spiritual submission. Hate takes a step back and shakes his head, sighing in disappointment. Hate: “Please, my Angel. You clearly don’t understand me. Azagtoth, hand me your toothpick.” The surprised Azagtoth fumbles around a bit with his hunting knife and then hands it to Hate, who picks it up and offers it to Angelica, handle first, his face somber and waiting. Hate waits until the crying and sobs die down to near silence, and then speaks again. Hate: “My Angel, if you truly wish to submit to me, if you truly want to be a part of me forever, then you will take this which I offer you and spill the blood of Azagtoth. I want you to offer him as a sacrificial lamb to me, to show that you truly want to serve and please me as the only purpose of your life.” Slowly, Angelica takes the offered knife, the weight of the blade feeling alien in her hands. And yet, when she picks her head up and looks at Hate and the awaiting Azagtoth, she is overcame with a want deeply rooted in her soul. She realizes that nothing that Hate asks from her is too much, that nothing she can imagine is too big for him to command her to do. As she looks at Azagtoth, she sees a ghoul who is the last wall between herself and Hate. A fire of determination fills Angelica’s eyes as she takes two steps to bring herself in front of Azagtoth’s chair. The skinny man laughs and encourages Angelica to take a shot, fully convinced that she does not have the guts for it. As he offers his chin upward for a target, a painful image of Hate’s face flashes across Angelica’s mind, and she feels her body moving automatically… Azagtoth screams out in pain and surprise and holds his cheek with both hands. A dark crimson rose spreads on his white face and streams down on his clothes through his fingers as Angelica steps back, shocked and thrilled at what she has done. The edge of the knife in her hand gleams with red as deep as the paint on Hate’s head, and some of her excitement rubs off on him as well as he claps a couple of times. Just as Angelica turns towards Hate, her eyes filled with joy, Azagtoth screams in a way that sends shivers down everyone’s spines and then jumps off from the chair, tackling Angelica savagely against the floor. Some of the skinheads move to interfere, but Hate stops them with a flick of his hand. Azagtoth wrestles the knife away from Angelica with speed and ruthlessness. He can taste and smell his own blood streaming from the wound on his cheek, and he begins raining a blow after a blow on the head and body of Angelica. The girl screams out in terror and pleads for Hate and the others to help her, but to no avail. Soon, Azagtoth draws her blood on his fists, her lips quickly colored blood red from the bleeding in her mouth. After a few more punches, Hate steps forwards and places his hand on Azagtoth’s right shoulder just as the man draws his arm back for another punch. Hate: “That is enough, Azagtoth.” Grudgingly, the man lowers his fist and spits into Angelica’s face before getting off her. She instantly curls up into a fetal position and begins whimpering, her face puffed and bruised and streaked with bright red blood from her lips. This showcase of savagery against a woman shocks even the most hardened skinheads, but Hate does not seem fazed. Instead he helps Angelica up, pulling her on her feet despite her moans of pain. Azagtoth withdraws into the far corner of the room, trying to block the blood flow from his cheek with a pillow from one of the chairs. Hate stands Angelica up straight, holding her by her shoulders and looking straight into her eyes, clouded over by pain and terror. Hate: “It is OK now, my Angel. No one will ever hurt you again, I swear on my own head. As long as I live, nobody will lay a finger on you without me breaking it off. You are my now. I will protect you until the end of the world. You are truly my Angel now. Welcome home.” Crying, Angelica throws herself at Hate, who wraps his arms around the girl and holds her tight, as she sobs into his shoulder. She cries tears of pain from the beating as well as tears of happiness from the fact that now she truly knows where she belongs. She has committed to a relationship far more serious and deeper than even holy matrimony… And for the first time in her life, she feels truly like the people around her welcome her and want her to be there. She leans closer and whispers softly into Hate’s ear, her voice filled with happiness and gratefulness. The Inspiration Of Everything That I Wish I Was, And Nothing That I Could Ever Be.
