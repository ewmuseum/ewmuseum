Dealing with Reality

30 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-30-06 What causes fights and quarrels among you? Don’t they come from your desires that battle within you? You want something but don’t get it. You kill and covet, but you cannot have what you want. You quarrel and fight. You do not have, because you do not ask God. When you ask, you do not receive because you ask with wrong motives, that you may spend what you get on your pleasures.-James 4:1-3 Holy Bible NIV Dear Teresa I am back home in the states on a two week leave. I visited Mama. What the hell is your problem? First you beat the shit out of her nurse right in front of her two young kids and Mama, and now after purchasing the newest AWC magazine, I see you nearly nude? I’m worried about you sis. Scott contacted me and told me what happened between you two. Here’s a pre-stamped envelope to my base in Iraq. Please write me. If not me, find a doctor or something. Your brother, Private Eric Tomas In a glass ash tray about the size of a half dollar a half-smoked cigarette was left to smolder. Teresa sat on the un-kept hotel bed re-reading the letter from her brother. Even as an adult, fighting overseas in a war, determined to help protect his country, Eric still held the need to protect his baby sis. Teresa released a deep sigh before returning the letter into the envelope and placed it on the nightstand beside the bed. A knock came to the door. Teresa rolled her eyes and stood up knowing too well who would be knocking at her door at 9am. To her surprise, when she opened the door, no one was there. A small green vase stood in the place of who she thought would be Jack. Inside the vase was a single red rose in full bloom. Teresa bent down to pick up the vase and when she looked around, she saw no one. A card was placed on a plastic stem inside the vase with a message. I haven’t forgotten. She didn’t hear the footsteps when she turned around. To have a single red rose left at her doorstep just didn’t happen everyday, especially to Teresa Jane Tomas. She simply wasn’t the type. The sudden whiff of Stetson cologne tickled her nostrils like a giggling pixy full of mischief. “Whatcha got there?” He stepped behind her and peered over her shoulder. His arm snaked around her small waist. Teresa stepped away to place the green vase on the dresser. She turned around, crossed her arms over her chest and stared at him accusingly. “This isn’t like you Jack.” Jack shrugged and spat into an empty Mountain Dew bottle. A wad of Copenhagen made his lower lip look swollen. “I told you I wasn’t leaving empty-handed. Besides, my truck broke down. The poor thing over heated and well, you get the picture.” “Uh huh..” Teresa nodded and unfolded her arms and grabbed a Tennessee Titans cap, pulled her long hair through the hole in the back and placed it on her head just above her eyebrows. She knew Jack could’ve had his truck repaired hours ago if he wanted. He knew automotives as well as her, if not better. Together, they stepped outside to where the Ford F150 was parked. The morning sun was bright and the slight breeze served as a reminder of autumn that quickly approached. Jack led Teresa to the red pick-up truck and lifted the hood. He watched as Teresa leaned over to examine the engine. “I’ll tell ya exactly what the problem is.” Teresa said before reaching into the cab for a grease rag lying on the dashboard. Jack feigned wonderment as he spat what remained of the dip wad into the Mt. Dew bottle. He then threw the bottle into the bed of the truck on top of a small mound of similar bottles. “Let me guess. It’s a Ford.” They’ve had this argument one too many times before. Jack was a Ford man while Teresa favored the Chevrolet. It was the most famous past time of the Southeastern U.S. American besides NFL and NASCAR. “Damn skippy. I told you years ago the worst mistake you’ve ever made was buying a damn Ford.” “You’re full of shit Teresa. The worst mistake I ever made was with you. And where is your trusty Silverado?” There was never a winner in these arguments. This was no different. Jack reached into a large Igloo cooer that sat amongst the spit-filled Mountain Dew bottles for a beer. He tossed Teresa can and opened his own. A cold beer at 9am didn’t settle well with Teresa’s stomach. As much as she enjoyed drinking, a cold beer was never on her breakfast menu. She tossed the can back and leaned back over the engine. “I left it in the states. You honestly think I’ll ship my truck all over God’s creation and spend god knows what on the expenses? If I was such a mistake, then why are you even here?” “Uh huh” Jack took a drink of his beer and leaned his back against his truck. “I told you that yesterday. You might’ve been a mistake, but it was the best mistake I’ve ever made.” “Kiss my white ass Jack Slayter. You’re full of more shit than I am.” Teresa stepped back from the truck with a dipstick in hand. She examined it, wiped the oil off with a grease rag, and then leaned back under the hood to return it. “By the way, when was the last time you changed the oil in this thing?” Jack brought his beer down away from his lips. His brow furrowed in thought. This made Teresa grin. The smart mechanic as he appears to be, he neglects to remember to change the oil. “That’s what I thought.” Teresa slammed the hood closed and began toward her hotel room. “I keep telling you, coming here is a waste of time. Change your oil and go home. Oh and by the way, roses aren’t my type. You know that.” Jack watched Teresa walk to her door. He took a final drink from his beer can and casually tossed it over his shoulder into the bed of his truck. Not only he didn’t forget to change the oil, the darn thing has been leaking for months, he didn’t send her the rose. If he had, he wouldn’t have enough to buy the extra can of Copenhagen that was in his back pocket.
