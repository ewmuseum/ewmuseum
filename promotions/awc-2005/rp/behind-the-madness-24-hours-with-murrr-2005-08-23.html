Behind the Madness: 24 Hours with Murrr

23 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23rd August From the September 2005 Edition of Inside Aberdeenshire magazine. Words: Richard Bailey What goes on inside the head of Andy M.? A good question, and one that has never really been answered. On the outside, we all know the Atlantic Wrestling Club's own 'Scottish King of Cool' as a jovial but somewhat unhinged character. Either this self-made millionaire is as off-the-wall as we are led to believe, or a more complex character lies underneath the goofy exterior. So, what makes Andy M. tick? Spending 24 hours in the company of the up-and-coming AWC superstar, I aim to answer that question. Meeting Andy M. for the first time is much like meeting an old friend for the first time in a decade; he'll greet you with a warm smile, a hearty handshake, and will treat you like a member of his family. In fact, as we sat down for lunch in one of Andy's favourite haunts - The Beautiful Mountain, a Tallahassee sandwich bar ('it does great food, despite the hippy-ish name,' Andy will later remark) - it feels like I have known the man all my life. Such is the hospitality of the man. It should be noted that myself and Mr. M. have crossed paths in the past; however, we have never communicated. Unknowing to Andy, by pure coincidence, I once ended up sitting directly behind him on a transatlantic plane journey as his wrestling career was just starting to take shape. I remember that particular flight for one reason; I couldn't catch a wink of sleep down to one someone sitting in front of me. No, it wasn't Andy, but a seemingly insane woman who he had been sitting next to him. I bring this into the conversation; Andy chuckles, knowing immediately what I was getting at. 'Oh yes... THAT flight!' Andy recalled, 'Yeah, that woman was proper tits-up crazy, I tell you. She was talking about travelling to Egypt in a rubber dinghy and going fishing for crabs, or something along those lines. And she told me that her son was a world renowned aubergine pie taster, or something... and all the time I'm sitting there like 'whoa lady, you're scaring me!'' As our orders arrive - Andy's a generously-sized cheddar cheese granary baguette with a side salad, mine a good old-fashioned bacon, lettuce tomato - Andy shares jokes with the waiter who he seems to know personally, being a regular here. I comment that the personality of the woman on that flight draws parallels with Andy's current psyche. 'Yeah, I see what you're getting at there!' he proclaims as he takes a bite out of his lunch, '... but I'm far cooler than that sucker! Recently though, it seems I've been encountering a lot more psychopaths like that, though. I mean, one my second ever AWC Fresh! appearance I signed a girl's arm for her... then she's at the show the following week, and she's like 'hey, look at this'... she got my signature tattooed to her damn arm! I mean, that's flattering and all, but like I said to her, do you really want the signature of some wrestler inked onto your arm for the rest of your life? Oh well... I guess now that my profile's rising I'll have to get used to crazy stuff like that!' Having studied a number of sportsmen over my years as a journalist, I have come across very few - if any - who are as genuine, honest, or just interested as 'the Scottish King of Cool.' In a sport where arrogance is common - especially among the sport's elder statesmen - Andy M. is certainly a breath of fresh air. 'There's a whole lot of ego running around, that's for sure,' Andy comments. 'I've come across a lot of guys like that; I just tend to stay out of their way. I just can't be bothered with it, you know? I don't like egos and I don't like people who try to drag each other down - what's the point? I'm a simple guy, I like to chill out and do the things I love, and as long as nobody tries to get in my way, then I'm happy... and at the moment, things couldn't be better!' As Andy polishes off the final lettuce leaves of his salad, for the first time in the afternoon he will veer into the territory that AWC fans have recently entered. He will comment on the health of his pet fire-breathing badgers (which, at this point in the day, I'm not sure actually exist) and how he hopes Alvaro Reyes, his butler, has taken them on their daily walk. How did Andy name his prized badger, Snowball? 'I didn't,' he answered, 'that was the work of my good friend Jack Sullivan, who I was tagging with a couple of years ago. Pretty good name, I think you'll agree. Plus, it's totally deceiving... it's a pretty wimpy name for one badass motherhubbard of a badger!' A fire-breathing badger is a pretty intriguing choice of pet, and certainly a dangerous one. Why not go with a dog or a cat? Andy titters, “That’s boring! Well, cats and dogs are alright, but they don’t really do much, do they? This rare breed of Bolivian badgers I keep are total magic; they’re super-intelligent, and take good care of themselves. Plus, they’re pretty handy for torching anyone who screws with you!” I guess later on in the day I’ll discover if these creatures are really as they seem. Upon finishing a highly enjoyable lunch, Andy tips the waiter with generosity, and soon we’re in the “Andymobile,” heading for Andy’s home on the outskirts of Tallahassee. Andy’s choice of vehicle is rather odd – an old-school Volkswagen van with heavy modifications. The paintwork is certainly eye-catching – a swirling hotchpotch of red, lime green, yellow and sky blue, with the words “Andy Rules You” sprayed in white along either side – and the van itself runs as sweet as a nut. Clearly, a lot of hard work has gone into perfecting this mean machine. “Like my ride,” Andy asks. “I’ve spent quite a bit of cash on this badboy, but it’s been worth it,” he grins. But surely someone of such stature would be use to riding around in a chauffeur driven limousine? “Nah, not for me, thanks… I don’t want to become a total lazy sod! I hire a butler to keep the house in order, because I’m not around all the time, and the mess that Cayle, Grobschnit, and the badgers make can be horrendous! I’m not a total slave driver!” Arriving at Andy’s large country retreat – “Andyville,” as he likes to refer to it – is much like entering a royal palace. From the large, gated entrance, the house isn’t ever visible through the semi-dense woodland leading up to it. Eventually, after a short trip up the gravel driveway, Andy’s house becomes visible. A majestic mass of red brick and white trim, it truly is a sight to behold; a home fit for a king (a Scottish King of Cool, perhaps?). Andy inherited this home a couple of years ago following the simultaneous death of his Aunt Karine and Uncle Gregory – a local media tycoon – in a car crash. Andy, at first, had mixed feelings about moving into the mansion, he explains; “It was pretty odd for me, you know, some kid from a cul-de-sac in Scotland moving into this big beast… it was a little overwhelming. But it didn’t take long to settle in… it’s not a hard place to love really, is it?” It most certainly isn’t. Andy takes me through the stunningly well-decorated hallway, straight into the well-equipped kitchen. Here we find Andy’s butler Alvaro working alongside a hideous-looking green creature dressed like Dr. Dre, preparing all kinds of party food. Later on in the evening, Andyville was to play host to an exclusive garden party, friends and family invited to celebrate the recent rejuvenation of Andy’s wrestling career, which just a few short months ago seemed dead in the water. Immediately, Andy heads towards the green being and high fives him. Andy asks how the creature is doing; it replies with an almost incomprehensible jabbering, which sounded something like “izz goode, dood, fagitz.” Right. Wasting little time, I’m formally introduced to the creature; Grobschnit. As we shake hands, the alien eyes me up with suspicion, as though I was invading his territory. Andy remarks; “Don’t mind the ‘Schnit, he’s a little cautious around strangers.” His black, orb-like eyes are making me feel a little uneasy at the moment. Alvaro, Andy’s butler, is clearly a hard-worker, but you get the impression that he enjoys his job underneath his rough exterior. The perfectly-moustached Portugal native will later confirm this, telling me that although he has to put in a lot of hours, he’s paid well and has a free roof over his head, courtesy of Andy. Thankfully, we soon leave the kitchen – good job too, Grobschnit’s constant staring was starting to worry me a little. We head out onto a patio, where Andy’s younger brother, Cayle, sits reclined in a chair, strumming away on a Tanglewood acoustic guitar. On first impressions, Cayle is a pretty similar character to Andy in that he is warm and welcoming. He introduces himself as “a guy who leaches a living off of his older brother,” which is met with a chuckle from Andy. However, before I really get the chance to get to know Cayle, he departs; perhaps we had disrupted solitude that he had been enjoying? “Cayle is a little misled,” Andy will tell you. “He’s 25 years old, but I still don’t think he knows what he wants to do with himself. He attempted a career in wrestling himself a short while ago, but it didn’t work out.” How long has he been living in “Andyville”? “I took Cayle in as soon as he got out of the slammer, which was about six months ago. He did 18 months for drink-driving, and when he attempted to return home, his girlfriend threw him out. That’s loyalty for you. Is he a burden? Nah… he’s family, and it’s not like I have to worry about him getting under my feet or anything – look at the size of this place! He can stay for as long as he wants… until he gets himself back on his feet… but he’ll be out of here a damn sight quicker if he doesn’t stop stealing my guitars!” Andy laughs, pulling up the Tanglewood, before strumming out a couple of guitar riffs. As the self-proclaimed Scottish King of Cool sits on the edge of his chair, widdling away on the acoustic guitar, he is an icon of relaxation. The word “Shatterproof” is tattooed on one of his arms; is Andy M.’s life as care-free as it would appear? At that point, Andy’s cell phone rings. He answers it in a friendly-enough manner, but his tone soon changes. After a heated exchange with the person on the other end of the phone, he finally concedes defeat in the argument, and slides the Motorola back into the pocket of his $10 pair of Wal-Mart jeans. “That was my ex-wife, Jane,” he explains sombrely, eschewing the first signs of discontent all day. “Once again, she’s blackmailing me, so, it looks like my son Marvin will be joining us tonight. I love my son to death, don’t get me wrong, but I don’t think tonight’s party will be the right environment for him. Oh well, what can you do when the she-wolf pulls this “but you haven’t done ANYTHING with him for days” crap?” Clearly, Andy’s relationship with his former wife is highly strained. As I probe further, it is quite clear that Andy feels uncomfortable with it, but being a professional, he is quite happy to explain. “We married young and had Marvin young – he’s seven now. I was nineteen when we first got together, and at the time, things were great. But as my commitment to pursuing my wrestling career grew, everything began to turn sour. So grew bitter, and tired of me not being around; which is fair enough I guess. We split soon after, and we barely talk these days… usually, it’s just “You’re looking after Marvin tonight so I can go out to a nightclub”.” Perhaps the life of Andy M. isn’t as peach as we are lead to believe? Andy shrugs this off immediately, which a massive grin, similar to those that we’ve come accustomed to over the past couple of months. “Nah, I don’t let bullshit like that drag me down; if I did, I’d be a miserable twat all the time, and you don’t want that to happen, do you?” God no. As the day passes-by, Andy gives me a tour of the home and its grounds. He shows me through the numerous bedrooms in the home – all decorated as tastefully as each other – and even the infamous Andycave; a subterranean entertainment epicentre that is as kooky as the owner of the property himself. And of course, no tour of Andyville is complete without a visit to the badger cage. Yes, it’s true; all seven of Andy’s pet badgers can, indeed, breathe fire, as Andy is more than keen to demonstrate. As Snowball launches a barrage of flames in my direction, Andy is gleaming with joy. “Aren’t they just awesome?” he enquires with a toothy grin. Quite. I enquire as to what it is about these genetically-mutated beasts that fascinates Andy so much, he replies vaguely with “their all-round coolness.” Later on in the evening, Andy is entertaining around about sixty guests under a large marquee tent on his perfectly-kept lawn at the back of the house. Nobody seems to be dressed too formally, and everyone appears to be having a great time as Alvaro and Grobschnit zip around with complimentary glasses of champagne. As the sounds of the Offspring’s “Greatest Hits” CD (one of Andy’s favourite bands) play in the background, I find Andy mingling with the guests, a glass of lemonade in one hand, and the hand of his son Marvin in the other. Marvin is the spitting image of his father – short, black hair, perfectly white teeth, and rather tall for his age. He wears glasses, and seems understandably intimidated by the whole thing. Cayle, meanwhile, seems more concerned with trying to win points with some of the young ladies in attendance. “That’s just Cayle for you,” Andy laughs, before wandering into the house to put his son to bed as the hour grows late. The level of service offered by Andy’s butler Alvaro is exceptional tonight, and I’ve always got a glass of champagne in my hand (honestly, perhaps a little too often). Grobschnit, on the other hand, still seems to be sneering at me. Odd creature. During Andy’s brief absence, I meet his older brother, Sidney – himself a successful wrestling on the Florida indy scene – for the first time. While not as instantly welcoming as Andy or as brash as Cayle, Sid acts almost like the “guardian” of the M. brothers. Standing at a staggering 6’9”, speaking to Sidney would be made a lot easier with the aid of a chair to stand on. “My brother’s a good guy,” he asserts in a deep, rough voice. “He would never do anyone any harm without provocation, and always sets out to make a good impression. And there’s no one better to pass a couple of hours with – trust me. Sometimes he doesn’t take things as seriously as he should, but he soon brushes away whatever troubles may come his way. Also, I wouldn’t trust the guy with anything important,” Sid laughs, “he’s not that responsible. But who cares?” Indeed. Soon enough, Andy has returned, still drinking from the same glass of lemonade. “I don’t drink alcohol often,” Andy will explain, “don’t get me wrong, I’m not straight-edge or anything like that. It just doesn’t do that much for me.” After giving a short speech to those in attendance, Andy will again disappear into the crowd, catching up with old friends. The party would go on to continue until about 2am, when the crowd began to disperse and tired guests headed home to their bed. I’d definitely had too much champagne by this point; time to hit the sack. I woke up the following morning with a splitting headache – definitely too much wine. Either way, after washing the sleep from my eyes and throwing on a fresh pair of clothes, I found Andy M. in as jovial a mood as ever, tucking into a bowl of Lucky Charms. “No fancy nonsense for me, I dig Lucky Charms – they taste great, and that Leprechaun is just badass.” We talk about the day before, although Andy will soon have to head out on the road again – the Atlantic Wrestling Club calls. “It’s awesome to be back in the ring,” Andy proclaims, “absolutely incredibly. Pearl and the AWC guys have been absolutely brilliant, and I thank them for the opportunities that have been coming my way recently. Having been out of action for over a year, it was tough at first, but now I feel I’m approaching my peak, and I’ve got plenty gas left in the tank.” Has his infamous recurring back injury caused him any set backs thus far? “Not really, I feel the odd twang every now and then, but that’s it. Dr. Keaton, my physician, seems to think I shouldn’t ever be setting foot inside a ring again. Maybe he’s right, I couldn’t end up paralysed… but this is my lifesblood, I can’t just throw it all away.” And that, as they say, is that. Andy is whisked away on business. He offers me the chance to hang around at Andyville until it’s time to catch my flight out of town; but I don’t think I can stand another of Grobschnit’s threatening glances. Instead, I share a taxi with Andy to the airport, where we part with another handshake and, on my side, a far greater understanding of the man I have spent the last 24 hours in the company of. Behind the strange affinity for illiterate green aliens and fire-breathing badgers lies a big-hearted, friendly individual. After spending a full day in the company of this charismatic Scotsman, I can assure you that there is a pretty normal guy behind the insanity. He is a man who clearly makes the most out of things, and has dug out a wonderful lifestyle for himself and those he associates himself with. You get the impression that if you befriended Andy M. he would stand up for you until the end. While others may have abused his trust in the past, Andy has clearly taken it all in his stride. He loves wrestling, he loves music, he loves kicking back and having a good time, and clearly isn’t going to let anything get in the way of that. So what does make Andy M. tick? Is it a constant need to be in the public eye as an AWC superstar? Perhaps avarice and the pursuit of wealth? Does he associate himself with a grammatically calamitous create like Grobschnit for nothing more than attention? Absolutely not. Andy M. is man driven solely by the pursuit of joy and adrenaline; a thrill seeker if you want to be cliché. He will wrestle for as long as his injured back allows, will play guitar until his fingers fall off, and will continue to crack jokes for as long as there is air in his lungs. Still, I don’t quite understand the whole badger thing.
