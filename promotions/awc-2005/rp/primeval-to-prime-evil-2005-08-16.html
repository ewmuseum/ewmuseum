Primeval to PRIME-evil

16 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 16/08/05 The York Hall, Bethnal Green. Empty; it’s cracked plaster and mildewing smell harkened back to better times. Not so tonight, as *for one night only* the golden era had returned. This prestigious venue, more familiar to the British public as a pugilistic Mecca for the devotees of boxing, had been overrun by another crowd not too dissimilar in it’s bloodlust. The All-Star Wrestling promotion had hit London, thus providing the big draw for the old hall which was now dangerously bursting beyond capacity. Somehow this decaying construct breathed in this new life; sweating labourers, their slicked back hair still coated in brick dust, cloyed with the fetid perfume arising from the front row of handbag-toting geriatric grannies. Overhead, smoke curled lazily upward before lounging near the light fittings; nicotine yellowed bulbs tried to highlight almost a century of passive smoking, but to no avail. If violence was the foundation, then people and atmosphere were the bricks and mortar for this old hall. Tonight the roof could be raised but it wouldn’t fall in. The salt of the Earth - or the scum of the Earth - depending on which rung of the class ladder you nestled, were tightly packed to the rafters for some affordable entertainment. More than that, they had come to watch a social drama unfold, good versus evil, an anaesthetic to the humdrum of their ordinary lives. Fought out before them within that roped-off stage would be a moral play, good would overcome evil, and the resultant feel-good-factor would unite the crowd in a way not last felt in this part of London since the common solidarity shared during the bombings of the Blitz. An electric intensity to the air indicated the main event was about to begin. A real clash of the titans which had sold-out similarly dilapidated venues up and down the British Isles. It was a feud that regularly drew the lion’s share of the viewing figures on a Saturday afternoon. Big Daddy versus Giant Haystacks. And in all their previous clashes Big Daddy had won: every, single, time. In fact history would show that Giant Haystacks would never beat the nation’s favourite top hat toting, spangled cape wearing golden boy. The old and the young, but especially the old, projected an infectious - almost Churchillian form - of hero-worship upon their idol. There wasn’t a man or woman in the house tonight who didn’t want to see Big Daddy win. There was a young boy however... Ian could hardly breath. The smoky atmosphere attacked his underdeveloped lungs while the stench of unwashed bodies assaulted his nostrils. Worst of all though was the miasma of pestilence that constituted his grandmother’s *special* perfume; it literally burned the back of his throat. On past reflection, anything less than embalming fluid wouldn’t have masked the harridan’s natural aroma of piss. Indeed, it was the mad matriarch who’d dragged the pre-pubescent English to this special event in London; eighty miles south on a dusty coach with other old bags from the East Anglian branch of the Women’s Institute. Great. But this was no SAGA holiday to Yarmouth Pleasure Beach. A Hell’s Angels Chapter didn’t have shit on these old bitches - between apple pie baking and gossip mongering the old biddies had held a seminar on handbags. Yeah handbags. The seamstresses among them informed the sisterhood of the benefits in reinforcing shoulder straps with cross-stitching, insuring they wouldn’t snap during prolonged whacking. Each had their own ingenious tips for adding ballast: from the subtle (bags of marbles and loose change) to the unsubtle (a brick). Kop one of those in the side of the noggin and you were looking at a special date with the A&E nurse in casualty. And so it began. With the appearance of Big Daddy clapping his hands and rousing the crowd, the elderly seized their moment for a charge on the front railings. Their ferocity matched any of the old firms involved in England’s notorious football hooliganism; smashing aside chimney sweeps, Chelsea pensioners, eel sellers, pearly kings and queens and any other cockney stereotype unlucky enough to get in their way. Caught in a tide of blue rinse, Ian washed up against the railings, nearly getting his head stuck through the gap. “WE SHALL NOT, WE SHALL NOT BE MOVED...” the crowd roared his trademark anthem much to the Daddy’s approval. With wrinkled, knobbly, knees pressing into the back of his kidneys and sharp elbows resting against the crown of his skull, Ian finally caught a glimpse of the heroic wrestler as he wobbled past the narrow vision slit posed between the bars. The youngster’s as yet unbroken voice let out a shrill cry: “BOOOO, YA FAT BASTID!!” Despite cupping his hands around his mouth, the taunt was drowned out in the sea of approval. *CRACK* Searing pain blindsided the back of Ian’s head. He looked behind and up, moist eyes misting his sight - his grandmother scowled down at him. “You little Turk, where did you learn to use such foul language!?” “But Granddad George sez it all the time...” “...your Grandfather is an idiot” the witch scolded. “If you don’t want to end up like that work-shy ragamuffin - of which I have the grave misfortune to call *husband* - I suggest you find a proper role model. Look at Big Daddy, look how the crowd cheer and love him, you would do well to take note of his example!” You think that now days a boy flying around on a broomstick pretending to be a witch is a gay role model for kids? Well try a fat man wearing a spangly cape replete with glitter-coated top hat. “Aww Gran...” “...and don’t call me that! It makes me sound old.” Her gaze floated doe-eyed over Ian’s head, settling on the star of the show. Under his breath Ian muttered, “coffin dodger.” *CRACK* “Less of the lip boy!” Stupid. He hadn’t even wanted to come with the stupid old cow to this stupid crappy place to watch stupid boring British wrestling. He remembered wistfully back to a big house in the neighbouring village; Granddad George had slipped his skinny grandson through an open window on the ground floor allowing Ian to unlock the back door. This was their favourite game - a jaunt Granddad George liked to call “Treasure Hunt.” This particular house had loads of neat new things in it, all top-of-the-range latest technology; a beetamax tape recorder, a Commodore 64 cassette-deck computer, and something known as satellite television. Remarkably it enabled you to view what Americans watched a million miles away. With the sound turned down low (in accordance with the “Treasure Hunt” rule of keeping quiet) they had watched many weird and wonderful things like channels devoted to Jesus Christ, unrelated girls kissing each other on the lips and, most amazing of all, American wrestling. (Incidentally, other rules of “Treasure Hunt” included: collecting items with a value of ten quid or more in a sack and running like hell when sirens and blue flashing lights were seen/heard.) American wrestling was everything British wrestling wasn’t - fast, exciting, high flying - minus half the rules. Better still, they didn’t try to spend two hours applying a hammerlock on the mats. After seeing such a spectacle it was hard to return to mundane Saturday afternoon belly-flopping. The only good thing to happen so far tonight was Kendo Nagasaki had beat Pat Roach in the previous match, much to the crowd’s disapproval. (He won by spraying a fine mist of some dodgy green substance into the bearded man’s eyes – a move that greatly impressed the young English.) Now a microphone dropped from the ceiling attached to a long wire allowing Big Daddy the opportunity to preach to the masses. He called out Giant Haystacks calling him a liar, a cheat and a scoundrel. Ian’s face felt sticky (unhelped by the spit-wash his grandmother had given him with a soggy tissue upon arrival at the hall, Nagasaki’s poison mist would have been preferable) and he could feel the grit underfoot through the flimsy soles of his Dunlop plimsolls. Worse still, even more so than the aching in the back of his head, was the incessant itching of his woollen jumper. He wore the garment with shame: a Big Daddy piece knitted from a coarse grade yarn, perhaps the coarsest his grandmother could find, maybe even industrial grade wire wool. Every fibre of his being, every nerve ending, screamed out to be scratched. ‘Big Daddy’ was monogrammed on the front. Underneath was a knitted pattern of the man himself: only it looked more like Zippy from Rainbow after a hideous burns accident. Forced to wear it on a regular basis, Ian had painfully found that the jumper was to bigger kids what blood was to sharks. Edward the Confessor once wore a vest made of untreated goat’s wool as a daily penance. When he died his subjects discovered that the king’s body was covered in grazes and sores and lice as a result. Well Edward the Confessor’s vest didn’t have shit on this jumper; not only did you look a prize tool with arms too long “to grow into” and rashes that made ‘Naam’s Agent Orange look like mild blistering - the king of England didn’t have to worry about having the snot beaten out of him at every street corner. The irony was Ian didn’t even support Big Daddy, didn’t even like the righteous role model. Ian loathed both garment and wrestling figurehead with every irritated fibre of his being. Then the booing echoed around the arena. Such spontaneous pantomime could only mean the entrance of the villain of the show, the fall guy. The latest itch was between his shoulder blades and no amount of yogic contortion could provide relief. Worse still he was nearly trampled underfoot when the Women’s Institute surged forward; demented screeching harpies from the bowels of Hell. Spitting insults that made sailors blush, they lunged out with weighted handbags at the looming figure of Giant Haystacks. Six foot eleven inches, forty five stone, matted hair and wild beard. Part of his diet was to eat three pounds of bacon and a dozen eggs for breakfast; born but two decades later and this guy could have crushed Dr Atkins’ fat-busting empire under his less than athletic but well nourished frame. Primeval It occurred to Ian that Giant Haystacks could wear nothing but the goofy Big Daddy jumper and still be called Sir. The Monster of the Mat growled at the crowd, profaned at every Big Daddy sign, beat his chest and acted the Kong-like monster that regular Saturday spectators knew him to be. It instigated a vitriolic response that upped the ante of the crowd coming ever closer to that of a riot. Old women foamed at the mouth, lashed out with modified handbags that glanced off the mammoth’s hide along with the insults. Old men rose on unsteady legs from bowed wooden benches to shake their sticks in moral outrage. Boys threw popcorn until, that is, Haystack’s evil eye singled them out from the masses - sapping away any faux bravery the anonymity of the mob had hitherto granted them. Now Haystacks was upon him. Ian wasn’t compelled to throw abuse at the man who might crush his top-hatted tormentor: and even if he had been, he wouldn’t have. In genetic terms from a survival of the species point of view, cowardice and intelligence went hand in hand. The English Clan were renowned for their *intelligence.* But his Grandmother was another - embarrassing - matter entirely (having only married into the gene pool). Off she went, howling like a banshee, swinging like Foreman after Ali. Small dog syndrome: yapping like a Jack Russell this would have been the biggest mismatch since Titanic versus the Iceberg. Ian wouldn’t have blamed Haystacks if he’d laid the old bint out sparko in front of him. In fact, Ian was secretly hoping he would. But the gargantuan had other ideas; shaking with a belly laugh he turned to leave shrugging off the abuse with nonchalant hand waves. Granny was even half clambering over the railings in the wake of the retreat, torso bent forward, black ankle-length skirt hitched up dangerously high. And for Ian it was all a bit of an anticlimax. Until Giant Haystacks pivoted sharply and gave his grandmother the middle-fingered salute mere inches from her bristled face. Only Ian clapped for pure joy. Two things prevented a lynching in Bethnal Green after that: The first was the sheer physical presence of six hundred and thirty pounds walking upright. The second was the indisputable fact that once in the ring Giant Haystack’s lack of moral fibre would lead to his downfall Big Daddy would defeat him again; setting right some of societies inequities in the process. The smaller underdog at a disadvantage (in theory) because he wouldn’t cheat simply to win, would prove with reserves of pious indignation that there were always exceptions to life kicking you in the balls. He’d grant the crowd a little hope. Extortionate mortgage? Crippling debts? Shackled to an ugly moose for a wife? *For one night only* all would be forgotten when the nice fat man beat the nasty fat man. Now that’s magic. But the young Ian was a realist. He knew the effect was temporary. Come tomorrow, at the first street corner, he’d be taking another kicking for wearing the crummy jumper. And so the drama continued, they berated the evil monster, hounded him all way to the ring and all through the match until the ringing of the final bell. His Grandmother was unbearably ecstatic. He’d heard the phrase “he’d sell his own grandmother given half the chance” (mainly by the police in reference to Granddad George’s opportunistic nature) but Ian wondered if anybody had *actually* done that. Could be worth looking in to. Although realistically you’d probably have to pay someone to get rid of the old bag. When the crowd finally filed out, the ring still slick with sweat, ropes still shaking from that final Big Daddy Splash, there wasn’t a man or woman that night who hadn’t been disappointed to see Giant Haystacks lose to Big Daddy in the old hall at Bethnal Green. There was a young boy however... * * * Eighteen years later. “Tell yer wot... how bout you go fack yerself eh?” Behold - Ian English’s verbal eloquence. “Wot d’yer mean *farmyard?*” As unique selling points go in the world of marketing, that one came straight from Animal Farm, and we’re not talking George Orwell. “You sick bastid, if you fink I’m gonna shag some pig up the arse you can sod off!” Who did they think they were talking to? English was PRIME’s greatest, laziest, can’t be arsiest, Hall of Famer, retiring at the ripe old age of, well, twenty-four. “Who the Hell is this again?” *Pearl* eh? That was a woman’s name. Sounded like a man though; might be one of them he-she freak things Ian guessed. And AWC? Obviously the block English had ordered BT to put on his phone number, specifically in regards to shite wrestling organisations, had been wasted money. “Yeah, yeah, invitational? Well ‘ow bout this fer an invite? I invite you to suck my...” He stopped mid sentence. When on a rant, the synaptic journey from ears to brain meant there was about a five second delay in cognition. Had the transsexual mentioned Giant Haystacks? *The* Giant Haystacks? What must’ve sounded like a dramatic pause to the gender-bender at the other end of the line was, in fact, English taking a long contemplative swig from a can of Stella. “Okay, I’ll do it.” He wiped the foam from his lips, then nearly choked the lager back up at the next proposition that came through the earpiece. “For FREE? Sod off! Do I look like mutha-fackin’ Bob Geldof?? I ain’t no charity, if you wanna feed one-eyed AID’s infested orphans in Africa then go give Bono or some other Paddy twat a call. I’ll do it... for a FEE!!” And that was that, *for one night only* English had been lured out of retirement by a geezer-bird called Pearl from a backwater fed called AWC, an achievement failed by PRIME and others before. But why? Maybe English was becoming a sentimental fool, though the idea of some pissant nobody, or even worse, one of those crowd-pleasing baby-faced muppets poncing up and down winning a tournament dedicated to the biggest monster in British wrestling just seemed wrong. Which was strange as Ian wasn’t aware of having any morals or principles... it’d been a while since he’d hurt somebody though. Yeah that was probably the real reason. He thought back, bitterly remembering his Gran’s love of Big Daddy. It was later found that outside the ring the fat prick used to beat his wife (ironic considering Haystacks was taunted for picking on people smaller than himself), was occasionally violent and permanently arrogant; often showing disdain for fans he met on the street. The flip side was Giant Haystacks was a shy, quiet man, a gentle giant who lived a peaceful life. It just reinforced English’s opinion of wrestling fans. They were all morons, sheep that were willing to believe any old bollocks spoon fed to them. Haystacks understood and played upon this, like a master pianist at a concert hitting all the right keys to become the greatest heel of his time. But even Haystacks was a fool in so many ways – the fans didn’t recognise he was the real underdog, Big Daddy’s fall guy until the end of time, well, until obesity related health problems claimed both their lives. The real underdog had never won. Would never win. That was the *real life* reality check and real moral of the tale in this Aesop fable, something the fans couldn’t and wouldn’t digest. English’s whole career, whole success, was built on revealing this horrible truth. Life was shite and then you die, to be bad was good. The young Ian in his itchy jumper had been tipped over the edge; with every defeat Giant Haystacks had suffered; with every freshly knitted Big Daddy garment that appeared in his wardrobe (the woollen Y-fronts had been the final straw); had vowed that never again would the villain of the show be the fall guy. He wasn’t Six foot eleven inches, he wasn’t forty five stone, hair was clean(ish), beard was styled. Diet wasn’t to eat three pounds of bacon and a dozen eggs for breakfast every morning. Ian had never done a gut barging belly splash in his life. (Or needed to kill another person for some cheap heat, wrestlers now days were truly desperate.) But he was one thing. PRIME-Evil. When he walked before the herd mentality of the crowd he wouldn’t be acting, he really was pure unadulterated loathing spite on a stick, ready to be shoved up the collective arse of the masses. No moral drama, no social anaesthetic, just wholesome anathema. English stood, stretching out for the first time in... in a while. There wasn’t long before he returned to his old stomping ground of East Anglia (he hoped the police had forgotten all about that “Treasure Hunt” misunderstanding of years ago). The match would be in a few days. Plenty of time to sink a few more pints then; after all, he wanted to be as inappropriately dangerous as possible when operating heavy farming machinery. Ian placed the half empty can of Stella on a drinks coaster* and briefly wondered in passing how his grandmother was getting on, after he’d sold her... *To be honest it wasn’t a drinks coaster per se, it was some nobody’s letter asking for sponsorship that would aid in his application to PRIME - a retarded type of fellah, judging by the spidery handwriting - went by the name of Tom Shipley or Tim Shitely, or whatever. * * * Korea. It’s clear that cost cutting had begun in the first stages of construction for this building. It was less than ten years old but already the plaster on the walls had cracked, the smell of mildew hung heavy in the air. With the prevalent aroma of sweat you’d be forgiven for thinking this was some oriental version of York Hall. But it isn’t. The constant buzzing in the background isn’t a flock of nectar hunting hummingbirds but a thousand tireless sowing machines, pumping thread-loaded needles through hundreds of shoes a day. Most of the industrious workforce who labour here for a mere pittance are local women from surrounding villages. One isn’t. A sour, round-eyed, woman with pale skin sits in a corner reeking of piss. She is the most industrious of them all; a prize catch the management treasure like a favourite cow in the herd. They don’t pat her (remember she smells of piss) and they don’t milk her (obviously that would be wrong and better left to specialist internet sites) but they do jabber at her in a foreign tongue. Best of all, so mastered with the needle is she, that she doesn’t require a sowing machine. Skill like that can only be found at church halls during Women’s Institute meetings. The other women haven't got shit on her. Ever wonder who does the *swoosh* on your favourite pair of Nike’s? Ian English didn’t... He knew.
