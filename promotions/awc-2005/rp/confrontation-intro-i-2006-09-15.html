Confrontation - Intro (i)

15 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sept 15, 2006 September 10th, 2006 - 4:00 AM New York City provided a rather frightening scene on the night of September 10th. While the “Big Apple” was never a pleasant environment by any means, mother nature chose to unleash her fury on the eve of the 9/11 catastrophe with a terrifying storm - fierce licks of wind howled through the gaps between towering skyscrapers; strings of lightning lit up the sky; sheets of rain and hail relentlessly came down on the streets administering considerable damage on vehicles; and claps of thunder boomed intensely, refusing to permit the city's residents a few meager hours of sleep. As terrifying of a scene it proved to be, it painted a rather enticing picture, though this was not of high interest to many. Cedric Riley was no exception. He hadn't slept well in days. Each night his thoughts were haunted by voices of those he acquainted himself with in either past or present that he couldn't put name to, complimented by disturbing nightmares to instill a certain night fear in Cedric Riley. Tossing and turning amidst a clutter of bedding, Cedric had attempted and failed everything in forcing sleep upon himself – a warm glass of milk, a trek around the house to clear his head, and even the classic trick of counting sheep – he was fully sick of those wooly, brainless creatures. The words that echoed through his mind made the outdoor noises seem calm, and as hard as he tried to push them out of his thoughts, the more overpowering and frightening they returned, as if intending to haunt him into insanity. And that was something he doubted he could ever come to terms with. Cedric impatiently rolled out of his bed, as if to deem sleep a lost cause. He ventured over to the door and brought the room to life with the presence of light. He almost welcomed the change and turned on the television as well, increasing the volume in the process. The over-exaggerated announcements of a late-night news reporter blasted through his hotel room, as if he were trying to drown out his present problem. He went into the bathroom with no real intent, but found himself staring at his reflection in the mirror, something he wouldn't want to put upon anyone. He looked like shit. The shadows under his eyes and tussled hair made him look as if the last three days were a drinking binge, accentuated by a patchy beard that even Paddy O'Shea's would put to shame. Turning on the tap for cold water, he had to force himself to look away from his haggard reflection. He doused his face and mopped his hair in cold water to the point where the beads trickled down his front and back, giving him a chill. He put on his glasses that made him look more aged than he already appeared; this was an unfortunate and depressing accomplishment. After a deep sigh he returned to his bed, something he wasn't looking forward to – but where else was he to go? As a matter of fact, what was Cedric Riley doing in New York City, anyway? Being a former CIA agent, Cedric Riley gained a handful of contacts in the political and judicial spectrum, although he did not expect respect from others out of being on first-name basis with these people; he even withheld his trust and respect from a fair number of the “important” people he shook hands with and put on a fake smile for during his tenure in the agency. But certain names he did acquaint himself with. Since his transition to a career in wrestling, however, he intentionally lost touch with a number of these contacts out of fear for revealing his secret. Or the secret, whatever it is known to be among “them”. Or whatever it may be considered; by that time it was essentially buried, something that Lucas Emerson could keep to himself. Although he was crafted expertly in deception and deceit out of vigour training with the agency, Cedric was comfortable with resting his life in this man's hands. This level of trust had saved Cedric's life once, but that is a distinct chapter in its' own; for our purposes, that event acted as the glue that bonded their friendship. Lucas Emerson was a veteran agent who had been in the agency long before who we know now as Cedric Riley arrived as a acne-prone intern, and remained there in the months to follow. He called New York City home, but to him was just another place to rest his eyes for he did not have a wife nor family to go home to; but he boasted a polished resume and an impressive list of acquaintances. It was simply the lifestyle he chose, something that made him quite distinct from our protagonist Cedric Riley. This thought in its own is somewhat ironic, as AgentDash had also chosen a more dangerous way of life in wrestling as well. But the two were an effective team both with uniform or without, and a phone-call from Berkeley, California to the home of the Yankees was enough to reunite them. Cedric Riley had other reasons to escape his “home” in the southwestern state, however. He had began to feel trapped, and as he was already expected in New Jersey - the neighbor to the current setting - for an inactive part in Coast to Coast, it was an easy choice to return to New York City to visit Lucas during his trip across the country. What better way to clear his head, right? Maybe, but so far, the improvement was minimal at best By the time the fierce weather died down to a calm, comparably soothing breeze in the autumn night, Cedric had found sleep after great difficulty. It was no less than two hours later, however, that the sun peaked its head over New York capital, which was enough to rouse his light, uncomfortable sleep, one haunted by the same voices highlighted by a disturbing nightmare that ran like scenes from a horror movie. 7:00 AM He willingly threw the comforter aside and rolled out of the crunchy hotel bed, then opened the curtains to a typical morning-after-storm scene, complete with the sounds of early-morning traffic. New Yorkers were quick on the horn on this particular morning, but Cedric invited the disturbance as a distraction to his counter-thoughts, and pulled open the sliding door to the balcony outside. This “luxury” overlooked the stingy downtown atmosphere, or as much that could be seen past the maze of buildings that white-collared employees were beginning to file into at this hour. Even a considerable distance from the industrial sector, the unpleasant smog of New York City filled Cedric's nostrils. Fresh air may have done some good to clear his head, and while he invited distractive noise, this was something he certainly didn't want to cope with. From behind him erupted the shriek of the ringer on his cell-phone. He sighed and shut out the unattractive outdoor scenes and retreated to answer his buzzing cellular. He flipped it open, and resisted the temptation to tell whoever it was to fuck off. “Hello?” “Cedric, my boy! Bloody hell, how are you?” erupted from the telephone with a thick British accent. Lucas was born in America but his family history was rich with last name's like “Hyde” and “Clarke” that implied a British background. “Fine,” he lied, trying to embrace the overwhelming enthusiasm at such an early hour. “Good to hear, good to hear ... sleep well?” “Yeah,” he lied again, then added, “Great.” “And your flight?” “Uneventful.” That was a surprise. “Well, sounds like you're in the right mindset for a catch-up over a cup of coffee,” Lucas invited. “How about 9:00 at your hotel?” Cedric checked his watch. By the time he got ready, it would be close to that time. “I'll see you there.” “Excellent.” By the time he was off the phone, Cedric's bout of voices-in-head had ceased; the presence of human conversation perhaps calmed him to some extent. Although it was true he had formerly concluded that it was only a night occurrence, he shied away from referring to it as a Jekyll vs Hyde instance – this would make him mad by comparison, something he certainly feared. He wasn't going insane. It was a phase, and nothing more, or so he tried to convince himself each day it worsened. His belief in his own thoughts was admittedly falsely justified; he made selective observations of his own behaviour in order to theorize to the result that he aspired for – complete normality. He admissibly passed this weakness by as a small problem – a glitch, nothing more – but it was slowly becoming apart of his life as the days turned into weeks, and furthermore into months. But maybe, just maybe, this cool, autumn day in September would yield different results, or so he forced himself to believe. In the long run, he needed to, but for now he would cloak his unstable mental state as well as he could. The intention here is not to make this a melodramatic, life-changing event for this extraordinary man, and he willingly convinced himself of this idea. 9:00 AM Cedric walked into the hotel restaurant as his watch beeped on the hour. His stroll lacked it's usual confidence and composure; he looked somewhat paranoid with his hands pocketed in a pair of black sweatpants that matched his unzipped sweater and gray undershirt. He spotted Lucas in a corner table and joined him, anxiously taking notice of other customers momentarily moving their eyes in his direction. His friend wore an enlightening smile on his aging face, although his body was in the shape of a 27-year-old. About to take a drink from his mug of coffee, he suspended it just off the table as Cedric pulled in a chair across from him. Lucas observed him with interest – it was out of habit for him to treat everyone in this manner. He took a sip from his cup and set it back down on the table, then raised himself to a half-stance and stretched his hand to the middle of the table, offering a handshake. Cedric grasped it firmly with a nod and an accepting smile. “Good to see you again, Lucas,” he acknowledged, loosening up a bit now that he was settle in his chair. “And the same to you, chap,” Lucas returned before finishing the last mouthful of coffee that remained in his mug. “That bloody waitress said she would be back any minute,” he added with a glance over his shoulder. Lucas was the real-life counterpart to Jason Statham's (The Transporter) typical movie character, without an exclamation point on the “Kick-Ass” feature. The comparison was undeniable - he had sharp senses and an element of mystery, was known to be deceptive, and could lay the smack down if need be. He was also handsome and affectionately modest, but quick-witted, sarcastic, and downright honest. He may also be seen as romantic, but as a bachelor at his age, one could only be doubtful. What he lacked in a sentimental relationship he made up for in friendliness, however. (Although it could be argued that he isn't missing out on anything – for our purposes, he was.) One thing he wasn't fond of in Cedric, however, was his new choice of lifestyle; wrestling wasn't exactly something he embraced. Lucas was rather “old-fashioned” and “rubbish” was what he considered wrestling, as well as a “useless cult phenomenon”. Both of them accepted this view, and the topic was avoided. “So, how are things at the agency?” prompted Cedric, trying to kick-start a conversation. Lucas shrugged, but his expression showed he cared. “I'm on extended vacation right now.” “Vacation?” Cedric never had that luxury. His weeks off were highlighted by interruptions via his cell phone. “Keep this on the down low ...” he began quietly, “But I'm starting a new position in a month or so. One of the higher-ups had enough ... went into some sort of mental breakdown. I never liked him anyway.” “What?” Cedric responded, almost confused, trying to think back to his own memories from the job. “You heard me. You'll never guess who it was ...” Lucas trailed off as the waitress came to their table wielding a pot of coffee. She filled his cup, then offered some to Cedric. Unable to focus due to the lack of sleep, he accepted. Normally his stomach didn't agree with the black poison, but needed something to keep him going. She departed, walking away with a shake of her behind likely out of habit. Cedric returned to the topic at hand. “Who?” Lucas took a long drink from his mug and swallowed. “Graff.” Cedric nearly choked on his own mouthful. “You're kidding me?” he asked. As his past made clear, him and Graff were fairly accustom to each other also. As close as boss and an employee can be, or in that matter, as close as they were permitted to be. Their involvement climaxed through what was a very rushed time in Cedric's life, but from what he remembered, Graff had never shown any signs of what Lucas was informing him of now. Maybe they weren't as close as he thought. Or maybe ... “Not a word of a lie. Just wasn't very stable in the head, I suppose,” he finished. That statement was rather ironic concerning Cedric's own present mental uncertainties. Cedric preoccupied himself with his cup of coffee as if to ignore Lucas touch base with the personal issue he chose to avoid in casual conversation. He hadn't mentioned it, not even in a casual manner to Natalie, who remained across seas; he was worried it would invoke an unwanted dispute. Realistically, he was hiding from it. He straddled the fence that divided denial and acceptance, and perhaps small talk with the reputable man across from him provided the perfect time and atmosphere to make the final leap. It might be troubling to put it off and look for another comforting moment – and if Cedric was waiting for the opportune time, this moment of silence was it. “So, Lucas ...” he began nervously, letting the words come out without any prior thought. How do you talk about something so selfishly disturbing? He spit the words out as awkward as if he was talking to a psychiatrist. “Can I ask you something?” Lucas had busied himself with this morning's paper, but could multi-task efficiently, and looked back at Cedric in a strangely tense moment between the friends. “Anything ... what's on your mind?” he invited soothingly. Cedric flashed a light smile, as if to show his embarrassment of his inferior position. “Have you ever ...” he paused for a second, then continued, “How should I put this? Have you ever encountered anyone in the agency that ... uh ... starts hearing ... things ... after they've left?” He stuttered through, anxiously avoiding too much eye contact. Lucas paused for a second and absorbed the question in a serious manner – or, at the very least, he looked serious, something that Cedric trusted was a good thing. After a sip of coffee he uttered a short sight. “... I was afraid this would happen,” he said calmly. Cedric's expression showed that he was almost unsure of how to react to such an uninformative response. Lucas was merely constructing the right way to put his point across. “First of all, I won't go on assuming that you are the one hearing voices unless you tell me,” he explained, expecting clarification, emphasizing with a tone of voice that could only indicate he knew anyways. Cedric hesitated. “I'll take that hesitation as a yes,” Lucas began. “Essentially, you have a guilty conscience. Now that's an amateur word for people getting inside your head, but when they keep coming back and haunting your nightmares and preventing you from sleeping, that's when things are getting serious ... seriously wrong. To be fair, we should take into consideration that you are a former CIA agent, which provides a whole other tangent. You've killed, hurt, and betrayed people out of your job description in order to complete the mission, right?” He looked at Cedric questionably. Cedric opened my mouth to respond, but was cut off. “Right. And bloody hell, boy, you're still young! As trained as your mind may be, it still isn't as weathered as say an old coon like mine, and when you leave the profession those memories don't just leak out. They stay with you forever ... they stay with everyone forever, some just have a stronger impact on an individual than others. You are one of those cases. Those voices in your head ... do you recognize any of them?” Cedric hesitated again. This discussion was taking a quick turn from a casual reunion between friends to an intense therapy session. But he made a good point – the voices weren't unfamiliar to the former agent. His mind was stable enough to interpret that. “All people you've confronted in the past, and probably made their lives miserable. That's not saying it was a bad thing you did – quite the contrary, actually – but to a young mind, horrors like that certainly can come back to haunt,” he explained. I paused for a second, thinking about what he was saying. It all make perfect sense, but I still felt there was a gap in what I was trying to find. “Mind you, I'm not exactly the expert on psychology,” he added, as if hinting something. “I'm not going to see a psychiatrist. I just need to ... figure things out for myself,” Cedric decided. “Cedric ... you left the agency and all things considered, everything went relatively good even though the whole drama spelled disaster. And now, you've jumped into a lifestyle that is almost normal compared to your former ways ... but the mind doesn't adopt like the body,” Lucas philosophized. “So how do I help that?” Cedric wondered aloud. Lucas paused, almost purposely, then said simply, “Confront your conscience.” “And how am I suppose to confront people that are dead or either want me dead?” the 'subject' returned sharply. “Always the smart-ass, aren't you?” Lucas commented. “Maybe you should just ... start small. See where things go from there.” “'Start small'? What the hell is that suppose to mean?” Cedric pushed, becoming impatient. “Everyone has skeletons in their closet, Cedric, or at least a few bones. Confront them. Once you accomplish that, even the people you have affected that you are unable to confront will become a mere memory instead of a driving force of your mad alter-ego.” Lucas finished with a punchline that was true and he expected to be tried. He got up from his chair and pulled his leather jacket off the back of the chair and suited up. He threw a few bills on the table and offered a handshake to his old friend. “Thanks, Cedric,” he said as they grasped hands. “Good luck.” And he was gone. Cedric remained in his chair, staring dazedly out the window at the pedestrians that passed by. They sucked on cigarettes, carried handbags, and read the New York Times – just like in the movies. In the past year or so, the events in Cedric's life could be compared similarly, although very uncategorized and certainly not worthy of a motion picture. Jason Bourne, anyone? Bond? If only it was that simple, he thought, or even that entertaining. He was restless, sick and tired of trying to deal with his own problems and even more so everyone else's. Everything he encountered seem to be thick with drama; his life read like a suspense book out of the bargain bin, and he hated loving it, but it was true. Trouble followed him around, kept him in line, and it was only a matter of time before he lost his marble. Could it have come at a worst time? How would if effect his performance in the ring? Would it enhance it, in a different way than the druggies in the Empire? Maybe. Last time he was in the ring he was in deep shit, literally, so it could be argued that things couldn't get any worst when it came to the AWC. He would push on. He remained unsure if therapy was the answer, but in the meantime convinced himself that the best form of theory would come from the comforting and satisfying feeling of accomplishment in the squared circle. He had been posed to escalate on the proverbial ladder times before – he had wrestled in the main event against reputable superstars and was granted a chance at the Countdown Trophy, yet he had nothing to show for it. He had nothing to boast about ... and he craved that unparalleled feeling of success. All he had was his laundry list of people and problems to deal with, but always outside of his wrestling life. Why did he focus so much on his own clichéd happiness when AgentDash was miserable? What happened to the prospering superstar he once was in Sean Aspinall? Or the successful secret agent in Charles Kensrue? Who was Cedric Riley? Perhaps he was cursed under his third identity; maybe his turning point in life was realizing he would have to abandon memories of past success and build a future. And he was unable of doing that. But for the time being, wrestling was the most important aspect of his life and he needed to remind himself of that. Would he take Lucas's advice? He certainly had skeletons in his closet, but did he have the power to confront them? Something told him that he should. But as everyone will find in life, there is a difference between thought and follow-through.
