Operation 'Rasheed' pt.1

6 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/03/06 Operation: Rasheed pt. 1 Most of you will have heard me say Operation: Rasheed, is one of the biggest accomplishments in my military career, but I have never fully explained why, and the reason is that it chokes me up to tell people the story. It was one of the worst days in my life, but yet it was the greatest accomplishment in my whole career. I know many of you thinking that that makes no sense, that the worst day of my life, that chokes me up when I tell the story, was the greatest accomplishment in my whole entire career. It confuses me more than anyone, but if you listen to the story it may clear things up, even if not that well. --- A thick sandstorm covered all entrances to Rasheed airport (which is about 160 miles from Baghdad), no teams on the ground could approach the airport without either getting Shot, or separated from their platoon/unit when approaching the airport. So basically it was a job for the 187th Airborne, the premiere squadron trained for masked air assault in Arab states. Upon receiving conformation from Lt. Phillips that we had to go in for an air strike, we were called into briefing almost automatically to be briefed about “Operation Rasheed”; we were given the basic plot and formations we had to take, and assigned our units. I was placed as team leader of my three active Privates, and one medic. My four Men were: Pvt. Luke “Nova” Jennings (Automatic Rifleman) Pfc. Michael “Party Boy” Foster (M203 Gunner) Cpl. Gordon “Fuzz” Grahams (Rifleman) And Cpt. Mike Tilger (Medic) Together we had to parachute into the hangar sector smack dab in the center of the airport, avoiding battles at all costs, but some of us will be unfortunate and land straight in the middle of an Arab colony, and be blown to smithereens by RPG’s, rifles and automatic weapons. The only real hitch in the plan is that our recon flights have confirmed a tank in Arab possession, and until this sandstorm clears, although on the ground we will have at least 27% more visibility due to shielding from the buildings, but we will have virtually no visibility when we get to open areas such as runways and parking lots. Many of my men are nervous and literally shitting themselves as we clambered into the apache 25 minutes after briefing. And I am no better; I am quaking in my boots about this mission. The British SAS or SBS, Or U.S Navy Seals would only act out this sort of mission, but alas, the Navy seals we have in our platoon are all ground based, and only trained too jump in clear conditions. Although we are trained to tackle this sort of mission, we would normally have a full mission objective, a recon flight giving us a clearer view of what enemies are concealed inside the airport, How heavily armed they are, and an updated GPS, instead of “We Assume there is a Tank”. But as Team Leader, I must command my Team (Zulu Team), and meet the objective as best as I can. It also helps that us and Oscar team, who are in the same aircraft as us, have made a pact, and we are going to try and tackle this together, with one team checking corners while the other team covers, and so on and so forth. Which will also be a plus, because I know if anything happens to me while in Battle, Zulu will be in good hands with Sergeant “Bo” Williams. As we reach the peak of our flight, and are about to jump, the bustling conversation, and the newly relaxed attitude which had built over the hour and a half duration of the flight, disappeared in almost an instant, as everyone rose to their feet, and stood silent. The sound of the rotors was the only noise that could be heard as the light next to the door changed to Amber, and everyone turned a pale white. But although everything should have been tense and tears should have overwhelmed the young privates faces, both teams stood silent, staring out of the windows at the drop zone, and to try to get people psyched, Fuzz blurted out, “What we cant see Cant hurt us”. A short giggle was heard from Oscar teams Corporal, and then the buzzer went the light turned to green, the door flew open and I took an almost star position in the doorway.
