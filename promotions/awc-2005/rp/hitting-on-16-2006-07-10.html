Hitting On 16

10 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10.07.06 “Allow me to introduce myself. I’m Josh Marquez and I’m a gambler.” The man speaking is seated on a white sun lounger at the side of a large, irregularly shaped swimming pool. The sun is high in the sky, reflecting the scene in his aviator Ray Bans. He is shirtless, tiny droplets of sweat glistening on his tanned skin, rivulets of liquid making their way down his torso until they are abruptly stopped by his red board shorts. “I’m also the newest member of the Eastern roster of AWC and I’ll be making my debut this week in a three way fury match. Since both of my opponents have been seen before on Fresh, it only seemed right that I introduce myself to you before the match.” Marquez sits up slightly, his right hand going to the wooden table beside him and lifting a bottle of beer, Corona, from the ice bucket. He takes a swig of the amber liquid before replacing it, the head of the beer rising up the bottleneck briefly before receding. “Sin City is a city that some people hate and some people love. Being a native of Las Vegas, I’m in the second camp. The thing is, having been born and raised here, you only really have two career choices - work in a casino or be a gambler. The thought of working 8 hours a day hoping that people won’t win money didn’t appeal to me so I went the other route, taking on the house.” He pauses now, his attention momentarily drawn away by a bikini, or rather, the phenomenal curves that the bikini barely manages to enclose. Not an uncommon sight round these parts at this time of year it has to be said, but it’s still a joy to behold. “I made that decision a long time ago and it’s served me well. Sure, I’ve lost money, it’s an inevitable part of this game, but I’ve won a hell of a lot more than I’ve lost. The secret of my success? Unpredictability.” He jumps up to his feet now, almost catching the camera off guard, as it wobbles and gives us a fantastic close-up of the pale terracotta ground round poolside before refocusing on Marquez. Now he is on his feet, the extent of his musculature and physique can be clearly seen, with not an ounce of fat apparent. Laughing, he slips his feet into a pair of flip-flops, grabs a t-shirt from below the lounger and beckons the camera to follow him. “Just like that right there. People are always expecting you to act a certain way, to do a certain thing in a particular situation. It gives them some sense of power, an illusion of control over you. Step out of that predictable zone though, and you quickly notice that everything changes. You confuse people, make them question everything, take their mind out of the game so to speak. In my world, that’s a very useful talent to have.” Slipping the t-shirt on, Marquez makes his way inside now, the lettering over the heavily tinted glass doors indicating that we are entering the Bellagio, one of the most exclusive casinos in the world. As he makes his way towards the elevators and hits the call button, Josh continues his monologue. “I’ll never forget the looks on people’s faces the first time I decided to hit on 16. They looked at me like I was an idiot, the dealer’s eyes lighting up as she prepared to relieve me of my $500 stake. Of course, a 5 came up and they quickly changed their opinion. In fact, the next hand I remember one of the older guys at the table doing the same thing and losing everything. It’s always been like that though, I’ve always done things that others won’t and more often than not it‘s all worked out.” The elevator arrives and Marquez enters it, pausing his speech. The mirrored walls create a strange effect as he pushes the button for the 13th floor. Almost silently, the box begins to ascend, but the silence does not last long, as Josh picks up from where he left off. “It’s not just at the blackjack table or the roulette wheel that I take risks either, something that all the AWC fans are going to find out very soon. You see, I view my signing of an AWC contract as just another gamble. Is it too early in my career? Am I really ready to mix it up in the big leagues? Who knows. I could fall flat on my face. Looks like we’re here, floor 13, as always.” Exiting the elevator, Marquez turns to his right down the hallway extracting a key from his pocket and slipping into the lock of room 1313. As the door opens, we are confronted with the sight of a plush suite, wooden floors throughout, our eyes immediately drawn to the large double doors leading to a balcony overlooking the Strip. “Then again, I could turn out to be one of the finest, most successful competitors the company has ever seen. I’m sure my opponents feel the same, Brett Jackson looking to prove a point but feeling slightly insecure about going solo after teaming with his brother for so long. And The Equalizer, wanting to make a splash but anxious that his crap name may hold him back.” Marquez moves out onto the balcony now, the sounds of the metropolis drifting up from 13 stories below creating a wall of background noise as Josh approaches the conclusion of his speech. There is a slight breeze at the minute, blowing his t-shirt tight against his abdomen. “So we have an interesting situation. Three guys with only one winner, all wanting to make an impression. One with a history in AWC, one with an intimidating name and one who nobody has ever heard of before. I’m the unknown quantity, the dark horse so to speak and I will do whatever it takes to win. I’ll take a risk, go for the big move even if it’s not assured of success and by doing that, I will win. It’s as simple as that.” Now Josh turns his back to the camera, looking out over the city and running his hands through his hair as he finishes his promo. His voice drops to a lower level as he says his final words, a more intense tone present and his words more slowly delivered. “I will always hit on 16.”
