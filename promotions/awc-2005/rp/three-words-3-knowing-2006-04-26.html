Three Words [3] - Knowing

26 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 26, 2006 Not a day goes by in one’s life where they are not faced with challenges, some more difficult to handle than others, but all the while we stand our ground and try to keep alive. We take no notice of how ungrateful we are being one careless slip away from losing everything, or being in the wrong place at the wrong time. We sympathize for those innocent ones that lose their lives because of a simple miscue, yet disregard the fact that we are in the same position from one day to the next. Of course, some days provide more danger than others, and that is to be expected. But to stand up for yourself and what you’ve accomplished in your life just to live another day is something to be proud of; Natalie Garner was experiencing this first-handedly – facing death as an innocent, young woman who had potential to do remarkable things with her life. And on top of that, she had worked too hard for too long to have it all taken away in one fell swoop. At the very least, she had fight left in her, and would go down swinging. We should recycle the idea that Natalie Garner has softened up since having her child, because it could be true, it could not. Her career in the CIA was short-lived coming just out of training and then getting pregnant. She couldn’t claim to be experienced in the field, but knew enough about survival to know that she and Kathy were in a very precarious position. She also knew very well that she was too young to die; that she had too much left to experience and learn in a life with so many open doors. She couldn’t help but be sentimental about the actuality of the circumstances, and felt guiltily selfish sitting with an older woman contemplating her own problems. The rain had begun to come down a little steadier now, chilling the air and forming a thin layer on the deck. Both Natalie and Kathy watched the guard continue to make space in the barrier around the boat, space to toss them into the bottom of the river. Natalie could see it in Kathy’s eyes – well, she didn’t even have to observe her to know that she anticipated the worst. “It’s going to be alright, Kathy. We’re going to make it out of this somehow.” A soft smile flickered across Kathy’s face, as if she imagined what it would be like to be that blessed. “You’re an optimist, aren’t you?” “Everyone says that. I think I just like to see both sides of everything …” “Well, that’s what an optimist is, isn’t it?” “What can I say, I have faith,” Natalie declared, leaning back onto the guardrail. Kathy sighed. “As you get older you’ll find that fate sometimes has its way of working against you, no matter how hard you try and fight it. It’s just … there.” There was a break in the conversation, but Kathy continued. “What about you and Charles? I couldn’t help but notice you’re not wearing a wedding ring, yet you two have a baby …” Natalie nodded solemnly. “It’s complicated.” “How so?” Kathy kept the conversation moving. Natalie merely shrugged. “It’s hard to explain, I guess.” “Just ask yourself one question.” “What’s that?” Natalie asked, fearing that she already knew the answer. “Do you love him?” Kathy said very straight-forwardly. “Yes.” Natalie replied simply, looking away. “And have you told him so?” “No. It’s just … I don’t know … these last couple years have been confusing. And that’s an understatement. Besides, I’m only twenty-three years old …” Natalie went on, trying to find more reason to support something she didn’t even think she wanted. Kathy laughed. “I remember when I was that age. Guess you could say I was in the same situation, but I always knew I’d be with Lenard. I wish I could see him one more time.” Natalie frowned. “You still can, Kathy.” “I hope so,” she hoped very unconfidently do end the conversation on a depressed note as the guard approached them, gun in hand. He had finished making room in the barrier to get rid of the two frightened woman in his control, and order that he was expected and intended on following through with. “Alright, tea time is over. Say your last goodbyes,” he scoffed, walking towards them. Natalie no longer felt anything towards the men in command; her hatred had amounted to a point where she only acted hostile towards them out of spite. But it was hard, because she was scared - scared enough of losing everything, and to put that into simpler terms … she dreaded losing Cedric. In this position, he was the only thing she had left, but her fear that time was running short was gone, because time was up. Her only fear now was taking the plunge into the sea … away from everything she had ever known. Away from Cedric. As the gunman approached them, Natalie momentarily lost all train of thought, but came back to reality unwarily as she heard a loud crack. At first she thought that the guard had shot Kathy and screamed, but quickly realized he had collapsed in front of her in an unconscious heap. Behind him fell Darren, the Captain, who had slammed a blunt object into the back of the guard’s head. Darren had blood trickling from different points of his face and was barely conscious, his muscles hardly able to move. On all fours, he tried to get to his feet, but every inch of energy was spent in the attack and he forced himself to take in a few breaths of air. An overstatement – he was barely breathing after being beaten mercilessly. Both Natalie and Kathy, who found some life in the them, looked on, unable to help being bound to the rail. Darren noticed this and grabbed a knife from the unconscious guard’s pocket. He crawled towards them and cut the ties from their wrists, and afterwards collapsed again onto his back. Natalie and Kathy each grabbed a shoulder and hoisted him into a position to lean against the rail as a clap of thunder came from above, dramatizing the scene even more. “Oh my god, Kathy … he’s …” Natalie sputtered out, her words failing her. “He’s not dead, Natalie.” “I can move,” Darren grunted, pushing himself forward but failing to do so. He was breathing heavily and looked like it pained him to do so. He clutched his ribs as if they were crushed from the repeated kicks from his brother, and the blood trickling down his face from his eye and mouth made him look dire. He was simply a victim, with a swollen head. “I have to go, I have to stop him …” Darren continued to mutter, barely getting the words out. “You’re in no condition, Captain …” Natalie began, but Darren cut her off. “You don’t understand. You don’t know my brother – this is my battle to fight …” Darren tried to explain, using the guard rail to heave himself to his feet while clutching his ribs. “But you’ll be killed!” Kathy protested. “So will everyone else on this ship once it sails into Pier 39. Were you not listening to him? He has this ship wired with TNT. He doesn’t care if he kills himself in the process – he’s selfish in that way that he will stop at nothing to get what he wants. The only thing to be done is to get the ship headed away from the Pier, and even then there are no guarantees. I have a score to settle, and lives to save. I have to deal with my brother. He’s my responsibility. You two can try and save the ship. There’s still hope,” he finished, then limped forward as fast as his legs would carry him in search of Victor. Natalie and Kathy couldn’t find the words to protest and did a double take. Natalie had the instinct to slip the gun off of the unconscious guard, check if it was loaded, and follow after him. Kathy awkwardly grabbed the pistol that Darren had left and followed suit. Cedric Riley watched on as the gun man pointed his weapon directly at his face, cocking the safety off and preparing to pull the trigger. It was a surreal feeling to believe that your life was about to end. With one quick movement, the man could end such a promising life and change the destiny of so many people at the same time. As he pondered the meaning of his own life, Cedric realized that he had no idea where Natalie was right now. For all he knew, she might be dead or close to death right now. It comforted him to know that he might be able to see Nat soon. He wasn't a religious man, but if there was an afterlife, surely they would meet there. But what if she isn't dead? The question continued to race through Cedric's mind as he contemplated his next move. If she still was alive, and Cedric died, what could he do? It was then that he made up his mind that he would try to save Nat, and at the same time, save the ship, if that was at all probable. Cedric was tied in the back to Lenard, but for the past three minutes, Lenard had been loosening the ropes that bound them together. Cedric could feel the hold on the ropes slackening, but it would probably be another three or four minutes before they would be released. Cedric would be forced to try to distract this guard that was so intent on ending his life for a little while longer until he would finally get the chance to escape. This could prove to be difficult, but Cedric was an impulse talker. “So, that’s how you’re going to end my life? Just stand there, with me helpless, and pull a trigger? Sure is brave …” Cedric shot at him sarcastically, aware that his situation couldn’t get worst without death, which he was facing anyways. “Save the cheek, Dash. I’m the one with the gun pointed to your forehead, and you’re the one with hands tied at my mercy. Remember?” The gunman returned expertly. “So, you’re saying that because you couldn’t beat me in a fair fight, you have to dispose of me with an unfair advantage? Is that your ploy?” Cedric kept going, pulling anything out of his ass. “Speaking of ploys, this game you’re playing, I’ve dealt with it a million times. Nobody cares if I can beat you in a fair fight, just because I know I can doesn’t mean I have to prove it. No, Cedric, there’s no way you’re getting out of this one. And if you have any last words, I hope you’ve said them, cause I no longer care …” Another guard burst into the room and caused the gunman to deter his attention from Cedric. It was the other man from before who had left for the upstairs, and he looked a little panicky. He tried to speak, but Cedric’s threatener cut him off. “What the fuck are you doing? I thought I told you to go upstairs!” “And I thought you told me you were going to take care of this!” The new guard shot back. “Nevermind, I am.” “Well what’s taking so long? Why are you holding this off?” “Is there a problem, or why do you keep holding me off?” The gunman shot at his compadre. “The women are rioting upstairs, risking their lives. They’ve finally found out they’ve got nothing left to loose. One of them got ahold of a gun and killed Jason … we need you. Get this over with,” he explained, and turned his back to go back upstairs. Cedric, in the meantime, had been putting all his focus into untying the ropes that bound him and Lenard together. Out of sheer luck the ropes slackened enough for Cedric to take his hands out. As the guard turned to leave the room, Cedric seized the oppurtunity and launched himself towards the gunman and tackled him to the ground, sending his gun sliding across the floor. Lenard reacted quickly as well, and charged towards the other guard who turned back into the doorway only to be hit with a tackle. Cedric heard a gun fire and the bullet ricocheted off the roof and side of the room, but pinned the enemy to the ground. The gunman scrambled out of Cedric’s grasp and was to his feet, but weaponless. Cedric was quickly in a ready position as well, his fighting training coming in handy. He easily executed a roundhouse kick that connected right to the guard’s skull. Whether or not he was unconscious Cedric didn’t know, but he was pleased nonetheless as he headed towards the door to help Lenard. The other guard had regained his composure and had ahold of the gun, and was fumbling to hold it in Lenard’s direction. Cedric swiftly knocked the gun out of his hand, but in return got an elbow to the face. He felt blood trickle from an opening in his eye, but looked back to the guard to see that he had made himself vulnerable to Lenard who was ready with an aggressive right hand that spent the guard reeling. He collapsed into the wall as Cedric got to his feet. “Let’s get out of here before they shake it off,” he directed Lenard, and they headed to the stairs, closing the door behind them. Lenard hurried behind him as the clunked along the thick, metal staircase towards the upper deck. “Where are we going?” He asked. Cedric hadn’t thought about that. “Natalie and Kathy were on the upper deck, I think. We should go there …” “What about the ship?” Lenard asked as they pushed forward quickly through a door into a hallway, trying to find anyway up. “Hopefully looking for them will lead us there …” Cedric said, hoping dearly that Natalie and Kathy were still alive and that the ship could still be saved. Those guards would be back soon, but they would deal with that later. For now, their destination was yet to be decided. Nat and Kathy crept up to the helm room slowly as the rain continued to fall on to the ship harder and harder. Nat's hair was soaked and continued to cling to her head as she wiped away any stray rain drops hit her in the fact. Kathy followed closely behind her, armed with a hand gun as well. Taking the helm would not be easy, but it had to be done. Nat and Kathy walked up the rain soaked steps slowly, now being able to see a guard at the helm, directing the ship to it's inevitable destination. The door was perpendicular to the guard, and if they walked up to it directly they would surely be seen. Nat needed another plan. There were two of them and only one guard...if they surprised him, they would be able to subdue him quickly. Nat was crouched behind the door as Kathy had her hand on the door knob. Nat nodded at Kathy and she swung the door open, both of them running in with guns drawn. The guard turned suddenly, surprised at seeing the two armed women before him. Nat told him to get on the ground and to put his hands on his head, or they would shoot. The guard looked confused, as if he didn't know what to do. He was not expecting such an event to occur. They waited for a few seconds, and then Kathy fired a shot near the guard's head which richocheted off a window. The guard was startled and reached for his gun, causing Nat to fire at his shoulder. The man reeled back suddenly, clutching his wounded shoulder, and fell back through the window, falling down to the wooden deck. Nat breathed slowly and prayed for forgiveness as she had just taken a man's life. Kathy made her way to the helm, and looked out the window. She could already see the main land, and the ship was on a direct course for the pier. She turned the starboard wheel a cool 180 degrees, and suddenly the ship began to move around, finally reversing direction and going away from the pier. Nat breathed a sigh of relief as now the lives of the people visiting the pier was no longe at stake. Now all they had to do was to find Lenard and Cedric, and somehow find their way off this ship. Nat turned around to find herself face to face with Victor Dickenson, who had his gun extended towards her. He wore a sinister smile. “Why, you two have been naughty girls, haven’t you? I expected to have a few misfits trying to save the day, but never would I have thought you two would have the gall to disobey my command. Well done. You should give yourselves a pat on the back,” he mocked, his gun at ready. Nat lifted her gun. “There are two of us and one of you. Don’t try and play with us. We know you have the odds stacked against you.” “Well, I’d have to agree. You do have the numbers … you have the odds … now what can we do about that?” He shot at her. “We won’t be doing anything. Now you put your gun down,” she confidently told him. “Interesting. I think you should put yours down, because I am the one who has control of the TNT rigged to set this boat on fire. You may have got here in time to turn this thing around to avoid the Pier, but you forgot about my Plan B, didn’t you? This ship is going down, honey,” he said, and added a smile. “Turn the bomb off,” she stated simply, keeping a straight face and gun at ready. “Or what? You’ll shoot me? That’ll do a lot of good, seeing as I’m the only one who can turn it off. Tisk tisk …” Kathy lost her temper and ran instinctively towards Victor. “You son of a …” Victor grabbed her impressively and held her at gunpoint. “You see how quickly the odds can change? Now you put your gun down, or your friend here will die. You have three seconds.” Natalie threw the gun down. “Now let her go.” Victor smirked, and pointed the gun at her. “You’ve made a huge mistake …” The door abruptly came open again, this time revealing Darren Dickenson, who steadied himself in the doorway. He was bleeding profusely and looked like he would collapse any minute, but found enough energy to execute a sneak attack on his surprised brother. He knocked the gun out of his hand and pulled him into a choke hold. Kathy sprawled free across the floor and found the free gun, and pointed it at Victor. She had no choice but to hold fire due to the fact that it was a struggle and she risked hitting Darren, who had little chance of winning the fight due to his condition. They were in trouble … Cedric and Lenard darted up the hallway, trying to make their way to the dining hall as hastily as they could. Only two guards remained to keep control of the ship. There had been six working at the start of the holdup, but two of them were now held up in the room, one was with Nat and Kathy, and one had been murdered by the women. The odds were even now and it would be a shoot out. Cedric worried about whether or not Nat was alright. For all he knew, she could have been dead at that time. He prayed that she wasn't. The only reason that he could go on right now was the thought that he could save her. He knew she was strong, but how much could one do against armed thugs. Just as Cedric and Lenard were making their way to the dining hall, they saw another one of the men in black running down the hall way towards him. He was bleeding from the arm and looked like he was just running from fear. Cedric and Lenard stopped, extending their guns at him and telling him to stop where he was. The man would not stop running towards them and Cedric fired one shot, clipping him in the chest. The man fell short, falling to the ground unmoving. Cedric took a deep breath, as he knew he had to kill out of necessity. Just as he did, a group of women came stampeding down the hallway towards them. Cedric suddenly realized what was happening. The women had forced the guard from the dining hall and he was being chased by them. Some of the women were armed with knives that they had probably gotten from the kitchen. They stopped running as they saw Lenard and Cedric, and they all calmed down from the mad rage they were in. Cedric spoke with them, asking them what had happened. Apparently all of the men were now free and they were setting up the life boats for an escape. The ship was turned around now, heading in the opposite direction of pier 39. Cedric realized that someone had gotten to the helm and changed the course of the ship. One guess was that it was Nat. Cedric told Lenard to get into the ships and make sure everyone got out. He would go search for Nat and Kathy. Lenard objected, wanting to accompany Cedric, but Cedric insisted that someone had to make sure that everyone got off the ship in tact. Cedric ran off into the darkness, heading for the helm hopefully for a reunion with Nat. The hallways were now dark, and the lights were going out. A large shock hit the ship, and Cedric was thrown into a nearby wall. Cedric realized that one of the charges must have gone off in the ship, and the ship was now losing power. Cedrci got up, holding his shoulder a bit, and ran towards the ship's deck where he could get to helm. As he reached the outside, he realized that it was pouring out and watched as lightning lit up the night sky. He looked out towards the deck to see large groups of men hoisting life boats over the side to be used. Cedric then looked over at the helm, where he saw Nat and Kathy standing horrified. Kathy had a gun in her hand as Nat seemed to be just standing there. He then looked closer to see Darren and Victor struggling with one another. Cedric raced down the deck, hoping to be able to get there in time to stop Victor. As he ran up the stairs to the helm, he watched as Victor threw Darren over the side of the stairwell, causing him to crash into the ground. Victor laughed hideously as he saw his brother fall to his death. That's when he spotted Cedric standing there on the stairs. He gave him a dirty look, and then jumped at Cedric, causing them both to fall down the stairs. Nat and Kathy both ran out of the helm room, with Kathy pointing her weapon at Victor, but still didn't have a clear shot. Victor had his arms wrapped tightly around Cedric's neck and was squeezing as hard as he could. Cedric struggled to get air in and popped Victor in the eye with his thumb. Victor screamed in pain as he grabbed his eye. Cedric then flipped him off his body, striking Victor in the face. Victor was still thinking and kicked Cedric back with his legs, causing Cedric to hit the wall with great velocity. Victor got up and hit Cedric in the gut and then the face. He laughed as he got out a knife from his pocket and drew it near Cedric's throat. Cedric could only sit there and watchVictor come closer and closer to ending Cedric's life. Before he could do anything, a shot rang out, and Victor fell to the ground in a heap. Cedric looked over to see a ball of smoke erupt from Kathy's gun. Nat ran down the stairs and threw her arms around Cedric, who reciprocated the embrace. She was ecstatic to see him alive, and he was relieved to know that she was alright. Suddenly another shock rocked the ship, as another charge went off, sending the ship into another jolt. Cedric looked off into the distance and saw Lenard calling them over to one of the life boats. They ran over to the side of the ship, getting into a life boat, and lowering themselves to the water. Everyone had a paddle, and they were paddling themselves away, trying to make as much distance as possible between the ship and the life boats. The winds howled loudly as they tried to fight against the current pulling them towards the ship. Finally flames began to erupt from the top of the ship. Suddenly the ship exploded in a vicious eruption, causing debris to rain down in the air and towards the life boats. Cedric grabbed Nat and placed her underneath him to protect her from any harm. Finally, they were safe and the ship sunk to the bottom of the sea after a horrific explosion. Cedric watched as he saw Coast guard ships approaching the life boats, probably prompted to the scene after the explosion. Cedric looked back at Nat, who was clutching him closer to her. He had almost lost her. She had almost lost him. This was a trip they would never forget.
