The Deception

29 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29th August, 2006 Location: New York City, New York Time: 10:02am As I perused the contents of the case file of the Ruth Beckford case in my lavish hotel room, I felt an eerie calm coursing through my body, relaxing my muscles. Truthfully, it was the first time in the last few days I had gotten the chance to sit down alone with my thoughts. My wrestling career continued at pace, with Phil Allen and I winning our first match a week and a half ago. Such trivial matters couldn’t dominate my mind now, however, with my brain working overtime to attempt to solve this case. With no previous experience of working with detectives, life was tough. Each day I would enter the 5th precinct in downtown New York to a cold reception from the officers, all of them with their heads buried in mundane paperwork. The chief would greet me with that plastic smile of his, and I would go about my business, meeting up with my team of the best five detectives available to me. The Ruth Beckford case was a time sensitive one, and each and every one of my detectives knew that. Two of them operated in the field; one at the station and one was responsible for the operations of the others running as smoothly as possible. With the case file and its contents written in shorthand, only detectives of the law would understand it. The buzzing of my cell phone woke me up from my daydream. I sipped on my cup of coffee as I answered the call. “Mr. Reno, this is Detective Johnson of the NYPD 5th precinct,” the voice on the line declared. Detective Johnson was the man in my team of detectives who oversaw operations from a safe distance, keeping the men I worked with in line, while at the same time ensuring all of their operations either in the field or in the station were being logged. Johnson was a self-confessed control freak, and I respected him for his honesty. His tone was grave, however, and I sensed a growing apprehension as he spoke. “Yes, Detective? Is there anything I can help you with?” “It has come to my attention that your friend Dean Fisher has been engaging in some rather underhanded dealings recently regarding the case we are currently working, Mr. Reno.” I wasn’t surprised by the words Detective Johnson spoke. In fact, I had already prepared myself for a move from Fisher. Ever since he and I had met up with the reporter, Jake Wilson, Fisher had acted out of character. Making no attempt to seize the case file for himself was the first unusual step for him. This would undoubtedly have damaged his reputation, having no file to work with for what could potentially be the biggest case of his career. In short, Fisher was not himself. I had to know why. “Go on, please,” I replied, cautiously. “One of the detectives in our team has a connection to one of Fisher’s detectives. He informed me this morning that his contact accidentally leaked information to him about Fisher’s plans. Mr. Reno, what I’m saying to you is that Dean Fisher has several promising leads on this case already, and we don’t know how, quite frankly.” The first question that came into my mind was “How?”. I knew Dean Fisher for many years; there was no argument that he was a clever man. I never expected this from him, though. The facts began to add up, and I realised that the reporter we met with, Jake Wilson, perhaps had more information than he let on. Had Fisher done a deal with him before our official meeting? I ruled out nothing, because in politics, anything was possible. “I see,” I began, formulating a response to this stunning news, “Is there any way we could contact his team of detectives through our detective to pick up some of these leads?” “I doubt it, Mr. Reno. Each of the teams is sworn to secrecy. If we found a lead, we would not leak it to Fisher’s team. They are the rules we are bound to, I’m afraid.” Johnson was right. We were compelled to obey the rules. The two political parties had entered into this case as separate bodies, each working strictly alone. The two could not and would not under any circumstances share information. Politics was a game where if you had the upper hand, you did you utmost to retain it. Fisher had the upper hand over the Democrats currently, and unfortunately the news was about to get worse. “There’s more, Mr. Reno,” Johnson said, interrupting my train of thought. My silence told him to continue. “Fisher and the Republican Party have today announced in the New York Times that they have already solved the Ruth Beckford case. A source close to Fisher has said that Miss Beckford is being taken into custody this afternoon.” “Bullshit, Detective. That’s nothing more than a play from Fisher to force our hand. He has leverage over us, and he’s using this morning’s paper and this leaked story to make us walk away from the case. I’m not giving in to his demands, Detective.” I was fuming, so much so that my grip had tightened on my coffee cup to the extent that my hand was burning slightly. Fisher had outsmarted me on this occasion, but it would be the last time. Fisher was a beatable adversary, and I intended to bite back against him while the wounds my party suffered were still open. If Fisher desired a political duel, he had one coming. My immediate plans to seek revenge on my political adversary Dean Fisher had unfortunately gone slightly askew due to the arrival of my tag team partner Phil Allen in New York City. I had requested his presence here to advise me of the current situation in the AWC; I was in the dark at present, having been away in New York for the majority of the last week and a half. I greeted Allen in a businesslike fashion, sparing the pleasantries to the best of my ability. I had far too much on my mind to have a friendly chat with my partner about women or anything else for that matter. All that mattered at this moment in time was finding a way to sabotage Fisher’s plan to one-up my party, and on a less important note, be brought up to speed about my faltering wrestling career. Granted, along with Allen, I had won my first match recently, but that seemed to fade into obscurity when I pictured Fisher manipulating the NYPD into his own lapdogs in order to score a huge political victory. He would, of course, toss the officers aside afterwards like old pairs of shoes. The intrusive voice of my tag team partner interrupted my train of thought suddenly, and I broke out of my furious mindset, composing myself. “So, are you ready to meet up with the guru I told you about?” I had no recollection of Allen mentioning a guru of any kind, and to be honest, if the guru wasn’t an expert in inter-party politics, I wasn’t interested in the slightest. I decided to humour Allen. As we headed out of JFK airport in one of the thousands of yellow taxis available, Allen elaborated for me. “I mentioned it to you, Wes. I’ve scheduled a meeting for an hour from now in Downing Street in Manhattan with a guy named Rob Mitchell. He’s a tough guy and he’s been in the wrestling business in one shape or form since he was in diapers. He’s your go-to guy if you need advice in your career. You, my friend, need advice. Now, I’ve laid off you with the kidding around recently, but that’s mainly because I need you to concentrate for our match on September 1st.” I contemplated the idea of meeting this wrestling guru, as Allen so fondly called him, and decided it could do me no harm. If anything, it would serve to take my troubled mind off the phone call I had received this morning about Fisher, and everything else related to the man, in truth. As for the match on September 1st, this came as a shock to me. As a politician of many years experience, however, I was always in a state of preparedness. “The guru sounds like a good idea, Phil,” I replied lethargically, “and as for the match, I figure I’ll be prepared for it.” It was as clear as day; Allen instantly sensed the pain in my voice. Maybe I should have hidden it better. The inquisition was about to begin, I guessed. Before Allen could unleash his obstacle course of questions on me, I stopped him. “Phil, before you ask, everything’s been going a little haywire for me in the last week. I’ll fill you in on the details later, but it involves one Dean Fisher and his underhanded tactics to get one over on me. You know, this kind of shit goes on every day in politics, and I usually I turn a blind eye. Not today, though. I’m actively seeking a way to get revenge on Fisher, no matter how I have to do it.” I shot Allen a cold glare, a statement of my intent to do what was necessary. Politics was a dirty game, Allen knew, and I wanted to make it perfectly clear to him that wrestling would not be my priority until I had this issue dealt with. For once, Allen nodded in an understanding way, tucking into an unfinished packed lunch he had eaten on the flight. The taxi driver’s snorting intermittently didn’t unsettle us; the only unsettling thing was not being aware of what my next move would be. And in politics, that was the worst fear of them all… Robert Mitchell was a well-groomed man in his late forties, with a chin one could spot from a mile away. Mitchell had pronounced facial features, green eyes and scars all over his body. Although his torso was partially covered by a wife beater, I could see what wars he had been through in his career. His muscular arms were his most fearsome feature; I could only imagine the pain one would feel at the hands of this man. His apartment was covered in wrestling memorabilia, from framed photographs of great matches in the business to a bunch of used canvasses from days gone by. The apartment itself had the feel of a wrestling establishment. Phil Allen fetched a beer for himself and Mitchell as I drank more of my lemonade. Allen had expressly forbidden the consumption of alcohol while I was training to become a professional wrestler, and I had to abide by the rules. Still, as the two men before me knocked back their cans of Budweiser as if it was an old ritual, I wondered what had happened to Allen’s strict regime. “Wes, you want a beer?” Mitchell asked, speaking in a thick New York accent. “No thanks, I’ll pass, Rob,” I replied, secretly wishing I could have said yes. Allen gave me a knowing wink, as if to congratulate my abstention from alcohol. “That’s cool, that’s cool. So, you’re here to speak to me about your wrestling career, huh? What exactly would you like to know?” Allen interjected as I hesitated in formulating a response. I hardly knew this meeting was taking place, let alone what it was about. “I’ll start by telling you, Rob, that we have a massive tag team match at a pay-per-view event known as Coast To Coast. A large wrestling company known as AWC is running it, and Wes and I are wrestling for the number one contendership – “ Allen was cut off by Mitchell, who raised his hand and nodded in the affirmative, having heard this before. Mitchell struck me as someone who had an inside scoop on the wrestling world. I shuddered to think of the consequences if you were to oppose this man. Fear was Mitchell’s ally in gaining information; if he could frighten his target, then he could extract information from it. “I’m well aware of your match, Phil,” Mitchell assured, nodding and sipping some more of his Budweiser. “In fact, I also happen to have a few connections in AWC. They’re in the upper echelons of the company, mostly. The only advice I can give to you both is to be on your game, respect your opponents and believe you can win.” I shrugged, nodding in acquiescence. My tag team partner was not so convinced. “That seems like pretty obvious advice, Rob. Don’t get me wrong, I appreciate your efforts to assist us. What I really need you do, though, is teach this kid here just how important this match will be in his career.” Allen and Mitchell smiled wryly at each other, their muscles simultaneously flexing and bulging as if to accentuate the tension evident between them. “There’s not much more I have to say to you, Wes, but I will tell you one thing,” Mitchell continued, although I detected a hint of intensity in his voice, as if he was attempting to threaten me. “Be ready for the career in wrestling if that’s what you’re getting yourself into. It’s a damn tough way of life. You travel constantly, your family become fantasy characters you see them so rarely, and it takes its toll on your body.” Mitchell cleared his throat as his words echoed in my mind, sending a chill of fear down my spine. He leaned in close to me, his beer breath invading my nostrils and spoke his parting words to me: “Ask yourself the question, Wes. Are you really ready to commit to the wrestling lifestyle? If so, go ahead. You’ll be famous, your political stock will rise dramatically and you’ll be well paid, even better than your pay as a politician. If not, then walk away while you still can.” Allen placed his hand on my shoulder in an almost protective move. It was as if he didn’t want me to hear this speech from Mitchell which shed a revealing light on the wrestling business that I had never once fathomed before now. Suddenly I considered the effects of what I was doing in the long term. Was it really worth sacrificing all that I cared about for wrestling, for money? Mitchell retreated to his television in the back room to catch up with the latest in the wrestling world, as Allen and I left his apartment. I had just been given the harsh reality – now it was up to me to accept or reject it. For now, however, the issue of Fisher’s deceptive moves still occupied my mind. I explained to Allen that I was heading to the only place I could access Fisher and those he worked with – the 19th precinct of the NYPD in New York City. My political onslaught on Fisher was not far away… The 19th precinct of the NYPD was located only twenty minutes from Rob Mitchell’s apartment in Manhattan in a relatively nondescript location. The majority of people living in and around the precinct were well-off and crime rates were minuscule compared to those in the Bronx and elsewhere. As I made my way through the building, thoughts of utter hatred and confusion crossed my mind, a mixture of regret about joining the wrestling business and fury over Fisher’s cheap tactics against me. I reached the ninth floor and the elevator doors opened before me slowly. The cacophony of detective’s voices rang in my ears as I made a right turn into the cubicle I knew belonged to one of the men working under Dean Fisher on the Ruth Beckford case. In fact, the man who sat before me was none other than the detective who had informed Detective Johnson of Fisher’s underhanded deeds. When I received the phone call from Johnson this morning, this day to be forgotten had only begun. As I stood menacingly before the detective, who was dressed in a suit and tie combination which hardly befitted him, his wavering eyes told me what I needed to know. He knew where Dean Fisher had gone. “I’m going to make this really quick for you, Detective. I’m a busy man with a tight schedule, as you are well aware, and I need you to tell me where I can find Dean Fisher,” I probed, my calm voice betraying the anger I felt. The detective’s badge read “Matthews”. I could tell Matthews was anxious about this confrontation, despite the fact what I was doing was not strictly allowed. In fact, I wondered why he wasn’t advising his superior of my presence right now. Of course, there was only one answer. Fear. “Yes, I can tell you where Mr. Fisher is, sir,” Matthews stammered. He pulled up a computer file which had all the details of Fisher’s schedule today in it. Reading from the file, he continued, “Mr. Fisher has gone to meet up with his girlfriend, a Miss Lilly Baines.” I broke out in a sweat upon hearing the name of the woman I held in such high regard for so many years. Lilly Baines and I had been the best of friends for most of our teenage lives. Completely taken aback by the words Detective Matthews had just uttered, I shook my head and asked him politely to read it aloud again. There was no mistake. It was her. I sifted through the facts in my head and suddenly an idea came to me. In a daze, I thanked Detective Matthews for his assistance and made my way back to the elevator. As the shaft made its descent, the idea took shape. I had my path to revenge laid out in front of me. It was so obvious to me now, although I would do it reluctantly. I had to covertly investigate Dean Fisher’s relationship with Lilly Baines in order to dig up some so-called “dirt” on their relationship. I did not intend to harm Lilly Baines in the slightest, but I felt a strange pang of jealousy as I pictured them together. Lilly Baines had to escape from the monster that was Dean Fisher as soon as possible. Perhaps I was being a little selfish, but I knew when I was doing the right thing. This was one of those occasions. The elevator doors opened and I maneuvered my way past a crowd of officers entering the elevators, all of them returning from their lunch breaks, I assumed. My idea was concrete now, and I would release my own article to tarnish Fisher’s reputation. If this was successful, any information released by him about the Ruth Beckford case would be discredited. The public would not trust him. All I had to do now was enlist the right kind of assistance. There was only one man who I could trust. Rob Mitchell’s apartment was nothing short of a wrestling shrine, and as Phil Allen perused the various wrestling memorabilia that Mitchell had accumulated over the years, Allen was reminded of the importance of wrestling to the fans who watch each and every week. Allen knew that Coast To Coast was at the top of the pile in terms of the events he had wrestled at up to this point in his career, and he was eager to impress on the biggest stage on September 1st. “Hey Phil, I hope your boy hasn’t gone off to hurt himself. You need him for the match, you know,” Mitchell stated matter-of-factly. Allen nodded, grinning. “I know I need him, Rob. He needs me too. I’m glad we had that chat with him today. I think he needed it. There comes a point when you have to be straight with people. I just hope we didn’t scare him out of wrestling, that’s all.” Mitchell shook his head in the negative and sat down once again to enjoy more of the wrestling tapes stockpile he had recorded. “Not a chance, Phil. Reno’s a strong minded kid. I could tell it from the moment I met him. He’s gotta be the first politician to become a professional wrestler, right?” “I’d say so, yeah,” Allen replied, realizing just how unusual Reno’s position was. “So that’s an achievement in itself!” Mitchell boomed, applauding a well-executed move on-screen. Allen paused for a moment before speaking. “Do you think I should call him? You know, to make sure he hasn’t done anything stupid?” “Nah, he’ll be cool. Give him time to think about it,” Mitchell advised, then paused the tape, turning to Allen with a serious look on his unshaven face. “Phil, I have to ask how you’re feeling about this match. You’ve hardly mentioned yourself since you got here.” Allen hesitated, but answered in his trademark tone of confidence. “What I really want to know, Rob,” Allen said, “is if the match is ready for me.” “Good response, Phil,” Mitchell replied, though his attention was almost completely on the television screen. Allen pondered Reno’s whereabouts in silence for a moment or two, not realizing how concerned he would be about him if he left. Allen had never been a father figure before, but with his fiancé pregnant, he would have to get accustomed to it. Allen opted to put his thoughts to the side for the moment, and joined Mitchell to enjoy some classic wrestling matches, hoping all the while that at Coast To Coast, The Coalition’s match would also be a classic. It was 4:30pm and I still hadn’t eaten lunch. My hotel room was quite reflective of my current situation – dirty, disorganized and aloof. Political magazines were littered all over the carpeted floor; the bed sheets were tossed aside; my daily planner was as good as useless on a day like today. By this time on any other day, I would be heading home after a hard day’s work. Today was different. There was no structure, something I badly needed right now. After making the decision to dig up some dirt on the blossoming relationship between Fisher and Lilly, I did feel slightly uncomfortable about the proposition. I was no detective, I had no investigative skills. I would be a fish out of water. I had to enlist assistance. As I reached for the phone, I recalled how influential this person had been in my life. This person had made multiple sacrifices for me, and I hadn’t contacted them in months, maybe even a year. I picked up the receiver and dialed the number, my hands shaking uncontrollably. After four rings, the person on the other end of the line picked up, his hoarse voice instantly recognizable. “Yeah, who’s this?” For a moment, I couldn’t muster the courage to speak to the man on the other line. Memories flooded back out of the blue. I remembered his powerful speeches, how he used to mould people into his followers. His journey to the top was a swift one. “It’s me, Wes,” I managed in a barely audible voice. He didn’t respond immediately. I sensed the same emotion in him as I felt myself. We were uneasy, and I almost hung up. Then he spoke. “Wes, how you doin’, kid?” “I’m really good,” I replied sheepishly, “but I actually called to talk to you about something that’s come up in the last day. It’s regarding Dean Fisher.” The name brought pause to the man who I barely knew anymore. It meant something to him. I knew then I had an ally in the one man I was certain I could trust. “Can you help me out, dad?” I asked, feeling like I had finally broken the ice by calling him by his title. “I’d be happy to, son,” my father replied. “I suggest we meet somewhere to discuss it.” “I’m in New York,” I responded. “I hope that won’t be a problem.” “Not at all,” he replied sternly, “I’ll be there in the morning. I know how potentially devastating this could be.” Although I had told my father little or no information about the situation at hand, the mere mention of Dean Fisher’s name triggered a defensive response in him. His assistance in this matter would be invaluable. No one had more connections in the political world than my father. Fisher was a dead issue, as far as I was concerned. “I’ll see you tomorrow morning, dad.” “Goodbye, Wes.” My father hung up abruptly, leaving me alone in my hotel room to contemplate the case and how I would approach everything. The sound of traffic on the streets many stories below hummed in the background as I lay down on my bed for some respite.
