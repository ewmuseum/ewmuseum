The beginning.

1 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 01, 2005 The man with the rubber boots stepped into the elevator behind me, but I didn't see him at first. I smelled him though - the pungent odor of smoke and cheap wine and life on the street without soap. We were alone as we moved upward, and when I finally glanced over I saw the boots, black and dirty and much too large. A frayed and tattered trench coat fell to his knees. Under it, layers of foul clothing bunched around his midsection, so that he appeared stocky, almost fat. But it wasn't from being well fed; in the wintertime in L.A., the street people wear everything they own, or so it seems. He was black and aging--his beard and hair were half-gray and hadn't been washed or cut in years. He looked straight ahead through thick sunglasses, thoroughly ignoring me, and making me wonder for a second why, exactly, I was inspecting him. He didn't belong. It was not his building, not his elevator, not a place he could afford. The lawyers and agents on all eight floors worked at hourly rates that still seemed obscene to me, even after seven years. Just another street bum in from the noise. Happened all the time in downtown L.A. But I'm sure they have security guards to deal with the riffraff. We stopped at six, and I noticed for the first time that he had not pushed a button, had not selected a floor. He was following me. I made a quick exit, and as I stepped into the splendid marble foyer of Drake & Sweeney I glanced over my shoulder just long enough to see him standing in the elevator, looking at nothing, still ignoring me. Madam Devier, one very resilient and quite hot receptionist, greeted me with her typical look of disdain. 'Watch the elevator,' I said. 'Why?' 'Street bum. You may want to call security.' 'Those people,' she said in her affected French accent. 'Get some disinfectant too.' I walked away, wrestling my overcoat off my shoulders, forgetting the man with the rubber boots. I had a meeting at 9, then some important conferences with important people. I turned the corner and was about to say something to Polly, the secretary of Agent Mike McDouggell, when I heard the scrunny man speak. 'Ethan, welcome, I have good news for you.' I turned and saw Mike standing at the doorway of his office, dressed in his usual white shirt, black tie, black suit and 3/4 sweep hair cut. He was the prototype nerd who flunked out of Law School and ended up an agent. 'Finally,' I said. 'Shut up,' he mumbled, with a great deal of composure. 'Come on in, I've got another meeting in a half hour.' I walked through the large wooden door, Mike's office was a prototype blue-collar American's office, filled with American flags and eagles. A picture of him and his newly wed wife hangs proudly behind his desk. The room was dominated by a long table, covered with documents and papers that only seconds ago seemed terribly important. A row of windows overlooked a parking lot. Two doors led to the hallway. The stench that I had smelled before seem to have followed me throughout the building. Or did it... Without warning the man with the rubber boots slammed the door behind me, and slowly waved at Mike and I assume myself. 'I'd like you to meet a representative from Atlantic Wrestling Club,' Mike proudly introduced the smelly man behind me. 'Oh splendid...' My first instinct was to bolt, to lunge with arms and legs flapping and flailing for the door, and hope for luck, that I wouldn't touch that repulsive human, but my knees shook and my blood ran cold. How could this be, Ethan Phoenix, the hottest star in Wrestling only a few short years ago, makes a decision to return, and the ol' faithful Mike the Agent can only have enough contacts to get Atlantic Wrestling Club? A representative who hasn't seen a paycheque in years? Why not CORE? Why not something in Primetime? Has my stock fallen this far already? 'Ethan, Atlantic Wrestling Club is probably the most highly touted new organization. I've taken serious consideration about this, and I think it will be perfect for you.' Mike of course, is calm and collective. I, on the other hand, am about to regurgitate the McDonald's I had for breakfast on the run. 'Of course.' View Ethan Phoenix 's Biography
