The Bizwrite Saga, Ch. 3: The Brotherly Love

12 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/12/05 We weren’t too close to home at all anymore, traveling across the country to see a lady that could potentially lead to big trouble. It wasn’t certain what we could expect, but none of that mattered right now… “SWEEEEEEET EMOTIONS!” It had been five hours since we started traveling this day, and for about four and a half of those hours, Liam has been belting out every song on the radio. He’s been singing songs he knows… “Exiiiiit light….Enterrrrrr night!...” Songs he doesn’t… “Something something something on a Craazyyyyy Traaaain!” And just plain…yeah… “I ammmm the best mtoehrfcuker…I am the best!” “Now, Liam, which song is that?” I flashed a smiled at him and gave him a sly little glance from the corner of my eye. I could see him shaking his head and laughing a little bit. He could never handle being teased. “Shut up, you fucker.” “I’m just saying, if you’re going to sing, make sure it’s a song that actually exists, ok?” We kept on driving on the lonely road, my brother and I, for what seemed like hours until we finally saw something in the distance. It looked like a diner of some sort, just sitting there by itself. It looked lonely. “Wanna stop, Liam?” “Yeah, sure.” Once I pulled over, I parked between another beaten up truck and a small little car, which were some of the only cars in the parking lot. There was no name to this place, so my assumption that it was a diner was based on the fact that a) it had an inside building, and b) it had a gas station out front. Even if it wasn’t a diner, maybe there were some sweets or something we could grab. We stepped inside and no one paid us any notice. We stood there for a minute, looking around the room. There was one man, grotesquely over weight, eating what seemed like was an entire fried chicken. “Gross.” Liam muttered. Another man was sitting next to his daughter at the bar, not saying a word. She was playing with her food while he was staring at her, trying to cajole her to say something with his eyes. It was pathetic. Liam and I walked to one of the few tables and took a seat. The table was filthy, but it was still the cleanest one in there. “So…is anyone going to wait on us?” Apparently not. For 10 minutes we sat there and received nothing. Finally, right before we reached the end of our patience, an old, hunched back lady who looked more miserable than Tim Shipley having sex with a woman came out from the back room. She trotted over to where we sat and slowly pulled out a pad. “Uhhhh…Coke…Cheeseburger with lettuce.” “Ummm…Pepsi…” “We don’t have that.” “Ok, a Coke…and…” “Will you make up your mind?!” She was now yelling. I looked around to see if anyone noticed, but they didn’t. I guess they had bigger problems than us. “You have fried chicken?” Without answering, she wrote something down and turned around, heading to the back room. We took that as a yes and towards each other. Liam was shaking his head and smiling, almost chuckling to his self. “Crazy bitch. This better be the greatest food in the earth if we’re getting service like that!” “Liam, you know it won’t be…look at this place! Look where it is!” We had to pause for a moment so Liam could realize all that. After a few moments, he burst out into laughter, seemingly out of nowhere. “Jesus, at least it’s something, eh?” We didn’t say anything for a few moments. We stared at the menu, trying to see if maybe we ordered the wrong thing. The menu was pretty plain, though, so I doubt it. “Say, Liam, what’re you thinking about all this?” “Well, I’m thinking that I probably could’ve ordered something better than the Fried Chicken…” “No, not that! I mean…this! What we’re doing! Where we’re going! All that bullshit!” He paused for a moment. Silence. “Eh, you don’t want to hear it. Trust me, you aren’t going to like it.” “Oh, shut up and tell me, you fucking loser!” He looked down at the table and sighed, shaking his head. He then lifted his head up and stared straight at me, not blinking and sure as hell not smiling. “I think it’s all fucked up. I think you’re just bringing me here out of selfishness. You’re clinging to a past that doesn’t exist anymore, and you’re visiting a bitch who wants you to see a future you won’t be able to accept…” “Yeah, well…” “I’m not done yet. I know that this n*gger is going to be there when we arrive. Will I actually kill him? Or will I respect Liz and just walk straight out to the car? Who fucking knows. I said I have no respect for that whore anymore…” “Please, Liam, watch the…” “I’d shut the fuck up if I were you, Tim.” Silence. “Anyways, as I was saying, I have no respect for that whore anymore. Once she spit on the whole Nest, I had to say ‘Fuck You!’ And she didn’t even TELL you she was seeing this guy, that she was going to leave, did she?” Silence, for I knew the truth. “It’s all fucked up, Tim. It really is.” “Then…then why are you coming?” He shook his head before he could muster any words. I could see a few tears forming in one of his eyes as he tried to hold back his emotions. “It’s cause I love you, Tim. You know I’ll always have your back, through thick and thin. What am I going to do, let you do this by yourself? What if you run into trouble? All they have to do is just see one of your tattoos, and a group of n*ggers could kill you, right there. God wouldn’t be able to do a thing…” “*GASP* Don’t say that!...” “It’s true, Tim. He wouldn’t be able to do a fucking thing.” And it was. On my back there was an Eagle, spread out, ripping a black man in half with his talons. On one fore arm was the word “WHITE”, the other had “LORD”. Right above my belly button was a cross which had Jesus crucified, with a whole bunch of Jews and n*ggers standing underneath him, yelling and taunting. “So that’s why I’m here, Tim. Cause if we’re going to die, I wanna die with you. If we’re going to live, the same. I can’t let you get in trouble, and if anything ever happened to me, I’d want you to be there too. I love you, man.” He stared at me with a smile as the waitress came over, dropping the food in front of us with a slam. She then pressed the cokes into the table and glared at the both of us. “I don’t accept crying in my restaurant. So stop.” Well, tough shit, bitch.
