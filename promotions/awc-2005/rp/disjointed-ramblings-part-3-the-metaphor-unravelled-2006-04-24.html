Disjointed Ramblings Part 3: The Metaphor Unravelled

24 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/24/06 I had reached reformation in physical form, and I was somewhere in the mind of my empty vessel, and familiarized myself with the surroundings in the mental battleground to regain control of myself. I walked through tulip fields. Beside me, there was a pond. I looked into it. The water was so clear that I could see right through to the brown, virile dirt beneath it. The pond drew me in, and I bent down to touch it until I saw my reflection, and it startled me. I was wearing a long gray overcoat, with a knife blade poking through the long, dangling sleeves that hid my hands. “Soy el diablo,” I whispered to my reflection. I don’t know why I chose to whisper this in Spanish. Perhaps my reflection only spoke Spanish. Or perhaps it just sounded even more ominous and more shocking in a foreign language. I backed up into a bent-kneed stand, and remembered the deal I made with the devil a long time ago. “You do realize that if I grant this to you, you will cease to exist as you know yourself,” he remarked, “instantaneously.” I nodded my head, not quite knowing where my soul would end up. “And just to play my own advocate here, doesn’t it weigh at all on your conscience that a young woman whom you have never met is paying the price for your slim chance at salvation?” “The nonexistent have no consciousness.” “I didn’t say that you’d cease to exist, you just wouldn’t exist as you know yourself.” “Then I’m willing to carry that burden.” “Hold your palm up to me.” I’m a tricky fucking bastard, huh? I’ve been in here since then; since I dug my nails and dispelled my being into his blood. And then I used him as my own vessel, through death, back into an empty shell. I played dumb. I’ve done this before, and that’s why I’m immortal, because I can pass through body to body. I stood back up in surprise not because I feared what I saw, but because it was successful yet again. I was overjoyed. I am not the devil. I am Garbage Bag Johnny, and I collect people’s souls. I am the me who is eternal. I am the part of myself that fears my own non-existence, the part that wants the world to recognize my significance. I am ego unbridled, pride unsurpassed, and delusion realized. *** I had been plodding along in this waxy tube for some time now, and I had escaped the vaporous arms of the living growth, trying to infect me, turn me into a pathogen. And then I saw past the yellow. I ran as fast as I could, kicking the muck up, and staining myself with it, until I reached the end, tripped and fell through into suspension in brightened blackness. I was floating in the air, because I had reached the earth’s core. A glowing gray organ pumped and whistled, sending life sprawling back up to the surface through strong, straining veins. It was so tired, but it pushed on with such endurance. Maybe it knew the answer. Maybe it would tell me, so it could finally rest, and stop beating. I floated over to it, put my mouth on its cold, moist skin. “Do you know the answer?” I summoned to it, like I was lost in the desert and saw a mirage, but just kept stuffing my mouth with sand because I thought it was water. It kept emitting its beat and its low sustaining hum, the kind of sound a bee would make if it buzzed into a megaphone. It knew, I could feel it, but it kept its secret. I started pounding on it, beating on the giant organ in the center of the earth, demanding an answer, but I received no answer. I started to think that the answer was a myth, that there was no answer, and that the whole fucking quest for an answer that humanity had undertaken was just a self-perpetuating, pie in the sky pipe dream that gave mankind a reason not to do itself in. As it turns out, that reason was running thin anyway. Technology gave man a method to destroy itself. And fewer and fewer people seemed to be searching for anything anymore. Man was giving up, and through the growing vaporous connection that bound into a growing internet of existence, the end of the search drew nearer and nearer. And eventually man would find that there was no answer, no other reasonable thing to do than destroy itself. The answer that did not exist could only be found through not searching for it. “The answer that did not exist could only be found through not searching for it!” I bellowed, mimicking my own thought, and I felt the brightened blackness shrink into dimmer and dimmer shades. That was it. I began to panic as the beating randomly slowed, and what I could see of the organ weakly bubbled rather than beat as it shriveled into something reminiscent of the heart of a peach. It was pitch black, and I was scared. It was silent. I huddled in the empty core, stuck in a gravitational malaise where every direction was up, and I couldn’t move, entrapped in this womb by pressure from all sides, forming a wrapping around me. I was a mummified corpse in this sarcophagus, this earthen tomb. I wanted to shout out again, so I yelled loudly, over and over, until I was out of breath. It was a summoning. It would have been almost like a prayer had prayers not been so utterly useless. And then I knelt down into recluse. I closed my eyes and took myself to a safe place; an empty place with flowers. *** We had gotten to driving. I was uncomfortable with her in the car right next to me. Her cut off white t-shirt just barely exposed her belly button. It peeked out from between her white shirt material and her denim jean skirt in the middle of a strip of a white, paste colored stomach. I couldn’t stand this. I hated the sight of belly buttons; ugly round little indents with a creased fleshy knot fished somewhere within. I tried to break the uncomfortable silence of driving a stranger around without destination. But the only words that leapt from my mind to my tongue were my voiced discomforts at the site of her blasted belly button. “Can you pull your shirt down over your belly button?” I asked as courteously as I could while still expressing the serious nature and intent of the question. “Huh?” “Just pull it over your belly button. I don’t want to see it.” My voice was a bit more direct this time, but hopefully non-threatening and nowhere near severe enough to give her any reason for concern. She did as she was told, though. She had to because I had paid her. I could tell that she was put off by this, and she was growing restless from all the silent driving, probably in a series of overlapping circles – figurative circles, that is, because the streets run in straight lines, for the most part, and the shapes we were driving in were more or less parallelograms, some with extra sides. “Look, Jenny, I have to be honest with you,” I finally spat out, quite sure that there would be no subtler way to tell her that I had sold her soul to the devil than to tell her flat out. “How did you know my name was Jenny? I told you it was Starla before we got into the car,” she seemed worried, like I was going to rape her or something- rape her after I had paid for her services as a prostitute. It was all a bit ridiculous. Or maybe she thought I was the government. She had to have been paranoid. She had done lines in the bathroom before I had gotten there. There was still powder dabbed on her smeared lipstick just above the upper lip. “Nobody’s name is Starla,” I said, trying to be witty and reassuring. “I came looking for you. It’s kind of a long and complicated story. I’m kind of the guy who bought your soul on ebay.” I paused, and looked over at her. Her eyes glazed over with a veil of desperation, like a cast away seeing what looks to be a ship off in the water or a traveler stranded in the desert, fooled by a malicious sun casting mirages down upon the earth. “Do you still have that certificate? I’ll give you anything for it. There’s no price I wouldn’t pay for it. I’ll give you everything I have.” I tried at several points to cut through her hysterics, but I’d just shout out blunt beginnings of words, half-syllables, and errant noises until finally she settled down and let me speak. “I, uh, I sold your soul to the devil,” I said, expecting the oncoming backlash that brewed inside of her. “You fucking cunt!” she screeched back at me, swatting at my left arm, shoulder, and face. Then she fell silent for a while, and I kept driving, with my hands at two and ten and my eyes focusing on the middle of my lane in the horizon; until she spoke again. “I’m not going to fuck you now, you know,” she said. “If I wanted to fuck you, I wouldn’t have told you that,” I replied. She looked at me frankly. “I want my certificate back. You better fucking go and get it back for me,” she said, and I chuckled at the implausibility of her statement. But she wasn’t laughing along with me. “Stop laughing. If you don’t get it back, I’ll kill you.” “Wait, wait, wait, wait, wait! The soul isn’t a piece of paper. It’s not a certificate. It’s all in your mind, your fall from morality is. Restoration is a lot more complicated than that. I can show you how you can set things straight again though, if you come with me.” I awaited her response, and it barked staccato at me in short one word sentences. “You. Son. Of. A. Bitch. Give. Me. My. Soul. Back.” She was convinced that I still had her soul. Sometimes, that’s what going bat shit insane does, though. Then I heard her fumbling around in her purse. “I’m going to kill you,” she muttered again angrily in a cocaine-buzzed angst, but it was already too late. As I held a gun aimed right between her eyes with my left hand and steered the car, finally finding a way out of town, she had just found her pistol in her purse, and understood by my body language that I wouldn’t hesitate to kill her if I heard her make one move. “Jenny, put the purse on the floor, sit still, and calm down. I’m trying to help you out, but you’re going to have to do exactly as I say.” “Are you going to kill me?” she asked fearfully. This was good. This meant that she could emphasize with the victim, and lacked that murderous capacity. “Yes, my dear, but it’s for your own good.” I replied, being now ably dead honest. “And then I’m going to kill myself.” I kind of chuckled and watched the land open up and the stars replace the streetlights. And then, to get my kicks, I bade her plead for her life as if I was her maniacal husband, about to murder her after she caught me cheating on her with the lieutenant governor in a seedy motel just off the bullshit exit ramp that leads to nothing but dirt fields. I liked to create little situations like this. They were what kept me level. “You don’t have to do that, Johnny. We can work our own shit out. I’ll stop the drugs and the sex and everything I’ve done wrong. We can both start all over. We’ll get honest jobs, work hard, have a couple of kids. We’ll have a family, Johnny.” “I hate kids.” I replied nonchalantly with a hint of spite and a handful of being a complete and total badass. I continued, “We both have to die, Jenny, or neither of us have a chance to put things back the way they used to be, and that’s what you want, right?” She nodded, sobbing slightly, because I bade her to nod, and she had to do anything I said. It was much simpler to just command her to understand than to actually explain the deep rooted intricacies of my plan. It wasn’t her fault though. Women are bad at understanding things like underhanded schemes or advanced algebra. They do, however, understand a smack in the face. That’s for certain. But in all fairness, that’s what mostly everybody understands nowadays. “Then trust me,” I winked, “and everything will turn out fine.” The rest of the drive was peaceful and quiet. She cried without noise, and I loved her like an angel then, if only then, for foolish reasons, like the way the countryside turned pitch black, and it was only her and me and nothing else forever. It was perfect. The path of meandering country roads left us isolated underneath a midnight veil. I stopped the car, and we got out. I held her hand and aimed at her head, but we couldn’t really see anything but vague shapes of each other’s lines and edges anyway. BANG! I waited for the silence to refill the air after the echo of the gunshot dissolved into the sky. I thought if I had to die, I might as well end it romantically. BANG! *** I was back at the place with the pond and the flowers, but the clouds above looked like they were about to rip apart into a downpour. The tulips were wilting, as if some disagreeable funk had passed over them and choked them with its stench. I walked past the pond as the gray rain began to fall. Dead poi floated in it as it became murky and opaque. I continued on, frantically, haunted by the now palpable presence of the invader. I needed to find him and expel him from my territory before he infested it with his contagious discharges. They were killing the plants, and staining with sacrilege this peaceful hideaway. I ran, seeing the gigantic shadow cast behind the man as the sun set. Its tip was big and bright, and it looked like a rounded sliver, shining out in one final blaze as it lowered beneath the horizon like a dead man in a casket. The man stared towards the sun, draped in an overcoat, while the sun skulked away in visible increments. And then when the last ray of light narrowed to a thin line and then sucked into itself from parallel sides, he turned towards me and started walking, slashing with a sharp knife the tops off of the flowers, whose colors had withered to blacks, whites, and grays. The knife cut silently, without the normal whooshing sound of struggle that a duller knife would make as it hacked; hacked, not sliced through the rows of stems. “I presume I need no introduction,” he muttered, smirking to himself underneath a deranged, diplomatic grin. “Now, to settle the debt on this little wager we made.” “What wager?” I honestly didn’t remember, but then there were a lot of things I didn’t remember. The majority of my life was forgotten, as were things I had forgotten during that time. I had forgotten the things I had once forgotten, which makes remembering them even more of a pain in the ass. I was flying blind, unable to see a bluff, and at the mercy of this vindictive shadow. He laughed aloud. “Oh, that’s right. It’s a pity that you woke up with a clear head for once. Anyways, I thought we might be able to spare the drama, so why don’t you kindly vacate this mind.” I owed him this body, and was unable to defend myself, but cessation of my mind and body would put me in a very undesirable situation. I was ready to fight that son of a bitch even though he had a knife that would shred me into a pile of flesh and bone. Yeah, I bet that knife could cut through bone. “Hey, man! Wait a minute!” A voice heralded out of the distance, and God, wearing his “FLORIDA” t-shirt underneath an open brown bathrobe came trolling down the field. Both the devil and I turned and looked at the approaching luminary. “Ah, fuck. Here comes this guy,” the devil begrudgingly groaned. “You two know each other?” I inquired. “We used to be roommates back in college, but the devil was always a pretty big dick.” “Fuck off, God. You never did the dishes.” “I didn’t make any of the fucking dishes, you asshole.” “The dishes aren’t important. I’m here to kill both of you with this knife.” The devil lunged forward, trying to impale God through the stomach, but God had cat-like reflexes and jumped back just in time to avoid the stab. “Whoa, man. Put the knife down. I’m sure we can talk this out,” said God, but I suddenly grew enraged and interrupted him. “What the fuck? You’re supposed to be almighty, and your best defense against the devil trying to cut you a new asshole is talking things out? What kind of supreme being are you?” Both of the contrasting representations of good and evil turned and looked at me, a bit startled or even bemused at my outburst. The devil temporarily loosened his arm and let it hang limp beside him, quelling the stabbing threat. God turned to me and started laughing. “What do you think? Do you think God is this gigantic spirit that shoots lightning bolts out of his ass? I’m just a regular guy like you and the devil. For all I know, we’re probably just all parts of someone’s imagination. Maybe we’re just fictional characters that some guy is thinking of right now.” “I thought you were all knowing, or something. What was all that bullshit earlier about searching for an answer?” “It was just bullshit. You seemed like you needed some direction, so I made some shit up to give you something to do.” “Well, I found the answer. There is no answer. People just keep searching and searching, milling around like cows grazing in a mud patch. There’s no grass, and you can’t tell the dirt from your own feces. It’s pathetic. Life keeps breeding on and on for no advanced purpose and no advanced meaning but to keep reproducing endlessly until the population chokes the world, and the world can’t sustain itself anymore. We’re nothing more than a fucking disease that spreads and leaves the earth deceased!” I was going absolutely mad at this point. They both wanted control of my head. I wasn’t about to let either of them have it. The devil threw his knife down, and it stuck handle-up in the dirt. “He’s right, you know. But I still want control over his mind and body.” “Yeah, me too,” replied God. “But I still don’t agree with him. Some people get tired of living, so they create progeny to do it for them. But I could never get tired of life’s perennial reciprocation. I could watch that looping on beautifully until it dies out, which it eventually will, but not in one lifetime; not for many.” The devil, cynical by nature, put in his piece. “But mixed in with that beauty is tragedy. It’s an unavoidable, bottlenecking experience that satisfies a deep human urge and develops into a deeper human addiction. And it culminates at that point where it all ends. The most beautiful thing about the beautiful is its slow, sad demise into obscurity. The past only exists in memory, and complete annihilation is the only way to remember and create a more beautiful past.” They were both really idealists with simple philosophies. Their goals belittled mine with their individual pleasures and drive to attain them. My goals were selfish and arrogant, and theirs’, though simple, aimed to paint and repaint what life appeared to be and what life was, together, in one overlapping diagram. Apparently too, God had been doing the same thing that the devil had been doing, however the two had different strategies pertaining to stealing fresh mind’s for their own immortal good. The devil stormed in through the front door, taking a more direct approach and immediately taking over the mind before anyone could do anything about it. God had a more subtle way of moving in slowly, making precise, calculated moves to gain influence and trust. And when he had you in the palm of your hand, then he’d strike like a python, wrapping around its prey. They were the two greatest operators in the immortality business. They were the fucking kings of this shit, and much purer in their idealism than I could ever be. But I had to stop them both, because as chance would have it, they just so happened to simultaneously have been working on the same head to inhabit. “Neither of you can have it!” I exclaimed jealously, as indicated by the exclamation point. I needed to put an end to this recycling of life. It couldn’t go on. How many others like me had there been, who struggled and ultimately lay incomplete, strewn somewhere along one of the pathways of potential. God rebutted, “That’s a pretty selfish viewpoint. You don’t have enough experience and memory to know how addicting it is to exist. We couldn’t have kept living and living from soul to soul just to have it end. What would be the point of the past?” “What’s the point of the future then?” The devil then chimed in. “Look, if you don’t want to live, then don’t. Leave, and God and I will keep fighting for it. It should make no difference to you if you’re dead whether we’re alive or not. The two worlds rarely have anything to do with one another.” “Fine. Whatever. You two can fight it out.” I said. But it was all part of a plan to outsmart them. If one of them killed the other, then I wouldn’t have to defend myself against two of them. Upon hearing this, God charged at Satan, but the devil reached down and pulled his knife out of the dirt. God tackled Satan and the two began tumbling on the ground. God threw punches at Satan while trying to avoid the wild knife swings that jutted out between Satan’s arms being positioned to defend his face. Suddenly, God rolled off the groggy devil and held the side of his head. Blood trickled down the side of his neck. “You son of a bitch! You cut my fucking earlobe off.” The devil pushed himself up to his knees, but all three of us froze in place as a gunshot rattled the air. *** I lowered the gun and looked in front of me as the three other versions of me stood staring at me, breathing heavily from all the clamor and excitement. Jenny was standing by my side, looking in tints like her former, unblemished self. It must’ve been the weather. “Alright, you three, cut this shit out, or I’ll blow all of your fucking brains out.” I was no marksman, but I had upgraded for this portion of my search for truth from a pistol to a shotgun. Precision took a back seat to raw, head-exploding power. “Now, follow me. We’re going to settle this rationally,” I instructed as coolly as a man holding a still smoking shotgun could. So Jenny and I led them back to the sandy place in front of an open sided shelter made out of wooden planks, built for shade. I had a writhing, thirty nine pound bag on my shoulder that I placed on the sand behind me. Nobody asked about it. I made everyone sit in a circle in the sand, and I took out a couple of joints from my pocket to pass around. Then I changed my mind, and decided we should all crawl on top of the wooden dugout, and smoke on the roof. We all made our way up and sat on the slatted boards that untrustworthily managed to hold our weight. “Okay, now I think what we all learned is that messing with life and death and the human soul really causes a giant shit storm more than anything. What we need to do now, is put shit back the way it was before this whole mess with space and time happened.” “Why do we need to do that?” the ignorant reconstruction of myself asked. “I don’t know. It’s just common sense. In every movie ever made about time travel or space travel, man extends the bounds and limits of his knowledge, fucks something up, and has to return it to normal before everything’s just fucked up forever for everybody. So we need to basically come up with a way for me to get back into whatever present time it was before any of this happened.” “Why do you get the body?” “Because I have a shotgun, so I’d end up killing you all anyway if you refused. I just kind of want this to go with all of us on the same page. It’ll be a lot easier if we do this my way,” I said. I had felt somewhat responsible for the creation of all of these characters, and knew I needed to destroy this deformed growth that pimpled out from reality. Everyone agreed, adhering to a lack of any other options, and we passed the joint around and smoked together. At worse, the whole situation could play out again and again, without end, and form a knot in time, maybe slow it down, like it was a hose, or kill it, like it was a noose around the world’s fat neck, squeezing it into an hourglass figure; or a sideways infinity symbol. “So, any volunteers to go first?” asked the devil. As expected, there were no volunteers. We settled this the only way we knew how. We’d pass the joint around until it was gone, and the last person to take a hit would be the first to die. And when the smoke cleared, the devil had the last of the roach. “It’s cleared,” he said in a somber tone. He stood up, and I stood up with him. I straightened the gun out with my arm and shot an eye at him over the barrel. “Do you want it in the head or the chest?” “Head.” Shooting someone in the face with a shotgun is a pretty blatant way to kill someone. I was hoping for some softer method that perhaps wasn’t as messy, but at least this way, I’d know I got the job done. BANG. And chunks of his head flew everywhere. The roof and the ground below the dugout were splattered with bony hunks of raw brain meat. It wasn’t a pretty sight, seeing a head punched to bits like that by a tiny bullet, especially since the head that exploded was identical to my own. It made me feel like vomiting, but I kept swallowing the bile back down every time I felt it rising in my stomach. It felt like I was sucking on a hot coal. God stood up next. “I don’t think I can watch that again. Please, shoot me. In the back of the head. I can’t watch.” God turned around, and I cocked the gun up again. “Don’t worry,” I said. “I can’t either. Maybe it’s better that you’re facing the other way.” But I had already shot him somewhere in the middle of that sentence. And he fell too with half a head left hanging on top of his neck. The third me stood up and offered to take this bullet as well. Given the mental faculties of a grown man with the experience of an infant, life had traumatized him. He was too delicate to be thrown into a state of awareness about things without getting used to them. I felt bad, when most of his body fell limp and the rest of it propelled off somewhere. I felt bad, maybe just a little bit. Jenny and I climbed down and walked back over to the bag. I thought about untying it, but reconsidered. I sat down next to it. Jenny sat down next to me. “So this is all I do? And I’ll be fine?” “Yeah,” I forced out, with my head in other places than the conversation. “It doesn’t seem like you’re fine.” “That’s because I’m not done. There’s one more composite of myself that we haven’t gotten rid of yet, and the function of life can’t continue with two points sharing the same space along the flow of time. Now get out of here, Jenny. I don’t want you to see this.” Jenny stood up, sadly, with her blue eyes dripping down on me. She turned around, head down, and hesitated to take a step before turning back to me one more time. “So what do I do?” There was a long pause before I answered words that I was once too afraid to admit. “I don’t know.” I stood up. “Just go, please. This is all I know.” She turned again and started walking off, and I started fearing that she wouldn’t turn around this time. God, how I wanted her to turn around just once more, to see if she loved me or not, but she didn’t turn around. So I panicked and yelled after her. “Jenny, wait!” She stopped, and slowly turned again, not her whole body, but just her neck so she could see me out of her tilted blue eyes. She waited on me. “Good luck, Jenny Taylor.” She smiled and turned again, so I turned in a manner so that I couldn’t see her walk off. At least, that’s how I would have written it out. I went back to the bag, struggling with the top of it before untying the bag’s exit. A child crawled out. It was me when I was younger. He stood up and looked at me, slightly amused. He thought my beard was big and funny, and my long hair and tired skin made me look old and droopy. I must’ve looked harmless and unimposing, like a mime who didn’t want to be a mime anymore but didn’t know how to be anything else. I looked at him, smiling, finding the wonder in things as ordinary as a mess of hair hanging from my chin. He admired it so honestly, even if it didn’t always come off as admiration. And he had his whole life ahead of him still. I wondered how I’d blow my own head off with the shotgun. My arms were shorter than normal, so I’d have to tie a string to the trigger and make a pulley system, but that seemed too logical. Or I’d have to take off my shoes and use my toes, but that was such a graceless way to die. I even wondered if I could just fire the shotgun straight into the air and wait for the gravity to drive the bullet straight back down and right through the center of my cranium, down through my spinal cord, shattering me, tearing me apart. And then I thought again about how withered and tired I was, how old and droopy I was. I couldn’t turn into this again. I’ll let him stay pure and innocent and unchanged by all the cold, dark colors that his eyes were too unfocused to see. I’ll let him stay bombarded with that light and those bright, vivid colors that popped out rather than sucked in. Yeah, that’s how I’ll remember him. The gun trembled in my arms. BANG And then a few more times for good measure, until it clicked. *** The next day, I woke up, and the world still was confusing to me. I coughed painfully, and felt like a piece of my lungs had broken off and fitted itself in my throat. Something was lodged in there, squirming around and tickling and burning the cilia in my trachea. I pulled my blanket tightly around me and kept swallowing saliva to moisten my dry throat and stop the coughing. But my mouth was pretty dry as well, and I was shaking, entrenched in a deep haze that gassed up the room. As I lay there, I tried to pull everything together into one tight conclusion, and I wondered what was next while searching the floor for a gallon container with an apple juice label full of water and a lime that I had lodged in there, but could not get back out. I had woken up to a bleaker reality, now knowing that the present is the only state that exists with so many rules and boundaries, limiting the body and the mind. It’s depressing, actually, to think about how most people just attach themselves to this reality then, too invested in its existence as the only true one. Of all the realities I have been to, this one isn’t the worst, but it’s the most heavily and unknowingly oppressed. The only price totaled is the dragging of a thirty nine pound bag full of an imperfect past through a dark tunnel. I stopped dragging. There were other realities with no burdens strapped to you, no rules imposed upon you, and no guilt necessary to confirm your own existence. And that’s what made me angry. I think all of us were a bit angry. Inside, we were all still so irefully violent. We urged to injure and kill. Man is, at heart, nothing more than a carnivorous, oftentimes cannibalistic biped that roams around with his shit stuck to his own fur, looking for something or someone else to maim with a pointed rock. There’s nothing wrong with that in and of itself, though, you see. I don’t see why we can’t celebrate violence, trade punches rather than salutations, and have it all be in good sport. The assailants would help their victims up after a good thrashing, dust them off, and both parties would be on their ways after a refreshing tussle. Violence has been getting some bad spin, alright. Lots of things have. Even I’ve gotten a bit of a bad rap. That’s why I stay away from reality as much as possible. I decided long ago that this consciousness wasn’t worth my time, but only consciously now. There was no point in searching here anymore. The answers were too heavily guarded, buried under layers of histories and other various attempts to conceal and confuse the truth. I planned on leaving this place permanently, but first I had to destroy it, so that I could never return. When I woke up, just a bit ago, I felt as if I had indeed been reborn. I felt empty, like a flesh covered cavity that only posed as a human being. I felt like the connection between the world inside my mind and the world outside of my body had shut off. I was completely detached from the world a commanding majority envisions as real, from the events that happened there and the people who lived there, And from the rules and boundaries imposed there. The rest of my life will act out bacterially, spreading my ego unbridled, pride unsurpassed, and delusion realized. I will blitzkrieg, or I’ll stalk from the grass, sometimes for days waiting until the right moment to strike out. I untangled myself from the tangle of blankets; and metaphors, and ricocheted off down my familiar trajectory. I refused to sleep again. I stayed up for hours, days, even years, until I grew old, trying to outlive the species, and then the planet. I spent day and night, stomping and pounding and trying to do any small thing I could think of to make the world stop in front of me and follow me to sweet, sweet away. But as I aged and crumbled, I ached, sore, tired and withered, shivering in a corner with unkempt patches of gray hair jutting out in random sparse bursts on the sides and back of my head. My beard grew tangled with thin hairs peeling around each other, and hanging to the ground. My teeth had long since rotted away, and my eyes didn’t open anymore. My muscles were too worn to lift my eyelids back up, imagining all the beautiful things I had seen in the past, back when they were alive too. So I never knew if I outlasted everybody else or if I outlasted the planet, but I swear I was awake in there. I just couldn’t see on account of my eyes having been weighted shut by my eyelids. I think I did win though, in the end. Yeah.
