The Death of a Dream: Chapter Two - Cold Reality

28 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28th June 2005 Pact Her voice came floating from the bathroom like angels' breath. 'I just don't see why we have to stay in a hotel when you live like 30 minutes drive outside the city.' I stood on the balcony of our suite in the cool breeze of the June evening. 'I can't go back there. I just can't. Too much has happened…too much has changed. That's not my life anymore. It's…' She came up behind me and linked her arms around my waist. I felt the soft swell of her breasts caress my tortured spine, their yielding firmness pressing against each and every damaged nerve and smelt the scent of lavender that followed her wherever she went. 'What do you want with an old man like me?' I sighed. 'You're not old…you're experienced.' I let out a derisive snort and turned around to face her. 'I'm fucked up, babe. That's all. Fucked up and half dead.' 'Don’t say that! I won't let you talk about yourself like that.' She turned away from me, head bowed. 'Hey…hon…don't.' I sat down on the bed in front of her. 'I'm sorry, okay? It's just…things have taken a turn for the worse and…well…I don't see a way out.' I lifted her chin with my hand. She blinked back tears. 'You're afraid, aren't you?' she asked. Sheepishly, I looked away. 'There's nothing wrong with being afraid, Chris. You've more right than anyone to be afraid. I mean…your back…and that horrible man, Kitsune…' 'That horrible man has only ever had my best interests at heart.' 'Give me a break, Chris – can't you see he's using you?' I looked away again. 'It's part of the deal. He helps me, I help him.' 'And how, exactly, is he helping you? Chris…it's alright to be afra…' 'I've never been afraid of anything in my entire life, I'm not about to start now!' 'AND MAYBE THAT'S YOUR PROBLEM. You're so fucking fearless you don't give a shit about anyone or anything but yourself. It has to change, Chris. It has to or it'll kill you.' And the most painful thing? I knew she was right. --- Early the next morning I left her sleeping and took the trolley back to Yonkers. Alighting, I passed the medical institution that stitched me up after Eiji's inexcusable betrayal. Inexcusable? That's a laugh. I killed the man's father. He has every right to retribution. I just…didn't expect it. I thought…well, it doesn't really matter what I thought. It happened. It was inevitable. And yet I loved him. I still love him. And I think he still loves me. He just needs to find it in his heart to accept that what happened to Yuki was an accident bourn out of necessity. I'd spent most of my adult life rationalising my actions in terms of their effect on me and those around me. If something I did harmed someone, I'd write them off as 'collateral damage,' 'necessary casualty' or similar. And yes, it worked. It helped me sleep at night. But now I had more to consider. Something had re-entered my life that had been absent for far too long – love. Affection. The warmth and presence of another human being. I didn't think it possible, and yet it has happened. I'd not known this girl for a month and yet I already knew that I loved her. It seemed…impossible. Completely ridiculous. I’d gone for years without the love of another person and now I knew I could never live without it – without her – again. I'd do anything for her, anything just to make sure she was safe. Anything. I turned east off of Main Street onto my block. It hadn't been a month since this quiet suburban lane had been shattered by an act of vicious violence. As I neared my home, the memories came flooding back. The steel-toed shoes digging holes in my chest and back. The baseball bat splitting open my skull. The bitter irony of the barbed wire crown forced upon my head like Christ's coronet of thorns. I bit my lip as I tore down the 'CRIME SCENE – DO NOT ENTER' tape and pushed open my front door. --- Alexi We're dead. Pact's finally lost it. I don't care what you think, Keitaro, he's gone off the sodding rails this time. He's shacked up with some $10 piece of gutter trash. Yeah, I know every man needs to take care of business once in a while but Kei…he thinks he loves her. Why would I joke about something like that? No, no, ask yourself – why would I joke about something like that? Look, the bottom line is this screws everything up. Everything. So you'll sort it out? You'll do what has to be done? Oh yeah, I'm over the moon with freakin' gratefulness. G'bye. --- Pact My blood still adorned the floors of the porch and hall, bits of my skin and flesh adding macabre flecks of colour to the minimalist white walls. This is the place I once called home. No longer. How am I meant to stay here now? After all this? I wandered upstairs to my room and, kicking off my shoes, lay down on the bed. I allowed my eyes to play over the dresser, filled as it was with memories. My commemorative LCW Championship belt, my old service revolver, my medal of honour for valour in combat, the decades-old photograph of my sister that still made me cry when the mood took me. Fran…I miss you. I miss you so much. I wish I could bring you back. I wish I could… The tears ran down my cheeks, their saltiness bringing redemption for past sins and acceptance of my fate. Kitsune doesn't want me to wrestle to make him money. He wants me dead. He wants me…dead. The coldness of the realisation swept through me. I allowed it to run its course. If it's true…then such is my fate. But I am damned if I'm going to remain his pawn for any longer than absolutely necessary. With that, I picked up Francesca's picture and headed for the door. --- 'Chris, have you been smoking again?' 'Ahh, you caught me,' I said. 'Sorry, hon – when you get to my age you get kinda set in your ways.' 'Ahh, I guess I'll get used to it. Everyone is so health-conscious these days!' 'Tell me about it!' I looked her up and down, smiling. 'I love you, you know? I mean, really love you.' She smiled back. 'I know. I know.' She headed for the shower and I turned on the TV, flicking through the local stations. Try as I might, I couldn't ignore the top news story on Channel 6 – 'Blaze in Eastern Yonkers Kills Mother, Son.' I set Franny's picture on the nightstand. It's you and me now, babe. Just you and me…
