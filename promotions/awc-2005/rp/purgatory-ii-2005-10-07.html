Purgatory II

7 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/10/05 My wife Laura, and son Rua…. Ever since I had realised what tragedy had taken place before me, I was gone. The unholy, unchallenged, brutal, destruction of the one thing in my life that mattered to me – my family. But there was nothing I could for them anymore and this pained, this hurt deep down. So I retreated inside and outside. To Siberia, the most lonely place I could think of…and my soul, where I would endure the pain of this realisation for the rest of my days. Nothing would stop me from feeling this way, from leaving myself to my fate. I would rest eternally in exile, like the broken man I deserved to be. Nothing held satisfaction or desire anymore. Nothing… Instead I would rot, I would decay in Siberia, let the cruel winters and harsh environments take me, mould me into what I wanted to be – nothing. As the long nights would creep in around me, despair and punishment were my friends, hope and light enemies. I wished for no retribution, no saving grace. The world was dead to me now and so it always should be. This I felt was just, this was my punishment for my crime. To live evermore in purgatory and contemplate the reality of my actions. So it came as a surprise that after two years…someone found me. Someone who knew who I was. *** It was a dark evening as they all were, however something about the night air changed the mood. It was biting cold but in it there was a cool and calm caress, tempting and relaxing me, urging me to let go and be free. It danced and dashed around me all at once and I felt exhilaration for the first time for so long. Immediately and pain and despair came upon me and rebuked such evil thoughts for me, turning me back to my sentence. Staring into the black abyss outside my bunker hut, it all seemed to be trivial. I was one man amidst the true power of nature and god. I was simply insignificant and miniscule in the grand scheme of things. But for today, somebody felt I mattered. I entered the bunker and looked around. As befitting my self-imposed exile, I refused to furnish the homestead (if you could call it that) with anything bar essentials. Apparently the bunker had been an old ammo dump of some kind and I kept the feel, dank, grey and rusty, just like the old Soviet weaponry. A simple wooden table and chair sat in the centre, a kitchen counter hastily installed when I got here so that I could do some simple cookery. Both grey and dull, how the table and chair were grey I couldn’t figure out because no paint stained the innocent raw material. Perhaps the stain was lying deeper beneath the surface, just like mine. Off to one corner were the living quarters, another box room with one window slit and a steel bed, no bed linen or extravagant apparel. Just a steel bed. Beside this was the toilet and that was in a nutshell my entire home. It fitted my mood. As I looked around and headed over to the kitchen a dull thud clanged against the reinforced steel door. I guess I’d picked the bunker because of the door. Strong, robust, gaudy and impressive, it was built to keep people out of the bunker, to keep people away from what was locked inside. But this knocking was new. Nobody from the nearby village cared to come visiting, they knew I wanted none. In fact, word had spread throughout the countryside of my intention to be apart from society and other than when I visited to pick up supplies or on occasion ran across people while I was hunting for furs and other trinkets that I could trade for supplies, I was non-existent to the people. Almost a myth. So who was knocking on my chamber door this evening? I peeled back the slit to look out and investigate, conscious that regardless of whether or not the person could see me, I would inevitably have to open the door to get rid of them, one way or another. Resigned to my fate I peered out. And was surprised by whom I saw. It was an old man, balding on top with a bit of red hair pluming out of the sides of his head, almost like a grove of herbs. Thick goggles clouded the eyes and prevented me from seeing whose eyes laid beneath. The man danced on the spot, trying to keep warm as he obviously hadn’t been fully prepared. The guy was wearing a simple business suit after all and not much else. It seems he had been dropped in the middle of nowhere just on the spot for a distinct purpose. And I knew exactly why. The man standing in front of me, waiting for me to answer, to reveal myself and talk, to listen and judge what was to be done was an old associate. But not a mafia associate, no. He was a much simpler man than that, which made his arrival all the more puzzling. He was my old wrestling trainer from New York. Dan O’Grady, the firebrand of the Bronx. Nothing got in his way in his lifetime and nothing swayed him to do this or that, he made his own decisions. But I knew deep in my heart that if he was out here something was wrong with him and something very wrong at that. I had to find out…
