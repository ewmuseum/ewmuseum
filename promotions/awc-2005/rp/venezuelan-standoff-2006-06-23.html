Venezuelan Standoff

23 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/23/06 Amber lifted her eyes from where they had been buried in the crook of her arm, looking around her as if at something unfamiliar. She sat atop the bed she had shared with Anton so often, in the room that she had come to call home. Dressed in blue jeans and a white babydoll t-shirt, she didn't seem like someone who should be as relentlessly depressed as she was. Yet, all it took was a look at the black jungle that hung over her shoulders and the rings of sleepnessness and sorrow that circled her eyes to see that she was a troubled woman. More than troubled. She managed to push her legs forward out of the huddled position that she'd held for what seemed like days, letting them dangle over the edge of the bed before dropping down onto the floor and pushing herself up. She was torn up inside, more torn than she had ever been before. She had just found someone that she really liked after being in one bad relationship after another, someone who she could really get along with. Someone who seemed to genuinely like her; she wasn't going to assume that anyone loved her, no matter how much he told her. She was getting settled, she had thought it might even lead to something great. And then… and then she had to fuck it all up. She afforded herself a defeated little whimper before shuffling forward out of the room. What had she been thinking? With Luis? The man knew what he was doing, she had to give him that much, but he was revolting all the same. Anton was right, completely. Why should she expect him to trust her? Even so, she desperately wanted him to, needed him to. Anton didn't understand about swingers, about one night stands, about not calling the other in the morning and not caring either way. The American way, was it? She supposed it was. Amber wiped her eyes off and pushed herself away from the wall; she had collapsed into tears again without her even realizing it. The whole week had been murder for her psyche. Anton had refused to talk to her beyond what was necessary, and even that was with a scowl. Mostly, he sent Nneka to tell her things. He had distanced himself from her, made her feel worthless. She knew that he thought as much of her, but it didn't make it hurt less. What she needed was a release. No, not a release. An escape. She took a deep breath to steady herself. How much money did she have? Just marijuana wouldn't do, not for this; pot was for recreation, not for fleeing reality in the way that she wanted. She glanced over at Luis's door. The man was a rat, a creep, a slimy motherfucker; he was also invariably in possession of high-grade cocaine and conveniently not around. None of them were, not now. They had all left Amber the pariah alone in the house. She straightened up and collected her nerves before marching resolutely towards Luis's door. Pushing the door open, she slipped into the dark room and quickly found the light switch. She closed her eyes as the white light filled the room and felt her way across the wall for a second or two before she opened her eyes; her fingertips graced the white-painted dresser. Hands trembling, she pulled open the first cabinet. Clothes. She scowled and rummaged through it. Her fingers hit the bottom quickly and she threw her head back, biting her lower lip hard. She let out a sharp sigh and pushed the drawer closed, reaching down for the next. She went through this one rather quickly, finding more of the same; her upper lip quivered with anger as she noticed that these were primarily Nneka's clothes. She shoved this one shut and then pulled out the next. More clothes! Goddammit, where did this man keep his… stash. Her fingers found the little bag, filled almost to bursting with white powder. She licked her lips and lifted it away from its nest of fabric, setting it atop the dresser. She had found her jewel, but where there was one, there were bound to be more. She went into the last drawer, having to crouch to reach that low. Her fingers deftly pushed away white underwear until she hit something hard. Metallic. She blinked and grasped it, pulling it to the light and being surprised to find herself holding a handgun. She wondered fleetingly why Luis had never brought the thing along, but soon enough she found herself entranced by the thing. It was sleek, attractive even, as if someone wanted it to be bought. To be used. She closed her eyes, trying to block out the image, but it was red behind her vision. It had to be fate. It had to be. What was she doing with her life? She had been a non-stop parade of shit her entire life. Get beaten up or left by some abusive guy, then run to drugs when it was all over. And here she was again, about to drown away her sorrows in cocaine. What could this solve? It never solved anything. It just made her feel a tad bit better, enough so that she could seek out someone new to tell her that she was worthless. She was a burden, wasn't she? A burden on everybody, on society. She was inescapable, and yet, she could escape. Luis's white narcotic wasn't going to help her or anybody. Not this time. What she had in her hand now, what she feared to hold but yet needed to keep, was the only way she would escape this cycle early. It was the only way she could find herself without anymore sorrow, without any more pain. Without feeling worthless again. Tears streamed down her cheeks as she turned towards the door of Luis's room. Amber had never thought it would end this early, but she supposed that nobody ever did. In a daze, she wrote a hasty letter to Anton and folded it up. She turned and started towards the living room, but a fit of weeping assailed her before she reached the couches and she sank down to the floor next to the wall. So this was it. It was all over. She managed to bring the pistol up and slip it between her lips. She squeezed her eyes shit. Here it came. The ultimate release. Bang. Anton laughed as he took the stairs three at a time, glancing back down at his yellow-clad sister. The Nigerian man, dressed in a loose gray t-shirt with the words CRUSH! KILL! DESTROY! emblazoned in cracked, red letters across the front and black shorts, was easily beating Nneka up to the room. She was trying hard, he couldn't deny that, but she didn't have the conditioning he had yet. She wouldn't for quite a bit, at that. It was a simple pleasure, though, besting his sister athletically. He hadn't enjoyed such in a while. His steps slowed as he neared the suite. Every time he was about to enter that room, every time he thought about it, he thought about her. Amber. The cheer was swept away from his features and he began to move slowly. It had been a long while since he had confronted her, since he had distanced himself from her. He didn't put her out of the house because she had nowhere to go and he knew she would eventually take the hint and find somewhere. Somewhere was she wanted, or at least where she wasn't despised. She hadn't gone quite yet, but she had been moping; she made a good show as she tried to get Anton back, but Anton wasn't falling for it. He believed he had done a good job in wrenching his heart away from the fair-skinned temptress. It had been easy once he could focus his anger into something, once he could convince himself that she would never love him, that her contempt could run so deep. That Luis had been right. Oh, how that stung. Luis had never let him live that down. Somehow, the behavior was more acceptable out of Luis than it was out of her; he had been used to how Luis acted. Amber was out of the blue. He accepted that, but then again, he should have seen it coming. He should have. If not Luis, it would have been someone else. He shook his head in disgust as he reached the room door and felt inside his pocket for the key. He unlocked the door and pushed it open, stepping into the white-dominated home. He took a deep breath and tossed the keys towards the couch lazily, continuing through the house. He blinked as he saw someone slumped against a wall. No, he hadn't seen it. Had he… In an instant, Anton was moving. Two strides took him across the room and his arm arced forward. His cupped hand slapped at the white wrist, knocking it to the side. Bang. The gunshot ripped through the relatively-quiet air like a diver into a new pool. The woman against the wall looked startled, staring upwards with tear-streaked cheeks. Upwards at Anton. The Lion ripped the gun from her hand and flung it across the room. ''What are you doing!?'' Anton bellowed, in English so that she could understand him. ''Anton, I—'' ''You what!? What do you want to say? That you were trying to… what is this?'' Anton noticed the folded piece of paper and stooped down to pick it up. He turned it in his hands, but he didn't open it. So, she was serious, it seemed. Shaking his head, he ripped the paper into confetti. Amber watched him somberly, tears flowing silently from her eyes. ''What are you doing?'' ''Leaving,'' Amber said somberly. Anton drew his eyebrows together. The woman thoroughly vexed him. Worst of all, Anton found himself concerned for her. Concerned! He hated himself for it. ''If you are leaving, then pack your bags and go,'' Anton said, half-numb. ''The gun will not buy you taxi.'' ''Anton… don't,'' Amber said. ''Don't play dumb with me.'' She turned to her side and curled up into a fetal position. ''If you're just going to yell at me, then please save your breath. Leave me alone.'' Anton looked down at her, studying her for a long time. Here she was, beaten up. She could have done this with a little make-up, he supposed. Waited with the gun in her mouth until he was about to come, then acted as if she was going to commit suicide. The paper could have been anything. It could have been a bill, for all he knew. And immediately when Anton tried to rationalize this, he threw all the reasons out. It was a gut instinct, of course, and Anton disliked gut instincts because he was an avid patron of logic and reason. Logic and reason said that he should leave her where she was. And yet… ''Come,'' Anton said tenderly, squatting down and gathering her up in his arms. She gave a little gasp of surprise as she was lifted off the ground. He turned towards his room and started towards it. ''Anton!'' Nneka screamed at him from behind. Anton stopped and closed his eyes. He didn't want this, not now. ''Anton, what are you doing? Why are you… Anton!'' She didn't want Amber to know what she was saying, so she spoke Igbo. At least, that's how Anton reasoned out her language choice. ''What is it?'' Anton responded lamely. ''Anton, no,'' Nneka said, moving to his side. Her eyes were like shimmering white crystals as she regarded her brother. ''No! She will hurt you again. You cannot… you cannot let her back in as if she has done nothing wrong!'' Anton sighed. ''Help everyone in need,'' Anton said deliberately. ''That is what Papa always said. Look at her, Nneka. She is weak. She is helpless. She needs me.'' ''But you can't!'' Nneka argued. ''She is a bitch, the worst whore, the… Anton!'' He was moving already and he kicked the door of his room shut behind him. Nneka's screams came muffled through the door as he laid Amber down, kissed her forehead, and bidded her to sleep; they haunted him for hours afterwards. Anton sat up, his chair next to the bed and his eyes on the sleeping woman. She slept as if she was ill or wounded, trying to replenish strength. Dimly, Anton wondered if the kind of wound she had undoubtedly suffered could hurt her as much as a physical one. He ground his teeth together and exhaled sharply through his nose. ''She's gonna kill you, man,'' a Spanish-speaking voice came from behind him. ''I know,'' Anton said. Luis squatted down beside him. His eyes were locked on Amber as well. ''She's pretty,'' Luis admitted. ''But that doesn't mean you should sell yourself out for her.'' ''I know,'' Anton repeated. Luis glanced up at him and shook his head. ''You're a lost cause,'' Luis said with a little smirk. ''Got a big ol' rock for a brain.'' ''Shut up, Luis,'' Anton growled. Luis chuckled. ''I'm not sorry for fucking her,'' Luis told him flatly. ''And I didn't come here to apologize for anything. But you had a chance, man. You had a chance to let her go. You had a chance to turn her out onto the street. And you…'' Luis let his voice fade into a light breath. ''What did you want me to do?'' Anton asked him, looking at his manager for the first time. ''Did you want me to let her die, right in the house?'' ''You know that's not what I meant. I just mean, man, she's… she's gonna fuckin' kill you. I don't know how else to say it.'' ''I know,'' Anton said, shutting his eyes. She'd kill him like a cancer that ended with a gunshot; slow, debilitating pain for years and years and then finally, a sudden death. And how else could he take it but like a terminal patient? He couldn't do anything about it. He had to accept this as his fate. He'd never let Amber go, not as he needed to. ''I can have her on the next train out of town,'' Luis said confidentially. ''Think about it, will you?'' ''She's mine.'' Luis stood up and shoved his hands into his pockets. ''I'm gonna kill her before she kills you, Anton,'' Luis said firmly. Anton looked back up at Luis, setting his jaw. ''Then one or all of us is going to die,'' Anton said. Luis frowned and turned, silently walking out of the room and closing the door behind him. Anton sighed heavily and stood up himself. Amber shifted in her sleep, tossing her head to the left. A smile crept unbidden onto his face as he watched her admiringly. She needed him and he would be there. He would always be there.
