Never break the chain

21 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8.21.06 “What do you mean, you’re not going to help?!” Johnny lowered his clasped hands from his brow, allowing him to look an incredulous Darcy Markson in the eye. He stared at Darcy for a few moments, incredulous himself that his decision warranted an explanation. “¿No comprénde the English?” Johnny said, in a laughable attempt to express his sentiments with a foreign tongue. “I can try the española if you like, but as you can probably tell I’m not very good.” How can he do this to me? Darcy wondered. Johnny “Hawk” Harrelson had to be the most reckless bastard he’d ever met. It was practically a foregone conclusion in Darcy’s mind that Johnny would help him rescue Jane from her terrible predicament. Now, he found himself gravely mistaken, and his plans sinking fast. “No…you’re right,” Darcy said with a sigh, his voice weighed down by the prospects of concession. “I don’t need an explanation.” “Good. I’m glad you see it-” “We can skip the discussion and you can just put my balls directly in the vice,” Darcy finished, catching his long-time friend off guard. “What the fuck, man? How am I supposed to pull this off without any backup?” “Easy,” Johnny answered, rising from his seat across from Darcy in his newly refurbished kitchen. “Step one, stop acting like the kind of dumbass who doesn’t realize he’s going to get himself killed if he doesn’t slow down and think this through. Step two, call the police, and let them handle this shit. Why you haven’t figured that out already is beyond me.” “I already told you,” Darcy retorted sharply, his blood beginning to boil. “If word gets out about this-” “Yeah yeah yeah,” Johnny interceded. “Poor little Janey’s political dreams go down in flames. But you know what? She’ll be ALIVE. And every minute you stick to this terrible, terrible plan of yours, the worse the odds get that anybody will see her ever again. And if you ask me, that’ll put an even bigger damper on her career.” Darcy’s jaw gaped wide enough to allow an 18-wheeler through, cargo hold and all. He couldn’t believe the words coming out of the mouth of his best friend. He’d known Johnny for nearly 20 years, and aside from the time Johnny intervened to stop Darcy from pissing on a “WELCOME TO TEXAS!” road sign in a drunken stupor while in full view of a squad car on their senior road trip, this was the first Darcy could remember Johnny offering the voice of reason. But reason be damned, Darcy thought. Everything was at stake here, far too much to let something as silly as common sense get in the way. “If this were five years ago, I might have agreed with you,” Darcy replied softly, referring to just before his horrific back injury sent his life on a downward spiral. “But you have no idea what it’s like to have everything you live for stripped away. Jane made it clear that the police can’t get involved, no matter what happens. That’s why I agreed to help her in the first place.” “That’s something ELSE I don’t understand, D!” Johnny answered, as he moved towards the refrigerator. “I thought you hated Jane.” “I don’t hate Jane…” Darcy said, sounding exacerbated at Johnny’s claim. “Yet you’ve told me on several occasions how it wouldn’t bother you one bit if the cosmos aligned to have a big asteroid drop in from outer space directly on her skull,” Johnny answered. “Even that notwithstanding, it still doesn’t explain why you’re suddenly so willing to put your ass on the line just so Jane can keep making copies for some congressman.” “Because I understand what’s at stake here! You would too, if you had any dreams of your own!” Darcy shouted. He hadn’t expected that he would have to explain his reasoning to Johnny, and there was no ignoring his point. Just a week earlier he would never so much as entertained the notion of risking his life to save the girl that cleaved his heart in two. But somehow things had changed dramatically. He now felt obligated to stand up for his former love, for reasons even he had difficulty putting a finger on. He attempted to grasp the cosmic significance of it all as the last few days had passed, thinking back to his own experience in this sort of situation. Sure, Darcy survived his stories-high plummet from atop the Triple Cage five years earlier…but at what cost? He was physically still alive, but the injuries he suffered led to his entire life’s ambitions stripped away, snatched from him as if someone had ruthlessly removed his spinal column. By acknowledging Jane’s insistence that they undertake the risk of keeping the police out of the matter, Darcy understood that Jane’s desire to make a difference in the political arena surely must have rivaled the passion he had competing in the squared circle. If Darcy was only able to rescue Jane from her kidnappers by sacrificing her life’s dream…it would hardly count as a rescue at all. But why me? What makes this my responsibility? This too was something Darcy wrestled with, able to come up with a conclusive answer neither for himself, nor for Johnny here and now. In truth, the overwhelming factors at hand rarely took the time to properly explain themselves, and this case was no exception. Darcy’s biggest influences here and now were his gut instinct and his conscience, both siding vehemently in favor of his search-and-rescue mission, no matter what the cost. Even if he stood to alienate the loved ones around him who couldn’t see why it was so important…even if he stood to lose his life in the process. By now Johnny had returned to his seat at the table, taking a swig from the water bottle he’d fetched from the fridge. His eyes narrowed at Darcy’s words, and he briefly contemplated ejecting the mouthful of water into Darcy’s face before electing to take the high road and swallow it instead. “I’m going to pretend you’re not a complete asshole for that needless cheap shot and try to get the situation through your thick-ass skull.” Johnny said, putting the water bottle to rest on the table and proceeded to dial down his fingers. “First of all,” he began, bringing his left index finger down to meet its right-handed counterpart,” you have NO. EARTHLY. IDEA where Jane is. None. You’ve got nothing. She could be anywhere. She could already be dead, for all we know.” “I highly doubt it,” Darcy swiftly replied. “They left a ransom note. They want money, not blood.” “Alright,” Johnny conceded. “But that brings us to point two,” he continued, moving on to his middle finger to emphasize his argument. “Your only lead is an address in the shadiest part of Oakland, supposedly the last known residence of the guy who handled the ransom note. How do you know you can even find him there? And what makes you think he’ll even know where Jane is? From what you told me, his rap sheet makes him sound like an entry-level errand boy. I doubt he can tell you anything worth a damn. Didn’t they give you a place to deliver the ransom? Would it be tremendously more worthwhile to show up there, instead of chasing some kid?” Darcy shook his head. “He handled the ransom note, Johnny. He might not be high up on the ladder, but he was in on the kidnapping. I’d bet every nickel I have that he’s seen Jane more recently than I have, which is good enough for me. And speaking of nickels, I haven’t got any, which is why I’m in this fucking mess in the first place. Unless you know where I can find 300 Gs lying around, this is all I’ve go to go on. I’d be an even bigger fool than you think I am now if I went near the drop spot without the money.” “You’re a fool either way! And you know why? Point three,” Johnny shouted at his friend, moving the count down to his digit housing his wedding band. “They’re going to see you coming a mile away, Darcy. Don’t you get it? Why do you think you even got to that ransom note before anybody else?” Darcy rolled his eyes. He had the ransom note, that was all that mattered. “What do you mean?” he queried. “I mean, the kidnappers broke in through the back window, making one hell of a mess. They must have known somebody would see what happened and the police would be involved. Do you really think it was merely CHANCE that you got to the ransom note before anybody else? They left that note for you, and they knew you’d picked it up because they knew you were coming, god dammit!” At that, Darcy froze. Until now, he had considered all the leads he’d been granted as to Jane’s whereabouts to be because of the stupidity of the henchmen involved. The broken glass, the ransom notes ripe with fingerprints, the permanent listed address of a man guaranteed to be involved in the kidnapping…Darcy had believed these all to be working in his favor. “They might not know who you are,” Johnny continued, a little less animated than before. “But they know somebody was supposed to meet Jane at that hotel. They know somebody has their ransom note, and they know the police aren’t involved. Believe me, they’re just itching for that somebody to try something stupid, Darcy. And if you play into their hands, you’re going to make this situation worse. What’s your big plan, even if you somehow figure out where the hell Jane is? Bust in guns-a-blazing, lift her over one shoulder and bust out without digging yourself an early grave? This isn’t a John Wayne movie D, this is a suicide mission, whether there’s two of us or just one.” Darcy pondered these sentiments for several moments, clasping his hands and bringing them to rest against his lips. He stared into the eyes of his friend, who in staring back could clearly see the difficulty of the predicament practically pouring from his expression. But after several moments, Darcy merely shook his head and rose from his kitchen-side seat, looking at Johnny with disdain. “Enough of this crap,” Darcy viciously eschewed. “We can sit here all day debating whether this a smart choice, or a stupid choice, or whatever judgment you feel like throwing my way. What you fail to understand is that this is the only choice.” He pulled on the loosely-fitting grey hoodie he’d placed earlier on the chair, and rested both hands on the top of the space it had occupied. “I can’t give them the money because I don’t have it. I can’t go to the police, no matter how much you think I should, because it will only fuck things up. You and I are the only ones who know Jane is missing, the only ones who CAN know, and if you won’t fucking help me, then I’m doing this solo. Thanks a hell of a lot for making this infinitely more dangerous, but it has to be done.” With that, Darcy slammed the chair into the table, more to catch Johnny’s attention rather than do any real damage to the furniture. He pulled the hood over the top of his head and stormed for the front door, expecting Johnny to argue back as he did. But with his hand already grasping the door’s sizeable brass latch and Johnny remaining mum, Darcy looked over his shoulder at his friend once more. “Nothing left to say? Aren’t you at least going to try and stop me?” Johnny let out a deep sigh in response before he gave an answer. “I’m not going to try and stop you because I know that YOU know I’m right. And I know you’re smarter than this, and I know you’re going to get about halfway there before what I said to you sinks in. I’m sorry if you’re pissed at me for this, but I don’t want to see you get killed over this. And I’m not going to help you do it.” Darcy nodded, but without another word he pulled down the latch and exited Johnny’s home. As the door slammed, the two unwittingly shared the same exact thought. He should really know me better than that. *** Darcy brought his car to rest just outside of a brightly-lit dance club on the outskirts of San Francisco. From there, he looked on as his target exited his own vehicle and headed for the club’s entrance. He did take some of what Johnny told him under consideration. It seemed to add up just a little too much in the wrong way for Darcy’s liking, and the last thing he was looking for was to walk directly into a trap when the situation called for the highest precision and precaution. He also considered that Johnny was probably right about the fruitless effort that would result from accosting the low-level errand boy that Darcy had singled out to be involved in the kidnapping. It was highly unlikely that Jane was being kept there, and it seemed even less likely that he’d find out where she was should he simply walk up and ring the man’s doorbell. Darcy did elect to go to the henchman’s house, but instead elected to stake it out, waiting to see if his suspect would unknowingly lead him to a location a bit more likely to house the damsel in distress. After a good hour and a half of waiting, Darcy’s patience was rewarded as he saw a shadowy figure exit the run-down dwelling, entering an old ’95 Chevy Lumina and taking it westbound. The club at which the two cars came to rest was only a handful of miles away from the house that Darcy had been watching, but the infamous California traffic, especially that across the San Francisco-Oakland Bay Bridge caused nearly another hour to pass. However, what Darcy lost in time he made up for in assured camouflage, as the sheer number of cars making their trek across the bridge along with him kept the man being tailed from knowing that his movements were being watched. It was well after midnight when Darcy and his “guide” arrived at the dance club. The words “THE CHAIN!” flashed brightly in hulking letters above the door’s entrance, as actual bike chain adorned the surrounding walls and entrance, even serving in place of the normal velvet rope used to keep patrons in order. The club looked like an S&M fetish hole, but somehow the club had managed to attract a plethora of respectably-dressed club-goers, patiently waiting to pass the mammoth bouncer and enter the dance arena. But one man stood out among the crowd as not being nearly as respectably dressed – the man that unwittingly led Darcy here, who now advanced toward the entrance of the club. Sporting a hoodie of his own that glowed a deep crimson under the club’s fluorescent marquee, he paid no heed to the growing line of prospective patrons and exchanged a knowing handshake with the bouncer. Darcy was unable to get a good look at him until he appeared in the light, but on the instant he did a light bulb went off in his head. He flashed back to several days earlier, when he first discovered that Jane had been kidnapped. A bearded man with gauged ear piercings, evidently looking to enter the stairwell that Darcy had just exited, looked Darcy square in the eye as they crossed paths. Darcy couldn’t make out what it was exactly, but he had some sort of multi-colored tattoo running down his neck, and presumably further beneath his red hooded sweatshirt. The two locked eyes for so long that Darcy opened his mouth to offer a greeting, but just before the sound could escape his lips the man turned away. The man now standing only a few yards from Darcy bared the same visage, as well as identical gauged piercings and the body art creeping down his neck. After the handshake, the bouncer granted the man access to the club’s interior. This is it! The break I was looking for! Darcy thought to himself, almost in a congratulatory tone of inner voice. The very man he’d seen ominously leaving the scene of the crime was the very same man who had laid hands on Jane’s ransom note, and had now just entered a seemingly-exclusive club that, judging by his apparel alone, he had no business entering. It all added up…Jane had to be inside. Darcy restarted the engine of his hybrid sport utility vehicle, moving it from the fire lane running alongside the front entrance where the music could be heard blaring even from outside the club. After a few seconds he pulled into an adjacent street, where he was fortunate enough to chance upon a space just large enough for his car. It came to rest underneath a street light that was curiously unwilling to offer its twilight glow, and so Darcy weighed his options in both silence and darkness. An early look suggested that the front entrance was the only way in, which was definitely a no-go. Even if he got inside the club, it seemed highly doubtful that he’d find Jane tied up in the middle of the dance floor, making the entrance of little use to him. In addition, the small bit of fame that came with the territory of suplexing guys in spandex night in and night out on live television would ensure that more eyes would be on him than he particularly cared for, especially given the circumstances. He would have to find another way in, and he’d have to go about it quietly. Exiting via the driver-side door, Darcy kept his eyes peeled for any Snooping Sallies as he made his way back toward the club. He kept his distance from the entrance and the entering patrons, wondering if any of them even had the slightest notion that there was a hostage somewhere in their midst. Using his peripheral vision as his chief guide, Darcy maneuvered his way to the side of the building, never once lifting his eyes from the growing crowd that could potentially take notice of an interloper sneaking behind the premises. In his time spent in the Brazilian jungle, Darcy had learned how to move with such stealth and precision in stalking his dinner without either the prey taking notice, or even the smallest insect living among the wildlife. As it happened this lesson adapted quite well to sneaking around in the city, and silently Darcy thanked the natives who allowed him to harness the skill. After a few long minutes, Darcy had guided himself to the club’s rear. It was impossible to say for sure whether any eyes had lied upon him in the process, but he was able to take comfort in knowing that if anyone had, they had at least not advanced upon him yet. The rear of the building was far less impressive than the club’s front – no shiny entrance lights, no chain decorations, and most importantly…no security. From the look of it the backside of the building resembled far more that of a back alley rather than the back of a dance club, as the glow of the street lamps bathed the back wall in an orange lather, with all the warmth that only a desolate downtown can provide. But fortunately for Darcy, the club’s rear shared one very crucial characteristic with its forward partner – an entranceway. Obviously not intended to attract club goers, the door stood small against the wall, painted an unimpressive maroon-brown as if to give the feel that it was retreating into the wall. A few concrete steps led up to the door from the side, with the largest slab guarded by a two rows of black guardrails protruding from beneath the cement. Figuring the coast to be clear, Darcy finally reached into the sleeve of the hoodie he had tied around his waist and removed what he hoped to be the equalizer for this operation…a small, Walther P38 nine millimeter. The handgun was an antique that belonged to his father, bequeathed to the elder Markson by an uncle who had served in the second World War, and captured the pistol from a fallen Nazi. The semi-automatic was over half a century old, but as a child Darcy’s father had laid waste to more than his fair share of aluminum cans to display its perfect working order. Nearly a decade had passed since then, but the pistol still looked to be ready for duty. Okay…what now? Darcy pondered, as he came to a crouch with his back against the concrete steps. Johnny was right yet again – while Darcy might have a firearm of his own, it was highly likely that the kidnappers inside were packing their own heat as well. Kicking the door down and shooting everyone in sight a la the Duke was certainly an awful plan, and he dismissed the notion rather quickly. But a better idea was proving much harder to come by. Darcy gripped the cold metal of the trigger handle as he struggled to think of a way in. The best strategy he could come up with was full of holes; a knock on the door would attract someone’s attention, who would likely exit the door to investigate. Darcy prayed that it would be only one curious mobster, or else the plan was shot to hell right there, as he saw it unlikely that he could deal with being outnumbered. Darcy hadn’t so much as handled a gun since he was 10 years old, and wasn’t even sure if he could summon the courage to end one man’s life if it came down to it, let alone two…especially if they both had guns of their own to end his. But if it was just one man, he had a chance. A few simple wrestling maneuvers could take the goon out of commission, where Darcy could pump him for information. It was a longshot, and it was downright stupid, but there was no time left to hesitate. After all, it was his only choice. Here goes nothing… Darcy brought his thumb down on the pistol’s hammer, with the familiar click of the hammer revealing that the Walther was just as ready as he was. Darcy moved ninety degrees about the concrete stairs to bring his back against the building, the door now just to his left. But as Darcy reached to rap his fist against said door, something came into play that he hadn’t quite counted on. It had not occurred to him in the slightest that not only was he being watched, but he’d been watched for quite some time by the advancing shadowy figure ever since he departed from Johnny’s. And only a fraction of a second before Darcy’s fist made contact with the cold hard steel, the figure made its move, appearing from seemingly out of nowhere to pin Darcy against the club’s back wall. He was so stunned that the Walther slipped out of his hand, spilling downward and coming to rest at his feet. As if it were the theme for the evening, Darcy’s attacker too wore a hoodie, of the black variety with a zipper that was pulled all the way up, and some sort of patch that Darcy could not identify. With the gun out of commission, the assailant used the hand pinning Darcy’s wrist that had previously held the firearm to pull the hood covering her face, revealing a full head of red hair. Mystery solved. “Amy?! What the fuck?” She leaned down in the blink of an eye to retrieve the gun he'd dropped, and slipped it under her belt with precision that surprised Darcy as she spoke. “Jane's with me. I don't think we should hang around here for me to explain, but you don't have to do this.” “JANE?! But how?” Darcy couldn’t help but shout. From around the corner another figure peered into view. First a face, then slowly came the rest of the silhouette into view. The streetlamps were insistent in providing an orange tint to everything, but as the figure slowly approached Darcy could make out her long, blonde hair and familiar slender figure. Instantly a countless amount of questions invaded Darcy’s mind, but he silently echoed Amy’s sentiments. With the hostage evidently already rescued, there was no need for a rescue mission, and the faster they stopped hanging around dangerous criminals who were most assuredly looking for Jane, the better. Darcy nodded, prompting Amy to release her grip. Jane waved them in her direction, but the two made it all of several paces before a new voice entered the scene. “That’s far enough, ladies.” The rear door burst open, and three men clad in expensive-looking black shirts adorned with gold chains appeared with the light from the room backing them. True to Darcy’s prediction, they were indeed all packing heat, and with their sights trained on Darcy and Amy it appeared that escape would be impossible. “I have to say, I didn’t think it would be quite so easy,” said one of the men, although Darcy was unable to make out exactly which one. But I do believe thanks are in order for delivering my prize to me. A token of my esteem, if you will…” Darcy never saw the shovel coming. *** Eventually Darcy came to from his shovel-induced sleep. He found himself tied to a chair in the middle of an unfinished basement. It was impossible to say for sure, but Darcy guessed that it had to be the club’s basement. It looked remarkably unspectacular; around them nothing covered the bare, concrete floor. Nothing adorned the walls, either. They were hardly walls at all, but rather large lengths of wood packed with insulation. A lone lamp protruded from the ceiling, bathing the room in a fluorescent 60 watts. Not exactly a room one pictures in an upscale dance club - but then again, Darcy imagined that very few people came down here…and even fewer likely made it out alive. Darcy struggled to escape the bonds holding him to the small wooden chair, but it soon proved completely futile. To Darcy’s left lied Amy, bound in an identical predicament of her own, and who also was just awaking from an induced slumber. Darcy looked away from his girlfriend and over to his right, where he found his ex-girlfriend also tied to a chair, but still fast asleep. Pausing to gather strength, Darcy gave one last attempt to free himself from the chair, but the attempt failed miserably. He turned to watch Amy do the same, and as she relented in failure the two briefly locked eyes before a door behind them flung open. “Your timing was impeccable, I must admit,” came a voice through the door, a voice Darcy recognized as the one who spoke just before his lights were turned out. “We’ve been looking for Miss O’Connor here sometime, in hopes of obtaining the money that was due to us. But you managed to keep her hidden for just long enough that she became unnecessary.” The voice carried from behind Darcy to the side, and finally to his front as the man walked into view. He was very neatly dressed in a grey sport coat and pants, with a black shirt and tie peeking out from the buttoned coat. His hair, also grey, slicked back with the aid of some kind of styling gel, presumably in an effort to hide the balding that crept up from the back of his scalp. Darcy could have sworn the man was of Italian origin by the look of him, yet he spoke without a trace of the romantic tongue’s accent. The two men he brought with him, adorned with the same black shirts he recognized from before, also spoke with no accent; for that matter, they did not speak at all. “What the hell are you talking about?” Darcy answered, his voice seething with a quiet rage. Jane was kidnapped. By you assholes.” “Ah, the plot thickens!” the lead man shot back, with a smug look on his face. “Allow me to fill in the blanks for you, and maybe working together we can ascertain what really happened here. I sent my boys for little Janey here at the hotel, only to find that someone had beaten us to it. Now, normally this would be cause for concern, but since no one else really had much of a reason to be looking for her, it was only a matter of time before she turned up. So we laid out a fairly ingenious plan, if I do say so myself. Made quite the mess of things, dropped a ransom note, made it look like the whole thing went off without a hitch. You see, my boy, we knew all along you were keeping contact with your friend here. We knew you were due to meet her at the hotel, and would arrive there long before the police. And I had a very good feeling that in this…terrible excuse for a rescue plan, there was an excellent chance that you would save us the trouble of looking for Jane and lead her to us all on your own. All too easy, when it comes right down to it!” Darcy made not so much as a sound. Johnny was right – all the clues that had led him here turned out to be a carefully laid trap, and Darcy had taken the bait like a fool. But this begged the million dollar question…if Jane hadn’t been kidnapped by these guys like he’d believed, and she hadn’t been hidden away by Darcy like they believed…then where the hell had she been all this time? At that moment, Darcy suddenly remembered Amy arriving to stop him from entering the club, with Jane in tow. Suddenly it seemed like the redhead was holding the final pieces to the puzzle, a puzzle that Darcy was quite eager to solve. He turned to look at her, but before he could begin his query the man interrupted. “Actually, you know, I’ve had enough mystery solving for one day. It’s all irrelevant anyway. Jane’s dad paid up after all. I shudder to think where he came up with the money on such short notice…but the debt has been paid. All of you are of no concern, no matter how you ended up here.” “Yet here we are in your dive of a club, forced to attend your little basement party,” Amy snapped at him. “When are we breaking out the ball gags and leather masks, creep?” “The reason you’re here,” the man replied, narrowing his eyes in Amy’s direction, “is to play a little game. You’ve given me quite the headache over the past few days, wondering how I’m supposed to collect a ransom without a hostage. The ransom might be paid in full, but it would only be polite of me to return the favor that you’ve provided me. Boys, loosen my good friend’s ropes, if you would.” One of the men flanking the sharp-dressed kidnapper moved behind Darcy’s chair and unfastened the knots holding him hostage. The other kept his pistol trained on Darcy’s forehead. “Slowly,” he barked, as the man in the grey coat motioned him over to his side. Free of the ropes’ clutches, Darcy briefly considered an escape. It seemed unlikely enough that he would make it out without being shot, but even if he did Amy and Jane would still be in their clutches. Even free of his bonds, Darcy remained their prisoner. “You seemed so eager to use your little pistol here, so I figure I’ll give you the chance,” the man said, placing the Walther back in Darcy’s palm. “And congratulations are in order to you, because you will have the opportunity to survive this evening. As will one of your friends here.” Darcy looked at the small pistol lying in his hand, then back up at the beaming man who gave it to him. He gestured in the direction of the two ladies. “I call this game ‘Three enter, two leave.’ As a price for making my job harder than it needs to be, I’m going to make things slightly difficult for you. Right now you’re one of the two, and you get to pick who joins you. Either little Janey, on the left…or the bitch with the mouth, who I can only assume is your broad. She must be a little spitfire in the sack for you to put up with lip like that.” Darcy swallowed hard, as he was obviously faced with easily the most difficult choice of his life. Up until this past week, Darcy would find it be difficult to be moved in the slightest if he learned that Jane had experienced a gruesome demise. But since she’d returned to his life, causing this whole mess to begin, everything had changed. He was suddenly heavily invested in not just her survival, but her happiness as well, both of which were now in serious jeopardy. Where a 19-year-old Darcy Markson could have killed her without a second thought, Darcy knew there was no way he could be responsible for Jane’s death, especially when she was completely innocent of wrongdoing in this entire affair. But as Darcy looked over into Amy’s piercing eyes, his heart nearly broke right then and there. There was no way to prove it for sure at this juncture, but Darcy knew she had something to do with Jane’s disappearance. If the decision was to be made clearly on a standpoint of guilt, Amy would have to serve as the victim. But the sudden boulder Darcy felt in his stomach reminded him of just how much he loved the redhead, no matter how much trouble she’d put him in. If he had to shoot her, he might as well shoot himself. “You’ve got five seconds,” the man said, the smugness gone from his voice. “And you’ve got one shot, and I highly advise you not to fuck it up. If I don’t see one of these bitches drop like a rock, you’re the one who won’t be leaving.” Five seconds wasn’t enough time to ponder ramifications. Five seconds was only enough time to make a choice. And with the clock ticking, Darcy had time only to raise his arm, lock the hammer, and make the choice. The gun spoke as its contents left the barrel with lightning speed. To be continued…
