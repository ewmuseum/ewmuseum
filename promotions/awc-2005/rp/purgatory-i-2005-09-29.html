Purgatory I

29 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29th of September Snow melted against the clear grey window from which I sat monotonously staring out of. Endless plumes of snow fell from the sky like an anarchic war of attrition was being held eternally. Bleak and barren, it patterned my heart to the core where a hole, a large one, now existed. There were no words I could say to break this moment, break the enchanting disillusionment. ‘They’re gone…’ The same words escaped from my lips that had left them every day for the past year. Three hundred and seventy six days to be exact. Three hundred and seventy six days in which lament and desolation have ruled my heart like an iron curtain. And no matter how I tried, nothing would ever take them away. They held sway with regal and arrogant pomp, turning the leaden screw to halt any mutiny for freedom. And all because… I’m in New York and the thickening brown, squalid smoke envelops everything around me. The heels of my spats click irritably along the pavement, a staccato gunfire effect emanating from them. It’s pretty late and up above the moon can barely be seen, trying its hardest to shed light upon the community but always never quite making it. Time is passing quickly and in the back of my head I know that I’m late. Farrelli does not like anybody being late. Mr. Farrelli, my boss. Short, lithe and slimy as the sewers, Machiavellian instinct oozed right out of his pores. On his own he was a simple cowardly man but behind his little crime syndicate he felt invincible. He liked to refer to himself as the sword of Sicily, a namesake few were going to question when his goons were around. He felt that as a promoter of Italian interest, the namesake would do more to rally latent Italian-Americans to his cause. It was an effective and brutal device to manipulate and bend minds to his will, in reality. To be fair to him it was working pretty well at the moment. A sizable chunk of New York was now under his ‘jurisdiction’ as it were and I believed (though I wasn’t sure at the time) that he was beginning to buy out some of the NYPD. All this was circumstance and up in the air but as Farrelli said, never let a good rumour lie. So now here I was rushing down a block and turning the corner, into another street that looked just like every other street in Manhattan. Endless waves of iron and silver, grey windows with double-sided glass, the kind that lets people look out but you can’t look in. It’s amazing how people project feelings and perspectives onto objects like that but if ever I saw one, double-sided glass was human fear and protection personified. I picked up the pace now, no time to waste as it was already gone and rushing by it was all minimalist blur. Everything seemed pretentious, whether it was double-sided glass, a car showroom, people locking up their art nouveau galleries, or something else, and it was another plane of existence to me. I didn’t care about any of it; it stunk, just like the city, of corruption. So of course, Farrelli liked the place. Turning once more I entered onto the street where the meeting was taking place, a small palatial garden house, a house that Farrelli had ‘acquired’ as it were for his own business. It stuck out like a sore thumb and whenever I had the pleasure of heading into it during the daytime, people stopped and stared, wondering how I was so lucky to be initiated into mystery. Oh if I could swap with them I would… But instead I paced up to the door, placing a fist firmly on it and entering, opening up to the blooming jungle of flora and fauna. Creepers, roses, ivies, posies, even trees were to be found inside this secret garden. All of them sprawled out in the reception; an elderly man, green in all that he wore beside one solitary plain pink faded cap, was tending the garden. His back creaked as he leant out over his precious children as he called them and I gave a nod of my own cap, receiving one for my troubles and a toothy smile. That was Mr. Kaplinsky. I wonder where he is now…probably dead, just like everyone else… Passing by him I strode up the white marble steps of this faux Victorian building. Two goons stood at the top of these steps but nothing that was going to stop me. As I passed by a simple ‘you’re late,’ was intimated from them, their body language telling me all I needed to know. Farrelli was not in a good mood. Pressing on passed through a mahogany doorframe and entered into the main floor, mutterings coming from up ahead of me. I knew I was late and I knew I had to give information to him but damn how was I going to sidle up there without him noticing. By the time I reached near enough it was too late to bother thinking of anything. ‘Mr. Murphy, so good of you to join us.’ Farrelli dryly commented on me and then continued to dictate to a couple of goons at the front. It gave me a chance to survey the surroundings of the floor. It was a small gathering for once, only about seven or eight underlings present to either be admonished or impart wisdom to their boss. None of them looked familiar to me, and all of them were the same sycophantic pricks that had always been around to lap at Farreli’s feet but never go any further than that. The tired cliché of cement shoes were usually what happened to them when Farrelli tired of them. It helped to keep up the disparity between him and his goons, Farrelli called it. Each in turn wore a faded blue suit, the customary goon attire of Farreli’s distinction anyway. Faces blurred and melted into one another as I spied them but that soon faded once Farrelli called my name… ‘Jack, get over here.’ The number parted and I walked like Moses across the Red Sea. Of course it wasn’t half as long but it definitely felt like it. Eyes watching me, and now the faces began to come into full glory. As I walked to the front, people sneered, jeered, leered, mumbled, grumbled, fumbled and each was a valid depiction of all that was happening in front of me. One of them was even rubbing his hands with glee. Something was off, like they knew why I was here. ‘Have you brought the information that I asked of you?’ His hand reached out inconsequentially, almost disdainfully, like I was about to kiss it, as the old patriarchs would have expected of their servants. ‘Yes, here.’ I pulled a document out from the briefcase I had with me and handed it out to him but immediately a man to the side snapped it quickly from me. He pulled back and looked at it. ‘It’s exactly as you said boss.’ ‘I told you all didn’t I? Didn’t I?’ Every goon in the room nodded their head and agreed furiously with their boss. Now it occurred why I had never seen any of these before. They were new to the fold. Completely new. ‘Jack you’ve been working for the NYPD haven’t you? You’ve been trying to fill us with false information and corrupt the flow my operations haven’t you?’ The tone of his voice was very mellow and calm but there was a sinister, malevolent undercurrent wrapped deep below the surface. It wasn’t very easy to pick up on but having been a part of his organisation for so long you being to see the inner workings of a second rate criminal. You see Farrelli is only an underling himself. Granted a very powerful one but he’s working for somebody else who’s pretty big in the New York area overall. I was handed an opportunity by Farrelli’s boss and ever since Farrelli has treated me like dirt. Now I was beginning to see where this is heading… ‘Ah keeping quiet are we? Prefer to hold your convictions to yourself? Very well. Bring them out.’ A flick of his fingers and somewhere from the back a door opened. I began to turn but a clubbing blow to the back of my head left me unable to. I swiftly realised that I was being completely surrounded and there in the middle of the circle was Farrelli and myself. Behind me I heard a soft sobbing and then I saw a woman being dragged out in front of me. She was totally lost in what was going on, where she was and how she got there. Glazed eyes drifted over the circle but no sort of recognition crossed her mind as she did so. Her dress was torn to shreds, ripped at the seams and it looked as if she had been roughly handled. Scratches and bruises littered her body like she had been bombarded by rocks and as I looked at her it reminded me of the story of the woman who was to be stoned to death. Let he without sin cast the first stone…. There was no consideration for that line anymore. Especially not here. And worst of all, this woman was someone who knew me, who I knew, had known for ten of the most blissful years of my life. ‘So Jack? I trust you expected this to happen?’ White fear erupted in my body and I froze, unable to do anything. Absolutely nothing. ‘Well, if you’ve no words to say, I’ll tell you this; you’re free.’ Now confusion reigned over my heart and mind. ‘Don’t look so surprised Jack; your friends in high places have spared your life. However, you have failed my stipulation and they can do nothing to stop the fact that I have jurisdiction over whether your acquaintances live or die!’ It all went slow motion then as Farrelli pulled out a simple pistol and aimed it at the woman’s head. Then, her eyes glinted and she stared directly at me, finally seeing me for who I was. She said, though they seemed like a whisper, one final saying to me… ‘I love you Jack…’ Then a boom and I closed my eyes because I did not want to see what was happening before me. It was too much. A laughter resounded around me and I kept my eyes shut. I heard the click of spats and someone put their hand on my shoulder. ‘So you don’t want to watch? Well I suppose we better take you away before you see the second killing.’ I opened my eyes suddenly and looked frantically, hoping beyond hope that it wasn’t who I thought it was. But it was. ‘RUA!!’ I called out his name but then darkness as literally everyone clubbed me to the ground and kept it that way. Darkness became my friend for the next couple of days… When I woke up in the hospital they told me I was found by the road four days previously and they questioned what had happened. I answered as best I could, making up some sort of ridiculous story to cloak the real reasons. For all the injustice that took place, I knew it was not the way to go about things by blabbing it all to someone who couldn’t do anything and it would only get me killed. After a few more days I was released and sent on my way after only a mild dislocation and a sprain. I knew what had happened; I knew that I couldn’t stay. All these thoughts ran quickly and I didn’t even pause for considerable deliberation as I was used to. I figured I had the rest of my life to play with those thoughts. Packing my bags and selling off the apartment only took about two weeks; everyone it seemed needed an apartment. Buying my ticket I left all within the space of a month. Next stop; Siberia where I could contemplate the deaths of those two people for the rest of my life… My wife Laura, and son Rua.
