Missing Home while at Home

17 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17th August 2005 Mickey arrived home in the early morning after the show in Dublin, and to say his visit was unexpected in an understatement. As he pushed the front door open on the cottage he grew up in, he was welcomed by his father carrying a shovel. Fortunately the AWC wrestler was not injured as he managed to dodge the attack and then notify his father that he was his spawn. Moments later and the elated welcome home Michael had expected, was delivered by his mother and father. Waking up early the next morning, Mickey was happy to be put to work by his father. The money he was sending home to help with the running costs of the farm had helped his parents to their heads above water but nothing more. They couldn’t afford to hire a labourer, which meant that Mickey’s father was left much of the work to do. So his return had been timely. He was able to get up and milk the cattle, allowing his father a well-deserved lie in. As Mickey crossed the courtyard of their Leitrim farm, he was surveying his surroundings but in a very different manner to the last time he had walked towards the cattle shed. As he walked past the tool shed that lay open, he looked in and saw the shovel, the troll, the hay fork, the hay cutter, the hammer, the saw and a couple of spare tractor tyres. But instead of his usual thoughts, that would entail deciding which of the apparatus’ he would need to use in his day, The Farmer had very different ideas about each of those tools. In fact, you could say that he now say them as weapons. Stopping to look in the door, Mickey felt strange in himself. He was a farmer turned into a wrestler. A man that had joined the AWC ranks for the money, someone that had used his friendship with Alistair Seamus Sheridan, or ASS, the failed AWC wrestler to get himself a job. Mickey Fitz had always told himself that he was only becoming a wrestler to set himself up comfortable and then he would return to the family home. But as he stood there, on the threshold of the shed door, surveying the tools his only thoughts were the Giant Haystack Invitational competitors and the amount of pain the “weapons” lying innocently in front of him would cause. And then it happened. If The Farmer had of been watching himself he would been totally shocked as his next action. The Farmer smiled. Yeah, I know everybody smiles. But Michael Fitzpatrick-McCarthy smiled for a reason that he himself thought not possible. He was smiling about the match he was soon to compete in. He was excited about competing in the Relentless Championship match. The Farmer was excited about wrestling. Maybe wrestling was no longer. Wrestling was slowly becoming his life. Of course, Mickey hadn’t realised this transition in himself. He was still caught up in the hardcore madness of his tool shed. He was still standing in the doorway. He was still smiling. The sound of a cow in the cattle shed drew Mickey from his twisted daydream, but he thought nothing of it. Looking up The Farmer walked off towards the milking shed. Over the next couple of days, Mickey’s thoughts around the farm became less and less farm orientated and more and more wrestling orientated. Everything was being considered for its uses in the farmyard match. He had seen pictures and videos of the layout of the area and it was a typical farm, in fact it was common knowledge that AWC had paid a local London farmer to be allowed to tear apart his farmyard. Everyone involved was sure the costs were more than covered. Mickey was seeing the gates as weapons. He had pictured himself crushing James von Doom’s head in the gates. The spades and shovels were obvious to him as weapons. The haystacks that his father kept in the main barn were like toys to Mickey. He spent hours in there, lifting and tossing the beasts. He was comfortable carrying haystacks. While on his father’s quad bike going up and down the hill, he had perfected one handed steering with the other being used to hit his Harvest clothesline on the scarecrow or in The Farmer’s mind, Steven Xandrous. His wrestling orientated behaviour continued throughout his days with his father. As they stood searing sheep, Mickey felt himself being tempted to throw his father over his shoulder and power him to the ground with a spine buster. As Mickey tried to herd some of the sheep back into their pen, he watched as one tried to run away and through his head he wanted to just spear the bastard to the ground to teach it a lesson. One of the mornings, Mickey had been woken by the sheep screaming and appeared to see a couple of wild dogs terrorising his animals. The normal reaction for the Fitzpatrick-McCarthy’s is to shoot the wild dogs before they can do no harm. But on this occasion, Mickey wanted so bad to grab the three feet tall animal by the neck and throw it the whole way to the ground. But sadly his father chose the usual option and shot the little fuckers. But standing there as he watched the two wild dogs go limp, he was knocked with a sense of realisation. It hit him. He realised what we all knew already. Wrestling was no longer his job. Wrestling was becoming his life. “Shit!” he snapped, as his dad pushed past him. His father shot his a confused look but his bed was calling him louder than he wanted to talk to his son. As Mickey stood in the five o’clock morning air, The Farmer became aware of what he really wanted to do. He didn’t want to be at home. He wanted to be in a wrestling ring. He wanted to be a Relentless Champion.
