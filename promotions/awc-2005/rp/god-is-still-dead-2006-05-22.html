God is still dead

22 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/22/06 Twelve o'clock in the afternoon, and the skies look as dark as they would at twelve o'clock in the morning. If this were shack in the state of West Virginia, and Paw was sitting out on his rocker, smoking a corn-cob pipe, he'd say something like, 'Smells like rain.' Of course, if he were from West Virginia, there was a good chance his father was also his grandfather, but I digress. The coming of rain has a way to make things look even more sinister than they already would. Take for example a tree swaying in the wind. On a clear day, this looks innocent, unremarkable. No one takes notice to a tree rustling when the sun is shining, but when it looks like it's going to pour, well, it looks like a scene out of Twister. Next thing you know, a cow's flying past your car. Or not. Still, it's an ominous scene, and it was right outside of Captain Suleimon's window. Funny you should mention that, because while it wasn't raining yet outside, Armageddon could very well have been happening inside. 'I can't believe this injustice!' The Great Captain was screaming. Hayvan sat in another room with his headphones on, listening to something really loud. Cradle of Filth, that sort of thing. He knew what was coming. A tirade, mostly about how Harber was the White Devil for torturing him so. He partially knew it was coming because it always comes, but mostly, he found out because he accidentally opened Suleimon's mail and saw the booking sheet for this week. Dogan, however, had no idea what was coming. Then again, he did. Resigned to the fact that his cousin was a miserable of the highest order, every day he expected Suleimon to pick an argument with him, the free-thinking, liberal, tolerant-to-the-West soul that he was. Dogan wasn't completely innocent in this regard; he'd often take the bait. It'd make for some decent fireworks, both Hayvan and Rahman, when he was around, would agree. 'Well, what did you expect?' replied Dogan, playing devil's advocate. 'You really haven't done anything of note or gotten into any kind of groove since Twilight of the Gods. Harber is in a bad way for tag teams, and he's trying to spark something new. Have you ever put yourself into someone else's shoes for once? Have you ever thought of being a team player for once?' If Suleimon were a white owl, the caption underneath his startled facial expression would read 'O rly?' Slowly, he began to shake his head, slowly at first. He bent his head down as if to show disappointment in his cousin. Then, suddenly, a loud roll of thunder shook the room, and rain began to fall from the sky as if the proverbial floodgates had been flung open. The winds howled like a coyote on the lone prairie, and just like on cue, the tree that had been swaying precariously in the breeze was now being forcefully bent at an arc. Right then and there, as if Suleimon and nature were perfectly in synchronization, the Captain's head-shaking became violent, as if he were trying to scramble his own brains and pour them in liquid form out of his ears. He opened his mouth... 'For me to be a team player, I'd have to be on a team!' Each word carried exponentially more weight than the previous one. His shout even rattled Hayvan over his heavy metal. 'Every week, I work in direct competition with every other wretched, undeserving soul on this roster. I slave and I toil to bring each and every single one of them to submission, on their knees in front of me. For me to consider AWC a team, I would have to respect these peons as teammates, and I shall do no such thing!' Another clang of thunder crashed in the background. The sound of Dogan clearing his throat was entirely muffled. 'You know, maybe you wouldn't have such a hard time if your attitude was a little better.' Suleimon's steely glare said everything he needed to say without a single utterance from his throat. Dogan saw it and read it, but he ignored all of it. If antagonizing one Tariq Abdul Wahad Suleimon was a slippery slope, let's just say Dogan came well-prepared with chains on his tires and some damn good brakes. 'You know, maybe if you were a little friendlier around the locker room, or maybe if you went to mosque and prayed once in awhile...' A look of sheer and unbridled rage began to burn in Suleimon's eyes. The tree bending in the wind came into focus again. Instead of present day, it was 1988, in a village outside of Istanbul. To call it a suburb would be overestimating it. The roads were dusty, not a speck of asphalt to be found. Shacks lined those dirty paths where horses and donkeys defecated freely. In what was the equivalent of East Bumfuck in Turkey, only the poorest of the poor habitated these shacks, living in the most decrepit of conditions. Because of his father's untimely death in prison, Tariq's family had to move there, living off a limited pension from the government, which was only enough to buy meager amounts of stale bread and nearly wilted vegetables. Desperate, they moved into a shack that had been deserted for nearly three years, or so the locals had told them. It felt like it was deserted for far longer. They couldn't sense any sign of human life... just rat droppings, dead flies. There was nothing much to do here either, unless you liked cleaning up shit. Mostly, people sat around bored, baking in the summer heat, discussing the way things used to be, how they'd laugh with their families and take walks in the park, spend money on a movie if they had saved up just enough for the month. Tariq didn't care too much to talk. Besides his clothing, the only worldly possessions he took with him from his Istanbul home were his ragged books about the Ottoman Empire, the glory days of his beloved home country. Everything else he had was pawned by his mother. He had no qualms about the globe going, or his rug, or any of the vestiges of his faith in Allah, trinkets and whatnot. His books though... his books were all that he had left. They were his salvation. They kept his hope flickering that maybe some day, he would rise over his oppressors and stand tall. Despite the fact that he was living in a place where he couldn't stand to breathe let alone sleep didn't bother him, as long as he had his hope. Tariq wasn't the only one who didn't want to live there though. People by and large didn't want to spend their days wasting away in a decrepit old village. They they lived there only out of bare necessity. The old, crippled, the ones who were laid off from their jobs and were too lazy to go looking for another. Those reasons were why it was so strange that a small group of young, able-bodied men came to the town one day. There were five of them, and none of them were older than the age of 26. The villagers buzzed as to why they could have been there. None of the men talked to anyone. They looked around, in the holes in people's shacks where there'd be windows. They all had menacing looks on their faces too. Young children would come up to them and say hello, and they would coldly ignore them. Then, one day, without warning... *RATTATTATTTATT* The sharp sound of gunfire pierced the air. Their voices soon followed, barking instructions for people to come out of their shacks with their hands above their heads. As the entire populace of the town was gathered in the main common, the leader of the group, a man with short, straight hair and a five o'clock shadow. 'We are members of the Turkish Communist Party!' he barked over the buzzing of the small but noisy crowd. 'You may have heard rumors of our party's demise, but they're greatly exaggerated. We are sweeping the countryside, taking over small villages, banding together our strength so we can take the country for the ideals of our comrade Marx!' The crowd buzzed even more. The women lamented, their cries shrill, causing their children to react similarly. The men silently became indignant, but dared not speak for fear of being shot by these men. They didn't know what they were going to do. Their sheds had no phone service, and no one could leave the village for fear of being shot. They were essentially slaves to these Communists. Tariq thought to himself that these Reds were full of something akin to the brown piles that dolloped the dirt streets. He wasn't too aware of current events, but before he was forced to move out into the boondocks of the boondocks, he had watched the news nightly, about how the Communists, even in Russia, were losing substantial power. Still though, he knew he couldn't do anything. They had guns. He only had blunt instrumentation. The last time he checked, guns > blunt instrumentation. After that fateful day, the gang made the villagers do work for them. Tilling fields, most of the time to little or no avail, shoveling shit out of the streets, refurbishing their shed into something more than a shack in the middle of nowhere. It was ball-busting labor. The sweat poured off of each of the villagers' brows. Every night, after whatever menial tasks the armed Communists would have them do, Tariq's mother would come home every night and say impassioned prayers. Prayers to send deliverance from these godless oppressors. Prayers to protect her two sons just in case the unthinkable happened and she died out in the field. She prayed so long and hard that sometimes she didn't get to sleep until midnight or after. The excessive prayers got to Tariq. Not so much at first, but with each passing day, having to watch his mother bow down on a stretch of floor with a torn garment she had fashioned into a prayer rug, hearing her pray for something that would never come. She thought he was irritated because she interrupted his reading, but it wasn't that. It was something completely different. Every time he heard her pray, painful memories flooded into him, the nights he prayed for his father to be delivered from prison, the phonecall his mother got telling her that her husband had died in prison. Then, one night, he heard his mother pray... and something inside his brain snapped. He couldn't take it anymore. Each word thudded inside his brain, and finally, he had reached the breaking point. 'YEEEEEEEAAAAAAAAAARRRRRRGGGGGGGHH!' Startled from her rug, his mother turned around into the corner where Tariq was laying. 'Are you alright?' Tariq sat there in silence, almost catatonic the way his eyes stared wide and bright, latching onto nothing in particular. 'Tariq... are you alright?' He hyperventilated, the shockwaves still pulsing his body. He didn't hear his mother those first two times; his eyes were glossed over. 'Tariq!' The third beckoning finally aroused him. 'Oh yes... I'm... I'm alright... just had a bad dream.... that's all.' She knew he was lying to her; Tariq almost never slept at this hour. She was willing to overlook that fact though. She had tasks more important to worry about. As for Tariq... well, he began to settle into a state of thought. If only there was a way... The Communists were overlords during the work day, but once they had deemed it was enough they would leave the villagers alone for the most part, to let them do their thing. As long as they didn't leave the village, they wouldn't have a need to be shot. One of the Communists thought it strange though that the teenager kept going from shack to shack. He went back to the ringleader. 'Mahmoud, I'm getting a little suspicious about that young kid. He's going around to everyone's house. I don't like it.' 'Omid, be cool. He's a kid. He can't do any harm. Plus there are only cripples, women and lazy slime in this village. Hardly enough to do any harm.' 'If you say so.' - That night, the skies grew overcast and the wind picked up speed. The lone tree in the middle of the common, a small tree, blew in the breeze ominously. Omid, the suspicious one, walked around with an air of paranoia around him. He knew he shouldn't be too frightened, but something spooked him. Something about the kid. He heard a rustle and jumped around. It was nothing, just the tree in the wind, wind which started to pick up. Rain started to fall, lightly at first. Omid looked around, carrying his rifle tenatively. He heard another rustle and jerked his head around. Nothing, still the tree, but as he turned his head around to face forward. *THWACK!* Omid fell to the ground. Tariq dropped his axhandle and snatched up the rifle. With the gun in hand, he walked hastily towards the Communists' master shack, a shack that, thanks to the slave labor of the villagers, had turned into something resembling a house. It had five rooms, all unfinished, and a living area with five cots in it. Four of them were occupied. Omid's was the only one that wasn't, because it was his turn for nightwatch. With the exception of Mahmoud, everyone slept with their rifles on beside them. Mahmoud actually had a small handgun in his boxers. Crrreeeeeeaak The front door opened slowly, but it still made a noise. Tariq slowly stepped in, hoping not to awaken anyone. 'Huh?' Too late. One of the men started rubbing his eyes. 'Omid? What are you d...' Click! Tariq cocked the rifle and pointed it at the Communist. 'Oh shit!' the man said as he sprung to his feet. 'Nope... not today,' Tariq said. 'Don't touch your gun or I'll shoot you.' The man didn't touch his gun, but by this time... Click. 'I should have listened to Omid.' From the master cot, Mahmoud stood up, pointing his pistol at Tariq from about two dozen feet away. 'I'll have no qualms splattering your brain all over that wall. And maybe I'll even have your mother clean it up...' Before Mahmoud could do anything, Tariq shouted 'NOW!' In through the window busted Tariq's brother, armed with a broken piece of brick that he smashed right upside Mahmoud's head. The shock made him drop his pistol. As it fell to the ground, it discharged, hitting one of the Communists in the thigh. AT that moment, two other men burst in and leapt at the other two Communists. In a matter of 15 minutes, all four men in the shack were subdued. The next morning, Turkish police arrived at the scene, thanks to a frantic midnight dash by the only woman in the village who could ride a horse to inform them. The five thugs were carted off, and the police told Tariq's mother of his heroics. When Tariq stepped through the door, his mother raced over to him and hugged him. 'Oh, my angel... you're okay.' Tariq smiled as his mother, thankful that he was alive, kissed him on the cheek. 'I knew I was going to be alright. You didn't need to worry.' 'But I did... and look, God has delivered you back to me safely. Praise Allah! He is good! He answered my prayers!' As his mother uttered those words, Tariq forcibly broke free of her embrace and stepped back. He had a look of utter disbelief on his face. 'My angel... what's wrong?' He shook his head, almost looking as if he were going to cry. 'You...' Now his mother looked as if she would start to shed tears. 'My son! What is wrong?' 'I... I... can't believe it... you still think it was God who delivered you.... don't you... don't you see? I was the one who devised the plan. I was the one who knocked out the patrolling guard. I gathered the townspeople... and you... you say it was some ghost? Some phony spirit? 'I SAVED YOU FROM THOSE PEOPLE!' His mother shirked back. She couldn't find the words, and now, neither could Tariq. The next day, Tariq packed his things and left the village. He went back to Istanbul to look for a job, a new way of life. Even though he was only thirteen years old, he needed a better life than this. While he would see his mother again, their relationship was never the same. She remained a devout Muslim. And Tariq... well... (As a postscript, when he reached Istanbul again, he found out the Communists weren't sweeping the countryside. It was one band who happened to be bored with being small fish in a big pond.) The anger in Suleimon's eyes could not be held back any longer. It burned through his face and left his mouth as if he were breathing fire. 'God? God? Where was God when I broke into this business or when I was facing adversity after adversity? God hasn't done a damn thing for me, and if He even exists, He's done nothing but work against me. Everything I've done, I've done myself. Everything I do, I do myself, I don't need Allah or Victor Cage or anyone! Do you hear me?' He was hyperventilating. Dogan stepped back... he hadn't seen the Captain this possessed before. 'You disgust me, leave my sight at once!' With that, Dogan did what Suleimon had asked him to for the first time in a long time. The Captain was silent for the rest of the night, but the storm raged on outside, thunder, hail, driving rain. And that tree, that lonely little old tree, looked almost as if it would be ripped from the ground, roots and all.
