The Beginning Of A Long Road

16 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/16/06 Damien seemed to have it all, a pretty girlfriend, a loving family, and all of the athletic talent any high school student could dream of. Though by the look of him he could obviously play any sport he wanted, the idea never really appealed to him until the beginning of the seventh grade when his father convinced him to go out for the wrestling team at his middle school. The first match he ever wrestled was at Sparta High School in a junior tournament. The 13 year old was already nervous about wrestling his first match in of a large crowd of people, but adding to the nervousness was the realization that the name opposing his on the bracket was that of a female. He almost ran out of the gym. His dad coaxed him out of the car and led him back inside where it was almost time for him to take the mat. When a voice roared over the loudspeaker, “Match number 67 of the day, mat one, Damien Wilson of Bernardsville taking on Natalie Davis of Middlesex.” Damien’s heart sank. He was unsure about the WHOLE idea of wrestling, but now adding in the fact that his first ever match was against a girl just made the whole deal seem sour. He didn’t want to disappoint his father by not wrestling or by losing so with his stomach turning and head swimming he stepped onto the mat. Under his stiff new wrestling shoes the mat felt a lot less soft then it actually looked, and his level of nervousness increased. By now Natalie had taken the mat as well. She was about five feet, four inches tall, about two inches shorter than Damien. However she had a distinct girth advantage and the thought of being trapped under that for a pin fall troubled his mind greatly. The crowd stared anxiously at the mat and at Damien, probably because he looked like what some people would call a fish out of water. Turning quickly from the crowd his eyes met his fathers and all seemed well. He tried to block out the crowd by looking at other things around the mat. It was maroon in color with yellow paint marking the outer and inner circles, and it had two older looking girls sitting at a table with some sort of flipping card scoreboard. Suddenly relaxed, he paced to the center of the mat and put his foot on the line the ref had told him to. Within one minute and three seconds the match was over and Damien’s had was raised in victory after a quick takedown and half nelson for the pin. He was addicted. The next two years of his life before high school he ate, dreamed, and breathed wrestling. His father drove him all over the state to tournaments and wrestling clubs so he could get all the experience he could. His father Tim was happy to do it, because he enjoyed watching his son have so much fun doing something and working him as hard as possible to get as good as possible. Damien ended up wrestling almost two hundred more matches before he entered high school, and he and his father were both excited about the next level of competition, and even possible scholarship offers. Damien entered Bernard’s High School in Bernardsville, New Jersey at the age of fourteen, quickly catching the attention of every coach in the school. This was largely due to the fact that he had matured early, already being about 5’10’’, having a very solid build, and chinstrap that turned to a goatee at the bottom of his chin. Most thought he was a transfer from some other high school and were shocked to find that he was fourteen, which was young for his grade, and a freshman. Futile efforts were made by the football, hockey, and baseball coaches to convince him to come out for their teams. Damien’s mind was set on one thing and that was making the varsity wrestling team. At the end of November his opportunity came knocking when the winter seasons started. He was listed in the Star Ledgers as a promising newcomer and possible challenger for the state title at 189 pounds. When it came time to wrestle off for the varsity spot, he handed a convincing defeat to a returning letterman. This is when his trouble began. Now, since Damien had made the varsity squad, the upperclassmen were much more willing to accept him as a friend and invite him places. He went to his first party the following weekend and was coaxed into a couple of shots and beers. People at the party chanted his name and the words forever will echo in his head “that freshman is a tank, man”. But he wasn’t a tank. Upon arriving home he caused a ruckus loud enough to wake the dead. He did not wake up anyone that had passed on, but he did wake up his father and mother who knew he was plastered. The led him to his room and tucked him in, planning to punish him the following morning. The were awoken later that night by the sound of him throwing up all over the kitchen and bathroom, which they forced him to clean up in his altered state. The next morning everyone was silent at the breakfast table, not wanting to speak of the night before. Damien apologized over and over, and to his surprise, was not grounded. Mr. and Mrs. Wilson figured that his hangover, throwing up, and having to clean it all up was punishment enough. The rationalized this because it was the first time he had ever done something wrong like this, and figured it was a one time deal. It wasn’t. As Damien’s status around school improved, so did his wrestling ability. He was still attending clubs after his practice and school and was quickly becoming recognized as a serious threat to the defending New Jersey state champion at 189 pounds, Scott Newfork of Phillipsburg. His record was 21-0 going into the final team match before the state tournament. The final dual meet would be against Phillipsburg and he would get a shot to see if he would have what it takes to defeat Newfork in the state tournament. The bout started to go down hill quickly with Newfork ahead 4-2. He would never relinquish his lead, defeating Damien 17-2 by Technical Fall. Damien was ashamed of his performance and it was obviously magnified by him losing his chance to become the first freshman in school history to be 22-0. His record of 21 wins by a freshman still stands. Damien cruised through the state tournament, save one scare when an opponent almost caught him in one of those bullshit flashy moves. Damien’s skill was too much though and he was able to reverse his way out of it and put himself in the finals. Once again he would meet Newfork, only this time it was for the state title at 189 pounds. Damien would lose one again, this time by the score of 2-1. Even though the score was close, it was the most crushing defeat of Damien’s life. Second in the state was not good enough for him and he promised himself he would cut the bullshit drinking and partying and focus all his energies on winning the state title in his sophomore year. The papers felt this was a lock because Newfork accepted a wrestling scholarship to Harvard and was graduating. It was a lock and Damien captured his first state title in his sophomore year at the age of 15, in rather convincing fashion. His final record was 33-0, not letting up a single point in the second half of the season. Relieved that he had accomplished his goal he slipped back into his old habits of partying every weekend. He was talked into smoking marijuana at one of these parties and it quickly became an addiction. About halfway though his junior season was the first time he tried cocaine. When the coach of the wrestling team found out of his destructive habits he tried to intervene, saying that all of this could kill him and if he didn’t stop it would cost him his wrestling and his life. Damien stopped…for a while. He captured his second consecutive state title that year and was receiving scholarship offers from schools around the country. He now stood at 5’11’’ at a full 200 pounds in the off-season. He had a pretty girlfriend and life seemed to be going good. Spring finally rolled around and he had some free time to just kick back and enjoy being young. This spelled disaster for Damien. He went back to smoking, and drinking, while doing lines every now and then. His girlfriend also abused cocaine and they both made regular trips into the city to buy some for themselves. One night, after returning home from the city, with quite a but of coke in his backpack he passed out on the couch, backpack open. His parents came home and saw the bag. This time they took swift action by sending him to rehab during the spring season. He checked himself out without them knowing and surprised them by returning home. Not willing to play games with their son’s life the parents demanded he check back in or they would kick him out. Damien chose the latter and was legally emancipated on July 1st, six days before his seventeenth birthday. Free of his parents and school he went nuts over the summer, immersing himself in the drug culture and lifestyle. After about a month of so of destroying himself he and his girlfriend broke up and Damien slowly began to get clean. By the fall he was ready to attempt to move back in with his parents. They would not have any of it and kicked him out for the final time. The brutal realization then hit him. He was a seventeen-year-old kid, no high school diploma, no car, no job, and no money. Most of all, he had no wrestling, and this pained him greatly. Now going hungry he had to think of a way to get some cash and to get away from New Jersey. He took a bus to Pennsylvania and tried to start over, working as a bus boy in a locally owned bar. Over the course of two months he noticed that every Tuesday the bar would be filled to capacity with men trying to get closer to the television sets. He paid it no mind, thinking that it was Football or some sort of boxing event. Finally the suspense became to much and he approached one of the patrons about it. “So, uh, who’s playing tonight?” asked Damien in an unsure tone. “What do you mean who’s playing, kid? We’re watching the AWC, it’s Tueday Night Fresh, kid! You been living under a rock or something?” The world of professional wrestling was one that perplexed Damien. He had seen a show or two in his younger years and brushed it off as brutal, not like the wrestling he did because he didn’t think that it required any skill. When his shift ended, he stayed and watched the close of the show with the men in the bar. The moves, the pain…the wrestling, was something he longed to do again. He compiled what few possessions he had and hitchhiked himself to the location of the next AWC even at the Cintas Center, hoping to get a word with someone who could get him a tryout…
