Bird Songs and Sunbeams

30 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Aug 30/06 Johnny steps up to the fork in the path and comes to a complete stop. Birds chirp their melancholy songs as the sunbeams dance amongst the tree branches and leaves. Johnny wipes sweat from his forehead and pulls the headphones off his ears to settle around his neck. Eyes moving from left to right; dressed in a white muscle shirt and black tear away pants he looks as any other jogger pauseing on a sun dappled path through the trees. 'The choice is always easy when there isn't a choice to make at all.' A voice, aged and wise but still strong, floats along a sudden breeze from behind. Johnny turns to face the source of narration, running fingers through his blond hair to pull it from his vision, blue eyes narrow on the figure. Kakhis and a ball cap, Peirce Lavelle t-shirt, an old man out for a walk. 'On the one hand you have a wife, a daughter, and friends. And on the other hand you have the pursuite of glory, the legacy that might make you immortal.' 'Who. How do you. What the fuck man?' 'As to who, my name is Gus. It doesn't matter just yet. You are faced with a choice, a conundrum, a decision to which only one of two goals may be attained. Down this path you have a family, and down this path you have a future.' 'How do you know my family?' Johnny cuts him off as he's about to continue, shoulders squared the much larger Lexicon advances, an increasingly aggitated frown burrowing on his forehead. 'I know that you have a beautiful new wife named Talaina that doesn't know the things she should about you. I know that you have a brilliant daughter living with your ex wife in Japan that hasn't spoken to you since she went there last June. I know that it breaks your heart every night to think she may not want to come home next week and that you know she wont if you're wrestling full time. There's no life for a child on the road, your new wife isn't a mother, she hasn't been sober a full day since you met her.' 'Stop it. What do you want?' 'Nothing.' 'Then what's this about?' The old man nods slowely and moves towards a bench, a bench Johnny can't fully remember was there before or not. The wrinkles at his eyes relax as he sighs deeply and looks up into the branches hands crossed in his lap. 'It really is a gorgeous day.' He remarks casually. 'Whatever.' Johnny shakes his head and turns around. 'Everybody spends their lives scurrying around collecting and amassing fortunes, security, striving for something better without an end ever really in sight. There's always something new, somewhere to go, someone to impress.' Johnny stops, not truly intending to leave, and turns around to face the man again. 'When is enough, enough?' The old man looks at Johnny with a settled expression that lasts a long moment. Johnny shrugs. 'You had questions.' 'How do you know so much about me?' 'You asked that one already. Just trust that I'm here to help, and if you can do that then the next question is obvious.' Johnny's jaw flexes as he scrutinizes the grinning man sitting before him.'Which path should I take?' The old man smiles the smile a teacher must get when his prized pupil masters a lesson. 'The question I cannot answer. The choices are presented everyday and everyday you choose your path but it's the culmination of all of those days that determines the path you chose.' 'Is my daughter alright?' 'Funny.' 'What?' Johnny shuffles his feet. 'Your daughter is sick, a touch of the flue, but she's excelled in all of her summer courses and is proving to be quite the little genius. Your ex-wife is a devoted and diligent care giver for a child not her own, you're a luckier man than ever I've met but it wasn't the path you chose.' 'I didn't think I'd chosen yet.' 'Every hour of every day.' 'I would have been there for my daughter, I didn't know she existed until she was twelve years old. I don't know how to be a father and I'm still trying to figure out how to be a husband. Koryn and I. Well. We were over a long time ago.' 'Claire.' 'Claire?' 'It's your daughters name.' 'I know that.' Johnny snaps. 'This whole time you've not said her name.' 'What's that supposed to mean?' 'It means whatever it is you don't want it to mean.' The old man grins and tips his hat as Johnny chews on the bait. 'How is Talaina?' 'She's fine.' 'So far from home you'd think she would feel misplaced. A new hotel every week, a husband she barely knows. You're wedding was gorgeous though.' 'We were drunk and I bribed the priest. Same night I quit drinking.' Johnny sneers. 'Which begs a question of my own, if I may. Why?' 'Why what?' 'Why marry a girl you don't even know? Your lifestyle isn't suited to marriage.' Johnny finds himself unable to hold eye contact. 'I don't know.' He confesses. 'To fill a void?' 'You're the therapist here, why don't you give me a hint?' 'The answers aren't always mine to give. I can see you're not enjoying this conversation so let's talk about something else.' 'Like?' Johnny's frustration comes through in his baritone. 'Let's talk about your career.' 'I'm a wrestler.' 'I know. This sunday you have a big match, probably one of the biggest opportunities you've come across in the last two years. Presented in this match are two very obvious choices.' 'Do I work with my team and go for the win or do I take out Red Rock and go after the title belt.' 'You're catching on. Not all the choices in your life are as blatant, but the ones that are usually mean the most. With one choice you can actually support the people relying on you for once.' 'This business isn't about relying on other people. If Red needs me to keep that belt he shouldn't have it in the first place. I'm the wild card and everybody knows it, nobody in this match can say they know me any better than the other. I have all the cards in one hand and I'm peeking top deck with the other.' 'Which makes the choice your own. You're seeing now, you're the corner stone, the unknown Maverick moving in under a layer of obscurity. None of your opponents hold the edge of mystery, they can bring their best but they can't bring the kind of uncertainty you're bringing.' 'What should I do?' 'I can't tell you that, I'll not play God.' The old man gets to his feet with little effort, showing he's yet quite spry despite his obvious years. 'Who are you?' 'You asked that one already too. But you are astute, my name is not Gus, but a name doesn't matter. When I was young and fit, handsom and not so hunched over, I ran this very path and came to this very fork. I stopped.' The old man steps up beside Johnny, the two of them turning to face the forking paths. 'I tied my shoes.' The old man turns, straightens his back as best he can and looks Johnny in the eye, blue meets blue and the man speaks. 'An old man appeared, he was crippled and bitter. He was very angry, frustrated with the world's inability to make him better. Sad that he couldn't make his family care when he made the entire world remember what he did. He pointed down this path.' The old man points to the left. 'He said that this is the long way but at the end is a park with every ride and a picnic is always set. He then pointed down this path.' To the right. 'And he told me that this was the short path but at the end is a train station where the train master will give me a one way trip to anywhere I want to go.' Johnny swallows as his eyes move from path to path. 'Which path did you take?' 'It's not important, what matters is that moment changed my life, never before had I stopped and realized how many choices I will have to make, and determine what was truly important in my life.' A long silence hangs tentatively before the old man raises and points down the left path. 'This is the long way, and to be honest the path isn't easy, but at the end is a happy family waiting in a park for you with a picnic already set.' The old man points down the right path. 'This is the short way, but at the end is a career where anything you want is possible.' 'You want me to make a choice.' Johnny states matter of factly. 'You're making the choice as we speak. When you talk about your family, your daughter and your wife, you're uncertain and confused. But when we talk about wrestling you are confident and without doubt.' 'You're saying I should put my family first.' 'I'm saying there is no right or wrong, there is only your choice. There will be sacrifices made both ways.' 'Haven't I sacrificed enough?' Johnny spits with venom. The old man shakes his head sadly before looking Johnny full in the face. 'No.' 'That's bullshit. So what happens if I don't like my choices.' But it's too late, Johnny turns away to emphasize his point and when he turns back the old man is gone. Turning and peering about produces nothing so Johnny turns back to the paths. And just stands there for a long moment, the birds continue their melancholy song and the sunbeams carry on dancing amongst the branches and the leaves. He looks down to the path at his feet and pauses. Johnny kneels down to tie his shoe laces.
