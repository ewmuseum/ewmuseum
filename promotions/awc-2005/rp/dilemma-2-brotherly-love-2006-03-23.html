Dilemma [2] - Brotherly Love

23 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 23, 2006 Cedric Riley felt lost. It wasn’t that he needed directional guidance. No, it was more of an emotional feeling, but not the kind you’d find on MySpace, where “eclectic” teenagers are desperate to find themselves or their true purpose in life. Dash’s situation boasted a little more realism, with plenty reasons of support: Natalie Porter, found at the heart of it all, had called in a bomb threat along with his name, which resulted in the AWC recruit to spend the majority of his visit to London in the sin bin. His original intent in visiting the Britain capital was a momentary reunion with Nate Aspinall, Cedric’s misguided younger brother. That false hope was lost in the process of Cedric being held at Interpol as a suspect for terrorism involvement. He would eventually return to America to compete in AWC’s weekly extravaganza, Fresh!, which would fatefully end in defeat. Call that a recap, broken down into nothing but the essential details. The whole story is archived here. Fresh! had just ended, and our protagonist lay admissibly on a hotel bed, still in his street clothes and the bed remaining made. The room gave off a feeling of loneliness, which engulfed the spirit of Cedric Riley, as he gave no effort in trying to piece his rattled life together. Normally an uneasy man, he was perfectly comfortable lying on that mattress for the remainder of the night, showing no enthusiasm in turning off the light or television that buzzed noticeably in the background. He didn’t expect any phone-calls, for his popularity was rock bottom along with every other aspect of his life; but on the odd occasion the phone did ring, he awoke from his slumber and answered the call, with the hope that it was Natalie calling him. That was wishful thinking. The front-desk lady asked him politely to turn his television off, for his neighbors were unimpressed with the peevish sound at three o’clock in the morning. The gall of these people, he thought, almost trying to fool himself into thinking he was paying attention to the news that was on BBC. His interest for the night peaked as he listened to a story about an illegal ring in London of high-society danger and deceit. With no further details provided, he willingly clicked the TV off, and returned to his doze. The thought of London presented a wave of nostalgia, thick with assumptions of Natalie’s actions, Nathan’s whereabouts, and what was going on that he needed to be clued in to. His curiosity for his significant other tipping him off as a criminal had taken control of him; commandeered his mindset to the point of lunacy. Convinced that his brother has his hand in it, he was overwhelmed with possibilities and had tried desperately to fill in the blanks but in the short period of time came up with nothing but dead ends. The doors he wanted access to remained closed, and he was clueless as to where he should go next. His contacts at the CIA proved to be useless other than telling him it was Natalie, or at the very least, her phone that called in the anonymous tip-off. And although it made sense, because Interpol had clued him in by saying the caller was of “importance” or something along those lines, James Graff provided no further leads as to why Natalie was avoiding any calls. Cedric was limited to a handful of contacts in London, and they too proved to be ineffectual. All the AWC wrestler had accomplished was running his telephone bill sky high with calls across seas. That didn’t bother Cedric, though, as he had much bigger problems on his hands. He had to consider his wrestling career as well. Admittedly, he did not have the start he had been hoping for. His situation outside of the business provided an excuse he thought was decent, but knew deep down that when he stepped in the ring he was free from all distractions. But once he stepped outside the squared circle, he could think of nothing else but Natalie, London, and Nate. He didn’t know where his brother tied in, but knew that he had to somehow, and would find out why, one way or another. The phone rang, again. As if motivated by his thoughts, and convinced that this call would give him the start he needed, Cedric Riley jumped to attention and picked up the receiver. There was something about a phone call at three o’clock in the morning that interested him, even if it was the front desk. “Hello?” “Cedric? Is that you?” The caller yelled into the phone, as if it wasn't connecting properly. Dash immediately recognized it as Natalie, and alertly jumped off the bed into a stand-up position. “Natalie! I must’ve called you a dozen times!” “I know, Cedric, I know. I’m sorry, it’s just …” Her voice, as noticeably hesitant as it was, was cutting in and out as if she was on a mobile. 'What-- are you on a cell phone? Natalie, where the hell are you?' Cedric asked impatiently, his knowledge of his significant other tipping him off as a bomb-threat resurfacing. 'I'm in Providence, rather, on my way; I couldn't find a direct flight. Listen, I have something to tell you-' she began, but was cut off abruptly. 'Yeah, you've got some explaining to do, all right. Calling in a bomb threat with my name? Not answering my calls? What's going on, Nat?' He shot at her hotly, pacing his Providence hotel room. 'Ced, I know you have every right to get short with me, but I have my reasons for doing what I did; whether you like it or not doesn't matter, I suspected it was for your own good, and it ended up that it was...' she tried to tell him, struggling to find the right explanation, as if she was being cautious with information she had. 'You're not telling me something, Natalie. But I do know one thing: sending in a tip-off of a bomb threat about me, I ... I just don't understand,' he finished, almost exasperated. He heard her draw in a sharp intake of breath, and frowned instinctively. This worried him - his intent wasn't to treat her rudely, he merely wanted to know of the circumstances that involved him, and more importantly, why. 'I'm sorry, Nat--' 'No, Cedric-- don't,' she began sobbingly, gathered herself, then continued, 'it's me who should be apologizing. I should've told you as soon as soon as I found out, but I was ... I don't know.' 'Just tell me, Natalie.' Cedric paused, effectively. 'Please.' She sighed. 'It's your brother.' Pause. 'Spare me the act, Ced; I know you're not surprised.' 'You're right,' he admitted, adding a sigh, then thought out loud. 'What did he do now?' 'It's a little more than that, Cedric. He screwed up. Big-time.' 'Wow. Honest.' Cedric remained unsurprised, and his tone of voice noted that. His brother wasn't a stranger to questionable situations. 'Nate came to me in search of you, hoping that you could help him out of the hole he's dug himself. I knew there was a reason behind him wanting you to come all the way to London, but he was pretty good at keeping it a secret, insisting that he only wanted to see you. I called you anyway, simply because I didn't know what else to do,' she explained. 'You should have-' 'Let me finish, Ced,' she requested, and then took his silence as a sign to continue. 'Anyways, I waited it out, but kept my ears and eyes open, and did a little bit of digging. Not enough to get me in danger, but I was overwhelmed with curiosity as to why your brother just happened to be in London. The CIA agent in me resurfaced, and I hate to brag, but I knew what I was doing. And I found out that your brother has stumbled into the wrong crowd; from what I’ve heard, there’s a lot involved with this ring: gambling, theft, money, and even murder. Call it a mob at best, either way, you’re brother is involved somehow, and something must be reason enough to come to you for help. That’s why I wanted to stop you from getting to London. I didn’t want you to get involved; you don’t need this kind of drama right now. What he needs help for, I’m unsure, but it’s probably the law or his pack leaders after him, and in that case he’ll either end up in jail, or worst: dead.” The finishing word rang out across Cedric’s receiver so prominently he was almost knocked off his feet. He slowly sat down on his bed, with the measures of a man who had just been exposed to unfathomable news. A mysterious expression masked his face, one of anger, grief, irritation, and dreadfulness. The preceding weeks of his life had been noteworthy, but incomparable to this bombshell; it was as if his problems with life, wrestling, and his past had evaporated without a trace. Nathan had been known to be in tight spots before, but Cedric thought it unimaginable for him to endanger his life. He was lost in a state of mixed emotions that puzzled even him, and almost forgot that he was still on the phone. “Cedric? Are you there?” He tentatively gathered himself, checking back into the real world, and replied, “Yeah.” One word was all that he could muster together, and he leaned forward with his chin on his free hand, propped up with his elbow on knee. On the other end of the line, it was clear that Natalie was trying to find something to say, but knew if she didn’t choose her words wisely, she would jumpstart the bitter side of Cedric Riley. Considering she’d already done that, she gave it a shot. “So, what are you going to do?” She asked cautiously. Cedric took a minute to think about that. Rather, he took another minute to think about it, because he’d been trying to come up with a plan of action ever since hearing the news and thinking of the possibility. Which, needless to say, wasn’t a decent span of time. “I have no idea, Natalie.” 'Well, before you go make any decisive gestures, you might want to hear the rest of the story,' she hinted, casually adding on that last few words. 'What!? There's more?' He returned. 'See, that's the reason why I'm on my way to Providence ...' Nat began. Cedric had almost forgotten she was in his area code; it had gotten lost while he was processing an influx of other negative information. 'I'm not the only one here. Your brother is, too. Or at least I think he is.' Cedric processed that thought, and the possibilities ran wild in his imagination, each of them just as possible as the next. 'Huh,' he replied stupidly. 'Well, that makes things interesting.' 'Indeed.' 'Well, if you plan on stopping him, you can abandon that; it's hopeless. Nathan is a loose cannon. Which means that unless he knows this hotel I'm staying at, I have to find him.' Natalie sighed. 'That's what I wanted to avoid; it's the last thing you need, Ced. You have a life to build. What about your new career?' 'Oh, don't worry about that. I've done enough up to this point to be eligible for a tournament consisting of nobody but 'zeroes',' he said. 'Besides, this life you speak of that needs building, it's still missing it's main support beam,' he added lamely. 'And what would that be?' She asked, as if it was necessary for the conversation, but her tone suggested that she already knew the answer. 'Don't pretend like you don't know, Nat ... once this whole thing with Nathan blows over, we need to make that leap we should've done the minute we left the agency.' Silence. And then as if fate were to have it, a knock came to the door and jerked Cedric's attention in that direction. Natalie heard it as well. 'What was that?' 'Somebody knocking on my door,' Cedric responded, getting up from the bed. 'Oh no ... he must have found you,' she concluded worriedly. 'He couldn't have,' Cedric said, 'besides, I wouldn't mind having a heart to heart with the kid. And give him a swift kick in the ass to boot.' This got a laugh out of the anxious Natalie Porter. 'I'm not too convinced that you'd be difficult to find; you're in a televised business, for one thing.' 'One can never be sure. I'm going to go ... I'll be waiting for you at room 351 at the Carlton,' Cedric told her. 'Yeah ... I kind of already knew that,' she admitted. Well, that would be a good reason she called my room, thought Cedric. 'Well, nevertheless, I'll be here.' 'Okay, bye.' And the conversation ended. Cedric didn't stop to consider the fact that if Natalie could find where he was staying, the same circumstances would apply to his brother Nathan. Nor did he clue in to the strangeness of somebody knocking on his door in the middle of the night; all of these small details seemed to be lost in a wave of confusion and anger. The fact that he had wrestled earlier was also catching up to him; he had to fight back a yawn in preparation for whoever awaited him outside his room. Again not thinking clearly, Cedric missed the chance to forewarn himself as he disregarded the peephole and opened the door straightaway. He was unsure of what to expect, and even more uncertain if he wanted it to be his brother. What would he say? What would either of them say? There was so much tension between the two even without Cedric's newly learned news, it was hard to imagine that they could have a civil conversation. And in the heat of the moment, there was possibility that a fight would erupt; they had been known to get rough with each other before. Sometimes, Cedric felt convinced that the only way to deal with the young, rogue adult was by force. The older brother even hesitated to call Nathan an adult; he was barely 20, and couldn't care for himself without their mother still pampering him at that age. Cedric swung the door towards him, bracing himself for the good, the bad, the ugly, or a combination of all three. A young adult stood before him who looked to be in his early twenties, with a boyish grin complimented by his hotel outfit. Beside him was a trolley, laden with a couple plates of food and a bottle of wine. 'Room service!' He said cheerfully, as if unaware that it was almost four o'clock in the morning. 'Here is your dinner, sir ...' he looked at his watch, 'Rather, breakfast.' Cedric gave the server a bemused look. 'Um ... I didn't order this.' 'Oh?' The man asked curiously. 'Says that it was to be delivered to room 351.' 'Well, this is my room, but it isn't my food.' 'It's mine,' a voice butted in suddenly, causing both Cedric and the server to look to the side, where a young man had entered the hall from one door down. Nathan Aspinall. It's hard to believe that the last name of Cedric's brother had been lost in the thick of the ex-agent's history. But the family name trudged forward, although at a slow pace in the source of the rogue young adult. Cedric wished that his path of being a CIA agent didn't involved changing identities; the truth was, the only name he had stuck with long enough to get familiar with was Sean Aspinall. But that was the past. The reasons for being Charles Kensrue, and being Cedric Riley to follow were explainable ones, and something he had to live with. He also had to live with the fact he was standing a few meters away from his brother. 'Nathan,' he threw out into the open, hoping to break the ice, which was thick enough to make them both uncomfortable. But the younger brother wouldn't have that. 'Good to see you remembered my name,' Nathan said, mockingly. He feigned amusement. A small smile flickered across the ex-agent's face. 'Good to know you're still a comedian.' Nathan returned a bit of a grin, then turned to the server, who was an uncomfortable presence. He motioned towards him, then handed him a few bills, and the server was in the elevator and gone. An intense moment of silence between the brothers followed, their eyes flickering on and off each other. 'We need to talk,' Cedric said. 'Come into my room.' 'You read my mind.' They left the server's forgotten trolley in the hallway and entered Cedric's hotel room. Nathan casually collapsed on the bed, but the older brother remained standing, pacing the hotel room, surveying his guest. Nathan was fit for a young man. He also had a handsome look, but the kind of handsome you'd find in a grungy old bar with people betting family valuables in poker games. You could see the resemblance to Cedric in Nathan: the scruffy, dirty-blonde hair, the brown eyes. Cedric wore black, thick-rimmed glasses, however, which made a significant difference, whereas Nathan's defining feature was the patches of hair growing on his face. At first glance, he looked like a success, complimented by a leather jacket and Levi 71s, but he was far from being accomplished. At least legally. 'So there's something I've been wanting to tell you,' Nathan began, swiping the remote off of the bedside stand and flickering on the TV, surfing the channels mindlessly. Cedric leaned against the door, still trying to determine what Nathan's goal was in this laid-back attitude he presented. Although, looking back to past visits, Nathan usually was always fairly laid-back; you would never know by his looks, attitude, or personality that he had made some wrong choices in his life. It was irony to most, but Cedric knew inside and out, Nathan had chosen the wrong lifestyle. 'And what makes you think I don't already know?' Cedric replied, almost daring his brother to lie to him. Nathan stopped turning the channels, and the TV settled on an old horror, then chanced a look at Cedric. Cedric had an intimidating smile on his face, his eyes transfixed on his target. Nathan pulled himself from the middle of the bed and sat on the edge, his elbows settled on his knees and his hands intertwined. 'Well, what can I say-' Cedric cut him off. 'There's nothing to say, Nathan!' He shot at him angrily. 'Why do you always have to fuck up, huh? Did I not tell you last time I hauled your ass out of jail that if you go on the way you were, like you clearly have, that you'll end up in jail for good, or worst, dead?!' The wrestler's fuse had blown now; he sounded dangerous, tearing into his own brother menacingly, with no mercy. 'How did I know you were going to react like this?' Nathan accused him. Wrong move. 'Like what?! Like I've been putting up with your bullshit for years, and now you're coming to me to help you get out of trouble, again?! I'll give you a fucking reaction!' He spat, full of rage, then proceeded to grab Nathan by his leather jacket, lift him by his cuff and press him into the wall with a resounding thud. 'How the fuck do you think Dad would feel if he knew what you've gotten yourself into?! He only wanted the best for you-' 'Excuse me? You're unbelievable! Dad never gave two shits about me. He was always all high and mighty on the fact that his oldest grew up to be a world-class wrestler, a CIA agent, and now who knows what you are. An aspiring President? An Olympian?' Nathan mocked, standing up for himself. 'That has nothing to do with me. This has to do with you,' Cedric finished effectively, then loosened his grip on his brother's jacket and walked away. 'Well, how about I don't need your fucking help!' Cedric turned to him, and shot him a dangerous look. 'It's too late for that, Nathan. You've already asked me for help by coming here tonight.' Nathan shook his head and ran his hand through his hair. 'Is that a yes?' 'That's a consideration. There's only so much I can do, Nathan, as I've made fucking clear already. Piss me off anymore and I'll change my mind.' 'How much do you know?' Cedric looked at Nathan again, then away from him as if he were trying to cool down. He shrugged. 'Mob activity.' It wasn't a question. It was a statement. Nathan sighed and returned to a sit-down position on the couch. 'That much is right, yeah. There's some other stuff involved, too-' 'Spare me the fucking sad story and give me what's important,' Cedric shot at him, threatening to go off again. He looked up at the ex-agent, then away from him. 'Fine.' Sigh. 'I owe some guys some money. If I don't pay it in 20 days, they'll kill me. And I can't go to the cops, or I'll have to tell them my side of the story, and that will get me years in jail.' Cedric had an overwhelming urge to tell him that'd be exactly what he needs, but didn't. Not yet. 'How much money?' He said straightforwardly. Nate looked at the ground, avoiding eye contact. 'Ten thousand,' he said quietly, but enough that Cedric could hear it. 'Jesus Christ, Nathan, what the fuck have you gotten yourself into!?' Cedric reacted, pacing the room. 'You're unbe-fucking-lievable, you know that?' Nathan put his head up and looked at his brother. 'I fucked up, okay? I know that. We've been through that. Now what I need, other than a swift kick in the ass, is some support, alright?!' They caught each other's eye, and a tense moment of silence followed. 'Now will you help me, or not?' Nathan asked, his tone implying that as much of help he needed, he wanted his brother to be honest. Cedric gazed away from Nathan. 'Give me a week to think about it.' 'I don't know if I have that much time ...' 'Well, it'll take that long to come up with that kind of money anyways, so you're going to have to deal with it. Now get out of my room. I need to sleep.' Cedric finished dramatically, then opened the door himself and looked at Nathan, waiting for him to leave. Nathan slowly got up from the bed, walked towards the door, and looked at Cedric as if to say something. Cedric talked first. 'Don't say thank you. We only talk about what we need to do to get you out of trouble.' Nathan nodded, obeying Cedric, then walked into the hall. Cedric slowly closed the door behind him, and before it clicked shut, it was pressed open from the other side. He swung it back, expecting his brother on the other side. Natalie stood there with a uncertain look on her face, and was motioned inside by Cedric. 'So, what's the story?' Natalie asked. 'I'll make a long story short: Nathan needs ten thousand dollars or he'll be running away from people who want him dead,' Cedric summarized tiresomely, sitting on his bed and undoing his belt with the intent of going to bed. Through all the havoc, he had forgotten that the time now ticked into the five o'clock hour. 'Are you going to give it to him?' She wondered cautiously. 'Yes. He's my brother, I can't just let him get killed ...' 'No ... I meant ... do you have that kind of money?' 'Yeah,' he responded simply, as if to imply that as a former CIA agent, he was knowledgeable of investments and had the money to do it. He needed to; David Harber didn't pay huge sums to wrestlers who didn't win matches. 'So why did Nathan look so concerned?' She added to her string of questions. 'I didn't tell him I'd have it right away. There's a lesson to be learnt here, Natalie, and I'm going to make the most out of it. Nathan will be stressing out over this an incredible amount. I'll make him tag along with me until after Twilight of the Gods, all along acting as if I was pressed to come up with that money in time. And before you say it, no, it isn't harsh. Nathan needs to learn some responsibility, Nat, and if this is what it takes, so be it. Now, if you don't mind, I'm going to bed,' he finished, almost grumpily. It was understandable at this time of night. Before the two could fall into a deep sleep, the phone rang, again. Cedric looked at it as if he was imagining it, but the noise was annoying enough to be real. He picked it up, then greeted, 'Yes?' He sounded very annoyed, and for good reason. 'Hello again, sir. I've had another call concerning a disturbance coming from your room. After the warning earlier, actions are required to be taken, and-' Cedric slammed the receiver down forcefully. He grabbed the phone and ripped it out of the jack, then threw it against the wall. It shattered into pieces. ... Very reminiscent of his life.
