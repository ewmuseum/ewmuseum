The Loss And Curse Of Reverence

7 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/2/06 This is how it is: Around him, the city falls to deathlike silence as the twilight gives birth to the night once again. The building he is standing upon slowly fades away as the shadows take hold and melds into the hollow, ruthless inner city landscape, a territory of leaky roofs and broken brick walls, of barred windows and drug-addled alleys. Somewhere further off, the sounds of a police siren going off can be heard. He stands with his back straight, hands stuffed into the pockets of his trenchcoat. He keeps his head up, eyes alert and vigilant – or so he believes. In truth, Hate’s eyes are glazed over, the eyes of a person who is in a different world entirely. This is how he sees it: Around him, the skies slip into darkness, but the city remains alive. The vibrant, neon-colored signs from far away grow into the size of stars, bathing everything in brilliant multicoloured radiance. The noise of the city by day slips into an unsettling drone of the night, monotonous and yet ever-changing. The drone is of voices: millions of voices, all praying, asking, yelling, talking, whispering, discussing this and that, melding into the background like the water running down from the pitch-black sky. As the night progresses, the buildings around him grow taller and taller, reaching like spires made of bricks and windows high into the sky. Hate can only watch in amazement as his world reveals itself to him in a whole new different light. A light he KNEW he had shut off a long time ago. But the light had come back: when his whole reality plunged into the darkness, it was the only thing lighting the way. Hate: ”It all seems so simple, standing right here, the whole of creation curving around you. You can see everything for what it is. No shrouds hang in front of your eyes, no malicious intent clouds your vision, looking from here. And in this world there is a place for me: a place reserved outside of everything else, a sphere where I cannot be touched nor seen nor heard. Perfect loneliness. Perfect BLISS. No-one here to blame me. No-one here for me to hurt. All me and my impulses have is this vast, vast space in which to occupy ourselves, unhindered, unstoppable, eternal. And no-one here to hear me speak.” Azagtoth: ”I hear you, my leader.” Hate leans over the edge of the building and looks down upon the street that passes the rundown hotel. Even though the hotel itself is rising at the same rate as the buildings around it, towering and speeding towards the distant stars, Hate can see the figure on the street quite clearly. Torn black clothes. A mess of black hair, spreading out from a white face now riddled with drops of red. The whole body, lying in a pool of blood, riddled with holes redder than anything. And yet despite all the carnage, Azagtoth lays like he was merely tired. He speaks with ease, his cool, clean voice carrying all the way up to the ears of Hate standing at the edge of the roof. Angelica: ”And I hear you, my love.” Hate turns his head, tilting it to look over his own shoulder. Angelica, dressed as plainly as ever, her delicate features smooth and somewhat unreal, standing in the nightly rain without getting a drop of water on herself. Hate knows, deep within his head, that she or he or everything is just an illusion: but he refuses to acknowledge it, rather hanging on to his bliss than face reality. He looks down upon Azagtoth once more, and sees the youth smile and wave at him from his bloody resting place. Hate: ”I have decided that you can’t be real. I saw what happened: you are dead.” Azagtoth: ”Well, my leader, that might be the case. However, are you absolutely sure? Maybe what you THOUGHT you saw was me getting shot to death: maybe I’m all well and good, waiting for you down here.” Hate shakes his head vigorously. Oddly, he seems to think clear despite the drugs in his system. This is what it must be like to finally master the substances: visit the places and not be hindered by them. Fine with Hate. Hate: ”I have already determined that this world is the fake one. Therefore, you are dead in the real world. I have spoken.” Azagtoth laughs, a high-pitched, shrill laugh that would send shivers down anyone’s spines were it real. But since Hate knows it’s not, it has no effect. The boy keeps on laughing, his body jerking in the spreading pool of red that now seems to cover quite a lot of the stairs he is laying upon. Azagtoth: ”Ah, you got me there, my leader. But it never hurts to try, no? Anyway, there are things I would like to say to you, ’from beyond the grave’, sort of thing. First of all, I think we can drop the mannerisms and the speech: I am dead, and you’re stoned out of your fucking skull. No need to be formal. Good? Good. Second of all… You do realize what you have done, right? You let me go outside and get shot, like you’d let a stupid mutt go outside and get run over by a car. Because that’s what I always was to you, right? Just a dog to prance around you, get scratched and take a dump on the carpet. OK, so maybe I never did the last part, but you pretty much had me on a leash for the whole time. And all the time I could only wag my tail and run blindly alongside you, never indulging myself beyond the bounds you set me. I believed in you, my leader. And now I lie here. Is that justice in your opinon, my leader?” Hate clears his throat and sneezes some highly suspicious-looking stuff out of his nostrils before concentrating his gaze, now swaying, at Azagtoth’s prone corpse. Behind him, Angelica hasn’t spoken anything yet, content on observing the scene. It is quite certain that she cannot see what Hate sees, and certainly cannot hear what Azagoth speaks. Most certainly, she is real. But that can be doubted as well. Hate: ”Believer! Do not speak to me of justice, for I have not seen any during my time on this planet, and I doubt I ever will. There has never been any justice for my actions, nor any justice in my actions. I have dealt without law and order, I have given without thinking about right or wrong. Ï have always given just as I have received.” The statement makes Azagtoth laugh, in a nonsensical way, but what in this dreamworld would make sense? Azagtoth: ”Love the voice, I really do. Makes you sound like a king, my leader. ’I, Hate, Monarch’, that sort of deal. Very inspiring. I think I could feel my adrenaline rushing if I wasn’t dead. Haven’t understood the thing with glands yet, now that I am dead and all. But as I said, there’s no need for the speech thing anymore: not for you or not for me. I ask you again, my leader, do you realize what you have done? You betrayed me. You betrayed my trust and let me die. Now, fancy-talk your way out of that one, my leader.” Hate’s face grows red with anger: it doesn’t resemble his usual warpaint, but a clever poet might draw the comparison. However, this isn’t being written by a clever poet, so comparisons aside. Hate: ”Betrayer? Betrayer!? If you feel like you are eglibed to use that label with me, Azagtoth, then it would be best you not speak to me at all any more. You, this world… I can wish it into being, I can unwish it in a heartbeat. I CAN. You have my word on it, Azagtoth: when have I broken my promises with you?” Azagtoth: ”When have you broken your promises with me? Are you perhaps joking, my leader? But hey, don’t take it from me. After all, I am merely shaped by your mind and imagination, right? Right? That’s what you say, my leader, and your words have never failed me before. Let’s not be so sombre about it, huh? How about we sing a song, eh? I know a good one. It goes something along the lines of…” When Azagtoth sings, Hate instantly clasps his hands over his ears. The voice that emanates from the pale, delicate face is not unlike the death throes of a person being choked to threat, each syllable full of malice and pain as it is torturously forced out of the vocal chords in a slow, punishing rhythm with no end or pauses. Hate watches as Azagtoth sings, knowing the words by heart as it was a song he used to sing with Azagtoth quite often a few years ago, and looks on horrified at the smile that spreads on the dead-white face. Azagtoth: ”Dethrone the son, the crucified one! Dead and begotten, unfortunately forgotten! Death on the cross, life was your loss! You fell from Roman hands in thy holy lands!” Hate: ”Azagtoth! I do not appreciate this sort of behavior! You claim that I have betrayed you, and yet you carry on this treasonous behavior! Rest in the knowledge that this will not make me think any higher of you: it shall only increase the pity and sorrow I feel towards your unfortunate fate. But right now, what you are is a taint in my world, something to be cleansed. I shall do just that.” The singing stops for a moment, and Hate is almost ready to claim victory, when the immoving body begins to laugh, a sinister, maniacal laugh that seems to never end. Hate stares, dumbfounded, at his shot lieutenant, who stares up into the sky, eyes tinted with strong malice. Azagtoth: ”What are you afraid of, Hate? I am a dead body! I have more metal in my body than any of your skanky gothic bitches has in their entire jewelry box! I can’t move from this spot, on account of a broken spine, and yet you say that I am a taint? Right, right, excuse me while I crawl off and die on some other porch. Now, where was I? Ah, really… The light won’t shine over your Holy Empire, as it ends in a roaring storm of fire! The Christian serpents can escape, or stay and face the oceans of fire, blood and…” Hate straightens up and takes a few steps backwards. No, this couldn’t be. Azagtoth was ruining his world. As he looked on, the rain from the sky ceased and the water evaporated, like it had never happened at all. The buildings were no longer tall spires: now they resembled the abandoned warehouses and run-down apartment buildings that they were in reality. Gone was the drone of a million strong: replaced with the ringing, ear-shredding singing of Azagtoth, somewhere far far away and yet so close… Clasping his head, Hate stumbles backwards, until he hits something. Angelica. Hate grabs the girl with both hands, looking into her face with maniacal eyes. She still seems unreal, still seems somewhat out of place and soft… Maybe it was not gone fully. Maybe there was hope left. Angelica: ”My love, are you… are you running out? Should I bring the satchel for you? Please, tell me and stop staring at me like that, please…” Suddenly Hate snaps out of it. He sees it all. The decaying inner city. The nightly, chilly roof he is standing on. And in front of her, Angelica, a regular, ordinary girl. Hate sweats profusely as he pronounces a few syllables of complete gibberish and then goes back over to the edge, wobbling like a drunken sailor in a storm. No Azagtoth. No body. No blood. Just empty concrete stairs and a street to go along with it. Hate holds his head and breathes deeply. His world was gone. He was back in this one, back in the one with the traitors, the betrayers, the believers. Back with the people he could and wanted to hurt. Back with the people who would never understand the wonders he saw. The wonders that were awaiting for him in the brown leather satchel in his impromptu office, containing needles and that heavenly brown liquid… But not tonight, not with people needing to get their asses kicked tomorrow. Slowly, Hate sits down on the roof, and lets out a sigh. And behind him, Angelica looks at the big man, both genuinely concerned and smiling out of pity. Her treacherous woman’s soul was already formulating a plan. How easy it would be to manipulate a man not completely in this world, she thought as she walked off of the roof. And for the first time in a few weeks, Angelica smiled. Smiled like a demon. Cleanse the Impure. Raise the Warcry.
