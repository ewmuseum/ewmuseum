The Long and Winding Road: Chapter Two - Warnings/Promises

16 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 16th May 2005 Pact “Tommy who?” I yelled into my cellphone. Alexi couldn’t have chosen a worse time to call – Chimaira had literally just come onstage. “No, not Tommy – Taylor. Taylor Troublesome.” “Taylor Troublesome? I thought I was facing Christopher Ashton?” I replied whilst batting off various members of the pit. “Yeah, so did I – apparently you roughed him up a bit too much last week, he’s not going to be able to make it to the show.” “Shame…damned shame. I was looking forward to clocking up a victory over WHUMPH~!” Some fat crowdsurfer who must’ve weighed about 400 pounds landed right on the back of my neck, knocking me to the ground. My cellphone clattered to the floor and smashed into a hundred pieces. “Better luck next time, old man!” he taunted. I fixed him with an icy glare. “Old man?” Luckily for him at that moment the band launched into ‘The Dehumanizing Process’ – in seconds he was all but forgotten as I let my gloriously deconstructional theme tune wash over me and allowed the violence of the pit to swallow my whole. --- I woke the next morning on the floor of the club in a pool of my own vomit. Blearily, I opened my eyes and took in my surroundings – black walls, complex lighting rig, long bar (of which I must’ve consumed about half last night, in terms of stock), irritated looking man in suit, bored looking man in overalls. “Yesum – been there since last night. Couldn’t move him – he’s heavier’n he looks!” “Thanks, Cletus – I’ll take it from here.” With that the man in overalls shuffled off, pushing his broom in front of him and shooting me a wink. There was something very familiar about him, but I couldn’t quite place it. “Sir,” the man in the suit continued, addressing me now, “I feel I should inform you…” “…blah blah have informed the police blah on way blah blah?” I finished. He did a double take. “Uh…yes.” “Story of my life, son. Story of my life.” I dragged myself, slowly, to my feet. I reached out a vomit-caked hand to shake his. “Thanks for your hospitality.” With that I lit a damp cigarette and made my groggy way towards the exit. Alexi Our Benefactor was very explicit – I must never refer to him by name until he deems the time to be right, which is kind of frustrating cause it means I can’t tell anyone, not even you, the specifics of my brilliant plan to put Pact back on the path to greatness. It seems that, for now at least, my genius is to go unappreciated. Don’t get me wrong – I mean, it’s not as if EVERYthing that’s going to happen is going to be my doing. Fuck no! I mean, all I am is a catalyst – I helped to get things moving, get them going down the right path, got in touch with the right people. In truth, most of things we’ve planned have stemmed from the Benefactor’s ideas. He’s truly a great man. I just hope that Pact sees things the way I do. Well, it’s just…the last time they saw each other they weren’t on the best of terms. Harsh words were traded, punches were thrown…just chalk it up to the impetuousness of youth I guess! I mean, Pact surely can’t hold a grudge this long. 25 years? It’d take a petty man to hold onto a hatred for that long. Even if that hatred was somewhat well placed. There IS something about the Benefactor that gives me the creeps, though – his voice. It’s entirely devoid of emotion, gravely...kinda the way you’d expect Satan’s voice to sound. Not that I’d call him Satan, personally. I mean, the guy’s helping me out, he’s doing me a huge favour. He’s got a big heart…it’s just that voice. Cold. Grating. Ahh! Gives me the willies even just thinking about it! Hmm? Worried? Me? No! Well…kinda I guess. Who wouldn’t be? I’m sure if you were in my position you’d be kinda worried. I mean, Pact’s more than just a client – he’s a friend. But it’s like I said to the Benefactor – he’s become a liability. The string of losses he’s suffered recently is…unacceptable. There comes a time where business has to come before loyalty, and the way things are…I’m losing money on him. I mean actively. Bookings have dropped off to the point that what he’s making doesn’t cover my fee anymore. He can’t even get booked on TNA. TNA for fuck’s sake! You’d have thought the Fat Man would’ve jumped at the chance to get another vet in the main event, but no. “Sarray, pahtner, ain’t no place for hem her raht niow.” No place! For him! Once the most feared man in professional wrestling, holder of over 30 championship belts over a career spanning three decades, and now he can’t even get booked in the indies. What the fuck is up with that? It makes no sense! So yeah. I’m nervous. I’m worried – for several reasons. I don’t know what…psychological effect…the reappearance of this man into his life will have on Pact. I don’t know what’ll happen when they come face to face for the first time. I don’t even know if what we have planned will work. But I do know that something has to be done, and I can’t do it alone. What? BETRAYAL? No! This is for his own good! He’ll see that. He’ll realise that. He knows how influential the Benefactor can be, perhaps more-so than anyone. It’s for his own good, and even if he doesn’t initially, he’ll thank me for it. He will. I know it. Anyways, I’ve gotta go meet him now. No, Pact. Yeah. I’ll speak to you soon I guess. Yeah. Take care. -click- Pact I staggered out into the midday sun, shielding my eyes from the glare with a gloved hand. How much did I actually have to drink last night? I couldn’t be sure. The nights on the lash were starting to merge into one big bender – I couldn’t work out where one evening ended and the next began. I hailed a taxi and told him to take me to the offices of Killbob Representation for my weekly meeting with Alexi, my agent. I was still somewhat irritated that I wouldn’t get the chance to take out some frustration on Ashton, but what can you do? “Still,” I thought to myself, “it would’ve been good to cut loose. It’s been a while since I’ve had the opportunity to just…well…go sick on an opponent. Right now, all I need is an excuse. Not even a reason. All I need is an opening or an opportunity. I’m feeling like a loaded gun with the safety off – all I need is a knock and I’ll blow someone away.” But I knew, deep down, it was all bollocks (as my dad would say). Age and the punishment I’ve inflicted on my body over my career were most definitely catching up with me. At that time I was just about keeping my nose in front but I knew I couldn’t keep it up forever. And the thought that something serious could happen (in terms of my health and the safety of the people I work with in the ring) scared me shitless. What was even more frightening was the fact that I knew when I was in the ring I’d FORGET all my fears - I’d just be focussed on the job in hand…and I couldn’t afford to do that. I knew that I was staring paralysis or worse in the face every time I stepped into the ring and at the bottom of it all I just didn’t care. I was doing what I loved and so long as I was doing it I was happy – damn the risk to anyone else. So, when the bottom DID fall out on me, part of me was shocked. A bigger part of me knew it was inevitable, and as such I resigned myself to my fate. We’re getting very close now to a pivotal moment in the story of the latter days of my career. I sit here now looking back on it, and obviously I have the gift of hindsight…but what you have to realise what happens next is out of my control. Forces I have no power over are beginning to come into play. Please, I beg you, do not judge me. What I do I do to survive, and I present it to you as it was. I don’t ask for your forgiveness – just your acceptance. View Pact 's Biography
