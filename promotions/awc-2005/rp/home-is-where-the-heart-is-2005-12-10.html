Home Is Where The Heart Is

10 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/10/2005 'You little sonofabitch!' The words echoed throughout the spacious living room as the sound of a falling lamp pierced the heavy air. It was nearing midnight when Victor D'Angelo arrived home - drunk as a skunk, and smelling twice as bad with the crusted pieces of vomit hanging from his chin. It was a typical Saturday night at the D'Angelo residence; Victor would head down to the local pub on the corner, Franky's Fish And Pub, and wind up being removed from the premises due to an altercation with the men's room toilet. The lamp shattered on impact; a million pieces of crystal scattered the floor, which only seemed to anger the uncontrollable beast, who was closing in at a surprising rate. Due to his current state, you'd generally think he'd have lost a good part of his agility and speed, but perhaps his incomparable experience of being a lush worked to his advantage. The boy - a young man in his mid teens - dove for cover behind the safety of the living room sofa. The flower decorated sofa had been his greatest ally in his inevitable war with his father, and it generally bought him several moments to devise an escape route. Not on this night though. Victor grabbed the bottom of the sofa and had it tumbling head over heels in a matter of moments. The man was ridiculously strong, years of steel working to credit for his hardened body and was fairly agile for his large size. The boy crawled on the tips of his fingers, moving with purpose as if a space of security really existed. A thundering blow met the bottom of his stomach - a size twelve boot had found its target, sending the boy tumbling into a heap. Victor moved in for the kill, his mouth almost foaming in anticipation... 'Not so tough now, are ya'z?' he screamed, inching ever so close, 'What?! Cat's got your tongue, boy?' Damian was aware of the situation. Nothing he could say would help him avoid the beating he was about to undertake - not an apology, not even begging. He'd tried every method in the past; at one point early last summer, Damian had actually tried fighting back...He spent a cool week in the hospital in what was claimed as a biking accident. Yeah, right...fell off his bike and hit every tree down the fuckin' mountain. Victor's fist rose above his head - slowly cocking it in his desired state, before unloading a vicious right hand to the chin of Damian. Blood trickled instantly, a small stream began flowing rapidly from the corner of his lip. 'You're sure quiet, boy' Victor replied, staggering over top of the cowering boy. 'Sure ain't sayin' nothin to my face.' Just remain quiet. Stick to the plan. Avoid further punishment. 'You cocksucker!' Damian spit from his mouth, an ample amount of blood finding its way onto the face of Victor. Sonofabitch. His right hand rose to the sky once again, fury rising through his entire body, before he unloaded the blow...which was met with the solid, wood casing of the bookshelf that was pulled from the wall by Damian. Damian scooted from his current position and made a break for the confines of his one safe haven. The bedroom. The antique, cast-iron lock had been his best friend for the past few years, keeping him safe while a raging lunatic prowled the house. Just three more steps. Victor was closing the distance, moving with an unforeseen quickness. Only one more step – he was tired – tired of being beaten up, tired of taking these beatings week after week. Victor lost his balance and the oak tree came tumbling down; his hands failing to brace the fall, his body landing with a sickening thud, sliding gradually until it met the bottom of Damian’s door. Damian was safe. All he had to do now was wait for Victor to pass out – likely within the hour, and at that point he would be able to continue on with his pleasant life. Life had such a beautiful way of reminding you that it loved you.
