One for Luck, Ch. 8

11 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11th December One for Luck, Ch. 8 (Prometheus: Section 6) Foreshadowing devotion Shakespeare once wrote that “life is a play, the world is its stage and the people are the actors, devote to the drama, until their final curtain call, and then, they are replaced, the play will never end, each new scene will have new issues, it is up to the actors what they choose,” The many things in life, all condensed into the metaphor of a tragedy, played on a stage for the many onlookers and viewers and all the while each murder, death and suicide acts to the idea of being replaced by another actor, another person to take your place. It seemed Alex Strider’s death would be the same thing for Prometheus, although I wasn’t sure whether he really did die of a drug overdose, perhaps he wasn’t as lucky as me. I would never know what happened to him, but I guess I felt somewhere in between the emotions of hate, justice and then grief. I was shocked by his death, but unlike the public, I knew what he had been up to with Prometheus. Suspect to the truth, he was a criminal, endorsing a drug and illegal merchandising. Like Strider, I had now been pulled into the world of dangerous liaisons. Life was proving to be fatal for me, each step could be my last and no matter what happened last week, it didn’t matter, I just worried about the next day … I stood outside the Hilton hotel, gazing at the spinning doors, my duffle-bag slung over my shoulder and my hands in my pockets. It felt wrong to be bringing Kelli into my constant troubles, but with Alex Strider dead, things had just got a lot more dangerous in L.A. I knew, deep down, that I was alone in this epic battle between right and wrong! But who’s to say I was doing anything right. I stood back and turned on my heel, when a police car almost took me down, breaking a couple of inches from my legs. Two very robust looking men, in suits, stepped out of the car in unison. Their badges glimmering against the suns rays. “Pierce Lavelle?” one of them spoke with a deep accent. I couldn’t quite pin what it was. “Yeah,” I replied and removed my hands from my pockets and went into a defensive posture, crossing my arms over my chest. “We need to call you in for some questioning,” the other spoke, removing his glasses. “Huh? What did I do?” I asked, now panicking about the last couple of months. I could go down for a long time for trying to do the right thing. As my grandfather had said, the good guys always get burned. “We need to pull you in for some questioning on the murder of Alexander Strider. We aren’t accusing you of anything, yet. Please, come with us peacefully, or I’ll have to apprehend you and read you your rights in front of all these people,” the other spoke. “I see. I’ll come, quietly,” I said and followed the two cops to the car. They pushed me into the back seat and drove off down the street, taking a left into Santa Monica and out onto route 405. I couldn’t quite understand the kind of predicament I was in. If they were to look up my file, they would see that I had been in hospital recently, almost dying from drug consumption (unwillingly) and also the real reason I was here, thanks to Tony Rush placing all the details into my hard drive on my lap top (notebook). All they needed was a search warrant. “My name is Detective Longley,” one of them said, behind the shadow of black sunglasses. “This isn’t your first time in a cop station, is it Lavelle?” he asked and smiled. The man stared at me through the rear view mirror, a sinister grin on his face; smiling from ear to ear, his teeth illuminating the fact he smoked. It wasn’t a cop station we had arrived at, instead, without my realisation the second detective had taken a turn off route 405 and into a desolate area, with nothing more than dirt, ocean and a warehouse. I panicked a little and went for the door, it opened and I flew out, crashing to the ground and rolling. I felt my head hit against some stones and my jeans ripping with the friction. The car came to a sudden hault, the tires flaring dirt and small pieces of stones and grass as they screeched in a 90 degree turn. I froze and sat up, coughing with the amount dust around me. I had cut my head and wounded my arm. The car’s engine began to run. I knew what was coming. I stood up, with some pain and kept running, listening to the car behind me, I dove to the left and watched the car speed on by, it turned around again and came at me, and I waited till the last second and dove to the right, landing next to the warehouse. It was abandoned, but the door was ajar. Not thinking properly, I raced inside and heard the engine to the car turn off. I immediately ran through the dark, damp dwelling and kept running, not looking back. It was a shipping warehouse, sheets of plastic hung from the roof, with crates lining against the walls and middle of the room. It was two floors and I couldn’t find an exit. I hid behind a box and tried to control my breathing. I pulled out my mobile and watched the bottom of it fall off, along with the battery crashing to the dirty floor. “I know you are in here Pierce, I heard that crash,” one of the fake cops said. I heard the click of a gun and the burst of fire and saw a quick flash and some shrapnel of wood fly past me. They were going to destroy this place from box to box, crevice to crevice until they found me. Another burst of light lit up the room and I saw my way out - a shattered window at the back of the factory. Waiting for the warehouse to fall into darkness again, I ran for the window and ran smack into one of the fake cops. He swung his pistol at me, hitting the side of my mouth. I felt around and found a steel pipe; hoisting it up, I turned and struck him. He fell and I continued to run. “He’s running for the exit,” the man whimpered. I turned around and saw the two of them, standing still, watching me. The predators against the prey and now they had me in a very tight space. They shot at the exit I was looking for. That wasn’t going to be an option. I turned to the small stairs and ran up, listening to bullets ricocheting against the steel stair case. I dove onto the landing and took a breather. The room was open, without corridors or boxes. It had large wall to ceiling windows lining the room. I ran to one of the windows and saw a large drop into the ocean, with some rocks. “Looks like we have him surrounded,” one of them spoke. They both laughed and moved into the light. The one I had hit with the pipe had a large gash along the side of his face. I stood with my back to the window and swallowed. These two men had one purpose, to kill me. “Lavelle! Lavelle! Lavelle! You are more stupid than you look,” the one on the right chuckled. “You’ll see the same fate as Strider. You were a pain in the backside for a very large corporation,” the man who called himself Longley spoke. “Prometheus,” I muttered. “Correct…” the man who called himself Longley replied. “…and you pissed off some big heads,” the other said, joyfully. “Do you feel lucky?” Longley said pointing his gun at me. I inched slightly backwards and gazed over my shoulder. It would be a long fall, and I may not make it, but things didn’t look like they were going to improve anytime soon. “No need for Hollywood emphasis,” I said with a cocky grin. “He speaks,” the other said, raising his gun to me. I took a step back, took a deep breath and jumped just as Longley fired a bullet. I felt the glass shatter behind me. The bullet flying right toward me, piercing my shoulder. I fell toward the deep blue coloured ocean, my eyes closed … “Shit,” the men said in unison and ran toward the window. My eyes remained closed, and I could feel the burning of the bullet drilling through my body, till it came out the other end. I landed in the ocean with a heavy thud, the water engulphing me. I opened my eyes and saw I had made it passed the rocks. The salt water began to sting my eyes, but I persisted and remained under water, feeling faint. All I could see was the darkness beneath me and the red crimson coloured blood trickling out of my wound to the surface. My body cringed in pain with the loss of blood and the lack of oxygen, but I could see the silhouettes of the two assassins standing on the strand, gazing at me and the blood. I acted like I was dead, my body limp and still. They moved away from the ocean, believing they were successful. I rose up, feeling the heat of the suns rays and gulped for as much air as possible. I had been hit in the shoulder and my clothing was tattered and ripped. I listened to the engine disappear into the vacuum of Los Angeles. I swam slowly to the strand, with only one arm, until I could use my legs to drag my body out of the ocean. I crashed onto the sand and coughed up some sea water and lay on my back, gazing at the seagulls circling over head. The light’s were fading on me, a tear trickled down my face, the warmth of my blood dribbled down my body until it met with the crashing waves. Despite the suns ray, I was ice cold, my heart slowing and my limbs stiffening. “Help … Me,” I muttered through my dried my mouth to the seagulls. They ignored me, constantly circling above me, making no effort to help, of course they wouldn’t, they were after all, just birds and I most likely would become lunch for some Ravens. It was only normal for the balance of nature, just like Shakespeare said. The pain was slowly disappearing and I could hear voices, perhaps I had died, it didn’t seem like Heaven, but who’s to say what Heaven looks like. I heard the deep barking of a dog and the yelling of a couple of men, I cringed with the thought of the assassins coming to finish me off. When I opened my eyes I was met with a large, sloppy kiss from a towering dog. Two dock workers stood, scratching their heads, the other was on the mobile calling for help, saying it might be a shark attack. Does this look like a shark attack? I asked in my mind. The other two knelt down and removed their jackets placing them on my body. My eyelids were over powering me, all my body wanted to do was sleep. “Stay awake dude,” a voice spoke softly. “…” I muttered and felt my heart struggling in my chest. My eyes remained open, with tears trickling down my cheek. I could hear the sirens, the trumpets of angels and the calling of death … At the round earths imagined corners, blow Your trumpets angels and arise, arise From death, you numberless infinities Of souls, and to your scattered bodies go… The sirens flushed through the streets of Los Angeles … my murmuring heart beating against the waves of chatter from the two paramedics. My mind hazy and the words of John Donne flashing around my head; I should have stayed with English Literature in school, what? Why I am thinking of English Lit … WOW … everything’s hazy … kind of … Sight fading, voices speaking and a thumping migraine … “I need 12cc’s STAT,” I heard a voice yell. “He’s going into shock,” the other retorted, placing her cold fingers on my neck, feeling around for a pulse. I could feel my body struggling to maintain the lack of blood now in my body. I wondered how much I had lost, but it was refreshing to know that the paramedics knew it wasn’t a shark attack, especially since most of the sharks had migrated down to California as it was freezing in Los Angeles…SHARKS … DAMN … I’m tired … so t-i-r-e-d… A light to free me from my burden And grant me life eternally Should have been dead On a Sunday morning Banging my head No time for mourning Ain't got no time I awoke in a small four-by-four cell, my arm bandaged and wrapped in a small sling. I was still in the same, torn, dirty clothing that I had almost died in, but the fact that remained was, what am I doing in a police investigation cell? Everything was a blur from my jumping out the window to now, I felt I had lost some part of my life - time stopped, just a few hours and then played, like a banged-up video tape. It didn’t matter though; all I could focus on was the window to the left of me, the steel table in front of me and the massive headache pounding away at my skull. I was becoming delusional, paranoid and fearful. What was I doing in Los Angeles? It was only bringing me pain, hurt, heart ache, distrust and a lot of violence. These were things not many twenty-three year olds had to face. In contrast to my torn life, I should be at College, worrying about end of semester tests, where to bring my girlfriend on our next date, what will happen in the next NFL game and things like that, instead my life was very much the façade of the rock and roll anthem of the 1970’s ‘sex, drugs and rock and roll’ and the inevitable from all of that, death. I was surprised not to be dead, but I knew now, I wanted out … The door opened and my grandfather walked in, followed by his lawyer, Martial J. Duggan. I had the pleasure of meeting this man at the age of seventeen when I had found out about my grandfather’s court case. He stopped by to get a report from my father on the clinical practices of plastic surgery. Of course, back then, I saw him as cold, callous and stubborn, much like my father. “Pierce, what happened to you?” my grandfather asked placing a hand on my shoulder. “I come home and hear that you were shot, taken to hospital and now being interrogated by the LAPD in connection to a murder …” he spoke his words cautiously. “…I really don’t remember much …” I said. “Start from the beginning,” Mr. Duggan stepped forward, placing his jotter on the table and took a seat opposite me. “I-eh …” I was confused by his suddenness. “Pierce, this is the best attorney in Los Angeles. Now listen to him and he’ll get you out of this, but you need to tell him everything that happened, word for word. Then it’ll be given to the investigators in charge of you,” my grandfather spoke and leant against the windowsill along the fake, glass window. “Okay,” I said and Mr.Duggan nodded. “After the FRESH show, I stayed over for a night before my next flight back to Los Angeles. When I woke up that morning to get into the taxi, I found the AWC daily edition news report and on it, it stated Alex Strider was killed in his hotel suit, thing is, I was no where near him that night and I know who did it, because once I arrived back at Los Angeles, and outside the Hilton hotel, a cop car arrived and two men (impersonating cops) got out and said I was to be taken in for questioning on the murder. Anyway, I did as instructed, under the illusion they were real cops. Next thing I know, I’m being driven off route 405 near to the marina. So I jump out of the car and hit the gravel and sand, grazing my head and ripping some of my clothing…” I looked around at my grandfather; he stood still, hand on his chin, worry on his face. Mr. Duggan looked up at me and mouthed that I continue. “…they turned the car around and came at me, so I dove out of the way and ran to an abandoned warehouse, which wasn’t actually abandoned, it was still being used for shipping crates. I hid for a while, but it wasn’t good enough. They had two pistols and began to shoot the place to sunder, so I ran for the nearest exit, but bumped into one of them, using a steel pole that was on the floor I defended myself and ran, but I couldn’t use the exit intended,” I took a breath. “How come?” Mr.Duggan asked, looking up from his jotter and blue biro. “They were sniping me, treating me like prey, circling me …” I said. My grandfather moved forward and stood behind me, resting a hand on my shoulder. “Do continue, Mr. Lavelle,” Mr. Duggan spoke gently from under his glasses. “…so I ran upstairs, they shot at the steel steps, but the bullets ricocheted. I managed to catch my breath, but I was trapped. It was an empty room, surrounded by windows and it was a long drop into the ocean. I stood against one of the windows, my back to it. They came up, smug and grinning. They planned to kill me, just like they had Strider. Said I was messing around too much in LA, causing trouble. So they shot at me, I jumped backwards, crashing through the window, the bullet hit me…”I paused and took another breath. “…all I remember is hitting the water, everything else is very much a large blur,” I said and leant into the chair. “Very well, Mr. Lavelle. I shall check into the hospital that facilitated you and find out about the surgical procedure, but everything else adds up to what the investigators have found,” Mr. Duggan spoke with a heavy New England accent. “Will he be able to return home?” my grandfather interjected. “I’ll do everything in my power, John,” he said and stood up, closing his briefcase and leaving the room. “I couldn’t get some Paracetamol, could I?” I asked toward the fake window, knowing damn well there were cops behind it. I sat, recapping more of the events, but I couldn’t quite get passed crashing into the cold, crystal blue ocean. Everything had been removed after that, but the men’s faces were unforgettable. I knew if I were to see them again, I’d hide or run, I wouldn’t stay and fight. I considered myself lucky to be alive, because if the bullet hadn’t killed me, missing the ocean by a few inches would have. I remained in the small four-by-four cell for a while, sipping gently on a cold glass of water, supplied to me by my grandfather. I hadn’t seen him or his lawyer for about forty minutes and I was beginning to worry that I’d never leave. I couldn’t quite picture the sort of life I could live, but the night’s stars and moon offered a new view. Its light was a glimmer of hope; the stars twinkled like light bulbs, each one blinking with a new idea, a new thought, a new dawning. Yet I remained trapped in a never ending twighlight zone, believing some things could be better than they were and others, would never change, but who was I to ask what was normal. “Yo, rise and shine …” a voice said from behind the door. The door opened and Martial J. Duggan walked in, accompanied by two detectives. “Am I free to go?” I asked with some optimism. Martial J. Duggan nodded his head; he turned to one of the detectives and asked for my release. The detectives smiled and obliged to the request. “I’m Detective Henderson,” the man said while removing the handcuff around my good arm and the chair. “You are being released without charges, Mr. Lavelle,” he said and put the handcuffs into his belt. “Without charges? Then why was I held here?” I asked infuriated by all the time I had wasted. “Precaution,” he spoke in retaliation. “You must understand, when a murder happens we must cover all angles, but you’ve given us more than sufficient information to take you off the suspect list, we are sorry for the inconvenience,” Detective Henderson said with a real tenderness, he seemed like a loyal cop, dutiful and willing to protect and serve. “I understand,” I replied and moved my arm in hope of bringing back some circulation. “Mr. Duggan, you are free to take your client out of here,” the other detective spoke. “Understood, Detective Jones,” Mr. Duggan replied and led me out of the room and down the corridor. He carried his posture well, considering he wasn’t a slim and slender man. He was tall, sturdy and packed a lot of excess weight, but seemed to have a very atomic presence to his profession. “You got off easily, Pierce,” he spoke and stopped at the sign-out desk. “Thanks for your help, Mr. Duggan,” I replied. “Please, call me Martial,” he winked and signed his name into the lawyer documentation pad. “Your grandfather is outside in the limo. I’ll see you around, Pierce,” he said and left. I hoped not! The only reason you see lawyer’s is when you are in trouble, or if someone died and you have to discuss the will. I took a deep breath, smacked a smile on my face and braved the harsh cold night of L.A. The wind whistled as the doors opened, newspapers trailing against the ground with each gust. I stood still and gazed at the night’s sky, the moon still watching me and I smiled. In the corner of my eye I saw the puffs of smoke. A man was stood under the shadows of the police station, the red light of a cigarette butt glowed incandescently against the night’s morbid sky. I didn’t have time to speak, he was already breathing down my neck as I turned around. He was a tall man, brisk and menacing. “I see you got off,” he spoke. “Excuse me?” “No charges …” he said again. “How’d you …” He stepped forward again and handed me a brown folder. “…know,” he finished my sentence for me. “I was hired by a man to investigate you, turns out you are one in a million …” “…what’s that?” I asked. “Innocent,” he said. “Doesn’t come around too often,” he spoke with a deep, crisp tone. “Count yourself lucky,” he said and began to walk away. I stared at the small brown folder, and remembered he said he was hired by someone. “WAIT!” I yelled through the streets. He stopped dead in his tracks, but didn’t turn around. “Who were you hired by?” I asked. “Rubin Demorto…” he said and disappeared around the corner. RUBIN DEMORTO! I stood in shock, that couldn’t be, he was dead. Something very strange was going on. Nothing made sense, nothing. My few seconds of thought were broken by my grandfather rolling down the tinted window on the limo. “You do want to get home to your girlfriend, right?” he said with a smile. “Eh- yeah - of course,” I said and waited for him to open the door. The limo drove off down the L.A. streets and into the monotonous variations of car horns, motors and exhaust fumes. Things weren’t adding up. Something wasn’t right and I needed to find out what was going on, and who was behind this whole mess I find myself stuck in … The rain trickled violently against the windows of the limo as it speed on down passed Central city and up toward Hancook Park and back around into route 305. I could only sit back and remain silent. I could feel the slight bit of sanity fading and the last light of hope shimmering, like a candle against an open window, but eventually a breeze strong enough would vanish its flame for good. “I should be dead, shouldn’t I?” I asked, my gaze staring into the abyss. “I- … -death is something that happens to everyone eventually. You were shot, and in most cases of hit and runs people come out okay, not instant death. You were lucky Pierce, you truly were,” he said with a smile. “I don’t think I was lucky. My life is falling to pieces; my wrestling career is a mess. My boss thinks I’m holding out on him. I await his call on my fate. I could be suspended from further action … There is no such thing as a normal life, right?” I said, now focusing on the one drop of rain that managed to stay on the car window, but soon it vanished, a droplet in the wind. “Normality does exist, kiddo,” my grandfather said, breaking the silence. “I know things at the moment seem hard and tough and let’s face it, the Lavelle family has never been normal, especially with my son Brad, but there is hope for everyone Pierce, not everyone is cursed and subjected to hardship, once this is over, you’ll see I’m right,” he said with a wink. “I don’t know, I’ve been hoping for a normal life for a while now,” I said, still gazing at the rain trickling down the window, my back to my grandfather. “I know kiddo, but sometimes you have to jump a few hurdles to reach the good things in life. I once had a perfect, normal life with your grandmother, and when she died, I felt everything had been taken, but chances exist, kiddo. You came along and gave me back some warmth and hope … I ain’t gonna give up on ya yet,” “Thanks ol’man,” I smiled. “I resent that, I’m very much young, see,” he smiled and patted my back. “It’s always good to smile,” he said and pulled a rose out of his long trench coat. I turned to face him and smiled, well tried but the events of last weeks FRESH were still on my mind. Adam Dick had destroyed my relationship with Pearl Harber and the fans. “It’s lovely granddad, but I’m taken,” I said. “Don’t be silly, kiddo. You have to impress your lady friend. Can’t come home battered and bruised, without something to cheer her up,” he said and handed me the rose. “How do you always stay so optimistic?” I asked, intrigued by my grandfathers humour and love for life. “Because kiddo, I have only these little precious years left, I spent most of my life working, struggling and sometimes hating the life I was living, but being old has its advantages, you see things clearly, without pressure. Always listen to your elders …” he said and asked James his chauffer to stop the car. I pulled the hood of my jumper over my head, placed the rose into my sling and opened my door, feeling the rain dash through the small gap of an opening. The wind whistling through the small space. My grandfather still had a smile on his face. “Thanks for the lift,” I said and stood up. The car door shut and drove off. The rain was a fresh relief from the cold sea-water, stuffy police stations and humid limos, but before too long I was becoming drenched. I raced up the steps of the Hilton hotel toward the main reception for a spare key. All I wanted to do was to see Kelli, to take a rest and forget all about what had happened to me. The elevator door beeped open; I walked with stride toward the corridor where our hotel room was situated. I was hopeful that Kelli could be an ear to my problems, but also a chance to escape and try something normal, like going to the cinema or dinner. I was starving. Flipping the key around my fingers, I began to whistle, staring at the red rose. I placed the key into the lock and heard mumbling and then suddenly, yelling. I burst the door open. The hall was dark, but I could hear Kelli yelling, her voice squeaky. “Kelli…” I said and ran into the hall, but it was the man’s voice that seemed familiar. I turned the corner and saw Kelli, standing, shock written all over her face. The man emerged from the shadows. I dropped the rose, its pettle’s crumbling as my hand gripped and let go. The rain clashing against the window creating a unanimous reflection … “…Adam…”
