No More Bets Part 1

Roleplay: Triangles Title: No More Bets Part 1 Los Angeles, California San Pedro was engulfed in the raging storm as the waves roared along the sandy shore. The docked ships rocked in the violent, tossing waves that churned under the gale force winds and blistering rain fall. Ralph Fleming – head of the Council - stood in the shelter of a warehouse in a posh suit, his eyes lingering on the desultory murky clouds that rumbled above him. “What’s the E.T.A?” Fleming asked his men, keeping his back to them. His men remained seated, dressed in black attire and assorting through various weaponry and tactical machinery. One man lifted his head. “Any minute now…” he said. In the distance a large silver tanker glinted as the pitter-patter of raindrops danced off its hull. Fleming and his men began to move forward, their eyes watching the vessel come to a stop on the sandy shore. A large bulk head door opened and out of the dark emerged ten men dressed in suits, each more sinister looking than the other. “Mr. Yamasuko, I hope your journey was pleasant,” Fleming spoke through the thunder and rain. “I’ve had better,” Yamasuko said. “Where’s my shipment?” Fleming asked. “First…My money,” Yamasuko demanded. Fleming crossed his arms, a smug grin lingering on his clean shaven appearance. In the distance one man sat mounted on a shipping crate, the rain drops fluttering past his scope; seeing the signal, his finger pulled taut on the trigger. The deathly silence of the bullet whizzed through the rain till it collided with Yamasuko. He collapses to the ground with a thud, sand and silt surrounding him. Yamasuko’s men pull out their weapons. “FIRE!” Fleming bellows running behind his men and taking position behind a metal shipping crate. The commotion ceased in a matter of minutes, an eerie silence befalling the dock, blood began to seep into the crashing waves, creating a deep brown sludge as it washed onto the sandy shore. Fleming emerged and found only one of his men still alive, laying on the floor, his breathing wheezy – it was the sniper – he’d been shot and had fallen to the floor, his gun lay under him. “Help…Me…” he whispered, blood trickling out of his mouth with each breath. Fleming pulled his Glock tightly into his fist and squeezed off three rounds into the man’s chest, a sickening smile lining his lips. Around him is nothing more than a mass of dead bodies, blood oozing from ever orifice to form a vast puddle. Fleming begins to move away from the vessel, the shipment missing, he darts to the row of black Jeeps. “Well?” the voice asks, starting the car. “It’s done…” Fleming says menacingly. Adam Duncan, head of Warner Enterprises, sat in his chair in Lennox, facing his wall-to-ceiling window and gazing out into the gloomy skyline that lay in a shroud over the City of Angels. Duncan sat dressed in a black suit and stark red shirt, his grey hair glistening against the overhead light. The week was only beginning and his day was to become more interesting as he turned and picked up the phone, he began to have those irritating, self-doubting, precognitive thoughts… Duncan had only one person in mind when his fingers began to dial. The Lawler-Hoffman merger was coming to its final few hours and with the media pressure his stress was becoming an overwhelming factor. The phone rang and on its third click, the man answered. “Mr. Moore?” Duncan began. “Yes, who is this?” Moore questioned into the receiver. “My name is Adam Duncan, head of Warner Enterprises,” Duncan responded, a hubris smile from ear to ear. “How may I help you?” “As you may know the Lawler-Hoffman merger is nearing its completion, but there are some…complications,” Duncan teased. Duncan inched back into his seat, toying with his silver biro laced with gold filigree, his eyes cast unto the shadows beneath him. “What complications?” Moore questioned, beckoning a response. Duncan flicked the pen aside and turned his chair toward his desk. “Money complications, now if you’ll be willing to hear me out…perhaps we can cut a deal and you can regain Mercy.” On the other side of town, Moore sat in his office on the 6th floor of Dietetics Pharmaceuticals, the silence in his office felt deathly as he finally heeded to the man’s whim. “When?” Moore asked. Duncan smiled cunningly. “Culver City, warehouse 15 at 5:00 p.m., don’t be late.” “Alright,” Moore’s response was cautious as he slammed the receiver down and gazed up to his clock - it was 3:15 pm. Meanwhile, with his fingers entwined together, Duncan sat in his office with a smirk along his face, gazing toward his Rolex and staring into the abyss. Hailey Rose was a dominating presence in the USA Today printing department; her eyes searched every crevice of the daily edition before print, making the last necessary changes. She was dressed in a lovely, yet glamorous suit skirt and jacket combination, her long brunette hair hung in wisps around her shoulders, making it increasingly hard for her male colleagues’ to remain focused and take her seriously. “It looks good…” she spoke softly “…just get it to print before the Ice Queen has us all!” Rose said to everyone before turning to the door and leaving with a smile. It wasn’t often that she could honestly say she was finished up for the day, but today as she raced up the hall and checked her watch, she was finished for the day. The main office was virtually empty, bar the Ice Queen (Chief Editor, Lucy Cartwright) who remained seated in her office till closing hours. “You finished up Hailey?” the Ice Queen bellowed her question. “Yes…” Rose responded, bluntly. Fastening up her briefcase she turned off her computer screen and turned around to see a familiar face stood in the doorway. “Know anywhere that does a decent cup of coffee?” he asked. “Pierce Lavelle…must say I didn’t expect to see you here.” Rose said as she greeted him with a handshake and smile, escorting him out of the office. He was dressed lavishly and the hint of a light scented cologne added to his fresh appearance. “Well, I was wondering if you could help me…” he said. “Over coffee?” Rose implied. “Of course…” Twenty minutes later both Rose and Lavelle were crammed into a seat in Starbucks and sipping down two cups of black coffee. Lavelle’s eyes lingered on Rose for longer than they should have as she simply smiled and returned to the letter he had given to her on the walk down. “Do you think it’s possible?” Lavelle questions silently after burning his lip on his coffee. “The fact this letter suggests that there are multinational businessmen fronting the worlds black market and go by the name…” she paused “…The Council.” “Yeah, that…” he mumbled, gripping a napkin and dabbing his lip with some water. Rose smiled eagerly and placed the letter back into Lavelle’s chest pocket, her touch gentle. “Well, it ain’t as far fetched as it sounds.” Rose pondered a moment and yanked her blackberry out of her briefcase before accessing the internet remotely. Lavelle inched closer toward her and whispered in her ear. “What are you doing?” he asked. Rose turned to face him, their noses touching. “Researching.” Lavelle discretely pulled away and nodded his head while he pushed his coffee aside and opened up his mobile, he turned back toward her. “Why?” “I’ve heard of something like this before, and if there’s any truth to your grandfather’s letter, I want to find it…” Rose said while she typed in letters. Lavelle paused a moment as he skimmed through the letter and noticed a recurrence in the amount of times “the Council” and “D.P.” were used. “What if there are similarities between this council and my grandfather?” he asked. “Your grandfather…in cahoots with black market deals men? Are you sure?” Rose questioned, coyly. Lavelle shrugged his shoulders. “Anything’s possible with my family!” he spoke, glumly. Rose got up out of the crammed seat and began to move quickly. Lavelle was slow to follow as a group of teenager’s bombarded the counter for more caffeine and doughnuts. “Wait, where you going?” Lavelle asked. Rose turned toward him and patted his shoulder with a sweet smile. “Going to the National Library, if I find anything worth mentioning, I’ll ring you.” “O.k., I guess I’ll do some research of my own then,” Lavelle said as he led her out of the Café and into the freezing winds. He waved her goodbye and headed with a speedy pace toward his Dodge Viper. Bel-Air was like a giant chasm that sucked all the air into one big hole and caused a violent torrent of gales and bangs. Edward stood alone in the mansion atop of the West hill and gazed blankly toward the howling winds and raging rain. As he turned in his stance he moved toward his desk – strewn with photos, names, numbers and documents – a small phone lay under a bundle of photos, all of the same person. Edward pondered a moment as he slipped a cigar into his mouth and relished its savoury taste. Picking up the phone he blew out a large plum of smoke and smiled. “This is Edward…I need to speak with Montoya,” he directed. “I assume this isn’t a friendly call,” Montoya’s voice was hoarse. “Everything is going as planned. Lavelle has the letter.” Edward placed his cigar into an ash tray and shut the large velvet blinds, filling the room in darkness. “What does he plan to do?” Montoya questioned. “He’s snooping with the aid of a reporter, Hailey Rose,” Edward said. An eerie silence oscillated between the two before Montoya returned with an order. “Find out everything you can on this…Ms. Rose, and then report back.” The phone went dead with the final order. Edward picked up his cigar and gazed toward the pictures on his desk, a steely gaze arose within his grey eyes. Culver City was engulfed in motor bedlam as the rush hour traffic just began to flow out onto the streets. Moore was seated in his Lincoln and drove along the backstreets of Culver, his eyes on his yellow post-it and his acclaimed directions - warehouse 15 . As he drove along an imitation marina, he kept a close watch on the car’s clock as the neon numbers would flash ever closer to the time of his meeting. Although Moore had acquiesced to the man’s whimsical question, a strange churning sensation of nerves ruffled his turkey sandwich he’d had for lunch. Something wasn’t right, he thought. Pulling his car to the side he spotted big white letters painted onto a black hanger bay door, he’d found the place. He got out of his car, buttoned up his suit coat and immediately felt the chilling winds on the back of his neck. Outside warehouse 15 a black Mercedes lay unoccupied. Crouching down he read the licence plate and laughed quietly to himself - DUNCAN 007. “Charming!” he muttered. Moving with a cautious pace he slid through the small space created and plunged into complete darkness. “Hello!” Moore beckoned, his voice echoing against the surrounding walls. A sharp pain arose on the back of his neck as he watched the floor draw ever closer, till he was lying motionless on the cold concrete bottom. A sense of been dragged along the ground moved through the tips of his fingers as his legs began to pull. “HELP!” Moore cried. A bright light sprung to life and Moore found himself seated on an aluminium chair, facing a very tall but stocky man, dressed in a posh suit and enough wax to keep his hair flattened for a millennia. “Take it easy, Mr. Moore,” he spoke with a smug grin. Behind Moore emerged Rachel Lang, Matthew Paige and very well built old man to whom Moore had no affiliation with. “What’s going on? Rachel? Matt? What are you both doing here?” Moore was frantically spluttering his words. The refined man moved forward, his eyes an icy white. “I’m Adam Duncan, we spoke on the phone.” “That’s great! What the hell is going on? Why does my head hurt and …” Moore was silenced by a swinging right hook from the mysterious man who moved back without so much as an ounce of conscience as Moore spat his own blood onto the table. “Please just listen.” Paige stepped in. “We need your help in closing down Dietetics Pharmaceuticals and extracting the money within it,” Duncan placed his chips on the table, so to speak, and gazed at Moore who took a few moments to register the extent of the man’s request. “Why?” Moore questioned. “My bosses…want to remove Mr. Lavelle and own all the assets…in a manner of speaking. If you help, you will become a very rich and well placed businessman!” Duncan mused. Moore looked toward Rachel. “Are you in on this too?” Rachel Lang gazed at Moore blankly. “I’ve been in it for a long time!” Her voice was strong and domineering, an evil façade lingering on her frail feminine features. Moore turned to Paige. “You would condone murder?” Paige nodded his head with a sickening smile. Moore was rigid, cold and felt very much alone. “I will not help you take down that young man!” Moore spat with a fiery resonance. Duncan laughed, menacingly. “If you won’t join us…then you become a part of the problem.” Moore looked toward Duncan, nausea rising in his throat. “You know what to do Alex,” Duncan said and turned his back. The mysterious man, named Alex, moved forward, a silver berretta in his hand as he twirled the silence cap into place. The man raised the nozzle till it rested on the centre of Moore’s head; he could feel the cold metal. Closing his eyes he took a deep breath… Lavelle was seated in his Viper, the radio going through the motions as he skimmed through his grandfather’s private books and came across one name that held a huge amount of shares in the company. He circled the name with his red biro and pulled out his mobile. “Hailey, it’s me…” Lavelle said, flicking the biro against his jotter. “Hey,” Rose whispered. “You still at the library?” Lavelle asked, pointedly. “No! I just whisper all the time on the phone…” Rose said sarcastically. Lavelle nodded with a cheeky smile and pulled up the bundle of pages he’d leafed through until he found the page of importance. “I might have found something,” he stated matter-of-factly in a wistful bliss. “Alright then, spit it out, it’s almost closing time…” Rose demanded. Rose was seated in the back of the grand library hall; the gargantuan wooden shelves engrossed her with books on History and Palaeontology as he struggled with an ancient computer, flicking through old articles. A small yellow notepad placed beside her, in which she used chicken-scratch writing to form her notes. “Can you look up the name ‘Alex Hewitt’?” Lavelle asked. “Why this guy?” Rose wondered as she keyed in his name. “He’s got over ten million in shares and he’s not in any of the official books…” Lavelle recollected for Hailey as she scrolled through the registration and data archives in the library. Lavelle felt the ruffling of a migraine creeping up over his left eyebrow. “Pierce…” Rose spoke with a glum tone. “There is no record of a man named Alex Hewitt born or raised in Los Angeles.” Lavelle slammed his pen onto the dashboard and leaned back into the car seat. “O.K., try a search for the whole of the U.S.” “That could take me hours,” Rose said with a hint of displeasure. Lavelle nodded. “Look, there’s something about this guy. Just call me if you find anything…” “And what are you going to do?” Rose questioned. Lavelle thought about her question a moment as he slipped the files back onto the passenger seat and put the car into drive. “I’m going to the office, there’s got to be something there.” “Alright, I’ll let you know if I get anything,” Rose said and hung up the phone. Lavelle pulled off the curb and began the tedious task of ploughing his way through traffic. Dietetics Pharmaceuticals looked as bleak as always as he pulled off the motorway an hour later and gazed toward the black glass towers. He quickly moved through the corridor and out of the approaching storm, still clutching onto the small page. As the elevator doors opened he cautiously looked around and pressed the button for the 6th floor. Lavelle wasn’t sure what could be happening, but he was having one hell of a long day and was coming up with blanks everywhere he turned for answers. The 6th floor was quiet and empty as the doors pinged open and he entered the grey carpeted room. All the lights were out as he struggled to find his bearings amongst the desks and wires that protruded from every socket. He found a long line of wall units and began to search each file alphabetically. With the aid of a small torch, he’d found on a desk, it wasn’t long before he’d found a small file on Alex Hewitt. In the distance a figure watched, his eyes masked by the darkness of the room with a sickening smile pursed along his lips he pulled out his sat com remote and dialled a number. “Yeah, he’s here,” he whispered. Lavelle looked around, he needed more files, the one he had was a personnel file. He needed documents of accountancy and money exchanges. He stood up and looked around, trying to gain recollection as to where the accountancy office was. “What do you want me to do?” the voice spoke. As the mystery man turned around, he’d lost sight of Lavelle and panicked. “He knows about Hewitt,” the voice proclaimed, turning nervously to see Lavelle stood in the doorway facing him. Lavelle’s eyes lingered on the blue screen that lay in Paige’s hand. The door shut behind them, leaving them in silence and darkness. “WHAT DO YOU KNOW!!!!” Lavelle yelled and darted forward. “WHO’S ALEX HEWITT!!” Paige in a panic produced a weapon and fired… A burning sensation pinched Lavelle’s chest. “Wh-at…?” His voice went weak as an electric current flew through his body, causing his heart to slow and his throat to close up. The blue screen began to fade as he collapses through the glass coffee table. Paige’s hand still clutched onto the stun gun, his eyes cast onto the lifeless body of Lavelle. “Oh Jesus!” Paige exclaimed, realising he’d sent enough volts to knock-out an elephant into Lavelle’s chest. “What happened?” the voice on the sat com echoed. “I think I killed him…” Paige spluttered. “Check for a pulse,” the voice ordered with a rasping tongue. Paige was pacing as he moved toward Lavelle, running his hand through his greasy hair. “This is all fucked up…We’ve killed Moore and now…” “Just check for a pulse,” the voice bellowed. Paige knelt down and felt Lavelle’s neck. He pulled the sat com back to his lips and spoke quietly. “He’s alive…barely…” Paige proclaimed, sitting against the wall and starring blankly toward Lavelle’s body. “Get out of the office, we’ll send Lang in to keep up appearances…” the voice spoke. A beeping noise broke Paige from his reverie, his sat com battery had keeled over. Standing up with shaken knees, his nerves shot, he left the room as it was and darted toward the fire escape stairwell. Mercy Hospital was abandoned, the last few remaining doctors and nurses packing up their items and heading for the double doors. One nurse remained stood in the hall as she tended to a patient. Outside the hospital a large black van pulled into the car park, the sliding door opened and men emerged dressed in casual attire and holding large crates. Adam Duncan stepped out of the passenger seat with a pleasurable smile across his lips as he gazed toward his next project. “Isn’t it amazing!” Duncan mused. “Our new home!” The men burst into the hall startling the young nurse and her patient. The patient rose out of bed, his thin brunette hair being blown by the raging winds. He was no more than five years old, and remained passive as he watched the men. The young nurse moved forward. “You can’t be in here,” she ordered. The many men stared toward her with blank faces. “Get the hell out of this hospital.” Duncan moved through the crowd and stopped in front of the young nurse, reading her name tag, he smiled. “Juliet Dawson, nice name, but you’ll find we can be here.” Duncan placed a hand on her shoulder and she quickly swiped it away. “I’ve got patients to attend to.” “Actually, patient, and he’s leaving,” Duncan said as he snapped his fingers. One of his men moved forward and grabbed the young boy taking him outside to the remainder of nurses and doctors. “Anyone else in there?” the doctor asked. The man shook his head. “No, this is it!” he said, handing the boy to the doctor. His feigned attempts at dishonesty were helped by the boy who was deaf and frightened. Juliet Dawson was a strong willed and witty woman and was not going to give up easily. Her long brunette hair, pale white skin and luscious green eyes made it hard for Duncan to focus. Turning to his men he spoke. “Set up camp, we’ve got a lot of work to do.” “You will not use this hospital as a place of crime!” Dawson bellowed as she stared in horror at the weapons lying within the large wooden crates. Duncan moved forward, a smirk on his face. “You’ll find that I can do anything I want…” Duncan swung his fist and connected with Juliet’s temple, Juliet Dawson hit the ground with a vicious thump. She lay unconscious beside his feet as he stepped over her body. “What’ll we do with the woman?” a man asked. Duncan stopped and looked at her lying on the ground. “Take her to that solitary confinement room,” Duncan ordered. Three men moved forward and gripped her lifeless body as Duncan shut the hospital doors. The 6th floor of Dietetics Pharmaceuticals was abandoned once more. Lavelle’s eyelids fluttered as he moved along the ground, feeling the cracking and crumbling of broken glass beneath him, leaving a sharp stinging pain in his palms. A small hole, the size of a pin prick, lay in his chest as he turned over and felt his body for anything major. With his throat parched he rose off the floor and had an overwhelming sense of nausea and unsteadiness wash over him. “What the fuck!” Pierce exclaimed as he recalled the events and felt the fluids of his own vomit rise in his throat. With a little more poise he stood for a moment in the darkness and closed his eyes when his mobile went off. Fumbling his way into his pocket, he held the mobile against his ear and coughed to regain his voice. “You coming down with a cold?” Rose asked. “No, I’m fine,” he said, blatantly lying. “O.K., I found a few leads from researching your company’s back logs.” Rose spluttered in words that barely made sense to Lavelle as his ears were still ringing. “What do you mean?” Lavelle asked. “Well, you mentioned a form of pay-off to this chap, Alex Hewitt, so I did some digging and the computer threw up three names that have all got huge amounts invested off the books.” Rose was ecstatic, she’d found her diamond in the rough, the big fish of a story. Lavelle waited a moment, blinking a couple of times to regain focus. “Who?” “Rachel Lang, Matthew Paige and Mirudo Montoya…” Rose spoke hastily. Lavelle slumped backward, leaning against the wall he slouched to his knees and ran his hand through his hair. “Pierce, you there?” Rose asked. Lavelle felt betrayed and alone. “Yeah, I’m here.” “You think they might be connected?” Rose implied with delirious bliss of imagining herself and a Pulitzer Prize. The elevator doors opened and immediately startled Lavelle. He rose to his feet and peered through the small blinds; Rachel Lang was in the building and was going for the light switch. “Hailey, I’ll call you back.” Lavelle flipped off his phone and exited the office, he didn’t realise how bad he’d been hurt and looked until the lights came on and he saw his reflection in every window. “Oh my god! Are you alright?” Lang asked, slipping her handbag onto her desk. “No, Paige attacked me.” Lavelle moved closer toward her. “He did?” she questioned, playfully. “What are you doing here?” Lavelle pondered with a toying question. “Forgot some papers,” Lang retorted. Lavelle darted forward gripping her neck as he shoved her back against her desk. “WHO ARE YOU WORKING FOR?” Lavelle bellowed. Lang couldn’t breathe; she felt his finger tips driving into her neck. “WHO ARE YOU WORKING FOR?” he asked, again. Lang’s hands searched for anything. His vein began to pulsate. She gulped…not a good sign…and then…hope! Her hands came across a circled metal ball, heavy, but durable and she drove it into the side of Lavelle’s head. His grip released instantly and he flumped to the floor. She gripped her neck and coughed. “The council, you asshole,” Lang spat. Hailey Rose emerged from the National Library with a sense of worry and doubt in her since her phone call with Pierce. The winter winds were drawing in and she couldn’t shake the feeling of apprehension. Opening her mobile she dialled a number that became almost second nature – 911 – and requested Detective Mastriani. “Detective Jess Mastriani?” Rose questioned. “Yes, who’s this?” Mastriani asked in a light Tennessee accent. “Hailey Rose, I’m a friend of Pierce Lavelle’s.” Rose moved through the street, keeping the phone to her ear and looked both ways before crossing over and heading toward her Lexus. “What can I do for you, Ms. Rose?” Mastriani asked. Rose was going to lay all the chips on the table and hope for the royal flush. “Lavelle was doing research into ties between Rush’s murder and his grandfather’s old company. He – We’ve - found something and I can’t shake a feeling of doubt…” Mastriani was silent a moment before speaking. “Sorry, run that by me again.” “We’ve got information that could help you find the missing agent, but it also proves that there are black market tradesmen in Los Angeles and I think Pierce has walked into something big,” Rose said, now realising the cause of her worry. She could hear Mastriani scribbling her words down as she spoke. “Listen, Ms. Rose, we’ll come see you. Where can we meet?” Rose looked around and turned to the small Pizza Hut. “In the Pizza Hut in Willowbrook, just outside the National Library.” “We’ll be there soon,” Mastriani spoke and left. Rose looked toward the library before turning and entering Pizza Hut. Juliet Dawson was tired and sore as she sat in the corner of the room and gazed longingly at the locked door. The hospital was quiet and her own blood had dried on her green overalls, the gash in her lip barely noticeable to her anymore. The cold night air blew through the broken vent. A shuffling of footsteps and the sound of dragging feet made Juliet alert as she stared toward the door and noticed a movement of shadows on the outside. The voices remained quiet, barely audible as she sat in the corner. The door opened and two men emerged dragging with them a lifeless body, they gazed toward Juliet, she looked away as they dumped the body and left the room with a smug grin across their faces. “Paige gave him quite a jolt!” One man mocked as the keys in the door locked. “Duncan said he was so nervous he’d killed the dude!” Once more the hall fell into darkness and Juliet stared toward the lifeless body of a man, his head was badly bleeding, his palms lacerated and his posh black shirt singed on the stomach. She moved closer toward him and rolled him over, resting his head on her lap as she pulled the towel that had been left for her and began to tend to the man’s wound. Mirudo Montoya stood on the outside of the door and gazed in toward an unconscious Lavelle and nurse he was barely familiar with. Adam Duncan stood nervously to one side. “How’d this happen?” Montoya asked, continuously staring at the nurse and Lavelle. Duncan looked at Montoya, his shifty eyes darting around the bleak hall. “He got too close,” Duncan responded. “He knows?” Duncan nodded, even though Montoya still refused to look at him. “Everything…” “Then we need to clean up our loose ends,” Montoya said. “Excuse me?” Duncan questioned. Montoya moved passed Duncan and pulled out his mobile. Duncan turned to the window and gazed in at the scene inside, there was already a connection and he loathed the unconscious Lavelle’s talents with women. Montoya waited a moment and spoke. “Fleming, I think it’s time.” Duncan listened to the eerie tone emerge from Montoya’s lips. “Is it inevitable?” Fleming questioned Montoya in a refined London accent. Montoya sighed a moment. “Yes, Sir” “Then remove all pieces of the puzzle,” Fleming ordered. Duncan shuffled his feet, feeling uncomfortable at the thought of things to come. “I will, Sir,” Montoya responded with an astute determination. “I want that damn money, Montoya,” Fleming responded. “Take whatever measures…necessary!” Montoya nodded his head as he flicked off his phone and turned to Adam Duncan. “What’s the plan, Chief?” Duncan said while he raised his hands in a gesture of hopelessness. He remained nervous with a defensive posture. “Got to clean up, that’s what…” Montoya smiled and with a speedy deliverance shot Duncan three times in the chest with his silencer. Duncan’s body hit the floor with a chilling thud as Montoya turned his back and looked toward Alex Hewitt. “You know what needs to be done…” Montoya said. Forty minutes away, Edward sat in the comforts of his large velvet chair sipping on Irish brandy and listening to Mozart’s Requiem. The wind’s whispers echoed against the glass windows and wood panelling. Swirling his brandy he bobbed his head to the piano and violin solo and turned to see a tall figure basking in the moonlight, his eyes a deep and disturbing red. Edward’s heart raced, he knew what was happening, and he’d played a dangerous game. “Who are you?” The man shot a bullet straight into Edward’s forehead. The glass of brandy soared as though in slow motion till it shattered on the wooden floor. Mozart’s Requiem came to its end as the killer gazed around the darkened room, a trickle of blood slid down Edward’s nose. The main hall was aghast in moonlight as the killer poured canisters of gasoline down the long winding stairs. He stood in the doorway with a menacing smile as he lit a match and threw it… Mastriani was feasting on a thin crusted Hawaiian pizza. Collins was sat in the corner, sipping a coffee, finding it hard to digest his food as he listened to the good looking reporter’s tails. “So, you think that all these businessmen are here…in Los Angeles…to collect what they own?” Collins stated, pushing his drink to the side. Rose wiped her mouth and gulped down some Fanta. “Yeah, pretty much,” Rose said. “Look, sweet cheeks, from what you’ve told me, it seems farfetched…” Collins spoke. The phrase ‘sweet cheeks’ made both Rose and Mastriani raise their eyebrows in distaste. “Actually, I think it’s quite clear,” Mastriani said. “Look, I know it sounds stupid, but this isn’t unheard of…right?” Rose couldn’t stop thinking about that phone call with Pierce earlier and her mind was not at rest, she knew this tail to be true. There was a crackle on Mastriani’s radio, Rose listened intently. House fire…Bel-Air…West Gate…All units… Rose jumped out of her seat, she couldn’t breathe. Mastriani gripped her radio and called in. “Wow, love, you alright?” Collins asked, placing a hand on hers. “No…That’s Pierce’s house…” Rose said. Collins realised it too. “Holy shit, the chicks right…” Mastriani rolled her eyes. “Woman, Collin, the ‘woman’s’ right…” “Right, yeah, sorry…” Collins said turning to Rose. “I’ve got some sexist issues…” he said with a smile and hurriedly sped after Rose and Mastriani, chucking a forty dollar bill onto their table. Juliet laid the man gently on her leg and closed her eyes. Lavelle’s eyes fluttered and in front of him was a dazzling looking woman, his immediate thought – I’ve gone to heaven – but as his delirious state subsided and his logic set in he knew exactly where he was. He was resting on her thigh as he awoke in a dazed and nauseous state. Sitting up, she held the towel to his wound. “How you feeling?” she asked with a caring tone. Lavelle looked toward her, he felt like crap. “Just peachy!” he said. He now noticed the damage done to his clothing and body by his recent assaults; he turned toward her with a smile. “I’m Pierce Lavelle,” he said, extending her a bloodied hand. She took it lightly and shook it. “Juliet Dawson” He looked around and noticed the lack of light and the locked door. “We’re prisoners, aren’t we?” he stated sarcastically and rose to his feet weakly. Juliet nodded her head with a sigh. “You should stay sitting down.” “I know nausea and all that…” he said looking back toward her with blood shot eyes as he peered through the thick glass window to the darkened hall. Juliet stood up and moved back to the corner she’d first sat in and hunched up, she was playing brave but in truth she was scared to her wits end. Lavelle sloped against the wall and looked at his body, the gashes in his hands had already dried up and his shirt was singed, but his head pounded. “I managed to stop the bleeding,” she said. “Thanks,” He looked toward her. The stench of alcohol and cigarettes lay on her skin as she gripped the thin strap of her bra and rested it back on her shoulder. A lone tear ran down her cheek as she gazed toward the Las Vegas skyline, the lights had become a ray of hope in all her darkness. She knew she wasn’t going to leave here. She rose off the circular bed and remained rigid as she wipes her smeared lipstick off of her face. The men’s voices became louder and more distinguished. “You heard what the boss said,” one man’s voice spoke. “Yeah. He wants all the loose ends taken care of, I got it…” the other responded with a hint of annoyance. Lois’s heart raced as she listened, she turned and pulled out the small key she’d taken. She moved toward the balcony and carefully unlocked the sliding door. She was going to have one moment of bravery… “So, where’s this hot chick at?” The door opened and the two sleaze-balls that were Montoya’s goons entered the room just as Lois opened the door. They gazed toward her, holding their weapons, they moved forward and yelled for back-up. “She’s one woman, Mack.” “That’s what I’m fuckin’ worried about.” Lois heard them approaching and stood on the balcony frame, gazing down to the slender buildings and bright lights, the strip barely visible to her. “Holy cow, she’s gonna jump.” She closed her eyes and felt the bristling wind rush through her body, chilling her skin, the tears felt warm against her cheeks as she turned toward them, their eyes meeting. “Come on Lady, come down…” One of them moved forward, extending his hand. Lois let the tears flow and leaped backward, they rushed to the balcony in shock. She disappeared beneath the cloud of smog that lay over the strip. The sound of screams and crashing of cars rose into the night’s sky. One of the men leant backward, cursing. “Just settle down” “Settle down, she fuckin’ jumped…That wasn’t part of the plan.” The man turned around, a stony gaze within his eyes. “Call Montoya and tell him.” The other man nodded and watched his partner enter the apartment and begin to clean up all the evidence. “Mr. Montoya…” “Yes? Is it done?” Montoya asked. “She’s dead, Sir…” Montoya shut his mobile and slammed his fist against the wall. The young woman had jumped, that wasn’t part of his plan, and it never was. His only bargaining chip now lay stuck to the Las Vegas streets and he was running out of options. “What do you plan to do now, Montoya?” Fleming questioned, blowing his cigarette smoke toward the A/C unit. Montoya raised his shoulders and sighed. “She wasn’t supposed to die,” Montoya said. There was a hint of upset within him as he gazed toward his mobile and a photo of her dead body. Fleming nodded his head, patting his friends shoulder. “You said she meant a lot to the man, so why not use this as the ice breaker.” Fleming’s eyes were as dark as the night’s sky. “You mean, pretend that we mean business…” Montoya suggested. “Which we do,” Fleming reassured. Montoya nodded and rushed toward the small room, opening the door with a massive push, Lavelle and Juliet rose to their feet, Juliet sliding behind Lavelle who stood with a fiery rage as he saw Montoya enter. “You son of a bitch!” Lavelle yells and charges at Montoya, but Montoya ruptures Lavelle’s yelling with a punch to the gut. Lavelle bends over coughing. Juliet goes toward him, but Montoya threatens her. “Don’t talk about my mother like that,” Montoya says smugly. Lavelle looks at Montoya. “What do you want with me?” Fleming emerges and stubs his cigarette out, Montoya remains in abusive control of Lavelle as Juliet steadily remains shoved in the corner. “Hand over your bank code and all the funds of Dietetics,” Fleming ordered. Lavelle’s jaw tensed, this is what they wanted, money! “And if I don’t,” Lavelle stood up with an angered expression. Montoya turned to him. “Then your friend Lois Wood will die.” Lavelle smiled. “You’re bluffing.” Fleming opens his mobile and radios a command. “DO IT!” he says with a fiery passion. Lavelle darts forward yelling for them to stop but Montoya shoots him with an electrical jolt knocking him to the floor with a stun gun. A few minutes subside, Lavelle tossing and turning on the floor. Montoya kneels down and flips his phone open, showing a blurred image of the dead Lois Wood to Lavelle’s face. Lavelle rises off the ground; he notices a small time and date at the top of the photo, almost blurred out and swiftly punches Montoya, dazing him for a moment before charging toward Fleming. An electrical current pulsates against Lavelle’s back as his body contorts and twists with the surging of pain till he collapses to the floor, motionless. “We aren’t bluffing, Mr. Lavelle…we’ll be back for that number.” Montoya and Fleming leave, but Lavelle’s still motionless on the floor. Juliet crawls over and checks for a pulse – there is none. She begins to do CPR, steadily counting each beat and placing her lips on his, blowing into his mouth. “Damn it!” Juliet exclaims as she resorts to thumping his chest. A spluttering noise emerges from Lavelle’s mouth, taking in deep breaths as he turns over and looks at the door. The house was a murky black, ashes and debris lined the mountain top that once held an exotic Spanish villa. Collins pulled the squad car to a skidding stop next to the forensic and recovery van and jolted out of his seat, moving toward the chief examiner, Stephanie Madison. “Just in time, Detective,” Stephanie said as she looked up from her jotter. “The place is nothing but ash,” Collins kindly pointed out in frustration. “It’s a homicide case, Detective,” Stephanie said as she shoved her jotter into his chest.
