Aftermath Part One: Disapproval

10 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/11/05 Aftermath: Part One I entered my father's office, already knowing the contents of the conversation that would inevitably take place. It's only natural for a father - and a rather stern employer - to be worried about me, but knowing Cornelius Stakiri the way I do, it won't just end there. He'll try to talk me out of the 'whole wrestling' thing and try dismiss my chosen career as pointless and a waste of my time and abilities. He'll try, but he already knows that he will fail. I'm too stubborn to take his advice and he knows it. Nevertheless he'll try, I mean he has to right? He's worried and he loves me. As I took a seat opposite him, he smiled at me. That was something I certainly didn't expect. Despite the inevitability of things, there are always things that can surprise you. I guess that's also a rule of life. I notice the pens and the folders resting upon his desk. They're messed up and all out of place. I squirm in my chair, never taking my eyes off them. Don't do it, I tell myself. You can beat this...but what my affliction made me do, was as inevitable as it all. My father smiled at me, silently watching the visibly obvious inner struggle to control my compulsion rage within me. In the end he simply nodded at me, 'Go right ahead.' 'Thanks,' I muttered back to him, quickly going about rearranging his files and pens so that they were neat, tidy and more importantly perfectly parallel. That's obsessive compulsive disorder for you. No matter how much I have tried to shake the disorder, it will not go away. Perhaps it is the price I must pay for the job I regular do for my father. Perhaps I deserve everything I receive. Perhaps... “So,” my father began, more to get my attention than anything else. “I watched the show the other night...” “Before you start questioning my decision to enter the world of wrestling,” I cut him off, “you should know that no matter what you say or do, I will not change my mind. I will still wrestle.” My father grinned and raised his hands defensively. “I wouldn’t dream of it, my dear.” I was about to launch into a scathing attack on how this was my life and he had no right to interfere, but then the meaning of what he’d said to me sunk into my brain and I stopped him my tracks. I was shocked more than anything else. “What?” My father chuckled slightly. “I’m through giving you a hard time about that. I guess I was just making sure that’s what you wanted to do. Your determination not to quit is quite breath-taking, at least, it is around here...” “Problems?” I enquired, still quite taken aback by my father’s sudden change of heart about my ‘other career’. “Just work stuff. Morons from the physics department can’t tell their asses from a toboggan. It’s just annoying...” I nodded in response, wondering what it really was bothering my father. Mistakes from the physics department wouldn’t usually get him this angry. Unless, of course, it was regarding something abnormally important. “But that doesn’t change the fact that I’m worried about you Alexa,” He continued. I sighed. It didn’t take him too long to get back on the subject. Is this a new tactic of his? Lowering my defences by claiming that he has no problem with my wrestling career and then hitting whilst I’m not expecting it with the many reasons he thinks why I shouldn’t even be a professional wrestler. My father noticed my sigh. “Now, now, Alexa hear me out. That sick bastard, what’s his name...” “Chainz,” I filled in. “Yes, that’s it,” He agreed. “This Chainz character attacks you like a coward after a match and goes about it in a pretty damn sick way too...” I sighed. “I can’t disagree with you there. He’s one pretty damn sick puppy, but I know how to deal with sick puppy’s.” “Termination?” My father ventured. I smiled at him. “Something like that.” “Don’t want to tell me about it, huh?” My father questioned. “Nope,” I told him. “I’ll deal with on my own thank you very much.” “So, you don’t need me to send some of the boys round to deal with him, then?” My father asked, playfully. “Dad,” I began. “I can take all of your guys without breaking a sweat. That wouldn‘t do anybody, any good.” “Of course not,” He finally conceded. “If we’re quite done, I have a match to prepare for,” I told him, already leaving my seat. “That’s not what I called you here to discuss,” My father informed me. “Oh?” I said, sitting back down in my seat. “I take it you have an assignment for me?” My father grinned. “Something along those lines.” I don’t like where this is going. “Wanna clear this up then dad?” My father’s grin widened. “Not really. I’m not entirely happy with this myself.” Now I really don’t like where this is going. “What is it?” My father sighed. I knew he genuinely didn’t like this situation because he almost never did that. “One of my ‘colleagues’ has use of your amazing abilities...” “You’re loaning me out?” I asked him, a little shocked. After all, during the first week of my life he told me implicitly that I was to work for him and him alone. I was too precious to be misused by other people. My father sighed again. He stood, turned his back on me and stood facing the magnificent view from his office window. You could practically see the whole of New York. “I know, I know. I promised you that I wouldn’t be doing this, but I have to this time. He has something that I want and I need you to do this for him in order to curry favour with the psychopath...” “It’s okay dad,” I told him calmly. “I’ll do it.” He turned to me and smiled. “I knew you would, Alexa. You’re my little angel and you never let me down.” I smile despite myself. “It’s not a problem father.”
