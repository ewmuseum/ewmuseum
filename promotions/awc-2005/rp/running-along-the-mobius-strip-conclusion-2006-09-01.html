Running Along the Mobius Strip (Conclusion)

1 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 1st 2006 I had never seriously considered the role of fatherhood for myself, but there I sat in front of my newly hatched offspring as they wriggled like the hairs of Medusa before me. The damn things were hideous, gigantic tube shaped creatures with ambiguous protrusions poking out, attempting to be limbs. Their faces were nearly human, although completely hairless and with little accentuation on any particular feature apart from the mouth. Their jaws, not the solid, bony mandibles associated with human mastication, were not limited to hinging. Instead, they stretched and slowly shrunk like elastic bands lined with triangular teeth, all ending at sharp points and extending around the opening in two semicircles that almost connected. I sat, my gaze cutting through the dimness, in a terrified stupor. The whole ordeal disgusted me as my eyes fixed on those gnashing jaws. It was apparent that they chewed their way out from the inside of the eggs, the edges of their lines of teeth dripping with shell and yolky entrails. They shrieked as their heads swung aimlessly around, chewing at the air- their vocal capabilities flexed to their fullest potential as they cried out like tracheotomy patients screaming through their stomas. It struck me as odd, and although I had often drawn comparisons in my mind between the newborn and the elderly, there was something about these ungodly mutants that was even more reminiscent of old people. It wasn’t as much of a similarity, so to speak, as it was a mnemonic trigger. They made me think of old people- and of me as an old man. I’d be a dignified skeleton, probably still resigned to my fate in this hole. My hair would grow outward and upward in white gray tufts, not like the hair of the dumb which has a gravity to it and a straightness. It all connects to electrical activity in the brain or something. And the worms, they would crawl around me and over my femurs because they have no concept of reverence for the dead- no conscious one, anyhow. But they would honor my memory and my fatherhood as a simple matter of survival. In no time at all, the hole would become far too crowded, and with their unbreakable teeth, they would chew themselves out, unleashing an accidental plague on the world above, inheriting whatever I had long forgotten was up there. And my corpse would be the only one they would leave buried. The others would just lay untended on the streets as food or fodder, half chewed and a quarter swallowed. A man, they say, is measured by the size of his headstone, and after all else, the parasites will be the last to die. They’re durable that way. I had a change of heart after that glint of prophecy. Yes, I was sure it was in some way foretelling. Man was bound to kill himself off eventually, most likely by disappointment. With all of the talk about signs of the apocalypse and nuclear weaponry and global warming, I think everyone would be a bit put off if it was all just hot air. And then, literally from the earth, the species I fathered would chew its way up through the dirt- the endurance of worms coupled with the intelligence of man- and take its rightful place as the benefactors of the most bequeathed inheritance. But still, they were the ugliest things I’d ever seen, and even if it made sense to me at the time that in the event of mankind’s demise, these creatures would be the most likely to survive and best equipped to lead, there was something unsettling in the fact that the seeds that created these monstrosities were formed in my own genitals. This alone gave me half the mind to lay my testicles out on the ground beneath me and smash them with a rock, but the other half of my mind cringed in pain at just the thought of this, and besides, there were no rocks around. And the thought ached in me that it was ideal to assume that these beings in their infancy had inherited the positive aspects of their parent species rather than the negative ones. Perhaps they were helpless and dumb along with being ugly, and we were all doomed to watch population increase as space decreased until nothing moved, just sat rubbing against each other in some sort of claustrophobic orgy, choking on the sounds of our own futile attempts at dislodging. I knew my initial line of thought defied all reason. How silly and somehow whimsically self-aggrandizing it would be to assume that the next evolutionary step- the one that would allow some shadow of human existence in a post apocalyptic wasteland- would be the byproduct of the shell of a man inexplicably imprisoned in some dirt chamber from which he cannot extricate himself and a promiscuous she-worm that exploded during fertilization? And yet I couldn’t bring myself to kill the poor bastards, all gratingly pining out their unquenchable hungers. I couldn’t stand the sight or sound of them, and I certainly didn’t want them around- who knows what they may become? But they were all that I had now, and right there I knew that every mother who ever told her son that he could be anything he wanted to be when he grew up- even the president of the United States- I knew it wasn’t quite a lie, no matter how far from the truth it was. *** Every morning, all the imprisoned and unsuccessfully rehabilitated addicts were herded sheepishly to a square room with folding chairs arranged in a semicircle around a large, rectangular dry erase board for group therapy. Every morning, though, the disappointed grumbles of half-awake residents itching for more sleep or any sleep at all would wake me from my claustrophobic closet coma. I found sleeping in Lou’s janitor closet was fairly easy, nestled tightly in between rusty, metal shelves lined with a cornucopia of sanitizing solutions and rows of paper towels and other various wiping devices. But the grumblings always awoke me, not because of their volume, but because of the thumping loudness of my own guilt as its dull, hot mallet burnt a hole near the occipital curves of my skull. The residents, you see, never really slept. They stayed awake at nights plotting ways to get the next fix, tossing and turning in fits of withdrawal that the night watchpersons were advised to ignore unless intervention was absolutely necessary. Once in a while an outburst would arrive, raucous or violent enough to administer sedation in the form of injection or subduing via electrical methods. I’d hear the nonsensical verbiage thrown about, bouncing off of the walls like a herd of hippopotami loose in a laundry dryer. Normally, though, I could hear the bedridden saps suffering under their tightly clutched wool comforters, too heavy for the summer with the added heat of brow line sweat beading from need, grinding their teeth and singing their crooning grasshopper songs as enamel wore down to the gum-line. Triggered to my feet by the usual morning events, I carefully squeezed my way up to a leaning balance, snaking my way up the small boxy space fit for me in the closet. The disgruntled murmur allowed me to slip out of the closet undetected, blending in with the slowly brooking throng that swept past with terrifically unenthused propulsion. I filtered myself in towards the middle of the throng, strategically equidistant from the counselor at the front of the parade and the two white shirted personnel pushing along stragglers who ever so often tried to lull behind and sneak off down some corridor- if not to escape completely, at least to escape the painfully orthodontic processions of group therapy. The parade filtered into the therapy room, reluctantly taking chairs as the counselor sat down trying to demonstrate a guise of poise and control. With all the seats claimed, some as footstools for the legs of apathetically cross-armed patient, I hung in the back corner like a wispy ghost swaying on shifting feet as I thumbed the loose corners of a cliché inspirational poster that some authors decide it clever to write about in their stories. As I played with loose tape that had grown too old and dusty to properly stick, I read the text on the poster, right underneath the kitten standing on its hind legs as it pawed and gripped at some loose, yellow yarn- yellow being a color, obviously, that encouraged the use of willpower. But more intriguing words were scrawled on the flipside of the poster with an arrow pointing down at an extra piece of tape placed near a crease, seemingly so that this particular corner of the poster did not touch the wall. “Lick the sticky tape, dude. (Be wary of words that start with T)” The advice seemed sound. I had long been wary of words that started with the letter “T.” I had been wary of perpendicular shapes in general. I strayed away from foods that started with the aforementioned letter of doom- or more accurately and specifically, unproven but mild irritation. I kept no company whose names started with that alphabetical menace. Names like Tom or Terrance- they summon images of men as tall as they are suave; the kinds of men that steal your watch while you think they’re shaking your hand, the kinds of men with closets full of black bodysuits. Cat burglars; which reminds me, “Lick the sticky tape, dude. (Be wary of words that start with T).” “Why not?” I asked myself, shrugging my shoulders before licking the tape. The gently applied piece peeled off of the back of the poster and stuck in my mouth as I tasted the slightly sour, chemical flavor adhesively attached to my tongue. My thought process was interrupted rather bluntly by the firm, imperative voice of the young counselor. It sounded out of place coming out of her smallish frame, like she had swallowed a megaphone, as she mustered all of the force she could from her tiny clenched diaphragm. She looked mousy; it must have been her haircut- the sprigs of hair curling out from behind her small ears or her nose scrunched and dotting the middle of her face. Either way, if she hadn’t broken my concentration, I would have noticed the faint hint of urine that had been dabbed onto the tape with a Q-tip as the tape remained stuck to my tongue. “Sir, please take a seat in the circle,” she commanded, the veins on the sides of her forehead already blue from the tightness with which her hair was pulled back. Soon the veins would pulse, approaching aneurysm, as she fruitlessly tried to assert progress. But it was apparent that the restlessness of the group, their cynicism and perspective rejection of improvement through sobriety. She stunk of fear and uncertainty, forced by inexperience to take a counseling job that no seasoned counselor would want. It wouldn’t be long, I supposed, before her will to make a difference would be crushed underfoot the fact that she was here to fulfill a simple façade in exchange for a humble biweekly transaction of some number, cloaked into meaningless anonymity by the decimal cents. And then it occurred to me that the kitten poster was for her. Perhaps part of her believed in things like that. Perhaps yarn and kittens made her smile. And perhaps I was looking to much into things, my mouth taped shut, the stale, ugly flavor locked in my mouth as I turned my head, pretending to look for a seat (though I knew none were empty) while really just trying to avoid eye contact. Upon my visually scanned cue, the counselor, name-tagged Anne, turned her attention to a tall kid with loose fitting clothes and shaggy hair that blended down over vacant eyes. “Felton, get your feet off of that chair.” He acquiesced wordlessly, speaking instead with the quick, careless movement into a lean that made him appear even lankier. I cautiously moved towards the seat, one eye on the lanky melt of arms and legs and possibly other limbs that stretched out with a structured lugubriousness; like molasses seeping through straws. My other eye wandered to the other side of my head, surveying my other neighbor, a plainclothes kid with a bulky frame and short cropped hair. His head was big, inflated like a balloon as I could hear his Styrofoam brain squish around in his cranial fluids. His mouth sunk perpetually open, and this activated a contained rage. I wanted to ball up little rips of paper and toss them into his mouth or do something equally and trivially wayward until he finally noticed. I wanted to point out discreetly the fact that he looked like a butt, stooped atop a fatty neck. I wanted him to get angry in the sensitively provoked way that such numbskulls do, and I wanted him to start a fight with me, puffing out his chest like a chicken, oblivious to slaughter; arms spread in dare and question. I immediately hated him for his lipid, amorphous blobbing. I wanted to challenge him to a duel, twenty paces out and turn and fire at ten paces, right in the back of his bulbous head. Instead, I sat down and folded my hands in my lap as mousy Anne looked at me. “Is this your first time here?” she asked rhetorically, having never seen me before. “Why don’t you introduce yourself to the group and tell us a little bit about yourself?” I stood up and tried to answer, feeling like an immigrant with my hands confusedly toying with each other as they hung off my arms. The tape that stuck and dislodged itself to my tongue and the roof of my mouth burdened me with an indecipherably mumbled dialect, but I feared that removing the tape would make me look like a bumbling fool; one who dines on tape. Conjunctively, I was wholly unaware that the little bit about myself I was asked to tell was a euphemized way of inquiry concerning my itinerary of abused substances. “My name is Jesus Christ, and I’m looking for a band of disciples to do my bidding. Anyone here a meth addict?” I looked around, and on the whole, everyone seemed a bit confused although many gave subtle tell tale gesticulations to confirm my suspicions. It occurred to me that I still wasn’t quite sure what bidding I needed to be done. I had no set goals, only hazy hints. I needed to find my own purpose and identity, and I needed to avert becoming the bare gummed, old man made of dust- the one who can only see himself and cannot be seen by others, turning their heads away from the ugly image of dilapidation that portrayed their own dim futures. It hit me then that I had to die young, gloriously in some ritual of self sacrifice. I suspected it would have something to do with urine samples. Then the contradiction in my logistics started to burn through. I needed that element of my secreted anonymity- the element that I had been covering with a loose sheet, so to speak, that only ghosts over the veneer while keeping the shape perfectly in tact. An image came to me, bubbling off of the whiteness of the dry erase board into a muddy color shades lighter than black. It was the old man’s basement again, lined with those preserve jars on rusty metal shelves. The walls were made of ground that had been carved out, the walls proving not necessary to put back up. Despite the same setting, no old man feverishly tended to the jars of preserves (and nobody could quite tell what he was preserving now) - but the old corpse of a young man. Anne bit the tip of her pen cap, nervously. “Why don’t we move on to someone else so…our new member can see how this goes. How about you, Veronica?” Veronica was young and completely without regard for morals, sexual or otherwise. This is what I looked for in a woman, but the same applied for the other males here, regardless of age all the way to impotence. She looked out of place, almost like she was colorless. Everything about her was pale except for the things she wore, which were an opposing black. Sometimes a sinful red sprouted out from here or there. She was completely disinterested in life and waxed and waned for an atheist death although deep down she was too scared to die, not completely content to drift neutrally down the slow, anti-scenic stream of conscious time but left without any more of a feasible option. She was talking in staccato bursts, with friction forced grain in her voice- a froglike croak- barely answering, only noticed because it was part of the program. She begged for someone to kill her so she wouldn’t have to. I guess she also had a thing for heroin. Not my thing- I never minded needles, just spoons; silverware in general. Leave it for the upper class, I’d say. I was completely oblivious to what Veronica was saying though- or more accurately, what she was not saying. My attention was focused squarely on the rectangular dry erase board. The overwhelming white parallelogram seemed to be shifting and melting, clarifying as if the opaqueness was slowly fading or getting stronger until the voice of some god spoke to me or through me. He was the god of morphing faces, his shining white visage bubbling out of the board as he spoke in green marker lines that would rearrange, changing angles and reforming joints and curves. I read them aloud, standing up, and noting their importance with my vocal fluctuations in some sort of Hitler diatribe, mustache commandant and thrust arm pulling the sun to Krystalnacht. My words were not anti-Semite in nature, but instead were tongued with a more contemporary message. I don’t remember them exactly, but from what was later retold to me, I started off with a convincing call to overtake the facilities. I then shifted gears and launched into a lecture on the evils of self manipulation, noting the unreasoned importance of keeping the urethral tract clean and unclogged- even brainstorming for some apparatus to shave the cilia on the inner walls. Finally, I reverted to gibberish, witchcraft as they called it! The chanting, a newly unique mixture of ululations, falsetto, and tribal clicking- all in whiteboard summoned Latin- reduced Anne by voodoo magic to shrinking and pruning until she was no larger than a raisin and no less wrinkled. Finally, sources say I jumped through the window, breaking the iron bars in twain, and flew away in golden spiral patterns mathematically significant in position to the sun. Even now, I’m prone to believe that some of this may have been hyperbole, but I have yet to differentiate the true parts from the truer ones. *** I had become petrified of death; those writhing vesicles having wormed out like paper snakes, growing ever the more gruesome (though with a slight paternal resemblance, nonetheless) with insatiable appetites and a Siamese tendency to chew on one another. With mammal genealogy, the once smooth skinned newborns were sprouting beards here and there, in no normally such bearded places. I felt that death was somewhere caked within the dirt walls, slowly scratching grain by grain away with his dulling scythe, inching closer to breaking through and severing me from life with muscular, powerful saws using his worn blade. Air of finality swept around me, though it was the same stale air that lingered about, growing thinner and thinner, since my entrapment. I was afraid to move. I curled my arms around myself. I shuddered at every sound. I broke into feverish sweats. The worm-men, my progeny and my only company, were developing at a rapid rate. They had the lifespan of worms with human development incremented along a tinier scale to match. They were speaking in a wormlike coo, not formulating words yet, but sounded omens that called back to the existence beyond an earthly consciousness- one to which they were still vaguely tied by umbilical string, and one where souls were recollected communicating through the last gasps of dying men. My speech too had devolved back to a series of meaningless utterances with no confirmatory ears to grant understanding to my words. Oftentimes, I spoke to myself, but became easily distracted by the overtures of saliva that splashed underneath my tongue or the deserted dryness that sometimes crept about, turning my tongue into an inflated sponge. I’d hold my tongue with my thumb and forefinger, rubbing its dehydrated scales and humming as if I was the patient of my own dentistry, trying to answer questions while having my mouth prodded about with rubber glove tastes and torturous metal tools (though gently applied with only scrapes and clangs). I started hearing those clangs. They rang out through the hollows of my mouth. I was speaking in them, those robotic tongues! And the worms! They responded! They responded like I was some surrogate mother in a monkey experiment. My mechanical clacking cackled vociferations supplemented the cooing like echoes through aluminum tubing. The noise that resulted was a cacophonic, eerie ooh that reverberated, playing ping pong off the flat walls and enveloping us all in some sort of conjoining bond between creator and seedlings. I had greatly underestimated these creatures as never in my estimation could I have ever fathomed their understanding of human emotion, but it was their- glinting not so much in their beaded black eyes, but in the leechlike clamping and sucking of their glinting, triangular teeth. One of them, a big, brutish sock shaped sack of mounded and globular fleshiness, mouthed to me in a relatively distinguishable tone, guzzling liquid throat pouring out a first word. “Ma-ma!” Yes, it was there, somewhere within the growing and dying flame of sound that cluttered up the space and made the thin air even thinner. I crawled to my son on hands and knees- the largest out of the pack. “Boy, you’ll grow up to be a Clydesdale of a man-worm, one day! You’ll see! Now eat your sprouts!” And then, the little bastard bit onto my scapula. His brothers and sisters hissed with delight. It was comical to them, me, their beloved and nurturing provider and creator molded into the butt of a cruel worm joke. There I was, flailing around with the worm securely fastened into my shoulder skin, clamped on the nub of the bone’s blade. But I couldn’t stay angry. The other worms, in a fit of infant mimicry began gurgling out, “Mama!” In natural repetitions they sung out, “Mama!” like the seizure dance I employed to shake the largest of the offspring from my back was some Athenian comedy, the chorus springing to life in song to translate the unfolded scene. I fell limp, agitated from all of the excitement, and the teeth broke their hold. I could feel the swelling begin already on the torn ringlet of skin, mangled like a tattooed circular saw blade. I breathed heavily as the worm slithered back to its brethren to tangle thoraxes and bite at each other’s faces. I was used to the sores and bruises as I had developed quite the world class collection down here, my skin molding like an already dead lunchmeat with small blights and spots freckled around it. I couldn’t help but feel an odd sense of family developing. There were the worms there, slowly learning to speak and chew, growing sharper fangs and stronger jaws. And there was death lurking somewhere within the walls- death, like a bare gummed old man, sitting in a rocking chair with a glass eyed stare that penetrated through time to the hopelessness of the future and the distance of the past. “I got hands, damn it!” he’d say in a musty, cantankerous hoot. “Get your mitts off my oatmeal!” The stubborn, pathetic reaper, in his old age is just a mirror image. The faraway specter of death to the bewildered and confused youth is much the same as the youth himself – a fierce, virile slayer who swings his bladed apparatus indiscriminately ignorant to the mercilessness and random inconvenience of his actions. As time changes vision, shrouding it behind clouded, optometric deteriorations, death too lowers his hood, a skeleton with a skin of melted Swiss cheese plastered about the bone stares back. And it is this sad realization that you have become the wet mold over a skeleton that is crumbling and corroding that stops the heart and wills it to stillness. In much the same way, you can’t bear to feed the old man his oatmeal, sticking a spoon of soggy oats into the wrinkled orifice that resembles less and less a mouth and more and more an anus- one with pinkish white lips. But even more, you can’t bear to watch him fail to feed himself, dripping the brownish sludge into his lap and on his chin and everywhere else it’s not supposed to go. It’s heartbreaking, then, to begin to fear your own reflection, but when you haven’t seen it in so long, you become certain of things. You tend to notice your shape and how it fits into new hunches and gaits. You grow paddle hands to oar yourself away to isles of mermaids, and the brief flashes of lights and feelings of dizzy inertia’s gravitating force become present for longer stretches of time. It was the goddamn light all along. The heat it sent me through flashes and panics and hallucinations. The turbulence, it fast forwarded my mental clock, leaping it ahead a few hours hear and there. It wasn’t the eye of God, drawing me to perennially sunned heavens. No, it was never that. It was the mind itself unraveling in wormed threads, writhing and cooing and biting like babies or old men or somewhat psychologically atrophied young men, haggard and bound. It was that mind forgetting what darkness was when darkness was all that he had – until it was the light; that empty vacuum, that left him standing in the darkles space that wrapped around everything visible. It was him having slipped through tiny pores that sneeze out molecules of being, hypoallergenic. I was the result of a mucous expulsion, peppered through grain sized holes into an external paralysis, unable to move because of the relativity of reacting matter that takes the space where your arm once was before it moved; or your leg; or whatever. Hard to fathom, I presume, but as the room turned beneath the reappearing light, the glow shined over everything- the deformed half breed children and the unsettling pebbles from where death poked his scythe. It had all left beneath or within the glow, as did all but my frightened consciousness falling one way or the other- probably the opposite way from which I was supposed to fall- before regaining my bearings and wondering. “Huh? Seems to get harder to see every time that happens.” *** I woke up fitted inside a plastic trash can with some old mulch- the kind that smelled like mowed lawn laid out overnight in the rain. I woke up with grassy clippings pasted dewily to my skin like accidental camouflage, still in complete darkness save for tiny dots of lights poking through small holed openings in the plastic container. I woke up because the container was moving, the thin dawn beams like searchlights running along my arms and my chest and my face. I heard a familiar voice struggling outside as he pushed the trashcan. Lou Chambers, in the wake of sunrise, grunted and panted before taking a rest. His lungs were a sickly yellow with cauliflowers of ashen designs brushing inside. He took short breaths and coughed often. I peered out a hole at him as he bent over, his buttress arms propping him up by his knees. He wore a blue work uniform. “That’s the heaviest garbage can I’ve ever pushed…man,” he said to himself, even using the punctuate “man” in solitude. He sat down cross legged and took a hitter out of his pocket as I, awake, pushed my way out of the can like a newborn chick, albeit one covered in trimmings, breaking out of an eggshell. “Jesus Christ, man. You almost scared me half to death!” Lou exclaimed, clutching his heart as if he had been frightened somewhere between perhaps thirty-eight and forty-five percent to death. I still wasn’t sure how I had ended up canned in the refuse bin. Rumors that I had flown there had abounded, but my own recollections were jaded with unaccounted for strips of time, film reels with frames eaten out. In events such as this, my delusions of grandeur swelled and I believed that my mind had transcended dimensions and thus my memory, missing from this one at present time, was somewhere else, possibly in some higher plane bearing a cache of its own, full of messages I had to deliver to this one. I was like a permeable man trying to drag a sack of bricks through a wall. And that’s when it occurred to me that this transcendence was what I was about. It all started as a quest for answers; answers concerning my identity. Now, I didn’t care. It had come to the point where I had become too unmotivated to search for truth, and now I was content living in the costume I had worn myself into. I was content being Jesus Christ, and that contentedness drove me to believe that proving a false identity was far superior to recovering a true identity. “Do you believe in reincarnation?” I asked Lou. “Hey, sure! Why not?” he answered back, repacking the now cashed hitter. “Lou, did you know that Christianity is based on reincarnation?” “No, man, that’s Buddhism!” “It’s Christianity, too. It’s all based on my second coming.” I had done some research and some thinking after the whiteboard melted in my mind and all the shapes and colors around me realigned to transform walls into cosmic doorways and windows into celestial telepaths. When I spoke, I spoke in conveyance, unaware of what I was saying, but residue filtered through me- my mind opened up like a portal with two trapdoors that shut simultaneously to lock in the remainder before it slowly oozed out through pores. Until then, I was wholly convinced that I needed to martyr myself to solidify some sort of superseding foothold on the world. I would leave a massive fingerprint, but one with no patterned curves or hook by which to identify me. Now, that was no longer my concern. The psychedelic sludge that was still arcing to a level bubble tension inside my head signaled that martyrdom would be a complete disaster. I could only live as Jesus Christ in the eyes of the world as a maddened kook with claims of fantasia shaped wizardry and cauldron crystal magic. I could only legitimize my holiness through mortal public sacrifice and generations of folded and refolded mythology, so creased and misused that it bears no resemblance to the square of origami paper from which it began. I wasn’t wholly against what further distorting a mistruth to somehow sanctify it. I was more against what the final shape- a pocked and molded crumpling- would actually glorify. A second crucifixion would prove my second coming to be legitimate, even though it wasn’t. But that would only in turn prove the legitimacy of the first- of Christianity as a whole- of Reverend Pendleton’s own zombie dance dogma and of Ron’s staunch urine sampling and white walled re-socialization. To truly save the world and leave it in a constant loop of static ignorance would take my quiet removal from it in such a way to bury me out of the world’s sight without any of the confusion of deaths and raptures and misguided martyrdoms. Lou passed me the hitter. I put it up to my mouth. “Dude, your weed smells like piss.” I sniffed the hitter and made a disgusted face, swimming in the pungent vinegar of its crotched aroma. “That’s cause it IS piss!” Lou said smiling in revelation. “Let me show you something.” Lou led me around a corridor, an alcove created by the art of modern architecture. Nestled in the fenced compound of one of the many crooks of the building’s exterior- one guarded from all doors and windows and tucked away in a place with no reason for visitors or wandering curious alike (in a place behind the up drawbridge and moat of wooden fences and dumpsters and more wooden fences) – there was a small garden, tended carefully by Lou. Cannabis plants grew in hearty stalks as tall as men, budding with moist, green flowerings that glinted under the rising sun. The smell of urine crashing against old refuse formed a vaginal slit in space- a black hazy vesper that could be reached into disregarding the fear of losing an arm to the other side- the deep black crevice from which no man had ventured with safe return. I sniffed, loudly, visibly, flaring my nostrils in regrettable discomfort. But the fucking marijuana stalks, man. They looked like baby redwoods. “Don’t mind the smell…man,” Lou started. “I fertilize my crops with a little bit of the urine from the sample room. Urine that tests positive for drugs makes a drug ten times as strong. Stoner piss makes weed like ten times more potent.” I nodded in contemplation, having déjà vu about something slightly more uric in the flavor on the back of the tape and on the membranous tissue of my tongue. “Yeah, Ron told me that they’d been running tests about that kind of shit. He knows about the secret garden. He’s cool with it as long as I let him run some tests on the plants and the urine concentration in them. He sells the shit man, to like Guatemala. And once he’s perfected this to a science, we’re both going to be fucking billionaires.” “Why the hell are you telling me this?” I though Lou was just a burn out having lost his mind or starving for attention. But he wasn’t. He was a shrewd businessman and only an amateur gardener. His whole getup was a fraud. He dropped the act and my blood coursed with ire. “We tested your sample, Mr. Christ. Your urine is pure. Almost one hundred percent drug positive and a smaller percentage of water than any other sample we’ve seen.” He pulled a vial of urine- my urine- out from a pocket and held it up into the light, briny solutes still thickly dispersed throughout the mixture even after days- darkening the contents to a near brown. My memory lacked me again. I was confused, though not quite betrayed, and my neck frilled with perceived threat. I backed away slowly, my arms reaching out to steady myself- or maybe to appear larger. “What drugs did I test positive for?” Lou smirked. “All of them.” I gritted my teeth, back and forth like the enamel bows of mouthy meth-headed violinists. I clenched my fists and swung one towards Lou’s jaw. It stung him sharply, and he was taken back. He reached for his pocket with one hand, carefully gripping the warm vial in the other. I swung again, this time cutting under the bridge of his nose from the bottom. His hand still in his pocket, clutching a shocking device, he fell back. His body went stiff as a board- all except for his free arm which carefully clutched the sample as he fell and gently guided it to the ground. But soon, that arm went stiff, too. I pried the vial from his fingers, one digit at a time, and I broke the seal. Pinching my nose, I tilted my head back and swallowed the contents. And then, before memory could transcend away again, I slung his smaller frame into one of the dumpsters. I knew now why there could be no second coming. Even I, as an opportunist Christ, was far too covetable for a society driven now by a need profit rather than a need for guidance. Let Sodom and Gomorrah burn themselves, I say. I drank the vial, unbelievably, I say! And I, either to be shunned as a pariah or hailed as a messiah, would be forced to give up on dreams of finding an identity or disciples. I, betrayed by those in sheepskin drapery, would have to hide a fake identity deep down inside, next to a meaningless real one that would never resurface. I was the holy mother, a-womb with the purest of drug burning inside me. My urine could fuel my prospective disciples- the jaded purposeless addicts who sat around in incommunicative circles or shook and shivered in white rooms planning out their next hit. It held escape. It held freedom. But in the wrong hands, it held profit, and that made the whole thing impure. And besides, I didn’t want other people smoking my pee. *** The worms had finally separated, now squirming about and each fully capable of their own speech. The collision of sound was like the searing of broken propellers. As hideous as the hydra of semi-human faces was, the dispersion of necks and heads and wriggling bodies was unsettling. There was a disbanding of solidarity of sorts, and the whole family atmosphere had been smashed by an aura of chaos. The moving slithers in all directions unsettled me, forcing me to turn this way and that until I had completely lost direction and had to turn towards silence as well as sound. The voices too, picked up words, seemingly recognizing themes from my own thoughts if not my unconscious speech. For my speech and my thoughts were two completely indistinguishable things or one thing in completion by itself. My verbalizations and thoughts were one in the same, some being spoken or half-spoken, but when heard triggered the thought in full. “There is no way out,” hummed number seven, and “Death is somewhere in the dirt here” reminded number five. I had not yet given the worms names, as I couldn’t differentiate them myself. The only feasible solution would be to give them all the same name, or label them by number according to their clockwise position in relation to me at any given time. So, their numbers oftentimes changed and four was now seven again and seven became six and so on and so forth. This method presented many problems, such that the numbers changed both when the worms moved and when I turned, and the two happened in simultaneity quite commonly. This was easily averted with the priority that ease of the presence mattered more than any stone set manner of naming. Furthermore, sound had become my primary sense of identification, sight being utterly worthless, so there were only as many children as I could hear at one time. “The room’s going to turn again,” predicted number two, and “Your sores are all infected,” diagnosed number three. And then number three and number two intermingled and switched, crawling around the room in opposite directions and then both becoming the other. I stood constantly in the center, fearing that they would corner me and eat me. They sometimes became hungry and tried to eat each other, and only the faster eaters would survive, but soon one would be numbered as the one he misplaced, or one would keep his own number and another would slide into the open number. It was all a matter of science and complex mathematics of the present, like I was some toll operator at only one point of a function along the X-axis, taking change from the motorists passing by with no time for conversation only counting. “Twenty five, fifty, seventy-five, eighty,” I counted exact change. “Have a good day.” And nobody would respond, because people in their vehicles became part of their vehicles- like a gear shift or a parking brake that stuck up from the leather upholstered seating. And I was no worm here, then, but a man made of dirt, crumbling to dust. “You’ll never tie up the loose ends,” shouted one, and “Your explanations have no continuity,” critiqued four. And then eight swung around and, staying true to homonyms, devoured one, and six, in the spirit of tired riddles, was afraid of seven because seven ate nine. “SHUT UP!” I yelled. “SHUT UP AND LET ME JUST DIE IN HERE!” I sat down and calmed myself amidst the crawling worms, still talking to myself or thinking and hearing my own thoughts over the silence and the chewing- because there was always silence even with the noise, almost contributing to it. It was always there, like Death in the dirt. “This is a mighty big hole for a coffin, I presume.” I put my finger to my lip. “It’d fit me nicely if I were about thirty feet taller.” And then I began drumming my finger against my lip, but I heard the chorus arising again. It was laughter, all synchronized, and when I became alert to it, it would stop until I had regained myself again. And then it would continue, back and forth in coordinated bursts, almost musically as if the worms were reading it off a sheet of notation. “CUT IT OUT, I SAY!” But then twenty-two became one and twenty three became two. *** The wonder drug of my own endocrine system coursed through my veins, pumped by the blood of my heart into the distant reaches of my body. The areas between my toes felt webbed, and my bloodshot eyes, each tiny vein having been popped, swayed between vision and inversion. The pupils of my eyes would harden and my irises would liquefy- the light leaking in around the black discs in an imbalanced earthquake that timbered deep inside of me. The discs formed together to create one concentric blind spot. It looked like an elliptical head; a stormy eye behind passing cloud cover that lingered to trade ions. I fell over each time, losing a part of myself to the sky- to the messianic, transcendent wormhole that borrowed from the heavens, using me as its sacred vessel- me, the only one crazy enough to digest the undiluted mixture seep through my body. It wasn’t ecstasy or any normal euphoria that addicts roller coaster up and down for. It was immortality, and it was death, all blended into one bitter tonic. I felt like a vampire or a werewolf or some other creature that could only be killed with certain restrictions. Except there was no antithesis to the toxins I had ingested- none that I knew of at the time, anyway, so I did the only thing I could. The sun had come up, and staff workers were aware of my involvement outside. Nobody bothered to remember that Lou had gone missing. Nobody bothered to look for him there in the bottom of the dumpster with pickup day approaching. They had concentrated on me as I grabbed fists full of dirt, ripping it out of the ground, grassy at first, but then increasingly less so. The dirt, it only got browner, full of rocks and roots and then nothing at all, but I tore and tore, building a small hill next to a small hole with each getting slowly larger. They tried to stop me- tried with all their might- but I was impervious to their electric shock and their attempts at restraint. I didn’t even know they were there- couldn’t see them. I only saw the black saucer in front of me, a speaking mouth like a sunflower’s head, aglow inside a ring of light. It spoke like a mouth, the blackness almost solid almost like an open mouth where reverberating words, pulsing vibrations, slid up and out and into me and down again. And soon the hill had gotten as tall as I was and so had the hole. And at this time, they had brought out the rubber bullets and the tranquilizer guns, but they bounced off with a thud and then nothing or stuck in with a zip and then nothing. I climbed down into the hole and I reached out, pulling the hill back on top of me, packing every grain of dirt back to where it was before so that the ground didn’t appear to be disturbed at all. And at the same time, I reached for the dirt at my feet and pulled it up, four armed or six armed like Vishnu or some other Hindu deity waving his arms in a glorious flash. It was all so mechanical and surreal, and it all happened so fast that I didn’t believe it had happened any more or less than anyone who had tried to stop me. I pictured them above, all examining the dirt for cracks or disruptions, amazed and in disbelief upon finding the ground just as they had left it the night before. And even though they had seen it with their own eyes- all collectively!- none spoke of it because it didn’t make sense and couldn’t be explained. Profit and skepticism, the two tenets of society now, prohibited them from considering it a miracle, and the manner in which it was conducted- so ignorant to social mores- discouraged them from agreeing it spiritual or religious or whatever it wanted to be called. For me, though, even though my motions were precise and mechanic, the experience was emotional. Even though I was impervious to those attempts at restraint and carried out my burrowing coldly and with carefully plotted calculation, I felt something inside- something no word could label. It was an emotion that wouldn’t be considered an emotion until you felt it yourself. It was a rare moment where paths cross, going both directions, transcending one dimensional direction for the other and vice versa. It was like seeing yourself leave just as you were coming back and tipping your hat and saying “good day, sir” and thinking nothing of it until you had stopped and passed yourself. And then turning for a double take and shaking your head because the other passerby was no longer there. And eventually, I came upon an enclosure, dirt on all sides- not a perfect rectangular prism, but damn close. Some fine work, I say. Damn close. *** The worms had grown to be a nuisance, or so I had thought, until I realized that it hadn’t been the worms at all. It was far too late for that, though, as I had spent the last thirteen hours stomping and stomping, flattening heads and bodies and tearing them apart so the halves wouldn’t grow back and my problems wouldn’t have grown manifold. I stomped until flesh and dirt mixed, lightening the walls a bit with moisture- moisture and silence. People think silence is a dark color, but it really isn’t. It’s very light and airy. I looked around when the room turned that silent color, and my breathing had begun to die down from its rapid-fire artillery pace, and it was so bare and filled with the smell of regret and urgency and impulsiveness. I realized that I had stomped my own half human offspring into nonexistence, though I knew they were once real and palpable; not hallucinatory. I knew that they, too, had once lived and knew what it was to live but that now they knew too what it was to die. I broke down, sobbing, envious. Though don’t be mistaken, it wasn’t for love. They were just numbers to me, but rather jealousy. I was a number now, or a concept- and that concept was the concept of only. That concept needed no numbers. One is pointless without two or three or et cetera. Thus, I was nothing. Names had disappeared and words had disappeared, and all that was left was the dust of my feet mashing the bare dirt floor with nothing between my flesh and the flesh of the earth but the memory of another flesh. It wasn’t guilt that made me do what I did next. It wasn’t guilt or anything related to the impassioned infanticide that persuaded my next action. It was the sensation of looking up and feeling the walls begin to tremble and the room begin to turn one last time. It did, and there I stood, bathed in a pillar of light with the black shape staring down from the orifice above. I couldn’t see the slight tear, the open lipped, open throated slit from which the words came. It’s difficult for me even to call them words- it was like spit, a salivary excretion of wet emotion dripping down from the hole within the brightness, transcending heaven in a magnificent brightened shower that consisted of one drop here and then maybe one drop there far off where I wasn’t certain. I needed to taste this again. I’d eat all the dirt that surrounded me to taste more…so I did. I put my mouth up against the ceiling and licked the dirt, the dryness and texture rather acrid on my tongue, but the taste none too unpleasant. I licked the dirt until it began to crumble off, and weaken under the wetness, and then I took a bite. I chewed, and I thought of spitting it to the ground, but instead I swallowed it. I was a worm now, burrowing my way up faster and faster until I no longer needed to use my hands to eat and therefore only craned myself within my upward tunnel. And after hours and hours, my head poked through the surface in the dark, covered in blood, body still squirming through the earth’s dilated cervix. I pushed myself up to my feet, problematically trying to control my bearings. My back ached, still hunched, and my knees wobbled, used to crawling. I eventually straightened out. The ground beneath me felt anything but firm. I dared out my right foot to take my first step. And then my left. And then soon to come, my first words. The world turned beneath my feet as if I were balancing on it as it floated in the middle of some endless ocean. The city lights and neon signs shined vividly though the streets were empty and the lights inside the rows of stores were off. I walked along the edge of the road, tripping over misjudged distances, hurling myself forward with an uncertain perseverance. There was a strange urgency to my movements all the while, though I had no concrete mission other than remaining in constant motion. I was being propelled by the slow momentum of my rigid stumbling, my body hunched forward against the heavy mass of air that conjoined in front of whichever direction I faced. I stopped walking for a moment a few blocks back, and the peculiar distribution of gravity pasted me backwards to the ground, forcing me to laugh uncontrollably while immobile on my back near the curb. But the laughing subsided, and I was able to roll from side to side until I flipped myself onto my stomach, where I was able to push myself back to my feet after a considerable amount of struggle. Returning to my feet, though, I was facing the direction from which I came, and I found turning around to be an obstacle. I kept walking instead, passing the point I started from and continuing in the opposite direction. “I’m going the wrong way!” I shouted while laughing euphorically and taking exaggerated steps, carefully one foot at a time. “I’m going the wrong fucking way!”
