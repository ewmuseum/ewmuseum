The Ceiling, pt. 3: Confusing the Devil

6 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/6/06 For every sinner, there’s a saint. There’s a man who can look at adversity and laugh, thinking to himself “I can overcome this, I can spit in this shit’s face and laugh it off.” And without a problem, that saint goes about with life and moves, freely and smoothly, like a sail boat through the calm water. Slowly he divides each little current, gracefully gliding to his destination. But like all boats, there is no real destination. The sailboat just flows. There is not stopping, there is nothing, just a stream of consciousness that pushes it towards that horizon until the sun sets, and when it rises the next day, the sail boat continues. The sinner and the saint can co-exist, and they can be separate. But there is always a sinner. And there is always a saint. ------------------------------------------------------ Liam Martin is a simple man with simple needs and simple wants. A bible. A community. And a smile when he knows that his God is shining down upon him. Today was one of those days. He was on his own, leaving his brother to sort out his own shit, and it was his day. Liam was going to visit his friend in Watertown, a suburb of Boston, but he had some other things to deal with. Recently, Liam had heard that Joe Myers, the local convenience store owner, needed some help. Some hoodlums, fucking around, and Liam was going to help him out. But you can’t just visit someone in the Eagle’s Nest without some type of pleasantry. Liam approached the door and walked in. The place wasn’t anything special—it had all your basic bullshit in a convenience store, like the 18+ array of items behind the counter, the gum assortment, the chips, the canned shit. All your basic bullshit you’d find. But the fact that it was the neighborhood convenience store accounted for something. It was a landmark now—if you needed a pack of smokes, you went there. Joe Myers was a neighborhood legend, solely because of this store. At the counter was Big Herman Moore, a local butcher who was chatting it up with Joe. He was easily around 300 pounds and no more than 6 feet tall, his big round belly the result of one too many sausages he freshly made every day. His voice though had a hearty touch that was a mirror of his physique, neck down. “…you shoulda seen ‘er, Joe, biggest tits I ever saw! I was thinkin’, why you buyin’ ham, when you could have a bit of this bologna?” All three of them chuckled, which prompted the two men to look at Liam with surprise. They both lit up, though; if Joe was a legend, the Martin brothers were fucking Gods, and no pun intended, I guess. “Hey dere Liam, how goes it terday?” There was a tendency to “r” Joe’s word. A real city-like slang, something you’d find in a place like this. “Oh, you know. Just stoppin’ through, buying some cigarettes.” “Cigs? Bad for your health!” “Yeah, like you should be talkin’, tubby!” The three laughed again. Even though they were a religious group, nothing was sacred. If you could jab and rub someone the wrong way, you did it. A real close group, these fuckers were. “Ha, fuck you, buddy. Anyways, I oughta be outta ‘ere, got a place to run.” Big Herman Moore turned and walked out as good byes were exchanged and Liam walked to the counter afterwards. Liam leaned down in order to get to ear level and looked around. The place was empty. The head turned back to Joe. “So, Joe, what’s the deal?” Joe moved in close to Liam’s ear, squinting with a slight touch of anger. “Fuckers, dere all over my place, Liam. Stealing my things, fucking trashing on me, I hate it! I’ll kill dem arll!” Liam placed a hand on Joe’s shoulder. “Chill, man, chill!” “CUT THAT NIGGER SPEAK!” *SLAP!* Liam pulls Joe close to his face. This is where the Furious comes in. In Liam’s life, even though he questions his own beliefs at times, he is strong enough to NOT be put into class with those God damned rats. And to be put with them by ANYONE is an insult. “Now, Joe, I’m going to say this slow, and I’m going to say this once, so listen up. I love you, Joe, but I would not hesitate for a second to murder you, so I’d take my advice and henceforth place all concentration on ALL you’re about to hear. Never, ever, EVER compare me to that fucking trash of a race…” “Sourry, Liam…” *SLAP!* “I said listen up, not speak! Now, I shower every day, I pray to the White Lord, and I believe that his salvation will carry me onto greatness. So when you say I talk like a nigger, you not only spit on me, but you spit on the rest of the Eagles. Are we fucking clear?” “Yesh, yesh, we’re clear!” The grip was loosened and Joe slid back to his perch behind the counter. For a moment, Liam unrustled the t-shirt he had stuck to his skin, but moments later, he was back leaning in. “Joe, I did that because I don’t want to have to kill you. So, as long as you NEVER bring that up again, we can just call it…water under the bridge?” The head was nodding before more prodding, showing that a little threat of blood never hurt any cause, eh? “Now, Joe, who are these characters and where can I find them?” “I dern’t know all dere names, but I heard one say he was slaffter, or something. ----------------------------------------------------- “Fuck yo’ bitch ass, Liam, chain’t no good ‘round here!” Liam stood above the man he was pummeling to nothing when he looked up to see S-Laughter, pronounced slaffter, real name Solomon and the street name was a play on the word “Slaughter”. This man, along with about 10 of his friends, was staring at Liam as he paused from beating up that man. The barrels of their guns also stared too. “Fuck you, nigger! This motherfucker was in the nest, and you better fucking believe that…” *CLICK CLICK CLICK CLICK CLICK CLICK…* The sounds of guns cocking back can make any man freeze, doesn’t matter how much of a holier-than-thou bad ass you are. “Chain’t talkin’ much now, is ya?” hahahahahahahahahahahahahah. Hahahahahahahahahahah. Hahahahahahahahah. And the laughter echoed and echoed as Liam stood there, defenseless, only to be silenced by a quick shot to the foot, scurrying away, and a loud thud on the ground. ---------------------------------------------------- His foot still stung that day as he heard the echoes of the laughter in his head, that fucking nigger S-Laughter shooting him right in the foot. Was he going to enjoy this kill? Was he going to smile when he saw the blood shoot out of the back of S-Laughter’s head? You bet your fucking ass. ---------------------------------------------------- And as he sat there he thought about all the things passé, growing up with brothers who never questioned a thing. He saw the beauty of life and the pain and misery of death, and all the things between. Now was not about questioning, but rather about remembering. It was as if his wake was happening before it even began. He and his brother were the perpetrators, the relators, the guys who would regulate the style of their neighborhood. So it was run off jean jackets, that was the style back then. And it was low top black Chucks, that was the style back then. And it was white t-shirt tight, that was the style back then. And it was khakis, dirty and rough, that was the style back then. And it was crew cuts, closer than close, that was the style back then. And it was being alive, that was the style back then. But that was then and this is now, a faith he embraced, and looking back only asks how? It was the style back then, to listen to your pops and all the elders who spoke their word, to shut off your eyes and just open your ears. You could cry but you couldn’t open the ducts, they were closed by the closed mindedness of the mess that was flowing like a fucking river into the stream. So then it was guns tucked in, that was the style back then. And it was nigger, jew, and spic, that was the style back then. And it was hard, rough, and ill, that was the style back then. And it was crazy fucking nuts, that was the style back then. The scariest part was that none of it would change, that it’d just escalate until it seeped into the brain of the masses and shatter the glasses of the leaders, and every minority is fighting an Eagle. So unless it goes through, the bullet striking from the barrel rammed into his mouth, you best believe the style is hell, and the style will be hell, and the style is carnage and all that. But this wasn’t a time for thinking of the chaos, this was a time for remembering. First kisses passionate, it was the style back then. Chewing Bazooka Joe, that was the style back then. Endless cans of coke, that was the style back then. Sitting on the bars, that was the style back then. Races in the park, that was the style back then. Innocence, that was the style back then. And now, that style was gone. This fucking style, you can try to only live a life of remembrance before you take your own as Tim was doing, but this fucking style of his was too toxic and incapacitating to ever try to understand. The gun was the exorcist, sending this demon away in hopes to save. And saving a community, that’s the style right now. ---------------------------------------------------- Usually, Liam Martin is simple with his weapon choice. A handgun is usually efficient enough, something like a .45 will due. But this was different. This was a matter of fucking pride. Because, you see, it’s bad when you get killed by a nigger or any of those rats, but that means you die a martyr. Your blood hits the streets and soaks in like all the other Eagles who fell doing the same as you. Soul becomes legend, and so fourth and all that boosh. Fucking dying for a belief, you know? But when they leave you to live, that’s fucking something. That means the fucker doesn’t even see you fit enough to waste the risk of killing you. To them, you’re more a hassle dead than alive. More of a hassle dead or a live? A carrot. Turnip. Limbless dog. All those things become more important when you’re just fucking left to live. Liam wiped the proverbial spit off his face when he walked to his truck and looked inside the duffle bag on the passenger side. Inside there it was, his scythe, the AK-47. Usually simple is the style for Liam Martin, but this wasn’t any nigger that he was dealing with. This was fucking fate. The AK-47 is, arguably, one of the best assault weapons ever made. It fires fast, it hits hard, and it’s just a fucking badass weapon when you have to murder. This one is usually saved for only the biggest of problems, and fate is a fucking problem. ---------------------------------------------------- What community was there to save, though? What was being resurrected? What was really important about saving these demons? Guilt is something that, when it hits, it’s hard to shrug, so the fact that any of it was being shrugged off by this idea of devils leading demons was amazing. Because, for every image of childhood style, there was a foul beast crashing down and ripping those children apart with its green, grotesque teeth. And with this, Heaven and Hell circulated inside the chamber of that gun securely placed between the jaw and head of Tim Martin. The metal cold. The metal perculiar. The metal of the weapon had an interesting taste. It was smooth, but also dirty and disgusting. It was elegant, but also jagged and edged. It was thick, but also soft and nothing. This was the type of thing that would take away life. The opposites attracted, and as Tim sat there, gun pointed in mouth and mind pointd in the gutter. The problem wasn’t pulling the trigger. The problem was wondering if it was the right thing to do. ---------------------------------------------------- It was odd. Liam drove into the neighborhood, and no one tried to stop him. Usually, the blacks scurry away, but this time they just stood on the side of the road. Something wasn’t right. Something was off. He pulled over and stopped the car, grabbing the bag before he pushed his own door open. Once he hit the pavement, he stopped moving with a gasp: there, everyone was lined up, staring at him. All the black people, just there. The fuck?, Liam thought, looking around. Never before had this happened. Why weren’t these fuckers cowering in fear? Why weren’t these fuckers praying it wasn’t them who was going? What the fucking fuck? But, no matter; Liam Martin just carried his bag and walked down the street, staring forward as everyone stared at him. The crowd was endless—it was as if they were fucking WAITING for him, just EXPECTING him to be right there. But he kept on going. He knew where S-Laughter was and, finally, without much longer, there it was. It was a big apartment building and outside of it was the biggest crowd. Liam stood in the road, staring up and down the side of the building while all the blacks stood there staring at him. This was hopeless; there was no way he was going to get in without getting arrestd. Fuck this. As he was about to give up, though, something odd happened. The people in front of the stairs to the building just parted. Like the God damned red sea or something, they just parted. One by one, they moved to the side to reveal a stairway. It was old, chips off it all over the place and the gray of the old cement was littered with cracks and all that bullshit, like some kind of ancient roman ruin. The strangest fucking thing about the entire situation was that, on these stairs, was one solitary kid. He stood there solemn and strong, going eye to eye with Liam. His shirt was absent, and his pants were ragged and busted. His dirty feet looked good compared to the filthy flip flops that were on his feet. This kid was nothing and would probably end up nothing, not because he wanted to be, but because he had no means of getting out of the gutter. Slowly, Liam approached the stairs. With each step, the crowd’s eyes grew wider and their feet stepped further and further back, but the little kid, he did not move. He was stuck there and as Liam finally got to the base of the stairs, the kid remained the same: solitary. Things seemed too perfect, so the mood needed to be broken. In order to do so, the bag was dropped to the side of Liam and he reached in, zipping it open first before pulling out the weapon, the AK-47. And I wish I could say that the crowd dispersed and ran away, screaming. I wish I could say that the kid broke down into tears and turned away, running back into the house. I really wish I couldn’t. But I can’t. The crowd kept its ground as did the kid, still keeping contact with Liam Martin solely with the eyes. Coldly, he stared and received the same back from that fucking little black kid, just two eyes. “Please.” Eyes were cocked in confusion. Please? “Please? Please what, kid?” The gun was held firmly in the hands of Liam, ready to lay into fools if it came about. But the kid just stood there. He fucking stood there. Fucking there… “Don’t ruin his face.” “What? What the fuck are you talking about?” “We all know why you’re here. Just don’t ruin your face. Respect our angels.” And with that, the kid stepped to the side. This was the cue. Liam Martin was here to slay an angel, not a nigger. Not a rat. Not a demon. A fucking angel was going to fall at their hands. Too late now to run away; it was already a commitment. --------------------------------------------------- In all my years as a Martin, I never thought I’d be here, about to blow my fucking brains away. But that’s the fucked up thing, because now it all makes sense. I need to save the community, I want to save the community, and if my presence is absent, then I can destroy those who invade my soul and want me to do only horrible, horrible things. If there was a regret, though, it would be not having a son. Someone to call my own, to be a second me. Someone I could lead. I’ve always wanted a son because that’s just the type of person I am. Leadership is not easily attainable, and I believe that I have mastered it on all levels. I can lead an army of working men, so how would I not be able to lead a child of my own to great things? He could be an accepting individual, and I wouldn’t tell him what to think or believe. It would be amazing. His name would probably be Isiah—I’ve always liked the name. He’d do well in school, graduate at the top of his class. When he’s heading off to college, he won’t have to pay a dime of it, but he’ll still have fucking money to do shit with. All the colleges will be throwing their money at him in order to rope him in. But fuck that. Isiah would be a fucking genius, nothing to fuck with. I’d be proud of him. But that’s just it—the no-child thing is a regret. I’ve already chosen my path and the winds are pulling me with a sense of strength that I can’t turn against it. I’m like a sail boat caught in a gust; only able to ride the gush of air wherever it may take me, and this is taking me to hell. As I lick the barrel of the weapon, I look deep into my soul to see my son…and there he is…smiling… --------------------------------------------------- Each stair was the keeper of a creak that echoed throughout the apartment, ready to alarm whatever was there. But there was nothing, no one. No bums in the hallways, half ready to just fucking die. No dudes just passing by. No one sticking their damned heads out. Just Liam Martin, step by step, AK-47 in hand. As he escalated, he thought about what that fucking kid said. The whole Angel thing…the problem, though, was there wasn’t any guilt; rather, Liam felt more powerful. Look at me!, he’d think, I’m fucking going to kill their martyr! It was a crazy feeling. But those ideas hit a brick wall and sank to the bottom of Liam’s chest when he got to the hallway at the top. At the very end was a door. On the door was the word “S-LAUGHTER”, carved into it, probably with a knife, the letters straight and edged with a certain attitude to them. These were not just letters on a door. They were a calling. As they called, these letters, Liam’s body forced him to proceed forwards, huge steps being taken towards the door. Each time his heel struck the thick, off-white floor there was a deafening “thud” that seemed to put everything into slow motion. Things were more defined as the door grew larger and large. In a moment, Liam was face to face with the door of “S-LAUGHTER”, the old dried blood and whatever else that crust was sprayed all across, signifying years of struggle in this hallway. Now that struggle ends, at least for him. The door was pushed open, but it required nothing really much to do so; with a slight tap, it slowly slid to a full open-position. Revealed was a small hallway and the noises of someone in the other room. A small person. Liam squeezed into the hallway, looking to see what it was when he got to the living room… “Yo Liam, what it is.” The gun was drawn to Liam’s face. This mother fucker! Sitting there! With a…a… “Man, t’afraid ta talk?” A fucking kid? A man, his wife, and an infant boy, sitting there in the living room. S-Laughter held the boy in his arms as his wife caressed it, the little hairs on its head tenderly and naturally curled. The face was hidden away, but the gazing stares of S-Laughter and S-Laughter’s wife was enough to distract Liam away from the kid and keep the AK-47 trained on them. “I’ve come to…” “Yeah, yeah, yeah, I know, nigga. Too proud, kid, and chou takin’ me fo’ dat, huh?” “I’m not too…” “Just save it, man. But, rather chou than some othah nigga. Look at me, man. Got a kid, wife, shit, mo’ than half the niggas I know! Why shouldn’t I wanna go down blazed like dat?” Liam kept the weapon trained on S-Laughter, but was now confused. “The fuck you saying?” “I’m sayin’, man, is that I lived the life of an angel! Shit, all da money I made? After my family and my moms, it go towards all the niggas who need it! The schools, tha church, all dem get it! These niggas love me, man! Why they gon’ wanna watch one of dem idols grow old? Y’think they wanna see me die in my arm chair puffin’ on some damn pipe, or get blasted by some G who take most of us away anyways, huh?” Liam refused to say anything. “Man, I know you gon’ smoke me, and I accepted that. And my wife here, she know she gon’ get it too, that’s just how it is, right? Well, nigga, I only got one request.” Liam lowered the gun about 6 inches, allowing S-Laughter to feel some comfort. “What’s that?” “Take mah son.” “What?!” “Take my damn son! Bring him up! Teach him good! None of dese other nigga gon’ raise him right, like a damn leader! You gotta show him the good life…somethin’ these niggas can’t!” “Bring up a black baby?” “Chou don’t do it, chou don’t walk out of here with a kid, you got yo’self a body bag you gon’ fill, and that’s the way it is.” Take the kid and potentially be outcast from the community or just destroy the kid inhumanely, or refuse him and be murdered. The choice was evident when Liam slowly walked up to the infant and grabbed him. He walked into another room and laid the kid on the bed. Then, he returned back to the living room. S-Laughter and his wife were there, looking at the floor. “It funny, chain’t it.” Check the clip, lock it in, aim the gun. “Ch’always walk around hard, y’know, but damn, once you lookin’ down death, even the hardest nigga become soft.” Deep breath. BLAT! BLAT! BLAT! BLAT! BLAT! BLAT! BLAT! BLAT! BLAT! BLAT! BLAT! The bullets ripped into S-Laughter and his wife, animating their bodies for a moment before they just fell limp. The blood splattered and puffed all over the place, with their fairly nice living room now covered in the mist and guts. S-Laughter’s jaw was creaked open a little as, with his last ounces of life, he looked around his living room, trying to find something. His wife was faced down on the floor. They were in tact as angels, looking like that till the fucking end when they finally closed their eyes. Once Liam turned his back on the bodies, he never looked back. Returning to the bed room, he took a glance at the new child of his as it lay on its back. The child was of fairly dark complexion and looked like most chubby black infants, but something was odd about him. It was in the eyes. Because, when the child stared at Liam, it stared with affection. Not with any sort of hate, or some kind of detest for Liam, but only with love. Liam couldn’t dwell on this, though, and quickly wrapped the child up. He raced through the hallways and down the stairs and when he got back out to the street, the crowd was waiting there, as was that kid on the stairs. Liam held the baby in one hand and placed the gun in the bag that was still on the bottom of the steps. Before he departed, he turned to the kid. “You’re angel just lost his wings, but I left him cherubic.” And with that, Liam quickly walked back to the truck and sped off back to the house. --------------------------------------------------- The time was ready for me now. I needed to stop dwelling on everything. This was it. Now is the time. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. Now. JUST FUCKING DO IT! Wait, what the fuck is that? Who’s there? Who’s on their way inside? What the fuck… THUD! THUD! THUD! Hm. Well, I guess I can wait. I guess I can answer the door. Let me just get off my lazy ass and head over to the fucking door. I lean in…WHO IS IT?! “IT’S LIAM!” Fuck. My plans, ruined. I can still do it, though. I’m at the point of no return, I’m at…the doors open…and he shoves out a child. A fucking black child? My dreams? That was the baby I saw! Smiling! Isiah! I scream, “A SON!” He screams, “A SON!” And the meaning of this burden hits as we embrace each other with tears, holding the child in our hands. A son. A son. A…son. (OOC NOTE: The whole “That was the style back then” part was paraphrased from a Why? Song, and the ending was parataken from The Who’s Tommy.)
