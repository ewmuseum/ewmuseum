A New Beginning #1: 'Primed And Ready'

7 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7th July 2005 26th May 09:31 Snore. Rustle. Beep! Beep! Beep! Groan. Click. Sigh. Rustle. Snore. Beep! Beep! Beep! “What the hell –” Beep! Beep! Beep! Reluctantly, I rolled onto my back and slammed the top of the alarm clock down. The beeping ceased, but a stabbing pain shot through my hand and up my right arm. Wincing, I struggled to remember why my hand was – I peered closer – a mess of purple and blue. Then, with a wide smile, I remembered why I was happier than I had been for months. Oh, what a rush! It had all gone so quickly. The show had been spectacular... I’d breathed every breath, sweated every droplet, worked every spot with the superstars dominating the ring just a few yards from my ringside seat. I’d been enraptured right from the fireworks at the beginning. Hessian (even more intimidating in the flesh) throwing Vampir Nosferatu down a lift shaft; the captivating war of words between Tony Rolo, Hoyt Williams, Boda and Killean Sirrajin; the brilliantly worked main event between Emilio Rage and Karina Wolfenden for the PTC Global title – ReVolution 64 had been nothing short of magical. Prior to my sudden invitation one week earlier to attend ReVolution as a PTC guest, from none other than Hin See, wrestling had been booted completely out of my life. I’d left Core on March 31st 2005, having finally made the decision that wrestling and studying at Harbrook was too volatile a mix, planning to still actively follow the brand of entertainment I had come to love. But the events of the very next day had shaken that will ragged. It still made me sick to my stomach to think that the internet forumites had passed off Tony Aliso’s assassination at the hands of Knucks as an April Fool’s joke. What kind of sick organisation did they think Core Wrestling was, to lead millions into believing a man had died, all in the name of fun? No, it was real... too real. Tony’s death had made such a big impact on my life, just as Tony’s life had done in the six months prior to that horrible, horrible day. People still asked me how I’d regarded Tony. Had he been a brother to me? A close friend? Or just a business partner and nothing more? To me, it doesn’t matter what Tony was to me. I can’t just stick a label on him and reap the sympathy. The point is, he’d died, and I cared. Cared perhaps too much, as my mother had gently suggested as I wept interminably at the funeral. Cared perhaps too much, as Kasidy had told me after hearing my plans to hunt Knucks down and exact revenge. Even Kate had – finally – let go. And full credit to her, for it had been no easy task. A whirlwind romance it had been, yes, but Tony had been the one person she cared about the most. I was still haunted by the events of his last few weeks on this earth. To think that I’d prevented Kate from speaking to him as he lay, drained, on the hotel bed alongside me... that might have been one of the last conversations between the two lovers, should it have gone ahead, and I’d been too selfish to allow them even that. Did I blame myself? Partly. I wasn’t stupid, nor was I self-indulgent enough to turn this situation round into a pity case for poor old Tim, who was still living, who could still breathe the cool, fresh air, who could still see the sun rise and fall, who could still hear, and feel, and touch, and talk, and laugh, and cry. And cry I had. But I didn’t want pity – I didn’t need pity. I didn’t need anything from anyone. I’d made a vow. A vow for justice. And one day, Knucks would pay. But you have to respect the dead. I didn’t want Tony turning in his grave. I didn’t want new developments while the wounds were still fresh. Give it six months, I’d told myself. Six months. Sure, the trail might run cold. But Knucks was clearly more than we’d first thought, and I was confident that I would be able to find someone who had encountered this twisted Western take on the Mafia. But blame... back to the blame game. Could I really attribute Tony’s death to my actions? Granted, in retrospect, things could always have been different. One fan had even been cruel enough to tell me to my face that if I hadn’t have left Core, Tony wouldn’t have been in the car on his way to the airport to fly home, and there would have been no sniper, no gunshot, no smashed windscreen, no blood spattered on the beige leather seat. I’d dismissed it, naturally, but once I’d got to bed that night and started to reflect on it, it had crossed my mind that there was a grain of truth there. If it hadn’t been for me handing in my notice, we would have been booked in a match the next week on Pride, and there would have been no opportunity for him to fly home. Then there were all the little things... all the little details... how I could have handled myself differently. But Knucks would have got there in the end. Although we had been taking some care, after Tony had finally explained the burden he’d been carrying for weeks without telling a soul, there was always another day, another sniper, another gunshot, another smashed windscreen. Another spattering of blood on another beige leather seat. The harsh truth was, once Knucks wanted Tony dead, they were going to get their man. The mystery was why they wanted him dead at all – from what he’d explained to me, Tony hadn’t done anything other than what he’d been told to. However, I wasn’t so naïve as to think I’d been getting the whole truth from my friend. Some facets of his story hadn’t quite gelled. But of course, I hadn’t pressed him. You don’t press a man with a death sentence hanging over his head. I wish I had. And so, my love affair with professional wrestling had ended. Understandably, the death of my tag team partner, my Pioneer-in-crime, had taken its toll on my mental well-being, and I’d decided it was for the best to separate myself entirely from the game. Rubbing salt into wounds always hurts, and I could do without constant reminders of what had been and what never would be again. It was difficult. I got the yearnings – Wednesday nights had been particularly twitchy for me, as I hadn’t experienced a free Wednesday for six months due to that being the night of Core’s regular live Pride broadcast. There was always that underlying desire... that knowledge that now I’d had a taste of wrestling, I just couldn’t give it up. I ignored it. I forced it away. I held it back. I couldn’t afford to let wrestling wreck my life... not again. I was finally getting back on track at Harbrook... the top marks were mine once more. And with finals fast approaching – the very reason I’d left Core in the first place – what I needed most of all was to be able to concentrate. But then the phone rang. Three short minutes later, my old life was back. I was going to ReVolution. I was going to watch PRIME’s superstars duke it out from a ringside seat. I was one of those few privileged invitees, and that thought made me proud. I realised in that moment that I’d done a lot more since September 2004 than just wrestled. I’d won some matches. I’d gained the respect of thousands and the adoration of some. I’d placed a highly commendable third in Primetime Central’s Seven Of The Best tournament, losing out only to second-placed Scott Monroe and PRIME’s own Karina Wolfenden. I had, with the assistance of my tag partner Tony, captured my first gold in the form of Core Wrestling’s Tag Team championship. I was more than just a wrestler... I was a good wrestler. For one night, I would let loose. For one night, I would enjoy myself. For one night, I would allow myself to get caught up in the spectacle, camaraderie and breathtaking athleticism of pro wrestling. But the night of Wednesday, 25th May, 2005 had been so much more than that. The wrestling was great. The promos were awesome. I could learn so much from these guys, I’d found myself thinking. After just half an hour of ReVolution, I’d found myself wishing that for the six months of my life I’d spent wrestling, I’d been with PRIME. ReVolution was cohesive yet frantic, spectacular yet consistent. Not the thrown-together jumble that I’d been a part of in Core. An itching. I’d scratched my head. A burning. I’d thrown a hand to my arm. A buzzing. And then I’d realised. For the first time in my life, my body was telling me what it wanted to do. My body wanted to get in that ring one more time. To take a bump one more time. To experience the thrill of hitting the Eternal Hope one more time. To take the microphone with shaking hands and lurching stomach one more time. To look out at thousands of smiling faces one more time. And just minutes later, I’d got my chance. It had been nothing more than an all-out brawl. A situation I would normally have avoided like the plague. But I didn’t care. Any chance to use my lazy muscles; any chance to recreate former glories. I hadn’t known who I was hitting, nor had I cared. Only when I’d gone down under a heap of bodies had I really taken stock of my surroundings. Every last PTC guest had joined the brawl, and PRIME’s finest were relishing the chance to put on an unsanctioned show of power. With blood in my mouth and fire in my eyes, I had thrown myself back up, only to receive an immediate forearm across the nose. My final reminder that the world of wrestling was a world of pain. And then it was all over. The brawl was finished. Disappointed, I’d pulled my head off the steel ramp, absorbing the unique atmosphere of the night for the last time. My view, however, was obscured. By a leg. A female leg. A female leg I knew. “Karina?” I’d breathed stupidly. Why was this a big deal at all? I’d had a match with her once, and I’d lost. She’d gone on to win the PTC Global title and then, sensationally, reject it, just moments ago. We had no connections... I’d never spoken to her. But a sudden inspiration had just hit me. “Karina!” I’d repeated, more loudly. She’d looked down at me. Yes! I’d thought. Here was my chance... A raised eyebrow. A glare that screamed out ‘who are you?’ A sneer. Karina Wolfenden had turned and walked away. I threw off my duvet and sighed. Perhaps this was a stupid idea after all. I was hardly a household name. And anyway, who wanted to take on a new signing with the knowledge that they were leaving for definite in just two months? But I had to know. I couldn’t pass this up. At last, I had a goal. I had something I wanted, and I would do whatever I had to do to achieve it. Tim Shipley, for one summer only, was returning to pro wrestling... in PRIME.
