She Didn't Want to Go

15 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-15-06 Tennessee She didn’t want to go, but she went anyway. The woman will eventually forget who she is so why bother? Knowing her existence was an accidental result from an affair that should never have been didn’t bring anymore comfort than the heat wave that has plagued most of the nation. Teresa pressed on the brakes as she neared her mother’s recently paved drive. In the front yard were the two young children playing. The mother was sitting in a rocking chair beside Mama Tomas on the front porch. A gray SUV of some sort parked to the left of the two story farm house. Teresa also noticed the large apple tree that she and Eric used to climb and pick apples from as children was gone. Light blue vinyl siding replaced the wooden siding Eric painted white every spring. After parking her truck at the front steps as she always had, she reached into her front pocket for her smokes but quickly changed her mind when she looked up to see her smiling mother watching her. Teresa smiled to herself and stepped out on the truck. “Teresa, what a surprise.” The mother/live-in nurse said as she stood to greet Teresa. Mama Tomas was still seated in her rocking chair. A half made afghan spread in her lap and she held a pair of crochet needles in her hand. A thick ball of multi-colored yarn rested beside her chair. Teresa lightly brushed past the hired nurse and gave Mama Tomas a hug. “Good morning sweetheart. There is a kettle of chicken and dumpling on the stove if you’re hungry.” Mama never missed a stitch as she spoke. Her aged eyes looked upon her daughter with faint recognition. The two young children ran into the house as they continued to play chase. Where had the time gone? It seemed only days ago when she sat in this very chair watching her own children play in the front yard. Now as a grown woman, her baby daughter stood looking down to her with the same baby blue eyes she had the first time Mama held her. “Thanks Mama, but I’m not hungry.” “The doctor gave her a new medication. It won’t stop her condition, but it will slow it down tremendously.” Teresa glanced over her shoulder at the woman that took over her home. Although her mother appeared happy and well adjusted to the change, Teresa couldn’t help but hate the woman. Teresa and Eric were her mother’s family, not these strangers. While one hand held Mama’s, her other clinched tightly into a fist. Anyone that would notice would see her knuckles white. The woman hid her true feelings for the ‘White-Trash Redneck Wrestler’ behind a smile. Mama Tomas didn’t see it, but Teresa saw in the woman’s dark brown eyes. The feelings were mutual. “Thank you.” Teresa forced the response between clenched teeth, and then returned her attention to her mother who was lost in her own world making an afghan with just a ball of yarn and two crochet needles. For a moment, the old woman reminded Teresa of a young child oblivious to the world around her. The picture Tiara gave Teresa of Mama Tomas and Tiara’s father still remained in Teresa’s wallet, but for her mother’s health, Teresa chose not to mention the matter to the old woman. For Mama to know she is loosing her memory little by little was stressful enough. She wouldn’t add to the stress by mentioning her knowledge about the affair. Mama looked up to Teresa with a confused expression, her fingers still at work with the needles and yarn. “Have you been eating right dear? You’re not looking well.” The nurse exhaled a deep sigh and stepped inside the house to look for her children. If the poor old woman only knew what her baby girl was really doing with her life, she’d fall out of her rocking chair dead. True, Mrs. Tomas knew Teresa’s chosen career, but she didn’t know about the drinking and lord know what else redneck women do behind their mother’s back. The sin called professional wrestling was forbidden in this house and for Mrs. Tomas’s sake, it was for the best. Teresa sat in a rocking chair beside Mama Tomas. August have been a hot month in Tennessee, but from under the shade of the numerous trees scattered around the property, it would be difficult to believe it was actually 90 degrees outside. All was quiet except for a passing car and the squeaking of Mama’s chair as she casually rocked. A jet plane cut through the sky thousands of miles over head leaving a trail of smoke in its path. “Who are those people Teresa?” Mama was looking ahead. Teresa followed her mother’s eyes but saw no one. “What people Mama?” “Those people in my house. They come and go as if they own the place.” Teresa shook her head. She was speaking of the family her brother Eric hired to live with her. It didn’t matter that the mother was a nurse. In Teresa’s eyes, the family was taking advantage of the situation. Eric sends money to pay the utilities and Mama’s medication. Not to mention he pays them a hefty sum for caring for Mama Tomas. All the nurse and her husband have to worry about is the cost of food. “Eric hired them to come live with you, remember? Since we are working away from home so often, we don’t want you to get lonely.” The old woman dropped her needlework in her lap and released a deep sigh. “Don’t lie to me Teresa. You and your brother don’t love me anymore.” “Mama…” “Don’t Mama me young lady! When your father got sick, I had to take care of all three of you by myself. Now he’s gone and you and Eric might as well be too.” Both mother and daughter looked to each other with pain welling in their tear filled eyes. The screened front door opened and the nurse stepped out. She gave Teresa threatening stare as she help Mama Tomas to her feet. “Come Mama, it’s time for your medication. There’s a hot bowl of those chicken and dumplings you like so much waiting on you. The recipe you gave me is wonderful.” Mama Tomas looked to the nurse with a warm smile allowing her to help her out of the rocking chair. All Teresa could do was sit and watch the intruding stranger lead her mother into the house. The unfinished afghan was left on the chair. Teresa stood, grabbed it and the ball of yarn from beside the chair. Mama was sitting at the kitchen table when Teresa walked in. The nurse was washing dishes at the kitchen sink and a metal kettle popular southern dish made basically of boiled chicken and thin strips of biscuit dough with other seasonings for taste. She set her mother’s unfinished needlework on a chair watched as her mother ate. “I’m sorry for making you upset Mama. I do love you.” “Why don’t you leave your mother alone Teresa. Haven’t you made her life hard enough?” The nurse had her back to the sink. She was drying her soapy hands with a dishtowel. Mama continued enjoying her meal as if she was the only one in the room. Teresa causally approached the nurse, gave her arm a firm grip and led her through the back door right next to the kitchen sink. Once they stepped onto the large back porch, Teresa whipped the woman around and released her arm. She watched her stumble to the edge and roll down the steps onto the ground. “What’s your problem Teresa?!” The nurse asked Teresa as she stood up holding her arm. She began walked backwards when Teresa walked down the steps and grabbed a garden raked that was propped against the house. How dare this woman even speak to her? Life as she knows it is gone and this woman has taken away what is left of her home. Teresa held the handle of the yard rake with one hand and untwisted the rake with the other. She could’ve used the iron rake to literally beat the woman to a bloody pulp, left her lifeless body for her husband to find when he returned from work. The thought of it all brought a thirst for blood to her lips, but the remembrance of the two young children eased her urge to commit the self-fulfilling crime. Teresa was no murderer. Once the iron rake head was lying on the ground, the nurse attempted to run around the house but didn’t get far because the back yard was fenced in. As she desperately fumbled with the latch to the gate, Teresa grabbed her dark brown hair and yanked her back a few feet, then hunched forward in pain as the wooden rake handle made sudden contact to her stomach. No words were uttered from Teresa’s lips. Her baby blue eyes fell hard on the nurse. Not an inch of her features hid the mixture of emotions that has plagued her for the past few weeks; hatred, pain, disappointment, loss, and self guilt. The wooden handled landed hard on the nurse’s back and she fell forward now crying in pain. Teresa hit the woman again and again and again; her ears def to the pleading from the nurse to stop. “MOMMY!!!” The simple cry of a young girl stopped everything. Perhaps it was a higher force working his/her powers to prevent Teresa from ending the woman’s life or Teresa’s own heart, tender for the innocence of a child. She looked toward the house and standing on the back porch was both the young boy and girl watching in horror as their mother was beaten like a dirty rug with a wooden stick. The girl, no older than 3, was crying as she held her brother’s hand. The boy, all of 5 years had tears streaming down his cheeks and the seeds of hatred sprouting in his dark eyes. Teresa dropped the wooden handle. The children’s mother lay on the ground sobbing with pain and perhaps a broken back. “Leave Teresa.” Mama stepped out of the back door. She was in her grey bath robe and house slippers. Her arms wrapped around the boy and girl as they clung to their newly adopted grandmother. “Mama,….I…..” “You heard me young lady. They’ll be no fighting. You know the rules. Now go to your room until your father comes home. You should be ashamed of yourself, scaring these two young’uns like this. Now get going before I take that stick to you.” Mama Tomas gave Teresa the same look she used to give her when she got into a fight with the neighborhood kids as a child. In her failing mind, that is what she saw; her 12 year old daughter standing over Mrs. Peterson’s girl holding the handle to the yard rake. In Mama’s mind, she didn’t hold the nurse’s kids, but the siblings she babysat for the extra income to pay for Teresa and Eric’s school clothes. A beige Toyota Camry began to slow near the drive. It was the father coming home from work. Teresa stepped over the still sobbing nurse and unlatched the gate. She walked to the front of the house where her truck was parked. The father was just putting his car in park when she waved to him before climbing into her pick up and leaving the house she once called home. Louisiana She didn’t want to come there either. To have another shot at the countdown trophy was a plus, but knowing who she must face to reclaim it put knots into her stomach. Having wrestled Anton Assault for the Frontier belt was torture enough on her body, but Chainz could definitely end her career if he so chose to. No, the reason she didn’t want to come to Cajun country wasn’t because of another man. The only man that had brought her to her knees took her heart from her chest and to this day, he still held it. Thing is, he didn’t know it. The Louisiana sun was much more humid than the sun back home in Tennessee. She wondered if he would come to the show; wondered if he would try to see her. No, he wouldn’t. After their last fight, it was obvious what little they had left was gone no thanks to her naturally. Teresa slid the key card unlocking her hotel room. She tossed her belongings across her bed and the bags slid, hit the wall and fell on the floor. Finding an ashtray, she pulled out her pack of smokes and fell back on the king size bed. Her tongue moved over the filter of the unlit cigarette as she stared at the ceiling. The room was quiet and peaceful. It seemed as though the mother she always loved and the warm inviting home that always was, wasn’t there anymore. After what happed the day before with her and the nurse, she knows she can never go back. It could be only time before the cops come knocking on the door with an arrest warrant for domestic assault or worse, attempted murder. There was a knock at the door then silence. She was mentally tired. Perhaps the knock was merely her imagination taking over. Another knock was heard. Teresa sat up on the bed. She replaced the unlit cigarette to its pack and stood up. They were here. The father must’ve seen his wife, devastated children and called the authorities to come after her. Teresa wraps her shaking hand around the handle and twisted. When she opened the door, she fainted. Teresa woke to a cold washcloth draped across her forehead. She was lying on the hotel bed. Her eyelids fluttered open but her eyesight was still blurred. A large figure sat at her side watching. “I was wondering when you’d wake up. I know I have that effect on the ladies but never thought you’d faint at my feet too.” The Cajun accent hit her ears all too well. He did find her after all. Her hand reached for his. Their fingers locked and as her vision came to focus she could see Scott’s smiling face. She sat up. “I thought…” “You’d never see me again? Come on Teresa, you know us better than that.” “…But…our last fight…You said….” “..I was going to finish the film and come home?” “Yeah” Teresa reached into her pocket for her smokes, but they weren’t there. She looked to Scott. He pointed to the trash can. “You don’t need those, Baby. They’ll only give you lung cancer and make your teeth yellow.” Scott released her hand and lay down beside her. She sighed too tired to fight about it. She’ll just dig them out of the trash after he leaves. “Why are you even here Scott? I’m not the woman you used to...” “Love?” Scott was sitting up now. He held a gentle grip to Teresa’s shoulder. They shared a quiet stare. He could see in her eyes she still loved him. It was true; she has changed from the woman he used to know. Although she had made some poor decisions, he too was a fault for the failure of what they had. Nonetheless, their bond kept bringing him back to her. “Yes..” Teresa turned her head away knowing if she kept staring into his soft green eyes what would follow. Although her body begged for his, it wasn’t how she wanted. How could anyone love the woman she has become, or even make love? She began to tell him about Tiara Russell being her half sister and how that came about; her mother falling into the arms of Tiara’s father when Captain Tomas was away. She told of her recent visit to her home; how everything was changed, her mother’s Alzheimer’s worsening, and the attack on the hired nurse to care for the sick woman. Scott watched her back for a few seconds. She wasn’t only tired physically, but emotionally. He did the only thing he could think to do. He wrapped his large arms around her and simply held her. She didn’t flinch. Her body fell into his arms as if it was made to. He didn’t have to say anything. She knew he was back, for how long, she didn’t know, but for now and for that moment, he was all she had.
