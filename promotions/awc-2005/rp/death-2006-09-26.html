Death

26 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 26, 2006 Title: Death Section > Chapters Section 1. The chill of the winter winds breezed past, as he flipped a coin with a nonchalant flick of a finger. The crystal blue in his eyes gazed sharply across the street as the cry of the dime crashed on the gravel. With his light blonde hair waving in front of his eyes, he relaxed. “This should be easy enough,” he whispered, the sound just about audible to his own ears. Straightening his black leather jacket, he sauntered across the road, slipping past speeding vehicles with startling ease. “Do you really have to call now?” he frowned; just a split second after the shrill of his mobile phone grazed the air. Looking up, the white walls of the hospital stared back at him, willing him on. “You have about ten minutes left.” The voice on the other line spoke, crisp and smooth. A small smile played on Bryce’s lips, as silently observed the metallic rings on his fingers. “I know.” “So what exactly are you waiting for?” “Exactly what you just told me, ten minutes.” A moment later, he snapped the cover shut accompanying it with a sarcastic laugh. He still wasn’t satisfied with the way things had been worked out with God. In a way, he knew it was his own fault. He made a request, and now he was living in it. A touch of annoyance came over his pale face as his thoughts raced furiously in his head. “Let’s just get this over and done with.” Section 2. Emily couldn’t take the pain anymore. Deep down, she had been screaming every moment ever since the doctors told her that her father had not that long to live. With tears drawn down her beautiful face, she was drowning in her own depths of despair. “Just tell me, will he make it?” she pleaded, tugging at the white shirt that he was wearing. He shifted his eyes and looked at the young nurse beside him, each not wanting the responsibility of breaking the news to her. He sighed, and straightened himself. “I’m afraid I don’t think he’ll be able to last the hour,” he replied in a clear voice. Her knees went weak with fear, as she stumbled down to the cold white tiles. Fresh tears started streaming down her cheeks, as she couldn’t contain herself any longer. She prayed, but somehow she knew that it was just too late. The nurse tried to help her to her feet, by slowly reaching for her hand. “Just leave me alone. Please.” She managed to stutter her words through her barely audible voice. “I need time to myself.” Emily looked up, and then she saw something. Barely. Hope. Section 3. I don’t think you know me. It doesn’t matter, you don’t need to. That’s why I’m here. He thinks that just because he owns the universe, he can make me do whatever he wills. Whatever he wants. I admit, he is right. I am but a pawn in his deadly games, but no one ever said anything about a pawn not being able to topple the king. You see, this time it’s different. It’s no longer playing in a realm by my rules. When I asked him for the chance to experience a mortal life, I never foresee the gleam of chance in his eyes to make me feel pain the way I’ve been inflicting on others throughout the eternity of time. I made a mistake, I never saw his smile. He thinks that by flicking his finger and forcing me to life the life of a wrestler, that I will be overthrown. That the grandeur of my throne will be tarnished and spat upon. He thinks he has finally put one over me. I say he is wrong. I say the games have only just begun. He feels that others will suffer me with their hands, and that mere mortals will drown me with their passion and heart. I can only smile and say that he overestimated his children. You see, I can never be overthrown. Maybe now you can begin to realize why I’m here. In your heads, you are beginning to understand the depth of the consequences of me living the life as a mere mortal. But it just doesn’t end there. I will paint you a beautiful picture of eternity out of imaginative dreams. I have shown you the beginning. Now I will finally show you the end. Section 4. “Who are you?” He managed to stammer as he peered through his deep brown eyes. His voice was now but barely a whisper, the pain seeping through his veins like poison contaminating his blood. He couldn’t feel his legs any longer. He didn’t need to; it was no longer there. Sloan tried to make his finger reach for the dirty blanket to cover himself, but numbness was all he could even muster from his broken body. “You already know who I am.” Bryce smirked, sauntering across the room, glancing sharply at him every couple of seconds. “How did you get in? No one is supposed to be in here except my daughter.” His eyes darted to the doorway of the room, where all he could see where flashes of bodies fluttering by without even a care in the world. “Magic.” He stared at Bryce with unblinking eyes, fear rushing through his spine as his body wavered and trembled in his wake. He knew it was time, and all he could manage was but a silent prayer. “Praying will do you no good right now. You know why I am here for.” Bryce smiled wickedly, smacking his lips with pure desperate satisfaction, relishing the fear that he was stealing from this man. He brought up a finger to his lips, motioning quietly to the man lying on the bed in front of him. “This will only take a moment.” Section 5. She feet shuffled against the floor, the screeching sound from the friction thundering through the walkway. The crash kept playing in her mind over and over again, as she saw herself arriving at the scene, screaming for her mother to wake up. She never did. Now it seemed that her father would suffer the same fate. She shook her head, viciously. Trying vividly to rid her mind of her destructive imagination. The pale soft hands covered her head, as she stumbled against the wall of the hospital. A split second later, Emily saw nurses rushing towards the room in which her father was in. The look in their faces only told her one thing. She wished she was wrong, but somehow she knew it was over. Hope? Not a chance. Section 6. Emily stood there, gazing forlornly at the marked grave of her father as everyone else started leaving in their droves. Tears welled up in her eyes, but yet she could not cry. Part of her wanted to die with him, to remain buried forever. She no longer had anything to live for. To her, this was this end. Observing the rough of the stone, memories started flooding through her head like needles driving them down onto her skin. Beautiful dreams, had turned to utter sadness. The complexity of it all, she could not yet grasp. Sudden anger shot up her veins, as she stared up into the heavens. Silently, she cursed. And then again. Her eyes sparkled. “There is no such thing as God.” Section 7. Bryce strolled into the beautifully furnished room, his crystal blue eyes oblivious to the surroundings around him. Except for the three figures in front of him. He smiled. “You wanted to see me?” he remarked sharply, straightening the creases on his black jacket. He stood in front of them, his eyes darting from side to side warily. He’s a half-mortal now, the imaginative pain of a bullet threading through his skin as his flesh disintegrated into a whirlwind of blood crossed his mind. He winced to himself. The man in front of him grinned, nodding his head at the two bodyguards at the side of him. “Yes, I have a job for me. Someone told me that you are the best at what you do.” He replied. “So what is the job?” Bryce questioned, his brow arching slightly as the took a slight step forward. “I assume you know how to kill.” Bryce smiled. “Better than you think.” The man nodded his head knowingly, and then slipped a piece of paper into his hands. With a flick of a finger, Bryce opened it up with two words sticking out in front of him. A girl, wrestling in the same federation as him in his mortal life. “Are you sure you got the name right?” Teresa Tomas. Section 8. A couple of minutes passed, before Emily realized that someone was looking intently at her from a distance. She didn’t notice him there before. Her eyes squinted, trying to make out the person leaning against the only tree that was buried in that yard. Her eyes widened, she saw the same guy at the hospital as she went down on her knees in the doorway of her father’s room. He was there, outside the room, leaning against wall. She never thought anything of it then, but somehow her mind questioned his intentions now. Emily took a last look at her father’s grave, before summing up the courage to walk over to the stranger. “Who are you?” she questioned, as her steps took her closer to him. She crossed her arms, as she stood about a meter away from his face. Bryce smiled back, before turning away. She grabbed his right hand with such vicious velocity, that it took him by surprise. “I asked you a question. Who are you? What are you doing here?” Emily practically shouted those words in his face. He waved her hand aside, before looking straight in her eyes, mesmerizing her with the cold ocean blue. “That’s a simple answer,” he replied. He slipped a red rose into her right hand, before looking up straight in her eyes again. “To you, I am Death.” To Be Continued.
