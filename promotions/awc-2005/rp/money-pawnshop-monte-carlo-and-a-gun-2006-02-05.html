Money, Pawnshop, Monte Carlo, and a gun

5 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-5-06 Darrel sat in a late 1980’s model Ford truck. It was a faded blue and grey two-toned, long-wheel-base and regular cab. He stuffed a wad of Copenhagen in his lower lip then looked at his watch. The late afternoon November wind brushed against his bare forearms and through the cut-off sleeves of his flannel shirt. He removed his camouflage cap and used the same arm to wipe the sweat from his brow. The cap came from Wal-Mart and the fishhook that decorated the bill came from his tackle box. Sure, he could have bought a hook specially made for cap bills, but he was cheep like that. After Darrel spat the dark colored dip into an empty Mt. Dew bottle, he looked over his shoulder to the man sitting quietly beside him. “Damn women. You can’t count on ‘em ya know. Never on time. What the hell you doin’?” The other man stood good six foot five inches and built like a horse. He raised a brown eyebrow as he looked in the side view mirror of the passenger side. He was combing his thick brown mustache with a small black comb. Everyone knew him as just Russell. He did not want to be there but the promise of good money is hard to turn down. As far as he was concerned, Darrel had to meet some guys and settle a money problem. Extra muscle was needed just in case. All Darrel had for back up was his cousin Teresa. Russell knew her, seen her on television a few times. Seriously, what damage can a female wrestler do anyway? _____ She had a couple of hours before her meeting with Darrel; a couple of hours to come up with the five grand he owed this Miguel. Ordinarily, she would tell Darrel to fix his own problems but she knew he would just get himself killed for being stupid. Teresa could not live with that. Family was family, bad blood or not. An idea came to mind and at the next stop, she made a left toward Downtown. Downtown, a very small peck of a place just outside of Nashville served for the most part a tourist attraction. One would receive the feeling of nostalgia if he/she would drive though the narrow street. On both sides was what was left of the ‘mom and pop’ stores. What was once family owned and operated was bought out by the county for the sake of historic preservation. In other words, ‘Mom and Pop’ no longer own these businesses. The half century remolded buildings was the only remnants of the town’s true history. Among these businesses was a pawnshop. The building itself was made with brown and sand colored fieldstone. A white sign hung over the brown door. In a rusty red colored letters read “Bob’s Pawn Shop”. Teresa pulled into the gravel parking area in the back and parked the Monte Carlo. The traffic was growing due to the upcoming lunch hour. Not many tourists this time of year, so the traffic was bearable despite the two lanes and one traffic light. A bell on the door jingled as Teresa stepped inside. A tall lanky red headed fellow stood behind the glass display counter wiping it with a paper towel. Inside the case was a variety of second hand and pawned jewelry-mostly small diamond rings. Handguns of different makes and models were also inside this glass case. Hanging from the wooden walls were dozens of shotguns. In the widow, a red Fender Strat guitar sat with a five hundred dollar price tag tied loosely on the neck. The room smelled of gunpowder and Windex. “Teresa! Long time no see. What you been into girl?” The lanky red haired man dropped the paper towel into a small trashcan giving Teresa his full attention. He appeared to be close to her age, maybe older. Teresa scanned the room and picked out a 12 gauge that hung on the wall. She aimed it at nothing in particular as she examined the gun. “Where have you been all these years Bob? In a hole? Turn on your television sometime. Listen, I’m not here for chit chat.” Teresa replaced the 12 gauge back where it hung and walked to the door. She turned the Open sign around so that it would read “Closed”, locked the door, and then approached the counter where Bob stood. He watched her with suspicion. “Whoa Tomas, I’m not into that stuff anymore. I got out about 2 yrs ago. Honest to God, I’m clean. So don’t even bother asking.” A large semi drove by causing the windows to rattle inside. Teresa leaned over the counter and gave Bob a smile. Her baby blues eyes stared a hole through his lying green eyes. “Bullshit Bob. I know you a bit too much. It was no secret the shit you were getting into in high school. Your lucky ass was just too smart to get caught. I’m not an idiot. It’s not like you to just up and leave easy money. This pawn shop is just a cover up.” Bob’s suspicion rose. The expression on his face tightened. She and Bob went way back. They were good friends. In high school, he got caught in with some dealings she did not want any part of. He eventually went his own way digging deeper into the underground while Teresa dug her own hole by entering the wrestling business. Bob opened his mouth to speak, but Teresa interrupted. “I need your help. It’s a pretty huge favor but I can repay you in time.” This was a long shot, Teresa knew. Who in their right mind would ask for five grand and actually expect to get it? Bob sat down on his stool and watched her as she explained the situation. “I hope Darrel realizes how much he owes you. I can loan you the money, but considering that’s a hell of a lot for a small town like this, I need collateral.” Teresa raised her eyebrows feeling almost stupid. This WAS a pawnshop but what does she have that she can hock? Bob crossed his skinny arms over his chest and tilted his head toward the door. “I watched you drive by earlier in that Monte Carlo. Give me the keys and I’ll give you the money.” “What?! My Monte? I can’t just hand you the keys. What am I supposed to drive? I have an appointment soon and I can’t be late. C’mon Bob. Help out an old friend.” Bob sighed and slid off his barstool. He paced the small space behind the glass counter for a moment while Teresa watched impatiently. “Listen, if you won’t help me, fine. I’ll just leave. But if those damn Mexicans kill my cousin, his blood is on your hands and when I get done with ya, you’ll wish you were dead.” Teresa turned to leave. Her hand reached the doorknob. “Teresa, wait. I’ll give you the money, but you have to do this one thing for me.” He called her bluff. Teresa knew he would. He was no cleaner than the inside of her car’s muffler. Like everything else in life, nothing came free, especially money. Teresa turned around and faced Bob. The tall lanky red haired man had resumed his cleaning of the display case. A cunning expression played on his face. Teresa narrowed her eyes in a suspicious stare. She knew where this was leading and she didn’t like it one bit “No, Bob. Just loan me the money and I promise, when my career builds back up, I’ll pay you back. You got my word.” Bob shrugged and he placed the dirty towel in a basket and set the glass cleaner on top of a file cabinet behind him. He had her where he wanted her and she knew it. He could use the extra help and who would suspect THE American Woman, Teresa Tomas to be involved with the organization? “It’s you’re call girl. Take the money and do a few simple favors for me or walk out and risk your cousin being killed. Teresa sighed. She could have easily walked out of the pawnshop and let Darrel deal with his own problem. It’s only a matter of time before he would get himself beat down, busted, or worse, killed. She also knew that she could not live with herself if Darrell got himself killed. Teresa would agree with Bob’s deal only to save her cousin’s life and nothing more. “Fine, I’ll do it. Nothing too big. I’m not a criminal.” ______ The grey Monte Carlo emerged over the horizon. Its square headlights, solid grill and dark tinted windows made the machine look menacing. Its duel exhaust only added to the illusion. By the time Teresa parked the car beside Darrell’s Truck, her cousin was already approaching her. He rested his hand on top of the car and leaned down into the driver side window. Teresa was sitting back in her seat. Her wrist remained on the wooden steering wheel and her eyes stared out the windshield. Through the corner of her eye, she could see Darrel as he spat a dark color into his Mt. Dew bottle. “It’s about time you got here. I was beginning to think you bailed out on me. Got the money?” Without even blinking, Teresa reached across the seat and grabbed a black duffel bag. She slung it out the open window to Darrel. For all she has done for him and this was how he treated her. He did not realize how lucky he was that she had not stepped out of the car yet, because if she had, she would have kicked his ass. Teresa reaches over the dash and grabbed a pack of cigarettes. It was then she noticed the tall brown haired main leaning against the hood of Darrel’s truck puffing on a Marlboro. Teresa lights her Winston Light and nodded her head toward the man. “Who’s that?” Darrel had opened the duffel bag and was rummaging though the money. He shrugged. “Him? Oh, that’s Russell. I met him at Joe’s Bar last night. Told him about my situation and he volunteered to help. All I had to do was buy him a carton of smokes.” Another vehicle came to view. It was a dark green SUV. Darrel looked up, noticed the SUV and began to walk toward his truck. Russell had flicked his cigarette to the side and was watching the SUV approach. Teresa stepped out of her car and she too flicked her finished cancer stick to the side. She approached her cousin and his new friend. If all went well, Darrel would give Miguel what he owes and that will be the end of it. If not, let us just hope his new friend is as tough as he looks. A set of Latino twins followed by a shorter man with a mustache stepped out of the SUV. All three men had dark hair, and darker eyes. The man with the mustache stepped out from in between the twins and approached Darrell. He caught a glimpse of the duffel bag then Teresa and grinned. “You flatter me D. The money and a chica? Really, you didn’t have to” The twins chuckled finding amusement in the man’s insult to Teresa. Compared to all the men in the vicinity, Teresa looked to be lacking in strength. Teresa stepped up as if she were to attack the short man, but Darrel pushed her back. “Take the damn money and go to hell Miguel. Your sorry wetback ass is lucky I’m giving you the money” “Darrel!” Teresa snapped. “Shut your trap and give him the bag. Let’s get this over with.” Darrel growled under his breath and shoved the black duffel bag of money at Miguel. In the meantime, Russell stood silently smoking another cigarette. It was too late for Teresa’s clumsy-mouthed cousin. Miguel pulled out a handgun he was carrying and having expected a trap, Darrel too brought his gun. Before Darrel could have a hand on his gun, Russell grabbed him by the arms and pulled him back. Miguel pointed his gun to Teresa. “Hey puta, grab his gun and drop it” Carefully, Teresa reached for Darrel’s gun and the rest happened so quickly, she hardly had time to say “Dammit”. As soon as Darrel’s gun hit the ground, he kicked Russell in the shin with the heel of his steel toe boot. Russell groaned, but kept his grip on Darrel. A gun fired and a scream echoed in her head. Teresa felt a massive body slam her into the ground. It was not until she felt something warm soak her t-shirt that she realized what had happened. Miguel and the twins were gone. Russell too left and Darrel was on his knees crying like a baby. The massive body was lying on top of her still. She pushed at the body but paused when she saw the face of her older brother Eric. He had taken the bullet.
