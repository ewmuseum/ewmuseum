i've seen what happens to the wicked and proud when they decide to take on the throne for the crown

15 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: earlier What the hell is that shit, I ask? ”What the hell is that shit?” “I just-“ ”What the hell… is that shit, I mean, honestly? You, $#@&%@, you of all people know why I’m doing this in the first place.” Currently thirty-five miles out of Wickenburg, Arizona, and I’m about to get on the transfer to Interstate 17 in about twenty-five more, and from there it’s smooth sailing ‘til Phoenix. ”Aside from the mountains…” She mumbles somewhat. I bite my lip. ”This whole thing, it all started out because of one common goal: success. It’s in the same basket as recognition, wealth, security, and a having a fucking good time. Now until a few months ago, I’ve made somewhat of a reputation to live like a fucking rock-star.” It’s hard to grip the steering wheel and look so intensely at the road, avoiding stares. Sometimes I pretend like I’m rocking out when I talk loudly, which completely backfires in my face when I’m arguing. It’s sort of hard to maintain an edge when you’re insecurities of driving past strangers gets the better of you. She’s awesome at compensating for my nuances, but these wacky ideas she keeps having- ”It isn’t wacky.” An interruption; how surprising. “Who’s to say you can’t turn heads and make people recognize you while wearing a hood?” My eyes flutter down at the blinking orange annoyance in the dash. “You aren’t even that good and you don’t have to be, but with experience and your knack of causing trouble, I don’t think we’ll have any trouble getting your name out there.” The gas-light keeps fading in and out. ”You don’t have to carry the burden of your name in somewhere new, where no one will even anticipate the type of threat you can pose. A fresh name and face that can take the world by storm!” I need gas. ”Will you please just focus on this for one second-“ I’ve got to ge—“gotta get over!” “What the hell are you doing?” she asks, grabbing the coat-hanger-handle-thingy. I don’t even have to look at her to know the look she’s giving me: single raised eyebrow, snarled lip, squinty eyes. But it doesn’t matter. I grab the wheel and swerve over. The exit’s coming up and I really have to get gas. I cross one lane, and it’s clear. This time of day, no one’s really speeding. It’s easy to swerve between a few bastards who are so bored in life that they won’t even speed on a highway. ”Watch out.” She worries. Second lane = Crossed. Flash a cocky smile. Cock your head and extend your chin. “That’s just the way I do.” And finally, add the wink. Not really seriously, but because it’s what an asshole would do. And the third lane is- Honk. ”SHIT!—oh my God, did I hit him?” No crash. Relax. ”Relax, and look at your frickin’ blind spots for a change.” I glance at my rear-view, and an angry, middle-aged man was gripping his steering wheel as if it were his fucking M4 in an Afghani desert. He was driving a Benz, so I can sorta see why he’d be pissed. ”Well, it is a Mercedes.” No shit, but, hey, no problems. I didn’t hit him, an accident was avoided, it’s all good. The exit’s coming up, and this guy will be off my ass. ”He has to drive by, you know.” She says. I don’t look at her though; I keep staring in my rear-view mirror. Staring at him. “Once he gets enough room, he’s going to quickly dart out to the side, pass by just as fast and give you, probably the dirtiest look you’ll ever get in your life.” “But why?” Why can’t this asshole just fucking drive past me and ignore me? His car wasn’t hurt. ”He doesn’t care. Well, he cares, but-“ And sure enough, he whizzes by, and I receive the full-frontal snarl. Just as quick as I saw it he was gone, and I was left with crushed self-esteem. ”-but he has to make himself feel better somehow. At least this way he is expressing how he feels short of putting a bullet in your head. There are people that do that, you know...” Whatever. I’m off the freeway. There’s a 7Eleven right here and I need gas. “Take it you can’t throw down a few bucks?” I ask. Again, the dirtiest look I have ever seen. “Holy shit, you need to learn when I say things jokingly or not, that’s the whole point-“ ”It isn’t what you say, I know what you’re going to say. It’s how you say it.” Blah blah blah, I throw the shit-heap in to park and head inside. It’s a set of glass doors covered with cardboard, a ghetto way of keeping out light in the fucking bright desert. I’d do the same. The store is ordinary; a few aisles littered with candy, a fridge on the back-end for drinks and shit, an ice cooler on the right of the check-out area, and some fucking crazy-looking Apache or something working at the counter. And you guys may think I’m kidding, but I’m not. This dude was fucking crazy looking. His front teeth, they stuck out like something from Spy Kids: 3-D, them shits were about to slit your throat from over the counter. And the freaky thing was that HE HAD TWO ROWS OF THEM. No shit, two sets of front teeth, one after another, all angled out so it looked like he was unsheathing an army of swords every time he smiled. You can believe me, this mother fucker was smiling. ”Sup, man.” He says. I dig in to my pocket, pull out a stack of loose bills and coins, and slam it on the counter. Judging the clerk alone, I do not trust a single product in this store and sure as hell wouldn’t be getting gas here had circumstances be different. Counting the change, I mutter—“Uh, 11.68 on pump…” Shit, what pump was I on? ”My name is Squirrel.” Wait a second, forget the pump. What the fuck did he just say? ”I’m sorr-“ ”Squirrel. My name is Squirrel, dawg.” Oh shit. ”You look like you like to party...” I exchange glances. “Guess what?” He actually pauses, and the sad thing is I could see it from even before I walked in the store. “So do I.” ”That’s awesome, man! Hey could you do me a favor I really need this gas on whatever pump is out there so if you could just please that’d be really great-“ ”Dawg, I know EVERYONE in South Phoenix. EVERYONE. You ever need anything, hit me up, and I will hook you up. Anything you want, dawg. Just ask for Squirrel.” BUTWEAREINFUCKINGWICKENBURGTHATSOVERAHUNDREDMILESAWAYYOUSTUPIDFUCK. ”Alright, well, can you hook up some gas?” ”Sure, how much do you wa-“ ”ELEVEN SIXTY-EIGHT!” I turn out the window, but cardboard blocks my vision. Behind the counter, a window is un-blocked, giving Squirrel a clear view. “Can you see my car? I forgot which pump it was on.” ”Which car is it?” he asks, looking out the window. ”It’s the one with the-“ ”Holy Jeez, is it that pile of shit?” And its times like these I thank God for Indians. ”Yes. It’s that. Which pump is it on?” ”Eight, dawg. Don’t worry, I’ll hook you up. And remember… South P-Town, ask for Squirrel.” ”Whatever.” I ran out the store and to the pump. Another person pulls up; I notice we’re the only two cars here. The other driver exits his car across the lot, and begins to head inside the forbidden gas station. ”I strongly recommend not entering that store. Unless you like crack-head injuns, of course.” He was continuing on his path, unphased and ignorant of that which is Squirrel. Quick, think of something that will scare him! ”Uh… he eat’s the faces of cats and squirrels! There’s blood all over the counter!” The man shook his head in disgust, and decided to pay with his debit card. Good choice, and now he doesn’t have to lobotomize his self to live normally once again. Myself, on the other hand… “That crazy, huh?” she says. I take the nozzle, unscrew the hatch, and slide in the pump. Everything is good to go. So, how come when I select my fuel grade, it says I have to go pay inside? Maybe it’s a mistake, I think. Maybe I have to press it again, so I press it again. It still doesn’t work. So, I panic. I begin to look at the other pumps, for some sort of sign. And off in the distance… “Holy shit!” This is wrong. I paid for gas god dammit! ”Yes! This is greater than anything I ever could expect.” From where I hear this, it’s this guy, in the corner of my eye. So I look towards him, the guy I advised not to go inside, and guess what I see. Go on, fucking guess. Pump 8. “I just got about twelve bucks in free gas!” I glance at my own station, and see the number 5. Now you tell me, what was the point of all that? ”No one should ever have to go through that.” I said, sticking my head in the car. And for some reason the creepiest smile that was glowing from her pearly whites was the seldom scene that could provide comfort at this moment.
