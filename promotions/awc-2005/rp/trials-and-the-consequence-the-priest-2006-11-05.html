Trials and the Consequence - The Priest

5 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/11/06 A small plink as something liquid hit the cold marble of the church was all that could be heard when I walked in. No lights, no candles were on, only the moonlight of the evening shed any luminance in the holy building. Me, still clothed in nothing more than my boxers, still feeling slightly cold at this point, and the eyes of God hanging from the cross that lay before me. The plink of liquid slowed down as did I and unconsciously I brushed my back. My hand lay smeared with blood. Ignoring it I padded as best I could down the cold hard stone of the floor, heading towards the sanctuary at the back of the church, a place where I knew that Father Pearson would be. It was a glimmer down at the end of the path I had to walk between the pews, my fading eyes belying my sapping strength. I had to make it to there, I had to reach that door and see Father Pearson. A slip on the smooth marble and I crashed sideways into one of the pews, its jutting wood stabbing into my side. I winced and felt a bit of blood pour from the side, tarnishing the pale and shiny floor. Grunting I bit down hard on my lip to keep from crying out and disturbing the serenity of this place, hoping to make it to the door before someone could stop me and leave me short. It was close now, so very close and I reached out, seeing the door and all the potential and hope behind it. The burnished wooden door loomed large and begged for me to land my hand on the golden handle, to push it open and find the person I wanted. It called out to me…and I slumped forwards, thudding against the door and seeing it fly open in front of me, my body being tossed with it to the ground. In front of me lay feet…and then darkness. *** Again a splashing of liquid, but this time it was clear that water was cascading down from my forehead and soothing the cuts and bruises on me. Some of them burned with bits of gravel and dirt locked in them, but hands were tending to me, pushing them out to heal my skin. The hands were rough and unforgiving in their attempt to do such a thing but I could not stop them from their work. It was necessary, it was needed and I endured the pain. ‘Why are you here Jack?’ The voice was trembling with every word that escaped from the lips of the man, and the hands began to mimic this, shakily attending to my wounds. The man awaited my reply. I didn’t even need to open my eyes to know that it was the priest himself. ‘…Don’t you know the answer to that already priest?’ My own voice felt raspy and raw. My hand tugged at it, trying to soothe the pain, but it was nothing that wouldn’t be dealt with all in good time. There were more pressing needs. I opened my eyes and looked up at Father Pearson, the priest of Kingston. He was different from before. Where strength resided now was the ageing signs of worry and hardship. His hands still shook and you could see the amount of wrinkles and marks of time were catching up with him. Perhaps the age with which I thought he was, was just a bit off. He still stood there and sighed, judging me from the look on my face. His sighs were slow and resigned, but wary, almost nervous at the entire scene that he now faced. His words that escaped were careful and guarded but gave away far too much. ‘Where is she?’ ‘I don’t know…maybe you should be telling me why she was put in that position in the first place.’ He broke down then, sobbing into his hands and turned away from me. Steadying himself by placing his hands onto the table that now lay in front of him, he looked outwards, towards the window where the moon shone into the room. He responded, but nervously and cracked. ‘It is…I cannot bear to think of what is happening…I never feared that this would come…’ He turned around and looked at me, pity in his eyes. ‘I never imagined that you would be…it was never their intention, at least not that I understood…’ Trailing off he fell to his knees in front of me. ‘Please forgive me.’ ‘Forgiveness requires penitence Father. You, of all people, should know this.’ He heaved back to his feet and nodded. ‘I understand. What do we do now though? Something is wrong in all of this and I fear Sylvia is in great trouble.’ ‘First of all you must tell what is going on priest.’ He nodded affirmatively. ‘Of course of course. Well, this all came to pass as a direct result of your involvement with Adam. It is Adam’s flight from AWC that has brought about all this…suffering.’ He turned away and looked at the light, ashamed of the words that would be spoken and that would come forth from his lips on this night. ‘As you remember Adam’s antics were always involved with some sort of murky underside to them and it is no secret that on occasions, Adam’s success were not based solely on his wrestling talent. Even back then Darekewerx had an interest.’ He paused and returned to facing me, this time with a far more stern and ready face to deal with the situation. ‘Then of course he left, much to the anger of Drakewerx. It was never in their plans that he would be able to go at any point other than when they said. After all, he had a debt to pay.’ ‘A debt?’ ‘Yes, Adam may not remember making such a bargain but the events which would see his rise to the top of AWC were tinged with problems that Drakewerx always just happened to solve for Adam in such a way that he might believe it was of his own doing. Of course, having spoken with the company, I am under no such allusions.’ ‘So how does this figure in me?’ ‘Well Jack…your involvement in securing Adam a way out of the federation earmarked you. Quite literally, the debt was passed onto you without even a care for your feelings on the subject. That is when they contacted me, hoping to find a means with which to control you. In truth, Adam sparked this chain of events, though he is by no means the only offending in the proceedings.’ ‘What of Sylvia then?’ ‘That is something for which I must take the blame most certainly. I allowed her to be my instrument within the company and oversee the proceedings from my end. In fact a lot of what was done to you was of her suggestion, much to the pleasure of the company. However, I don’t think they figured into Sylvia’s desire to become a journalist, the price she commanded from the company, above her willingness to work merely as an agent.’ He sighed heavily, furrowing his brow at this. ‘I fear that my instilling of pride in her talents are responsible for such an event and for this I am truly repentant, willing to do whatever is necessary to bring about her safe return.’ ‘So what of this sex scandal all the way in Newark?’ ‘It…bears a mixture of truth and lies beneath it. I shall say nothing more on the matter Jack. We all have our troubled pasts no?’ Can’t say he was wrong on that one. ‘Very well. Then what do you suggest we do from here?’ ‘I was about to ask the very same thing to you Jack. It is clear who is in charge now. I am a man, nothing more, looking for forgiveness.’ The thought occurred that something was amiss about the whole conversation, but it was nothing more than Pearson’s wish to not speak further on certain topics I suspect. Not knowing exactly what to do, I asked the obvious question. ‘Tell me everything you know on Drakewerx.’ And the lesson began.
