English Summer Rain pt1

4 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/06/06 Sorrow was feeling ponderous, and this was worrying him. As far back as he could cast his mind; he could not remember a time when anything good ever came out of him feeling ponderous. The rain was falling outside, as he sat in a deep armchair by the bay windows. He had always loved a rainstorm, ever since he was a little child sitting on the windowsill of his mothers’ house, watching the patterns made on the glass pane. The drips were hypnotic, but it was the smell that had always brought it home for him. It was so fresh, and the baked earth almost admitted a sigh when the first light drops touched down. It was like a second chance, rinsing away the old and bringing in the new. English summer rain was a joy to behold. Believe it or not it doesn’t rain all the time and summer would often be hot and dry, punctuated by sudden, irregular torrents of the freshest rain imaginable. But now he felt nothing for the downpour. This was American rain, and it held no excitement for her. It was superficial and stereotypically American. Sorrow longed for the freshness again, but it was so far away. It was nearly a year to the day since he last felt the tickling drops on the back of his neck, but that day bore far too many other memories. Thoughts gathered like the grey clouds of above, but instead of cooling him, these made his blood run cold. But these were not points he would allow himself to contemplate just now. There was too much pain that was far better serving when locked away in a remote part of his brain. He would have use for it later, and was quite contempt to keep it there. For now, he continued to watch the American rain and its superficial patterns. He missed home a lot more than he had ever let on, but this was the way it had to be. He’d given up a lot in his life, mostly for his own personal gain, but rarely for his love for someone else. Lacuna was almost like a drug to him. She was an enigma, he needed to crack the exterior and get his sarcastic, dry humoured cousin back. He still wasn’t too sure why. So he had moved them to the US as part of her rehabilitation. He found a small house he had lived in before when working for some shady characters, one which he knew he would be well away from prying eyes and press. The press were a large factor in moving. Ever since the cow in the hospital and her little tip-off they had been followed night and day. It was slightly mystifying, he had to admit, because they had been out of the limelight for so long. Sorrow had only really ever hit the spotlight once or twice in his career, but it was not him they were after. The phone had rang night and day, journalists begging for a quote or a tagline they could use. Tabloids looking for a response to a ‘three-in-a-bed drug fuelled orgy’ story, or conservative broadsheets trying to find another high-profile reason for deporting all drug users to a remote island somewhere for the good of there beloved country. To begin with Sorrow had answered them all personally, a lot of these journalists had found themselves coming up against him in protection for some other target that they would hound to death if they had half the chance. Some of them had required certain contacts that were deemed officially beyond their reach by Sorrow’s overlords, and he had to make ‘certain adjustments’ to their stories. None of it was a past Sorrow was proud of, but it held a lot in the making of his character. He refused to write any element from his past as he relied on it to be who he was. But for the time being he was about to take a step back and jump straight back in. He needed a favour, and he knew someone who would be able to help him with it. The only problem would be the fact that he would be alerting everyone he had ever had contact with to what he was after, and this could be a fate worse than death. Still, he had to do it. Lacuna was locked in her room. He hadn’t heard from her in a couple of days, except for hearing her padding around the house in the dead of the night on a trek to the toilet or the kitchen. She had avoided him since her silent interrogation the other day, and now preferred to act by night. She had retreated back inside herself, and this was more confusing to Sorrow than anything that had happened thus far. He didn’t know of her dream, and the anguish that it had caused her, and that she was currently very worried for Sorrow’s safety although she too was not sure why. She had failed to work out the message she had been left with, refusing to leave it for dead as a cryptic note from her subconscious. Lacuna deeply believed there was a reason behind it, and one day she would find it. Sorrow was willing to risk leaving her locked in the house for the time being. He had people to meet, and felt secure after locking external doors and windows. She would probably be asleep, he reasoned, as she was moving all night and would have to spend some time recuperating. He jabbed at the buttons on his phone and his call was connected before he had managed to put it to his ear. He had forgot how professional the whole set up was, and these people were eager for his custom. “Dave, mate, long time no see” Sorrow forced a chipper edge to his voice, trying to hide how nervous he actually was. He failed, sounding more confused than upbeat. Dave noticed. “You’ve got me in the middle of something, fella. Any chance you could save your falseness for when I give a damn and we can talk then” he blasted, Sorrow’s face recoiling into a grimace. He didn’t realise quite how much of an edge he must have lost over the years. Despite this, both were happy to hear the voice of the other. They had long been associated, with Sorrow briefly providing Dave with around the clock protection when a strange mix of police and criminals had threatened his business. He had hurt a lot of men for Dave, and there was a favour or two owed in Sorrow’s eyes. So he tried again. “You know what it’s about, so don’t play ignorant with me sunshine. You’re never busy for people like me and, lets face it, I haven’t seen any of that protection money in a long time.” Dave was cordial in response. “Well maybe you should try offering some protection in return then. Just last week I had feds crawling all over my ass and…” He was cut off by an indignant Sorrow. “Shut up before someone does it for you. Now do you want my business or not?” There was silence on the line. Sorrow cracked his knuckles nervously but the sound given down the phone was of an altogether different nature, almost threatening. “Business?” gulped Dave. “Since when have you been interested in business with me? You were always so anti everything I’ve ever done. You tried to kill me yourself once. No, I don’t do business with people like you. You’re gone, the boss told me himself. You’re nothing now. He’s got tens of people like you now, I don’t need to be scared of you.” An audible sigh emanated from Sorrow. Yet another misunderstanding. Playing the good guy was so much harder than it looked that it was beyond a comical mistake now. “Wrong sort of business.” He spat, abruptly. His voice found some its old edge, and the words finally hit home with Dave, who laughed down the phone. “Meet me. Usual place. We can talk there” Sorrow hung up. His job was a step closer to completion. __ Sorrow had always liked to travel incognito, but with a bit of money in the back pocket it had become a little on the difficult side. Long and hard he had reasoned with himself, arguing with every principal he still held, but eventually he had lost the battle and the war. On his driveway stood a Jaguar XJ8 in British Racing Green, shining and perfectly restored. It drank more than Ireland on St Patrick’s Day, but it was the pride of his life. Every inch of it was British, every fix and restoration right down to every stitch in the leather interior. He remembered vividly that Lacuna had turned an altogether different shade of green when she had first clasped her hazel eyes on it. Seeing his face reflected in the gleaming bodywork gave him such a lift that he was almost relishing his meeting. It wasn’t easy for the big man to climb in comfortably, but very little was for a guy of his size, but that was to be of no complaint. He just loved it too much to find fault. He turned the ignition and sat back, a broad grin shooting up across his face as the jag roared into life. Pity he had to sully this car with such a dirty mission. It’d take hours to get the memory cleaned and purged. He set off, and around him happy suburbia faded into the middle of nowhere, and the growling under the bonnet became a backdrop to a mind far too active for his own liking. But, as he told himself for the millionth time, he was not doing it for himself. He had to do it, and there was no choice in the matter. He was dirtying his principals, carving out a little part of his legacy that we would happily forget in the times to come later. But there was no way he could not experience this, there was too much at stake to play the morals and principals game. He drove on, too deep in himself to enjoy the feeling of the car beneath him. Everything was changing now, or if not changing then about to. He had to know what it was that drove Lacuna, what had done such a thing to her. Time for deliberation had passed though, and he was far beyond the swaying of his own frail mental state. He had begun to notice it himself over the last few days. His mind was getting old, as was his body, and he wondered quite what he would have to live off when one or the other had truly given in. One last hurrah, he thought, as he pressed the pedal to the floor and shot forwards. Mid life crisis? Yeah, maybe. Wrestling would be the key, and before long he and Lacuna would be tasting the spotlight again. Sorrow would prove to the world that age meant nothing, and he would take her with him. He cursed himself for feeling so selfish, telling himself he was doing it all for Lacuna, but maybe he had learnt something before the story had reached its conclusion. Perhaps there was more to this than a crusade, but a subconscious reason could have provided his drive. But now, he needed to know. He pulled over the car, eyeing a longhaired man dressed in black with a nervous suspicion. He wound the window down and the man crossed towards him. “Nice ride you’ve got here” he said, an Australian accent prevailing over the words. Dave had lived in America for the last five or so years, but retained his accent as a matter of pride. Sorrow nodded. “Business” he said, bluntly. “You got everything?” The man patted his pocket, and in particular a small parcel wrapped in brown paper. “Everything you’ve ever wanted and more” he said, suppressing a smile. “Never though I’d see the day…” A hand shot through the window and grasped Dave by the throat, effortlessly lifting the short man from the ground. Sorrow was not in the mood for gloating, especially not with roles reversed as they were. “How many people know about this?” Sorrow spoke slowly, each word loaded with so much emotion that Dave even stopped flailing and Sorrow placed him roughly on his back. “Just me and you, mate. What else did you expect from your old pal?” Sorrow smirked, a plan formed in his mind. “Lets keep it that way. You’ve beat cancer once right? Wouldn’t want you to lose your balls another way.” He cracked his knuckles. Influence goes a long way. Dave squirmed. He knew full well Sorrow had done it before. Whether he would now or not was best not left to chance. “Lets just say I owe you this one” he said as he tossed the brown parcel through the open window and clawed his way back to his feet, trying to keep as much dignity as he could. Sorrow wound up the window. Now was the time for the next problem. __ He closed the front door quietly behind himself and placed an ear to the door of Lacuna’s room, met only with a torrent of silence and a sharp pang of guilt. This had to be done he though, he thought, steeling himself for the job ahead. There was a room prepared out the back; he’d readied it all earlier in a bid to win over the maelstrom of his mind. He stepped towards it now, flicking off the lights and drawing the curtains over the windows. Everything had to be right, everything in position. He rubbed his bicep nervously. He dropped himself heavily to his knees and pulled a lighter from his pocket. He placed the package down gently and whipped off his belt, tightening it around his arm. This had to be done, he told himself, gritting his teeth and filling a spoon with brown powder. His mind raced, his brow dripping with sweat so thick it threatened to drown him before he could even reach his target. He had to do it; he was doing it for his family. He needed to know what it was like. He had to. He filled the needle and gritted his teeth so tightly that a trickle of blood ran from the corner of his lips. Then it hit home. Nothing good ever comes from Sorrow pondering.
