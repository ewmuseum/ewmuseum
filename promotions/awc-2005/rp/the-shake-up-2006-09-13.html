The Shake-Up

13 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13th September, 2006 Location: New York City, New York Time: 12:32pm One hour ago… Dean Fisher adjusted his necktie in his typical meticulous nature in front of the large body length mirror before him. He knew that today was his opportunity to deal a blow to his rival Wesley Reno like none he had ever dealt before. A buzz of anticipation coursed through his veins, causing his heart to skip a beat. Up to this point, he hadn’t considered the genius behind his plan, though he had gone over it in his mind a thousand times. It was simplicity at its finest. As he admired his reflection in the mirror with a cocky smile plastered across his face, he was certain there would only be one winner today, and that would be him. The win would be for the Republican Party. He straightened up the lapels of his suit jacket, then turned on the spot to examine the hotel room around him. It was in perfect condition, thanks to the cleanliness of his new girlfriend, Lilly Baines. His mind drifted back to the night they had first met – the electricity, the awkward conversation, and the meal that followed. It couldn’t have gone any better. Her company was a huge asset to him, particularly when one considered the close ties she had to Wesley Reno. The old adage was correct. Behind every great man stands a great woman. As Dean Fisher wandered over towards the bed to collect his belongings, he was confident that this woman was behind the right man. The hotel room’s phone began to ring, its high-pitched tone irritating Fisher as he picked up the receiver. “Yeah, what is it?” “It’s me,” a voice replied, the cold nature of the man on the other end already palpable. Fisher registered the serious tone of the man. It was almost time for the plan to unfold. “I’m glad you finally called. I thought you never would. That doesn’t matter, though. We have to concentrate on getting this job done today. Do you have a partner assigned yet?” “Yes,” the voice replied, sounding impatient and somewhat agitated on the phone. “He should be easy to handle.” “Good,” Fisher responded, nodding to himself. “Call me back as soon as you have more information.” “Of course, sir.” There was a brief moment’s silence, neither man speaking as both composed themselves for the task at hand. “Oh, and one more thing,” Fisher continued sternly, “I don’t tolerate mistakes. Now make this happen.” Fisher hung up then, realising that he had more than made his point. The message was loud and clear. By the end of today, Wesley Reno and his attempts to seize the political prestige of solving the Ruth Beckford case would be over. The rain that pelted down outside the 5th precinct of the NYPD only served to dampen the already hostile mood in the building as my father and I walked the corridors in search of a coffee machine that wasn’t out of order. Our search turned out to be in vain, as a friendly, fresh-faced officer informed us that the only place to get coffee in the area was at the now infamous Starbucks. I opted out of the coffee and instead concentrated on the real reason we were here today – a meeting with two of my father’s most trusted compatriots, both of whom would undertake the task of digging up “dirt” on Fisher. Daniel Reno was never one to pull punches in the political world, taking every bit of assistance necessary to get a job done quickly and efficiently. Age had perhaps taken away some of his tact and quickness of thought, but his instincts were as sharp as they were when he made his first appearance before a packed house at a Democratic Party conference in Wyoming all those years ago. Since I called him to enlist his help, things had changed for the better for the Democratic Party in relation to this case. The pendulum was firmly swinging our way; all we required at this stage was a winning card, something to deflate the opposition completely. This winning card came in the shape of discrediting the representative of the opposition party, who just so happened to be my archrival, Dean Fisher. I had discussed the issue of Fisher with my father multiple times, but always on a more personal stage. This was a different scenario, the backdrop now the political world. More mistakes could be made. There existed many more pitfalls for both of us. With this in mind, we decided to make this perfectly clear to my father’s colleagues before they tried anything. Whether I liked it or not, I was dipping my foot into a dangerous pool. If I didn’t orchestrate this in the correct manner, my career could be dead. The meeting with the two men assisting us, namely Joseph Lynch and Ignacio Torredo, was for the most part uneventful. Lynch and Torredo were by the book characters, who seemed more than happy to help out an old friend. “Do you both understand what we’re trying to do, gentlemen?” my father asked, his voice unwavering and fearsome. “Yes, sir,” Torredo replied. Torredo was a stout character who wore glasses and had thick, black hair, which was beginning to show some signs of grey. I nodded to Torredo as he accepted the task at hand. “Excellent. And Joseph, do you understand?” “Well, I actually believe we could have taken a more direct route to rid ourselves of the Fisher problem. I don’t mind, though. You’re the boss. Let’s do this.” I sensed a marked level of surprise in my father’s demeanour after listening to Lynch’s comments. He must have expected a more reassuring answer from his long time friend. “So,” I intervened. “I trust you understand how precarious this situation is. You have to ensure that Fisher doesn’t discover what you’re up to. Stay discreet at all times. If you’re ever in danger of being caught, come back here and we’ll reassess the situation.” Lynch and Torredo, both of whom were dressed in inconspicuous street clothes to ward off any suspicion from the public, acquiesced in unison with nods. Knowing that their brief was over, they headed out into the rain, which had now become nothing more than drizzle. I hoped the favourable change in the weather would be indicative of a turn for the best in my political career. As much as I trusted my father’s judgement, I felt compelled to question him about Joseph Lynch, whose answer to his task was less than reassuring. “Dad, you’re not at all worried about Lynch, are you?” “Why would I be?” he shot back, the indignity in his voice taking me aback slightly. After a moment to compose himself, he held his hand up to apologise for his behaviour. “Don’t worry, Wes,” he assured me. “Lynch is just the hothead of the pair. He’s the rebel, but by God does he know how to get a job done.” I stared out the window of the NYPD 5th precinct’s second floor, the droplets of rainwater on the glass unsettling me somehow. “I hope you’re right, Dad.” Phil Allen tentatively made his way through the revolving door of the public library on the corner of 4th and 9th. Initially, he ducked out of the way of any passers-by to avoid being recognised, but soon realised that the majority of people who frequented libraries had no inclination to meet a professional wrestler. Allen removed his jacket and approached the reception area where a middle-aged woman sat playing with her hair. Her reaction to Allen’s arrival was almost mechanical in its execution. First, she put on the most pathetic fake smile he had ever seen, followed by a theatrical hand gesture to get his attention, not that she didn’t have it already with her antics. “Sir, how may I assist you on this day?” “Um, you could be a little less enthusiastic about this dead-end job of yours,” Allen mumbled, more to air his views in the open than to hurt her feelings. Fortunately, she didn’t hear a word. The receptionist, whose name according to her nametag was Rachel, asked Allen to speak up. Allen obliged. “Yes, I was just looking for a computer I could use, please,” Allen requested in his most polite voice. It sounded unnatural to him, almost alien. Rachel nodded fervently, detaching herself from her desk and leading Allen towards a bank of computers in the dark recesses of the library. It was back here, in the mythology section, that no sane man or woman ever wandered, Allen believed. He considered that the computers were on demand, and hence they would be placed in an area of the library that was not visited often. In this way, these books would be hired. Of course, maybe he was giving the library too much credit. Rachel bent over and switched on the computer with her password. Her perfume was actually quite soothing. Allen expected it to be some wild African fragrance that would resemble toxic gas to reflect her over-the-top personality, but he was incorrect on that one. “So, what exactly are you looking for on the computer?” Rachel asked, clicking the mouse on the search bar. “A bit of this, a bit of that. You know, the bog standard kind of stuff people would look up.” Judging from the contorted face Rachel was sporting, she wasn’t quite sure what he meant. Allen thanked her for her assistance, however, and got down to work. His task this afternoon was to help out in the investigation into the Ruth Beckford case. Admittedly, he had no chance of finding top secret documents on a library computer, but anything that he could find on Beckford herself or indeed Dean Fisher would be a help to his tag team partner. In recent weeks, Allen had felt rather left out in the cold as regards the investigation. The only time he had spent time with Wesley Reno was in the ring and a few minutes pre- and post-match. He didn’t quite understand why he had developed a guilty conscience. His behaviour towards Reno when he had first met him still played on his mind – the somewhat unnecessary action of badgering him to train, when in fact he had turned into a quite capable competitor in the ring without excessive work, or indeed the sabotage of his date with Lilly Baines. Allen recalled the date and attached significance to Fisher now dating Baines. There must have been some political war being waged between Reno and Fisher, with Baines in the middle. Was this all over a woman? Allen highly doubted it. Still, searching for any comments Fisher had made about Baines in any newspapers could yield some promising results. The search began. Ignacio Torredo delicately placed his sunglasses onto the brim of his nose, unable to withstand the burning sun’s rays anymore on this dry, hot afternoon. Joseph Lynch, in contrast, seemed unaffected by the sun and concentrated solely on the fibre optic equipment before him which, having been planted a few minutes before by Torredo, were now displaying images of Dean Fisher and his girlfriend, Lilly Baines. Lynch’s brow creased in frustration as he watched the couple. “What’s the matter, Joe?” Torredo asked, looking over his shoulder at his partner. “Oh, nothing,” came the unconvincing response. Torredo turned and placed his hands on his sides, a sign that he didn’t believe Lynch in the slightest. He wanted answers. “Come on, Joe. Don’t tell me you’re thinking of backing out on me now. I need you to work the fibre optics here. You do that better than anyone. So, are we on the same page?” Lynch still didn’t look like he was on board. His facial expression completely changed as he stared at the LCD screens in front of him in horror. Torredo instantly knew what had happened. “They’ve been disabled! I don’t know how, but they’re out. All of them,” Lynch shouted, the panic evident in his voice. “I’ll go over, Joe. I have to see what the problem is.” “No, Ignacio! Fisher will know you’re around. He’s due to leave the hotel for that meeting in ten minutes.” Torredo stood silently for a moment, all the while locking eyes with his partner. “Then I guess I have ten minutes to find out what the problem is and get them working again.” Torredo was a man of action by reputation, and he dashed to the door of the secure room they had borrowed in the hotel across the street from Fisher’s in an instant. Within a moment, he was gone. Lynch went to the door Torredo had just left through and opened it, ensuring that no-one was in the vicinity. He wiped a bead of sweat from his forehead in a nervous fashion and reached for his cell phone. Dialling a number he knew by heart, he pressed the green button and waited for the person on the other end of the line to pick up. “It’s me again. I’ve sent him up. He should be there in two minutes. Prepare to send them down.” Lynch’s instructions were irrefutable. His tone left no room for negotiation. “Good,” the man on the other end replied, pleased with what Lynch had just told him. “I’ll send them down immediately. Mister Lynch, make this work and you will become a very rich man. Remember that.” “Yes, sir,” Lynch responded, his mind firmly focused on the rewards he could reap from this job. Lynch hung up, feeling not an iota of guilt about what he was about to do. Approximately ten minutes from where Daniel Reno had posted his compatriots to spy on Dean Fisher, both Wesley Reno and his father had stopped by a restaurant to eat lunch. The Bolano Restaurant had always been a favourite of Daniel Reno during his frequent visits to New York in the past. This was his first visit in years, and he could scarcely recognise one member of a staff he once knew like the back of his hand. I looked into the contemplative eyes of my father across our table and recognised the pressure he was under at this moment. I knew that things between he and my mother were on hold, and if there was one thing that could deflate my father’s enthusiasm, it was an argument with his wife. Our conversation before this period of silence had been primarily about my political ventures of late. My cell phone began to ring. I picked up to find it was Phil Allen on the other line. “Phil, what’s up? Where are you?” “Oh, I’ve just been snooping around in a public library about ten minutes from where you are.” “Right. And may I ask what you’re doing in a library?” “I’ve just been looking up some stuff about Dean Fisher that might interest you. Interesting details, Wes. You should check them out. They could really help you nail this guy.” My attention was distracted by my father, who had also received a call on his cell phone. This was no ordinary call, however. My father was animated as ever, and was desperately trying to get a point across. I asked Phil Allen to wait for a moment as I asked my father what the problem was. “What, are you out of your mind, Joseph? You can’t even be thinking of this! It’s insane!” Daniel Reno bellowed, clearly furious with the man on the line, who I could only assume was Joseph Lynch. Then it hit me. Something terrible was about to happen there. “Don’t even – no, I don’t agree with you at all! Wait, wait!” My father cursed under his breath, running his hands through his thinning hair in frustration. “Dad, what’s going on? What has Lynch done?” “Wes, there isn’t much time. We have to get there now to warn Dean Fisher. Lynch wants to kill Fisher, Wes!” “What? Why?!” I could hardly contain my incredulity. Thoughts rushed through my head. If Fisher was killed or harmed in any way, I would become the scapegoat, being his main political rival. The New York Times, and every other newspaper for that matter, would have a field day with the story. I snapped out of it, realising my father was calling for me to follow him back to the car. I rushed after him, raising my cell phone to my ear, just now remembering that Allen was on the other line. “Phil, we have a massive problem on our hands! Get over here now. Maybe you could get here faster! We have to warn Dean Fisher!” “Why, what’s happening?” “Lynch, one of the men we hired, wants to kill him! I have no time to explain! I gotta go. See you later!” I hung up quickly, jumping into the passenger seat of my father’s hired Mercedes. The New York traffic was positively chaotic at this time of the day. I hoped and prayed that one way or another, we could get through to Fisher. Calling his cell phone wasn’t an option; he would never answer it to me. Calling the hotel to warn him was an option, too, but I hadn’t got the number and I wouldn’t be allowed to speak to him if I identified myself as Wesley Reno. My father agreed to call the hotel while I was driving to attempt to contact Fisher before it was too late. My career was on the line, and I had ten minutes to rescue it. A frantic Ignacio Torredo stepped through the golden doors of the hotel elevator. He was going against the mission protocol by coming to the floor Dean Fisher was staying on. Just as he stepped out of the elevator shaft, however, two figures in the neighbouring elevator caught his eye – a man and a woman. Before he could get a decent look at them, they were gone. Torredo cursed his luck, assuming the two he had just spotted to be Fisher and Baines. Despite his certainty that the pair had already left, Torredo moved with trepidation up the hallway towards Fisher’s room. The fibre optic devices were in place along the floorboards, invisible to the untrained eye. He knelt down and checked them meticulously. There seemed to be no malfunction. His cell phone chirped suddenly, causing him to jump with surprise. He quickly scanned the caller ID before picking up. It was Joseph Lynch. “Joe, did you get the fibre optics working yet? They seem perfectly fine on my end.” “No, not yet,” Lynch replied, sounding hassled. “Ignacio, stay over there and wait until I call you to let you know I’ve got them back on. Do some double-checks, whatever you need.” Torredo detected a fear in his partner’s voice. He almost hesitated to ask what the matter was, but he was unsure why exactly. “Joe, is everything alright? You seem preoccupied…” “I’m fine,” he snapped. “Now get back to work, Ignacio.” Lynch killed the call in an instant, leaving Torredo alone on the 10th floor of the hotel, at a loss for what to do next. Obviously, he should have followed orders and found out what was wrong with the fibre optics, but he sensed that Lynch was doing his utmost to keep him away. Torredo, going against his conscience, left his equipment on the floor and began searching for a vantage point from which he could keep a close eye on his partner’s activities. Joseph Lynch had already set up his sniper rifle on the 10th floor of the hotel opposite Fisher’s. From here, it was a clear shot. There were no distractions; Torredo had been dealt with. The sabotage of the equipment had gone off without a hitch, and he was actually shocked that his partner hadn’t sussed out what was happening. Nevertheless, the time had come. To make a name for yourself, you had to make an impact in the world, positive or negative. For years, he had concentrated on doing the right thing for people. Things had drastically changed, however. Now he was compelled to do whatever it took to make an impact. I felt my heart race uncontrollably as we edged slowly closer to the hotel in the New York traffic. Admittedly, I was under severe stress at this moment in time. Nothing I had experienced before could have prepared me for this moment. My father sat beside me, his hands clammy on the steering wheel from several tense moments of waiting. I tried in vain once again to contact Dean Fisher via the hotel telephone. I got the same result – no reply. I couldn’t take it anymore. I had to make my way to the hotel by foot. “Where are you going? You’ll never make it there in time, Wes!” The warning from my father wasn’t convincing enough. Whether he liked it or not, I had a better chance of stopping Lynch on foot. I shimmied my way between a cab and a Ford as I made my way towards the hotel. Now on the sidewalk, I dodged people left, right and centre. In the back of my mind, I had already given up. My watch read 2:00pm. Fisher was due to leave his hotel any moment. In the distance, I could hear a gunshot, mixed with people’s frantic screams. I ran as fast as I possibly could and noticed the crowds gathering around two bodies laid out on the sidewalk – one a man, the other a woman. They were unmistakable. It was Dean Fisher and Lilly Baines. Dead. I instinctively looked to the sky towards the building where Lynch had set up his equipment and saw him. He stared at me maniacally, not caring about what he had just done. Within a moment, he disappeared. Everything around me came to a standstill. If Fisher was indeed dead, and the men I hired could be traced to it, then my career was as good as over. My breaths became shallow and laced with anxiety. I had no escape route. I had to face up to my fate, whatever it would be.
