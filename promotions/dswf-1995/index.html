Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

D.S.W.F. staged its own wrestling as a strictly real-roster promotion: every match across its four surviving broadcasts books real, contemporary professional wrestlers under their own names, with no invented character appearing anywhere in the record. Its earliest surviving broadcast, Monday Night Mania on 31 July 1995, already carries four championships in play — Bret Hart as World Champion, Sabu as Intercontinental Champion, Shane Douglas as Television Champion, and The Public Enemy as World Tag Team Champions — with no document recording how any of the four were won.

That broadcast ran the opening round of a tournament: Diesel beat “Stunning” Steve Austin after Ric Flair's ringside interference backfired into an accidental collision, a masked Yokozuna beat Vader, and Shawn Michaels beat Steven Regal with a chop-and-elbow sequence finished off by a superkick. Between matches, Bret Hart sat for an interview previewing his own bracket path against Flair and weighing a possible meeting with his friend Diesel, telling the promotion's interviewer that friendship would come second to the world title itself.

By 20 September the promotion was running three shows across a single weekend — Friday Night's Main Event, Saturday Night Superstars, and a Monday Night Mania billed as subject to change around a coming pay-per-view. Friday night's own card opened with Razor Ramon against Raven, a match Arn Anderson turned with a ringside DDT onto a chair before the 123 Kid ran in to help Raven steal the pin; The Undertaker followed with a squash of Kevin Sullivan that spilled into a Scott Norton run-in, a save from 911, and a handshake between Undertaker and 911 once the dust settled. Its main event saw Intercontinental Champion Sabu put Randy Savage through a table for the pin, closing with Savage stretchered out with an arm injury; the card ended with 911 losing to Ric Flair's Figure Four after another Norton interference, and 911 and Undertaker challenging Flair and Norton to a tag match on the promotion's own upcoming pay-per-view.

That pay-per-view, War Games, was set for 24 September 1995 with a nine-match card headlined by a Survivor Series-style elimination match pitting Bret Hart, Shawn Michaels, Randy Savage, Sting, and Diesel against Kevin Sullivan, The Sandman, Cactus Jack, Sabu, and Vader, alongside a Texas Death Match for the World Tag Team Championship between The Public Enemy and The Gangstas and a two-out-of-three-falls Television Championship match between Shane Douglas and Tommy Dreamer.

No document survives naming when D.S.W.F. was founded, how its own four championships changed hands before 31 July 1995, or what happened on the War Games card its own broadcasts spent two months building toward; the promotion's own record ends with that pay-per-view still to come.
