The World Fighting Council is a Booked Electronic Mixed Martial Arts Fighting Promotion designed to accurately capture the flavor and feel of shootfighting found in all parts of the world from the United States, to Brazil, to Japan, etc., etc..
Fight outcomes are determined by a combination of strategies (90%) and roleplays (10%). Mixed Martial Arts Fighting (also known as No Holds Barred Fighting & Shootfighting) is an exciting and competitive fighting sport in which many different styles and techniques of the martial arts are represented.
The WFC seeks to capture the competitiveness and excitement presented by actual shootfighting promotions such as the UFC in the United States,
Pancrase, RINGS, Shooto, & Pride in Japan, and the various Vale-Tudo fighting promotions in Brazil.
2.0 A Booked E-Fighting League Why a Booked League? Simply put, a booked league is a vastly more fair, fun, and interesting way of running a e-fed, as opposed to a simulated league.
There is little effort to a simmed league, whereas a booked league requires EVERYONE'S participation in order to be successful. Booked leagues are able to reward handlers for there hard work with earned victories & successes, whereas simmed leagues don't take into account participation and instead rely on what basically boils down to randomly simulated results.
2.1 The Booking Committee The Booking Committee is the body responsible for deciding the fights for each WFC sanctioned card. Sometimes two fighters may challenge each other and the Booking Committee would be very likely to sign that fight right away. More often than not however, the booking committee will make fights according to rankings and win/loss records of fighters.
Besides making fights, the booking committee is also responsible for determining the outcome of each fight. When deciding the outcome of a match the committee looks at strategies (known better as "strats") and roleplays
(flashes,interviews, press conferences, press releases, and anything else your mind can cook up) to determine the winner. Strats count as 90% while roleplays count as 10% towards fight outcomes. (More on this in 3.5 "Strating and
Roleplaying") In some cases this process is very difficult. Regardless of the outcome of the fight it is our hope that everyone will enjoy the efforts put fourth by both the handlers and staff of WFC.
In addition to making and determining the outcome of fights, the booking committee is also responsible for releasing the official WFC rankings and deciding on such things as injuries and title contention (which is based upon ranking and won/loss record). Rankings and injury listings will be included in email updates after each card.

3.0 The Federation Itself 3.1 Rules & Regulations WFC rules are as follows: -All matches in WFC are held inside a standard wrestling ring with only minor changes. The changes include tighter, more stable ropes and instead of the three tiered turnbuckle system we use one large protective pad (ie
Pancrase). -Each fighter recieves a maximum of 3 Rope Break points. If, to escape further punishment from a possible submission hold or from striking on the ground, a fighter grabs the ring ropes he referee will force a break, sending both fighters their respective corners, and restart the fight.
-If a fighter exceeds this limit it's a technicall knock-out (TKO) and his opponent is declared the winner. -Each fighter recieves a maximum of 3 Foul Points. If, during the course of the fight, a fighter losses those 3 Points he/she is instantly disqualified and is put up for review by the WFC Rules Council
-Headbutts, elbows, and knees are allowed to be delievered both standing and on the ground. WFC Rules Council: +Fouls will be given for the following: -eye gouging (punishable by point deduction or disqualification based upon the fighters ability to recover from the illegal contact) -biting (punishable by point deduction or disqualification based upon the fighters ability to recover from the illegal contact) -fish hooking (punishable by point deduction or disqualification based upon the fighters ability to recover from the illegal contact) -small joint manipulation ie trying to break individual fingers (punishable by point deduction or disqualification based upon the fighters ability to recover from the illegal contact)
-groin shots (punishable by point deduction or disqualification based upon the fighters ability to recover from the illegal contact) -kicking without protective gear(punishable by point deduction or disqualification based upon the fighters ability to recover from the illegal contact) -No kicking of a downed opponent. A downed opponent is considered to be down only when on all four points- hands/elbows and the knees. -hair pulling (punishable by point deduction to a maximum of 3 points) -grabbing the ropes to prevent progress on the feet. (punishable by point deduction to a maximum of 3 points) -Kicking is only allowed if the kicker is wearing special protective gear (such as specialized shoes and/or shinpads) or barefoot -Closed fists punches only allowed if wearing striking gloves -Fights are won when a competitor is knocked down for a ten-count, forced to tapout (or verbally submit), loses a total of 3 Foul (or Rope Break) Points, or is ruled unable to continue by the referee or ringside doctor. 3.2 The Titles The World Fighting Council recognizes the following Championships/weight divisions: 1)WFC WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT (200+lbs) TITLE 2)WFC WORLD MIDDLEWEIGHT (170-199.9lbs) TITLE 3)WFC WORLD LIGHTWEIGHT (150-169.9lbs) TITLE Additional weight divisions and subsequent titles may be added as the need arises. 3.3 Title Contention Title contention is based upon WFC's official rankings. These rankings are determined by win/loss record and quality of opponents. For example a wrestler who is 4-0 against unranked opponents, will most likely not be ranked above a wrestler who is 2-2 against opponents who are ranked.
Title shots will be awarded bases on rankings and other outside factors including record against the champion and past 4 match results among other things.
Ranked fighters who defeat the title holder in a non-title match will automatically receive a title shot.
While Title matches will be booked by the booking committee usually every other card, champions may take the option of defending the title other times by selecting and asking for title matches against a certain opponent, or accepting the challenge of another fighter. However, titles may only be defended against those ranked in the top 5 of the official rankings. This ensures the level of opposition is of the highest caliber.
3.4 Cards The WFC presents 4-12 major cards per year taking place around the world. These major cards feature the best fighters in the most competitive fights. From time to time WFC will also present a special "New Contenders" show. These "New Contenders" shows will feature new unestablished talent on the undercard attempting to make a name for themselves by taking on other
up-and- comering or established WFC stars. "New Contenders" shows will also generally feature 1-2 major fights.
While the WFC is based in the United States (Charlotte, NC to be specific) cards will also be held around the world. 3.5 Strating & Roleplaying To more accurately portray a shoot fed, fight outcomes in the WFC are determined based on 9/10 Strats and 1/10 roleplays , whereas other feds are determined based purely on roleplays.
We feel this is a vastly more realistic way of determining fight outcomes since under this system the handler who better understands their own fighter's style, as well as their opponent's, and is able to craft a detailed plan for defeating the opponent, becomes the victor.
While roleplays are not as important as strats they do play an important role in the league. Handlers are expected to submit a short pre-fight interview and a post-fight interview to air on or after the card (post-fight interviews air only provided your fighter wins).
Handlers are also encouraged to submit a a pre-fight press conference and/or post fight roleplay (Flash, interview, press conference, etc).
Handlers can submit articles for Puroresuringu Nikki Magazine ( http://www.[detail withheld].com/pnmagazine/ ) in place of pre-fight press conferences and/or post-fight roleplays.
Pre-fight press conferences and post-fight roleplays will be mailed, the week before and the week after each card respectively, in a packet entitled
"Ringside." 3.6 The Staff The WFC has assembled a fine collection of officials, announcers, and referees. Steven Sczar - The producer & owner of the World Fighting Council. Sczar is a former pro wrestler and no holds barred fighter whose career was cut short when he suffered a neck injury in a pro wrestling match one year ago in
Michigan's Motor City Wrestling. He is only 31 years old and still in tremendous shape. While Sczar is a wealthy man (due to his lucrative career as wrestler as well as a hefty injury settlement with MCW and a good insurance policy) he relies on financial backing from ABC & ESPN to make WFC a world class fighting promotion.
Christopher Reece - The WFC's lead announcer. Reece, a former PJW & SHVF announcer, is a skilled and knowledgeable broadcaster. At age 34, Reece has followed shoot fighting for many years via magazines, newsletters, and tape trading.
Corey Dunn - Provides color commentary and analysis for the WFC. Dunn is a legend in the martial arts world. His experience in the sport brings invaluable insight to the announce team.
Kelly Myers - Press Director - Kelly is a 26 year old former female journalist for the USA Today. She's very good looking (typical all-American blond bombshell), multi-lingual, and surprisingly knows her stuff when it comes to fighting.
Jeff Lane - Head Official - Lane is a 15 year veteran referee of boxing as well as no holds barred fighting. He is fair, objective, and generally doesn't make mistakes. He is known as an official who believes in letting the fighters determine the match, and thus rarely stops matches unless absolutely necessary. At 36 years of age, 6 feet, 2 inches tall and 233 pounds Lane is more than capable of keeping fights in check when things get a little too physical as well as enforcing the rules.
Tajiri Hayashi - Official - Hayashi at age 29 has an amazing 10 years of experience as a referee in Japan. Hayashi speaks impeccable English and
Japanese making him a very valuable asset in matches involving Japanese fighters who speak little English. Hayashi is an excellent official, but from time to time has a hard time controlling fights due to his small size.
Ben Jones - Official - Jones is a relatively new referee with only a one year of experience as an assistant referee in small mixed martial-arts events held in Iowa. He is a full time medical doctor which in the eyes of the WFC makes him more of an asset than a liability. Due to his inexperience, from time to time he does make mistakes and relying on his medical background he has in the past been accused of stopping fights too soon.
