Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The World Fighting Council opened its doors on 4 February 1999 with a founding player's guide styled, in its own words, as “Mixed Martial-Arts at Its Finest” — a booked shoot-fighting promotion modeled after real organizations like the UFC, Pancrase, RINGS, Shooto, and Pride, deciding its fights nine parts strategy submission to one part roleplay through a two-person booking committee.

WFC named three weight-class titles at launch, the Heavyweight, Middleweight, and Lightweight championships, with no champion yet crowned in any of them, and built its rules around shoot-style scoring: rope breaks and foul points that could end a fight by technical knockout or disqualification. Its fictional broadcast staff included producer and owner Steven Sczar, a former pro wrestler whose own career had ended in a neck injury; lead announcer Christopher Reece; color commentator Corey Dunn; press director Kelly Myers; and three named officials, among them a fifteen-year boxing and no-holds-barred veteran, a ten-year Japanese official fluent in both languages, and a full-time doctor working his first year as a referee, each given a distinct strength and a distinct flaw in the ring.

Three days later, on 7 February 1999, a debut vignette introduced WFC's first signed fighter, Dean “D.K.R.” Roy, training at a makeshift octagon in a Detroit wrestling school and dispatching two unnamed opponents inside it.

No sanctioned card, bout date, or title match survives beyond that vignette. No closure notice survives either, and whether the WFC ever staged an octagon fight for one of its three championships isn't known.
