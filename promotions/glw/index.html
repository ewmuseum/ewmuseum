Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Great Lakes Wrestling billed itself, in capital letters, as "the Best in the Midwest" and, just as emphatically, as "NOT AN E-FED!!!" — a TNM circuit, run on a tabletop wrestling simulation rather than played-by-mail roleplay, staging real professional wrestlers under their own names across sixteen dated cards out of Chicago between 6 January and 31 March 2001. Masato Tanaka, Lance Storm, Ken Shamrock, Tank Abbott, Rhino, Bam Bam Bigelow, Juventud Guerrera, Jerry Lynn, Vampiro, and Gran Hamada all wrestled here, split across five named tag teams and stables carried on the fed's own tag-team ranking table (The Extremists, The Armstrong Family, The Elite, The New Powers of Pain, The Movement, The Superstars of Grunge, Suicidal Tendencies, and The Perfect Alliance) and a billed pair of identical twins, The Headhunters.

Four championships changed hands across the run: the World Heavyweight Title only once, when Masato Tanaka won a 30-man Deadly Draw match; the Midwestern Title three times, with Doc Dean losing it to an injury replacement and winning it back three weeks later; the World Tag Team Titles four times in twelve weeks; and the Television Title four times, once for a single day, when Colorado Kid's own 30-minute time-limit title win, three falls to two, lasted exactly one night before Flash Flanagan took it back. The fed's own last title-history entry and its own separately-dated Current Champions page disagree about who actually held the tag titles as of 31 March — one calls Jesse James Armstrong and Ken Shamrock still champions, the other calls the belts vacant, and nothing in the surviving record resolves which is right. An announced next supercard, headlined by a Fatal Fourway for the World Heavyweight Title, never got a result: this GeoCities account was not captured again after 7 April 2001.

Two documents linked from the fed's own front page — its singles and tag-team ranking tables — survive in the same accessioned corpus and are served here alongside the sixteen results cards; every wrestler's declared win-loss record on file traces to them. Thirty further individually-captured promo pages from the same corpus (segment transcripts, mostly post-match interviews) were not read or served this pass.

Storylines

Doc Dean's crooked Midwestern Title Written by AI, 2026

Doc Dean won the vacant GLW Midwestern Title in the fed's own opening ten-man battle royal (13 January 2001) and spent his entire reign finding ways not to defend it clean. He survived a first challenge from Brad Armstrong only by disqualification, when the Extremists ran in (20 January), then "injured" himself out of a rematch with Mark Schrader and had Brian Adias stand in as his own replacement — only for the two of them to reveal it as a scam, Dean clocking Schrader with a hidden crutch shot so Adias could finish him (3 February). Dean's luck ran out the following week when Schrader beat the injury-replacement Adias clean and took the title (10 February), only for Dean to win it back thirteen days later with a pair of brass knuckles thrown to him by Adias (23 February). He kept it the rest of the way on the same footing: a crate shot supplied by Adias in a Falls-Count-Anywhere match (10 March) and another set of brass knuckles against Jerry Lynn at Super Cade I (23 March) — the belt never once changing hands on a clean fall after Dean first won it back. The same tactics carried into his stablemates' own business the following night: with the referee distracted, Dean himself blasted Mark Schrader with an enzuigiri thrown from a hidden metal plate, knocking him out for a Bam Bam Bigelow countout win (24 March).

The Extremists against the Armstrongs and Ken Shamrock Written by AI, 2026

Lance Storm's Extremists (Storm, Jonny Storm, Rhino, Ace Darling, Devon Storm and manager Dawn Marie) feuded with the Armstrong family and Ken Shamrock across the entire run. The trouble started on the fed's opening card, when Storm and company tripped up Brad Armstrong to cost him a match with Big Titan (6 January), then cost him the Midwestern Title itself a week later — Storm, having already been eliminated from the title battle royal, returned to ringside to distract Armstrong long enough for Doc Dean to steal the win (13 January). Brad Armstrong's own extended leave of absence from GLW was announced on the same card that introduced his replacement, "Roaddog" Jesse James Armstrong, as the newest Armstrong brother (17 February). The World Tag Team Titles changed hands between the two sides four times across the run: Scott and Steve Armstrong first won them from The Headhunters with an assist from Ace Darling and Devon Storm's own interference (24 February), lost them back to Darling and Storm a week later (3 March), and the title finally left the Extremists for good when Jesse James Armstrong and Ken Shamrock won it from Darling and Storm (31 March). The feud's biggest card was Super Cade I's own ten-man Elimination Match, the Armstrongs/ Shamrock/Tank Abbott side winning 5 falls to 2 with Jesse James Armstrong, Ken Shamrock and Tank Abbott the survivors (23 March).

The Perfect Creation turns on Sebastian P. Sterling Written by AI, 2026

"Greatness Himself" The Perfect Creation and Sebastian P. Sterling ran together through the opening two months, alongside Sterling's manager Mike White — Sterling costing World Champion Masato Tanaka a clean win by disqualification once TPC broke a 2x4 over Tanaka's back (10 February). The alliance ended without warning on 10 March, when TPC helped Sterling to his feet after a win over Indiana Kidd Jr. and then dropped him with the Spinning Northern Lights Bomb, punching White in the mouth when he questioned it. Sterling got his receipt one week later, tripped up by Gran Hamada and finishing TPC with the Sterling Suplex for what the fed's own broadcast called a huge upset (17 March) — the last meeting between the two men on file.
