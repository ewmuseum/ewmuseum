One other heavyweight is awaiting confirmation, and that will take the roster to a point where there are just one heavyweight, two super-middleweight and five jisatsu spots left to be filled. The jisatsu section is obviously the one we're concentrating on filling, and that division's head booker [name withheld] is working hard to get it full. * With those additions, the schedule for the first Battlefield on August 8 has been altered slightly. Instead of having a jobber match, The Banshee will face Tenno in a super-middleweight tournament match, and a jobber match will be added for Shumasita. Everything else remains the same. * Remember - match strats are due in by Saturday midnight EST!!!! Sending in strats is not compulsory, but will greatly increase your chance of winning! * Another reminder that the deadline for RPs for the Monday night Sound-Off is Midnight Sunday EST! OK, enjoy tonight's helping of superstar interviews!!! The skyline glows magnificently in the dark sky. The camera zooms down to reveal the United Center. There is a hustle and bustle outside of the arena. Tons of fans congregate outside, discussing the night's events. Some fans sport the colors of their respective countries. Next, we cut to the inside of the arena. Some fans are still in the building, cheering on. We pass by Derek Mota, hoisting the I-Crown title high into the air. The camera moves on down the hallways. It stops in front of one door. The nameplate reads, "Kashan Akuma - Asia Champion".) VOICE: AHHH DAMN!!!! (We now cut to the inside of the locker room. Tables and chairs are overturned and other items are strewn around. The I-Crown Asia title is thrown over to the side.) KASHAN: This is unbelievable, you know that. It is simply unbelievable. I come here to Chicago to handle my business and take the I-Crown home and what happens? I come out empty handed. I waste all my time coming to this god forsaken country only to be robbed. That's right, I was robbed. Say what you want to say, we all know that I should be the I-Crown champion. It'll eat at your soul Fletch. You won't be able to live an enjoyable life as long as I'm not rightfully crowned. Sooner or later, it'll catch right up to you. (Akuma shakes his head in disgust. He kicks a bottle out of his way and walks over to an overturned chair. He sets it upright and takes a seat in it. He grabs a bottle of water off the floor and takes the cap off. He takes a sip and then sets it down.) KASHAN: Derek Mota was lucky. My loss was his incredible gain. He should've never gotten past me. The poor officiating costed me my chance at glory. No one, and I mean no one, can kick out of the AkumaPlex. Definitely not from the top rope. When I get my video footage, it will all be proven. Then you fools who doubted me the whole time will be forced to realize tha I am not a talentless, cocky, loud-mouthed youth. Your so-called champion didn't prove a thing but his underhandedness. I busted my ass the whole night only to lose to him, totally fresh. This issue is far from over. I was not only screwed but I was robbed of my chance to show Wuzho what I was talking about the whole time. Ha ha, I don't forget. (Akuma stands up and looks much calmer. He sets the furniture up to how it was before he knocked everything out of place. He grabs the I-Crown Asia title and cleans it off. He puts it around his waste. Next, he picks up a towel and wipes himself off. He leans against the wall.) KASHAN: But I didn't get this time to talk about that crap. The I-Crown is over. I have to look to the future. My future is Gunryo Pro Wrestling. Yes, a mighty puroresu federation has popped up in my homeland. Where the ring is the battlefield. Where everytime I step in the ring it is a war. I look at this as a chance to show the non-believers that the my shortcomings were a fluke. This is my showcase. This is my world. When you're in Japan, you step it up a notch. The American fans just don't deserve to see this stuff. (Kashan is much more relaxed now. He walks over to the sofa where Shane Redding is seated and takes a seat on the arm. He leans back and takes a deep breath.) KASHAN: I see some familiar faces here in Gunryo Pro. Definitely. Some familiar faces I won't mind stomping into the ground. All my life, I have been a hero in Japan. All my life, others have tried to take that away from me and failed. That will not change. These fools have followed me wherever I have gone. People like Banshee, Kabuki Kid, and the Daimyo remind me of the good times when I would stand tall and the fans would be chanting my name. They would be lying lifeless on the mat. Ahhh, memories. KASHAN: Alas, the format of this federation saddens me. I must share the spotlight with the "overweights" and the garbage wrestlers. These fat overweights are simply middleweight wrestlers who could not cut it. They had weight problems and thus they were relegated to their own division. These garbage wrestlers are not even wrestlers at all. I do not call hitting someone in the head with a garbage can or sticking a thousand tacks in your opponent wrestling. But superiority comes with a price, their has to be someone there for you to embarrass. At this moment, I have nothing to say anymore. I am just sick of the wrestling scene as of now. (Akuma walks away from the couch. He takes the I-Crown Asia title belt off his waist and places it on a shelf. He grabs a t-shirt and slips it on. He takes a pair of khakis and takes them into the washroom with him.) SHANE: Hey, Kashan. You know, I've had something on my mind. I've been thinking this thing over for quite a while. KASHAN: Yeah, what is it. SHANE: It's about this documentary thing. I just can't end it like this. There's just so much more left for me to do. I'll make you a deal. I want to follow you through your trials and tribulations in Gunryo Pro. How about it? This just can't end like this. Not here in Chicago. KASHAN: No problem man. I knew you couldn't resist it. Kashan Akuma is just too popular of a product to leave. This is gonna be a big hit Shane. A big hit. Those marks in America are really going to enjoy this. A whole season of Akuma highlights. Hell, we could make it into a series. (Akuma steps out from the washroom. He is wearing an I-Crown t-shirt, khakis, and a pair of Nikes. He has sunglasses on and his hair is wet and spiked. He has a cell phone in hand.) KASHAN: Now if you don't mind fellas, I'm going back out there. There's just too many groupies out there to take advantage of. I'll see you guys later. (Kashan stops at the door for one second and points at the camera. He smiles and then leaves the room. Right in front of the room he puts his arm around a female fan who has been waiting for him. After about five seconds, he ceases training, stares directly into the camera and smiles.) Wildcat: Greetings G-Pro. As most of you already know my name is "Wildcat" Jim Lewis Jr. Many of you have heard about me, but for those who haven't don't worry, by the end of the month, I'm sure I'll have made a lasting impressing. I have decided to join G-Pro for different reasons than most. Not to expand my career, make allot of money or as a means of attracting women, but as an opportunity to express my pure wrestling skill, gain new abilities, and to restore honour and integrity into our once great sport. It will also give me an opportunity to represent my country Australia in an honourable way to our Asian Neighbours'. Wrestling has been in my family's blood for generations. It started with my Grandfather William Lewis who was a great amateur wrestler in Ireland. Unfortunately, a knee injury brought a premature end to his active participation. However, his passion and pride of the sport never ceased even when he and his wife immigrated to Australia. Through all the hardships of been European settlers he was able to emerge the same pride and passion of wrestling into his three sons, who all become stars in the Australian Wrestling Federation. Collectively they where known as "The Lewis Trio" and They where the most fear group in the AWF for twenty years. In total, they won 13 Championships including both singles and Tag Team competition. Although they had great courage and teamwork, their greatest asset was their diversity. My Uncle Bruce was one of the most feared brawlers and my other Uncle Randy was a one of the first high-flying super star. But the greatest of the three was no doubt my father the original "Wildcat" Jim Lewis who was a technical superstar. Individually he won 5 singles and 2 Tag Team Championship, a feat that has not been equalled to this day. Naturally, I was expected to follow in my father's footsteps, as a little over two years ago I joined the AWF. I'll never forget the friendships and the good times that I had in AWF. I was my first chance I hade to wrestle professionally and it made a man out of me. I wrestled all around Australia, but I couldn't match the success of my Uncles or Father, in fact, I didn't even win a title. Although I enjoyed my time in the AWF, there where far too many unrealistic expectations placed upon me and, late last year I decided to leave the AWF and head overseas. I was fortunate enough to be scouted by the New Era Championship Wrestling while doing some small Indy work. The NECW was pure class; I stayed in the best hotels, ate at the best restaurants and wrestled against the best opponents. The first couple of months where difficult and it was hard to get noticed, particularly considering some of my opponents where "Too Cool" Chris Hopper, Playboy Ronnie D and Gary Albright. But after a couple of months of hard work and persistence, I was achieved one of my greatest moments of my career when I won the TV Title. I defended the title as much as I could determined to be a "fighting champion". Unfortunately, I lost the title to a person called the Canadian Savage, and a week later NECW was forced to close. When NECW closed, I was shattered. I entered the fed as a young, naive Australian just hoping to make it into an international Federation, and I left it a former Champion. I joined the AEWF and IeWC in quick succession, and I have enjoyed relative success in both of those Federations. I again became a champion in AEWF by winning their King of the Air (Light Heavyweight) Title, proving that my first title wasn't a fluke. Although I have enjoyed my time in American Federations, I feel that they lack a certain quality, and that quality is honour. I feel that there are too many competitors who wrestle just for the money and the glory and not enough for the love of the sport. When my father was starting out, he wrestled free; I don't think anyone today could say the same thing. But I understand that the executives of the G-Pro feel the same way I do. The feel, that honour is more important than glory. The G-Pro will allow me to showcase my naturally wrestling ability. Who ever wins in the ring will, be the better man, no matter what. Naturally, I'm not going to find the new competition easy. I will need to concentrate on my skills and even learn new ones. But I know that with a little bit of hard work I can make anything happen. That's how my father would've wanted it.
