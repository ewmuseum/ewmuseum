Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The International Championship Wrestling Council re-launched in January 2008 — its own served news page recalls an earlier ICWC "first developed by another writer under the UWOW banner" years before — as the third member fed under the UWOW banner, debuting with Greed at the Georgia Dome under General Manager Eric Bischoff, a card built around the surprise arrivals of Hulk Hogan and Randy Savage and the shock return of Chris Benoit alongside a reunited Radicals. Every named competitor across its surviving record is a real professional wrestler: a sixteen-man single-elimination tournament crowned the first champion the same night, Dean Malenko outlasting a field that included Kevin Nash, Bill Goldberg, Bret Hart and Sid Vicious to beat Owen Hart in the final.

The World Heavyweight Championship changed hands five times across the surviving record: Hulk Hogan took it from Malenko in a Lumberjack Match at Capital Combat (2008), Jeff Jarrett from Hogan at the UWF's own School of Hard Knocks, Kurt Angle from Jarrett the same night he won a three-tier Chamber of Horrors cage match, and Edge twice, the second reign unifying the XWF's own World Heavyweight Championship at Capital Combat (2009), defeating XWF Champion Brock Lesnar and Hulk Hogan in a three-way match. The Intercontinental Championship is a continuation of a UWF belt rather than a new creation: Ultimate Warrior arrived as the reigning UWF Intercontinental Champion and, per both feds' own ledgers, brought the title with him.

The fed's own General Manager chair changed hands twice across the surviving record: from Eric Bischoff to Vince Russo by October 2008, Russo's single card as GM — Russo's Revenge, headlined by the Chamber of Horrors — crowning Kurt Angle before Russo himself was fired; and back to Bischoff for Capital Combat (2009), sent in as UWOW owner Matthew Hopkins' own enforcer while doubling as General Manager of the XWF the same night.

The surviving capture holds the fed's own news page and title-history ledger in full alongside all six of its dated 2008–2009 show scripts — Greed, Sin City, Capital Combat (2008), Spring Stampede, Russo's Revenge and Capital Combat (2009) — every one read whole and structured match by match. No roster page survives in this capture, though both the fed's own news page and its title-history page link to one — an honest gap, not a completeness claim. The United States Championship's own table breaks down into an unstructured name sequence after its first, tournament-final reign; no surviving WON BY/WON FROM/WHEN columns remain to structure it further.
