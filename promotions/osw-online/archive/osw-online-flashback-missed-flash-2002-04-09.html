Flashback (missed flash)

9 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
My apologies to Zach on missing this, it was simply misplaced in a rush. ******************* == JEREMY STEELE == ******************* [Black.] VO: Wrestling is an addiction, you know. [Fade in.] VO: It takes a hold of your body and your mind. [SCENE: River Valley, Michigan, a small, working class suburb of
Detroit. More specifically, a high school campus. The only high school campus in this town, River Valley High. The school is old looking, probably refurnished recently, but just the same the architecture of the building dates it over half a century. We pan past this building and its grassy green lawns and freshly painted asphalt parking lot, we see it's flagpole by the front door, and Old
Glory proudly waving in the early spring wind.]
VO: I've tried to walk away more times than I care to remember. Each time, the pull is stronger. Each time, the itch gets harder and harder to resist.
[We cut to the side of the school and to the adjacent football stadium with its eight-lane track circling it and its two sides of bleachers.
The field, like its neighbor building, is old. It has a small town sensibility kind of look to it.]
VO: Late at night, long after my wife has put our son to bed, I hear the voices in my dreams... the screams of a thousand faceless heads creating a sound like a lion roaring.
[The camera begins to zoom in closer on the field. Someone is standing in the middle, staring up at the stadium seats. As we get closer, the broad-shouldered figure of a man come into view. Tall, well built, athletic. Black workman boots adorn his feet. A pair of faded out carpenter jeans and a half buttoned flannel shirt over a plain undershirt make up his attire. Black sunglasses hide his eyes, with blond bangs framing either side. The man slowly takes off his sunglasses, squints for a moment, as if it hurts to see, then blinks, pupils open. A slow smile creeps across his face and a deep sigh reasonates from within.]
MAN: Once upon a time, my friends and I used to step out onto this field every Friday night. Once a week, teenage boys became golden gods. Things were simpler back then. I can still hear the roar of the crowd in those very seats after a big tackle or a great catch.
Gives me goosebumps every time. You wanna know the funny thing about it? [The man laughs to himself, as if remembering the punchline to an old joke.] MAN: I never cared too much for football. It was fun, don't get me wrong, but it was just something to do for fun to pass the time and to get in shape.
You see, wrestling was my first love. Inside that school, there's a little gym that they renamed in my honor about five years ago. It doesn't take much in this town to be a legend. I kind of lucked into it. So, as fate would have it, the place I used to affectionately refer to as the Sweat Box is now forever known as the Jeremy Peter Steele Gymnasium.
[He pauses, shrugging off the significance of this fact. The camera turns a bit, taking him all in. The man is indeed Jeremy Steele, a man who's name meant something in pro wrestling once upon a time.]
JS: The sounds of a couple hundred people in a high school gym can't compare to the sounds of a couple thousand in some big dome covered arena. I should know. I've been in every size gym, auditorium, and arena in the United States and Canada over the course of my amateur and pro wrestling careers. I've come to the conclusion that the size of the crowd doesn't matter. It's the intensity.
That, folks, is why I came back to wrestling. Not for money, not for titles, not for any of those superficial poor excuses to wrestle in the first place. No, I came back because of a craving for one of life's greatest pleasures.
The thrill of the pop. [Steele pauses, rubbing his chin. He closes his eyes for a moment, probably reliving some former memories in his head. Again, he smiles.
He speaks again, eyes still closed.] JS: Not so long ago, when I was still an up and comer, I used to be obsessed with being the best. I prided myself on being a competitor, a true athlete, a true student of the sport. I began to believe the hype behind me that touted me as one of those blue chip, can't miss, next big thing prospects.
It's funny what injury and a divorce can due to your plans for stardom. I was right on my way to being something big. I was on the cusp of superstardom. Then, just as fast as I had risen to the top...
[He snaps. Opens his eyes.] JS: It was all over. I crashed and burned. I couldn't handle the pressure, I guess. Just like that, I went from being the next big thing to being just another face in the crowd. I tried hard to get my spot back. I was doing it for all the wrong reasons, though. It took me a long time to realize that.
I walked away because I lost the passion. A new marriage and fatherhood sobered me up to the things that are truly important in life. I vowed then that I was done with this sport. I was a family man.
[He shakes his head with a sigh.] JS: Well, a few financial problems later, guess where ol' Jer ends up? Back inside the ring, whoring himself out for a few bucks to make ends meet. I hated it. I hated every second of it. I walked out again, sick of myself.
A few months later, I let my good friend Dallas Reed talk me into promoting. I still needed money, and he had plenty of it. He'd help me out if I helped him out.
Well, there's a reason why I was a wrestler and not a promoter. Dal and I sure tried hard, but boy, that sucker went under faster than the
Titanic. The good news was I dug myself out of the money hole and I didn't need to stick around.
So I didn't. [He pauses. Squinting into the crowd once again, Steele bites his lip before going on.] JS: Time went on. My baby boy began to grow up before my eyes. It was nice to be home with the wife and kid instead of on the road four days a week. Still, in the back of my head, the voices of that crowd just wouldn't fade away.
Every time I walked away, I had walked away from a lot of missed opportunities. They nagged at me. I hated wondering to myself, "What
If?" The critics had tagged me as a bust long ago, and it hurt. I didn't want to be remembered as a guy who wasted talent. I didn't want to go down in the books as a guy who could've been somebody.
I blame it on my dad, really. His stubborn pride was passed down to me. Like my father, after working my tail off for years, I'd be damned if I let anybody soil my good name.
But that was just a side issue. Just an added incentive. The real draw was the pop. I needed to hear it again. I needed to remember how alive it made me feel.
The first go-around, I wrestled to be the best. The second, I wrestled to make money. This time?
[Steele strokes his chin again, and beneath the face of a thirty two year old man, the face of a young boy full of exuberance peers out.
He can't help but smile yet again.] JS: I'm wrestling for the thrill of the pop. [He shakes his head, sighing.] JS: Wrestling is in my blood. It's my life. It's just not possible for me to walk away for good. Not as long as my body's still able.
So, one more time, I've got to lace up the boots and show the world that this old hoss can still go.
[Pause.] JS: You know, I hesitated when I got the contract for OSW. I had been talking to them for weeks and I had thought it over for a long time, but I still wasn't sure. Then my wife, Cameron, turned to me and said, "Jer, you've got a little boy who's never seen you in the ring before. Do you want your son to grow up never being able to see his daddy do what he loves?"
I signed my name right then and there. Scotty, I hope you're watching with Mommy. Daddy's going back to work. [With that, Steele puts his sunglasses back on and slowly begins to walk off the football field. The camera lingers on his back for a few seconds before we fade to black.]
