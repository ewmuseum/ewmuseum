House Show Report

4 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
OSW House Show Report by [name withheld] I arrived late at the Schoolhouse, but found out a few things from the "OSW Cult" (Note: You may remember these guys from my last House Show report -- The OSW Cult likes to call themselves the smarkiest fans you'll ever see. Adored by the fans, hated by most wrestlers). First off, a few notes: -- Travis Cade once again showed up early in preparation for his match tonight against Lew Spectre. He trained in the ring beforehand with Hokori Ketsueki, the same guy challenging OSW Old School Champion Def Bellows to a non-title match. I heard the two put on quite an enjoyable exhibition match which the other wrestlers came out to watch. It looks like Cade was the first to arrive at the arena... take that for what it's worth, but the guy is getting on the good side of fans quickly. I'm jumping ahead here, but he also stayed at least an hour or so after the show, signing autographs and was the last person to leave.
-- But that's just small fry... not only in the OSW Cult, but throughout the arena there was talk about "foreign investors" offering an OSW buy-out and matching talent exchange possibilities. Even Keith Robinson commented on the rumors on Flashback, which further fueled speculation and some pretty crazy ideas. Once again, take it as you will, but it's obvious that somebody backstage is leaking news.
-- Another leak concerned a possible _BIG_ announcement for the upcoming Pay Per View, which news MAY also coincide with the signing of a "Wrestling Legend". Some members of the Cult had ideas, with plenty of names thrown out (many of them laughable, but you never know).
Schoolhouse in Portland was once again sold out, with plenty of fans of all ages in the crowd. Nice to see that the OSW can still put on a clean show with plenty of wrestling, compared to those OTHER leagues out there, which try to push the exact opposite.
After some corporate shilling from Jeff Cunningham (including a new t-shirt for Travis Cade) and the national anthem (sung by some local dude who actually wasn't too bad), we finally got to the matches:
~~~~ 1. 3T0 Irish Mafia -vs- Simpson Brothers "Shamrocks and Shenanigans" brings out the 3T0 Irish Mafia, to little crowd reaction. The crowd reaction actually goes down, however, as the Simpson Brothers come out. A few people go to get some nachos. Shawn McShae starts off with a drop toe hold/side headlock combo that just totally confuses the hell out of Josh Simpson, who quickly escapes to tag in the elder Alan Simpson. Shawn chuckles, tagging in
Johnny McShae. The match is pretty downhill from here, back-and-forth but sloppy and unfocused. The brutality finally ends with a double dropkick by the
McShaes, which is quite obviously too much for Alan Simpson, as we've got a pin. Winner: 3T0 Irish Mafia (double dropkick, 3:36) Rating: DUD This one was supposed to be the match to get the crowd into it, but it failed on many levels. But once it was over and the
"You F'ed Up!" chants finally died down, everybody looked at the lineup and saw THIS match: ~~~~ 2. Preston Mayfield -vs- Blackout Burns "Back in Black" by AC/DC brings out Blackout Burns, who gets a mixed but existent reaction. "Soft as Snow (but Warm Inside)"
by My Bloody Valentine takes AC/DC's place over the P.A.... and out stumbles a _VERY_ drunk Preston Mayfield. A bit of confusion between Referee Corolla and Burns, but Mayfield stumbles forward in an attempt at an attack. Burns, at this point, has become pissed, and he sidesteps Mayfield, locking him up for a vicious double-arm
DDT. This gets a quick, yet disappointing three count. Winner: Blackout Burns (double-arm DDT, 1:59) Rating: -* And yes, this is the first match to EVER get a minus rating on the House Show. Burns couldn't do much of anything but end it quickly. Crowd was disgruntled, booing after the match. Some members of the Cult threatened to leave, but then laughed it off. A loud "FIRE
MAYFIELD!" chant started up amongst the Cult. Somebody should tell President Ice that... maybe next time he'll schedule better matches for a House Show.
~~~~ 3. "The Natural" Travis Cade -vs- "Strictly Business" Lew Spectre Carly Simon's "Nobody Does It Better" brings out Lew Spectre, to a heel reaction. Spectre makes his way to the ring quickly, focused and completely ignoring the fans around him. The music changes to "20th
Century Boy", to a surprisingly big face pop. Travis Cade embraces the reaction when he comes out, high-fiving fans along the aisle, making his way completely around the ring before turning his attention to Spectre in the ring, sliding in when it's safe.
The two technicians circle one another, looking for a lockup. Cade in, Spectre goes low for a single leg takedown that Cade rolls through and out of. A VERY nice technical exchange involving 3 side headlocks, 5 hammerlocks, 2 armbars, 3 armwringers, a front chancery, 2 inside cradles and a kickout that gets the "OLD-SCHOOL~!
OLD-SCHOOL~!" chants going quite heavily. Cade starts to win a second exchange, but Spectre goes low and drives Cade's shoulder through the corner into the ringpost to secure the early advantage. Spectre stomps away at Cade, showing little interest in Referee Corolla's instructions. Spectre slides to the outside, pulling
Cade with him and driving Cade's head into the apron twice. Spectre slides into the ring to break the count, then back out and looks for a snap suplex... blocked and reversed by Cade, to a BIG pop! Cade slow to his feet, sends Spectre in and follows him.
A few fans in the "smart" section of the crowd start a "CADE! CADE!" chant that picks up steam as The Natural gets back up. Cade pulls Spectre up, Spectre fires a wild right that Cade counters overhand into a Fujiwara armbar. Cade continues to work over the arm for a few miutes, using a hammerlock bodyslam, leg drops to the arm, a hammerlock northern lights suplex and a shoulderbreaker, but the pin attempt only gets two.
The crowd is now solid behind Cade, and his inexperience shows as he's distracted just long enough for Spectre to get a thumb to the eye, atomic drop... up into a fireman's carry... airplane spin...
and Spectre drops Cade gut-first onto the top rope! A few fans pop the old-school move, though most are jeering as both wrestlers try to collect themselves on the mat. Spectre is the first back to his feet, and a
BLATANT kick down low gets a stiff warning from Referee Corolla. Spectre goes for a pin... but is caught off-guard by a quick Cade inside cradle that only gets two.
Spectre back up, stomping at Cade in anger and frustration. Spectre pulls Cade up, sends him into the ropes, Cade slides underneath Spectre, reverse waistlock... German Suplex is blocked, then Spectre hits a back elbow, backing Cade into the ropes. Spectre ties Cade's arms in the ropes, and stomps away at a helpless Travis Cade as the crowd jeers heavily in disapproval. Corolla finally gets between Spectre and
Cade, forcing "Strictly Business" away from the Natural. As the referee untangles Cade from the ropes, Spectre uses the distraction to untie one of the turnbuckle pads and expose some steel. Spectre pulls a freed Cade up, sends him into the ropes... Extreme
Closeup (hotshot) attempt is countered by a Cade spinning DDT. Spectre struggles to his feet, Cade from behind with a Tiger Suplex, rolled into a double bridging armbar, which eventually forces a submission.
Winner: "The Natural" Travis Cade (Cavalcade, 21:43) Rating: **** Now THAT'S what we were all waiting for, knowing Spectre could pull a good match out of a hat. Funny thing, is that this is Cade's first great OSW match. Now why isn't this guy wrestling on Insession? I think most of the crowd agreed with me. But what's up with Lew? Some people around me were talking about him finally retiring or... *gasp*... going back to Richard
Ray Vaughn the 3rd and Big Sir. Onto our Main Event... which should be interesting. ~~~~ Non-Title 4. Hokori Ketsueki -vs- Def Bellows Bellows is coming off one of the worst weeks ever for Superiority Complex. Sexual Energy loses their tag team titles, then Chris Colton no-shows a tag match with Bellows, and THEN Sexual Energy end up costing Bellows the match. "Dare" by Stan Bush brings out Hokori Ketsueki, to a decent face pop. Ketsueki gives a few high-fives along the way to the ring. The cheers quickly and decisively turns to the loudest heel pop of the night, as the
Old-School Champion Def Bellows appears. Bellows glares at Ketsueki in disgust as he slides into the ring, leaving his
Old-School Title belt at ringside. The match starts with a couple of Bellows drop toe holds, followed by a couple of disrespectful slaps to the back of
Ketsueki's head. The crowd jeers the gesture, causing Bellows to turn and remind them that he's their god. Ketsueki up, charges Bellows from behind, but the Old-School Champ steps aside and guides Ketsueki past him and right up and over the top rope to the floor. Bellows climbs the turnbuckle for his classic Titanic pose, once again bringing loud jeers from the Schoolhouse faithful.
Ketsueki slides back into an assault by Bellows. Bellows sends Ketsueki into the ropes, Ketsueki blocks a hiptoss and hits one of his own, to a pop! Bellows gets up, wearing a look of total shock that gets plastered by a standing dropkick. Bellows slides out of the ring for a breather,
Ketsueki into the opposite ropes, suicide dive through the ropes misses, and Ketsueki takes a sick bump that silences the crowd.
Bellows, ever the opportunist, drills Ketsueki stiff in the face with a kick, then sends him back into the ring. Bellows shakes his head in disgust, going for a very lax pin that barely gets one. The Echelon of
Excellence pulls Ketsueki up into a DDT, then gloats for the crowd, who continue to jeer the Old-School Heavyweight Champion relentlessly... well, with the exception of the section of the crowd that has slowly but surely become
Bellows marks. They love this. Ketsueki pulls himself up in a corner, Bellows charges... and Ketsueki sidesteps. Bellows stumbles backwards out of the corner... right into a
Dragon Suplex by the foreign sensation. Both men down, as the crowd tries to rally Ketsueki back into the match... both wrestlers slow to their feet,
Bellows with a right hand... blocked! Ketsueki sends Bellows into the ropes... spinning heel kick! Ketsueki hits an Asai Moonsault, but it only gets two. Ketsueki pulls Bellows up... thumb to the eye by the Champ. Bellows shakes off the assault, taking Ketsueki down with a hard spinebuster then heading right to work with stomps, obviously not interested in playing games anymore. Bellows pulls Ketsueki up into a standing headscissors...
PILEDRIVER! But Ketsueki kicks out, to a pop! Bellows is stunned, trying for another cover, and again getting two. A third try is no more successful, so Bellows kicks Ketsueki in the head.
Ketsueki trying to struggle to his feet... Bellows scoops him up, MICHINOKU DRIVER II! But again, a kickout at the absolute last second, a look of shock on the Champ's face, and a face pop! Bellows back up quickly... and the jumping vertebreaker drives the nail into the coffin.
Winner: Def Bellows (Definator, 18:11) Rating: ***1/2 Gotta love these non-title matches, especially on a House Show. That's when surprises happen, and Bellows wasn't expecing much from Ketsueki. But then, THIS happened... After the match, Bellows assaults Ketsueki, taking out some unknown aggression on the youngster. Bellows slides out of the ring, grabs a chair, slides into the ring... _MEGA_ FACE POP~!!!
Def Bellows quickly slides out of the ring as Travis Cade slides in, covering Ketsueki and keeping a cautious eye on Bellows. The Old-School Champion glares angrily at Cade, before grabbing his title belt and leaving under the watchful eye of The Natural.
~~~~ House Show Rating: **1/2 It's amazing how two matches can save a show... AGAIN. If President Michael Ice wants to keep people coming to these House Shows, he needs to change things around. This is the second week in a row that the last two matches saved the show. If this isn't an indication on how these wrestlers who
AREN'T on Insession are doing... I don't know what is. A lot of talk tonight concerned a LOT of the WWO wrestlers. What happened to them? Shouldn't the OSW have scooped them up? You know as well as I do that's what the OSW needs right now. Hopefully this wrestling "legend"
they're thinking of bringing in (and PLEASE don't let it be Don Cameron... he's WAY past his prime). So overall, ALMOST two thumbs down on this show... but hey, it's nice to see Travis Cade get some respect from the crowd that he deserves. This guy's been working his ass off lately, and getting a huge win over Lew
Spectre should help him. Thanks for reading, we'll see you next House Show! ©2003 [name withheld]/Old-School Wrestling Productions http://www.rocksolidcorp.com/wwo/
