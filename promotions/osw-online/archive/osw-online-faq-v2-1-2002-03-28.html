FAQ v2.1 (Good guys needed!)

28 March 2002

OK, before the FAQ... I've been getting a lot of heel applications lately. While I like that, as it means I can pick and choose... but I'm not getting enough face applications.
So, here's the deal. I will take ONE more heel tag team and TWO more heel singles wrestlers from among the applications I have... then I will close the door to heels. However, I still have room for about 5 quality face singles and 2-3 more face tag teams.
So, Old-School Wrestling is looking for the good guys in e-wrestling. Please, no "liked" heels or disturbed characters please, as we draw a more strict face/heel line than many feds in OSW, and we have enough disturbed faces at the moment. :) I'd like a couple comedy faces to liven things a touch, as well as a couple "old-school" type faces to
"defend the name Old-School", so to speak... especially grizzled veterans who may be considered "past their prime" and want to
re-establish themselves in an indy fed. Well... that's a little specific I suppose... I'll be happy with any solid face. ;) Please feel free to visit our webpage, OSW Online, for an application and to get on our mailing list.

1.1 In-Character OSW stands for Old-School Wrestling. Owned and run by tag team greats Michael Ice and Alex Perkins of Fire and Ice, OSW is an indy fed centered in Portland, Oregon... training home of Fire and Ice and a legendary city in the wrestling world. Working under a slightly limited budget, Ice and Perkins decided that OSW would finally return the focus in wrestling where it SHOULD be... on the ring.
Wrestling in a remodeled warehouse called the "OSW Schoolhouse", OSW not only admits its independant status... it embraces it. Similar to the old ECW Arena, the 1,000 fans the Schoolhouse holds are crowded together, with all the seating close to the ring. Ice and Perkins want to be sure that when fans come to see a OSW show live, that they don't have to watch backstage segments on a jumbotron or see silly and pointless gimmick matches. By returning the focus where it should be,
ON THE RING, and allowing the fans to crowd near the ring, OSW hopes to revive the spirit of wrestling... by going back to its roots.
1.2 Out-of-Character Old-School Wrestling is a booked e-wrestling federation, with a few twists as you'll see as you read this FAQ. We've been open since our first card, on February 5, 2002. OSW uses a "closed flash"
system, meaning flashes are sent to the flashmaster and posted in one show. No guestbooks, no open flashing. As well, angles carry more weight in OSW than in many feds. We run on a bi-weekly schedule, with one card and two flash shows every two weeks, in addition to an update.
As mentioned in-character, OSW is an indy fed based in Portland, Oregon. The wrestling is low-budget (no pyros, no jumbotron, etc.) and "classic" (hardcore is discouraged). As well, unlike many e-feds today (as well as the WWF), all the action is held IN THE RING (see
1.3 for more info). 1.3 Old-School? I'm going to clarify my feeling of "old-school" now. OSW is not an attempt, despite many people's initial thoughts of "old-school", to recreate the NWA. OSW, as with any modern e-fed, will still rotate around storylines and angles. And high-flyers and brawlers are more than welcome to apply.
Where "old-school" comes into play in OSW is in the focus. As mentioned earlier, we don't have a jumbotron. To alter an old cliche, if something happens backstage, and no cameras are around to see it...
did it really happen? In OSW... no. If it doesn't happen in the ring or on the flash shows... it doesn't happen. Period.
As a corollary to that... OSW will allow NO MORE THAN THREE (3) IN-RINGS PER SHOW. And priority will go to in-rings that extend/start angles, especially in-rings composed by more than one handler. We're not going to be like most feds, and run a show half-wrestling and half-angle development. As a result... OSW shows will seem small, but will be nearly constant action, with talking thrown in occasionally for effect. Remember when wrestling was like that? Actually wrestling? Me too.
2.0 Who runs OSW? ================== 2.1 In-Character As stated before, OSW is co-owned and co-run by "Iceman" Michael Ice and "Red Hot" Alex Perkins, better known as Fire and Ice. The duo have been wrestling as a team since 1994, in such esteemed promotions as SCRA, WWO, UEW, GFWA and CCW. Ice has also done significant booking work for a few federations, and their close friend Mark
Edwards, who was Vice President then President of NCCW for 2 years, is helping as an advisor and head booker, though he stays completely behind the scenes.
2.2 Out-of-Character OSW is run by me, "KrazyAce" [name withheld]. Yep, I got the booking itch bad this time, I went and started another fed. ;)
For those of you who don't know me, here's some credentials so you know I'm not totally full of it. As I said above, I handle Fire and
Ice, who've been seen all over e-wrestling, and are currently active in the WWO. I also handle Chris Walker, former AEWA Television
Champion and currently active in AWMC, as well as the currently-retired Shade Roberts and Rachel "Ice" Edwards, if anyone remembers those names from a year or so ago. As far as fed running, I was co-owner and VP of New Classic Championship Wrestling (NCCW) for about 6 months, then became president when my co-owner retired from e-w and ran it fairly successfully (depending on which former NCCW'er you ask ;)) for almost a year and a half before losing my drive.
Well, the drive's back, and so am I. And with a few tweeks (like going bi-weekly), I might just be able to keep my sanity this time. ;)
3.0 How are winners decided? ============================= 3.1 Flashes First and foremost, flashing or roleplaying is how you succeed in OSW... and in any e-fed in existence. ;) All the general flash rules apply here. I would like to stress quality over quantity... though there are two flash shows, two crappy, 10-line flashes will not get you a win over one well-written flash. Flashes are expected at least once per week from every wrestler, and if you go 3 or more weeks without flashing you can be dropped from the roster. Why join a fed and not participate, anyway?
On-card flashes are also allowed... sort of. Due in part to budget restrictions and in part to concept, the federation did not invest in a jumbotron of any kind. So anything that goes on backstage... stays backstage. Besides, there isn't much backstage to show anyway. If it doesn't happen in the ring or within the ringside area, the fans don't see it. As well, there will be a MAXIMUM of
THREE (3) in-rings from wrestlers not involved in that week's card on any Insession. So, if you don't have a match a certain week and you want to do an in-ring, you'd better talk to me about it, because it's my discretion whether or not to use ANY non-relevant in-ring. OSW is a classic fed, the show is for wrestling, not to be loaded with in-rings like many e-feds (and the WWF, for that matter) are doing now days. If you want to get your point across, there's two flash shows a week, and you're free to do an in-ring any week you have a match...
Sorry, but that's a pet peeve of mine. :) If you want to do one, e-mail me with your plans and we'll talk. 3.2 Heat/Angles Heat is important in OSW. Wait, let me repeat that... HEAT IS IMPORTANT IN OSW. There. :) E-wrestling is about the stories you tell with your character, the stories behind the battles, not just the battles themselves. If a wrestler with little or no heat in the fed faces a wrestler who is incredibly over, and the angle would be better served if the over wrestler won, then he'll likely win. Consider this motivation to talk to your fellow handlers and work out angles...
Building a good angle or feud not only gives you material for flashes, but it will help you truly succeed in OSW. We DEEPLY encourage handler interaction here in OSW... so keep that in mind coming in.
3.3 Participation The third important influencing factor is how active you are. Quite simply, if two wrestlers seem even flash-wise and heat-wise in a match, I'll go with the one that's been more active in the few weeks prior to the match. But these all run together... building a good angle will require participation and give you material for flashes...
you getting the point yet? ;) 3.4 Strats While not as important as the first three, strats, or strategies, can influence a match decision as well. If I get a really intriguing strat that either starts or continues another angle, but would alter the booking drawn from the first three categories, I have no problem running the strat regardless. Have I pushed across the importance of angles and heat in OSW enough yet? :)
4.0 So, what shows do you have? ================================ NOTE: This schedule is subject to change, as anyone who's ever run a fed knows. :) 4.1 OSW Insession The whole enchilada. This is where all the action happens. OSW Insession will feature anywhere from 5-7 matches, fully-commentated by Sam Collins and "Cool" Keith Robinson. Held every other Tuesday, live from the OSW Schoolhouse in Portland, OR.
4.2 OSW Flashback One of the flash shows, on Saturday the week of Insession. As a part of "Cool" Keith Robinson's contract, he was given one show of his own each week, and this is it. Robinson will give his "expert analysis" on the happenings of Insession, as well as show flashes, followed by his own thoughts on those as well.
4.3 OSW Interactive The other flash show, on Wednesday the week after Insession. Hosted by the versatile and unbiased Jeff Cunningham, he'll show flashes, as well as preview the upcoming Insession. Occasionally he'll also do an interview with a wrestler on the show.
4.4 Supercards At the moment, OSW has no PPV contract, but will still schedule and hold supercards. There will be 4 supercards a year, 2 of which are theme PPV's (I'll tell everyone which and what they are when the time comes :)). I feel that any more than 4 is overload and doesn't give the appropriate time for people to develop angles and feuds.
Supercards will be announced as they come up, to keep the surprise going. However, expect a supercard every 8-10 weeks. The first supercard has yet to be announced.
5.0 Who works here? ==================== 5.1 Michael Ice The co-owner of OSW, Michael Ice is also the president and head booker. He's the man in charge, and unlike many presidents, he's actually fair. For the most part, he stays behind the scenes, only appearing when he's needed. The fans didn't come to OSW to hear the boss talk, they came to watch the talent compete.
5.2 Alex Perkins The other co-owner, Perkins is the acting Vice President of OSW, and is also head of security. A monster of a man (6'8", 290 and one
HELL of a mean streak when mad ;)) and a very experienced wrestler, he has no problems breaking up a fight if it gets excessively out of hand, generally leading his security crew to the ring.
5.3 Jeff Cunningham The most unbiased, straight-forward reporter you have ever met. Cunningham has perfected his craft, and in addition to the resident interviewer in OSW, Cunningham's sharp, crisp voice will introduce the wrestlers to the ring as well. In addition to those duties,
Cunningham is the C.E.O. of the company and a member of the booking staff. And while that fact stays completely behind the scenes and is never brought up on television, for fear of Cunningham being unable to be unbiased, some wrestlers backstage still listen to what Cunningham has to say when he's talking, knowing the power he has.
5.4 Sam Collins The play-by-play man for OSW, Collins is a rookie to the booth. His knowledge of wrestling is incredible though, and often makes up for his lack of experience.
5.5 "Cool" Keith Robinson A long-time wrestler who has been color commentating since 1995, Robinson is the voice of experience on OSW broadcasts. He is a huge fan of old-school wrestling, and isn't afraid to share his rather opinionated, yet very intelligent, remarks often. Some say he also has some booking power in OSW, being old friends with Ice and Perkins, but that's an unconfirmed rumor...
5.6 Referee Bob Walter One of the two referees on the OSW staff, Walter is an unbelievably good referee, having honed his skills for years.
Rulebreakers will have a tough time sneaking in cheap shots with Walter in the ring, and "Old Bob" isn't afraid to call for the DQ. He seems to always be right on top of the action, in the right place at the right time as if it were a sixth sense.
5.7 Referee Tony Corolla The other referee in OSW, Corolla... well, he's certainly NOT Bob Walter. Corolla seems to fit the "greasy Italian" stereotype perfectly, and is far from the most talented referee. He's strong, and won't take a bump for anything... but he'll never count you out, rarely DQ anyone, and some say he can even be bribed.
6.0 How about the gold? ======================== 6.1 OSW Old-School Title The big one. The Old-School Champion is the king of OSW, the best wrestler in the fed. This title must be defended at least once every
2 months on Insession, and on all supercards. Any additional defenses are at the discretion of the champion.
6.2 OSW Television Title The next step down from the Old-School Title, but only a step. The Television Champion will have a hard time retaining his title though, because it must be defended every other Insession, as well as all supercards. Any additional defenses are at the discretion of the champion.
6.3 OSW Tag Team Titles The belts to have if you're a tag team, these are the tag equivalent of the Old-School Title. Again, the best team in the land generally holds these titles. These titles must be defended at least once every 2 months on Insession, and on all supercards. Any additional defenses are at the discretion of the champions.
7.0 Anything else I need to know? ================================== 7.1 Rules I shouldn't need to do this. :) You know the usual, no harassing remarks in OR out of character, no swearing (please adhere to this, a couple censored swears are OK but nothing excessive please), no whining if you lose, just ask me why you lost and I'll tell you...
yadda yadda yadda. I'll define these later if I have problems, but I trust everyone knows how to be professional about this. :)
7.2 Final Comments Yeah... have fun. The only reason I created OSW was for me and a bunch of handlers to have a little fun. Don't take this game too seriously, don't take it personally, and just enjoy it. :)
