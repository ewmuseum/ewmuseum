A May 1995 Usenet e-fed under the kayfabe byline “Jacob ‘Jake’ Kane” that crowned its first champions within days of launch and built nine title lineages across a bang-mark subject tag — WAR!ZONE, WAR!REPORT, WAR!fare — with a charter membership in the FORTRESS interfed alliance. 72 shows and 86 recorded reigns survive into a 1996 relaunch reaching 1997. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

WAR! Wrestling Federation opened under the kayfabe byline Jacob “Jake” Kane, who dated its founding to April 1995 in the federation's own later FAQs and put its first card on 2 May 1995 in a still-later players' guide — both his own words, not quite reconciled. Its own FAQ describes what it thought it was: “a federation where you enter characters and try to guide them to success... WAR! is as much about the battles outside the ring as it is inside it. In a way, it is very much a soap-opera... It is the emphasis on the characterisation of each wrestler that distinguishes WAR! from other feds.” Numbered broadcasts under a family of bang-mark names — WAR!ZONE, WAR!REPORT, WAR!TIME, later WAR!fare — carried the story, with an on-air broadcast team and an in-story president, General Andropov, presiding over it.

Its first season built around the Masters of War stable's feud with Seth Blitzspear: the Masters beat a rival stable called the Four Seasons, but it was Blitzspear, as the reigning World WAR! Champion, who ultimately beat the Masters of War themselves. That season ended for real reasons rather than fictional ones: in June 1995 General Andropov was assassinated in-story during a motorcade in Dallas, Texas, and the federation shut down soon after on its own operator's account of financial irregularities. It came back that October with “Bullfrog” Eric Helmroy installed as vice-president, and Blitzspear opening the new season as champion before “The Economist” Randy Blake took the title off him across three straight count-out and disqualification finishes.

A third age followed on a wave of new talent — Tyler Stevens, Rick Showboat, Bane, Big Duke, Lord Byron among them — and the Horsemen of the Apocalypse, fronted by the Reverend J.R. Death, dominated until they rebranded as Armageddon under Tonnage and went to war with rival stables of their own: War!Force, the Clique, the Rich Boys, Street Level. By the middle of 1996 a merger with a fed called WXW brought in a wave of outside talent and a three-fedhead structure the promotion called its fourth age, its interfed and WXW titles folded into a single Unified Championship; T.J. “Thunder” Decan won a Countdown to Extinction tournament that June and, when Blitzspear later relinquished the World WAR! title outright, sided with Tonnage rather than chase it back. One of its own fedheads also used this period to help start a spinoff fed, RAW, outside this record's own reading.

The federation kept running into 1997, surviving a mid-year split into parallel “Old War!” and “New War!” continuities that its own in-fiction ownership-change angle was careful to reunite that October without ever breaking its own numbered lineage of champions. Jacob Kane closed the promotion for the first time in June 1998 to, in his own words from the following year, “pursue life,” reopened it around the turn of 1999, and closed it again on 22 June 1999 at a supercard called Extinction '99: “Well, once again, I bring WAR! to an end, exactly a year from the last time I ended it, and six months since restarting... WAR! was always an escape for me — a world I created, owned and had power over... Sad to see it go, but pleased it is over.”

It did not stay closed. By that November a Players' Guide describes it merged into a new, colon-punctuated “WAR: Wrestling Federation” under a different, much younger president, its International Championship retired in favor of a new National WAR! Championship. Nothing after that November 1999 document is in the surviving record.
