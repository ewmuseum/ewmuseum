The Rant: One Ring

For as long as fantasy wrestling has been around, there have been different communities. In the beginning, you had a segment on every bulletin board provider – PRODIGY, AOL, Juno, RSPW (then RSPWF), etc. Each group did things differently and had a slightly different focus. With the advent of the broader Web – the ability to have chat rooms, to build web pages, etc., you began to have even more splinter groups – the Summit, NWC, etc. Now you have federations identified by where the majority of their community has discussion: FWrestling, PTC, EWN, X-Net, etc.

No, this isn't one of my infamous history lessons. It's simply quick background to put this in perspective – from the beginning of the game, there has been a desire to “unify” things under one banner. We went through it on PRODIGY, which was more of a homogenous community anyway. Various folks have tried to do it through forums or resource websites. Look through any set of forums (with FW Central being the possible exception) and you'll see a post pop up every few months asking about having a ‘unified' event or talking about more collaboration, or an attempt at an ‘interfed,' and on and on.

It's time to kill the myth of e-fed “unity.” Collaboration? Absolutely. Interfed events? Sure. One fed/site/person to rule them all? Definitely not.

There are too many differences. In the way feds are set up (their basis), the way they're run, the frequency of cards, the ages of the handlers, the way they like to communicate, the time they have to spend on the hobby – there's just simply not one way to do things.

And we like it like that, don't we? We certainly don't want to be ‘told what to do.' Any attempt at reining in ‘disruptive elements' turns into a discussion on censorship. Any suggestion that another way of doing… anything… turns into a flame war. Discussions on angle vs. roleplay turn into personal attacks on feds or handlers.

So then what's the point of something like the Daily Dose? It's not an attempt to ‘unify' communities. It is, however, an attempt to highlight what's going on in them. There will always be multiple places to play the game, but a little crosspollination can't be bad, can it?

I've been around long enough to know that to keep you guys reading, there will have to be some controversy to keep it interesting. I'll be sure to take care of that. But I'm hopeful that that won't be the only reason to read. I'm hoping that I'll be able to hit some of the big stuff, but also to pull out some of the gems in the rough that you haven't seen. I also plan to give folks a relatively open forum in which to highlight the writing they're doing, plus the help or feedback that they're asking for.

That being said – I hope to do this about three times a week. If there's a positive response and the e-mail keeps coming in, then hopefully I'll add some special guests who can fill up the rest of the days. So, if you're interested in submitting, simply send your comments/feedback and news/want ads/reviews and discussion to me at [detail withheld].

Out Of The Box The winner of the “Match Most Likely To Make Your Head Spin Just From The Stipulations” appears to be the main event for PTC's Super Series. Let me try to set the stage as best I can from what I've read… (I know you guys will correct me if I get this wrong.)

Super Series is a new PTC special event that serves as a precursor to GTT5 (PTC's fifth annual Golden Turnbuckle Tournament). Highlighting a number of top matches from its member leagues, SS also has some major interfed matches. The Main Event is setup to fill the vacant PTC interfed championships – the Global and Extreme Titles.

So get this – as far as I'm aware, it's the first “Scavenger Hunt” match. Held on the campus of Harvard University, the goal is for competitors to find and take one of the titles to a PTC official. Once they do, they win. Can you imagine the possible carnage? Wrestlers battling in the bell tower of Eliot Hall Tower… fighting down Cambridge Street… knocking each other through the racks of clothes at the campus bookstore?

And as if that weren't enough – listen to some of these stipulations: — The Uber Judge enters the match with the Extreme Title in his possession. — Karina Wolfenden and Seymour Almasy are tethered together to start the match. They can only be untethered by finding the participant who holds the key to their bonds.
— Rich Rollins is in the match – but if he is knocked out or pinned, that person ‘steals' his GTT “pick your opponent prize” won during Infinite Gauntlet. (Think “Money In The Bank” but with the power to choose your tournament opponent.)

I haven't seen anything so confusing, and yet so possibly entertaining, since the days of the WHEEL of DEATH. Then again, it could go the way of that one match, whose name escapes me, that was built around some mammoth, ridiculously-sized cage and featured every wrestler in the promotion – I honestly don't remember if that was a PTC match, or PCW, or just which league it was in… I just remember that it was more about the scenario then it was the characters. I think this one has the chance to show some great action by using the scenery as a tool to drive the character action.

If I had to handicap this one (which would be a bad idea since I haven't read all the roleplay, I'd certainly focus on:

— Shadow's neo-gothic dilemma — Hoyt Williams' cultic documentary — Brandon Youngblood's broken-hearted singlemindedness
— Seymour Almasy's fantastic quest to recover his identity
— Uber Judge's hilarious quest for Absolute World Domination

… out of those folks I'd have trouble picking my favorites without diving in some more and understanding the backstory. But I certainly was drawn to the Uber Judge immediately. And Youngblood's battle against trying to balance his personal life and his wrestling pulled me in as well, although that might be because of the similar stories I've run with Hornet over the years.

Apologies to anyone I left out by virtue of time. I believe that the Super Series card is currently about half-written and in the final stretches before being posted.
