Your Dose for the Week of January 30

31 January 2006 (record-derived page)
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

So I started this one almost two weeks ago at this point, right after the big weekend that saw fWo Countdown, ACW Legends and EPW Aggression 22 posted, among others. Since then, the NFW has posted their three Christmas/New Year's shows, and LoC has posted it's PPV-setting-up-edition of Violence, among others.

So if you've think I've had a chance to read all that – then you're insane. Especially given [detail withheld]. I did, however, make it through Countdown and the majority of Aggression, so I'll include the notes that I had there pre-evilness – in case anyone cares.

With Countdown One in the books, the Fans' Wrestling Organization becomes the Numbers Wrestling Organization has the insidious “black shirts” take over, apparently on orders from one John Smith aka Chameleon aka Green Guy with a major assist from Neo aka I used to be a dead guy. If there's one thing that the fWo has always done well (and there's certainly more than one), it's the way they use the fans as another character in the story. While I think the ‘mob' mentality has possibly been used one too many times, this one certainly had a different feel, as it was an organized movement – a direct assault against the fWo, as opposed to the others (that I remember) where the riots had more diffuse direction.

So a couple of overall thoughts, with some directed ones along the way:

You should all know that I've been a huge Ivy fan for a number of years (what, almost 12 since Pete joined the CSWA in 1994?). I still am – anything Ivy in the fWo is top-notch, and Travis obviously agrees. The only ‘neutral' comment – this felt a little too much like when Ivy figured out the Miles/Horsemen of the Apocalypse stuff – just too late to do anything about it. I understand the thought behind it – use Ivy to get the backstory that fills in the gaps between the historical segments and the new action – it'd just be nice once to see Ivy figure it out and do something about it.

The danger with fed-wide angles is always that the matches can become secondary. To some extent, that's okay, because the nature of the angle may demand it. In this case, I think the only one that was really affected was the main event. I need to read through it again – but it almost seems a little contracted, but that may have been only a feel based on the magnitude of what happened next.

One stylistic thing: in the past, the fWo ran play-by-play for their major PPV events. More recently, that seems to have started to go by the wayside. I'm a huge PBP fan, but I think the change has been a good one – the rest of the fWo shows are in a more prosaic format. I think the Flying Frenchie/Brand Frontier (which was in PBP) actually stuck out as a sore thumb in this case. Good match, but the PBP didn't work and wasn't necessary to tell the story. And since the commentator characters of Finn, Armstrong and Roberts aren't used enough to be developed, they just came across as bland.

Overall, I think the show does what it needs to do – it lets you know that a new chapter of the story, maybe a whole new volume, is coming. Who plays a major role in that is up for grabs. It'll be interesting to see what's next and how traditional fWo fans (and the haters, of course) react.

Oh, one other thought – Don, you're one sick puppy. Truly, truly sick. See the New Year's Desolation segment on the show for more.

With Aggression 22 Empire Pro Wrestling continues its pattern of hugely solid shows with a lot to keep folks interested.

Because I love them, I'll forgive their blatant stealing of the CSWA's video montage openings… considering I'm pretty sure it wasn't an original idea of ours. Although, who knows… I'm old… I forget things.

So a couple of general comments that I hope ‘translate' well: EPW has a great knack for making sure the amount of segments and matches mesh well, for making sure that the “pre-card” intros help set up the storylines for the rest of the card, and they're one of a few who have stuck primarily to “on-camera” segments that are believable. “Phenomenal” Frankie Scott's ‘search' for the boss is funny while not crossing over into slapstick. Even Joey Melton's over-the-top humor is tempered by Lindsay Troy and has a purpose – to show the ever-deepening interplay between the two characters.

In addition, the matches are long enough to be interesting, short enough to be readable, and almost to a T, tell more of the story of the characters involved without having to result to complete Dusty finishes or schmozzes.

I haven't had a chance to read all the way through ACW Legends yet, so my apologies.

Impressive, wot?

LoC's preview site for their “Self Destruction” PPV is a great way to rundown the card, and the Promo Showcase is a great catch-up as well – very similar to the “roleplay card” idea that I'm hoping will help rejump the CSWA.
