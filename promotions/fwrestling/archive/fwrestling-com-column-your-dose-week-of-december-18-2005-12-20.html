Under Review: A1E

So given my last attempt at a review, let's see how this one goes. I will warn that it'll be less detailed – since the volume of detail given to LoC Violence 11 led to me not finishing reviewing the whole show! So this time, I'm throwing down with the latest edition of A1E's Thursday Night Warfare.

For those who may have been under a rock, or who are content to play in their own sandbox, A1E operates off the message boards at A1Wrestling.com and on their own website at http://www.a1e.ca. The league is roleplay-based and decisions on winners are actually made based on votes by the “Booking Committee” and the members-and-fans-at-large. In the latest update, the vote from the BC and the “at-large” vote count the same toward the overall result.

I've been a fan of the A1E folks since the inception on A1Wrestling. If memory serves, they are actually a branch off the MBE tree, a league called “Message Board Entertainment” that started on the 1Wrestlingtalk forums when I was a webmaster there oh so long ago. The original gimmicks were similar to the ancient “be your own wrestler” leagues – the message board denizens themselves became wrestling characters. So when you see names like “BobbyR,” you'll know why. MBE shut down in late 2003, although the “MBE Cup” was run earlier this year. That information is important for the rest of the review, of course.

As usual, keep in mind that I'm neither completely up to date on the fed's current angles or characters (although I certainly know of many of them from over time), so I'm coming in pretty fresh.

TNW opens with a 10-bell salute to Eddie Guerrero, led by commish Nathan Houston devoting the show to his memory. Nicely done, although after reading it, I'm wondering if it shouldn't have been devoted to the memories of Kevin Nash and Scott Hall.

The big screen brings the fans into a warehouse where we find out that a “shadow syndicate” is organizing for the night. The “MBE army” is mobilizing – and despite being held down by the evil “A1E production crew” (huh?), they're ready to strike… and mangle Psalm 23 at the same time.

Mr. Amazing defeats Damon Blackburn before the A1Etron heads back to the warehouse. We begin to see some faces, but not that of the “ORGANIZER.” For a newb like me, the segment is a bit confusing, but it looks like the MBErs are ready to go after Anarchy Inc. and to set up the main event for the evening.

You wouldn't necessarily think it from the names, but I'm possibly giving my vote for match of the night to Slambo the Clown vs. Eddie Weston. The setup explained, even to a rookie like me, that Eddie Weston was worked over with barbed wire at the latest PPV, and that dear ol' Slambo is a member, albeit a joke one, of the HPSC (the Highland Park Social Club). We also find out that Sal Scantlin is apparently paid by the number of times he says “fairy” in his color commentary.

This match shows one of the ways A1E shines – while a mix of play-by-play and “event” writing (paragraphs giving the action), matches certainly tend toward the “old school” – where the action in the ring is paramount and momentum is the key. While there could be more commentary, it's clearly used to get the characters across and to give some insight into where they've been and where they're going. Slambo wins this one in an upset.

It's the first attack of the night as the new A1E Champion, Richard Farnswirth, is attacked by the MBE Syndicate's Professor Tremendous as he steps out of a limo.

And then back to the ring for the A1E Triple Star Championship match. HSBC member Duchess takes on champ James Irish – in a ”Tennis Shoe Search Match…

Wha?!?!

We eventually find out what the match is, and more importantly, why it's happening. Apparently Irish gets to choose his own stipulations, and obviously does so in a way that tips the match in his favor. But don't you just feel the backfire coming?

The match is set up so that four shoe boxes are attached to the turnbuckles – three with weapons and one with a pair of tennis shoes. If you get the shoes, you have to hold them and pin your opponent at the same time to win. I'm giving this one the benefit of the doubt and assuming that I missed something in the roleplay for the match. Surely… right?

One comment on the matchwriting itself – this was one of the more blatant places on the card where the mix of narrative and play-by-play just didn't work, IMHO. Lots of narrative, and then two lines of PBP, wash, rinse and repeat. The PBP didn't add anything until the end, other than allowing Scantlin to yell “FAIRY” a few more times.

We get the requisite backfire on Irish where he ends up with the shoes, while Duchess has weapons. Thankfully Slambo gets involved and messes everything up, doing the spot we all know and love where he holds Irish in place to be walloped by Duchess, only to get smacked himself. Irish hits his finisher, grabs the tennis shoes, and makes the cover to keep the title.

In contrast to some of the other MBE “invasion” stuff, we get a true segment following the match between Irish and Kanna Kirishima, who is on the MBE wagon. Kanna sets up a pitch to bring her old friend over to the dark side, but Jimmy Shamrock (James Irish, of course) lists the historic reasons that he's got no desire to jump on the MBE gravy train. Kanna fires back, accusing Irish of being “A1E's court jester,” before heading out. Good segment, great character development. Much more effective than an attack. Might have been even more effective as a warning shot “fired” before the attacks.

But then, just to show that Kanna Kirishima can not only talk a good game but is faster than a speeding bullet, the next segment has her nailing Beast with a chair in another “Syndicate” strike.

But it's a double strike as MBE's Star Rider and Bobby R then take down Chip Friendly and Duchess of the HPSC. I could only wonder where Slambo was, personally. (Note that a Chip Friendly vs. Jack Gilkison match is scheduled after this attack, but it wasn't posted as of the time I read it – I imagine that Gilkison wins by countout or over a beaten-up Friendly.)

And just because three's a crowd, we get the man with one of the best names ever, Maggot, attacking one with the worst, Big Dog, who is left laying in the locker room, although his hands are still marvelously taped for action.

JA hits the ring for an interview spot, which Sal assures us is so that the fairy can announce his retirement. Sadly for the color commentator, JA says he's not done, and warms my heart by referencing the CSWA Greensboro Championship, although not by name, which is respectful at the same time. His response to Gilkison is a bit meaningless, but I gather it's a segue so that he can move on to his real announcement – which ends up being his participation in A1E's “Pier Six Brawl” (cool name, btw) at the New Year's show. Presumably he wants into the Brawl because the winner gets a title shot – and then we're given one more “FAIRY” yell by Sal, just for good measure of course.

Housefly vs. Dan Ryan gives us a dream match of the night. Fly was the A1E champ just before Farnswirth, and if you don't know of Ryan's exploits, then I can't help you. Very good technical match and very well written. The PBP is used more effectively here, though still true to the league's PBP/narrative mix. We get some nice false finishes followed by a finisher reversal that leads to Ryan getting the win.

And then, we get an MBE run-in as Hida Yakamo drop Fly with a cricket bat of all things. And wonder of wonders, Dan Ryan doesn't help him out. And because karma is a you-know-what, Ryan gets dropped with a steel chair as he steps backstage. Count victims six and seven if my fingers are correct.

It's tag action for the rest of the night, starting with the A1E Champ teaming up with Gilkison again Beast and Big Dog. Sal reminds us that Farnswirth and Gilkison are the top two men in the company, but fails to remind us that Beast, Big Dog and Farnswirth have all been laid out by the Syndicate at various points tonight. We do, however, get some backstory to let us know that Beast and Big Dog don't necessarily trust each other. As we get more “fairy” shoutouts by Sal, Dog takes the bulk of the beating early until a hot tag. Beast jumps in hot and nails Farnswirth with a GORE…or not, as the champ takes his head off with a boot.

Beast redeems himself with a Guerrero-homage frog splash before a double hot tag leads to a Big Dog “Dog Pound” finisher moment, broken up by the Champ. Gilkison gets his moment to nail his finisher, only to have the ref pulled out of the ring by Beast. Things break loose outside as our beloved Slambo the Clown makes an appearance, only to be countered by Dan Ryan. It's Katie-bar-the-door in the ring (actually our sweet commentator Kayla goes with “donnybrook”) while the ref gives up and throws the whole thing out.

And so we get to the Main Event… can you guess what's coming? Red and black for life baby!

The tag titles are on the line in a TLC match, as Anarchy Inc. (Torment and Haven) defend against the Midwest Mafia (Irish Red and Tha Eliminat0r). Now I know what you're thinking… no, it's not Eli Flair in a 1994 throwback jersey… and it's not that “other” Eliminator who was a jobber… it's the Eliminat0r, who shall henceforth be referred to as T0r, just so I don't have to type as much.

We get some good setup advising us that this is a longstanding feud of violence and blood, and that both teams are claiming this is the blowoff to the feud. First good intros of the night lead to a cute spot where the Mafia charges Anarchy as they enter, only to be held back by security. There's even a point – the ref won't let the match start until he has control and gets the titles to hang them above the ring. Well done and logical. Who would've thunk it in today's wrestling, right?

The Mulkeys… excuse me, I forgot it's not 1986, the Mafia and Anarchy Inc. finally get hold of each other. All you really need to know about the match is that it's a hardcore spotfest, as a TLC match should be. The commentators even let us know that the bloodlust is so high that the teams are bent on destroying the other rather than going for the titles.

But that “logical” thing? That goes out the window at the end, as we get some spots that are reminiscent of the time that New Jack almost killed that fat rookie wrestler kid. Irish Red pulls out a switchblade and I quote, “drags the blade of the knife across Torment's forehead.” Well good for him, other than the fact that I'm just assuming those security guards that separated them to begin with must be developmental wrestlers from the local promotion and not real security, right? Cause why else would they let the man not only “slash” Torment, but then “position the knife near Torment's right eye.”

Thankfully the commentary lets us know that the crowd is horrified. Haven makes the save and we eventually get our first climb-the-ladder-for-the-belts spot, which Torment breaks up, ignoring the fact that he's bleeding like a stuck pig and may be dying in front of our eyes. He does manage, however, to be the next one up the ladder for the belts, at least until Irish Red stops him, and in doing so, drops a convenient bag of thumbtacks across the ring.

And so we know what spot must come next, as Red hits a move dropping the now stabbed and stuck Torment onto the tacks along with himself. And the crowd, once horrified, is now “going nuts.” We get another top-of-the-ladder spot from Haven and Red, which leaves everyone laying. Torment eventually grabs the titles from the top, presumably his last act as he dies from blood loss, well, other than standing in the middle of the thumbtack strewn ring celebrating.

And then… duhn duhn duhn….

Our MBE friend Whitenoise is back…and if it's not enough that Torment may be dead from having his forehead skinned (or at the very least have that nasty Dusty Rhodes forehead for the rest of his life), Whitenoise does his best Eddie Gilbert (or in fantasy, Craig Miles) impression by shooting a fireball out of his mouth courtesy of some grain alcohol and a lighter. Thankfully, however, the ringcrew is ready for all emergencies and “douses him with water” that is apparently on hand. Perhaps a fan's soda was used, but the commentary says water.

Torment gets tossed out of the ring to fall asleep and die, while Irish Red and Tha Eliminator are left docile. Then the MBE Syndicate is on its way to the ring. They surround Red and Eliminator… only to shake hands and high five the two of them. What could this possibly mean? Well, other than the fact that they're in on it, right?

Irish Red lets us know that he's the recruiter that has put the Syndicate together. He was tired of all the backstage whining and decided to bring in “the originators.” He says MBE is the foundation and in GUNS-fashion, they're not accepting any more excuses.

Not done yet…but just a sidenote – wouldn't this have made more sense if Red hadn't just lost a grueling match that left him with thumbtacks in his rear end?

And because this must all apparently be done in one show… we get a response!! Kayafaber (who presumably has been AWOL for a long time) appears in A1E colors, clearly ready to take on all 8 members of the Syndicate by him… oh wait, he's with them too. We even get the T-shirt-under-the-T-shirt, although no spraypaint.

But the Syndicate is kind… they take a moment to begin throwing MBE merchandise to the crowd. Then our dear play-by-play commentator Kayla gets in the ring, takes a cheap whore comment, slaps Kayafaber, then asks him how he could possibly, POSSIBLY be such a turncoat.

Kaya walks us through his recruitment, how he hates this business and the sport itself, but he loves the people in the ring with him. He guarantees us that this is a shoot, which I'm assuming means that the A1E production staff is just too stupid to stop broadcasting.

He dares Houston and the A1E to prove him wrong… and we find out that Torment is not only apparently not dead, but has recovered enough from losing two liters of blood to jump in the ring along with Haven and attack the entire Syndicate. We get some run-ins from the back as A1E apparently realizes that after seven attacks, they might want to fight back. And so we leave the site of the enormous brawl as security finally realizes that they need to do something… cause I can only imagine what Red's gonna do with that knife now.

The Wrapup: Needs more Slambo! I'd much rather have seen more about how Slambo responds to being the “joke member” of the HPSC and less of “How to Fillet a Wrestler.” Nothing wrong with the MBE “invasion” in principle (see the “GXW invasion” of years ago in the CSWA), but it was just too much at once. And the fact that no one seems to figure out that after three…four…five…six attacks that they shouldn't be on guard. Because so much of it was built on the “invasion,” I'm not sure what else to focus on – Duchess/Irish didn't quite work as a comedic match. Slambo/Eddie was well done, as was Housefly/Ryan. The other tag match needed more buildup around the Beast/Big Dog trust angle. I would've paid to see more moments like Kanna/Irish, rather than the nWo-like swerve—swerve—we gotcha—now-look-at-us stuff at the end. Again, made little sense to have the “reveal” after Red/Eliminator had just lost a huge match. I understand that it might not be “good business” internally to have them win the titles as part of the swerve, but it certainly didn't make for a good spot for them to announce that no one in A1E is ‘worthy' after they just lost a bloodbath.

It'll be interesting to see what comes next. I've seen some folks say that the “MBE Syndicate” was a one-night thing to get the characters re-introduced. If so, it was a shoddy way to do it. If, on the other hand, it's really an MBE vs. A1E angle, then it'll be a doozy to see how they get out of it and ‘back to normal'. That would require that some characters, especially on the MBE side, are ‘expendable' and not planning to continue.
