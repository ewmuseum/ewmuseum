Your Dose for the Week of November 28

2 December 2005 (record-derived page)

See! I've gone from “monthly” doses to bi-weekly. This can only mean good things. In this edition: some hype near and dear to my heart, a look at LoC, a couple of ways to constructively hype your favorite league, and a call for World Champions everywhere.

The Hype: CRUISE of DECEPTION

There are those (namely Steve) who think I should once again run down the litany of offenses – where major wrestling companies have stolen from the CSWA (among others). He believes the recent Dhavari ref angle is a mite too close to the old Manuel Juarez “NO ENGLISH” ref angle in the CSWA. He also thinks the “bleeping” of the “You Suck” chants for Angle is a ripoff of the CSWA's Thomas declaring Chad Merritt's name censored on all CSWA shows.

Myself, I believe that these recent coincidences are just that, coincidental. So instead, I'd rather take the time to turn the tables. With the CSWA's latest “hiatus” coming to an end, why not start hyping what has the potential to be one of the most drastic comedy angles in the CSWA since the “Disney Days” era where Sammy Benson got in a drunken fight with the animatronic bears in DisneyWorld's “Country Bear Jamboree”, and then got knocked off Splash Mountain by the Mighty Morphin Power Midgets.

Yes, you heard that correctly.

So where do you go when you've run all the angles you can think of? When you've recycled history until the Windham saga is all but unintelligible? Where do you find inspiration? From decades-old “Days of Our Lives” soap opera angles, of course.

Again, you heard me correctly.

And so, the CSWA's movie/television parodies go to another level. They used to be just for pre-PPV introductions, or for comedy blowoffs to ownership angles. But now, a whole series of cards dedicated to what may truly be one of the most shocking, heart-wrenching stories in the history of Daytime – the CRUISE of DECEPTION.

How can you ignore this tagline? “A network television premiere mini-series. A voyage of love and revenge, that you'll never forget. Journey into the unknown..." I mean, we're talking about a storyline that ends with a woman getting dropped into a vat of acid for goodness' sakes. (Days' Cruise Storyline)

And thus, a gimmick is born. Just to bring you up to speed, when we left the CSWA at the end of its last “season,” it had belatedly celebrated its ANNIVERSARY by crowning a new Unified Champion, none other than Troy Windham, who barely defeated Dan Ryan in the final match. Hornet beat Eli Flair in his (Eli's) CSWA send-off, only to be viciously attacked by a masked man revealed as Ruben Ross. Stephen Thomas had cemented his ownership of a financially-struggling CSWA, only to find out that there's a mysterious hacker bent on humiliating him in anyway possible.

So with the next major event set as FISH FUND, the CSWA returns to the high seas on its semi-annual cruise. Thomas is thrilled with the response from CSWA17 and brings the Parsons Cruise Liner II out of mothballs, packing his wrestlers, crew and a bunch of fans on a ship.

Ever heard the saying “He who forgets history is doomed to repeat it?” Well, Thomas isn't known for his long-term memory, except when it comes to his hatred for his co-founder Chad Merritt. The original “Cruise” ended with the ship going down, literally. While others since have been more tranquil, this one proves to be a throwback. Thomas may be in the Presidential Suite, but the Hacker is the one at the wheel.

So in fine Real World-question fashion, what exactly does happen when you take dozens of wrestlers and ringcrew, and hundreds of fans, and hold them hostage on a gigantic floating sideshow?

What happens when:

—Thomas gets locked in the Presidential Suite with his “wife” Hortense, who he only married to get just enough shares to retake control of the company?
—Hornet, Poison Ivy and Teri Melton are locked in a room together with no way out? —The wrestlers know they're held hostage, but still have to perform? —The fans begin to understand that a normal cruise doesn't keep them stuck aboard ship? —Cameron Cruise searches for his manager and friend, twelve-year-old “Make A Wish” cancer patient Billy Cundiff.
—JA forms a “Scooby Gang” of fans to find and take down the Hacker. —The Hacker's plans begin to emerge, with the help of his friends.

I make no promises about the vat of acid thing, but hey, who knows?

CSWA: CRUISE of DECEPTION

Under Review: LoC

So, as you'll see below, I made it just over halfway through Legacy of Champions' eleventh edition of Violence before breaking off. I thought I'd try going through a card segment by segment much like I would as if I were editing a CSWA card together. During that process, I'm usually looking for what doesn't make sense or what doesn't fit together, so you'll probably see, on the whole, what looks like more negative comments than positive. I'll recap at the bottom and give some overall thoughts.

I'm not totally convinced that kicking off your show with a homosexual handjob joke is the way to go, but hey, I got the point – Brandon Thomas is ticked off at losing the Legacy Title to Ninja K, and he's gonna make sure everybody knows it. In Bischoffesque style, even. Yawn.

Before we get to in-ring action, it's time to see somebody arrive at the arena sans partner, and then an interesting segment where Mystique Darkheart (isn't that the chick from X-Men?) introduces some sad pathetic submissive creature she has on a chain to the Williams family. How very kinky of her. Nice segment with just one technical annoyance – a “ravenous” woman would be one that's hungry. Perhaps the author was going for “rapturous” or simply “hot.”

Cordova vs. Phoenix Riktor started with the requisite tributes to Eddie Guerrero which are appreciated, and turned into a great lucha-style match. At least until the end. The cutesie “we're buddies” aftermath would've been fine, had the finish itself not been taken directly out of Guerrero's playbook and used at least twice by the WWE since his death. It's one thing for Chavo Guerrero to do it against JBL, or for Rey to pull the “Eddie tribute” against Big Show, but somehow here it just fell a little flat. I completely understand what they were going for, and appreciate it. A tribute is great – direct paint-by-numbers copy, not so much for me, although maybe completely different for others.

Riktor offers to let Ninja K even up the handicap match that Bisch… Thomas set up for him, followed by the continuation of the shortest segments in history as we find out that Corey Thompson is still sans partner and confused.

More segment goodness as we get the set up for Now's tag title shot later in the show. We find out that the wrestlers are smarter than their “agent” as they ruin the ‘surprise' he has in store for them – the title shot. Followed by the conclusion of Corey Thompson's continuing “where's my brother and tag partner?” saga which ends with the tag champs throwing out enough profanity that on TV their comment would have looked like: “This….is probably scared…to go up against…the.” Throw in a racial slur and you've quickly got the CHEAP HEAT~! Yay. Thompson uses all his pent-up rage to then destroy a jobber tag team.

We get plenty of insight into Ryan Billows, the former A&F-model cum boy-next-door-with-more-muscles, as he finishes a photo shoot to talk to Riktor, who recruits him to stand with Ninja K for the main event.

Derecho gives us a history lesson explaining why his dead wife is actually alive and at his side. If I've learned anything in this game, it's to keep dead wives dead, right Pete? Derecho quickly changes the subject to tell us about his Underground Title match with Sylo, who he completely ignores to talk about Tyke. Then he tells Tyke and all of us ‘retards' listening about why he is, indeed, the greatest wrestler in the history of the business. And apparently he doesn't like Brits. If only it had been Canadians he despised, this would've been a good heel promo. Oh, and if the wife had stayed dead, of course.

Good segment with Tyke's backstage response to Derecho, which focuses more on why Tyke may have lost his edge, and with it, his fan support. Plus, anybody who uses Brit lingo like “mucker” just has to be cool, right?

And then it's Savoy time. You truly do have to love any guy who can turn muff-diving into a gimmick and an entertaining, yet not too raunchy segment. Who am I kidding? Raunchy and funny.

The Equalizer shines up his new Relentless title and lets the Williams know that he's always thinking ahead, followed by a quick trip inside the mind of Turk, who pulls off the best segment of the night to the point.

The ubiquitous Laurie Williams shows up yet again, this time with Lance Hawkins, who quickly intimates that our dear Laurie may have a hygiene issue. With that out of the way, Laurie calls Hawkins injury on the carpet, booking him in an arm wrestling match. Where's Hacksaw when you need him!?

Hopper and Metz defeat Ehren the King and Creig in a match that continues to show how strong LoC's tag division is. Straightforward match with some well-written miscommunication at the end by the winning tag team that could be the beginning for something down the line. Well, after they get back from DisneyWorld I guess.

See, now I'm confused. Is Billows the all-American boy, or just a hick? Or both? Because when a segment starts out with “'Dagnabbit,' Billows hollered,” I begin to think that this whole Murdoch resurgence has gone too far. Despite the hick beginning, the segment stays strong, as we not only follow Billows triumphs and travails with his new cell phone, but get a quick one-sided conversation between Billows and Ninja K.

The Legion of Dairy, everyone's favorite lactose-based tag team, has a tet-a-tet with the tag champs, marking the second team of the night that Malik and Duzza have ticked off. And then it's time for an “Over the Top” moment via Lance Hawkins, his son, and his arm wrestling match. Great parody.

Halfway through, and my fingers are getting tired. Derecho and Ninja K have a run in with a good segment cementing Derecho as an over-the-top (not in an armwrestling way!) know-it-all-yet-clueless heel. Frost vs. Fanatic is used as a vehicle to expand Turk's return against Fanatic. Well-written in showing Turk's distress, his change, and his hatred.

So, keeping in mind that I haven't had a chance to read many cards recently, and so I'm not up on all the ongoing angles, you just saw my initial take segment by segment. Now some overall thoughts…

The match-to-segment ratio seemed off at first, but as I look at the credits, it's about 3 or 4-to-1 which isn't bad. One reason I think the Turk segment and interference came off so well is that is didn't try to be “cute,” like so many of the other segments, which seem to try and be smarky at themselves. Derecho and Ryan Billows seem to be almost exact opposites, which would make for an interesting angle – the clueless know-it-all and the babyface who knows more than he shows.

Speaking of Billows, I cheated and skimmed ahead – the phone's in the peanut butter? I think Ryan's been smoking peanut butter again while writing Savoy segments.

Anyway, this was entirely more work than I hoped for. Over time, I'll check back in on LoC angles. I'm open for suggestions, critiques on my critiques, or other shows that I need to check in on. Just e-mail me at [detail withheld].

How's that for getting out of finishing something? Huh? Impressive, I know.

Overdose

The new name for all that “circuit news” that you guys are supposed to be blowing my e-mail box up with. I (sniff) guess you just didn't believe I'd be back, did you?

How's this for timely, though? New Alberta Pro Wrestling is set to release its Black Thursday supercard today. And I must say, I miss the days when “supercard” was more used than PPV. The full news item is in that lovely “Take Two” section on the main page (just below where you clicked on this lovely item).

Some additional info on NAPW since they were gracious enough to send stuff in:

NAPW is on the look-out for talented performers to challenge the upper-card and main event. D!, The Beast, Stylin' Kyle, Static, Ravager, The Plague, J.C. Cook, Misery... fantastic roleplayers looking for more challengers!

NAPW particularly is seeking TAG TEAMS - face and heel.

Rookie e-wrestler? NAPW features Action!, a second show each week designed to give rookies a place to learn the ropes of e-wrestling, including an Action! specific title - the television title. The focus is, of course, on fun, and NAPW feels that rookies need a place to learn and hone their skills. NAPW Action! is the place to do that, and a ticket to Monday Night Fights is there for anybody who shows growth and skill.

NAPW is a roleplay fed based out of Edmonton, Alberta, Canada - regional but accepting wrestlers from anywhere in the world.
