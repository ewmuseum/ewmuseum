The site the CSWA bought in March 1998 when it left PRODIGY, which grew into a portal and forum host for dozens of federations and wrote the hobby's own beginner's guide: what fantasy wrestling is, where it came from, and how to write a match. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The domain's own FAQ names its founding moment plainly: a federation's move. In March 1998 the operators of a federation that had spent six years on the PRODIGY online service's own WRESTLING BB bought fantasywrestling.com and moved their operation there; the hub's own later features overview calls that arrival “the reason the site was originally created.” The federation carried its own paperwork with it, and the domain grew a forum system beside it under the same name its predecessor's own league register had already coined for its paperwork years earlier on PRODIGY. In 2002 the address itself changed, from fantasywrestling.com to fwrestling.com; the institution did not — one hub, continuously run, under two names in sequence.

By the mid-2000s the hub had built six instruments around that one address. Free hosting carried more than 900 live accounts by July 2006, two gigabytes of them, with no advertisements and no waiting list. A league directory gave each member federation a profile of its own — founding date, play method, card frequency, join status. A league event database logged federations' own cards with a community star rating attached to each. A per-league top-ten system let a federation post its own monthly rankings, which in turn seeded ballot nominations for the hub's own “FW Top 20,” an AP-style poll cast by the hub's columnists, contributors and member fedheads. And a community wrestler voting list gave the hub's own membership one cross-federation page to name its own year's standouts.

The hub explained itself in its own voice throughout — a standing FAQ walking a newcomer through how a federation got hosted, how it entered the directory, how a ranking got submitted, and what the “FW Top 20” actually counted. It ran a message board, FW Central, carried over from the PRODIGY era as one of its own rooms rather than a second address, and adopted a member federation's own in-fiction television network, ESEN, as a cross-promotional badge open to any affiliate willing to apply. Where the record shows the hub stepping outside its own hosting and ranking role at all, it is toward its own membership rather than away from it.

The portal's own instruments stopped between 2010 and 2012; a league directory entry from as late as January 2012 was still being filed after the news desk itself had gone quiet. The forums outlasted the portal by a decade, carrying genuine activity to 2022. The hub's own web server and forum database failed in 2026 with no apparent maintainer left to fix them, ending a run that had carried the same community, under two addresses and three generations of forum software, since the Prodigy era.

PRODIGY's Wrestling Bulletin Board (WRESTLING BB)

Predecessor to FWrestling.com (1998)
The federation that anchored the PRODIGY WRESTLING BB's own League Info register moved to fantasywrestling.com in March 1998, and the new domain's own FAQ names that arrival as the reason the site exists at all. The new hub grew a forum system it called 'FW Central' -- the identical name the PRODIGY board's own League Info register and weekly news edition had already coined for the same multi-federation paperwork role. One continuing community institution under a new address, not two unrelated hubs that happen to share a name.
