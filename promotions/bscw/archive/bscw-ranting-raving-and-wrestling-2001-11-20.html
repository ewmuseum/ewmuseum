Ranting, Raving & Wrestling

20 November 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hey there, wrestling fans, I'm Damon Gleave and good old "Gleaver
2000" is back for another edition of "Ranting, Raving and Wrestling."
Today we take a look at BSCW's latest supercard, "Ascension," as well as give you the low down of all the shiznit you'll need to know. Sound good? Let's go.
Ascension took place in Poughkeepsie, NY, in front of a hot crowd that really helped give the show a tremendous atmosphere. I really enjoyed the show overall, and think it's just another example of the consistently excellent product BSCW give its fans. Let's take a look at what happened, shall we?
Match #1 was newcomer Damian Payne versus the big, bad veteran Blacktop. These guys' styles went well together, as they're both big, strong guys who can move. Blacktop is probably a little bigger and stronger, Payne a little younger and quicker. Blacktop, however, showed some youthfulness in this match, hitting a pescado to the outside, as well as a sunset flip powerbomb sending Payne to the floor. He continued to work on Payne's back throughout the match.
After getting a little help from the Rebel, who came through the crowd, Blacktop hit the Career Ender for the win. After the match, Blacktop was going to hit Payne with a flaming chair until Max C made the save, kicking some serious ass. This was a fairly solid opener and the guys worked hard.
The post-match angle helped establish the Payne/Max C team, and gets
Max over even more as a face. We'll see if Payne's push continues after a loss or not, and where Blacktop goes from here.
Next up was a big difference from the first match, as Jaji and Reaper tore the house down in a battle of speed, skill, and high-risk moves.
The pace of this match was off the charts, with Jaji especially delivering scintillating moves with a regular basis, including a
Sasuke Special and reversing a top rope move into the Jaji Stinger.
After a long, hard fought battle, Jaji hit the Series for the win. He wasn't finished, however, as he hit a double underhook piledriver on
Reaper off the top rope, called Jaji's Death Drop. Reaper did a stretcher job, and put over Jaji well as a little killer. We'll see where this goes from here. Once again, the guys worked very hard, the usual in BSCW, and I loved this match.
Todd Royal took on Blake Storm next. Todd Royal looks to be the real deal, but he honestly didn't have anything to work with here. Blake
Storm doesn't look any better in this incarnation than he did as J.C
Swinger. Sure, he's big, strong, and has "the look" and a big entourage, but his skills in the ring are pretty bland. Still, Royal was able to carry him to a decent match. Royal gets the win with his
"Todd Damn" dragon suplex and hopefully moves on to bigger and better things.
Richard Harris and Whiskey Jakk blew off their feud... or will they meet down the road? We'll see. Anyways, this match was a big ugly brawl between two big men. They fought backstage, where I laughed as
Whiskey Jakk pulled a "fan" in front of a chair shot. Whiskey and
Harris then tossed the fan back and forth before Whiskey said "the hell with it" and slammed him on the concrete. After delivering a gutwrench powerbomb to Jakk right on the floor, "The Truck" was jumped by Matt O'Riordan and Debonair, and beaten down. Whiskey Jakk made his way back to the ring, where he asked the ref to count out Harris. The ref refuses. Whiskey decks him. The VailTron goes on... and shows
O'Riordan and
Debonair lying at the feet of Harris! The crowd goes nuts as Harris storms back to the ring for another confrontation. Sure, it's overbooking, but at least the crowd is in to it. Harris kicks ass, and gets many near falls. They brawl outside the ring and dismantle the entire damn Ascension ramp way structure before Whiskey Jakk hits the
Bamboozled out of nowhere to get the win. Great match and great effort by the two big men.
Hold on, we're not done... Hannibal Carver, who did a run-in in the previous match, runs in to challenge Whiskey Jakk. What follows is a huge battle between two hardcore bastards. Tables, chairs, kendo sticks, they're all used. Carver then gets a branding iron... and uses it on Fantasy! Good God. Whiskey Jakk goes nuts and kills Carver with the Branding Iron, Bamboozled, and a Jakked Up powerbomb to win, then carries Fantasy backstage. This was my favorite match of the night.
Wait, did I say favorite? That's a hard selection, because Dan Owens and Shrapnel had a classic in the semifinals of the Cruiserweight title tournament. This was much more of a technical battle, as Owens dictated the pace early. Shrapnel proved he could wrestle, though, and really carried this match. It was a great, fast-paced technically sound match that the crowd was in to. Shrapnel won with the Shrapnel
Spike, and moves to the finals.
Tyson Bryson took on Joker next, and after jumping the "Master Clown" from behind, these two had a battle of good solid wrestling and lots of cheating. There was a funny part early as Matt O'Riordan (in a full body cast pretending to be Bryson) and Bryson himself did the old
"Doinks in the mirror" thing from Wrestlemania 9. Ha. Bryson gets the surprise win by rolling through for the pin on a moonsault by Joker.
Shrapnel versus Bryson... very interesting. The main event had "Deadly" Derek Irvin versus Justin Keith in a ladder match for the world title, and Shane Delaney's honorary title belt. At the beginning of this feud, we all knew Justin Keith was used as veteran face to get the young heel Irvin over. Recently, Keith also told management he'd like some time off, making the end to this match academic. The two men still delivered a great effort, although this wasn't a big spotfest ladder match like some fans have come to expect.
This was a more old school ladder match with solid bumps, great psychology and a hot crowd. A few run-ins hurt what could have been a clean match, but I understand why they did it, to set up the
Irvin/O'Riordan vs. Max/Payne match next show. Irvin destroyed Keith's left knee in this match, and ended up winning after hitting the One and Only off the ladder, the DDI, and a Pedigree on the ladder. He then climbed and got his belt.
Next card is "The Process of Weeding Out" and it looks solid. President Vail has decided to add Taurus and Richard Harris to the main event as well, which can slowly start the DDI/Taurus feud we all hope to see. The card has many interesting things about it. Just consider that Damian Payne and Richard Harris also have a match to see if they get a Hardcore title shot later in the night, and it's possible one of those men wrestles three matches. Also, DDI and
Whiskey Jakk defend the tag titles earlier in the night, but don't wrestle together for the main event. Whiskey Jakk also wrestles in the
N.A title rumble, as well as Taurus. Confused? You won't be, just watch the show.
Now it's time for news and notes! "Deadly" Derek Irvin signed a contract with RAW, but will still be fully competitive in BSCW. DDI and Debonair are both in MOTR VI, with the best indy talents in the world. Good luck to them.
Rumor has it that the tag team Kill Army may be coming to BSCW... what a tremendous addition that would be. If you want to check out Kill Army, check out MOTR, where they will be in the tag team title match along with Debonair and several other teams.
Who is going to get the main event push to feud with DDI? It looks like BSCW is still deciding on that. They really have three viable options:
Max C: The big man is in line for a big push, so why not now? BSCW officials believe he and DDI could have some terrific matches, as
DDI's bumping ability would go well with Max C's size and strength.
Taurus: He has a natural issue with DDI after the "Deadly One" ruined the Grinder. This is a feud that has been building for quite some time, and when it goes down it could be one of the best and most brutal feuds in BSCW history.
Whiskey Jakk: This big man has worked his ass off of late, competing in two brutal matches at BOTH recent super cards. His ability to get good matches from anybody despite his size is amazing, and his work ethic has never been questioned. And, if you don't sense tension between him and DDI, then something is wrong with you. DDI blamed
Whiskey for Fantasy's branding, but still wanted him to stay and help him in the main event. Whiskey instead went to the hospital with
Fantasy. There are so many things for them to fight over; the world title, Fantasy, leadership in Greed. And with them being tag team champions, we could see dissention quicker than we think.
Sykopath should be back into action at P.O.W.O. I don't really know why they filmed him getting hit by a car unless it was to say that it would take being hit by a car to keep this guy from wrestling at a show.
Shrapnel versus Tyson Bryson should be an interesting match for the
Cruiserweight title, but all signs point to the former gaining the belt.
We hear that it's possible that Dan Owens, Blake Storm and the Mob
Squad could all already be in the dog house in BSCW. We don't really know why, just stay tuned.
Could Grinnin be coming back? Who WOULDN'T like to be able to say
"Gobsmack" again? Reaper is done with BSCW. Marty Howe is still a question mark. That's all for now, catch you later! ------------------------------------------- Rich E-Fed Head / Master Of All That I Survey BSCW - http://efeduniverse.com/bscw
