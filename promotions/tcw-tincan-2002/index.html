Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Tin Can Wrestling opened at a New Year's fair in Reading, Pennsylvania, on 6 January 2002, drawing a crowd of roughly two hundred and seventy between a balloon-animal stall and a hot dog sale. Its own promoter doubled as ring announcer, wheeler-dealer and target of the show's own running jokes.

Touring on a shoestring, it played a charity show in St. Hilliard, Ohio for the Lions Club, opened for a death-metal band's gig in Kansas City, and closed out its run staged at the premiere of a fictional Hollywood film, folding real celebrities into its own comedic angles alongside its invented roster.

Its own house style leaned hard into parody: a bear-suited wrestler, a self-styled poet who wrote his own reviews, a punk veteran feuding with a rookie half his age, and a heavy-metal preacher called Sabbath squaring off against a masked hunter.

Across its four shows, the promotion's own poet-wrestler pulled off the promotion's biggest upset, pinning a drunken veteran clean; a triple-threat main event ended by disqualification rather than a fall; and its most punishing hardcore brawl put a bear-suited wrestler through a table.

Its own handbook framed the whole guiding philosophy in one line from its promoter: he liked wrestling plain and simple, and he liked money.
