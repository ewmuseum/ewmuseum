TRAUMA:fighting staged mixed martial arts rather than wrestling — a steel cage, no championships at all, and eleven fighters carded exactly once, on a house show in April 2003. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

TRAUMA:fighting was a fight promotion, not a wrestling one. Its own Concept page set it against UFC and PRIDE, insisted that “professional fighting isn't just entertainment, it's a business,” and promised four channels of it on Direct TV to “over 2 million homes in North America.” What it actually staged was a cage — a circular steel mesh shell inside a square wrought-iron frame, canvas on the floor, a door welded into each side — and eleven fighters with no championship between them.

It told its own beginning as a serial. In two Chapters posted before it ran anything, its owner Adriano Kaitos buys the cage over the telephone for twenty-six thousand, rents a truck for five hundred and fifty, hauls the thing across New Jersey himself, has it lifted into a warehouse floored with golden sand and lined with high-school benches, and stands in an alleyway afterwards smoking, on “a day that he would remember for the rest of his life.” The Concept page named four men behind the promotion and said two of their names had been classified; the two it did name were Mr. Kaitos and co-owner Sean Williams.

Its roster cards read like fight records rather than wrestling bios. DaShea Strong's lists fifteen judo throws under their Japanese names; Wednesday's lists twenty strikes, kicks and chokes; Nick Davis boxes and kickboxes; Joe Wise mixes aikido with street fighting; Phoenix Rose arrives from Manchester by way of Tampa Bay, billed as returning from a death staged somewhere else. Criminal Intent's bio is written with his own name struck out of every sentence that would have used it — “*censored by police*” — because the fiction has him taken out of a life sentence to fight. The application form the promotion posted asked for a character's life “from birth to present day” and for ten to twenty-five moves.

It staged one card. House Show 1's results went up on Saturday 19 April 2003, four fights long: DaShea Strong beat Criminal Intent, Wednesday beat Joe Wise, and Colin Gear beat Phoenix Rose down badly in the main event. Of “Hysteria” Tony Anderson against Carson Nash the promotion recorded two words and no winner — “Both men fired.” Its own pay-per-view area, a month earlier, had read “March: Soon.”

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. Its board carried thirty-one posts and eleven registered members through March; its roleplay index listed nine numbered promos by 20 April; a roster card captured on 22 May points at two more, numbered 23 and 25, which is the last sign of anyone still writing for it. Nothing dated after May 2003 survives. Through all of it the site sat on ewmania.com's own hosting and carried Primetime Central's button on every page, in the alliance's own “PTC Cable Federations” tier.
