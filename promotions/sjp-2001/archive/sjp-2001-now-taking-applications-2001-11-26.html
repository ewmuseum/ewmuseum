Sekai Joshi Puroresu: Now Taking Applications

26 November 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
SJP is a fantasy wrestling organization dedicated to simulating the wonderful world of Joshi Puroresu (Japanese women's pro wrestling). What does that mean for SJP? Well, it means that we're looking for handlers interested in roleplaying female characters that are a little on the serious side. Nothing wrong with a comedian (what would GAEA be without Sakura
Hirota, after all?), but we'd like to avoid characters interested primarily in conversing about the size of their chest.
So how does this all work? Good question. Shows will take place once per week. Most shows will be written in an abridged, "report" style, with the exception of the month-ending television taping. Television tapings are where storylines are more likely to be built upon. Most television tapings will be held in medium sized venues. However, once every 3-4 months there will be a taping in a larger venue. These "supercards" will signal the end of a tour, and will feature a large number of matches, most of which will be blow-offs for various feuds, some of which will be gimmick matches such as cage matches, 2/3 falls, etc. Titles are generally only defended at television tapings. Currently, SJP sanctions the following titles:
a.. The SJP Kokusai Seshuken (International title belt) - Generally defended once per tour.
b.. The SJP Shin Nihon Seshuken - Generally defended once per month at a television taping.
c.. The SJP Kokusai Tag Seshuken (International Tag title belts) - Defense schedule may vary.
THE SJP FAQ Q. Is there an in-character staff? A. Yes, there is. Glad you asked: a.. Executive Booker - Ayane Hikiji. 37 years old, forced to retire from wrestling at the age of 35 due to severe knee injuries. She started her training at the age of 15, and made her professional debut two years later.
Originally thought to be too small for wrestling at only 5'2" and 110 pounds, she became known for her high-risk offense and willingness to brawl with women much larger than her. Because of her style, she became insantly recognizable, but was also plaugued with injuries for most of her career. By the time she was 28, she had already had surgery done on both of her knees, her right elbow, and her right shoulder. At the age of 32, she underwent surgery to remove bone spurs from her neck. After her last knee surgery at the age of 35, the promotion she worked for forced her to retire, giving her a fairly large sendoff, but making it clear that they did not want her to wrestle in their rings again, for the sake of her health. Ayane took their advice to heart, and entered civilian life, using the money she'd earned during her career to purchase a gym/training center in Osaka, where she worked as a trainer in between vacations until SJP scouted her as an executive. Ayane Hikiji is still the same strong-willed woman she was in her youth, but age has made her slower to anger, and a fairly good leader.
b.. Head Referee - Emiko Fukami. 30 years old. Emiko has worked as a referee in various independent promotions for the past 10 years, and has regulated pretty much every type of match in existence. She calls her matches with the cold, calculated (and fair) eye of someone who has seen it all done before.
c.. Second Referee - Kenshiro Sasaki. 25 years old. While not entirely inexperienced, he is generally relegated to regulating the undercard matches. He previously worked for a popular, now-defunct garbage wrestling promotion, and tends to be a little more lenient than his boss. Not much, though.
Q. How are wins and losses determined? A. Every week before the show is to be written, each handler will be responsible for sending in an interview (the shows are to be posted on
Sunday, and the deadline for interviews is Thursday)
Q. What's with the stats? Are you using a sim? I thought this was a booked promotion..... A. The stats are indeed used for a sim program, however, SJP is a booked promotion. The simulator is merely a match writing tool. Rest assured that your wins and losses are ultimately determined by how well you write interviews. Basically, I use a "points" system for your push. For each week that you participate by sending in a roleplay, you receive 2 points. For each win (tag wins included), you will receive another 2 points. Since wins are dependent on the quality of your roleplay, these points are basically a reward for good roleplaying. If you are unable to participate that week and give me fair notice, you will gain 1 points for participation that week, plus any applicable victory points. If a character does not participate and does not give a fair warning (i.e. just up and disappears), they will lose one point for that week. Please keep in mind that having more "push" points does not necessarily mean you will win every match. It is quite possible for a person with fewer points to win. I just wanted to create a rewards system for people who put in the time to participate.
Q. Where can I learn more about Joshi Puroresu? A. Check our links section. Q. How often can I/must I submit roleplays? A. Once per week. If you *must*, you may submit a 2-part roleplay, but quantity will not garner you a win. Q. Are run-ins allowed? How do they work? A. As a general rule, run-ins are not allowed. There may be occasions when outside cheating is allowed by heel factions, but this will be very rare indeed. Q. Can I use other wrestlers in my roleplays? A. Not without their permission you can't. Q. How do on-card interviews work? A. It is not recommended that you write your roleplays as on-card interviews. However, in cases of story progression, you may send in an on-card interview to take place after the match (it is fairly common in
Joshi Puroresu for the wrestlers to grab a mic after a match and congratulate/badmouth each other).
Q. What are the limits on non-Japanese (Gaikokujin) characters? A. This is a little tricky. SJP is obviously supposed to be based in Japan, and Japanese characters are greatly appreciated, but I dislike placing limits on foreign characters for two main reasons:
1.. I feel that people write their best roleplays when using a character they are most comfortable with. By forcing people to use Japanese characters, one risks having them "fake it" and turn in a product well below their abilities.
2.. I feel that by placing a specific limit on non-Japanese characters, I would be reinforcing the idea of Japanese xenophobia, which, while definitely present in the culture (as well as U.S. culture and pretty much any country on earth), is not something I feel like propagating.
In any case, I am not going to specifically limit non-Japanese characters. I would suggest you give one a try, unless you have your heart set on something else. I would also like to say that I would love to see some characters from really out of the way places, hearkening back to the days when wrestling promotions really had a "world wide" feel. If you don't want to be from Japan, instead of just going for an American, try a character from Mexico, Brazil, Australia, South Africa, Egypt, Greece, Samoa, Korea,
Russia, France, Finland, India, or any one of a dozen other countries on earth. While I want SJP to reflect the traditional joshi puroresu style, I really would love to see some creativity from our members.
Q. What are the limits regarding what is acceptable in roleplays? A. Well, the usual limits on excessive profanity, sexual content, racism, and sexism are in place. But these are mainly just general guidelines. If, for example, you wanted to play a Korean character who felt unhappy with the way Koreans have been historically treated in Japan and wanted to mention this in your roleplays, that would be okay to a certain extent. If it is felt that a roleplay has gone too far, the handler will be contacted and asked to be more careful in the future. Only if the person continues to write patently offensive material will diciplinary action (e.g. being asked to leave) be taken.
