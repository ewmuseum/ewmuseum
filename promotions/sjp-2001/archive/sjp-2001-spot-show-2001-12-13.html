Sekai Joshi Puroresu: Spot Show, Yokosuka Athletics Meeting Hall

13 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Sekai Joshi Puroresu ran a spot show on December 13, 2001 to stir up interest in their official launch in January 2002 at the Yokosuka
Athletics Meeting Hall. Prior to the show, they had limited edition t-shirts and other merchandise available, as well as opportunities for fans to meet the women and get autographs. After a short in-ring introduction of each of the women, the show began in earnest.
Archana Krishna vs. Nanako Yamazaki Blazingly fast workrate joshi style match for the opener. It's never easy to be in the opening match, but these two not only warmed up the crowd, they garnered more than enough heat for themselves. The match settled down to a nice pace after Krishna took control with a powerbomb that almost took the win. Yamazaki did her best to retake control with a flurry of elbows and a sharply angled dragon suplex, but to no avail. Krishna escaped a second dragon suplex attempt and countered with Masala for the pin.
Krishna d. Yamazaki (10:38, Masala) Leanna Love vs. Ukyo Watanabe (FREE) This match started out very slowly, with both women taking the time to feel out the other's style. This didn't last very long, however, as they were in the middle of a full-fledged brawl, Watanabe's specialty.
Love was hardly a pushover, however, and had a slight height advantage, and she made good use of her leverage to wear down Watanabe with a series of throws, culminating in a near-fall following a
Northern Lights Suplex. The finish came after Ukyo Watanabe sent
Leanna Love into the ropes and Love came back with a Rolling Savate
Kick, followed immediately by the Love Tap for the pin.
Love d. Wataname (12:44, Love Tap) SYLVIE vs. Matsui Type-S (FREE) Matsui Type-S is a masked shooter trained in the U.S. and Canada, and provided a good counterpoint to SLYVIE's varied style of striking and submissions. After an initial exchange of strikes that allowed SYLVIE to make judicious use of her reach advantage, the match settled into a mat-based affair after Matsui successfully managed a double-leg takedown, settling into SYLVIE's guard. When SYLVIE once again took control of the match, it returned to the stand-up position, allowing her to take advantage of an opening to trap Matsui in the Rolodex
Propaganda for the submission win.
SYLVIE d. Matsui (14:02, Rolodex Propaganda) Hitomi Yasuda/Yumi Naoka (FREE) vs SPARROW Yakota/Jun Motokawa (FREE) Naoka and Motokawa started the match off quickly, with Motokawa diving outside onto Naoka early on and brawling outside, but the match soon came back to the ring, where the two continued with high-impact offense. Yasuda was tagged in around the five-minute mark, and went to work on grounding Motokawa. After a near-submission to a rolling hiza-gatame, Yakota tagged in and the match remained between Yasuda and Yakota until the end. The two traded offense for quite some time, with neither establishing a clear advantage. As time began to run out, they went through a lightning-fast series of near falls, but to no avail. The match ended as Yakota hit Yasuda with a stiff thrust kick, which Yasuda took head-on and countered into a reverse ankle lock shortly before Motokawa dropkicked her in the head to break the hold, seconds before time ran out.
Time limit Draw (15:00) Post-match, both women demanded extra time for the match, but referee
Emiko Fukami refused, saying that there was simply no time left. The show ended with the rest of the roster filling the ring and bowing to the appreciative crowd.
In a press conference held after the show, matchmaker Ayane Hikiji promised a rematch between Hitomi Yasuda and SPARROW Yakota sometime after the promotion's official launch in January 2002.
