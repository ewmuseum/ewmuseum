Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Ultimate Pro Wrestling opened for business on 20 September 2016, billing itself from Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania as “the first of many broadcasts” of a bi-weekly studio show, Showdown, built toward quarterly pay-per-views. It did not start its championship history from zero: its own title ledgers carry an older lineage forward from a predecessor league, named in this corpus only by the initials “TTW,” which had “closed down” at the end of November 2015. UPW picked the belts back up rather than retire them.

Over its own seven months of tape, UPW ran two one-night tournaments to fill vacant titles, crowned a new World Heavyweight Champion in a four-way match, and staged its first pay-per-view, Survival, in Chicago. Its own Television Championship changed hands twice in the space of five weeks. A developmental territory, the IXWA, was billed on the front page as “the breeding ground for future UPW Superstars.”

The record ends mid-stride. UPW's own Schedule page previewed a card for 25 April 2017 at the Toyota Center in Houston — dark matches listed, a title defense on the marquee, a promise to name the next Hall of Fame inductee. The league's own site now states, on every one of its title displays, that it shut down 23 April 2017. The Houston card was never held.

Storylines

The arcs below are recovered from a full read of all nine held broadcasts (eight Showdown episodes and the Survival preview show).

Rick Styles's descent, from a stolen title shot to an arson attack Written by AI, 2026

"Top Dog" Rick Styles spends UPW's own debut broadcast simply declaring himself above its World Heavyweight Title tournament, then escalates week over week: ambushing John Herdick before a bell ever rings and having valet Ralph urinate on him afterward, an angle the broadcast itself later just calls “Peegate.” A hand-picked special referee, Herdick himself, tries to turn the tables during Styles's own number-one contender's match against Derek Rage, foiled only by a Jeff Keenan run-in. The arc's own last chapter, on UPW's own final broadcast, is its ugliest: Styles ambushes Aaron Lewis before a scheduled match against Malcom Shabazz, snaps Lewis's own leg against a guardrail, douses him in gasoline, and sets him on fire — security extinguishes it before Thomas Grissom, sucker-punched trying to intervene, can do anything else. UPW's own closure twelve days later means the promotion never answers for it on screen.

Malcom Shabazz's “Hard Truth” and a threat against a family Written by AI, 2026

Malcom Shabazz builds his entire UPW run around a claim that the promotion and its own fans treat him unfairly, backed by a stable of masked “Hard Truth Soldiers.” The feud that follows, against Liam Donegal, turns violent fast — a masked assault that opens manager Adam Lazarus's own head on an exposed turnbuckle, then a televised threat against Donegal's own wife and infant son, shown silently on the arena screen. UPW books an “I Quit” match to settle it at Survival, but Shabazz never gets there: arrested backstage for a brawl that hospitalizes Thomas Grissom, he forfeits his own North American Championship tournament match to Harrison Daniels III instead. He's still billed against Rick Styles on the card the league's own closure meant was never held.

The Dominion's rise, and the two title changes it bought Written by AI, 2026

"Legionarius" Antonio De Luca founds the Dominion on UPW's own second broadcast, running in with Lance Donovan and Tyson Bishop to cost Liam Donegal a match. The stable spends the rest of the promotion's life running interference for itself: a season-long war against the O'Malley Brothers that scraps their own in-ring debut three separate times, and outright theft on two title changes — De Luca's own North American Championship win over Glenn Chambers, and Windham Brody's own Television Championship win over Brandon Franklin on UPW's own final broadcast, De Luca coaching Brody through it from ringside. The same night, the Dominion ambushes Ricky Courage on the entrance ramp moments after he signs an autograph for a disabled fan — the broadcast's own closing image of what the stable has become.

Glenn Chambers and Dylan Cardinal, a feud that starts with a hospitalized teenager Written by AI, 2026

"The Basilisk" Dylan Cardinal lures Glenn Chambers's own amateur-wrestling protégé, an eighteen-year-old college student billed only as Derrick "DJ" Jackson, into an “unsanctioned exhibition” and beats him badly enough to hospitalize him, faking a bandaged arm for the whole match to sell the trap. Chambers arrives too late for the save, and the fallout drives him for months: fan well-wishes for Jackson air on two later broadcasts, Chambers powerbombs Cardinal through a merchandise table (“that was for DJ”) after a chair-brawl through the arena, and Cardinal blasts Chambers with a red mist to cost him UPW's own first North American Championship. The two never settle it on screen before the promotion closes.

Soldiers of the Sun survive a defection from their own Titan Juarez Written by AI, 2026

Titan Juarez abandons his own stablemates, Ricky Courage and STRIKER Kato, mid-match against the Warriors, aligning with the champions before turning on them too by chokeslamming both Dominion members during a title defense he runs into uninvited. He rejoins Soldiers of the Sun for good by UPW's own next broadcast, and the trio wins the World Tag Team Championship at Survival in a Fatal Four-Way. Courage's own arc threads through it quietly — a rookie wrestling, by his own account, to know the wrestler father he never met, devastated by Juarez's defection and vindicated by the reunion that follows it.

Tripp Skylark's dark turn, and the mystery man behind it Written by AI, 2026

Tripp Skylark loses to Billy "The Regulator" James on the Survival preview show and snaps on the spot — attacking the referee, sprint-tackling James on the ramp, and beating him bloody with a steel chair before security intervenes. UPW's own final broadcast confirms it wasn't a one-off: Skylark debuts a fully dark persona against Harrison Daniels III, watched approvingly from the aisle by an unnamed hooded man flanked by two unnamed seven-foot bodyguards. The promotion closes before naming either of them.

The World Title chase: Chase Williams, Aaron Lewis, and Glenn Chambers Written by AI, 2026

"Conceited Bastard" Chase Williams spends UPW's own opening months circling Aaron Lewis, attacking him after matches and squashing an overmatched Marco Matters as a message, before losing to him in the Fatal Four-Way that crowns UPW's own first post-relaunch champion. The belt changes hands to Derek Rage at Survival, and by the promotion's own final broadcast the chase has a third man in it: Glenn Chambers wins the Ultimate Rumble, beats Williams in the main event, and closes the broadcast in a silent staredown with Rage, confirming a Slamfest VI title match UPW's own closure meant was never shown.

Loose ends at closure Written by AI, 2026

UPW's own last two weeks open more than they close. "El Patron" Felix Sandoval spends a whole broadcast auditioning tag-team partners, rejected in turn by Windham Brody, Damian Payne and Frank Anthony, and never finds one. "Playboy" Ronnie D is announced as the first of five inductees into a 2017 Hall of Fame class due to be honored at Slamfest VI; the induction itself is never held. And Harrison Daniels III's own friendship with Percy Monroe and Patricia Monroe curdles into something UPW's own record leaves permanently unresolved, the couple's own screwjob help against Chad Allen shoved away by Daniels the same night it wins him the match.

Affiliations

IXWA

Academy — feeds into UPW (2016)
UPW’s own front page bills the IXWA plainly as “the breeding ground for future UPW Superstars,” and the Survival preview show separately frames it the same way while naming three IXWA roster members — Archibald Smythe, Ryan Keys, and “Showtime” Shawn Watson — drawn into the Ultimate Rumble as a UPW opportunity.
