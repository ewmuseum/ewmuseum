A parody promotion staged as if perpetually set in 1985, restaging period-appropriate gimmicks and rules under an in-fiction president persona. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The American Wrestling Federation staged itself as a promotion permanently set in 1985, run on a sliding calendar keyed to its own real posting date: its relaunch FAQ (18 June 2004) explains, “If I'm writing this FAQ on March 27, 2004, than the date in AWF is March 27, 1985. When 2005 rolls around, it'll be 1986 for the AWF.” Its earliest surviving broadcast, Saturday Morning All-Stars, carries the in-fiction date of 24 April 1985 from the Baltimore Arena and opens with the first match in its own history: Justin Straight defeating “The Wildcat” Jim Lewis, called by Dave Kelso and former wrestler “Diamond” Jimmy Cage.

A June 1985 relaunch, presented by its president, James D. Quayle, restaged the promotion under new ownership out of Dallas: “Iron” Mike Cady quit outright on learning of the new owners' criminal past, “Big Time” Todd Johnston was released as “not charismatic enough,” and a new stable, The Cold Warriors, was installed under a manager billed as the Texatollah, alongside a fresh local signing, “El Bronco Cabrone” Manolo Escobar.

Its rules held the whole roster to a strict period standard: finishers limited to moves considered legitimately dangerous in 1985, from the piledriver to an “illegal” Heart Punch; theme music restricted to songs already released by the promotion's own in-fiction date; and gimmicks drawn from stock types of the era, “Big Nasty Russians” and “Grizzled Veterans” among them.

Beyond that June 1985 relaunch update, nothing further survives; whether the promotion ran the card it previewed for 27 June isn't in its surviving record.
