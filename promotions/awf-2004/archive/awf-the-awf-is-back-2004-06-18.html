AWF — Rules

The AWF Is Back — And It's Still 1985-riffic!!!

Jun 18, 2004, 12:00:00 AM
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
-=-=-=-=-=- I. What is the AWF? The American Wrestling Federation (AWF) is the resumption of Teddy Booze's insanely cool idea that shouldn't be allowed to die, brought to you by James D. Quayle, who handled Danny Beale in the original incarnation.
The retro style is going to sweep e-wrestling like a giant push-broom of goodness, and we are at the forefront.
-=-=-=-=-=- I-A. What makes it so different? You see, here's the catch. The AWF takes place in 1985. That's 19 years ago. As such, the entire fed, its angles, its wrestlers, and its attitude in general will attempt to emulate that golden age of innocence. It's simple: If I'm writing this
FAQ on March 27, 2004,
than the date in AWF is March 27, 1985. When 2005 rolls around, it'll be 1986 for the AWF.
The AWF will be portrayed as a mostly regional organization, whose weekly syndicated TV show airs on Saturday mornings. All of the angles, and wrestlers will be expected to stay in-line with the time period.
-=-=-=-=-=- I-B. Who's booking this crap? I, Jim Quayle, will be doing the booking. I'm fair, but honest. I don't do political pushing, which shouldn't be an issue for a completely new fed that should have entirely original characters (unless you were in one of the proto-feds of the early 80s back on CompuServe or Prodigy or some crap like that.)
-=-=-=-=-=- I-B-1. Haven't I heard of you freaks before? No. I am NOT the former Vice-President of the United States. For one thing, I can spell. :) Actually, I've been around e-w for quite a while, and of course, handled here originally. But, to be honest, my previous reputation (unless you already know my work), shouldn't precede me . . . Not that it wasn't good, I just have a very deserved reputation as a troll and egotistical
prick. :) -=-=-=-=-=- II. Events Events for the AWF will mostly take place in the Southwest, since the new owners have moved the organization to Dallas to be closer to their oil interests, but there will also be west coast and midwest tours. (they will always have names like "Midwest Madness" and "California Dreamin Tour
85!") It is, however,
a national organization with a decent enough budget to have celebrities for occasional megaevents.
-=-=-=-=-=- II-A. Weekly Shows Right now, the weekly show is going to be AWF All-Star Wrestling. It will be a Saturday morning show. As you may be aware, the golden age of wrestling occured when cartoon addled youngsters would get up early to watch the exploits of the evil
Brother Love and the Powers of Pain. It was a beautiful time, and as such , our weekly show will be a Saturday Morning show. There will be prelim matches (see below) as well as
angle-furthering matches. -=-=-=-=-=- II-B. Megacards In 1985, Pay-Per-View was not a viable option for pro-wrestling. Even the WWF hosted its super-event "Wrestlemania" on closed-circuit television. Fortunately, the AWF has come to an agreement with Home Cable Entertainment to broadcast our quarterly
Megacards on its cable network. Maybe one day, every home in America will see our talent through the magic of cable television!
-=-=-=-=-=- III. Rules and Regulations (aww crap) Yeah, there are rules to abide by. Most of them involve roleplay and keeping with the timeline of the organization.
-=-=-=-=-=- III-A. Applications Okay, first off, any application that does not meet our criteria will not be accepted. We will tell you why, and give you the opportunity to fix whatever's wrong. When selecting a finisher, please do not pick something like "Double Powerbomb into a crescent kick" or anything like that. Back in 1985, moves like the DDT, the Piledriver, and the
Heart Punch (yes, it will
be an "illegal" move in this fed, but that's cool if you're a heel)
were finishers. Not only that, but deadly and feared finishers. Moves that Brock Lesnar could shrug off today would have knocked someone unconscious in 1985. Keep that in mind.
-=-=-=-=-=- III-B. Theme Music Theme music is very important for wrestlers. I realize that back then, most wrestlers did not come out to commercial music. However, we don't exactly have the option of creating our own themes for this, now do we? :) So, basically, the idea is
this: When you pick a theme for your wrestler, make sure it was at least a song that would have been out by the date that the fed is on. If you need help, look up some Billboard Top 100 hits for 1985 or earlier.
(Don't worry, I'm not going to look it up to make sure it really didn't drop in '86 or something, just don't do anything obviously wrong like picking something by an artist who was in kindergarten in '85.)
-=-=-=-=-=- III-C. Wrestling Styles/Acceptable Gimmicks Okay, let's face it. Back then, there were no super-highflying luchas. "Superfly" Jimmy Snuka and "Macho Man" Randy Savage were considered ariel artists. The former did only a big splash, the latter used axe-handles and an elbowdrop. Do you see what I'm getting at? Also, no hardcore stuff. Nothing with barbed wire. Back then, a chairshot to the back could knock you unconscious.
And a ring bell could break your neck. You can be hardcore, but keep in mind that 1985's definition of "hardcore" or "extreme" was "The Ultimate Warrior." And yes, I realize that
Kevin Sullivan and Abdullah the Butcher used to slice people open with can openers and stuff, so there will be the very rare occasion that I will allow a monster heel into the organization with such intent. Of course, there are some gimmicks from back in the
80s that will always be
welcome here. They include, but are not limited to: Big Nasty
Russians, All-American Heroes, Grizzled Veterans, Face-Painted Steroid Freaks, Mouthy/Wily Male
Managers, Any gimmick where
the wrestler had a day job (ie, Big Bossman was a corrupt cop, Ted
DiBiase was a rich man),
Female Valets, huge mean guys that like picking on fans. And so on, the possibilities are endless, really. Just keep in mind the timeframe we're working in, and realize that no
"nekkid big tittied chix" will be accepted. (Well, he says this too well to cut up. However, I'm a bit more open stylistically, and that's why the fed has been moved to Dallas, where you got the occasional bit of lucha and puro and hardcore back then.
That said, though, I still want to keep the feel and I don't plan on accepting a huge influx of those types, either, although I'd love to have a few of each.)
-=-=-=-=-=- III-D. Roleplaying RPing is the most fun part of being in an e-fed, now isn't it? Okay, so, here's how it works. Your roleplaying and strategies will determine your wins. If you have the better angle, the better RP's, and the better strategy, you win. Final decision always goes to the booker in question. Deadlines are as follows:
- AWF All Star Wrestling- Due by 11:00PM EST on Thursday Night - AWF Megacards- Due by 11:00PM EST on Friday Night If you fail to RP too many matches, I will find a way to injure you and remove you from story lines. And if this is done without any feedback, your character might just stick around as a squash puppet, especially if you do this in the middle of a huge push.
-=-=-=-=-=- III-D-1. Strategies Okay, basically, if you want your wrestler to behave a certain way, or wrestle his match a certain way, email me before the RP deadline. How hard can that be? A good strategy is what I usually consider to be the tie-breaker between two dead heats.
It can contain as much or as little information as you want, and will be appreciated as they give the writers better ideas on how to do your characters.
-=-=-=-=-=- III-D-2. On-Cards On-card interviews were huge back in the 80s, and the AWF hopes to be no exception. There will be a segment on each All-Star Wrestling called "The Gold
Mine," hosted by
"Golden" Tommy Walker. One wrestler will be selected each week to be interviewed in this show and do an on-card. Anyone else wishing to do a promotional
On-card may do so as well. -=-=-=-=-=- III-D-3. Language Guidelines. NO NO NO NO NO NO NO NO NO NO NO NO CURSING. Absolutely positively not. Not even the smallest resemblence. Back in 1985, you couldn't even get
"shut the hell up" on the air on Saturday morning, let alone "kick your ass" or
"god damn shitsucker." Pretend your mama's watching.
-=-=-=-=-=- III-E. Booking Booking will be done by myself and also by my Vice President. -=-=-=-=-=- III-E-1. Weekly Cards Most people (and certainly the most active ones) will be booked for the weekly card. If you're not booked, feel free to do an on-card to stay in the spotlight. Or attack someone if they don't have an angle. There will also be preliminary matches, (as they were called back then) or squashes (as they're called now) against
no-name no-talent bums. -=-=-=-=-=- III-E-2. Megacards This is where titles will be on the line, legends will be born, and men will become Gods. The AWF will have four megacards a year, broadcast live on Home Cable
Entertainment. -=-=-=-=-=- III-E-3. Prelim match guidlines (AKA squashes) Okay, so, if you're not involved in an angle or feud, you may find yourself in a few matches against "preliminary" opponents. Basically, this is a squash match to show off your wrestler's talents. The prelim bums are fictional, and all you have to do to beat them is RP. Of course, if you don't, I'll just find some way to embarass you. You can interfere in someone else's prelim match, or even attack them afterwards, as well.
To keep the feel, there will be a lot of these on the regular weekly cards. As more guys join, there'll just be more shows. Like when the
WWF had like 20 different syndicated shows each week . . . OK, probably not that many.
-=-=-=-=-=- IV. On-camera Personalities Yeah, they had people talking back then too. -=-=-=-=-=- IV-A. Commentary Both AWF All-Stars and the Megacards will be called by commentator
Dave Kelso and his
color man "Diamond" Jimmy Cage. Kelso is a by-the-book face commentator. Cage, is, of course an a-hole heel who will root for the bad guys.
-=-==-=-=-=- IV-B. Interviewers There are three main interviewers. The first is on-card mic guy Tim
Richards. Tim is a small fellow, and easily intimidated. The second would be
"On-the-Scene Reporter" Tammy Devine. Tammy is a large-breasted
bubble-headed bleach blonde. The third is "Golden" Tommy Walker. Walker was a pro-wrestler until an errant piledriver ended his career. He now hosts "The Gold Mine." The Gold Mine is his weekly segment where he interviews a wrestler, often with the intent of making fun of faces, or praising heels. He wears a neckbrace, as he knows no face in 1985 would ever dare strike him. Tommy is a complete jerk and even uses his shows to set up sneak attacks for the heels to get the jump on the faces.
