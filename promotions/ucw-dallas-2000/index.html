Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Ultimate Championship Wrestling opened its recruitment handbook on 18 March 2000, staging itself around a “middle-aged, billionaire owner” named Rex Robeson, who bought a bankrupt promotion's assets and installed a President, Yert, to run it. Yert's first act was buying and refurbishing the historic Sportatorium in Dallas, Texas as the fed's own headquarters and training center; his second was signing the promotion to one of Robeson's own broadcast stations, RGN. The staff table ran down to five referees, with Yert's commentator Kris Karter billed as “the Jim Ross of the UCW,” a demoted promoter turned bitter interviewer in Paulie Smith, and a cut-rate replacement announcer in Rikky Johnson.

The handbook set its own terms on the ground: a twenty-wrestler roster capped at twelve singles and four tag teams, ranked not by win-loss record but by a six-rung ladder running from Jobber up to Superstar, two roleplays a week as the house standard, and three championships — World, Television and World Tag Team — with no weight divisions. A follow-up essay from the same hand pitched a far larger design behind it: UCW as the first of a “big three” of major feds, banding smaller independents into an NWA-style alliance beneath them and crowning an annual, undefendable champion in a Super Bowl-style tournament at year's end.

By 27 March the roster had filled to within six spots of its cap, and the promotion announced its first card, Superstar Saturday Night, for 1 April 2000; a further post the following week confirmed the show had run. No result of that card survives, and the promotion's own record of its original run ends there.

The same voice returned in November 2001, describing the promotion's first run as having built “a group of about 10 reliable handlers” within a few months before it was shut down rather than left “to die a slow death.” That post announces a modest relaunch under the same name, with titles withheld until a promised December supercard — the last document this promotion's own record leaves behind.
