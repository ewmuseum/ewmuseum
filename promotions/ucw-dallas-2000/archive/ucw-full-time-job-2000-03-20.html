UCW (Dallas, 2000) — Rules

Ultimate Championship Wrestling: Think e-wrestling has to be a full-time JOB to have fun? We don't.

20 March 2000
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[detail withheld] http://www.geocities.com/sportatorium/ Ultimate Championship Wrestling --------------------------------------------- [Intro] My name is [name withheld]. I have had experience running a fed in the past, off and on for nearly two years, although it is irrelevant since no one has probably heard of it. What is relevant is that I’m opening a fed once again, with two goals in mind: #1 for it to last for at least one year, and #2 for everyone to have fun. Somewhere along fun lost meaning for me, but now I have really come to appreciate it. If you enjoy e-wrestling to win or for titles, then this may not be the fed for you. Those two things, I agree, are important, but they should be no basis for this hobby we call e-wrestling. I will fulfill the position of Owner/President, while a friend of mine and longtime e- wrestler (about as long as me…which is three years), [name withheld], will serve as VP/Commissioner.
[History] The story begins with a man named [name withheld]. In the summer of 1997, he came under the ownership of International Championship Wrestling. Over the next year and a half, he changed the company's name to the EWA and it quickly grew into one of the biggest and most popular promotions in the world, and founded ICON Entertainment as its producer. However, the business got to him, and for personal reasons decided to resign as CEO in early spring of 1999. Soon afterwards, the EWA folded and ICON
Entertainment went bankrupt. For the next six months all was silent. But then came Rex Robeson. Only 32 and a multi-billionaire, he was tired and bored of the media empire he had created and turned to the sports entertainment industry. Being a fan of wrestling in his youth, he wanted to make the best possible federation he could. After seeing the ICON Entertainment label, he bought it at a relatively low price. His new promotion would be called
Ultimate Championship Wrestling, or UCW for short. All he needed now was a man to lead the company to the top. His eyes fell upon the former
CEO of ICON Entertainment, [name withheld]. Being a Dallas native, one of his first actions as President was to purchase the historic and now neglected Sportatorium in Dallas, Texas to serve as the site for UCW headquarters. Also located inside is a training center for new talent. His second action was to sign the UCW to a major network. Thanks to Robeson's influence, he was able to get a contract with one of Robeson's spinoff stations, RGN. for as much airtime as he wanted. Now, with no major competition, UCW is in position to become the premier federation in the world. I guess we'll just have to wait and see.
CHARACTER STAFF Owner: Rex Robeson - The middle-aged, billionaire owner of the UCW. He loves the sport, and will make sure that he'll do everything in his power to restore it back to glory.
President: [name withheld] - The man Robeson hired to take the UCW to the top. He knows what he's doing, and loves doing it. And with the financial backing of a billionaire owner coupled with a long-term contract, he will be doing it for a long time.
Face Commentator/Interviewer: Kris Karter – A physically fit, 29 year- old African American, and the first employee hired by President Yert.
He was Yert’s main correspondent back in the ICW days, and has now worked his way up to the top. He is the Jim Ross of the UCW.
Heel Commentator: J. T. Drew – The slightly overweight, balding, middle- aged "sidekick" of Karter, although you’d NEVER hear him say it! He is big enough and actually once was a pro wrestler, but a knee injury prematurely ended his career. Maybe that’s why he’s so cynical. Either way, he calls it as he sees it, or should we say, as he wants to see it.
Interviewer: Paulie Smith – Before it was bought by Yert and company, the Sportatorium was the home of the ACWF, headed by Paulie Smith.
After the takeover, Smith was demoted from promoter to this position. He was obviously upset, and seems to show it in his constant criticism of talent. Head Announcer: Rikky Johnson – Yert decided not to go back to Michael Goldstone, who had gone on to fame with his now famous "Let’s get this party started!" intro, because of the money, and instead went with a younger and cheaper verison. This 20-year-old kid with the funny name wants to be a wrestler, but for the moment is content making 30K a year announcing the stars instead of jobbing to them.
Referees: David Sweet (Head Referee) James Cogbern Dan Brown Mark Jones Samuel Thomas [Type of League] The name of the fed is Ultimate Championship Wrestling [UCW]. It is booked, with winning based on the angles the wrestlers involved in. A website is set up at
http://www.geocities.com/sportatorium/ , but the main forum for the distribution of roleplays and cards will still be through e-mail. This fed also has a fantasy history. It's the year 2000, but it's a return to the era of the early 80's when there were no primetime feds.
The only feds are independents spread out over the country. The UCW steps up like the WWF did to band these together and bring wrestling into the limelight. So, don't mention any WWF or WCW characters/angles
NOR any e-fed related stuff outside of the UCW. We want to have fun, but we also need to stay in character.
[Roster Size] Right now there will only be 20 wrestlers total, consisting of 12 singles and 4 tag teams. With a roster this restricted in size, wrestlers won’t be lost in the shuffle and the workload will be kept down to a reasonable level. Also, every wrestler will be able to have a match every week.
[Characters] You can have characters based on real life people, but only original wrestlers will be accepted. Original REALISTIC wrestlers. Being that this is fantasy WRESTLING and not SCI-FI fantasy, we don’t need super heroes or monsters or alien beings. In addition, two plus two is four. You are only allowed up to two wrestlers, either one tag team or two singles wrestlers. There is only one division, so whether or not they’re male or female means nothing to me.
[Rankings System] Ranking is not done by records. The only wrestler in "real" wrestling that I know has ever used records is Goldberg, and that was part of his angle. So, instead, ranking will be done by popularity and overall performance by the wrestler. The different levels are as follows:
Superstar Main Eventer Hard Worker Mid-Carder Low-Carder Jobber Everyone who joins will start out on the low-carder level and will have to work their way up the ladder.
[Roleplays] Just to set things straight, this is not the Ten Commandments (nor the Five Pillars, nor any other major religious statute) of e-wrestling. But it is of some importance. For the UCW, participation is like a… salad. The roleplays are the dressing, but the angles are the vegetables and the real meat (pardon the pun) of it all.
Two roleplays a week is a perfect number. Anything more or less than that and you lean too far away. We all know what happens when you don’t roleplay at all, but if you roleplay seven days a week I’m going to get really sick of reading your roleplays. Unless they’re so interesting I have to read them, but you’d really have to be good to do that. In-rings are preferred over roleplay packet ones, plus they increase the quality of the card, so try to send those in if you can.
Profanity seems to be a hot topic these days in e-wrestling. Well, maybe that’s not true, but it’s a way to introduce it. As far as its use in the UCW, there is none. Okay, okay, as much as it is used on prime time TV. Which means, anything worse than "ass" is a no-no. But if you DO use them, you must [bleep] them out. I read all the interviews before sending them out, but I don’t want to waste five minutes going down all of them having to put in [bleep]’s where I shouldn’;t have to. And don’t use words pertaining to sexual organs that are inappropriate. I don’t think I need to list them.
Roleplays will be sent out in packets by myself or my VP to the entire list every day or so, depending on how many roleplays are sent in on that particular day. Speaking of the mailing list, don’t use it unless it pertains to the UCW. I don’t like to get chain letters, and I’m sure the handlers who join didn’t join to get chain letters, so please don’t use the list to distribute them. And if you want to advertise for your own fed, please ask for my input first. I’ll usually allow it as long as you tell me beforehand.
[Strategies] This is not a major determining factor for winning. Meaning, it is not required for you to send one in. However, it is very helpful in setting up angles with your opponent, as well as being a huge help to the writers (Derek and I). The matches are generally better when you send a strat in, and it doesn’t have to be too terribly long. Just include a few of your strengths over your opponent, your gameplan, a few high spots you’d want to do, and any interference you might add.
For us to accept it, though, please send it in at least two days before the card goes out. I’m not going to rewrite the whole card the night of.
[Cards] There is only one weekly card, Superstar Saturday Night, but as I said previously, with a small roster every wrestler will be involved in some way. Supercards will be held once every 1 ½ to 2 months. I remember a time when the Big Two (Three?) held PPV’s only five times a year. That was when a PPV was really worth the price you paid and you weren’t bogged down with 3 a month. And they will be SUPERcards.
[Championships] There are three titles, World, Television, and World Tag Team. The World Title is reserved for superstar wrestlers and the TV Title for main eventers. There are no weight divisions. I want everyone to have a chance at every title, and there’s never enough interest in a lightweight division. And, although it’s an issue I’ve never addressed before, I’d be willing to accept a woman wrestler into the league.
Whether or not they could hold a title would be based on the same credentials as a male wrestler, yet I’d have to say it would be a little harder to accomplish.
[Application] If you’re interested in joining, then fill out the application below (for a tag team, fill out two apps and substitute the tag team finisher and tag team moves for the singles ones; also, put the name of the tag team down and do only one bio if possible). Send it to
[detail withheld] . Your Name: E-mail Address: Name: Age: Height/Weight: (If it’s too unrealistic, I’ll let you know.) Hometown: Alignment: (Don’t just tell me heel/face. Tell me what KIND of heel/face you are. Do you cheat sometimes, or do you ALWAYS go by the book? Do you have any morals? Loyalties? Or are you the Satan of wrestling with no remorse and no regrets? This IS info I need to know.)
Entrance Music ("Title" by Artist): Gimmick: Appearance: Person Your Character Resembles: (This is for the bio on the homepage. If you already have a pic, great. Give me the url and I’ll use it. If not, tell me who he/she looks like (someone who I can find!) and I’ll find one to put up.)
Ring Attire: Background (history/bio): Wrestling Style: Up to five trademark/signature moves: Finisher and description (if it’s not obvious i.e. the Powerbomb): Sample Roleplay: (This doesn’t have to be spectacular for you to be accepted. I just want to know how well you can rp and that you will have at least one roleplay in case you disappear.)
