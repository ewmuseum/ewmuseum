Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The GIWF launched in February 1999, built from scratch by the handler who played Coma in the IWF, who brought its roster with him to run the whole promotion himself. The GIWF had the shape of a real company at amateur scale — two weekly shows, seven title lineages tracked reign by reign, and a full pay-per-view calendar: Maelstrom, Fear the Raine (its own self-declared best PPV, a steel-cage blow-off), Stable Wars (a four-team tournament whose format the UWF would run for years), and an Extinction whose World Tag Title main event was staged in a literal pub outside Dublin.

Its legends are the shared universe's founders: Hamish McGrubber, the 7'2" Highlander and a UWF Hall of Fame inductee; Curt Raines, the second World Champion; the Dark Lords; and Al Crud, a two-time GIWF World Champion and a fixture of its main events, who would go on to a second act as the UWF's comic-relief absentee owner — the single strongest thread tying the two feds together. Lew Spectre, "The God of Wrestling," carries across unchanged: same name, same screwjob playbook, a continuous character. And Jay Phoenix had his GIWF-era prime — the acclaimed Inferno mystery gimmick, and the GIWF's own Feud of the Year 2000 against Luca the Imp.

Storylines

Two weekly shows — Wednesday Night Global and Sunday Afternoon Suicide — billed beneath the inherited "In Your Computer" brand. The arcs below are the programs the fed's own awards actually crowned, recapped from the shows themselves rather than just the ledger's own dates.

Fear the Raine Written by AI, 2026

The fed's self-declared best show of 1999, and its main event delivered: Curt Raines defending the World Title against Hamish McGrubber inside a steel cage, at Fear the Raine. McGrubber's Highland Fling nearly ended it outright, but Raines kicked out and climbed the cage himself for a senton bomb — McGrubber rolled clear, and both men lay out for a double ten-count before McGrubber used the cage itself to drag himself upright first. The match shared "Best Match of 1999," and the win opened McGrubber's own sixty-day reign, the longest single World Title run the surviving ledger records.

Stable Wars Written by AI, 2026

A four-team elimination tournament, and the structural ancestor of the UWF's own annual Stable Wars, which the later fed ran all the way to a sixth edition from Madison Square Garden. The format outlived the fed that invented it.

Extinction — the pub in Dublin Written by AI, 2026

The World Tag Title main event of Extinction was staged, in fiction, in a literal pub outside Dublin: champions Pete Steele and Al Crud defending no-DQ against the Fomorians on the challengers' own home turf. Crud spent the match drinking — every beer offered to Steele instead, then the bar's own liquor bottles when the Fomorians dumped him across it — and the finish came when Crud, trying to help, swung a car door at Tethra and instead knocked Steele out cold, handing the Fomorians the titles by accident. The single most on-brand artifact of what this fed was.

The Luca–Phoenix feud Written by AI, 2026

Jay Phoenix and Luca the Imp ran a program the announcers themselves called too long to recap on air. Its own showcase chapter, at Halloween Hell, put Phoenix's own Native American Title on the line against Luca's Lucabot suit in a no-countout Buried Alive match: Lucabot had Phoenix's "Inferno" persona stuffed into the plot before Phoenix himself ran in and helped shovel the dirt on top of it, revealing "Inferno" as a decoy body double the whole time — Luca's own dancer, wrestling under the mask. The angle escalated into a TLC match that put Phoenix off a ladder and through a table, paid off at Style and Substances and voted GIWF Feud of the Year 2000 outright, 37.5% of the vote. It is the program that defined the fed's final year, and the reason Phoenix's induction — blank in every copy that survives — was finally written; the full account is in the Halls of Fame. One era-typical beat of the angle is left out per the standing rule; the wrestling is kept in.

The Inferno reveal Written by AI, 2026

The GIWF's most acclaimed piece of craft: an established man under a masked new guise, "Inferno," who took the Television Title on 23 July 2000 and was revealed on-air to be Jay Phoenix. He was not returning from the IWF ("I missed the IWF days," he wrote): he had made his name in the PWW, and by his own account took Phoenix "in a mask and called Inferno" over to the GIWF "and started out as a rookie" (his own words on the PTC forum, "GIWF Memories," 29 November 2005). The angle was written and executed by Phoenix's own handler and nominated for Angle of the Year 2000. The award-show host later admitted on the record that he had no idea who was under the mask until Phoenix told him over ICQ. A mystery that fooled even the people running the awards.

Affiliations

Xtreme Ultimo Wrestling (XUW)

Shared continuity with GIWF (1999–2001)
XUW’s own wrestler-database bio cards list “UWF” among more than one wrestler’s other-federation credits; XUW ran the same roster-and-ranking engine (“I.M. Productions”) as the UWF; XUW’s forum carried the same community culture already familiar from the UWF and GIWF; and XUW’s own “The Bastard” Al Crud shares an exact name, aka, and — per the same handler’s own account — the same handler as the GIWF/UWF’s own Al Crud. The two are read as two houses on one line, though the character connection is treated as likely rather than certain pending stronger evidence.

rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy (RSPW-F)

Community — GIWF (1999–2001) was present here (1999)
GIWF’s own run on the newsgroup is positively identified at roster level. “[GIWF] WE’RE BACK!!!”, 25 April 1999, sits cleanly after the wing’s own founding of February 1999; on 5 August 1999 a fourteen-thread [giwf]Meet: series introduced its roster one post at a time, naming Commissioner Rosso, Hamish McGrubber, The Cockney Rebel, Lew Spectre & The Phenonenons, and ten more — every character those threads name already appears on GIWF’s own held roster. Recruitment continued on the group into 2001 (“[GIWF] Looking for unknowns...”, 23 June 2000; “GIWF --come join”, 3 June 2001), though GIWF never carded a show there: its own show name, GIWF Global, never appears in the newsgroup’s own index.

Chris Williams Memorial Cup

Crossover booking with GIWF (1999–2001) (2001)
GIWF staged a running of the Cup in 2001, its own broadcasts billing the same night inconsistently on air as both the tournament's second and third annual. No winner survives.
