Foreboding Thoughts II...

18 July 2003

Date: 2003-07-18 ... and fell to the floor of his parent’s house. Damon looked up and saw a large, burly teenager walk into the living room with a look of nervous determination on his face. “Dad, my bus is leaving soon.” His father, sitting in a nearby recliner, continued reading his newspaper. “Fine,” he said from behind the paper. “Make sure you have all your things. What you don’t take, I’m throwing out.” A middle-aged woman walked into the room. “Why do you have to be like that?” She asked. “Why can’t you just accept that he doesn’t want to follow in your footsteps?” The man crumpled the newspaper in his large hands. “A henchman for the IRA? Bunch of son of a bitches who will continue the strife that our country has suffered. Nothin' but a group of faggots!” The father stood up and walked up to his teenage son with a look of disgust on his face. “I didn’t raise a faggot, did I boy?” “No Dad,” the boy said. “It’s just something I want to do.” A large hand flew, and struck the boy across the face. The woman shielded her eyes, shrinking away from her husband as he slapped his eldest son repeatedly. The boy made no attempt to protect himself, even when blood began to trickle from his mouth. Tom stood behind his younger brother. “I never understood why you didn’t take his head off that day.” Damon stood up as the scene unfolded. “He was pissed that I didn’t do what he wanted. Any time I didn’t think I’d make it to the Supreme Council, I remembered that beating. It was always enough incentive to keep me going. Some people say that love is a strong motivator. For me, it’s always been rage.” The boy’s face was a swollen mess. His father stopped his assault and stared at his youngest son in disgust. As he spoke, Tom watched Damon mouth every word that his father spoke. “You’re a loser boy. You’ll never amount to anything. If you step out of this house, you’ll never be allowed back. You’ll be dead to me. You got that?” The boy nodded and picked up his duffle bag. Damon followed the boy as he walked out of the living room into the kitchen. His mother was there, cutting carrots by the sink. “Make sure you eat your vegetables,” she said, choking back tears. “I love you too Mom,” the teenage O'Halloran said. He walked up beside his mother and kissed her on the cheek. She gently touched his swollen face and gave him a rag-covered piece of ice. “Put this on your face. I know you’ll be great,” she whispered. “Make me proud.” The young man smiled, turned, and walked out the door. “That was probably one of the best days of my life,” Damon said as Tom walked up to him. “Really? What was your worst?” Tom asked. “Same day,” he replied.
