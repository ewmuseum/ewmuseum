Lay Me Down...

3 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-08-03 When Mary O'Halloran was a young girl, the children at school would tease her about her smell. Mary’s father, a fisherman by trade, wouldn’t spend a dime on his family unless it was absolutely necessary. His boat had the finest toys and equipment; his family didn’t even have a decent washing machine. So when he crammed his clothes (ripe from weeks on a fishing boat) in with the rest of the wash, all of the laundry ended up with a faint smell of fish. The male O'Halloran's usually escaped unscathed from the children’s catcalls. At least, once they got older (and bigger). But Mary, being the only female O'Halloran child, would suffer the wrath of the schoolyard tauntings. The name-calling would last until Mary burst into tears and ran away, embarrassed and ashamed. Then came the Dogs of War. Damon was the biggest of the brothers, but Tom and Nick were no small potatoes either. With a combined weight of near six hundred pounds, the O'Halloran Boys were the last people you wanted to have angry with you. They were big, and nasty. They were also very protective of their baby sister. If even one of the O'Halloran boys found Mary in tears, he quickly rounded up the other brothers. The Dogs of War was a name that suited them: they were pack animals, and they’d fight anyone. If the name-calling student were lucky, they’d get word of the approaching maelstrom and hide for the remainder of the day. If the O'Halloran boys found the unfortunate person first… they’d usually end up missing a few days of school via an extended hospital stay. Even suspension didn’t help. If even one brother was in school, the others would be lurking nearby. They loved to fight and they loved their family. They were a tight unit, in school and in life. “All for one, and one for all” was the saying of the motley Musketeers. As people get older, feelings and relationships change. But deep down, the bond between brother and sister would remain. Memories could be relived, better times could be remembered. Which was why Damon flew cross-country when he heard about his sister’s assault. The next eVolution Cell Champion, Damon O'Halloran, stood in his sister’s hospital room. Days before, he despised her. He blamed her for his loss of custody of his daughter. But now, watching her breathe with the aid of a respirator, her face covered in ugly bruises, Damon knew that he was to blame. He was always to blame. He blamed himself for Tom's death. This was just another straw added to the pile of guilt he carried with him. Damon watched Mary sleep, forcing the doctor’s words out of his mind. Words like ‘vicious assault’, ‘spinal damage’, ‘wheelchair’, ‘potential loss of sight in one eye’ were brushed away as Damon’s hand touched his baby sister’s hair. Doctor’s weren’t always right, Damon told himself. After all, they told him that he’d never fight again. Fighting. Damon knelt beside his sister’s bed, resting his head gently on her hand. He had won his match at ReVelations. He wanted to wiun his match against Norman the following week so bad. He had to get focused, if not for himself, for his sanity. An example had to be made. This time, it was Damon’s sister. Next time, it could be his daughter. Standing, Damon gently kissed his sister on the forehead. The feeling of powerlessness was one O'Halloran didn’t relish. For his sister, and for his daughter, he had to do something. Something.
