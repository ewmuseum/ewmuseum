Something Special.

31 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-08-31 Larry wiped down the bar for the sixth time in the past half hour. Business was slow in the pub, with only a few patrons ordering anything at all. Most of them were seated at various tables, chatting with each other or watching Sky Sports. Larry considered turning off the television, or at least changing the channel, to drum up some more business. People were distracted from their drinking. Except for the big guy in the corner booth. He was sitting by himself, draining Bud after Bud like he was on a mission to extinguish every drop in the bar. That seemed to bother Larry a little more than the lack of business that the rest of the bar was providing. The big guy looked like a rough customer. He was sporting a shiner, and had a finger taped to a wooden splint. He was a scrapper, no question about it. But while the rest of the place was drinking to enhance their social mood, the big guy seemed to be drinking to escape something. The waitress stepped to the side of the bar. 'Two more Bud for the big guy,' she said. Larry picked up two tall glasses, placing the first under the tap. As he filled the glass, he noticed the waitress looking over in the big man’s direction. 'He sure can put them away,' she said quietly. 'Over a dozen in the past hour.' Larry handed her both glasses. 'I hope we don’t have to carry him out at the end of the night. He looks like he’d be a handful.' He shot a smile at the waitress. 'But I’m sure you wouldn’t mind getting your hands on him.' She smiled, slapping Larry on the shoulder. 'Shut up!' She set both glasses on the tray and carried it across the room. He did look quite yummy, she thought to herself. But he didn’t look like he was in a very social mood. When someone is drinking with a purpose, it’s best just to let them be. Plus, she knew who he was and knew better than to bother him unless he wanted to be bothered. 'Here you go, Mister O'Halloran.' She set both glasses beside his huge arm, plucking the empty glasses off the table and placing them on her tray. He slid two bills over to her, but otherwise remained motionless. 'Can I get you anything else?' Damon turned his head slightly. 'No,' he said simply. As the waitress produced his change, Damon shook his head. 'Keep it.' She paused. He’d been telling her to keep the change since he started ordering. But over the past half hour, he’d been paying more. Normally she wouldn’t object to a sizeable tip, but Damon O'Halloran was known as something of a powder keg. The last thing she wanted to do was get on his bad side. 'Thanks Mister O'Halloran.' He gulped down the first glass and slid it over to her. 'Might as well take that one back with you,' he said as he licked the foam from his upper lip. 'And call me Damon.' 'Okay... Damon.' She thought it was a little odd that he hadn’t even looked at her since he sat down an hour ago. He was definitely going over things in his mind, and everyone else was taking a back seat to his thoughts. 'Sarah, don’t pester the man,' Larry teased. The big Irishman turned his head and, for the first time, looked at the waitress. 'Your name is Sarah?' he asked. Sarah looked into Damon’s bloodshot eyes. He was sporting a bit of a mouse under one of them, and his nose looked as if it had been broken and freshly reset. He looked pretty roughed up, which was something she’d never seen in all the times she’d watched him on television. 'Yes it is,' she said with a smile. He turned back to his second beer. 'That’s a nice name,' he said faintly, as if lost in thought. 'That’s my little girl’s name.' 'What a coincidence.' Sarah quickly wiped up the rings of condensation around the table. 'Well, enjoy your beer.' Damon looked up at her again. 'You must be a wrestling fan. You knew my name.' 'I’ve seen you on T.V.,' she said. 'My brother and I watched eVolution all the time until you guys came over here to perform, now we'd have to get Box Office to watch an event.' Damon nodded lazily, the alcohol finally starting to faze the big man. A few tables away, a man wearing a Manchester United jersey snapped his fingers. 'Chop chop,' he said loudly. 'We’re parched over here.' Sarah turned, but Damon gently placed a hand on her upper arm. 'I don’t get to see my little girl very much anymore. It’s nice to talk to a Sarah, even if she’s not my Sarah.' He set his second empty glass on her tray, the foam remaining on his upper lip. Feeling slightly uncomfortable with O'Halloran’s hand on her arm, she tried to disarm him with charm. 'Well I’d be happy to talk with you,' she said. 'I just need to take care of this order first. But I’ll be right back.' O'Halloran waved his paw in the air. 'Screw ‘em,' he said. 'You sit down over here and I’ll get your boss to bring us something to drink. Are you hungry? I could order you something from the kitchen.' 'The kitchen’s closed for the evening,' Sarah told him. Damon shook his head. 'Not for me,' he replied. 'Nothing’s closed for Damon O'Halloran.' The man in the jersey began clutching his throat. 'Help... I’m dehydrating,' he gasped. The other two men at his table smiled and laughed slightly. Sarah chuckled. Damon O'Halloran didn’t see the humor in it at all. He slid out of his booth and stood up, the knuckles in his clenched right hand popping. Sarah placed a restraining hand on his chest. 'It’s okay Damon,' she said, quite worried about his intentions. The table full of men also appeared concerned. 'Easy there, big guy,' the Man U. guy said. 'I was just screwing around.' Damon stopped and looked at the tiny waitress. She was gutsy, spunky. Just like his little girl. Somewhat proud of her moxy, O'Halloran uncharacteristically decided to let it pass and began to sit down. 'Christ, get a sense of humor,' the Man U. muttered a little too loudly. The two men at his table started to warn him. Their eyes grew wide, their mouths tried to form words of alarm. But all he heard was Sarah shout 'No!' The back of his head was grabbed in a big mitt of a hand. Before he knew what was happening, his face was bouncing off the table top. The first time sent a stab of pain through his head as his nose cracked and folded at an ugly angle. The second impact cracked his teeth, punching the remaining ones through his upper lip. With watering eyes and blood running down his face and neck, he felt himself being lifted high into the air. Seconds later, he felt himself dropping. His head was suddenly jerked straight down, finally bouncing off the hard carpeted floor. A bolt of lightning shot down his spine. A flash of light, stars in his eyes. Unconsciousness. Damon O'Halloran got to his feet. People were quickly clearing out of the bar. The bartender was on the phone, shouting something Damon couldn’t quite make out. But the sight of the waitress standing several feet away, the fear evident on her face, snapped Damon out of his thought of pummeling him even further. He stepped over the bloody, motionless man and walked slowly towards Sarah. Frightened, she backed up until she bumped into the table. Damon stepped on the glasses she had dropped on the floor, crunching them under the sole of his army boot. 'I’m sorry you had to see that,' Damon slurred. 'Your old man’s not a very good person. But you be good, yeah?' Sarah blankly nodded. Larry stepped out from behind the bar. 'Back off O'Halloran,' he said, trying to sound threatening but hoping the monster wouldn't call his bluff. Damon’s jaw clenched. 'You and Griffin, you bastards,' Damon spat through gritted teeth. 'You just want to keep me from being happy. The slightest bit of pleasure in my life, and you think you can keep me from it. You keep me from my girl. You keep me from the belt. But it won’t last forever.' Damon raised his hands, as if giving up. 'It’s your world, boss. But if I can’t be happy, you can bet your ass I’ll make life Hell for everyone else.' Larry quickly moved to Sarah’s side as the brawny fighter staggered toward the exit. 'Dark days are coming.' He kicked the door open, cracking the door frame. 'And there’s only two things you can do about it - ' Damon disappeared down the hall before he could finish his statement, leaving blood, carnage and nightmares in his wake.
