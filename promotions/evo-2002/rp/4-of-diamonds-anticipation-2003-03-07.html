4 of Diamonds - Anticipation

7 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/7/03 Now what? Sitting alone in the restaurant, eating a plate of steak, Jason wondered what spur of thought had brought him to this very spot at this very time. The smell of a freshly cleaned carpet and a dozen different feasts filled the parched air. The lighting was dimmed to a point where one almost had to squint just to see the walls. Mindless conversations rambled on in the background, with men and women dressed in clothes that made you wonder if there was really an economic problem in the country behind it all. And Jason just wondered where he was going from here. The rare steak was quite delicious, but almost worthless to Jason. His taste was on. His mind was wandering. Nothing reacted and none of his senses cared for it. They were just acting off the fuel of a subconscious pendulum, swinging in the desolate wind of misery, ticking to the pulse of his mechanical heart. “Is everything satisfactory, sir?” asked Jason’s waiter, a tall, skinny man, reeking of stereotypes of a million waiters before him. Small, black mustache. Mouth formed into a small smile. White tuxedo coat. Short, black little tie. And of course the tray, resting under his arm. “Yes, everything is ok.” The waiter nodded, and walked over to another table, to give the same pointless advice on what they should eat tonight, how they should take it, and make sure that he works his ass off to get his tip just the way he likes it. The steak and the potatoes and the salad and the drink – all were slowing disappearing just before they were finally gone. Now all that was left was a dirty dish, stained with grease and small fragments of his favorite starch, a fork, saliva still clinging to the stainless steel, and Jason himself, wrapped in wonder and bewilderment of life. One by one, people ate and left, each in a different style. Some had their arms around each other. Others, who were alone, threw their jackets and overcoats on and walked into the night. Others would stand, shaking hands and bidding a fond farewell. Until we meet again, old friend. Until we meet again. None left the same way. None resembled the other. Life was unique, Jason found, yet so repetitive. The world had so many different sides to it, yet in many ways they were all mirrors. The more things change, the more they stay the same. It was when a man, different from the waiter earlier, politely walked up to the lonesome shell of a man and said “Hey pal, sorry but we gotta close” that Jason finally acknowledged being alive again. He rose up, and dragged his feet behind him as he stepped out into the winter evening, fresh snow on the ground from the night previous. Jason looked across the way, and there stood a phone booth, silent and inert, staring back at Jason in its own manner. He decided that if he was going to be in Boston once again, he was going to get something done. Jason stepped into the phone booth, and searched his pockets for a quarter, pulling out his deck of cards with the four of diamonds sticking out on the bottom. He found himself two quarters, and recited a number by heart. Maybe this will do.
