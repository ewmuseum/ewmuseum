From the brain of James Kimson: July 29th, 2003

29 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-07-29 My Father told me when I was 12 years old that you should always “expect the unexpected”. Dad wasn’t the mos successful man in the wrestling business, and he’d be the first to admit that. But with that being said, Dad wasn’t a fool. He knew that although he never made it big, never worked anything more then a TV Squash match for Vince back in the mid 80's he always knew how to take care of his family. I can always remember birthday’s, family holidays camping and things like that. Dad was the king of hooky advice to, like “Keep your stick on the ice” and “Look before you leap”. I guess that’s just how he was raised. And while in a lot of way’s I’ve tried to emulate him as a Father, as a wrestler I basically took everything he did and did the opposite of it. Dad wasn’t the best worker, but he took good bumps. Although yes I admit I do take good bumps, and my track record speaks for itself I am a good worker, Dad wasn’t ever much of a crowd man. If the fans only knew how warm a person he was on the outside then maybe he would have made it big and we, as a family would have had all the luxuries that my family now enjoys. A lot of times I’ve gone off the record and said that I what I do for 3 reasons. First, because I can provide for my family of 4 children and my beautiful wife and they don’t have to risk themselves to do it. My Wife raises my children at home because its what she wanted. So I went on the road. It sure as hell hasn’t been easy. I love my wife and children as much as anyone, I just have a funny way of showing it I suppose. Not to many “Daddy’s: are international wrestling icons. That’s not something I particularily asked for at the beginning. The Second reason is because I am good at it. I’ve proved that, I’ve done things not many other men have done and I hold my head up high every night after the camera stops rolling, win or lose, wether my face is covered in blood or in pride. Thirdly is because there are millions of people all over the world that would pay their hard earned money. 60 bucks for front row tickets just so that they could see me perform. Think about that for a second. 60 bucks! No, i stand by the ticket prices that exist in eVo, I don’t sit in on finace meetings and argue that if the average teenager or young adult makes 6 dollars an hour that he had to work over 10 hours (due to taxes on his pay check) just for 3 hours of entertainment to sit through say 2.5 hrs of it for 30 minutes of James Kimson. 30 minutes of me. And who am I to charge 6 dollars an hour for a 10 hour work day? I guess in the end the kid, teenager or the 21 year old man doesn’t rationalize it. It’s because he’s a fan. It’s because he believes in me. Someone I never knew believes in what I bring to the table. My convictions, my morals, my will to win, my drive to become something better then what I am right now. My father told me when I was 18 and working in New York as a wrestler and personal trainor that no matter what you always give the fans what they want. If they want a picture you give it to them and don’t charge them. If that 15 year old kid wants an autograph you break out your pen and sign. If that kid wants to ask you about what your favorite match was you answer. These people work hard and are only there because they believe in YOU. Then he told me seconds later that you have to believe in yourself to, or else they’ll know you’re a fraud. I never thought of myself as a fraud. While the world of Professional wrestling has been exposed thanks to “Vince” in recent years I’ve never felt ashamed to be what I am. I always felt an overwhelming sense of pride. People blow things out of proportion thinking everything is scripted from one day to a year ahead of itself and you can’t know the truth unless you are a professional in this business. But I’m not writing this down in the need to debate or justify professional wrestling as a sport, or if it’s real. We all know that it’s real, I get hit with a chair and it hurts like a son of a bitch, and when I bleed it’s a serious issue. I remember back months ago in my match with Sovereign at Unbreakable when he had his arm sliced open when I threw him through a window. Sovereign’s life was in danger at that point in time. It took his quick thinking to save his life and he still hasn’t recieved clearence to come back last time I spoke with Leya a month ago. Yes, I still call her and we are brother and sister but there is a huge hole between us. William is my brother in law and I think that we solved some of our issues between us like men that night. But enough of that. So I suppose you are wondering what my point is aren’t you? Well I just arrived home from England last night at around 5 in the morning and my brain is hurting from the 5 hour time change and the fact that I have a swollen jaw and a bruised bicept from my match with Xander. It was a hell of a match and I felt that it was something that should go down as one of the most physical Universal Title matches in history. Xander is a tough cookie, I haven’t ever seen anyone kick out of my finishing move ever and to that I give him credit. But it was the trip itself that is making me write this. Two things happened over the last 2 weeks to me that have altered my sense of purpose. No, I’m not born again so to speak. It’s nothing to do with God or the industry as whole. But when we were in London I was staying at my families home there. I bought a loft in London during my stints wrestling in Europe a few years back and Vicky said she wanted to keep the loft because she loved London. To be honest I couldn’t blame her, it’s nice to leave Buffalo every now and then, and the Kids have friends here as well. Anyway, I receive a phone call from a friend of mine who worked at a local children’s hospital and said that he heard Evolution was in town and that I would be headlining a Pay Per View event. He explained that there was a young fellow that was looking forward to going but that he wasn’t going to be able to go because he was sick and from an orphanage, no coverage and most likely on his death bed. So I made my way to t he hospital to meet this kid. His name was Willy and he was 14 years old, laying on his bed coughing his lungs up from asbestos poisoning. He was wearing tattered rags, his hair was matted and dirty as he hadn’t recieved the greatest care. Not to rant on health care here folks but I instantly saw his frown turn upside down when i walked in the room. I’ve visited a lot of sick kids in my days but Willy for some reason seemed different. I spent the day with him as he was without family or friends and discussed his life long dream to become a professional wrestler. I found it touching when he told me how he would sneak into pubs so he could watch me on UKWF Television. He told me that he wanted to come to the PPV and so I made him a promise that I would get him tickets if he promised me he’d get better. That gave us 13 days, I felt that it was a long shot for Will, because he was really ill. But miracles happened. I fronted all of his bills and bought him new clothing and the whole nine yards. If he wasn’t going to make it to the show then that would basically mean poor Willy wouldn’t make it out of the hospital, and I wanted to make him as comfortable as possible. I said I’d come back in a few days to visit. 3 days later after Legends was finished and most of my obligations were met most of the wrestlers head back to America for a few days to work some other shows and make some extra money. Trips to Europe dispite common knowledge are hard on the pocket books and often times, in extended tours the guys squander money and end up making very little profit. Me, I decided to stay because I had made a certain promise to a certain boy. When I arrived the Doctors described Willy’s condition as terminal and that they could make him as comfortable as possible. I said that if he was going to die that I wanted to take him with me to Trafford with me on the 27th for the Pay Per View. They of course objected and said the decision was to be made by Willy. Expect the Unexpected ran through my mind when I think about it now. Willy agreed that if he was going to die he wanted to enjoy it. I didn’t expect that, I guess my motto of never give up didn’t apply to the dying and I understood after some deliberations. I discussed it with Vicky and she said that if I was going to do it that I should at least keep Willy in the hospital until the day of the show and I agreed it was best. The week passed, I visited Will every day pretty much. I was so moved by his plight that I couldn’t leave the poor kid alone to die, and there wasn’t even any guarantee that he’d make it to Revelations as it was. It was breaking my heart to be honest. About 3 days before Revelations, actually about an hour after Legends was finished taping I got a call saying that William D MacInnis passed away while watching Legends... he died watching me wrestle. Expect the Unexpected huh? Your damn right, I never felt more empty, i should have taken him with me to see it live. He said to me that morning to “go get em champ.” I didn’t feel like much of a champ at that point in time. I knew that Revelations was around the corner and I had a feeling that Dan Somers was going to decide to keep the belt on Xander despite our conversation a week before when he explained that Asis was going to run in and cost Xander the title. But now, when the match was over it was a DQ. Not only did I not win the title for Willy, but I lost the match. I felt my heart in my throat choking me. So not only did I screw up and not get him his dying wish of seeing me wrestle in person and winning the Universal Championship but I lost the match and a bit of self worth. Maybe I can make it up to him. Maybe I can remember him in a good lite. He was a smart, caring child who’s life was neglected from day one. And the one person that was kind enough to take care of him had let him down. At least that’s how I felt. But somehow I have to make it up to Willy. Someone how I have to right the wrongs that I’ve made. I don’t know why I’m writing this down. I can hear Logan crying down the hallway, he usually gets up really early when I’m not home, or so Vicky tells me. This was a personal issue, not a professional one. But it’s that one time when the two merge, and break your heart.
