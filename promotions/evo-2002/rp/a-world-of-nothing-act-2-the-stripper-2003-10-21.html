A World of Nothing Act 2 - The Stripper

21 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-10-21 The room smells like sex and booze. But, then again don't all strip clubs smell like sex and booze? A many different assortments of strobe lights shoot and spin around me to make me think the girl dancing half-naked on the stage is pretty when she's really not. That's the secret. That's why it's dark in strip clubs. That's why they have strobe lights and loud music. To distract you from the fact that the girl on stage is ugly. From time to time, it's not even a girl. But when swept up in the many strobe lights and loud rock music, a customer can see a man as a woman and a woman as a man. That's exactly what strip clubs are for. For you to have what you want. For you to make the decisions. To delude you into thinking you're the ruler of this place. But you never are. Bruce is sitting across from me with a bottle of vodka on the table beside him. In his lap is a drawing pad and in his right hand, which is the size of the stripper's breast, holds a pencil. He's drawing the girl on stage, but not really. Really, he's drawing how he sees the girl. He's making her more beautiful than she really is. Bruce never draws reality, even though he says he does. He draws fantasy. He draws how he sees things. 'You know,' I speak up, trying to be louder than the thumping rock ballads, 'I'm getting tired of my wrestling gig.' 'Say what?' Bruce calls. Of course, it's impossible for him to hear me practically. 'I said I'm getting tired of this wrestling gig!' I call back quickly, louder this time. This time he seems to pick up bits and pieces of what I said. Enough to put it all together. Enough to give a shocked look. Enough to sputter out a bit of alcohol on his drawing bad. 'Sean?! Are you bloody mad?! Look how much money you're making off this shit!' 'Didn't anyone ever tell you money is only a earthly possesion?' I ask, offering a quick grin. An odd smirk. And then, I sip my drink. 'I think it's time I hung up my boots... but now if you'd excuse me...' I stand slowly. This is the moment in which his mouth is gaping. But I don't mind. I pretend not to notice. On my way to the bathroom... I met a stripper named Sam.
