The golden corn tickled my ears as I pranced happily to the chirping of the birds. My father stood, holding a firm grip on his shovel in his right hand. So much so that his usually pale fists were now coloured in a shade of bright red. Filled with the purities of the beautiful spring day, I pulled down on my father’s worn, old denim dungarees, which had been at this time, coloured brown in patches. He looked down to the corn, stretching high above his dungarees that had been folded above his knees and became drunk on happiness. Though the past number of years had proved bleak in terms of harvest, my father had high hopes for this year’s. The air, which had entered through his nose, seemed to put him at ease. The sun shone down on the corn’s golden complexion which seemed as if it had never touched, unspoilt. Through my eyes, its complexion was never dimmed. My father raised his rugged left hand while tending the shovel with his other and squeezed my tender cheek gently, confirming what we both thought. “Yep son,” he said confidently scratching the back of his sunburnt head. “Looks like its gonna be a good harvest.” At this stage, those words seemed extinct. Farmers around this time seldom spoke hopeful words but rather took a pessimistic view reckoning that the sun’s been shinin’ too much for the harvest. My father, always the optimist, never questioned the harvest. I simply nodded my head in agreement as I watched him cock his wrists as he dug the shovel into the soft soil. Lately, my father had done little more than farm and prey in the hope that grandpa had been hibernating rather than dead. “Isn’t his time,” he’d say. “Yo’ Gramp is a fighta’ you hear?” Though I hadn’t reached adolescence at this early stage of my life, I had seen him like a white soul snatched away. Soon the sound of birds chirping and corn bobbing to and fro was to be overcome with the horrid sound of a motor-engine. As the loud creak emerged from the breaks of the car my heart stopped for a number of seconds before continuing at twice its original pace. The way I saw it, the loud creak came as a signal of God to run, the signal that Clarence had arrived. “Runnin’ like a beauty,” he’d tell my mother before patting its brass-coated emblem in satisfaction. “Now hows about we sink ou’ teeth into some of that apple pie yo’ Joe’s been tellin’ me about?” His voice echoed through the meadows like the sin it was against eternal life. I watched his courteous entrance from afar and due to personal reasons, was about as close as I ever wanted to get on this beautiful day. Finally, I arose from the spot I had been looking from and continued on my way to the wooden swing my father had built for me last Christmas. Though its support came in the form of a long blue piece of rope looking ready to collapse at any moment, it was held on through the high regard I held my father in. On each movement, my feet dug into the dusty dirt road, not wishing to go any higher. The sound of the motor engine once again started as it passed the leafy meadows, past the diverging dirt roads before finally coming to a halt my very feet. A small quantity of spit exited from the right side of his cider-dampened lips as he looked like an animal that was just about to devour its prey. A middle aged man, Clarence was, his grey roots meeting with a shade of brown half way which overpowered the grey roots, holding supremacy for the time being. The most striking image was his green eyes, which the suns ultraviolet rays seemed to illuminate. A farmer himself, his attire was similar to that of my father. Sitting down on the swing next to me with seemingly no intention of moving he bobbed to and fro slightly before coming to a halt. “Now boy, how ole you say you were?” He questioned, the very reason for his question useless but giving him some incentive. “Nine,” I replied in my high-pitched, unbroken voice. “Nine, yo’ getting’ old Terry,” he muffled [withheld — a passage held under the museum's content rule] Senseless words muffled from his cider-dampened lips in his pathetic attempts to keep me silent.
