La Chasse Pour Un Fantôme - Partie Cinq

21 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 21st She looked around a little un-nervingly. The spot he chose for them to meet was, actually, a location. A restaurant to be exact, but a non-populated one. The dim lights made her feel like she was in a movie, as she slouched a little in her booth seat. 'So, why did you pick this place?' He took his sunglasses off, but they didn't offer an answer. He, instead, pulled out a lighter, and removed a cigarette from his box. As he lit the stick, he placed the lighter on top of the box and slid them her way, but she stopped his hand. 'You're getting a nasty habit of picking up nasty habits.' 'I like it here. The foods nothing spectacular, but your left alone enough. We've been here five minutes and havn't even ordered drinks yet.' 'Good point. A nice, shady spot. This where a lot of your deals go down?' 'It's not like that.' 'So what is it like?' He leaned forward, as she did in return. But before answering, he tipped off some of his ash into the tray. 'You wouldn't understand.' 'Sounds like something you'd say. Take your coat off, we'll be here a while.' He took another puff of his cigarette, but eventually leaned back and removed his coat. He threw it against the wall, as it landed in a black heap. He was wearing a black club shirt with blue flames, and khaki pants. But his mellow attitude, and empathetic expression didn't show any real signs of enjoyment. 'Alright. You wanted me here, and now you've got me.' 'So, what's the great Marcus Kane doing these days?' 'Well, don't you mean who? I mean, since you've stopped chasing after my dick, it had to get its own play didn't it?' He let out a yell, but quited quickly through clenched teeth. She wasn't wearing heels, otherwise he'd probably be bleeding. 'Man, you always were a bitch.' 'Your right. Because a whore fucks everyone, and a bitch fucks everyone but you.' 'Cute kitten, really fucking cute.' He took one last, short puff of his cigarette before putting it out. 'But seriously. What have you been doing?' 'We still doing small talk?' 'Yes, we are.' 'I'm reading more. Chuck Palahniuk mostly. Some book about a child named Ender who saves the world. Machiavelli too.' 'Become a book worm have you?' 'A student mostly.' 'Really?' 'I can do things now that you'd never picture me doing.' 'But do I really want to?' He looked her straight in the eyes. He had been maintaining eye contact throughout their conversation, but now he was looking inside her. He reached across the table, pulling out another cigarette and lighting it. He took one long, slow puff. His mouth opened slowly, as he let the smoke seep out in front of her face. She didn't even change expression. She didn't even blink. 'You never knew anything anyway. I'd rather not change that so late in the game.' 'Is that what you think? That all this is some kind of risque moment on a timeline that's been pre-ordained to end soon? Sorry to alter your reality, but maybe you should embrace the real one, not a fantasy world. When you shut your eyes, nothing changes around you. Nothing has ever been resolved, and nothing has ever been improved. If this is all a game, then the players are taking a break, and making up their own rules.' He nodded his head with wide eyes. Not out of disrespect, but for the sake of acknowledging her point of view. He looked off to the side, and saw a waitress finally approaching them. He flicked his hand a bit in her direction, and Fiona looked on. He looked back at her with a smile. 'Have you decided on your drink yet?' Let go of all the hatred, the unsolved mysteries and hidden truths. And Fiona smiled right back at him.
