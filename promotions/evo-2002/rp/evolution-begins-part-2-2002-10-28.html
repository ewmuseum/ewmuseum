eVolution begins part 2

28 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28th October 2002 Jack Gelfand, MD, assistant medical examiner of New York County, looked up from his desk, ‘Hello Sergeant Ross.’ ‘Morning Doc.’ they were old friends. Many a stiff they had shared between them. ‘You look at the headless body?’ ‘I wondered what brought you to my door. Just a quick once-over.’ ‘You gonna cut him up?’ ‘You bet. I want to know the cause of the death.’ ‘Loss of head?’ Ross offered. ‘Maybe. Maybe that’s a cover up. Maybe he OD’d. Maybe he was poisoned. Maybe anything. Until I look, I’m not going to know.’ ‘That’s what I thought.’ Ross hesitated. ‘He was into martial arts.’ Gelfand sat back. It paid to listen to James Ross. ‘He was?’ ‘Calluses on the edges if the palms and knuckles. He’s in terrific physical condition. Young.’ Ross continued, ‘And maybe take a look to see if he was killed by a blow, or other karate type violence.’ ‘That’s the idea, Doc.’ Ross said. ‘Funny about the head. Can you cut better then that?’ ‘Not with a scalpel. Not a shred of skin above the edge of the incision. A regular surgical job. Minimal splintering of the bone.’ ‘Maybe he was guillotined.’ Kai waited until he was invited, then sank to his haunches and bowed deep. After he had placed the leather pouch in front of Matsushima, he looked up. Matsushima remained impassive. When he had fled to the United States for his second tour of duty, he had realised that his life as a business executive and a marriage without sons had trapped him permanently, so that the promise of his noble heritage would die with him. In the absence of a suitable destiny in this life, he had contemplated suicide. In a year and a half fate had turned his rage into cold calculation, his despair into hope, and his impotence into effective dance. Like all philosophers, Matsushima thought as he reached out to heft the leather bag that Kai had placed before him, ‘I would wither without disciples. The young man on his knees had tried to rob me. He was nearly my match; his schooling in the arts was good. I had it in my power at the time to kill him, and saw that he cared as little as I. I had a vision. I was born a samurai into the Shaolin way in a world that no longer treasures their ways. He would learn them’ ‘Why was there violence?’ Matsushima asked without emotion. His voice was a nasal tenor, his English flawless though accented. Kai weighed his words. ‘The man racially attacked me, Matsushima-sama. I was forced to knock him out.’ ‘Were you forced to kill him?’ ‘I did not kill him!’ Kai answered. He repeated a lesson learned when he was 14 years old. ‘It is forbidden to take life unless there is no other solution, or capture or betrayal of a clan member is imminent.’ ‘Nonetheless, it is in the papers and on the radio that he died.’ Kai wanted to blurt out that it was impossible. He searched for words that are more eloquent. ‘I struck him above the kidney with the heel, and then with the toe to the jaw. Unless my aim was poor, they were not killing blows.’ ‘I saw a policemen on television. His name is Ross. He is a martial arts expert. He said the blows were delivered by a man schooled in the arts.’ ‘If he says they could have killed then he is not an expert, Matsushima-sama.’ Matsushima sat in deep concentration carefully considering his words, ‘He was beheaded…’ the delivery was impeccable. ‘Beheaded? As in decapitated.’ ‘Hai.’ ‘Matsushima-sama, I could not decapitate a man, I do not have the weaponry.’ Kai contemplated his words… ‘Someone else must have decapitated him Matsushima-sama.’ ‘Just so.’ The calm voice took an edge. ‘You may read all of what he said in the paper. If it was not you who killed this man, then who?’ ‘Could it have been James Ryan?’ ‘The man was left, his head severed from his body. Ryan?’ there was a flicker of the eyelids. ‘Tell me what happened. Leave out no detail.’ Matsushima listened with patience. There were no Chinese in New York yearning to return to the ways of their warrior ancestors. They had come to sell video games and learn to dance in discotheques. Nevertheless, here in front of Matsushima, was the material of a Shaolin warrior. Not a hardened criminal, the way he had been portrayed in the press. There were no accusations, but it could be linked back to Kai, if the policemen was as smart as Matsushima suspected. To live life as a Shaolin, you cannot fear death or anyone. Kai was the first disciple of Matsushima; even though he was a near equal of the taijiquan supreme master. His clan like his philosophy was ecumenical. Blacks, whites, and Puerto Ricans – a dozen of them. They had all been lost and past caring. He had given them strength through learning – Zen Buddhism and Martial Arts – the way of the Shaolin. They would learn from Kai and from Matsushima, from their discipline they had rediscovered a will to live. Matsushima’s eyes raised a fraction as he experienced a moment of cold lucidity. For the propagation of their faith and his philosophy, there had to be risk and reward, and the funds to go on. Unknown to Kai, Matsushima was running a crime syndicate using his disciples as a means of attack. For the Shaolin warrior in New York City in the year 2002 the only open road was crime. Disparaging of society, Matsushima never seemed to hesitate. He had attained his end. He had become a master of the Shaolin, as his family history had foretold him. That he had become a common criminal was of no consequence or moment. He focused his attention on Kai. Kai finished his story, bowed his head, and waited. Matsushima asked in Chinese, ‘Kai, what are the guiding principles of the Samurai?’ ‘Filial piety, obedience, loyalty, and the sense of one’s indebtedness to one’s superiors.’ The reply came in sharp Chinese. ‘How is the failure to observe these principles regarded?’ ‘As a disgrace.’ ‘Am I a disgrace then?’ ‘I do not follow you Matsushima-sama.’ ‘You say that it is a disgrace to not follow these principles; then I must be a disgrace. Kai jin, I have not informed you correctly of my past activities. I have been running a crime business from these sacred walls. Your fellow disciples have been aiding me to commit several robberies across the New York district…’ Kai felt a rush of adrenaline. A part of his mind called out to him that this was a seen from a book or a movie. The thought was immediately submerged in his total commitment. ‘I must leave…you have disgraced your family and their name, geijin, Matsushima, if only they could see you.’ ‘Kai jin, suwatekudasai.’ Matsushima ordered Kai to sit in Japanese. Kai stood ridged with a look of cold calculated contempt in his eyes; for Matsushima had committed the ultimate insult to a Shaolin monk. He had taken place in criminal activity using the arts. Kai had chosen badly in his first friend in New York. Perhaps next time he would choose someone a little more unorthodox.
