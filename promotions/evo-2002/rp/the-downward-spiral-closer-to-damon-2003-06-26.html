The Downward Spiral: Closer to Damon

26 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-06-26 I looked down at the grey carpet which was divided into four sections on the floor. Four that is, before the halves and quarters which I never included. I saw them to be imperfections, complexities in what is laid out to be such a simplistic object. To the passer-by, this carpet looked like your average, fit for working class carpet. To me, however, it was so much more. In this carpet was my childhood. Somewhere in that rugged, grey carpet laid complexities that I could never understand. The time had been 6:54. Fifty four minutes before six. I had expressed emotions of all sorts here and sought to express any feeling toward this sight. This carpet was the same way it had been when I was a child and had not yet been renovated though it was crying out for help. Four swans remained outside in the lake. Four and no more as the three little ones could not be included as they would have to go through life’s changing course before one would refer to them as a swan. Things seemed clear through this window. Clearer at least. Six steps and six alone to that ghastly site. The image brought back the memory of watching in terror as my father committed that sin against eternal life. I saw them, like whit souls snatched away at that very moment. I must have been seven when I saw those kittens drown. Seven years before fourteen. That bucket, that cruel sin against mankind. I watched in terror as he pitched them into the bucket. “Fucking shits,” he would label them. He would tell me how things would be better off this way as the water gushed in and their limp paws would scrape off the bucket making a frail sound. With one, final scream he would throw them out on the field letting them rot alongside the cow dung and rotten potatoes. In his eyes they were decaying matter. In his eyes we were decaying matter. “Fucking shits,” I now label them. Three kittens drown that day, not cats, kittens. They say one death is a tragedy while a million is just a statistic. I wished I hadn’t come here. I wished I hadn’t looked at that rugged, grey carpet. This drew yet another long sentence in my life. It’s punctuation marks being death, hatred and despair. Its metaphors, clichés and adjectives being my emotions. Thing had never been so unclear.
