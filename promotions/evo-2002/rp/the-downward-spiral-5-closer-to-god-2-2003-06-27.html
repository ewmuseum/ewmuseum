The Downward Spiral 5: Closer To God 2

27 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-06-27 I stood , leaning against my car at the side of the dirt road staring down at the meadow that seemed to go on forever. Infinite. Endless. The bright orange sun illuminated the tall grass, interspersed with an assortment of beautiful, unspoilt flowers. Random haystacks dotted the landscape. Here in this field was God’s true majesty at work. This meadow was serenity, perfection, Godliness. I imagine this meadow as the world itself. Without sin. Perfect. This is the way the world was intended to be. A Garden of Eden at my very feet. A Garden of Eden without Adam and Eve to destroy it. The trouble with serenity and perfection and Godliness however is that it’s all too easily disrupted. And now the sound remains etched into my mind. The sound of violent squawking, ungodly screeching. I looked up at the clear, beautiful blue sky and there it was. A barbaric fight between two jet black birds. Crows, I suppose. The sun made my rugged skin glisten with sweat, sweat that ran down my forehead, down between my eyes, down my nose before jumping off to join with the Earth. I pictured these crows as sin itself. The crows clawed at each other like rabid savages. Their beaks ripped and tore at each other in such cannibalistic fashion. I listened enthralled. What could they have been fighting over? Food? Maybe nothing at all? Their sounds echoed through the garden, through the field. I watched in morbid fascination. It was like a grand boxing match. It was like two dragons fighting. Poetry in motion. And the peace of Eden was no more. They say pleasure is the path to joy. The two creatures indulged themselves in the pointless act of violence. Until one of them had blood forming crimson streaks in its black wings, the other with an eye missing. The bright, ultraviolet rays shone down. They moved like old feeble men. At the eighteen minutes, sixteen seconds mark one let off a final, deafening cry. Or better yet, picture the two birds as Evolution Fighting.
