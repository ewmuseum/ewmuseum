And A Sense of Satisfaction

20 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/20/03 Now let me give you this image. Because this shit is going to stay with me for days to come. As I he fell to his death, all five of my senses filled together, forming one bond that of enjoyment that I would enjoy forever. I could taste the remains of my beer, along with my own impression of fulfillment with what I had done. I could hear his fear induced scream, from top to bottom. I could touch the bridge and feel the vibration of his voice bouncing off the rails. I could see his body, flailing in the wind as he drew closer and closer to his demise. And, mixed with the freshness of the air, I could smell the breathtaking stench of death. All at once. All together. All for me to enjoy. When he reached to water, his body bounced off the jagged rock he hit with staggering velocity and landed in a shallow section of the river. I squinted down at him, and saw his eyes open and blood draining out of his lifeless carcass. A stream of blood, only black in the fading daylight, floated into the water, to eternally join the sea. He was dead. And I had killed him. After taking in the moment for at least a minute, I walked over to his Honda and pulled the keys out of the ignition. I went to the back, and with my jacket as a guard against any fingerprints, I opened the trunk. Inside was a suitcase, which he just miraculously left open for me. The rest of the pictures, the negatives to go with them, and the video were there. I took them all, and tossed them into the back of my car. All this, and not a soul in sight. I put the keys back in, and started up his Honda for the next passerby to see. I walked over to the edge, looked down for one last glance at the body of a man I has just ended. He was still there. Bleeding in the reflection of the moonlight bouncing off the rippling water. A great image if I must say. I climbed into my car, and drove away, leaving the bridge to lie in oblivion. In the confines of my hotel room, I watched the video. God damn, I must be great in bed. She moaned and screamed for a good hour or so. I took all the evidence he had for me, and brought it over to the tub in my bathroom. I closed the door, and with the matches I have just for occasions such as this, I set them all ablaze. Pictures. Negatives. Video. Gone in a matter of seconds. And basically, that was that. Awaking on Easter Sunday, I turned on the television to hear of a poor, struggling, barely hanging onto the clothes on his back reporter that had committed suicide. Robert Galliano was his name. He had driven up to a bridge, took a smoke of a cigar that had almost set a fire that would have ravaged the forest, and jumped to his death. The police found his car, engine still running, parked next to him and his body, lifeless on the riverbed. I watched this. I heard this. And in a weird way, I tasted, smelled, and touched this. Fuck, it felt great to be alive.
