Heaven 3: Capricorn

9 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-11-09 His eyes were coated with a pale, creamy white skin. Two black pupils were overshadowed by his scruffy grey eyebrows with hints of black peering out from his grey roots. He looked no older than sixty-five; his dimmed complexion told a million lies however. Three large parallel wrinkles stretched across his forehead. His attire consisted of a plain, white robe with several Japanese symbols on the back. “Father,” my rasped voice echoed. He tilted his head slightly; almost a nod then pointed a long, wrinkled finger in my direction and clasped a cigarette between his left fingers. This was a man whose confidence alone could enlighten an entire nation. His trembling right hand shoved the cigarette between his lips and he took another everlasting pull. “Ain’t no father no more,” he answered, so expected, yet so painfully unexpected. His eyes stared directly through a strand of hair dangling from the left hand side of my forehead to where he saw a chamber full of hatred. Hatred for the father. “That’s a long time ago sonny boy, ain’t had a visitor up these parts in a long time now, so what brings you up here?” Each word struck a painful blow to my mid-section. Like a slave being whipped by his master, each left its own mark. He walked nonchalantly in my direction, his footsteps thumping like cannons being unleashed. A few croaked coughs and he rested his hand tentatively on my right shoulder. He began to whistle in a muffled tone, and then swept back his greasy hair. Every word this man spoke was finely justified. “So you think your ready then sonny boy?” He opened his mouth and blew out a cloud of smoke revealing four or five crooked, yellow teeth. Had I not been so rattled, so mystified and enthralled I would pass this man off as a lunatic. “Ready?” I stared solidly into the creamy white skin covering his pupils. The truth had never sounded so mystifying. He was a self-righteous prophet, he resembled everything a man could be and the fact that looks can be deceiving never seemed further from the truth. “Yeah. I’m ready.” I stated the simple fact. His sadistic stare told more of a story than any writer ever could, more than the Stephen King’s or Henry Miller’s could ever describe. That crooked grin. That greasy grey hair with a hint of black. “Well I hope ya ready, looks like you been charged with murder.”
