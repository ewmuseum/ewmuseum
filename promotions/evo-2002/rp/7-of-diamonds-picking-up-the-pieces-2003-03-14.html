7 of Diamonds - Picking Up The Pieces

14 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/14/03 Sleep was long and tedious, but awaking to see the sunlight shining through the open window gave Jason a hope unusual to him. Warmth thawed away his misery, as he opened his eyes, smiling with the thought of another day’s chance ahead of him. Maybe this sun was rising over the evening when he would be able to turn everything back around for him. The day in which everything that he had been waiting for finally came to him, and his quest ending in the same city where it began. The thought gave him the energy to pull himself out of the bed. The air was a bit chilly, but Jason couldn’t feel the coolness, despite the fact that he was only wearing a pair of boxers. He walked into the bathroom, turned on the hot water faucet, and cupped his hands. Splashing water against his face, he awoke that much more, and became more apparent to himself, staring into his eyes. In the mirror was his bag, which had tipped over in the night, allowing all his stuff to fall out and decorate the carpet. Some of his clothes. Shoes. Deck of cards, most notably a loan card. A diamond. Seven to be exact. He looked down at them, and then the memories of the last few weeks came to mind. Words came to mind, but he dare not speak them. He might run out of luck, and then where would he be? Jason found a smile form on his lips, and felt the pulse of his heart as he realized that he was alive. Alive and well, to do with his life as he pleased. All he had to do was look out the window at the world and decide just what he wanted to do next with his days. He could go back to the world he had created for himself in one of the world’s most popular promotions. There, he could be watched by thousands of men and women, all watching his every move and admiring everything he would ever do. There, he had a name and a voice that gave him a small piece of the world to hold in his pocket. There, his happiness was a lie, but it seemed to be all he had left. He could stay here, and try to slowly piece together the shredded remains of the life he gave away long ago. Everything was here, decaying in the moonlight, waiting for the nightly creatures to come and take them to their final resting places. Jason had returned, and now he wanted to collect it all back before it was too late. And now, with the sun rising over the horizon, he felt that he had a chance, just one last chance, to make it all better once again. He could also just give up, and put the gun to his head. But that was a distant memory, and a choice that was no longer an option. Jason was already dressed by the time he woke up from his mind wandering. A nice, clean shirt, a pair of denim jeans and a newer pair of sneakers, along with a comfortable leather jacket that he had only bought a few hours ago. He walked out of his hotel room and down the hall into the elevator. There, a bellhop greeted him with a smile. “How are you, sir?” “I couldn’t be better. I just couldn’t be any better.” The bellhop nodded, and stared forward at his hazy reflection in the gold doors of the elevator. Jason could only continue on with his grin and his happiness. Maybe now he could be happy again. Maybe now, with a world out there for him to be apart of and a life waiting for him to hurry and pick it up, he could see the light once again. The doors open, and the bellhop said “Lobby, sir. Have a nice day.” “Thank you. You too man.” Jason stepped through, out the front door, and into the cities morning breeze. A deep breath and a stare later, Jason was walking down the street, awaiting whatever was coming next.
