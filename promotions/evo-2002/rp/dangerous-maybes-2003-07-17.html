Dangerous Maybes..

17 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-07-17 As the 727 taxied down the runway, Damon O'Halloran sat comfortably in first class watching the ground disappear below him. A small bird strayed too close to the plane and spun wildly in the air as the huge jet sped past. Hell, I probably spend more time in the air than that bird does, Damon thought to himself. He was a man on the go, a man in demand. Every day was an adventure when you were Damon O'Halloran. Damon leaned back in his plush seat, his eyes closed behind his sunglasses. After going over his mental itinerary, he decided that he should probably try and get some sleep. Over the next few weeks, the only sleep he could afford to get would have to be done on planes, or in cars. So many asses to kick, so little time. The tanned, curvaceous brunette sitting beside Damon nudged the big man. “Sleeping yet?” Susan asked. “Not yet,” Damon replied. “Someone insists on waking me up every time I close my eyes.” O'Halloran tilted his head to the side, looking at his manager. They had left early in the morning, so Susan didn’t have time for makeup. That didn’t stop her from looking good. She was one of those ‘natural beauties.’ Damon’s eyes glanced down at her sweatshirt-covered chest, to the round globes protruding behind the fabric. Well, mostly natural. But all good. “Well, before you doze off can you answer a question for me?” His head rolled back. “What is it with you and questions? Can’t you just take things as they are?” Susan wouldn’t relent. “Could you explain to me what exactly you were thinking when you dangled the Daniel Somers by his ankle some twenty feet off the ground?' Damon smiled. “That’s easy. I was thinking about dropping him.” Susan shook her head. “You have interesting ways of negotiating a contract. Isn’t that my job? I am your manager, you know.” “Not in eVolution you’re not. That place is off-limits to you.” Damon’s voice was firm. “You don’t come with me to arenas, you don’t even associate with anyone remotely connect with eVolution. Other than me, of course.” He leaned back again, apparently ending the conversation. Outside, the ground had long disappeared. The sun shone over the fluffy white blanket of clouds. Everything seemed so peaceful. For a moment, the beauty of it all distracted Susan and she smiled. But Damon’s snoring snapped her out of her moment of serenity. “Damon, wake up. We’re not done yet.” Yawning, Damon sat up. “Fine. Talk, so I can get some sleep.” “Okay, let’s start with this whole eVolution business. Why on earth would you join a promotion like that? They’re animals! You realize that they don’t wrestle. From what I’ve read, wrestlers traditionally don’t last very long there.” “Yeah, I know. I've heard of all the guys who have left after a week because they couldnt take the heat. Guess it’s too violent for them. But me, I live for violence. I get paid to kick ass. eVolution just lets me do what I do best: hurt people.” Damon could tell Susan wasn’t buying it. 'Look, all my life, I was born to do this. I was born to go into that ring and do what I do... whatever I did to Daniel Somers to get that contract is my business. Hell, I shouldn't even be here, I should be back in the Asylum where I belong.” “So why now? Why did you force Somers to give you a contract?” “Easy. I found out that I have some unfinished business to take care of, nobody needs to know at the moment.” O'Halloran slowly cracked the knuckles on each hand. “You know how I hate leaving loose ends.” “You mean Somers?” Susan asked. The big man shook his head. “No, it's not him, I have a big impression to make and there is one man who will feel the ramifications of my actions. In this cell division, I have a reputation to live up to. I can’t explain it. It’s like when people see me, and they stand in awe of my greatness. When I see this guy, I just want to beat him into a small pile of bloody hamburger. It’s an aura thing.” 'Damon, these guys are nuts. They've been known to pull knifes on people in the ring.” The stewardess smiled as she stood in the aisle, offering pillows and newspapers. Damon took a pillow, and watched the stewardess as she walked away. “They can use that knife to butter their toast in the morning,” he said in a low voice. “If he wants to mess with me, he’d better get a gun. Hell, he’ll need a bazooka. A knife’s just gonna piss me off. The last time he pissed me off, the only thing he won brain damage.. go figure” Susan tried to grasp Damon’s reasoning, which sometimes made about as much sense as an Ultimate Warrior promo. “So let me get this straight. You joined eVolution so you can beat people up and finish off this mystery guy?” She didn’t need to know more than that, so Damon nodded in the affirmative. “You got it, Sue. eVolution thought they’d seen some scary sick bastards before. They have abso-fucking-lutely no idea what I’m capable of. I beat the living shit out of people in wrestling. What I do in the ring is borderline illegal. You put me in eVolution with no rules? Start lining up the bodybags. I’ll toe-tag people left and right, and I sure as Hell won’t need a damn gun or knife to do it.” The stewardess returned, pushing a cart of drinks. As Susan took a bottle of water, Damon sat back in his seat and turned his attention to the clouds outside. eVolution, he thought to himself. If they only knew…
