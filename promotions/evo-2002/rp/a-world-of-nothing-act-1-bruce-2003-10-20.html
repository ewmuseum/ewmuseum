A World of Nothing Act 1 - Bruce

20 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-10-20 'There's a world of nothing at your front door, so why the fuck are you staying inside?' That'd be the voice of my overly obese next door neighbor Bruce. Bruce, he's the type of guy that one might want to stab in the head several times. And why, may you ask? Well, you'd have to know the guy to understand. He's the holier than thou type. The type to believe he's more superior and all that nice bullshit. He always has these witty little comments. These little 'trademark' saying. And every Monday? He comes to my home. He intrudes upon my humble abode. And why, may you ask? Well, I would tell you but quite honestly I don't know. I don't like him. He knows I don't like him. Yet, he comes here and he sits his fat ass on my couch and he watches television while I try my hardest to avoid starting a conversation with him. But I always fail. Every time there's a new conversation. Another pointless ramble about random happenings in our life. He seems to be interested in three key things: Politics, religion, and Chinese food. Usually, we'll end up going out and doing something. Using each other since we've got no one else. We'll go to a Won Ton Lou's, the local Chinese restaurant ran by an American man. Some times, we'll go catch a nice movie. There's the occasional bar hopping as well. Both of us drowning our sorrows in the bottom of shot glasses. For whatever reason, I find his company to be the reason I let him hang out with me. Maybe I feel sorry for him. Maybe, and just maybe, I'm just bored enough to humor his belief that we're friends. Maybe... I feel sorry for the bugger. But, to be quite honest, the chances of that are slim to none. But, for whatever reason, he's at my house right now and he's sitting in my recliner and eating my potato chips and he's telling me I need to get out more. And I'm answering: 'And this world of nothing is supposed to be appealing how, Bruce?' 'Well, it ain't really,' He says in his usual deep voice. 'But, then again, a world of nothing is better than a house of nothing.' 'My house, you mean?' 'Bingo, bucko.' And you wonder why I don't like the guy? 'Well then, Bruce, what do you suppose we should do to occupy ourselves?' I always set myself up like this. Why? God knows. But I always set myself up to go somewhere with this walking lard. 'Let's go to a strip club.' 'A strip club?' I ask, somewhat surprised and somewhat not. Nowadays, it's impossible to surprise me. I'm almost incapable of experiencing what human beings have donned 'surprise'. After all I've been through, surprise seems rather... dull and stupid. 'If you say so, Bruce... Let's go.' Why do I always agree? Fuck if I know.
