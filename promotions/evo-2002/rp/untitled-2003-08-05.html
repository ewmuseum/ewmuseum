Untitled

5 August 2003

Date: 2003-08-05 The deep smell of opium lingered in the air. Here, in Suite 63 of The Schön Hotel and Casino, I could see the entire city. Neon lights shined from a nearby club. The lights magnified the beauty of the room by millions. Heaven couldn't look this good... yet, the minute I stepped in and got that scent shoved up my nose I knew that there was something wrong. I didn't bother to look out the window for long. I didn't bother fixing a drink as I usually would and I didn't bother sitting down to take a look at the front page of the newspaper. I began to go toward the scent. My wedding band shined on my hand. It twinkled from the overheard hanging light as I crept toward the bedroom, the source of that deep opium smell. A part of me was nervous. A part of me didn't want to go into that room because of the fear of what was to come. A part of me, however... was curious. Fear is fifty percent horror and fifty percent curiosity. Right then? Curiosity was overriding the fear. Somewhere in the hotel was an identical wedding band. One that belonged to my wife of two months. She went by Judith Lee Sterling... I didn't know if that was her real name or not. She wouldn't tell me. It was that sort of enigmatic mysteriousness that first turned me on to her. It was that sort of enigmatic mysteriousness that made it all the sweeter when I fucked her into the bedsprings. It was that sort of enigmatic mysteriousness that led to me falling for her. To me loving her. To me marrying her and to me happening to be the man to come upon this predicament. As I cracked the door open, the first thing I saw was a bottle laying on the floor. Next to the bottle was a pile of Quaaludes scattered about.That smell had increased to be even worse than before. I walked in and turned, seeing the bed in clear view. Seeing the needle shining in her vein like a diamond. Seeing the heroin laying beside her. The crack in the bowl. She was half alive and half dead, I believe. Her body wasn't making any movements other than the occasional shake. For a moment? I stared. Too shocked to say anything. Too shocked to walk toward her. For every action there's a reaction. My reaction was to stand and stare. Have you ever seen a deer in the headlights? With that stupid look of disbelief of it's face right before it's folded in half by a semi? That was probably the look that was on my face. I looked like I was about to be road kill. I looked like I was about to be another crushed carcass littering the highway. I looked like a bloody idiot as I stood there. Staring. Watching. I remember stepping toward her. Every step I took, she took a breath and I prayed it wouldn't be her last. A Quaalude was crushed beneath my right foot. Step. Step. Step. Step. Crush. There went another. I believe my eyes were still wide open and my mouth still wouldn't shut. Shock is a funny emotion, isn't it? I told my legs to stop moving... and they kept walking. I told my mouth to close... and it wouldn't. I tried to blink... but there was no use. She took another breath and she didn't take anymore. The radio on the nearby table was stationed on 96.9. Classic Rock. 'Yesterday' by the Beatles played as I stood and stared at her dead body... I stood, stared, and did the only thing I could. I turned away. I began to walk out. Walk away from this. Eyes still wide. Mouth still gaping. Like a deer in the headlights. I never went back to the hotel. I was contacted and told my wife was dead and I put on my best act of surprise. They had no proof that I was in the room that night... and thus I wasn't arrested. But not a day goes by that I don't think about Judith. Not a day goes by that I don't see her laying there. Nearing death... and not a day goes by that I don't wish I had never gotten married.
