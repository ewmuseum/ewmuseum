A Visit Overdue

2 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-12-02 Not night time, but dark none the less, a gloomy over cast day, the way it always seemed to be when he ventured outside. The sun shies away, hiding behind the clouds, not letting a morsel of its light or warmth spill out and touch his being. A bitter wind whips him with it’s fury, perhaps trying to banish him back to the darkness, trying to convince him that the outside world is no place for him. The elements were against him, but he wasn’t going to let them deter him. He was somewhere he wanted to be and for once he was there for the right reasons. Hannah Samson Born 8/1/60 Died 15/3/02 It was definitely something he felt he didn’t see often enough. But at the same time, standing there in that Chicago cemetery, a whole world away from eVolution, staring down at his Mother’s grave, he almost didn’t want to be there. He didn’t like the way it made him feel, he didn’t enjoy the feelings of loss and remorse, they weren’t emotions he was used to experiencing. The rage and the anger were clouded out and he could faintly remember the feeling of love, the way he used to feel for his Mother, the one and only person he ever thought deserved it. He hadn’t felt this way in a long time. It felt alien to him, it was entirely wrong. He almost felt human again, it wasn’t something he’d had to deal with for a long time, it wasn’t something he wanted to have to deal with. As the fierce wind blew his hair in his face, and made his long black coat dance behind him like a trailing soul, he stood and for a few minutes, he remembered. He remembered the touch of her lips on his forehead, the smell of her perfume, he remembered the way she’d cradle him in her arms when he was upset, the way she’d whisper in his ear and tell him everything would be okay. He closed his eyes and he remembered. A single tear rolled down his cheek and for a brief moment the wind was still and the sun shone through the clouds. For a brief moment the monster lay dormant and the man was in control. But then the sun retreated once more and the wind started up in a furious rage as he really started to remember. He remembered when his parents had started to fight, the arguments they would have as he lay in bed trying to will himself to sleep. The way their voices would get louder and louder until he would start to hit himself in the head to knock himself out and drown out their angry voices. He remembered when his Father turned to a bottle for comfort, thinking the soothing blanket of alcohol would cover his eyes to what was happening in his life. He remembered standing and watching his Mother walk out of his life. He remembered being thirteen again, standing on his front door step, watching his Mother drive away in a taxi, down the street and out of his life. That was the last time he had seen her. Ten years ago now, yet he could still remember every little detail about her. The anger and rage returned, burying the pain of loss deep down inside again. Once more he stood there as Asis but he had not forgotten. He had not forgotten the man responsible for all this. The man that his Mother left his life for. The man who split up a family. The man who had gotten his Mother killed. He was locked away now, the system had done its job and as far as the police, judges and court system were concerned, they’d done their job, case closed. Asis had just reopened it. Time for a visit.
