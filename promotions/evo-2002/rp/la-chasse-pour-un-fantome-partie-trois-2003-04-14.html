La Chasse Pour Un Fantôme - Partie Trois

14 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 14th Fiona Preston did not want to be here. Fiona Preston was disgusted at the living conditions of the apartment she was in. Fiona Preston was kicking herself in the ass for being here. Fiona Preston does not have a fondness for strange people. Fiona Preston hates Hawaiian people. Fiona Preston hates this. Yet, she stood there. An apartment with stained carpet and torn curtains. Curtains, made of black bed sheets no less, stapled to the wall. Most likely because the operator couldn't figure out how to attach the rods correctly. Pizza boxes, and tv dinner containers were strewn about. Instead of jars of sugar, or flour, there were only containers of syringes lying neatly, still capped. Bubble packets from various medicines more than filled the trash can, and some still laid, half-empty, on the counter. Said surface contained dried vomit, a dirty and filled sink. The entire place was a disgusting rut. The only thing that made her madder was the man she was talking to. A dealer/junkie known by his Hawaiian name Kalela. This man was a worthless shit, but he seemed to be able to help her somewhat. 'What the hell are you looking for?' 'Well, the info he gave me before.' He was using. He had this shit grin on his face, and his eyes were bloodshot and glazed over. It just made him look like an even bigger asshole. She had spent all this time searching, found a contact, and the asshole shoots up on something. 'How the hell do you even know him anyway?' 'Ha, I met him a few months ago. Or maybe I mean he met me. He he, can you believe I don't remember?' 'Yea that's great, keep going.' 'Oh. Right. Well, however we happened to meet, he was looking to score. And usually, I don't deal in that stuff. But it was all he wanted. I tried to get him to buy what I had, but he just kept saying no. Took a while, but I found a guy who said he'd hook me up, so long as he got a cut. And you can bet I jacked up the price, considering the work I went through.' 'And he had enough?' 'He was never short. Usually had enough for twice what he bought actually. I remember this one time when I wasn't sober, I tried to steal his wallet. It was so funny.' 'Yea, I'm sure it was.' 'I have a scar where he ripped the skin on my arm with a needle. He wasn't too happy, ha, but I was laughing.' 'Look, I'm on a time schedule here. So if you could hurry up.' 'Oh yea. Sorry. Now, what was it you wanted again?' 'Listen carefully you fucking junkie. I want his address. You told me he gave it to you. So find it amidst your trash so I can go.' 'Hey, easy sister. If your not in a rush, maybe you could stick around and we could party.' 'Yea, there's nothing I'd like to do more. Just get me the fucking paper.' He still gave her the same stupid grin, as he ran his hands through some memo pads. He let out a chuckle as he tore off a yellow sheet. His robe dragged across the floor, as he handed her the paper. 'This is it, you're sure?' 'Hey, name's on the front isn't it?' Her eyes scanned over the paper a few times to make sure she saw what she thought she did. 'What was it he had wanted again?' 'Crystal meth. Surprised he got such an intense batch everytime too. Definitely had some shit going on. Not like the chemistry we got.' She looked up from the paper at him, and let loose a wild punch to his jaw. She kicked him in the balls, twice, as hard as she could. He was on the floor, bleeding from his lip, with tears forming in his eyes as he expression went blank. 'Don't ever look for me.' She kicked the door shut on her way out, as she proceeded to walk down the rotting stairs of the apartment building. She kept repeating the address over and over again, like it was some kind of rite or mantra. As she stepped out onto the street, she realized her search was almost over. But with it's approach, more issues had come into play. Issues that would need dealing with soon. 'Marcus. What the hell do you need drugs for?'
