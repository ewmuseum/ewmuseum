What Are Friends For?

7 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-10-07 I made it. I made it big. I'm known by many, hated by most, loved by few. Who am I, I'm Simon Krask. And if you have something wrong with me, just tell me outright, because it makes me feel good. It makes me feel like I've accomplished something big in my life. It's good for me. It's like Chicken Soup for my Soul. Simon is in his kitchen, in his not so classy, but comfortable home in Boston, Massachusetts, and he's putting together a sandwich for himself. He cuts a bunch of pieces of onion, throws it on the bread, some extra sharp cheese, some mustard, and then finally the pastrami. He takes a bight of it, then he pours himself some coffee from the already brewed pot in a mug. He sings to himself, mumbeling some unknown verses, then he leaves the room. He comes back in with a coat slung over his shoulder, and he puts a set of keys in his pocket. He grabs the mug of coffee, and his plate with his sandwich on it, walks over to the dining table, grabs a notepad, and a pen. He scribbles down some stuff on it, smiles, and walks out the side door. He looks back, and sighs. 'Moving on. Again. I'll be home soon,' he says aloud, before walking through the door, and closing it behind him. A car starts, and the garage door sounds, and we hear the car pull away. Outside, we hear two quick honks. Over by the stairs, an ugly man walks around the corner, yawning, and rubbing his eyes. He looks tired, and worn out. He's Mexican also. He walks over to the refridgerator, and opens it up, but closes it, empty handed. He turns around to stretch, but notices the pad of paper and pen on the dining table, and runs over to it. He doesn't even look at the pad of paper but instead, eyes the sandwich and coffee. He looks around, and grabs the sandwich and the mug, and walks away into the living room smiling. What a good friend.
