Tiocfaidh Ar La...

18 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-07-18 The clouds hang low over the mountaintops. A darkened sky fills the scenery with a sense of foreboding. The lush hills are cast in shadow from the billowy material shrouding out the sun. If smell were a sense available through transmission it would surely fill the nose with sensations of a fresh rain, a rain that could be seen as a cleansing wash over the countryside. A rain that is all too frequent in the hills of Ireland. Yet this man is not in Ireland... for he will never be in Ireland again whilst he is still on this earth. This man stands alone in a suburb of New Jersey. More alone that he has ever felt before. This season is expectant of such conditions though and most in the area go about their daily schedules not giving the passing weather much thought. One man does not though. One man looks out of a window and sees what is going on around him with new eyes. Eyes cast not from a monotonous life, nor from being oblivious to the miracles of a new day, eyes that have, for such a short existence, seen much. These eyes look out now though and for the first time in a long time see the miracles around him. He sees that the rain is indeed cleansing the earth, taking away the old and bringing in the new, much the same that has been done in his life recently. He has recently lived through much though and realizes that change is inevitable. He has also come to realize that you do not look so much into the past for if you do you will trip on your way into the future. Given the circumstances surrounding the last two weeks of his life that could not have been made more evident. The tragedies the world has suffered as well as the ones closer to home. He has come to appreciate even more the things in his life that he has either been given or has earned through hard work. Determination has always been a key ingredient in the making of his character, determination that he learned from his parents. He has questioned the definitions of certain things in the last few weeks as well. He thought he knew what love was. He was sure of it. He was sure of it until the day he saw his homeland for the last time. That day he felt what love truly is. It had a new meaning. One that he was sorry to learn but one that he now is glad he knows. He also questioned what priorities were. He knows better than ever now that he has always known what priorities were and he did in fact have them straight. He was right about that. As he thinks about these things he has moved from his window and came to the backdoor of his house. The screen door pushes open as he steps out and looks to the sky. He thinks to himself that he is glad to be alive. That for the first time he feels refreshed, like nothing can stand in his way. He has often said it but this time it is fact. As he looks around his massive backyard he makes his way over the far hill. Looking down in the meadow the outlines of a small stonewall is seen. Encased in the wall are small stones or miniature statues, it is a family’s gravesite. He approaches the gravesite and looks around at the well-kept headstones and markers. The one closer to the far wall is newer and still is uncovered with grass. Many flowers and plants surround the headstone, which is topped with the Claddagh symbol. That one stone in particular he heads to. As he does he thinks about one other definition he has questioned recently, the definition of a hero. He has thought long and hard about his hero, IRA gunman Jerry Quinlan. He has thought back on many a sleepless night about everything he had done for him and his family and he has come to realize the world has a skewed perspective of what a hero is. A hero is not an overpaid athlete who simply goes out, plays and takes home millions of dollars. Yet here they are, day in and day out, being touted as heroes. Role models, yes, Heroes, no. A hero is not someone that seeks the glory and fame that accompanies the title. If a person goes out to do a good cause only to make himself into a hero then he is not. A hero is not a person that once he has accomplished his goal looks around for gratification. A hero does what needs to be done and then looks around and feels humble that he was given the chance to do it. A hero does what no others will do and then questions himself that maybe he could do more. He knows that now. He knows of one man that deserves to be called one. He looks down at the headstone as he holds up a bright golden plaque. As he stares into his refection on the surface of the placard the glare grows brighter. The clouds have started to part and the first rays of sunshine to touch the ground all day bound through the sky and dance off the face of the now gleaming award. He looks down at it for a second. “Tiocfaidh Ar La.... Our time will come.' whispers the Irishman. Never before has that been truer. He lays the plaque against the headstone and kneels there for a second.
