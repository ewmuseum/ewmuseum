Jimmy Ventin - Finale - Boys Don't Cry?

9 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-11-09 A barren wasteland of shatted ink pens and the remains of shoes leaving their streaks of black on the cement flooring. The sounds of oofs and groans escaped from the bathroom and then the obscene insults followed. 'Get up, faggot!' Another Jimmy beating was at hand. I stepped into the bathroom slowly, almost cautiously, and immediately I was given front row tickets to one hell of a massacre. I stepped in and watched as four or five jocks beat at Jimmy. His shirt was torn from his body. His face, at this point, was already a battered pulp of blood and what I conceived to be flesh. His limbs bent in ways that they shouldn't. Just then, I noticed the small knife lodged in his upper arm. Tear streams on his bloody face, you could hear his cries for mercy. His begging. Then, it all came to a close without one more word. The jocks rushed passed me and I walked to the open stall where Jimmy laid, wallowing in the dirty bathroom floor like an animal. He looked up at me through bloody eyes. Bruised. He said, in a rough shattered voice, 'Hey, Sean. How's life treatin' you?' He was coughing up blood. When staring down at a soon-to-be-dead body, one can only speak in vowels. Days later, the four or five jocks would be charged as adults in a court and found guilty... but neither of us knew that yet. Jimmy always had a way with words. He was a natural poet, a god when it came to literature and writing. Thus, the way he spoke didn't surprise me a bit. 'How does one sense danger afoot? Does it come as a giant, breaking the world into shattered stones? Or a mighty titan?' He wasn't talking directly to me. He wasn't talking to himself. He was simply talking. When one's about to die, they hallucinate. So, perhaps, he was hallucinating. I still don't know. 'I... wish I were to have another life. To do all this over.... Must the show go on?' Sitting here, on my Island of Purgatory, those words remain etched into my mind. The way he spoke. I remember everything he said and I remember him giving one last breath before dying. Jimmy Ventin's last words were 'must the show go on?' And, luckily, they didn't. We all received what we wanted in the end. Death to Jimmy Ventin. However, when I saw him wallow on the ground like that? I couldn't help but feel something go off within me. I was seeing something right then that no child should ever see. I was feeling emotions right then that no child should ever feel. To say I didn't go home and cry that day would be a lie. To say I went home not trembling would be a lie. Tomorrow, another one of God's Roman tragedies will unfold and one of us will end up wallowing on a dirty mat, bloody and bent. But none of us will go through what Jimmy Ventin went through. None of us. Let's be thankful, shall we?
