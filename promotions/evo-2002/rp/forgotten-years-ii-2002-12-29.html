Forgotten Years - II

29 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/29/02 With the stench of his unlikely loss still turning his stomach, Jason walked the halls of a long since emptied arena, towards the exit. As he walked through earlier that day, he could imagine himself returning with a belt hanging over his shoulder, and a smile across his face. The face of a champion, he thought to himself. The anticipation, he thought, would be enough to get him through the match and give him back what he had held so high before. All he had to do was get through him. Get over that one last obstacle. Then everything that he had lost would be his again. Everything that he wanted to hold once again would be in his grasp. No one would be able to stop him. No one would take it away from him. This time, he would be the best, and around his waist that belt would remain. But it’s funny, isn’t it? How a second, just one second, can change everything. One second can give you everything that you ever dreamed. One second can take the world away from you with one violent blow. One second could be that only thing separating happiness and sorrow. Victory and loss. Life and death. And now, it would be the presence of one second, a thousand milliseconds, one sixtieth of a minute, which would be his downfall. All this thought, ironically, occurred within the space of a second. Jason reached out for the door, pushing through with all his quieted rage. He was about to leave this memory behind when a hand grasped his shoulder. “Jason, Jason!” “What is it?” he asked violently, almost ready to hurt this stranger. “A woman left this here for you.” “A woman? Who?” “I don’t know Jason. We were putting the ring in the truck, but all this time she was sitting in a seat, alone. She eventually got up, and walked up to me. ‘Can you give this letter to Jason Jameson for me?’ I said ‘Sure’ and… so here.” Jason grabbed the envelope quickly. “Thanks” he said, sarcastically. He turned around, and allowed the old, gray door to close behind him. He walked, too lost in thought to care about anything anymore, especially that God damn envelope. After all that happened to him in the last couple of weeks – losing the Continental title, going through a gauntlet match to get his one last shot, and then losing because of a mere second – he didn’t really want to think about anything else except giving up all hope. There was nothing that was important to him anymore. Nothing that he wanted to do. No one he wanted to see. Nothing he wanted to be. He could die right then and there, and I didn’t think he would care. Life didn’t matter. Nothing mattered to him anymore. Nothing would ever matter to him ever again. He was nothing. Nothing. Nothing. Noth… His eyes looked at the name on the envelope. His mouth dropped open. His heart stopped beating. His eyes were preparing to water. One word, written with the lead of a pencil, said it all – Marilee.
