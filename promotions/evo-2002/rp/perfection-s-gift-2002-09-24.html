Perfection's Gift

24 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9.24.02 The world is but a tree in the forest of the universe. We are the leaves that grow on the branches of that tree. We soak in the sunlight, and try to provide for the whole. We live our lives, knowing that there is no end. We are happy on our little branches, clinging to our lives as the wind blows through. We see no end in sight, never realizing that winter is on its way, and the time to die is upon us. The summer warmth turns into autumn cold, and we shiver, tired and naked, hopeful that the sun will return to save us from this cruel fate. But all that we see is grey, and nothing more. We die, and our skin changes colors as we decay, preparing to return to the Earth from which we came, falling gracefully to the dirt – our final resting place. But what of the tree? It lives on, and gives life to others, who will in turn share the same fate as us, unaware of the cold death that waits. But we can never share anything with this tree again. We are nothing to it. A past never to be looked back on. A being that never was. In the end, when the tree dies, and all the leaves return to the ground, the forest will live on. And so will time. Can’t you see that you are nothing? Can’t you comprehend the fact that while you think you are something of importance, you are really just another leaf lost in the wind? Tumbling through the grass? Waiting for its grave to be buried and its life to be extinguished? Can’t you understand that you are absolute scum in the comparison of the universe? I can see all this, and much more. I can see your green beginning to brown already, and your stem beginning to let go of its grasp on life. But you think that you will be there forever, waiting for the world to end. For you tree to fall to the ground and decompose until the end. You think you are forever. You are nothing to time. Nothing but a short time companion, who will walk beside it until it tired of your presence and throws you on the side of the road. You see a world in your hands. I see a world crushing you under its boot. I have tried so hard to teach you to accept the ground as your final resting place. I have tried so hard to teach you to let go of your lives and accept fate’s end. I have yelled into your face, and warned you of winter’s nearing every second of every day. But all you do is smile and ignore me, imagining that the sun will always warm your body as it shines in the sky. My mouth shuts, and all I can do is watch was you all fall, one at a time, begging for the sun to return and save your pitiful little lives. And as I await my own dreary fate, all I can do is laugh as I watch you all sink into the mud that lies at the feet of our world. Evolution is a branch that sticks out of this tree’s carcass. It is strong and rigid, yet bendable and breakable. It is there now, but can easily be snapped off by the right wind. We are the leaves that exist on this branch, fighting each other for the sunlight that shines down from the heavens above. But I don’t fight for that sunlight so that I can be the most beautiful leaf on the branch. I fight for that spot in the light so that I can live longer then the rest of you. I don’t want the others leaves to look up to me as their hero or their envy. I want them to know that I am better then them, and that I am the perfect leaf. The one sent here to take this lies out from your eyes, a show you the true light. The one that you will all beg for help in the end. One of these leaves, that we will name the badger for nominal purposes, thinks that he can overcome me and take the sunlight from me. He thinks that he has the right to take over this branch and make a place where only his kind can survive. He thinks that he is the superior creature on this tree. He thinks all this, but has no knowledge. The badger is nothing in the eyes perfection. I am perfection, and this twig, this branch, this tree, is mines to control. In the end, it will only I how survives, and be the last one to hit the ground. That will be my gift as perfection. I will be given the chance to see you all die before me. I will see the wind take you away, and witness the snow bury you. I will eventually join you in your graves, but not before I witness your deaths. That’s my gift for being perfect. What’s your gift for being imperfect? The last sound you will ever hear will be my laughter as I watch you die.
