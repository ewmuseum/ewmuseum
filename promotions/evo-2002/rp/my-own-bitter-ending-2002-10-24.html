My own bitter ending

24 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 24/10/02 Its time. The waiting is over. Time for him to accept the path that has been chosen for him. There is no turning back. Theres no escaping. But even if he had that chance. Would he take it? The scene opens within the walls of the Louisiana Superdome. The mammoth structure stands so peacefully seemingly unaware of the massacre it is going to hold within its walls in just a few days. Wandering the halls, the camera begins focusing on one locker room in particular entering through the doorway a cluttered table can be seen. Filled with empty bottles of water, wrist guards and tapers and headband. Also hanging over the chair is L2J’s wrestling attire. The camera pans across the room to the less cramped side of the room to find L2J standing in the open space who is only wearing a pair of baggy cargo trousers. He has his back turned and seems totally switched off from the world as the reporter approaches. Suddenly L2J springs into life and begins a set, resembling tai chi around the open space. The reporter remains silent, watching L2J gracefully move around the small area. His moves suddenly become more aggressive opening up with acrobatic moves that look almost like a combination of Thai kick boxing and Lau Gar Kung fu. The reporter seems to almost be watching a dance as L2J throws punches and fists without losing control for a moment. L2J’s eyes are open but he is cut off from the world, his mind is totally focused on what he is doing. Until the reporter makes the mistake of speaking. REPORTER- L2J? we were wondering if y- L2J suddenly springs back into reality and in swift spin he has thrown a fist towards the reporter. His knuckles stopping millimetres away from the reporter whose reactions aren’t quite as quick as L2J’s fist. After a moment he stumbles away from L2J’s hand who quickly pulls on a shirt, grabs a sportsbag and barges through the cameraman. The reporter gives chase. REPORTER- L2J you are mere hours away from what could possibly the biggest match of your entire career. How do you prepare for an encounter that is most certainly set to be a blood bath? L2J maintains his silence which has lasted for almost a month, his eyes just stare down the hallway and continues marching towards his car. The reporter turns to his cameraman and shrugs. REPORTER- William Forth has been very co operative in the matches you two have been thrown into. He could almost be considered an allie. Are you willing to let past die, forget your differences and let bygones bygones. If so, how will that change your strategy going into your match at faith? No answer. The reporter is running out of ideas. REPORTER- Alternatively. William may tenaciously defend his title and do whatever it takes to retain the gold. And with the stipulation still yet to be announced the outlook is not looking too good on you part. How are you expecting to overcome a near seventy pound weight disadvantage. Finally there is a response. L2J- Mind over matter. L2J then pie faces the reporter stopping him dead in his tracks as L2J marches on towards his car. The reporter turns to the cameraman to address the fans. REPORTER- Well there it is folks, your cruiserweight hero looks confident going into the war that will be faith. And though the weight disadvantage is great, one thing L2J definitely has in his favour is a sound mind. The cameraman cuts the footage and reporter drops his arms looking very disappointed. REPORTER- Mind over matter. A finally get a chance to talk to someone worth interviewing and all I got was “Mind over matter”. Damn it I hate this business The reporter turns to see the superstar at the other end of the parking lot throwing his sportsbag into the backseat of his 500 mustang and then driving up the ramp and onto the street. L2J is surprised to see how dark it is outside. Obviously his training has gone on even longer than he had thought. L2J once again seems absent from the world and almost mindlessly stares at the streetlights as they drift past. But his mind isn’t empty. All he can think about. Is the universal title. L2J reaches the middle of a bridge and brings his car screeching to a halt. He closes his eyes, then grabs his bag and steps out the car. He looks over either shoulder down each end of the bridge as he walks towards the edge. He drops the bag beside him and closes his eyes again and listening to the water below him.He thinks back to the last time he stood in a place like this. Thinks about the feelings he had at that time. And how close he came to actually doing it. Taking the last step. He thinks about the things he wouldn’t have achieved if he took that step. All the things he would have missed. And whether it was worth it all. Whether it is worth all the memories he has to carry today. He reaches down into his sportsbag and pulls out a VHS tape. He rotates it in his hand and looks down on it. He turns it to see the label. Hell in a cell 98, L2J vs Tyrant vs Ace. L2J takes one long deep breath and closes his eyes. His mind is suddenly flooded with the memories. The steel, the pains of glass, the blood. The broken shrapnel, digging into his back, suplex after suplex. The feeling. The feeling that could only be bettered by not coming away with the title. The months of hospital treatment, the stitches, the casts, the bandages. L2J loosens his grip on the tape and places it on the rail in front of him. He reaches into his bag again and pulls out another tape. “L2J vs Shifty 2000”. A lesson learned for L2J. How things can never go as planned. How the titles was all but his but when it isn’t your time, the whole world can be against you. He remembers how the fight was in the bag. How he thought it was he time, he remembers the turnbuckle breaking, the feeling of the his feet falling away from him. And the months in hospital that followed. The memories. He places the tape on top of the precious one and reaches into the bag one last time. “Ten man triple cage match 2001”. The feeling of not being in control. The feeling of not being able to rule the game. The odds just being too against with 9 other factors to bring into the equation. The memory of being that close. The title was in his hands, he was four feet away…Four feet….Four feet. That meaningless distance that made all the difference. Four feet the was enough for the title to be pulled away from him. The memory of how he couldn’t hold on. The memory of how he let Cori Simmons slip through hid fingers. The memory of that night. He had come away from that match unscathed but it was when he felt the most pain. Being so close, being painfully close, the memories. Ever clearer because this was the feeling he had the last time he stood on this bridge. How close he came to his own bitter ending. Memories. He places the third tape on top of the previous two and puts both hands on the railings. He looks over the edge and remembers how close he came. But that is not when his story was meant to end. He was meant to come back here. He was meant have another chance. He slowly puts his hand on the tapes and helps them off the edge. He watches them fall. His memories fall away from him, and vanish from him. Memories. Memories are irrelevant when your fate has been chosen. He is going to fight to end his torment. To make the years of pain worth while. His story will end at faith. But is his destiny his own bitter ending.
