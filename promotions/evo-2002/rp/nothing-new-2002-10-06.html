Nothing New

6 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/6/02 There was, of course, nothing going on that was noteworthy in his life. Well, to him at least. Sure, there are those that would consider being a professional wrestler in one of the world’s most popular federations, being admired each and every week on television by millions of viewers spanning over every continent on the planet, minus one obvious one, any ordinary person would think that this was very notable position in life. But this wasn’t just another person. This was one of the most disturbed souls around, who had no real reason to care about anything or anyone. The restaurant Jason found himself in was just as trite and unnoticeable as the rest of them had been. There were the miserable red seats, made up of some material that tried really hard to be leather, but fell short by about a mile or so. The table that had been aging for the last twenty or some odd years, littered with scratches and a few swears written on it with pens and such. The windows, marked with a million fingerprints over their lifetimes, and tape marks that were for some useless poster that was probably burned as rubbish a long time ago. Green plants in every corner, but whether they were real, alive, or dead was anyone’s guess. And of course, the biggest characteristic of any restaurant – the people. Eating and conversing and swallowing and drinking and laughing and staring and ignoring and listening and talking. They were always the same. Always taking part in their same old routines. Always being their same old selves. There was nothing new with them. They were always the same people. The same losers. The same nothingness that dirties the world with filth and grime. A weird thought to think while you’re eating, Jason thought to himself as he took a bite of his sixteen ounce steak. A family, consisting of a father, mother, daughter and son, walked into the room, each of them wearing a nice suit or dress. It was three o’clock on a Sunday afternoon, Jason noticed. Some churches would be getting out right about now. All those nice Christians going to pay homage to their creator every Sunday, most of them doing so not really because they love and respect him, but so that they don’t spend an eternity in the other place. Jason momentarily thought what it would be like to be God, and then realized he would hate being worshipped every second of every day. What good what it give him? The son looked over to Jason, and smiled with an optimism that only a child could truly have. Jason mind thought up a thousand situations all at once, none of them really pleasing him in any way, shape, or form. The boy, about ten years old, started to walk towards Jason, disregarding the ordinary family that he lived with. Jason swallowed the mash potatoes he had been working on and placed the fork down next to the steak. He smiled, a bull shit smile if he ever had one. “Hey. Are you Jason Jameson?” As if you don’t already know that I am you little shit, Jason thought to himself as the boy smiled up at him. “Yes I am. Want an autograph?” Jason found the seriousness in his voice to be very surprising. “Oh that would be freaking awesome.” “Leonard!” his mom yelled at him. “Sorry mom, but this is Jason Jameson. He’s a wrestler, and he said he would give me an autograph.” “Leonard, he’s busy eating, and I’m sure he has somewhere to go.” Jason would have been happier if this mother just dragged her son away, but Jason decided that he would stop being the heartless asshole for a few seconds and give the kid and autograph. He pulled out the wrestling magazine he had been copy of, turned to the Evolution’s Digital Destruction advertisement page, took out a marker, and signed his signature right above his picture. “There you go. Have a good day… Leonard.” He said, and smiled. This time, he found, he meant it. This time, the smile was genuine. “Thanks a lot Jason.” The boy began to walk back to him family, but then stopped and turned around. “Hey Jason.” Jason looked up, the smile still stuck to his face. “Kick Eric’s ass man. He disserves to get his ass kicked.” “Leonard! Watch your mouth!” He laughed, rolled his eyes, and turned around to rejoin the family he had lived with all his life. Jason stared in his direction for a few seconds, before turning back to his meal. Jason was either the same old meal that he always ate, in a restaurant that was just like all the others that came before it. But this time, it was just that much better. And that gave Jason a weird sensation – hope.
