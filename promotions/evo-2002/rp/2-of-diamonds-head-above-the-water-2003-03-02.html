2 of Diamonds - Head Above The Water

2 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/2/03 The loss was over a week ago now. Just another miserable moment to remember everyday for the rest of his life. But, as the usual mood stated, none of that mattered anyway. The dreary hours past by one after the other, and nothing new ever came of them. The occasional event took place, giving Jason some hope for the resemblance of something that appeared to be a life, but in the end it was just more crap to add to the pile. Nothing new. Nothing new to add to my disgrace, he told himself in his weary mind. Flashbacks to a time that made sense were not uncommon with Jason, as he seemed to do it in his every waking moment. Not to mention every moment slumbering as well. Memories were all that he had now, and a sense of unwillingness to fight anymore as their shadow. Jason once thought that he was saved, and yet he was only being dragged along for a few more miles of hope before reaching the dead end. And a cliff hung over the edge, waiting for the fall of an angel. With Sacramento on the slice of the horizon, Jason drove slowly down the traffic-jammed highway. The brakes kicked in every few seconds, but for the most part it was a smooth drive in the warm afternoon. The stench of failure was high. After the hard fought match, and after near falls and high risk moves and everything that goes with it, Jason would end up being the one to fall. And at the hands of James of all people. The same man that took all the glory for Jason’s win a month earlier. The same man whose blood Jason wanted to splatter against the mat. If Jason could have one last chance, if he could have just kicked out one more time, if he could have gave him his own taste of Perfection just once more, then maybe he wouldn’t be sitting their in the car, his steering wheel collecting the sweat of his palms and his mind wandering away from the vehicle. Now he was on his way down the same old road, chasing pipe dreams down a drain. He thought that if he fell far enough, maybe he would drown and forget everything else that had happened to him in the past. Odds were severely against that ever coming true. Hope was slowly leaking away, and there was no rag to cut off the bleeding now. Legends was next, but Jason already knew that he wasn’t scheduled for a match. He really didn’t know why he was going, but he felt, deep down, that he didn’t have a choice anyway. Jason had to find something, anything, to hold on to. To carry him along the water, keeping his head above the water. And as Jason looked over to the deck of cards thrown all over the passenger seat and saw the two of diamonds face up, on top of the deck, he realized that he tasted a little salt.
