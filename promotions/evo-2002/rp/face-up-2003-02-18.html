Face Up

18 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/18/03 Watching as a cigarette’s empty smoke climbed into the rain, a man in a nice suit sat in his car, staring at the man leaning up against the bricked building behind him. With a heavy storm coming down, the detective watched as the man he had been tracking for a few months now awkwardly stood in the rain, showing no sign of disgust or anything. He was simply standing there, his trench coat acting like a drain pipe for the falling water, his hat creating a waterfall just above his eyes. The detective knew something was up. The time passed by. The thunder roared above. The sounds of rain splashing in the streets were the only sound, minus the occasional laughing couple walking on the sidewalks. Except for a few people walking by, the detective’s eyes never left the man in the trench coat. The hours ticked away, and the day was dying, but the scene was still the same. Raining hard. Sinister man. One cigarette after the other. One match brought to life only to be killed by the wind. And through all this, two glaring eyes watching. Waiting. It was around midnight when the man in the trench coat was joined by another, shorter man. He was soaked to the bone, and was shivering with his hands in his pockets. A conversation was born, streams of exhaling breath dancing in the air. The short man handed trench a few bills, and the man in the trench coat pulled out a gun. The detective knew better. One would think this was to kill the short man. But not the detective. The short man was actually an eighteen year old teenager, who was buying this gun off this man. For months he had been following his deals, and now it was time to take him down. With a subway flying through above, the detective tossed open his car, and walked out into the rain. He quickly ran over to the meeting. “Hold it right there jack!” The boy, gun in hand, ran down the alley. The detective was able to grab the trench coat, and tossed the handcuffs on him quickly. “Shit, officer, I didn’t do anything. Honest. That kid was trying to sell me…” “Oh, shut up. You’re going away for a long…” BANG The sound of a gunshot rang from the alley. All the people around screamed, and the detective hand cuffed the dealer to a nearby rail. He then ran into the alley, gun in hand, searching for the source. The boy lay on the ground, crimson rain flowing down the side of the buildings and into the street. A bullet hole in the temple was the source of this river. The detective looked down at the boy, a sense of shame in his heart. Innocence lay dead before his very eyes, and there was nothing he could do about it. All he could do was call the ambulance with a body bag, take in the gun for evidence and… “What the fuck?” The detective picked up a blood stained card that was face up in the rain – the king of spades with the word “Jay” written over his face. The detective ripped the card up, and tossed it into the garbage. It had been one shitty night.
