TooLate - Bygone Present

17 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/17/03 'Jason. It’s good to see you again, man.” Francis stood up, and for the second time in their knowing each other, he gave Jason a hug. The last time, Jason didn’t really want to hug back, but this time, he had no problem once so ever. The two, in an awkward place for a hugging between two men, stood there for a second, not caring about any of the prying eyes around them. “It’s good to see too, Francis.” Francis let go, and showed Jason his seat, a smile on his face from meeting his old friend once again. “Listen, Francis, I’m really sorry about the last time that… that we talked.” “Jason, don’t bring it up. It’s in the past and I’ve forgotten all about it.” “I just wanted you to know I’m sorry. I didn’t mean to…” “Forget it, man. Just forget it.” Jason nodded, still quite embarrassed about what occurred that day. “So how are you? What have you been up to?” “You know, the same as before. Still wrestling. Don’t really know why anymore, but I still am.” “Yeah I know. Did you know that Marilee went to the last pay per view event that Evolution held? The Nightmare?” “Before Christmas, yeah I know. She gave me a letter.” “She did?” “She wrote it while she was sitting in the audience. And I tell you Frankie, if I had known that she was there watching me, Shodan wouldn’t have stood a chance. I wouldn’t have given him a chance.” “Why do you still care about these titles, Jason?” “To be honest, I don’t know anymore. Don’t think I ever did. I just went after them like a fly to garbage. And got nothing from them but more pain.” “Come back, Jason. Leave the fed and come back to Boston.” “I can’t. Not yet, Frankie. I still have some things to take care of.” “Like what?” “You wouldn’t understand. You don’t see the things…” “Sovereign.” Jason looked up, surprised, before remembering that Legends was broadcasted on world wide television. “You just wouldn’t understand Francis. I can’t sit back and watch him take lives into his own hands like that.” “Jason, listen to me. This world is going to kill you Jason.” “No it’s not. It’s just…” “Yes it is, Jason. Look what it’s already done to you. Look what it’s done to others. Just like that guy. L2J.” Jason didn’t even flinch at the mention of his name, because he knew that it was true. This business had a way of doing that to you. And Jason could feel it ripping him apart slowly. “If you were here, Jason, maybe Marilee wouldn’t…” Francis suddenly stopped talking, as he caught the attention of Jason. Francis looked away from Jason, and took a sip from his beer. The tension in Jason was too much to be ignored, as Francis realized the mistake that he had just made. “What? What happened, Francis?” “Jason, you aren’t here anymore. You left…” “What are you talking about?!” Francis frowned, and looked back up into Jason’s eyes. “His name is Bill.” “You mean William?” “Yeah, but we all call him…” Jason got up, walked out of the bar, and into the snow. Francis’s voice rang in the background, but it wasn’t important anymore. Nothing was. Was it too late, Jason? As of right now, it was.
