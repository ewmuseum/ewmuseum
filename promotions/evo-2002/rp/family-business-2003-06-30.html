Family Business

30 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-06-30 The dressing room at the Twin Peeks Parlor was filled with women in all states of undress. In the background, dance music could be heard, as the women walk to and fro getting their wardrobes together and getting ready for their gigs. In the back corner of the room, there sits a dark haired woman with her back turned. She seems to be applying makeup. As the camera closes in, it is seen that she is touching up her white mime makeup while smoking a cigar. She puts the makeup down and begins counting the money from her last dance. These guys are so stupid, she thought. They come into the bar, throw their money away on girls that aren't going to put out for them, then go home broke and drunk to their wives and children. It sickened Dilyah to her stomach, while at the same time, making her smile. This was the life. All you have to do is take your clothes off, do a provocative dance, mingle with the poor schmucks for a couple of hours, add a couple of lap dances, and come away with several hundred dollars. She took a deep drag off of the cigar, and then put the money in her lock box, closing it and spinning the numbers. She liked working with these other women, but she didn't trust them as far as she could throw them. Just then, a man poked his head behind the curtain and looked around. Seeing Dilyah, he smiled. 'Dilyah,' he began, 'you're next up.' Dilyah smiled and put out the cigar, looking in the mirror make sure her makeup was perfect. It was her trademark; white makeup with red lipstick and dark eyeliner. She had on a blood red negligee, with string bikini panties underneath. Covering it all was her long, black trench coat. She stepped out as her music began playing and began doing her exotic dance for the men around. She glanced around as she danced to get a feel for the crowd. They were hungry, all of them. Well, all accept this one, over in the corner, staring into his beer with an angry look. Uh-oh, she thought, this might not turn out to be such a good night after all. She finished her dance, causing the guys in the front row to almost jizz on themselves as they fought to put money in her panties. She teased them a little bit just before her song ended, then she put her clothes back on and stepped out to mingle and work her time on the floor. A few lap dances later, she finally found herself face to face with the frowning figure in the corner. She sighed and sat down at the seat opposite him. 'Having fun,' she asked, giving him a winning smile. 'No,' he said, not looking up at her as he took another swig of his beer. Dilyah rolled her eyes. 'Come on,' she began, 'what's your problem? You didn't have to come here if you didn't want to see me working.' 'I came to see if the rumors were true,' he said, finally looking up at her. He had soft brown eyes, a dark complexion, and black hair, worn shoulder length. 'What rumors,' she asked, sitting down across from him. “Why aren’t you home with your wife?” 'She’s out of town,' he said. 'Family business.' 'Ah, so you decided to hit the titty bars while she was away,' she said sarcastically. 'I wouldn't have to hit the titty bars if the only person left in this world that I give a damn about outside my wife wasn't here,' he said. 'And why am I hearing that you’ve enrolled in a professional wrestling school? If you aren’t already screwed up enough, lets throw professional wrestling in as well. Damn, Deloris, you gotta slow down.” Deloris was Dilyah’s real name. The one this man would probably continue to call her for the rest of her life. 'Its my style, you know that,' she replied. 'Your style,' he said, staring coldly at her. “Dee, I can’t see you getting beat up by some guys for the rest of your life. Is that what you really want? I mean, these aren’t very nice guys.” 'I'm not exactly a good girl either, now am I.' She returned the cold stare for a moment, then softened. 'Look. I joined the professional wrestling school so I could make something of myself. I don’t want to dance my life away, and I’m not gonna go star in some porn flicks to make a quick buck, so I decided on wrestling. If you can't deal with that, tough.' He smiles into his beer. 'Oh, I can deal with it, Deloris. After all, its not me that’s going to be thrown around that ring every night. You wouldn’t catch me doing that.' 'Oh, and I should be like you, a drunk bastard hanging out in titty bars,' she asked, icily. He shot her a hurt look, then stood up, throwing the beer bottle against the wall and pointing at her. 'I was here to make a little peace, but I see you're not into that. Good-bye Dilyah, or whatever the hell they're calling you these days.' He turned and stormed off. “Derek,” she got up and stormed after him, whipping him around. “Listen, I’m trying to make something of myself. I don’t have the luxury of a college education. Mom and Dad died before they could send me to college, and since that bastard of an uncle of ours locked down his fortune in court battles, its not like we’re gonna see any of that money any time soon. I have to do what I have to do.” Derek looked down, then back up. “Listen, Uncle Jake is a son of a bitch, yes, but he’s gonna lose. He’s not entitled to any of that money and he knows it. Grampa cut him out of the will and put a statement in it that he was to receive none of his estate.” “Oh, yeah, but now that its our father’s estate, he thinks he can cut us out too,” said Dilyah. “Stupid bastard. I wish he would die!” Derek smiled ruefully. “Don’t make wishes like that. They may come true.” Dilyah blinked at Derek. “Derek, what are you up to,” she asked, suspiciously. “Family business,” he said. “We’ll leave it at that. Listen, I have to go now. No matter what happens, believe this. I have assured us our birthright. If you must be a professional wrestler, then go ahead. I’ll be watching you, sis.” He turned and exited the bar. Dilyah watched him go, shaking her head. She didn’t know what he was up to, but she was sure her uncle was in for a big surprise. She shrugged and headed to the back to count her money. It had been a good night for the most part. Still, perhaps she could do something to smooth things over with Derek. After all, a girl couldn’t choose her family members; they were sort of chosen for her.
