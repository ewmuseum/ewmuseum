Intermission, pt.4

4 June 2003

Date: 2003-06-04 The house show is over. The fans that came to see the circus that eVo put on to promote the upcoming legends have filtered out of the arena, spilling into the night like the parasites in search of a new host. They had sucked all of the energy, entertainment and light out of the small arena that they could. Car engines grumbled to life as a doting mothers closed passenger side doors with a light “thump”, fathers rubbed soft hands through eyes that felt hot and tired while children clamored for more eVo action. A bald black man sat in a sketchy locker room also ran his calloused hands across his eyes that felt like they were full of sand. His seamed and worn face was lit by the dim halogen light bulb that cast a pale light in the locker room. Khadafi grunts softly as he slings his thick duffle bag over his shoulder, plugging a smoldering Camel into his maw before shoving the rusty door open. He winced as the cool Canadian air cut through his thin black Mettalica shirt; the holes in the fabric letting the icy fingers clutch at his flesh moments before the eVo fighter turned into the alley next to the arena and walked towards the bus stop. The smoke from his ciggie drifted into the clear night, drawing his attention away from his black eye. Ali laced his hands behind his head, stretched out his legs, and stared up at the stars which were painted across the black canvass of the night. Calm descended him. He let his aches slip away like the smoke from his camel as it haloed his head with blue-gray color. His mind drifted to Debbie and how she had reacted after Khadafi had unceremoniously knocked her ex-boyfriend out in the parking lot of their St. Paul apartment. She had hemmed and hawed about how Alex probably wouldn’t want to be her friend anymore. Khadafi had turned on his heel, jumped in his car and left. Now he was in Canada and ignoring her phone calls. He had gone so far as to throw his cell out the window of his car when he was in the throws of his despondency. Khadafi knew he would call her back, he always did, but he just wasn’t ready to put up with her bleating. Not yet. Not when he had to fight the amazing Malcolm X wanna-be. Ali shrugged off thinking about his next fight as he exhaled smoke, shooting a lazy “o” from his mouth with practiced ease. He saw the bus coming to take him back to the hotel eVo had put him and several other company employees’ up in.
