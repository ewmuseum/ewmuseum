Average Thoughts

22 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/22/02 Does it matter to you? Did it ever matter at all? He closed his eyes, and could only see what his mind told him to see. Most of the time, when he took part in this little action, he would be thinking of the family that he left behind, or the fiancée that he never married, or the choice that he made and had stuck by for over three years and counting. That was what he usually thought about when he closed his eyes and let his head tell him tales of the past. But now, the only thing that Jason could truly think about was Eric Badger, the man that had cost him what could have been his first Evolution championship, and gave him his first loss in the federation. That’s what he thought of, as he stood there, his elbows resting on the guardrail that surrounded the rooftop of his hotel. When he opened his eyes, the city of Miami was there to greet his return to the real world. It was a rather famous city, mostly for its beauty, television shows, babes, drugs, crimes, and basically anything else you could think of when it came to your average major American city. The coast lined up along the left side of Jason’s view, with the movement of the waves catching his attention momentarily. The sun was setting over the sea, and it gave the city a rather striking visual effect that would look really nice hung up on your average American wall. Jason’s mind wandered out of its normal thought period for a second and thought about what it would be like to live a normal life. Not to be famous. Not to be poor. But just to be average. Would he rather have that then this? Would he rather be on the bottom of society’s chain then be there? Would he rather live a life of misery then be the typical person who eats, shits, works, and sleeps to their own beat? Maybe then, he wouldn’t be thinking about men like Eric Badger. Even when he tried to think of something else, the badness always returned forefront in a matter of seconds. That’s a ritual that he found very annoying, especially when trying to forget something that no average human begin could ever forget. The sun’s last wink of light splashed out over the world before it went to sleep for what could have been the trillionth time. Jason’s eyes glared down at all the city lights as they slowly flicked on, one by one, in a pattern set forth by the average world. Jason stood their, staring down for what could have been mere seconds or many hours, until no other lights went on. When this happened, Jason turned around, and headed back down the stairway, so that he could bathe in his misery in the comfort of the average American hotel room. His feet sung some random tune that meant nothing to any ear that would have the opportunity to hear it. His eyes blinked every few seconds. His hair moved ever so slightly to the small breezes of air that fluffed through it. His muscles contracted and relaxed as they carried his body towards his destination. The average human body, Jason thought to himself, sort of getting pissed off at his trite sense of thought. From there on, for the next few moments, the only thing that was relevant to him was the words whispered into his ear by his own mind. Average this. Average that. Is that all that ever mattered to you, Jason? You never wanted to be your average American boy. You never wished that you could have the average American life. You didn’t want to own the average American home, or have the average American bank account, or drive the average American car. You always wanted to be so much more then that. You wanted to be remembered for being something great, no matter how you reach that status or how far you had to crawl to get there. It wouldn’t have mattered to you if you cured the world’s hunger or if you annihilated the human race off the face of the Earth. All you wanted was for “them” to remember your name, and respect that name for the rest of time. But how would you ever see any of that if you were average, Jason? How could you ever hope for any form of grandeur if you were average? You couldn’t Jason. You never could. By this time, Jason was lying in his bed, looking up at the ceiling, lost in his mind. That’s why you’re going to prove to Eric that you aren’t some average man. You will show him that you are better then he could ever be. That you are better then Somers. That you are the best that this world has ever seen. You are not average Jason. And soon, they will all know that. Jason laughed a small, almost indistinguishable giggle, and then closed his eyes again. Normally, when Jason did this, he would think about the past that he left behind, or the choice that he made over three years ago, or the average life that he would never have. He sometimes thought of men that tried to hurt him, like Brad Kelley, or Daniel Somers, or Eric Badger, like he had been thinking about a few minutes ago. But now, he was just excited to not be average. And even more excited in knowing that Eric Badger would be the first to find this out.
