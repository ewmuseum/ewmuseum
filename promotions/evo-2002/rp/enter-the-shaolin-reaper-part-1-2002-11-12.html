enter the shaolin reaper part 1

12 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12th November 2002 He looked, checked, once, twice, and thrice. There was no one in a 100-metre radius let alone the close vicinity of which Kai stood. In front, a derelict and dilapidated building, which smelt like fermented urine. Kai tilted his head backwards towards the sky, feeling the tepid drops of rain filter from his head, down the contours of his face and into his loosely fitting shirt. He had realised that since leaving the monastery in China, he had become mentally weak and physically less sharp. He was warned that the comforts of the American way of life would make him soft. No wonder he had been taking more than his share of hits in the ring, against lesser opponents. Why was this occurring? A bed, television, fast food, comfort and America had made the Shaolin warrior weak. Kai looked towards the warehouse and smiled, for he knew that all that was about to change. He had sold his apartment yesterday, for one hundred dollars. These dollars would provide him with the essential things to live from. He carried with him a small black single strap bag. Its contents were simple; change of joggers, change of shirt, bread, Shaolin cling shoes and a set of fighting gloves, hand woven to fit his exact hand size. He found a small entrance, through a dismantled window frame, and ascended the thin wall, to climb through the space and jump down into the building. He looked around and raised an eyebrow. “Home…its got space.” Although Kai spoke softly, a feint echo could be heard throughout the warehouse. Kai began to get settled. When he had finished his chores, he sat cross-legged on the cold, damp floor in front of a large assortment of Shaolin artefacts. Rocks of odd mountainous shapes thrust up at random in a sea of carefully raked sand. His eyes moved from one prominence to another, and then tried to take in the whole. There was order, yet there was mystery. Was that what he was supposed to see? Like Shaolin-, the warrior code- like giri- the principle of duty and obedience- the pictures seemed to jump out randomly, they demanded contemplation without the promise of understanding. Kai could see there was beauty in the principles of rediscovery. Something he had forgotten since leaving the temple. He had chosen badly in associating with Matsushima. Kai was unaware that his activities of meditation and prayer were drawing attention from the other inhabitants of the warehouse. A slightly built man stares, his white eyes dilated, he stands in the shadows looking on in terror. The man brushes a small dreadlock from off of his face, looking, staring, studying, but trying not to insinuate anything that will give his position away. He rose and went to an area where the amount of glass on the floor was minimal. He began his loosening exercises, first for suppleness, then for speed and guile, and then for devastating strength. When Kai walked into the middle of his mental work area, he stood in the middle of the room, feet spread to shoulder width apart. He looked towards the small picture of the Buddha he had hung near his bed space. Images of Strictly Prohibited raced through his mind, blurring his concentration with paradoxes of what might have been if he had not interfered. The thoughts were disbanded like fog clearing: when a crow flew from the rafters making a dart at Kai. He stayed focused…keeping his hands at his sides; he bent forward from the waist until the top of his head touched the floor. He maintained the position for a moment. When he saw the watching eyes he stayed focus, he straightened, bowed to the small shrine, and backed to the wall. Kai squatted slowly, and then rose again, twenty times, carefully controlling his breath, exhaling sharply when he came upright. With the exercise, his mind calmed. Having properly stretched his thigh muscles, he did a split on the floor and touched his head to each knee fifty times each. With every one, the speed increased, till Kai’s movements almost blurred into a flourish of activity. This continued for a further half hour. After this period of loosening and bending, he whirled into a frenzy of activity, spinning around the gritty and jagged floor, striking out with his four limbs, in series and in tandem, in unison and alone. The man continued to watch from his covered position, awed by the knowledge that at the termination of each blow was the power of death over life. It was with this impact that Kai was going to rebuild his life- destroying, then rebuilding on new foundations. It was hard to believe that little more than a year had passed. It had been the Christmas week; but for Kai, a strict Buddhist, it was a time of reflection and contemplation. For he knew that in two days, he was going to leave the monastery that had harboured him for twenty-one years. Kai’s workout was ending, as he knew he could not sustain the level of concentration required for Taijiquan. He stood, mulling over thoughts as the lactic acid in his quadriceps led to a painful cramping sensation. He sunk to his haunches, and then sprung up to a perfect 540º Crescent Kick. Kai walked to his bundle of possessions and sat down on a sprawled rug. The lights flickered on the block that runs east to Fifth Avenue, this was known as the diamond centre of New York, and it was closing time. Carlos Estevez watched disinterestedly as Hasidic Jews in their beaver hats and frock coats streamed from stores that lined the street. He wanted no truck with them. When they were gone, the army of guards from the private detective agencies that were hired to watch over the block would be gone with them. Carlos was interested in a straggling customer, or perhaps someone headed towards the department stores on Fifth Avenue, which in the spirit of Jack Frost would be open for business until ten p.m. As the lights winked out, Carlos noticed a lone figure walking west on the uptown side of the street. He was heading to the industrial area. There was a delivery bay half a dozen doors in from Sixth Avenue. The man would pass right by it. Carlos and his friends slipped into the doorway. He was a little guy. It would be quick. They would pull him in, take his watch and his cash, smack him upside his head, and take off for the waiting car. Kai Kua was preoccupied with thoughts of how he was going to get by that month, and how he would obtain food. He had had an unpleasant day with the inhabitants of the warehouse, who had interrupted a series of religious meditations Kai was staging, in honour of the Buddha. In addition, at the insistence of his company he was to observe the geigin laws. Although geigin is a word of Japanese origin, Kai used to it to refer to all people who were non-Asian. Kai was first alerted by a shadow on the pavement: then a flicker of movement. Kai shifted the weight to the balls of his feet and cleared his mind, all instinct and reaction. Two of Carlos’s companions were to grab Kai by the shoulders and pull him forward into the doorway. Then Carlos would hit him in the face with a gloved hand, and he would be frisked and shaken down. It would be over in thirty seconds. Then a swift kick to the groin to keep him quiet, and they would disappear. Kai was clothed in a Lycra Nike training top and baggy orange pants made from cotton. His feet were covered with tight fitting rubber Nike plimsolls, his face looked seemingly relaxed. When the two sets of hands appeared from the darkness, Kai Kua was expecting them. He dropped his sandwich, and dancing away from one pair of arms, grasped the other, and using the owner’s weight and leverage snapped them like matchsticks at the elbow. The hulking teenager fell to the ground, choking on his own vomit unable to scream. Kai pulled the second assailant towards him and delivered two kicks, smashing his kneecaps. A third who was in the doorway ran, his coat flapping behind him, slipping and sliding on the wet pavement in his anxiety to escape. Carlos Estevez stood his ground above his crippled companions. “Run.” Kai barked with contempt, he was pissed off. “Run with the other dog.” “Fuck you. I ain’t no dog.” Kai kicked a prostrate boy, who gagged and groaned. “You will finish like him.” “Fuck you. Whny’t you call the cops?” ‘I need no police.’ Kai’s hand flicked out so quickly that Carlos would not have known he was struck, save for the pain. “You are common geigin. Do you have a gun or a knife? Why do you not use it on me?” he struck his face again, splitting his lower lip. Carlos spit on the ground – a tooth, some blood. “Why don’t you break my neck?” “Are you afraid?” “No.” “You know I can kill you, but you are not afraid.” “What do I have to lose?” Carlos stood there for a moment, he wondered, what went on in Kai Kua’s mind during this half minute silence that elapsed in a dark doorway. Kai looked up, “If you have the courage to die, then you have the courage to live. Leave this place and this trash.” He kicked the boy with the broken legs under the chin with the point of his feet to still his whimpering. “If you ever wise to begin to climb out of the gutter, then you must leave these geigin and start to do things your own way.” Carlos stood there, with a void and blank expression. Kai picked up his sandwich on the floor, broke the part that had made contact with the floor off, and handed the clean piece of bread to Carlos. “Our paths will cross again.” Kai adjusted the waistband on his pants and continued to walk towards his humble abode. Today he had taken another step forward to fulfilling his covenant with the monastery he left, to fulfil living a monastic style life. He had offered advice to a child in need, and had worked out harder then he had on previous occasions. He did not know it now, but he would meet Carlos later…
