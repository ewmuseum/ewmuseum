Date: 2003-07-29 He sat all morning on the bright red, cushioned family seat in the third row of the Cruise’s Café. He sat there not as Terlayne Sterling the fighter but Terlayne Sterling the person. He fiddled with his white, empty coffee cup, swerving the remaining powder-black drops around. His departure from eVolution was an untimely one which few understand. It all dated back to a House Show about a month ago when he began to feel a strain in his right calf muscle, this was the most probable cause of his loss, and the reason he walked out on the eVolution. He’d visited a doctor (well, more an old friend than a doctor. Ronald Rumstead, Ron to his friends had been their family doctor for years and a close friend of his fathers.) Who would assure him that it was just a strain. Ron was a tall man, standing at roughly 6’2’’. He’d been a regular at his local bar, ‘The Two Cents’. For anybody in the wrestling business, a visit to St. Michael’s Medical Facility could mean two things, time off temporarily, or a premature end to your career. Sometimes you would be forced to take time off over something you never expected, how about a dislocated wrist you’d had since you were six years old? The misty morning after the House Show went off the air he drove forty miles to St. Michael’s in the old El Derado which was given to him on his father’s death. He’d treated the car as a member of the family and even inserted a leather interior. Though he rarely drove it, and kept it in the dusty garage he still loved every inch of that shiny white exterior. At nine forty five he pulled up to the cobbled pathway that stood in front of the four hundred square foot red brick building. Ron’s office had been the top of five flights of stairs which had been reasonably troublesome considering his calf strain. Ron’s office seemed purposely decorated to blend in with his mature, joyful persona. It reminded Terlayne more of a psychiatrist’s office rather than that of a doctor. His shelves were covered in an assortment of leather-covered books, obviously sophisticated ones which had been a rarity in any doctor’s office. He’d sat on the green, leather sofa on many occasions, not under these circumstances however. Ron stroked his beard self-consciously, holding the charts in his left hand clasped between his index finger and thumb. His face remained expressionless giving nothing away. Eventually, somewhere lost deep in his beard his lips opened. ‘Well Terlayne, looks like I can give you the go ahead,’ his deep voice uttered, a sigh of relief escaped from Terlayne’s coffee-dampened lips, filling the room with a silent gasp. ‘But, eh, you might want to consider your priorities, looks like you’re gonna be out of action for ooh, say a month?’ A month out of action would be a month without wrestling, a month without wrestling would be a month without life, and he suddenly felt the hand of God deliver a tremendous blow to his midsection. Making it impossible for Ron to read him, a grin slowly appeared on his rugged face. ‘A month?’ He muttered. ‘A month sounds... Good.’ Ron looked at him, his deep blue eyes burning a hole in his head. ‘Now you won’t go and do anything silly will you? You really should be relieved, a month’s nothing in your business. With so many people coming and going they won’t even notice you gone.’ A.) His biggest worry, those not noticing him gone would lead to him falling down a spiral. ‘Well I guess I should be relieved then Ron?’ He asked, his grin disappearing and being replaced by a nervous look. ‘Look, thanks for your help Ron,’ he finished, rising slowly from the leather sofa. Ron reached over to him with his big arms and hugged him as tightly as he could possibly muster. ‘You take care of yourself Terlayne, I don’t want anything happening to you,’ he could hear his compassionate voice lost in his large chest, underneath his arms. He embraced Ron with a final hand shake and exited through the double, wooden doors. That month without wrestling would have saved his career
