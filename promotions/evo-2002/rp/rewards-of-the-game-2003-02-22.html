Rewards of the Game

22 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/22/02 Too late? The time slips away, but the spirit still lives within me. All I have to do is embrace it. But who watches with me? A wandering mind, Jason looked out over the city of Anaheim, dreams that he once held only shadows of what they once were. All the times that he did this and every vision that came to mind when he did was now only remembrances of a life he lost with the shots of a few bullets and the tears of a loved one. Now he was lost, with the number of choices slowly decreasing. Another event was just around the corner, the distant arena only a small signal of the battle that was to come. To tell the truth, he didn’t really care about the match. Not one bit. Win or loss, it didn’t matter anymore. The rewards of the game had changed, and none of them appealed to Jason. All he wanted was admiration. Respect. Love. Beating Sovereign and Kimson within an inch of their lives wouldn’t give him that. And that was all he desired from life. Too late? Maybe, just maybe, I still possess my chance. Lost somewhere in my essence. Waiting for me to find it. Jason walked over to the door, and tossed it open. Staring down at the lush red carpet and the architecture that must have taken years to design, he searched for any sign of relief from all his inner suffering. Nothing emerged. He could remember when he used to lose hope, and that nothing was important to him. Then he met a man that gave him back everything he had lost. Jason was once again happy, and felt like he had something to hold on to. Everything was his again. And then Jay reminded him just how useless he was again. Too late? The light has been drowned in blood. The sorrow is surging through me. And again I feel nothing. And memories came flashing back to Jason. He could remember all the little things in his world, and his soul cried out to hold them once again. But hope was flowing away, and now there was nothing to live for. If Jason could find one thing… Just one thing… I highly doubt you will, Jason. Jay, speaking like a true prophet. Now all Jason had left was his sorrow, and a deck of cards. Too late? Yes and no But tomorrow’s sorrow Is yesterday’s memories Maybe if I try And escape fate My hope will return Not too likely, Jason. Just give up.
