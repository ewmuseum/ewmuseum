His passion.

31 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-08-31 The clothesline took Damon O'Halloran down, flat on his back. Before he knew it, he was being pinned by the one person he could never defeat. One… two… “… Three!” Sarah O'Halloran cheered. The eleven-year-old jumped into the air, quite pleased with herself. “I beat you again!” Damon sat up and feigned disappointment, conceding his defeat. “You sure did pumpkin. You sure did. Now go get ready for supper.” Sarah rushed out of the living room, running past her aunt. “I beat him again Auntie Mar!” Mary O'Halloran smiled as he niece disappeared into the bathroom. “Good for you Sarah. You showed him who the boss is.” She poked her head into the living room and saw Damon sitting on the carpet. “She got the better of you again, old man?” she asked with a wry grin. “Bite me you hag,” O'Halloran said as he got to his feet, a hint of a smile upturning the corner of his mouth. “Girl’s got skills.” “Yeah, with her patented Wrap-Daddy-Around Her-Finger finisher, you don’t have a chance.” The spread on the table was impressive, a grand feast. For Damon, it was more of a Last Supper. The food looked great and smelled even better, but he knew he probably wouldn’t eat much. He never did when it was time to go back to the nomadic lifestyle of the professional fighter. He’d be leaving his daughter behind again, and that was always hard for the Coleraine native. But it was something they both knew he had to do. Understanding it was one thing. Accepting it was another. “Penny for your thoughts Damon. You look troubled.” Mary looked at her brother with an understanding gaze. She knew it was hard for him, for all of them. She also knew Damon would never openly admit it. The man had a tendency to believe his own press clippings and always put on his 24/7 Badass face. “I think your gimpy eye is acting up again. Maybe you need your drops or something,” muttered O'Halloran as he dumped a spoonful of mashed potatoes onto his plate. Denial via assholism. Textbook Damon. “She’ll be okay Damon. You just get back on the road and take care of business. We’ll take care of things here.” Damon began sculpting his potatoes with the end of his fork. “Are you planning on watching me this Sunday? I’m going after Terlayne and Sean.” “I’m gonna watch!” Sarah said in no uncertain terms as she plunked herself down in her chair. “I can watch, can’t I Auntie Mar?” “We’ll both be watching,” Mary said, speaking to both Sarah and Damon. Both seemed satisfied with the response. “Are you gonna win the match, daddy?” Sarah asked her father. “Probably,” Damon said as he dropped another scoop of potatoes onto his plate. “Not a whole lot of competition for me there. Besides, who’d be a better choice for Mr. Griffin then me as champion?” “Jenny’s dad says that wrestling’s fake.” Sarah looked concerned. “I said he was nuts, ‘cause of all those bruises and scars you got in the ring.” “Baby, you tell Jenny’s dad that if he thinks what I do for a living is fake, I’ll come over to his house and show him some of my fake moves. If he’s not crippled and still breathing by the time I’m finished, he can call it fake all he wants.” Damon never knew how to answer that question when people asked. He had his skull cracked open during a match, and that wasn’t fake. He’d crippled people in the ring before, and none of that was fake. Sometimes during a match, things could get very real at a moment’s notice. Damon O'Halloran was an athletic performer, and that was real. That’s what mattered. Besides, Sarah liked watching Damon wrestle. Real or Fake wasn’t an issue with her. Fighting for his biggest fan was always number one priority for Damon O'Halloran. He’d never slack off because he’d never let his daughter down. Never. Mary put an end to the conversation. “Enough business talk. Let’s enjoy our meal so you two can wrestle some more before we have to go to the airport.” “Best out of three,” Sarah goaded her father. “I’ll give you a chance.” For the first time since dinner started, Damon managed to smile. Mary watched the elder and junior O'Halloran eat their meals. Both held their forks the same way, both cut their meat in the same fashion. They had a bond that, no matter how many miles were between them, would never be broken. People often asked Mary why Damon acted almost the same in and out of the ring. He always seemed to be rude, crude, loud, and arrogant. He thought he really was better than everyone else. “Because he’s an 'Act of God',” Mary would say to dismiss the topic. But how could she explain that Damon felt the way he did because he had something that nobody else could ever have. He felt he was the richest, most powerful man in the world because he had a commodity that was more valuable that money, gold, or diamonds. It was even more precious than his very life. He had Sarah’s love. The love of his daughter made him feel unstoppable. She was his world, which made everyone else come in a far distant second. But those kinds of things don’t sell t-shirts. “I Heart My Little Pumpkin” on the front of a T would probably get Damon drummed out of the business. So she let people believe what they wanted to. What people believed, thought, or said didn’t matter to Damon O'Halloran. No person, no book, nothing would tell him how to behave. For Damon, there was only one Divine Being he had to answer to. And right after supper, he was going to lose a best of three series to her. Again.
