Taste

12 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/12/03 I’ll tell you, being alive is a shit load better then being trapped in that hellhole Jason called a mind. Everyday, all I could hear was him talking to himself, his crackly voice echoing over and over again like the resonance of a gong. If I were real then, I’d have a never-ending headache. But now, in this world, I had escaped from all of that. This was the real world. No longer a prison for holding me, pretending that the guard really had control over anything. Now I was free, shackles completely broken off and real air for me to breathe. And look at my wrists. They don’t have a scar on them. But whatever. Now wasn’t the time to get all caught up in the past. I had a life to live now. Walking the streets of Little Rock, I decided, was like watching a live version of “Deliverance”. All around me, men walked by with snake boots and denim overalls and scars on their faces. Sure, this was the kind of stuff you would see in the wrestling world anyway, but never did I really expect there to be any real people like this. Redneck, I thought, was just a stupid insult. Now I know better. And then I saw a sight for sore eyes, one that I had been waiting for ever since I stepped out of my shell and into the limelight of the world – a bar. Yeah yeah, you’ve been drinking since you were old enough to realize that alcohol was such a fabulous drink, but I haven’t had that kind of luck just yet. Sure, I had that one drink a few weeks ago, but that’s not important right now. “Sam’s Pub. Sounds like a nice place.” It better fucking be a nice place is more like it. I opened the door, stepping inside to see some more white trash sitting around, acting as if their lives are as unimportant as the stereotype says. I walked over to the counter, and took a seat, torn and used. Caught a whiff of something that my stomach didn’t appreciate. Thank god it went away before I hurled all over the bar and ruined any chance of getting myself a glass of grandpa’s cough medicine. “What the hell do ya want?” asked some ugly, fat ass standing behind the counter, his left eye slightly twitching and a dusty beard covering his face. Miserable excuse of a man if there ever was one. I’m sure Jason would agree with me. “Shot of whiskey.” He gave me a dirty look, and pulled out a shot glass, slamming it down on the table like a mallet. Pulling out a bottle, he stared up at me, and poured into the glass, splashing a little around my glass. I picked it up. “Thanks.” Asshole. I should kill you. Now let me tell you. I was with Jason for a few years, and in that time I had seen him do some pretty stupid things. I mean this guy could really be clumsy at times, like the time he fell off the apron during his entrance. Thank god it was a house show or else he’d have never lived it down. But, God damn, I was about to show my true humanity. And do something really, really brainless. Well, consider the fact that up until a few weeks ago I was only a figment of a man’s imagination, and his other personality trapped inside of his mind, you can understand that I’m not really used to being alive, so I have never really tasted too much of anything except a few hamburgers from McDonald’s, which sucks, and hot dogs from 7-11’s, which I’m don’t even think is legal, so I haven’t really been into a bar, and after this I’m not sure I’ll be returning, so I have never tasted any alcohol like whiskey. So when I took that first sip… HOLY SHIT! Immediately, with absolutely no hesitation at all, I spat up all that whiskey, all over the floor, counter, and stool next to me. A dozen or so faces turned to see me, and a dozen or so laughing voices started up. I looked up at the bartender, who joined in the laughter, mocking me. Any other time I’d kick his ass. But I suppose that if I were in their shoes, I’d have done the same. “Can’t handle your whiskey you little girl?” asked some asshole behind me. I looked over at him, and gave him one hell of a look. His laughter stopped, as he knew I meant business. The rest of the laughing, however, didn’t stop. I took the glass that I put down on the counter and tossed it to the floor, shattering it into pieces that went flying all over the place. The laughter died, and yelling started instead. “What the fuck are you doing? Who the fuck do you think you are, comin in here and…” “Shut you’re fucking mouth!” I yelled at the bartender. “Here’s a twenty. Keep it. You touch me or laugh at me again, and I’ll kill you.” I dropped the bill, and walked out of the bar, pushing the door as hard as I could. And then I smiled. Life was pretty good after all.
