Awakening - Sympathy For The Devil

6 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-06-06 I didn’t get to kill him, now did I? Is that so much to ask for? Death. A little, meaningless death. Shit, people are dying all around us every second of everyday. It’s not like watching someone get killed on television is something new. Pick up the remote sometime and jerk around with those numbers. You’re bound to stumble upon one act of pure violence or another. I can still smell the stench of his blood on my hands. Mixed all together with the pieces of steel from the chain and the sweat flowing from my pores as I trampled him for what he thought I’d let him get away with. Remembering back on days like this, I see Jason’s face through the haze of memories. He was a small, weak little man, if I can recall. Didn’t do much of anything before I showed up on his doorstep one evening. But after that day, after that one shining moment of ours with that bastard in the streets of Boston, he became a whole new person. Whenever the rage built a hole through his eyes, you could envision a pristine bomb counting down inside his veins. He would be waiting for that moment to strike, like the snake crawling in the grass by your feet. He would listen to the silence, waiting for the trap to spring open and snatch his prey in its merciless grip. He would then continue to rip the throat out of his victim, claw them with fist after fist, and leave them to leave bleeding on the gravel. It was times like that when Jason would make me proud. But now, instead of being the mentor watching is apprentice learning to live life through his own rage, I was now the one performing. I was now the one in the driver’s seat, steering towards that inevitable road block that wouldn’t withstand my four wheel drive. Jason would have parked and sat there, waiting as the rain fell from the heavens. He would have thought of the consequences of his actions before he put the pedal to the metal. And ever if he went through with it, he would park behind the wreckage and stare at the rubble, rain dripping from his hair and his bones chilling in the wind as he wondered if he should have gone through with his engagement. I, on the other hand, really wouldn’t think twice, and I wouldn’t give two shits about who I hurt in the process. That was the major difference between Jason and me. He cared. I don’t. Too bad Thug doesn’t know these things. Maybe he would have been able to save his nose.
