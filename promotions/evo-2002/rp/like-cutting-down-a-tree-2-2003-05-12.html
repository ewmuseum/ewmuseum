Like Cutting Down A Tree - 2

12 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-05-12 I run my hand through my hair. It's gotten so long in the past few months. Someone told me under the right light, I look a bit like Kurt Cobain. I just smirked. I went home and shaved later that night to disprove the theory. People don't even recognize me now. There's not a thing special about me anymore. Just the things I do, which are so few and far apart, that I can adjust myself to a decent lifestyle. But maybe sometimes I miss it. The recognition. The admiration. It's not contrived, but it was false at times. And when it wasn't, it made me want to fake it. But I faked too hard. And now I'm dead. Figuratively. The car slowly creeped into the parking lot. Only a couple cars were parked on this side of the building. Both were in 'Staff Only' spots, the red and white signs hanging above them like an eyesore on the brick wall. Much like the Jetta beneath one of the signs, it's cleanliness mis-leading you into what goes on inside. They seat you in what they'd have you think is a waiting room, but it is more like a public office. It's got furniture, but the workers would never sit on them. They leave you alone with just yourself, in my case with Marcus, and they go in back to prep the body. One of them doesn't actually touch it, which is apparent in the absence of gloves. It doesn't have anything to do with nausea either. It's more of 'Who's turn is it?' than it is not holding your lunch. And people spew a lot. Most can make it to the bathroom, or hold it till outside by their car. But you can always see a color fade where they've cleaned over a certain spot countless times. The pattern on the floor tile not as bright as the other ones. That white sheet is never enough. It's purpose, for you, is to serve as a shield, and a safety valve. But no matter how you look at it, there will always be a body underneath it. And it will always be someone you know. Unless your lucky, and some other person got killed. Then you can smile and walk away knowing your lover or father or sister didn't get their head blown off. But that sheet is so white, and so serene. Why pull it off? Why bother to see who it is, when it could very well kill you inside? If it's because you have to know, it's not good enough. And with a gloved hand, he slowly peels it back, and you see the person, lying their with their eyes closed. Sleeping peacefully, in perfect harmony. As your cries break the silence, and tears streak down your face, the doctor is getting his form. He only needed your signature on this piece of paper, but to get it, he had to do this to you. He had to show you her body. And you never thought you'd see her like this, when she talked to you on a cold, December night. You never imagined seeing her in this position. Not before you at least. It never seemed like a feasible thought. And now, she's lying before you. Fiona Preston. Dead. Her skin that pale color. Her eyes shut. Lips light purple. Oh God. I think I might stain the tiles.
