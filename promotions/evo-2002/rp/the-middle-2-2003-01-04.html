The Middle - 2

4 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Post-PPV 'I'd sell My soul My self-esteem a dollar at a time One chance One kiss One taste of you my magdalena' Magdalena.A Perfect Circle He tried to choke out a response, but all he managed was a faint cough. She continued, however, despite his new malfunction. 'You must be doing good, considering the size of your locker room.' She said. Now he looked around. It was spacious, though not as large as the higher talent such as Xander, and Shocka. He realized it was more suitable than what some people got. The thought didn't cross his mind that some people weren't a champion. She continued, however, despite his continual glance of things. 'So, champion huh?' She spoke while standing up. 'I guess your like some fine wine, slowly getting better with age.' She ran a finger down his bare chest. 'Do you taste any better?' He wanted to swat her hand away. The anxiousness in his eyes were a blueprint of how he wanted to end this. But his hands remained at his side. Not even a twitch stirred about them. She continued, however, dropping her hand and continuing to walk around the room. 'I've been watching you for a while. Someone pointed you out to me, so I ordered that pay-per-view Faith.' She topped walking and looked to him. 'You remember Faith right? Quite an impressive job. Especially since the stipulation was laid on you at the last minute. Was almost surprised to see you go through with it actually.' His face started to contort, and he took a step forward in defense of her comment. His expression was just anger. She continued, however, seeming to enjoy his pain. 'Quite a risk you took, just giving the title up. And for what? Morals and ideals? Pft, funny thing, seeing you of all people stand up for something like that.' She too was now growing a little sterner, as she confronted him. 'You could've kept it. But instead you threw it away. Getting used to that sort of thing eh?' 'That was different,' the statue said. 'And you know it.' 'Do you remember why we started this? For reasons you've obviously forgotten. For someone you've obviously forgotten.' He smacked her, hard, across the face. Her head stayed that way, until she turned back to look up at him. Her dyed hair blocked the red mark. 'You can't hide it. We both know it's true. So don't fight it.' 'I'm not fighting anything.' She smiled. She smiled something sinister. 'Are you sure about that?' His eyes formed a question, as doubt was taking over. 'I was here tonight. I saw you out there. I saw you. I saw the smile you put on walking out. I saw the looks of determination. That satisifed feeling that people felt when the ref held your limp hand up. I saw the signs of weakness. But I saw the signs others didn't. That sign of weakness as you realized those people were cheering for you. That look in your eyes, as you realized that you won. Your eyes were hollow. Un-disturbed, non-existant.' His face became emotionless, as he realized the things she was saying were true. He had won, but it had meant nothing to him. Just something else he had accomplished. He just wondered...is this it? 'Are you sure you know what your doing anymore? Are you still wrestling, or are you just taking up a hobby? You fight so hard, and all you feel from it are empty victories. So why keep doing it? What's left inside to motivate you? I'm all ears.' 'Get out.' She smirked, and brushed her hair back. She started her walk for the door, but stopped before closing it. 'I'm gonna make it here, and there's nothing you can do about it. Because I won't make the same mistakes as you. Because I still remember.' The door clicked shut, as her words bounced around the room. 'I still remember.' And for the first time, he wondered.
