Rememberance

25 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-11-25 Dear Zak, I hope this letter reaches you in good time, I sent it three days previous to your birthday, but as I didn’t have an address for you, I left the delivery to the hands of your company. I also know that you’ve been over seas a lot recently, so if this is late, I apologise. Well, I guess first off I just have to say happy birthday. Twenty-three years old and you’ve probably made more money than I’ve made in my entire life! But before you get the wrong idea, this letter is not about money. I don’t even know where to begin, I don’t know how to start apologising, but I’ll try. When you last saw me a year ago, I was a mess. Like you said to me that day, I hadn’t changed in the four years since you last saw me. I was still an alcoholic, I still hadn’t done anything about getting a job, I was still running my own life into the ground. I won’t sit here and tell you that your Mother leaving me made me turn into that man, because we both know I was headed that way anyway. But I will tell you, that that man is gone and I truly mean that. I’ve sobered up and am a regular with alcoholics anonymous. I’ve been going to counselling to deal with my anger issues, I’ve even got myself a steady job with a security agency, that I’ve been with for over 6 months now. Next thing you know I’ll be telling you I’ve found God! But you and I both know neither of us believe in that, never have, never will. I remember seeing you on your birthday last year. Despite the years of self-abuse, that’s the one thing that sticks out clear as day in my mind. Probably because it’s the reason that I’m the man I am today. Your visit made me realise the way my life was headed; it made me realise how I was going to end up if I carried on that way. You saved my life. I also remember the horrible thing I said about your Mom that day. Truth is, I hadn’t thought about her in a long time. After she left, I tried to forget about her, ultimately that’s what all the drinking was about I guess. I was just so shocked at hearing of her death, I didn’t know how to react. But that’s no excuse for what I said that day. I go visit her as often as I can. At least once a week, I always put fresh flowers on the grave. I miss her. I miss you too. Seeing the way you reacted was also something that shocked me. Scared the hell out of me as well. Seeing all your built up rage being vented on my old car, and witnessing your power, was something I’ll never forget. I keep up to date with what you do in your life now, I watch you go to work every Thursday night. I hate myself for what I’ve done to you, for the monster I forced you to become. I watch you go out in arenas all over the world, so full of rage and pain, I see the way you take it out on other people and I despise myself for doing that to you. Look, I know I haven’t been the best Father in the world, I often wonder if I’ve even done anything in any moment of my life to make yours easier, but I’ve changed, I really have. I wish you’d give up this life of destruction you live, even if it’s only just for a day to come visit me, to give me a chance to prove to you that I’m a changed man. I know I don’t deserve anything from you, I know that my words must sound hollow and meaningless, I know that you probably despise me with every fibre of your being, but Zak I love you. I’m so sorry for all that I’ve done in your life, I wish I could take it all back, but I can’t. I just wish you’d give me a chance to make it up to you. Happy birthday Zak. I love you. Dad. ……. A tear falls on to the letter. “Dad.” A moment of weakness, a stab of emotion. ……… It passes. The tear turns to a flame as the match burns through the paper underneath. Zak was buried, over the years he’d gradually been pushed deeper and deeper down. Now, like his Dad had said, all that was left was the monster. The words were hollow and meaningless, there was going to be no last chance. He didn’t care that the letter was almost two weeks late for his birthday, in fact he was surprised he even remembered when his birthday was, it meant nothing to him anymore. It didn’t matter that he’d written a heart felt emotional letter. It didn’t matter that he’d apologised for what he’d done, what he’d said, or even that he’d apologised for how he’d treated him. His Father had made him what he was today. There was no need to apologise for it. He liked it.
