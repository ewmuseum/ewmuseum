A Night in the Life of Sean Sterling

7 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-08-07 Bob stood across from me wearing his white apron that was stained with a bit of BBQ Sauce. Nothing out of the ordinary. Bob's full name was Bobby Marcus Kindle. The details on Bob were that he was fat, or so society would say. His hair was thick and blonde and split down the the middle to reveal a crooked part that would easily remind you of a thunder bolt. His hands always tended to sweat a lot and everything he cooked seemed to have that salty taste of... sweat in them. It was practically his secret recipe. Speaking of cooking, Bob ran a diner. Pop's. I asked Bob why he called the diner 'Pop's' once and he told me that it was because he always wanted to have kids. Since he never did, he hoped that maybe the customers would call him Pop. It didn't work, to say the least. Once a person ate at the diner once, they never came back for seconds. But back to the details. Bob liked the color blue. Navy blue, to be exact. Underneath that BBQ stained apron, he'd always wear the same exact navy blue shirt. Day in. Day out. I'm not sure if he ever took it off... I just knew it reeked. Bob always smelled rather unflattering. It reminded me of how smoke smelt. I didn't have the heart to tell him to take a bath. Bob didn't have a computer, but if he did his password would be 'password'. Bob used to have a dog named Doyle. Doyle was a Dodson. Or a wiener dog. Whichever. Doyle got hit by a car around two months back and Bob was all alone. He still hadn't grown very accustomed to it. How I came to befriend Bob isn't really a interesting story. We were both boring people. Living out boring lifes. Wishing for a boring death so we could go to a boring afterlife or be reborn with a new boring life. We had a few things in common. I didn't have much in common with hardly anybody. Bob was that small sigh of relief. That bit of fresh air. A free psychiatrist to confide in. A friend, if you will. Tonight? We were 'celebrating'. Bob had went to the doctor today and his hemoglobin had inclined, for one thing. For another... I got my first job since Judith's death. A touchy subject, to say the least... But I had been hired by another promotion. A brand new beginning. It was funny to be considered a nobody again. Bob was actually the person to tell me about the promotion. I declined what he said. Told him there was no way I'd be going back into wrestling. But after a few drinks, I'd agree to anything. You do the math. So, in reality, I really had nothing to celebrate. Besides Bob's hemoglobin. eVolution, they call it. 'So, you know, I was reading this book that was talking about how finding Jesus Christ could give you a reason to live. Help you find your own personal meaning to life, you know?' Bob asked me with a lazy yawn. When he yawned? His stomach looked like a volleyball had just inflated inside him. Bob had been on a religious path to find Jesus lately. I found it fucking annoying. I really didn't want to see Bob join the ranks of those Christian fuckheads. He was trying to turn me on to the whole 'religion' thing too. The first time he invites me to church? I'm running far away. 'The meaning of life is for us to die, Bob. In reality, we're put here on this green Earth of ours to be food for worms. The minute we're buried? We've succeeded in carrying out or meaning as long as we weren't cremated. In reality, the world is just one big circle. There's no real meaning.' Was I a little too harsh? 'Some one's cheery,' Bob muttered under his breath as he put away a couple dishes. When he bent over to put the dish away? He looked like he was about to break in half. 'And just why are the worms here?' 'For the birds. I told you. Endless circle.' We had conversations like these every Tuesday night. We were both lonely men. If people knew how much we met? They'd probably think we were gay. It was nothing like that though. We just used eachother to find something called happiness. It seemed to me that the difference from Bob and other people was that Bob? He actually listened where as other's just waited for their turn to talk. The conversation carried on for hours and we finally said our goodbyes. No hugs and no pats on the shoulders. Just a simple 'Got to go' and a simple 'Bye' in reply. I left and he went back to work. Nothing special. No sentimental bullshit. To call Bob a friend would be a lie. Bob was a reflection of me and I certainley didn't consider myself a friend. Outside, the usual sound of honking horns and sirens filled the air. The oh-so-familiar sounds of the city. Quiet-phobes. That's who lived in the city. If I had my way? I'd have all of them shot. Just for some peace and quiet. Absolute silence. I'd have the neighbor next door to my apartment shot. The neighbor who plays his stereo all bloody night. I'd have the taxi-driver who likes to honk his horn shot. Let's not forget the crying baby upstairs! Shoot it! I'd have Bob shot too. And then, when I was alone in the city... Surrounded my silence... Surrounded by a ghost town with only me... I'd sit down. And I'd close my eyes. And I'd listen to the absolute silence. True serenity can be achieved through absoltue silence. I'd sit for hours and simply listen. On my way home, I passed a car accident and I was reminded of what I told Bob. About how the meaning of life was to die. The body was just being put into the ambulance. I wondered if Jesus was watching this? The smoke that flowed off the car made my headache.... so I walked a little quicker. When I got to the apartment... everything was normal. The stereo next door was blaring and the baby was crying upstairs. ...I found myself wishing I had a gun on me.
