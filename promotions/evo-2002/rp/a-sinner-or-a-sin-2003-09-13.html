A Sinner Or A Sin?

13 September 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-09-13 'Forgive me, Father, for I have sinned….' The dry voice broke up, plain, blankly, no sense of guilt, no sign of remorse. The dark vestibule filled by priestly flesh, but with no inner soul of conscience. Piercing light shot through the puncture holes separating this darkened shadow, which was once a man, from the riches of Catholicism and corruption. 'Yes, my child….' - an unearthly reply greeted. One where wisdom was put on as a pretence, where concern was an act. He wanted nothing but my donations, his greed, his self-belief. His child? He wasn't my father. He wasn't bothered, it was part of his job. I continued with the normality of this parody. This charade of penance. Who was he to grant my penance, anyway? Who was he to hand me my blessing? Was I even regretful for my regressions? No. 'Forgive me, Father, for I have sinned…' - the repeat was as static as before. Stating the fact but in no direct detail. How could I show emotion to this, to anything. Forgiveness? But was I sorry? Did I feel ashamed? Within this chaplet of holy sanctuary, I felt burdened. As if a presence was driving me into further depths of anger. I didn't find peace with myself, nor did I feel this urge to amend my wrong, I wasn't looking for forgiveness - I was gloating at this egregious wrong. Could I have been this transgression summed up within this picture of woe. A portrait, one which Van Gough would emulate about, of decadent sorrow and pain. Did I seek forgiveness? I sought retribution. 'My child….' - the oh so highly placed priest uttered. Like fuel being thrown onto the naked flame of anger - a fire of hatred burned within my chest. Trapped within this decayed estate, trapped it seemed by the shifting tides of religion and faith. What faith? I had to faith! I had nothing to believe in. Like the crucified image of Christ which had been splattered from wall to wall, like some display - I had suffered the same fate. I, too, had carried a cross. A cross of guilt. A cross of pain. A heavy burden that I had to bare. A burden for society - a guilt for nothing more than my mere, miserable existence. But now - now they will know. They will see what it's like to wake up and pray for some divine intervention to end their insignificant lives. I want them to know guilt. I want them to know hurt. I want them to know suffering. I want them to know MY pain. 'I will be the judge of your sin - fear not, I shall be lenient.' - It seemed too patronising to be kind. 'Who are you people to judge me?! What gives you the right?! Huh?!' - The cold voice was now bitter, angered and full of hatred. 'Why my….' - He seemed taken aback, shocked or possibly displaced to the question of his authority and 'god given right'. I cut him off. 'Forgive me, Father, for I have sinned…' - Echoing a deja-vu occurrence. But was I truly what they define as a sinner? Or was I the sin?
