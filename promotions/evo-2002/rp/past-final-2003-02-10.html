Date: 10th February, 2003 It was inevitable to argue with reason. The young boy was sitting there with his hands placed upon his forehead looking down at the shaggy carpet that stared back at him. Fear, anxiousness, the unorthodoxed mixture flowed through his entire being slowly.. but purely. How long would he have before he would have to meet his first decline, and the last thing that he might remember. He had five hours to decide on a plan of action, a way of getting through this twisted enigma of a life that he had. It shadowed what his future would behold. Was it death? Success? The question had been eating through him like the vermin inside of a wound. It was slowly running around inside of him, he would surely rot from the inside out with all of this mixed emotion. He had to hide it from the outside world, for the sake of what he had to do, and for the sake of keeping himself from a lecture from his father followed by his 'iron' left hook. How would the young toddler do this? He was sure inside of the age of being a teenager, but to the competitors he would have to rally up against, he was nothing more than a toddler, an infant compared to the masculinities known as fighters, known as men. It was scaring him obviously, the sweat slid from his palms down to his forehead and rolled down through the centre of his nose. He felt that that was what his life was going to do. The droplet of sweat continued to decline down the slope of his nose and fell off the tip plunging lower.. lower.. farther.. farther... until... SLAM! It had met it's destination, it had met the rock bottom and the decline was over. It had wasted itself into dissolution and the existance of that was scattered away. The boy imagined his day of being like that, the persperation continued to run all over his face, his hands, everywhere, the anxiety was getting to him, but not being anywhere close to the fear that he had building up inside of his body. The fear inside of him was somehow conjuring up other methods to blocking away hope, any sign of light, any kind of help that would stop him from this chaos, this madness. Excruciating was the pain, the hope that he had inside of him was molting, similar to the feathers of a bird. Flying away gracefully, never to return until what he had to do was over and completed. Thoughts of the many had began to clot up his mind, none just negative, but some being positive, none of them strong enough to break through the veil of fear that covered his entire being. He was stunned all of a sudden, by the manly, deep, gruffy voice that had projected toward him, not in a statement. But in a question... 'Wha'sup boy..', had shot his way from the man. Shaun had quickly turned his head behind him to notice that his father was standing there watching him, looking him straight in the face. A face that could be framed and admired. He was a hero of somesort, but a lie is what had to be done, not only to cover up the secrets that he was holding from his parent, but to keep him from cracking. 'N-nothin', n-nothin's up f-father.. I-I mean d-ad..', he stammered at his father. Being a father of a fifteen year old would obviously keep you from believing him, Shaun had knew that he failed in his attempt to cover himself with the truth. His father continued to question him.. 'You sure? You sweatin' like you wearin' a sweater somewhere in Ecquador..', he joked. Shaun had no room for laughter at this time of trauma. He kept his blank expression on and with that mistake, his father had continued to ask questions in hope of gaining a small hint on what was going on. 'Iss somethin' wrong wit chu'. You ain't laughin' at nothin'. You just sittin' there. You can tell your old man...', he lifted his hand after the statement and lunged his hand toward his mouth holding a large ruby colored orb. He sunk his teeth into the spectical and chomped down with his jaw's power and held the badje centre in his mouth and lunged his mouth, up then back down in that repetitive order. 'N-nah, pops.. it ain't nothin' wrong wit me. Aye, I-I gotta get goin'..' Shaun had lunged himself forward from his seat and headed over toward the door. He continued over toward the door before his father's blunt comment caught his attention. 'You on drugs, son?', he was serious. The tone in his voice had gave that much away but he wasn't all there. Shaun had turned his head around in disbelief, atleast that's the facial expression he had given away, sure he had smoked a couple of mexican red hairs, and an ounce of purple haze, all marijuana, before. He was a pro at the smoking game, but he couldn't tell his father. Obviously, Jevar read the facial expression and stepped down from his constant pressure. He had been fooled by the facial expression, 'Aiight, i'ma buh-leave you, but if you are...' Shaun had lunged himself from the room, from the porch, from the yard, from the house, into the street. It was time for him to meet with his fear, and nothing was going to stop him, not even if he had wanted it to... [~l-a-p-s-e~] [~e-s-p-a-l~] Shaun, no, Angel was standing in the middle of the large octagonal shaped cage. It was about twenty minutes into the fight and he had his arms bared up in his boxing position, being right handed he held it from the opposing South Paw. He watched as the large, very obese man known as the bear had lunged himself forward and charged right into him. Angel's body had crashed into the cage, the small trickles of barbed wire had stitched into his skin, he could feel the blood run down from his back. The Bear was standing there, he had lifted his hands up and clinched them together, slamming them, no, driving them into the back of Angel. Angel had fell forward and landed chest first on the hard canvas. He was lying there, trying to shove himself up as the count began. One.. Two.. Three.. Four.. Fi- It would go no father because he was standing up with his hands already in position before five could be officially counted. The Bear, thinking he had won, was taunting over toward the fans, his hands dangling in the air and his musty body shooting it's odors over at Angel. Angel had edged closer and shook his fist some, his normal signal that he was going for a devastator. When Bear turned around.. CRACK~! BAM~! CRACK~! CRACK~! Bear had turned around to a four fist flurry of punches directly into the soft spot of his face, his nose. The Bear had fallen, but along with Angel. Angel didn't fall to his back, or his chest, but to his knees. The Bear wasn't moving. He was just lying there, motionless, still, null. He was not able to fight and for that, Angel, was declared the winner. The happiness of Angel would be celebrated now, but would be forgotten by him sometime later. [~l-a-p-s-e~] [~e-s-p-a-l~] Shaun was happy, he had over come his fear, although knowing he would have to make up a lie for his father, he would tell him he had gotten into an accident on his friends bike. Shaun continued forward only to be cut off by his former opponent. The Bear.. 'Hey boy.. you got you some skills therr' but I don't take losin' that easily, you understand?', he confirmed with his strong country accent. His breath reeked of chewing tobacco. 'Tough...' Shaun had said, taking a step to the side and starting forward. In his path was the Bear once more. 'I don't think you understand, I want the money that you got by beatin' me..' 'How 'bout. Nah?', Shaun joked. Not knowing of the small slinder metallic instrument that Bear had contained in his hand. 'Fine then...' Cock.. BOOM~! He had awaken, surrounded by a crowd of people. He held his head feeling a sharp stinging, and he looked around. ..oz.. Is that my name? That is my name. No.. my name is Shaun.. but I am Oz. Yes.. I am Oz. Oz...
