Immortality

5 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 5th, 2003 (Most of this has been translated as best I could from the words spoken to me by Yoshii himself and what he was willing to tell me. He speaks little English and for some reason he has also kept the majority of his own history a secret from even me.) Yoshii had grown up in a very strict Japanese family and always he looked forward to a brighter future. His grandfather, Kenta Tanaka, was a former General in the Japanese army who served during World War II. Following the Japanese defeat his grandfather had returned a different man and disgraced in the eyes of many in their hometown of Osaka. Despite no longer serving in the army, Kenta, made it a point to drill obedience into his children for his entire life. It was a procedure which had worn off well on his own son Jenga who had then passed down his disciplining to his own son Yoshii. From an early age Jenga had lectured Yoshii on the importance of being obedient and respectful to the people he encountered but his superiors most of all. It was a lesson that did and didn’t mix well with the in bred personality of Yoshii. Unlike his father and grandfather before him, Yoshii had no interest in the military lifestyle that they both had led for their entire life. Yoshii had wanted to lead his own life and it became more and more evident the older he had gotten. Before long despite his father’s best attempts Yoshii was off on a path of his own chosen and found himself away from his home for various lengths of time as he began a life in the training of martial arts and wrestling. He had began training at the young age of 13 and for years he trained and perfected his fighting style to the point where there were few left in the world who could contend with his fighting skills in a straight up shoot fight whether it be in a wrestling ring or a martial arts dojo. Imagine my surprise when just six years later I had discovered this young and raw talent competing nightly on independent shows while I was touring Japan. He unlike many of the Japanese wrestlers wasn’t just a bland wrestling technician. He actually had the charisma to go along with it. Granted he couldn’t speak English and that could cause a problem for him in an American promotion but there were ways around it and that would be where I came in. I was determined to sign the young man and bring him overseas to become a star in America. Convincing him had proven to be quite a bit harder than I expected it to be. Despite his rigorous upbringing Yoshii had proved to be fiercely loyal to his family and was hesitant to leave them and the fact that they did not at all support his career choice did not help matters any. It took weeks upon weeks of pleading and bargaining before I was even able to convince Yoshii to give it a chance. I have got him that far and now it was time for him to test out his mettle and find out if he can be as good as I think he can be. Soon like Godzilla on Japan, the American wrestling industry will be set upon by “The Immortal” Yoshii Tanaka.
