Intermission pt.5

10 June 2003

Date: 2003-06-10 Khadafi was back with Deborah, sitting in the darkened den of their St. Paul apartment his eyes trained on the cool gray clouds that filled the sky. They filled his picture window mindless to the questions that ran through his clean shaven head. He had flown from Canada to Syracuse just for to weigh in for the Thursday night show. It wasn’t a big deal but Ali was a bit surprised to find himself as the only fighter slated to weigh in that day. “Yeah”, the eVo official told him eyes of the scale marking down Khadafi’s official weight. “eVo doesn’t require you guys to weigh in. I think only Trevor Norman is scheduled to weigh in too. He’s going to do that right before the show on Thursday, though.” Khadafi had raised an eyebrow at that last comment. He had known since he had signed onto the lumbering hulk of the cell division that it was going to be a little rough in the start. Hell, they were just bringing this brainchild back to life and it was kind of expected that there were going to be a few no-loads. Ali could see that just by looking back a few days at his last opponent. An ex-wrestler, no matter what he said whose promos gave away his background more than anything else. It would be a matter of time before the wrestlers, the hangers on, and the backwoods barflies dropped out of sight and the ones left standing would be the true nitty gritty that made up eVo’s cell division. “What about Knight?” queried Khadafi. He was talking about his opponent for Thursday, checking if he was going to weigh in. “I didn’t see his name on the list that the suites faxed me, man. From what I hear, he’s still up there in Nova Scotia or some crap like that.” Khadafi nodded as he stepped into his shoes and then his blue jeans. He had heard similar things himself. It really came as no surprise that Eric Knight wasn’t going to weigh in. Knight had never struck Ali as one who took his job seriously despite being dubbed the first fighter in eVo. It wouldn’t really come as a surprise when Khadafi clubbed him into ANOTHER unceremonious knock out. Khadafi nodded slightly to the official on his way out not really knowing where he was going. He had ended up with Deborah, sitting in the den of the apartment that they co-owned. He sat there and wondered questions that he knew he might not get the answers to. Why weren’t the other fighters showing any life? Where had Eric Knight gone? Not that Ali really cared, his emotional attachment to Knight had ended as soon as he saw the whites of the poor mans eyes roll up into their sockets with an audible click, but he worried about a no show. Could he take fighting week in and week out? Wrestlers could do it because their shit was as fake as Jenna Jameson’s tits, but fighters- QBX being set aside because he barely counted as a human being- needed a little more rest than that. A wrestler could be thrown through a table and brush it off to do a back flip off the top rope five minutes later, but it was hard to step inside into the cage with the dim rattling of a slight concussion still clouding the fighters head. Were the other “fighters” really going to stand the test of time? Khadafi didn’t know the answers to these questions. He could only sit and watch as the cell division either flourished or floundered. He could only sit and wait for Thursday Night Legends when it would be the two person party that had become such a big part of his life that his fiscal success hung in its perilous clutches. .sit. .wait.
