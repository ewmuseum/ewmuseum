Final Journey.

23 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-08-23 Beep… beep… beep… I remember when I first started wrestling. My first match was in some hole-in-the-wall beer joint. But to me, it felt like Madison Square Garden. I was actually doing what I’d wanted to do since I was a kid. Not many people get to live their childhood dream, and I was always thankful for every day I got into the ring. What can I say? I love fighting. Doctor, how’s he doing? Not good ma’am. We’re not sure how long we can keep him stabilized. We ran a bloodwork on him… did you know what he was putting into his body? I don’t keep track of all the suppliments he takes. I’m his sister, not his keeper. Uh, I wouldn’t call these ‘fitness suppliments.’ Some of these are stimulants used on farm animals. I was always strong. Even in school, I was bigger and stronger than the other kids in my class. Hell, I was stronger than anyone at my entire school. But pro wrestling is hard on the body. When you’re on the road three hundred days a year, fighting different opponents nightly, you start to look for an edge. Overpowering my opponents forced them to change their style, altering their game plan. It’s awfully hard to dropkick a mack truck, or suplex a bull. Did my strategy work? I’ve got a string of titles that’d say so. But look at where I am now. Ms. O'Halloran, is there anyone else we should contact? Any other sibilings? Parents, perhaps? Everyone that would care is already here. Let’s keep it that way. My old man hated the fact that I wasn’t going to be a commercial fisherman like him. He said that wrestling was nothing but a bunch of ‘faggots in tights’. This coming from a man who’d spend months at a time with six other guys, crammed in quarters just a little bigger than a phone booth. When I left home, he told me I’d be a failure. I’d never amount to anything. I fly first class all over the World, stay in luxurious hotels, and date beautiful women. He gets to roll around on a wet deck with a big, slimy fish. Who’s the one who didn’t amount to anything? But Mom, she was great. She believed in me, even at the risk of pissing off my dad. She never said much, never openly complimented me or gave me encouragement. But I’d notice a look, a hint of a smile, and that would be all I needed to know. She got sick a couple Christmas’s ago. Now she lives in a nursing home and has to wear a name tag on her wrist. It’s the only way she remembers who she is. Daddy? Daddy wake up! Please wake up! I don’t think it’s a good idea for her to be here. My niece isn’t going anywhere. That’s her father lying there. She’s staying. Any idiot with a dick can be a Father. It takes a special person to be a Dad. When Sarah’s mom left her on my doorstep, I had no idea what to do. My career was just taking off, my dream coming true. I was just a kid myself, for crying out loud. My sister and I talked about it, and we agreed that she’d raise Sarah as her own. Mary couldn’t have kids, so this was my way of giving her the family she could never have. She was happy, Sarah was happy, and I was happy. What happened to him? Essentially, his internal organs couldn’t keep up with the stress his body was putting on them coupled with the beating he served last Wednesday night. They simply gave out. The fact that he’s still alive is surprising, given the magnitude of the coronary failure he had. After a couple years in the business, my other brothers got involved. One disappeared and we’re pretty sure he’s dead. The other had a mishap in the ring and crippled himself. Another was killed before my very eyes as a child. Maybe their fate is my fault. I didn’t deserve to be happy. I didn’t deserve to be healthy. I just didn’t care. When you just don’t give a damn, it’s easy to abuse yourself. You don’t think twice about injecting stuff into your body. It’s not even an issue to pump medication into your body, knowing that they could weaken bones, tendons, and put ungodly stress on the organs. You just don’t care about that. It’s all about getting huge, looking good, and getting stronger than your opponent. Of course, it’s easy to say you’ll stop. It’s something else entirely to do it. Auntie Mary, do you think it’d be okay if I held his hand? Sure thing, pumpkin. I think he’d like that. There were times I tried so hard to lock away my feelings for my little girl. I knew what it was like to have a father who was never around. I hate the son of a bitch, and unlike him, I care what my kid thinks of me. But I wasn’t ready to give up fighting, and I’ve seen what happens when you get your personal life involved in the business. Bastards like me like nothing more than exploiting that. Innocent people get hurt. I couldn’t live with myself if something happened to her. He squeezed my hand! I felt him squeeze it! I’m not a very nice person. If you weren’t related by blood, you were nothing. I don’t apologize for anything I did in the past. It wouldn’t change anything, so what’d be the point? What’s done is done. I believe in answering for your crimes. Maybe that’s what this is all about. I made my bed, and now it’s time to suck it up and sleep in it. Beepbeep beepbeep beepbeep Doc, what’s wrong? What’s wrong with him? I think you two better leave now. Daddy? DADDY? I look back on the past decade plus, and I can’t help but smile. I bust my ass to get to where I’m at. I’ve had my share of critics, and my share of fans. I’ve even had people try to copy me. Often imitated, but never duplicated. Some people think I’m famous. Some people think I’m overrated. Some think I’m scum. Maybe they’re all right. Maybe they’re all wrong. Maybe I’m just lucky. You people have to go! Nurse! Code Blue! Oh God… Damon… No… Out! Now! Daddy no! Daddy I love you! Don’t go! Please! Championship Titles. Loving family. An awesome legacy. I may be a son of a bitch, but I’m one lucky son of a bitch.
