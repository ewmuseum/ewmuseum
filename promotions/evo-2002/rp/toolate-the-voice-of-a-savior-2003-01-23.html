TooLate - The Voice of a Savior

23 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/23/03 His car being gassed up in the building next to his current position, Jason sat at the counter of a lonely diner in the middle of an Arizona desert. Jason could hear the sound of a jazz song, playing from an aged jukebox in the corner of the room. Semi trucks, like an army platoon, stood still to one side. Their drivers were busy stuffing their faces and talking with old companions, remembering the days when their backs didn’t hurt so much and they were about a hundred pounds lighter. The aroma of eggs and steak rammed through his nose, making his mouth water and his stomach beg. “What can I do for ya, mister?” asked a mildly aged man, a bloody apron around his slightly chubby body and a Diamondbacks hat over his graying hair. “Scrambled eggs and some steak, please.” “Ha. Mister, you’ll fit right in around here. All these bastards love my steak.” “I love your wife too, Mike.” said one of the truck drivers from his seat, working on a piece of his own steak. “Fuck you, Sam.” said the old man, smiling and laughing, with no offense taken at all between the two obvious long time friends. “Don’t listen to that fool. He’s just in a good mood since those God damn Raiders got into the Bowl. One steak and eggs coming up.” The smile on his face grew and Jason could only enjoy the man’s presence. It mad him feel like a human being again, if that were at all possible. Jason looked down at a pamphlet of some nearby gym, before the sounds of the bell hanging above the door rang out in the diner. “Hey man,” said the owner. “Whatcha doing back already?” “Need some of that steak for the wife, Mike. She’s really had a craving for it lately.” “Or you just love my shit and you don’t want to admit it.” “Ah, damn. How’d you figure it out?” A man in a long, black coat and a black hat walked up to Jason’s seat, and took the stool next to him. Jason looked up at the old man, and gave him a friendly nod. “How are you, my boy?” the old man sad, tapping Jason on the back of his sweaty T-shirt, and not seeming to care at all. Jason smiled. “Good.” said Jason, looking away from the man and down at the floor. “Good, huh?” said the man, the doubt of Jason sincerity there. “How are you, son?” he asked again, a little more serious then before. Jason’s smile turned into an upside down frown, as he realized that it was no use lying anymore. What good would it do him? “Not so good now that you ask.” he responded. “Ah. Seems like everyone nowadays carries one burden on their shoulders at one time or another. You live, thinking that the world is the worst place in the world, but it really isn’t. Believe that, son. So what ails you?” “Everything. I can’t think of anything that doesn’t bother me.” “Well, what would you consider the worst of your troubles?” “I think it would have to be my former fiancée.” “Of course – love. If it isn’t the biggest heartbreaker of them all? What happened? Fight? Infidelity?” “I don’t… I made so many mistakes. You could say that it was a whole bunch of little things that led to one big thing. I ruined it.” “Not at all, sonny. Just go back and explain everything to her and I’m sure…” “That’s just the thing. I went back, and just when I was going to try to get her back, I found out that I was too late. She was already with someone else, and there was nothing that I could do about it.” “Are you sure about that?” “Yeah, pretty sure. I just gave up all hope. At any point, I could have gone back to her, and sat down and figured everything out, and I’ll be all right. If I could have just woken up and realized that shit that my life was without Marilee, then I would be with her right…” “Marilee, is it? I tell you, son, love can be a tough thing for a young man. But let me remind you of something. Hope is always there, and I promise on my name that you will find love once again.” “But all I want is her. She was everything to me.” “Who says that she won’t be this love that I speak of?” Jason looked into the eyes of the man, and realized that what he was saying was how he truly felt, and Jason wondered if this would be true. “I’ll tell you something. I was once like you. Felt like I lost everything, and that I had nothing left to live for. Everything that I loved was gone, and life just seemed to be ending slowly for yours truly. In those last moments, when it seemed that nothing would be the same again, I took all my hope. I took all of the hope that I had left, fell down to my knees, and prayed to God for forgiveness.” “And then what happened?” Jason asked sarcastically. “The world was given back to me.” he answered. “I met my wife a few days later, and my life was set in place again. My children were born one after the other. Money was no longer an issue. I would never have to gaze into depression again.” “And all of this from God, huh?” Jason responded, still sounding bitter and disbelieving. “Yes he did. There isn’t a doubt in my mind that the Lord was that one that forgave me, and gave my happiness back.” Jason looked down, still somewhat reluctant to believe, but seeing that there must be something behind all of the world’s hope. Maybe, just maybe, He wanted the best for him. But then again, maybe He didn’t. “Here you are, mister.” said the owner, “And here you are. Tell your wife ‘Your Welcome’.” “I sure will, Mike. Have a good one.” the man said, grabbing his bag and standing up, putting his hand on Jason’s shoulder one last time. “Don’t worry son. Things can only get better. I promise.” The man in the black coat and hat started to walk away, before Jason asked one last question. “I’m sorry. What’s your name?” The man turned around, the smile never leaving his face for a second. “Jonathon. Jonathon Orson Brinkley. And you, my son?” “Jason. Jason Jameson. Thank you, Jonathon.” “Not a problem my son. Just remember – hope.” Jonathon turned around, and pulled open the door, the bell hanging down ringing his exit through the diner. Jason stared at the empty space where he once stood for a few seconds, before sitting down again to eat his eggs and steak. Nothing else would occur, except one realization of the name he had just heard. And his mind revealed it to Jason. Jonathon Orson Brinkley. J.O.B. Job
