8 of Diamonds - Verdict

16 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/16/03 So there he was, staring through the window of the restaurant, watching as the woman he was once engaged to and the man she was now with ate together. Snow falling, Jason looked inside from the warmth of his car, staring at Marilee smile and laugh with the man that was able to take away the last ounces of Jason’s hopes. Just crumpled them up and threw them out into the gutter, to soak up the rain and scum of rats. He could tell she was happy. He could see the joy of life behind her eyes and could literally feel the ambiance of pleasure from where he sat. Jason wished that he could have her in his arms again, and could kiss the lips he could still feel on his, but she was no longer in his grasp. Another man, with a heart and a life that had so much more to offer, was the one who got to hold her close in the late hours of the night. He would be the owner of the eyes she would stare into every morning, a smile gleaming through her hair and her face resting on a pillow. Times like this one Jason would drop into instant tears. But not now. His breath collected on the cold window of the car, a misty safeguard between his eyes and all the beauty in the world. His hand wiped it away, only to leave a blurry image of Marilee. He wished he could walk in there and take her back, like the movies would have allowed, but he knew that it was hopeless. Nothing he could say or do would change the past. Locked away without a key, all he had left was her, and now that was gone. Jason gave her one last glance before starting up the car again and turning into the Boston traffic. A voice of reason spoke to Jason as he drove along the road, watching the dreary flights of snowflakes falling before his headlights. Jason’s own thoughts were, he found, so supportive and yet so unaccommodating. An oxy moron all their own, Jason didn’t want to hear them, but listened none the less. I can still remember the last day that I saw her. It was the day that would change my life. The one day that I wish I could go back and silence. Like that North Star in the night sky. It’s the one you follow and the one you can never forget, but it never changes. The star will always be there, beckoning you and staring a hole through your essence. I wished that she was still with me, but now she is in the eyes of another man, who will give her everything I never could. A promise of devotion that you threw away with the decision of a lifetime. Why I did that, only God himself may ever know. But can’t I feel the pain? Feel it ripping apart what little I have left. The very being of my soul being torn apart from the claws and fangs of my misery. The sensation of feeling all that you tried to hold dear being eaten alive within your own heart – oh man, how I could never imagine it hurting so much. But in the end, the only man blameworthy of this pain is me. I am my own executioner and punishment. I set into course the long road out of this darkness, and now I must suffer the consequences of my own choice. My final punishment. My verdict. Jason, waiting for green light, looked out the window, and saw a small bar with a sign glowing through the white darkness of the snowy night. The “Eight of Diamonds” was its name. Jason pulled the car over to the curb, parked the car, and went inside to see how well alcohol could get rid of agony.
