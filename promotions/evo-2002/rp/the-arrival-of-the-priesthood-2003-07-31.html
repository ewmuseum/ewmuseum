The arrival of the Priesthood

31 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-07-31 “I’d always loved the Priesthood. Being the youngest son of a devout Irish Catholic family left me with few career options. None of my brothers were Priests and every Irish mother wants at least one member of her offspring to don the cassocks. So, in my teens, I was sent off to Bible school to study for the Priesthood. And I have to say I enjoyed it. I excelled at the theology and learning of the scriptures. I was always heading the class and, when I left my schooling to return to Rathluric, my hometown, I was earmarked as one of the most promising young Priests in the area. “Back home in Rathluric, I took a position at the church as Father Jack Keane’s assistant. He was a good man and a good priest, honest and hard working. I enjoyed working for him immensely and he enjoyed having me around. He was nearing seventy when I arrived and he was pleased for the help. Yeah, it was a good life. And easy too - the folk in Rathluric were a pleasant, god-fearing crowd and I’d known most of them for years. However, the celibacy vow always chaffed at me. Going through puberty and being told that you can never get laid puts a dampener on your outlook, I can tell you. “The day came for Jack’s retirement from the priesthood and my succession to the head of Rathluric church. It was a night of celebration. My brother owns a pub in Rathluric and, that night, I drunk enough whiskey to poison a small nation. With the abandonment that only comes to the truly wasted, I took a young local lass up to the church and… well, let us just say that her father was not pleased to find the local, newly appointed head priest with his trousers down, drunk, in the presence of his pride and joy. My ousting from the church was remarkably quick, as was my alienation from Rathluric society. The only person who still liked me was Father Jack Keane. He understood about youngsters and what happens when they get boozed. That was four years ago. “When I left Rathluric, though I have been defrocked and banned from the church, I still considered myself a son of the church and took to preaching to the most hopeless souls I could find, starting in the bars and dives of Cork City. A few patrons of one place took a dislike to me sharing the good word and decided to give me a few choice words of their own, using their fists. To my surprise, I found it easy to beat them back and kick some religion into them. This became a regular thing as I headed north towards Dublin. Drunkards and other undesirables in taverns would frequently attack me and I would defeat them and ram some psalms down their throats. After a couple of years, reputation got around of this arse-kicking Priest and people challenged me to fights. I saw my gift of fighting as a present from God. I reckon he wanted me to beat the testaments into unruly folk so that’s what I did. “About a month back, I was surprised to see Father Jack Keane, now at the ripe old age of seventy-two, approach me just after I’d given the good news to some folk with my boots. He said he’d been watching me over the last few months and saw my talents. He suggested I stop beating up the same lowlifes and join an actual large fighting federation, one that has televised shows. That way, I’d be getting paid for my work and could spread the word at the same time. I considered the idea and saw that it was good. We decided that he should be my manager and he contacted some wrestling and fighting federations, both in Europe and America. “So, a week back, I was interviewed by Evolution and, presumably because they think it’ll help them get to heaven, they hired me. So here I am, and I’m here to succeed. The more I win, the more coverage I’ll get. The more faith I pummel into disbelieves, the further my word of God will spread. “So, my sons of Evolution, you’d better start looking busy. God is coming. And he’s brought his special face-stamping boots.”
