TooLate - Second Reunion

19 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/19/03 With only a few days left before he must board a plane to Arizona, and with the evening cold rampant in the air, Jason Jameson walked into a bar, distinguishable from the one last night, and sat down at the counter. Beside him sat a long friend by the name of Francis, whom Jason had grown up with and learned to love as a brother. The two sat in silence, each sipping their drinks and string into a mirror across the wall. Cigarettes and alcohol mixed together in an aroma of pity and loss. All around, people wore fake smiles, conversed about things that weren’t that important. All of them were there to drink their lives away, and to feel the pressure of not having one to begin with. And there Jason and Francis sat, minds wandering in every direction. “If I had known, I would have never read the letter that she gave me.” Jason said, staring down at the reflection of his face in his beer. Francis nodded, not really agreeing but not really disagreeing either. “She only met him a few weeks before Christmas, Jason. And it didn’t start getting serious until New Year’s.” New Year’s, Jason thought to himself. What great timing. “Is she happy, Francis?” Francis sighed, and looked over at Jason, resisting his want to not answer. “Yeah, I think she is Jason. I really think she is. It’s the first time she’s been with a guy since… well, you know.” Jason nodded. “Yeah. I know.” “I’m sorry, Jason. I wish I could do something about this, but I can’t…” The sounds of a cell phone interrupted Francis, as he reached down and pulled up the phone to his ear. “Hello. Yeah. Yeah ok, I’ll be right there. Sure. Ok. Bye. Jason, I have to go.” “I’ll tell you this right now, Francis. Hope is evil. It’s only there to carry you so far before it gives you up. It just eats away at you until there’s nothing left. Hope is the flame and I’m the candle.” Francis patted Jason on the back. “Don’t do this, Jason.” “Do what?” Francis looked at him, gave one last glare of regret, and then walked outside, leaving Jason alone with his beer. Jason sipped it down, and then asked for another. The people around him walked out one by one, leaving him at a speed for too immediate for him to care. Before he knew, it was the last call of the night, one of which Jason completely ignored. The drinks went down, the bartender collected him money, and Jason got up. Jason adjusted his jacket, of which he had never removed to begin with, and prepared for another evening of wandering the city, when from behind him came a voice. “Jason! Is that you?” And as Jason turned, there she stood.
