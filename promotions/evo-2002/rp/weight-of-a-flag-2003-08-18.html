Weight of a Flag...

18 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-08-18 God it’s so hard to breathe. Feels like I've got King Kong sitting on my chest. Every breath I do manage to suck in is like pure liquid fire. I've got stitches all over my upper body, no to mention the scores of bruises. I'm guessing I've also got a few cracked ribs. Just another day at the office. Sunday in New Hampshire, I had to cut a promo on Sean Sterling. I watched it backstage afterward, and I couldn’t believe I managed to pull it off. At least Cameron Griffin gave me the night off, given my exploits the week before in winning the Cell Title. I was battered, pummeled, and left for dead. If it hadn’t been for all the drugs I managed to pump into my system, I never would have made the flight. I never would have survived a simple promo last night. Today, I’m paying the price. There are some things you take for granted when you fly on a jet. Cabin pressure, for one. Sure, your ears pop and ache a little. I’ve got a five hundred plus pound bench press, but I’m shaking just trying to draw a breath. Kong's making himself quite comfortable. Big damn ape. “Are you alright sir?” The stewardess has been hovering by me the entire flight. Guess it would look bad if I happened to die in her section of the plane. “I’m fine,” I tell her, hoping she’ll buzz off. “Just a little gas, that’s all.” Damn. She doesn’t buy it. “You’re looking awfully pale. You don’t seem to be breathing well.” That’s what the doctor told me on Saturday. Being battered in a Cell tends to create some serious injury. When they helped me into the back after my match, they thought I was dead. Probably should have been, with all the blood I had lost and punishment I took. They wanted to take me to the hospital. But you don’t always get what you want. I, on the other hand, do. And I wanted to leave. So I did. I try to sit forward, but Kong pushes me back into my seat. “I said I’m fine. Go pester someone else.” She pastes on a fake smile and saunters down the aisle, fluffing pillows and handing out bags of peanuts. I don’t need my pillow fluffed, and I certainly don’t need any peanuts. I’m starting to think that maybe what I need is a doctor. That thought stays in my head for about three seconds. I’m an Act of God, I tell myself. I’m the Cell Champion. I’m the guy who sends people to the doctor, not the guy who goes to the doctor after taking a little abuse. Besides, I’ve got too much on my plate right now. Places to go, asses to kick. Damn it, I’m Damon O'Halloran. I’m more than anyone can handle. And there’s only two things they can do about it… Nothing and Like It. The lights are dimming, and for a second I think I'm passing out. Instead, it looks like they're showing a movie. I try to get comfortable, hoping a good flick will take my mind of things. They're showing King Kong. Irony is a bitch.
