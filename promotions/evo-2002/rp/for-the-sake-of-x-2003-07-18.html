For the sake of X...

18 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-07-18 There were few things Damon O'Halloran bothered to let himself care about. He didn’t care about money, power, or influence. He already had all three, but not once were they a goal of his. They were simply the by-product of O'Halloran doing what he did best: beat the unholy piss out of people. O'Halloran never once catered to promoters, bookers, or anyone in authority. He didn’t have to. His theory was simple enough: “I’m Damon O'Halloran and I’ll do whatever I want. Don’t like it? Tough shit.' Damon rarely ever got personal, inside the ring or out. He didn’t really like anyone, having a general animosity toward everyone. The brutal beatings he’d inflict were never personal, they were just part of what one would expect from opposing the Irishman. In other words, Damon just didn't give a damn about anybody. In fact, in his ten-plus year career, only a handful of people ever really pushed Damon to the point where he openly and flat-out hated the person. 'The Black Label' was going the right way about it. Damon couldnt let this cowardly act go unpunished. He wondered to himself what did 'X' see in the mirror when he woke up in the morning? He calls himself a man, but takes it upon himself to blindside his opponent. In O'Halloran's eyes, all he was was a coward. And Damon will show him pain come this Wednesday he was sure of that. Where did 'The Black Label' lie in this hierarchy of pain? To earn a status of suffering, it all depended on what the person did to push the IRA man over the edge. Some tried to end his career. Some attacked people close to O'Halloran; friends, loved ones, even his siblings. But no one had ever questioned O'Halloran's hunger, his motives for getting into the business. It was not something he chose, rather forced upon him, yet here was this man questioning O'Halloran's beliefs? No one dared. Simply put, it was suicide. As far as Damon O'Halloran was concerned, 'The Black Label' had just loaded a gun, flipped the safety off, and shoved the barrel into his own mouth. All that was left was for someone to pull the trigger, liberating chunks of X’s brain from its skull casing. A great distance separated the two men. But no distance was great enough to quench O'Halloran’s thirst for painful, murderous bloodshed. Soon they would both be in the Cell, facing each other. This time, Damon wouldn’t be playing savior to a victimized wrestler. He’d simply be the man standing across the cell from him. The gun was in X’s mouth. Damon couldn’t wait to pull the trigger. Couldn’t wait.
