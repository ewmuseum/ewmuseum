Criminal Intentions...

18 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-07-18 I hate fighting in the cold. I can almost see my breath, standing beneath the overpass in a less-than-wholesome part of Los Angeles. The lights mounted on the pillars have the entire area lit up. The crowd of people standing under here with me don’t seem the least bit cold, despite the fact that few of them are dressed for the weather. Me, I’ve got a sweatshirt on while I do push-ups on the street. You’re looking a little confused. Especially you, yeah you with the glasses. What’s Damon O'Halloran doing under some bridge? Who are all these people? Well, I’ve got a few minutes so I’ll bring you up to speed. eVolution's Cell Division is one of the top fighting leagues in the world. But, like in wrestling, many have to cut their teeth in the smaller promotions. Well, this is one of them. Many of the fighters here are lower class citizens, looking to make a few bucks. These are some of the grittiest type of fighters: the ones who fight to survive. In this crowd of people, some will be fighting, some will be betting, and some are here just for the free show. The premise behind these fights are simple: you fight until only one guy’s standing. There’s no ropes, no ring lights, no cages. Just the cold concrete beneath your feet. These fights aren’t exactly contained, but everyone knows to keep the fight under the bridge. If you clear the overpass, someone from one of the surrounding buildings might see you and call the cops. It’s pretty hard to make money from jail. Okay, you with the glasses again. You’re looking downright lost. What’s that? Am I here to fight? Actually, yes I am. But I’m not fighting for money. No, tonight I’m fighting because some guy cut me off in traffic earlier today and I couldn’t catch him. That pissed me off. When I get pissed off, people get hurt. So I made a few phone calls, talked to some of my underworld people, and they tipped me off to this fight. Hey, it’s a safe way for me to vent. Bite me. I stand up and peel off my shirt. My muscles are warm now; I shouldn’t have to worry about cramping up. Plus, the sweat I have makes it harder for my opponent to get a good grip on me. The crowd starts to spread out, forming a circle around us. Guess the betting’s closed. You might think that I’d be the odds-on favorite because I’m, well, because I’m me. But I can tell my opponent won’t be a push-over. He’s got a few tattoos on his arms, his tank-top is splattered with dried blood. So is his jeans. Must be his lucky outfit or something. “Go!” the ringleader of this circus of carnage shouts. We circle each other slowly. I’ve got about a hundred pounds on this guy, but he doesn’t look intimidated. In fact, he’s almost smiling. “C’mon, mister wrestling superstar. Come get some,” he says. Guess I have to pidgeonhole this idiot with guys like Xander and S. Guys who think I’m just a wrestler. I didn’t join eVolution for fame, and I sure as Hell didn’t join it to wrestle. I came to eVolution to beat people up, clean up a few loose ends, and just show the World that I’m just as good a fighter as I am a wrestler. Maybe better. Ouch! He caught me daydreaming and landed a good shot to my ribs. He’s fast, and quick on his feet. I make my way around him, throwing big bombs at his head. He easily ducks out of the way, peppering my stomach with jabs. Each punch he lands makes a wet smacking sound. But I keep moving forward. I don’t back up. I never back up. The crowd moves with us like a single, living beast. It moves around cars parked along the street, always keeping us in the middle of their ring. The man starts throwing a few leg kicks at my thigh. I know enough to pick my leg up when he hits it. Still, he knows what he’s doing. So do I. He’s a good fighter, and he’s landed a few good shots on me. I can feel the welts he’s raised on my stomach and thigh. I’m going to have tender ribs tomorrow. But pain has always been a friend of mine, one that’s a constant companion to me. I’ve learned how to handle pain, to accept it. Pain doesn’t hurt, unless I’m the one dishing it out. As he is about to find out in five, four, three, two… For a split second, he’s startled as he backs into the concrete support pillar. He was so busy thinking about me, that he forgot where he was. He’s got his back against the wall, and he’s run out of dodging room. I’ve played with him long enough. Playtime is officially over. He tries to step past me, but I grab his shirt and slam him against the wall. I clamp my hand around his throat, stepping close to him. At this range, he can’t throw kicks or punches with any kind of effectiveness. He’s in grappling range now. He tries to bite my nose. I slip beside him and half-nelson his arm, exposing his ribs. He grabs my ear and tries to pull it off. I return the previous favor by hammering on his unprotected ribs with my knee before slamming his face against the pillar. Nothing like a broken nose to take the wind out of your sails. Stunned, he puts up no resistance as I lift him up by the throat and slam him down to the street. His head bounces off the pavement, his body goes limp. It’s safe to say the fight’s over. As the crowd cheers, their bloodlust satiated, they turn their attention back to the ‘ringleader’ to collect their winnings. But as I watch the pool of blood forming around his head, I can’t help but think that I’ve seen this somewhere before. Then it hits me. Legends.... June the 12th... cometh the man.... cometh the hour! It's time to make an IMPACT on eVolution.
