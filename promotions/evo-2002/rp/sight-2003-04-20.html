Sight

20 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/20/03 “Hey. Aren’t you Jason Jameson?” Now let me tell something people. Of all the senses we have, I think sight is the most important. Without the use of our eyes, what would we have to look forward to? Listening to the world we can never see. Tasting the fruit of life without witnessing its birth. Feeling the warmth of a sun we can never watch set below the horizon. It is what makes us alive. But I’ll tell you… “Yeah, I’m Jason Jameson. Who are you?” Sight can also be one major bitch. “Hi. My name is Robert.” “What can I do for you Bob?” “Robert. And you can do plenty for me. As I’m sure you will.” When I first saw this guy, remains of a beard on his face and a dirty, brown trench coat covering his body, I knew he was going to be trouble. You should have seen the stupid grin on his face, or the sweat trailing down his hair and onto his face. Pitiful excuse for a man I must say. Just pitiful. I didn’t know whether to spit in his face and slam a beer bottle over his skull. But I just smiled, and listened to what he had to say. “I’ll ask again ‘Robert’. What can I do for you?” “Oh, nothing major Jason. Nothing too major if I must say so. But I don’t think you’re going to take this too well. Not at all. I know I wouldn’t.” Right then and there I should have dragged him outside and beaten him to a pulp. Maybe then he would have learned his lesson. Maybe then I wouldn’t need to go through any of this shit. God damn did I hate him for this crap. “But listen. Come out to my car in a few minutes when no one is looking, and we’ll talk.” Stupid bastard. If he thought that I was forgiving, he had another thing coming. He started to walk away, and part of me had this urge to get up and throw him over the counter into a mirror or something. But instead I just sat there, and had my drink. I ordered another and just sat there, acting like nothing was happening. In about twenty minutes, with three beers safely in my stomach and rage building in my mind, I got up and paid the bartender for the beers. Gave the lucky bastard a ten dollar tip too. Then I walked out, and saw the man smoking a cigarette in his car with the window down. I walked over to him. “When you get to you’re car, open this and then follow me.” I almost slapped him then, but I didn’t. He handed me an envelope. A brown, manila envelope with a few pieces of something inside. I walked over to my car, not saying a word to him but remaining completely silent. I just stepped away and climbed into the front seat, prepared for anything. Except for the ten or so pictures of me and that hooker, taken from the window of our hotel room. I looked over at his car, seeing him start it up and head out of the parking lot, taking a left. I knew that whatever was to come next, it wasn’t going to be pretty. I started up my car, and drove after him. I think I may have once heard this quote. A picture is worth a thousand words. Not sure. It’s also worth about a million swears.
