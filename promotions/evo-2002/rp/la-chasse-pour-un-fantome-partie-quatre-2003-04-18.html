La Chasse Pour Un Fantôme - Partie Quatre

18 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 18th OOC Note - Sorry, but I won't be here tomorrow to post this. Just pretend that it really was posted on a Friday. Enjoy. The car slowly creeped up into its parking spot at the end of the road. As the engine died, the door opened, bringing with it the annoying bell of the vehicle. As the figure stepped out, he shut the door, adjusting his sunglasses. He took note of the Silver Pontiac across the street. Though it wasn't the only vehicle on the road, it looked vaguely familiar. He opened up the side door, and pulled out his long, black coat. Careful he was, to place it on, and made sure it was rested tight around him. He took another paranoid glance around the street, before pushing the car alarm button on his keychain. With that, he turned, and melted into the shadows of the apartment building. * * * * * * Upstairs, he fumbled foolishly with small baggies and balloons. He tried to have an organized system, but he was so wasted most of the time that he couldn't function properly. This time, however, he wasn't using. And it was a good idea. The man coming up had requested such a large supply, that anything less than sobriety would've been a gamble. This customer had been coming recently, getting the same amount of Meth each time. But today, he had requested a large, more intense batch. An amount that was so hard to procure, he wondered if he should even sell it to him. But, him having agreed to pay four times his normal amount was a business venture he needed. For a while now he was looking to get out, and try and ease off of the life he had been living. A release of some sort, and he had told the buyer about this. He understood, despite his hollow expression, and agreed to pay the extra. Now, was payday. He continued to get the drugs in order, as the time when he said he would arrive drew near. * * * * * * She slid out of the bucket seat, and closed her door shut, quietly. Some small bits of wind blew some stray hairs into her face, but she brushed them back behind her ear. Her eyes gazed over the outside of the familiar building, as she got a brief sense of nostalgia. She looked to the right, and saw his car sitting there. She'd been ducked, slouching in the passenger seat for almost an hour, thinking and pondering what to do. How should she approach him? What would he do if he saw her? And the questions she wanted to ask kept circling inside her head. But she'd always come back to the fact that they didn't really know eachother. Instead, they knew of eachother. But she needed to know things. Things he could tell her. So she had to confront him. She looked around one last time...and walked into the coffee shop behind her. * * * * * * His fingers brushed across the wad of money in his pocket, as he pulled out his gloves. He reached into the pocket on the inside of his coat and pulled out a white handkerchief. Placing the gloves on, he held onto the cloth in one hand, as he removed a gun with the other. The black glimmered with what light shone on it, as he trudged up the staircase to the fourth floor. He worked the handkerchief across the gun, making sure he spent an extra moment or two on the trigger and grip. After finishing, he returned the handkerchief, and took out a silencer from his opposite pocket. As he slowly screwed the addition onto the piece, he looked up towards his destination. His prey was supposed to be making sure the drugs he wanted were arranged so that he could pay, and leave. He had still brought the large amount with him, despite not planning on paying it. But he was smart, and wanted to make sure he got what he had come for. But if it went well, then he'd be doing himself, and the Hawaiian dealer a favor. He said he wanted a release. And he's get it. As he walked down the final hall, he replaced the gun into his pocket, making sure it was well-concealed. He raised his hand to the door, and knocked twice. * * * * * * He turned his head, looking a little startled. For some reason, he felt uneasy about what was to transpire. He looked to the dusty wall clock he had hanging. The buyer was early. He tied his robe around him, and picked up the box of drugs he was about to administer. He heard the knock again, and set the box down on the counter. He opened the door to see him standing there, with the same hollow expression he always wore. The facade had been getting a little old, but he didn't really know if it was one. 'You're a little early.' 'Does that mean your un-prepared?' 'Oh no, I have what you wanted. I just didn't expect you early.' 'Good. So where is it?' 'Well, you have enough payment, right?' 'Do you doubt me?' 'No, but I don't trust you.' 'Clever man. Here, this should more than cover the price you told me.' He grabbed the wad of money, but the man didn't let go of it yet. He started to pull on it, to get it out of his hand, but he didn't let go. 'First, give me mine.' He didn't release at first, but let his hand down with a sigh. He turned to walk to get the box. But, he heard the man walk behind him. 'You can wait here you know.' 'No thanks.' He sighed again, as he continued into the kitchen. He placed his hands on the box, and BANG His body fell to the ceramic floor, a small amount of smoke pouring out of his head. He got his release. * * * * * * She walked back outside, holding the cup of coffee in her hand. She didn't want it, nor did she have the desire to drink it now that she had it. Maybe he'd want it when he came out. Still trying to figure out an approach, she leaned against her car. She'd been tracking his movements for a day or so, taking note of different habits he'd accustomed. One which caused problems the whole time, was his reclusiveness. She did hope, however, that it didn't rub off. Despite the hatred, or contempt she portrayed, the purest parts of her wanted to see him. Not because she was seeking solace, or trying to make some cliche amends. But because she needed to see him. Because without him, she realized how lost she became. She just needed to get through Marcus first. If all went well, he wouldn't be too hard a shell to crack. Letting the cup fall against the curb, she walked down to the cross-walk. * * * * * * He made sure that the gun was secure in Kalela's hand. If Rigor Mortis set in wrong, then the gun's position might shift wrong. He didn't plan on anyone caring about the dealer's death, but he had to make sure he couldn't be accused if it did. He pulled out his handkerchief, and wiped his brow with it. Despite all this, he gave a slight grin. There was nothing more gratifying than finishing what he had intended to do. He twisted each footstep around the body, making sure not to step in anything. He gave a light hop, to remove himself completely from the kitchen. He leaned across the counter, and pulled his box over. He still checked inside, making sure he had the supply he requested. When he realized all was done here, he left, closing the door behind him. During the long walk down the stairs, he kept looking around to see if anyone was watching. Once he left, he'd be home free. But if he was seen by one of the scarce residents who lived here, it'd be over. But he felt a bit of relief, as he finally got to the last set of steps. And then, walked out the door onto the sidewalk. Where Fiona Preston was leaning against his car. Gun in pocket, drugs in hand, tongue in cheek. * * * * * * She stared at him for a moment. Making sure it was the man to whom she sought. He had changed a bit, if it was him. His hair had grown quite long, and the color was a little darker. He also appeared in all black, down to the lenses of his sunglasses. He also had a scar on his neck, about an inch long. 'Marcus?' 'Fiona.' 'We need to talk.' 'It would appear so.' * * * * * * And suddenly, the gun in his pocket, the box full of crystal meth, and the dead body of a drug dealer no longer mattered.
