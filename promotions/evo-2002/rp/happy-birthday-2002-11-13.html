Happy Birthday...

13 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13th November 2002 Once again Asis has had a busy week, and at the command of Xander took out more than person within eVolution. The Five Star Fags were destroyed in the locker room and left sprawled on the floor, and despite not actually doing much, Asis and Jinx successfully proceeded into the final of the Tag Team Tournament. A challenge was made by Scott Frost to Asis, any match that Asis could think up Frost would be willing to take him on in. And while Asis has probably got the thought of Tag Team Gold on his mind, and coming up with sadistic matches to torture Frost in, none of this is being thought about now. Not even his upcoming match against Xavier Citizen is on his mind… (Daytime. A time most would never see Asis. Confined to darkness nearly his entire life, confining himself to spare himself the judgmental glares of other people at the expense of his unusual appearance. He hadn’t minded though, being inside in the darkness was not something that he hated, it was always a place where he had felt comfortable. But nevertheless here he is, standing in the sunlight. All alone in the graveyard, the sun shining brightly, the wind seemingly not there, the birds that had not yet disappeared because of winter, happily singing away in the trees. And within this picturesque scene stands a man most refer to as a monster or a freak. “The Demonic” stands dressed in his usual black attire towering over a recently planted tombstone, just staring at it. The tombstone reads, Hannah Samson Born 8/1/60 Died 15/3/02 I know I haven’t come to see you… (There is an obvious look of pain on Asis’ face, as he seemingly can’t finish what he started to say. The emotion of being at his Mother’s grave is something that is too hard for him to bare, even for a man who seems to have frozen away all emotions except for malice and hatred. A tear runs down his cheek as his body begins to violently tremble, but not from the usual outbursts of rage that have become regular in Asis’ life, but from sadness. He shakes until he cannot support his own body anymore and he drops to his knees in front of his Mother’s grave, tears streaming down his face. Eventually he regains enough of his composure to get back to his feet, although he is still crying.) Mum I know I haven’t come to see you since, well since it happened. Listen to me it’s as if I think you can actually hear me. To be honest Mum I never believed in God, heaven or any of that. I know you did, but I don’t suppose that being the way I am really helped inspire any faith in me of the divine being. I wish I could’ve come to your funeral. As much as it would’ve been difficult for me to say my final goodbyes to you, I would’ve rather had the choice of doing it. But no doubt he would’ve been there and I wouldn’t have been able to be there without tearing him apart. Knowing that the man that took my Mother from me, the one person in this world that understood me, he took away and is lying in a grave in front of me. I didn’t even know that it was him that had killed you then… (Asis bows his head again, holding on this time not breaking down, just obviously thinking, tears still streaming down his face.) I went to see he him not so long ago, visited him in prison. I’m not sure he’s doing so well but he’s not been killed yet so he can’t be all that bad. I promised him I’d share the same space as him again one day and I’m pretty sure it’s going to happen. I know you wouldn’t want it to, but to be honest Mum you didn’t make any effort to get in contact with me for eight years before you died, you didn’t exactly have the greatest influence on my life after you left me and Dad. Did you even know what I do now? I wouldn’t be surprised if you didn’t. Why did you leave Mum? Was it because of me? I know I wasn’t the easiest of kids to bring up, but I tried Mum, I tried so hard. But you don’t know what it was like to be constantly teased, not only because of your appearance, but because of your life as well. I tried so long and so hard Mum I really did and I’m sorry that I made you go away. Sorry I made you leave for another man. Sorry I made you leave for another man that killed you. Im sorry Mum, I’m SORRY! …I’ll make him pay. (The next destination is a pleasant looking suburban home, somewhere just outside of Chicago. The house looks nice from the outside, the kind of place that looks more expensive than it really is. It’s slightly darker than it was it earlier on, an ambient orange glow fills the sky as the sun sets in the background. Asis approaches the door of the house almost cautiously, and knocks. No reply comes, so again Asis knocks on the door, slightly harder this time. When still no one answers the door Asis forces his way into the house, not breaking the door of it’s hinges, but doing enough to get in. The inside of the house is dramatically different to the outside perception of it. The walls are stained a horrible yellow colour and the carpets have obviously not been cleaned for a long time. The entire house smells of a rancid mix of cigarettes, alcohol and general damp. The smell would make most people retch and although it does not effect Asis, it definitely is not pleasant. As Asis looks around the place a man stumbles out from the kitchen. He has got scruffy grey hair and looks like he hasn’t shaven for a couple of weeks. His clothes are covered old food stains and sweat marks. With a bottle of vodka in one hand and a cigarette in the other, he squints at Asis trying to make out who it is and is taken aback when he figures out who it is.) “Zak? Is that you son?” Asis’ father says squinting through a haze of a decade of alcohol abuse. Immediately when Asis’ father speaks to him, despite being six inches taller and a good eighty odd pounds heavier than his father, Asis assumes the position that he used to when he was in his teenage years, suffering beatings at his father’s hands. His hands go behind his back and he instantly stares at the floor. “What are you doing here? I haven’t seen you in…I don’t even know how long.” “Four years.” Asis timidly says, not looking up from his position. “That long. Jesus. So how have you been? Been keeping yourself out of trouble?” Those last words bring back old memories for Asis, the phrase that used to send shivers down his spine when his father said it to him when he was younger. It always meant that he knew Asis had done something wrong, and that meant that Asis would be on the receiving end of his Dad’s fist, or the nearest blunt heavy object. “Damn kid you’ve gotten big. I mean you were never a small lad, but fuck me you’ve gotten even bigger.” Asis’ father looks him up and down, as if he knows that he got be on the receiving end of a beating this time. He takes a long swig from his vodka bottle and takes his last inhale of his cigarette before throwing it on the carpet and stamping it out. For the first time since being in the house Asis looks up at his father and looks him straight in the eyes, something that he hasn’t done since he was a young boy of maybe eight years old. “You really don’t have a clue what I do in my life now do you? But then you never knew much about my life did you Dad? You only knew what you chose to know, and you only chose to know that I got in trouble a lot. And do you know what? Half of that was because I was sticking up for, stomping on the other kids for them calling you a drunken looser. Now that I see you haven’t changed your ways in four years I wonder why I respected you in the first place.” The outburst shocks Asis, as he never thought he would have the guts to say what he really felt to his father, but it shocks his father more. He nervously drains the remaining vodka from the bottle and drops it on a chair next to him, the whole time not taking his eyes off of his son. “So, have you seen your Mother recently?” The words come out of his mouth, but Asis does not hear them properly. He does not know whether it is a sick joke or whether his father honestly does not know about his ex-wife’s death. “You honestly don’t know do you Dad? She’s dead. She died eight months ago Dad. She was burnt alive in a car crash. How did you not know?” “Well after she left me I never heard from her again. Not that it matters, the bitch got what she deserved.” Despite what he feels, Asis finds he still cannot strike out at his father. Despite the years of beatings, the neglect and the fact that his father just openly in front of him said something about his Mother that he would kill anyone else for, he still has too much respect to lash out at him. Instead he turns his back on him and walks out the house, vowing to himself never to come back again. As soon as he gets out the house he roars in a way he never had done before and begins to punch and get at his Dad’s old car, smashing the windows and leaving dents in the bodywork. His Dad stands watching him out the window of the house, awe struck at what he’s seeing. Asis only stops pummelling the car when he is too weak to lift his limbs to strike at it anymore and the car itself is barely recognisable as being a car. There is no glass left and the framework is horribly bent out of shape. Asis collapses on his knees in front of his father’s house crying and struggling to breathe. Suddenly he looks up, the remaining sun’s rays glistening off the tears on his cheeks. He smiles sadistically and then runs off down an alleyway, his next destination being Denver, Colorado and Legends…
