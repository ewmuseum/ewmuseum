6 of Diamonds - Memories in the Glimpse of a Sunset

10 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/10/03 Above the world, eyes trapped on the setting sun in the distance, Jason’s mind wandered about the last few days, picking individual moments and studying them to see if he could understand what happened to him. Nothing really substantial came to mind. “So she’s still with William?” “Yeah, unfortunately. I haven’t talked to her but I was still told that they’re still together. He also said that it didn’t seem to be ending anytime soon.” “It doesn’t matter. It was my fault, and I don’t expect anything to change anytime soon. I drove Marilee away, and I don’t expect anything to get better for me now.” A silent drone ripped through the air as the plane headed towards the West coast, and back towards the life that he had chosen so many years ago. Behind him was the very city where his trek had begun, and something inside of him hoped that that’s where it would end. But he felt that an end would never come, and that he would be destined to walk the Earth without a shed of happiness. To be a drifting soul lost in the night, watching the stars in hope of a navigator to lead him. And then seeing nothing but clouds. “Why don’t you come back, Jason? For the love of God, have you ever spoken to your mother?” His mother? Once, when he was young, he had fallen off his bike and landed on his left side. Scrapes and cuts decorated his arm with blood, and he ran, crying home, searching for riddance to his pain. His mother got him in the car, and drove him to Children’s Hospital. He was given a few stitches, each one more painful then the next. He didn’t cry though. Jason fought back each of the tears, and even though a little water escaped his lids, he remained silent. And his mother, with a smile on her face and warmth in her eyes, stood there the whole time, holding his right hand. “Jesus. I haven’t talked to her since… since the day I left. How is she Francis?” Jason wanted to hurt himself for ignoring her all those years. He could imagine her, getting older by the days, and then finally finding out he was in professional wrestling of all things. Imagined her hands shaking and her eyes tearing as she watched her one and only child being beaten every time she surfed the channels. Imagined her holding a picture of the family – her, Jason, and his father, all smiling and frozen in time. He wished, at that moment, that he could see her again. “I haven’t seen her in quite awhile, but I know that she’s healthy. But, I’ll tell you, I don’t know how long I will be able to say that Jason.” “What do you mean?” He could remember her saying that it was going to be ok. That the doctor would have those stitches would be done in no time flat, and that we’d be out of here and eating a big cone of vanilla ice-cream. Jason was in pain, but he fought all of that and looked up at his mommy. Smiling and happy that she was there with him, to share her love with him forever and ever. And when he thought of her once again, a tear fell from his eye. “Jason, she’s getting old man. You don’t have much longer to be with her, and I don’t think you should be spending that time away from her, wrestling in that God damn federation. You should be with her.” Jason decided that he would go to this Legends, and be in his match, and then go back. Fuck the price. Money was meaningless to him, and he had plenty of it to go around. Why not spend it on the last pieces of hope he had left? The sun shot out its last rays of light in one fantastic burst of light before dying for the evening. Jason wiped away his tears, and fell asleep, last catching a glimpse at the six of diamonds on his tray table game of Solitaire.
