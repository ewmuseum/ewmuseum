TooLate - Dreams To Ashes

18 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/18/03 Sitting in obscurity, staring through an empty hotel room and out into the city he had once called home, Jason’s life had all gone to shit in the matter of a second. A small whisper of a word changed everything back to the way it was before, if not worse. The dreams had been raised from their dreary grave, only to be anchored back down below. In his hands, rumpled and stained with sweat, the letter was held, its fragility all but noticed in everyway. The paper, brown and delicate, had been held numerous times in the weeks that it had been created, all by the same pair of fingers. The same pair of eyes interpreted the language every evening, through the glare of a single lamp’s glow. The same soul cried out through its words, longing to embrace the essence of the being who wrote down the image of every anticipating thought he had once held. Now it was just another symbol for the lost. Jason had spent the night walking the snowy streets of the city of Boston. No direction and no destination, he wandered the voracious of a nightly city, imagining that the vapor of his breath was his dreams coming to life, only to be drowned in the cold air. Jason walked through the people, all happy and proud and warm and humble, and felt like a stranger roaming an eccentric land. He was slowly becoming welcomed again, and was slowly being the man that he was before. Now all of that was gone. Once more, life was just a big mystery to him. He was just another wanderer, waning through life and not knowing why. And of all things, it was because of someone named William. Now was that ironic or what? The phone rang, and woke Jason from his self-loathing daze. At first it was all unknown to him, as it seemed that he couldn’t remember just what that ringing symbolized. Then he decided that it wasn’t important. An answering machine picked up the incoming voice. “Jason, this is Francis. I just wanted to tell you that I’m sorry… sorry that I didn’t tell you earlier. It just never came upon me. I don’t know why. But I just called to see if we can talk somewhere else. I tell you everything. Tomorrow at eight at The Junkyard. I’m sorry Jason.” Jason heard the voice of his friend disappear. He took the letter, written by hand by the woman who was once his fiancée, and brought it down to the kiss of a lighter’s flame. The paper, blazing, fell to the carpet and disintegrated into nothing. Ashes stained into a plush carpet. And tears to wash in with it.
