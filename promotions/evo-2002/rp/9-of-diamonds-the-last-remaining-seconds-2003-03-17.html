9 of Diamonds - The Last Remaining Seconds

17 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/17/03 Slightly hung over and blankly staring at his reflection in the window, Jason rode the second flight from Boston that he had taken within the last few weeks. Just like last time, he had done nothing, but this time he felt so much worse. The last flight his hope was still intact, and he still had the chance to turn it all around. Now he had witnessed first hand the lie that his hope had been all along. Jason sat, playing the same Solitaire game as last time, and just like last time, recollections of the vague yet altogether familiar memories that drowned away all his desires. Sometimes he wished they would go away and leave his wandering soul alone. Others he wished he could be as warm as he was then again. One night in particular. Jason had just turned seventeen, and it was New Year’s Eve. Of all the days it could have been, huh, Jason? Marilee had been with him all day, traveling all over the city and to every little corner shopping and watching and eating and having an all around good day. Jason looked into her eyes, and could remembering thanking God every time he saw the whiteness of her smiling teeth and felt the touch of her smooth skin. Jason loved her more then words could express, and life was forever filled with happiness, never to die. And now I can smell the stench of death. That was the first night he made love to her, in her empty home, on her queen sized bed that her parents had no problem affording once so ever. He had always imagined losing his virginity would be more of a party. This was completely passionate, and through it all he wasn’t thinking of how good she was or how many times he could get off. Jason was only thinking about her, and realizing that she was apart of him. Nothing would change that. Except you. With only a little while left before the midnight hour, Jason and Marilee went, hand in hand, across the city to where their families were celebrating at Jason’s house. All his friends and relatives were there, waiting to say goodbye to another year, and welcome the new one with open arms. Champagne and cigars and party poppers and every other holiday luxury littered the already crowded house. The two went out onto the porch, and counted down the last remaining seconds in each other’s arms with the Boston Pops echoing through the city air. The ball in New York City fell on a million televisions. The usual explosion of voices followed, and song started up. Fireworks lit p the night sky, and glowed in her eyes as she stared at them. They kissed, neon red and green and blue and white shining on their bodies, giving the moment the right surroundings. Jason closed his eyes, and felt her lips against his. Felt her heart beat ripple through his body. He wanted to feel her in his arms again, and hold her closer then he ever had before, and be with her once. Just one more time. The nine of diamonds felt a tear drop to its face. And the plane continued on, leaving the past to die peacefully.
