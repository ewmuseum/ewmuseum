Why?

29 July 2003

Date: 2003-07-29 Apoligies? I didn't want to do this and that was something they would have to realise. 'No comment' I screamed down the phone as I began to lose my rag in the heat of the moment. It must have been the tenth call in the past hour, all from reporters looking to understand just why I had been lying to MY fans for so long. The thing people wern't understanding was this was as much a forced decision, as much as being one I chose to make. In life there are somethings that just matter a damn sight more than wrestling. A few years ago upon leaving FAW I took some time out to rest my weary limbs and spend some 'quality time' with the family which was growing up without me. Jordan was five years old now. Daddys little solider was now walking around the planet like a fully fledged adult in the making. I would often watch him play and wonder just what he would become, then at the same time I would wonder in the same breath just what it would take. Children were an expensive luxury. Sure I had the money; a number of cheques in the bank from days gone by showed that Jordan would not go wanting in his life time in that sense. College was a mere drop in the ocean should he chose to go in that direction, while in the mean time he would undoubtably feel the fruits of my labour. The fire engine was the start, in years to come I knew no matter how strict I would try to be, there would always be the temptation to spoil Jordan, and any others that should follow on behind him. Children though were more than just financhially expensive, they were emotionally expensive. I remembered how my father would toil with his temper on a daily basis thanks to my rebellious streak against authority. In many ways though I had already had an insight into father hood. My father served in the services for 25 years; thats only a few years less than amount I have tormented this planet for. During that time me, my mother and my brother were left to cope. It wasn't his choice to go away to sea and leave us for years on end, it was his very livelihood which demanded such commitment. My brother, Ryan, was and still is very much my little brother. Its amazing that despite the fact he is nineteen now, I still continue to refer to him as my lesser. Ryan is my baby brother and he knows no amount of facial hair or adultly behaviour will change that. I remember when he signed for UWF; it was a day of huge celebration in the Mason household. At seventeen Ryan had already been signed to his first promotion, although the fact that he had been ear marked at the tender age of eleven meant it was somewhat inevitable to the same extent. I remember the feeling of pride which penetrated right through my body and the sheer feeling of excitement about what the future might hold. My father was away at the time, but he had made sure to remind me of my duties on that day. 'Sean your next in command' he had said, 'be sure to step into my shoes' I didn't need to be asked twice, while my father was away I made sure to step into his very shoes and attempt to provide the solution to the fatherly void. Since then its become a habit. Ryans fortunate enough to have two fatherly figures around him and in honesty he loves it. Having achieved so much in the wrestling ring its flattering, if not slightly expected, that he looks up to me. I remember when he signed for UWF, he pulled me to one side and asked for some advice. I wasn't shy and I gave him what advice I could; it just turns out that he needs more from me, like I said children can be an expensive business. I've been fortunate in my career, in the sense that injuries have been few and fair between. Perhaps I was even more fortunate in the sense, that during my break through years they were non-existant. At the age of nineteen, nobody should have suffered two broken ankles; yet my brother had been delt this very cruel hand in life. Ryan was on track for the very top, the makings of a new superstar many said, but no much talent you have, its hard to make a name for yourself on one leg. The boy had been so unfortunate. Instead of gracing many terraces watching on at awe at my brother in action, of late I have been perfecting my bedside manner. At nineteen Ryan just didn't deserve to have gone through the emotional rollercoaster. Nevertheless us Mason's are fighters and given half the chance we milk every moment for its last ounce of worth. All Ryan needed was half a chance to prove himself and I knew he would take it with both hands. Forget his medical problems of old Ryan was looking forward, its just a shame some people were looking back. Why did I lie to my fans? No, no, theres a more important why before you get that far, that being; why Dan Sommers? Why?
