Dimished.

17 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-10-17 The empty bottle of Jack Daniels bounced off the purple carpet and quickly came to rest under my feet. I never drank; I couldn’t stand to drink the intoxicating liquids served up by a barman who in reality knew about as much as I do about serving up the popular social drinks. What had Cameron Griffin in store for me? I had been a pawn for some time now, ever since I signed my name on the tattered contract it was laminated by his desire to make me suffer. What he didn’t know was the fact that in signing the contract I had cleverly masterminded a plan to swipe the Cell Division from his grasp. He always seemed like the blind type. Blinded by his own bullshit. He was like a puppeteer picking our very moves to his desire. I was sick of acting the way he wanted me to, sick of being a convenience for him. So the question remains, even though I wasn’t as much as a social drinking, why was I drinking? Because I’ve lived with this excruciating pain for the last week. I even came to hate my very own reflection. I didn’t look in anything which reflected my scarred face. The scars were deep, but not as deep as the emotional scars. I refused to look in the mirror. I’d even smashed the bathroom mirror with a lead pipe to avoid accidentally seeing my reflection staring coldly back at me. Then, three subtle knocks interrupted my train of thought. I raised my voice and told the visitor to enter. The wooden door slowly creaked open and a slim, young girl stepped in. It was Carmen. She gazed coldly at him as she walked slowly into the room with just enough living space for my convenience. She wore a purple laced dress as if she had plans for a romantic evening with some random man. I waved at a nearby leather chair which she was much obliged to rest on, taking a clean whiff of me breath. She gave me another solid stare before speaking timidly, “Been drinking?” I nodded slowly, unsurprisingly. “Listen Terlayne, I... “She started, staring into my cold blue eyes, then fiddling with her long nails. “I don’t like seing you like thjs you know.” She continued as if I hadn’t been self conscious at the time. My head continued to throb, her attention now focused on the empty Jack Daniels glass. I fell to one side of the chair; I didn’t want anyone to see me in this condition, especially her. “I think you should leave,” I replied with no knowledge of the reasons for her visit. “I think that would be best too,” she replied, rising slowly from the chair. She walked by the door and placed her hand on the door knob before coming to a halt. “See you tomorrow, right?” I nodded slowly once again and she was gone.
