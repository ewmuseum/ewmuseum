Sunlight Or Flames

19 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/19/02 Outside, wandering in a maze of trees and bushes, roamed the creatures of the night. Another individual traveling with them was the wind, pushing through anything that stood in its way, chilling them down to their bones. The moonlight dimly lit their path, but was more a torment then an actual source of light. It just seemed to be smiling, laughing at the little ones that looked to it for guidance. But all it did was grin its grin, and buckle its chin. God damn moon. The moon reflection hit the windshield of the car. That reflection shined into the hazel eyes of Jason, casting an image of magnificence that would eventually go unnoticed by the reluctant nomad. In fact, he paid no attention to the white gleam illuminating his vehicle. Too many thoughts contemplated to pay any attention to a glowing rock in the heavens. The surrounding forest, blemished by the darkness and hiding in the gloomy shadows, watched as Jason’s car ripped down the empty, desolate road. Occasionally, Jason would see some signs of life as he passed by a house or a gas station, but for the most part it was only him and the nightly darkness. The headlights were the torches that carried our traveler through the sinister setting. His eyes guided him towards the light of the tomb. The hero would reach the end, and come out clean on the other side. The only problem was… “What light will that be shining on the other side?” The words escaped his lips. And then they were thought over. Just what light would shine? Would it be the sun, warming and forgiving, providing the life bearing light that Jason needed? Or would it be the flames of Hell, ablaze in the abyss of a nightmare far too unbearable to imagine? He could feel the warmth of the heavenly star, yet he could smell the stench of sulfur in the air. The future had always been a problem that Jason had to deal with every moment of his depressing life. But now it was a matter of keeping control of his life. Now he had to win. He didn’t have a choice anymore. It was once about wanting to win. Wanting to taste victory. It was only a desire. Now it was a necessity for survival. He walked in the shadows, his image a beckoning silhouette of a monster. He watched through the eyes of an aura filled with hatred and evilness. He was there, staring at his back, waiting for that moment of weakness. Then he would come out, and slit his throat. Jason would be over, and he would control everything. Failure was not an option. Jason didn’t know what would occur if he were to fail. Or maybe it was because he didn’t want to know. A fate worst then death. That’s what he thought it would be. Would he be thrown into his own dark forest, ending up as one of the nightly creatures? Would he stand in the shadows, staring at the desolate road that use to be his life? Would he see another car traveling that road, with another driver behind the wheel? Was this frightening prediction just a nightmare, or was it a reality that waited for Jason at the end of the tunnel? Sunlight or flames? Everything or nothing? The moon disappeared behind a cloud, traveling with the wind on its back. The darkness enveloped the world. The forest became nothing but black. Jason’s eyes saw nothing but the shifty yellow streaks that made up the center of the road. He tried to listen to the echo of his car as he drove by, but the engine drowned all that out. He tried to close his eyes and listen to the wind in his mind, but fear gripped him and pulled him back. He tried to breathe serenely, but the stench of sulfur was too effective.
