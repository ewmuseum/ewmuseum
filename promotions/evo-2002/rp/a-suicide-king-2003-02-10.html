A Suicide King

10 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/10/03 With a deck of cards in his hands, Jason sat at the bar’s table, looking out the window and shuffling at the same time. Every now and then a passerby would stare at Jason, either recognizing his face from television or just wondering what kind of man would sit in such a position, but Jason wouldn’t glance at them. He wouldn’t acknowledge their existence with a friendly smile or a suspicious glare. He would just let them on their way, and continue to shuffle and bridge the cards. A bottle of beer sat on the edge of the table, ever so slowly getting warmer and warmer, waiting for just a sip by someone interested. The dim lighting gave the atmosphere of despair that was no doubt apart of Jason’s mood. Inside his disillusioned mind, a swarm of thoughts blocked out all existence of the world. There was only him, his thoughts, and a deck of cards, face down. It seems that I have been here too long, waiting for the wind to change and then wondering why it still pushes me away. But now, the breeze slows, and it seems, if only for a moment, that the times are about to change. My voice echoes through the frill air, wandering into the canyons of my misery, shattering the walls away. I approach the edge, staring down into the hazy mist of was once my agony, and now I can see the river below, flowing away through the ricochets of time. I dive in, prepared to follow the water all the way. But I ask myself. Is this my suicide, or is it my only way to sovereignty? The door swings up, the wind roaming through, giving the room sudden life. A man, face young and clothes clean, walked into the dim lighting and stared forward. His expressionless mouth was the only part of him truly visible, a shadow casting down from the hat worn on top of his black hair. As he took the hat into his hand, the light rushed over his eyes like a wave of lost hope. His glare ripped a hole through anyone who saw him watching the fear behind their eyes. Many look away, afraid to feel the touch of his angered soul any longer. All except the man who didn’t stare at him in the first place, sitting in his seat, shuffling a deck of cards like there was no tomorrow. A smile formed on the face of the mysterious stranger standing in the doorway, as he walked over to Jason and sat down behind him, almost appearing to be back to back with the forsaken man. If anyone had been looking at the two of them, they would have realized the mirror image taking place before their very eyes. Two men, almost identical except for the slight beard on the smiling face that could pass for the same man. It almost seemed to be a nightmare come true. “When was the last we saw each other, Jason? It’s been too long.” Jason sat silent, uncaring and weary of the world, his hands shuffling the cards. Conversations sprang up from all around, none of it worth the two cents of a man sitting alone. He didn’t care for their words or their thoughts. Jason was waiting for something better. Something that would wake them all up. Maybe that something was sitting directly behind him. “What does it matter? It’s good to see you again old boy. We should really do this more often.” Again, no response. No reaction. No concern once so ever. “I have been watching you some more, Jason. I’ll tell you, I like what I see. And yet I still have this sense of… I don’t know. Emptiness, I guess. You are trying so God damned hard to change, but part of you knows that it’s useless.” The sounds of another bridge echo from the tabletop. The beer remains untouched and waiting. The ears of loneliness are hearing, but unresponsive and unemotional. They search for something, yet don’t move to find it. “I know that you’d never accept this. Nor could I in your place. But sometimes, Jason, you just have to believe in the end. You have to take what life gives you, with no choice at all. Sometimes you have to close your eyes and dive right in.” Memory comes into play, and Jason is thrown back to the thoughts of the past. Standing on the edge, looking down below, and feeling the wind carry you down. The final splash of life before the blackness that waits. It was there, and maybe it was his only true way out. “Suicide may be on your mind Jason, but it isn’t on my mind. I don’t need that escape. I’ll make my own. I’ll tunnel my way out of this vengeful trap, into the sunlight of the long since setting day. I will raise my hands to the sky, and thrust out two middle fingers, skin glistening in the light. I will smile, and know that you have failed me.” Jason stopped shuffling, and started putting the cards down face up. One at a time. “My naked body will be warm. My empty soul filled with joy and anticipation. My mind clear and ready.” And one at a time, the face of the king of hearts repeated, over and over again. “You will be through. Forgotten. Abandoned like an inept leaf, adrift in the wind, waiting to finally rot away.” And still, way past the universal fifty-two, the expressionless face of the suicide king kept coming and coming. Again. “You will finally be over.” And again. “You will have finally lost.” And again. “And this soul will finally be mine.” “Last call!” Jason shot up from the table, the sounds of a bartender waking him from the dream. A dozen or so people lifted their drinks, and downed them quickly and wordlessly. The beer glass, which had been untouched and slowly dying, was now empty. Jason’s memory told him he hadn’t touched the drink, yet it was gone. Just another lost entity of Jason’s miserable past. Just as he was about to get up, he looked down at the cards. All of them were face down, except for two that were perfectly balanced back to back. The king of spades and the king of hearts. Jason looked down, and realized that he was looking into a mirror image taking place right before his very eyes. Except for the color, one black like an empty soul and the other as red as bleeding heart, they were almost the same. Almost the same body, but two different souls. “When was the last time we saw each other, Jay? Not long enough.” Jason picked up the deck of cards, and walked out into the dying day.
