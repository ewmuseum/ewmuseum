I am Outblaze, and this is My Time.

2 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2003-10-02 A storm like no other has been brewing for days. The beauty of the red and orange sunset has been replaced by rolling walls of gray. The critters of the night have silenced, calmly and patiently awaiting the storm. Hell knows the city could use it. It's been dry for weeks on end. (the camera pans the sky, showing the dire appearance of the atmosphere above and around.) The wind bends and twists tree branches in every direction not meant to be bent in. A streak of white lightning rips across the sky and down to the surface off in the distance. Slowly a rolling thunder follows behind, ending with an amazing clash. Suddenly, as if on cue, gigantic rain drops pummel the ground from the grey walls above. (the camera scans the surface and focuses on a figure standing on a wooden and weathered bridge. the bridge crosses a creek of sorts. slightly larger and more restless than the average.) The figure stands, face up to the sky, letting the rain drops fall upon him invitingly. He is clad in dark clothing consisting of black leather pants with red flare stripes, motocross style buckle boots and a black form fitting shirt. He has an outter covering of a full length black trenchcoat. The tail of the coat whips about violently in the wind as the figure turns to the camera. (the camera zooms inward for a closer look.) The man pulls a pack of cigarettes from an inside pocket of the trenchcoat. He opens the pack and studies it for a moment before pulling one from inside and placing it between his lips. He struggles to light a match in the pounding rain. Finally one lights long enough to set the cigarette tip ablaze. The man takes a long drag and exhales the smoke slowly. Next, he turns to the camera as if to speak... 'Not many people have seen my face and took notice to who or what I am. It's nothing to hold against them, fore they do not know me. Beyond that, it is understandable that they are too engrossed in their own lives to take notice of anyone else other than themselves. It's human nature. A natural law of sorts. For years now, I have been struggling to find myself. After years of horrid memories and intolerable abusing, it is time to stake my place somewhere. I see it as time to make myself known. Let the world see the outward blaze of glory and meaning within. It's time to set a fire so large that in can be seen from even the dimmest of stars in the sky. You see, I learned a lot as boy. I took a lot of crap as a boy. I was always under attack as a boy. I learned the art of loss as a young man. I learn the art of survival as a young man. I lived within a dream as a man. I lived in a dream of dominance and strength. I learned to live oblivious to all the wrong and flaws within my life. I've survived numerous sloppy attempts at suicide. I've survived a long duration of pain and self-blame. I've survived an awakening. I've learned to open my eyes and see the world around me in its truest of forms. I've learned to see the black and white and forget about the non-existant grey. I've learned to set a path. A path that has lead me here. Tired of the mental punishment dealt to me by myself and others, I've gathered a sense of determination. Determined to show the world that I am no longer a boy. I've grown from a young man. And I have a point to make and hold. I will become a standard of sorts. A principal to which people adhere by. An example of dominance and superiority. You see, I've learned one big thing in life. You beat a man and you are a fluke. You beat many men and you are a champion. You beat everyone and you are a legend. I've decided its time to climb from my grave that I've so blindly dug, and dust myself off. It's time to blow some minds. It's my time now. A legend in the making. A threat amongst the shadows. I am Outblaze. And this is my time.' Another streak of lightning rips the sky as Outblaze tosses his cigarette into the creek, turns his back, and walks away. (the camera watches on as Outblaze disappears into the mists of rain. his trenchcoat and hair flailing in the wind. he never turns back.)
