Forgotten Years - III

31 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: New Year's Eve The present contains nothing more than the past, and what is found in the effect was already in the cause. - Henri Bergson The sounds of fun rang from the streets of the city below. A holiday was being celebrated by all those worthy enough to have lives. Through the misty window if a second floor hotel room, two eyes stared down mournfully watching the people living their worlds. Usually, he would either be cursing these people to an entirety in Hell, or pitying them for being blind to the reality of life and death, or wishing that he could be apart of the blissful paradise. Today, his mind was set elsewhere. All because of a letter that he never thought he would receive. The envelope was resting on the table, bathed in darkness and almost glowing. Unopened, it almost seemed to beg for a worthy observer to take in its words and its message. But Jason wasn’t prepared to take on such a role. In his past, there had been only happiness. No grief or misery. Only a sense of everything blocked by nothing. There had been the world at his feet, and the love of her in his heart. The rage building inside of him was too silent to be heard. Its existence was completely unknown to him. He stood there, reminiscing over what he left behind. The mood set upon his mind was darkened by the setting sun. And the realization that the letter sat there, waiting to be unlocked and read. But he was afraid. Afraid to see if that little boy was still alive inside him, waiting for something to come along and give him back his hope. Afraid to realize that this heartless demon of a man was all that was left. Afraid that whatever lay inside that white packet was only a final nail into his already descending coffin. His head slowly turned around, catching the light from the street lamps below on his face. His eyes, two burning candles waiting to be used, glared at the desk standing in the distant corner. His breath was calm, but somewhat quick. His heart raced with the thoughts of the hidden words within its essence. His mind was not clear in the slightest, but rather an ocean of a million thoughts. Some scared him. Some gave him hope. But mostly, they confused him. Sometimes, he figured, the anticipation is worse then the actual action.
