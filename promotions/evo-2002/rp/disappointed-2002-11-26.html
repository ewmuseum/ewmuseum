Disappointed

26 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: The Day before Thanksgiving Disappointed and somewhat weary of the days to come, Jason walked through the process of another long day in his fleeting life. The snow fell to the ground like little grains of salt from the clouds. The cold Canada air created swirls of mist above the heads of the many travelers. They rose into the nightly air, somewhat glowing in the artificial neon streetlights, and disappeared through the dark sky. Jason’s feet collected the snow accumulating on the sidewalks as he marched through. His bag banged against his waist with each step. His body was numb, despite the jacket and extra layer of clothing. The harsh reality was catching up with him a little faster then he would have liked. But then again, that’s the way it usually was. A light shinning through a decorated window cast a gleam into the streets. As he neared it, his curiosity took his mind off his depression and his cold. Shadows danced in the snow. Music ran through the air, adding to the song of the breeze and the wheels of cars whooshing through slush. A rhythm of heart beats emanated, for Jason was sort of hoping that whatever this was, he could walk in and take a seat. A sign saying “Jake’s Pub” glowed with red and flashing white. The windows were fairly dirty. The music had been nothing more then an old and rusty song playing from an aged juke box standing alone in the corner. The shadows were just of men playing pool next to a window, a fluorescent light blinking in and out of life above their heads. It wasn’t what Jason had hoped for. Not in the slightest. The doorknob, cold and squeaky, reluctantly turned as Jason’s rotated it. The door threw itself open as the wind rushed through. Jason stepped in, a few heads turning to see the stranger take his first steps into a new territory. Suddenly, he wondered why the hell he came in. His legs and jacket dripped with melting snow. His face recovered as the warmth filled him. His red skin returned to its pale white. But none of that mattered. No one noticed, and Jason truly didn’t care. His first steps toward the empty table in the back were another action that went unnoticed. The broad shoulders of a few Canadians greeted him as he walked through. The red carpet below his feet was decorated in brown half melted ice. The television set showed some random news broadcast, muted. Jason tossed his bag to the opposite side, and took his jacket off in one quick unzip. A bartender of some kind walked over to him, dressed in a soiled and wet red plaid shirt. All he asked for was a beer. Cigarette smoked roamed through the air. A bathroom door stood next to the table, stains embellishing its brown skin. The table had many scratches in it, ranging from ‘Class of 2000’ to ‘Fuck you Paul’ to ‘For Lesley, Call [detail withheld]’. A few drops of any random alcohol could be found when one looked at any section of the table. The slightly husky bartender dropped the glass of bear in front of Jason with a light smash against the table and a splash all over the table. Jason smiled a sarcastic grin, nodded a thank you, and glared at his back as he walked away. Jason took a sip, and grimaced as he tasted its bitterness. He was prepared to throw the glass down, but decided against it. He didn’t want to create another reason for him to be treated with hatred by all the people inside. Tomorrow was Thanksgiving. Jason could remember a few years back. A holiday that he could remember celebrating with loved ones. Sitting down in a warm home. Sitting at a nicely decorated dining table. The smell of turkey and potatoes and stuffing and pie hanging in the air. Love warming the heart. A smile glowing eternally on his face. And now where was he? “Happy Thanksgiving.” The words meant nothing to him when he said them. They only reminded him of how disappointed he was.
