Jack of Diamonds - Take A Look

29 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/29/03 Las Vegas The glamorous city herself, so filled with life and death. Money to be thrown around. Lives to be made. Lives to be ruined. Sins to be fulfilled. A million and one different ways to end up with nothing around every corner, beckoning you and drawing you into its gripping trap. The desert night rapt the sparkling city, but none were aware of its existence. This was when Las Vegas was alive, with the sounds of laughter and catcalls and swearing and honking filling the air. Women barely wearing anything at all and men with rings on their fingers carrying gems the size of golf balls walked the sidewalks, smiles glaring on their glowingly silken faces. Jason walked the streets with them, faking a smile of his own to go along with their freely manner. No real emotion was behind the muscles of his mouth, as there was no real thought roaming through his mind. Stars glow in the sky above him, or maybe it was the images reflecting from some nearby spectacle of lights, but none the less, Jason saw them. Jealously of the divine specs of sand against the night sky came into thought, as part of him damned them. Didn’t have to worry about such things as happiness. They didn’t have plans to make or dreams to live out. They were there only to glow in their heavenly seats, radiant for the rest of time. The thought made Jason feel worse then before, yet his feet constantly trotted along, the same sneakers he had worn during his most recent win still on his feet. Sounds of familiar spaces become resonant in his mind’s empty thoughts, hearing her voice over the sounds of a sin city. “I love you, Jason. I love you. I’m sorry.” Years bleed away from contemplation, as the reality of where he is comes back to him. His eyes look up to the MGM, not only his hotel and provider of his nightly drinks, but the place where his first title defense would take place, against the same man that he had pinned to win the championship. He offered the rematch, but it definitely didn’t say anywhere in the rule book that he had to like it. Stop right here. Now wait. Standing their, leaning up against the building, was a man with a deck of cards, dirty and tired. He looked like he hadn’t lived in a home for years. He looked up, and stared into Jason’s face. He took the top card off the pile, and threw it at Jason. The jack of diamonds. “You…. you will see da world before you, my man. Oh, God! You will be happy. Lordy lord.” “Umm, thanks.” Jason said, stepping inside quickly before the man could say anything else. Open the entrance. Penetrate. Jason knew that he should sleep right then and there, but part of him knew that sleep was going to be only a memory on that evening. Jason’s envisioned a nice, quiet night staring out his window, eating some ordered food from the table and mesmerizing himself in the patterns of lights and the fountains dancing in the wind. He knew he stood in one of the dirtiest cities in the world, but dirty didn’t necessarily mean ugly. But, despite all that, Jason knew he wouldn’t be sleeping anytime soon. Hit the button. Wait to ride. Staring at himself in the golden doors of the elevator, Jason laughed, wondering how his life could have been so fucked up yet be so pleasing. How anything could be such a conflict to itself boggled his mind, making him imagine just what the world would be like if he hadn’t gone back to Boston and reminded himself of the world he shitted away in the past. Doors open. Step inside. But none of it mattered, for Christ sakes. None of it. Jason leaned up against the back of the elevator, taking him up to his room, where he would engage in a blissful mix of insomnia, drunkenness, and self pity. He was fully aware of the event only hours away, and of the importance. But something within made him understand that nothing mattered. Christ almighty, none of it mattered. Exit to your story. Leave behind. Reaching Jason’s floor, the elevator came to a halt, opening for its guest and waiting for him to exit before continuing with its daily job. Jason wanted to close his eyes, and feel the kiss of air, as he accidentally stepped out of a window and plummeted to his death upon the concrete, wear some spectators would gossip, smoking their cigarettes and drinking their cocktails. Instead, he stepped onto the red carpeting. He walked to his room. Twist the key. Now rewind. Jason threw himself onto the bed, and before he even knew it, he was trying to get to sleep. Trying being the key word in that last phrasing. His thoughts ripped through him, his eyes flitching behind his close lids, as his nightmares came to him, even without the expected necessity of sleep to go along with them. In that room, lying in that bed, Jason realized the three stages of time all at once. The past – clogging his soul and creating a mix of blood and tears that he wished he could wash away with a simple. The present – trapped within a sleepy soul, choking on its own desolation and misfortune of being trapped in the body of this waste of life. The future – well, unknown. As the future should always be. Hear the knock. The door shook. Jason opened his eyes, as he heard the knock on the door. Probably room service. He’s seen one too many messed up rooms in this life on the road, this wouldn’t have been anything special or anything new. He’d just get up, solve the problem, and go back to his so-called sleep. “Well, whoever this is, you have the wrong room.” Jason said, not looking up until his sentence was over. And with your eyes. Take a look. And then when he saw Marilee, her eyes already filling with tears and her smile shining through, his heart stopped beating. There she was.
