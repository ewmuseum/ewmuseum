Matt Hunt — eVo career history

Matt Hunt Career History Matthew Steven Hunt was born into a English upper-class family on April 7th 1969 in Plymouth, Southern England. His father, Carl, is an executive within his father’s (Matthew’s grandfather’s) contracting company (the largest in South-West England and third largest in England) and his Mother, Christina, works as a head nurse at the local hospital. He also has an older brother called Andrew who is five years his senior and he continues the family tradition of working in the family business alongside his father and grandfather. Sister Gina is two years younger and works as a freelance photographer for the numerous local and regional newspapers. Although the family construction company has brought wealth to Matthew's life, he felt that he wanted a life on the other side of the fence, so to speak. Despite the option of all the money in the world, he politely refused the financial assistance and wanted to prove to himself that he could make it “on his own”. The family accepted his wishes and gave Matthew all the emotional support. Matthew - or Matt for short - is married to Manchester-born Stephanie and has two children; Carl (4) and Jessica (7). The three of them currently reside in a New York suburb after deciding to move to the United States, as Matt’s wrestling career made the leap across the pond from Europe. At the Southway Comprehensive School , Matt excelled in his favourite subject; English Literature. He was the polite and well-mannered student, but often had his moments where he would act the class clown to get attention. He was also the sportsman, although he only had eyes for one sport in particular and it wasn’t wrestling…despite the antics of Giant Haystacks and Big Daddy often grabbing his eye on Saturday Mornings. Matt’s main love in life was football, or soccer, and often used his size advantage (he was 14st at aged 17) on the pitch. Of course, people debated whether he should chase his dream of following in his hero’s – English Football Captain Bobby Moore - footsteps and become a professional footballer. By the time he left the secondary school, Matt was a hefty 17st and stood at 6’4” and had his heart set on playing professional football. However, the top English clubs in the area turned him down because of his abnormal bulk and his dreams of playing the field were slowly fading quickly. At that point - mid-1988 - Arthur Hunt's (Matthew's Grandfather) construction company had branched out into other European countries, such as the Scandinavian region and Germany. More importantly, a larger main office was developed and completed in Manchester – which would become the firm’s headquarters. At this father’s request, Carl and his family were to move to so he could work in the Manchester branch. This is where Matthews’s life took a different direction from where he had expected. Aged 19, Matt had chosen not to pursue his education and roamed from job to job, basically gaining experience. The offer to take a position in the family business was presented every so often, but Matt continued to decline the offer….claiming that he wasn’t the corporate suit type. To cement his independence, Matt moved into his own apartment on the outskirts of Manchester and continued to jump from job to job – ranging from working in a supermarket storeroom to a chimney sweep in the rough areas of inner-city Manchester. Upon glancing for yet another new line of employment, Matt came across an advertisement for a small-regional based wrestling school. For some strange reason, the idea of becoming a professional wrestler seemed to click and after days of pondering the thought, he eventually enrolled…much to the amusement of his friends and surprise of his family. But to Matt, it made sense. He was a big man – standing 6’5” and weighing 260lbs – what other profession would you see guys of that size? He always kept himself in good shape and watched what he ate – drilled into him from such a young age by his Mother. So he embarked on this new career change, wondering if he was making a big mistake or taking an calculated risk. It was exactly plain sailing for the young big tree, he had a lot to learn and often returned to his empty apartment full of cuts and bruises that were sure to last a good few months. At first he was a slow starter, but eventually he got the hang of it…even if it wasn’t for the head trainer – Sean Maloney – to give him a kick up the backside every now and then. During the years that Matt spent at the school, he became friendly with Sean Maloney and his family and by the time that he eventually left, he was honed as Maloney’s star pupil…one tipped as the future of wrestling. Although Matt, being the modest fellow he was at the time, simply shrugged off the compliments and enjoyed the moment. He was obviously enjoying it way too much as he got together with Sean’s eldest daughter Stephanie – who was around the same age as Matt. The two dated for well over a year before she moved in with him soon after. The Maloney family had no problem with this, as they had known Matt for a considerable amount of time and knew him pretty well. With Matt now in a serious relationship and living a small apartment, it was time to make the first step in his wrestling career. He took up various independent bookings and received a low wage for his services, ranging between £20 a match to £300, depending on win and performance bonuses. With the Manchester wrestling sconce slowing down, Matt took to the road in his car and drove from city to city, town to town working on numerous cards for a small fee. Although he wasn’t earning as much as he would in his family’s company; the experience he was gaining would be priceless. This meant that Matt spent considerable amount of time away from Stephanie, who was working at one of the major hotels in inner-city Manchester, as a receptionist This time was a test for this relationship, the distance and time apart would either make or break them…it turned out that it made the relationship stronger then ever. Course, Matt would return every month for a few days, often a week/10 days – before resuming his road trips and frequent bookings. After a few years of bouncing from city to city, Matt had built himself a reputation as a hard-hitting brawler with immense strength, as well as being a wiley mat technician. News was spreading that his work rate and in-ring ability was second to none and before long, he was appearing in the same venues and earning a lot more money. However, as it seemed that his career was finally going somewhere, the English wrestling scene slowly started to fall by the mid 1990’s and interest waned. Sensing that things could be even tougher than they already are, Matt decided that he would like to move into a different branch of the wrestling world…and North America was his choice. Following the test of strength for a number of years, Matt and Stephanie decided to get married and he officially proposed in June 1992. The date for the marriage ceremony was set for August 31st 1993 and it was held in St George’s Church in Northern England. It was a grand affair, with both families contributing to the cause and making it a day that nobody would forget. But lets get back to the wrestling aspect of this biography… Using Sean Maloney’s contacts on the US East Coast, Matt and Stephanie left for the US in November 1993 for a new life, deciding to set up camp in the Manhattan section of New York. For the first few months, life was hard as Stephanie could only get a part time job and similar to events in England, Matt was on the road in the tri-state area, spending even more time away from his new wife. However, things looked up when Matt claimed a place in the Tri-State Wrestling organisation and was to wrestle on independent cards within touching distance of home. Fortunately enough, Matt’s work was mainly in and around New York City so he and Stephanie were to continue their martial relationship. As Matt worked the cards, he gained even more experience than he did in England, earned more money and slowly moved his way up the wrestling ladder. Although he held no singles titles in his time in the TSW, he earned a reputation as a hard worker and slowly gained a small following. Life was seeming going great and it got even better when Stephanie gave birth to baby girl Jessica in April 1996 and baby boy Carl followed in December 1999 – just a few days short of being a Christmas baby. During this three year period and beyond, Matt cemented himself as an upper card talent and while playing the heel character, he annoyed with his egoistical manner and entertained with his mouth, never knowing to shut up, as it seems. He was an ever present in the area, frequently working shows and earning a more than decent (although not great) wage in return. When it seemed that his hard work would be rewarded with a run at the few singles champions, Matt received a call from his native England in November 2000. The UKWF were a growing organisation that had started in Leicester, central England, in 1997 and they were soon building their own support. Young businessman Mat Mannion was at the helm of this ship and had seen Matt in action, clearly impressed. He was looking for new talent and while scouting around the North West, he met Sean Maloney…who recommend Matt. The Temptation to return to England was too hard to resist, so a few weeks after initial discussions; Matt, Stephanie (who was heavily pregnant with Carl) and 3-year-old Jessica returned to England in November 2000. The Hunt’s were back in England after a six year break, close to family once again. Although Matt signed a contract with the UKWF upon returning to the British Isles, he didn’t officially make his first appearance on UKWF television until February 2001. Matt was carrying a little wear and tare from the States and felt that he needed a few weeks to recuperate before starting this new tenure, coupled with the fact that Stephanie was due to give birth and Christmas coming up. Matt and the UKWF officials agreed that he wouldn’t start working until another 7 weeks later. Following the arrival of baby Carl and the hectic seasonal period, Matt spent January 2001 getting into shape and losing some of the holiday fat, in time for his start date with his new organisation. He gave himself a nickname and it has stuck with him ever since, a name that is instantly recognisable to those who have come across this brash Englishman…The Postman. Although the name indicates a comical character of some sorts, Matt simply explained that a backstage agent, while working in the tri-state area, gave him the moniker a few months after he joined. Although it was a backstage nickname, Matt decided to unearth it and use it for his upcoming tenure in the UKWF. The Postman nickname goes on Matt’s expert and stunning delivery of certain manoeuvres and the fact that his in-ring ability and work rate was second to none. Despite running jokes and contrary belief, the name has no connection with the Royal Mail or the US Postal Service. The time soon passed quickly enough and on February 2nd 2001, Matt debuted at the UKWF’s PPV as a masked attacker in the Hardcore Title match-up and even left the arena with the Hardcore Title strap, taking advantage of the “open contract” stipulation in the contest. In the coming months, Matt made a meteoric rise up the UKWF ladder. Soon after winning the Hardcore title, he vacated it and made the Intercontinental Title his next target. It wasn’t long before he had that title around his waist, securing the strap during a long battle with Neacare. However, Neacare reclaimed the title during the same period in a battle that would never be forgotten. However, Matt would be locked up in another unforgettable feud with LoZ where so much hate and emotion ran wild for short period of time. This program managed to give Matt more exposure and elevated him as a top-tier performer. Although some believed that he wasn’t ready to be alongside some of the federation’s mainstays, some did believe that he was. Once the feud with LoZ ended, the World Title was his next target. At the ?!?!? PPV in April 2001, reigning UKWF Champion Smoke was to defend the title at any time to any challenger during the duration of the event. Many stepped up to the champion, but nobody could de-strap the champion…until Matt appeared towards the end of the event and took advantage of Smoke’s fatigue and pain to become the UKWF Champion. There was no time to rest, as a returning James Kimson – former UKWF Commissioner – reappeared after a three months hiatus and made the UKWF Title and Champion his main priority, This started a long-running feud that still hasn’t been resolved to this day. The two battled back and forth, swapping trash talk and trading attacks. The two would eventually meet at the May PPV, where Kimson was victorious and claimed his first UKWF Title reign. Backstage problems started to arise and the company was in turmoil, James Kimson decided to vacate the title weeks after winning it, suggesting that he was sick of the political games, and left the company altogether. This started a domino effect where half the roster decided to leave and due to this, owner Mat Mannion decided to close the organisation altogether. So in June 2001, Matt was homeless, so to speak, and had to decide whether he wanted to remain in England – where the wrestling scene was virtually non-existent – or return to the States and resume his career with the Tri-State Wrestling organisation. After discussion with Stephanie and the rest of the family, Matt decided to take a break from in-ring competition and decided to return to Manchester and assist Sean Maloney (now his father in law) in the running of the wrestling school where Matt learnt the ropes of this profession. A few months later in August, Matt received a call from Dave Crimson – who was the UKWF Talent and Relations Officer. He mentioned that he had recently purchased an organisation called International Wrestling Championship and that he wanted Matt on the roster as the IWC’s top player. Furthermore, the IWC would be invading another company called No Holds Barred Championship – owned by Rob Powell. The fact that the NHBC and IWC were both based in England made Matt’s decision a little easier to make. He agreed and joined other wrestlers such as Ben Brians, James Lord and Robbie K – to a charge on the NHBC, who had individuals like Hex, Mike Strong and Oz McCrate. Matt won the IWC Title almost immediately and was given the in-ring leadership of the IWC by Dave Crimson. The IWC seemed to have the upper hand in the battle with the NHBC over the coming months, until Robbie K screwed his IWC team-mates in a 20-man main event at the November PPV and joined the NHBC. There was another problem – weeks earlier, Hunt and Robbie had beaten the Mikes Brothers for the IWC Tag Team Titles…so despite being on opposite sides, they were tag champions. In a number of situations where Hunt tried to stack the odds against his reluctant partner, the titles finally changed hands and returned to the Mikes shortly there after. As Robbie took a short break, Hunt – still IWC Champion – focused on NHBC’s World Champion Hex and the two made a memorable feud which was full of mind games and trash talk. Robbie K soon returned and he resumed his battle with Hunt, who also had Hex to contend with. In late November, a ground breaking match was signed for network television….both IWC and NHBC Titles were on the line in a Barbed Wire Ladder Match. A match of this kind hadn’t even been seen on Pay-Per-View, so the occasion was tense to say the least. After a bloody and tiresome battle, Hunt climbed the ladder and grabbed both belts…with the assist of former UKWF owner Mat Mannion, who had made a shocking appearance. Both titles were with Hunt, who was now making history, and both titles were in the “enemy camp” Matt was now on top of the mountain and never gave a minute’s peace that he was the only man on there. The personal feud with Hex continued as an old friend of Matt’s appeared. Sean Page and Matt had spent a while in the Tri-State Wrestling organisation, often competing as a tag team. Sean wanted to recreate the glory days, but Matt felt the youngster was a burden on his double championship reign. Instead greeting him with open arms, Hunt treated Page like his personal dogsbody…and he was willing to do to anything and everything that Hunt ordered him to do. As December and the Christmas period approached, NHBC Owner Rob Powell had his own Christmas present for the collection of IWC and NHBC wrestlers. At the December PPV, there would be a six pack challenge – with both the IWC Title and NHBC Title would be on the line with Robbie K, Hex, Ben Brians, Mike Strong…and Sean Page as Matt’s challengers. There was another catch, the man to be pinned would be forced to retire. A big catch Of course, Page’s inclusion didn’t sit too well with Matt who believed that he didn’t deserve to be in the double title match. This match would be his biggest challenge of his career, the bullseye on his chest was bigger than ever…and five men, including his personal slave, were gunning for him. As expected, the match was a brutal affair and despite his efforts, Matt wasn’t the man to stand victorious at the conclusion of the contest. That man was Hex, who was now both the IWC and NHBC World Champions. The fact that he had just lost to title wasn’t enough of a problem; Matt was the man who Hex had pinned and therefore main stipulation came into effect…Matt Hunt was forced to retire. However, no sooner had Hex been handed the straps than he had a bombshell of his own to drop. He was going to retire himself, thus letting Matt off the hook – so to speak – and also vacating the IWC World and NHBC World Titles. But he wasn’t finished there and before the PPV telecast was complete; Hex introduced his roster replacement in the NHBC…James Kimson. Matt’s nemesis from the UKWF and a reunion almost a year later. So Hex retired and left the NHBC and although Hunt and Kimson had their history, they didn’t butt heads immediately as everybody expected. Course, Matt was irate on the inside – but he didn’t want o show some much emotion to a man that he has failed defeat in two previous big-time meetings. So if Kimson wasn’t Hunt’s main problem, who was? Well, that would be his personal dogsbody, Sean Page, who felt that his performance in the Six-Pac Double Title Match deserved some respect from The Postman. OF course, Hunt shrugged it off and thus a rivalry between the two began. Page acted the arrogant, prima donna youngster and Hunt, the wiley veteran with still a trick up his sleeve. Due to this new feud, the people got behind Matt and he was now the fan favorite…a role that he had never previously experienced. Page revelled in his new found public hate and it further fuelled his ego that he was “the best damn wrester in the business”. The pair battled for a short time before the destination of the two vacated titles were determined – the NHBC Title would be decided in a 8-man tournament and the IWC Title would be determined in a 15-man battle royal, with the final two competitors meeting at the January PPV for the gold. During the run up the finals of both “tournaments” – Sean Page, James Kimson, Matt Hunt and Ben Brians met in the Semi Finals of the NHBC Tournament and Sean Page and Matt Hunt were the remaining competitors in the IWC battle royale, outcomes to be decided at the PPV. However, the run-up couldn’t go nice and easy – there had to be a spanner thrown in the works and that was that Kimson and Hunt actually formed some kind of friendship! And furthermore, they emerged as the NHBC/IWC Tag Team Champions during a tournament to determine new titleholders following the decision to merge the two separate organisations tag titles. So now Hunt and Kimson were a tag team and seemed to be on the same page, working efficiently as a tag team and being civil towards each other. But despite what was on the surface, there was doubt hanging in the air over the real emotions underneath the skin. The January PPV rolled around and Kimson and Hunt weren’t required to defend their tag titles as they were taking part in the singles title matches. As it happened, Kimson was defeated in the Semi-Final against Page and Hunt was victorious over Brians in the other. So now the same two men would compete for the IWC World Title and the NHBC World Title, but in different matches. Page would take the IWC World Title in match one and Matt started his second reign as NHBC Champion in the other. Amazingly, Kimson – being a hot favorite – never came close to winning the strap. Just as things seemed to be getting interesting, the future of the NHBC/IWC war was abruptly ended in early February, when Rob Powell and Dave Crimson embraced on what was to be the final NHBC network televised show. Apparently, this NHBC vs. IWC war was collaborated by the two “chairman” and that Powell was the truthful owner of the IWC. They both insisted that they wanted screw around with everybody’s emotions…and it seemingly worked, by looking over the last few months of events. Seeing as it would fitting to end the last show with a bang, both titles were to be put on the line in the same match and thus, amalgamating the two titles into an Undisputed Champion. It was to be a triple threat match, with Kimson, Page and Hunt battling it out for one last time. Following the most heartfelt encounter, where emotions ran higher than ever before, it was the young, cocky Sean Page was walked out of the last televised show as the Undisputed Champion…leaving Matt Hunt and James Kimson dumbstruck as to how the young upstart managed to escape with the unforgettable victory. So just like that, the organisation as a whole was closed down and once again, Matt was homeless. Since that day in February 2001, Matt decided to temporarily retire from the world of professional wrestling and decided to spend more time with his family as well working with the running of his father in-laws successful wrestling school in Manchester. For the last 18 months, Matt hasn’t been seen on television nor inside a wrestling ring – except for the one erected in the wrestling school. He’s kept in shape and spent time with his family. But now he feels that 18 months is a long time and believes that he’s ready to return to the ring on a professional basis once again. Hunt first appeared on eVolution television on August 31st 2003 at the X-Rated PPV as a mystery opponent to face then-eVolved Champion Scott Evans. Hunt won the non-title contest which ignited a brief rivalry for the eVolved title. The Hunt-Evans feud never had time to kick off when Aaron Justice entered the fold, claming that he was the challenger to Evans' eVolved title. Hence, the three - Evans acting the babyface, Justice the arrogant heel and Hunt the tweener - tangling themselves into a triangle of mid-card terror. This set up the inevitable Triple Threat Match at the September PPV entitled Digital Destruction. At the conclusion of a rather technical and power-based contest, Justice's stablemates - Rimian and Tok'n - assisted their ally to nail Hunt and allow The Perfect Design to walk out of Ireland as the newly crowned eVolution eVolved Champion. At this time, Hunt had attracted a small cult following and as the months continued, the people got behind him, taking a liking to his rough British style of mannerisims, wrestling style and trash talk. The rivarly for the strap focused more on Hunt and Justice, as Evans had bigger fish to fry...namely then United Champion, Thug roC. So it was Justice and his stable - consisting of QBX, Rimian and Tok'n - raging war with the newcomer Matt Hunt. Irate that The Select had cost him the eVolved title at Digital Destruction, Hunt vowed to mow through all members of the group...concluding with another contest with Aaron Justice for the eVolved championship at the October PPV, Faith, and in the confines of a steel cage. Despite only managing to extract revenge on only Tok'n and Rimian, Hunt made it to Faith and he and Justice battled in a brutal bout, which was one of the contenders for Match of the Year. Following steel Chairs a-swinging and blood a-pouring, Hunt somehow emerged from the Toykodome in Japan as the new eVolved Champion...and a damn headache. Due to the nature of the match, Justice took time off to rest and later resurfaced recently as the newest addition to the PRIME roster. Battered and bruised, Hunt soildered on and locked horns with United Champion, Thug roC. It's safe to say that these two didn't hit it off and thus, Hunt's next rivalry began with another titleholder. Hunt was given a great opportunity on November 26th Legends to add to his gold collection when he, former rival Scott Evans and Johnny Charisma challenged Thug roC in a four corners match for the United Championship. The title never changed hands thanks to Damon O'Halloran who interfered on Thug roC's behalf and attacking Hunt, thus allowing roC to retain his strap. The following week - December 3rd, Hunt was given another big opportunity as current Universal Champion and former heated nemesis, James Kimson, gave him a title shot out of respect. The match was a classic between the two, their first meeting for nearly three years, but was ruined thanks to the interuption of Thug roC and Damon O'Halloran who attacked Kimson and Hunt respectively. Twice Hunt had two great opportunites to wear another title and twice, he had been denied by Damon O'Halloran...which seemed to make the situation between the two worse. Following the conclusion to the November 26th United Title match, eVolution Commitee Member Robert Brooks ordered a re-match of sorts in a United Title/eVolved Title unification match, winner takes all on the December 10th Legends. In one final twist, due to recent events, Brooks stated that if anybody was to interfere, then their respective contract would be terminated immediately...a decision that did not sit well with roC nor Damon O'Halloran. Hunt took the opportunity and defeated Thug roC with a simple victory roll, unifying both the United and eVolved title in the process. But as if by fate, Hunt and Kimson were paired together only weeks after their Universal Title match, in a tag team contest against their respective foes...Thug roC and Damon O'Halloran...on the December 17th Legends. Prior to the bout, The Hype and The Postman shook hands and wiped the slate clean, stating they had other things...or should that be people...on their minds. Thug and Damon were victorious Thug roC then decided to target Universal Champion James Kimson, leaving Damon O'Halloran to step forward and challenge Hunt for the new United Championship as eVolution approached the holiday season. Jugding on recent weeks, Englishman Hunt and Irish-born O'Halloran are only going to guide their rivarly to meteoric heights as the new year approaches. It's been brutal, physcologial and personal...and it can only get worse. We can only wait and see what will happen in 2004, when Hunt puts his United Title on the line against O'Halloran at the January 1st Legends next week. What a way to kick off the new year for these two!
