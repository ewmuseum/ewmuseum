Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Net Championship Wrestling opened out of Denver, Colorado with a handbook posted 7 April 1999, staging its own front office as a small cast of invented characters: President Brian Warr, a onetime wrestler under the names “The Iceman” and “Shadow Warrior” before he turned promoter; Vice President Boaz Sity, a former fake Television Champion who left over a contract dispute and returned to run the business side; and three house commentators, Tom Madness, Chris Dale and Jason Low, the handbook noting Low was hired specifically because “he wanted a commentator who says things as he feels them.”

The handbook set a thirty-wrestler cap, an all-original roster policy enforced against any “Copies” of real stars, and three championships: a World Heavyweight title defended monthly against a top-five contender, a Madness title defended under no disqualification or countout rules, and a Tag Team title. Its own weekly shows were Saturday Night Spotlight, the flagship, and Tuesday Night Thriller, a recap and rumor program built to develop angles between cards; pay-per-views were planned every six to seven weeks rather than monthly, “like back in the old days, when a feud was a feud and took 2 good ol' months to be over.”

No card, roster, or title result survives beyond the handbook itself, and no further document under this promotion's own full name survives in what remains.

Whether the roster ever filled, or either title was ever contested, isn't known from the surviving record.
