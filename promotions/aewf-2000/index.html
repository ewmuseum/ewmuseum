An Australian real-wrestler-portrayal e-fed returning in September 2000 after a year-long gap, its Halloween Havoc pay-per-view staged around real WWF commentators and a cameo appearance by the sitting US President. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Australian Email Wrestling Federation announced its own comeback on 26 September 2000, dating its first run to June 1996 and its own “unfortunate demise” to May 1999, and noting as its own trivia that one of its wrestlers had been the first-ever winner of the Scoops Wrestling Hall of Fame tournament, in early 1997. Its relaunch rules described a promotion built entirely around real wrestlers rather than invented ones: each new member could enter from one to four of them, in singles or paired into a tag team or two, backed by no more than two managers per stable, with active members — those sending in interviews and feud ideas — promised a higher profile than the inactive.

Its own return card, Halloween Havoc, aired from Madison Square Garden on 31 October 2000, called by Jim Ross and, in a running joke about his day job, the sitting Governor of Minnesota, Jesse “The Body” Ventura. The broadcast played a single mystery all night: nobody, on camera, knew who the AEWF's new president was. A backstage hunt for him ran the length of the card — an interviewer working down a list of guesses before opening a door marked “PRESIDENT” onto Bill Clinton, who insisted he was only President of the United States and sent the search on to Ralph Nader and then Al Gore, whose own press conference on the decline of entertainment was interrupted mid-sentence by an in-ring beatdown — before landing, at the end of the night, on Vince McMahon, revealed to have been the AEWF's first-ever president and reclaiming the job.

The card itself opened with the Road Warriors against the Dudley Boys in a table match the Dudley Boys won, and ran through a falls-count-anywhere match between the Ultimate Warrior and the Rock that the Warrior took only after a run-in from the Patriot tripped his opponent; a Caribbean strap match in which the Barbarian made Hulk Hogan submit for Island Fury; the promotion's own first-ever Bed of Nails match, won by Alex Wright over Al Snow and staged alongside a segment in which Snow's mannequin “Head” left him for Buff Bagwell; and a barbed-wire match Vader won over Ric Flair with Steve Austin's interference, which Austin's own post-match interview tied to Flair's backstage politics against him in WCW.

Its main event, an Ultimate Survival Death Match between Mankind and Ken Shamrock, went to Mankind on a three-count awarded while both men were down — a decision the broadcast's own commentary called unfair on the air — and the loss was followed by Shamrock's manager, billed only as the Baron and signing off with a DDT the broadcast's own commentary guessed belonged to Jake Roberts, laying the referee out. The broadcast closed on a tournament to crown a new AEWF World champion and a teased Ric Flair–Hulk Hogan match, both promised for the following Saturday.

Nothing in the surviving record shows whether that tournament, or the Flair-Hogan match, ever took place, or how or when this second run of the Australian Email Wrestling Federation came to an end.
