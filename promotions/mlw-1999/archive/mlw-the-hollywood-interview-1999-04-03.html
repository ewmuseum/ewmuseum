The Hollywood Interview

3 April 1999
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This is a two part "in-character" interview that MLW President "Hollywood" Chris Hebert did over the last two MLW: On The Road shows with host Tonya Wallace. *********************************** PART 1 [Tonya and Hebert sit on the outdoor terrace of his Manhattan penthouse, high at the top of a highrise. The view of New York's famous skyline is breathtaking on this chilly, but sunny afternoon. Tonya is wearing zip-up black leather mini dress with lacey boots while Hollywood wears a brown suede jacket, tan khakis, brown Gucci loafers and a pair of black Serengeti sunglasses, while he smokes a cigar with one hand and holds a scotch on the rocks with the other.] Q. Soo, Mr. 'ebert...tell oos 'ow yew go' started in th' wrasslin' business? Hollywood: Well, back in late '94, I was in my mid-20's, hanging out in Toronto and trying to make a living as a writer, when a friend of mine invited me to come to a wrestling card. I was a fan as a kid, and hadn't been to a show in years, so I said what the hell and went. The organization was called the NWC, which later would become the now world-famous NPWA. Anyway, we managed to get backstage, and I talked to the wrestlers and crew, and I was hooked. I got in touch with the Dutt family, who were the owners of the NWC, and they gave me a job in their marketing department in early '95. After about 6 months or so, they came to me and asked me if I thought I would be any good as a booker. I had no idea if I would be or not, but I basically bluffed my way into the job. [Hebert laughs] Hollywood: Anyway, I was part of the booking team of the NWC for quite awhile, including when we changed our name to the NPWA. Then, the unthinkable happened. The Dutt family, while retaining ownership, had to step back from the day to day operations of the NPWA. Someone had to become the acting president, and none of the other bookers wanted the job. The NPWA came very close to closing that day, but once again I assured the Dutts that I could do the job, and I became the de facto president of the NPWA! I couldn't believe it, from the marketing department to running the big show in the space of two years or so. Anyway, I ran the NPWA to the best of my ability, and if I can toot my own horn for a second, the NPWA rose to previously unachieved heights under my watch. It turned out that I was actually good at the job! I liked the feeling of being in control of the whole operation, and calling the shots. I answered to no one... until the day the Dutts came back. Once again, I was a member of a team. I remained on as part of the NPWA team, back to booking basically, but I wasn't satisfied... I wanted to be my own boss. Q. Soo wha' 'appened next? Hollywood: When the Dutt's came back, one of their initiatives was to start up a second wrestling organization, a training ground or farm system if you like, for the NPWA, called Toronto Pro Wrestling. They hired a guy from Minneapolis named Kevin Bakewell to run the whole thing, and it was a fairly big success. I saw in Kevin someone who seemed to like booking things the same as I did, and ran the TPW in a similar fashion that I ran the NPWA in when I was in charge. We went out for a few brews one night, and got to talking... you know, "what if you and I blew this pop stand and started our own wrestling federation?" Well, maybe it was the beer, maybe it was the need for our own autonomy, but we up and did it... we both resigned from the NPWA and I moved to Minneapolis and together we created Twin Cities Wrestling, or as our legions of fans called it, the Tee Cee Dub. [Hebert chuckles] Q. Th' TCW was a 'uge success, an' it 'ad a big cult following... wha' 'appened? Hollywood: We became victims of our own success... we kept signing more and more, and bigger and bigger stars - Doc Toxic, Chris King, NHL, Major Victory, Tiger Tong Po, Valtharius The Mad... in a nutshell, we went so deep into debt, and owed so much money to the IRS, we went bankrupt. Kevin was tapped out, and I had to go back to Canada... luckily, I managed to get my old job back with the NPWA so I could make a living, but the old feeling was still there, so, while still working for the NPWA, I also opened my own little indy fed, called the Maple Leaf Wrestling Association. And wouldn't you know, but a lot of the TCW guys were loyal enough to come work for me at drastically reduced salaries... but, the old debts from TCW caught up with me and I was forced out of business. Q. Bakewell tried ta restart TCW shortly after thet, dinna he? Hollywood: Yes he did, and although I didn't take part, it was inadvertently the reason that I managed to pay off all my debts and start the MLW... Q. Explain thet, if yew would.... Hollywood: Bakewell managed to sign NPWA World Heavyweight Champion "The Hollywood Heartthrob" Dave Diamond to a TCW contract. I knew Dave of course, as I was still working with the NPWA. Well, I called him up in Minneapolis and managed to convince him he needed a manager. Q. Yew seem to 'ave talked yer way inta every job yew've 'ad in th' wrasslin' business [laughs] Hollywood: I've always been a good talker... who knew it would finally pay off [laughs] Anyway, Dave agreed and I became his manager... as most people know, Diamond was the co-founder of one of the most successful stables in wrestling history, Hollywood After Dark. I ended up managing the whole lot of them... and made a *ton* of money doing it as they were in big demand at the time. I still manage Dave in the GLCW to this day, although the rest of HAD have basically disappeared. Anyway, with the cash I made from managing, I paid off the IRS, and Revenue Canada from my MLWA fiasco, and started the MLW, which so far has been a huge success, both creatively and financially. Q. Soom of th' MLW wrasslers taday 'ave been wit' yew since th' beginnin', 'ave they no'? Hollywood: That's right, NHL, Chris King, Valtharius... they've shown amazing loyalty, wanting to work for me no matter what crazy venture I've started. There have been times I'm sure I didn't deserve that kind of loyalty, but I thank them from the bottom of my heart for giving it to me. Q. Why dunna we play word association..... Kevin Bakewell Hollywood: My best friend in the business... and the man who taught me a lot about how to make a successful wrestling organization. Q. [name withheld] Hollywood: My other best friend in the biz... he's always allowed me to come back to my job in the NPWA when I've fallen on my ass in my solo ventures. He's also the one who gave me my first job, so I owe him a lot. He's actually little younger than I am, but we've become closer since his old man, Vince Dutt, Jr. has retired and left the NPWA in his hands. Q. Dave Diamond Hollywood: Dave's a character... he plays this lady-killing, jet-flying, limousine-riding playboy character, when in reality, he's a quiet kind of guy, who likes nothing better than sitting at home with his beautiful wife Lindsay Jackson and watching a movie Q. Eddie Van Gibson Hollywood: Eddie reminds me a lot of Dave in his younger days... although I think EVG believes his own press. But he's young, he's gotten a lot of success real fast... I guess I can't blame him for being a bit egotistic. Q. Nelson Larson... NHL Hollywood: What can I say? He's been around for a long time, he's a big success and he's shown nothing but loyalty to me over the years. If only he'd learn English! [laughs] Q. Zombie Rogers Hollywood: I don't know what to make of this guy... I had never heard of him before I signed him. His popularity is astounding really... he's a heat machine, which is good for me I guess. [laughs] He's a little too... "dark" for my liking, but that's what sells these days. Q. The Glitter Boy Hollywood: A true enigma... here's a guy who's been in the business forever. He's been a superstar and has won almost every major championship there is... when he's on, he's the best. But he goes through periods where he just vanishes for lack of a better word... doesn't do interviews, wrestles like sh<bleep>t, or no-shows for matches even. Look at what happened in the GLCW recently... he was on top of the world, was the Great Lakes Champion, was teamed with Dave Diamond in Hollywood After Dark... and he's thrown it all away. Q. Chris King Hollywood: [laughs] I remember when he was a green rookie... I think it was in Bakewell's TPW. He was pretty bad. But, he's come a helluva long way... it really turned around for him when he started this whole "King Of Extreme" thing in TCW. Now he's a superstar. A lot of guys jump on the Extreme bandwagon, but he's been doing it for quite awhile now, and he does it damn well. So for him, it's worked out great. Q. Matt Muentes Hollywood: He reminds me of me. [chuckles] He basically did what I did back in the NPWA... he talked his way into a job with the MLW, and I'll be damned if he isn't one hell of an executive. I only hope he doesn't continue to follow in my footsteps and leave to form his own federation! [laughs] Q. An' finally... "Hollywood" Chris Hebert Hollywood: [pauses thoughtfully as he takes a sip of his scotch] Misunderstood. People forget that the whole "Hollywood" thing is just a character, a persona. Basically, I'm still a kid from Canada who loves wrestling and wants to bring the best show he can to everyone who watches the MLW. Have I made mistakes? Hell yes, but everything I've done has been sincere, and out of love for the sport. I've carefully cultivated this whole heel owner image, so I guess I'm a victim of my own success once again, but I'm not really the bad guy you see on TV each week. ***************************************************************************** PART 2 [The MLW's newest and by far sexiest host, and centerpiece of MLW: ON THE ROAD, Tonya Wallace, and President Hebert sit on the balcony of the Marriot Hotel in beautiful, glitzy Hollywood, California. The ominous and infamous "H-O-L-L-Y-W-O-O-D" sign looms in the background beneath the scortching California sun. Tonya wears a purple one-piece bikini and stylish sunglasses, her hair done up seductively. While Hebert is clad in tan shorts, a dark-green golf shirt, sandals, and Raybans. The pair sipping from a bottle of scotch.] Q. Mr. 'ebert, wha' 'ere soome o' th' 'ighlights thet stick ou' in yer mind durin' yewr career be'ind th' scenes? Hebert: Jeez, that's a tough one. There have been so many great cards and matches over the years. I guess the two best matches that I ever booked in the NPWA would have to be the first Dying Breath match between "Blackheart" Brian Masters and Steve Sampson in April of '97, and the Shopping Spree match between Senator Carthy McJoseph and the late Southern Rocker, god rest his soul, in November of '96. Most of the credit belongs to the wrestlers involved of course - hell, those were two of the most exciting matches I've ever had the pleasure of witnessing - but in terms of being the man who put them together and booked them, they are among the high points in my career I'd have to say. In the TCW era, hmmmm... to be honest, the best ideas came from Kevin. [laughs] He's the guy who came up with the whole "Quickie" idea, which was a masterstroke in my opinion. I guess my defining match as a booker would have had to have been the Bonfire Submission Match between The Glitter Boy and Dave Diamond. It was a great match, and the ending, with Diamond burning the heavyweight title in the bonfire and basically telling the TCW to stick their championship and their federation where the sun don't shine... [laughs] Oh sure, it was all a work, but the marks in the crowd really got into it. Dave really started to get major heel heat from that point on, no pun intended. MLW-wise...well, let's just say I don't think we've seen the best yet. I think the Pay Per View in May, "Unforgiven," will surprise and shock a lot of people. Q. Cin yew elaborate on thet, per chance? Hebert: [grinning devilishly] Not really. [laughs] Q. Since we're talkin' about th' MLW now.. th' motto o' Maple Leaf Wrasslin' is "Wrestling For The New Millennium." 'ho do yew see carryin' th' MLW banner inta th' 21st century? Hebert: Well, the wrestling business is a tough one, and lord knows if we really will still be here in the new millennium, although I plan on doing everything in my power to make sure we are. As for who I think will lead us there, I think there are some obvious guys, the big stars like Eddie Van Gibson, Zombie Rogers, Devon Starr, Heaven And Hell, NHL... then there are guys that are on the verge of superstardom, like Ron Rivers, Chris King, Lord James Gray, even Steve Lake I suppose, who just need that little extra something to put them over the top. But I also think there are some real dark horses on the roster right now, guys who aren't necessarily well-known in wrestling circles, or who haven't hit main event status yet, but I think that if they stay the course over the year, they could easily be the superstars of the new millennium. I'm talking about guys like Blake Alexander - I see something in this kid and think he could be the next big thing. Q. An' 'ho is there ou' there in th' wrasslin' world yew'd like ta get on th' MLW payroll? Hebert: Well, there are always those big name draws that any promoter would give his left testi... [Hebert stops, remembering that he's speaking to a lady and chuckles] ... I mean, his right arm, to sign - your Brody Thunders, your J.W. Hardins, your Bishops... but, if you mean me in particular? Yeah, there are a few. Q. An' cin yew name any names for us? Hebert: Well, one of the first guys I called when I decided to start the MLW was "Blackheart" Brian Masters. I was a huge fan of his in the NPWA, and I wanted him to be the cornerstone of the MLW, but I couldn't talk him out of retirement. He's happy raising his daughters at home in Tempe, Arizona, so I can't blame him for that. Then there's former NPWA superstar Jonathan Shock. I actually had him in my office, a contract sitting on my desk for him to sign... and for whatever reason, he backed out. Shock always was a little flakey back in the day, and I guess he still is. [laughs] I'd love to have Rick Kilgore... Q. Kilgore? But dinna 'e cause yew an' Dave Diamond a lot o' trouble in th' GLCW? Hebert: Sure, why not? Business is business, and having Kilgore on the roster would be good business. The guy's a heat machine. Oh, and then there are the preliminary discussions I've had recently with a pretty well-known group of wrestlers from one of the "big two" Canadian promotions, and I don't mean the NPWA. Q. Soo it 'as ta be th' SCRA then, right? Hebert: [merely shrugs with a "you said it, I didn't" grin on his face and sips his scotch] Q. Wha' cin yew tell us about this Hollywood Tribute Cup on On The Road this week...'osted by yers truly, o'course? [Grins.] Hebert: [laughs] What, you don't think I deserve a cup named after me?? [laughs some more] Well, we're sitting around the table, me, Matt and Daniel, talking about what we're going to do on the On The Road show from Hollywood, California, when Muentes comes up with this idea for a Cup, named after me. I figure I should be able to get some heat with my little speech when I present the cup to the winner, so I said sure, what the hell, let's do it. Q. So wha' do yew see in the future for th' MLW? Hebert: Great wrestlers, great matches, great angles. I think there will be some very intriguing things happening in the next few months that will make a few people in the industry take notice, and maybe Maple Leaf Wrestling, and the wrestlers who make it as good as it is, will finally get the respect they deserve. =-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= Christopher J.
