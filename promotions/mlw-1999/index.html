Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Maple Leaf Wrestling ran under the motto “Wrestling For The New Millennium,” and its own president, “Hollywood” Chris Hebert, told its own origin story in a two-part in-character interview broadcast 3 April 1999: a career that ran through Toronto and Minneapolis bookerships, a bankrupt Twin Cities venture, and a first, short-lived Maple Leaf Wrestling Association he had to close over debts, before he “paid off the IRS” managing other wrestlers and started this promotion “from scratch,” which by early 1999 he called, in his own words, “a huge success, both creatively and financially.”

Its own house style ran a twice-weekly Ground Zero broadcast alongside a touring interview-and-results programme, On The Road, building toward pay-per-views. Five championships anchored the roster — Heavyweight, Canadian Heritage, Northern Hardcore, True North and World Tag Team — contested in casket, scaffold, ladder and battle-royal formats. Hebert's own on-screen booking ran on a recurring screwjob: its own broadcasts recap him striking Zombie Rogers with a crowbar so “Simply Divine” Blake Alexander could steal the Heavyweight Championship at the promotion's own May pay-per-view, “Unforgiven” — the identical crowbar-and-screwjob tactic he had used to take the same title away from Eddie Van Gibson earlier in the record.

In April 1999 the promotion joined two other federations under a shared banner, the Coast to Coast Wrestling Alliance, for a three-day pay-per-view, “A World of Pain” — ten matches across three nights, sanctioning a joint championship of its own.

Its mid-year pay-per-view, Maplemania (17 July 1999), settled the vacant Heavyweight Championship in a double scaffold match built two stories over the ring: wrestlers climbed and fought across both levels until Valtharius the Mad bit a chunk out of Blake Alexander's shoulder mid-match and swung a rival by the ankles as a weapon to clear the lower scaffold. The arena went dark, a returning Nemesis powerbombed “Superstar” Devon Starr twenty-five feet off the top scaffold to the ring below, and Valtharius touched down to become champion, to its own broadcast's bafflement at why Nemesis had helped him at all.

No closure statement survives among these served pages. The promotion's own last-recorded activity is a recruitment notice and a wrestler's account of a pay-per-view advertised for 22 August 1999; nothing further under Maple Leaf Wrestling's own name continues past early September that year.
