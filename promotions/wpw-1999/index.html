Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World Pro Wrestling announced its own opening on Saturday, 9 January 1999, with a roster of twenty-five and an open call for more: “We are looking for good, would help if you could Roleplay 60+lines if you want to stand any chance of winning the World title (Which is actully the second best title). It is for Real and Fake Wrestlers.” The same post listed real wrestlers still unclaimed by any applicant — Chris Jericho, DDP, X-Pac, Billy Gunn, Road Dogg Jesse James, the Godfather, Val Venis, Ken Shamrock and Test among them — and promised two cards a week plus a pay-per-view.

Its surviving record is a two-stop West Coast Wipeout tour that spring, Sacramento on 31 March 1999 and San Francisco two days later.

Sacramento crowned a new Intercontinental Champion out of a four-man tournament: Barry Windham beat Chris Candido in the final by disqualification, ruled a title change when the fed waived its own disqualification rule after Francine interfered, and Chris Benoit and Dean Malenko came out to celebrate with him — “the Horsemen are reborn.” Elsewhere on the card the Miracle Violence Connection (Steve Williams and Terry Gordy) retained the World Tag Team titles by disqualification against Public Enemy after Horsemen interference, X-Pac beat Chris Jericho for the World Light-Heavyweight title, and Tommy Dreamer retained the Hardcore title over Cactus Jack in a ladder match. The main event, a First Blood cage match, went to Stone Cold Steve Austin over the Rock after Windham slipped him an object to draw blood — and ended instead with Shawn Michaels running in to beat down Austin, Windham and the Horsemen together.

San Francisco, two days later, turned the tag title around: Malenko and Benoit beat the Miracle Violence Connection two falls to one to take the World Tag Team titles. X-Pac retained the Light-Heavyweight title against Taka Michinoku, and Windham retained the Intercontinental title in a non-title match against the Rock, who was left in the ring at the show's close with Austin at his side in a staredown against the retreating Horsemen.

No further card survives past that second stop; how the West Coast Wipeout tour continued, or how World Pro Wrestling itself ended, isn't in the surviving record.
