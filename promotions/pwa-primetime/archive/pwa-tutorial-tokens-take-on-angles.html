Token's Take on Angles by [name withheld]
4/03/03

Well... how the hell do I start?

Anyway I'm [name withheld], and I handle Token Weed in the Asylum, Rush in fWo, and Brad Schilling in A!W. I used to be a rather active handler, and some of the guys who have been around for a long time may remember me. Anyway I get to write a tutorial on how to plan an angle. I dont see how you can write a tutorial on it, because you never have a set way on how to do stuff. Now time to start this sum bitch off.

Ok, a good idea for planning an angle, is to plan with characters you enjoy reading. It makes the angles more enjoyable. Write the angle for characters that you feel you could write ideas for, and could picture how they would act with each step through their minds. Recently I participated in an angle with two handlers, and two characters I enjoy: Ash, who handles The Freak, and Ben who handles Hypnosis.

Ash is rather new to the Asylum, while on the other hand Ben has been around for 2 years now, and I've been around for about a year and a half. Ben and I had discussed maybe running programs before, but never got the chance to. I wanted to work with Ash because he's a workhorse; he busts his ass for the place he writes for, lots of time on his hands. He and Ben had an angle planned out, and I came to both of them and asked if there was any way I could get in on it (I was totally open at the time, and mostly everybody was booked up).

So I get in this angle with two of my friends, and two characters I enjoy reading, right? Let's continue from there. You need to have a way for your characters to interact with each other. Ash and I both handle sick and twisted characters, but they vary so much in how they think etc... that they are beautiful when working together, or against someone. Hypnosis, on the other hand is a fan favorite, has always fought against the much-hated Joe Campbell, and anyone else who opposed anything that was good.

The characters had a way to develop hatred, and we managed to do this to the fullest extent, to the point where we were main eventing the shows (note: Ash's character wasn't on the main event level, but is now one of the Asylum's biggest stars. Ranks up their with Villam Ender, and Hypnosis.) Your ideas need to be full of twists and turns that are going to be put into a good plot. You also need to leave a few open doors, where the characters can interact with each other. Ash, Ben and I managed to get it to the point Chris had beef with all three characters, and Cheno had beef with Weed. Opens doors for the future, and makes a good filler program for a week or two.

The doors left open shouldn't be huge, just large enough for the rest of the roster to utilize here and there, makes angles flow etc... Makes the shows seem more interesting.

Anyway, make sure you make your angle plan is organized. I usually write down what happens each show, and manage to leave doors open for other characters to exit and leave, keeping the focus on two main people. Make sure the organization is clear, and let the other person make changes to the angle if desired. The two of you BOTH have characters involved. And make sure that you maintain enough flexibility to get others involved if needed and that you stay in close communication.

And most of all: Tell a good story and have fun doing it.
