Jason Snow's Tips to PWA Success

16 February 2003

Jason Snow's Tips to PWA Success
2/16/03

Before I get started, let me give you a little history on myself and my affiliation with PWA. My character name is Jason Snow, and in early May of 2001, I entered PWA with bright eyes, a lot like many of you have right now. People that I remember being in PWA at the same time are Vampir Nosferatu and Tom Walczak, both of whom went onto successful careers in major WT federations. I graduated alongside Vampir Nosferatu, and we went to PCW2. Since then, Ive been a grader and a scout, and Ive graded everyone from El Diablo (when he was Venom) to Rent-a-Hero (when he was Da Backseat Boy), to Angelo Deville. Ive seen countless stars pass through the system and countless would-be stars fall into the gutter because something didnt quite go their way. Ive seen people called up long before they were ready and Ive seen people stew in the Academy long after they are.
And now, Im going to give you my tips on how to get the most out of PWA, regardless of how close you are to graduation. If youre a rookie waiting to post your first RP, if youre a guy whos been posting regularly for months, or if youre on the verge of getting a major WT contract, I hope that these tips and guidelines will help you. No matter what your status in the Academy is, if you want to get the most out of your experience here, I suggest you read all the sections.

If you want success in PWA and later in WT, this is your e-fedding bible.

For the Newbies

So youve decided to come to PWA.

Great! We welcome everyone! But let me clear a few common misconceptions out of the way.

Misconception #1 -> PWA works miracles. All you have to do is glide through the system and youll come out a better RPer. Its like a car wash. You sit back and let brushes do the polishing.

First of all, PWA is a lot like a car wash, but maybe not the car wash that youre picturing. We give you a bucket, a scrub brush, and we tell you what spots your missing while youre washing your own car. We dont work miracles here. We dont miraculously transport English dictionaries from our heads and into yours.

I cant promise you that PWA will make you a better RPer. What I can promise you is that PWA will turn you into as good of an RPer as your willing to work to be. We might not wash the car for you, but we do supply you with all the tools you need to get it spotless. The question is: how dirty is the car, and how hard are you willing to work to clean it?

(Note: This is only an analogy. PWA has nothing to do with washing cars.)

Misconception #2 -> PWA feeds PTC and WT. You work in PWA until you get offered a job in a federation other than PWA.

This might be the most common misconception among new PWAers. I cant recall how many times Ive seen a PWAer post something along the lines of, Well, Ive been here two weeks and GFW (Generic Federation Wrestling) offered me a spot, so I guess this is goodbye.

PWA feeds WT federations. WT federations are PCW, OSW, PWC, RUSH, and PURE. Should you be offered a spot in another federation, know that taking it means youve been wasting our time. Theres nothing we can do to forbid you from leaving PWA for a PTC fed or any fed, for that matter, but thats why we want you to realize the commitment that youre making before you post your first RP. By entering PWA, youre telling us that it is your goal to make it to a WT fed. Of course, some of you will realize that PWA, and/or WT, is not for you, and thus you will leave. Thats fine. But what we dont like to see are people who use the system to improve and then jump to the first outside-of-WT federation that offers them a spot.

Let me make this clear. Were not doing charity work for you in PWA. We invest our time in effort into you in hopes that some day, sooner or later, you will come into one of our federations and contribute. Make no mistake, we want you to get better, but we want you to get better to make us better.

Let me put this into perspective. The average grade, for me, takes about 40 minutes. If you write 15 RPs in PWA and then jump to a non-WT fed, how much time have we wasted on you? We want you to improve, and we want you to make us better. Not another fed. A WT fed.

Misconception #3 -> PWA is quick and easy. You post an RP, by the morning its graded, and in a week or two you move up to a major federation.

99% of complaints from students are: My RP has been up for three days and no one has graded it! Tough. As I said before, it takes about 40 minutes for us to grade an RP, and a grade is a service to you. Your RP will be graded, but it will be graded when it strikes a grader to do so.

Right now, PWA is sitting in the most luxurious lap it has ever been in. At the most, RPs have waited about 4 days for a grade. On average, most RPs are graded within two days. Thats a lot of speed for the system thats in place.

Let me shed a little light on this. PWA has as little as 10 students, and as many as a 150+ at any given time. When I was in PWA, the classes were large, the graders were rough, and it took an average of a week to get an RP graded. That doesnt matter. What matters is that you keep working, trying to improve on your own, as well as letting the graders give you their two cents.

As for the amount of time PWA takes to go through, that varies. Some times feds need talent PWA is drained to send people up. At other times, theres no room in the WT feds and students are left down in PWA to develop. You might get out of PWA in a week or you may be in PWA for three months. Whatever the case, use your time here wisely and make the most of the experience.

Misconception #4 -> PWA sucks. You write an RP and then have people rip it apart.

I cant help but chuckle at this. Why? Because in every federation you go to, youll see guys asking for feedback on something theyve done. Theyll do this in private or in public. PWA offers people the same thing, only on a much larger scale.

In PWA, you get individual attention from some of the best RPers in the world. Ask around. Very few people will tell you that theres a better collection of RPers than the ones in PCW, which seems to be the last major RP-based fed. These guys take time to tell you, personally, whats good and whats bad about your RP.

So the next time a grader thrashes through your RP and tears it to shreds - be thankful. The person who did that went to the trouble of going into detail for you of things you need to work on in both your writing and entertaining skills. Use it to your benefit! Dont complain that they dont know what theyre talking about! Every reader counts, and represents a taste in RPs. Use it to your advantage.

Tips

See What Works
The first thing youre going to want to do as a new student is ask around and find out who the big names in the community are and take a look at their work. Or you can look at posts on the fan boards and see who the buzz is around. Read major federation RPs as much as you can! After a while, youll begin to see certain similarities between the RPs, and youll learn what is done, and what is not done. Youll also pick up a few little tricks in writing.

Get to Know the Community
Youll find that 99% of the WT community is open and friendly. Take some time to visit the chat, or hit your fellow students up on AIM. Talk to major federation players as well. Most people will be more than willing to have a conversation with you. Part of the fun in this game is the friends you make, so dont hesitate to start.

Get in Touch With Graders
There are a few graders and staff who keep themselves too busy to have casual conversation with you, but for the most part, youll find that most graders are pleasant an easy to talk to, so long as you dont pressure them to grade your work. Getting to know your graders will help you immensely. Even though they might not be grading your work, particularly, you can find out some good tips and habits.

Pay Attention to Other Students
Youll learn from another students grade just as you will learn from your own. Read all of the grades that you can. Pay little attention to the numbers, but focus heavily on the comments given by the grader.

What Not to Do
Dont Take the Numbers to Heart
Everyone starts somewhere, and whether youre getting single digit grades or grades of 70+, it makes no difference. Im more proud of a student that comes from getting 5 and 10% who eventually turns himself into a major federation prospect than I am a guy whos been getting 70's since coming to the Academy. Your goal in PWA should not be to get high marks. Your goal in PWA should be to improve.

Dont Ask Graders to Grade Your RP
This will usually result in a zero. Dont even hint around that you want a grader to grade your work, or theres a good chance itll get a zero. We see a lot of this and have no tolerance for it.

Dont Get an Ego
Nothing is more annoying than seeing a PWA student think hes the top man around WT because he got a good grade. The fact is, it doesnt matter how good you are, when you get to a WT major youll be a big fish in a huge pond full of fish that are just as big. Getting an Ego early is starting on the wrong foot and its frustrating for us to see. It only hurts you in the end, because the bigger your ego is, the more of it there is to tear down when you move to a major federation.

For the Intermediates

So youve been in PWA for a little while now. Maybe youre in a rut, maybe youre doing ok, or maybe you think youre not getting the attention you deserve. This section is for you. (You should also read over the newbie guidelines, because that applies to just about everyone)

Tips

Dont be Afraid to Experiment
A lot of times when a person hits a wall in PWA, they can get around it by making a few adjustments. While writing is always an important part of RPing that were all still improving with, dont forget about the character that youve created, which is by far the most important of things in an RP. If your character isnt interesting, then I dont care how well you can write - youll go no where. Dont be afraid to tweak things in PWA. Experiment a little. Work on perfecting your character while youre here. Use PWA like you use draft copies. It doesnt really count, so feel free to try out new things. Look for something that works!

Read, Read, Read
Nothing improves your writing skills like getting familiar with the language, and if youre in a rut because of your writing, then I suggest you find things you like to read. You like sports? Read sports magazines. You like music? Read music articles. Read what you like. In school, they force things down your throat and a lot of kids get turned off to reading because of it. So find what you like and read up on it. Sooner or later, the dos and donts of the language will click for you.

Be Patient
A little known secret is that PWA is actually used to test your loyalty just as much as it is used to test your skill. If you stick with PWA through thick and thin, a fed head will know that youre a handler that he or she can count on.

What Not to Do

Dont Get Discouraged
A lot of people spent months in PWA. A lot more came back a second time to get back into a WT federation. If youve been in PWA for a couple of months now, dont be discouraged. Just use your time wisely, and take advantage of the extra attention that youre getting that those who were called up earlier didnt get.

Dont Start Asking About a Promotion
This is kind of like asking people to grade your RPs. As a PWA student, youre always under consideration and evaluation. Unless a fed head specifically requests applications, theres no point in bothering them with a promotion request.

For the Almost-Graduates

So now, your time has almost come to an end. Youre hearing the buzz that fed heads are interested in you, and you sense that a contract is coming your way. Is the PWA experience over? Nope.

Tips

Take Advantage of the Time You Have Left!
I cant stress this enough. Vampir Nosferatu and I were offered contracts on the same day, but theres a reason why hes considered the first graduate. When he got the contract, he left PWA. When I got the contract, I used every minute that I had before my major fed debut to develop myself in PWA. And in those few extra days, I became a better RPer. If fed heads are knocking down your door, dont think RPing for PWA is pointless. So what if youre not booked for a match? Its not about the matches. Get what you can out of PWA, and use every second that it grants you. Youll be thankful for the extra bit of feedback that you get, because once you hit a WT major, youre on your own.

Get to Know Your Future Fedmates
If youre not known in the community by this time, then you should get out and meet your peers. Itll come in handy later on when youre working out angles with these people if you have a bit of a background.

Be Very Critical of Your Own Work
A grader just gave you a 90%? So what? Soon, youll be in a WT fed and youll be a small fish again. Prepare yourself! Look at your own work with a critical eye! Dont leave it up to the graders alone! By the time youre ready for the move up, you probably can effectively criticize your own work. Do so. Try to perfect things. It will only help when you get promoted.
What Not to Do

Dont Get a Big Head
Nothings more annoying than seeing the guy come out of PWA thinking hes going to run through his WT federation like a knife through butter. It doesnt work that way. If youre the best in PWA, it doesnt mean youre the best in WT, and chances are, someones going to pop the air out of your head before you realize that youre making a fool of yourself. Ive seen people leave WT because they came out of PWA thinking they should dominate, and then found out just how stiff the competition is. Confidence is fine, but once you stop looking at things realistically, you have to take a step back.

Dont Assume the Work is Over
WT federations are every bit as frustrating as PWA, if not more. In a WT major, you dont have people guiding your career. You might spend months as a low carder, getting no respect, and theres no one to help you. Just because you come to a WT major doesnt mean theres no room for improvement. As long as youre always improving, then the game will stay fun for you, and youll love a WT major. Its when you think youre as good as you can get, which is never true, that your motivation dies and you dont want to play the game anymore.

***

Lets move on to some more general subjects.

I want to talk about grading and the inconsistency of it. Its a necessary flaw. I realize that a lot of you have written a decent RP and got a 90% on it, and then followed it up with a better RP and failed. Thats how life in PWA is. You have to take what you can from the grade.

One thing that helps is researching the grader. If you get a soft grader, you should know that he doesnt value the A that hes giving you very much, so neither should you. Put the grade in perspective, learn what you can from it, and move on.

Next, lets move on to things that you SHOULDNT do in RPs...

#1.) In-ring RPs are a huge no-no. Do an in-ring, and youll be staring a big fat zero in the face. WT federations have set dates for shows, and those are where in-rings are used. Not in RPs. This is the most concrete rule of RPing for WT.

#2.) Watch the swearing, people. Dont get me wrong, my RPs are filled with cussing, but it has a point. Some RPs just fill themselves with cusses because they think it sounds cool. Well, guess what, it doesnt. It makes the RP seem tacky and lame. There are good and bad ways to use swearing in an RP. If youre using it for realism, or sometimes even humour, then great. If youre using it because you think it makes you look like a bad-ass - it doesnt.

#3.) Be careful with trash talking. Im not going to say dont do it like many do, because some people have a knack for it. Some old Big Alan Steele RPs were 90% trash talk and were some of the best work that Ive ever seen. But dont trash talk for the sake of trash talking. If youre good at it, and can make it entertaining, then all the power to you. But make sure that its entertaining, or hammers will fall on you. When done well, trash talking can be great. But remember, its very rarely done well. Dont attempt it unless youre very sure of yourself.

#4.) Dont go an entire RP without mentioning something about your characters features. Ive taken off thousands of points in my day, literally, because I finish an RP with no idea what the person Im reading about looks like. So you described him in your last RP? Describe him again. Just be clever with it. Describing your character doesnt have to be: Joe has blue eyes and brown hair and is wearing... etc. Lace your description into the body of your RP. You can mention a persons eye color when they look at something. Example: Joe turned his blue eyes toward the desk. You can mention his hair in much the same fashion. Example: In frustration, Joe grabbed a handful of his shaggy brown hair and gave it a tug. From these two sentences, I know as much about Joes appearance as I would from a boring canned description. You dont like writing them, and likewise, we dont like reading them. GET CLEVER WITH THINGS!

#5.) Dont use filler! Just the facts, please. Dont get me wrong, I want you to make the facts as interesting as you can, but if youre telling me about how someone digests an apple, Im going to get bored in a hurry. Dont describe a tree for five paragraphs, and dont tell me things that, as a reader, I dont care about.

#6.) Dont shy away from RP styles that arent in. WT goes through a lot of fads and changes from month to month. If you want to do a style thats not currently being used, feel free to give it a try. If you want to write RPs made for television, do it! Frisco had a great career doing just that. If you want to write in script while novel is currently the choice, do it! Do what works for you, and dont be afraid to do what you have to do to make your character shine!

#7.) Dont underestimate the importance of a gimmick. Look at PCW Champions. Look at OSW Champions. What do they all have? Strong gimmicks. From demons to sons of Zeus to Scientologists to homeless men, gimmicks have always played a huge part in success! They also make the writing a lot more fun!

#8.) Dont post an RP without proof reading!!! You dont even know how many points this will save you. Youll find that guys in major feds rarely proof read their work, but in PWA, its essential.

#9.) Dont write an RP just for the sake of writing it. For the love of God, you dont know how many times Ive read an RP and wondered what the point of it was. Make sure you have a story to tell!

#10.) Dont assume there are any concrete rules of RPing. That includes the nine previous. There are exceptions to every rule, and if you really think you can get away with breaking one, then by all means... But its a lot like English. You can break the rules of English and come out looking better for it, but you had better know the rules that youre breaking and why youre breaking them before you do. A good grader will always know the difference between an RP that is purposely breaking rules and doing it will, and an RP that simply is ignorant to the rules or breaks them in poor fashion. Rules are meant to be broken, but once you do, youre walking a fine line and its not easy to stay on.

***

Finally guys, if youre not having fun in e-wrestling, then either make it fun or get out. Never forget that were playing a game, and never let it become more than that. Remember that when thinking of your own situation in the game, and dealing with others.

I hope you find this beneficial,

~Jason Snow, PCW
