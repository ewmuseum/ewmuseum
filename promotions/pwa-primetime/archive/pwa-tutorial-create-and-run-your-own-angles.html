How to create and run your own angles! By [name withheld]

First off, many of you PWA students know me as the handler of Semore Smoke, the former NTICW World Champion and PWA graduate from October of 2001. Now, there is more to eWrestling then becoming a good and dedicated Role-player. There are also angles and feuds that need to be carefully planned for the best entertainment you can give your fans and do it all while having fun. Im known to run and come up with most of my own angles. Some have fallen apart, but that's always a danger in any angle that relies on other people. I orchestrated most of the Scott Taylor Vs. Semore Smoke feud near the end of my NTICW title reign and I even planned to drop the title to Taylor when I did. Timing and choosing how you lose your title is a big change from rp based fed. I also helped plan and start the MSCW stable in NTICW. And while this was a problem near the end, it shows the importance of working with reliable people. So, knowing what to do to run your own angle and make it successful is needed.

So, enough about me, now here are the questions that you need to ask yourself when youre planning a feud or angle with another role-player in your federation.

1. Are you willing to job or lose to your opponent for the better part of the angle?

You should always be willing to lose a few matches to help forward an angle even more. Now this is better suited in angle federations, but in PCW, youd be surprised how some of the upper card matches are planned out and angled, showing that PCW isnt totally Role-play, but like a 90% 10% ratio of the two. If youre not willing to lose to the person who youre planning with and if they arent vice-versa then the feud will fall apart.

2. What will cause the two of you to feud?

What is the difference between the two of you? This has to be planned and the best way to do this is by having either a slight gimmick change so it clashes with another character perfectly, or by finding a person who already clashes with your current gimmick. A good example of this was the old feud that is still talked about from NTICW and then PCW, Jordan X. Shabazz Vs. Mark Makonie. Thats a black wrestler who believes in equal rights for blacks against a neo-nazi that hates everyone from Jews to Blacks. The feud even made The News in its heyday as the Feud of the Week. So look for golden opportunities like that.

3. Are you prepared to write the necessary segments for the feud?

Everyone knows that to get far in a federation, you have to be active and dedicated to its shows. If you match write, then thats a great way to contribute back, but if you dont, you can also do it through forwarding your own feud and contributing to the show by writing several segments that fit perfectly and that make your matches and feuds interesting. It isnt just about Role-playing so much that you beat your opponent, but to climb up the card, you need to do at least 1 in-card a show, but Id say that its better if you do at least 2 to try and forward the feud. And as you see yourself progress up the ladder in the fed, youll also see yourself doing more and more segments for the shows, so prepare yourself for that.

4. How long do you want the angle/feud to last?

Most feuds rarely make it to two months long if not done correctly, so, therefore you should always plan how long you want it to last. Now the best feud could go as long as you want it, but not every feud can be planned flawlessly, so this is the most difficult part of the planning. But the ideal length is at least a month and a half, long enough to promote a good PPV match. But if you like the feud and it doesnt get old after awhile, you can keep it going for longer by adding in either other characters in the fed that compromise with the feud, or by trying to change the way you and your partner are feuding, having maybe a few swerves. A swerve for those of you who dont know is a surprise ending of a feud or angle. A good example would be the White Mexican turning out not to be dead, but being another character in OSW. People were made to believe one thing and the ending was totally the opposite, hence why its called a swerve. Maybe you could at the end find something out about your opponent that makes you two stop feuding, say you are brothers, or you both are actually dreaming everything bad that has happened to the two of you. This is an interesting concept actually because you could keep all the bad blood in Role-plays and do segments about what happened to try and forward the feud. Then in the end a psychologist tells the two of you in separate sessions that it was all a dream... well this is a crazy idea, something that should never be done, but it is something new, which brings me to...

5. Is your idea original?

Most feuds now are just rehashes of older ones. This makes for boring storylines and it just doesnt end up fun or entertaining. So try to think of something that is interesting and original, even if it's just a new twist on an old theme. Say you are doing an angle in which you're as a sexist character who attacks a woman and you say stuff like "Women shouldnt be in the ring but in the house cleaning, watching the kids, etc." Its been done, but perhaps at the end we find that your character has been left by his wife or that he had a brutal mother. Use your imagination. And don't be afraid to work with off the wall suggestions or direction from your booker.

Thats about all that I can think of right now, its simpler then it all seems, but then again, Ive always found hard stuff easy and easy stuff hard for some reason. You should always compromise with your partner in the feud and let them do some of the thinking and planning as well. The best way is for you both to come up with ideas and then combine those ideas into a good feud that makes for interesting reading.

Thats all for this tutorial and I wish you the best of luck in PWA...

-[name withheld]/Semore Smoke
