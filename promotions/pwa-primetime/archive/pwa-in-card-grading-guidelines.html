Grading Guidelines
2/17/03

The following are the grading guidelines for properly assessing in-cards submitted by PWA students. Note that the grading of in-cards is a bit different than grading RPs.

Size: The issue of size is simply this -- Did the in-card segment take too long to get to the point, did it wrap up much too fast, or was it reasonably well-done in terms of how much space it took and the flow of events? These in-cards are like mini-RPs, and the same issue of size must be taken into account. If the student basically says or does nothing for a five page in-card until the very last paragraph, this is NOT good. On the same note if the in-card is something that's nothing more than a couple of lines of text including the intro, speech, and conclusion, that's not as well, either. While there is no completely ideal size for an in-card, use your own judgment. In-cards especially are for the fans of the shows to connect and learn about the characters and whatever storyline they are trying to run at the time. If you feel unfulfilled with the in-card because the size is too long or too short, it should get a low grade in that category.

Size is graded on a scale of 15.

Ambiance: Ambiance is the same for in-cards as it is for RPs, but the grading is simpler. If the segment takes place in the arena, most e-fed viewers have a pretty similar idea of what a wrestling ring, locker room, catering area, and hallway look like. Not much environmental ambiance is necessary, save for describing objects that the character is directly interacting with. Atmospheric ambiance need only be for the crowd's reaction, and character/character-interaction ambiance is all that is really required, but even then it could be not detailed enough, too detailed, or just right.

In the case of "roving segments," those in-cards that take place in some location away from the arena, in that case the viewer may not be so familiar with the setting and more descriptive ambiance may be needed to set the stage. Atmospheric ambiance such as smells, tastes in the air, and describing the "feel" of the ground and the temperature is just filler because obviously if this were a television broadcast we couldn't tell those things from behind a television screen. Under no circumstances should we see any interior monologue. Your character's inner thoughts are not going to show on TV.

No matter where the segment takes place, it is graded out of 15.

Storyline: Obviously every in-card segment has to have a storyline of some sort. If there is no discernible plot or point to the in-card then I expect the marks here to be harsh. Cameras don't just record anything they come across. The segments that make it onto TV are there for a reason, because they tell a story to establish or advance some plot, whether it be an angle between two people or just character development for the one who submitted the segment.

Again, grading for this is left to what and how much the grader felt was accomplished by an in-card. If it told a good story that left you fulfilled but also wanting to see more from this character, it will get high marks. A decent/average in-card would be graded as such. An unfulfilling or pointless in-card would get the lowest marks.

This category is most important and is graded out of 20 points.

Full Grading Scale:

41-50 A This is a thoroughly enjoyable segment that gets its point across with almost the exact amount of length, description, and action going on. It's unique and entertaining, and succeeds in slightly elevating the wrestler in the minds of the fans (or lowering him if he happens to be a heel). An in-card such as this would be the kind that would be consistently expected of upper-tier wrestlers.

31-40 B While there are the occasional low-spots, this in-card is still better than just being "decent." The size may need to be worked on, and the ambiance and action may be lacking or too much in places, but the handler is getting the hang of things.

21-30 C A C-level in-card is one that needs improvement in all areas. It is not terrible, just average. The writer shows promise and an understanding in how to put together a good segment, but needs to refine his/her writing.

11-20 D Getting a D for an in-card segment means that it needs improvement and there is a possibility that a card editor would reject it. The size either too small or much too large. It has too little ambiance, or is cluttered with filler. Most importantly, the storyline for the in-card segment is severely lacking.

0-10 F Getting an F on an in-card means that it is unacceptable and would most likely be "binned" (tossed away by the card editor). There is much room for improvement if an in-card gets this grade, but unlike RPs, binned in-cards may be re-worked and tried again later. The storyline in such an in-card is very slim or nonexistent, the ambiance is on the extremes of too much or too little, and size is lacking whether in scarcity or overuse of text.
