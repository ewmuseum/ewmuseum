Secondary Characters, Primary Ingredients by Ivan Stanislav
3/07/03

There are several aspects of efedding that one must focus on. These things include gimmick, character, storyline, the past of the character, the future of the character, and his or her goals in life.

But an often overlooked and underused aspect of a character does not come from within, but rather from another person:

The manager, valet, or significant other.

It is not uncommon to see some sort of secondary character in ewrestling. Its easy to think up another character and integrate them into the story. They can be something as simple as a girlfriend or wife, or a manager or close friend. Hessian, for example, has Minion the Imp, a truly unique managerial character!

Bear in mind that if I mention these secondary characters as managers, I merely mean that as a secondary character.

Unfortunately for many, however, the managers are created somewhat quickly and without much thought. The stereotypical bimbo blonde character or sultry vixen is almost always used. Or the goth gal that is far scarier than her actual wrestler that she is watching over.

If one plans on creating a manager, as much thought must be put into it as was the original character and in some times, more thought. There are three major areas that must be focused on: Character, Storyline, and Purpose.

Character

Since you have already created the main character that you will be handling, some of the aspects of the secondary character will already have been handled. But there are still several areas that must be addressed and thought about. These questions you must ask yourself.

What is the relationship between my main character and his manager?

This question is one that can give you a wealth of knowledge. Just as your character has a purpose in life, so does your manager. He/she has wants and needs. He/she has an agenda. Are they married? Happily or no? Are they friends, or forced to work together for a reason? How much trust is there?

For Alexei Ruslan, Ivan Stanislavs manager, the answer is relatively simple. Alexei Ruslan has been a dear friend of Ivans for over ten years. Because of that it has created a strong, rare bond between himself and the stand offish Russian Bear. While Ivan Stanislav is the muscle, its Ruslan that is the brains. His background in the KGB and the FSB has made himself and Ivan into a rare and powerful fighting force.

What does the secondary character do that the main character cannot?

This question too is very important. Its imperative that you have a concise and thorough understanding of the secondary character, so that when others write him/her in a match it can be accurate as well. The manager is there for a reason. Not only that, but the manager can serve many behind the scenes purposes as well that can enhance your writing tremendously.

I had originally used Ivan Stanislav in an older fed but had discovered that it was very difficult to write him for a long period of time. Because Ivan the character had such a hard time interacting with people, it was difficult to maintain a story. Too many roleplays where Ivan just screamed at the camera got old quickly.

But with Alexei Ruslan, it added a completely new element to the character. Ruslan was a witty, smart, quirky character that easily conversed with Ivan. It enabled a multitude of situations that the two could get into and help further a plot. Writing with Ivan became infinitely easier because Alexei served as a foil to Ivan, in a way.

The secondary character is an extension of the main character. A supplement, if you will.

Also, the secondary character enables the main character to be portrayed in a more true way without compromising the gimmick. While Ivan Stanislav almost appears stupid to capitalist Americans, when dealing with Alexei, two of the same culture, he is revealed as a very intelligent individual. This can be seen in Ivans relationship with Tempest as well. Ivan is still an angry, overbearing and destructive wrestler, but when around Tempest we are able to see a more loving, honorable side that normally we would not be able to see and yet still know that Ivan is as strong as he ever was.

One thing you must remember is that the secondary character will rarely ever be regarded as a separate entity to the main character. In a rare case, the manager can serve as a stand alone character, but if not, the majority of the time he/she must be looked at in relation to the main character.

Storyline

As mentioned above, the manager has to be an integral part of the storyline of the main character. There has to be a reason why this person would devote his/her life to working with a wrestler, after all. This doesnt have to be spelled out to the reader, but the handler should have a fairly definite idea of why these two people are working together.

The secondary character, like the main character, should be viewed also in a more worldly sense. The character doesnt appear on wrestling shows and then lock himself in a closet somewhere until the next show starts and emerges again. They have a life of their own as well.

Some roleplays should have the secondary character in them. Sometimes it is best to have most roleplays, especially opening ones, with that character present. Seeing the way that character acts and reacts is important to first time readers. Only recently have I used Alexei Ruslan in a more sparing fashion because now, after almost two years of using him, most people understand the character well enough to not see him constantly.

Many people wonder how it is possible that I dont run out of material to write about. But considering that I have the story of Ivan Stanislav, and the story of Alexei Ruslan and Ivan Stanislav, it enables me to write so much more. Later on, as mentioned above, if the secondary character is able to stand alone, even more can be written. I have only just started to write Alexei Ruslan as slightly stand alone.

Purpose

By now, Im sure a few purposes of the secondary character can be noticed. But one must bear in mind that the secondary character is not there to cheer on the main character only during his match. But the character is there as an extension of the Main character. A supplement, if you will. I know many secondary characters that are never used, and only exist in the matches themselves. If that is the extent that the character is used, then a major group of possibilities is instantly being ruled out.

The secondary character is not only an extension but also a source of information about the main character. He or she can give a different perspective of the main character that most people may not see.

Alexei Ruslan sees Ivan as a hero and in many ways in Alexeis mind Ivan has done many heroic things. But to the common wrestling fan, they only see a barbaric bear of a man that imposes his will on others or crushes them.

Dynamic differences in viewpoint between individuals can really shell and color your main character in ways you never thought possible.

The secondary character is anything but secondary. Instead, he or she is integral to the story. Be it in the beginning of the wrestlers career, in the middle, or in the end, the secondary character can continue to influence those around your main character and draw information to the forefront that some people may not have known before.
