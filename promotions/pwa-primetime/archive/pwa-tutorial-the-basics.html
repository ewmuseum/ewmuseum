The Basics by Xavier Kannon
2/20/03

Just like a real life developmental program, those who stand-out for good reasons are always going to have an advantage. It could be your character, it could be your firm grasp of writing style or it could be your original roleplay ideas - even better, all three. Your character needs to be someone people will talk about, word of mouth through wrestlers to Fed Heads has led to many signings in Wrestle Online.

An old-school wrestler who¡¯s just looking for a break may sound good to some people, but characters like that can be found all over the Internet, and thus diminished it in the eyes of many. Nobody is asking for far-out gimmicks that some would say defy reality, but there is a line between realistic and generic. A lot in the WO Feds use their character to set themselves apart from everyone else; Be it Religious characters like Violence Jack, the Pharaoh or Xavier Kannon. Nationalities such as Ivan Stanislav. Or just bizarre twists that separate them such as Clyde and Syko the Goblin. All have recognisable characters that stand out from the crowd, and in PWA that¡¯s a great advantage.

But, it¡¯s true to say you won¡¯t be hired for character alone - You¡¯ll need the skills to back it up. A good PWA¡¯er should read RPs and grades religiously, everytime you see an RP graded it should be read, so you can then appreciate the grader¡¯s feedback. Out of 30 RPs on the board, 1 might be yours, but there are 30 gradings that can help you.

Taking skill out of the equation, there is then one deadly important factor - Presentation. Nobody is going to want to read an RP full of grammatical errors, spelling mistakes and poor punctuation. For the time spent on a spell-check and proof read, you could be looking at 20% extra. A well presented, left aligned, black font promo with no spelling or grammatical errors automatically has a look about it the says 'professional'.

Another important part of presentation is your style. Novel or Script. Neither is the better format, as both can be used effectively but when done badly can give a promo an immediate disadvantage. If you¡¯re confident with your vocabulary and enjoy writing descriptively and vividly, novel could be your natural format. Look to the likes of Angello Deville and Violence Jack for well written Novel style roleplays. However, if your grasp of English isn¡¯t quite so good, Novel can make your roleplays look simplistic and annoying to read, as nobody wants to read...

"All they say is I said," he said, "Like that."

...For a whole promo, which doesn¡¯t take any advantage of all the descriptive advantages Novel brings. Script style also has may merits, as it¡¯s the best format for if you like your roleplays fast, witty and vibrant, thus is the favourite format for comedy. Imagine reading the dialogue from your favourite comedy show or stand-up comedian in script form, then in novel form. While novel may be able to portray the emotions better, script style lets the dialogue speak for itself, is more snappy, and hits you more immediately.

Another factor, sometimes overlooked, is the character¡¯s name. Now, the name is not as important as the previous points, but is part of what makes up the character so will always be a factor. "Tha Xtreme Legend" HardKore Steve is never getting signed no matter what, but that¡¯s not to say you should just make up a name to be different.... Xakovisic Drefnahagler is an original name, but it¡¯s also stupid. If your character is quite realistic, then give him a grounded name, if he is quite gimmicky think of a way to tie it in without just dumping the gimmick right into it (ala Steve "Milkman" Carnage or Pat "The Ice Cream Man" Dwyer, leave something to the imagination). Imagine if Big Alan Steele had just called himself "The Greek God" or if Ivan Stanislav just went with "The Communist". Although it isn¡¯t the most important thing, your name will be that¡¯s mentioned most, every time you¡¯re booked, numerous times in your match etc.

I hope the tutorial is of help to you, and gives an insight into what WO Fed Heads are looking for. Good luck at the Academy, work hard and follow the Grader¡¯s advice and who knows? One day I might be facing you.

-Mat, handler of Xavier Kannon.

Xavier Kannon is a graduate of the PWA who later went on to win the PCW World Heavyweight Title. He now acts as the President of PCW.
