Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Elite Professional Wrestling opened its website in February 2001 with a pitch built entirely on scale: four annual pay-per-views staged in the Skydome, the Tokyo Dome, Mexico City's Azteca Stadium and Madison Square Garden, and a touring roster meant to span Asia and North America. Its site struggled at first — an early visitor could not get past a login screen — but by April it had a card to show for the promise.

That card was Spirit Carnival, its inaugural show, staged 7 April 2001 and posted whole to the newsgroup across four instalments and a finale. A Japanese-language announce table called the action, an English-language guest booth was brought on for one match and promptly dismissed by its own hosts, and a formal ring announcer worked every introduction. Four matches ran: an Englishman fell to a flying senton in the opener, a manager-led ambush decided the second, a diamond-dust counter ended the third in under five minutes, and the international stable Team Yamada dropped a thirty-one-minute main event to the pairing of November and Shadow Fire.

The night closed on a vote. Every wrestler on the roster chose between the two finalists for a new Spirit Trophy — Shadow Fire, the show's winning tag wrestler, and Ran Jishu, the losing one — and Jishu took it, to a heel's ovation from his own stablemates.

Later cards followed only as website links: an encore of a June pay-per-view billed by its own promoter as the best show of the year, a joint show with another newsgroup fed, and a shift to a text-summary format when the writing staff thinned. Through the summer the promotion kept recruiting for heavyweight, junior and women's divisions, still describing itself, in its own words, as a unique multi-continental vibe worth a look.
