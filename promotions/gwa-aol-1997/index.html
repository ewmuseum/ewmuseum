Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Global Wrestling Alliance ran as a live, dice-refereed federation staged inside AOL chat rooms, reached by keyword GWA, with an independently attested presence on that same platform back to 1994. The promotion's own recruitment copy claimed a much longer pedigree still — operating, depending on which year's post is read, “since 1991... with roots dating back to 1986” or, by 1999, in some form for fourteen years — self-stated claims the surviving record does not independently confirm.

Its March 1996 broadcast opened on its own announcer, Jim Floss, putting out a small fire the show's pyrotechnics had started on his own jeans, sharing the desk with a manager character who introduced himself, mid-broadcast, as “Pro-fessor Presley.” That October it stunt-cast Gene Simmons, Paul Stanley, Ace Frehley and Peter Criss, in full KISS makeup, as guest commentators for a night, complete with a bouncer waving ringside fans into Simmons's lap on cue.

By the autumn of 1997 the league was tracking its own roster in weekly rankings sheets naming a World, a USA and a World Television Champion alongside a ranked top ten drawing wrestlers from half a dozen other named leagues. Its Halloween in Hollywood special that October ran three to four hours of live chat-room action a night across two nights: a no-disqualification ladder match over a locked steel briefcase, a title-for-title tag match between its own World and USA champions, and a three-team Thunderdome War Games pitting entire stables against one another.

By June 1999 the promotion had, in its own words, “recently come very close to extinction,” and it staged a Re-Opening Pay-Per-View headlined by a thirty-man Royal-Rumble-style battle royal to bring in new blood, billing itself “the longest running live e-wrestling” federation on the service.

No document beyond that reopening survives; what came of it is not established by anything else the promotion left behind.
