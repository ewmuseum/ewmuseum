Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Network Online Wrestling opened 4 April 1997, founded by two co-presidents who had each already run and folded a federation of their own. Its own founding ad promised no more than hard work and an attempt to make the league last: “We don't garuntee to never be late, but we certainly try to be on time.” It ran original wrestlers only, on a weekly card called Thursday Night NOW, deciding three titles — a World Heavyweight, a Midwest, and a Tag Team Championship — by whatever format the roster settled once enough members had signed.

By its own three-month retrospective that July, NOW had grown to twelve writers and a second weekly show, and had already turned down an offer to become a regional under a larger loop fed, choosing independence instead. Its own account credits the fed's own name for real-wrestling websites like the EWA and MLWO promoting it. It ran a house style its own later FAQ describes as neither hardcore nor extreme, judged on a mix of roleplay quality, angles, and effort rather than a pure simulator.

NOW closed once, on 30 October 1997, and reopened by 22 November the same year. Its own FAQ, published New Year's Eve, renamed the World Heavyweight Title without the “World” and added a Junior Heavyweight Championship for wrestlers under 245 pounds, run out of a purpose-built NOW Auditorium in Phoenix.

Its fourth pay-per-view, Survival of the Beast 2, was booked for 3 January 1998 in Charleston, South Carolina, headlined by a World Title cage match; no result for that card survives. NOW's own site went up 31 May 1998, still incomplete by its own admission — “only about 5” wrestler bios of the full roster were online at launch — the last document in its record.
