A Japanese-wrestling-themed play-by-mail league, inspired by and partly staffed from the WeWF, that opened with forty-two wrestlers and four titles in September 1991. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

NeWA opened on 9 September 1991 as the Nippon Electronic Wrestling Association — “Introducing... the latest electronic wrestling league!” — a Japanese-themed play-by-mail federation the founding post names as directly inspired by the WeWF, its opening roster stocked in part with WeWF alumni, its own production credited to “the Lightfinger Sporting Network,” the same production banner the WeWF carried on its own newsdesk.

Eighteen days later, its first weekly report went out: forty-two active wrestlers, a full opening card, and a title lineage across the World, Intercontinental, Tag Team and TV Championships, staged as a broadcast with a rotating three-person commentary team — Joe Blimpko, Robby the Lame, and Raunchy Ronny Trumpet, the last of whom “knew” Lord Roderick from the WeWF, the two federations sharing more than a house style.

Its next attestation is a full fourteen months later, on 23 November 1992, in the middle of an argument about whether fantasy wrestling belonged on rec.sport.pro-wrestling at all, when one poster listed the federations then running: “I don't see what it's held on a pedastal above the EMWA, UeWF, PWA, NEWA, ILIW, GCW, SWL or WeWF.” NeWA is the fourth of the nine that sentence names — still active, still counted among a crowded field, more than a year into its own run. Nothing later survives.
