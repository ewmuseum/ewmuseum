A Prodigy-based e-fed that opened its membership to Internet wrestlers in May 1995, running house cards, a pay-for-view special and a weekly talk show under a World and North American title before expanding its championship list within weeks; the same operator and organization are independently receipted appearing on-camera in a 1997 CSWA angle, extending the fed's documented life across a two-year gap this corpus does not itself bridge. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

New Generation Enterprises opened its membership to Internet wrestlers on 17 May 1995, in a post from its own president describing an existing Prodigy operation now taking outside talent “because of several members having the NET since it is the hottest place to be.” It described itself running two house cards a week, a “PFV or Clash of the Champion type of TV Special event,” and a weekly talk show, with a World and North American Championship to start and more titles promised as the roster grew. Anyone who joined by that Friday was slotted onto the next house card, in Havana, Cuba.

NGEN's only surviving results document is the first round of its World-IC Tournament, taped in Augusta, Maine on 28 June 1995 as “NGEN's first stop on its ten stop capital tour.” The card ran on angles as much as finishes: Hideous Zuki was disqualified for blowing dust in “The Ambusher” Mike Trent's eyes, El Santanico's associate Big Evil powerbombed Razor through the announce table for a countout win, and Sleepwalker upset Godzilla with a surprise sunset flip after absorbing a beating. Beast, billed a three-time former YWF Champion, made Steel City Warrior submit to a figure-four; the Earl of Doom won by countout with a piledriver onto the concrete floor and, when the bracket turned up an odd number of wrestlers, was advanced straight to the third round by Commissioner Chris Wink. “Beautiful” Brent Foley beat Ol' Dirty Bastard with a move called the Bootysault, and Nebulous drew a bye into the second round.

The card was posted as its own first intermission, promising a second round to follow. No further round, and no house card or talk-show broadcast, survives.
