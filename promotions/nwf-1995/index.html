Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The National Wrestling Federation opened with a bare recruitment post on 6 July 1995; its own follow-up three days later described a role-playing structure built “like a D&Dragons format,” ranking every wrestler by stamina points into ten numbered classes, class ten reserved for champions. Its own first roster, posted 10 July, already carried two champions at the top of that ladder — Davy Airborne, Heavyweight Champion, and Captain America Rick Dorry, Intercontinental Champion — above dozens of ranked wrestlers and seven tag teams.

The promotion posted its own title history the same week, tracing both championships back to 1984 and crediting Heavy Hulkster Michael Hayes with seven separate Heavyweight reigns, the most of any wrestler on its own record, alongside John “The Butcher” Animal, Bryan Thomas “The Awesome 1,” and Matthew Powers among its recurring multi-time champions.

Its own July TV tapings crowned the Brotherhood (Curtis McGriff and Michael Jones) Tag Team Champions in a five-team elimination match after the title had gone vacant, saw the debuting KO Kid Kevin Allan upset Chris Certains with a cross-body block, and had Davy Airborne retain the Heavyweight title over Heavy Hulkster Michael Hayes after Hilton Flames interfered against Hayes. Supercard VII, announced for 14 August, built toward a triangle match for that same Heavyweight title among Airborne, Hayes, and Flames, a Tag Team Championship match pitting the Brotherhood against Carl Melbourne and a mystery partner, and a twenty-man battle royal; no results document for that card survives.

By its own ratings post of 18 August, the outcome was clear even without the results: Hilton Flames was Heavyweight Champion, and Carl Melbourne and Rick Dorry were Tag Team Champions in the Brotherhood's place. NWF Super Challenge 1, broadcast 21 August, filled in why — Death Inc.'s interference at Supercard VII had cost the Brotherhood their titles, and the two teams settled the score in a non-title match the Brotherhood won by pinfall. On the same card Flames retained the Heavyweight Championship over Mongolian Warrior by double countout, declaring himself, in his own words, “the heartbreakin', dream-makin', smooth operator,” and Rick Dorry retained the Intercontinental Championship over the Arabic Animal by pinfall.

No NWF document after 21 August 1995 survives, and its own operator never discloses a name anywhere in the record.
