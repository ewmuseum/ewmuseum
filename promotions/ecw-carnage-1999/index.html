A British-hosted Usenet e-fed that endured immediate community mockery for sharing its initials with the real-world Extreme Championship Wrestling, then ran its own weekly broadcast mixing invented characters with a borrowed real-wrestler persona. Four wrestlers are recorded across two served documents. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Extreme Carnage Wrestling introduced itself with a bare recruitment post on 23 February 1999, promising twice over that this would be “a professional site” that “will not close down.”

Its surviving broadcast, Thursday Night Fusion (24 June 1999), was fronted by commentators Steve Beauchamp and John “Aero” Smith and addressed personally, mid-show, by its own on-air promoter, who announced a new pay-per-view, Tyke-a-Mania, honoring the promotion's own World Champion and staged, in his own words, for 25 July 1999 at Elland Road in Leeds, England.

That night's card carried two of its four titles: Tyke defended the World Championship against old rival Lusty after a ringside judge ruled Lusty's own weapon illegal and forced a rematch, and Carnage defended the Extreme Championship against Magma in the promotion's own first parking-lot brawl. Its Cruiserweight Championship, crowned days earlier, was held by a wrestler billed as RVD, the ring name of the real-world wrestler Rob Van Dam.

An “I Quit” match between Snake Taylor and Dave Berkshire, refereed by a rival wrestler called the Goth, ended with Berkshire tied to a backstage structure and left for any wrestler in the promotion to come and beat on before the broadcast ended.

No further document survives after this broadcast.
