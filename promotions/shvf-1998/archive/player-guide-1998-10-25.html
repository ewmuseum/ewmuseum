Super Hyper Visual Fighting is a Booked Electronic Wrestling Federation designed to capture the flavor of strong style wrestling from Japan. Strong style wrestling (also known as shoot style) is a more realistic style of pro wrestling focusing on submission and mat wrestling. Strong style wrestling combines elements of pro wrestling with elements from no holds barred fighting to create an exciting, realstic, and brutal hybrid wrestling style. SHVF seeks to capture the competitiveness and excitement presented by real strong style promotions such as the now defunct UWFi and RINGS. 1.2 Success Through Dedication Success Through Dedication is the motto of SHVF. Simply put it means that the more you put into the your wrestler and the more you'll get out of the promotion. You must be willing to sacrifice time, energy and creativity into developing the wrestler you create. The more time and detail you put into devolping your character the more fun you'll have, and the more successful you'll be. The more effort you put into your wrestler, the more effort the staff will put into protraying your character accurately and helping you establish your wrestlers personality. 1.3 Warrior Spirits One priniciple of SHVF's philosophy is a term called Warrior Spirits. This is the notion that your wrestler is striving to get to the top of the promotion so hard that you/he will sacrifice his imagination/body to any lengths in the ring to achieve his goals. 2.0 A Booked E-Wrestling League Why a Booked League? Simply put, a booked league is a vastly more fair, fun, and interesting way of running a e-fed, as opposed to a simulated league. There is little effort to a simmed league, whereas a booked league requires EVERYONE'S participation in order to be successful. Booked leagues are able to reward handlers for there hard work by pushing there wrestlers where-as simmed leagues don't take into account partisipation and instead rely on what basically boils down the randomly simulated results. 2.1 The Booking Committee The Booking Committee is the body responsible for helping to decide the matches for each SHVF card. Sometimes two handlers may challenge each other and the Booking Committee would sign that match right away. Other times the booking committee will make matches according the rankings and win/loss record of wrestlers. Besides making matches, the booking committee is also responsible for determing the outcome of each match. When deciding the outcome of a match the committee looks at strategies and roleplays [flashes, interviews, press conferences, etc.] to determine the winner. In some cases this process is very difficult. Reguardless of the outcome of the match it is our hope that everyone will enjoy the efforts put fourth by both the handlers and staff of SHVF. In addition to making and determining the outcome of matches, the booking committee is also responsible for releasing the offical SSW rankings and deciding on such things as injuries and title contention (which is based upon ranking and won/loss record). Rankings and injury listings will be included in email updates after each card. Currently the Booking Committe is made up of only myself, [name withheld] [ [detail withheld] ]. Anyone interested in helping with the booking committee or with the writing of matches should feel free to email me. 3.0 The Federation Itself 3.1 Rules & Regulations While most basic pro wrestling rules apply to SHVF, there are some adjustments and additions. All non-title matches have a 15 minute time limit, while title and top contenders matches have a 20 minute time limit. If the time limit expires without a fall (submission, knockout, or pinfall) then the match proceeds into an over time period(s). Non-title matches have one, 5 minute overtime period. Title and top contenders matches have two, 5 minute over time periods. If at the end of overtime there still hasn't been a fall, then the match will be declared a draw. The only exception to this is at supercards where matches that go the full distance will be decided via judges decison based upon the SHVF points system. The points stystem is as follows: -2 points for each near fall or forced rope break -1 points for each takedown or knockdown No high flying moves that involve jumping off the ropes are allowed in SHVF. Using the ropes to increas momentum (i.e. running and bouncing off the rope) is allowed. This rule is inplace to help more accurately portray the strong style we are trying to capture. All the action in SHVF matches takes place in the ring. Leaving the ring for any reason during a match will results in immidiate disqualifiction. 3.2 The Titles Super Hyper Visual Fighting recognizes the following Championships: 1)SHVF WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT TITLE: The crown prize of SHVF. The holder of this title is reconized as the most dominent wrestler in SHVF. The World Heavyweight title is an honor reserved for the elite of the promotion. World Title defenses will be scheduled by SSW officals about once every month. Additional title defenses can be scheduled by the champion through the booking committee. In the event that a champion cannot (due to injury), or will not, defend the title, the champion will be stripped of the title. 2)SHVF SUPER-MIDDLEWEIGHT TITLE: The top title of the Middleweight divison. The holder of this title is reconized as the best wrestler 240 pounds and under in SHVF. Many compeiters in this divison later "graduate" to the Heavyweight divison. Super-Middleweight title defenses will be scheduled by the booking committee about once a month. Additional title defenses can be scheduled by the champion through the booking committee. In the event that a champion cannot (due to injury), or will not, defend the title, the champion will be stripped of the title. Additional Titles may be added as the need arises. 3.3 Title Contention Title contention is based upon SHVF's offical rankings. These rankings are determined by win/loss record and quality of opponents. For example a wrestler who is 4-0 against un-ranked oppenents, will most likely not be ranked above a wrestler who is 2-2 against oppenents who are ranked. Title shots will be awarded bases on rankings and other outside factors including record against champion and past 4 match results among other things. Ranked wrestlers who defeat the title holder in a non-title match will automatically recieve a title shot. While Title matches will be booked by the booking committee about once each month , champions may take the option of defending the title other times by selecting and asking for title matches against a certain opponent, or accepting the challenge of another wrestler. However, Titles may only be defended against those ranked in the offical rankngs. This ensures the level of opposition is of the highest caliber. 3.4 Weekly TV Shows REVOLUTION: Revolution, SHVFs main show, takes place every Sunday night. The show features 2-3 matches (including pre-match interviews and analysis). Interviews from wrestlers not partisipating on the show will aslo air on this show as well. Revolution takes place at various venues around Japan. Among those included are: Yokohama Bunka Gym, Osaka Buen Arena, Nagoya Aiichi Gymnasium, Tokyo Budokan Hall, Kumamoto City Gymnasium, Tokyo Korkuen Hall, Kanagawa-Odawara Arena, Fukui City Gymnasium, Tokyo Sumo Hall, Kawasaki Gym, Tokyo Ota Ward Gym, Tokyo Komazawa Olympic Park Gym, Sapporo Nakajima Sports Complex. SHVF will also release a flash packet 1-3 times a week entitled "Ringside." 3.5 Super Cards Super Hyper Visual Fighting presents 5 regular special events or supercards each year. Additional mini-supercards may be added if the need arises. ULTRA-CLASH TAG LEAGUE: This round robin tag league takes place throughout the month of January. Wins are worth 2 points and draws 1 point. The top scoring teams will meet in the finals for the championship trophy in Tokyo Budokan Hall. This is a highly antisipated event since this is the only time of the year to see regular tag action in SHVF. RISING GENERATIONS: Features a tournament between the rising stars and newcomers of SSW. The card focuses on future stars. This card usually takes place in March at the Fukuoka Dome. DREAM NIGHT: The card is headlined by a dream match selected by the fans [handlers/players]. All titles are defended and all the top stars are in action. Usually takes place in June at Kawasaki Stadium. LIONS GRAND PRIX: This card plays host to SHVF's annual 16 man tournament, the Lions Grand Prix. The quarter, semi, and final rounds all take place on this card. The tourny winner gets a shot at the World Heavyweight Championship, usually at Strong Style Evolution. Usually takes place in June at the Osaka Dome. STRONG STYLE EVOLUTION: SHVF's most important show. All of the top wrestlers are in action with all championships on the line. This card Usually takes place in November at the Tokyo Dome. The Wrestlemania of SHVF. 3.6 Roleplays 1-3 times a week SHVF will release a Flash Packet entitled "Ringside" featuring roleplays by the wrestlers. These should be quality flashes and not just simple one paragraph statements consisting of 5 lines of dialogue. It is expected that ALL TALENT that will compete on the coming week's Revolution card will submit a flash/interview segment. Remember that matches are determined based upon strats and roleplays so not submitting either can greatly hurt your chances at winning. SHVF also asks that wrestlers who are scheduled to compete on the coming weeks Fight Night card submit a brief pre-match interview to air diorectly before the match. Wrestlers are also encouraged to submit a post match interview or press conference. Roleplays aren't judged on quanity but on quality. There is no need to write a 5K roleplay if you can state what you want to state in a 4 paragraph roleplay. The same goes for strats. Roleplays can be interviews [with a SHVF interviewer (see staff)], flashes, press conferences, highlight packages, music videos, or anything else you can imagine that helps to devople your wrestler. 3.7 The Staff SHVF has assembled a fine collection of officals, annoucers, and referees. Hadi Moditian - The owner and producer of Super Hyper Visual Fighting. Moditian is a former wrestling annoucer, best known for being the President of Pro Japan Wrestling before it closed. He is 43 years old, and highly respected within the wrestling industry. He founded SHVF in 1998, after being out of wrestling for several monthes, with the hopes of revolutionizing the sport. Christopher Reece - SHVF's lead annoucer. Reece, the former "Voice of the PJW", is a skilled and knowledgable annoucer who knows what hes talking about. Reece has followed both professional wrestling and shoot fighting in Japan for many years via wrestling magazines and newsletters. He has been an outspoken supporter of the Strong style or wrestling since PJWs closure. Kunihiro Yamamoto - Provides color commentary and analysis for Revolution. Yamamoto is a legend in the martial arts world. His experince in the sport brings invaluable insight to the annouce team. Many consider him responsible for the devolpment of the strong style, thus his nickname is "Father of Strong Style". Kyoko Kosugi - Press Directer/Interviewer - Kyoko is a 26 year old former female journalist for the Tokyo Sun Magazine. She like others in the front office has former ties to Moditian through her job as in-ring interviewer and magazine editor for Pro Japan Wrestling. Shes very good looking and suprizingly knows her stuff when it comes to wrestling. Tajiri Hayashi - Head Offical - Hayashi is a 10 year vetran referee of pro wrestling and no holds barred fighting. He is fair, objective, and generally doesn't make mistakes. 3.8 [Style] Angles, Alignment, Stables, Etc. This section is meant to define the style of SHVF. Please pay close attention as you'll find this isn't your average e-fed in some ways. This is a pro wrestling league so obviously angles are allowed and encouraged. However, angles that take place in SHVF should be more traditional than prehaps other angles that take place in other feds(they should be similar to All Japan Pro Wrestling angles if you will). Some examples of possible angles include: Can wrestler A beat wrestler B, or can wrestler #1 execute <such-and-such move> on wrestler #2, etc. Another storyline or angle for a match could be that the two men have met twice before each with one win, who will win the rubber match to take the 2-1 edge? Another way this fed differs from others is in the heel/face catagory. While there are faces and heels to a degree in SHVF, and your asked to define your wrestler as face/heel/neutral on the form, there generally are no good guys or bad guys, just wrestlers. Since outside interfearance or chearting aren't allowed, its hard to establish a wrestlers alignment. The only way to do so is through interviews and attitude in the ring. Naturally a really arrogrant guy will be a heel. Stables, Dojos, and alliances are not only allowed in SHVF, they are encouraged! However stables take a different meaning in SHVF. SHVF stables should function more like mixed martial arts Dojos such as the Lions Den do. Members should train together, prehaps appear in interviews together, and be in each others corner for matches. But they should still wrestle each other when a match is signed between them. Also as covered before, since interfearance isn't allowed, there will be no stable mates running out of the back to interupt a match. An example of an angle that could be created from a stable would be wrestler A is defeated by wrestler #1. Wrestler A's stablemate, Wrestler B challenges wrestler #1 to avenge his stablemates loss. 4.0 Creating a Character 4.1 Application Analysis Keep in mind when creating your character the kind of promotion we are trying to simulate in this league.
