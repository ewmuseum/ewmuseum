The Boone Sisters Members: Alabama and Dixie Boone Team Alignment: Face Wins: Losses: Status: Active Description: Combined Weight: 222 lbs (Alabama 110, Dixie 112) Entrance music: "Sweet Home Alabama" History: Dixie Lou and Alabama Boone are half sisters and the daughters of Maddie Boone. They grew up together on Maddie’s dirt farm just outside Jackson, Mississippi. Maddie had 9 kids. Dixie and ‘Bama were the eldest. They quickly developed bad tempers, often arguing with their younger siblings or their mama. These arguments often led to fights. Dixie’s bad temper got her expelled from school for fighting in the Eighth Grade. Alabama was able to make it to the Ninth Grade before she too was expelled for fighting. The fights usually started over the same kind of things. Other girls insulting where the Boone’s were from, what they were wearing, they way they spoke, asking them for the correct time, telling them their shoes were untied. You get the point. They’d fight with anyone over anything. And usually they lost.
With no school to go to, they spent most of their time at home beating up their little sisters. This provided them the confidence they needed to go out looking for fights with girls their age. Fights they almost always lost. Alabama worked as a waitress at Denny’s for a while. This job ended with her losing a fight with a 65-year old grandmother customer who Alabama argued with over whether or not her grandson ordered a burger or a melted cheese sandwich. But in her defense, the grandmother was pretty rough looking. As she lay in a pool of blood and milk shake on the floor, she thought… “There must be something more out there.”
Dixie, being 18 at the time, found work as a stripper at “The Mud Flap Lounge.” This job ended abruptly when the 75-year old mother of one of her regular customers found out her son was frequenting the club. The religious old woman didn’t like the fact that her son was “whoren’ around with some tramp.” And blamed Dixie for “luring her son astray.” Dixie had never done anything with the man, but she didn’t like the tone in the old woman’s voice. Needless to say the old woman proved a much greater threat than Dixie had imagined. As she staggered home, trying to stop the bleeding caused by the woman biting her with her dentures, she thought… “There must be something more out there.”
But the last straw came at home. Dixie and ‘Bama were watching TV when two of their little sisters, Molly (age 9) and Shelley (age 7) changed the channel so they could watch cartoons. Dixie and ‘Bama didn’t like anyone interrupting Jerry Springer. So they started pushing their little sisters around as they had done for years. But this time fate dealt them a losing hand. After years of abuse, all their sisters including 5 year old Delia and 3 year old Rose, ganged up on their two older sisters and handed them yet another butt kicking. As they crawled to their room in the double wide, trying to fend off the rage of this under five-foot mob, they felt that this was their lowest point.
When Maddie came home and heard what happened, she decided enough was enough. She knew a wrestling promoter named Jasper Trout. After several calls to him he agreed to take in her two wayward daughters and train them to be wrestlers. She thought that constant exposure to “women who ken whup their li’l butts” might serve to “take some o’ the sass outta em.” But she was wrong.
Night after night the two girls got killed in the ring. They earned the nickname “The Stretcher Sisters.” But they kept coming back for more. This earned them the love of the fans. Well, that and their looks. They mistook the love of the fans for being good in the ring. This made them demand even more matches that were out of their league. It became a vicious cycle. The more they got beat, the more the fans cheered them on. The more the fans cheered them on, the better they thought they were. They better they thought they were, the tougher the matches they demanded. They tougher the matches they demanded, the more they got beat. This led to a “feud” (if you can call it that) with The Hellraizers. The two behemoths pounded Dixie and ‘Bama in five matches. Almost killing them in three. But the Boone’s kept coming back. It was during the last of these matches that a Battling Ring Angels talent scout saw them and signed them to a contract. They each put an “X” on the dotted line and were off to the big time.
