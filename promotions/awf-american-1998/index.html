Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Established, by a hosting hub's own later record, in September 1998, the American Wrestling Federation presented itself in its own March 1999 handbook as a fed born out of frustration: “I got tired of watching good role-players get the shaft because they were not popular with the president... I promise as President that if you out role-play your opponent you will win the match.” It planned two weekly shows — a talk-through recap, “Saturday Breakdown,” and a flagship play-by-play card, “Monday Night Mayhem” — pay-per-views every two months with names chosen by its own members, and three titles, World, Television, and Tag Team, each defended on a fixed clock.

Two days later, its vice-president reported the promotion still short of a working roster: “We presently only have three wrestlers, which is not nearly enough to open a federation,” and pointed hopeful sign-ups to a new web address.

By November 1999, one of its own wrestlers was still recruiting on its behalf, describing a fed that took only invented characters (“no Sting, Goldberg, Steve Austin, fake wrestlers only”) and calling it “one of the best e-feds on the internet.”

A hosting hub's own directory still listed it, as of March 2000, as an open, roleplay-based, invented-characters-only league; it is absent from the same hub's next capture, in June 2001.

No card, roster reveal, or match result of it survives under any address; how, or exactly when, it closed isn't recorded.
