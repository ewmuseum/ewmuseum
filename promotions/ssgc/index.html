Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Southern States Grappling Council opened in 2003 out of its own GrappleVision Studios in Memphis, Tennessee. Its own founding retrospective, “The Best of the SSGC,” posted 4 August 2003 and narrated by broadcaster Abraham Martin, opens by telling viewers they have “missed our first three shows, filled with action, young talent, and a whole lot of drive” before recapping the promotion's own Television Championship tournament from its opening card.

That tournament put four men in a gauntlet for the vacant title: “The Pretender” Eli Bridger, “The Jackal” Bryan Vincent, the rugby-bred Jason Lennon, and Memo Machado. Bridger used his own interview time to frame the belt as “proving I belong with the best,” but the title went to Machado, who knocked Bridger out with the belt itself while the referee was down and pinned him for the win — a finish the broadcast's own announce team called “a travesty of justice.” Machado marked the win with a backstage promo daring the locker room to “send Jason Lennon, hell, you can even send John Lennon and Yoko Ono,” and Lennon answered in his own interview, previewing a tag match against The Wolfman and “The Wolf” Lark Fenriz by promising “the sort of pain you never imagined possible.”

Its only other surviving show, Power Surge (15 October 2003), opens with Vik Ventressa outlasting Toxic in a back-and-forth opener decided by a flying superkick, and previews a card built around Gregory Brians against Jesse Bollinger, AsH against a debuting ArchAngel, a Television Championship defense between Hayden Grayson and Adam Cage, and a rematch between Bridger and Vincent.

How the Southern States Grappling Council ended isn't in its surviving record.
