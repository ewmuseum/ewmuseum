Welcome to the Virtual Wrestling Alliance!!!! Hey there! Welcome to an e-fed that I think will make it's name in the annodes of the e-wrestling world! The concept is simple, and it came from something i noticed recently with two different fed-styles. There are *plenty* of feds out there with just character-created wrestlers. That's good and all, but maybe you wonder how your wrestler would do against Bret Hart or Ric Flair? So, you go to a fed that has just real wrestlers in it, but you can't have a character-created wrestler there..not to mention, the real wrestlers that you'd want to use there are all taken, so all you are left with is Reno Riggins. Not much fun, huh? Well, this is where the VWA is different! This is the only e-wrestling fed (to my knowledge) that incorporates real wrestlers with character-created ones! Now you can find out if your guy has what it takes to take the VWA World Title from 911, or if you can succesfully defend the VWA North Am title from Shawn Michaels. All you need is some time and creativity, and you'll be set in no time! Before I go any further, I want you to know that you will *not* be able to control a real wrestler. This makes it more fair to everyone else, 'cause if ten people want to have Curt Hennig, and only one got him..then that leaves nine really annoyed people. The concept is simple: you don't own him in real life..so you don't own him here. (Somewhere down the road, I *may* make an exception with real/fake guys forming tag teams. I'll discuss this more in the Tag Team section.) ======================================================================== ======== CHARACTER CREATION: Ok, this is where the creative juices start flowing! TECHNICIAN - Technicians are the masters of holds and counterholds, the scientists of the squared circle. Bret Hart, Curt Hennig, and Ric Flair are some of the more famous technicians. BRAWLER - Stree toughs who know how to fight, in every sense of the word, both in and out of the ring. Brawlers can be ruthless and brutal when it becomes necessary (and even when it's not they still tend to be so). Stan Hansen, Terry Funk, and Razor Ramon are some well-known brawlers. POWERHOUSE - These are the big men with tons of muscle who like to toss their opponents around the ring like rag dolls. They tend to have impeccable physiques and look like human tanks. Lex Luger, Davey Boy Smith, and Sid Vicious are some well-known Powerhouses. AERIALIST - These wrestlers rule the air above the ring, and generally use a large number of spectacular, high-flying moves. Well known Aerialists include Jushin Liger and Jimmy Snuka. BLOB - These guys are huge, enormous, man-mountains. They love to smother their opponents with their girth almost as much as they enjoy eating. Yokozuna, Earthquake, and Mabel are all well known blobs. DEVIOUS CHEATER - These guys are the preachers and practicers of illegal moves. Rather than concentrating on wrestling ability, these monsters pride themselves on being able to get away with the most dispicable acts and not get caught. Abdullah the Butcher, Kevin Sullivan, and the late Eddie Gilbert are perfect examples of such men. RECKLESS MANIAC - These are the guys that most wrestlers would rather not step into the ring with. They execute all sorts of crazy, psychotic, high-risk maneuvers no other wrestlers would ever dare to attempt. They seem to enjoy using their own bodies as weapons as long as their opponents are injured as a result. Some of the more well-known Reckless Maniacs are Sabu, and Cactus Jack. MARTIAL ARTIST - These wrestlers seem to come right out of a Bruce Lee movie. They use extraordinary martial arts maneauvers in the ring to dazzle both their opponents and the crowd. Most, but not all, are Asian. Some of the more well-known martial artists are Ricky Steamboat, Hakushi, and the Great Muta. This is how your wrestler will act in the ring or at interviews. What you have to send me is a brief description of how your wrestler acts: Is he arrogant? Is he patriotic to his homeland? Is he a ruthless SOB? You get the point. Tips on how to do a good gimmick: (Here's some more stuff from Teeters) 1.) Before entering a league, it's a good idea to ask for a few cards to see what else is out there. It gives you a feel of what type of people are out there and will prevent two people from running characters with the exact same names or gimmicks. Do we really need 5 Undertaker clones running around? 2.) Before you pick a gimmick, here's a few things to consider : - Can I effectively run this character? Is this the kind of person I would be interested in writing for. For example, I once came up with a Russian Olympian type character. I had moves and an outfit and everything... then I had to write interviews and came to the realization... I couldn't come up with a believable stuff for this guy to say because I didn't know Russian and couldn't fake an accent. - Is this character believable? I personally like characters that are realistic. A guy who magically disappears or other such crap can really ruin the game. A human element helps certain characters. People have a tendency to go overboard when pushing a character (usually applies to heels) and then you get McMahon syndrome. Like a guy who was supposedly a drug dealer like character or a nuclear mutant suddenly becomes a face. Yeah, he was eating children yesterday, but now he's a fine upstanding individual! - Be careful with comedy gimmicks. They can be good and bad. Some guys can write comedy, others can't. Also, what you might think is funny, others might consider really lame. And in case you're wondering, I hated Doink (as a heel and face.) As a wise man once said "Dying is easy, comedy is hard." - Keep in mind, not all faces are friends and the same holds true for heels. Some of the biggest (and hottest) feuds in e-wrestling have been between guys of the same alignment. ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- So, this is what a sample sheet would look like: NAME: Derek Holmes HEIGHT: 5'11" WEIGHT: 237 lbs. Add on the sheet that he is part of a tag team, and mention his partner's name. Each of your members will get one finisher, and you will also have a team finisher. Example: Road Warrior Hawk's finisher is the top-rope clothesline, and Road Warrior Animal's is a powerslam, but the Road Warriors' combined finisher is the Doomsday Device. Get the idea? I'm still deciding this..but sometime in the future, I *may* allow creating tag teams that include a real and a character-created wrestler. If I allow this, then the rules for using a real partner are the same as having a real manager: You better know the guy well enough to do a convincing interview with him. If the tag team is temporary (say a one-shot on Wednesday Nite Havoc), then the rules do not apply, but I'll allow this *only* if it is a result of something happening on a prior show. Example: Bret Hart was wrestling Yokozuna..when Owen Hart ran in and attacked his brother. Yoko and Owen proceeded to pummel Bret until Derek Holmes came in and made the save. This prompted a tag match for next week between the four guys. ======================================================================== ======== INTERVIEWS: - This is the fun part of the whole thing, in my opinion..having your wrestler speak and act! This is where you show how nasty an SOB you are, or what you plan on doing to your opposition. Here are some tips on how to write a good interview (from Teeters): - Try to keep them coherent. Spell check them if possible. - Say what you want to say, but you don't have to write an epic. A lot of the best interviews I've seen have been relatively short (one to two screens long). Who wants to read through 80 pages of ranting? - Try to stick to your alliance. Faces in general shouldn't be egomaniacs and should generally try to be nice guys or at least likable. One of the many reasons I hate Hogan. - Try to avoid "cheap heat". Cheap heat can be something that's in bad taste. Try to avoid things that are racist or in bad taste (that beating up women). There are more effective ways to get characters over. I won Heel of the Year for a character who wrestled cleanly, This is something which is partially interviews, partially GM responsibility. One of the things I tend to hate is when players give their characters mega pushes as a face or a heel. If everyone in the game wasn't so lazy :-), I would like to see a somewhat frequent poll of the players to see who they think is good at getting their character over or not. There are several levels of characters : Over Heel : Ric Flair, old Shawn Michaels. The marks hate them, but they've got a style to them that they get some cheers. Not Over Heel : The Honky Tonk Man. This character sucks. Everyone hates them. Open chants of "YOU SUCK" occur when character is around. Over Face : The guy is over. Popular as can be. Guys like Sting, Bret Hart, and the new Shawn Michaels. Not Over Face : pre-turn Bob Backlund, Lex Luger. Yeah, yeah, yeah. Shut up. You've got the charisma of a rock. From there, the GM can gauge how over the character is. That way you don't have character who's interviews really suck or doesn't act like a face getting huge crowd pops. There are three classifications of an interview: 1) The "Flash" This is usually a one-sided affair. This places the wrestlers in a setting of their choosing, either the VWA Soundstage, the gym, the back alleys of Chicago, the secret hideaway of a criminal organization, you name it. You basically comment on whatever match/feud/"angle" you're involved in at the time, threaten your opponent, etc...the more imaginative, the better. 2) The Interview A third party is usually involved in the interview process, usually a random announcer from the home federation. This is placed in a question and answer format, where the interviewer asks the characters specific questions to be answered. This is usually the easiest kind of interview to script. (Lemme just add something here: If you are going to do this..here are the guys that you could use as the interviewer: - Chris Steeves: Color announcer for Wednesday Nite Havoc. Good for heel interviews. - Alan Marx: Unbiased interviewer. You'd use this guy if the interview was in the ring area. - Jack Saxon: The "roving reporter." Will go pretty much anywhere to give an interview. When you write the interview..be sure to use one of these guys.) 3) The Biased Interview (or Piper's Pit wannabes) From time to time a character will run a Piper's Pit style of interview segment, where they can praise other characters of their alignment and make fun of characters of the opposite alignment. These are VERY good to generate heat with, and are often the site of pre-match jumps from other wrestlers. Usually the person handling the weekly interview segment will dictate who is to be interviewed here. Real Wrestler Interviews Now obviously I'm not good enough to accurately write convincing interviews of *all* the real wrestlers (especially since I haven't watched WCW in a while, and I don't get ECW), so I'm gonna give you the chance to flex your creative skills yet again (and in the process help me a *great* deal!). You will get the chance to script what you think one of the real wrestlers would say. The same rules apply for these guys as it does for the creater-owned ones. I will take the best ones and use them on the show. Please keep in mind, that I'm sure *all* of you can do a great flash/interview for a particular. So, if it doesn't show up the first week, don't feel bad..I will most likely put it in on a later show. ======================================================================== ======== GAMEPLANS: Well, it's time to rumble! But what are you going to do? Are you going to attack the guy before the match begins? Are you going destroy the guy if you lose? That's where the gameplan comes in. Since the program I have is a booked fed (in that i just set up the matches..the rest is up to the computer), all I need from you is what you plan on doing before/after the match, and maybe some things you might do during the match. Squashes: (from Teeters) A squash match is a way for you to introduce the other players to what your character can do in the ring. Anyone who takes the time to do this will receive one rankings point for your character. RULES: 1) Squash Matches must follow the format outlined below. 2) Squash minimum - 5 lines; Squash maximum - 15 lines 3) Only one squash for your character every three weeks. SAMPLE SQUASH MATCH: Derek Machismo vs. Harry Janowitz (jobber) Derek strode out to "Invincible" by Pat Benatar, and that was an accurate summation of his dismantling of the hapless Janowitz. A clinic of power moves, ranging from atomic drops to chokeslams, battered Janowitz from one end of the ring to the other. After about five minutes of what was little more than a workout to Derek, he finished him off with his trademark finisher, the Powerdriver, for an easy three and the duke. How to write a Gameplan: Gameplans should be as innovative, as interesting and as effective as the situation warrants, but it should be REALISTIC. Any gameplans that say your 215 pound jr.-heavy will press slam the 568 pound Mabel or any that involves being raised from the dead will be tossed into the trash, and I will decide the strategy *for* you! So what makes a good gameplan? 1) Be as direct with what you want done as possible. The clearer the instruction, the better chance a fedhead will execute it to your satisfaction 2) Be tricky and creative if the situation warrants it. As fedheads, I am pretty liberal, and will allow a lot of things. I do have the right to veto a gameplan if I feel it is unrealistic or will hurt the natural flow of the game. ======================================================================== ======== TITLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES: There are five titles in the Virtual Wrestling Alliance: VWA World Title: This is the big one!
