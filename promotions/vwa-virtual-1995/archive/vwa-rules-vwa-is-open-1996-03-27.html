VWA Rules — The VWA is Open

27 March 1996
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
---------------------------------- Virtual Wrestling Alliance Rules (v. 2.2) By: [name withheld] ---------------------------------- Thanks to [name withheld] for letting me use some of the rules from his (now defunct) e-fed, the JCWF. In turn, he got stuff from [name withheld] + [name withheld] (EWC) and [name withheld] + [name withheld] (EWF). I edited some of the stuff, but the basics are still there. Thanks guys! Welcome to the Virtual Wrestling Alliance!!!! Hey there! Welcome to an e-fed that I think will make it's name in the annodes of the e-wrestling world! The concept is simple, and it came from something i noticed recently with two different fed-styles. There are *plenty* of feds out there with just character-created wrestlers. That's good and all, but maybe you wonder how your wrestler would do against Bret Hart or Ric Flair? So, you go to a fed that has just real wrestlers in it, but you can't have a character-created wrestler there..not to mention, the real wrestlers that you'd want to use there are all taken, so all you are left with is Reno Riggins. Not much fun, huh? Well, this is where the VWA is different! This is the only e-wrestling fed (to my knowledge) that incorporates real wrestlers with character-created ones! Now you can find out if your guy has what it takes to take the VWA World Title from Sting, or if you can succesfully defend the VWA North Am title from Shawn Michaels. All you need is some time and creativity, and you'll be set in no time! The VWA is sorta set differently from the other e-feds in one other aspect as well (in my opinion)...I have it so the VWA *interacts* (in a way) with the real world of pro wrestling. What I mean by that is not only do I have the real wrestlers in the fed, I recognize the champions, and I have any injuries, retirements, etc, be reflected in this fed. For example, when Shawn Michaels had his "collapse" back in November, and couldn't wrestle in the WWF, I didn't have him wrestle in the VWA either. On the other hand, I just finished up an angle where one of my characters spent the last few weeks in ECW to finish an something I had started between him and Shane Douglas. So, the VWA is set sorta in between e-wrestling and pro-wrestling, which I think makes it a bit more interesting and realistic. You don't get to *exclusively* own any of the real wrestlers in this fed. What this means is that...well...*all* of you own them! Any of you can write a flash or interview (explained later) for *any* of the real wrestlers in the fed; you can incorporate them in a superpower group (provided the group is made up of at least three other owners - y'know.. guys like you); I may even allow your created wrestler to form a tag team with a real wrestler (I'm still deliberating on this one..more later). The only thing you can't do is send in a strategy for any paricular real wrestler (unless I open it up for anyone to send in one). The reason I'm doing it like this is simple: If ten people came into the fed asking to own Curt Hennig or Shawn Michaels, and only *one* is allowed to control him.. that leaves nine really PO'ed people. This way, I believe everyone can feel they have a certain say in what the real wrestler can sound and act here in the VWA. Ok..enough of *my* rambling...let's talk about *wrestling*!! ================================================================================ CHARACTER CREATION: Ok, this is where the creative juices start flowing! This is the basic stuff you have to send me when you create your wrestler: NAME: HEIGHT: WEIGHT: ALIGNMENT: HOMETOWN: STYLE: ATTIRE: ENTRANCE MUSIC: MANAGER: (if there is one) MOVES: MOVE PERFORMED TO THE OUTSIDE: FINISHER (and description if necessary): MOVE PERFORMED BEFORE FINISHER: SECONDARY FINISHER (if any): GIMMICK/BACKGROUND: IN-RING ATTITUDE: I'm hoping that you can get by without me explaining name/height/etc., so I'll talk about the things that need more explaining. -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- ALIGNMENT: You have two choices here: face or heel. For those of you that don't understand these concepts: FACE -- Short for Babyface (good guy). Examples are Bret Hart or Sting. HEEL -- The bad guy/cheating wrestlers. Examples are Ric Flair or Lex Luger ---------------------------- STYLE: (from [name withheld] JCWF rules) This is how your wrestler will act in the ring, and how he might be outside the ring. The styles briefly are: Technician Brawler Powerhouse Aerialist Blob Dev. Cheater Reck. Maniac Martial Artist You may cross style (Brawler/Powerhouse) with the following rules: You may not cross style more than once (No brawler/aerialist/martial artist/technicians) and the cross class must make sense, at the least after you explain it to me (No blob/aerialists.....) TECHNICIAN - Technicians are the masters of holds and counterholds, the scientists of the squared circle. Bret Hart, Curt Hennig, and Ric Flair are some of the more famous technicians. BRAWLER - Stree toughs who know how to fight, in every sense of the word, both in and out of the ring. Brawlers can be ruthless and brutal when it becomes necessary (and even when it's not they still tend to be so). Stan Hansen, Terry Funk, and Razor Ramon are some well-known brawlers. POWERHOUSE - These are the big men with tons of muscle who like to toss their opponents around the ring like rag dolls. They tend to have impeccable physiques and look like human tanks. Lex Luger, Davey Boy Smith, and Sid Vicious are some well-known Powerhouses. AERIALIST - These wrestlers rule the air above the ring, and generally use a large number of spectacular, high-flying moves. Well known Aerialists include Jushin Liger and Jimmy Snuka. BLOB - These guys are huge, enormous, man-mountains. They love to smother their opponents with their girth almost as much as they enjoy eating. Yokozuna, Earthquake, and Mabel are all well known blobs. DEVIOUS CHEATER - These guys are the preachers and practicers of illegal moves. Rather than concentrating on wrestling ability, these monsters pride themselves on being able to get away with the most dispicable acts and not get caught. Abdullah the Butcher, Kevin Sullivan, and the late Eddie Gilbert are perfect examples of such men. RECKLESS MANIAC - These are the guys that most wrestlers would rather not step into the ring with. They execute all sorts of crazy, psychotic, high-risk maneuvers no other wrestlers would ever dare to attempt. They seem to enjoy using their own bodies as weapons as long as their opponents are injured as a result. Some of the more well-known Reckless Maniacs are Sabu, and Cactus Jack. MARTIAL ARTIST - These wrestlers seem to come right out of a Bruce Lee movie. They use extraordinary martial arts maneauvers in the ring to dazzle both their opponents and the crowd. Most, but not all, are Asian. Some of the more well-known martial artists are Ricky Steamboat, Hakushi, and the Great Muta. MANAGER: This is the person that your wrestler may depend on for either moral support, or for smacking the opponent upside the head with a chair if it helps you to win. When you send me your wrestler's description, send me a brief description of the manager..keeping two things in mind: 1. Does he interfere? 2. Does he have a foreign object, and if so, does he use it? I *may* allow you to use a real manager, *if* you can prove to me that you know the guy well enough for a convincing interview. For example, if you want Paul E. Dangerously to manage your guy..you better show that you could "talk" like him (to a point..I'm not expecting mind-readers here)! ------------------------- MOVES: (from [name withheld]) Moves are simple. For example, Sabu would come off the ropes, springboard off a chair and nail his opponent with a variety of moves (senton, plancha, legdrop, etc). -------------------------- FINISHER: Ahh..the thing that all us e-wrestlers want to be famous for: the trademark finishing manuver. This is the thing that will almost guarantee a victory once executed. All you have to give me is the name of the finisher and a brief description of what the manuver is like, or closely resembles (Examples: the Perfect-plex is considered a fisherman suplex, and the Sharpshooter is a scorpion deathlock). -------------------------- MOVE PERFORMED BEFORE FINISHER: This would be the move that would signal the beginning of the end for your opponent. Once this move is performed, the crowd knows what's going to happen next. Examples of this would be the Ultimate Warrior Gorilla pressing his opponent before the dreaded Big Splash; Ric Flair's kneebreaker just before the Figure Four Leglock is applied; or The "Stone Cold Stunning Ringmaster Superstar" Steve Austin using his Stun Gun before using the Million Dollar Dream. -------------------------- GIMMICK/BACKGROUND: Time to really start being creative here! This is how your wrestler will act in the ring or at interviews. What you have to send me is a brief description of how your wrestler acts: Is he arrogant? Is he patriotic to his homeland? Is he a ruthless SOB? You get the point. Tips on how to do a good gimmick: (Here's some more stuff from [name withheld]) * READ THIS ONE!!! * 1.) Before entering a league, it's a good idea to ask for a few cards to see what else is out there. It gives you a feel of what type of people are out there and will prevent two people from running characters with the exact same names or gimmicks. Do we really need 5 Undertaker clones running around? 2.) Before you pick a gimmick, here's a few things to consider : - Can I effectively run this character? Is this the kind of person I would be interested in writing for. For example, I once came up with a Russian Olympian type character. I had moves and an outfit and everything... then I had to write interviews and came to the realization... I couldn't come up with a believable stuff for this guy to say because I didn't know Russian and couldn't fake an accent. - Is this character believable? I personally like characters that are realistic. A guy who magically disappears or other such crap can really ruin the game. A human element helps certain characters. People have a tendency to go overboard when pushing a character (usually applies to heels) and then you get McMahon syndrome. Like a guy who was supposedly a drug dealer like character or a nuclear mutant suddenly becomes a face. Yeah, he was eating children yesterday, but now he's a fine upstanding individual! - Be careful with comedy gimmicks. They can be good and bad. Some guys can write comedy, others can't. Also, what you might think is funny, others might consider really lame. And in case you're wondering, I hated Doink (as a heel and face.) As a wise man once said "Dying is easy, comedy is hard." - Keep in mind, not all faces are friends and the same holds true for heels. Some of the biggest (and hottest) feuds in e-wrestling have been between guys of the same alignment. -------------------------- IN-RING ATTITUDE: This is what your wrestler would do in the ring to get the crowd to pop either positively or negatively. This could be something to get the crowd going, like jumping onto the second turnbuckle and raising your arms in the air; or maybe your wrestler says/does something that would signal that his finisher is about to come up. Examples of In-ring Attitude would include Curt Hennig saying "Now you're gonna see a Perfect-Plex!"; Ric Flair "Whoooooo"ing to the crowd; Jake Roberts spinning his finger in the air just before his dreaded DDT; or Cactus Jack going "Bang! Add on the sheet that he is part of a tag team, and mention his partner's name. Each of your members will get one finisher, and you will also have a team finisher. Example: Road Warrior Hawk's finisher is the top-rope clothesline, and Road Warrior Animal's is a powerslam, but the Road Warriors' combined finisher is the Doomsday Device. Get the idea? I have been considering as to whether a created wrestler can form a tag team with a real wrestler, and I figured that this can be allowed. *BUT*...if you do this...you *have* to work with me and/or one of the fedheads (as we are the ones that have the final say..and we are the ones that basically control the real wrestlers), meaning that you and I would work out a strategy *together*. You would not have sole control over the real wrestler. The bottom line is..if you want a tag team w/ a real wrestler, at least one of the fedheads must work with you. Simple as that. ================================================================================ SUPERPOWERS: This is sorta expanding on the idea of a created-real tag team, except that it involves several wrestlers, created and/or real. We have a couple of super-power groups in the VWA right now, and I think that they are really helping the fed in a way (and I'm not just saying this because I'm in one of them :). Here's how this is going to work: - You get at least three other owners together to form a supergroup, *if* you want to have some real wrestlers in the group (if it's just created, then you can have as few owners as you want (or even just yourself...whatever floats your boat.) - You mail myself or the other fedheads (e-mails at the end of the rulebook), telling us that you are forming a group and would like to have so-and-so join. - The rules regarding the use of the real wrestler in a tag team apply here as well (in other words...we're in the group too, involved at the very least with any strategy involving the real wrestler. After that, you can have us involved as little or as much as you'd like.) =========================================================================== INTERVIEWS: - This is the fun part of the whole thing, in my opinion..having your wrestler speak and act! This is where you show how nasty an SOB you are, or what you plan on doing to your opposition. Here are some tips on how to write a good interview (from [name withheld]): - Try to keep them coherent. Spell check them if possible. - Say what you want to say, but you don't have to write an epic. A lot of the best interviews I've seen have been relatively short (one to two screens long). Who wants to read through 80 pages of ranting? - Try to stick to your alliance. Faces in general shouldn't be egomaniacs and should generally try to be nice guys or at least likable. One of the many reasons I hate Hogan. - Try to avoid "cheap heat". Cheap heat can be something that's in bad taste. Try to avoid things that are racist or in bad taste (that beating up women). There are more effective ways to get characters over. I won Heel of the Year for a character who wrestled cleanly, This is something which is partially interviews, partially GM responsibility. One of the things I tend to hate is when players give their characters mega pushes as a face or a heel. If everyone in the game wasn't so lazy :-), I would like to see a somewhat frequent poll of the players to see who they think is good at getting their character over or not. There are several levels of characters : Over Heel : Ric Flair, Vader. The marks hate them, but they've got a style to them that they get some cheers. Not Over Heel : The Honky Tonk Man. This character sucks. Everyone hates them. Open chants of "YOU SUCK" occur when character is around. Over Face : The guy is over. Popular as can be. Guys like Sting, Bret Hart, and Shawn Michaels. Not Over Face : pre-turn Bob Backlund. Yeah, yeah, yeah. Shut up. You've got the charisma of a rock. >From there, the GM can gauge how over the character is. That way you don't have character who's interviews really suck or doesn't act like a face getting huge crowd pops. There are three classifications of an interview: 1) The "Flash" This is usually a one-sided affair. This places the wrestlers in a setting of their choosing, either the VWA Soundstage, the gym, the back alleys of Chicago, the secret hideaway of a criminal organization, you name it. You basically comment on whatever match/feud/"angle" you're involved in at the time, threaten your opponent, etc...the more imaginative, the better. 2) The Interview A third party is usually involved in the interview process, usually a random announcer from the home federation. This is placed in a question and answer format, where the interviewer asks the characters specific questions to be answered. This is usually the easiest kind of interview to script. (Lemme just add something here: If you are going to do this..here are the guys that you could use as the interviewer: - Chris Steeves: Color announcer for Saturday Nite Rumble. Good for heel interviews. - Tony Marx: Unbiased interviewer. You'd use this guy if the interview was in the ring area. - Jack Saxon: The "roving reporter." Will go pretty much anywhere to give an interview. When you write the interview..be sure to use one of these guys.) 3) The Biased Interview (or Piper's Pit wannabes) From time to time a character will run a Piper's Pit style of interview segment, where they can praise other characters of their alignment and make fun of characters of the opposite alignment. These are VERY good to generate heat with, and are often the site of pre-match jumps from other wrestlers. Usually the person handling the weekly interview segment will dictate who is to be interviewed here. Real Wrestler Interviews Now obviously I'm not good enough to accurately write convincing interviews of *all* the real wrestlers (especially since I haven't watched WCW in a while..yeah, like I'm missing much :), so I'm gonna give you the chance to flex your creative skills yet again (and in the process help me a *great* deal!). You will get the chance to script what you think one of the real wrestlers would say. The same rules apply for these guys as it does for the creater-owned ones. I will take the best ones and use them on the show. Please keep in mind that I'm sure *all* of you can do a great flash/interview for a particular guy or team..and I'm looking forward to reading them, but I may not be able to get them all on at once. I will try my hardest, though, to get them all on the shows. I will usually let you know what interviews/ flashes I'd like when I post my weekly newsletter. ================================================================================ GAMEPLANS: Well, it's time to rumble! But what are you going to do? Are you going to attack the guy before the match begins? Are you going destroy the guy if you lose? That's where the gameplan comes in. Since the program I have is a booked fed (in that i just set up the matches..the rest is up to the computer), all I need from you is what you plan on doing before/after the match, and maybe some things you might do during the match. Squashes: (from [name withheld]) A squash match is a way for you to introduce the other players to what your character can do in the ring. Anyone who takes the time to do this will receive one rankings point for your character. RULES: 1) Squash Matches must follow the format outlined below. 2) Squash minimum - 5 lines; Squash maximum - 15 lines 3) Only one squash for your character every three weeks. SAMPLE SQUASH MATCH: Derek Holmes vs. Terry Bulimic Holmes came down the aisle slapping hands with all of his fans, while Dangerously followed close behind obviously talking to someone on his cel phone. Once Holmes stepped in the ring, he showed the old timer in the ring why he is considered to be among the best technicians in the sport today. He never let up on his opponent, scoring several near falls and basically looking like he was toying with the man. Holmes ended it quickly when he applied his submission manuver, the Nightmare, on his hapless foe, to which Bulimic submitted nearly instantly in 0:03:20. How to write a Gameplan: Gameplans should be as innovative, as interesting and as effective as the situation warrants, but it should be REALISTIC. Any gameplans that say your 215 pound jr.-heavy will press slam the 568 pound Mabel or any that involves being raised from the dead will be tossed into the trash, and I will decide the strategy *for* you! So what makes a good gameplan? 1) Be as direct with what you want done as possible. The clearer the instruction, the better chance a fedhead will execute it to your satisfaction 2) Be tricky and creative if the situation warrants it. As fedhead, I am pretty liberal, and will allow a lot of things. I do have the right to veto a gameplan if I feel it is unrealistic or will hurt the natural flow of the game. Example: Derek Holmes vs. Bret Hart Obviously Derek knows what kind of match he is about to get into when he steps in the ring with one of the best wrestlers in professional wrestling. Derek is gonna want to keep this as scientific a match as possible, staying on the defensive for the first part, and looking for any openings in the veteran's strategy. He has a lot of respect for the Hitman, but as Hart well knows, business is business, and Derek is gonna be 100% focused on getting the 1-2-3, or the submission. He may shake hands afterwards, but not at the beginning of the match. This isn't out of respect...but to show Hart that he means business, as I'm sure Bret wants it to be as well. Example: Ric Flair vs. Sting Flair wants the rest of the Horsemen to come down the aisle with him, to show Sting what exactly what he got himself into this time. He has a coin roll stuffed in his tights to use in case there are any problems in the match. Woman has some powder ready as well. Flair will take his time at first...deliberately stalling in order to get Sting's dander up and obviously the impatient fool is gonna mess up because of that. Once he does, Flair will find the opening and exploit it for all it's worth. After all...he's a 13 time World Champion and "The Dirtiest Player in the Game".. what are the officials here gonna do? ================================================================================ TITLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES: There are five titles in the Virtual Wrestling Alliance: VWA World Title: This is the big one! The title that *everyone* will be gunning for! If you manage to win it, congratulations....you just became a marked man! VWA North American Title: This is similar to the Intercontinental/ United States title...this is also a hotly contested title. VWA World Television Title: Similar to..well..you know what it is. VWA World Tag Title: Just like the World Title, except for Tag Teams. VWA North Am Tag Title: Just like the North Am title, except for Tag Teams. You *have* to defend the title(s) whenever it is scheduled for you to do so.. They will be defended on the VWA shows and Pay-per-views: SATURDAY NITE RUMBLE This is the VWA's equivalent to Monday Night Raw/Monday Nitro on those *other* feds. Your hosts for the evening are Tom Summers (Blow-by-blow) and Chris Steeves (heel color commentator). There will also be Piper's Pit shows, PPV update reports, and any other interesting things I happen to think of. The VWA TV title *must* defended on this show, and the North Am (singles/tag) and/or the World Title(s) may be defended on the show at the champion's discresion or if situations require it. VWA LIGHTNING CLASH This is like (you guessed it) WCW's Clash of the Champions. Basically like Wednesday Nite Havoc, except just name/name matches. The TV title may be defended at the champ's discresion or if situations allow it. The North Am Title(s) *must* be defended at these shows. The World Title(s) *must* be defended at least once per two clashes. PAY-PER-VIEWS This is where the big feuds started on Rumble or the Clash will end. Most specialty matches will be allowed on these.
