Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Virtual Wrestling Alliance opened on 29 October 1995 with a broadcast it called Wednesday Nite Havoc, staging three qualifying matches for a World Heavyweight tournament its own commentary promised would crown a champion the next month at November Nightmare, a card its own broadcast frames in a sold-out Toronto SkyDome. Its founding rules document explains what made it different from the rest of the e-wrestling world it was joining: “This is the only e-wrestling fed (to my knowledge) that incorporates real wrestlers with character-created ones” — an invented wrestler could chase a title held by Sting, or defend one against Shawn Michaels, and the promotion said its own storylines would track a real wrestler's injuries and retirements as they happened.

Five championships eventually ran its roster — a World Heavyweight title, a North American Championship, a World Television Championship, and tag titles at both the World and North American level — and the promotion staged its changes as grudges rather than clean transitions. Mario Ducon, Marty Jannetty and Kendo Godai each held the top belt across its first year before Derek Holmes won it in the summer of 1996; a month into his reign, Patriot Games — a card its own broadcast opened with a dedication to the dead and injured of the Atlanta Olympic bombing the night before — turned the title over to his own closest friend, the Aerial Marauder, in a match built entirely on the two men's bond rather than any feud between them. Getting there had cost Holmes a stretcher job of his own: at Lightning Clash, he answered an ambush by Frank Knight and a special referee Bret Hart — the beating that followed an earlier “I Quit” match Holmes had actually won — by recruiting Shane Douglas as a tag partner for revenge. Two months before that, at Wrestle War, a double-ring, double-cage War Games match had settled which faction ran the locker room at all: Marty Jannetty, the Aerial Marauder, Wildheart and Thunderhawk, managed by Paul E. Dangerously, against Mario Ducon, Sam Holmes, Lex Luger and Reptile, managed by Jacques Parizo.

Weekly cards ran under rotating names — Saturday Nite Rumble, Wednesday Nite Havoc, later a Sunday Nite Rumble — for most of two years, and the promotion's own head booker used the newsgroup itself to ask its readers, nine months in, whether anyone outside the fed was actually reading the shows and what they thought of the writing. A web home page went up in August 1996. One reader's own convention-style write-up of an early card describes buying a title-belt replica and wrestler posters at the merchandise stands and betting with a friend on which real wrestler would turn up as the fourth member of a Four Horsemen-styled stable.

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. A reader asked after its most recent card as late as 29 January 1997, and a fellow newsgroup promotion's own broadcast nearly a year later, on 16 October 1997, still billed Derek Holmes — not the Aerial Marauder — as its World Heavyweight Champion, meaning the belt must have changed hands at least once more somewhere in between. Nothing further survives.
