“Coming of Age: Genesis.”

My life was hit hard, and was seemingly demolished right before my eyes. It wasn’t supposed to go down like it did. The schedule we had didn’t call for what happened. The day was supposed to be just my girlfriend (of two years) – Maria – and myself. We were supposed to be enjoying a relaxing day at the beach of Coney Island; and not worry about anything in the world.
But I remember the setting, and I have her life-changing words memorised. I couldn’t just forget them – and believe me, I tried. I tried like hell.
It was nearly evening, and the sun was setting. I couldn’t overlook those brilliant colours in the sky – red, orange, and pink – for they made the single most beautiful sunset I’ve ever seen. It was ideal; the day was simply perfect.
I sat on the cool sand, in just a pair of denim shorts, if I remember correctly. Maria sat between my legs, facing out towards the flat, never-ending ocean, the tides gently lapping at our feet and ankles. I recall thinking to myself, that ‘life was good’ in that solitary second of serenity.
And I remember when… “Vinnie,” came my name, called meekly by Maria. I felt as though I was prepared for everything, and anything that came from her mouth would just add to the flawlessness of our day. “I’m pregnant.” … And along came the perfect day’s anticlimax. I can’t remember if I even replied to her, and I definitely can’t remember if I even believed what had come from her mouth. The next thing I knew, I was slamming my bedroom door shut, and turning on the Rolling Stones’ Beggars Banquet. I threw myself onto my rickety old bed, and stuffed my face into a pillow.
If what she said were true, at least I would still have a chance at suffocation. I don’t know if I even brought my car home, or just ran, hoping to escape the potential truth. The only thing that was on my mind was if Mariabella was telling the truth, that I was going to be a father. And to my chagrin, I knew that she wouldn’t lie about something of this magnitude.
Around the time I finally came to, realised that I was still breathing (and that the music had come to a halt), I had to find my girlfriend. Had I been coherent after her news, I would have been sensible about the entire situation, rather than running for the hills.
When I got to her house, it was nearly midnight – I wasn’t waiting until morning. The moment she opened the door, I was apologising, fumbling my words. If there was one thing in the world that I never wanted to lose, it was my Maria. She had forgiven me, telling me that it wasn’t uncommon for a reaction like mine.
Until the morning hours we were sitting in her living room, talking about what we were going to do – how we were going to raise our unborn child, how we were going to support it – and of our future together.
Nine months later, upon the day of March 13th, 1975, our first child was born. At the unripe age of eighteen, Maria and I had a family. Our son, Giovanni Zanipolo Tilleli – referred to as ‘John’ – made us so. I wasn’t sure that was I prepared to be a father of another life, but Maria had confidence in me, which gave me a newfound confidence in myself to do well.
Our families never got on too well, and even with the birth of John, they refused to bury their strife. But we didn’t care: we were too busy living our own lives, taking care of another. Our families could have begun World War III, and demolished each other at that point in time, and neither of us would have broken daily stride.
Only a few months passed before we progressed our lives even more. On September 23rd, 1975, I proposed to Maria during a romantic dinner – John being babysat by a close friend of ours – and she said, “Yes.”
We were married on January 1st, 1976, our families not in attendance; and yet, we still didn’t care. We had everything we needed within each other: love, family, support, and a lifetime to spend together. We had been blessed with a healthy son to rise properly, and we had lives to live.
I never even thought I was taking a risk this time around when I thought to myself, ‘life was good.’ Because it was… ~ fin.
