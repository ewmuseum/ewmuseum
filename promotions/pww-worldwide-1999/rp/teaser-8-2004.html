PWW T-Minus [8]

February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
What this is One of the federation’s own countdown teasers to the reopening: a staged late-night television interview in which the PWW’s owner tells the federation’s own history, his career in it, and why he is bringing it back.

¦ PWW : T-Minus ... [8] ¦

... fade in Letterman: ... and the number one signs that your wrestling coach is nuts ... he teaches you a hammerlock involving a real hammer!
(Audience laughs) Letterman: You know what Paul, that used to be true a few years ago but the things that these wrestlers get up to these days, you never can tell
Shaffer: Totally right there Dave, one minute you see two old guys in the ring getting booed by grannies and the next thing you know it is two Playboy centerfolds in a ring of jello
Letterman: ... or was that just where you spent last night Paul? Shaffer: ... hey, you said you wouldn't talk about that! (Audience laughs) Letterman: Hey, it is ok we know that last night you and I were eating snickers bars on TV.
Shaffer: Yes we were. Letterman: And I'm holding up the Snickers and said if we don't get a truckload by tomorrow, something is desperate -- you're eating one and we're talking about how not only they're tasty they're like a meal in themselves. We were passing them out, so - and I --
Shaffer: Must have come today. Letterman: I thought I'd have trouble finding a place to park because of the big snickers truck. Zippo, nothing! Not a call, not a letter, not a courier, not a single nothing! And I said what is this, Vassar?
(Audience applauds) Letterman: So if we don't get the snickers tomorrow, then I'm going to turn on them and I'm going to drive them out of business. I'll start saying nasty unpleasant things about their product.
(Audience laughs) Shaffer: All right. Letterman: But I'm going to give them a 24-hour notice, because two weeks ago we were talking about M&M's and how great they are, well, today we received our free M&M's, but they're making a mistake too.
Shaffer: Wait a minute. Letterman: Here's what it is, the plain M&M's, you can't do any better than that, they were great the first time, they're still great today, they'll be great one hundred years from now, it's a simple beautiful idea, I can eat my weight in M&M's. Okay, in a pinch I'll take the peanut M&M's, in a pinch! They're okay, well, you think they'd recognize that and leave it alone, well now they've got like six different varieties of M&M's, and they're sending those over to me thinking I'm going to talk those up, but I'm telling you, they're no good. They're no good.
(Audience applauds) Letterman: They've got a new crunchy M&M. Shaffer: Yeah? Letterman: What is making it crunchy is what I want to know. Shaffer: I'm afraid to ask. Letterman: And they got some other cheese flavor, like a Parmesan, but god bless the M&M's, they delivered. These
punks over, the Snickers -- Shaffer: It's nice of you to give them 24 hours. (Audience laughs) Letterman: Yeah I know, but you know what? Shaffer: No, what? Letterman: Well one night we talk about M&Ms and then we get M&Ms delivered to the studio, and tonight we just talked about wrestling and women in jello and guess what?
Shaffer: Wow .. you mean we got some babes and jello arriving? Letterman: Not quite, but we do have our next guest ... the former World Champion wrestler, JAY PHOENIX!
(Paul and the band play as the audience applaud) "Day by day, watching you disappear Wishing that you were still here beside me On my own, swimming against the tide There's nobody on my side but your memory Then I'll rise, right before your eyes On wings that fill the sky Like a Phoenix rising Like a Phoenix rising Wings of fire, tearing into the night Screaming into the light of another day Carry me out of the hurricane Into the smoke and flame and we'll fly away And I'll rise, right before their eyes On wings that fill the sky Like a Phoenix rising Like a Phoenix rising Higher, higher hear the thunder roar from above Fire, fire, fire make me whole And I'll rise, right before their eyes On wings that fill the sky Like a Phoenix rising Like a Phoenix rising Wings of fire, tearing into the night And we'll fly away ... " (As the music fades away, Jay Phoenix makes his way out, and smiles at the band before shaking hands with Dave and sitting down in the chair closest to the desk)
Letterman: I didn't know the guys could play that. Phoenix: Yeah, I know ... I spent a while teaching it to them you know Dave. Letterman: What ... you play music as well as wrestle? Phoenix: I started playing the guitar when I was a young boy Dave, something that my grandfather taught me, actually one of the many things that he taught me growing up.
Letterman: C'mon, you are kidding me now aren't you Jay? (Dave looks at Paul Shaffer) Letterman: He is kidding isn't he Paul? (Paul shakes his head) Paul: Not at all Dave, Jay there spent a few hours with us this morning going over that song, he plays some pretty mean guitar actually.
(Audience applaud while Jay looks a little embarrassed) Letterman: Well color me impressed ... so not only do you have a pretty impressive collection of titles in the world of wrestling behind you, but you also get the Paul Shaffer seal of approval on the musical front, and that is not easy to do.
(Paul glances down at his notes while he shuffles them on his desk) Letterman: ... and in case you didn't know it folks, Jay here was part of the US Olympic archery team at one time
(Phoenix interrupts with a smile) Phoenix: While that is true, I never actually got to the Olympics Dave, as I changed career so to speak before I got a chance to take part.
Letterman: ... that would be when you started wrestling right? Phoenix: Yeah, that was when I first started, when I got an offer to meet the president of the PWW, a man by the name of Alan Miller.
Letterman: The PWW ... a place that I hear is very close to your heart. Phoenix: Indeed Dave, it is the first place that I wrestled professionally and the first place that I achieved real success in this industry ... it is my home still.
Letterman: ... so what sort of success did you get there Jay? Phoenix: Well I did ok Dave. (Dave glances at his notes again) Letterman: You were the only grand slam champion in the federation's history Jay ... meaning that you won every title that they had there ... I would say that is pretty much more than just ok you know ... I think that you are just being modest.
(Phoenix laughs) Phoenix: ... well would it help if I pointed out that I actually held most of the titles more than once Dave?
(Audience laughs) Letterman: That's more like it! ... and after you had won those titles and climbed to the top of the wrestling World, you changed pace and actually bought the PWW?
Phoenix (nodding): Yes that is right ... the PWW had gone through a bad period where the owners and management were more concerned with their own popularity and lining their own pockets than they were with putting out a good product for the fans, so thanks to the years that the PWW had given me ...
Letterman: Pretty lucrative years Phoenix (smiling): Yes, I cannot complain ... but thanks to them I was able to buy the PWW outright and take control of it in 2002.
Letterman: ... and what did you do? (Phoenix's face clouds over slightly, but then he gives a small self-depreciating smile) Phoenix: I closed the fed Dave ... it's stock was down, the locker-room were not very happy, and instead of trying to fight a losing battle I organized one final show, a super-card if you like, where everyone on the roster got to say a proper farewell to the fans.
Letterman: ... and what then? Phoenix: Well after that the PWW closed it's doors, and I went off into the independent scene for a while, working for other organizations around the World from time to time.
Letterman (reading his notes): Places like Primetime where you were a finalist in the first ever Global Title tournament ... and places like both the UWF and 4WW, names that are very respected in your industry, where again you achieved success.  In fact you are the wrestler credited with holding the most World titles within Primetime aren't you?
Phoenix: I don't honestly know Dave, I have had my share of good fortune and success it is true.
Letterman: ... and now you have new plans I hear? Phoenix: Yes that is right ... I have decided to bring the PWW back, and to open it's doors to a new generation of wrestlers, and a new generation of fans as well.
Letterman: After a two year break, what made you decide to do that Jay? Phoenix: Simple really, recently I organized the fourth annual Chris Williams Memorial Cup ...
Letterman: I caught that show, excellent event Jay, and something that you should be proud off, especially as it was to honor a fallen colleague
Phoenix: ... yeah Chris was a colleague in the PWW, but he was also a friend, and it was at the Cup that I realized how much I missed the PWW, how much I missed the excitement that it brought me, and more than that how much the current stars and fans are missing out on.
Letterman: What do you mean? Phoenix: I mean that I want to bring the PWW back to give wrestlers the same chance that I got ... a place where they can prove themselves, show their stuff and learn at the same time, a place where they are not judged on who they are but on what they can do ... I want them to get the same chance in this business that I got ... and I want to give the fans a taste of true excellence, something that the PWW will guarantee to bring them!
(Audience applauds) Letterman: Big words Jay, can you live up to them? Phoenix: Dave, I have made a career about living up to my words, about facing challenges and overcoming all the odds to triumph.  I mean look at me, I am not exactly the biggest man in the World being only five foot eleven, but I have worn the PWW World Title on two occasions ... and numerous other titles as you said as well.  I was told that I wouldn't make it in this business, as I was too small ... well I proved them wrong, and I am going to do the same again only this time it is not just for myself, I won't be fighting in the ring ... I will be running the PWW and showing the World what quality means today.
Letterman: Well I wish you all the luck in the World with it Jay, and I know from seeing you and hearing the desire in your words that if anyone can make this happen you can.
Phoenix: Thank you Dave, and if I can say one thing more? Letterman: Of course you can Phoenix: I just wanted to let people know that the PWW is now open for business and for the first time ever we are holding open auditions, so if you think that you have what it takes to be a star of the future ... a PWW superstar ... then simply get in touch with us.
Letterman: Who knows ... the Jay Phoenix of the future may be watching this right now! Phoenix (smiling): who knows Dave, who knows? Letterman: ... well let me just say now, thank you for being here, and good luck with everything.
(Audience applauds) Letterman: We'll be right back with Snow Patrol, who are going to perform for us. (Commercials) ... fade out
