Making a Character: Play Face or Heel?

undated (captured 10 February 2005)
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Making a Character Play Face or Heel? This is a tough call. Though Vince McMahon is a big speaker on how there are no good guys or bad guys anymore, he's full of crap. Fans cheer faces and boo heels, right? Well, there's no telling who the fans will cheer these days or in what country they will be loved, but play your character face or heel.
Face means they will usually play to the crowd. They care about the fans cheering them on. It gives them energy and makes them better wrestlers. It often means you'll be the one taking the beating during the match as usually in wrestling it's more dramatic to have the good guy down and out and suddenly make a miraculous comeback. A face usually has more of a sense of humor than a heel and isn't as much of a trash talker.
Heel means you don't give a damn. You don't give a damn about anyone but you. Oh, you might be in a stable, but you'll stab them in the back eventually. If you don't, they'll stab you in the back. It's what heels do. You'll usually dominate the match, but will take more clean pins than the face does. Your ego will be your strength and your weakness all rolled into one.
Tweeners are common, but eventually you're usually face or heel. The reason is when a feud is happening, the fans will usually choose sides in it. If they don't, it means they have no cares who wins the damned thing and you're dead in the water. (Watch a Hardcore Holly vs Billy Gunn match sometime and see how this doesn't work out.) If you do choose to be tweener, make your story an interesting one and be ready to fight everybody.
Don't flip flop on your alignment. It weakens your character and you'll run out of ideas quick if you're changing sides all the time. If you do make a face/heel turn, make it dramatic and preferably at a pay per view where the stage will be more public. It adds to the drama of the pay per view and makes it the bigger event anyways.
When you're a face and you trash talk, remember the fans are watching. I see some curse-riddled tirades from face characters that sound more heelish than Triple H and it baffles me. These people insist they are face, but fans turn on wrestlers for saying far less than some of these "face" guys do.
Regardless of whether you are a face or heel, stick to it. If you lose a match or two, don't assume you're "not getting over." The vote may have been closer than you think. Just work towards your goal, stick along a single path and you'll usually have some form of success. If you have a strong character with a rigid set of moral, or immoral values, you're ready to become a major player in the fed world.
