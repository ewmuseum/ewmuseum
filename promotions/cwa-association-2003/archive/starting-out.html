Starting Out

undated (captured 10 February 2005)

Starting Out Okay, so you are looking to start an e-fed and you're really anxious to get going. However, if this is your first try you need to know a few things in order to survive.
1. When building a site, keep in mind a few things. It needs to be easy to navigate and needs to have some sort of color scheme to it. The more organized your site looks, the more likely people are to snoop around a bit and possibly apply. If you're not a web site genius, you can find free site templates while starting out to give you the bascis you need to get your site going.
2. Hosting is of ultimate importance. Is your site, not built, free of advertising? Are your boards easy to read with no limit to the size of posts? People hate pop-ups and if you shell out a few bucks to register a domain name and rent yourself some web space, you'll impress people. It shows your fed isn't fly by night if you pay for it.
3. Be careful in what you do to recruit people. Do not go to another fed's boards and e-mail people there to join your site. Don't spam people and expect them to like you and sign up. This is a prime way to keep an empty fed empty for good. Instead, make affiliates, find newsgroups and forums which allow fed advertising and get friends of your involved in the process.
4. Most feds fail due to poor planning. This isn't just a web site matter. Some feds have the site and hosting is great, but they lack foresight. Once you have around 6-8 members, get the first card up and plan things out. Get those people you have to find people they know to sign up and get the ball rolling. When starting out, patience goes a long, long way.
5. Be ready for the unexpected. Make lots of friends in fedding. Netowkr yourself to many feds if you have the chance. At some point, a fed will close and you might wind up with the members of that fed falling into your lap. Suddenly, you are leading a major fed when it was empty mere weeks before that. Don't plan on a fed closing to help you out, but it can be a Godsend.
6. Take polls on what the people you have want. Poll them on a good date for the events, on which set of boards to use and how the results should be written.
7. Don't plan on staff. I snicker when I see a guy starting his first fed asking for a graphics guy, writers, a GM, a VP and any other job they can think of. Be prepared to do it yourself and I mean all of it. No matter how dedicated your staff is, they will never be as dedicated to your fed as you are.
8. Never steal. Don't steal a layout, a roleplay, a logo, or a web page. If you're found out and exposed, you look like a complete loser.
