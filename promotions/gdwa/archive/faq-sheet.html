GRAND DRAGON WRESTLING ALLIANCE The G.D.W.A. FAQ Sheet ver. 4a Table of Contents: Introduction Why You Win How You Win Flashes and Roleplay Challenges Titles GDWA as a booked fed Booked Angles Regarding Strategies GDWA Character Sheet Info I. INTRODUCTION Glad you have interest in the best Women's league on the net! My name is [name withheld] and I'm your Head Booker and President of the Grand Dragon Wrestling Alliance. The GDWA is a booked fed run primarily on character roleplay, strategy, angles, and other player involvement. It is very intense and only the best make it to the top. Competition is everywhere and only the greatest survive. The object of the GDWA is to have fun. After all, this is just a game, so don't take this too seriously! We're all in this game to have a little fun. So play it that way. Contacting other handlers for angles is not only allowed but encouraged. We're all looking to have some fun, and if we all play to have fun, then we'll all have fun. Don't take this too seriously. If you ever have any concerns or questions contact me, and we can talk. We run a card once a week called the Tuesday Night Catfight (or the Saturday Nite Special) which will entail no less than 3 singles matches. Each handler (that's you!) is allowed 1 singles wrestler. Upon garnering experience in fed, it is possible to upgrade to 2 wrestlers, though your second one would be put on a Waitlist. My job will be match making, booking and writing the cards. The excitement is really going to be coming from you, the handler. Get the idea? Off the bat, let me inform you of what you already know. For every win there is a loss. So don't get discouraged. Second, remember that this is purely entertainment, and you should be willing to seperate yourself from your character's accomplishments. Experienced handlers know of those 1 or 2 guys in every league that obsess over their wrestler and lose perspective. This game is all about fun! Never forget. II. Why You Win Winning is what we all aspire to do! So understanding your responsibilities and what you should expect from me is crucial in your tenure in Grand Dragon. The booking takes into account both IN RING work--which is exemplified by your strategies--and OUT of RING work--which is exemplified by flashing (first and foremost) and roleplay/angles. What I value most as a fed head is interactivity with the promotion and other handlers. Flashing is most important in Grand Dragon, with strategies being a close second. So the premium is on your presence in the GDWA, and not how well you construct a strategy to win. Let's construct a scenario to make this point clearer. 2 wrestlers, let's say Officer May Order and Daisy Butterfly, are wrestling soon. On the night I work on the card, I attempt to book the match. Daisy Butterfly's handler has done little to build up a storyline for this match, has flashed infrequently, yet turns in the BEST DAMN STRATEGY I'VE EVER READ!!!! I'm impressed with this work and it becomes the talk of the town with my e-wrestling buddies in other promotions. Officer Order's handler on the other hand has flashed extensively and built this match up to great expectations. The league can't wait to see this match...but the handler of said wrestler was out drinking last night and turns in a horrible strat. The result of this difficult booking scenario is in favor of Officer Order. The match might result in a draw or a count out victory for Officer Order; depending on what will serve the league best and yet be fair to both participants will decide how I book it. Neither woman presented a balanced effort, which would have been ideal, but the roleplay is favored over strategy. Although an inebriation has prevented the handler from putting forth the wrestler's best effort, the handler has placed Officer Order in a good spot. The handler has put more work into the match over time, and this is far more favorable than Daisy Butterfly's handler who's best effort was only a strategy. The reasoning behind this is the following: putting together a strategy merely benefits 1 person. Constructing a storyline, along with building up heat, adds to the totality of the match INSIDE and OUTSIDE of the ring!! Fellow handlers anticipate a great contest. In short, Officer Order's handler has given more to the LEAGUE as a whole than Daisy Butterfly's handler. II. How You Win First and foremost, find a nitch in the wrestling community, or make yourself one! Wrestlers can easily get lost in the mix if their personality is not distinct and seperate from 'everyone else'. Know what her personality is really like and reflect that in her actions and her flashes. So get to know the fed, and then get to know your wrestler. Second, flash reagularly and remain involved in the promotion. There's nothing worse than having dead weight in a promotion. Those handlers who become inactive will be dropped from the promotion. Re-entry is contingent upon room on the contention and her ranking on the Waitlist. If you need time off, just inform me and we'll excuse your absence with word of a nagging injury or something; in the best case scenario, you set up an angle where it allows for your wrestler's disappearance for a few weeks. Third, send in a strategy. Strategies should be no larger than 2KB, or 300 words. Don't include comments such as,"Since my wrestler is the best wrestler in the GDWA, there's no way she can lose!" This is not endearing to your fed head. This will all be discussed in greater detail later. And last but not least, send in your materials on time. Missing deadlines can cause one to fall short of their wrestler's expectations. II. Flashes and Roleplay All About Flashing Send in flashes reagularly. Flashes are just as important as the strategies/angles you role play with. From a booking standpoint, I judge flashes quantitatively and (most importantly) a qualitatively. Quantitative Expectations Those wrestlers in the superstar position--the Top 10 of contention and higher--flash 3 to 4 times a month on the FRIDAY NIGHT TEASE. Those wrestlers in midcarder position--the lower 10 to 15 spots in the promotion--tend to flash around 2 times a month on the FRIDAY NIGHT TEASE. Those wrestlers that are inconsistent--the rest of the contention fillers--tend to flash 0 to 1 times per month on the FRIDAY NIGHT TEASE. The FRIDAY NIGHT TEASE is your place to make a woman out of your wrestler. This allows her to show *who* she is and *why* we are listening to her. Qualitative Expectations A flash should seek to accomplish the following goals over the course of a month. 1st. Your flash should show us who your wrestler is! Especially for newbies (newcomers) to Grand Dragon, this is all important for us to know. Once we begin to understand who she is and what she's all about, other handlers will interact with her. Furthermore, we will care whether she wrestles or not every week, which in itself can lead to success. 2nd. Your flash should either create, further along, or conclude a story line. The bottom line is, a flash should have a point to it. Don't flash for flashing's sake; neither do we want a litany of how great your wrestler is week in and week out. Forge a storyline that will engage and involve other wrestlers. This is the heart of e-wrestling!! If you find yourself at a loss for words, discuss the actions of a fellow wrestler from the other night's card. This is a craft that will develop over time. 3rd. Your flash should compliment your role play; roleplay will be up for discussion later. Flashes should attempt to stir up other handlers to action. It is a motivating force that is the life blood of a fed. The imaginary settings, the magnitude of your wrestler's personality and the great plethora of your words is only successful if you can stir up the wrestlers in your league. Moreover, if you are at the bottom of the contention, this is especially important. The only thing harder than getting on top is staying there. So keep our attention focussed on her and we will desire to see her more and more. Flashing is important for various reasons. It keeps people tied to the fed and keeps the excitement going. Further, it gives heat to the angles that are ongoing in the fed and inspires roleplay from your enemies and allies. (Besides, it also lets the booker know that you care about the fed.) Angles, angles, angles! That is what e-wrestling is all about. Once you get in the groove of things, you will find yourself in the middle of a story line; that will be either by design or by accident ;) Your ability to further and expand (and improve) upon angles will give you a push as well. Roleplay Roleplay is simply what you do outside of flashing to create a story. This usually involves your wrestler acting virtuously or scandalously in the arenas the GDWA competes in. Although it is now en vogue to run-in on someone every week, only those interferences that have meaning and at least minimal forethought will be taken seriously. Also, run-ins are the least imaginative roleplays. Be creative and explore the realm of possibilities. The classic face off in the middle of the ring is still an effective way to build heat. Saving a wrestler from outside interference is an obvious means by which, not only can you get a match signed against this newfound enemy, you can find yourself in the middle of a story line. The backstage brawl, stealing the property of someone, meeting your ally/adversary in a non athletic environment like the parking lot, and over all feuding are just some of the many ways your roleplay can build heat. What I'm looking for is how your wrestler interacts with others and why. Make sure there's a point to what your wrestler does. Strategies are just as important as all of the above. They are 1/2 the reason you win...and lose. More in depth information regarding strategies (strats) is forthcoming in the FAQ sheet. III. CHALLENGES Challenges are made through flashes (on the FRIDAY NIGHT TEASE) and the GDWA Update. The GDWA Update is released every day until all the matches are signed for our card...the Tuesday Night Catfight! Though you make challenge away, that doesn't necessarily mean they will accept. For a match to occur, both parties must accept unless it is demanded upon by the booker. (For example, I might force-book if it would draw a lot of heat!) IV. TITLES Since reopening, The Grand Dragon Wrestling Alliance has 2 singles championships and an annual Ironwoman singles tournament. TRANSNATIONAL CRUISERWEIGHT CHAMPION: Must defend every 4 weeks. May defend against any cruiserweight, as all who meet weight requirements are considered eligible contenders, but any cruiserweight contender with a winning percentage of at least .500 that challenges MUST be given a title shot. If there are ever 2 of such contenders challenging at once, the champion has a choice of opponent. May co-hold title with multiple titles: i.e. the World Heavyweight Championship! WORLD CHAMPION: Must defend the title 1ce a month. Conteders must be within the Top 10 of Contention. After 2 title defenses, the Cruiserweight Champ may demand a shot! The World Champ must agree after the demand is made, but may choose the specified date to which she'll defend within the month. Contention for the World Title is based on a wrestling factor of 4 matches. So a wrestler may be undefeated, but if she hasn't wrestled 4 times, she can't become #1 contender or amongst the top contenders. If you defeat a champion in a non title match, you may demand a title shot from her, and she must accept!!!! V. GDWA as a Booked Fed What makes the Grand Dragon Wrestling Alliance so different than most promotions is that we put the handler first in the promotion. We are a promotion that allows the handler to make the most of him/herself however they wish to. You ask,"But why a booked fed?" For starters, a booked fed is one in which the Booker, me!, discerns from all of the information given to me who wins, how that wrestler wins, and why they win. This makes strategy all important, as it will be one of THE decisive reasons you win a match. If you think about it, most Simulated Feds are booked anyway. If you have experience in a simmed fed, you know that for any given match, a simulator will come up with 3 diffrent results. Our fed allows for so many possiblites: screw jobs, run ins, foreign objects, bone rattling post/pre match attacks, etc. The league-- and all of the action, angles and storylines in it--are in your hands! It is up to you to be a success or failure. The GDWA is much more like the old NWA; we bring the SPORT back into wrestling. This mandates rules and the enforcement of those rules. Wrestlers don't get senseless title shots or pushes in our league. We have a contention sheet and clear expectations for champions. In short, it gives me the fed head less power and you the handler more control! VI. Booked Angles From time to time, I as fed head will deem it necessary to excercise my perogative and throw a monkey wrench into the workings of the promotion. One example of this 'booked angle' making is by incorporating injuries. Injuries: In order to insure that everyone in the promotion gets a fair shake of getting on a card, from time to time wrestlers will incure minor and/or major injures while wrestling in the ring. Minor Injuries entail a sore wrist, a sprained finger or a pulled hamstring. Neither inhibit a wrestler's ability to wrestle in the upcoming weeks, but it forces the handler to second guess whether he decides to take the chance. A savvy handler might just sign a contract with your wrestler, knowing that she's hurt, in order to pick up a win. It is far easier to wrestle an opponent when they aren't at 100% than when they are. Major injuries are of such severity that doctors strongly urge the wrestler to sit out. This entails exhaustion, concussions, tworn ligaments, bruised/cracked ribs, etc. It is not a smart thing to wrestle while hurt. Not only can you worsen the injury, but an opponent will not only take credit for defeating your wrestler but putting her on the shelf as well. These 'booked angles' level out the playing field in many ways. It gives hungry yet less experienced competitors a better chance to pick up a victory over a veteran, it allows hot wrestlers to take a break while lesser exposed wrestlers fill up the card, and also is a great angle maker. Many an angle has surrounded GDWA legend Officer May Order and the injuries she has accrued. Other 'booked angles' might include the slow counting referee, notable bias by a GDWA commentator, or the strong financial repromand set down by an official. These are occurrences that keep everyone on their toes and insure Grand Dragon never gets predicatable. VI. Regarding Strategies A strategy is a tool to decide how you wrestle, not how you win. Winning is implicit in your strategy, but one should not assume that because he/she turns one in, that they will attain victory. As I see it, there are two main factors that are involved in constructing a strategy. YOUR WRESTLER (weaknesses and strengths) First of all, look at your wrestler sheet and ask yourself what your wrestler does best. What are her strenghts and what are her weakness? Perhaps she's a fantastic brawler but a pathetic High Flyer. Perhaps she's got tremendous speed yet has no endurance. Go to your strengths and minimize your weaknesses. Then, think about what kind of *offensive stand* your wrestler will take. Do you want to focus on a body part? Do you want to keep the match fast paced? Do you want to keep your opponent on the mat? Finally, think about a *defensive stand* for your wrestler. What will she do given a certain set of circumstances? If your opponent is 6 feet 2 inches and your wrestler is 5 feet tall, how will you hold up against powermoves? Will she roll to the outside to protect herself, remain near the ropes, or drive into your adversary? This leads to another aspect of your strategy: taking your opponent into consideration. A strategy that does not consider who your opponent is is usually shortsighted and one dimensional; depending on who you wrestle, she will fail. YOUR OPPONENT (weaknesses and strengths) First off, discern exactly who it is you are facing. What kind of reputation does she have? What storylines is she part of and who is she possibly feuding with. Find out if she is a fan favorite (babyface)or a rulebreaker (heel). Obviously, you must take factors into consideration if she is a rulebreaker rather than a fan favorite or whether she has a manager/bodygaurd or not. Secondly, scout your opponent if you are able. What moves does she like to execute? Can you think of any counters? Do you possess them? Are you able to counter very well? (A wrestler who employs mostly power moves will counter a Full Nelson differently than a tecnically apt wrestler will.) Third, after you've discerned who she is and what she's about, decide what your opponent's strenghts/weaknesses are. If you handle a brawler, DO NOT use technical holds & locks against a scientific/technical wrestler... Y O U W I L L L O S E ! ! ! Perhaps your opponent is not very strong. Can you out muscle her? Perhaps your opponent has low ring savvy and is constantly getting 'lost' in the ring...can you catch her off gaurd? Watching your opponent in other matches, checking for injuries and in ring tendencies they possess are crucial for those with championship gold on their mind. This is what scouting is all about! Aside from all the other factors I take into account when I book matches, strategies can really get you over. Strategies DO NOT insure a victory, but they can give you a push in match ups. For more information, click here for a prototypical example of a GDWA strategy. Strategies should be sent in before or soon after you have been notified that your opponent agrees to wrestle you. As you can see, this can have a great affect on match outcome. VII. GDWA Character Sheet Information Now that you are ready to construct a wrestler, you have some factors to take into consideration. Originality in your character is looked upon rather favorably. This involves gimmicks, nicknames, etc.....But remember, realism is of utmost importance. The below listed rules I take for granted that everyone can utilize: punch, kick, stomp, side headlock, Irish whip into turnbuckle or ropes. Anyone can do these moves, so you get them right off the bat. If you have a wrestler who actually has one of these moves LISTED on your character sheet, I double the affects and make it more proficient. For example, everyone knows that former WORLD HEAVYWEIGHT CHAMPION 'Fear Factory' Lanny Manson was the best puncher in Grand Dragon Wrestling Alliance! Fists of Fury is not just a nickname, punching is something she is proficient in; she can jab the HELL out of you. On the other hand, when Zaranna punches away at the 200 plus pound Ma Porter, and Porter reacts with a grin.....well....no punching power and no strength! One might consider utilizing a short range attack in their wrestler's arsenal. Just make sure that it fits in with her style of wrestler. Ringside Assistance and Non Player Characters The Grand Dragon Wrestling Alliance is just like every other wrestling promotion...made to be imperfect. There's outside interference, stables and drama that can add to the excitement of this twisted sport. Alas, there are some rules you will need to adhere to before we move on. First, the GDWA does not allow for men to hit women. Although this can create some unfavorable conditions for the men in the promotion, I don't like the idea of men beating on women in any capacity...women beating on women is just fine! So, if you have a manager, roadie, bodygaurd, etc. that physically manhandles a women in Grand Dragon, he is automatically expelled and your female wrestler on suspension. Secondly, the use of managers should not be purely for interfering in your matches. You must have method to your e-wrestling madness. Therefore, a one point deduction in Ring Savvy will be affixed to your wrestler for utilizing a manager. This means you will need to mention your manager's assistance in your strats in order to maximize his/her presence by your wrestler's side. If this is not tenable for you, yet you want to have your wrestler assisted in some way, you can still utilize a manager as part of your 'storyline' in your flashes. He/she will just not be present at ringside. Thirdly, the use of a bodygaurd is similar to that of the manager. A bodygaurd's purpose is to protect your wrestler. Her service will cause a one point deduction in your wrestler's Intelligence as long as this bodygaurd is in service. This means you will need to mention your bodygaurd's assistanec in your strat in order to maximize her presence by your wrestler's side; describe what she is doing and what role she will serve. Stables. All promotions have them! In the GDWA, a stable is comprised of various handlers, no more than 5, who gather together to attain a goal. I don't impose any penalties for being in a stable, but require specific instructions as to how stablemates will react during matches. I take very little for granted and need to be instructed as to how to handle the stable. It is suggested that the various handlers discuss this arrangement prior to the booking of a match so that no party within the stable feels that their wrestler is misused. ATTRIBUTES Now, as we are a booked fed, I take into consideration certain attributes in order to ascertain the flow of a match: Strength, Speed, Agility, Ring Savvy, Endurance, and Intelligence. These attributes determine how well you moves are executed. A short break down of these terms will be helpful. Note: In all cases, 0 (zero) is average. Strength This is generally how strong your wrestler is and how great an impact your wrestler's maneuvers will have. Strength also measures how much damage a wrestler can sustain. A male wrestler with average or 0 (zero) strength would be Jeff Jarrett. A wrestler wtih exceptional strength or 4 (four) strength would be Lex Luger or the Giant. Speed This is basically how quick a wrestler is: how fast they can get to their feet after hitting the mat, how quickly they can run off the ropes, how quickly they climb the turnbuckles, etc. Cruiserweights are almost always faster than heavyweights. Agility This is not simply one's atheltic ability but one's gracefulness: how well you can Springboard off the ropes like the Cruiserweights in WCW, how well you can fly off the top turnbuckle, acrobatic stunts like Planchas, Topes, High Cross Bodies, etc. Bret Hart is probably about average or 0 (zero) while Rey Mysterio Junior is definitely a 4 (four)! Endurace This is probably the single most overrated of e-wrestling attributes. But don't get me wrong, this is nevertheless important. Endurance is a measure of how much 'wind' your wrestler has, and how well they can come back at the end of a match. The Ultimate Warrior has a -4 (negative four) endurance by the time he runs from the top of the rampway to the ring. Ric Flair, back in the 80's and early 90's, used to have a 4 (four) endurance. Intelligence This is THE most underrated attributes in the e-wrestling universe. Intelligence in the GDWA is a measurer of circumstances in the ring: how well they hold up under pressure & fan cheering/booing, how they react to outside interference, how well they are able to cheat/deflect cheating, etc. This is a predicter of your wrestler's IN RING grasp and manipulation of distractions. Hacksaw Jim Duggan is the dumbest wrestler I've ever seen earning a -4 (negative four) on my scale whereas 'Stone Cold' Steve Austin--and once again, the Ric Flair of the 80's-- would earn a 4 (four). Ring Savvy This might be the least understood attribute in e-wrestling. Savvy measures your manipulation of your wrestler's ring work: how well she judges distances from the ropes, how well she prevents others from reaching the ropes, how well she lands maneuvers, how well she reverses manuevers, etc. Diamond Dalls Page would probably be average or 0 (zero) here whereas Bret Hart in his prime would earn a 4 (four). These attributes are also affected by your wrestler's weight and height of course. A wrestler with a 4 strength and at 115 pounds is not as powerful as a 185 pound wrestler with a 4 strength. That's about all there is to Grand Dragon. The rest you'll learn along the way as you get to know the fed. Hope you fill out an application and become one of our greats! See ya later, Lawrence GDWA Fed Head/Singles Booker Back to Main Page
