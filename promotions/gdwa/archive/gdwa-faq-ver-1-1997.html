My name is [name withheld] and I'm your Head Booker and President of the Grand Dragon Wrestling Alliance. The GDWA is a booked fed run primarily on character roleplay, strategy, angles, and other player involvement. It is very intense and only the best make it to the top. Competition is everywhere and only the greatest survive. If you ever have any concerns or questions contact me, and we can talk. I'm willing to put just as much energy into the fed as you are. We run a card once a week called the Tuesday Night Catfight (or the Saturday Nite Special) which will entail no less than 3 singles matches and 2 tag matches. Each handler (that's you!) is allowed 1 Tag Team and 1 singles wrestler. Upon garnering experience in fed, it is possible to upgrade to 2 wrestlers, though your second one would be put on a Waitlist. My job will be match making, booking and writing the cards. The excitement is really going to be coming from you, the handler. Get the idea? Off the bat, let me inform you of what you already know. For every win there is a loss. So don't get discouraged. 1.2: How to Win First and foremost, find a nitch in the wrestling community, or make yourself one! Wrestlers can easily get lost in the mix if their wrestler's personality is not distinct and seperate from 'everyone else'. Know what her personality is really like and reflect that in her actions and her flashes. So get to know the fed, and then get to know your wrestler. Send in flashes reagularly. Flashes are just as important as the strategies/angles you role play with. We have 2 flash shows: the Thursday Night MVP and the Saturday Night Tease. This is all important for various reasons. It keeps people tied to the fed and keeps the excitement going. Further, it gives heat to the angles that are ongoing in the fed. (Besides, it also lets the booker know that you care about the fed.) Angles, angles, angles! That is what e-wrestling is all about. Once you get in the groove of things, you will find yourself in the middle of a story line; that will be either by design or by accident ;) Your ability to further and expand (and improve) upon angles will give you a push as well. Strategies are just as important as all of the above. They are 1/2 the reason you win...and lose. More in depth information regarding strategies (strats) is forthcoming in the FAQ sheet. 1.3: CHALLENGES Challenges are made through flashes (on the MVP and the Tease) and the GDWA Update. The GDWA Update is released every day until all the matches are signed for our card...the Tuesday Night Catfight! Though you make challenge away, that doesn't necessarily mean they will accept. For a match to occur, both parties must accept unless it is demanded upon by the booker. (For example, I might force-book if it would draw a lot of heat!) 1.4: TITLES The Grand Dragon Wrestling Alliance has 4 singles titles and 1 tag team title. WORLD Tag Team CHAMPIONS: Must defend the title 1ce a month. Conteders are all Tag Team with atleast 1 win under their belt. CRUISERWEIGHT CHAMPION: Must defend every 5 weeks. May defend against any cruiserweight, as all who meet weight requirements are considered eligible contenders, but any cruiserweight contender that has a winning percentage over .500 that challenges MUST be given a title shot. If there are ever 2 of such contenders challenging at once, the champion has a choice of opponent. May co-hold title with multiple titles: World Tag Team, WH Title, I-Title &/or the World title! WESTERN HERITAGE CHAMPION: Must defend 2ce a month. After 3 title defenses, may demand a shot at the Internet CHampion. The champion must agree, but may choose the specified date to which she'll defend within the month. INTERNET CHAMPION: Must defend 1ce a month. Contenders must be within the TOP 10 of Contention. After 2 title defenses, may demand a shot at the WORLD CHAMPION! The World Champ must agree after the demand is made, but may choose the specified date to which she'll defend within the month. WORLD CHAMPION: Must defend the title 1ce a month. Conteders must be within the Big 5th of Contention. After 2 title defenses, the Internet Champ may demand a shot! The World Champ must agree after the demand is made, but may choose the specified date to which she'll defend within the month. Contention for the World Title is based on a wrestling factor of 4 matches. So a wrestler may be undefeated, but if she hasn't wrestled 4 times, she can't become #1 contender or amongst the top 5 contenders. If you defeat a champion in a non title match, you may demand a title shot from her, and she must accept!!!! 2.1: GDWA as a Booked Fed What makes the Grand Dragon Wrestling Alliance so different than most promotions is that we put the handler first in the promotion. We are a promotion that allows the handler to make the most of him/herself however they wish to. You ask,"But why a booked fed?" For starters, a booked fed is one in which the Booker, me!, discerns from all of the information given to me who wins, how that wrestler wins, and why they win. This makes strategy all important, as it will be one of THE decisive reasons you win a match. If you think about it, most Simulated Feds are booked anyway. If you have experience in a simmed fed, you know that for any given match, a simulator will come up with 3 diffrent results. Our fed allows for so many possiblites: screw jobs, run ins, foreign objects, bone rattling post/pre match attacks, etc. The league-- and all of the action, angles and storylines in it--are in your hands! It is up to you to be a success or failure. The GDWA is much more like the old NWA; we bring the SPORT back into wrestling. This mandates rules and the enforcement of those rules. Wrestlers don't get senseless title shots or pushes in our league. We have a contention sheet and clear expectations for champions. In short, it gives me the fed head less power and you the handler more control! Talk to some of our current GDWA'ers and see what they have to say about the league. In my explanation, let me give you an example which speaks to the GDWA best: In the early weeks of the Grand Dragon Wrestling Alliance, "Fear Factory" Lanny Manson wrestled Ma Porter in a return match up. Well, before before the card, each handler sent me in a strategy of how to deal with their opponent. Manson decided to attack VERY STRONG and work on the legs, hoping to take away the power game of Porter. She was willing to bend the rules as far as a FACE is able to before breaking the rules. Porter had no real strategy other than her surprise which was the premierof her manager coming down to ringside. She wanted her manager to 'assist' if things got out of hand; using whatever methods he could to assure Ma Porter her victory. The result: The referee had been knocked out and "Fear Factory" Lanny Manson had Ma Porter wrapped up in her patented Trash Zone Octopus Hold. Ma Porter's manager entered the ring and tried to deck Manson with Brass Knuckles but was thwarted. Manson retaliated, and nailed him as well as Porter with the Brass Knuckles. The match resulted in a DQ win for Ma Porter. Other kinds of strategies are outside interference, ring psychology, etc. Just watch a Monday Night Raw/Nitro or some sort of wrestling show on the weekend and STUDY what happens before and after match ups. Though the WWF/WCW may have some silly angles at times, those angles can make a difference in the league. With more player involvement, we can only get better! Watching your opponent in other matches that she is in, checking for injuries, and habits that they tend to have in the ring are crucial for those with gold on their mind. This is what scouting is all about! Aside from all the other factors I take into account when I book matches, strategies can really get you over. As you can see, I took into account that Manson was willing to cheat, she was attacking the leg and was in a complete rage, and also that Ma Porter MAY have been beaten if not for her manager's save; the very fact that Porter even had a manager was a surprise to Manson and the entire wrestling world! Strategies DO NOT insure a victory, but they can give you a push in match ups. Strategies should be sent in before or soon after you have been notified that your opponent agrees to wrestle you. (Preferably you will include a flash.) As you can see, this can have a great affect on match outcome. 2.3: 2 examples of Strats Here are examples of typical strategies (called strats) that I receive. They range from the ill prepared and the succinct to the well compacted and the verbose. (NOTE: DO NOT send one shorter than Strategy #1, but don't send one the size of strategy #2....) STRATEGY #1 ~~~~~~~~~~~ 1. Keep Browne off balance with speed and quickness (series of drop kicks or clotheslines) mixed in with technical wrestling (hip toss, arm bar, etc...) 2. Use moves off the top rope when Browne is stunned or weakened. 3. If Browne gains a significant advantage then roll of of the ring for a quick break to stop the momentum. STRATEGY #2 ~~~~~~~~~~~ One word. Outshine. Sierra intends to outshine Wendy Wildchild Marshall. Marshall may be faster than Sierra, but they are probably equally agile, Sierra's also probably a little tougher and definitely savvier inside the ring than her opponent. Sierra's going to start the match on the ground -- as soon as the bell rings she's going to go for a rolling chopblock, taking the legs out from underneath her. Sierra will immediately follow-up with an armwringer superkick and back Wendy into the corner with JUMPING EUROPEAN UPPERCUTS ... Sierra's going to take advantage of her greater weigh and reach ...delivering from long-range until she darts in close to score with her inverted bulldog neckbreaker. That should break Marshall down sufficiently to be whipped into the ropes, back and forth for moves like flying dropkicks, hurricanas, SOMERSAULT CLOTHESLINES or SOMERSAULT AXEKICKS. Towards the end of the match, Sierra wants to reveal her HANDSPIN HELICOPTER KICK ... when she dodges a rushing Wendy Marshall she'll flip into a handstand and spin, whipping her legs out to kick Marshall down with a few boots to the face. Sierra is going to focus her attack VERY STRONG on the HEAD and NECK of Wendy Marshall. Sierra will test Marshall's ENDURANCE. She is lighter than Sierra and Sierra will put her FULL 140Lbs behind EVERY KICK and PUNCH. That should wear down Marshall, take away her speed and leave her sucking for wind, not very effective or crisp in her aerial moves. DEFENSE: In such a fast-paced match there is bound to be times when Sierra will not get the benefit of the advantage. Marshall is still quick and has a variety of surprise moves off the ropes like her SPINWHEEL KICK ... Sierra is going to use her ENDURANCE to withstand these quick pins and keep enough reserve to kick out from surprise pins. Marshall isn't very RING SAVVY and she might fall for the oldest tricks in the book. Sierra will forever OVERSELL moves that connect, feigning greater injury so that Marshall might be goaded into HIGH-RISK moves which Sierra will use her AGILITY to dodge, either by cartwheeling away, rolling out of harm's reach or leaping up and KICKING MARSHALL out of the AIR. Sierra will always use her AGILITY to COUNTERBALANCE suplex attempts and throws and land on her feet, ready to strike with a SUPERKICK or DROPKICK. If Marshall misses a move and gets caught up in the ropes ... like Sierra hopes... Sierra will go for her handspring headscissors piledriver or her springboard moonsault reverse DDT. She'll always be out for the QUICK PIN, but is really looking to finish it with her HIGH SIERRA, so she will prefer to go back on the attack rather than take the fast victory. After being thrown off the ropes, if she's not too groggy, Sierra will launch herself for either the springboard moonsault reverse DDT, the somersault clothesline or the somersault axekick. HIGH SPOTS In every match wrestlers typically get some high spots. These are some combinations that would really bring attention to Sierra so she intends to use them. Ducking under a SPINNING WHEEL KICK Sierra rebounds off the ropes and comes back with her HANDSPIN HELICOPTER KICK. Sierra counterbalances a throw to nail a jumping somersault clothesline Sierra doubles Marshall over with a boot to the gut and comes off the ropes for the FORWARD SOMERSAULT AXEKICK. POSSIBLE ENDS If Sierra is meant to win, she will do it in one of two fashions. Wendy Marshall might throw caution to the wind and leap up on the top rope in less than perfect shape. Sierra will play possum, but then charge the ropes and flip up for her handspring headscissors piledriver. She will then go up top for her High Sierra moonsault elbow drop. If Sierra dominates ... she will end it by nailing the inverted bulldog neckbreaker and the somersault axekick and then going up top for the High Sierra. If Sierra wins she will celebrate with the whole arena by doing acrobatics and flips and bows in the ring while being showered with glitter sparkles. She'll head into the fans and show love to everybody who supported her. Medusa Rage will not interfere in this match at all. In fact, she'll barely be noticed through the whole thing. This is Sierra's night.
