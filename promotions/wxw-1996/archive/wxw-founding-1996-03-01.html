New Fed Announcement - WXW

1 March 1996
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I am announcing the opening of a new e-wrestling federation, the WXW. What does WXW stand for? Truth, justice, ... oh, you meant the letters. They don't stand for anything, but I'm open for suggestions. I know new feds open daily. And most don't last. So why waste your time with this one? Because you'll miss out on all the action. This fed will last. I have been a vice-president for the WHWF for two months, writing long, play-by-play style matches. I've been involved in e-wrestling for about a year, with world famous e-wrestlers "The Economist" Randy Blake and Booster
Dunne (well, sort of world famous). Now I'm starting my own fed with many new ideas, a new "booking" simulator, and a new creation sheet. I need handlers with new and interesting wrestlers, angles, and plots.
This fed will be using a simulator that I created myself. It will allow booking decisions to have an impact on the outcome of matches, but that will not be the only determining factor. The head booker (me) will assign each wrestler a push rating, which will depend on several factors:
1) the wrestler's recent performance (winning or losing streak) 2) the wrestler's recent participation, i.e. flashes, interviews, angles, etc. 3) what the booker would like to see happen, although this factors in the least. This push rating will vary the probability of one wrestler or the other winning. However, even the least pushed wrestler can beat the wrestler with the highest push rating at any given time - it's just less likely than the other way around. This system includes the randomness some handlers like in simulator-run feds, along with the benefits of a booked fed (i.e. roleplaying/ interviewing matters, etc). Interference from managers and run-ins can also occur as required and have an effect on matches.
But enough about the simulator, now more information about the federation itself: 1) There will be two cards a week. One card will be long format (play-by- play) with about 3-6 matches, depending how much time I have. The other card will be short reported, i.e. a few sentences describing the highlights and finish of each match (WAR!-style, if you're familiar with that fed). *Every wrestler will have at least one match a week!* (Except if the wrestler is injured or suspended, of course.)
2) To start with, there will be a limit of about 30 singles wrestlers and 10 tag teams. A handler will be only allowed a maximum of two singles *or* one singles wrestler and a tag team. Of course, you may have just one singles wrestler, or just one tag team. 3) There will be two singles titles (World and Internet) and one tag title (World). 4) The president will not have any of his own wrestlers in the WXW, except for jobbers if needed. 5) Interviews, flashes, run-ins, angles, and the like will be encouraged. In fact, they will help your wrestler's career. 6) Only male fantasy wrestlers, please. No real-life wrestlers or cheap knock-offs. 7) Both newbies and experienced people are encouraged to join. The Wrestler Creation Sheet This will be at the bottom of this post. Please fill it out and send it back to me at < [detail withheld] >. But before you fill it out, you should read the following section which explains what you are recommended to include on the sheet.
---- First, the "Wrestler Info" section. This is pretty much straightforward. ---- The next section is "Description of Wrestler." Here you want to tell me the wrestler's gimmick and his usual clothing. As well, you may want to tell me a bit about his background. Has he wrestled in other e-wrestling federations? Has he been a champion? Does he have amateur experience?
Is he a grizzled veteran or a starstruck rookie? Is he a madman or an intelligent heel? Is he a crowd-pleaser or does he just go about his business? And whatever else you think would be relevant here.
---- The third section is "Statistics." This is the most important section for the simulator. Here you will divide 100 points among five wrestling styles
(technical, aerial, power, brawling, and martial arts). There is no maximum or minimum required for each style (though you can't use negative numbers).
They just have to total 100. It is strongly recommended, however, that you do not assign any particular style a value greater than 50. It is also suggested that you do have at least one style with over 30 points. This is because the simulator chooses what moves you can do from a list for each style. These moves range from easy to difficult to perform and the more difficult it is, the more damage it does when executed. If your score in a style is very high (above 50), there won't be any extra moves you will be able to do that you couldn't with just 50 points. So anything above 50 is really just wasted. However, since you want to be able to do the higher damage moves in at least one style, at least one category should be given more than 30 points. Perhaps I have only confused you more.
A further *important* note on assigning points to styles. The style(s) you choose to give the highest points should correspond with your wrestler's physical size. For example, if your wrestler is supposed to be 500 pounds, you would not put a lot of points into "aerial." One of my pet peeves is when a wrestler is supposed to be 600 pounds but can do moonsaults and has the technical ability of Bret Hart. Then he has a barbed wire baseball bat "just in case." Why the baseball bat? If you're a 600-pound more aerial version of Bret Hart, I think it's just a bit of overkill. So, please don't make your wrestler like that. Likewise, if your wrestler is supposed to be the size of the 123 Kid, don't put all your points in "power"
and "brawling." It just doesn't work. The 5 styles: TECHNICAL - a lot of wear-down and submission moves, like: suplex, Boston crab, headlock, inside cradle - examples of real-life wrestlers: Bret Hart, Ric Flair AERIAL - quickness, agility, and daring are displayed with many high-flying moves, like: dropkick, plancha, cross body block, flying clothesline - examples of real-life wrestlers: Eddie Guerrero, Brian Pillman POWER - moves requiring strength, like: power bomb, powerslam, chokeslam, backdrop - examples of real-life wrestlers: Lex Luger, Vader BRAWLING - straight-ahead, fists flying style, moves like: clothesline, kick, bodyslam, knee lift - examples of real-life wrestlers: Jim Duggan, Cactus Jack MARTIAL - fast, uses hands and feet well, moves like: ARTS chop, spin wheel kick, nerve hold, handspring elbow - examples of real-life wrestlers: Hakushi, Rick Steamboat Many wrestlers can be defined as a blend of two or more styles. Shawn Michaels is aerial/technical; 123 Kid is martial arts/aerial; Hulk Hogan is power/brawling. All incorporate a little of each style in their repertoires, except Hogan, of course.
---- The fourth section, "Finisher", is pretty self-explanatory. Please describe the move if it isn't obvious.
---- Finally, I ask you for "Three Major Spots." A spot can be just a high-impact move that your wrestler does, like a powerbomb or an elbowdrop off the top rope. It can be a setup move for your finisher or just a routine move that your wrestler does. You should keep in mind what your primary style(s) is/are when selecting these moves. For example, a primarily aerial wrestler will want to have high-flying major spots. A major spot does not have to be a move, however. You can be creative here. A heel may use a foreign object on his opponent. A manager may get involved, distracting a referee or bashing your wrestler's opponent. A face may draw strength from a cheering crowd.
These spots will be used in writeups only, so they can be almost anything. Well, if you've read this far, why don't you fill out the sheet below and mail it to me? You have nothing to lose, except a little blood and a couple of teeth. If you're still not sure about something, if you have any questions, just mail me.
