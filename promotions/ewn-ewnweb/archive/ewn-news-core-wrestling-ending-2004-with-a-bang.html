Core Wrestling: Ending 2004 with a Bang!

20 December 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

This coming Wednesday night, Core Wrestling will do something it rarely does. It will be broadcasting the weekly television show PRIDE in an Extreme format. The whole concept of extreme is something rarely done in Core, as more often than none matches revolve around athletic ability and not pain tolerance to withstand five chair shots or falling off of a ladder through a table. And extreme itself does not necessarily mean all that in Core either. But what it does mean is action above and beyond the usual on Core each week.

As part of the year end shake-up and a sign of things to come in 2005, the new Director of Operations for Core Wrestling has put together a card worth tuning into the WB. Director Robert Maxwell, the former Real and Graphic Entertainment Vice President who was involved in a scandal eight months ago when he and another employee attempted to kill Owner Tom M., has signed a card that includes the following:

- The cW Classic Championship being defended in a "Triple Threat Submission Invitational" as British Bomber, Ned Jennings, and champion Veronica Paige put their guts to the test.

- Brian Wes takes on Daniel Adams in a "Taipei Death Match" after Adams suffered an injury at Ringmaster almost one month ago. Wes took the blame for it all and Adams will look for revenge.

- Pat Dwyer and Chris Collins will be forced to set aside their differences from RaGE as they team up against Tony Aliso and Jake Summers in a "Tag Team Ladder Match."

- The cW Pride Championship will be on the line in the main event as William Gwynn defends the title he won at Ringmaster against 2004 Ringmaster Champion Nick Worrell inside of an "Enclosed Steel Cage."

The card proves to be a big year-ending show for Core Wrestling as last week Core Legacy Champion Jason Hughes retained his tite against Munson following blatant interference from the Director of Operations who has a lot of explaining to do. The whole locker room is scheduled to be on hand at Pride 14 in Little Rock, Arkansas.

Tune in Wednesday because you will not want to miss any of the action of "Extreme Pride" as Core's 2004 year draws to a close.
