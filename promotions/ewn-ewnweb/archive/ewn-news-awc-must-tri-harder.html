AWC: Must Tri harder?

19 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

While the consensus at AWC's head offices in Atlantic City and London has been that Triangles 2005 was a solid pay-per-view event and at times dazzling, internet message boards have been abuzz with criticism of the event.

Triangles, which aired eight days ago, was based around its main event, the Triangles match itself. A David "Pearl" Harber creation, many at AWC consider the match one of the best in the short history of the promotion, but smarks have already jumped on the match for being "confusing", "impossible to follow" and "ridiculously construed". The abundance of multi-man matches on the card has also led some critics to label the show "one huge clusterfuck", which understandably upset our likeable Entertainment Manager.

Perhaps it would have been wiser to introduce all the fans to all the concepts before the night of the show itself. Harber kept his cards close to his chest, hoping that suspended hype would add to the show's value, but some fans felt let down by the "tiny amount of genuine wrestling" and the "illogical and pointless" Pyramid Scramble match - no details of which were available before the show. No title changes may also have been a reason for fans feeling short-changed by the longest show in AWC history.

Regardless, let's take a look at what actually went down, for those living under a rock:

We kicked off the show with the first Triangles qualifier, in which three teams of three faced off to determine which matches they would progress into. The first talking point was the identity of each of the "mystery competitors" scheduled for this match. On a team with Livewire champion Jack "The Bull" Murphy we had the returning duo The Furious Fists Of God. Lining up with Johnny Noble and Butterfly Hamada, to the immense appreciation of the fans in the FedEx Forum, was former Frontier champion Andy Murray, back from injury. The mercurial Ellis Nash was the fourth and final mystery element of the night, teaming with Hate and Norman Quiry.

While the match was messy, and at times disintegrated into melée with little to no teamwork, most agreed that it was a good opener with plenty of entertainment value. Murphy, with the assistance of Liam Martin, got the pin on Nash to take his team through to the Triangles match; Nash, Quiry and Hate moved to the losers' battle royal and Noble, Hamada and Murray had to settle for the Transatlantic title contendership mini-tournament.

After a silent vignette featuring Paddy O'Shea, we moved back to ringside, where T.T.S. announced that they were withdrawing from competing in the upcoming Triangles qualifier, in which they were scheduled to team up with Tony Aliso. It was then revealed that Alex Strider was the man Jack Murphy had been talking to on the phone a couple of weeks back, the first ever Transatlantic champion getting the upper hand on the man some regard as the future of AWC by distancing himself from Murphy's campaign for old-school.

Next on the schedule, we saw Paddy O'Shea and Red Rock finally put aside the differences that came about during the ill-fated interpromotional feud with UWF, during which Red Rock, contracted to both feds, was caught in the middle. The two solidified their friendship in preparation for their qualifying match; the third member of their team would be the elusive Crimson O'Malec, quiet since his recent return to the ring.

Next on the schedule was that match itself, and it was a brief and confused affair, Adam Dick's Eaterplex '05 on Red Rock securing the 1-2-3 sending himself, his reluctant partner Tim Shipley and Patrick Mapleleaf through to the big main event. Due to a terrible error in the match conclusion, the team that lost actually went through to the Transatlantic mini-tournament while Aliso and T.T.S. were scheduled for the battle royal. By rights it should have been the other way round as because of miscommunication Red Rock ended up being pinned. Luckily, nobody noticed... until now.

Following the match, Harber declared that T.T.S. would be fired if they didn't turn up to the next match they were booked for on the night. They reluctantly did so, but the battle royal itself was a farce, with none of the competitors able to find a rhythm before The Unfuckables, losing their patience, hit the ring and cleared it, announcing that none of the contestants were worthy of challenging for the Frontier title, which was set to be the prize for the winner. Mike Wade, the current Relentless champion, declared that instead he should have a shot, but as things stand this looks unlikely; Pearl is unwilling to let competitors hold multiple titles in the light of the Educator debacle.

The first match of the mini-tournament came next, a Three Way Fury encounter between Andy Murray, Butterfly Hamada and Johnny Noble. With the fans split between heroes Noble and Murray, no one was really expecting the sole female in the match to make an impact, and interference from enemy Jack Murphy was intended to make sure of this. Unfortunately for The Bull, Murray's sense of sportsmanship prevailed, and the Scottish King of Cool dragged Butterfly over his own body to give her victory, knowing that she would have had it won already but for the chair shot from Murphy. Very cool.

The twisted psyche of Michael "Chainz" Sloan was then explored in a backstage segment between Alexa Kendericks, Maddy Estelle and Chainz' girlfriend Tracy. The examination was inconclusive, Chainz remaining something of an enigma as he slapped interviewer Estelle on the butt and walked out.

The second match of the mini-tournament was cut short as Crimson O'Malec's corkscrew splash spot went horribly wrong. The Irish-American crashed head-first into the announcers' table, sustaining some serious neck injuries, and O'Malec will be out for an indefinite period. It is unknown whether a return to active duty will in fact ever be possible. This horrific moment left a bitter taste in everyone's mouth, but remarkably, O'Malec's partner Paddy O'Shea seemed not in the least concerned as Red Rock, in shock at the injury, was pinned for the second time in the evening. O'Shea thus qualified for the Transatlantic championship contendership match, where he would meet Butterfly Hamada.

The Pyramid Scramble was up next, and after James Brunt had explained the rules it was a tight and furious affair fought inside a 10-minute time limit. In retrospect, perhaps fifteen or even twenty minutes would have been preferable, with a rather low number of falls qualifying Chainz as the new #1 contender to the Relentless title, but the show was long enough as it was and perhaps any extension of this heavily-gimmicked "game show" might have been unwise, turning off the purists. Mike Wade retained his title belt despite a strong challenge from Alexa Kendericks in particular. As a reward for her efforts she has been scheduled for a Livewire title shot on this week's Fresh! by Entertainment Manager David Harber. Unfortunately, one of the biggest talking-points of this match was a terrible injury sustained by The Farmer, who fractured his leg in an accident involving a ladder on the side of the pyramid. It has been confirmed as a clean break, which is good news, but Fitz-McCarthy will be out for "one to three months", with much depending on how The Farmer responds to physiotherapy beginning in four to five weeks.

A blip in continuity now was the fault of the production crew. An early cut away into a time paradox meant that Paddy O'Shea's sudden emergence to chase up the pyramid after Mike Wade, and Wade's escape sliding down the other side before turning the pyramid into a blazing inferno by virtue of the lighter he took from a fan right at the beginning of the match, was lost to all except the fans in attendance. O'Shea escaped unharmed, sliding daringly down the side between two banks of fire, to continue chasing after his nemesis backstage, and we caught up with them in a corridor. Wade shut himself in a locker to frustrate O'Shea, but the popular Irishman had an idea...

There was no time for it just yet, though, as O'Shea got the call from a stagehand for his match with Hamada. Striding out unprepared, O'Shea nevertheless fought a great match, and managed to secure a victory by two falls to one in the Falls Count Anywhere encounter, garnering some cheers from the long-time wrestling fans in the crowd as he reincarnated his old finishing move the Shamrock Leglock to force Butterfly to submit, thus handing O'Shea #1 contendership to the Transatlantic title at the December supershow.

AWC interviewers Sarah Kennedy and Maddy Estelle were then shown jawing backstage before we caught up with Paddy O'Shea once more, mid-execution of his dastardly plan. Mike Wade has not been seen since he was despatched to Mexico on the back of a pick-up truck, and we are in the dark as to whether he will make it to Newcastle, England for his scheduled main event this Tuesday with partner Adam Dick against The Pioneers in the third match of the "Unfuckable team invitational tour bonanza".

Finally, it was time for the Triangles match. Taking place inside a huge metal mesh structure divided into four, nine competitors fought it out for the three prizes that sat quietly on the roof while the action below was pandemonium. These were: the Transatlantic title belt, which Pierce Lavelle managed to retain; the Grand Slam Package, entitling the holder to a shot at each of AWC's championships, which Jack "The Bull" Murphy secured; and the Mystery Prize, which Tim Shipley fought tooth-and-nail for, winning out over former foe the Face-Eater. Murphy and Shipley's victories are particularly impressive considering the huge bump they took together; most thought this would put them out of action for some time but doctors have confirmed no major damage to either man. Unfortunately, the injury woes tonight continued, as Patrick Mapleleaf contracted a spinal injury after taking a reverse Death Valley driver from Anacletus Alcaeus. Some fans saw this as fitting, following an injury to Mapleleaf earlier in the night that was apparently faked. Damage to vertebrae will keep the Canadian out indefinitely.

David Harber today released a list of AWC superstars that have been released from the company following Triangles. Some have been let go after long-term injury and will be re-signed immediately if they show ability to perform in active competition; others are recent signings that have disappointed in their showings so far. New injury victims Patrick Mapleleaf and Crimson O'Malec have been included on the list as they immediately asked for their respective releases after the show, unhappy at the risky environment in which they had to wrestle. Harber reluctantly complied with their wishes. The Farmer, however, remains on an AWC contract.

The list is as follows:

Alcaeus
Juri Hyobanshi
Kris Krimzon
Patrick Mapleleaf
'Mare
Crimson O'Malec

Titan
[name withheld] the Handler
Steven Xandrous

This week, AWC returns to Europe, with Fresh! coming live from Newcastle's Telewest Arena. The card has been released, and features The Unfuckables vs The Academy, Andy Murray taking on newcomer Acey Lavado, The Furious Fists Of God against last-chance-salooners T.T.S., two who impressed at Triangles going head to head as Butterfly Hamada meets Chainz, and Jack Murphy defending his Livewire strap against Alexa Kendericks. Also expect news on the future for big winners at Triangles Jack Murphy and Paddy O'Shea, and Tim Shipley will be opening his briefcase to reveal his mystery prize live on air!

Shockingly, Pearl has revealed that the East Atlantic announce team of Hans Heinemann, Miguel "The Midget" Rodriguez and DOOOOOBLIN DAVE~! will not be returning for this run. This decision is unlikely to go down well with fans, who regarded DAVE~! and Miggy in particular as cult legends, but perhaps whichever new announce team is chosen will give AWC's European shows a little more credibility. It is thought that Pearl will not take the option of simply using Truth Waters and George Cassidy for all shows, preferring the variety afforded by using two different teams for shows either side of the Atlantic.
