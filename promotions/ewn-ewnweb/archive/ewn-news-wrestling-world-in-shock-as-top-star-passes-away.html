Wrestling world in shock as top star passes away

24 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

News of another death in their ranks has rocked all those associated with the Atlantic Wrestling Club as reports that filtered out yesterday via the internet were confirmed this morning by David "Pearl" Harber.

Speaking in his capacity as Entertainment Manager for AWC, Harber issued a short statement that first ever Transatlantic champion "The Mongoose" Alexander Strider was found dead in his hotel room just before noon on Wednesday when a maid came in to clean the room.

Harber would not comment on the cause of death, widely rumoured to be drugs-related, but insisted his "deep regret" of the loss of one of his most bitter foes.

A federal investigation is currently underway; the promotion itself is also now under very close scrutiny. This is the second death in a month for the company, following the car accident that killed "The British Bomber" Steven Smith on the first day of November, and one wonders if (or perhaps when) the authorities will step in to insist that a stronger set of rules is enforced. Harber's response to accusations that his booking style is too risky was that neither death was linked to a match booking he made, but what if one of the numerous serious injuries sustained at AWC Triangles two weeks ago in Memphis had in fact been a death? It is thought that Pearl is likely to tone down the schedule for this coming week's show in Manchester's huge Evening News Arena. A transcript of Tuesday night's show will be available exclusively on the AWC website later today. Alex Strider did not appear on the show in any capacity.
