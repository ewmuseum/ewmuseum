A webmaster's hub for the fantasy-wrestling hobby — free sub-domain hosting, craft tutorials, community-made tools and a published rubric for reviewing other people's federations — whose own About page counts its 2004 to 2006 run as one iteration among many, the earlier ones unrecorded. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

EWN kept a list of facts about itself, and the first one was that it had “opened and closed more then any strip-mall.” Thirty-odd redesigns, all of them some variation of blue; a longest run of fourteen months; over fifty columnists, forum mods and reporters across all of it. The iteration that survives is the one that opened at the end of August 2004 and posted for the last time on 4 August 2006, and it is the only one anybody kept.

What it did was host. EWHub.com sub-domains were free to any federation it approved, answered “within 24 hours”, and the application asked how long you had been running — a hub screening for feds that had already proved they would last. It sold design, Poser art and scripting to the same community, it kept an arena list so that writers could book real buildings at their real capacities, and it pointed at fifteen partners and federations from a wall where every row still reads “Info Coming Soon.”

It also taught. Four tutorials survive: two on PHP, written for the webmasters whose news boxes and card archives depended on it; one on finishing a Poser render, on focal length, cast shadows and the time a render is worth; and one on how a wrestling match is written, which runs to twenty-four thousand characters and argues that a match is an essay — entrances for an introduction, the bell for a thesis, a body that has to earn its own finish. Its last line is the hub's own answer to everyone who called match writing a dying art: “A great writer forces themselves into a bind of rules and boundaries, and creates even greater art out of it.”

It graded federations, on a rubric it published — Graphic Design, Writing, OOC Atmosphere, Overall Content — and the one review that survives grades only one of the four, because the federation it was reviewing had no working site up and no readable boards. It gave that federation an A+ for storytelling anyway. And it carried the community's own software: ewMatchWriter and ewShowPoster, both by one of the hub’s own handlers, the second of them giving “fedheads the ability to archive old shows.”

On 22 September 2005 its front page said “eWN is going through a revamp stage. Please bear with us while we add new features and update the site,” beside a link to the Red Cross's Hurricane Katrina fund. The revamp never arrived. The news feed ran eleven more months underneath the notice, a new masthead went up in April 2006, and then it stopped. What its earlier iterations were, and how many times it had already done this, is not in what survives.
