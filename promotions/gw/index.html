Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Global Wrestling wrestled in an octagon. Its own show script describes the thing at length — eight turnbuckles firing pyrotechnics, the ring set down in a pit with the crowd raised above it on two levels, a short ramp running back into a corridor of high metal walls — and one of its own wrestlers, asked backstage whether he was looking forward to getting into the ring, stops himself and says “er...Octagon?” The weekly show was called Evolution, it was billed as airing on FX, and by April 2003 the fed had talked the network into a second night a week.

The register was shock television and it was applied without much of a brake. On 31 March 2003 the show opens with a cloaked man slamming a car door on The English Gent’s head in the parking lot; a little later the ring announcer reports from the middle of the ring that a wrestler has died during the week, the arena holds a moment’s silence, and Deacon Holmes — who has just been awarded that man’s match by default — takes the microphone and dedicates the silence to his pet fish. Five weeks later, at WrestleCade III, the opening match is fought in the car park and settled with a garbage truck, and Leviathan spends the undercard cutting a woman open on a cross inside a chalk pentagram in his own locker room while security beat on the door.

Underneath that ran one long argument about who owned the place. Bubba McCoy owned it and did half its booking, and spent every day of the surviving record in a coma with poison in his bloodstream; Salaok announced that he owned the other half and set about using it, firing the World Champion, handing the belt to somebody else, cancelling the countout rule and ruling that the next match Brick lost would be his last. Brick lost it. He also drove a Lincoln Navigator into the arena at WrestleCade III, put Salaok through a set of steel steps with the Bricklayer, and pinned him in the middle of a cell.

The other spine was an order rather than a stable. The Harvesters say they have existed for centuries, and they induct at sunset at the edge of a gully, nine men declaring nine names in turn — of Fear, Minds, Pain, Suffering, Violence, Silence, Sorrow, Blood and Tears — with the last man pulling back his hood to be Sage Sabbot. Silent Wind had been the Harvester of Death and had left, which the order refused to accept: “Remember Jude, you are The Harvester of Death still.” His father is Quartz, and their last match is fought on a rooftop across the road from the arena, shot from a helicopter. Quartz calls him Shale and asks him to come home. Silent Wind tells him Shale is dead, and presses a detonator.

Six championships hang along the foot of the fed’s own front page as belt graphics that swap, on hover, for the current champion’s headshot, and two of the six show a silhouette the site files as mysteryman: one for a belt that had just been stolen out of a locker room on camera, one for a belt nobody had won yet. The World Championship left the building altogether — Silent Wind defended it against Christopher Camero on Primetime Central’s own New Era supercard on 20 April 2003, in what the GW commentary team noted was a traditional square ring, and lost it there. The PrimeStar title was won and settled in a single night’s tournament; the Platinum title went to Jay Robson the same evening, and four men had him tied in the ropes with a stack of chairs before he could leave with it.

What survives is 23 pages of the fed’s own site, captured across eight weeks in the spring of 2003: two full show scripts, three cards whose results were never captured, a newsdesk running in three channels, and one post — numbered 82 — from a promo board of which nothing else remains. The fed was older than that. Its own commentary dates a three-way tag feud to three years earlier, its own newsdesk bills Camero a Hall of Fame member and Ben J. Pailthorpe a two-time champion with eight years behind him, and none of those reigns is anywhere in the record. In April it announced it was moving to a domain of its own; nothing of that domain reached the archive. The last thing it wrote is a wrestler profile on Leviathan, timestamped 18 May 2003, whose text nobody captured. The pages themselves went on being crawled, unchanged, into December 2004.

Storylines

The record holds two of the fed's own nights whole — Evolution of 31 March 2003 and WrestleCade III, five weeks later — and both carry the same two threads to their next beat. The arcs below follow those two beats across both shows, every quote checked against the served text.

Salaok's coup, and Brick's answer Written by AI, 2026

Bubba McCoy owns Global Wrestling and spends the entire surviving record comatose with poison in his bloodstream. On 31 March, co-owner Salaok has already fired the World Champion and handed the belt to Silent Wind, cancelled the countout rule, and ruled that Brick's next loss ends his run in the fed. Brick calls Salaok out mid-show and accuses him outright of poisoning McCoy — "Who would gain the most if Bubba were gone?" — and Salaok never denies it: "You have no proof... but you don't hear me admitting to it either, do you?" Brick loses the main event anyway, on Salaok's own order to the winner, and is suspended. Jesse Briggs and Ely Braxton crash the ring in a stolen car waving papers Salaok calls a fraud, and all three men are arrested; Salaok's own price for dropping the charges and lifting the suspension is a handicap match the following week, three of them against four of his.

The rematch is the Hell in a Cell at WrestleCade III, and Brick wins it clean — a Powerslam onto the steel steps, a Bricklayer that leaves Salaok "bleeding profusely from his face, chest and legs" — over two of the Harvesters climbing into the cell to save their own man. "West: The winner of the match…BRICK!" The show cuts straight from that pin to the main event with no further word from Salaok on the record.

The Harvester of Death, and the fire on the roof Written by AI, 2026

The Harvesters induct at the edge of a gully at sunset, nine men declaring nine names in turn — of Fear, Minds, Pain, Suffering, Violence, Silence, Sorrow, Blood and Tears. Silent Wind was the Harvester of Death and left the order, which will not let him go: on 31 March, Salaok and Sage Sabbot beat him down in front of four watching Harvesters while Salaok needles him — "Remember Jude, you are The Harvester of Death still" — and Wind answers before he's overrun: "I was one of you once upon a time. But times change Harvester of Fear."

His father is Quartz, a Harvester himself, and their match at WrestleCade III is fought on a rooftop across the road from the arena, shot from a helicopter after the building's own PPV wraps. Quartz calls him by his birth name and asks him home — "Shale! Why fight, Join me! Return home to the Harvesters!" — and Wind answers "Shale is dead! I am Silent Wind," pulls a detonator, and levels the building. The broadcast cuts to the rubble: Quartz is found alive; the only trace of Silent Wind is his own mask, "ripped, red slashed black," lying in the wreckage. The fed's own last dated writing is three weeks later, a Wrestler Spotlight column whose body was never captured, and the record never returns to say what became of him.
