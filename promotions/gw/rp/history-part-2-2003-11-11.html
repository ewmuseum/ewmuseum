'History', part 2

11 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-11-03 Five years later? There are days when you really feel you should have done some things different in your life. Should have learned to control yourself. Should have told her no. Should have tried harder to explain things to her dad when he caught you in her bed after you couldn't tell her no. Definitely should not have jumped out the window. Definitely should not have run with only the clothes you had. Should have tried to make up with him, or at the very least get the rest of your things. Then maybe you wouldn't have spent the last few years on the road, wouldn't be barely getting by from day to day. Yeah, there are days where you wish you could go back and change things, and while he was being choked with a bicycle chain by some Yakuza-wannabe punk, Akodo Rokku definitely was finding this to be one of those days. For one thing, he thought, if he had just worked things out with Minako's father, maybe he wouldn't have this bicycle chain wrapped around his neck right now. Maybe they'd be married, they'd have two kids, he'd have a steady job, a warm home, a loving woman in his bed? But no. Instead here he was, working as a bouncer at a club in Tokyo, where if you tell some rich punk to take his hands off of a lady, he and his friends decide they need a piece of you, and you end up being strangled with the aforementioned bicycle chain. Rolling his eyes, Rokku put all that out of his head and focused on the now. As one of the punks lined up for a kick at his midsection, Rokku grabbed the wrists of the kid with the chain, and dropped to the floor, pulling his strangler down with him. At the same time, the other punk let loose with his kick -- and drove his boot right into the side of his friend's head. The impact of the kick sent the strangler tumbling over the bar, where he collapsed, unconscious -- but most importantly, it made him let go of the chain. Rokku immediately whipped the chain off of his neck, lashing it around the ankle of the stunned kicker. With a hard jerk, Rokku yanked the punk's standing leg out from under him, dropping him flat on his back. The instant his attacker hit the floor, Rokku was on his feet, and bringing a hard boot down onto the fallen punk's stomach. The punk hacked, nearly coughing up his dinner, then fell silent when Rokku pivoted and drove a heel into his temple. The ring of people widened significantly as Rokku as Rokku glared murderously at the one remaining punk kid of the trio. 'If you like walking,' said Rokku his words burning themselves into the punk's mind, 'pick one of them up, and walk out of here. I don't want to have to carry all three of you out to the sidewalk.' 'A-arigato!' yelped the punk, his resolve shattering. He immediately hauled one of his unconscious friends onto his shoulders and broke for the door. Rokku grabbed the other unconscious punk and dragged him to the front door, tossing him out on the sidewalk. 'And stay the hell out!' hollered Rokku, as the quaking kid shoveled his two friends into their car, and peeled out of there like God was after him. 'Guh,' grumbled Rokku, fishing a cigarette out of his pocket, 'I am way too young for this crap.' 'I quite agree.' 'Gah!' Rokku yelped, his cigarette and lighter slipping from his hands in his surprise. Before him stood a strapping older man in an emerald-colored business suit. He wore his graying hair in a classical topknot, and his face in a friendly, easygoing smile. 'That was quite impressive back there,' he said, as Rokku stared at him dumbly, 'the way you dealt with those troublemaking punks. You're quite a fighter, son. Good-looking, too!' Finally, Rokku remembered how to speak. 'Look, uh, guy? I'm not? y'know? uh? like that?' The stranger's smile broadened, and he shook his head. 'You're misunderstanding me, son. I'm not here to make you that kind of proposition.' Rokku raised an eyebrow in confusion. 'Then what kind of proposition are you here to make, Mister??' 'Yoritomo,' replied the man, draping an arm over Rokku's shoulders and guiding him back into the club, 'Yoritomo Hari, but just call me Hari. Tell me son, have you ever considered professional wrestling?' *** Hours later, Rokku tossed his keys on an endtable and slammed the door of his tiny one-room apartment shut behind him. A few quick twists and the locks were sealed, and Rokku was free to collapse onto his bed. Rokku closed his eyes, and reached into his pocket. He pulled out the business card Hari had given him, feeling over the letters embossed on the front. I. W. L. The Imperial Wrestling League. Slowly, Rokku reopened his eyes, looking at the emerald letters, then flipping the card over, and re-reading the address and phone number. Wrestling. He'd done it before, a few shows for various indy feds, just to put enough money in his pocket to pay bills. But did he want to do it for a living? Could he really dedicate himself to being not just a fighter, but an entertainer, someone who put on show after show, went through fight after fight, every night of his life, all for the pleasure of the crowd? Rokku thought back on the other jobs he'd done over the last few years. Most of them didn't make him very proud. At best, they were nothing to write home about, and at worst -- well, at worst they were things he hoped his mother would never hear of. Sighing, he glanced at the four walls of his apartment, then stared up at the ceiling. Minutes later, he sat up, and set his alarm for 7 am. Hari-san had said to be there at 9 -- and he wanted to be sure he was on time. After all, what the hell did he have to lose?
