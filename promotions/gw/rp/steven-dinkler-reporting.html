Steven Dinkler Reporting

Date: Sunday Smooth. graceful. refined. None of these words could be used to describe the unorthodox devil may care wrestling style of Deacon Holmes. A much more suitable adjective for his uncanny ability to continually snatch victory from the jaws of defeat ,in grueling and often times death defying matches, would be unstoppable. Unstoppable to the point that not even a lack of actual wrestling knowledge, or training for that matter, have done nothing in the way of hindering his meteoric rise to stardom in little more than a year. What he lacks in traditional wrestling ability, he makes up for with a compact yet winsome appearance, charming wit, a winning smile, and last but not least... a total lack of regard for human well being, even his own. Oft putting life and limb on the line in matches, Deacon has been known to do whatever it takes to emerge victorious and displays immeasurable resilience each and every time he steps into the ring. At nineteen years of age, Deacon Holmes has probably suffered more injuries than he’s spent years on this earth, and been removed from the arena on a stretcher headed for the nearest hospital on more than one occasion. Only one of those occasions in defeat, a tie. In the books it’s not recorded as such, but in Deacon’s mind if he didn’t win, he lost, regardless of if his opponent didn’t win either. That passion and desire and will to succeed combined with extraordinary athletic ability and good looks take Deacon Holmes far beyond the realm of wrestlers, and into that of a bonafide star. His talent and knack for making headlines and stealing air time wherever possible have landed him on several magazine covers, and even garnered a very lucrative shoe endorsement deal from Nike. With good reason, as his custom made shoe, the Nike Shox Air Genocide, have successfully bolstered the company’s sagging sales figures. It’s also driven several analysts to classify him as unbearable in victory, yet unbeatable in defeat, due to the fact that he always comes back exponentially focused after a loss. A completely focused Deacon Holmes is an infinitely dangerous, and rarely seen weapon. Rarely seen because whether throwing caution to the wind and electrifying the audience with a bounding leap off of a chair, over the ropes, and onto an unsuspecting opponent outside the ring, or dropping his opponent with his scintillating “Final Flash” DDT, by hook or by crook... Deacon Holmes know how to win. It’s been said that speed can make up for a lack of skill in most anything, it’s also been said that catching Deacon Holmes was as hard as nailing jello to a wall. In the ring he’s quick, he’s aggressive and in your face, yet at the same time elusive and seemingly always out of reach. By far the most accurate description of Deacon’s matches and the breakneck pace at which is moves is “A black flash followed by a three count and an all night victory celebration.” What goes on during that “black flash” though is what’s made him one of the most popular members of the Global Wrestling roster and boosted his merchandise sales up to the top of the company regardless of if the fans agree with everything he does or says outside of the ring. Deacon Holmes combines subtle grace with brutal impact during matches. Every match he showcases an impressive array of suicidal attacks, on many of which he appears to be up in the lights before crashing down onto his opponent, shielding his body at the critical last moment. The higher the structure the better, and much to the delight of the fans he’s yet to find one he’d deem too high. The scars on Deacon’s slim yet extremely well sculpted physique don’t even tell half the story of his gravity defying madness. In spite of the cuts, injuries, and multiple bone breaks he’s suffered that would more than likely end someone else’s career, Deacon has managed to gain acclaim in Global Wrestling for the chances he takes not only to win matches, but excite fans as well. He has landed on every part of his amazingly resilient anatomy, including his head, more than a few times. Knowing he’ll never be hailed as any sort of mat technician or ring general, he plays to his strengths, which would more than likely be thought of as weaknesses for anyone else. Deacon shortens his career with every risk he takes in the form of odd angled impacts with his opponents. Never has such little regard for one’s own well being done so much for that being’s career. No one in the history of wrestling has ever had such an impressive rookie year. Only a precious few wrestlers have been able to gain as much mainstream recognition as Deacon Holmes, and none have done it in such a short amount of time. Beyond living up to his moniker as “The Poster Child For Don’t Try This At Home” in the wrestling world, Deacon’s become one of the most prominent celebrities in any form of entertainment. Everyone wants a piece of the nineteen year old trash talking, catch-phrase spewing young man that refers to himself in the third person in fan mail and doesn’t seem to know the meaning of the word quit. Fans of his aerial insanity can’t seem to get enough of the nineteen year old’s unorthodox style no matter how many shows a week he appears on or how many matches he wrestles in. Deacon appears on Evolution weekly, and due to popular request has showed up on Overload twice. He’s also no stranger to burning the candle at both ends... or just saying to hell with it and dropping the whole thing in an incinerator. For several pay-per-view events Deacon has wrestled in more than one match, and notched his record at four in one night. He’s even taken over the air waves on two separate occasions and renamed the show Deaconpalooza. Due to the amount television time he receives, he’s also attracted the attention of several analysts, most of whom bash him for his lack of training and his unorthodox style. Saying he’s “As skilled a wrestler as a crocodile is a pianist.” While they may refer to him as unskilled, one thing they can not call him is unimaginative. In an age where moves are often copied and rarely invented, Deacon has conjured up an incredibly fast double shooting star press that not only boggles the mind, but dazzles the eye as well. He’s given the same name as what seems to be his in ring motto, gravity be damned. When asked to define his style once, Deacon referred to it as “Street Kune Do”, saying that it’s a lot like Bruce Lee’s Jeet Kune Do in which everything is done in response to the opponents actions... except he never knows what exactly what his response will be. Which leaves the question of how do you scout your opponent when not even he knows what he’ll do next. He’s got no known patterns, he doesn’t signal or telegraph anything, and his only tendency is winning. So much so that him holding several singles titles at a time. Even without a belt he’s regarded as a champion due to the primestar title being retired during his second reign with it, making him it’s permanent owner. Though still technically a champion, Deacon lost his last remaining active title just two weeks ago at High Noon in a heated main event match up in which he faced off against Train and Brick. When asked about losing at the pay-per-view, Holmes replied that although he no longer held the title, he hadn’t lost and that it was Brick who lost, as he’d been the one to get pinned. Except with grittier terminology, and much more cursing. The loss came when he was pushed from the top turnbuckle by Brick just as he was preparing to take to the air. As a result, his hundred and eighty four pound frame came crashing down across the unforgiving steel of the guard rail, successfully cracking two of his ribs and sidelining him for the following edition of Evolution in which he was to face Train in a one on one match for the title. It would have to go down as without a doubt the cruelest loss the young superstar had ever been forced to suffer through, writing in pain outside the ring as his beloved title slipped from his grasp. After a week off to rest and recuperate Deacon now prepares to face the newcomer Juri Hyobanshi, who’d had an impressive showing against current cruiserweight champion Max Roswell just the week before. Due to their evasiveness and reliance upon speed over power, Holmes and Hyobanshi are near mirror images of each other in the ring. Each utilizing suicidal flying maneuvers and whatever else seems to work at the time to subdue their opponents. When asked about their similarities, an infinitely sure of himself Deacon Holmes replied simply “She’s no me.”, then adding later “Fuck with the best, go down like the rest.” His comments came as no surprise as Deacon’s built much of his success to downplaying and degrading his opponents. Deacon sees no point in standing tall if he can’t stand on top of someone else, he doesn’t consider himself a victor without a victim. Competition makes his blood boil, not because he enjoys testing his mettle against others, but because he likes to win. In order to do so, you’ve got to be hungry, and Deacon’s desire consumes him like a towering inferno does a pile of dry kindling. Very few persons, comparatively, know how to desire with sufficient intensity. They do not know what it is to feel and manifest that intense, eager, longing, craving, insistent, demanding, ravenous desire which is akin to the persistent, insistent, ardent, overwhelming desire of the drowning man for a breath of air; of the shipwrecked or desert-lost man for a drink of water; or the famished man for bread and meat. Desire is the first principle of success, the second is courage. There is no substitute for guts, and Deacon Holmes is bursting with them. It was once said that the scars one acquires while exercising courage will never make them feel inferior, and looking at Deacon’s body explains a lot about his attitude. Often portrayed in the media as a cynical, untrusting, raging hormone crazed brat with little to no control over himself. His temper is ever at a short fuse and he carries around a twenty ton chip on his shoulder. None of this is helped much by the only person he’s chosen to forge a close friendship with, John Zero, who at times serves as confidante and even mentor to this troubled young man. In times of doubt Zero has always been there to usher and goad Deacon on, telling him “God hates a coward.” when he seems to be having second thoughts about rash actions. Cowards falter, but danger is often overcome by those who nobly dare. -Steven Dinkler
