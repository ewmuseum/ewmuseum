Celebration

3 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/3/04 The mood in the air was one of celebration. “Celebration” by Kool and the Gang played from the stereo. Brick’s friends and family had all gathered at the home of Kyle and Monica Fontaigne to celebrate his victory at High Octane. For six weeks, Brick had to deal with the racist Mark Makonie. Finally, Brick got his hands on him in a combination I Quit/Chain match. It was a hard-fought and bloody match, but in the end, Brick pulled out the victory by making Makonie give. Brick had accomplished his goals with this match. He finally beat the hell out of Makonie and he finally proved him wrong. He proved that being Black didn’t make him inferior. He proved that Makonie wasn’t perfect, just because he was White. “If I can have everyone’s attention, please,” said Aaron. “Let’s all raise our glasses and toast to our brother, our friend Jamal “Brick” Randall, on his victory at High Octane.” “Amen!” everyone said in unison. “So, big brother,” asked Monica, “how does it feel to send a message to every racist that was watching?” “I know,” interjected Kyle. “I especially liked it when you lifted Makonie up over your shoulders and threw him out of the ring onto those racists. That was just beautiful.” “I know,” replied Brick. “But nothing felt as good as hearing that son of a bitch…” “Jamal!” “Sorry, Mom. As hearing Makonie say ‘I give’.” “I know,” replied Ely Braxton. “I watched the whole thing from back stage. Sorry I didn’t make it out there in time.” “That’s okay, man. Good thing the Legion showed up.” “You know, Brick,” said Christina McCoy, “I wondered what you meant in my office when you said that you ‘knew people’. Did you mean the Legion?” “I plead the fifth.” “Where are they, anyway?” asked Jesse Briggs. “I invited them to the party, but Akodo said they were gonna be busy. I told them it was cool.” “I’m still proud of you, Jamal. I knew you were going to win your match.” “Nice to know somebody did. I wasn’t so sure. I figured it could go either way.” “Just about everybody backstage was rooting for your son to win, Mrs. Randall,” added Christina. “So many people were cheering when Brick won.” “Actually, you’ll never guess who wished me luck in my match at High Octane.” “We know, Brick. Al Sharpton, Jesse Jackson, Louis Farrakhan, we heard it already.” “Yeah, I know, that’s what I told you. But the real surprise was Deacon Holmes.” “WHAT?” everyone said in unison. “No, seriously.” “As in ‘professional pain in the ass’ Deacon Holmes?” “Yeah. One day, we bumped into each other. He had his girlfriend with him, and I don’t mean John Zero. Anyway, at first, I thought we were gonna get into it. I was ready for him, but then he just looked at me and said, ‘Good luck at High Octane’. Then he just walked off.” “He said that to you?” “I couldn’t believe it either. I stopped him before he could walk away, I said, ‘Hold up a minute. What do you mean good luck?’ Then he walked up to me and said, ‘Look, I still think you’re a tired-ass old man who needs to retire and stop sucking up my damn spotlight. But as much as I can’t stand you, I hate punks like Makonie even more. So, if one of us has to kick his ass and it can’t be me, then I guess it has to be you.’ Needless to say, I was a bit taken aback. Then I told him that I appreciated what he said and I turned away.” “Wow,” said Christina. “And then the little bastard tried to jump me from behind.” “Typical,” sighed Aaron. “Of course, he didn’t get me this time. Just goes to show that my facing Makonie didn’t change a damn thing between us.” “Guess not.” “Anyway, forget all that. This is a party. Let’s celebrate!” And as the music changed to “Outstanding” by the Gap Band, everyone got back into the celebrating mood.
