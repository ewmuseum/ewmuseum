It was less grating on the nerves that way, especially here? now. She was late. He checked his watch for the thousandth time, two minutes had passed since the last inspection. His worn leather jacket felt especially heavy tonight, but he knew that was his imagination, it was really just the items in his inside pocket. A zip disk? and a small crushed velvet box. One was weighing slightly heavier on his mind at this particular moment. Movement caught his gaze, and he turned around just as she crossed the room. Jade. His sweet, lovely Jade. The smile that lit up his face transformed him, from merely passably good looking into something much more handsome. He slid off the stool, and she stepped into his embrace, hugging him tightly. It was as though the rest of the crowded room ceased to exist for a moment, so lost they were in each other’s eyes. He ran one hand though her tousled brown hair, causing the snowflakes caught in between the strands to melt. She stood on tiptoe, and their lips met as time ground to a halt, seconds dripping from the bloated clocks like rain falling in the desert. She tasted like sweetness and light, and smelled like the clean, crisp scent of fresh snow. She broke away from the kiss; a smile playing about the corners of her mouth as she playfully swatted his arm. “Well, hello to you too. Did you miss me, Jackson?” She’d always called him by his last name, never his given name, not that it mattered much either way. She could have called him anything, and he would have answered. She held the keys to his heart, the problem was she knew it. He was easy to control, or so she believed then. She took his hand and they made their way to the corner booth? their booth. The high backs, and dim lighting made for a perfect romantic dinner location, or perhaps the perfect place to conduct a secret meeting. It had been used for both? the red and white checked tablecloth knew all of their whispered secrets. He helped her out of her jacket, hanging it on a hook beside the booth. He slid in across from her, still wearing his, already fidgeting in that way that annoyed her. She could tell he was either preoccupied, or nervous. Maybe a little of both. “Jade, I?” he hesitated, raking a hand though his dark hair, his eyes refusing to meet her clear, direct gaze. The jukebox behind them began to play a warbling Elvis tune? ironically, “It’s Now or Never?” he took it as an omen. Clearing his throat, he reached into his inside pocket, closing his fist over the tiny box. He stood on legs that felt as reliable as toothpicks, and crossed to her side, dropping to one knee, proffering the closed box. “Jade, I love you” will you? marry me?” Silence fell over the table, dropping heavily like a burial shroud, so absolute was the hush. After a moment, she blinked, and then began to laugh, soft chuckles at first, growing in volume. Anger darkened his features? was she laughing at him? He moved fluidly back to his feet, dropping the box atop the table before turning his back on her, his expression betraying his hurt. He wasn’t aware that he was moving, until he was passing the jukebox. The hurt boiled over, and he drilled a fist into the glass bubble, cracking it, making the Elvis record skip. Voices shouted at him to stop, but he just kept walking, moving on autopilot, until he pushed out into the snowy night. He heard her footsteps behind him, he heard her call out to him, but he didn’t look back, or pause until he felt her hand touch his arm. “Jackson.” One word, his name, but it conveyed a wealth of emotion. A plea? sorrow? reprisal? all in one tiny word. He stared at the ground, at the shimmering carpet of snow, refusing to look at her. She tried again, her heart on her sleeve, so against her usually cold demeanor. “I’m sorry” you took me by surprise, is all.” She touched his face, her hands were like ice, bracing against the feverish flush of embarrassment that still colored his cheeks. He groped for words, and after a significant pause, some came. “I guess I can assume the answer is a negative, judging by your response?” “Jackson,” she retorted, her voice brittle, “look at me, please?” He forced himself to meet her gaze, as she took his hands. Tears shone in her eyes, as she stood there in the snow. Something hard scratched his palm, and when he looked down, there was the diamond, glittering on her finger, outshining all the snowflakes as they drifted lazily from the heavens. She stretched up, kissing him for the second time that night, and this time he tasted the salt of her tears, or were they his own. She wrapped her arms around his neck, and whispered beside his ear, sealing both their fates. “Yes.
