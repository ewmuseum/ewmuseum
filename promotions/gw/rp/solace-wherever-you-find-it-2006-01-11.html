Solace Wherever You Find It

11 January 2006

Date: Week of 1-11-06 (PPV Promo) No matter where it was located, it seemed like there was an unwritten rule that hospitals would all share the same decorator. Some maker of crappy pastel furniture and fixtures must have made a deal with the Devil years before to keep that business. He paced around the waiting room, each footstep echoing loudly off the linoleum floors and bare walls. The black tailored suit and stylish tie looked out of place here in the rural, relaxed atmosphere of this “long term care facility” as he had been told to call it over the phone. He didn’t care what they called it, he knew it was a nuthouse, a loony bin, a psycho ward or any of a hundred other names for a mental hospital. Just standing in the waiting room, inhaling the cleaning fumes Jake felt queasy. Is this where they’ll throw me when all is said and done? Do those who have seen what men of science refuse to believe get locked away here? Jake stopped cold. He didn’t understand why the thought had come springing into his mind. What was this’guilt? Jake began wondering what was happening with him. The truth shall set you free? No, that was the problem. In this case, the truth would only get him locked up in a cell next to her. She was imprisoned by the thing within her soul. The entire debacle was something he understood from a technical standpoint, but he hadn’t shared it with anyone. Jake was sure that Xavier could tell him plenty about the problem, but he didn’t want to open up any of those old wounds for his brother. He had been off for so long his body had healed itself completely, but his spirit wasn’t quite there yet. He didn’t need to push his brother, not now. As for himself, Jake wondered what the future would hold for him. The disaster in Tokyo was done with, and in a few more weeks they would hopefully be in possession of the book. The long ship from China would be docking in Houston, but it was several weeks out. Tokyo had changed everything for them all? Jake was stirred out of his internal musings when the security door buzzed and opened. A young doctor, no older than thirty, came walking out with his mussed hair, horn rimmed glasses and white coat. “Mr. Hix?” He asked, the slight drawl of another Southern state obvious. Jake offered his massive hand, making sure he gave plenty of power without cracking the good doctor’s bones. “Yes. Dr. Grant?” “Yes sir.” The doctor had a warm smile, not yet completely worn down by the surroundings and the unfortunate circumstances of his mostly hopeless victims. “I’m sorry, I was trying to place your accent. Southern no doubt by the grace of God, but not Texan. Is that Alabama I hear?” Jake made small talk, always so much easier to deflect suspicion and doubt from an educated, professional man. Hell, Jake wondered if he thought he was just a brute in a suit. “South Carolina actually, but not too far off.” Dr. Grant smiled. “I understand you were interested in seeing one of our patients.” “Well, one of your patients is’was I guess’a former co=worker of mine. We had a close relationship, and this has’well, no, this wasn’t a shock. I just want to be sure she’s okay, and see if there’s anything her friends and co=workers can do to help her.” Jake explained. Dr. Grant folded his arms over his chest, “I see, and this is Elia Smith?” “Elia, yes.” Dr. Grant normally would have refused, but the Director himself had strongly recommended he help this man as much as possible. Despite the expensive suit, Dr. Grant did not feel this man was too savvy or politically connected. Probably an inherited wealth from his family, which would explain his connection to the Director that granted such privilege and access. “Well, her condition is, unique. I can’t talk about the specifics you understand due to her family’s privacy concerns, and of course our doctor and patient confidentiality. However, I can assure you that she is being treated by some of the best doctors available. I am not sure how long she’ll need to remain with us, but we’re doing everything possible to help her.” Dr. Grant had the confident tone that the medical schools must have offered an elective course to cover. Not everything doc’not everything? Jake nodded, “That helps allay some of my concerns doctor.” “I’m glad I could be of assistance.” Dr. Grant was ready to get back to work. Jake wasn’t letting him slip away with a few words. He nodded as he spoke, “I’m excited she’s in such capable hands, but I also need to see her Dr. Grant. I know she’s in a’a state, but a friendly face can’t hurt from what Director Murphy explained to me.” Dr. Grant maintained his smile, but inside he was livid. Sure, Director Murphy who hasn’t gotten off his fat ass to treat a patient in ten years! But I’m sure he knows what is helpful for therapy! “Well, I guess a short visit wouldn’t be inappropriate.” Dr. Grant turned sideways, inviting him to follow. He led him over to the security doors, and used his access card to free the electronic lock. Jake followed him into the empty white halls beyond the door, “Now, because we’re already treating her with medication she may not be completely lucid. I don’t want this to worry you, sometimes it takes time and a cumulative effect for the medications to really begin making progress.” “She may not remember me?” Jake was concerned. Dr. Grant was afraid he wouldn’t understand, “Well, that is a possibility. Or she may not be receptive to conversation, she could be asleep or any number of other issues could prevent this from being a meaningful visit.” Jake simply nodded, acknowledging the possibility he might not get what a normal visitor expected. The moved down several long hallways, where he saw few patients, although the ones he did see were quite far gone from the reality shared by most people on the planet. Finally, they came to a room with a good sized window with safety wire set within to prevent the patient from escaping. Some of the more deranged had actually managed to break out all the glass and cut themselves on the wire, but preventing escape was key in this particular facility. Inside the room, padded white walls and floor surrounded the white metal frame with a mattress. Jake noticed there were no sheets, and he was going to ask when Dr. Grant spoke. “The sheets?” Dr. Grant seemed to sense the confusion. “Some of our patients have turned even those into weapons, and she tried to’capture I guess would be the best description’one of the orderlies. We felt it safer to remove them for the time being, until she stabilizes.” Jake looked back through the window, seeing the woman seated on the edge of the bed. Her hair, long and dirty, clumped together as though the knots in her hair simply didn’t matter to her. She was barely covered by the thin, stained nightgown showing her legs all the way up to the hip. He could see injuries, scratches made in obsessive manners as though she spent hours doing nothing but scratching herself to try and stay awake. She didn’t move, didn’t seem to sense his presence at the door. The drugs and everything else seemed to have isolated her from the world, keeping the demon within at bay. Dr. grant could see the disappointment in his face, “I’m afraid she’s in no shape for visitors. She’s under very intense medication you understand, and she remains fairly catatonic.” “Can I’can I watch her for a while?” He began to wonder about their relationship. The man seemed, deeply disturbed about the condition of this woman. He decided to let him remain, “Of course, I have a few things to check on. I can escort you back out once I’m done.” Dr. Grant stepped forward, knowing that people often tried to be strong when they didn’t need to. “Or, if you need to leave sooner you can go back to the reception desk and someone will walk you out.” Jake nodded, “Thank you doctor.” Dr. Grant moved down the hallway, and then he disappeared from sight. Jake waited a few moments, making sure Dr. Grant was gone. He turned back, and Jake felt his heart skip a beat as he stepped back from the wall. Elia was there, somehow hanging upside down with her horrid hair falling down as her eyes stared at his. The blankness was gone, and she knew exactly who he was. “You never told me?” Jake simply continued staring at her. She seemed to spin in mid-air, now standing behind the door. Jake realized he shouldn’t be able to hear here, these were soundproof rooms for the most part, designed to keep the screams and anguish of these patients from intruding on the workers and each other. Yet, as she spoke he heard her loud and clear. “Jake’does the time to kill make you feel good” When’flames’you never?” She changed. The arrogance in her eyes disappeared like a fading picture to be replaced by uncertainty’and fear. Terror really, like nothing he had ever seen before. She looked all around, then locked back onto him. “Jake” Where am I? What am I doing here? Is’have you seen?” Jake stepped forward again. He pressed his face in close to the glass, “Have I seen what?” Shaking, the words seemed almost impossible to form. Her lips quivered, and finally her small, scared voice broke through the silence. “The monster?” Jake knew, understanding what was happening with her. He opened his mouth, but she was shaking now. Violently shaking as though some terrible seizure was gripping her form. The sunken eyes, emaciated form and pale skin looked like it could all give way, breaking the frail form fully right before his eyes. He could hear the screaming, like thousands of children crying out in the darkness, almost overwhelming him completely. Jake shook off the sudden frozen panic, willing away the feeling her dreaded cries brought forth from deep within him. He expected people to be running down the hallway to see what was happening, but they weren’t. He didn’t know that the only sound at the moment was his feet echoing with each step they took. She stopped. The abrupt change was nowhere near as frightening as the hunger and hatred he saw now when her red, bloodshot eyes locked tightly onto his blue eyes. “You never told me Jake?” He turned. Jake Hix began walking down the hall, away from the sounds clawing at his soul, away from the dark power he sensed inside that room and away from the problems he wasn’t ready to face. He turned, looking for the receptionist’s desk. He kept turning, always finding another long white hallway that led nowhere. He kept walking, desperately seeking the escape he found himself needing. Jake turned, and he saw two huge men wearing the white orderly uniform he had seen all day, but their faces were hidden behind white masks’highlighting their beady, black eyes. Each man was massive, easily half a foot taller and a foot wider than he was. Each man latched onto an arm. Jake tried to rip his arm away, but the man’s grip was too powerful. He couldn’t even fight back as they lifted him off the ground. Jake tried to kick, but he barely touched them, and to no effect. The two men took him inside a padded room, throwing him to the floor. Jake stood, but despite his best efforts the two behemoths wrapped him inside a white straight jacket with little effort. Jake ran into one of them as soon as he was released, but Jake found himself bouncing off the hardened muscles of the hidden man. As he struggled on the floor, they left him alone in the room. Jake rolled around, feeling the wrenching pain in his shoulder as he tried to get back up. He finally pushed himself to one of the walls with his feet, and in the corner was able to regain his feet. He looked up, seeing Dr. Grant there with his arms behind his back. The doctor had a disappointed look as he mournfully shook his head adding a “tisk tisk” for good measure. “Mr. Hix, we can’t have you injuring yourself. I think we’ll start you on a heavy tranquilizer.” Dr. Grant brought his hand around from behind him. He had a large needle filled with red fluid. He jammed the needle through the straight jacket into Jake’s bicep, “Now, why don’t you take a little nap, and we can talk when you’ve calmed down.” Warmth, like some plush, soft blanket wrapped around his body tightly. He felt heavy, the lids of his eyes trying to close as he fought against them. He was losing, falling to his knees as he stared up at Dr. Grant with a smug smile seeming to contradict the concern he also saw on his features. Jake was falling into darkness, hearing the scratching of claws and whispers in the darkness all around him as he tried to fight against the emptiness. They were waiting on him, waiting to feast upon him in retribution for all the terrible things he had done AND all the good things he had done. He was damned for everything, and they wanted their pound of flesh and pint of blood over and over again. He stopped, finding his center once again. He had a sense of himself as he quit looking out and turned his vision inward on himself. The spinning stopped, and he was no longer falling. He kept focusing on his own righteous rage as he pulled his cross arms away from his body. The fabric ripped away, as Jake found himself with arms spread wide and? He stopped his angry yell. He was in his suit, arms spread wide as he looked through the window into Elia’s fiery eyes. No one had come to take him away. No one had restrained him’that was the scariest realization. Elia spoke with lucidity he didn’t think she could have, “Come back’fire and darkness’tell me the answer’the question lingers Jake?” Jake Hix turned, knowing where he was going this time. He was back in the world he knew, where doctors and psychiatrists would lock him up if he spoke the truth about what he was doing as he worked against his brother. Maybe he was crazy according to the rest of the world, but the rest of the world hadn’t seen the darkness he had seen. The rest of the world thought the monsters in the night were imaginations wonderings’not the awful truth that some of the monsters were there, and they seemed to be waiting for him. Alone In The Crowd What am I doing here? Jake asked himself as he sipped the scotch at the bar. Here he was, ready to start the New Year as a new champion, and he was being a depressing, sullen fuck at the bar. The somber mood, the melancholy slipped over him like it shouldn’t have been able to anymore. Unfortunately, controlling the winds of mood and passion were not like controlling his business interests. Everything was wild here, uncontrolled outbursts of all sorts of emotions. Perhaps this was part of being what he had become, seeing what he had seen? Japan? The Mental Hospital? No matter, he was still seated there dealing with the problem all on his own. All around him, there were young men and women enjoying the nightlife Dallas had to offer. He could tell many were getting closer and closer to sealing the deal as it were, but he couldn’t even have a simple pleasure like that anymore. He realized, much to his own chagrin, that if he pursued anyone during this time they would simply be at risk. The endless streams of interviews and appearances were becoming a burden, not because he didn’t enjoy them, but because he had nothing to look forward to later. As the salsa rhythms pumped throughout the club, he found himself slipping into even more of a melancholy. She sat down next to him. A short black dress, not sheer but definitely alluring barely covering the tanned legs and ample blessings. Her green eyes sparkled out from beneath the curly red hair. She was eyeing him in the mirror, but he was basically dismissing it. She ordered her drink, something fru-fru and fun he supposed, but not a real drink the way he looked at liquor. It occurred to him, perhaps this was the way to let his frustrations out. She was certainly beautiful, although at the edges he could see she was fighting her own age. She was probably in her late twenties, maybe early thirties. Older than the crowd’but then again, so was he. And often, the older women were the ones so much more willing to please. Jake Hix motioned the bartender over, “Donna, give me another. I’ll be right back.” “Sure thing Mr. Hix.” She smiled pleasantly. Jake headed down the tightly packed walkway here in the VIP. At the corner, he turned back to catch the red head speaking with Donna. Doing your research, honey? Jake Hix hit the bathroom, relieving himself at one of the stalls. At least the club bothered taking the time to make sure the bathroom stayed immaculate. One thing Jake knew, sometimes letting something so simple slacken was just easy to do for a large club. They wanted to stay around for a while, and paying attention to the men’s bathroom was a sign they were serious. He went back to the sink, the bathroom attendant there ready to help him if he needed it. Jake washed his hands, and then he threw some water over his face and head. The water dripped off, falling down onto the granite surface. He looked up, through the days of haze and weariness penetrating down to his bones. He stared in the mirror, looking for the man he once was. Before regret and conscious, back when all he needed was a name and a stiff drink to get to know a woman for a few minutes, a few hours or an evening. Come on, you know this is what you need. She’s just a spent evening, nothing more and no concern. They won’t care about her? Jake wondered if he was trying to convince himself with the thought. He accepted the white towel from the attendant, a real towel not some cheap piece of disposable waste. He dried off his face and head, and then made sure his hands were waterless too. Jake looked over the man’s wares. “Did you need something sir?” The man asked. Jake pointed to one of his cologne bottles, “Is that Acqua Di Gio?” “Yes sir.” He picked it up and showed it to Jake, “Would you like to refresh yourself sir” Please, feel free.” Jake took the cologne. He sprayed it onto wrists, rubbing them together and then applying it to his neck. He sprayed a little onto each finger, applying it around his ear so if she leaned in she would smell that and not his sixteen-hour day. “Long day sir?” “You ever in your life known any other?” Jake mused with a small smile especially for this man. He was old, lines worn deeply into his face from years of hard work in unfavorable conditions. A small chuckle, born of experience and regret returned the sense of melancholy. “Never in my life sir, never in my life.” Jake Hix pulled out the folded bills held together by his platinum money clip. He pulled them out, and counted off three hundred dollar bills. Jake dropped them into his tip jar, “Have a few on me, and find a day that isn’t quite so long.” The man thanked him, but he was already gone. Back through the throng, where young, lithe bodies parried and dodged each other artfully in a dance around since man needed to procreate. Here he was, still a predator, still a dangerous man despite his advancing years. No one in this club could stand with him, and he still pulled all the stares despite not being the youngest man in the club. He returned to his seat at the bar, where Donna hadn’t let anyone intrude on the space of one of her favorite customers. No, she would keep it empty for him alone and the generous tips he always passed her way because she served him exactly how he liked. Jake sat down, and Donna made the drink, setting it before him immediately. Tonight was rocks’sharp, jagged slivers of ice torn away from the huge block just how he liked them. He sipped the scotch, not as good as what he kept at home lately, but still one of the best in the entire world. He caught the furtive glances. She was judging’evaluating’how best to approach this man. He wondered what she might know about him, but he didn’t care. This wasn’t about getting to know her; it was simply about getting to use her flesh for the release he needed so much. “Anymore furtive glances my way, and I’ll begin to think you’re stalking me.” Jake said before crunching down on the ice sliver he had sucked into his mouth. She was caught. Quickly, the embarrassment was replaced by the false bravado and her voice was not at all shaky when she responded. “Well, I just thought do you come here often seemed a bit too?” “Clich?”” “Exactly.” She sipped her wine, deep red like the lipstick around her wide mouth. “Simply saying hello to someone has become a chore now. You can’t appear too eager?” “Or too reluctant.” “Right. You have to strike the balance between everything.” Jake smiled at her, looking not to his left, but at the mirror behind the bar, “Balance should be for accountants and acrobats, not for these situations.” As she laughed, he turned. There it was, the slight startle in her eyes when she faced him. There was power there in these cold green eyes, and she was reluctant to face him. She pushed all of that away though as she tried to stare back without revealing any of herself, making him work for it. None of that worked though, and after just a few minutes of small talk she was completely naked before him, unable to hide anything. He had charmed her, and not he left Donna five of the bills he had left the attendant before escorting her out of the club. Her name was Rachel. The moon hung low in the sky, a quarter moon although he could make out the outline of the darkened sections in the hazy night sky. He was at the building downtown, not taking a woman such as this home. So now, beneath the night sky he stood against the brick wall in nothing but his boxers. He had proven himself again, and the release from their hours in bed would satiate his flesh for a while. As he stared into the Dallas night, his thoughts drifted to the man he would square off against. He had to face him soon enough, and at the still moments in the night he remembered. He is my brother? He is my family? He is a monster? He must be destroyed? Those were the thoughts that haunted him in the dead of night. He was still lingering there when she walked out. Unlike Jake, she did not shy away from her nakedness. She had nothing covering her firm body with soft curves except for the occasional strand of hair blown there by the night wind. She cozied up against his body and whispered in his ear. “Are you ready for another round?” Soft, inviting warmth in her voice and teasing breath on his earlobe. Jake leaned in, returning the torturous tease to tantalize her. “Why don’t you find out?” She kneeled, and the wine colored caress ensured he was ready and willing. Thoughts faded away, and he was just flesh once more.
