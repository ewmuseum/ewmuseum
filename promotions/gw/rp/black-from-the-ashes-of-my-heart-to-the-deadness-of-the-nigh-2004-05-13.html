Black - From the ashes of my heart to the deadness of the night

13 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05.13.04 'Sweetheart, will you get that?' A phone rings in the distance, slightly muffled along with the voice. Slowly, clarity takes hold and the vision blur dissipates. A man in his mid-thirties sits in a large la-z-boy recliner in the middle of a neoclassically decorated sitting room. Not too far away, a pair of French glass doors stand open, allowing for the intake of a Spring breeze throughout the room. The night is overtaking the sun, the luminosity of the day fading into a deep oblivion. The headline on the front of the paper reads, 'Echelon Incorporated Under Investigation?' The man is gentle with the paper, a pair of thin frame spectacles resting upon his nose as he stares at the words, his eyes assembling the letters and his mind associating their meanings. Graying at the temples, the man's hair was once a lavish brown. His eyes are hazel and brilliant, even with the age visible within them. The phone rings again, disrupting his concentration on the page before him. He glances back over his shoulder towards the dark hallway leading into the rest of the house. Again, his eyes return to the paper, scanning the words with expertise. He quickly switches to the next page, his hands moving in a practiced rhythm. --- Stephen Perdue, born 1971 in an extraordinarily warm August. Hailing from upstate New York - The Niagara Falls area - Stephen proved to be a child prodigy in mathematics and other logical sciences. His parents, June and Frank Burns, were extremely proud of their child on his acceptance into the Stanford business program. It was here that Stephen would excel in business and earn the basics and expertise that would lead him down a path to becoming a business mogul. It was also here that Stephen met and fell in love with his future wife, Lois Houlihan. The two married after college immediately after Stephen's twenty-third birthday. Within a year of the marriage, Stephen was promoted to business manager of Griffin Arts in New York. A month passes and Griffin Arts is purchased by a young upstart Psychological firm known as The Reno Company. The Reno Company, based on donations, funding, and research in the fields of psychological medicine and methods, sought to expand their borders into the entertainment industry by promoting specialized programming for children and adults alike. The owner of this company, Maverick Reno PhD, met with Stephen and the two instantly hit it off. Within another month, Stephen Perdue was the number two man in charge of The Reno Company and its subsidiary, Griffin Arts. It was Stephen's near-perfect training in business that would take The Reno Company, primarily the Griffin Arts division, from a fledgling corporation to a powerful mogul in the business world. With Reno's direction and plans for The Reno Company, there were no limits. By this point, both men were making millions per month. 1999. Maverick Reno returns to the business that gave him the financial backing to begin The Reno Company leaving Stephen Perdue as CEO of this massive empire. --- Again, the phone rings. Stephen sighs and folds the paper before standing and walking steadily towards and through the hallway. He calls out quietly for his beloved wife, 'Lois?' but receives no response. He drops the half-read news paper atop a phone table positioned against a wall in the hallway. He picks up the phone and speaks into the receiver as he leans slightly, peeking through a doorway and into another room of the household. 'Hello?' The word comes off as more of a question than a greeting. There is silence on the other end, followed by a click and a dial tone. Stephen creases his brow before hanging up the phone. He presses the down button on his caller ID, his eyes widening as he reads the number; 'Perdue, Stephen. #2 - [detail withheld].' Perdue glances up before turning towards a door and entrance to one of the home's two stairwells. --- In 2002, Maverick Reno turned full control of The Reno Company over to Stephen Perdue. For two years, Maverick and Stephen were in equal command of the company but as of October, Stephen would be in full control while Maverick used a large sum of profits to fund another company, The Echelon Incorporated. Within one year of it's foundation, The Echelon Incorporated owned twenty-two companies all over the world and was partnered with forty more including The Reno Company in New York, Pierce Imports in Boston, Soma Incorporated in Eastern Europe, and numerous others in Japan and South America. The Echelon Incorporated quickly earned and pocketed three times the amount in one trimester that Reno Company earned in a solid year. Stephen Perdue grew more and more curious, having seen the transition from savior and charitable owner to power mongering and monstrous as performed by Maverick. The two close friends grew distant over time with Perdue continuing upon his family path and Reno digressing and surrounding himself with The Echelon's Consiliu - John Zero, Deacon Holmes, Sean Price, Jason Hughes, Leinad Soma, Tyler Pierce, Naria Xiou, and Aiden & Tobias de Sarret. It wasn't long before evidence surfaced. --- Stephen Perdue slowly wanders up the stairwell, not flipping on a light switch. From inside his pocket and pulls a cell phone out; he kneels in the stairwell, moving his head to watch both entrances. Quickly he dials a number and holds it to his ear, his voice steady yet filled with terror. 'Stephen here. Run a check of the house.' There's a moment of silence and no response prompting Stephen to grow even further in curiosity. He flips his cell phone shut and opens it again, quickly making the same call while edging, again, towards the lower door. This time, the muffled voice is heard on the other end. Stephen speaks in a low tone, his eyes hovering between the doors. 'Kevin. I need you to run a check of the house.' Again, no response to his request. Stephen grows agitated and snaps his phone shut and shoves it into his pocket. Slowly he rises and ascends the stairwell, his eyes peering through his correcting lenses at the door knob above. He slowly extends his hand and takes the knob, gently turning it. The door creeks and slowly opens, allowing light to pierce the crease between door and frame. Stephen places a careful foot onto the hardwood hallway floor. Not a sound. Ring. Stephen jumps, his heart following his lead and leaping from its resting position clear into his throat, shoving his Adam's Apple out of the way. He grabs his chest, breathing heavily now as he slumps to the floor, his back flush to the wall opposite the door from which he came. His hand rests upon the door knob still, his mind racing with a million thoughts. Ring. The phone has begun to ring yet again. Stephen slowly slides himself upward, his back pressed firmly to the wall. His eyes jerk to the left and then right, his head moving with a whip effect behind them. Slowly he edges left towards a small hall closet. He hand slides along the wall, finding the knob. Ring. He slowly opens the door and slips inside, his eyes moving rapidly to take in every detail of the hallway. The door makes no noise as he places his foot against the bottom, keeping the closure slow and steady. --- Stephen Perdue, upon reviewing a few financial reports for the Reno Company, noticed an error in a piece of data referring to over-sea's importation. Intending to fix the small glitch - what should have been a three had been made a thirteen - he walked down to the financial office. It was here that he discovered that the thirteen had not been an error. Thirteen importations had been received from ten different companies in Europe and one in South Africa. Curious and obviously confused, Stephen set out now to discover the contents of these mystery shipments. It wasn't long before Stephen realized that the only records of these shipments were the signed invoices minus the merchandise logs. Again, Stephen was perplexed. After a few calls, he finally gave in and decided to report the error to Maverick Reno's secretary, Naria Xiou. Naria told Stephen to simply disregard the mistake because The Echelon would take care of the issue. Curious, Stephen insisted that he speak to Maverick on the topic; his request was denied. In a matter of days, Stephen found evidence that the crates had passed through Reno Company docks but were quickly removed by Echelon owned trucks. The contents, whereabouts, or reason for the secrecy were still unknown. Not giving up, Stephen hired a private investigator to watch the Reno Company docks. Within the week, a fourteenth mystery shipment arrived; one not mentioned on any Reno Company orders. The two crates spent less than five minutes between trucks as the Echelon had a crew to remove the crates waiting. His personal investigator then discovered that the trucks were then being driven to a warehouse just a block away from The Reno Company's rented docks for storage. Photos were taken as well as audio recorded documentation of everything that went on at the docks. --- Quickly, Stephen turns and ducks down into the corner and whips out his cell phone, hoping to make a call. He dials. beep beep hum beeep bep ... Stephen brings the phone to hear ear. The muffled recorded voice of a woman is audible, barely discernable at this distance. 'We're sorry. The number you are trying to reach is currently out of service. Please hang up, and try your call again.' A pause... and a dial tone. Stephen drops the phone and shakes his head before standing and reaching onto the top shelf. He reaches and grabs, searching out and finally finding. A colt .45 revolver complete with six shots. Now with gun in hand, Stephen leans out of the closet and slowly looks up and down the hallway. There is no noise and no movement. The hall is blank; Stephen slips from the interior of the closet and presses his back to the hall to begin his slow and methodic creep towards a cracked doorway at the opposite end of the hall. Slowly. Stealthily. Silently. Carefully. Nervously. Stephen reaches the door and slides down, his heart beating heavily. The phone downstairs has finally stopped ringing and all is silent. Stephen brings the gun to his forehead, whispering a prayer that Maverick had taught him years before. Silence... Stephen shoves the door open and drops to his stomach, aiming his gun high with his eyes searching. A chair is seated in the center of the room, a person seated in the chair. Black mask, black turtle neck, black pants and boots. A pair of black gloves. A glock is seated in the person's lap. The chair slowly rocks, to and fro. Stephen lies in the floor, panting heavily with his gun carefully aimed at the person in the chair. He demands, 'Who are you!?' Nothing. Silence. Tension. Brewing emotion. ... the world slows down. Very slowly Stephen gets to one knee, never relinquishing his aim on the person. A voice.'Glad you could finally make it, Mr. Perdue.' The voice comes from the direction of the person in the chair, the mask covering any movement made during the sentence. The lights are out, masking the room even more. Stephen gets to his feet and takes aim fully again, his eyes never leaving his target. He hits the light switch - nothing. --- Finally, Stephen had enough information to launch a full federal investigation against the Echelon Incorporated. But did he? No. Stephen held off because of his long friendship with Maverick Reno. Month's passed as Stephen attempted to break the new exterior shell that encased Maverick. He worked and worked, trying to learn of the changes that had overtaken his long time friend. Nothing worked; nothing could break the shell. That's when it happened. 'Aging New York Tycoon John Russell and family slain! Police have no leads.' That was the headline in the New York Post. Stephen would attend his funeral days later, expecting to see Maverick Reno there. There was a wreath decorated in white and black roses with, 'exanimalis maeror' (Deadly sorrow) completed with, 'From us to you - Maverick Reno.' Suspicion was enough. Stephen contacted a New York state District Attorney and handed over the files. The information was scattered with reliability few and far between. It was enough for a desperate legal system to launch an investigation; just not enough for a timid judicial system to grant a warrant. Time would pass and the press for a warrant would die down. The plain fact of the matter: More evidence was needed and nothing could be done until more showed up. The evidence existed. That would become a problem. --- Silence wouldn't reign long. Stephen screams at the top of his lungs, demanding a name from the figure, 'TELL ME WHO THE FUCK YOU ARE!?' Again nothing; Stephen begins to breath heavily, his adrenaline pumping as his fight or flight response system kicks in. His secondary life functions cease. -His urinary tract simply ceases to work. -His digestive tract simply stops processing. His physical characteristics change slightly. -His eyes widen. -His body grows tight and ready for action. -His sphincter puckers. The voice comes forth again, the figure speaking. 'Glad you could finally make it, Mr. Perdue.' Followed by a slight giggle. The voice was female and now with his eyes wide and finally adjusting to the light, the figure is that of a woman. Stephen takes a step back, his gun beginning to quiver in his shaking hands. Again, 'Glad you could finally make it, Mr. Perdue.' and nothing more. 'Just this and nothing more.' Stephen becomes anxious and panicky, sweat pouring down his forehead. His body is shaking, begging him to denounce his fight and run - fly away. The figure continues to rock, the chair making a squeak every now and again. Other than this, the figure has not moved, glock still resting in her lap. Squeak. 'Please! Tell me who the fuck you are!?' Stephen shouts, his composure leaving him. Squeak. Again, the monotone response. 'Glad you could finally make it, Mr. Perdue.' Perdue presses his side to the door, steadying the gun now as he aims it towards the woman's chest. Silence. Squeak. Silence. Squeak. Stephen aims and approaches with one step. Another. 'Answer the damned question!' Stephen is losing it, his mind racing and his heart beating. Squeak. Beat. Squeak. Beat. He aims the gun, anger filling his body. The figure speaks, 'Glad you could finally ma..' BOOM ... 'ke it Mr. Perdue.' -- Darkness. When it consumes a person, they are likely to do anything. Able to sign a contract with the devil. -- The smoke begins to clear. The rocking chair is on it's back, the figure lying on its back, arms spread. The glock now lay on the floor. The squeaking had stopped. -- Able to do whatever it takes to get a job done. -- The voice had continued even after the shot. Smoke still filled the room at the point where Stephen stood. Aside from Stephen's heavy panting, silence. -- Willing. -- 'Glad you could finally make it, Mr. Perdue. The voice. --- It wasn't long before Stephen worried for his family's safety. With help from the US Marshal's and the FBI, Stephen and his family were removed from their home and placed in another in Washington County, New York. With security in place, Stephen ran The Reno Company remotely. Ironically enough, the company he ran was owned by the man he feared the most. Maverick had changed so much; he no longer felt remorse for his actions. Stephen had lived in the Washington County home for three months. On May 13th, 2004, the threat became no longer a threat. A threat is only a threat as long as it remains idle. When it is no longer idle, the threat is no more. --- Three feet away, Stephen stood with his revolver still poised to fire again. Movement had ceased all together. Smoke bellowed from the torn fabric upon the chest of the would-be assassin. There was no breathing or panting; life extinguished within the feeble human frame. Stephen was tense. Clapping. An applause came from behind. Stephen spun suddenly, turning to aim his gun. A hand grabs his wrist and forces it down. BOOM ... Another shot is fired into the hardwood floor of the home. The gun is jerked away and spun in the assailants hand; it's quickly brought back, cracking across the forehead of Stephen Perdue. His face hits the floor, cheek first. His eyes blur and then nothing; blackness. The voice...'Glad you could finally make it, Mr. Perdue.' This and nothing more. Time passes. Seconds. Minutes. Hours? Stephen finally jerks up, his eyes still blurry but atleast he's finally awake. He looks around. He's strapped one of the posts of his four post bed. The would-be assassin still lay in the floor, the wound no longer smoking. He quickly looks around and makes out the shape of another figure. Another and even a third. Stephen quickly looks around, surveying his surroundings. Before him, the glock that fell from the assassin's lap. He reaches and can barely read it... he aims and 'click.' Nothing. ... It's not loaded. The glock wasn't loaded. One of the figures turns slowly, revealing it to be Maverick Reno. The others slowly come into view as well; Aiden and Tobias de Sarret. Maverick reaches into his jacket and pulls out a small hand-held recorder and places it just within reach of Stephen. Stephen looks down at it and then up at Maverick. 'Oh don't look so surprised, Stephen. You knew this day would come the second you handed Larry Owens those files.' Stephen's eyes widen even more. The scene flashes away. --- A man sits at a desk in an office. Surrounding him are file cabinets, books, and other random accessories. He's leaning back, asleep in his chair after a very long day. A knock comes at the door and the man sits up and glances around. Everyone else has gone home and he's the last in the office. Quickly, his voice kicks in as he stretches. 'Come in.' The door slowly opens and in steps Aiden de Sarret. The man at the desk appears busy, shuffling a few papers. 'What can I help you with, sir?' 'I thought you might need a light, Mr. Owens.' The comment strikes Larry Owens, the DA Reno mentioned, as very peculiar. He looks up, crinkling his brow with pure curiosity. 'I'm sorry?' Owen's questions, placing the papers back down upon his desk. Aiden reaches into his duster and pulls a flash light out, holding it up. Owen's is still confused. 'I still don't understand. Why do I need a flashlight?' 'Because of your blind. It's thin and we could see through to some extent with the light on, but we couldn't get a good shot.' Aiden states matter-of-factly as he flicks the light on, aiming it directly at his head. The light is so large in diameter that it casts a shadow within a circle of light upon the drawn shade behind Larry Owens. Owen's eyes flinch with the light, trying to shield himself. 'Turn that damned thing off. Who the hell do you think you...' The blind flies up, glass shattering behind it. Larry Owen's face lands on his desk, blood seeping out in every direction. The shot was silent originally and even with the addition of Larry's thud and the shattering glass, still goes unnoticed. Aiden turns the light off and returns it to the inside of his duster. Now he brings out a handkerchief and wipes off his face, blood having splattered all over the room as the snipers bullet pierced District Attorney Owen's head. 'From us to you, Mr. Owens.' Aiden spits out. He quickly goes to the file cabinet and picks it; he pulls a file marked 'Echelon' from inside and slips it under his duster. He flips the light off and locks the door before leaving the way he entered. The scene returns to Stephen and Maverick. --- 'Good job, Stephen. You put two and two together.' Reno leans down and picks up the recorder and hits play, kneeling before Stephen. It plays. 'Glad you could finally make it, Mr. Perdue.' the recording. The voice was just a recording. Stephen looks at the fallen assassin and strains to reach it. Reno smiles and takes out a knife, slitting the restraints. Stephen falls to the ground, having been straining in the opposite direction. Stephen crawls hurriedly to the corpse, his eyes peering down at the mask as his hand grabs the bottom. Slowly. Carefully. He lifts the mask to reveal a woman in her mid thirties. She has brown hair, once well kempt and now disheveled. Her eyes are cold and lifeless, her face paling. Blood seeps from the corner of her mouth; her husband had shot her at point blank range in the chest as she slept. 'Stephen, you and I have known one another for a long time. But for the life of me, I had no idea that you were the type to kill your own wife. Were there problems in your relationship? Was she cheating on you? What was it, Stephen? What did she do to you?' Reno's voice echoes as he starts out of the room, the de Sarret's having already left. Stephen kneels and buries his face against her upper chest and neck, sobbing uncontrollably. 'Let your remorse be your punishment for betrayal. Misericordia es momento'. Reno stops at the doorway, his long black leather trench coat dragging the floor behind him. Stephen leans up slowly, still sobbing uncontrollably. He glances at Reno's back as Maverick glances back over his shoulder. 'Your gun is in the restroom. I removed all but one bullet; I suggest you put it where it will do the most good.' With this, Reno walks away and down the hall. Outside: Tobias de Sarret and Aiden de Sarret stand in wait, a Lincoln Navigator a mere twenty feet away in the driveway. The door opens and out steps Maverick Reno. The two men turn to look at him as he walks past them. He quickly adjusts his Mandarin collar and then the collar of his trench coat, his hair moving with the wind. The de Sarret's fall in step behind him as they make their way to the Navigator. Above, we see the bathroom that leads to the bedroom where Stephen was left to mourn the death of his wife. A flash of light and the sound of a gun discharging. Not one of the three turn to look, knowing what has happened. They climb into the Navigator and slowly pull out of the driveway. The scene fades to black. I'm coming for you.
