Conversations with the Dead pt.1

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: #2 MOTM As his footsteps echoed through the empty space he felt as if he was walking back in time. The light from the sun outside cut through the darkness inside the old 'Bottoms Training Facility' through the open double doors. The old parkay floors creeked with Train's every step, under the weight of his wide frame. It had been a couple of months since the last time he was here. As he walked down the narrow hallway, lined with trophy cases filled to the brim with trophies, medals and plaques... tangible evidence of the hard work of so many who'd spent hours in this place training to be the best, he looked at the state of the place he'd bought so long ago and felt guilty. Everything was covered in dust with a cobweb hear and there to break up the monotony. He'd had every intention of fixing the place up. It had been the plan for a while. A long, long while. At least that's what Train told himself. In the front of his mind he thought it to be the truth, but in the back of his mind he knew that wasn't really why he'd bought it. When Isaiah, the man who gave Train the skills to accomplish all he has, owned this place, this place was all about the community. Helping out. Giving back. Now that Train owned it, it was all about him. It was the place he retreated to when he need inspiration or motivation.... when he was staring down the barrel of something big. He hadn't been here since the showdown... since High Noon. With the Platinum Title on the line needed to remember why he was in the postion he was. Why he was poised to go from Kingpin to Champion. That's why he returned here then. It was this place. Not just this building, but the man who owned it. Isaiah had grown up in the same streets Train had. Made it out. Turned into a champion. He paved the way. More importantly he made Train believe he could do the same. For a while... but that was just a dream at the time. Life has a way of snuffing those out. Quick. Maybe it was just too easy to do it. Maybe that's why Train fell back into 'The Life' so easily after Isaiah died. Maybe he just liked it. Whatever it was the dream didn't look like it would live any longer than Isaiah. But then again, they say all things happen for a reason. Maybe Train needed to decide instead of having it decided that achievement in the ring was what he wanted. It took a while, but it finally came. The phone call from GW. The opportunity to get out. To start over. No matter how predestined or ordained it might have seemed at first, it was a hard choice to make. At 27 Train made for a pretty old rookie. But, then again, on the streets he might has well have been Methuselah. At 27 most of the cats from his side of town had been dead for 6 or 7 years or a couple of years deep in bid upstate. The fact is, statisticly, he'd been living 'The Life' on borrowed time. Train had never really thought of it that way when he was in the midst of it. He was just doing his thing. Getting paid. It wasn't till he actually started making his living in the ring he finally figured that out. Not that it scared him. Hell, he was a man. He made his decisions and sometimes they catch up to you. They still might. But, in the meantime, he wasn't going to do anything else but live his life or be anybody else, but who he was. Train slowly strolled down the hallway, letting his eyes pass over the dusty trophy cases and it's contents, the memories of seeing these nearlly every day in his younger days flooding back. Finally he'd taken his fill and proceeded down the steps to the main floor of the gym. The light raining down from the skylight at the center of the tall, open space illuminates the area well. Now that he'd had some workers take the boards off all the windows to let in the sunshine the place seems to glow with at least a sparkle of the life it once had. Opening up the doors had let the place air out a little. The smell of the musk that's been build up over the years brought back memories too, but who needs to be that nostalgia? Train stepped down off the last step onto the gym floor and stood for a moment, looking around. His focus stopped on the rickety ring at the center of the gym. He could almost see himself training in there with Isaiah and all it's stages. Getting tossed around like a rag doll. Finally being able to fend off an attack or two, but getting circles run around him instead. Then to help Isaiah up off the mat after he dumped the old man a time or two. 'Those were the good old days.' Train thought himself with a chuckle. After a few moments he brought himself back into the present moment and decided it was time to stop stalling. To do what he'd really come here for. Train turned to his left and walked the few feet to he door with the reinforced glass window. The word 'Office' sits boldly, even if slightly faded at the bottom of the glass. Train just stares at it for a moment. The guilt hits again. The selfishness of his visit weighs on him. He wasn't here to build the place into what it used to be like he promised he would. But, as he thought to the backpack he had slung over his shoulder and it's contents, the guilt eased away. He might not be there to rebuild...but he was going to contribute. Train dug into his pocket and pulled out the special set of keys that normally spent most their time in his sock drawer back in Dallas and rifled through until he found the one he's looking for, silverish and slightly tarnished. The key slides in and the knob turns with unexpected ease. The atmosphere of the office has been undisturbed for quite a while, but air inside seems fresh to Train. He steps inside and stands in front of the old desk where he'd stood numerous times before. The highly organized clutter of papers and folders made it look as if Isaiah had been sitting there just a moment ago. The row of large bookcases lined up against the left wall were still filled with books on every sport imaginiable...though mostly wrestling. Greco-Roman, professional...whatever. And the tape collection of matches still lined the entire length of the bottom shelf, two tapes deep. Train scanned each one and could almost remember which matches were on which tape, having spent so much time watching them. Gleaning as much technique from them as he could. Train grinned. But, while it was comforting to see not much had changed, none of that stuff meant that much to Train. It was the stuff on the top shelf he wanted to see again. Isaiah had been a fairly big name in the wrestling business back in the day. The trophies of his conquest before returning back to the neighborhood to give back. State Titles like Florida and Georgia. US Titles. Canadian Titles. Tag Team Titles. Even some that Train can't identify them as their lined with Chinese/Japanese lettering. The replica plaques lined the top of the bookcase and sat at the top of the office like a lighthouse. Train remembered looking up there when he was young and thinking he could do what Isaiah had done. They had been a beacon. With Master of the Mat hours away... They still were. Train moves over to the on the wall to his right and pulls the drawstring. The slotted blinds slide up with a slight struggle and the light comes in. Train surveys the scene, this time in the light. The setting...the feel... It's perfect. It's time to make his contribution to Isaiah's legacy. Train slowly moves over to the bookcase, sliding the back down from his shoulder, placing it on the floor. With a pull of the zipper the bag is open. Train reaches down and pulls out the highly polished, solid piece of maple he's been hauling around all day. But this was no ordinary plank of wood... Not with the GW Platinum Championship mounted on to it. Or at least a replica of the belt Train had come here the last time to inspire himself to win. It belonged here now. Train stretched his frame and slid the plaque into the precious bit open space at the top of the row. the plaque tilted back and rested against the wall with ease. As if the space had been set aside just for it. Train stepped back and looked at the row of belts and trophies. The Platinum gleamed brightly, but even with the dust the others seemed to shine just as brightly to him. Train nodded to himself. 'That's yours dogg.' Train said outloud, speaking to a prescence. 'I owe you that one. Good lookin' out man. When I win this Master of the Mat... I'll bring you that strap too. I don't know where you right now, but come the 26th, wherever you at... Pull up a chair man. I'm a show 'em how we get down. Word is bond.' Train stands silently for a moment, as if expecting a reply... as if he knew he'd been heard. As he stands in the office of the man who inspired him to be what he is and does what he does the silence almost serves as an answer. As a vote of confidence. Train just nods again. He steps back to the window and pulls on the string. The blinds close. The scene goes dark.
