Lessons of Thunder, End

13 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 13th, 2003 The top of the Tokyo Tower. As I looked out over the city from it at daybreak, I found a view quite unlike anything I'd ever seen before. The city stretched out for miles around me, just on the verge of wakefulness. As the sun began to creep over the horizon, the jungle of metal and glass shimmered with its light. It was absolutely beautiful. 'Isn't it, though?' I jumped out of my skin -- or for that matter, off the building. Sighing, I glanced to my side and saw exactly who I expected. Leaning against the safety fence was a bare-chested man covered in multicolored tattoos, his mane of long black hair swirling in the wind. 'Hitomi Kenta,' I said with a chuckle, 'you always seem to know what's on my mind, Black Dragon. How do you do that?' 'It's a gift,' replied Kenta, smirking. 'I'm glad you came, Steel Lion.' 'Well,' I said, running a hand through my hair, 'if I hadn't, Yoshiko would have killed me. But why did you want to meet me at the top of the Tokyo Tower of all places -- and at sunrise? You wouldn't believe the favors I had to call in to get up here this early.' 'Is it really so difficult?' asked Kenta, cocking his head quizzically. 'I come here every sunrise.' I raised an eyebrow in disbelief. 'How do you manage that? What, do you know the head of security or something?' Kenta shrugged. 'This is where I must be, so it is where I come. I can scarcely remember when it wasn't so.' 'As mysterious as ever,' I laughed. 'I'm glad to see you haven't changed while I've been gone.' 'I haven't?' said Kenta, with genuine surprise, 'Then, I must be doing something wrong. You, however, Steel Lion. You have changed much since last I saw you.' 'What makes you say that?' I furrowed my brow, wondering what Kenta meant. 'I can see it in you,' replied Kenta, 'you radiate change like a burning flare. There is new strength in you, Steel Lion. Strength born of love. Strength born of torment. Strength born of experience, and strength born of wisdom. Much has happened to you since you left to find your own Tomorrow. Your eyes are older, wiser. You are more focused than you have ever before been. Tell me, Steel Lion -- what has changed you so?' For a long moment, I stared at him. Kenta had always been strange -- if Sakura was esoteric, then Kenta was downright mystical -- but this was something new. I felt like his eyes were inside me, reading my feelings, my experiences. It was unnerving and liberating at the same time. I couldn't help but answer his question. 'So many things, Kenta. Going to America -- it was the most exciting and dangerous decision of my life. And it's been fantastic -- but it's also been horrible. I've made new friends, but I've made just as many enemies. I've had my eyes opened to the potential I was holding down inside me, but I've also seen what hides in the dark corners of the world. I found a father, and then had to kill the monster he'd become. I found love, and took it from another -- who then saw fit to try and take it from me. It seems like every time things look up, some kind of darkness comes and drags me screaming back down. You say that I'm stronger, more focused, and you're right -- I'm determined to claw my way out of that darkness no matter what it takes. All that I've experienced has made me stronger -- but sometimes, I wish none of it had ever happened at all. Sometimes, Kenta, I wonder if all that's in my Tomorrow is more pain -- for me, and everyone I touch.' For a while, Kenta was silent. Then he clicked his tongue chastisingly. 'For all that you've learned, Steel Lion,' said Kenta, 'you've forgotten more. You wondered earlier how I knew what you were thinking. Let me show you, Steel Lion. Let me help you see the world as I see it. Close your eyes.' Curious, I did as Kenta instructed. A moment later, I could feel his fingers on my temples, his thumbs pressing lightly over my eyes. He whispered something in a language I couldn't understand, then drew his hands back. 'Open your eyes, Steel Lion,' said the Black Dragon, 'and look at the world.' Slowly, I drew my eyelids back -- and I gasped at what I saw. There were lights. Lights everywhere. Everything sparkled with millions of brilliant pinpricks, the city, the sun, even the air around me. I looked at the fence and marveled at the tiny shimmers as they danced along its lines. The world around me was alive with lights and colors, zipping and dancing around like a rainbow of fireflies in a hurricane. Awe filled my heart, and I turned to speak to Kenta. My jaw dropped. Kenta's tattoos, once just colored ink, now were jet black, so black they shined. Furthermore, they writhed and moved on his skin like living things, twisting about him like a great ebon dragon -- his namesake. Kenta himself shined with an inner light that cowed me, leaving me almost speechless. 'Kenta,' I said, in hushed tones, 'what? what is this?' 'This is the world in its truest form, Steel Lion,' replied Kenta. 'What you see now are spirits. The living, thriving spirits of every man, every plant, every object, every gust of wind. They are the vital energy of the universe, Steel Lion.' 'They're? they're beautiful. I don't know if I've ever seen something so beautiful. Why did you show this to me, Kenta?' 'To remind you, Steel Lion. You've been fighting the shadows with grim determination, but you've been fighting such darkness that you forgot how to see the light. And that's my lesson for you, Steel Lion. There is always light, there is always beauty. It's inside of everything, every. You must never forget that. You must never forget that there is always hope.' Without hesitation, I stepped forward and threw my arms around Kenta, hugging him like a brother. 'Thank you, Kenta,' I said, holding him in the embrace, 'I promise that I'll never forget again.' Kenta laughed, and hugged me fiercely in return. 'You'd best not, Rokku-kun,' he said, 'because enlightenment doesn't strike twice.' I immediately broke the hug, and held him out at arm's length. I stared at him in shock -- and then I burst out laughing. 'You know,' I said, when I finally got myself back under control, 'I think that's the first time you've ever actually called me 'Rokku'. You should do it more often, all my other friends do.' 'Really?' asked Kenta. I nodded. 'Well then,' he said, 'I guess I'm catching up on those changes after all.' *** Hours later, I descended the Tokyo Tower. For the longest time, I had simply sat, observing the world Kenta had given me a glimpse into. But now, his gift was fading away, and the world was returning to its more mundane form. It was somewhat disheartening, but I refused to let it get to me. I swore to myself I'd never forget what I'd seen, and to mourn not seeing it would be like forgetting it already. My mind caught up in a fading dream, I wasn't paying attention to where I was going, so when I walked into the wall, I found myself on my ass, rubbing a sore nose. Only, when I looked up, it wasn't a wall I'd walked into at all. It was a hulk of a man who looked like he could have been carved straight from the side of Mount Fuji, his hard jaw clenched and his features fixed in a scowl. 'Why don't you watch where you're going, pretty boy?' he said, practically spitting the last words at me. 'Why don't you watch where you're standing, you big ugly statue?' I retorted, meeting his gaze and locking it. Our staring match only lasted an instant, before the Stone Crab cracked up, and I immediately followed. 'Hida Tsuneo,' I said, as he helped me back to my feet, 'I missed you, you big lunk. What brings you to the Tokyo Tower? This can't be coincidence.' 'Nope,' said Tsuneo, cracking his thick neck, 'Yoshiko said we could find you here, man. She was expecting you back a heck of a lot earlier, I guess, so when we showed up she sent us out to get you.' 'We?' I raised an eyebrow in curiosity, 'who's with you?' Suddenly, I felt thick meaty arms wrap around me from behind, lifting me up in a ferocious bear hug. 'Rokku,' exclaimed Moto Yotogi, the Ki-Rin Khan, as he swung me around like a rag doll, 'you dog! We let you go off to America and you shack up with the Crane! How the hell have you been?' 'Juuust grrreaaat,' I croaked, before Yotogi finally decided to set me back on my feet. I staggered a moment before regaining my breath, then pounced on him, hugging him back. 'So,' I said, after letting go of Yotogi, 'Yoshiko-chan sent you down here to collect me, then? I do have my own car, you know.' 'Well, that's not exactly it,' said Tsuneo, scratching his head. 'Yeah,' said Yotogi, 'she also said something about how we're supposed to train you or something, teach you our secrets?' 'Yeah,' I replied, sighing, 'it's this crash training regimen she's got me on before my World Title match. Actually, it's been going great. But it's exhausting as hell.' 'That's what we were thinking,' said Yotogi, 'so we talked things over on the way down.' 'And?' I asked. 'Well,' said Tsuneo, 'we figured, sure, we could go about things the same way as everyone else has, reveal our hidden powers so to speak -- but what the hell would be the fun in that, right? And since really, our tricks are just a lot of strength and a lot of tenacity we came up with a much better way to show you how we do what we do.' Realization suddenly dawned on me. 'You mean?' 'Hell yeah!' exclaimed Yotogi, 'The Boys of Thunder are back, and every sake house, kareoke bar, and dance club in Tokyo will quake with terror!' 'So,' I said with a smirk, 'party time?' 'Oh yeah,' replied Tsuneo, 'party time. You ready for this, Lion?' My smirk stretched into a wide grin as I produced my sunglasses from my pocket, snapped them open, and slowly slipped them on. 'Gentlemen,' I said, 'let the rocking? Commence!' Sometimes, it's just time stop taking everything so seriously, cut loose and have a little fun. Even if it does turn out to be at the expense of Downtown Tokyo. And that, my friends, is the final lesson.
