Sequitur sexta pars .:: ::: ::. “Let not thy fear harm thee, for any power that he may have shall not prevent thy going down this crag...” The snarling face loomed from the rock face beside the spiral staircase, leading ever downwards. “Pap” Sat’n! Pap? Sat’n! Allep?!” The voice was shrill and screeching, sounding as though if it had the ability to physically do so, it would dive through her eardrums and split the flesh. She would have clapped her hands over her ears, only she regarded that as fearful, and she was trying her hardest not to frustrate the Italian Gentleman with her fear. Instead, she simply froze. Leering orange eyes fixed on hers, and the shrieking little beast tittered and cackled, cavorting about with clanks of its leg irons on the little rock it was chained to. The Italian Gentleman sighed, but she got the impression that it was merely a noise; no breath came from those parched lips. “Let him not impede you. A simple imp, a noise-maker, really. You have no reason to fear his screeching, for he cannot impede your forward progress.” She nodded, refusing as yet to speak. There were no words right now, no words to express how strange, stranger this predicament was becoming every moment. If she closed her eyes, would it go away? Then, a horror seized her. It would be far worse to close her eyes and tempt fate in this place than it would be to simply endure whatever happened. She stepped past the squealing little bastard, and claw-like fingers stretched to reach her. She winced away, but a bolt of lightning split the nearby sky, shocking the little claws back into place. The smell of singed flesh curled upwards with the inky smoke. “Pardon him,” came a familiar voice, “It seems even thousands of years won’t teach the little boor to behave when there are guests about.” She turned, surprised, to see the handsome face of Minos, smiling over her left shoulder. He gestured them forward with his fingertips. “Shoo! Shoo! Tourist hours are over!” The Italian Gentleman reached a well-formed hand forward and gently took hers, pulling her along. The long fingers reminded her of times past, of once having the reassurance of someone’s hand to clutch? but she had gone off on her own purposefully, and there was no sense lamenting it now. WHAM! The great stone door slammed forcefully behind the both of them, and the gust whooshed forward from it, nearly knocking her down the stairs, were it not for the reassuring strength of her guide. And that was when her eyes took in the next sight. Baleful howls split into harmonies, mixed into cacophonies, twisting round one another in a deafening racket. Like so many other instances on this journey, she found herself completely unable to concentrate, unable to even know whether she was in control of her own mind, or buried under this unendurable mire, commiserating, no, suffering with these wretches. Clang! Her eyes were suddenly yanked from their downward, fixed contemplation and towards the sight to her left: a man, features disfigured and mottled with grime, chased a weight down a hill. Once he stopped its rolling, he forced a stooped shoulder underneath it, and began pushing it back up the hill. She wanted to cry out to him, as she saw the man opposite him, pushing another weight up towards the same point, that it was all useless, that they would clash up at the top, and lose all their work, but? Clang! Again, the weights tumbled down to the bottom of the hill, grotesque little plays upon humans chasing them down to the bottom of the hill. Clang! Clang! Clang! Over and over again? everywhere that she could see. Stooped little beings shoving weights up hills, clashing with another figure, and chasing their weight back down to the bottom. And below the anguished howling of workers losing their work, which was now becoming only background noise, there were voices, tiny pervasive whispers which only seemed to further agonize these damned. “Why keepest”... Why keepest?... Why keepest?...” “Why squanderest thou”... Why squanderest thou?... Why squanderest thou?” She blinked, unable to grasp what was happening, looking up to the Gentleman for his assistance. “I” I don’t understand,” she called, as loudly as she could muster over all this noise. He turned to her, gleaming eyes raking over her from underneath his wide-brimmed hat. And with the patience of a father speaking to a child, he began to explain. “In life, these two kinds were opposite to one another” one held what he had, unwilling to share with another. The other wasted constantly, never prudently saving a penny, resorting to begging and sucking the resources of his relatives.” “Avarice and prodigality.” He sniffed. It seemed to be a noise of slight schadenfreude, sadistic amusement, perhaps. “You remember your catechisms well. T’is a shame that won’t do you much good.” A flash of blinding white light. “and then, black nothingness, as though she was drifting along on her own, no direction, no guide. A terrifying black nothingness, a silence that was strangling even the weak mews that were screams dying in her throat before they could be born. A voice split all that, a smooth, deep voice that vibrated through her entire being. “Open your eyes, Lia” are you too frightened to see the truth?” It was a familiar voice, and yet she could not place it entirely. Her eyelids were forced open. In front of her sprawled a manor house, from long ago memory. Without being inside, she could hear the sounds from afar, a little girl laughing, a man and a woman laughing with her? a conversation about giving money to the families camped on the estate’s outer grounds, and a decision not to. A thought from her husband that it would increase their dependence, rather than teaching them a way to earn sustenance for themselves. Her husband had always been pragmatic? Drifting as it was, her mind attempted to focus on her husband, the tall, handsome figure at the hearth-stone, pulling her chin up to look down into her eyes, and reassure her that everything would be alright while he was gone to the city-center. She couldn’t remember his name? It was right on the tip of her tongue, but she couldn’t pull it from her memory. The voice bounded through her thoughts, interrupting. “All that wealth, Lia” and you couldn’t afford to spare one penny on those people. Do you know how many of them starved that winter because they were too poor to buy bread? Do you know how many of their children died?” She tried to open her mouth to protest, but found that she was unable to do so. “And now” even more a sad and pathetic state.” An image of herself wandering the streets of Cleveland, practically wearing rags, flashed into the picture frame in front of her. She stumbled towards the entrance to the sewer conduit that lead to her ex-flatmate Riven’s haven. Riven, of course, was dead now. Sorrow overcame her, remembering how it was for her sake that Riven died, or at the very least, that he died because he’d been living with her, and The Den wanted her so badly that they would stop at nothing to kill everyone related to her. “Nevermind feeling sad about him. What you should feel sad about is yourself” how once, you had it all, and then you had nothing because you refused to hold on to it. Wasteful, wasteful Lia, how could you ever waste so much with so little thought to those who would have killed or gone hungry to have what you had? So much blood spilt, so much time wasted, so much money put to use for nothing.” Again, she tried to protest, tried to tell him that no, that wasn’t the case, she’d been just as lost as anyone who could have used her help, but it was all for naught. And the voice came again, fraught with scorn. “Save your pathetic apologies. Your contrition will come in your screams.” Flash? And so it was again that the agony of waking from a dream-state gripped her, and she found herself stumbling forward across the rocky, muddy ground, dirtying her boots with sludge. The Italian Gentleman caught her by the collar’barely’before she face-planted gracelessly into the dirt. She wanted to thank him, but her throat was thick with guilty sobs, guilt over things past that she could not possibly atone for. Emotionlessly, he pulled her back to her feet, a hand wrapping her waist to hold her there in case she should become weak again. “Please” take me somewhere else.” Her voice was shaking, breaking down into weakness. And his firm hand gripped her back, and pulled her along bruisingly brutal. “My pleasure.” She squinted her eyes shut, trying her best not to hear the assorted clanks, wails, and devilish whispers that echoed all around. The speed didn’t matter, she just stumbled along at the behest of the Italian Gentleman. And before she knew it, they stood at the edge of a massive lake which she hadn’t even seen upon first entering this area. “The lovely River Styx, Signora,” he said, inclining his head to her politely when he spoke it. She opened her mouth for a moment, but spoke nothing’the sight was enough. Water bubbled forth from a spring high on a cliff, trickling down the rock face into the pool below, steam rising from its heat. The river below boiled and seethed, bubbles surfacing to the top and popping, emitting a fetid stench into the surrounding air. She coughed, gagging on it. And then her eyes cast downward, and she was shocked at the sight’a writhing mass of humanity. Screams of rage echoed dimly, as though coming from a place buried deep underground, and these pathetic souls writhing in the lake’s mire struck at one another. But not just with hands? with feet, with head, they tore at one another with their teeth, howling in agony and anger. Immediately, she knew where she was. And she knew, also, that the guilt this time would be even stronger. “Those who were consumed by rage must always fight on the shore of this lake. And below them, those who languished in sullen anger” it is their everlasting sighs that cause the bubbles to rise to the surface of this lake and burst.” Horror seized her stomach. “People” Buried beneath this?” He nodded, and her stomach heaved, threatening to release its contents? save that she had not fed in quite a long time. Flash. Again, she was whipped away into that nothingness where she could feel nothing but guilt. Deep, thick laughter. “Oh only think of this one, my dear. Wrath. You were quite accomplished at that one, were you not?” A shiver seized her body, images flashing in the frame. Corvus. Wherever he was now, she didn’t care. She didn’t care what happened to him, whether he was alive or dead, or what self-righteous journey he was taking this week. But she remembered now, remembered the vile and all-encompassing hatred. The revenge that she’d wanted. She stood on an empty Cleveland street, her head throbbing from where Corvus had tried to make an example out of her by embarrassing her, no, trying to end her career in front of an arena full of WMW fans. Wasn’t it acceptable to be angry at him for that? To want revenge? No, if you listened to what the virtuous said, you were to take your abuse. You were to assume it was penance for some past wrong, or that it would make you stronger. Well fuck that? it wouldn’t have made her stronger. It made her angrier. “Oh,” said the voice, rife with amusement, “Wouldn’t you like us to all assume that’s the way it goes” That you were justified to be angry with him? You went out of your way to stab him in the back? what excuse can you have for that, other than that you were guided by your extreme anger, not your reason?” And as always, she could not speak. “There were others, though. Don’t you remember your anger at those members of The Den who were trying to kill you” Your anger at Dawn ad usurping your position in WMW, and keeping you from being where you wanted to be at the top? Don’t you remember your anger at Virgil, when he left you? Your anger at Dante for not coming himself to find you? Even your anger at yourself for not remembering what you thought you should have remembered?” The laughter twisted into a sickening sadistic glee. It dripped from the walls, smothering her in her nothingness. “Your life has been filled with wrath, anger” You know it to be true, and whether you want to deny it or not, you cannot? You know it to be true. And it will only get worse, my dear.” Release. .:: ::: ::. “We to the foot of that high tower had come, our eyes went upward to the summit of it?” The unreadable Italian Gentleman had dried her tears, and restored her to sentience. She walked alongside him now, her hands clasped demurely together. Silence reigned between the two of them, as it had tended to do since the very beginning. She felt she could not speak in the presence of someone so obviously greater than herself. Her eyes glanced across the banks to a high stone tower. Even her good eyesight could only barely make out how ornate the carving on it was. As she watched, a burst of flame sprung from the top of the tower. She could almost feel the heat from it on her skin, and she flinched, the natural Red Fear bubbling forth. Far off down the horizon, another flame answered the first. Her curiosity piqued, she swallowed the R’tschreck, and turned to the Gentleman. “What is that?” “Hm?” She nodded towards the tower. “Ah,” he said, pausing for a moment, “A form of communication. As to what end” you may see for yourself.” He nodded towards the vile river, and she saw then a small boat cutting its way easily through the currents. A voice rang out as it approached the shore. “Fallen soul” preparest thou for thy journey!” The Italian Gentleman shook his head. “Nay, Phlegyas. We shall only be with you to pass the river.” The stoop-backed figure in the boat glared, showing his ragged teeth. “Then get on, and let us get it over with.” She hesitated, but her guide pulled her onto the boat. The stench of the river was nearly overwhelming, but the boat soon pulled away from the shore’leaving no opportunity to bolt. The Styx seemed to suck the boat out into its middle, and towards something that seemed like it must have been a whirlpool, with a pull that strong? but the sure-rowing of Phlegyas the oarsman guided them quickly towards the opposite shore, and the giant citadel she could see now looming. And yet, it seemed as if nothing could be simple. From the middle of the river, the mire rose, and a figure was buried underneath it. “Lia Vlaszy!” She turned her head in shock towards the figure, surprised to hear her own name called. And when she realized who it was? “Lucien Forlani” Fancy meeting you here, seeing you buried in this mire. How appropriate.” Her surroundings forgotten, a grin twisted across her face, her fangs distended. Forlani’s features were barely readable under all the dirt that wrapped round him like a shroud. “It is your place too” How long have you remembered me for what I did to dear old Vaclav?” A growl ripped forth from her chest. “What I wouldn’t give to see you soused in your own shit, Forlani.” The prow of the boat mowed over him, pushing him back down into the mire, and there were no more words for him to pass, only a protesting mewling, well-befitting the wretch he was. The Italian Gentleman sighed. “Have you learned nothing in all this time?” She fell silent, struck by his accusation. “Before you,” he continued, “The city of Dis. Perhaps you may already hear the wailing from within. Now we reach nether-Hell.” “Your entrance, fools,” Phlegyas growled. She was all-too-happy to get off of the boat and onto the shore as the Italian Gentleman hung back, speaking a few words to the boatswain in order to assure him of their innocent intentions’she could hear Phlegyas lamenting loudly of the punishment that he would receive for conducting them on their journey. And suddenly, there was a whoosh of wings from high above, somewhere on the citadel walls. Before she could discern what sort of being now approached, her arm was seized in an iron-tight grasp. Gasping in pain, her head whipped to the side. A pair of glowing white eyes bore into hers’she would have struggled away, only it would have meant much more pain to do so, and she could hardly stand more than she was currently in. The being who held her, aside from the gleam of his eyes, wore dark iron half-plate, engraved with symbols she could not begin to make sense of. Long black hair trailed down his back, and a pair of great black feathered wings hovered over the both of them, casting shadow on her cheeks. “Greetings, wayward traveler,” hissed a voice, coming along with a grin. “Let me go!” She struggled, trying to wrench her arm free, and began to wonder if getting free would just mean amputating her arm from below the elbow, so tight was his grasp on her. He wrenched her closer, right up against his chest. “What sort of manners are those” You snap at me like a rabid dog when it is you who intrude on our territory. You lost all right to complain for your freedom when you entered those gates. Abandon all hope. Remember? Remember?” She winced, but could no longer squirm. She inclined her head with a slight nod, and the being smiled again. “My charm has claimed another victim, I see” were all the women who entered this place so quiet, I’m afraid I’d want to keep them all.” He pushed her away from him a bit, looking her over critically. “Hmm” and perhaps you would be useful for bearing my spawn as well? yes, yes, I think I shall speak to someone about keeping you. Surely your punishment can’t be that important.” “Azrael!” The roar from behind them surprised them both. He loosed his grip ever so slightly, and turned to look at the Italian Gentleman. Still, he appeared impassive’but the heaving of his breath showed his anger. “Leave her be.” The command was stony. Yet, in the face of Signor’s great anger, the being now named as Azrael? only laughed. “And what will you do, impotent little mage” Look at her,” Azrael said, nodding in her direction, the iron-grip tightening once again, “She is dead. What reason should she have to escape her punishment this long?” The Italian Gentleman trod closer, his boots crunching along the gravel banks that made up the outer entrance of Dis. “She is not here to be punished. It is not her time. She is a traveler, and will continue her journey unimpeded.” Azrael shoved her aside, stepping towards Signor, a smirk of bravado covering his fine, handsome, terrifying features. “And what if I choose not to let her do so?” A sound of metal unsheathing echoed. “Then I will take matters into my own hand.” The smirk faded off of Azrael’s face, fading into a derisive sneer. “And you are willing to lay down your life for her?” “I am.” Azrael’s hands reached with sureness behind him as he unsheathed the large sword on his back, so heavy that it had to be held in both hands. “So be it then.” .:: ::: ::. “Here await me, and thy weary spirit comfort and nourish with a better hope; for in this nether world I will not leave thee.” .:: ::: ::.
