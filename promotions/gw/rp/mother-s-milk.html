Date: #1 MOTM She hadn't changed a bit. Anthony James rested his sore body in the supple leather seats of his rented Cadillac Escalade EXT, glad to finally have a week away from the ring. But, despite being surrounded int he luxury and comfort of his vehicle it came as no surprise to him that he was completely uncomfortable. It had been so long, but he watched her as she moved down the street. Determined spirit. A stride wide and posture held perfectly upright by pride and work ethic. She looked exactly the same as the last time he'd seen her. How long had it been? It was an intriguing question. The weight of the guilt that already sat firmly on Anthony's shoulders grew heavier realizing he didn't really know the answer. She manuevered gracefully through the Chicago street, doding traffic, both car and foot, holding a set of paper grocery bags covered and reinforced with the plastic variety on the outside clutched in her arms. The bright yellow floral pattern on her loose and flowing black sun dress waved subtlely in the chicago wind... Her long and once dark hair, that had now gone from dark to salt and pepper, followed suit. Her light chocolate complexion glowed in the hot sun as she perspired just a bit as she continued her journey. Anthony didn't even notice the tiny smiled that curled his lips upward. He now knew the answer to how long it had been. Too long. But it was ok. He worried about her, but it seemed all the information he'd gleaned from others in the family was true. Mom looked good. As he continued watch her move down the street he watched her and remembered. Remembered how he'd always thought she was the strongest woman he'd ever met. Remembered that everything he'd ever learned about not quiting and continuing to fight when all seemed lost he'd learned from her. What else could he have learned. Well...actually that question was easy. He could have learned to give in...to give it up. That's hardly a lesson that his father would have let come out of his mouth. It wasn't even something he would have wanted to believe himself. Unfortunately reality is a bitch and she'll say what you won't. Dad had been a good man. Unfortunately it was a bad world and he wasnt' equipped to hang in it. Raising a family in a world that is happy to look you in the face and tell you they're rooting for you then waits for you to turn your back to tell you won't ever be or accomplish shit isn't just hard...it's painful. He needed something to kill the pain. Like it did for most the heroin fit the bill for him. It killed the pain. To bad it did the same to him in the process. Anthony had been a little to young at the time to understand what all the yelling lat in the night, just after his father had finally wandered in from whatever hole he'd found to crawl into, was all about. His brother Leon did, but he'd been to kind to share it with Anthony when ever he'd crawled into bed next to him when the fighting got to be too much for him to handle. He just told him everything was going to be ok and to go to sleep. Anthony always took his big brother at his word and proceeded to drift of. Know what he knew now about life Anthony knew his mother probably wanted to do the same. Just drift off somewhere never to return. Never to return to the nights of wondering about how the bills were going to get paid with the portion of a dwindled paycheck that had never been that big to begin with. Never to think about what she was going to tell her two sons about what was happening to their father. To never have to watch the poison he volutarily shot into into himself eat away the man she loved. To escape the reality that she had to hold off on telling him about the child that was growing inside as they argued because she didn't know what his reaction would be. Nope... No amount of Calgon in the world can take you away from some shit like that. But, then again, she never he doubted she ever asked to be taken away. There are some people who will always be firmly planted in reality and refuse to meet it any other way, but head on. That was Anthony's mother. That was Annabelle Elizabeth James. No nonsense. Though, if you caught her at the wrong/right time she'd say 'No Bullshit'. Mother of two boys, pregnant with a daughter, dealing with a father that had cracked under the pressure and basicly gone awol she never folded. She had the hand she was dealt and that was what she was going to play with. No money. she got a job in a department store behind the cosmetics counter, pregnant and all. Father of your kids didnt' know how to act, coming late...high... As much as it tears you up inside you put his shit out in the hallway and get the super to change the locks. He can bang on the door to the cops come to get rid of him. In the meantime you can curl up in your bed in the fetal position and try not to let your crying wake up your children. She stayed strong the way she always had. Even on the day of her husbands funeral...just a few days after he'd been found OD'd in an abandoned building. She was still hung in. For her kids sake and for her own. At times it looked like there was nothing that could really phase her. Appearances can be decieving. Just like the unsinkable could be sunk the unshakeable can be shaken. Anthony and Annabelle found that out the hard way the day Leon died. It had been different when Anthony's father died. To the best of his recollection it had taken months before he finally realized when people say they're gone they meant dead. And dead meant forever. There no illusions or misunderstandings the next trip through the valley of the shadow of death. Anthony hadn't feared evil. He was a little to numb to fear much when Medical Examiner's office called looking for his mother...wanting her to come down and identify her son's body. Anthony had never thanked God for much, but he thanked God that day that she hadn't been home...yet. They asked for her work number, but he simply hung up on them. He couldn't let her get the news at work. He couldn't not be there. But he wasn't sure he could handle telling her on his own. As far as he'd been concerned all that preaching the pastor of his church did on Sundays wasnt' much more than hot air, but his mother didn't think that. That's why Train had called him. No matter what he thought of the pastor's message he could honestly say he was a good man. He'd come over less than an hour after Anthony's frantic message filtered down from the secretary of the church to him. It wasnt' long after the pastor arrived, doing his best to speak Anthony about the love of God and how it would get him and his family through this, the message itself being ignored even more than usual while a simultaneously numbed and Terrified 14 year old could only think about what this was going to do to his mother. It was long before the key hit the lock... the message hit her ears... her tears hit the floor...and her body followed suit. In the years subsequent years Train tried not to think about it, but in his weak moments Anthony emerged and wondered wheter it had been worse for her because she'd felt it was coming. His mother wasn't a stupid woman. She knew what her son was doing. Coming in all hours of the night sporting jewelry and clothes way out his income bracket...which was zero as he didnt' have a job. At least not one that was acknowledged as an actual profession. He was 18... technically a man who could make his own decisions...but not in her house where she payed the bills. That's why she'd kicked him out about 4 months earlier. He was hustling...his choice of outfits always displaying the same shade of green that she'd noticed quite a few 'young men' in the neighborhood had taken to themselves. She figured out what he was doing. They argued constantly about the dead end street her son was traveling down. He didnt' see it that way and pushed the pedal to the floor. Train didn't have to figure anything out though. He knew from the jump Leon was running with the Southside Clique. Knew he'd been making money off of weed and coke. Knew he'd graduated from standing on the corner to Lt. on the streets. Anthony knew his brother was not to be fucked with. It was just another reason Anthony wanted to be just like him. But Leon would never allow that to happen. Every time Anthony would asked to be put down on the set the only response he got was a smack up side the head and hearty 'stay in school'. Obviously his brother had known the dangers and resigned himself to them. Probably the reason their mother could never get through. That's why she put him out. She knew how much Anthony looked up to Leon...and their little sister adored him too...her room becoming a little more crowded every week as Leon brought a new stuffed animal to join the never ending tea party. Train also did whatever he needed to not to think about the day of the funeral, but Anthony, who sat just below the surface, was a pest. He never forgot. He'd always seen his mother walk tall. It was hard seeing members of the church having to carry the grief stricken woman out after the services. It scared him, because the who time it went on he hadn't seen her stop crying. Not once. And not the little sniffles crying, but tears streaming down her with the constant pounding of a river that had overflowed it's levy. He'd never seen his mother inconsolable before. It was excruciating to watch. It was even more painful to wonder about her after. The days on end where she'd barely eat...barely venture from the bedroom...hide under the covers from the sun. The church rallied around her, but she didn't do much to acknowledge to him. Eventually a social worker came by and determined she wasn't able to work and made sure she was placed on SSI and got the family a few food stamps more than they'd had before. Anthony had to fight the urge to be angry at her. For not being able to take care of herself, let alone him and his little sister. He struggled not to hold it against her that he had to watch her crumble just when he needed her the most. Then again, there hadn't been much time for all that introspective, sentimental bullshit anyway. Fuck welfare, food stamps and SSI...that shit wasn't paying the bills like it should and she couldnt' do anything about it. The collectors were calling and Anthony tried to explain the situation, but they didn't give a damn. They wanted their money. Anthony had been told his whole life with a lot of hard work and studying he could go to college get a good job and bring the family up with him. He'd followed that theory. He was nearlly a straight A student his first quarter in high school. But all that delayed gratification shit had gone out the window. He didn't have time for it. His family didn't have time for it. His mother, without the benefit of Oprah and Dr. Phil to let her know she was clinically depressed and need to get medical attention for it, could barely stand on her own two feet. The landlord and the grocerry store wasn't going to wait for him to 'make it' the right way. He only had one choice to provide for the people who needed it. He got down on the set. There was love for him because of Leon who everyone respected. They started him off bigger thany likely would have anyone one else and the money rolled in. The apartment building was hardly a paradise so it didn't take much to pay off the rent to the end of the lease. The light, water and gas was chump change. He made sure it was all paid out of his pocket every month. He made sure food was in the fridge and had dinner on the table for his sister everynight. He always took his mother a plate...even helped her eat on occassion. In her state it wasnt' much of a mystery why she couldn't do the math and figure out all the little pitences from all the social agencies put together wasn't enough for them to really live on. That's why she never suspected what Train was doing...never saw him walking in Leon's footsteps. It's why she could never see her child carrying the weight of the world like a grown man. That is until she finally started to get better. It was funny how he'd gone from Anthony to Tony. From the little kid that wanted to provide for his family to the kid who just loved living 'The Life'. 14 years old with money and women...not girls... women. School and grades? Fuck 'em. Somebody stepped out of line with him? No need to be reasonable or diplomatic. He called up his boys and they stomped that fool. No questions asked. No real reason needed. He was quickly becoming the man. Something that makes it hard not to loose your perspective when you're still a child. You couldn't tell him that though. Not even the guilt got to him for too long. It came in waves, nearlly knocking him over, but evaporating as quickly as it came. And he had every reason to be guilty. As much as he prayed his mother would recover, deep in his heart...he hoped she wouldn't. Because he already knew she'd put an end to it. Unfortunately no one can avoid the enevitable and Tony was no different than anyone else. It had to come to a head sooner or later. Train was hoping for later. About 5 months after he'd buried his brother...whether it was sooner or later is a matter of opinion...but it did come. ===== ===== ===== ===== ===== May 3, 1990 The faux marble lined lobby of the Chicago Public Works building was filled with the squeak of rubber across tile as the long line snaked it's way through the maze of red velvet ropes on the journey towards the wall lined with teller windows. Most people stared off into space and anything else they could think of to take their minds off the tedium as they wait for the city's bureaucracy to dig further into their pockets. Tony used the time to scan over his list of clientele, making his way through the numbers stored in his beeper. However, he only did it with one eye. The other was firmly focused on the woman sitting behind the glass partician just to the left of the one right in front of him. The thick curls of her dark brown hair served to accentuate her the caramel complexion of her flawless latina skin. And those breast, the only real part of her body he could set, accentuated every damn thing. Tony figured she was about 19 or 20, but oh well. His 14 year old body had sprouted a few inches in a short time and, though it had been rough for awhile, the goatee was coming in real nice now. Who could resist that. Train made himself look very noble as he reached the front of the line and let the little, old lady behind him go first, but there was no real nobility there. Caramel skin hadn't finished with the guy in front of her at the time and he had to hollar. Finally she finished up. Tony stroked his barely there goatee and approached all the confidence of a Mack. 'What's up Mommi?' Train asked with a sly smile that went well with his young, but handsome features. 'Not too much. How can I help you today?' she said in a flat tone that attempted to rebuff what she knew was coming from the young man who'd swaggered up to her station. Never realizing that her soft tone, tinged with her latin accent would only serve to entice him further. 'You could let a brotha have them digits first of all.' 'haha... Mmhmmm... I'm tell number 237. Anything else? 'Ahhh, that's cold. C'mon now...you just gonna do me like that? I'm just trying to let you know how fine you are.' 'Well thank you. Now do you have a bill you need to pay or something? Because otherwise people are waiting behind you.' 'Aight, aight,' Train said, still grining, trying to slow down the tide of 'no's' that were trying to wash him down stream. 'Here you go.' he said, handing her the tear away part of the utility bill that stated the amount that needed to be paid and a hundred dollar bill to pay it with. 'Thank you.' She said, unable to hide her amusement. 'No problem. Now how about those digits...to your phone...at your house.' She grinned a little with her eyes firmly locked on the computer screen as she punched up the account to note the payment. 'Now why would I give you those?' 'Cuz if you don't I can't call you and find out when we can go out and kick it.' 'Uhuh...' say she begun, finally setting her eyes back to him. 'And where are we gonna go kick it? Chuck E. Cheese?' 'hahaha.... Oh, you got some jokes, huh? Tell you what... I was thinking more like Tony Roma's or something, but hey... You want Chuck E. Cheese, that aint' no problem girl. I like pizza, plus...' Train stopped midsentence to reach into his pocket, quickly returning with a wad of bills bound with a rubber band. he quickly slides it off and fans it out to make certain she understood there was more than enough to go any place they might choose. 'I can get us plenty of tokens.' Tony says. 'Nice allowance.' 'Allowance my ass. I been cutting lawns for all this.' 'Cutting lawns? Uhuh...where?' '97th Street.' 'hahaha... Sounds more like you been cutting concrete.' 'Man's gotta do what he gotta do. You know what I'm saying?' 'If you wanna go out, be serious... Where'd you get all that?' 'You ask a lot of questions girl.' 'How I'ma just give you my number if I don't know where you got all that money from?' 'Let me ask you something... When we're out in the club or wherever kicking it, is it do you think it's gonna matter where it came from?' 'Maybe not...' 'Really, I do.' said another voice that hadn't been envolved conversation before. The girl's head quicly cocked to the side to peer over Tony's shoulder, searching for the addition. Anthony didn't need to turn around and, in fact couldn't, for a moment. He had to take a deep swallow to wash heart out of his throat and put it back where it belonged, though beating far faster than usual... He knew his mother's voice when he heard it. He didn't even need to see her face to know she was upset. He deftly folded rolled the stack of cash back into a compact state and slid back into his pocket as if there were nothing out of the ordinary about a 14 year old carrying around what was about $900 dollars in cash. As if it happened all the time. His hope was if he didn't jerk as if it were some big deal he needed to hide he might just be able to talk his way out of it. It was possible...though he recognized that lie the second he told it to himself. He turned on is heels locking eyes with the woman who raised him to do anything, but what he'd been doing to get what he had. 'Boy...where did you get that money?' she asked sternly, fighting the urge to rail at the only son she had left. 'What you doin' up Momma?' he asked, ignoring her question as if she might forget it had ever been asked if he just blew past it. 'The Doctor said you still needed to be resting.' 'I'm grown, so you don't need to be worried about where I go and what I'm doing.' She responded in a matter of fact tone. 'I asked you a question. Where did you get that money?' 'What you talking about? I just went and cashed the social security and all that like I always do Momma. I'm just making sure the bills get paid like I always do. Why you trippin'?' 'You got all that left over even after you paid the rent?' 'Huh? Oh...naw... I ain't been to pay the rent yet. We got to the 5th. I just wanted to get the lights and water and all that taken care of. I was headed to the grocery store after this.' 'Really?' 'Yeah.' 'Mmhmmm... That's funny because... I cashed the checks this morning. And when I went to the landlord to pay the rent he told me rent was paid to the end of the year.' Damn. 'Whoa, hold up...' 'No, you hold up!' she yelled, now drawing the attention of the small group of people who hadn't already turned their attention to this family drama. 'Stop lying to me Anthony.' 'Whoa... keep yo' voice down, aight?!' he exlaimed in a feverish whisper. 'Let's talk about this...' Tony begun before stopping abruptly, the heat of someone's glare breaking his concentration on his attempts at damage control. Tony turns to his face towards the teller window to his right and the middle aged man standing in front of it. He'd obviously found this conversation that was totally unrelated to his life quite facinating. 'What the hell you lookin' at?' Tony asked him bluntly. The question quickly snapped the man back into the reality that he's better off paying attention to his own life, quickly turning him away from the conflict that had captivated him. The threatening rebuff of an on looker was nothing new for Tony. Unfortunately, he didn't realize the ease with which he did changed his mother's focus. Instead of Anthony she saw Leon. Tony had made himself feel slightly less embarrased by putting the man in his place, but blew any chance he had of fooling her...which was slim to begin with. He'd saved face, but undone himself. The second her young son opened his mouth to the man across from them so boldly, with so little respect she knew. She could see the foot prints of her oldest child and the oldest child she had left standing firmly inside them. Her son was a criminal. Annabelle felt as if her heart might beat through her chest, its palpitations driven by a mixture of abject terror and unadulterated rage. How could he do this to her? She'd already lost a son to the streets so he does what? Goes right onto the same streets into the same mess that killed her first baby?! What the hell could he be thinking? What the hell difference did it make what he was thinking? He knew better. She raised him better than that! Of course... Leon knew better too. What happened to Leon was his father's fault. Creating the need for her to spend all that time at work to make ends meet. No one to watch over him and show him how to be a man. Now... as much as it pained her to blame her lost child, he barred half the weight of responsibility with what was happening to her other son right along with his father. A part her understood what was happening. She knew Anthony was trying to look out for the family. Trying to be a man...it's just he didn't understand the definition. But definition or no...she KNEW he knew better! It was too much... All of those thoughts, raced through her mind in a split second, all stumbling over the top of eachother... it was too much....... Tony added in a extra couple of seconds of cold stare at the stranger to make sure he knew to keep his focus elsewhere. He turn his focus back to where it had been before. He never even saw it coming. The sound of the slap echoed through the corridors of the building as she brought her hand across his face, her flesh and bone solidly connecting with the flesh and bone she'd ushered into the world. Her flesh and blood didn't know how to react. His body hurtled back into the shatter proof glass particia, startling the woman who sat behind it out of her seat, sending her in the direction of security. Anthony felt the rest coming. He did the only thing he could, bring his arms up and cover himself up. She did the only thing she could think to do as well. She continued to slap away, bring her hands and arms down, time, after time, after time, after time.... Tears rolled down her face as she groaned with a mixture of every emotion a human being is capable. For his part Anthony tried to tell her to stop...to give him a chance to explain... It meant nothing. She continued to pound away at him. Finally there was a reprieve of sorts. Two building security guards, donning the tan and brown uniforms of county sheriffs charged in, grabbing hold of both of them. None of the blows had really hurt. He'd grown taller and heavier than her nearlly a year ago. But still...inside his head, it felt as if she'd knocked him into the middle of next week. Physically the blows had no consequence. Mentally they all felt like shots from a sledgehammer. He felt stupid for being surprised. Maybe it was his arrogance, but, somehow, he'd never seen this coming. Then again...neither had she. And she continued to rage against everything around her, including the officer holding her. It took everything he had to hold her. But eventually his interference caused her to turn on him, tornado of flailing arms, raining down on him like a storm. The guard handn't meant to knock her down. He simply wanted to turn her around in order to restrain her better. But when he spun her, the flailing woman lost her balanced and fell. Everyone saw it. Every set of eyes that witnessed it determined instantly it was an accident. All, but one. Tony hadn't been struggling against the other gaurd, so he wasn't really holding that tight, preparing to jump in on his partner's behalf if he needed it. His needed it the second Tony jerked away from him. The guard leaned down to try and grab hold of Annabelle. It was a clean shot. Smooth. Athletic. Violent. Train leaped forward on his left leg, dragging the right behind him. The second he planted the left he brough the right. If it had been a field goal try he'd have definitely gotten 3 points. Tony's Timberlands made contact at the sweet spot, right on the point of the jaw. He heard the crack...felt the bone give some... He was unconscious before he hit the ground. Then Tony was slammed to the floor and cuffed before he could even try to run. ===== ===== ===== ===== ===== Train quickly came back to the future, that slam to the floor jarring him even now. There wasn't much about that day he cared to remember. He definitely rememberd how lucky he got. That second guard layed an ass whuppin' on him while he was on the ground cuffed. The witness weren't much help, but as they say a picture is worth a thousand words. Which means moving pictures must be worth a million. Even with a wet behind the ears public defender Tony got off on the basis of the security camera footage, the case thrown out for excessive force during the arrest. He also remembered the look on his mother's face as they dragged him off. He called her name... She couldn't even look at him. He'd only spent a few weeks in juvey on remand before the trial and finally getting off. And he wasn't terribly surprised when she hadn't shown up at the trial. She hadn't even shown up at the jail to talk to him. Wouldn't accept his phone calls. It was also no surprise he found his things sitting in the hallway in front of the front door when he got back to his apartment. Fact is he'd been expecting that. He didn't bother knocking. He and his boys just grabbed it and went on. They never spoke another word between them. Any news he got came by way of his sister with whom he kept in constant contact. It didn't matter. He didn't need to talk to her to watch out for his family. He knew his mother likely hated it, but she was never allowed to spend another dime in rent or a dollar on utilities. They were always paid for. Joanne, Train's siser, told him she'd actually tried to complain to the city and landlord about it, but to no avail. Eventually it did some good. She saved her money and moved herself and Joanne into a townhouse about 35 minutes from where he grew up. It made him happy to hear. It was a smart investment. His little sis finally had decent schools to go to and more jobs so she could provide. It was a good thing too. Eventually he had to go down and it happened just about the time they moved. It was half bullshit. Some stupid possession charge. They were on a 'trying kids as adults' kick to show the constituants the city was tough on crime, but Train barely had half an ounce of weed on him so they could justify it. He spent 8 months in juvey for possession with intent to distribute. But everything happens for a reason. If not for that trip to the kiddie jail he never would have met Isaiah. He never would have learned to wrestle. And he wouldn't be sitting across the street from the house today, looking at how far she'd come. She didn't need his help. The problem was that he needed her's. Master of the Mat. It could make or break a career. Train had come a long way in a short time. he glanced over to the Platinum Championship belt as it sat nestled into the leather of the passenger seat. He'd brought the belt to a million different places, but this was the most important. It was his proof. Something solid he could hand to her. To show her that her son wasn't what she thought. He wasn't unworthy of her love any more. He could be trusted to do the right thing now. Of course she might just think he hasn't changed a bit. Now instead of a violent man selling poison for a living he was a violent man beating people up for a living. Wait a second... She wouldn't do that. Right? She'd do what any mother would do. She'd tell him what he needed to hear right now. 'I love you son.' 'I missed you.' 'You can do anything you set your mind to.' 'I believe in you.' After all... He wasn't a Thug. He was a Champion. That's what Anthony told himself. He reached over and grabbed the belt, pulling into his lap. He took his other hand off the steering wheel and reached for the door handle. He reached...and reached...but he couldn't grab hold. His hand was shaking too hard. Just a champion? Not a Thug? Who the fuck are you trying to kid asked Train. Annabelle nimbly scaled the three steps to the front door of her townhome and shifted the bag of groceries to her off arm, quickly diging throuh her purse for her keys. They don't take long to retrieve. She quicly finds the key she needs and slides into the lock. One twist and the door pops open and she steps in, shifting the bag back to the other arm. She's about to step inside when she hears an engine roar behind her. She turns, looking for the source of the noise. Just as she turns a jet black Cadillac Escalade tears out of it's parking spot and down the street. It pulls off and the noise dies down. She turns back to her routine, stepping into her home with her groceries. She takes a few steps forward and puts the bags down on the bottom step of the stairs to the second floor. Breathing a sigh of relief from the weight Annabelle turns and strides back to the door. She takes hold of the knob and begins to close it, but suddenly stops. A strange sensation suddenly posseses her. She opens the door back and steps outside again. She scans the street...finally letting her eyes stop at the end of the block...where she'd seen the strange truck turn and disappear. She shakes her head and steps back inside, fighting that sensation. That sensation as if... as if... as if she's lost something. Oh well.... Whatever it was.... It couldn't have been that important. She shuts the door and moves on with her life.
