Comic book store

24 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/24/03 It was new comics day in the comic book store. Various new issues, graphic novels, hardcover and paperback collections “Hey, you hear who wants to play Luke Cage?” the customer asked the clerk. “No, who?” “That guy from Global Wrestling, Brick.” “Yeah, I heard that,” said a store worker, who was fixing a display. “You serious?” “Yeah, they said it on the official website.” “Oh, great,” groaned another customer. “Another wrestler trying to be an actor.” “Yeah, I know. I’m having “Santa with Muscles” flashbacks.” “Not every wrestler is as bad an actor as Hulk Hogan.” “You’re right. A lot of them are worse.” “Well, there aren’t that many actors who could play Cage. Ving Rhames is big, but he’s too old. So is Michael Clarke Duncan.” “But, still, a wrestler?” “Dude, have you seen him on Global Wrestling” He practically is Luke Cage. Plus, it brings some name recognition to the movie.” “They don’t need some big star to play the part. Christopher Reeves wasn’t a big star when he played Superman. Hugh Jackman wasn’t a big star when he played Wolverine. Just get someone who looks enough like the character and who can act.” “I heard they’re looking for rappers to play the part.” “Like who?” “LL Cool J, DMX.” “LL Cool J I can see, but not DMX.” “He fits the part better than Vin Diesel.” “I don’t know why they’re making a Luke Cage movie in the first place. He’s just some second-string character.” “So was Blade.” “I just hope they don’t have him wear that ugly-ass costume from the 70’s with the tiara.” “It was a headband.” “Same thing.” “If they didn’t let the X-Men wear their comic book outfits, they damn sure ain’t gonna have Cage in his.” “Depends on whether or not they use the gangsta look they gave him lately.” “I hope they don’t use that miniseries as a basis. That was some bullshit.” “What was your problem with it?” “It was racist bullshit, all full of stereotypes.” “Well, Cage has always been a stereotype.” “That’s no excuse.” “Excuse me,” said a customer walking up to the cashier. The other customers and the clerk couldn’t believe their eyes. It was Brick. “Hey, are you?”” “Uh, yeah.” “Hey, dude,” said one of the customers, “are you really gonna try out for the Luke Cage movie?” Brick turned to the customer and with a smirk replied, “Maybe.” “OK, that’s The Crew #3, JLA/JSA: Virtue and Vice, Batman: Hush, Vol. 1 and Formerly Known As the Justice League #1. That’ll be?” Before the clerk could finish, Brick placed a fifty dollar bill on the counter. The cashier then gave him back his change and bagged the merchandise. Brick began to leave, but stopped short. “Which one of y’all was it that said wrestlers shouldn’t be actors?” “Uh’me?” A silence came over the store and then Brick asked “Where were you when WCW gave David Arquette the World title?” And then Brick left.
