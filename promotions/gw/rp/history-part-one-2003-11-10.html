'History', part One

10 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/10/03 Ten years ago... Winter is a funny season. Writers and musicians have used it as a source of inspiration for as long as writing and music have been around. And sometimes, there really does seem to be something magical about the snow and the ice, the bare trees and the biting winds. The way the landscape can turn into a pristine wonderland overnight, all white and shimmering?. It can be breathtaking. But when you're living on the streets of Kyoto, none of that means shit. When you're living on the streets of Kyoto, you're cold, you're wet, you're hungry, and you wish with every fiber of your being that spring would freakin' come already. Wrapped in dirty ragged blankets, and huddling next to a dumpster to try and block the wind out, a young vagrant was making just that wish. And when the wind bit into him again, the Fortunes heard a few choice words that would have made his mother scowl. Growling and muttering under his breath, the boy pulled his blankets tighter around himself and stared out into the night, watching snow flutter down in the streetlights. The whirling patterns of the snowflakes in flight slowly mesmerized the young man, and he began to nod off. Then the wind bit into him again, and all thoughts of sleep were thrown from his mind. Grumbling, he stared out into the street again, watching as a pretty young girl walked by. She hugged herself for warmth, rubbing her hands up and down her arms. It seemed that she was in a hurry -- such a hurry that she didn't see the five men step out from the alley, knives, pipes, chains, and other crude weapons in their dirty hands. The boy leapt to his feet, his blankets falling away as he forgot the cold. One of the men reached out, grabbing the girl by the wrist. The boy started running towards them. The girl turned, seeing her attackers, and screamed. The boy hurled himself into the air. The man holding the girl's wrist pulled out a knife -- and the boy's foot slammed into the side of his head. The girl stumbled forward and fell to the ground, still shrieking. The scrawny boy landed, standing between the girl and the muggers, wearing a defiant, challenging posture on his skinny frame. The man with the knife dropped it, and crumpled to the sidewalk, unconscious. For a split second, all was still, as if the winter had frozen time itself. Then the combatants exploded into action. The boy ducked a clumsily swung pipe, slammed a fist into its wielder's stomach, and dropped, sliding between the pipe-wielder's legs as a knife blade sliced through where he just was. Letting himself skid on the snow-slick sidewalk, the boy rolled to his feet, catching a steel chain as it was swung at his head, and letting it wrap around his arm. With a fierce jerk, he pulled the chain-wielding thug to him, slamming a knee into the man's gut, and an elbow into the back of his head. The mugger toppled, and lay motionless on the ground. The remaining three muggers spread out to flank the boy as he swung the chain in wide arcs, keeping them at bay. And the girl, frozen with both fear and awe, watched. She watched as the man with the knife moved in and thrust his blade at the boy -- only to have the boy catch the blade in the links of the chain, and send the knife spinning out of the mugger's hand. She watched as the boy moved with alarming speed, wrapping the chain around the disarmed mugger's arm, and twisting him into the path of the pipe-wielding mugger's swing. She winced as the pipe connected, and the boy lost an opponent. She watched as the boy avoided the pipe-wielding thug's attacks expertly, and looked at the three men out cold on the ground. How could this skinny, half-frozen boy, with his ratty blonde hair and his gaijin features, do so much? The girl looked back to the fight, and shrieked. The sound of breaking glass echoed through the streets as the fifth mugger shattered a bottle over the boy's head, having taken advantage of the man with the pipe's distraction. Holding his skull, the boy dropped to his knees -- and then screamed and collapsed as the man with the pipe slammed it across his back. The two muggers laughed, taunting the boy, and kicking him as he tried to struggle back to his feet. The man with the pipe raised it high over his head, preparing to finish the boy off. In a moment of desperation, the girl snatched up the first mugger's knife, and hurled it at the man with the pipe, praying for the Fortunes to guide the blade. The Fortunes, it would seem, were listening. The knife buried itself in the man's hand, and he screamed, dropping his pipe. The fifth mugger looked away from the boy as his friend screamed -- and then screamed himself, as the boy buried his foot in the man's groin. As the man dropped to his knees, the boy sprung to his feet, and delivered a spinning kick to the side of his skull, knocking him cold. Slowly, the boy turned to face the last mugger, who was pulling the knife out of his bleeding hand. The mugger looked up, and met the steely gaze of the boy's cobalt blue eyes. The boy began stalking towards him, and the mugger turned and ran -- right into the light post behind him. With a groan, he slumped to the ground, motionless. The boy let out a long, slow breath, and then dropped to his knees. 'Hey, no!' shouted the girl, finally picking herself up and running to the boy's side. With all her strength, she hauled him back up to his feet, supporting his weight with her petite frame the best she could. 'Come on now,' she said, as she began walking him down the street, 'lets get you somewhere warm and safe. It's the least I can do after you fought all those men to protect me.' 'That...' he trailed off as he struggled against unconsciousness, 'That sounds nice. Where are we going?' 'Home,' the girl said, 'Where my dad can try and clean you up, and it's warm and safe.' 'That sounds very nice,' said the boy dreamily. 'It is,' replied the girl. 'By the way,' she continued, 'I'm Minako. Thank you for saving my life.' The boy laughed, then groaned at the pain this brought him. 'You're... you're welcome Minako,' he said, wincing at the tenderness of his injured ribs. 'I'm... my name is Rokku. Akodo Rokku' 'Rokku, huh? That's an odd name...' And so they continued, vanishing into the snowy night.
