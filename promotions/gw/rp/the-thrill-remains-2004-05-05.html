The Thrill Remains

5 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 5th, 2004 **To be immortalized is to be put on a pedistle above all, or many other human beings in this world, or at least this is what it is to be immortalized in western culture. One is often immortalized after achieving something that is said to be going above the standards, or a series of accomplishments that altogether symbolize a great achievement. Many men over time have been immortalized for their accomplishments, whether it be in the field of sports, entertainment, politics or the such. But with immortalization can come envy, and if one who has been immortalized decides to stick around in the field that have been put up on a pedistle in, that person runs the risk of becoming a stepping stone for another to begin on their way to immortalization** - SAMSON COUNTY MORGUE, TEXAS - .:A much needed rainfall pours down on the regularly humid southern state. The skies are filled with dark, ominous clouds, that see numerous sharp flashes in between shots of booming thunder that shake the groud below one's feet:. CRASH! BOOM! .:The thunder hits hard and mercilessly in the Southern skies of the Lone Star state of Texas, sparing no one in its ear-drum popping slams. In front of us lies one of the most mysterious facilities known to man, the morgue. This particular morgue is home to several deceased Texas every day, and lies in the edge-city county of Samson, lying just outside of Dallas. The large building gets pounded with rain, and drops of water pound the walls of the structure as we enter the sterilized center:. .:Once inside, there is a room with a large circular desk in front of a long hallway. The walls are decked out in mostly silver, with black-tiled flooring. The place is spotless, as it is a morgue and there is no reason for it to be any scuffs in the floor. There is a man at the desk that is wearing a 'security' windbreaker jacket, with a button up shirt and tie on underneath. As we approach the man at the desk, he has his feet up on the desk and appears to be sleeping. The desk has several TV screens on it monitoring several parts of the morgue. Once we get to the security guard, he wakes up and is startled:. GUARD: Jesus Christ! (Gasping for breath) You scared the shit outta' me! Who the hell are you and what are ya'll doing here?! .:The guard looks around for a moment, but then snaps his fingers as though he has remembered something he had already been told:. GUARD: Wait, you have a camera. (Pauses for a moment, still remembering) Ohhh, you must be from one of them documentary-type shows. Well I'll tell ya' what, that freak wit' da' facial-paintin' is in the cold cellar right now if you wanna go back there an' see em'. I'll just have to buzz ya'll in, one sec. .:The guard reaches over to the far side of the circular desk, and presses a button:. BEEEEP! .:Suddenly a voice comes through the speaker box on the pannel that the guard pressed the button on. The voice coming through the box is accompanied by some static:. VOICE: Yeah, whattya want? GUARD: Hey, I uhh, have a guy here who wants to tape the creepy lookin' guy in the cold cella' wit' the fresh bodies in it. VOICE: Well tell them to make it quick, this face-painted fella' is just wandering around the celler with his eyes wide open, looking at the various jars and such. GUARD: Alright ya'll, you can go ahead. At the end of the hall, an' to the right, the door that says 'cold cellar' on it, that's the one ya' want. Have a good one. .:We are taken down the long hallway, past about four doors that look exactly the same as the next. Finally we see the door labelled 'cold cellar' and we enter to the inside. Once on the otherside of the door, we see a long isle of identical-looking storage freezers, a whole wall of them on either side. The isle continues for about seven feet before we reach a larger room, where we see a couple of metal tables, with cloaked bodies lying on top. We also see a metal shelf filled up with various jars containing human organs emersed in some sort of preservation liquids. There are about seven tables in this section of the cold cellar with bodies on them, and suddenly on the far left side of the room, on the final table at the end, we see Takahashi Jetsui poke his head out from underneath the sheet covering the body. Takahashi's long black hair hangs down over his red face paint as Takahashi stands up straight:. TAKAHASHI: (Takes in a large breath of 'fresh air') Ahhh, it's a beautiful thing really, death, I mean. There may be nothing else more beautiful that man will ever experience in one lifetime. .:Takahashi begins to walk around the first table in the row of dead-supporting tables:. TAKAHASHI: The emotions that one exhibits after the death of a loved one are emotions that will never be triggered by any other situation a man may find himself in. Giving birth to a new human life form brings upon physical pain, and emotional joy, but is followed by years of turbulance, including periods of rebellion and lashing out. But with death, there is no turning back. .:Takahashi proceeds alongside the second table with the cloaked dead body planted on top of it. As Takahashi slowly makes his way by the table, he sadistically runs his fingers across the dead body. As Takahashi commits this action, he seemingly feels a sense of fullfillment as with every inch felt, Takahashi closes his eyes and takes a sharp breath:. TAKAHASHI: Once a human dies, so many things potentially die with them including fond childhood memories, companionship, and love. There is no other feeling like the one after the death of someone close, and why should people be deprived of such a feeling? I am a firm believer... .:Takahashi reaches the third table, and slowly lifts up the sheet covering the body. A sadistic grin evolves across the face of Takahashi Jetsui as he peers under the sheet:. TAKAHASHI: That the feeling after a death is more of a therapeutic experience than a situation that should be avoided at all costs. I was thrust into feeling this stab through the heart very early into my existence, and now look at me. I am in the almighty position to spread this feeling unto others, and spread is what I shall do. .:Takahashi runs his down the sheet placed over the body on the third steel table, and once he reaches the end of the table, he grabs hold of the sheet by the corners, and quickly rips the sheet off the table, revealing the body underneath. The body is of a large male human. The man is apparently bloated, as his body looks very swelled up around the hips on his face. The deceased man has already started to turn a shade of blue, as the bloated, pale heap of skin lays there motionless:. TAKAHASHI: Who do we have here? A large man by anyone's standards, he looked like one of your typical jolly men of the world, most likely loved and adored by so many. .:Takahashi reaches down and lifts up a clipboard to his eye level. Takahashi flips a couple pages as though he is reading over them:. TAKAHASHI: Hmmm, in this situation this man died as a result of severe trauma to the skull. The blow was most likely made with some sort of blunt object, this usaully makes for a messy procedure. In my case, I like to try to keep things clean (putting the clipboard down), as the human body is a fragile thing, and doesn't take very much to feel pain. Whoever the human was who put an end to this man's life must have really had it in them to send this man to the great beyond without an ounce of mercy in them. For when this man was struck the first time, the damage would have already been done, the hemeraging would have started upon the first blow, and if not upon impact, then this man would have died merely hours afterwards. But this man suffered multiple blows to the skull, causing one to wonder what kind of situation the man was in to desereve such a severe punishment. .:Takahashi methodically places the now folded sheet over the man's face and proceeds to the far corner of the room, and begins to observe the jarred human organs on the shelf, with his hands clasped behind his back:. TAKAHASHI: Human feel a wide array of emotions that may provoke them to commit such violent acts as forcefully turning a man's skull into mush. Envy, jealousy, anger, revenge, all of these are considered suitable motives for murder in today's world. But what about thrill? The thrill of causing a human being to convulse uncontrollably with the swing of a weapon. The thrill of having the power to extract massive amounts of blood from a human's lungs. The thrill is never considered. The lust is never acceptable. There must always be a REASON behind an action. Well I happen to disagree with this connotation, as I myself love nothing more than feeling the thrill of MURDER. I myself love the thrill of putting another life form to rest, and implying the after-emotion on those around the 'victim.' .:Takahashi picks up a jar off the shelf. The jar contains what appears to be a human, and Takahashi begins to toss the jar into the air as he turns back towards us:. TAKAHASHI: I'm purely in this business for the thrill. The thrill of forcing others to feel and experience the pains that we as humans have been gifted with is my purpose for being here. Some do not seem to understand my lust for what I can impose on others, but one man soon will. The mental emotion has taken over this man, and only the physical pain awaits its presence inside the aforementioned man. And who knows, maybe the female and her significant will be reunited in the same grave side-by-side afterall. .:Takahashi turns back towards the shelf and places the jar back on the ledge that he had retrieved it from. Takahashi then begins to walk away from the shelf:. TAKAHASHI: Only time will tell what is in store for this man, but you must all rest-assured that it will be even more unpleasant than any criminal institution or nasty neighbourhood can offer. And the physical pain entailed will make the emotional pain an envied time period. .:Takahashi exits the cold cellar after walking down the long isle we previously were taken down, and through the same door we were taken through:.
