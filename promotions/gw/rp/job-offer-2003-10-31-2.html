Job Offer

31 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/31/03 All questions and no answers. Isn’t that how life always is? Always wondering who holds the keys to the right doors, or why the wrong people get to walk through them. Its funny how life works. Its also strange when you have opportunity fall into your lap. Johnie Ringo is no stranger to the weird and wonderful, but this time is a bit different. He receives a phone call from someone he’s never seen or heard of before and is told to come meet him. Why? An even more bizarre question is...why is he going to see him? What the hell am I doing? Johnie’s walking the streets of his hometown, Taos, New Mexico, on his way to see this mystery man. In passing by the no name shops and bars of 4th and Broadway. Glancing over, he stops to look into the window of Vince’s Tavern. The place is half full as patrons come to watch football with friends. Regulars have taken their stools at the bar, staring wishfully into their glasses, not caring for the game. They look into them almost like they were begging with their eyes. A washed over glaze glimmering, wishing things could be different. Things he could have changed. Losing all hope for normality. Johnie’s eyes refocus and he sees his own eyes in the reflection, still red with fury over arguing with his sister prior to the walk over. Slowly, he raises his hands to rub them and attempts to clear his head of it. It’d be easier to forget, but sometimes its not that easy. He keeps walking down over the cracks and lines of the sidewalks. The fall breezes brush across his face, cooling him off some. Nature helps in strange ways, I guess. The sun is barely peaking over the large apartment buildings across the street, gleaming as bright as it could. Johnie winces his eyes until they’re almost shut just to see in front of him. Maybe he should get sunglasses sometime. But he sees the parking garage just across the street. One of those spiral ramp lots that stick out like a sore thumb usually. Three levels high. He did say ground floor didn’t he? As he steps to the edge of the crosswalk, Johnie punches the side of his hand into the button on the stoplight. No traffic passing by, but you can never be sure. Some people just fly out of nowhere. The light’s not changing. What the hell? He hits the button again. Nothing. “Fuck this.” Johnie blurts out as he looks up at the pole. “It must be busted. Piece of shit town!” He leans his head forward and looks both ways before heading out. His pace is a bit quicker than before, but just to get across. Just before he reaches the end, a car speeds from around the corner and nearly runs him down. Johnie jumps back a bit and slams his fists onto the hood of a red Camero. “Watch where you’re fucking driving, asswipe!” Johnie keeps walking toward sidewalk, but the driver swings his door open. Stepping out of the seat is a rather tall man with too much gel in his brown hair. His jaw shaped as though someone chiseled it from limestone. Dressed to kill in a buttoned down blue shirt, trying too hard to show off some pecs. Johnie can smell the arrogance as he steps toward him. Along with the Cool Water he probably bathed in. “What’s wrong with you, mate?” echoes the Australian dialect off his tongue as he points towards the front of the car. “You dented my bleamin” hood.” Johnie, standing his ground, leans against the opposite light pole. “Is that supposed to bother me” I might have missed that part, bud.” “I just got this car. Now, I reckon, you owe me a apology.” “Well sorry, MATE!” Johnie gets off the pole and stands looking down, tapping his right Reebok on the pavement. “I don’t owe you a damn thing. And I don’t RECKON that, I know.” “Bly me,” the Aussie laughs cockily. “looks like we got ourselves a comedian here. He thinks he’s funny.” He cracks his knuckles one at a time. Keep showing off, fuckhead. You’re making this easier. “Not like it’s a gift or anything. Its just easy to one-up a grease ball like you.” Yep. Just keeps walking closer. “Aye. Ya got something against me, mate” Shall we settle this?” Oh yeah. Open invite. “Settle what” The results are already in. You’re a brainless moron that nearly run me over. I guess you’re blind too.” That got him. The Aussie’s face is showing some red, despite the dark coating that his leather skin taken. Clenching his right hand, he rears back for a swing. “Oh that’s it!” he yells as he aims for his head, but Johnie moves to the left slightly and brushes past. Bad move. Grabbing his shoulder, Johnie lifts his knee directly into the guy’s ribcage. The Aussie shrieks in pain, but not before Johnie tosses him onto and over the hood of the Camero. Johnie brushes off the arms of his leather jacket as a grin peaks over his face. “Bigger they are...the dumber they fight.” As he laughs, he gets a funny feeling. Almost like there are eyes on him. Instinctively, he looks around. Nothing. But he still feels it. Finally he snaps his neck up and there’s a figure walking away from the ledge on the second tier of the parking garage. I take it he’s not on the first floor then. ************************************************************************ *BONG* A bump of the elevator shakes Johnie a bit as it comes to a stop. The crate-like doors fold and cross over each other to open as he lifts them up to step out. Behind him, the doors collapse down on themselves. An incessant hum reverberates and rumbles as the elevator heads back down to the ground floor. Johnie pulls a crumpled pack of Camel Filters out of his left pocket and coaxes one out. As he lights it, he steps over the cold concrete looking all around at the lines of cars. Many different brands and models lined up, one after another. Busy day, of course. Scoffing to himself, he shakes his head and starts heading towards the back wall. Scanning over all the cars near the back, looking for one with a dark shade to it. A Mercedes, was it? Shouldn’t be too hard to find. This isn’t what could be considered “the ritziest part of town” however. “And there it is...” Johnie utters as a puff of smoke fires out his nostrils. He sees it before he’s even in the right row. Easy to find, I guess. He advances over to it through the pair of cars before it. Even in this dim light, he could see the fresh carwash shine on it. Good paint job. Blacker as the night itself. Sleek framework, slight back spoiler, thousand dollar rims maybe. His hand caresses over the back body like he’s gliding it over a lady lying beside him. Feeling every contour. Every perfection. Everything of it. “Must be some high-rolling jackass.” “Indeed!” The voice echoes from behind him, spinning Johnie around in a quick motion. Straining his eyes, he sees a figure in the shadows next to a large pillar. He can only see the glimmer of his shoes as the light catches it at a weird angle. “A high rolling jackass indeed.” The figure steps forward from the shadow as his shoes causing an echoing click. Piece by piece of him is revealed in the weak light. Dark, leather shoes with a hard heel. An obviously expensive, gold textured suit. Looks Italian, maybe? A Rolex that matches the gold chain around his neck. And a strong face with a dark pair of sunglasses hiding his eyes. He walks towards the front of the car, looking directly at it the whole way. “You must be Johnie Ringo.” The prosperous man before him strokes his hand across the hood, much like Johnie did, only with less adoration. More like how a man passes over his pet. Johnie leans against front passenger door, raising the cigarette to his lips. “Yeah. And who are you then?” The man looks back at Johnie and smirks faintly at him. “They say that’s bad for your health.” He clicks towards Johnie and stands before him. “Well if you don’t live your life how you want to, then give it up,” states as he takes another drag. “Fair enough.” Johnie pushes himself off the door and walks near this new acquaintance of his. “So are you going to tell me your name or what?” “Settle down, kid. You’re getting too jumpy with this. I’m here to offer you a job. That’s all.” “A job, eh” Why?” “Because I knew your father.” That one hit home. Johnie tries not to show it, but he feels a bit apprehensive about this situation now. “I remember how he worked. How he got a job done.” Johnie turns his head away to flick his cigarette. That’ll give him some air time. He starts walking the other way a bit before answering back. “What do you mean?” “I want you to do something for me. I want you to do what your father once did.” Should I? The man grabs Johnie’s shoulder as he goes to him and talks over his shoulder. “It’s a simple job. I will even pay you up front. And if you do good, I may offer you a job in the future. My people are VERY influential.” I could use the money... “When?” “Tuesday night.” “Who?” “Ace Cash. He’s easy to find.” Johnie lowers his brow, thinking. Going over his options. He turns around and faces his new temporary employer. “Fine. But I’m only working one job at a time. That’s it. I’m independent.” “Done.” He extends his hand to Johnie and they shake. Simple. Done deal. The man opens the rear passenger door and pulls out a brown paper bag to hand to Johnie. “Here’s $5000. It’s a start. The name is Tony Stecchino.” Johnie nods solemnly and takes the bag, turning around to go. Tony gets into the rear door and snaps his fingers to the driver. Instantly the car starts up and idles a bit before he heads out towards the back ramp. As Johnie walks back towards the elevator, another question comes over his mind. But he’s not going to answer it any time soon. What have I gotten myself into?
