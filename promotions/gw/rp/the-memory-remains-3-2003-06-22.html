The Memory Remains #3

22 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 062203 He rode in the back of the stretch limo, thinking, his mind wandering, trying not to let it wander, trying not to remember. The past was there. He lived it. He breathed it. He ate it. He resided in it, day in, and day out. Lily. The name echoed in his mind, back and forth, up and down, over and under. There was no escaping it. It was there. It was coming. Lily. John Zero slowly shut his eyes and let the images flood his mind. She was standing in the middle of the room when I came in. Just standing there, her auburn hair sweeping over her face playfully, a coy smile dancing on her lips. An angel standing before me. An explosion. A bang. A loud pop. I can’t remember which it was, but it happened all the same. And after that noise, that terrible noise, she fell. All the happiness fled from her eyes, replaced by terror. I ran to her, sliding down as I ran, catching her before she hit the ground. I held her close to me, looking into her eyes, those emerald green eyes, watching the life run away, seeking shelter elsewhere. I lifted my hand to her face, brushing the hair away, my fingers sticky with her dark red blood. Right before her eyes completely faded to nothing, she was able to tell me, the words almost a silent mutter, one last thing. “I did love you.” Then nothing. Her eyes went dark as the last little bit of breath her lungs held exited through her mouth in a small exasperated sigh. I screamed, tears flowing from my eyes by the bucket-load. I raised my hands to my head, cradling it in my palms. It was then that I realized that I was holding the gun. The limo had stopped. The driver opened the door, waiting for John Zero to exit. He stood there a good five minutes before sticking his head in, checking on his passenger. “Sir?” the driver asked. “Sir,” he said a little bit louder, reaching in, grabbing Zero by the shoulder, and shaking him slightly. In an instant, without opening his eyes, Zero grabbed the driver by the hand, twisting, pulling down and pushing in on the hand at an awkward angle. Zero then propelled himself out the door, running on instinct, flipping the driver to the ground and landing on top of him, pulling a dull, gun metal gray glock from his shoulder holster, nuzzling the barrel at an upward angle at the driver’s neck so that the bullet would pass not only through the jugular, but also out of the top of the driver’s skull. “Pleasedon’tkillme,” the four words ran together as the sputtered out of the now panicked driver’s mouth. “Pleasedon’tkillme. Pleasedon’tkillme. I’m sorry, mister. I won’t touch you. Pleasedon’tkillme.” Zero’s forefinger gently caressed the trigger of the gun, as he slowly opened his eyes. Anger fled from his body as a look of concern passed over his face. “What happened?” Zero whispered. “We got to your house, sir,” the driver also whispered, so as to not to agitate the man with the gun. “I opened the door for you, and you didn’t come out. I reached in a shook you real gentle like, and you grabbed my arm and did some sorta jujitsu shit on me, and pulled that peacemaker out. Honest, I didn’t mean anything by it.” Zero slowly stood up. “I didn’t hurt you, did I?” he asked. “I think my wrist is broke.” Zero nodded. “Get it fixed, send me the bill. I think I’ll drive myself from here on out.” He turned around sharply, and left the driver laying on the ground. I walked into my office, half expecting to see Lily waiting on me, like she did the previous day. But, from the way I saw Bryce grab her and drag her away, I was somewhat worried as well. She entered the room a couple of minutes later. Walking slowly, Lily drifted to her seat. Gone was the openly expressive woman that I meant the day before. Her hair covered her face and she wore dark sunglasses that hid her eyes. I tried to start a conversation, but she remained silent. Through the entire three-hour class, she never removed her sunglasses. When we were done for the day and walking out the door, I reached and grabbed her by the arm. She screamed and fell to the floor, crying. “What’s wrong?” I asked. “What did I do?” “Nothing,” she said. I knelt down to help her back up. Staring into a black pool with emerald centers, realization of what was wrong swept over me. “Bryce?” I asked. “Did he do this?” She nodded. “I think we should leave separately. He gets extremely jealous when he sees me with other guys.” I nodded and she left. That was the first time I seriously felt like killing someone.
