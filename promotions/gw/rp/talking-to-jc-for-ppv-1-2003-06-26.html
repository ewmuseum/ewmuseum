Talking to JC (for ppv #1)

26 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: june 26 Note: This is written to express the feelings of Derek Frost as a person, not a wrestler. This should be read with this thought in mind. Derek Frost is talking to his uncle/best friend on a personal level, not in character. Those who knew me, knew me well.. those who don't, can go to hell.. ---------- I walked into the gym, not knowing what I would do when I walked out of there. It scared me to death. I hate not knowing what I'll be like an hour later. But on this day, I didn't have any idea what I was needing to do. I did know that i had to do something. ---- 'Hey Derek. What's goin' on Bro?' Johnny said as soon as he saw me enter the room. 'Hey man. Damn it feels weird being in here again.' 'Why? The YB gym has never been a problem before. You've spent most of your career here. Except for the few small feds you were dominating when I found you and got you in UCWF for Ayers.' I look up at Johnny with a glimpse of longing in my eyes. 'UCWF is long gone, JC. We have too many guys here now that don't know the true meaning of work. I won't name any names, but Sean Price needs to get his ego in check and realize that sucking up to the boss won't get you where you need to be all the time.' Cruse shoots me a look of disbelief. 'What are you saying?' 'I'm not sure, Johnny. Everything is getting too political for me lately. I mean, It's pro wrestling, it's going to have politics, but this is overkill.' Johnny laughs a little, then mumbles. 'The guy with blue hair talks about overkill. How ironic.' 'Ha ha, very fucking funny man. Seriously, though. Certain guys backstage don't know that things are done for a reason. They complain to the ones booking the matches until they get things their way, and even then they feel the urge to tell other people, people who have put in more time here than they could hope for, that they don't deserve to be there. Some of these guys up and left the GW when it was looking like things were getting tough. They duck and run when things get ugly, and then have the balls to tell the real GW members that those who stuck through it from the beginning were 'running away' from problems.' I set down my bag and slide into the square ring to my right. Johnny follows suit and slides in, both of us in street clothes. He pulls a hair tie out of his pocket and pulls his long rainbow hair back, while I loosen the black and silver belt around the waist of my khaki shorts. We circle each other a little before locking up. 'Why should I stay? If I had anything else to accomplish, you'd think I'd have done it already.' I say while applying a wrist lock to Johnny's right arm. 'Because even the best guys don't go to the top overnight.' says Cruse, before dropping to the mat and rolling me face first into a boston crab. I reach the ropes and hold on, as JC lets go and lets me back to my feet. I rush him and he sends me to the ropes, which I bounce off of and catch him in a headscissors takedown, holding it into a headlock with my legs. 'I really miss the square ring. Bubba is trying to get too much out of GW anymore. He wants it to be huge, which it could be. He needs to realize that not everything is his way or the highway.' Johnny grabs my legs and rolls over, then slowly stands up, applying a version of a tree-of-woe on me. 'Derek, do you realize that not everything he does is bad?' Johnny drops me and I crash to the mat, landing in a heap. After a few seconds, Johnny extends his hand and helps me up. We sit on the mat, leaned against the ropes, talking. Just like we used to do in early UCWF. Before a show, after a show, and any time we were in a gym. 'Only after he does things that won't drive me and some of my fellow Old School guys away. The octagon ring was a change, I've adapted. I just have twice the turnbuckles to climb now. Some things are just too much to adapt to.' 'Do you know why I chose you to lead the Young Bloods in my absence?' 'Be...cause I'm your nephew, and if I screw up you can rub it in for years to come?' Johnny laughs a little, then looks at me seriously. 'No. I chose you because you're one of the best talents I've ever come across. I saw potential in you from the very day I met you.' 'You saw potential in me at your mom's house for Thanksgiving dinner in 95?' 'No, Derek. I mean the first time I saw you step in to a wrestling ring. I brought you and Cindy here as a chance to be with my neice, and she insisted you be with her. You got into the ring, and I looked over at you and had to do a double take, because when I saw you, I saw you standing on that turnbuckle with a world title raised above your head. I knew right then and there that you had the ability to do great things for this business.' 'Really...well where are these great things? Midcarding shows for the GW isn't exactly my idea of great things.' 'Be patient,' Johnny says, a slight smile coming across his face. 'Be patient, young grasshopper.' I laugh a little, then look at Johnny, letting him know I'm serious now. 'Why does it seem that everything is against me right now?' I ask. 'Maybe it's because you go out and pretend to have The Nevermore and The Upper Echelon chasing after you and the guys. Plus with all the stress you're going through trying to fix up your cars, and yes I know you stress over those, you love your cars, it's just building up on you.' 'What do you suggest I do about it?' 'Well, you could talk to some of the guys that are bothering you.' 'Riiiight, and have my words twisted around, dipped in gasoline, lit on fire, and shoved down my throat? No thanks. I'd rather spend a weekend with my mother-in-law.' 'Come on Derek, My bro's wife isn't that bad.' 'Shit Johnny, she's the mother of the woman I love, I know she's not that bad. I had to put up with her through the last two years of highschool. I've heard that if you can survive high school knowing a girl's parents, you can survive eternity with that girl.' 'Honestly I've never heard that, but then again I rarely pay attention to stupid cliches like that. I'm surprised you do. What's next, poety?' 'Nature's first green is gold, her hardest hue to hold...' 'Ok. Ok. Ok. Sorry I mentioned it.' Johnny says, glaring at me. I sit there, reciting the rest of the poem in my head. Finally, I reach the last line, and it seems to wrap up all of my feelings about the GW right now. 'Nothing gold can stay.'
