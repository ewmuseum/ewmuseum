The article (COTT RP 2)

27 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/27/04 It was news Brick had been anticipating for quite some time now. He had printed out an article from Comicscontinuum.com that concerned the making of the Luke Cage movie. It was an article featuring John Singleton, the film’s director. Brick had recently made an elaborate audition tape a few weeks earlier, which featured vignettes based on the origin of the Luke Cage character. He sent copies of the audition tape to Singleton, Neal Moritz (the producer of the movie) and Marvel Studios head Avi Arad It wouldn’t be the first time an actor made an elaborate audition tape to a director. Sean Young had done the same thing when she auditioned for the role of Catwoman in “Batman Returns”. Of course, Brick hadn’t appeared on any talk shows dressed up as Luke Cage the way Young had done, dressed as Catwoman. Brick read the article with baited breath as he sat on his couch. Any news concerning the Luke Cage movie, he welcomed. JOHN SINGLETON TALKS CAGE MOVIE John Singleton, director of the Luke Cage movie, told The Continuum that an announcement regarding the lead actor should be made soon. 'I got a couple of people in mind, but it's premature to say,' Singleton told The Continuum after the Spider-Man 2 panel last weekend at Wizard World Los Angeles. 'It's going to be announced in probably a week or so who's going to be Cage.' The film is set up at Sony, where Singleton and Marvel Studios' Avi Arad first worked to develop the character some nine years ago. The script is written by Ben Ramsey. 'It's dope. It's dope,' Singleton said. 'We're working on a new draft and we should be casting really soon.' Singleton told The Continuum the villain will be Diamondback, the original Hero for Hire comics villain who framed him and had him sent to prison. 'And I'm trying to get in some of the other characters, like Chemistro, and maybe the Wrecking Crew or some of the obscure Marvel villains,' Singleton said. Singleton confirmed that Cage will be using his trademark 'Sweet Christmas' line in the film. 'When I read the script, I bust out laughing when he said that,' Singleton said. 'But he can't wear the yellow shirt and he can't have the chains -- I'm not having that. And it's not like the Richard Corben one, either. 'I'm doing the movie for one reason, to have him break out of Seagate Prison with his bare hands,' Singleton said, laughing. Singleton said he hopes to get production started by late summer for a 2005 release. 'It's a fun movie. It's hip. It's so cool,' Singleton said. 'Imagine if 50 Cent got super-powers.' Brick could only wait and hope that he would be one of the people Singleton had in mind. Names he had heard to be considered for the role included L.L. Cool J, Ving Rhames, Vin Diesel and the Rock. He had gone on a number of Internet message boards where fans made their own suggestions. These suggestions included NYPD Blue star Henry Simmons, rapper Ice Cube, an actor from the movie “Underworld” and the actor who played the tile role in “Spawn”. He had read a few comments left by Internet fans who discussed his desire to play the role. Some supported him. Others decried it, complaining about “yet another wrestler trying to act.” Some suggested that there were other characters in Marvel that Brick could play. One suggestion was that Brick could play Tombstone, a minor Spiderman villain who had fought Cage as well. Another was Thunderball, a member of the Wrecking Crew. Another was Shotgun, an obscure character that had appeared in Daredevil and Punisher comics. But Brick was hoping that at the very least, Singleton would consider him for the lead role. Like Singleton, he was a fan of the character. He possessed one of the largest collections of Luke Cage comics in existence. He also owned a small Luke Cage bust. He even went as Cage on Halloween once. That was before his family stooped celebrating it on the grounds that it was a pagan holiday. He did however decline to collect the mature-readers-only miniseries that had come out a few years back. To put it mildly, it wasn’t to Brick’s liking. Also, Brick pretty much was Luke Cage made flesh. He had survived things that would kill or at the least cripple a normal man However, Brick knew, in all likelihood, that he might not get the part. He didn’t have the strongest acting resume. Outside wrestling and maybe a few fans of DMAK, he was pretty much an unknown. But, even if he didn’t get the part, Brick was that big a fan that he would see the movie anyway. He would definitely want to see if Singleton got it right. He had enjoyed Singleton’s remake of Shaft so he had confidence in Singleton as a filmmaker. Although, the one quote about 50 Cent did make him wince.
