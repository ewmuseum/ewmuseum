Vacation

1 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/01/06 The sun beat down upon Blind Rage as he leant against the railing, on the main promenade of the luxury liner, wondering for the hundredth time that hour just how in the hell Warrior had persuaded him to come along to this damned trip in the first place. Of course, it was necessary for Warrior and he, to take a break from both the wrestling industry and the crime fighting world, due to Warrior’s “health problems”, but to come here on the second annual superhero’s luxury Caribbean cruise was something else entirely. Yes, it wouldn’t have been so bad if they were actually invited. You can’t simply go to the travel agents and book a place on this boat - you have to be invited. It was a veritable who’s who of the superhero world, only the best were invited (though god only knows how in the hell Night Thrasher got in there. It was obvious to everyone that he was just a cheap Batman rip-off.) and the rest - like us - were left outside in the dark. When Warrior opened up the deal-clenching sentence with “Well there’s this hole in a fence by the harbour...” Blind Rage just knew he should’ve just turned the volume of the TV up. Basically, they sneaked aboard and thus far nobody had noticed. Apart from Robin of course. Robin, Batman’s “partner” had kind of a “thing” for Rage, of course spurned on by Warrior’s bumbling. So while Warrior was having the time of his life - mostly hanging around Wonder Woman trying to get a glimpse at her draws - Blind Rage had been desperately trying to avoid the “boy wonder”. Blind Rage stood upright, and re-arranged “himself” uncomfortably. His full length, black and orange, superhero costume was designed to withstand artic conditions, but only moderate heat. His massive frame was overheating and being trapped behind the mask didn’t help none either. He glanced over to his right to see Warrior playing ball in the swimming pool with some woman Rage didn’t recognise. She couldn’t have been too on the ball, because she was deeply engaged in conversation with the moron. You’d have to be part house brick to be on the same level of intelligence as he, but she seemed to be having a good time. Perhaps she was one of those “aliens” who didn’t know the human race that well and just hadn’t yet figured out he was a bumbling idiot. That scenario seemed to make the most sense to him. “Blind Rage?” A distant voice called out. “I’m looking for a Mr Blind Rage...” Blind Rage whirled around on the spot and reluctantly held up his right hand to signal for attention. The man behind the voice was a worker for the liner and, wearing his bright white navy-like uniform, he quickly made his way over. He knew it. He bloody knew it. The crew had finally figured out that Warrior and he were stowaways and they were going to throw them both off the ship. “I’m Blind Rage,” He told the worker, “what’s wrong?” “Nothing wrong, sir,” the worker replied. “We’ve just got a telegram for you!” With that the worker handed Rage a piece of paper and promptly left. Blind Rage warily eyed up the worker as he left, wondering whether he was a super villain in disguise or something, and then opened up the letter. It made for interesting reading and held an offer he just couldn’t refuse. ----------------- Two Days Later ----------------- “Why the hell did we have to leave?” Warrior grumbled, hoisting his heavy shoulder back through the airport terminal. “You know why,” Rage told him, having no trouble with his heavy shoulder bag whatsoever, “I was this close,” Warrior began, holding out his finger and thumb, indicating a small distance between the two, “to nailing that dumb alien woman and you drag us off the luxury cruise liner into a small ass plane.” “Stop whining,” Blind Rage told him. “Stop whining?” Warrior replied, the volume of his voice a little higher now. “I had to spent the better part of five hours sitting a screaming little kid, whose mother wouldn’t know how to look after a child if she spent two months living with Leonard Nimoy!” “Hey!” A woman’s voice from behind them yelled, clearly offended. Warrior turned around to notice it was the very same woman and child he was talking about. “Who’s Leonard Nimoy?” Blind Rage grinned. “He’s the guy from Star Trek...” “Doctor Spock?” The woman questioned. “Of course I wouldn’t know...he’s an actor!” Warrior sighed. “He also wrote a child care book...god you have a child, didn’t you even watch “Daddy Day Care?”” “No,” she replied. “I don’t like Eddie Murphy...” “You don’t like Eddie Murphy” Some people...” Warrior trailed off, turning his back on the woman and glaring intently at Blind Rage once more. “You’re just jealous that I could get a woman after me and all you got was an oiled up “boy wonder”!” “That was all your fault anyhow,” Rage began. “You’re the one who told Robin that I was gay!” Warrior chucked. “Yeah, that was funny!” Blind Rage shook his head, disgusted. “Anyway, this offer from the GW was too good to refuse...” “Why?” Warrior asked. “Because this is the place to be,” Rage responded. “Yeah...if you’re a professional wrestler!” Warrior replied. “But we’re superheroes!” Warrior exclaimed. “What the hell are you talking about, clown shoes?” Blind Rage questioned, rhetorically. “You’re the who usually jumps at the chance for this kind of thing...you like the pay.” “Yeah, but jumping back on the wagon so soon...” Warrior trailed off. Blind Rage developed a knowing smile. “You’ve planned to see that Alien chick again, haven’t you?” Warrior nodded, conceding the point a lot quicker than Blind Rage expected him to. “Yeah, she goes back to her home world at the end of the month!” “Look on the bright side,” Blind Rage responded. “At least she won’t have enough time to figure out what a complete boob you are...” Warrior nodded in reply. “Yeah, I guess...”
