The Glass House

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I hate messes. And I hate cleaning them up. It’s one thing to clean up your own mess. But, to clean someone else’s, well, it really pisses me off. I sit in the uncomfortable metal folding chair, looking at the captive. I should have known something was up when Brian came to me weeks ago, wanting to know the best way to hold a person hostage in a neighborhood. A glass house is what I told him. Construct a glass cube. Then around that cube, about four to six inches away, build another cube. So on, and so forth. The thickness of the glass would keep the person from breaking out. Plus, the air between the glass would act as a sound-proofer, silencing them. He had nodded his head in understanding and walked off. Brian was like that, would ask a question, get his answer and leave. Always thirsting for more information. I never would have guess he was going to build it. I stare at the glass house. It was beautiful in construction, the sleek lines of it. Everything built to perfection. It even came with its own hostage. She was standing now, pointing a long finger at me, her fire red hair framing her face. I could see her mouth move up and down, like she was talking, but now words reached my ears. She banged her hand against the glass several times, more vileness spewing from her mouth, more words that I could not hear. I stood up from the chair, walking to the glass house. I stood facing Lisha, Salaok’s wife. Extending my arm, and forefinger, I touched the outermost pane of glass, dragging it along as I walked over to the intercom. I finger the large red button on the intercom. You do realize that now one can hear you, don’t you? She continues to stare at me, pacing back and forth like a caged animal. A wild caged animal. You waste your energy Lisha. The only way out of this cell, is from the botton on the outside here. Or you could try and smash your way through ten layers of glass. But as you seem to have found, that is damn near impossible. She runs over and grabs the chair that had been placed in the cell with her, swinging at the wall closest to me. BANG! The chair strikes the glass. I can see a small crack form. I smile under my mask as the chair drops from Lisha’s hands. I remove my finger from the button, clicking the intercom off. CRACK! My head snaps towards the window. Catlike, I walk over to it, my right hand going instinctively to my waist, resting on the handle of the Glock tucked in my pants. Someone was out there. I could feel them there. Who was it though? My mind raced wildly, three images coming to mind. The Shadow Man. This answer, though is highly unlikely. He had been dormant for so long. No, I think he is long gone, for the time being. The second two images, though, came to me as one. Salaok and Quartz. Salaok, standing there, long stringy black hair hanging in his face, his curved serrated sword gripped firmly in his hands, swing it back and forth. Quartz, father, towering there, his massive arms swinging as he walked, popping each joint of everything finger. No, the image wasn’t right. Salaok had not been stalking me. Quartz had. I had caught minor glimpses him in shadows, only for him to have disappeared by the time I had gotten close enough. Yes. Quartz was out there. Watching. Waiting. I knew it. I almost wanted to wave. I walk away from the window and move back to my chair, positioning myself with my back to Lisha. What now? This had been Brian’s plan, his mission, his work. But where was he? Weeks had passed, but no word. So, now I’m stuck. Babysitting. And I hate babysitting almost as much as I hate cleaning up messes. I turn and look back at Lisha, who is curled up in the corner of her glass house. I walk over to her corner, kneeling down, looking at her. She had been the only maternal type person in my life, but still not much of a presence of that. But, she had been pulled in, a willing pawn to Salaok and Brian’s dance of death. A pawn, moved into position of Queen. A red queen, so to speak. She will be moved, from this place to that place. Square to square. Take other pieces, luring others into capture. Until the very end, when the King is in sight. And then?.. Checkmate. FADE
