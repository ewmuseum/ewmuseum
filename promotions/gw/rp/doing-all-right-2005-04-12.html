Well, it's been a long time, glad to see your face, I knew we'd meet again, another time, another place... Can't believe it's been so many years! You'd better grab a chair and a couple of beers. April 12, 2005 1012 hours GW Training Facility, Las Vegas It wasn't the first career slump for La Herencia de Herederos... Juan and Teresa had dealt with hardships before, including a string of multiple match losses in a row, more than once in the past. Nor would it be their last, they knew, by the time they were finally ready for retirement... They'd been in the business seven or eight years by now, and still had at least another fifteen to twenty to go. Not that any of these assurances made this latest slump, or any previous or future ones, any easier to bear or deal with; in fact it only made the competitive Luchadores with the fire in their hearts and bellies, even angrier at themselves over each new one. The husband-and-wife team weren't one to take losses lying down, and they always tended to blame themselves even when such blame was unfounded. They always found comfort in their training regiments... especially those where a good friend in the business would join them. In Mexico, they had several friends to pick from... right now they only had one, although they hoped that would change soon. For now, they treasured the time they could spend with El Serpenti, a.k.a. Gaspar de la Rocha. It was only recently they had begun calling him by his name, as a matter of respect for Lucha Libre's traditions. Still relatively new arrivals to the United States, Juan and Teresa had only begun to understand the need to let go of some of Mexican Lucha Libre's rigid customs and protocols. The Spanish-Mexican warrior made a perfect training companion for Juan and Teresa as they were put through their regiment; Serpenti being of very similar size and build, and having the same fast-paced, high-flying style typical of lighter athletes. He had no trouble adapting to the cardiovascular-heavy, lower-body-heavy regiment that Jos? Sanchez, Herencia's trainer, favored and used for all of his students and prot’g’s. Lookin' good in your three-piece suit, You know, I always knew you'd take the business route... You were always the one to follow the light, And you look like you're doing all right! Yeah... Jos? believed strongly in such calisthenics as jumping rope, sit-ups, squat-thrusts, jumping jacks and toe-touching exercises. He included a few basic gymnastic exercises such as cartwheels, and believed in lots of stretching to keep the body limber. He liked light free weights as a means of keeping the chest, arms, back, and shoulders in proper shape, and generally didn't use too many workout machines except the neck conditioner and exercise bikes, which he had Juan and Teresa make heavy use of. Another perk of this workout regiment was the ability to bring Melina into the workout room. Children under the age of 15 were restricted from the large workout machines, but were allowed to be brought into the room for a lighter children's routine of calisthenics, all properly monitored and supervised by an experienced GW trainer. 'Time,' called Jos?, and both Juan and Teresa concluded their rope-skipping regiment in perfect unison. Serpenti was about a second later, taking his cue from them. 'Another round?' smiled Teresa. 'What was it said in the American movie, Star Wars? 'Your eyes can deceive you; don't trust them.' Coordination comes from more than what you see. Juan and I are able to hear it, feel it and even sense it.' Serpenti chuckled lightly. 'Not to mention experience,' he said. 'That is something we will not have time for, facing Pure Thunder in a six-person tag match with Shadow teaming with them, and I with you. But s?, let us go again. At least with practice we can attune our sense of timing.' Jos? smiled and raised a hand. 'Close your eyes, all three of you,' he directed, and they complied. 'One, two, three... go!' Been singin' for my rent and singin' for my supper, I'm above the below and below the upper rung, I'm stuck in the middle, where money gets tight, But I guess I'm doin' all right! All three leather ropes commenced rotation through the ball-bearing handles. 'Beautiful!' called Jos?. 'Synchronization variable down to less than .24 seconds!' Stuart started up an electronic metronome to provide a pace, and Jos? gave instructions in his patient, mentor-like voice. 'Four breaths in... three breaths out. Four breaths in... three breaths out. Left-left; right-right. Up-down; up-down. Steady... good! That's how we do it back home in Mexico!' The three masked warriors smiled in satisfaction as Jos? nodded approvingly. 'Keep your eyes closed,' he directed. 'Concentrate only on the sound of the ropes rotating and turning. Don't look; feel. Use your ears and your sixth senses.' 'George Lucas must have filmed his movies in Mexico,' Teresa thought with a mischievous smile. That, or maybe Jos? was a bigger fan of American science-fiction movies than he had ever let on. He did attend college here... did he become more Americanized than they'd knew, during that time? 'Up, up; down, down... left, and right, and... time!' called Jos?. Motion sensors produced an electronic reading that brought a look of satisfaction. 'Perfect synchronization,' he said, smiling at the three masked Luchadores. I'm all, I'm all, I'm all right! It's a beautiful day not a cloud in sight, So I guess I'm doin' all right! I'm all, I'm all, I'm all right! Got a good old friend here with me tonight, And I guess I'm doin' all right! Taking a short breather to dab perspiration from their necks and lower faces and shoulders with terry towels, the Ramirez spouses and Serpenti let their attention be attracted by a light sing-song chant. 'East side, west side! Double Dutch! Uptown, downtown, all around!' Of course, Melina couldn't understand the words as they were chanted in English... but even if she could, she would have been paying no attention to the words themselves... just the rhythm as the trainers set a pace. Serpenti smiled. 'Quite the adept she is at this Double-Dutch rope-skipping,' he marveled. 'A few more years of practice and she should be able to compete in smaller regional competitions,' said Teresa, smiling back. 'No small surprise, since she is the daughter of professional athletes,' said Juan in a slightly mischievous tone, and all five of them chuckled, including Jos? and Stuart. The trainers called time and held up ten fingers, which Melina understood was a sign for a ten-minute break. She skipped merrily over to Teresa and gave her a hug. The child wore an El Serpenti T-shirt as part of her exercise clothes, along with a dark blue track suit, knee-length green-striped tube socks and plain but rugged Thom McAn sneakers. 'Maravillosa,' said Teresa, holding out a dollar bill. 'Aqu? est? un d’lar. Le puedes obtener una barra de chocolate.' (*Wonderful. Here's a dollar. You can get yourself a chocolate bar.) Melina went to hug Serpenti, and the Spanish-Mexican Luchador smiled as he held out a five-dollar bill. 'Tr’iganos cada un paquete de 'Peanut Chews' y puedes utilizar el cambio para una botella de jugo de fruta,' he said to her, pronouncing the name 'Peanut Chews' slowly and also telling her in Spanish what they were. (*Bring us each a package of 'Peanut Chews' and you can use the change for a bottle of fruit juice.) The child's expression took on a sudden look of worry, but she quickly bolted out of the door to get the items and Jos? signaled for some free weight dumb bell flys. The three masked fighters nodded as they settled themselves on benches, and Jos? gathered up six 20-pound dumb bells. Free weights Jos? did not set a pace for; every athlete developed muscle strength and mass differently. Pure strength could neither be rushed nor postponed in its natural development, and timing and coordination was a moot point. He merely did a deep-breathing exercise for Juan, Teresa, and Serpenti to listen to, and copy as they performed the reps in a steady rhythm. 'Teresa,' said Jos? suddenly. The three Luchadores were only about three-quarters of the way through the full regiment of sets. Well, we had a lot of dreams when we were younger, They thought we were crazy but we had the hunger! We Kept a lot of friends, skipped a lot of class, Been on top of the world and knocked on our ____ We lost touch, we lost in love, We lost our minds when things got tough... But beatin' time is a losin' fight, And I guess I'm doin' all right. Teresa opened her eyes and sat up. One of the children's trainers was standing beside Jos?, looking a bit anxiously at his wristwatch. 'Melina hasn't come back yet?' Teresa instantly became seriously anxious, and both Juan and Serpenti sat up in a heartbeat. 'Gaspar?' Juan felt horrible imposing on their friend like this, but they knew it was something he wouldn't mind at all. Serpenti quickly rose from the bench, but then nodded toward the doorway. 'There she is,' he said. Melina was only six minutes late after break time ended, but Juan and Teresa knew her better than that... they'd taught her the values of punctuality well. Maybe too well, and she feels hampered? Teresa thought for a brief second as she bit her lip. She watched her daughter walk timidly up to Serpenti and hand him three Snickers bars. Juan had to breathe a slight, barely audible sigh of relief at how easily Serpenti was able to flash a gracious smile and thank Melina for bringing the bars to him. Melina smiled in return, and Juan noted it was a smile of pure relief... the child had been very worried about something... but what? Juan thought to himself. She knows how Serpenti feels about her... that he looks on her as a niece. Melina followed the children's trainer back to the children's exercise area, dropping the wrapper for another Snickers bar and a bottle of water into the trash bin. ...and you can use the change for a bottle of fruit juice, Teresa distinctly remembered what Serpenti had said to her. The somber looks on both Juan's and Teresa's faces wasn't lost on their friend as the regiment concluded and the three of them and Melina prepared to shower and change back into street clothes. 'I am sure the slump will end this Friday,' said Serpenti encouragingly. I'm all, I'm all, I'm all right! It's a beautiful day not a cloud in sight, So I guess I'm doin' all right! I'm all, I'm all, I'm all right! I got a good old friend here with me tonight, And I guess I'm doin' all right! Well I guess I'm doin' all right! All right! Teresa smiled politely but then excused herself. She went to the concession stand nearest the workout room and approached the counterman, describing Melina to him. 'Yes, she came here,' said the counterman. 'She held up four fingers and said the word, 'peanut' very slowly and haltingly, like she was repeating something she'd been told. Unfortunately I don't speak Spanish, and I couldn't really speak to her in a way she'd follow. I knew she wanted four candy bars with peanuts, but I didn't know which one.' Teresa nodded slowly. 'Then what?' she asked. 'So I showed her a Snickers bar, the closest one to where I was standing at the time... Snickers bars have lots of peanuts,' said the counterman. 'She looked at me kind of funny, like she was afraid of something, and then finally put six dollars on the counter. I gave her the four Snickers bars and then gave her the correct change.' 'Did she ask you for a bottle of water?' Teresa asked. 'No,' said the counterman. 'She bought that from the vending machine over there.' He pointed to the machine he saw Melina buy the water bottle from. Teresa walked over to examine the vending machine. Each button was clearly marked with a small picture and name of a different beverage-- Veryfine orange juice, apple juice, grape juice, lemonade, and Evian water-- in both English and Spanish. Well I hate to see this evening end... God only knows when I'll see you again, just Send a fax or send me a letter, Or give me a call, that would even be better! Give the kids a kiss for me and say hello to the family, and Tell them all my future's lookin' bright, Well, I miss 'em but I'm doin' all right. I said I miss 'em but I'm doin' all right! When everyone gathered together at the exit to get lunch, Juan stopped and wouldn't move. Serpenti stood beside his friend, a comforting hand on Juan's shoulder. 'Talk to me, Teresa,' Juan said. Teresa smiled at them both, though she seemed a hundred miles away. 'I'm all right,' she said. 'Just a little hungry... both for lunch, and for an end to this damned slump.' 'I can promise you lunch,' said Stuart with a small smile. 'Ending the slump is up to you, when you and Serpenti meet Shadow and Pure Thunder this Friday.' Teresa laughed lightly. 'Then I'll make sure we get it,' she said. 'I'm doing all right.' But neither Juan nor Serpenti believed it. The two men looked at each other with the same expression of concern for a few seconds, before Serpenti nodded to Juan with a look that said, 'Give her time.' I'm all, I'm all, I'm all right! It's a beautiful day not a cloud in sight, So I guess I'm doin' all right! I'm all, I'm all, I'm-I'm-I'm-I'm-I'm all right! Got a good old friend here with me tonight, And I guess I'm doin' all right! Well I guess I'm doin' all right! I'm doin' all riiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiigggghhhtt!!!! I'm all right, Jo Dee Messina, “1998
