Transition, part 4

21 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 21st, 2003 'So, Yosh,' I said, as I spat blood onto the pavement, 'explain to me again how this was supposed to be a good time?' 'Well,' she replied, wiping sweat-slick hair out of her eyes, 'what I had PLANNED was a big surprise going away party for you with the rest of the IWL workers.' 'So,' I stretched my neck and heard a few pops, 'you knew nothing about the ninja?' Yoshiko gave me that look. You know the one. 'No, Lion. I didn't know about the freaking ninja.' *** Perhaps I should set the scene here. Lets rewind about an hour, shall we? It's been a week and a half since I handed in my resignation to IWL. I'm in the back seat of a car doing, by my estimation, about seventy miles an hour -- and I'm blindfolded. I should have realized when the blindfold came into play that my day was completely doomed. It's so rare something good comes out of being blindfolded. Not entirely impossible of course, I knew this girl once who? erm, anyway, it's rare. In the drivers' seat is fashion plate, martial artist, businesswoman, and one of the few people who can actually scare me: Kakita Yoshiko. This was all her idea. ''Shiko, seriously. Tell me where we're going already.' I swear I heard her roll her eyes. 'Lion, I'm not going to say this again -- it's a SURPRISE. Surprises don't WORK if you know about them in ADVANCE.' 'Maybe, but the last time I was blindfolded and led somewhere, Ishigami Kunoi hit me over the head with a steel chair. And you KNOW how much I hate that!' 'Yes, you big baby, I know. You're not going to get hit with a chair -- at least not unless you keep up your constant whining.' The conversation continued a lot like that for the next twenty minutes or so -- I'd let her blindfold me and drag me off to some 'surprise', so I figured I'd earned the right to annoy her. What were friends for, after all? Finally, the car came to a rolling stop. I heard her door open, then mine, and was yanked out of the car. 'Come on, Lion,' she said, dragging me by the wrist, 'this way.' I let myself be led along by the hand, listening for any kind of hints as to where we were going. The rhythmic patter of our footsteps was briefly interrupted by the squeak of a door swinging open, and then closing behind us. Shortly thereafter, we started to descend steps. I reached out, and found a cold metal railing. After going down what couldn't have been more than a level, we were walking straight forward again. I felt a curtain or something brush against me, and bright light began to seep in around the edges of my blindfold. Yoshiko came to a stop, and put a hand on my chest bringing me to a stop as well. 'Okay, Lion,' said Yoshiko, as she untied my blindfold, 'you can look.' The blindfold fell away, and I rubbed my eyes as they adjusted to the lights. My vision cleared, and I looked around. To the left and right of me were rows of seats, and as I looked down, I saw a sturdy metal ramp beneath me. Looking up, I saw the bright lights that had hurt my eyes, and finally, looking straight ahead? looking straight ahead I saw a four-cornered ring, with 'IWL' emblazoned on the mat. 'Yoshiko? this is IWL arena. Why'd you bring me here?' 'Well, that'd be the surprise I was talking about. So?. SURPRISE!' Yoshiko's voice echoed throughout the empty arena, but nothing happened. She furrowed her brow, looking perturbed. 'I said? SURPRISE!!' Still, only her echo answered her. 'What the hell?' muttered Yoshiko, 'where is everybody?' 'Otherwise engaged, Yoshiko-chan!' shouted a familiar voice. Suddenly, a huge figure burst through the curtains, blindsiding me with a punch and sending me tumbling down the ramp. As I rolled towards the ring, Yoshiko immediately dropped into a fighting stance, slashing out with her long legs at the knees of our assailant. With surprising speed, he leapt over her attack, executing a forward flip and landing a few feet away from her. It was just then that I regained my footing, and both of us got a good look at the attacker. Standing a full head taller than me was a mountain-like man in a fine teal suit. Jewelry glittered on his fingers as he flexed them and closed them into fists. The lights reflected off of his mirrored sunglasses and cleanly shaven head. He grinned broadly, like a shark. 'Yoritomo Danjuro,' I spat, feeling my body tense reflexively, 'good to see you. I suppose your daddy sent you?' 'Genbu-sama,' said Danjuro, his deep bass voice reverberating through the arena, 'did indeed give me the opportunity to deal with you personally. He gave you to me as a gift, Steel Lion.' 'Where are the other IWL fighters, Dajuro!' hissed Yoshiko, her fingers curling like claws. 'At home, if they know what's good for them,' replied Danjuro, laughing. 'Father made it eminently clear what would happen to anyone who got in the way of the Steel Lion's lesson in humility.' This time, it was my turn to laugh. 'Better men than you have tried to teach me humility, Danjuro. All of them have failed. You've never beaten me in the ring even once -- what makes you think you can take me now?' 'Well, I'm cheating, of course.' With that, Danjuro snapped his fingers. Suddenly, black-clad men sprung up all over the arena -- dozens of them. My jaw dropped. 'Ninja?!' I shouted, 'Where the hell did you get freakin' NINJA from?' 'The internet, of course.' Was Danjuro's reply. Then all hell broke loose.
