A Bloody History...What Does the Future Hold?

8 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 8th - 04 **For centuries this planet has seen the fight between good and evil stretched to the limits, and sometimes even beyond the limits. This battle has been faught between separte factions, it has been faught between individuals, and it has been faught within one's self, yet it seems as though we never determine a true winner of the battle. It seems as though no matter what side comes out victorious in a battle, the two sides are bound to meet again, in some cases with even more built up on the line than in the last battle. Death is sometimes thought to be the only way to definately clarify a true victor in these battles between good and otherwise, but more often than not death is a reason for more blood to be shed, a chain reaction will often follow whether violence comes in the form of suicide, gang warfare, or guerilla fighting. But as far as many men are concerned, death is the easy way out, suffering is often the preferred path to the victory** - SAN ANTONIO, TEXAS: THE ALAMO - .:The centuries-old rebel hold up - turned - war museum stands before us as the night's moon casts an ominous shadow over the historic piece of Texan heritage. The stars light up the night sky as the flags representing the state of Texas and the United States of America flow in the wind that streams across the battle-worn fringe just above the entrance to the Alamo. Suddenly we hear a voice coming from the left of our view, the voice is of Takahashi Jetsui:. TAKAHASHI: The Alamo. The place that has housed the anti-establishment rebels of the past, and has been the center piece of the Texas Revolution of 1835. .:The camera turns and we are exposed to Takahashi sitting on a wooden fence to the left, peering at the monumental building:. TAKAHASHI: Men who faught for freedom were held up here, and later murdered because of their titles as 'rebels.' But I pose these questions on you; how can humans who are supposed to be fighting the good fight be considered the bad guys? How can men that only want to bring good to their people be slaughtered in battle? How can these men even be opposed? The answer is more simple than may be first thought, it's because humans fear what is best for them. .:Takahashi gets off of the fence and steps into the dirt ground in front of the Alamo. The face-painted, trench-coated fighter proceeds towards the building. The wind picks up and throws a little bit of dirt into the air around us and Takahashi, as well as causing Takahashi's long black hair and trench coat to be taken in the current of air. Takahashi approaches the building and puts his head back as though he is examining the structure. Takahashi takes a couple more steps towards the building, and places his hand on the stone wall surrounding the front entrance and appears to get some sort of vibe:. TAKAHASHI: So much blood has been spilt over this structure. This building has seen the fight between good and evil close up, on the front line. Some of the greatest fighters of all times have been through here, and even some of the most notorious rebels this planet has ever seen have been captured and imprisioned in this place. .:Takahashi gives the building one last glance before proceeding to walk through the front doors. Once inside, we see a long hall of flags, memorials, plaques and statues. There are also what appears to be record books on top of podiums, protected under glass plating. Takahashi proceeds down the cooridor, turning his head from left right observing each of the pieces of Alamo history. Finally, Takahashi makes a left turn towards a wall behind a row of concrete pillars. This part is lit up by a row of lights sitting below the wall. Once Takahashi approaches the wall, we can see that the wall is filled with a row of paintings, with some sort of slogan overhead of the paintings. We look up at the slogan, and we can see in plain English that it reads 'Remember the Alamo.' Amogst the portraits that hang on the wall, we see pictures of Texas war heroes such as Sam Houston and David Crockett:. TAKAHAASHI: These are the 'heroes' of Texas. These are the men that are regarded by millions in the western world as the saviours of the south. Yet I am still questioning whether or not these men are truly heroes of the west, or merely gun-toating savages that stole from the poor, to make the rich even richer. However, my concern does not lie in the American mythology that is the Alamo, my concerns lie in the premise surrounding the years of revolution and battles. My concern lies in the irony that has come to define our world today, and particularly the recent events I have come across in my chosen path in the professional wrestling community. .:Takahashi turns to his right, and begins walking down the lang wall of celebrated texan war heroes, all the while his silhouette being cast on the rows of pillars be the lights beaming up at him from the ground, and reflecting off the walls. Finally Takahashi reaches the end of the row, and proceeds through a large swinging door, and down a staircase that appears to hace been recently redone:. CLICK! CLICK! CLICK! .:Takahashi decends down the concrete staircase patiently. After what seemed to be about four landings later, Takahashi reaches another door with a couple of sings on it. The signs read: PRISIONERS CELLS ================> <=============WASHROOMS SOUVENIR SHOP ==================> So Takahashi opens the door, and enters a hallway, lined by what appears to be the orignal stone walls that the Alamo was orinally built on. Takahashi proceeds in the direction of the souvenir shop and the prisioner cells. Once at the end of the hallway we see that there is another hallway, but this one is lined by prison cells as opposed to the regular stone walls. Takahashi procedes to the first cell on the left side, and we see that on the first cell, as on every other cell, is a plaque with a little tidbit of history on it. Every cell also contains wax replicas of Mexican prisoners. In this first cell is a statue of a man with a moustache, and what appears to be a Mexican soldier uniform:. TAKAHASHI: (Methodically walking alongside the row of cells with his hands behind his back) It's a humourous thing really. These men were all captured because of their actions, their actions of course including gunfire, hand to hand combat, and overall bloody battles. But this is not what strikes me as funny. What puts a small grin on my face is that these men were taking these actions why? They were taking these actions because they were fighting for their freedom, and the freedom of their families and friends. So in hindsight, these men were inocent of all crimes, and only guilty of trying to fight for what in naturally the property of all humans; freedom. These men were heroes in the eyes of the Mexican people, but scum in the eyes of the American southerners. .:Takahashi stops for a moment, and reaches into the pocket of his long, black trench coat. Takahashi pulls out a cigarette, followed by another reach into the inside pocket of the trench coat, with Takahashi retrieving a zippo lighter this time. Takahashi ignites the flint of the zippo with a quick spark, and then brings the lighter to the cigarette sitting between his lips. The cigarette gains a small glow, and Takahashi released a plum of tobacco smoke:. TAKAHASHI: This only proves how a people can change over the years. Back in the days of anti-Mexican mentalities, these men were shot, killed, and imprisoned on a regular basis, while the Texans celebrated every time they brought in a new catch. But these days, it seems as though the procedures have been switched, and the heroes are now the ones being persecuted for their actions, even actions that they had no part in. .:Takahashi turns to us and flashes a sadistic grin, before continuing on his way down the cell block in the basement of the Alamo. Takahashi walks a couple more steps in the slow manner that he has been all along, and finally Takahashi reaches the final pair of cells at the end of the block:. TAKAHASHI: In past times, these prisoners were rebels, they were indeed fighters, and they definately had anti-establishment beliefs and values. These days, in our backwards world, it's funny how the innocent 'knight in shining armor' can quickly be mistaken for the demonic, twisted parasite of society. All of his life, Jesse Jamester has been in the eyes of the public as the super hero, but at the drop of a hat, he was turned into the most sadistic criminals of our time. Isn't it funny? Isn't it funny how a man of such high regard in our business and go from the loving husband/hero to all, and suddenly be plucked from the plateau and placed in the same cells that at one time or another housed the most demented minds of our time? Dahmer, Manson, Ramirez, Bundy... and for a brief moment, JAMESTER. What a wonderful time we are in. .:Takahashi takes one last drag of his cigarette, all the while grinning and chuckling to himself as he sucks in, and then throws the cigarette to the ground. With a quickly spark of ashes, the cigarette hits the concrete floor, and Takahashi steps on it. Takahashi then leans his head back and allows the smoke to pour into the air:. TAKAHASHI: (Continuing his walk down the cell block) I am the bringer of pain for you Jesse Jamester, how does it feel? I feel a surgance like no other Jesse. I feel a tingle in every part of my body, every single time I think of what I have been allowed to do to you, and everything I will do to you in the coming weeks. I'm not done Jesse, WE'RE not done Jesse. .:Behind a couple looming strands of hair hanging in front of Takahahi's eyes, Takahashi peers into the camera and grins a sadistic grin:.
