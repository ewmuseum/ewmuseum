Paranoia And Preparation Part 1 (Wrestlecade RP #1)

14 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Week Of 5/14/05 Merriam-Webster dictionary defined paranoia as “a tendency on the part of an individual or group toward excessive or irrational suspiciousness and distrustfulness of others”. The word origin came from the New Latin and Greek paranous meaning “madness”. There were plenty who thought such a definition applied to people they knew, but fewer people realized their own behavior could fall into the category. He knew his behavior did, and he had lived with his own paranoia ever since they rescued the man. His life was ruined as far as he cared, and no one had been able to break him out of the funk he had fallen into. Long ago, he had abandoned everything he believed when he fled to America. Long periods of self-delusion, removal from the situation and otherwise embracing all science had to offer him had freed him from the past of darkness. All of that ended in the Dakotas when the dark soul was vanquished. There was a law he learned long ago. 1st Law Of Thermodynamics Energy is neither created, or destroyed. At the base, the very foundation of science was now feeding his paranoia. To accept the laws of the second life he had found he discarded the old rules. Now though, his own eyewitness accounts showed him that he needed to accept both sets of rules. He was faced with the prospect that everything he believed was true, from both lives. Such realizations were frightening. So much so, that he only controlled the fear through medication. Salvation from the second set of rules? Another of the small, pink pills went into his mouth. The bitter taste didn’t bother him. In fact, he had come to enjoy the taste because soon came the relief. He swallowed the pill down a dry throat, no longer needing plenty of water like he once did. So much has changed’I’ve changed Dirt and sweat seemed to cover his body, but he no longer cared. The family had moved away when the change had come, and the house had since been sold for cash. Living as he did, no one could find him unless he allowed them to. He paid cash, a year in advance, for the horrible apartment in Oak Cliff. No utilities in his name, all of that was handled by the management company for every tenant. No, he was off the grid completely now. Horribly furnished, the apartment didn’t offer him much except time for reflection. No television, no stereo and nothing worth stealing for any of the neighborhood hoodlums. He had six bookshelves, all made with plywood and cinderblocks. Resting upon those shelves he kept the research materials. Books from the first life, books from the second life and books from the new life all lined the shelves. Most of the time he worked on translating the books into English so he could understand them. Late at night, with all the windows drawn closed he retrieved the Dell laptop from beneath the hidden floor panel. All his handwritten notes were transcribed into Word. Cataloguing everything he needed was taking longer than he expected, but in translating by hand he was committing far more to memory than he ever did in medical school. The echoes of the haunting images and feelings of that night had released a vision in his own head. The old powers were bubbling up once more. Clarity was something he no longer had. The visions were jumbled, and he wasn’t sure what was coming. Except for the one clear part, he would see the dark one once again. He had been forced away, but he had come back somehow. Descriptive texts always seemed cloaked in metaphors that further disguised the text. He had never been a literary powerhouse, and learning on the fly was becoming bothersome. Of course, not learning would only lead to more pain and suffering. He would not allow that either. He had been chosen, for reasons unknown to him, and he would not ignore his calling a second time in his life. So he continued pouring over the research materials, learning all he could in the time he had before the end days arrived. Within the cramped apartment, he sat at the old wooden table sweating as the air-conditioner was not working again. The beads of sweat rolled off his baldhead, down the back of his neck to be absorbed by the white, sleeveless under shirt. Already a second skin, he barely took any note of the sweat as his mind focused on the text. Low candle light from the saint candles illuminated the passages. Saint candles were surprisingly affordable. The local dollar stores carried them, and so he had light and some extra protection as the unholy words spilled forth onto the pages. Sheet after sheet filled the spiral notebooks as his handwriting continued the flow of knowledge. Signs that his obsessive-compulsive nature had not abandoned him abounded here as the books lined up perfectly, the candle wax was never allowed to sully the table top and most notably his script-like writing was perfect in every regard. Time had lost meaning long ago, and he hardly ever realized the time when he worked inside the darkened room. He did not like using the lights because if his electrical bill was ever too high it might attract the attention of the landlord who carefully checked each bill. Knock Knock Sudden sound broke him from the translation. He put the bookmarker into the old tome and closed the cover down. Next, he placed the pen in the spiral notebook and closed it. Standing slowly, he realized he once again had remained seated too long judging by the stiffness in his joints. A stuttered, pained walk took him over to the door. Checking before opening, he saw it was simply his elder neighbor. He unlocked, unbolted, unlatched and unbolted again before opening the door. “Hello Mrs. Washington, how are you today?” “I’m well. I’m well. I was going down to the market, and wondered if I could pick anything up for you Robert?” She asked in the slow drawl behind a thick accent. Mrs. Washington had been raised in the country, and widowed now for ten years. Kindness touched his heart, and he smiled at her. “No thank you Mrs. Washington. I am doing fine.” “Okay, well if you ever need me to pick something up let me know Robert.” She said before turning around and making her way down the hall to the stairs. She had a waddle brought on by a bad hip and old bones. Fortunately, Mrs. Washington also had a sons and daughters living all over the country. The apartment she lived in was actually owned by her, a rarity in Texas. She refused to abandon it, and her family had provided her with a phone with unlimited long distance services. He had long ago “borrowed” her telephone lines for his use when she was away. Mrs. Washington always told him when she was leaving, and so he always knew when to use the phone under a false bottom of the dresser. Locking up once again, he stole away into the bedroom and removed the bottom drawer. There was two hundred dollars in tens in the empty space, but that was simply a ruse in case anyone broke in. Slapping several pressure points, the false bottom rose up on it’s own. There, beneath an inch of particle board, he had over one hundred thousand in ready cash and the telephone. No light was on, indicating the phone was not in use. He picked up the handset and dialed the voicemail number in New York City. New York City was his choice because anyone who looked for him there would be looking for a needle in a haystack. Specifically, one man who wasn’t really there among a population of eight million souls would be a hard man to find. Logging in took but a moment, and there was only one message. Mugawbe, this is Jake Hix. Contact me. We have business in the darkness once more. Disbelieving, he pushed the button to replay the message. Mugawbe, this is Jake Hix. Contact me. We have business in the darkness once more. Theoretically, no one except a select few on his list of contacts would have the phone number to contact him at. They also knew to address him only as Mugawbe as a code to say everything was alright. Finally, the last phrase was the final code phrase they agreed on long ago. Of course, he recognized the voice of Jake Hix. Then again, theoretically, someone could have been captured and compromised. The voice could be synthesized with the right software. Everything could be a set-up to root him out of his self-imposed exile. Choices abounded before him. Paranoia told him to ignore everything he had heard, and resolve himself to his own protections. Logic said communication was safe, and all the proper phrases had been used. Logic won out, and so he dialed another number that rang six times, then seemed to go dead. He dialed another number that would go to a cellular telephone he knew Jake always carried. “This is Jake.” “The darkness beckons.” The code phrase was the final security test. Jake’s response took a moment as he stepped away for privacy, “And yet we resist with the light of God.” “Jake, it has been a long time since we have spoken.” Robert replied. “Yes it has.” Jake agreed, but the trepidation was evident in his voice. “There are new developments Doctor. I need to meet with you.” “To what end?” His mind reeled against the idea of leaving his refuge for public. “The darkness is not contained anymore. He has returned, and what I will call havoc has come with him.” Now his voice betrayed his own fear, “He is back?” “Yes, and growing more powerful by the day. I must meet with you, and I must also speak with Veston.” Jake explained. “Can you arrange the meeting?” Fear and darkness seemed to creep over him like a cold wind embracing naked flesh. He shivered, despite the sweat rolling off his body he shivered at the thought of what was coming for him once again. “Doctor?” Jake said again. “Robert, are you still there?” “Yes.” He fought past the anxiety, thankful for the pink pill he had taken earlier. “I can arrange the meeting. We must meet soon. We should meet Wednesday, here in Dallas. I will make arrangements to meet at’we should meet at?” Locations swirled in his mind, but he dismissed them all. “What about Babe’s in Roanoke?” Jake suggested. “We can grab the table in the back and on Tuesday, the place will be empty.” “Sounds good.” He agreed. “Eight o’clock.” “See you then.” Jake hung up. Hands shaking, he replaced the headset in the cradle. The false bottom went back down, locked in place to not be seen. He grabbed the drawer he had pulled out, and took careful aim to get the tracks right. The bottom drawer slid back into the dresser, and he stayed there staying at the floor. The weight of the decisions he was making were beginning to press down on him, and he could begin to feel his chest tighten up as he knelt there. Staring into the dark carpet? Starring into oblivion? . . . . . . . Situated behind a dense jungle of tropical foliage on the Yucatan Peninsula stood a retreat away from the worries of the world. The secret was only thirty miles away from Cancun, but none of the wild partygoers to the annual Spring Break party city would ever know about the oasis so close by. Maroma Beach Resort is a hotel and spa. Every building there was handcrafted by local carpenters and builders, keeping respect for the environment firmly entrenched in their design. No machines or electricity were used in the construction of the buildings, and Maroma would never use more then one-tenth of the available 500-plus acres. The ecological balance would be maintained under strict guidelines established as part of the vision. The beach, pure white sand, was now his living room. He lounged beneath an umbrella, resting his tired body on the lounge chair. He was peaceful here, and needed a rest from the toils of his battles. Wounds were covered, but never closed. Damage was wrapped or cared for, but they had never been healed. Along the beach, he could gaze out over the azure waters. Finding solitude and peace in the deep ocean, he finally was truly at rest. Three months at the resort had restored much of his body, but more importantly he had finally restored his soul. Another month at the resort would bring back his youth, reversing years of abuse. The waitress came over. Consuela was an attractive young woman from one of the local villages, just like every other waitress. She approached with a smile that reflected the happiness of the world. She spoke in English, “Would you like another drink while you relax?” “Yes.” He looked up at her. Everything was so wonderful, he wondered why he had never taken such a trip before now. “Can you bring me a Pina Colada?” “Yes.” She nodded. “Anything else?” “Not now, maybe later.” He replied. For years, he had battled on a very small scale against evil. He always wondered if he would ever be called upon for a greater purpose. Fate did not disappoint him, and he was brought forth to battle the dark one among a host of heroes. For years, he had been named witch, conjurer, sorcerer, medicine man and all sort of names. Always, there was the sneer of contempt in the voice of those who spoke the words. Caring about such contempt no longer mattered. He now knew that his work had real meaning, beyond even the warding off of lower spirits. Sometimes, the satisfaction of a job well done could sustain a man through his life. Consuela quickly returned with the drink. Set in a green glass goblet, the sweet and cold pina colada was made very well at Maroma. With plenty of rum to keep him satisfied, he had been sipping the umbrella drinks the entire time only occasionally broken by beers. The drinks made him feel like he was more a vacationer than a beer. He stood up, taking the goblet with him as he set off down the white sand beach. Walking forward, he no longer had the deep pain in his back and legs. For too long, he had accepted the old pains as a burden he was meant to bear. Surgery, followed by physical therapy, had relieved so much of his burden. He never thought he would live without the pain again, but his new friends had helped relieve him through their contacts in the medical field. The scars on his back and leg were a small sacrifice. He didn’t hold much vanity for his appearance though’they were a testament to the wars. He walked back to the resort, and motioned Consuela over. “Consuela, can you get me lunch” Just whatever Chef Rowe is cooking. I’ll take whatever she’s making because it’s always fantastic. Just bring it over with a bucket of Modelo Negro.” He asked. Consuela nodded, “Of course. I’ll be along shortly.” Every cottage had their own little outdoor sitting area, but he liked the view from the beach. He retook his position in the same lounge chair with the small, red square table along with the oversized umbrella. He found sitting in the shade on days like these may have been the greatest therapy he had ever known in his life. Nothing compared to this. He found what he needed’finally. Chef Stephanie Rowe ensured that every guest of the Maroma Resort was delighted with the dishes she prepared. Chef Rowe enjoyed presenting a myriad of flavors to satiate even the most refined palate. Her menu incorporated a selection of fresh seafood from the reef. To ever guests delight, the lobster caught nearby was included as a standard selection in the main courses and appetizers. Part of the five hundred plus acres of Maroma was devoted to growing the produce Chef Rowe would utilize. There were fruits from the sea grapevines, fruit from the tropical trees, a small vegetable garden maintained by an old farmer named Timas and of course all the coconuts from the palm trees. Of course, the food was served on the finest bone china, fine linens and exquisite crystal. The Flaming Mexican Coffee was an especially popular way to finish the meal. Made with seven liquors, he enjoyed watching preparation but found the drink to sweet for his own tastes. As he waited for the food to arrive, he tried to decide what to do the next day. Maroma offered all sorts of delights and distractions. He had taken advantage of all of them. The so-called “Mayan Riviera” offere dplenty of explorations that were new to him. He had explored the Mayan ruins, seeing Chichen Itza and Tikal. Man’s hope for the future was reinforced thanks to the Sian Ka’an Biosphere Reserve. Binoculars, a bottle of wine and time were all he needed at the Contoy Island Bird Sanctuary. Just around the actual property he found plenty of fun. Kayaks, windsurfing, and kite boarding had occupied numerous hours during his days. He had glided over the surface of Maroma Bay on the luxury fishing boats. All sorts of fish made his way into the boat like barracuda, snapper, snook and grouper. Even more exciting was the deep sea fishing excursions in the yacht. He had to learn the art of deep sea fishing, and watched as the other guests scored marlin, sailfish, tuna, dorado, wahoo and barracuda while he only found water. Luckily, on his last outing he had finally caught a marlin of his own. The sailboat he had enjoyed was amazing. On the twenty-seven foot catamaran he toured the waters to find sea turtles, dolphins and an amazing rainbow of fish for entertaining his eyes. Seeing such beauty, he went back to the same beds for snorkeling and scuba diving. Once, he had taken advantage of the accompaniment of the marine biologist who studied from Maroma. The tour was educational, teaching as he watched nature’s fragile underwater beauty. The spa was one place he had never visited. There were all sorts of treatments that were available, but he wasn’t a fan of such treatment. Too much machismo was left in his body to give himself over to that kind of treatment. One treatment did appeal to him, the Maroma’s temazcal was an exotic Mayan steam bath. Designed to be a group experience for two to eight people, the ritual lasted almost two full hours. The participants would be provided an initial cleansing, purifying steam coupled with a mineral bath inside an energy-enhancing pyramid structure, an ocean swim and restorative tea after the experience. The ritual was based on the Indian sweat lodge used by Mexican tribes as well as by Native American. Supposedly, it could be a very spiritual experience. You could meditate within the heat, find your emotional core to release tension and some had even been led to see visions while in this state. Practicality also provided a physical cleaning of the body to remove toxins. Consuela came by with a plate loaded with food. She set the plates of steaming sustenance down before him. The soup smelled incredible, and he knew at once it was the lobster soup with coconut, wild spinach and cilantro. He saw the crab cakes with the fire and ice salsa relish and for the main course there would be marlin with Portobello mushrooms and sweet potatoes. The bucket of Modelo Negro beer smothered in ice went onto the sand next to him. Food came, and not long after restful sleep. . . . . . . . Jake couldn’t physically feel the cold air from the outside world, but he felt it deep inside his soul as he sat next to the fire nursing a pint. Unexpected cold from the snowstorm in the Midwest had brought the temperatures down in North Texas. The warmth rolled off the flames in waves. Jake took no pleasure or comfort in the heat, only staring into the fire like it was an entertaining picture into his a dream he was trying to remember. Loss was an overpowering emotion at the moment. Jake was whisked in his mind away from the cold, moist air outside. Memories carried him back to another time and place as he waited for the meeting with his contacts to roll around. He feared the meeting because he knew more loss had to be coming. The Texas landscape was covered in snow in Jake’s memory when he had lost so much. Jake was sitting at a table, enjoying his bowl of stew. Susan had made a hearty bowel of vegetable beef stew for them. Susan had tried to make everything special, knowing she and Jake would only have limited time together before Jake flew back to Florida. Jake was in a great mood. He had just won the Florida Heavyweight Title the previous weekend for a small Miami promotion, and it was the very first title he had captured. Jake knew he would be losing it in another few days, but the joy of the first title reign couldn’t be diminished. Jake could parlay the title win into more money, more opportunities, and perhaps most importantly something he did on his own outside of Texas. Jake was enjoying the stew. Susan brought Jake a cold bottle of beer out of the refrigerator. She lightly ran her hand across Jake’s shoulders before rubbing them firmly, “You’re tense.” “Part of the job Honey.” Jake laughed, “If I was any more relaxed I would be dead in terms of the job.” “Well, with the title things should begin getting better. Right?” Susan asked as she continued kneading Jake’s thick muscles. “Baby, things will most definitely begin improving. Things are looking good.” Jake took a large spoonful of the stew and swallowed it down. “Your brother called.” Susan said. Jake looked at Susan with a big smile, “Would you care to narrow that down just a little bit for me?” “John called.” “Big John?” Jake was pleasantly surprised. “What did John want?” “He said something about an opportunity. He wanted you to call him about it. Something about a league and their interest in you. Universe or something like that was the name he mentioned?” Susan shrugged Jake dropped the spoon and looked back at Susan, “Universal Wrestling?” “That was it!” Susan smiled sweetly at Jake. “Did he leave a number where he could be reached?” Jake was excited. “Yes he did.” Susan began looking around the room for where he had written the message. Jake Hix cracked open the beer and drained it. Jake waited, not too patiently, while Susan frantically looked for the message. Susan found the paper and handed it to Jake. Jake walked into the living room and sat down on the couch. Jake picked up the phone and dialed his brother. “Hello?” John’s voice sounded tired. Jake’s sounded full of life, “John! It’s Jake!” “Jake! Good to hear from you. You got my message?” John began to come back awake. Jake nodded even though John wasn’t in the room, “I got it. What’s going on?” “Pitt and I have been talking you up, and the tape from Florida just sealed the deal. They want to bring you up here for a Dark Match just to make sure, but unless you completely screw the pooch little brother then you’re going to be staring at a huge contract!” John was the one laughing now. Jake tried to speak, but he couldn’t. His voice wasn’t working at the moment. “You still there Jake?” John asked. “Yeah!” Jake was smiling like a kid on Christmas day. “When do they want me up there?” “We’re doing a Saturday show in Oakland, and they want you here for it.” Jake looked over at Susan, “Tomorrow?” “Yes. Get on a damn plane and get out here.” John yelled with a small laugh. “Okay, I’ll call you when I get out there.” Jake hung up the phone. Jake looked up at Susan, “I’m going to Oakland, and if I impress them I’ll sign the contract out there!” Susan actually jumped up and down as she shrieked. Susan ran over and tackled Jake back onto the couch, “I love you!” Jake held Susan softly as he looked into her sparkling blue eyes that seemed to mirror his own, “I love you too baby. I love you too.” Jake and Susan began kissing with a heated fever. Jake moved his hands inside Susan’s flannel shirt to the soft flesh beneath. They became entwined together. Jake finished his second pint. He motioned to the waitress. “Want another one sir?” The waitress asked pleasantly in a thick accent. The Tipperary Inn was a good Irish Pub in Dallas, and they hired plenty of Irish and British locals who still retained their accents. “Yes.” Jake said plainly. The waitress moved away to get Jake another drink. Jake simply kept staring into the fire. Jake had done a great job. The UWA Management asked Jake to actually win the dark match, and bring the crowd over to his side. Jake played to the crowd from the moment he stepped through the curtain to walk to the ring until he turned back for one last celebratory look before returning to the back. Jake had dominated the action, and working with someone he had never met before he felt that they had done a good job telling a story inside the squared circle. Jake had taken an early dominating stance only to be foiled by his masked opponent. To the crowd’s delight Jake returned to dominate and win in the end. John and Pitt were waiting for him when he came through the curtain. “Good job Jake!” Pitt slapped his brother on the back. John agreed, “Not bad little brother. I think you just put a big exclamation mark on the end of your name as far as the brass is concerned.” “I’ll second that one Jake.” Pitt was smiling proudly. “Well, it was the kind of match that highlights my strong points.” Jake said. “There’s a hot crowd out there tonight guys.” Pitt and John looked at each other briefly then laughed. “There’s always a hot crowd here in the UWA Jake.” John nodded at his brother as he spoke. Pitt concurred, “This is one of the greatest places to work, and you’re going to make a lot of connections here Jake. This is an opportunity to do whatever you want Jake. Take the opportunity to learn it all. Here comes Nigel now.” Nigel Hawthethorn walked up to the group, “Pitt and John, I think the production manager was looking for you.” Pitt and John just smiled as they walked off. Jake yelled after them, “I’ll see you guys later.” Nigel looked at Jake, “I’ll let you get cleaned up and dressed, but after that we’ll meet in my office.” Jake looked where Nigel was pointing. “Sounds good. I’ll be right there.” Jake quickly went to the dressing room. He showered, changed, and made his way back to Nigel’s office. Nigel’s secretary had him wait inside. While Jake was waiting in Nigel’s office in California, Susan was getting dressed in Texas. She had errands to run, but she made sure to take her cell phone with her. Jake would be calling with news about the opportunity with the UWA as soon as he could. Susan walked out to her Mustang and started up the powerful engine. Susan pulled out of the driveway, and made her way to Interstate 35. Susan was moving quickly down the highway to make it to an appointment in time. Nigel gave Jake his standard presentation. Nigel relayed the marketing power of the UWA, the salary structure, and the opportunities he would be given. Nigel felt he had done a good job, “So, what do you think?” Jake considered everything he had been told, “I think your market share has been consistently weakened over time. Television ratings are down, your last Pay-Per-View was a dud, and frankly the numbers I’ve seen from the house shows have been disappointing to say the least. Given this information I would not be looking out for my own best interests if I didn’t say that the low guarantee concerns me.” Nigel looked at Jake with shock and not a little bit of confusion. “Excuse me?” “Nigel, I keep close tabs on everything that happens in the business. Just because I’m from Texas doesn’t mean I’m some uneducated, redneck idiot. I like the look of this league, but I want a better offer than that. If it means that I continue pursuing the NWA’s interest in me then so be it. I think we both have a lot to offer each other here, but if that’s your final offer as you said then I thank you for the opportunity though I won’t take advantage of it.” Jake spoke quite plainly and directly. Nigel was put off by Jake’s stern approach. Nigel took it all in, and decided that he was right when he had decided to go after Jake. Jake could be exactly what the UWA needed, and Nigel had lowballed him. “I think we can come to an arrangement you’ll like.” Nigel and Jake worked out the exact figures. Nigel had his secretary type up the numbers into the blank contract he had waiting. Nigel and Jake signed the contract and both felt good about it. Jake left the office. He pulled his cellular phone out and called Susan. Jake was shocked when a man’s voice answered, “Hello?” “Uhm, never mind. I must have dialed the wrong number.” Jake was about to hang-up. The man’s voice was somber as he spoke, “Were you trying to reach Susan Jacobs?” Jake’s heart froze, “Yes.” “Sir, I’m afraid there’s been an accident.” Jake sipped his beer slowly. Interstate 35 merges with Loop 12 in Dallas, and there had always been problems at the intersection of the highways. This time the issue had been an eighteen wheeler driven by a driver on Crank. He had swerved over and taken out Susan’s Mustang. The vehicle flipped over so many times they never could give Jake the exact number. Susan’s small frame had been snapped in the tumbling, but the doctors assured Jake that she hadn’t suffered in the accident. Jake never drove on I-35 where it merged with Loop 12. He just couldn’t handle the memories it evoked. Jake had only two days to mourn for Susan. He put her in the ground on the third morning, and then Pitt told him that Nigel was enforcing the letter of the contract. Jake began his tour with the UWA that evening. Jake still mourned Susan. Tonight he nursed the beer while reliving the pain he had when she left this mortal coil. Jake was alone, and sometimes he was more alone than anyone else on the planet. Added to all of this were the upcoming events. The final snow of cold front of the season were simply the last bitter reminder of the losses he had endured, and the losses he would face in the future. For this stretch, Jake was staying at his brother’s home in Deep Ellum. The entire building was owned by the Hix family. The five story building included the garage on the first floor, a den with library on the second floor, dining room and kitchen on the third floor, office and spare bedroom on the fourth floor and the master bedroom on the top floor. Being Deep Ellum, the roof was also an area to lounge and relax with furniture, sound system and lighting. Deep Ellum is the renovated warehouse district located just three blocks east of downtown Dallas. Over one hundred and seventy sprawling acres, the section of town was known as the alternative from the 'norm.' Many if not every band of significance from North Texas was born or played the clubs on scene. Jake finished his pint, and dropped forty on the table to cover his drinks and a nice tip. He headed outside into the cold. He wondered why he found the mid-forty degree weather so daunting. As he motioned a cab over and slipped inside he realized it was not the weather he found so cold and daunting. He could feel the chilled air of the future. The past was haunting, but the future was frightening. . . . . . . . Vicoprofen, the slightly trickier cousin of Vicodin. Both of the medications had the prescription pain medication hydrocodone. However, while Vicodin also includes acetaminophen like that found in Tylenol, Vicoprofen replaces it with ibuprofen exactly like the active ingredient in Advil. The long list of warnings that had come with the prescription were like some kind of apocalyptic warning list of everything that could go wrong. Side effects cannot be anticipated for the individual on medication. What happens to one man may not happen to another, and there was always that incredible room for error and variance. The list went on and on. Less common side effects may include confusion, dark tarry stool, difficulty breathing, fever, flu symptoms, frequent urination, hiccups, inflammation of the throat and nasal passages, loss of appetite, mouth ulcers, pain, ringing in the ears, skin tingling, stomach inflammation, tension, thinking abnormalities, thirst, throbbing heartbeat. Rare side effects may include abnormal dreams, agitation, allergic reaction, altered vision, asthma, bad taste, bronchitis, chalky stool, decreased sex drive, depression, difficulty swallowing, dry eyes, elevated mood, hives, hoarseness, impotence, increased cough, inflamed sinuses, inflammation of the tongue or intestines, joint pain, low blood pressure, lung congestion, mood changes, muscle ache, pneumonia, rapid or irregular heartbeat, rash, shallow breathing, skin swelling, slurred speech, teeth clenching, tremor, urinary problems, vertigo, weight loss. The long list of dangers explained why doctors never covered them when prescribing them, but rather let the customer read the dangers after they had paid for the medication. So many effects that could be dangerous and debilitating, but would it be worth the risk? Pain tolerance was a widely varied ability. Some people got a paper cut in an office building and began crying. Then there were people who could break their bones, set them back into place and go on without ever giving more than a slight grunt of pain in response. Such were the differences in men as there always were. There was not one model for existence, and God had given each man their own unique abilities to handle different stresses and tests. The mold people spoke of was always the same, but the material put into the mold was always radically different. The make of a man was truly in the fiber of his being, and what he did with that fiber established his place on the earth. Some let themselves sit and waste away, others worked themselves tirelessly until they were the strongest they could be. Even the strongest of men had weaknesses? Even the biggest pain threshold could be eclipsed at some point by the continual onslaught of a life lived too harshly. That high threshold had been crossed, and that’s why there were two large horse pills sitting on the table in front of him beside the small glass of water. He picked up the two pills in his large, rough hand. Two small pills in proportion to the size of the man holding them. Sometimes being reminded of the strength of something so small can be helpful. These two pills would cause some kind of side effect, but no one knew what they would be until the pills were taken inside the flesh. He threw the two pills into his mouth. The chalky flavor of the medicine was horrid, like some fermented flavor of vomit and bite that hurt his taste buds. He picked up the glass of water and washed the pills down his throat. The song went through his mind in perfect time and harmony. A spoonful of sugar helps the medicine go down, The medicine go down-wown. The medicine go down Just a spoonful of sugar helps the medicine go down In a most delightful way The remainder of the water chased the pills down into his stomach. There had been no food today because time was not on his side. The flight had been rough, with way too much turbulence for his tastes. He had slept fitfully, never able to find a comfortable position to sleep in. The problems became too much to put up with so he moved over to the living room. He sat up in the chair there and finally slept for four hours. Now he needed the pain to slip away so he could enjoy the world a little bit. He needed to have some relief so he could enjoy a night of peace and quiet. The pills would take around an hour to take effect. He went off into the bathroom, turning the shower on. Always, the best part about hotels was the seemingly limitless amount of hot water available. You could take a nice long shower; enjoy the steaming effect inside as you cleaned up. When the steam was fogging up the mirrors inside the well-appointed bathroom with the golden fixtures and the granite tops he stepped inside. The hot water carried away so much of the pain he remembered why it sometimes helped to be on the road. The stitches had been taken out the previous day, and the ointments had done their job as there was only one very small scar. Even it was small and so close to the eyebrow that no one really noticed it. Jake had found his measure of revenge against Ice though, and that counted three times he had put down the Hall Of Famer. The loofah and soap washed away all the day’s sweat and dirt. The grime of life, working too hard and dealing with the pain made his body feel worn out and used too quickly. This was part of salvation, part of finding renewal in the world they all lived in. After the steamy shower he stepped out and wrapped one of the thick towels around his waist. He too the pearl handled straight razor in his hand and began sharpening the razor using the leather straps. He wanted the edge to be a sharp as possible before using it on himself. Hot lather covered his head and face. He expertly removed the slight shadow of hair growing there until the skin around his goatee was smooth and clean. Aftershave followed, the stinging not even noticed amidst the other pain running through his body. He finished drying off and then rubbed lotion over his body. This time it wasn’t the slick ointment, but a simple lotion to moisten his skin and keep him feeling a little more human. He let himself air dry before getting dressed, and as he slipped his black jeans on he could feel the drugs beginning to take effect. He finished dressing and left his hotel room. He wanted to relax, have a drink despite the medication’s warning. One drink and the drugs guaranteed he would sleep. There would be no memories of the horrors he had seen, no imaginings of the horrors he hadn’t seen and no remembering the words in the diaries. Not tonight. For once he would finally be free of all those burdens. . . . Kinan is the Maya word for “the source of the sun”. Similar to the Asian concept of Chi, Kinan is often referred to as the individual inner energy. Everything about the spa was designed to relax those lucky enough to get a reservation. Cream colored stucco finishes on the walls, hand carved niches, expertly crafted arches from local artisans,shell floors complimented with river stone borders, hard wood furnishings made from the surrounding forest itself and hand sanded and polished beams welcomed the guests. Veston was being provided with the absolute best care possible. He had enjoyed a long soak in the mineral waters of the jungle. Feeling relaxed enough, he was hand bathed by the beautiful young women before being led out to one of the beach palapas for a deep massage. Lasting over an hour, the oil glazed his ebony skin. He was relaxed more than he had ever been. There was nothing greater than what he was now experiencing, and he knew he would never feel better than he was at that very moment. The hands finally pulled away, his massage over and done with. Veston was led back over to the building. He sat down in a chair, and then a young woman came in. She began filling a small basin with warm water. She added ingredients to get the right mix for the beginning of the pedicure. Veston couldn’t imagine anything better than this as his feet were washed, scrubbed and massaged. The manager of the spa came walking in. He looked relaxed in khakis and a white shirt. “Sir, you have an urgent telephone call. Can you take it now?” Veston was on guard. Only two people knew he was a guest of the hotel. He nodded, wondering what might have gone wrong so badly that he would need to be disturbed. “Yes.” He took the cordless phone from the manager. “Hello?” “Veston, it has been a long time once again my friend. I had hoped to never call you again.” The voice was nervous, with plenty of background noise all around him. Voices evoked memories. Not always as powerful as smell, the greatest memory enabler, but still very strong. He remembered the man, their friendship and also all the dark times they had lived through. “Robert, where have you been?” Veston said without thinking. He thought about the line being tapped, but the odds were so small he wasn’t concerned. “Why are you calling me here?” “Do you remember when we were children?” Robert began. “The science classes from that teacher with the funny eye. The rules of the world. God had erected the world, and everything operates under the rules he established long ago. So matter is neither created nor destroyed, and energy is neither created nor destroyed. We learned the truth, and yet we forget to yield to it.” Veston couldn’t follow this, “What are you talking about Robert?” “He is back Veston. The Dark One has returned to the earth, and the death has begun anew. We must meet once again.” Anvils falling onto his head would have hurt him less, “How do you know?” “He paid a visit to his family, and a champion has already been chosen. The slaughter began there too, but we have hope. We must meet with the brother. We must spirit away the new soul to safety. That information rests with you alone. He will need our protection, and his brother will need our guidance.” Veston nodded, “I understand. When are we supposed to meet?” I agreed to Wednesday at a place here in Dallas. Fly in, stay at the Hilton at the airport and I will contact you there with more information. We will meet with the brother Jake to discuss the details. He wants options when you come, so don’t hold anything back.” “I will bring everything I have. We will be covered.” Veston assured his old friend. “Good.” The phone line went dead. The woman looked up, “Are you okay?” “Yes, everything is fine. I’ll just be forced to cut my stay somewhat short. Can they bring me lunch here?” Veston was trying to calm himself. There was nothing he could do to force the calm over himself. “Yes, we offer a special cuisine.” The woman called out, and another young woman, this one in her early teens, came in. She spoke in Spanish to her. ? We will have food brought to you here.” Good to their word, a wonderful meal inspired by the Yucatan itself was presented. The start was a nice carpaccio of zucchini, followed by an Aquarius salad and the main course was melon ravioli with crab and ginger and a tamarind sauce along with false cauliflower couscous with grilled langoustines and a hibiscus flower reduction. Everything was divine. The last bit of peace he would experience for a long time. The wars beckoned his return once more? . . . The Marquette Hotel had plenty of expensive furnishings. He was beckoned to enjoy them all, but for the moment the only one he cared about at all was the MARQ VII Lounge. Opulence was never lost in high end hotels. Red granite flooring met deep beige walls with crafted textures. The black granite bar top was kept immaculate facing the mirrored bar with plenty of liquor. He took a seat in the low back chair on the end and waited for the bartender. “What can I get for you?” The bartender asked as he approached. Jake thought about it for a moment. He wasn’t in a scotch mood tonight. He wanted something more clean, but at the same time he needed a mixed drink of some kind. “Surprise me, but make it a simple mixed drink with vodka.” He instructed. The bartender thought for a moment, then quickly mixed a drink up. NO umbrellas or other bullshit, he simply placed the glass on a coaster before the customer. The yellow liquid beckoned. “Low End.” He explained. Jake took a sip. Strong, and very fruity. He could taste lemon and’orange? “What’s in this?” “Fresh lemonade, vodka and triple sec.” Jake nodded as he tossed a twenty on the bar, “Doesn’t suck. Keep the change.” The Big Dawg continued sipping the beverage. He didn’t take note of the man who sat down beside him. The world was enclosed today, and so as the man ordered a whiskey rocks he still did not look over at him. The man took a sip. His dark, tanned skin was framed in a silver, pinstriped suit. Long black hair hung freely around his shoulders, and a powerful yet compact body was visible beneath the suit. “Mr. Hix, I have been asked to speak with you.” The man said when the bartender moved away. A deep measure was taken of the man quickly by Jake. He looked him over, and found something familiar about him. “Do I know you?” “No.” The man finished his whiskey. “But you do know my father. I am Graywolf. I have been sent by my people to find you. We have the information you sought regarding the one of whom we do not speak.” “You’re the son.” “I am.” Jake nodded, then finished his Low End. “What have your people found out?” Graywolf motioned the bartender over, “Another whiskey for me pleas4e. Do you want something else?” “Coke.” Jake answered. He didn’t need too much alcohol. The bartender provided them each with a new drink. Jake looked over at the tables near the windows, “Let’s talk by the view.” They walked over to the open table with plenty of emptiness around them. Jake sat down, feeling the effects of the pills and alcohol now. He was becoming a little numb, which he had wanted. “So, you definitely aren’t from the reservation.” “No, I live here in Minneapolis.” Graywolf explained. “What is it that you do” You dress like a lawyer, but don’t look like one. Too much hair, and you seem too calm. Most lawyers are uptight pricks.” Graywolf sipped his whiskey slowly, “I think it is the culture. I went to Yale, and work here in Minneapolis for a large firm. I handle all of the tribe’s business as a part of my employment agreement. No one tells me how to appear, and it has never affected my effectiveness.” “Okay.” Jake sipped the coke. “How is your father?” “He was injured during the investigation, but he will live. He is a strong willed man, and he will not let such slight burdens hold him down. I was asked to be a messenger because no one else lives off the reservation. I have brought all of the details of the investigation. Basically, they rooted out the supporters among our people. They have been’handled. No more will he find support on the reservation. We also found clues as to where he disappeared to after leaving the Dakotas. Those may help you track them down.” Graywolf leaned forward, “Most importantly, he was able to send me the data from the laptop he has. No one else has this, and he cans end me without a phone call or leaving his home. There is a lot of data, but much of it is simply reporting. I have highlighted the major points in the cover letter.” “Where is the data?” Graywolf pulled out a small stick from his jacket. He passed it over, and Jake saw the Lexar symbol. “I downloaded everything to the jump drive. You can access it through any USB port.” “I’m familiar with these. I use my laptop every single day.” Jake assured him as he pocketed the stick. “Good. How is your family?” “Those that live, are still alive. We have too much pain to face right now, so we are dealing as best we can.” “Our people mourn with you.” “I appreciate that.” Jake thought about the question he wanted to ask. He stared out at the night skyline for a little while before broaching the subject. “Where did your investigation think he was headed?” “That’s why I approached you directly. We believe he is heading home. He is going back to Texas, and we don’t want your family to be jeopardized any further. He may be looking for a final strike.” Jake thought as much, “I am making arrangements to prevent that even now. We will be protected, and he will not lay hands upon our bloodline ever again. The only family he will ever see again is me. That will be when I destroy him, once and for all.” Graywolf simply sipped the whiskey in silence. They shared the moment, no talking between them as they each stayed within their own thoughts. Each had burdens to bear, and each respected the role the other played.
