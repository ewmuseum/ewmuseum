Viva Las Vegas?

26 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/26/2005 Bright light city gonna set my soul, Gonna set my soul on fire... Got a whole lot of money that's ready to burn, So get those stakes up higher. Mr. Harrison peered curiously at Juan and Teresa; looking dumbfounded. 'You two just moved IN... second rent payment's not even due. I know I can be a little gruff sometimes, but I always thought I was friendly enough.' Teresa chuckled sheepishly and put a hand on Mr. Harrison's shoulder. 'Please, Mr. Harrison, you know why we have to move right back out, barely two weeks after moving in,' she said with a bit of a frustrated sigh. News had spread all over Dallas like chicken pox in a classroom full of first-graders. 'And we don't like it ourselves, either.' Mr. Harrison let a small trace of a smile creep across his features. 'I'd hope not,' he said with a playfully mock-indignant tone. 'Near whole town's up in arms over the announcement. Lot of folks here were right proud of being the home city of GW.' Juan had to grin at both his wife and soon-to-be ex-landlord. 'Just like Lucha Libre back home, eh?' he quipped, running his fingers through his collar-length black hair. The thought made Teresa have to laugh. 'Pretty close,' she admitted. 'Except Lucha Libre stayed put.' There's a thousand pretty women waitin' out there, And they're all livin' devil may care. And I'm just the devil with love to spare! Viva Las Vegas, Viva Las Vegas! Las Vegas. The whole GW promotion was pulling up stakes and relocating there. The city wasn't bigger than Dallas, but it was flashier; ritzier. The new home arena building would have bright neon lights and probably an employ of showgirls. 'Didn't get to know you that well in two weeks, but I think I'll miss you all the same,' said Mr. Harrison, accepting the apartment key back from the Ramirez spouses and clipping it back onto his master key ring. Teresa gave the septuagenarian man a hug goodbye. 'We'll miss you too,' she said. 'And the rest of Dallas. Ay, Madre de Dios, of all the cities in the entire country to have to raise a ten-year-old child in... a city where both gambling and prostitution are LEGAL.' She glanced toward Melina with a very nervous look in her eyes, and instinctively moved toward her to rest comforting hands on the girl's shoulders. How I wish that there were more Than the twenty-four hours in the day! But even if there were forty more, I wouldn't sleep a minute away! 'Couple of towns not far from Vegas, on the other side of the Nevada/California border,' said Mr. Harrison thoughtfully. 'Indio, for example, or Cochella. Easy drive along a roadway to get to Vegas, but isolated from its underside. Decent Mexican population in both cities, too. Your agent should be able to help you plenty with finding housing.' Teresa glanced at Juan with a bit of surprise, though she was clearly pleased to hear this news. Juan was too. 'It's definitely something to consider,' said the Crimson Comet. 'Raising our daughter in a city like Las Vegas is not very appealing. Maybe we're over-reacting, and maybe the city isn't so bad... but we're traditional Catholics; we frown on many things that are taken for granted in American culture. A bit of isolation from places where such broad and liberal acceptance of vices we try to stay well away from, would be very welcome.' Oh, the black jack and poker and the roulette wheel, A fortune won and lost on ev'ry deal! All you need's a strong heart and a nerve of steel, Viva Las Vegas, Viva Las Vegas! Mr. Harrison laughed. 'Where were you two when Bush needed ya?' he said, and smiled and winked. Juan's brow furrowed. 'Bush? You mean the American President?' He glanced at Teresa, who shrugged, and then back at Mr. Harrison. 'What would HE want with US?' Mr. Harrison laughed again and clapped both Teresa and Juan on the shoulders. 'Your votes,' he said slyly. 'You two would make great Republican Conservatives.' Teresa rolled her eyes but chuckled good-naturedly. 'Oh please,' she said. 'We're still trying to get settled in. We don't have time to worry about things like American politics.' The elevator door opened and Stuart LaPortia stepped out. 'Looks like you're ready,' he said with a slight smile. 'Ready to catch our flight?' Teresa sighed audibly. 'No, but as they say in the old children's game... ready or not, here we come.' She lightly stroked Melina's cheek. 'Diga adi’s a Se’or Harrison, preciosa.' (*Say goodbye to Mr. Harrison, sweetheart.) Melina grumbled under her breath. The child had finally begun to warm to the elderly landlord; now she had to leave him. 'Mis padres deben haber sido m’dicos,' she said to Mr. Harrison, giving him a hug goodbye. (*My parents should have been doctors.) Mr. Harrison's eyes twinkled as he laughed good-naturedly and ruffled Melina's long dark hair. '?Eso los dice!' he said, and gave her a peck on the cheek. (*That's telling them!) Viva Las Vegas with your neon flashin' And your one-arm bandits crashin'! All those hopes down the drain, Viva Las Vegas turnin' day into nighttime, Turnin' night into daytime! If you see it once, You'll never be the same again... Stuart helped carry down the carry-on luggage and put it into the trunk of his car. 'Like you requested, I found you a room at a small motel near the outskirts of town,' he said. 'Something comfortable but far from the casino strips. My brother-in-law is a realtor in California; I'll give him a call and ask him if he knows of any housing close to the city in a nearby town.' Teresa nodded. 'Ask him to look into the towns of Indio and Cochella in California,' she said. 'Mr. Harrison suggested them to us.' 'Got it,' said Stuart. 'Evolution will be on Tuesday again, so you have at least a full day to get re-acclimated. I'll get you directions to the new GW Arena so you can be ready for your match with Rayne and Lei.' 'We've been ready for the match for a week,' quipped Juan lightly. 'It's the rest of the fed that's fallen behind. Stuart chuckled. 'That tends to happen when the whole fed packs up and moves to another city,' he admitted. 'Speaking of which, I know your friend Jos? is on his way back up to the States, driving your car in from Mexico. Did you contact him and let him know?' 'Already done,' said Juan. 'He thought the timing was outrageous, but he's probably close to the Texas/New Mexico border by now.' 'Good enough,' said Stuart. 'I'll call him soon as you're settled and give him the address. Then, it's Viva Las Vegas for GW.' Teresa snorted. 'Las Vegas can VIVA all it wants,' she said sardonically. 'We just don't want to VIVIR in it.' The point of the joke was lost on both Stuart and Melina, but the two Ramirez spouses laughed heartily as the car continued on its way to Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport. I'm gonna keep on the run I'm gonna have me some fun If it costs me my very last dime If I wind up broke up, well... I'll always remember that I had a swingin' time! I'm gonna give it ev'rything I've got, Lady luck please let the dice stay hot! Let me shoot a seven with ev'ry shot! Viva Las Vegas, Viva Las Vegas, Viva Las Vegas! Viva, Viva........ Las Vegas! Viva Las Vegas, Elvis Presley, ? 1964
