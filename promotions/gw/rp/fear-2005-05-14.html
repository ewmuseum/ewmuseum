Fear

14 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05.14.05 With the luminous bout against Ice lingering over his head and an uber amount of feelings racing through his heart, mind, and soul, Ken Partridge, Jr. stood at the foot of his penthouse apartment's staircase in virtual perplexion. In nothing but a pair of charcoal and gray plaid sleeping pants, his sculpted abs shone in the moonlight as his usually styled black and red hair fell flat on his head. He was, for the first time in a very long time, confused. The man that lived without fear had finally become fearful. Not in regards to what may or may not happen in the Iron Man Match, or even if he may come up on the losing end of the stick, but about the life that had caught up to him. For years, Ken was invulnerable without ever thinking it. He was impervious to mental pain and anguish because he was both to stubborn to accept it, and to strong to give in to it. He had worked his way through a broken back, a broken neck, and two busted kneecaps only to stad above the rest. He was a man of steel. Unfortunantly, there was a kink in the armor. It lay just to the left of he sternum and approximatly a quarter of the way down the chest... his heart. Of all the fights he'd waged war in throughout his life, his heart was the one that he was simply never capable of fully defeating. Not on an emotional level, but on an anatomical one. At the age of seventeen, a problem had been discovered in Ken's most important human organ. He'd been born with a slew of heart diseases such as Tacheocardia, Mitral Valve attacks, and a dent which, to this day, has no name. The first time it affected him was in Kentucky, where he'd just begun college. Believed to be having a heart attack, Ken was taken by his good friend Veeko to the campus hospital, where the afore mentioned developments wee discovered. He overcame it, even after two surgeries and numerous complications. However, the one that had hit him most recently, had finally put things into perception. Gunner returned to Ken's home to find him face down in the living room floor with a phone against his ear. A slight murmuring could be heard seeping from his lips, but it was inaudible to the point that it could have been a fly running along the hardwood. Trying her best to remain calm, Addison dropped the bag of vegetables that were recently purchased to the ground and knelt to Ken's side. 'Sweetheart,' she said with an anxiously calm ring to her vice, 'Sweetheart, I need you tell me what's worng.' There was no response from Ken, simpy a worried voice on the other end of the receiver. Gunner picked up the phone in hopes of finding the problem at hand. She spoke steadily into it, trying her best not to cry. 'Hello,' she said before repeating herself. 'Hello? Who is this?' 'Gunner,' asked Ken's mother, Connie. 'Gunner, is that you?' 'It's me, Connie,' replied a half relieved Addison. If there were anyone that could help Ken get through anything, it was his mother. 'I need you to stay calm,' Connie began. 'He's having an episode.' She, like Ken, called it an episode. They bothe preferred to stray from the word attack in order to take some power away from the situation. An attack was something that couldn't always be rallied against, but an episode was something that occured on occassion that simply had and could be dealt with. 'He called me fifteen minutes ago out of breath entirely<' explained his mom. 'His chest was tight and he couldn't breathe. He said it took him ten minutes to make it the eight feet to the phone.' 'What do I need to do,' asked the always receptive Gunner, who has now running her hand through Ken's locks lovingly as a silent tears strolled down he cheek. 'Bring him to,' Connie detailed with urgency in her voice. 'Get him sitting down and talking. Carry on a conversation with him like nothing out of the ordinary is happening. Make him laugh.' The awkward silence set in for a few secons before Gunner could reply with 'I'll do my best.' 'He loves you, Gun, and he knows that you love him,' said Ken's mom in dismissal. 'Thank you,' she replied. 'He loves you, too.' 'I know,' said Connie iwth her worried smile shining through the phone. 'He always let me know.' With those words, the realization tha the time for conversation was over set and Gunner ended the call with the full understanding to call back when the smoke had cleared. When she looked at Ken, his eyes had already glazed over and were resting in the back of his head. At the point where most would lose it, she realized that this could only help, as it would get his breathing back on track. She kissed him gently on the cheek, which, as in movies of love and fantasy, brought him back to conciousness. She helped him the sofa where she began talking about the freshness and smell of the pantains that she had purchased at one of SoHo's street markets. She described the color and taste before ging into the fresh hearbs she had bought, dweeling on Ken's favorite, dill. She finally moved from food into high school follies and mishaps from yesteryear. Upon the story of an unfortunate incident involving her, her best friend, and a bowl of noodles... Ken finally laughed a Donnie Darko chuckle. He finally formed words in regards to the firetrucks and squad cars that were called to the school over a bowl of pasta in, what was now know throughout all of Minnesota as 'The Noodle Incident.' With relief settling in and tears on her face, she grasped Ken with all that she had in a meaningful embrace that few in this world could ever be so lucky to share or understand. Rocking back and forth with him, crying in unison, she could do nothing but declare her love. 'I love you so, so much,' she managed to work into words. 'I love you, too. You,' Ken paused with, 'you saved me. You've saved me my entire life. Even wen you weren't there physically, I had a soulmate for a guardian angel on my shoulder.' At the foot of his staircase, Ken could still feel the affects of the previous days incident. His body was weak and drained and his mind exhausted. He had come to the realization that this was a battle he could never win. A fight that he could never overcome. his was something that would never go away. It was something that would continue to attack him until the day he died. He was scared. For the first time in his life, he was scared, which brought baout even more fear. The invincible man that had never believed himself to be so much, was no longer invincible. Ken had realized that his arch-enemy wasn't Ice, but his heart. Much like Superman's wasn't Lex Luthor or Doomsday, but Kryptonite. They were both things that made them live, but both things that could destroy them both. Realization set in. His inadequacies had plagued him his entire life and he always perceviered. He had to do the same with this one. He may not have been able to beat it, but he could not let it beat him. Gunner had begged Ken in vain to cancel his match at WrestleCade. The plea was obviously shot down as Ken would not give in to a 'condition' and let it rule his life. He would not let it beat him, which meant that he had to do what he had always done. He had to fulfill his duties, not to Global Wrestling, The Elite, or it's fan base... but to himself. Ken was scared, which made him strong. As he peered up the stairs into the loft that housed his anxious neice and nephew, visiting from Birmingham to see Uncle Ken's big match, a smile spread across his face. He may not be able to beat the 'condition,' but his family could. His mother and step-father, his sister and brother-in-law, his neice and nephew, Josh, Ace, Bill, Ceci, Joshua, and, without saying, Gunner, were the ones that would over come it. The thought of seeing their faces on the third row brought a tear of joy to his eye as he made his way to his bedroom and slid between the silk sheets where Gunner instnctivly cuddled to him, making him feel secure and impervious to all pain. His fear... was his strength.
