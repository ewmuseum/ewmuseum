A Fallen Angel: Notification

19 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 19, 2005 As Harry stood at Biddle’s front door about to knock, he took a moment to compose himself. As a lifelong fan of professional wrestling, Biddle had been one of Harry’s all time favorites and as Hagen stood on Biddle’s front steps he had to suppress the fan boy within himself, he was here on business after all. So Harry Hagen straightened his black tie and took a deep breath. He then put on his serious countenance, a look that caused the lines on his weather beaten Germanic face to deepen, and then he rang the doorbell. A few moments later a well-dressed butler with an air of British arrogance about him answered the door. The butler didn’t say anything; he just stood there and waited for Harry to say something. “My name is Harry Hagen,” he finally said, “I’m a detective from the West Hollywood Police Department. I’d like to speak with Mr. Biddle; it concerns an investigation I’m currently conducting.” “Very well sir,” the butler replied, “please wait a moment. I shall go see if Mr. Biddle is able to speak with you at this time.” Before Harry could say anything, the butler had closed the door. Hagen’s foot tapped impatiently as he waited for the butler to return. Finally, after what seemed like a millennium, the butler opened the door. “Mr. Biddle will see you for a few moments sir,” the butler arrogantly stated, “please, walk this way.” Harry removed his hat and followed the butler inside. Harry was amazed by the sheer size of the interior of Biddle’s home. Everything in the house looked as if it cost thousands of dollars. The butler led Harry upstairs and stopped at a closed door. The butler knocked on the door; “Detective Hagen is here to see you now sir.” “Send him in.” Hagen entered through the door and found himself in the elegantly styled bedroom of “Mr. Main Event” himself, Mike Biddle. In the center of the room Mike Biddle stood next to his bed placing things into a leather suitcase, an activity that made Hagen even more suspicious of Biddle’s involvement in Hillary Watson’s death. “If you don’t mind my asking,” Harry began, “why are you packing your bags?” Biddle didn’t look up to reply. “Dark Rituals is in Alaska,” he replied, “and with all the pre-event promotion that I’m stuck doing in the area I need extra clothes.” “I see,” Hagen said as he placed his hands in his pants pockets and began to pace around the room, he looked down at the floor as if he were in deep thought. Biddle closed the suitcase and zipped it shut. He then turned his gaze to the detective. “So why are you here anyway Detective?” “Well,” Hagen said with a soft sigh, “earlier today a young woman was found dead in her apartment.” “So?” Biddle shot back with a soft chuckle. “Her name was Hillary Watson,” Hagen harshly replied. “Who killed her?” Biddle asked, his voice showing his obvious indifference. “That’s what I’m trying to figure out,” Hagen said. “According to Miss Watson’s neighbor you were the last one to see her alive.” “Is that supposed to make me the killer?” Biddle asked with an arrogant smirk. “Well,” Hagen paused for dramatic effect; “you are the top suspect at this point in time.” Biddle’s dark blue eyes rolled out of annoyance. “So you’re here to arrest me then detective?” Biddle asked. Hagen shook his head. “No,” he said, “I don’t have enough evidence...yet. But stick around town because I’ll need to bring you down to the station later to get some questions answered.” Biddle looked at his suitcase and then looked at Hagen with a very angry look. “I have to go to Alaska you moron,” Biddle said through his teeth, “plus I go to Las Vegas every week for the Evolution taping.” Hagen sighed, he knew that Biddle wouldn’t be thrilled about the standard procedure. “Tell you what,” Hagen said, “as long as you have a couple of officers with you at these events I’m fine with you going. Just as long as you call me as soon as you get in to LA, so I know that you haven’t skipped town.” Biddle sighed; he had long grown tired of the constant drama of Beverly Hills. “Fine,” Biddle acquiesced; “just as long as they’re discreet...the last thing I need is for the media to think that I murdered someone.” “Don’t worry about that Mr. Biddle,” Hagen said; “just make sure that you’re in LA when I need to bring you in for questioning.” “Sure,” Biddle sighed. “All right,” Hagen said, “see you then.” Hagen left, he needed to see just one more person today before he could go home to his wife and explain why they’re vacation plans needed to be postponed yet again. ***** The redheaded man sobbed uncontrollably after Hagen had given him the news of Hillary’s murder. To be perfectly honest, Harry had pegged Charles as a slyer version of Biddle but was shocked to find that he was a polite and sensitive man, just as the widow from Hillary’s apartment building had said he was. Finally, Charles wiped his eyes and took a few deep breaths. “Do you know who did this?” Charles asked, his light green eyes hopeful. “No I don’t,” Hagen disappointedly replied. “If there’s anything I can do,” Charles softly said, “anything at all just let me know Detective.” “There’s one thing you can do,” Hagen said. “Yes?” “After I get some more facts together I’ll need to bring you down to the station for questioning,” Harry said as he carefully watched to see what Charles’s reaction would be. “Am I a suspect then?” Charles asked, a shocked look on his face. How could anyone suspect him of murdering the woman he loved? “No,” Hagen said, “I’ll need whatever information you can give me while I piece together the events that led to this young lady’s murder.” Charles nodded his approval. Harry then stood to leave. “Thank you for your co-operation Mr. Magee,” Harry said as he shook the red headed man’s hand. “Anything to bring the murderer of my beloved to justice,” Charles replied. Harry then left, for now he would have to wait. The funeral would be soon, and it would be interesting to see who showed up and who didn’t. ***** “You’re late;” his wife hissed the moment he walked through the door. “Sorry my dear,” Harry said as he leaned down to kiss her on the cheek. “I had some business to take care of on the way home.” His wife sighed, despite her years she was still lean and beautiful and her long red hair only slightly grayed. “Don’t tell me,” she said, “you let those bastards talk you into taking another case...didn’t you?” Harry’s shoulders sank as he sat down on the couch next to his wife. “Mike Biddle may have murdered his former valet,” Harry said with a stressed out sigh. “And that’s more important than our trip to the Grand Canyon?” She angrily asked. Harry sighed. “No it isn’t Delores,” he said, “but this trip has been delayed for so long what’s another week or two?” “You always say that Harry,” Delores replied. “And this time I mean it.” “You had better mean it Harry,” Delores said, “you need to put yourself first for once in your life. It’s great that you help other people, but when are you going to help yourself?” “Right after this case dear,” Harry said. They hugged and as they embraced each other, Delores hoped to God that Harry really meant it this time. -To Be Continued-
