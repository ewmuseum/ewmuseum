It was a dark and stormy night, the perfect clich?, with just a hint of foreshadowing. Rain slashed against the window, lightning painting its flashbulb, paparazzi glow across the girl's face as the nurse checked her vitals. Sadness reigned in this room, etching its stain on her furrowed brow. The nurse frowned, brushing an errant strand of greasy hair back from Kitty’s parched brow. She was still burning up; her body wracked with fever? some unknown poison eating her up inside. The heart monitor beeped, slow and steady. Nothing more than background noise as the nurse sat down in the chair beside the bed. The energy in the room was reflected in the turbulent sky outside? something was about to happen. The girl reached up with a trembling hand, grasping the nurse’s arm. Her chapped lips worked, but her voice was barely above a whisper, lost in the noise of the storm beyond the windows. “Help me,” she whispered. The nurse stood, grabbing a glass from the table beside the bed, quickly filling it with water, placing a straw between the girl’s lips, letting her take a few gulps of the cold liquid. When she began to sputter, the nurse removed the straw, smoothing back her hair. The girl looked at her with wild, frightened eyes, her chin trembling as tears filled those lovely green eyes. “Please,” she managed, “you have to help me.” “Honey,” the nurse crooned as she patted Kitty’s head reassuringly, “we’re already helping you. You could have died.” The girl shook her head, blanching from the pain, but somehow managing to retain consciousness. “No, you don’t understand. He’s going to kill my baby. He told me he was. He said that he was going to sacrifice my baby to the darkness in exchange for power. And then he said he was going to have his way with me because Brad doesn’t want me back.” The nurse clucked her tongue, and checked the monitors; the girl was still burning up with a fever of over one hundred degrees. “You must be delirious. There’s nobody here that will hurt you. We only want what’s best for you.” “No!” She insisted, her voice breaking painfully, “you have to find my husband. Brad Jackson. He works for the WCWF. He’s a wrestler and a road agent. He needs to know where I am before” before? oh God, no!” Her gaze drifted past the nurse, to the man who stood in the doorway. The nurse followed her gaze, smiling at the man with the white hair, dressed in hospital scrubs and a white lab coat. “Good evening doctor.” Kitty screamed, a sound of sheer terror, and then grabbed the nurse, desperately pleading. Her voice broke painfully, becoming a garbled croak. “No, he’s not a doctor. He’s the one who wants to kill me! Don’t let him in here!” Gabriel stepped into the light, wearing a sheepish smile, shrugging his shoulders in defeat. “I’m sorry, Nancy. I’m afraid her psychosis is quite severe. She thinks that demons are after her baby. Pregnancy has done something to her mind. I hope she hasn’t frightened you with her dark talk. That’s all it is, I’m afraid, just talk.” The nurse shook her head, “I was just concerned about the poor little thing. Will she be alright?” Gabriel shrugged. “Perhaps in time. She’s only a danger to herself in this condition.” He withdrew a syringe from his pocket and busied himself for a moment filling it with a pale yellow liquid. He approached the bed with a smile that was just for her, one that radiated darkness and menace. “There, there, my angel. Calm yourself and sleep.” He plunged the needle into her arm, even as she tried to twist away from him. Almost immediately, the darkness crept in around the edges of her vision, but it wasn’t so fast that she didn’t see him turn with that same smile as he pulled back the plunger on the needle again, filling the chamber with air before driving it into the nurse’s neck. She moaned in anguish, and then drifted into the darkness, her last thought of Brad, and his strong arms? ---- Flashback’Thursday, April 14, 2005 2300 hours, local time “somewhere dark” ---- She awoke with a start, a scream already on her chapped lips, but no sound came out from her ravaged vocal cords. Panic sizzled along her nerve endings, making her hair stand on end, making her skin tingle with an unpleasant buzz. She blinked, once, twice even, fearing that she had gone blind since he’d spirited her away from the hospital bed. The darkness was that absolute, that impenetrable. Terror squeezed her chest, making the thick air even harder to breathe. She couldn’t move, couldn’t make a sound, and the more she strained, the more she was aware of the pain in her back that was stretching towards her stomach. Fear for her baby made her stop struggling, if only to catch her breath. Sweat dripped from her greasy hair, stinging her eyes, mingling with her tears. Her face was raw from them, her eyes permanently puffy, and still, she had no idea what this madman wanted, except to punish Brad. “Please, Lord,” she whispered, the sound nothing more than imagined, “send me an angel.” This had become her mantra, these words, or a variation, always the same theme. She’d never been religious, but now, it was the only thing keeping her alive, the only thing keeping her sane. ---- And all the people that you hurt Came down on you And I can't understand just why you went away I sat and waited for the day you'd come back home? ---- Flashback’Monday, May 16, 2005 2300 hours, local time Warehouse District, Chicago, Illinois ---- “Aye,” McLeod muttered into the phone that was pressed to his ear, “a man would have to be bloody insane to work for you, Jackson. You’re driving me to drink. Jesus, Mary and Joseph, these things take time, have patience, man!” Jackson’s voice came through the speaker, tinny but still audible. “Time is something I fear we’re running low on, Bruce. Just check into this for me, please. You know I would do it myself, if I didn’t have this goddamn Pay-Per-View in less than 24 hours.” “Right, although I still don’t understand why you haven’t taken a leave from there?” McLeod rolled his eyes, turning his back to the wind, pressing against the wall of the bus shelter. “I can’t. I have to make money, Bruce... I have to pay for” it’s about? pride I guess. I can’t let him take everything from me. Do you understand? I can’t take time off? I can’t afford a leave?” McLeod chuckled, “True, since you have already taken one of your senses, anyhow. This is your family” your wife? your baby. Don’t you care?” Jackson snarled, a sound borne of frustration, and then snapped his last words in clipped tones. “Listen, Bruce” I cannot afford to drop everything for a tip that may not even pan out. I don’t know for sure that she was abducted? hell the police even think I’m making this shit up. Just do it, let me know what you find, please.” And with that his voice was replaced with a dial tone. McLeod shook his head, and mumbled under his breath as he dropped the cell phone back in his pocket. He stuffed his hands deep in his pockets, mostly to disguise their trembling before stepping out into the brisk wind. He hunched his shoulders, making himself appear smaller as he eased from one shadow to the next. His destination was just ahead, amid all the rest of the harbor front squalor. The smell of fish was carried on the wind, a stink that never really went away from this area. He wrinkled his nose in disgust, and pulled himself up short. The address on the slip of paper in his pocket led here, to a pile of dilapidated, decaying bricks, and a slapdash of weathered planks and rusty nails. The boardwalk was littered with holes, and the black water was visible beneath, catching the moonlight. The street, if it could be called that, was deserted at this time of night, save the rats. It was the perfect place to kill someone, mob style. The river was a stone’s throw from the back entrance; a body could be easily dumped into the oily black water on a dark night without arousing the slightest suspicion. The building sat there, in utter silence, completely dark. McLeod shuddered with foreboding, and pulled open the door beside him, creeping inside the darkened fishery across the way. Perfect for surveillance. He paused, letting his eyes adjust to the gloom. Yes, this would be a perfect place for a quick stakeout, perfect for watching. Little did he know it was also the perfect locale for an ambush? Before he could react to the movement, detected too late, the damage had been done, the blow already dealt to the back of his head. Stars replaced the gloom, and he went down hard, the reek of old, dead fish filling his nostrils as he hit the floor. ---- Flashback’Tuesday, May 17, 2005 0101 hours, local time Circus Circus, Las Vegas, Nevada ---- Jackson sat alone, nursing a drink, a stack of poker chips on the table before him, resting in their plastic holder. Over four thousand in winnings, but that didn’t matter. This was just a diversion, the noise and display nothing more than a backdrop. He was only in town for the night, passing through en route to yet another arena for WCWF. His celebrity afforded him that much, at least. “Jackson,” it was more a snarl than anything else, but his mood had been terrible lately. “Brad,” the voice on the other end greeted him warmly, “I’m so glad I caught you. I was wondering?” static replaced the rest of the thought. “What” Bill, I can’t hear you! What?” Jackson idly slid the chips back and forth, listening to the rhythmic sound of their movements. This was the only order in the chaos that had become his existence. “Sorry, Brad. What I said was, I was wondering if you’d be willing to drop the title. SHE ISN’T DEAD!!” Jackson slammed his fist on the table, making the chips rattle ominously. People passing by stopped to stare as Jackson roared the last words. Without another word he snapped the phone closed, and glared at the people who dared look at him twice, his face burning with anger.
