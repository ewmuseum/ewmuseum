The Lie of the Looking Glass

28 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28 August 2005 .:: ::: ::. 30 Ř’jen 808 Praha (30 October, 808 Prague) (fragments of a diary entry, found written in old Czech) “My studies go well, or so Master Ionescu says. I wouldn’t know, I suppose, since there is no tangible way for me to measure my progress. Every day, though, I feel further from myself, the self that I was only a few short months ago, before all this occurred. Master Ionescu says that I have become something better, but it is so hard for me to agree with him! When I look in the mirror, I don’t see the pale flush across my cheeks anymore. My skin looks cold and dead, and in fact, it is. My lips show only the palest crimson hue, and only then when I am freshly fed, glutted on blood. It is only my eyes that remain as they once were, but against such pallor, they take on a frightening glow. In the streets, when I am allowed to go out, people are terrified; they shy away from me as if by instinct. And after I feed, I can’t look at myself in the glass’I’m too afraid of what I will see. I have not heard anyone speak of me as a woman since I have changed. True enough, they still call me Lia, they still call me “she?” but I am a Tzimisce now. I am part of a different world than the one that I lived in only months ago. I live alongside them? I coexist. But I will say what I am if Master Ionescu will not? The sharp, ugly teeth that I have not yet learned how to conceal? the cold of my skin? the bony protrusions that Master has made me to mold on my knuckles in order to learn how to use my powers? they all say that I am what he will not say I am. Had I known that my existence would become as this? I would have chosen burning at the stake, no matter what I would have lost. I look in the mirror again, and I wonder? what would my husband think if he saw this? If he saw the woman who bore his children drinking the blood of innocent paupers in the street? If he saw me corrupting the values of the church that he served with my very existence? I know that he could put a name to what I am; he would call me by the truth of it: I am a monster. .:: ::: ::. McFarlane Estate sometime in the evening Lia’s fingers pressed a fresh cloth to Cyrus? wound idly. By this point in time, they had healed enough that all that was needed was a change of the dressing thrice daily. Still, they would have hurt considerably’it was for that reason that she found cause to use her medicinal knowledge. He slept, under power of Lia’s mixtures, twenty-four hours a day, and had done so since he came to the McFarlane Estate. It was only then that Lia had the time and the ingredients to relieve his pain. A soft ripping sound emanated from the roll of medical tape that Dawn had gotten her, as she pressed one strip of it to the bottom of the last piece of gauze. It was a curious addition to her repertoire’she had never used medical tape to secure anything before, but Dawn had insisted that it would be easier than simply tying strips of gauze around his wounds. Lia was forced to admit that perhaps she was a bit behind the times? for someone like her, that was a staggering admittance. Tending to someone who didn’t speak back gave Lia a lot of time to think. That wasn’t necessarily a good thing. Mostly, she ruminated on last week’and there was a lot to think about, as far as that was concerned. Whether or not she wanted to admit it, Gray Brand was still in her head. He was perching there, like a fat toad, telling her all the time that no matter how much she cared about Dawn, deep down, she was just using her for a pawn. That no matter how much she cared, she didn’t care enough. And who was he to dictate that, really? He had no business presuming to tell her what she felt, presuming that Dawn was nothing but a pawn in her games. He was trying to aggravate her, that much was clear. But for what end? As rational a being that Lia was, as she sat in the third floor bedroom where Cyrus was staying, she knew that Gray’s reasons didn’t matter. What mattered was that he had gotten to her. He had played her like a psaltery. And more than that, he had gotten in the midst of something he had no right to get in the midst of. She was not above defending her principles’she had told him to leave Dawn be, and he hadn’t done it. That called for action. She didn’t know the official, front-office reason for her not being booked’other than perhaps Christina Reno was a little miffed at her at the moment, for whatever trivial reason’but she did know that for once, she was thankful for the inactivity. The inactivity gave her plenty of opportunity to follow her principles at her own pace. Whether ill-advised or not? she would meet Gray Brand in a singles match, and she would force him to acknowledge his mistake’not only that, she’d force him to eat his words. What weighed on her more heavily, perhaps, was Emoticon. She knew without a doubt’she knew by pure instinct’that she had made a mistake. Sighing softly, she bit Emoticon's lower lip gently, pulling away for a moment to gaze into his eyes. That, unfortunately, didn't quite knock her back into her senses. One look was all that she needed to bury herself in another kiss, turning her head to the side to press closer to him as she ran her fingers through his hair. The questions continued, assailing him mercilessly. Did she even know what she was doin-WHO she was’he couldn't even finish the thought. His chivalry had never actually, you know, WORKED before. All he had really done was compliment her and press a rose into her palm, and she was gazing at him as if she had known him for an eternity. Never before had he felt like this, felt so utterly overwhelmed; well, except for last week. But this was different. So much more intimate, so much more equal. They were kissing, an equal expression of affection. And he didn't want it to stop. He yelped softly into her mouth as she kissed him again, utterly unable to do anything but respond and enjoy. Coherent thought required far too much effort. This was far, far better. She almost pulled away- from the noises he was making, she was worried that she'd offended him. After a few more moments, she pulled away gently, her eyes half-narrowed- that familiar expression that we call 'bedroom eyes' if you will. She smirked. Lia shook her head forcefully, as though trying to shake the memories of those long moments out’moments that seemed to hang, suspended in time. And distilled within those moments, like inside ice crystals, was the one thing that she had been searching for nearly since the moment she’d been changed into what she was’love. It was like unto ludicrous for someone like her to be so insistent that she needed to be loved? wasn’t it? What right had she to expect it? She was not a being that inspired love; she was a taker of life, one who played god with the destinies of others, and was never punished for it, because it was her role in the chain to be mistress of such games. Fate had passed her by, taken everything from her in one fell swoop, leaving her only with the blood, degradation, and violence of another evening’s meal. Yet despite the bloodbath that was her existence, all she really wanted was to feel as though she mattered’to once again feel like the woman that someone had loved, the woman that someone had dedicated his life to defending. Hadn’t she had that once? What was stopping her from having it again? The answer, of course, was time. Time had long ago stolen the conventions that Lia associated with being loved. No more did roses send messages’pink for gentleness, white for purity, yellow for friendship, red for love. No more did the desirable men ride horses and wear finely embroidered tabards over heavy suits of mail. No more was a rose left on a windowsill at midnight a romantic gesture. The world of love, to Lia, seemed garish and overblown. A woman knew you loved her if you sent her a text message while you were supposed to be watching sports with your friends. It was all about the most’you bought her the most garish wedding ring, or the most garish car (weren’t all cars garish, though? Hideous machines!). All this in the name of love’but not even love. All this in the name of sex. Thought on the matter only caused Lia to reminisce, and to wonder when love had ceased to be an end of its own, and when it had become a path to satisfying a base carnal desire. Perhaps that was what she found so alluring about Emoticon. He professed to follow the proper code. He professed to be Chivalry’s last stand. From the very first moment that she’d met him, she’d felt a twinge of nostalgia, and then the first pangs of attraction, as he proved that he was more than a savant’that he actually had intent of following the code. Emoticon: You're a wonderful person, you know? For a moment, she was utterly floored by that comment. Admittedly, that was something that those of her kind didn't often hear- and lately, she'd been hearing that she was everything BUT a wonderful person. Shadow: I... am? She blushed, shaking her head. Shadow: No wait, now I'm going to sound like I'm fishing for compliments. I'm not really, but... people don't really ever say that to me. I'm not used to it. Emoticon: Don't mention it. You're beautiful, Lia. And intelligent, in an intriguingly dark sort of way. He looked up, hearing a gust of something that had blown the locker room door shut, causing the clicking noise that had briefly startled him. Emoticon shook his head to clear the sound before continuing. Emoticon: The fact that you're a vampire doesn't matter. Were you a human, I would feel the same way. I don't even mind that you're tall. The Soul of the Poet winked at her, her 5'9' slightly trumping his 5'8'. He turned, regarding the vase carefully, before pulling a single rose from it. Emoticon: Flower? He didn't give her a chance to respond, instead taking her hand, opening it, and clasping it closed with the stem inside. Emoticon: There. Perfect. Were she still the swooning type, she would have swooned at that moment’but the capacity to be so shocked by anything had long escaped her. Her jaded-to-the-world philosophy nearly obliterated every chance at emotion’other than a dull disinterest. She was a wave of calm amid a sea of overemotional, fawning humans. But she had been just like them once, and all it took was Emoticon telling her that she was beautiful to remind her of that. To make her long for it again. A red rose for love, respect, courage, and passion? The corners of Shadow’s lips turned downwards. Who could love something like her? For all Emoticon professed that Virgil was an arrogant, human-hating jerk, he seemed to fail to realize that Virgil Ambrosi and Lia Vlaszy came from the same cloth. If Virgil was an inhuman monster? then so was she. While outcasts might occasionally deserve affection... monsters never deserved anything but scorn. Sorrow surged forward, but Lia sealed it off upon hearing the tramp of footsteps nearing the room. As though her mind was a tourniquet, all flow of feeling stopped abruptly, and Lia’s face settled once more into the placid, imperturbable lines that most of her companions knew so well’whether this was Dawn, Alexis, or Virgil approaching, they didn’t need to deal with her moody fits. She reached over to the bedside table and picked up the glass that was waiting there, filled with the small amount of potent liquid that would send Cyrus back into unconsciousness. She looked down into the swirling blue-gray liquid, half tempted to quaff it herself? but that really wouldn’t have been very responsible of her. In order to better resist the temptation, she pressed the vial to Cyrus? lips, administering the potion. His limbs stirred briefly, a twitching sort of involuntary motion, then he swallowed. Seconds later, he passed into the stillness that meant another six to eight hours of unconscious respite. The doctor sighed, relieved. “How does he fare?” The slightly archaic phrasing and the Iberian lilt of the basso voice from behind her gave her no doubt as to who she was listening to. Her shoulders stiffened slightly. “I see improvement in his condition every day.” “That is relieving.” The mattress sunk behind her, and gently, Virgil’s hand slid around her waist. She looked down for a moment at the sharp-tipped talons he called fingernails, pointed spines so luminescent under the moonlight that they nearly seemed to cast their own half-moons of light. They contrasted sharply with the black velvet of her attire, which made her feel further alien from him. She sniffed. “To think, he wanted me to let him die.” Virgil hmphed. “I suppose we have all felt that way at one time or another. His pain must have been immense for him to say that.” “Iron will do that to a Kiasyd,” she said dispassionately, standing up, and slipping out of his grasp. She busied her hands with work on the table where she kept her supplies, but her mind was still running in much the same direction as it had before; she could feel his eyes pressing into her back. “So it is true what they whisper about his kind.” She smirked sardonically. “I wouldn’t think you were the type to listen to silly rumors.” Behind her was the shift of rustling fabric as he stood. “It is almost impossible not to, when your own lineage supposedly comes from the Roma.” She turned around abruptly, and was rather surprised by his closeness to her’she hadn’t heard him move any closer, but her own distraction could have been the cause of that. She backed up against the table, as though clinging to it for dear life. “Lineage is all too often forgotten, even among our kind.” He simply nodded in agreement. It was hard to remember everything, though, as the years slipped by into obscurity. The darkness of memory ate everything that it could possibly consume, and it was always greedy for more. Their eyes locked, in a battle of cerulean and jade’neither would look away. Neither wanted to, really, but there were too many things suspended, unsaid at the moment. Virgil was the first to break eye contact. “Lia” have I done something to upset you?” “No, I” why would you ask that?” Virgil was not an unobservant creature’she had fired back entirely too quickly to be telling the all of it. “It is the only reason I can think of that a savant imitating courtly love would be of any comfort to a being whom it is far beyond his capacity to understand, much less to love.” So that’s what this was about? simple jealousy? She was silent for a moment’and that caused him to look up and catch her wounded look. He braced himself for the coming impact, expecting a hail of vitriol, curses? anything but what he got, which was a few more seemingly interminable moments of stony silence? and then a question. “Don’t you ever wonder what it’s like to feel alive again?” He looked at her for a moment, silent, unable to respond in any way that would have matched the importance she obviously attached to the question. But she shook her head and went on. “No, no! That’s not what I mean” I mean? don’t you wonder what it’s like to feel like you’re new to someone? Like your every facet is a glorious revelation to them? That you are the jewel in their crown, the brightest star in their sky, the one person who matters most? above everything else? Above duty, honor, self-respect?” Vehemently, she shook her head again. “For the first time in centuries, someone called me a woman. Not a bitch, not a thing of evil, not a vampire, not a being, not a” a monster!” “You’re not a monster.” His blue eyes flashed as he responded, more out of reflex than anything else. Something whip-quick within him had made him say it. But it was the wrong thing to say to a woman who was questioning her continued existence more every night. “I’m not” Why don’t you ask Gray Brand if I’m a monster? Ask him what I do, he’ll tell you! I manipulate situations to my advantage, use people as pawns, uncaring of their feelings? and I do it all just for the feeling of a little blood across my lips. I kill people, Virgil! How am I not every bit the monster that everyone says I am?” “It’s more complicated than that.” She threw her hands up in the air’her outburst wasn’t really directed at him, but unfortunately, he was receiving the brunt of it, as one of the only people in her life who would really understand it. “Yes! Yes, it’s much more complicated than that! But tell me who is going to give me credit for being something more than a monster when they see the exsanguinated body of their sister lying in an alley” When they see me with her blood running down my chin? Who?! Who’s going to call me a woman, then? Who’s going to act like I have emotions other than base savagery?” She turned away from him suddenly, her arms folding about her lithe waist as though she was trying to disappear into herself. In truth, she probably would have were it possible at the moment. From the back, he watched her draw a shuddering breath. And then she spoke to him, her voice slowly flowing back into the calm, composed deadpan that it normally was. “I’m sorry,” she said, “You didn’t deserve that.” Something deep within him swelled, turned over like it was awakening. Yes, he had deserved it, he realized, because he hadn’t realized that she felt this way. She turned to face him, her green eyes jagged with pain. “Do you remember being human, Virgil?” He nodded, closing his eyes briefly. “Not well” most of it has slipped away, but there are a few bits and pieces that claw for the light now and again.” She swallowed. “Do you” do you remember your wife?” For a moment, something broke through the impenetrable calm of Virgil’s walls as well’broke through like a trebuchet’s stone. He sat down on the edge of the bed, running his fingers through his hair, which was all wild, loose blackness as per usual, running down his back like liquid ebony. His eyes were somewhere far away, the memory of hot Spanish breezes and the swaying fields of flowers outside the manor taking over all rational action in the present. “I loved her immensely?” His voice trailed off quietly, but then his brow furrowed, and he went on. “I can’t remember quite the circumstances of our marriage, but I remember small things. I remember that her eyes were the most unusual color I had ever seen” Her hair smelled exotic, like the perfumed silk that the traders would bring from the east, and she seemed unusually intuitive. I remember when I came back from my lord’s service, she would be waiting for me, placidly, with some needlework, or something of that nature? She always tried to look like she’d kept herself busy, but I could tell from the way her hands trembled at the sound of my footsteps in the threshold that she’d been just as unhappy in my absence as I had been to leave her?” He blinked. She noted that’he didn’t often do it, so her question must have stirred something within. Virgil cleared his throat slightly. “I” I can’t call up many memories of her, not even her name. But I remember considering the two of us very lucky by the standards of the time. Our marriage was arranged, but in it, we found great felicity? I was terribly happy?” But there, his story ended. His fingernails had dug in to the knee of his pants? and he could remember no more. He didn’t really want to remember any more. A sudden shift in Lia’s movement caused him to look up at her. Her eyes were red-rimmed. “I had that once” at least I think.” He tilted his head at her slightly, eager to listen to her speak, if just to clear the pall of sadness and loss out of his own head. She pressed on, very near hoarsely. “I remember less than you do” no names, no visions, no places? I don’t even remember vaguely what he looked like. All I remember is that I loved him, felt safe with him? I thought our love was of the type that was immortal?” She scoffed bitterly. “It seems that immortality is easier for people than it is for feelings. All I remember now is that I had love once, that once, I knew what it felt like to be the most beautiful woman that someone had ever seen” and I’ve been chasing that feeling ever since I lost it.” His eyes met hers again, but this time, it was only in empathy. They had lost so much, the two of them, and in such similar ways’he supposed that he could see, now, why so much of her time she spent trying to make up for being what she was. Once, she had been the queen of someone’s manor house, ruling it with gentility and prudence, but ruling it strictly. Once, she had been the reason that a knight fought so hard to come home from battle. And now, she was the manipulative beast. No one could look into her eyes and see the enduring qualities, so she tried to force them to see. And still, there were those who questioned her motives, and reviled her very existence. Mentally, he reached for her, straining to relieve the great tense gulf between them. It was only a feeling in the pit of his stomach, but instinct told him that if they could only resurrect the feelings they were chasing, wounds centuries old could be mended without a trace of their existence. .:: ::: ::.
