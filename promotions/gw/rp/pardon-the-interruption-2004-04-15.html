'Pardon the Interruption.'

15 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 15th, 2004. My gray Camaro lurched to a violent halt in the parking place labeled 'VIP' and I swung the door open bouncing off of the black leather interior with a surge of energy shooting through my body. It was a combination of both energy and adrenaline that fueled me on this sweltering April day as the sun glared angrily down from the Dallas sky. Today was really beautiful despite the temperature touching the ninety-five degree mark and staying there, yet at 4:36 P.M. it would only be another four hours before the sun dipped behind the horizon and night fell. 4:36 P.M.? Oh, Shit! I was today's scheduled guest for ESPN's 'Pardon the Interruption' program and I was sent as a representative of Global Wrestling and sent on the behalf of Wrestling world wide. My actions on this show would not only speak for me but for the entire Wrestling world as well. And it was 4:36 P.M. …Approximately six minutes after the show had hit the airwaves and thirty-six minutes later then I had agreed upon. This wasn't good. I only prayed that they didn't give my spot to some moron basketball player or a dumb jock such as that. That prayer was suddenly answered as I spotted an enraged errand boy stomping angrily from the TV station toward where I stood. The corners of my mouth titled heavenward ever so slightly as my face formed a smile at the anger this young man bore across his face. 'Where the hell have you been?' The freckle faced redheaded kid demanded, sparks shooting from his green eyes that hid behind black framed coke bottle lens glasses. 'Don't you realize you were due here thirty-six minutes ago!' He stupidly screamed at me and I say stupidly because it isn't wise for a little nerd to knock the chip from a dangerous mans shoulder. 'I'm here now, so just chill.' I said in a soothing voice as I walked away from him and toward the large brown metal door of the Television studio. 'That isn't the point!' He yelled loudly in protest to my devil-may-care attitude. 'It's all about principle, you were supposed to be here thirty-six minutes ago, damn it!' He once again screamed in my ear as he followed along in my footsteps. That however seemed to be the straw that broke the camels back as I snatched the nerd by his blue 'GAP' T-shirt lifted him into the air with one arm almost as if he were a rag doll. Holding him suspended in midair I drove him violently into the metal door that served as the entrance to the studio, a gasp of pain and terror escaped his lips and I assumed that his white khaki's would soon feature a dark stain on the front just any second now. Glaring at him I spoke my mind in a soft albeit very firm tone. 'One of us has a problem with perception. I'm here to get my little TV stint over with…the piece hasn't aired yet so I still have time. However the further you delay me the more time I'll take to beat your ass which means I'm going to miss the show altogether. So…let's go in, shall we?' I dropped him and he collapsed onto the sidewalk quickly scrambling away from me as I threw open the door before me and stepped inside. 'He's here!' I heard several voices exclaim and even a voice or two muttering, 'It's about damn time, too.' . Looking around I spotted a horde of bodies rushing toward me. The producer, the camera guy, the sound guy and even the make up girl. 'This way.' The producer quickly said as he hustled down a hallway with me this entourage and I in tow. The white walls and brown carpet rushed by as I made haste down the hallway my quick steps leading me to a room where the door was marked 'Private'. The door opened and I was quickly shoved into this room and into a black director's chair. Looking up my bluish gray eyes locked on the cameras pointed in my direction and a monitor off to the side where ESPN sports personalities Tony Kornheiser and Michael Wilbon resided on the screen locked in a heavy argument about the latest events in the sporting world. The bar to the side plainly displayed 'High school vs. College' as the topic and twenty-nine seconds left on that topic. 'Just look at the guys that have had success jumping from high school straight to the pros. Lebron James, Kobe Bryant…' Tony began as Michael cut him off. 'I don't care how much talent you have that wont compensate for maturity!' Michael shot back. 'All these kids see is girls, money and cars, then they go to the NBA and get in trouble because their not mature enough to handle that type of pressure.' Michael spat out angrily. 'Your right, I also think they need to go to college. I know the money is tempting but just like you said there is nothing that will make up for maturity and that's why a lot of professional basketball players go astray.' Said Tony as reluctantly agreed to Wilbon's statements. 'The last thing we need is more hoodlums running around in the NBA.' Michael said with an edgy tone in his voice as a bell suddenly rang signaling the end of that topic. Looking up at the camera Tony Kornheiser began his commercial pitch, 'Don't go away boys and girls, we've got to pay the bills and when we come back we'll spend five good minutes with today's guest, 'American Youth' Josh.' 'Who?' Michael threw in as the show cut away to air the aforementioned commercials. As soon as the show cut away the crew around me broke into a mad fit. 'Are you wearing that ?' The makeup girl demanded as she stared at my tattered Ralph Lauren jeans which, bore several gaping holes, black Converse Chuck Taylor's and a white Raglan that bore the Red Sox logo on it and the blue sleeves had long ago been ripped off. 'Do you see a problem with my attire?' I impatiently sneered at the flat-chested unattractive blonde. 'Aren't you at least going to brush your hair?' She said in a whimpering tone as she eyed my shaggy black locks which shot in every direction as if I had just rolled out of bed. My response to her question was a smile and a raise of the middle finger on my right hand. She quickly caught on as she moved away from me and the producer quickly spoke, his words almost stalled by the bushy black mustache that clung to his upper lip and moved when he talked. 'Now your going to be hooked up via satellite, have you done anything like this before?' He inquired as my mind raced to think of prior experiences. 'Nothing quite like this, but I get the idea.' I said as the audio guy dropped a microphone into my hand. With my right hand I quickly shoved the lower receiver portion into the pocket of my jeans and raced the wire up my body and underneath my shirt as I clipped it on the collar. I smiled as I was wired for sound. Glancing back up at the screen I saw Michael Wilbon take a sip of coffee and Tony Kornheiser brush off his black suit before the producer interrupted me. 'Are your ready?' He excitedly asked. My response was a simple nod as I saw a red light appear on the camera before me and behind it, a raised hand with five uplifted fingers. Five…four…three…the voice began as the fingers dropped, right after three the voice stopped and another finger disappeared…then another…and we were rolling. Glancing at the monitor in the corner I saw the topic bar read 'Wrestling' and a total of five minutes sat on the clock. The bearded mug of Tony Kornheiser stared into the camera as he began his return speech. 'Alright, boys and girls, we're back and just like I promised you we've got Josh with us to discuss Wrestling.' Tony said in his ever professional manner. Glancing over to the monitor I saw a split screen appear as my face shared one half of the screen and the two hosts' of PTI occupied the other. 'Josh, it's great to have you with us.' Tony said quickly to break the ice before diving straight in. 'I'm really happy to be here guys, thanks for having Me.' I said before Wilbon quickly cut in. 'Josh a lot of people in the business of sports don't really feel that Wrestling should be considered a sport, what makes you think that it should?' Wilbon inquisitively asked almost immediately striking a nerve. 'What makes them feel that way Michael?' I said with touch of anger in my voice. 'It's just that…the reality of Professional Wrestling is in question.' Tony said defending his running mate. 'You're questioning the reality of Professional Wrestling?' I said almost breaking into laughter. A smile spread across my face as I began to fight back for my chosen profession. 'Believe what you will guys, but if golf is a sport then why the hell isn't Wrestling? I mean, it's not as if I go out there ever Monday night and fake gravity, so if you want to learn about the reality of Professional Wrestling then step into a ring. Step into a ring and get suplexed. Step into a ring and get dropped on your head. Step into a ring and get slammed on your kidneys so many times your urinating blood for a week. But if you guys wont do that then you'll just have to take my word for it.' I said in a tone that was calm as I tried to keep my manner very professional on the air. 'But how do we know we can trust you?' Michael Wilbon spat at me. 'You don't have to trust Me.' I said softly. 'You don't have to trust me at all. In fact the only thing you have to trust is the X-rays that show where I've dislocated my knee, the X-rays that show where I've broken my leg, the X-rays that show where I've torn ligaments in my knee from Wrestling. That's all you have to trust. You don't even have to trust the gallons of blood that have gushed from my body. All you have to do is look at my X-rays and realize that the human body can't outsmart gravity, that's all the evidence you need to prove my sport is real.' 'But there are so many guys that make it look…well, quite frankly…childish and silly.' Tony Kornheiser firmly stated. 'Your right Tony.' I acknowledged before continuing on. 'There are a lot of guys in Professional Wrestling that don't take this as seriously as I do.' I said ending this with a dramatic pause before continuing on. 'There are guys who get hired on because management sees some nonexistent talent in them. In Wrestling the name of the game is putting asses in the seats so with some guys it doesn't matter if you have any talent at all as long as you sing and dance you'll become popular and management will book you on the show. If you want a good example look at Deacon Holmes. I saw the eyebrows of both men raise abruptly as I paused again. Due to his shoe deal with Nike and his appearance on the cover of Sport's Illustrated they were obviously familiar with Deacon so I was getting through to them. 'Deacon Holmes can't Wrestle his way out of an amateur kindergarten Wrestling tournament much less a professional Wrestling match. I'll give him credit for being a tough bastard but all he can do is punch, kick and jump off of the top rope. That is not Wrestling! Wrestling is two men stepping into that ring and tearing one another apart with suplexes, wearing the other down with holds and exhausting him by hitting big moves from the top. Deacon is a very, very poor representative of our sport and I'm a little ashamed so see him as some type of…figurehead, for Professional Wrestling.' I said as I momentarily ceased my rant. 'But you still haven't countered what I'm saying, he makes it look like a little kids game, and people struggle to believe it's real.' Kornheiser interjected. 'I'll be the first to tell you that Deacon Holmes is a disrespectful jackass, I mean…he should be fired after that stunt last week where he blatantly stole the Elite's theme music, but he's just like a host of guys in other sports. For example…What about Terrell Owens? Does he make you think football is silly because of the way he acts? Kobe Bryant? Shaq? Joe Horn? Randy Moss?' I snapped as both Kornheiser and Wilbon looked a bit surprised by the intelligent argument I was presenting them with. 'I guess you have a point.' Tony Kornheiser reluctantly admitted. The devils advocate Michael Wilbon was however quick to throw another blackball zinging past my head as he jumped back into the discussion with a simple question. 'What about the steroid use in Professional Wrestling?' I arched my eyebrows as I had been waiting for this question. 'What about the steroid use in baseball?' I shot back. 'Nothing has been proven yet.' Was the simple reply Wilbon gave me. 'Nothing has been proven yet in Wrestling either. I'll be the first to admit that I've seen other Wrestler's use steroids, in fact it's pretty wide spread and it's quite an epidemic. I've never touched them…I mean look at me, I'm barely over two hundred pounds and I'm hardly holding onto that weight.' I said dodging the shot fired my direction. 'You admitted that it's widespread and that you've seen other Wrestler's abuse steroids…if this is public knowledge then why doesn't management crack down on this?' Tony Kornheiser patiently asked. 'The same reason that baseball hasn't cracked down on steroids…the fans. The fans want to see two physically gifted athletes locked in a heated competition and they don't care how they got that physique. It doesn't matter if they did a hundred pushups that morning to stay in shape or if they just jammed a needle in their arm. Management knows about this just as much as I do…probably more. But their not going to crack down on it because we as Professional Wrestler's are very expendable. Most of the ones who use steroids are a dime a dozen so only guys such as myself who have worked their asses off are the ones management really cares about keeping around.' I said in a cold blooded tone. Both men had the appearance of surprise on their face as I had seemingly shocked them by taking everything they had thrown my direction and throwing it right back into their face. With their shots at my sport deflected they seemed to take a different route. 'When our producer told us that you were going to be one of the guests this week I did a little reading up on you. I couldn't help noticing that you were a standout athlete particularly in track and Martial arts. A second degree blackbelt in Taekwondo and you received a lot of interest from several big name colleges, what I think we would all like to know is…why did you take a risky venture like Wrestling instead of just running track in college? Tony Kornheiser inquired of me. 'Wrestling has always been a spectacle that sucked me in like a tractor beam, I've had an ineludible desire to become a professional Wrestler since the age of fifteen. I knew that running track couldn't fill that desire but it could help me in my career. I made the decision to become a Wrestler and my logic was the same as every aspiring athlete…I did it for the love of the sport.' My sentence ended calmly as for the first time today I wasn't forced to defend myself or my profession. Glancing up at the topic bar on the monitor I saw the timer now forty-seven seconds, turning my focus back to the camera the interrogation resumed as Michael Wilbon threw in a question. 'You've talked about your prior injuries so even non-Wrestling fans such as Tony and I have to admit that the risk for injury is high. Why do you keep going back into that ring, night after night, when you know that more likely than not there's going to be some night when you can't walk out under your own power? Wilbon asked peering into the camera over his glasses as the light reflected from his shaved skull. 'There have been several nights where I couldn't walk back to the locker room area under my own strength. There have been times when I had my aforementioned knee injuries there have been times when I had lost so much blood I couldn't stand any longer, there has even been a time or two that I've been knocked unconscious. But just like the concussion cursed Troy Aikman kept pulling that helmet back on I'll keep lacing those boots up and stepping into my field of battle. Once you minimize the violence Wrestling isn't vastly different from other sports. Of course the athletes are a lot different. Professional Wrestler's are generally a lot nicer, cheaper, and tougher than most other athletes. But I guess deep down every Professional Wrestler feeds off of his pride…and there is just an insatiable appetite for victory.' I replied in a mild mannered tone. 'We're running out of time so we just need to get in one last question before the bell rings, tell us Josh…you've ripped Wilbon's boy Deacon Holmes all show long. Who's going to win this week in your match?' Kornheiser excitedly asked. 'My boy?' Wilbon said with a furrowed expression before I spoke up. With a broad smile across my face I let my ego do the talking for me. 'It's pretty obvious Tony, I'm a former Cruiserweight champion, soon to be World Champion and I've got a Global Title shot coming up soon. You're interviewing a guy so talented he's getting three title shots in three weeks! It's a pretty easy match to call gentlemen…I've never lost in a ladder match and I wont lose here.' I stated with a playful wink into the camera. The bell suddenly rang, as Tony Kornheiser looked up a bit surprised that our five minutes had already elapsed. 'Josh, that's all of our five good minutes, thanks for being a part of the show.' Tony Kornheiser said. 'It was great to be on board guys, let's do this again sometime…and watch Global Wrestling's Evolution this week!' I said, finishing up the last six words by shouting them. …And just like that the monitor shut off, the little red light on the camera went dead and it was over. My five good minutes had came to an end. Who cares if Deacon has a Nike sponsorship? Who cares if Deacon has Sports illustrated? I had PTI…and best of all…I had trashed Deacon Holmes. American Youth - 1, Deacon Holmes - 0.
