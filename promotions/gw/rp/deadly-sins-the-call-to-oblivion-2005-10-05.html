Deadly Sins: The Call to Oblivion

5 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Oct. 5-8 .:: ::: ::. Sequitur pars tercia .:: ::: ::. Undisclosed Location Somewhere in Nevada October 5, 2005 Only the early drinkers were inside Hell’s Mouth at the moment. Shadow considered it a fairly apt name for a bar in this location, where there wasn’t much redeeming to look at aside from idiots drinking far past their limit and attempting to stumble out the door. It must have been something of a team sport in Nevada, since it was the one thing that she had consistently seen just about everyone in the whole damned state doing. An unidentifiable metal song thudded in the background. Obviously, it was supposed to set some sort of ambience, but she really found it irritating more than anything else. Then again, she wasn’t here to drink, and she certainly wasn’t here to enjoy the company. Shadow swallowed, hard, and looked around at the people populating the rest of the bar. She’d come in here on a two-day dry spell, and she was about to go nuts. Places like this usually brought in at least a few takers when it came to “volunteering” to be a vampire’s sustenance, but the place was so sparsely populated that she knew she couldn’t have risked feeding here without causing some major trouble. With her stomach growling angrily, she sighed and pressed her fingers to her temples. “Can I get you something?” The heavily Russian-accented voice intruded completely on her musings. She looked up to see a rather charming looking bartender, with thick shoulder-length black hair and particularly icy blue eyes. She turned to face him. “No, I’m afraid not. Unless you have some water on hand, but judging from the amount of drunks already in this place, you don’t.” She smiled wryly, and the bartender grinned a little bit as he walked over to the refrigeration unit and rummaged. He returned a few moments later with a bottle of water, which he set in front of her with that sly grin still in place. “These are early drunks. I like to keep water on hand for when they pass out at their stool later in the night.” She smiled, although only briefly. He seemed to take that as an invitation of rapport, and he leaned over the bar next to her. He watched her uncap her water, watched her sip the first couple of life-giving gulps out of the bottle’s neck. She closed her eyes, pleased at the icy feeling running down her throat. He only watched. When she was done, he extended a hand to her. “Dominic Ivanovich,” he said. “Well, that certainly explains the accent, doesn’t it?” she thought to herself, reaching out to shake his hand. “Ah” Ljudmilu Ionescu,” she returned as she shook with him. She hadn’t been using her real name since she left. It was just a lot easier to use a false one when you didn’t want to be found. Anyone coming into this place looking for a Lia Vlaszy would be out of luck. He nodded, smiling sagely. Like most Russians she’d met, the smile seemed to be half-sad, but that seemed to be a feature of the people, rather than some observable personal trait. “That explains your accent, then. What’s your story?” She regarded him suspiciously. It was rather difficult for her not to. “What do you mean?” He shrugged, the slight smile in his eyes fading into observant coldness. “Everyone has story when they come in here. Besides, you don’t look like you’d be out in middle of Nevada without reason.” She smirked. “Well, you’re right about that.” She paused. In that brief pause, her mind searched for a passable story. “I guess I am just trying to get away from everything. Haven’t had the best run of things lately” you know, trouble with boyfriends, no luck looking for a job. I could go back to Czechia, but I don’t want to let my parents know they were right about Miloslav. Sorry jerk?” She trailed off muttering, and looked the other way, hoping he’d buy it. Dominic laughed. “Hey, pretty girl like you shouldn’t have a problem finding another boyfriend to take care of her,” he paused, then grinned as he went on, “If I wasn’t on the job tonight, I’d take you out and show you a good time.” She raised an eyebrow, looking for the most part unamused. “How do you know I wouldn’t send you back here in a body bag?” Dominic threw his head back and laughed, tossing his wild dark hair. His cheeks flushed ruddy with amusement. “How do you know I wouldn’t do the same?” She smiled, raising her bottle of water once again. “Touch”, my friend.” Another sip passed into her body. Slowly, she could feel the cells beginning to stir with life once again, but she knew that with only water, her energy would quickly be fading. Already, the sweat that graced her skin during the day was a pale-ish pink, nearly clear. That wasn’t a good sign. She closed her eyes exhaustedly, almost resting her head on the bar. She didn’t know the name of the town she was in, and she hadn’t seen a friendly face in almost a week. Then again, there weren’t a whole lot of faces that were friendly to someone like her. Humans seemed to instantly know that there was something amiss, and shun her before she even had the chance to get close enough to do any harm. The sad fact was, most of them, she wouldn’t have even harmed, not unless they first raised a hand to her. Dominic’s observant eyes lit on the woman that he knew as Ljudmilu. Something didn’t seem quite right about her’she seemed too pale, too sickly to be able to travel on her own. She seemed like she should be in someone’s sickbed somewhere, maybe even in the coffin. He couldn’t put his finger on it’save for the fact that it all seemed so familiar to him. She opened her eyes to find the young man staring at her, his sharp eyes looking as though they were scanning for detail. She narrowed her eyes, half-threateningly, and he looked quickly away. Instinctively, she knew that he knew something was up. There was a sudden note of discord between their two spirits. Sudden frenzy to escape drove Lia to drink the bottle of water as quickly as she could. She would have taken it with her if that were at all possible, but it wasn’t’she needed as much energy as she could possibly get before she could even set foot on the road again. Dominic watched with interest. If she was really what she seemed to be, then there was no possible way she could be doing what she was doing. He didn’t know much about that sort of thing, but he knew, with that uncanny instinct of bartender’s, that water wasn’t what he should have been giving her to drink. Lia looked up at him. “Could I get another bottle of water to go please” I’ve got to get moving again.” He nodded to her, half-bowing really, and his boots thudded across the hardwood floor to the refrigeration unit once again. She stood up, fingering her wallet out of her pocket. He returned holding a bottle slightly under the bar level’and when he saw her wallet out, he shook his head. “No, no,” he said, “You don’t worry about paying me. I’ll cover it out of my tips for the night.” She opened her mouth to protest, but suddenly a large plastic bottle thudded onto the bar in front of her. She looked at him, her eyes narrowing at its purplish contents. “I’m not much of a wine fan?” He smiled again, that odd smile, with that seeking look in his eyes. “No worries. Consider this one on me.” He wasn’t going to let her refuse, she saw quite clearly. Quite simply, she nodded, and pulled the bottle towards her. He clearly viewed this as an acceptance. A cursory glance at the wall clock seemed to indicate something to him, and he leaned over behind the bar and flipped a switch. A neon sign buzzed to life almost right above her head. She looked up to read the flowing script. “Abandon all hope, ye who enter here?” She glanced over at Dominic, who was grinning at her. “Very cute,” she said, with a slight roll of the eyes. He grinned at her again. “I try my best to be cute for ladies like you.” She raised an eyebrow. “Now that you’re turning on the cheese, I think I’ve got to go.” “So soon?” She picked up the bottle that he’d given her and stood up from her stool. “So soon” Have a nice night, Dominic.” With that, she beat a fairly hasty retreat from the Hell’s Mouth, corny sign and all. She could feel Dominic’s eyes on her the whole way out, and she knew that only part of that was finding her attractive. Another part was him looking a little too deeply at her, and she didn’t like that at all. Once she was outside and to the bike, she uncapped the bottle, staring down suspiciously into the swirling contents in the half-light. Mottled patches of crimson stirred among the purple-burgundy. She passed it under her nose. The scent hit her like a freight train, and she felt her fangs distend almost reflexively. Blood. An entire bottle full of it. And then came the inevitable question’how much did he know? .:: ::: ::. The Middle of Nowhere Nevada The bike hummed monotonously underneath her. There was nothing else for it to do, and, after she’d gotten over the brief joy of having it crooning and growling gently at her commands, the bike had gotten quite boring. Now, it was quite literally the journey that mattered. The problem with that was that she really had no clue where she was going. At this point, every door seemed closed. It didn’t matter where’the middle of nowhere, suburbia, Las Vegas? she was still an outcast. It didn’t matter what she did. It didn’t matter if she was a gas station customer, a benevolent roadside angel fixing a family’s car trouble, or a nighttime predator ousting another in the middle of Las Vegas. The simple truth was, she didn’t fit in. In retrospect, it was impossible for her to explain how, exactly it had taken her so long to reach this conclusion. Hunting Grounds was the point of origin? she’d never worked so closely with humans the entire time she’d been in PTC, and she could almost say, right now, that it was a mistake. Zenith had only talked to her as long as she’d been useful in kicking WMW in its nether regions. And this week’here, she rolled her eyes, because she’d called in to the office’this week, she took him on in the ring for her championship. She wondered what kind of loyalty Zenith was going to show once there was gold on the line. She wondered if maybe the loyalty hadn’t been his motivation all along. Tessa had always shied away from her. If Shadow had known any better, she would have said that Tessa actively hated her, and the same with LeMaster and Miaskinov, who were simply spectral figures hovering around and pressing their watchful, distrusting glares into her brain. Vivica was perhaps a different story. It meant a lot to Shadow that Vivica could honestly say that she would stand behind her tag team partner, no matter the circumstances, but Shadow had to wonder about how far such loyalty extended. Vivica said she’d done her research, but for all that Shadow knew, Vivica had researched the cute little vampires that you saw in movies. She wondered if Vivica spent a day with her, a week with her? would she still have had the same sympathies? The same concerns? It wasn’t easy to convince herself that Vivica would be steadfast once she saw a crimson-lipped blood-drunk Shadow fresh from a kill. That left out Dawn, of course. It also left out Virgil, Alexis, and Cyrus. Her mind dragged to the McFarlane Estate, in its current state, and it was there that she really felt the most guilt. Dawn and Virgil hated each other. That was plain to see from the way they practically hissed at one another every time they passed in the hallway. And had it not been for her, they wouldn’t have had to be living in the same house, miserably feeling that they were each on the verge of being usurped by her concern for the other. Alexis Key? a young woman who was in the prime of her life, whose concerns and desires were completely subjugated to the vampiress who held her captive in the McFarlane Estate. Her involvement with Shadow had taken everything away from her’whether she had joined The Den voluntarily or not didn’t matter. What mattered was that Shadow was keeping Alexis from going on with her life, in one way or another; if not because of searching for her, then because of being captured and tucked away in Toronto to prevent The Den ever finding her location. And that brought about Cyrus. Cyrus, who should have been holed up in his library underneath the Catholic church in Zdice, Czech Republic. Instead, he was lounging around a Toronto mansion, without the books and the solitude that he most treasured. He was bearing it admirably, but by the time Shadow had left, she could tell that it was all beginning to wear bloody well thin on him’he wanted to go home. She was keeping him from doing it by insisting that he stay in Toronto until all his wounds from Lilith had healed, lest he try to journey again and further injure himself. They all had one thing in common: they were haphazardly thrown together by Lia’s sometimes fantastically fickle will. All of them had better things that they could be doing with themselves’Dawn becoming a PTC sensation, Virgil working with his political intrigues again, Alexis living her young life happily, and Cyrus back in Zdice, engaged in the scholarship that made him so happy. She was the one thing that had plopped all four of them in the middle of Toronto, and she was the one thing holding them in place there, attempting to make a square peg fit into a round hole. It worked positively dismally. She couldn’t figure out why. It was, at least partially, that her kind changed very little, and very slowly, if they changed at all. Humans bored of the indefinite wasting of time, the stasis with no foreseeable ending. And why should they not? Lia could not change what she was. But neither did she have the strength to simply cast off everyone who was close to her, and tell them that they’d be better off living their own lives without her influence. That was, after all, why she’d forced herself to leave. She never could have told them face to face, but hoped against hope that when she came back, they’d have moved on, realizing just how much she impeded forward progress. In the back of her head, though, a voice just whispered that she wanted someone to care. She wanted someone to be as desperate to find her as she was desperate to keep them close to her. But that was selfish. And she’d been selfish enough already. .:: ::: ::. Undisclosed Location “You said that you had something to tell me?” The darkness consumed everything. In the darkness, the messenger nodded. “Da. I was working at the Hell’s Mouth last night, and a very strange woman came in-? “Do you think strange drunks are of interest to me?” He bit off his frustration before it could stem into his speech. If master was in this sort of mood, it would not do to anger him. “She was not drunk, master. She was, in fact, one of your kind, if I judged her correctly.” “Hm” Tell me about her then.” Master seemed pacified, and that was always a good thing. He continued. “She was fairly tallish, dressed in very modern clothing. Slim-fitting black. Long black hair, particularly bright green eyes, and a Slavic accent. She said her name was Ljudmilu Ionescu.” His master twisted a long, dark lock in between slim fingers. “Ionescu,” he mused quietly, “That is a very old name.” The silence spoke volumes. The dripping in the catacomb briefly interrupted. “What did you do next?” He bit his lip. He wasn’t sure that master would be particularly happy that he let this one slip away. “She was drinking water-? “A simple illusion.” “Well, she seemed to really be drinking it. She was very eager’almost desperate’to leave, almost as though I had noticed something about her that I wasn’t meant to. I gave her a bottle of blood, and sent her on her way.” Master sighed. That was never a good sign. “I don’t suppose there’s much else that you could have done. Keep an eye out for her, and if she comes back to the bar, call me as soon as she comes in, and keep her there as long as possible.” “Yes, master.” “I’ve much to think about, Dominic. Run along, will you?” “I will, master.” Just like that, he was left to himself. He was quite uncertain what to think about this little development. Vampires often wandered in and out of the Hell’s Mouth, but mostly the young, inexperienced ones on their way to the Anarch Free State, stopping in for a quick feed. This one sounded powerful. And experienced in manipulation, to boot. Yet the way Dominic had described her manner left something for him to think about. He wondered, briefly, if he should notify any of his contacts about her to at least see if he could figure out who she might be. The name Ionescu rang a bit of a bell for him, but he couldn’t quite place it. He smirked. If she really was in that emotional of a state, it wouldn’t be long before she stepped into a trap that she’d need someone’s help to get out of. And there was very little that went on in his demesne that he didn’t know about. When she slipped and fell, he’d find out’and he might just be there to catch her, if he found her interesting enough. Otherwise, the coyotes or the werewolves one would provide a rather entertaining show. .:: ::: ::. The Middle of Nowhere Nevada The bike was beginning to choke with the dust that sputtered in its metal veins. She’d probably pushed it a lot farther than it was meant to go’it was a nice bike for show, and for racing, but it wasn’t quite meant for cross-country travel. The bike slowed gradually as she pulled off the road and into the dirt-lined emergency lane. By the time she got it stopped, her pants were covered with loose reddish-brown dirt from the calf down. The empty dirt of the field just twenty yards away seemed to call to her. Slavishly, she pressed on towards it, not having any clue why she might feel a connection to some place she’d never seen before. The clarion call was strong, though. She picked up her pace until she came to the last outpost of dry, waist-high scrub grass that seemed to be a demarcation between civilization, even such as it was, and the open, empty wilds that spilled eventually into blank, searing desert. The scent of wind passed her nostrils, but there was something still holding her back. She looked down at the bike, splendidly designed. It was, indeed, a fine machine, which had been Virgil’s main motivation for wanting to help Lia get it fixed. Virgil’s ideas? Dawn’s bike? She swallowed hard, speaking up under her breath. “My apologies, Dawn” I can’t piggyback on memories this whole time. Whatever I do has got to be done on my own?” Two more steps got her to the edges of the vegetation. She tucked the bike into it, noting that the overgrown shrubs just covered it up with a smile of grim pleasure. A moment of hush fell over her spirit as the ties were cut completely. There was a fleeting feeling of dark desolation, but she blinked and forced herself past it. Nobody ever got anywhere by sinking down into despair on the verge of freedom. She wasn’t that weak, no matter how she’d deluded herself into thinking so these past few months. Her fingers fumbled in her bag, pulling out the bottle of blood that Dominic had given her. It was half-empty, and she still didn’t entirely trust his motivations for giving it to her, but blood was blood; it was needed, and denying a need due to who gave you the means to fill it was stupid. As soon as the first taste hit her tongue, she was jolted with a violent shock. The animal took over, and momentarily, the entire bottle had been cleaned of its contents. It fell from her fingertips, the plastic thudding dully to the ground and rolling into the thicket of grass to rest next to Dawn’s bike. She didn’t turn to look at it. She just walked on, almost as though piloted by something outside herself. The wind rushed past her, flighty and wild, tangling through her long black hair and lifting it free-whipping into the air. The cool burn of wind and stinging dirt pressed into the skin of her bared shoulders, almost as though trying to drag her back to what she knew. There was no turning back now, though, and deep down, whatever you might have called her soul resonated with that fact. It was time to turn away before the addiction of connection made her into something pathetic. Lia Vlaszy was the solitary heiress of her family’s name. She was the only one who could uphold what it meant to be a Vlaszy, and one of the only ones left who could truly uphold the teachings of the old Tzimisce masters. But there was something more as well’when you boiled down to the core of her essence, Lia was not a blind puppet, following dictates she was given without question. She was and always had been innovation. She was questioning tradition. She saw the unbeaten path and took it, unafraid of the consequences. Lia Vlaszy was a seeker. Two triumphant arms were raised in a mimicry of wings, on a being standing solitary in the middle of her universe. Too long, she’d been trapped. Now that she had been reminded of her destiny, it was no longer a question of when she’d fly, but only how high she would be able to go this time, unimpeded by her hindrances. .:: ::: ::.
