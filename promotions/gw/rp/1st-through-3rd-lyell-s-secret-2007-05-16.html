1st Through 3rd: Lyell's secret

16 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
1st Through 3rd: Lyell's secret Reginald Atkins Global Wrestling Promo #3116 Date: 5/16/07 Location: Wrestlecade I wonder…what’s next? It’s a common phrase, but more appropriately to the moment it’s the line from a Chevelle song titled those last three words. The song isn’t entirely appropriate to my situation, it being more of a clichéd attack on the stereotypical downward spiral human existence seems to be finding itself in that every songwriter with a pen and a sixth grade education thinks is relevant to our lives. But there is a slight parallelism, if we take the time to take a look. Which we will, because otherwise what would I even bring it up for? The similarity is this: I’ve done my part. I’ve said my piece. I brought out the beast. The beast who tore apart BloodAngel in two consecutive weeks, then dropped a laugher on the opening show last week because he heard my music and saw my video package on the monitor. As if he was expecting me to show up accounting for another of his loved one’s deaths. I’ve done my piece. I hurt his soul, I tried to make him a man. It was my masterpiece, my biggest work of art to this date…and it failed. He’s still worthless. I’d think of more things to do, but Vinny is flaking on me and I have no idea where Lyell has been the past few days. You’re my only friend, you ratty old notebook that collects my thoughts. I shudder at the thought. I got the last laugh, and was so focused on that victory that I lost to a guy who’s obviously thought more about me in the past few months than I have about him. Karma. And if you try to make a joke out of that, notebook, I’ll throw you into the fire. I swear. Karma is laughable, a stupid person’s idea of what a noble person should be like. He got a victory over me in a match that meant nothing. He’s already lost the girl he wanted to shag, he lost the other girl that he wanted to shag, and he lost the staff member he loved so much. (Probably wanted to shag him as well.) Everything he had was gone, and all he has is a W over an unmotivated Brit. What does that amount to? The answer to that is the same answer to Chevelle’s question. Pete Loeffler might not be a philosopher, but he’s sure got it nailed on the head for this one. I wonder what’s next? Nothing. … I’m not trying to say I’m quitting; I’m not some bugger who pretends to quit and then doesn’t, or use retirement as a way to get sympathy and attention. When I say I’m going to do something, I do it. Look at Boswell for proof on that one. What I’m trying to say is that there isn’t any sort of addendum to my master plan. I know now that there’s no way for Nathan to ever listen to me again. I gave him every sign in the world not to trust me but it’s only when it involves his woman that he actually pays attention. Even in her death he’s minge-whipped. Sickening. So I can’t use him as the huge enforcer Lyell was supposed to be. I can’t send him on a tear through the federation, because he can’t beat anyone unless they are distracted. And I certainly can’t face him in the ring myself, that would be ridiculous. So I’m not really going to touch that situation right now. It’s probably better to focus on other things. Like wondering where the fuck has Lyell been? *** Lyell Jett is on a journey of self-discovery. After their last altercation, Lyell left Reginald’s home and started walking until he could steal a car. Once that was accomplished, he started driving, and he hasn’t stopped since. There’s no real destination in his mind; he just knows that he needs to figure out what hell has been going on in his life and what caused it all. Lyell was the first one discovered. Reginald was still focused on other things in the early years, but Lyell wanted to fight. So for a few months he went to Colorado, and he fought in a small promotion called Rocky Mountain Wrestling. They loved the fact he didn’t speak much English, and they loved the fact he was big. Lyell was a star for three months, and then they sent him home because the federation folded. Lyell would be seeing a lot of federations fold before he caught on anywhere. They finally made it in IWE. It was a small place, good for nothing more than carnage and a few short words. Reggie took care of the latter, and Lyell was more than happy to settle on the former. It was harmony. They fought, they won, they dominated. And the two were on equal plane, fighting for the same cause and happy to fight side by side. So what happened? Why is Lyell Jett driving across the country contemplating his slide from partner to sidekick? His name was Morocco. *** Lyell “That’s strange name,” you say in broken English, after it’s established that Morocco doesn’t understand German. You are smoking a cigarette and enjoying this strange club in downtown Philadelphia, Reggie nowhere in sight. It’s a good feeling to finally be more than part of a tag team. You feel free, alive, and perhaps a little drunk. “Well, it wasn’t my given name, of course,” the man drawls, his eyes flickering in a sort of interest towards you. You aren’t sure what it means, and you aren’t sure how to take it, but he keeps talking. “But I visited there a few times when I was younger and I loved it, so after my parents let me out I changed my name.” “Interesting,” is all you say. Inside you think it’s stupid, that no one should name themselves after a place. But you don’t know what else to say. You don’t even know how you got here, really: you were just in the hotel room, listening to Reggie practice what he was going to say to the team you were facing next week, some team from the Scrapyards. You wanted out. So you found this club. And Morocco. “I like to think that I took some of the qualities of the place and embodied them when I changed my name. I like to think that I’m free…open…” Morocco pulls a little bit closer, and you can smell his hair. It’s not a pleasant smell, but you can stand it. What you can’t stand is his hand on your knee. You don’t know what he’s doing, but only two words can describe him. Lodernde Fee. Flaming Fairy. But you’re not bolting for the door. You aren’t sweating. You’re not even moving his hand from your knee. You’re just listening to his voice, but not hearing the words, obviously, because he asks you again: “Are you free and open, Lyell?” You fight the words because you can’t grasp for them fast enough. English has never been something you’ve enjoyed, but in a situation like this it’s even worse. “Free? I’m…I’m no slave.” Morocco laughs indulgently, and the hand moves further up. Your posture gets better and you start to look around, hoping this isn’t a spectacle. It’s not. Similar acts are happening all around the bar, and you suddenly realize what kind of place you happened to find. You want to leave. But you’re still not moving. “You know that’s not what I meant. You see, Lyell, you seem like a guy who is restricted. Kind of repressed. And you just need to break out.” You let him convince you to smoke a joint behind the club, and then you continue to talk. You might have done more than talk, you’re not quite sure. All you remember is coming out of your haze to see Reggie driving a knife into the Fee’s back. He hits him, kicks him, yells at him. And then he comes for you. “You made me kill for you,” was all he said for a long time. You couldn’t say anything back, as you were too drunk and stoned to come up with a formidable argument, and even if you weren’t you were too shamed to fight back. Finally, he continues on his point. “You made me kill for you because you wanted to see what the other side was like? You’re sick, Lyell. You’re not the bloke I thought. I never thought someone so big and menacing could be a tosser.” You try to tell him you’re not, but you can’t really say it with too much conviction – partly because of the drugs and drinks, but also partly because you don’t know for sure if you’re not. You don’t know how these things worked. You’ve slept with tons of girls after shows, and you definitely could do the deed – you’ve just been indifferent about all the girls you were with. Kind of like you were with Morocco. In fact, you’ve pretty much just gone with the flow your entire life. You ask yourself if you would’ve hurt so many people if Reggie hadn’t wanted you to, and you don’t know the answer. Reggie tosses the body in a nearby pit and has you toss the dirt on him. You don’t really think about anything as you are doing it – you’re still in a daze. You come out of it every once in awhile to hear Reggie say something about your betrayal, and you hear him call you a fag at least twice. You do your best not to listen to everything he said. But you can’t block out what he says on the drive back to the hotel. “I won’t bring this up any more after tonight, Lyell. I’ll keep it a secret, more for my benefit than yours. I don’t want to be known as the guy who teams with a tosser. And I won’t be. So once our title reign is done, Enemies of the State is going to split up. I’m going to keep wrestling, and you’re going to get the fuck out of my way. Understand?” You nod meekly, because there’s no point to do anything else. “And one more thing. I killed that guy for a few reasons, most of them good ones. I did it to protect you. To help you. Knowing us, a situation might come up where I need that same favor. And you’re going to do it for me. Then we’ll be even. But until then, if you want this to stay a secret from dad, from others, from anyone you care about, you’re mine. You do what I say. And you don’t complain.” *** Lyell Jett had adhered to the agreement for a long time. He was the right hand man, the sidekick. And he didn’t complain. Until he was sent to hurt Arianna. To repay Reggie’s favor. And he didn’t do it. Because he finally knows the answer to the question: if Reggie didn’t make him do bad things, would he? No. He wouldn’t. So now he’s driving. Away from everything, from everyone. Away from himself. He knows it’ll all catch up. His past, his promise, his secret… But he’ll keep driving. Because when you can’t face your family, your friends, or even the mirror…the road is all you have.
