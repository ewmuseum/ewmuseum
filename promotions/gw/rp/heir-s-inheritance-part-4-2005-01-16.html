Heir's Inheritance Part 4

16 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/16/05 (OOC: All dialogue translated from Spanish except where shown in the dark goldenrod font color.) September 15, 1992 1108 hours 'We commit the body of our departed Mateo Ramirez to the Earth, and his soul unto our Lord,' said the Priest solemnly. 'Ashes to ashes... dust to dust... we lay our departed to peaceful rest. In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost... Amen.' Teresa Ramirez wept openly as the coffin containing the body of her late father was slowly lowered into the grave. Her sister and her fianc? stood on either side of her, sharing in her bitter tears. The handsome young man betrothed to Teresa had his fiancée in one arm, and his future sister-in-law in the other; all three of them crying and holding on to each other in an attempt at consolation. The finality of the situation was still fighting to sink in even as the Priest sprinkled holy water into the grave and over the coffin. The Ramirez sisters would never see their dear father again... and was it their fault that all this had come to pass? Follow my words, not my example, were the words on Mateo's suicide note. Only Teresa and her sister knew what this meant, and it was a shame so deep that Teresa could not even tell her husband-to-be about it. Mateo had committed suicide laden with regrets that he had taught his children how to wrestle and how to fight; simply because they were girls. If he hadn't taught them how to fight, he had reasoned, they never would have come to his defense in that ill-fated cantina brawl four years ago... they never would have been photographed and the defamation article published in Mexico City's trendiest newspaper... Mateo never would have been fired from his career as a Lucha Libre star... his family would never have become social pariahs... and all four of them-- Mateo, his children, and his now ex-wife, Lupe... would still all be one happy family living in the well-appointed three-bedroom apartment that could be considered a luxury dwelling by many in the poorer quarters of Mexico City. It could even be reasoned that if Mateo hadn't taught his children how to wrestle, Lupe would not have sold her body and her soul to a hollow shell of an elderly man with hands as cold as ice; whom even Lupe knew very well considered her little more than part of his collection of wealth, and a pretty face and body to sleep with at night. Had Mateo agreed with his children's assessment of their mother as a live-in whore... and held THEM responsible? Teresa's sister felt a sense of shame that she did not imagine could possibly get any worse. She yearned to pitch herself into the grave on top of the coffin and be buried with her dear father... buried alive to die a painful and lonely death that she feared, but was convinced she deserved. The lovely young woman's body trembled uncontrollably; her face showing a deep scarlet tinge from forehead to neck that showed even though her richly-suntanned skin. Her sister shared this deep rosy tone, shared in her grief and anger and shame... it was something neither of them could explain even to the one man remaining in their lives. Much less... 'Ay, Mateo... why? Why, my chinchilla?' All the grief was purged from the two Ramirez sisters in the space of a millisecond; the florid color in their faces remained, but this time it was bald anger and furious indignation. The younger sister hurled herself forward, dashing forty feet within a heartbeat... the back of her hand whipped across the cheek of the person who said those words, with a loudly resounding smack. 'How dare you call him that NOW,' she spat in a rage. 'Who in HELL do you think you are, using that term of endearment with him even as you hang on the arm of your pimp and trespass where you are not welcome!' For even a twenty-year-old young woman to speak like that to her mother, much less hit her with her hand, was unthinkable in this part of the world. Lupe's husband stepped forth and glared at his stepdaughter. 'Old as I am, I will put you over my knee and give you the paddling you deserve unless you apologize to your mother this minute,' he hissed in a dry voice. 'Never again in my presence will you lift a hand to my wife--' 'You mean your TROPHY,' shot back Teresa, standing beside her sister in defense. 'Just another shiny POSSESSION on your mantelpiece that you keep around to make you feel young again. You treat her like property and even she knows it... we work nine hours each day to pay rent in hard pesos... while we won't even speak of the mortgage payments YOU receive... so pardon us for not giving a damn! You want to come over here and DISCIPLINE us? You're welcome to try!' The look in Teresa's eyes showed that she wasn't kidding around... and Lupe's husband knew that in his advancing age, he would be no match for her. 'You will at least allow her to pay her respects; she is entitled to that much,' he insisted. Teresa's fianc? came up behind the woman he loved and put an arm around her shoulders. 'Do it quickly and leave us be,' he said stiffly, looking at Lupe with an expression full of distaste. Lupe returned the look given her, sneering coldly at the handsome young man as Teresa let him lead her and her sister a short distance away. Lupe kneeled in front of the grave and crossed herself, whispering a fervent Catholic prayer while clutching a bracelet of rosary beads in her hands. Her children felt only the slightest twinge of sympathy on seeing that their mother still did harbor some lingering feelings for Mateo, and was genuinely saddened at his death... but the girls did not bother to point out the obvious fact that it would have mattered much more if Lupe had bothered to show it BEFORE Mateo committed suicide. Lupe finished her prayer and got to her feet, following her husband back to their limousine. No further words were exchanged as the vehicle quickly pulled out of the cemetery and onto the road, out of the lives of the Ramirez sisters, hopefully for good. August 19th, 1994 1341 hours 'Smile!' said Teresa's husband to both Ramirez sisters, focusing the camera for the proper distance and amount of light outside the auditorium. The young women smiled for the snapshot, proudly holding their diplomas before them. It had been very difficult managing to scrape together the money for tuition, and just as hard attending college classes while holding down jobs to pay for them, but they had done it. Teresa had her Bachelor of Arts degree in political science, while her sister had a Bachelor of Arts in sociology. 'Oh papa, if only you could see us now,' said Teresa suddenly, having to wipe a tear away as she said the words. The man she loved came up and took her and her sister into a gentle hug. Nothing could be the same as their father hugging them, but for Teresa at least, it was more than sufficient in its own way. Being married was a healing balm that had been vital to her emotional well-being... it brought her a comfort she knew she could rely on. She snuggled into her husband's embrace, laying her head on his shoulder. She could feel her sister likewise drawing strength from him, even though their relationship was only that of brother-in-law and sister-in-law. Teresa could not help but pray to the good Lord that her sister find a man of her own soon... one who could not only give the girl as MUCH love as Teresa and her husband shared, but also the same KIND of love... that of two spouses together. 'Dinner is on me,' the handsome young man said suddenly. 'I hear they are serving a wonderful coctel de camarones at the Ramirez Diner--' 'I thought it was the Delgado Diner,' said Teresa with a giggle. 'You were mistaken,' said her husband, smiling back as he showed her his driver's license. Teresa gasped at the new license, blinking her eyes hard as if not quite sure she read it right. 'Even if town hall officials might think it far too improper to take my wife's surname instead of vice-versa, they cannot stop me from changing mine through proper legal channels... even to the same surname my wife had before our marriage,' he said with a broad grin. 'We are now Mr. and Mrs. Ramirez and I think it suits us fine.' Teresa put her hand up to her mouth, fighting a losing battle to blink back tears, before finally she threw her arms around her husband and planted a major smooch on him; her lips to his. She heard her sister sniffling quietly with tears of joy as well. Teresa's well-tanned skin suddenly took on something of a pallor to its tone, and she had to take several hurried steps toward the wall of the college auditorium building and lean against it. She took several deep breaths, regaining her sense of equilibrium. 'Teresa!' said her husband in a raised, urgent voice, rushing to make sure his wife did not lose her balance and fall down. The young woman's sister was at her side as well, taking Teresa's other arm around her shoulders. 'I think we need to see my doctor,' said Teresa. Ninety minutes later... The three of them were in the examination room of Dr. Castro, looking a little anxiously at the middle-aged, balding man in the weathered white medical coat; regulation stethoscope around his neck. At the moment, the stethoscope's earpieces were put to his ears as he pressed the plate to Teresa's heart first, then lower down, clear to her abdomen. Dr. Castro gave Teresa the reassuring smile she had learned to come to know over several years of his being her gynecologist. 'You might need to take it easy over the next eight months, and pick up some bigger clothes... that's all. And I suppose congratulations are in order, Mr. and Mrs. Delgado.' Teresa and her husband didn't correct Dr. Castro on the retention of the Ramirez surname... they were too busy gaping wide at the word congratulations and the term of 'eight months.' Teresa rubbed her belly tenderly. 'Doctor... you mean... I'm...?' Dr. Castro put the plate of the stethoscope to Teresa's stomach again and put the earpieces to her ears. 'Have a listen,' he said good-naturedly. 'Nice healthy baby, about a month along.' Teresa's husband gave a light laugh, putting his arms around his wife from behind as she slid off the examination table. 'I love you... Teresa...' Teresa leaned back, snuggling into her husband's embrace. 'I love you too...' Finally the three of them all gave a big Whoop! of pure joy; Teresa's sister and husband lifting the young mother-to-be about an inch or two off the ground and very gently swinging her around in a circle or two. Hugs and kisses all around followed, and even Dr. Castro was given a warm hug of gratitude by his patient. April 22nd, 1995 1828 hours 'This will be your new home, Melina,' said Teresa lovingly to her newborn daughter, carrying her into the apartment. The third paycheck, provided by her husband, had allowed them all to upgrade to a two-bedroom apartment with a working intercom and an elevator. Of course, the new expenses would be of concern, but Lupe's mother, from whom neither sister was estranged, had offered to help. The elderly woman was tickled pink to know she was a great-grandmother, and the Ramirez family had agreed to her being part of their lives and the baby's life in return for a promise not to divulge any of the information to Lupe. Melina was transferred to her father's arms so he could show her to her new crib, in her aunt's room. Teresa's sister now worked shorter hours after a promotion to short-order breakfast cook; it paid the same in net worth, but had fewer hours, so on a per-hour basis, it was a raise in wages as well. The hour spread even synchronized with Teresa's hours at her own job so that babysitting/day-care would not be required. Teresa wouldn't need to leave for work until just a few minutes after her sister got home. In a few moments, the child was sleeping peacefully in the crib, and the door gently closed. The Ramirez family would have a couple of hours to themselves before Melina became hungry and would have to be fed. They put away the baby bottles the clinic had given them, and the newborn-stage formula they had bought. 'I'll start on supper,' said Teresa's husband, who was still a better chef than either his wife or sister-in-law due to his job at the restaurant, and therefore handled most of the family's cooking duties. 'Turn on Noticias on the television.' The evening news program, Noticias, was one of the family's main ways of unwinding in the evening, and they all liked the crew working this particular shift. This program offered an English sub-titled version on a sister channel, and the Ramirez family had begun watching this sub-titled version to improve their skills in the English language. The news program featured some informative articles about a rise in employment opportunities, and some reports of current events about the country, including a mayoral election in Tijuana. Being citizens of Mexico City, the Ramirez family couldn't vote in a Tijuana mayoral election, so the news reports were of no consequence to them. The sports report was something they normally wouldn't have paid much attention to, but tonight was different. Soon as the camera angle switched to the main sports anchor, an inset appeared right beside her, with a photo of Mateo's face. Teresa gasped. 'Papa?' 'It was seven years ago today that Mateo Ramirez, as El Zorro Azul, won the Mexican All-Star Light-Heavyweight Lucha Libre championship. Ramirez was one of the most popular, skillful wrestlers of the 1980's era, testified to by the fact that he was able to win the championship for a third time, seven years ago at the age of 41. Many veterans of Lucha Libre still remember just how tough a competitor El Zorro Azul was-- and perhaps still would be even today, if not for the tragic fall from grace that he suffered in a gross violation of the code of machismo; seven years ago.' A photo from the ill-fated cantina brawl appeared on the television, and the news anchor's voice continuing. 'Newspaper photographer/reporter Miguelito Gomez recognized the fighting style of a handsome man defending the honor of his daughter in the trendy Maria's cantina on April 22nd, 1988. Four drunken men from America picked a fight with the gallant man after he took umbrage at a pass one of the men made at his daughter. The four drunkards eventually overwhelmed Ramirez by using hit-and-run tactics from directly behind and then started to wield chairs as weapons. The situation would have ended then, and no harm done, except for the man's daughter herself, and her older sister.' Another photo appeared on the television, showing Teresa and her sister standing back-to-back over their father as the four drunkards pulled their bruised bodies out of a splintered mass of table wood. 'The young daughter whom one of the men had insulted, took up her father's fight with the aid of her sister, in gross violation of the code of machismo,' continued the news anchor. 'The two young women fought and defeated the four drunkards, defending their father. Gomez published these photos in the newspaper the following morning, also showing close-up photos of Ramirez's unmasked face. Ramirez was fired from Lucha Libre two weeks later after fans began boycotting his matches in large numbers; many even boycotting further entire shows.' The news anchor's expression of sadness looked unbearably fake. 'How such a popular man and champion could have fallen so far, in many other parts of the world, would be a mystery that only a master detective could solve. Here in Mexico, it is heartbreakingly simple. To be recognized and photographed without his mask, and in all the situations to be caught in... being defended by adolescent young women in such gross violation of the code of machismo. In today's Lucha Libre, Mateo Ramirez remains a stark example of the dangers of teaching one's valets how to fight. Even today, in the new CMLL that features small shows in the larger cities, none of the few women who wrestle in the league are from Mexico, the last proud bastion of machis--' An angry CRASH of breaking glass and the crackling of shorting wires and electronic circuits quickly silenced the television set as the heavy geode resting on the coffee table was seized and hurled straight through the screen by the younger Ramirez sister. The geode was the last remnant of the good life they once enjoyed before Mateo was disgraced almost seven years ago. Teresa's husband came running in from the kitchen, holding a small portable fire extinguisher to make sure no sparks from the dying television set the area rug on fire. Melina awoke and began crying, sending Teresa to pick her up and hold her. The Ramirez family stared contemptuously at the dead TV set before unplugging it and retrieving the geode, which was a bit blackened from the shorted circuits and wires, but otherwise intact and no worse for wear. Neither Teresa nor her husband bore her sister the slightest bit of ill-will for her actions... though Melina was a bit less forgiving; the noise having jolted her awake from a peaceful nap and frightened her still-simple mind. Teresa put Melina in her husband's arms and walked over to the coat closet. A small trunk, evidencing very good care, rested on the floor inside. Teresa kneeled in front of the trunk and turned the key that was inside the lock. Inside lay the few of their father's clothes they had salvaged from when they moved out of their mother's mansion in disgust after they were both of legal age to do so. Teresa had waited until her sister was also of legal age; otherwise Lupe could have gone to court to have her younger daughter forcibly compelled to remain in her home, and Teresa would have had to sue her mother for legal custody, which would have been a very messy and public battle. Once both sisters were of legal majority age with the younger girl at 18 years old, Lupe had no power to stop them. Among the clothes they had salvaged was their father's wrestling outfit. All parts of the outfit were well-cared for and preserved. Not a single moth had ever gotten near any part of the outfit. Teresa was joined by her husband and sister in front of the closet. Reverently, they lifted part of the outfit from the trunk and looked on it with the deepest respect and awe. A tear rolled quietly down Teresa's cheek, as her sister began to weep. Her husband extended one arm to lay across both their shoulders, as he held his still-crying daughter in the other. As the light from the hall-lamp shone a soft, ambient glow on the Ramirez family, a solemn vow was made before the trunk, and the article of Mateo's outfit that was held up like some kind of sacred idol. 'We will avenge your name, Papa... we will prove that you were never a pariah, and we will restore the honor to your name... the family name... the RAMIREZ name. Watch over and guide the family, Papa... and give us all the strength to prove wrong all who ever shamed you!'
