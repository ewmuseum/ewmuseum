Pain|Hello

13 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-13-05 The skies grayed over, shadowing the land below. Silver drops of rain fell, splashing their fury onto the pavement, filling the holes worn by time. The brightest of colors were dulled by the dreary sky. The yellow lines, usually so prominent on the black ground, now seemed pale; the grass, usually green as emerald, was now a distressing mixture of black, gray, and brown. Muddy rain puddles overtook the blades, flooding any sign of insect life out of their home. The trees wet and without leaves, dripped with the rain, each thin branch twisting with another to form a barricade of leafless wood. She stepped into this world, weary and confused her brown eyes full of sleep. Stepping from the doorway, she unintentionally placed one of her black boots into a large rain puddle, splashing water over her red skirt. Only slightly aggravated, she ran a hand through her long raven hair before tying it up in a black ribbon. The light drizzle fell onto her sunken form, and so she looked up to the gray sky above. As droplets of water fell upon her pale, sullen face, she exhaled loudly. Looking away from the continuous mass of gray, the dark beauty began to walk forward, somewhat unsure of her surroundings. They were distorted, but vaguely familiar. She turned to walk away from the flooded parking lot and headed up the sidewalk to her left. Coming around the side of the red building, she found herself standing at the end of a large quad, full of what should be lush green grass and leafy trees. Instead, the area was dead. Red buildings surrounded the quad, each dark and distorted. Suddenly, she recognized the area. Starting forward, driven by some unknown force, she stopped at a sign and crouched to study it. Running a hand over it’s drenched wet texture, she felt each carved letter with care. “Playground school bell rings again Rain clouds come to play again Has no one told you she’s not breathing? Hello, I am your mind, giving you someone to talk to... Hello.” Stepping away from the sign, she cocked her head to the side and stared at the sign, puzzled. The rain began to fall harder as she continued forward, completely disregarding the lifeless from, carefully placed in the tree to her right. The form lay, without breath or blink, silently slumped in a few branches. Its arm dangled over the edge of the tree, as if trying to feel for something other than this tree’s twisted branches, eternally trapping it in its agony and pain. The form’s long black hair fell in waves along the side of the tree and in front of its face. Aradia continued forward, surveying this familiar place, so long ago pushed from her memory. The trees, plentiful and surrounding her, seemed to move in towards her, entrapping her in this bizarre, dark, and twisted world. Their shadows moved quickly along the sidewalk, as if the world were revolving around her quicker than she could keep up. Her heart raced, this place becoming more and more familiar by the second. Rain fell hard around her, each rain drop splashing against the cold, hard cement; each rain drop bringing back another memory. The memories were not welcome, in fact, they were uninvited. But yet they still came, rushing over her in a fury of fear and depression. The silence prayed at her conscious, making her paranoid and uncomfortable in this place. Then, a scream pierced the silence. Completely unperturbed by the scream, she turned towards where it came from, recognizing its tone and velocity. Down the ramp ran a girl, almost a mirror image of her, fleeing from the school building at her left side. Aradia closed her eyes, tightly, trying to fight away the thought, the fear, the feeling of disgust. Yet they possessed her being, forcing her to remember. She isn’t allowed to forget. Never, can she forget that day. Another sign appeared in front of the ramp. Aradia approached it cautiously, once again bending down to scan it’s wet surface. Her hair, now drenched, fell down along her face, almost impairing her vision. Undoing her ponytail, she carefully pulled it back once again, this time folding it once over, so that the hair would stay in one place. Then, she quickly returned to her deed, completely drawn to the image on the sign. “If I smile and don’t believe Soon I know I’ll wake from this dream Don’t try to fix me I’m not broken Hello, I am the lie Living for you so you can hide Don’t cry? She looked up, finding herself in the hallway of a school building. Standing before her was the image of herself, long black hair and all, smiling through her tears. It was suddenly apparent that the real Aradia was standing behind a piece of glass and was mimicking what the alternate version of her did, as if she were her reflection. The man, his face blackened by the lighting, came up behind her, putting a hand on her shoulder. She pulled away, saying something completely inaudible. Aradia, watching the scene unfold, found herself full of dread. The man pushed the reflection of herself back into the glass, causing it to spider web. Words began to etch themselves into the cracks of the glass. Aradia began to scream, pounding on the broken glass, hoping to stop the scene in front of her. Perhaps this time it could be different. She watched as the man threw her to the floor, ripping at her clothes. A shriek of absolute terror escaped her throat as she watched, tears streaming from her eyes. She watched and relived every moment, as she never had before. Muffled chokes parted through her lips as she tried to halt her tears. How can she live the lie? How can she keep this memory from haunting her? She can’t hide, it’s always there. There is no escape. The words in the web outlined themselves in red, and she looked at them through her sobs. “Suddenly I know I’m not sleeping Hello I am still here All that’s left of yesterday? Then it made sense: she was reliving this memory from the third person perspective. She was watching the scene unfold. This wasn’t a dream. No, it wasn’t a dream. It was a nightmare she had forced herself to relive in reality. She closed her eyes, hoping for it all to go away, praying that she’d awake once again to find herself out of the glass entrapment and back in her bed. When her eyes opened again, streaked with black from her tears, what she found was the image of her in a mirror. They stared at one another and she knew, then, that she was in two different worlds. The world of today and the world of yesterday. Tears fell from her eyes as she looked at yesterday’s form in the mirror, disgusted by it’s fake smile and perfection. In one swift motion, she punched the mirror, watching as it shattered into large shards. But she still stood there, staring back at her, the same smile and the same perfection. Screaming in rage, Aradia picked up a shard of glass, the sharp edges gouging slices in her hand. She began to hack away at her mirror image, watching as the crimson blood fell from the puncture wounds. But still the image stood and still it smiled, refusing to fall, refusing to falter. Defeated, Aradia threw the shard at the image and fell back against the bathroom wall, covering her mouth with her bloody hand. Silent sobs escaped her lips and barely made it past her hand. Slowly, she slid down the wall, huddled on the floor. With her head in her lap, she cried more, pieces of mirror all around her, reflecting the thousand different pieces of herself. All parts reflected, except for her face. Her identity was lost, drowned to sorrow. hello|evanescence was used in this post.
