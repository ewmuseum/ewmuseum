The Pierce Curse of May 30th.

28 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5.28.03 (ppv) ndMay 29th, 1998, Nantucket, Massachusetts The scene opens to a slightly different setting than anytime before. A beautiful sand covered beach lies to one side, nearly circling the area but not quite, leaving the land as a piece of the mainland. A three story home complete with a third story sun room, cathedral ceilings, marble columns, wrap around decks for both the bottom and second floor, and a carport extension near the driveway has been built on the beautiful shore. A cast iron fence separates the property from the main road just a little ways off from the estate. The scene pans slowly so that we can view this beautiful Victorian home. Silence is abundant if this observation excludes the crashing of the waves onto the nearby New England coast. The area is beautiful and fully picturesque, a true point for a person to lose themselves and to become free of all the attachments of the material world. A land of? The tranquility is suddenly interrupted by the pounding of bass, a 1998 Canary yellow Jeep Wrangler has pulled through the gate of the estate; its severely altered sound system can be heard blasting away with the top down, the driver, an 18 year old Tyler Pierce. His hair is slightly longer at this point, hanging about his head unkempt. Tyler thrashes with the music as he floors the gas, heading right towards the house. He slams on the brakes, the tires squeal and the vehicle barely comes to a stop, turning sideways and skidding towards the side of the deck. Between the music and the brakes, the noise has attracted the attention of those inside. A woman, in her mid to late thirties steps from inside the home quickly followed and passed by a man of approximately the same age. We’ve seen this man before; it is the senior Pierce himself. Both parents are dressed immaculately, the lady in a blue dress suit, and the sir in a dress shirt and slacks. He screams above the music, or at least attempts to, for Tyler to turn the music off. Another boy, slightly younger than Pierce walks out and stands between his father and mother, his dirty blonde hair blowing in the breeze. He’s also dressed superbly; the picture of The American Dream? excluding the bass pounding just feet away. Mark Pierce, Tyler’s father, storms off the porch and towards the Wrangler. With a quick motion, he reaches in and flips off the key. Tyler looks up at him, barely reacting to his fathers? show of authority. Tyler is dressed slovenly in a pair of jeans and a plain blue t-shirt. “What the fuck, dad?” The words seem to spew from Tyler with no emotion what so ever, the question of his sobriety answered in full in the way he slurs his words. Mark Pierce grows angrier and grabs Tyler by the arm, jerking him from inside the car. He speaks with power, power fueled by anger. “How DARE you use that kind of language with me, Tyler!” With a snap of his arm, he’s backhanded his son making it impossible, in his current state, for him to remain on his own two feet. Tyler staggers and hits the ground, his glasses falling less than a foot away. He reaches up, touching his lips gently, almost in slow motion. He looks up at his father as he wipes a small line of blood from his chin. Mark stares down at his son, speaking in the same tone as before. “What is this!” I give you everything, pay for the college you’re about to attend, buy you this car, guaranteeing everything in your life and you repay me like this!” By showing up on who knows what now, almost crashing into our home and scaring us all to death? Explain this to me, Tyler!” Tyler stares up at his father, a blank expression on his face. Most children would have a look of fear or at least obstinate sorrow on their face, but not Tyler. Tyler sits there, his bottom planted firmly on the ground, staring up at his father emotionless. He’s either incredibly brave or incredibly inebriated. “ANSWER ME!” Incredibly inebriated. With the stern command of his father, Tyler’s mind closes down. It gives up on the conscious world, causing Tyler’s heavily abused body to topple to one side, fully escaping from the situation and almost his life. The scene fades to black as Mark kneels over his son, yelling for someone to call an ambulance. May 27th, 1998, Nantucket General. The scene fades in but this time, to a hallway in a hospital located on Nantucket Island. A distraught Mark Pierce paces back and forth in the waiting area, his wife seated just a few feet away, her eyes red from the tears she’s shed in the last few hours. She stares silently, distantly at the floor now. She’s worried and scared but numb all at the same time. Tyler Pierce has been admitted to the hospital after mixing alcohol and amobarbital, more commonly known as Amytal. Mark Pierce paces still, his eyes on the floor beneath him. A doctor approaches the couple and instinctively, Debra Pierce stands from her place and takes her husbands arm and hand in hers, holding closely to him. She’s worried and fears the worst. The doctor has distinguishing gray hair in both his hair and in the beard that rests so cleanly on his face. He places a pin into his pocket and glances up from his clipboard at the couple. “Mr. Pierce, Mrs. Pierce. I’m Doctor Graham and I’ve been assigned to your son’s case.” The words are spoken gently, the subtext resting in the line is that there still is a case to be assigned to. Pleasantries are exchanged but the business at hand quickly rises to the front as Mr. Pierce speaks. “What about my son” Will he be ok?” The concern is obvious but we know there’s more. An anger is built up from these incidents as this is not the first time their son has been involved with the use of illegal drugs. “Mr. Pierce, your son, Tyler, has been taking barbiturates. Barbiturates are what some people call downers” your son has taken, I particular, Amytal. Amytal is a medically prescribed drug but taken as your son has, it can be incredibly lethal. You see, He’s been mixing alcohol with these drugs? hence the reaction that happened today.” He speaks as gently as possible so as not to upset the parents of the unconscious child two rooms down. “Tyler is asleep and he will be ok but I want to keep him here for a few days. I want to keep an eye on him and make sure he doesn’t relapse.” “Yes, yes” thank you doctor. Can we see him now?” Debra speaks finally, her only concern being that of her son. With a nod, the parents of Tyler quickly make their way to his door and inside. He’s lying in a hospital bed; an oxygen tube ran to his nose and an IV in his arm. A heart monitor has also been plugged up near the bed and is attached to Tyler. With the sight, Debra breaks into tears and Mark puts his arms around her. He holds her close as the scene fades to black. May 28th, 1998. Tyler is awake although still attached to the many machines in the hospital room, excluding the oxygen machine. Now much more lifelike, Tyler sits up in bed. Kyle Pierce, dressed in a dress shirt and slacks with no tie, sits across from him in a chair. Tyler doesn’t seem fully up to par but is ten times better than the day and night before. “What about the Princeton interview, Tyler?” Tyler’s reaction is still slow but not as quite as slow as the day before. He glances at his brother and then back down. “I won’t let this happen again, Kyle. I promise, I’m better now.” The words are empty. Kyle looks down and sighs, knowing he would claim this sometime sooner or later. “Tyler” Dad thinks you need to get into a rehab program.” Tyler’s reaction is not slow this time but instead, fueled? “WHAT” A Pierce in a rehab program!” Does he think I’m weak!” That I can’t kick this on my own!” Kyle glances at him, a little surprised at his reaction. “Tyler, just for a few weeks” to be safe. It’s the best thing, Tyler.” He now throws his legs over the side of the bed and rips the IV from his arm followed by the heart detector, his anger growing more heated by the second. “You think I’m weak too, don’t you, Kyle” You think I’m not able to take care of my self? on my own!” Kyle looks down and then back up at his brother, a senior by one year and sighs. “Tyler” I just want you to be the Tyler I’ve grown up with. Not this? I don’t want to see you back here and this is the best way to be sure of that.” Tyler is in a fit of anger; he’s practically fuming and it’s highly visible. A nurse comes into the room, having read the signals, or lack there of, from the machines that he disconnected. She sees the situation and quickly runs out, going to get help. With a hit, Tyler backhands his brother, sending him to the wall with blood tricking from his nose. “How dare you think I’m weak! I’ll show you how fucking weak I am!” Tyler swings at his brother, nailing him across the jaw and knocking him into the chair and over it. The chair and Kyle hit the floor as Tyler stalks over him. He staggers slightly, still weak from the overdose, and presses his bare foot against his brother’s throat. Security rushes into the room, one man tackling Pierce as he begins to stomp on his brother’s windpipe. A male RN rushes the room as well as the first nurse and he quickly pins one of Tyler’s arms down, the rent-a-cop holding down the other. Another cop and the first nurse tend to Kyle and his new injuries. The RN is quick to administer a sedative via hypodermic needle into the arm of Tyler. Within moments, his body goes limp and the officer and RN lift him and place him onto the bed. A nurse walks towards him and begins to reattach the equipment. Kyle coughs loudly, some damage already being done by his brother. The scene fades to black as Kyle is helped from the room by a cop and a nurse. May 30th, 1998, Abuse Rehabilitation Clinic, Nantucket, MA. Kyle Pierce stands at the foot of the steps leading up to the brick building. He’s wearing a bandage on his nose and a few stitches can be seen on his forehead. Another noticeable symbol of his assault is a black eye left by his brother’s vicious right hook. Mark Pierce as well as his wife Debra walk with Tyler towards the steps and his brother. Tyler never looks up at his brother as they pass him, his eyes diverted towards the ground. A few news reporters are being held back by the local police? they’re all trying to get a comment for their biggest scoop: “Tyler Pierce, son of Local Big Business man, Mark Pierce, is institutionalized for drug abuse.” Tyler’s voice is heard over the scene as they march towards the entrance to the building. May Thirtieth, Nineteen ninety seven. I marched, for the first time, with my parents into a building that would rule six months of my life. I was institutionalized for the abuse of barbiturates, particularly Amytal. They were afraid I’d kill myself if left to my own resources? this was the first time I shamed the family name. May thirtieth? there’s just something about that day for me that will always decide who I am? The scene fades to black. May 29th, 1999, Boston, MA. A year had past and so had one semester of Tyler’s freshman year in college. After spending six months in rehab, Tyler exited the program with very few flaws on his record. It was already November when Tyler was released from the program so he couldn’t have two full semesters at college. He would be a semester behind but it was something that needed to be done. The rehab, however, hadn’t done everything it set out to do. Although it had rid Tyler of his problem temporarily, the exposure and pressure during college from both work and peers drove him back into the same direction. Luckily, he was able to keep it hidden for ninety-five percent of the time. It was 11:30 on a Saturday night. May twenty ninth had come and it was almost over. Tyler sits, laughing it up with “the gang” from high school. They’ve all gathered, five of them, to share their stories of great and fabricated stories of sexual triumphs as well as indulge themselves in enough drugs so that the tall tales at least feel real. Tyler sat, enough alcohol in his system to put a small child in a casket, and told stories of his college days. “This one girl?” was a phrase heard very regularly on this night. As another guy, one with jet black hair told a story of twin lesbians, the other guys giggled. They all knew it was bullshit but none of them cared, it was still a good listen. Tyler bent over the glass table in the center of the group and snorted a line of powder. A bottle sat on one side of the table? none of the lines on the table were pure, they had mixtures of everything from powdered barbiturates to cocaine to powdered methamphetamines. It didn’t take long before one of the gang stopped talking. Soon after that, he stopped moving and then soon after that, stopped breathing. An ambulance was called and it arrived on the scene. The four boys stood around, half trashed by the drugs, half sobered by the events. William lay on the floor as a team of paramedics worked over his body. William went into cardiac arrest that night. He was rushed to the hospital once the paramedics finally got him stable. There wasn’t much more that could be said or done. No one was arrested for the nights events, by the time the police arrived on the scene the four boys had scattered. Names were spread though although, nothing could be proven. May 30th, 1999. The papers read: “William Hurst overdoses. Friend and former rehab patient Tyler Pierce, son of millionaire Mark Pierce, may have been involved.” May 31st, 1999. The papers read: “William Hurst, 19, passes away after massive overdose. Friends and family mourn his passing.” Nineteen ninety eight was the worst, I think. I was just too stupid to sober up then. Will died in front of all of us. The headline read of his passing but the pictures told of his friends who lived with the horrible memories. The picture on the front page was of Sam and me. We were dressed in suits, standing outside his hospital room, and we had just learned of his death when a reporter made a dive for the hall and took a photo without permission. I wish it had ended then? and it did. For a little while atleast. I literally locked myself away in two thousand.. I was determined that nothing much would happen? and nothing did. But then I relaxed? I fucked up again. May 30th, 2001. Nantucket, MA. Tyler sits quietly at a bar, drowning his sorrows in a shot glass. It’s painfully obvious however that this has not been his first, nor will it be his last shot of the evening. Shot is meant in more than one way. This is the anniversary of the night that he and The Gang lost a member to idiocy and to mourn and respect this day, he’s chosen to drink himself into a stupor. Tyler stares into his glass, Sam at his side. Both men are fairly trashed, having had quite a few shots throughout the night. Tyler and Sam aren’t the only two men out of their wits in this bar however; a pair of clich? biker’s stand at a nearby pool table, arguing over who should shoot first. “I really miss him, Sam.” The words were stiff but full of emotion. “I know, Ty. I do too” I’ve been thinking about him a lot lately.” The two again take another shot, simultaneously as if it were planned. The rabble behind them begin to argue. “I GET THE FIRST SHOT, DAMNIT.” The first screams to the second. Lets call the first Rabble, and the second Gorilla. Gorilla barks back, never backing down from a good fight as it would seem, “To hell if you do! I paid the fuckin” dolla fifty, I gettin? tha first damn shot!” The two yell back and forth as Tyler and Sam attempt to talk, their conversation being interrupted. Finally, the bar tender walks around and approaches the men, attempting to quiet them. He’s shoved back and to the floor, causing Tyler and Sam to glance back. In a sudden fit of anger over the situation, the drunken Rabble grabs a pool stick and swings it, slamming it against Gorilla’s side. He staggers back and into Sam, causing him to spill a shot on himself. Within seconds, Sam is up in arms. Tyler stands as well but not in time to catch an angered Sam who grabs the glass and slams it, shattering it, over the back of Gorilla’s head. He staggers forward, yelping in pain at first. A fight ensues where Sam is outweighed and out gunned, strength wise. With a single swing and hit, Sam is laid out on the floor of the bar and now, Tyler steps in to defend his fallen friend. With a jump and spin, the intoxicated Pierce barely nails the back of his heel into the face of Gorilla sending him back and to the pool table. Rabble begins to laugh at the display, only angering Gorilla more. With a quick movement, he shoves himself up and grabs the nearest moveable object he can find, which happens to be the eight-ball (go figure), and hurls it at Rabble, nailing him in the jaw. With a thud, he hits the floor. Sam has pulled himself back up and grabs a bottle from behind the bar, figuring to be clich?, and smashes it over the back of Gorilla’s head. The problem with clich? in this instance is that a glass bottle breaking over someone’s head will hurt but not render them unconscious like it does in the movies. Gorilla staggers forward and turns, grabbing Sam by the throat. Pierce is quick to react and leaps atop a barstool and then off, slamming a steel toed boot into the forehead of Gorilla, knocking him to the floor and unconscious with the powerful hit. Sam staggers back, about to vomit from a hit to the stomach that was delivered by the now unconscious clich? animal. The police were called at the beginning of the skirmish by a panicked bartender and the sirens are heard blasting outside the local pub. Rabble attempts to run for it but Pierce isn’t about to let him off that easily. “You still owe me a shot, plebeian!” With a jump, he lands on the pool table and drops to one knee; with deadly accuracy, he sweeps his foot around with high velocity and nails a pool ball, sending it flying through the air and into the back of Rabble’s head, sending him into and through the window. The police suddenly enter, drawing guns on Tyler and Sam as another team arrest the unconscious fool on the pavement outside. “You two are under arrest.” The cop spouts off, his comment matching the rest of tonight’s clich? events. Just to spice things up a bit, as the police officer attempts to cuff Sam, the sickness in his stomach finally has it’s way causing him to vomit, covering the officers uniform in phlegm and alcohol. The scene fades to black as Tyler is put into the back of a black and white unit along with a now unconscious Sam. Both Rabble and Gorilla are also arrested and put into other cars. The news paper read: “Tyler Pierce, Millionaire Mark Pierce’s son, arrested in drunken barroom brawl.” I was ashamed to look at my parents after it happened because I had done it again. I shamed my and my families name? again. Publicly I disgraced us? I was very quickly becoming the black sheep of the family. It didn’t end there though? at this time, I just stuck my head a lil farther up my ass and kept on walking. May 29th, 2002. Boston, MA. The night was a blast. A huge party celebrated the end of Tyler’s senior year in college. Most of the Gang had graduated the year before but due to Tyler’s two institutionalizations, he graduated a year behind the others. The Gang returned? well, most of them. The room bustled with something near a hundred people. No drugs were allowed in the party. You see, the gang had set it up and out of respect for William, they did two things. One, the disallowed any drugs in the home and they reserved a seat at their table. A name card had been placed there, the only one placed. It read: William Hurst ? we love you man. Tyler was in the middle of the action, as if the memories had been lost as he thrashed around, a bottle of tequila in one hand and a glass in the other? one that he kept refilling. The party blasted into the night with no end in sight. People dropped off gradually, leaving for home or for another’s home to celebrate in more personal settings. Most of the Gang was there until the wee hours of the morning. About two, Chris and Ed headed out to do some joy riding; Ed had taken William’s death more personally than the others as he had supplied the pills he had taken. He no longer drank and driving Chris home would have been no problem. Sam and Tyler remained? partying with the remaining guests. Four A.M. came and the party had finally cleared out. The guests had all gone their separate ways with only a few crashed out on the floor. The party had been held at Sam’s house? and by this time, he was passed out on the couch with a cute redhead. Tyler sat up still, the music a little lower than before. He drinks from his glass, already quite intoxicated. All would have been fine if I had stayed there that night. If I had just fallen asleep like the others. But things happen for a reason? the curse continued. Tyler stood from his seat and staggered, trying to recapture his balance. Finally he did and walked out of the home, no one logical was conscious to stop him. Neither of his brothers had been invited? neither of them knew. Tyler staggered across the lawn of a two story home in a rich neighborhood in Boston. With glazed eyes and breath that could kill, he climbed into the Jeep Wrangler parked outside and started the engine. I started the engine. I was behind the wheel and I was driving but I wasn’t there. I can see it like it was yesterday? but I’m not in the driver’s seat. I’m above? watching from the sky. Tyler drives at an excessive speed, cutting corners wildly in an attempt to get home before 5 am when his father would rise for his day. Tyler had to make the fairy? and it left in 10 minutes. He floored the pedal of the Wrangler, it’s powerful V6 engine growling as he moved through this part of the city. Headlights suddenly shinned onto the road before him at the intersection of the street that leads from a main road to Sam’s home. Tyler’s headlights weren’t on. The small, and somewhat beat up, Pontiac Trans Am came around the corner at full speed as well. Tyler’s reactions were slowed but still there; he swerves off the road, throwing dirt everywhere as his wheels dig into a yard that looks like it came out of Better Homes and Gardens. Within seconds, Tyler’s canary yellow Jeep wrapped itself around a weeping willow. The Trans Am also slammed on its brakes, sending it skidding in circles. Tyler wasn’t at fault? but it wasn’t in the clear. The vehicle slid off the other side of the road at full speed and slammed side first, into a light post. Sparks flew over the vehicle as the pole snapped in half and fell atop the vehicle, smashing the top in and hitting the passenger inside. Tyler is unconscious; he doesn’t see any of this. But he remembers it till this day? he will never forget. The driver crawled out of the wreckage and along the ground, trying to get away from the flames that had spread from the engine and alone the top of the vehicle. The passenger inside did the same? but he’s pinned in his seat by the light post. The young man who exited the car turns in time to see his friend struggling to get up. With quick reflexes, he rushes the car and dives inside, lifting the post enough so that his friend can get out from underneath. Both men exit the vehicle this time? but aren’t far enough away with the flames travel beneath the vehicle, igniting the gas tank. The explosion wakes Tyler up. He jerks up in his seat, blood trickling from his forehead and from his mouth. The door of the Wrangler opens and then smashes to the ground quickly followed by Tyler who crawls from the smoking wreckage of his once tough Jeep Wrangler. The screams of pain are heard? Tyler looks up to see the flames engulfing the yard across the street; a tree burns diligently, smoke bellowing in all directions. Two boys came slowly from the fire? but one was not walking on his own. Ed looked up at to see Tyler. “He’s not breathing!” Tyler’s energy returned and he jumped up, sprinting towards the two. They placed Ed on the ground, the side of his face burnt from the explosion, half of his body covered in third degree burns. He had grabbed Chris and shielded him from the explosion, taking the brunt of it as they flew to the ground. Tyler and Chris knelt over Ed until the sirens came. A small crowd had gathered? Ed would live through the night? but only to remain in the hospital. In total, he suffered two broken ribs, 3rd degree burns, a concussion, and a fractured leg from where the lamp post landed atop him. He stayed in the hospital for two weeks, never waking up from his comatose state. He came out of it? but he wouldn’t be the same. The wrecked was chalked up as an accident and Tyler walked away Scot-free? although, he covered the hospital bills for his friend. I will never forget that night. I will never forget the look of Ed’s face as I ran towards them? as Chris dragged him away from the flames. I will never forget how sober I was when I saw him nor how inebriated I was before that point. The look on Chris’s face as he yelled at me that Ed wasn’t breathing. That night haunts me? it was 12:01 AM., May Thirtieth, 2002. My life changed that night. I saw two friends of mine almost die, one put into a coma for two weeks. May 30th, 2002, approximately 7 am, Nantucket General Hospital The scene fades in; he’s pacing back and forth in the hallway of the hospital. A bandage has been applied to his forehead and the blood cleaned up. His clothes were dirty and disheveled. Blood splattered his right shoulder. But aside from the head injury, Tyler was fine. Chris sat feet from him, his arm in a sling and a bandage on his nose. Dr. Graham comes from a room just down the hall. With the sight of him, Chris stands and both boys approach him. “What’s the deal, Dr.” Is Ed ok?” Dr. Graham is slow to respond. He glances down at his clipboard and then back up at the two boys. “Your friend is alive” but he’s hurt pretty bad. We’ve telephoned his parents? they’re on their way.” “Can we speak to him?” “No” the answer was stern and simple. Dr. Graham had already turned away from the boys but with this Q&A, turned back. “He’s in a coma” he’s being moved from the emergency ward to intensive care. His parents? they’ve requested that neither of you be near him for his remainder here.” Chris turns from the doctor as he walks away. Tyler stands there, silent, dumbstruck. They glance at one another? nothing can be done. They’re totally helpless and they know it. “No more?” Chris glances at Tyler. “No more what, Ty?” No more drugs? no more drinking. That’s what I was thinking but it’s not what I said. “No more of this shit.” Was all that Tyler could get out. Chris just looked at his, still not knowing what he meant. His mind was in no state to attempt to explain himself? nor was Tyler in any state to want to do so. He called my parents and explained the situation as it was but then. Then he made a request. “Dad. I” I know that I’ve messed up and I’m sorry. I want to make this right? but I can’t do it on my own? not right now. I’ll pay you back? I swear but Dad, can you please cover Ed’s hospital stay? I love him? just like I love Chris and Sam? just like I love William. I ? I can’t do this anymore.” The scene shows Tyler’s face, beaten and scarred. The scene cross-fades, showing the same face a year later. Tyler Pierce is seated in the back of a limousine wearing an impeccable black suit, black tie, and blue dress shirt. The limousine slows to a stop and Tyler climbs from inside. May 29th, 2003, Fairview Cemetery, Boston, Massachusetts Three other cars are parked nearby. The scene pans around to show Tyler’s back and his destination; three men stand, in black and grey suits, around a gravesite. Tyler approaches, a bouquet of yellow and white roses in his hands. The four men all stand silent around the headstone. Sam is silent, his eyes staring at the engraved name. Ed is also silent; his eyes covered by a pair of sunglasses? his face still slightly scarred on the left side from his severe burns. Chris stands silent as well, a pair of glasses hang atop his nose. Tyler kneels before the grave and gently places the bouquet amongst three other bouquets. The scene pans up from the bouquets to allow us to read the headstone: William Bryant Hurst 1979-1999 A loved friend and son. He will be eternally missed by the many lives he touched. Our scene fades to black.
