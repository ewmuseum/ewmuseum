Caught in Eternal Flame

20 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-20-05 Brown eyes burning with desire, her white form pressed further into the counter, bringing her face closer to the mirror. Darts of pain and pleasure shot from her eyes and stabbed at her haunted reflection. Then every muscle in her body tensed as she hunched and howled, screaming at herself, screaming at the room, screaming at her life. Beads of sweat ran in streams down her delicate face. Looking up slowly, she began to breathe in and out, panting. A look of indifference painted itself across her face as she cleared her throat, sore from her screams. Quickly and silently, she threw herself down in the chair behind her, bored with the reflection in the mirror. Forcefully resting her chin on her hand, she stared at the black wall next to her, refusing to look back at the image in the glass. This week had become increasingly dull. The warehouse was off limits, on her own accord. Seeing the girl, tattered and bruised, would only inflict more pain? and possibly get her arrested. So boredom plagued her being. Bars in the area could not keep her interest and other clubs were just not her scene. Airhead bimbos with barely-there skirts and less-than-appealing tops are enough to make her skin crawl. Plus, the sexist excuses for men that frequent those places are not her idea of a good time. However, there must be something she can do. She has spent so much time wading in her own pain, hate, and self-pity lately, because she has nothing more to do. Even sitting in this chair now, twirling her long raven hair around one finger, has caused her to think about disappointments and failures just in the past week. Sighing, she jumps from her chair and looks around the room, darkened by the night outside. A few candles burn on the desk to her right, making the lighting more intimate, but creating an eerie atmosphere. For a few moments, she stares at the flame of each candle, captivated by their dancing beauty. If only her life were that of a flame. She would have one simple task: to continue to burn and light a room, or give a scent, basically create some mood. Her task would be over once the source of her flame ran out. Life as a flame would be easy and free, eternally dancing around the source of life. Fantastic colors would be her ensemble, heat would be her comfort. Her purpose predestined when the match is lit and her book closed when the candle turns to a dripping puddle of wax. How appealing. But what is her life, as a human? As Aradia Xiomara! Pain, despair, hate, confusion, no idea of true purpose. Those are the ingredients that create her flame; those are the aspects of her life that cause her to do the self-pity tango. On top of it all, she is forced to live in two different worlds: that in which she now stands and that of her career. Now, she must stand in this cold, dark room, dreaming of life as a flame, but knowing her life is inundated with misfortune and the ghost of memories long since passed. Memories that seep through her skin and back through her veins, coursing through her body in waves of anguish. Then there is the other world of her career, where she must make use of her false face; her professionalism mask. She must be perceived as angry, spiteful, and be equipped with constant attitude and sass. Everything about her job is appealing: the fights, the adrenaline, the release, the cheers and the jeers, the pretense and the make-believe, and even the losing and growing. But living life in a lie, putting on that false face, gets harder every time. She pretends to dance and have purpose, but in all reality she is more lost than a child in an accounting office. Aradia finds herself sprawled out on her bed, shielded from the world around her by the sheer black cloth that hangs in elegance around the bed. The dark oak posts twist up from their grounding place in pictures of hope and despair. Her soft crimson spread clashes wonderfully with her raven hair, as it finds itself sprawled across the sea of red. This life of hers is absolutely troubling. Why must she lay here, wallowing in her own self-pity, beating her situation into her head like a drummer with his passions? Why can she not be the flame, dancing around its life source? Then her eyes widened in sudden realization. Why shouldn’t she live like the flame? Why should she lay on this bed in pity, no matter how comfortable she is? A sly smile works its way across her lips as she springs from the bed and stretches. A black cat slinks into the room and upon seeing its master; it purrs wildly and prances toward her. Aradia scoops the feline up into her arms and begins to scratch its exposed stomach. The stomach of the cat is white, completely throwing off the idea of the cat being completely black. Aradia’s black nails become lost in the sea of white fur as she contemplates what to do with her evening. A Saturday night in this town is worthless, so she must find something better to do with her time. Enough time has gone by, hating herself and doing nothing about it. She nuzzles the cat’s face before it meows loudly and jumps from her arms. What is there to do around here? Ideas and thoughts run races in her brain; she trips each one before they get to the finish line. Working out is a lonely activity, going anywhere but the warehouse is troubling, and it’s not as though she has friends in this place. The friends she does have are back in Pittsburgh. Sighing loudly, Aradia walks over to her make-up desk and sits down in a hurry. She surveys her appearance, devoid of any make-up. A pale face stretched across bone. Brown eyes that pierce her reflection with daggers of jaded faith. A coy, twisted smile which barely makes its way past her light pink lips. Eyebrows thinly plucked, artistically twisted around a metal piercing. A tiny ruby stud flashes on her nose while the hoop in her lip moves as she drags her teeth over it. Suddenly drawn from her daze, she shakes her head and lets her hair fall in front of her face. Without any expression, she slowly pulls it back and clips it to the back of her head, making her face more visible. She reaches down to the desk and snatches a bottle of pale foundation. As she begins to apply it, she continues to make plans for the evening. Perhaps the warehouse isn’t quite as off limits as she is making it. The place is huge, dark, and loud. The chances of her being seen are slim. Plus, it’s been awhile since she found herself a decent man. Her escapade on Valentine’s Day does not even begin to count. She can still smell his gut wrenching cologne and the image of his cocky smile is still engraved in her brain. Its pathetic how she won him over, by simply distracting him with sex. It’s all men think about. Not that it’s necessarily a bad thing. But the men at work are less than satisfying. Pumped full of testosterone and, in most cases, steroids, she knows what is beneath the trunks will only be disappointing. In this dilapidated town, there are no men worth giving her time or life to. Then again, no man has been able to win her over since? him. She cringes and drops the mascara in her hand, watching as the black liquid flows across the desk and makes its way toward the edge. Cursing, she scoops up the tube and screws the lid on tightly. She picks up a rag from the desk and quickly wipes away the ooze, smearing the dark oak. Again, she wipes at the mark, but finds that she has stained the desk. Defeated, she throws the rag down and then looks up at her reflection. She suddenly realizes that she has finished her make-up. She arcs an eyebrow, unsure of when that happened. She must have been that lost in thought. She stands and studies her outfit. A black gown, tightly hugging her breasts and torso, accentuating her toned body. At the hips, the gown flows out more, leaving her walking room. Unhappy with this look, she walks over to her bureau and throws it open. Without a thought, she pulls out a black and red stripped shirt and then a tight black t-shirt with it. Pulling out pants and a short skirt, she shuts the doors and turns, unzipping her gown as she goes. As the gown slides down her body, she quickly steps out of it. A quick glance at the clock tells her that time is running out. Quickly, she throws on the black cargo pants. Next, she puts on the stripped shirt and then puts the t-shirt on next. After that, she puts on the black and red fairy-skirt. After sitting on the bed, she picks up her black combat boots, laces them, and then puts them on. Finally finished, she looks back in the mirror and feels a sense of satisfaction. The shirt brings a smile to her face. It reads: “Smile, Life Sucks” In big red letters. Sitting back at the desk, she opens a jewelry box and rummages through a few things before pulling out a black chocker of silver spikes, a similar bracelet, and dangling red and black beaded earrings. As she puts these things on, she looks at herself more thoroughly. Such a beautiful girl, graced with beauty. Her muscular body, intimidating yet beautiful, gives her leverage in the workplace. So many men are quick to judge her for her gender, before realizing her skill and strength. A smile creeps upon her now black lips, knowing that this always works to her advantage. Men, misinterpreting her skill, always find themselves in a state of shock after only a minute with her. She stands, smiling at herself. Her decision now on the warehouse, she quickly throws on the black leather duster than hangs on the back of her chair. She runs her usual check: cigarettes, ID, wallet, and condoms. Nodding, she walks out of the room and into the living area, disregarding the cats light footsteps behind her. She steps over the broken shards of mirror she has yet to clean after breaking it last week. Upon reaching the door, she snatches her keys from the ledge next to it and then throws the door open. Perhaps going out tonight is not the best of ideas. Opportunity is now banging at her door; Monday marks an important day in her career. After years of exhilarating training and painful lessons, she has reached a milestone. Thoughts of victory rush through her as she locks the door and descends the stairs of her apartment. Without second thought she throws open the main door to the apartment complex, rummaging through her pocket for a cigarette as she does so. Looking up from her pocket, she finds herself face to face with two men, both dressed in brown overcoats and with brown fedora-like hats on their heads. They stare at her with indifference, but a slight twinge of anger. Aradia takes a step back, caught off guard by their presence and size. One man looks at her with a crooked stare before speaking. “Aradia Xiomara?” She nods, recognizing her own name. Overtaken by fear, she takes another step back. Then, curiosity and aggravation take over, bringing back her courage and attitude. The men step forward and take her by the arms. She struggles, but they quickly throw her arms behind her and drag her through the door, as she kicks and struggles, fighting with them. “Don’t fight. You might want to come with us, we have something you’ll be interested in seeing.” She tries to argue, but one of the men quickly covers her mouth with a black bandana and then secures it tightly. Defeated, Aradia disappears behind a corner, the men dragging her roughly down the sidewalk. She stops fighting, deciding that she’ll figure out what’s going on and then make her escape, if necessary. Now completely out of sight, the night turns still, silence preying at the minds of those who walk the streets in the distance. A car door opens and then slams loudly, as another two do the same. Then, an engine starts and a black SUV pulls around the building, both men sitting in the front and Aradia in the back, sitting up straight. Her mouth still gagged and her arms still tied behind her back. The SUV drives quickly into the distance, disappearing down the street.
