Deadly Sins: Through Acheron's Waves

Sequitur quarta pars .:: ::: ::. “Midway upon the journey of our life I found myself within a forest dark, for the straightforward pathway had been lost?” Her fingernails dug into the surface beneath her. It was soft, moist, and pliant. She felt smothered, as though encompassed by it, but she had no conception of what it could possibly be. This darkness was all encompassing, weighing down like a ton of ice atop her, holding her in stasis. She started to struggle, but there was no movement occurring in her muscles. That was when the panic seized her, rent through every nerve and synapse inside her, shooting rapidly towards the surface like it was jet propelled. She opened her mouth to scream. “Ugh.” The taste that came into her mouth was raw and earthy, the material cold and grainy, though not sandy. She spat, and at that same moment, opened her eyes. Her hands were shaking with the effort of holding herself up from the earth. Her fingers were covered with the thick, spongy black dirt, and she could feel its coldness clinging to her face, could even feel the cool seeping through her clothing where the dirt was rubbed into the fabric fibers. Slowly, she got to her feet, her eyes casting from side to side. Thick tree trunks ringed her on every side, gnarled into spectral fingers. The canopy of dark green leaves overhead was so thick that only chance sunlight leaked through with the windy sway of the tree limbs. Yet even that was sporadic, only coming with occasional bursts’for the most part, this dark wood was silent. No whispering leaves sung in the calm. Surely, this was no longer Nevada. The panic began to rise again. Where the hell was she? How was she going to find her way out of here if she had absolutely no clue how she’d gotten here in the first place? Her stomach twisted in the beginnings of the blood hunger, and exhaustion quaked through her weary bones’but there was no blood in scent, and no water in sight. She took an uncertain step forward’and then noticed the shadows slinking around in the darkness outside this small grove of trees. The flash of sunlight dimmed as another gentle gust swayed the trees, and a pair of yellow eyes gleamed, the light shifting to reflect off of spotted fur. She opened her mouth, but again, she was helplessly mute. A growling rumble echoed through the trees of the forest, and there was another set of eyes passing under the shafts of golden sunlight, here a paw, a flick of golden, a fur-tufted tail. It was not the Red Fear that seized her, but something much more primal. Any powers of animal communication were lost to her now, amid these savage creatures’but then she smelled the most terrifying smell of all. It was unmistakably lupine. Her feet moved before her brain could urge her to rationality. She bolted through the nearest opening, the opening farthest away from the dizzying, intoxicating wolfish scent. With her nerves screaming as they were, it was impossible to think straight. But there was no need to think straight in order to run, to escape. And that was foremost at the moment, foremost in every vampire’s mind when they smelled a lupine’you run, or you die. The latter had not been in her plans for today. She closed her eyes for a moment, pushing ahead on the winding path with a burst of speed. There was no consideration for what way was the right way. The right way was where the scent was fainter, but it was hard to tell which way that was? she could smell it behind her’or was that in front of her? Was she running into a trap? Behind her, a howl split the air, and thunderous legs pursued her. Her lungs burned with the urge to scream, but that would have only helped her marauding pursuer find her more easily. Tears started to ooze forward, but that was not possible’Lia Vlaszy did not cry, she did not cry from fear. Yet she could not stop. Forcing herself onward, she could feel the weakness starting to feather up into her limbs, slowing her and slowing her? and then she hit the ground. Hard. The tears spilled forward, she screamed, expecting any moment to feel the teeth begin to tear her flesh. The first touch was cold, almost cautious. She screamed as loudly as she could? And after that, there was abject silence. A small huffy sigh penetrated first. She opened up her terror-squeezed eyes to see a pair of black perfectly polished dress shoes. “You realize you are safe on this side of the forest, do you not?” “Huh?” Her word came out gasped and uncertain’much like the thoughts that were running through her head. She’d smelled the lupine, still smelled it, in fact? but the two of them were alone. Perfectly alone in the calm, peaceful forest, slightly scented of animals and earth. “I said that your screaming is entirely unnecessary. The she-wolf will not pursue you on this side of the forest.” “The she wolf”! What about the lion? Or the? leopard?” “Neither will they. You are out of their territory, if you choose to think of it in such a way.” Slowly, she got to her feet. Her entire body was still trembling with exhaustion and exertion, but that didn’t stop her from taking in the gentleman who spoke to her. His attire was a three-piece Victorian-era suit, black satin pants with tiny white stripes running down them, and a white, immaculately pressed and ruffled shirt emerging from underneath a black satin vest of the deepest sable she had ever seen. A cape hung draped over one shoulder, undulating gently in the now-and-again fingers of wind that shot through the forest like brief arrows, only to fade away again. Only the hat was out of place’it perched on the crown of his head, the wide, sweeping velvet brim covering his face in darkness. The brim was tastefully ornamented with pearled shimmering accents, and a black feather swept dramatically backwards from the left-hand temple. Two rings of gold glimmered from beneath the hat’his pupils. No sunlight touched them, but they seemed to glow of their own volition. Her being seemed to tremble in his presence. “Who” who are you?” He bowed slightly, but only the cloak shifted with the motion’his face was still invisible to her. “An Italian Gentleman, who has a vested interest in your well-being.” Despite the cryptic answer, she was no longer afraid’she felt as though she knew him. “I?” She turned back, pointing into the forest. “Did you see” That? that beast that I was running from? I found myself walking through the forest, but then I couldn’t go any farther on the path? they blocked my way, the cats, and the? the?” The Italian Gentleman raised a hand, and pointed through a break in the trees. A sweeping, tree-covered mountain rose in the distance, seeming to end its upward descent only in a gleaming pinprick of light that seemed to touch the very firmament. “She guards the path to the mountain.” He seemed to look at her intently, peering into her very soul with a gaze that made her feel as though she was transparent. “I see?” “You see what?” She nearly grabbed onto his arm as he started to walk away from her. But that would have more than likely annoyed him, and she was too desperate for his protection to chance that. “I see that you cannot climb the mountain” The imprint of your soul will not allow it. There is another path for you. The wolf will not allow you past her? she feeds as a glutton on those who would pass her way, and is never sated.” He paused, the circle of his eyes glowing a little brighter, and then dimming slightly. “I will show you the way, at least insofar as I can. I cannot pass on to the mountain” another will be your guide, then.” She didn’t want him to leave’he was the closest thing to comfort she’d seen in a long time, and she couldn’t even explain why. There was no reasoning why a man dressed in clothes far too anachronistic for 2005 and pupils that glowed unceasingly should be more comforting than a werewolf, or more comforting than another guide. She looked up at him, nearly shaking. “Please” show me the way out of here.” He nodded, only grunting under his breath in a form of assent. With a graceful movement, he motioned her to follow, and pushed aside a branch to start down another path, which had been previously hidden to her eyes by the panic surging through her. She swallowed hard, and followed behind, the muscles of her legs carrying her on once again. Something told her that the journey ahead would be rather arduous. .:: ::: ::. “But I, why thither come, or who concedes it” I not Aeneas am, I am not Paul, nor I, nor others think me worthy of it.” For twenty-four hours, she had been under his watchful eyes. The grove he had taken her to contained a tiny hut, fit only for one person, but fitting two if necessity asked. There, he had fed her, and given her a cloak to wear on the next day’s journey. And as dusk slowly fell, and shadow passed over the land, they stood outside the hut, in the clearing, staring into the forest. Lia shouldered the cloak on, and nearly huddled in it, trying to avoid the breeze that yesterday had seemed so gentle, and now so cutting. She cleared her throat. “Signor, measure my courage by this statement if you must” but if the journey is so arduous, should I not just turn back? I did not come searching for heroics, I was only finding my way through the wood. I am no Odysseus, or? or Aeneas?” “Hmph.” The Italian Gentleman made a rather displeased noise as he turned to her. Still, there was no face, and there had been no glimpse of it all night, but his pupils glimmered goldenly at her. “This is no place in your journey for you to be afraid, not when you have come so far. Will you go home to those for whom you endeavor to change unchanged” Will you fail them?” Those questions hung heavily for a moment? he only knew what she had told him of her mysterious, self-driven journey, but he seemed to know volumes more. “I will tell you of what I have seen?” He sighed, then took a deep breath, as though beginning a long story. “I was among the others of my kind, the ones who have not passed from memory, but hang suspended, forever enlivened but deadened by the passing of time. I could see you, and though I longed to help, I could not. Look into yourself, and see how you have shut others out. You may well see how difficult I would find it, who only know you these few hours, to help someone who was so intent on helping herself without the meddling of other souls.” He nodded softly to himself. “But in our realm, where nothing changes, there was change. A woman came, a woman clad in shimmering white gossamer, who looked to me with tears in her eyes. “Help her,” she said to me, “Help her, because she is so lost in her way that she may never find her way home again. I’ve already let too much time pass by, and I only come to you in love, love of her, and does that not unite us” We must not let her falter.”” The Italian gentleman took a reflective step closer to the forest, staring deep into the dark, glimmering wood. “She told me that she had seen you on your way, that you had taken a wrong turn, and were trapped in the forest, that your own terror would be the undoing of you. I am not usually a man to be moved by pity, but she was so fervent” so urgent. I assented, and I came to your aid, as you see me now. I saved you from the beasts, I brought you here, sheltered you, fed you, clothed you, and told you I would show you where you wish to go.” He whirled around to face her, his eyes blazing in the dusk. “Why is it now that you hang back” Why do you wish to go back the other way when you haven’t attained what you set out to attain? It’s only been three weeks, and surely you don’t expect anyone to believe that you’re a changed person already?” He sneered at her’or rather, she felt that he sneered, since she could see nothing. He could have simply been gazing at her impassively. “She cares about you, that benevolent fae watching over your every step. And you know there are others, not just her. Others, who would put themselves beneath your feet if only it would make your journey smoother to walk upon their bones. Will you devalue their love by sending me on my way, and taking the easy way, running back home before you have discovered anything?” He paused, then shook his head vigorously. “Your journey will not be an easy one. You must journey to the depths of yourself, your fears, your faults, your every insecurity. You must conquer many demons along the way, your own, and the demons that others would set in your way as impediments. You know what you face ahead of you, and three weeks ago, you undertook the journey. Now you will turn back” Answer me.” Her soul felt as though it were blazing at his words’and it was not blazing with the heated anger of being chastised. It seemed to be leaping and dancing of its own accord, perhaps foolishly and recklessly urging her forward, but he was right. She had received the calling, and she could not turn back when she knew the right way lay ahead, with the Italian Gentleman at her side. Slowly, Lia nodded, the wind gently tucking strands of her ebon hair behind her ears. “My will is your own, then, Signor. Please, if you will still show me the way, I entrust myself to your care.” “Then we go,” he nodded solemnly, “Steel your heart, Signora. I make no promises about what you will find. Though you may have a guide, the terror of this savage garden is beyond terror for even the strongest souls.” She spoke no resistance, and he led her into the growing sheltered darkness of the forest, the whispering, bitter winds chanting spell-like behind their backs as the leaves enveloped the two side-by-side forms. .:: ::: ::. “Through me, the way is to the city of woes...” Long black hair whipped out behind her as the wind picked up in its pace. It was all she could do to hold the folds of the cape to her body. She squinted her eyes slightly, trying to read the sign. “Before me there were no created things,” she spoke aloud, “Only eterne, and I eternal last.” Her face took on a puzzled look, and the Italian Gentleman offered her no assistance. He presumed that she would figure out on her own what it meant. And in the instant that he saw her face blanch with confusion and not a small hint of fear, he knew that she knew. “This is?” she stumbled, trying to get it out, “It’s” hell?!” The wind howled at that moment, whipping past with more ferocity than it had previously blown for several days now, and that split them apart for a good few moments. There was no necessity for speech at this point in any case, but when she looked at him expecting to find sympathy as it died down, she found him laughing under his breath. It was not sinister, merely humorless and even in tone. “Where did you think we were going?” “I can’t! It doesn’t even exist, my existence is proof enough that there’s not a hell, and not a God either.” The wind died off rather suddenly, containing the breath of unbearable ice in it that made her shiver most violently. His pupils glimmered at her faintly, neither malevolent nor affectionate. “Are you so certain of that?” Her silence confirmed her doubt. “Come along with me, then” abandon your fear at these gates, and I will show you what you’ve come to see. I make no promise of what you will feel about it.” With gentlemanly elegance, he extended his arm to her, and she slipped hers through, resting her hand in his palm. He led on into the stone-streeted vestibule, unaffected by the gruesome carvings on the gate’but she froze almost directly inside. Screams struck her ears like stones thrown at a drum-head. They were piercing and dolorous, they were shrieks, cries and agonized wails of every description, blending together into a cacophony that it was difficult for any one voice to scream over’but it reminded her of so many echoes of the past. Her psyche felt as though it were shattering, a fragile glass rose in a hurricane. These were screams of pain, abject agony that filtered up from whatever was ahead of her. She froze in terror, unable to take another step. Too many times, she’d heard these screams, these screams from people she had loved that were now gone, consumed by the sun or burned alive? too many times the screams like these had been inflicted by her own hands. Suddenly, her knees would no longer hold her, and she clapped her hands around her head, but it was no use. The sounds still filtered through, and she joined them with her tears, tears that bubbled up like an Artesian well, without warning. It was all she could hear. The screams, screams, screams, screams? “This woe due to those who could make no decisions in their life. Forever they drift, unable to attain the glory and love of their creator, and unfit for the tortures below.” For some reason, that hit home. And it hit home very, very hard. From behind her, a pair of hands encircled her waist gently, almost lovingly, and helped her to her feet. The tears ran in streaks down her face, and she made no effort to dam them away. “What use is your pity?” the Italian Gentleman mused, almost to himself, “They are deserving of all they have purchased in life.” Her stomach turned, but she could not tear her eyes away from the writhing mass of bodies that she could see not four feet to her left, all straining to find something that had been unnameable to them in life, and escaped them now in death as well. She shuddered. “We cannot watch them all day,” the Italian Gentleman snapped gruffly, “Come with me” the boat’s here.” ??boat?” Her voice trailed off lamely at the end of the question as she turned in the direction that he was indicating and saw the rushing river that was before her. How could she have missed it before? And across it steered what seemed like a primitive gondola, made of groaning wood that seemed ready to split at any moment. “Woe be to ye on the western shore!” The voice made her cringe, an unwelcome visitor into her absorption of the spectacle. And then she saw that it came from the hunched, gnarled figure steering it across the tumultuous current. The figure bellowed on, its voice a growling bass that did not seem to befit the spindly precariousness of its limbs. “No longer aspire to heaven! I come to lead you to the other shore, to the eternal shades in heat and frost?” The Italian Gentleman appeared to roll his eyes. “He never gets tired of that speech, but I tell you” it becomes very tedious for me, no matter how much he enjoys it.” The oarsman trailed off as he saw her standing on the bank. His eyes passed over the Italian Gentleman completely and burned into her, flaming red disks that rotated round and round, hypnotically dancing, dancing... “Wandering soul! Withdraw from these dead’your body lives not, but your soul has not been called here. Begone!” The Italian Gentleman squeezed her hand, not in reassuring, but simply to signal her to stand steadfast. The flames of the oarsmen’s eyes intensified. “As ye wish, then. Stand there all ye like, but I’ll not take you across the river on MY boat.” “Silence your questions. The will is not yours. She is here because she has been called” by someone with more power to will her presence than you or I.” There was a standoff for a moment, between the well-dressed and solid form of the Italian Gentleman, and the spectral decrepit boatswain. The wailing on the boat covered the silence between them, and one soul tried to climb off. WHACK! A sharp rap from the flat end of the boatswain’s oar sent the would-be escapee tumbling back into the boat, but the swain’s eyes were still boring holes into Lia. He bared his teeth at her, and the growling voice rumbled forth, seeming to bring with it the heated breath of stinking sulfurous brimstone. “Board my boat, then. But best ye be careful that I don’t decide to capsize you in the middle of this river.” His growled threat was something she didn’t dare mock. .:: ::: ::. “..upon the verge I found me of the abysmal valley dolorous, that gathers thunder of infinite ululations. Obscure, profound it was, and nebulous, so that by fixing on its depths my sight nothing whatever I discerned therein.” It seemed at once to have been an eternity and only a matter of seconds between the time that they had boarded the boat and come to the point where they now stood. Her eyes suddenly felt like they had been closed, because where she looked was unfamiliar to her, and she did not recall walking there by her own feet, and yet the river and boat were out of sight. The Italian Gentleman spoke to her quietly, with a slight tremor in his voice. “We’ve come to the blind world” come behind me now, the walk is narrow.” She looked at him almost disbelievingly. “You want me to come along with you, but you sound like you’re about to die of fear yourself?” “Hmph.” There was that unconcerned, slightly annoyed noise again. “A moment ago, you didn’t believe in hell, and now you think me afraid of it. You amuse me.” He paused, the cool wind puffing forth from below, and causing the tiny strands of feather on his hat to wave like anemones. “What use would fear be” It is only pity for the people in my own station. Now come, we cannot hesitate before every doorway.” Properly chastised, she hung her head, looking down at her hands like a child who had been naughty. She followed the Italian Gentleman down a steep incline and into a new room, or, more aptly, plain, as the floor of it stretched farther than even her sharp eyes could see. Presently, her ears were overtaken by thousands of whisperings? no, not whisperings. For as she looked around at the bodies writhing on the ground, she heard no discernible words in any language, but only prolonged sighs. Sighs of desperate sorrow? but anything was better than those gut-wrenching screams. “You are so absorbed that you don’t even ask who they are” I will tell you.” The Italian Gentleman had proceeded on several steps beyond Lia, but he paused to look back at her with a glimmer of gold from underneath his hat brim. “There is no sin in their damnation, for they had no choice. My place is here. I had not the fortitude nor faith to reach the firmament by my own deeds, but neither had I a choice in whether or not I was damned.” He was silent with a note of bitterness. She looked at him out of eyes that seemed to hold a note of compassion, a need to reach out and experience the Gentleman’s pain. “Doesn’t anyone ever make it out?” “No,” he said, looking sharply up at her, his voice holding a jagged note, “Here is eternity.” She swallowed, and reached out for his forearm. “I’m sorry.” He pulled his arm out of her reach, taking a further step away from her. “Do not apologize to me.” She opened her mouth, but was immediately silenced again by a voice. “You’ve come back!” Shock reeled through her, electric and unforgiving as she looked to the source of the voice that addressed her. Jalena Vlaszy. The grandmother who had practically taught her everything that she needed to know about life’about being a wife, a mother, about being part of the family, and taking the loyalty oath. The old woman smiled at her granddaughter, and Lia started forward to embrace her. And there was Vaclav just behind her! Master too rose from the throng and stepped towards Lia and the Italian Gentleman, still wearing the noble’s clothing of his lifetime, stained with blood from the night he was slain by the Ventrue. His sad, sharp eyes dug into his pupil with a loving sort of glow. The time apart had clearly not made him forget her. Too, there was Anezka, her younger sister, looking up at Lia with pretty blue eyes that were not quite the family trademark, but were clear as the Zdice sky nonetheless. Anezka had never been the vibrant sister of the family, but she had kept the records, and learned the lore with a zeal that impressed everyone. All the Vlaszy had their own talents, they had said’where Lia’s were mostly clan magical in nature, Anezka’s one love had been the lore of their own long familial line. And perhaps she too had died protecting that name’Lia had never known. The last, standing in the background, her twin brother, Laszlo. The same twin brother that her grandmother had told her was still alive. Her face twisted in anguish’how could he be here? He was supposed to still be on the earth somewhere, it had been her task to find him before something happened to him’the blood was too precious to let too much of it be extinguished. Tears clouded her eyes again. “There are no questions we can answer for you,” Vaclav said to her, his solemn, sad eyes once again seeming so deep and lifelike, “It is best that we go on with our journey.” And so the six of them walked forward? four ghostly figures, shimmering in the light of the fire that gleamed up ahead, Lia, and her guide, who kept her hand pressed close to her side that she not get overexuberant and run back to the mass of bodies to find more acquaintances. “Would this have been my place too, had I died with them?” her trembling voice spoke up. “You think about that often, don’t you?” asked the Italian Gentleman, his eyes turning to her. She nodded. He shrugged slightly. “Who am I to say” It is possible that you would have been here? but you are a special case, then, are you not?” A chill swept through her’of course she was. She was different from them because she had halfway broken the curse. Apparently not enough to shake her damnation, but did it not make her different enough that she had seen the sun recently? The sun that the rest of the Vlaszy and Tzimisce had not seen for untold eons? Her silence continued until the Italian Gentleman led the entourage through the seven gates and into the sweeping grassy meadow of a castle. Here, the voices were soft, and they all spoke in a muted Old Czech that reminded her of her childhood days on the family estate. A longing for those old days twined painfully around her heart, but they were so long gone that it was folly to weep for their loss. For a long time, everyone had been silent now. For a long time, it was simply Lia’s eyes that roamed freely over the grassy plains of what had become the old Vlaszy manor house in Zdice, remembering every pleasant memory that she could call up’more of them now than had ever been there before, as though she were reliving every happy moment. But yet the smile on her face was tremulous, almost as though one were looking at her face through a mirror underwater. The gentle caress of the Italian Gentleman’s hand pressed into her back. “We must not be all day for here. I must take you on.” At his words, a terrible sense of dread filled the entirety of her body. As though she were being dragged bodily downwards, she took his hand again, and followed along, her mute lips unable to speak a word of goodbye to her grandmother, her siblings, her Master, who all faded away like insignificant shades’and unable to utter a single note of protest. .:: ::: ::. “Another way my sapient Guide conducts me forth from the quiet to the air that trembles; and to a place I come where nothing shines?” .:: ::: ::.
