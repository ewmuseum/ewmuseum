Coming Out (Part Six - Conclusion)

17 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02.17.06 A seventeen-year-old Ken Partridge, Jr. with bright blonde hair finished straightning a framed poster of Marilyn Monroe over the large retro green sofa in the basement apartment of his grandparents' home... which was his new home, as well. Looking out through the sliding glass doors that entered into his living room and gazing over his grandmother's large botanical-type garden at the setting sun, the aroma of hazelnut stemming from one of the many candles he had lit flooding his olfactories, a tear rolled down his face. This time last week, he's been with his mother and step-father in their home... happy. He missed that already. He missed talking with his mother and playing with his dog. The glare of the sunset was beginning to hurt his eyes. He walked from the doors to his bedroom, which was much different from the cold cement room that he'd moved into the day before. The porpoise color of blue that he'd painted it the previous day had dried and was quite calming as it wrapped around the black furniture in the room and the queen size bed that Ken only finished reassembling an hour before. The white sheets and comforter gave a nice contrast to the rest of the room that almost made it seem even larger than it already was. He watched the candlelight dance on the fresh color of the walls and was interrupted only when his phone rang. He broke from his trance-like state and scurried to the living room to answer it, picking it up just as it rang for the third time. 'Hello,' he spoke into the receiver as he sat on his sofa. 'How are you,' asked the calming female voice on the other end of the receiver. Ken immediatly smiled. His girlfriend, Megan, always had a way of making him feel better. 'How did you get this number so fast? It's only been connected for an hour,' he laughed. 'Your mother, Silly,' she retorted. 'I just got off the phone with her. She seems so upset.' 'She is. I wish there was something I could do, but it's all in William's hands, now.' The anger that Ken had formed in the direction of his step-father was overshadowed only by the love that he garnished for his mother. The man that he had looked at and referred to as 'dad' had turned his back on him and, at that time, Ken thought that he'd never forgive him. 'So,' she said, breaking the awkward silence that had lingered following Ken's last comment, 'you're in your new place?' 'Yeah,' he said, 'sitting here... all by myself.' 'You want some company?' she asked. 'Absolutely,' he smiled. 'Do you still remember how to get here?' 'I love your grandparents, Ken, how could I forget?' ---- 'Mr. Partridge,' chimed the voice over Ken's desk speaker. 'There's a Ms. Antonique Smith here to see you.' 'Let her in,' replied Ken as he pulled away from the pages Kelli Alberdeane had sent him in reference to his biography. The door opened as Ken stood from his desk to greet his former cast mate. She was beautiful. He mocha skin was defined by her gorgeous dark brown hair. She hugged him and kissed him on the cheek. 'I'm so glad you decided to meet,' she began, gushing in her sexy latin accent. 'When you got back to me and asked, I thought I was going to go into hysterics. I can't believe it, I thought I was being so idiotic loving you, but to find out that you feel th-' 'I didn't call you to my office to begin a relationship, Antonique,' Ken interrupted. She pulled away, sitting in one of the black leather chairs that faced his desk. 'I - I don't understand,' she said in disbeilief. 'What do you mean?' 'An,' he began, 'I'm engaged to Gunner. I love her... with all of my heart and, as beautiful as you are and as much as we shared during RENT... as much as I did long for you... Gunner will always have my heart. What we've been through... what we have... it's indescribable and nobody else can compare to that.' 'But,' she was now crying, 'you even admitted it. We shared something and you may not know that it's love, but I do. I know what it is.' Antonique was now standing again and pacing back and forth. 'I'm so sorry,' he continued, 'but I can't. I won't. Antonique, we share something very special, but I know that most of it was us getting wrapped up in our roles. Roger and Mimi, they have this amazing love. You can't portray them and not feel the same.' 'What I feel for you is NOT because of some Broadway play,' she screamed, slamming her hands down on his desk. 'It's real... and it's love.' 'Then,' said Ken calmly, 'I'm so sorry. I truly hoped that we could salvage some type of friendship out of this, but-' 'We don't have to tell her,' whispered Antonique as she made her way towards a still standing KPJ. 'She never has to know. I won't say a word. We can meet... indiscreetly. I have a loft in the city. We -' 'No,' he said, holding her arms to keep her from coming any closer to him. 'Please, Antonique, leave.' He released her arms as she turned to head for the door. As he hand grasped the knob, she turned to leave Ken with one final thought. 'We do the two of you get to be so happy?' She exited and slammed the door behind her. Ken shook out the cobwebs from his head and sat back at his desk. His buzzer rang again, this time the voice on the receiver was Matthew Moore, the man he'd appointed as manager of The Elements. 'Ken, I'm on my way up.' 'Why,' Ken asked with no feeling in his voice, but curiosity in his mind. 'I've booked a band that you may be interested in knowing more about. I want you to talk to it's front man... an... Eryc Morgan.' Ken and Megan laid on the sofa, holding one another adn allowing Ken to feel safe and happy for the first time in days. 'What was it that you has to tell me yesterday?' he asked. Megan tilted her head up to look at him with a sparkle in her eye. 'I love you.'
