Who I Am; Who I'm Not

12 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/12/04 'How much can you know about yourself if you've never been in a fight? I don't want to die without any scars.' -Chuck Palahniuk Holding my face in the palm of my hands, I search for an answer. A solution. I've been so confused as of late, it would be easier for things to come together for once. I don't even know who I am anymore. This world of greed has plagued my mind with confusion. I need to figure out who I am, for the first time in my life? Leinad Soma is a monster. And he reminded me of that in the pit. He has a desperate thirst for blood and he craves domination. I suppose I was trying to mold Leinad into something that he is not. I tried to make him laugh, I tried to make him smile. I tried to make him a statue for what I wanted, what I needed. What happened in the pit made me realize that Leinad Soma is still the same monster that helped murder me last October. Even thinking about it sends a chill down my spine. He brought me back, shared his home with me, and brought me into a world of comfort. I don't know, maybe the bastard of a man felt sorry for what he has done. But no matter what, he is still the same Leinad Soma. He knows who, or what he is and deep inside, he knows what I am also. I lift my tired head from my hands and look into the mirror in the room. My mirror. In my room. I look at the reflection that stands in front of me. Waiting, hoping, and wishing that it would just slap me in the face and tell me what I need to know. Looking at this elderly man with his Sage Sabbot shirt on’is this who I am now? Hell, I don't even wear the same clothing anymore, and even if I did, it wouldn't be for the same reason. I wear this shirt for self-promotion. I wear it to feel good about myself. I've been back in the Global Wrestling again for over three months and I've only lost three matches, two of them came by the former GW International Champion in Ken Partridge, Jr. And the other came from the reigning GW Heavyweight Champion, Josh. I suppose I want to pat myself on the back for my accomplishments, and maybe wearing this shirt is my way of doing just that. A reality check, I have never thought about how much I've changed. I'm in a game of pickle, I can't decide who I am. I start to remember who I was. The old Sage Sabbot. The Harvester of Tears. I made a name for myself. I was the GW Heavyweight Champion of the World. My name sent chills down spines across the globe. Back then, before my death, I was the most feared man in the entire business. And look at me now, an old wash-up who fears a mere mortal man. A man who complains about not being able to run circles around the competition anymore. A man who considers’retirement. Would the Harvester of Tears consider retirement? I want to shake my head side to side, but I'm too busy waiting for a response from my reflection in the mirror? What have I become? Many said that I've came a long way in Global Wrestling from my debut. And they are correct. I have accomplished more in my career then many greats in the GW. Greats whose names are mentioned on television. Names whose faces appear on boxes of cereal. Don't act like you don't know what I'm talking about, because you do. How many title belts has Ice won? How many big named superstars has Akodo Rokku defeated? Need I say more? Back then, I was beating guys like Jay Robson. Guys like Quartz, Salaok, Roz Blayze’several times. What has changed about me? Whatever it is, I don't like it. Things are about to change. When I seen Leinad's face after he assaulted Josh, I had to smile inside. I looked into his eyes and I seen shades of Salaok and Johnathan Davis. When he beat the living hell out of Josh, I pictured Quartz taking out his aggressions on Josh instead of Soma. Secretly, I missed those things. I miss The Nevermore and The Lair. I miss every Harvester. I miss’my family. If they've only seen what I've become, they'd spit at my feet. The Soul Survivor The Harvester Of Tears “are only nicknames anymore. I don't live by them. They are only a god damned shirt. Leinad gave me some things to think about, who the hell am I” What have I accomplished in these three months of my return? How many title belts have I wrapped around my waist? “I let out a deep sigh. I've failed my past. I've failed Leinad. I've failed The Harvesters. I've failed’myself. Like I've done Leinad, I've attempted to mold myself into something that I'm not. I've created a personality of something I'm not, like an invisible friend that has taken over. I have to realize who I am, and accept it. But it is harder then you think. Do I want to go back to what I was” Perhaps that is not the question I should be asking? Cherish doesn't even know the real me. She fell in love with a fake. I have to sit down and accept the fact that I am who I am. And without telling you, you know what that is. That is why it is so hard. I enjoy being what I am now, faking my entire life? I run my hands through my hair, my heart sinks for the thought of becoming the old Sage Sabbot. I take off my Sage Sabbot shirt and throw it to the wooden floor. My wooden floor. I run my hands over my body. I run my fingers across a bulging scar on my chest, from an accident that occurred when I was a child from burn. I search for more? Running over my shoulder, I feel a dented scar on my shoulder blade. I lower my head, to remember where it came from. Dead Zone, Brick and I in a hell in a cell match. He gave me a beating that I'll never forget. And this scar proves that I'll never forget it. I run my hands over my face, the scar just to the left of my nose, the place where the bullet that was placed is marked here. And will always be remembered here. I look the mirror, the scar that runs vertically through my left eye, I don't quite remember what that is from. I stare into the mirror, trying to remember. Building up rage and fury, I can't remember. The power inside my head shatters the mirror in front of me into pieces on the floor in front of me. My mirror. I shake my head side to side’I can't change what I am. I pick up a shard of glass and run my finger, slowly over the top. I lift my finger up and let the blood drip from my skin to the ground, onto the shattered glass. I suppose these scars are a way to mark the important times of my life. They give me a starting point, from the scar on my eye that I don't remember, to the finishing point, the bullet wound on my face. These scars, these battle wounds, are a time scale in my life. Every big thing that happens, every big change was marked with one. Realizing what I've become for the first time in my life, I mark the occasion with the shard of glass inside my hand on my chest. I run the blade from my chest to my stomach. The gaping wound opens, running cups of blood down my stomach. Drowning this creature I've made myself, and reviving what I am. I wipe a handful of blood from my stomach and put my hand over my heart. This is who I am, accept me if you want, but that won't even change a thing?
