The past is a lesson best forgotten

12 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The past is a lesson best forgotten Quartz Global Wrestling Promo #195 Date: 5/12/03 Location: In the mind of the beast Dust. Everywhere, dust. Nowhere, his son. The mask resting in the rubble but his body no where to be found. ---- Shale is dead father. You killed him.' The words from Silent Wind shocked Quartz like none before. The eyes of the two men lock while on top of the building and that would be the last time it would ever happen. The last time that Quartz would be able to look his own son in the eye. His own flesh and blood. The son that Quartz never wanted and now would have no more. As Silent Wind presses the button on the strange device the world collapses onto itself and the rooftop under their feet falls apart with them still on it. As the explosion rocks the city area and pieces of cement fly through the air, their bodies are sent plummeting down. They share a look on the way down and then it's over. Darkness. Dust. Dust everywhere. ---- With blood pouring from his many wounds, he pulls himself from the rubble as the sounds of sirens fill the air and the screams of the innocent by-standards continue to echo in the once still night. Pain. The one thing that Quartz had once learn to void himself of and now it run through his body like the Nile. Continually following and covering everything in it's path. His eyes adjust in the darkness and squint to see through the dust. Where was his son? Where was Jude? Stepping over the lumps of concrete which had broken on impact from the fall, he begins to look. Look for some sign of his son still alive. He couldn't have let his son win like this. This isn't how it was meant to end, Quartz knew that much. His son was meant to return home to The Lair, not die. It was in the scriptures that one of the next generation would be groomed into the next leader of the almighty group and Quartz was sure that his son would be that man. The dust continues to fall from the sky like snow and blanket everything in light gray. The flood lights were quickly being assembled in order to light up the area to search for survivors. An effort which would seem futile as the explosions had come from the inside of the building and went off, one at a time on each level going upwards. Anyone inside of the building would have died for sure. The only place where there was no explosion as such, was on the roof. The only place that there could have been a chance of surviving. Quartz was proof of this. But would his son be? Step after step through the rumble and finally his eyes lock onto something in the distance. Red Black. Rushing through the now crowd of people searching for bodies, he makes a straight path for the item. A man steps in his path and tries to stop the giant Quartz. Holding his hands on Quartz's chest, the man pushes him backwards. 'You can't go over there. It's not stable.' Reaching down and grabbing the man by the neck, Quartz throws him out of the way and continues on his path. The mask. The mask of his son. He reaches down and picks it up, hoping to find his son's body behind the mask but there is nothing but rumble. The mask must have fallen loose in the fall and been separated from his son's body. Holding the mask in his hands, he looks at it in it's torn and blood covered state before turning his head up to the night sky and letting out a deafening howl. The sound is more like that of a wolf which had lost it's cub, but this sound was coming from a man. A man filled with the pain he never should have known. A pain he was told to end before it had a chance to effect him. Quartz sits up in bed, his eyes wide open. A dream. It was back again. Last night the dream had left him alone. Let his body recover from the torment and pain that had filled his mind over the last two weeks. This wasn't right for Quartz. This shouldn't be happening is all he could think to himself. He needed answers but he was now the elder in The Lair. No longer did he have the elders of The Lair to turn to for guidance as they had all been assassinate during the war against Mephiston's armies. He needed his questions answered and yet he had no one to answer them. He couldn't take these questions to Salaok as he too was still young in his ways and in his life. But there was still one man who would be able to shed light on the questions and thoughts which flooded the mind of the man who had been void of all feeling up until now. He would go to see him in the morning as there would be nothing worse for Quartz than to go and see a man who normally wouldn't have anything to do with Quartz, than to wake him during the middle of the night. A man which Quartz had known for what seems a lifetime and a man who Quartz knew could match him in power and strength if it ever came to a fight. But that was something that Quartz wasn't going to be looking for. No, now Quartz was only looking for answers and guidance through the world which was now full of void. But for now there was need for one thing and one thing only. Sleep. Resting his head back down on the pillow which was drenched with sweat, he closes his eyes and hopes that the world of dreams would leave him along just for an hour or two. Darkness. The stone rooms was something that Quartz had always liked about the castle of The Lair. He stood there looking at the statues of the warriors of The Lair. Each man who had fought the battles of the Lair and lead the armies were here. The from the first ever generation of warriors to the newest member of The Lair, Salaok. A project which their master had been pleased with Johnathon and Quartz for completing. Why it was that Deth had wanted Salaok in the Lair so badly was beyond Quartz, but it was a task which he had wanted and wanted badly. So Quartz had taken great pleasure in doing everything that needed to be done to create the world needed to have Salaok join The Lair. Whether it had been introducing Salaok to Lisha, or killing Johnathon's and Salaok's father, it was something that he knew would have great purpose. The door behind Quartz began to open and the heavy dragging filled the room as the large wooden door scraped over a few of the bluestone blocks on the floor. As Quartz turns around and faces the door, there is no one there, but receives a tap on the shoulder from where he was just looking. Again he turns around and there between him and the statue of Salaok was Duriel. The powerful sorcerer of The Lair now stood only an inch from Quartz's face with a look of ice running through his eyes. A small grin formed in the corner of Duriel's mouth as he extends his hand which is received by Quartz as they shake. When ever there had been an issue that needed to be spoken about, Quartz had always preferred to go to Duriel, as his wisdom was matched by no other, not even the leader of The Lair himself. 'So, this is about the girl isn't it?' Duriel never needed to be told about the problem, he always seemed to know. How it was that he knew, Quartz could never understand, but it sure made the beginning of the topic a lot easier. Quartz nodded his reply before talking a few steps away from Duriel, knowing that Duriel wouldn't be happy with the news he was about to hear. 'She's pregnant.' In hearing this, Duriel's sigh was loud and full of disappointment. The conversation continued on late into the night as Duriel tried to talk to Quartz about what would have to happen if the child was to be brought into this world. About what would happen if the child was to not follow the line of life that his father had. All the questions that Quartz had for the elder, were answered, but never with the answers that Quartz wanted to hear. It had been upon hearing the news of the pregnancy that Quartz had suddenly been forced to look back on his life and think about things. Although he had never been ashamed of what he had done, he would always ben haunted by it. Haunted by the eyes of those who had been killed in his own hands. Haunted by the screams of pain and the pleas of mercy which came with every hit that had been ordered. In his mind, he had never wanted his son to have to live in that world and yet by the laws of The Lair, there was no other choice. No choice other than to let the child grow up and bring him home to The Lair. It was the same law that sure Johnathon and Salaok make their way into the Lair through their lives and now Quartz would have to see his own son be pulled into a world which haunted him. ---- His hands rested on the glass of the display cabinet as he looked down at the piece which the shop owner was putting into the brown paper bag. The shop owner looks up at Quartz through his one eye and his mouth moves back Quartz doesn't hear. He is too far off in his own world of thought. Thoughts of what would happen later in the night. 'Hey buddy, I said how many bullets you wanting?' Another fucking question which he hadn't put any thought to at all. But he knew what he was doing and the answer seemed to roll of his tongue with the cold emotion which had already filled him. 'One.' A single word, full of intent and power which made the shop own look at the giant before him with questions running through his mind. By in this line of business, there was no use in asking questions. Guns were bought for a reason and everyone knew that. The reason behind the purchase was never needing to be discussed. Quartz spoke up again as the man took a single bullet out of the package. 'And a silencer.' The thoughts in the shop owners mind were now confirmed and the atmosphere in the shop completely died as conversation stopped dead. It was strange to know that the person you are serving is about to kill someone. Knowing that it is you who is putting the weapon which will be used to draw blood and death into the killers hands. As the money and the parcel changes hands Quartz leans across the counter a small amount and makes sure that he has the man's eye contact. 'I was never here.' 'I know.' The shop owners response was as cold as Quartz's statement. Clearly in this world of hatred and pain, this had happened many times before for this man. And if not, he was damn good at making it seem that way. ---- There she lay, peaceful and beautiful. Her innocence and purity were two things which drew Quartz to her for reasons he never fully understood. He loved her and still did to this day. But this was the only way out of the bad situation that he could see. He couldn't let his child grow up to become what he had become and the only way to stop that was to make sure the child was never born. The rooms downstairs had all been trashed quietly while she had been sleeping to make it look like a break in and now there was just one last thing to do. He pulled the gun out of his jacket pocket and screwed in the silencer. It's not that he wanted to do this, but more of the mere fact that he had to. He had done this as a part of his life for as long as he could remember, so why would killing an unborn baby prove to be so hard? He raised the gun and aimed it at her, knowing that the shock alone would be enough to kill the child inside of her and therefore, the need to aim directly at the baby wouldn't have been needed. If he had, it would have looked somewhat suspect. With a final sigh, he did the one thing which had to be done in order to save the child inside of his beloved. He had to kill it to save it. BANG The bullet pierced her skin and her eyes opened wide to see a large figure run out of the room. Her screams of pain fill the night air as he walks down the hall away from her room and too the phone. In order to save her, emergency needed to know about this and so the call was placed. ---- He sits up in bed again with sweat running down his face. His eyes wide open, no longer wanting to let them close as the memories from the past were simply too strong now. They needed to be silenced again. He couldn't wait till the morning now. He would have to see the Harbringer now.
