Signposts Chapter 7: Aftermath

19 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 19/10/04 Winters jumped from the side of the building, onto the metal lid of an alley way dumpster, bullets still screaming past him even on the way down. His pursuers would not take the same route as he - the jump would be too much for them to take - but nevertheless, they would still be after him. He figured that the men on the roof had already signalled some of his friends, who were undoubtedly waiting outside the building - or at least on a lower floor - to come and get him. In essence, Winters didn’t have much time to play around with. He followed the alleyway, which ultimately led him around the back of the next building over, the end of which was blocked with a rather large brick wall. Upon seeing the wall, Winters immediately began to run towards it, leaping upward at the end of the run-up, easily managing to reach the top of the wall. He then used his upper body strength to haul himself up and over it, jumping down to the sidewalk below. He then headed towards away from the building as fast as he could, without making himself stand out like a sore thumb. The car that the brunette had provided him with, was on the other side of the building. Essentially, would have to go past the afore mentioned building just to get at the car. Something that he just couldn’t afford to do. He had to ditch it, stick to the alleyways on foot. That was his best option at this point. He had deducted the following thus far: presumably, these people were from his past, hunting him for some reason or another. Perhaps that is who caused the memory loss, but that explanation still did not make sense. Did they know what he looked like? Or did they recognise the brunette and the guards and put two and two together? Why were they after him? Did he do something in his previous life to piss them off? Now he would probably never find out. The only person that he knew for sure who knew about his past was probably dead by now, lined with bullets meant for him. Perhaps now, the only people who knew about his past were the men after him, but was it worth the risk of getting killed just to know about the past he may or may not have been trying to forget all along? He dismissed the idea as soon as it entered into his head. There would be no logic to that. For the second time in as many days he didn’t know what to do. The odds didn’t add up to provide him with any credible solution to any of his problems. He figured that was the main problem with odds, sometimes they just don’t go your way. However, sometimes they do. Winters noticed three black cars scream past him. They looked different from the other cars he’d seen thus far. They looked as though they didn’t belong here. It was the men that were after him and they’d missed him. Sometimes, luck was a necessary factor in the odds, no matter how much Winters disliked the idea.
