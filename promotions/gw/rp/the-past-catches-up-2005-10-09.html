The Past Catches Up

9 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/9/05 The last few rays of sunlight cast an orange glow through a small kitchen window, and directly onto Virgil's face, which didn't sit too well with him in the least, particularly because he was attempting to read a dusty book on the ancient dialects of northern Lithuanian. He had not yet gotten used to the brightness of the sun, nor the ability to withstand it without bursting into flames, and thus it still blinded him almost completely. He slid his chair over to the left, out of the direct sunbeam, muttering an archaic curse. Through the corner of his eye, beside some scattered grammatical information, a shadow seemed to move out of pattern with the bushes around it. His eyes darted to the side, waiting for another slip, provided he hadn't been seeing things anyway. Nothing. Then, the smell. Having been around Dawn, Cyrus, and Alexis for long enough to remember their particular scents, the absence of them had begun to set off more alarms than the presence, but this one was neither of the three, nor was it Lia. He forced a sigh. Of course she wouldn't be back. That would simply make life too simple. Although, he realized by now that complaining internally would do nothing to speed her 'vision quest', as he'd taken to referring to it. In fact, it would probably make the wait seem even longer. Suddenly, he could hear Damek barking out in the yard, followed by Jirina, and the scent gradually faded. Whoever had been out there was now quite gone, and indeed, most mortals who laid eyes on Lia's 'modified' pets were not too intent upon staying around for very long. However, now his curiosity was quite piqued, and he pushed his chair back to wander out the back door, into the yard. He sniffed the air cautiously, as darkness continued to slowly overtake the landscape. Other than the very faint scent of exhaust, the only humans he could smell were the ones that he knew. The over-exuberant panting of bounding dogs reached his ears, and Damek and Jirina stopped to stare at him gleefully, a rather bizarre occasion, considering how abnormal the dogs themselves looked. Virgil wondered if beasts such as this were really supposed to be so happy- looking at any point. Something hung out of Damek's mouth, which Virgil pulled on. Damek pulled back and wagged his tail, but let go after a moment, and Virgil stared at what looked like half of a symbol of some sort, embroidered into a black fabric that he was unfamiliar with. Obviously, the dogs had been slightly faster than the intruder. He turned it over, and the flat-backed opposite side proved to him that it must have been a piece of clothing at some point, but there was nothing revealing about the symbol itself, no writing, no particularly descriptive visual cues. He stuffed it in his pocket, and scratched both of the dogs on the top of their heads, just above the spikes that had been added at some point by their mistress. 'Hm. Strange.' He turned and slipped back inside the house, shutting the door quietly behind him. The dogs barked a couple of times, and he looked back to see them wagging their tails, apparently pleased to interrupt his journey indoors, which they were quite jealous of. He smirked and continued through the kitchen, pulling the symbol out of his pocket again, looking over it. Nothing more than a silver circle, containing an eagle with talons outstretched. Simple, whatever significance it was supposed to have. Virgil had a tendency to be rather absent-minded when he was contemplating something, and in one of his own homes, it wouldn't have particularly mattered, as he would have known where everything was. Crash! But this was Dawn's house, and Dawn's unfortunately placed hassock that found its way innocently under Virgil's feet, sending him crashing to the ground. He glared up at the ceiling for a few seconds, before picking himself up, and contemplating attempting to set the hassock aflame with his irritation. However, before he had the chance, he heard a voice from the top of the stairs. 'Virgil? Are you alright?' She started stepping quickly down the stairs, but stopped dead in her tracks when she saw the torn emblem that he was holding. Her eyes widened, and she pointed a finger at it, looking almost... scared? Virgil was slightly puzzled, and looked behind him. 'Where... where did you get that?' Virgil looked back to her, then down to the damaged eagle. 'This? One of the dogs brought it in. I assume some unfortunate fool was skulking about the premises before it ran. It left in a vehicle of some kind, I could smell the exhaust.' She sat down on the stairs, running her hands through the blonde roots that were beginning to show heavily through her dark red hair, a panicked look starting to show on her face. 'That's the Den's insignia.' Virgil raised an eyebrow. 'Hm. I wonder which of us they're looking for.' The expression on her face didn't change. 'They're looking for me. I don't even know how they found out where I was staying, but they probably haven't changed their minds about wanting me dead.' Virgil felt an odd twinge somewhere deep in the recess of his soul, if it could still be called that. Something about the Den attempting to cut Alexis' short life even shorter set off a protective nature that was different than what he felt when Lia was in danger, or even when he himself was threatened. The two of them, at least, could take care of themselves adequately, even without each others' aid, but Alexis was not quite so durable. 'I don't even have a gun... I don't even know how to use the stupid things very well.' Virgil pulled at his thin beard, carefully planning out a strategy. Were they to return, there would probably be more than the one that the dogs had caught... 'What manner of tactics do the terrifying vampire hunters use on their own?' Alexis shook her head, crossing her arms tightly and leaning forward on her knees. 'They'll probably send three or four operatives, after arming them to the teeth. Have you ever heard of that Rainbow Six game?' Virgil looked at her blankly. 'I guess I should have known that answer. They'll have already memorized every door and window in the mansion, and for all I know, they've been watching me while I sleep or something.' Virgil sniffed the air subtly. There was exhaust again. 'Will they stay together?' Alexis shook her head, somewhat sullenly. Being a condemned woman wasn't sounding too good to her, as one might imagine. But Virgil wasn't planning on letting her be killed, though it was still a mystery to him why it suddenly mattered so much that he keep this girl who was probably hundreds of years younger than him alive. 'No, they'll probably come in from different points. They're about as professional as anyone the government pays to train.' The exhaust smell became ever stronger, and Virgil could hear the dogs barking in the back. They were in danger as well, despite whatever enhancements they'd been given. He turned his gaze back to Alexis, unbuttoning the silk shirt he'd been wearing most of the day, considering it a liability. 'Then go find someplace to hide, now. I'll do what I can about them.' She stood up, but some protest lingered in her eyes, a stubborn spark. 'But I could probably...' Virgil's eyes narrowed, and his voice raised. 'I did not give you the option of doing that. Go.' She backed up the stairs a couple of steps, unnerved by the threatening words pressed through gritted teeth, but it had worked. Whatever resolve she'd had to fight was sapped now. 'I'll go to the attic...' Alexis turned, not wanting to deal with the repercussions of wasting any more time, and ran up the stairs. Virgil turned away from her to face the door, as a small black car pulled up outside. The door would be too obvious now, so he waited. Only three men climbed out, which made Virgil feel slightly more empowered about the situation, but it disconcerted him that he couldn't see any kind of armament immediately apparent. As Alexis had said, they split up, and headed away from each other. One of them happened to be stupid or bold enough to give the front door a try. Virgil heard the scratching of a lockpick, and smirked, as the deadbolt latch slipped open, and the doorknob slowly turned, in an attempt to suppress any noise. The door opened, and Virgil found himself face to face with a shorter man, clad in black, whose face was exposed. He took in a sharp gasp of terror at the surprise, but whipped up a pistol, and fired a pair of silenced rounds into Virgil's midsection. He looked down, and then back up, but didn't have the time to fire another round, before Virgil sprang forward, slamming into him with one of the shoulderblocks he'd so conveniently learned from wrestling, and taking both of them to the ground. The gun flew out of his hand, and skittered along the ground. Alexis' would-be assailant didn't have time to let out another noise, as Virgil's strong hands wrapped around his neck, and squeezed. Snap! He left him to die peacefully, as he dashed off to the east side of the mansion with unnatural speed, following the scent of blood. As he whipped around the hedge, he heard the crashing of glass, and spied another climbing in through the window. His mind had left him by this point, and he was guided by pure instinct as he locked his hands around the second faceless assailant's ankles, and pulled strongly backward, dragging him over the shards of glass sticking dangerously out of the window frame. The scream barely had time to reach Virgil's ears before a swift swipe of his claws tore his victim's throat out. Virgil looked around the corpse briefly, and pulled the same type of pistol the last one had used out of its holster, heading back around the mansion, seething with adrenaline. Alexis shifted nervously behind a stack of boxes in Dawn's attic, as she pulled her legs up underneath her on a wooden board placed across the rafters. She'd heard glass break twice now, and no other kind of noise from the bottom of the house- which, of course, had left her completely terrified. After all, there wasn't much reason for Virgil to worry about her getting killed, and he was probably looking out for himself by going after them, even though they wouldn't be at all prepared for dealing with a vampire, especially one as old as Virgil claimed to be. She buried her head between her knees, sighing silently. What was she supposed to do up here? They'd just search every part of the mansion until they found her, and then they'd shoot her. There was nothing she could do about it, except hope that Virgil could stop them beforehand. Her foot slipped suddenly, and smacked against a box labeled 'china', sitting atop a pair of larger boxes. It teetered dangerously, and Alexis dove toward it, trying to catch it, but only tipping it the rest of the way over, causing an alarmingly loud series of crashes as the contents of the box smashed and broke. 'Shit. A flashlight beam cutting through the dark halls of the mansion stopped suddenly, and the last living Den operative on the premises turned his light up to the ceiling of the top floor, realizing that there was even more to this gigantic house than he'd already looked through. Alexis pulled back behind what she could find for cover, now that she'd made so much noise, but it was already too late to hide. Boxes in attics didn't just fall over under normal circumstances. The beam of light ran across the indention of a hidden stairwell, with a cord dangling a foot below it. A small smile ran across the operative's face as he pulled on the cord, and the stairs slid to the floor. He climbed up them slowly, hefting his rifle off of his shoulder, and affixing his flashlight to the barrel. Carefully, he passed the beam over the stacks of boxes and trunks in the attic, and took a step forward, looking down to figure out where the plywood was laid and where it wasn't. He thought he heard a noise over in the corner, and spun to face his rifle in that direction. The floorboards creaked under his steps, and he could swear he heard breathing, before a pair of slugs tore through his shoulder and arm, causing him to drop the rifle. It slammed into a heavy rafter board, and fired off a few rounds, but before he could retrieve it, another bullet hit him in the small of the back, from closer range this time, sending him to the floor with a grunt. Virgil was on top of him in seconds, after throwing the pistol to the side. He reached up to the neck of his prey, and dug his claws into the shoulders, pulling down with all his strength, tearing ten deep trails down the dying man's back. A blood-curdling scream pierced the dark attic, as Virgil reached for the assault rifle, and pointed it at its owner's head, cutting him off with one last burst of muzzle fire. The mansion seemed oddly tranquil all of a sudden, and Virgil remembered that Alexis was hiding up here somewhere. Of course, he was slightly more skilled at tracking than any of her would-be assassins, and he bolted straight toward her, knocking the boxes that were in front of her aside with a heavy swipe of his arm. She screamed, and he picked her up, turning her over to her back. 'Alexis! Are you hurt?' It took her a moment to realize that the eyes staring into hers were Virgil's, and she shook her head... did he actually look worried? She hadn't thought that he was at all capable of feeling, well, anything. A quiet beeping disturbed the silence occupied by only Alexis' breathing, and Virgil's head whipped over to the very dead man nearby. Alexis blinked, and scrambled to her knees under her own power. 'That's his watch... but why...' Tires squealed loudly from the driveway outside the window, and Virgil and Alexis watched as a set of lights sped away from the mansion. Virgil muttered something she couldn't quite understand, but if the sentiment behind it was anything like what it sounded, she certainly felt the same.
