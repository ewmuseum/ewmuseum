Doing the right thing

26 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/26/05 Brick had gone through hell that night. He had gone through one of the most harrowing matches of his career. His face was covered with blood. His muscles and his joints ached. He had both taken and given the most severe beating he could. But what hurt the most was Brick's pride. Up until that night, Brick had never lost at Wrestlecade. From the very first Wrestlecade, he had never been pinned, made to submit or been counted out. The first three Wrestlecades, he had won. The Wrestlecade the year before, his match with Deacon Holmes and Train ended in a no contest. But this evening was his first Wrestlecade defeat. Further salt on the wound was the man who pinned him. The one man in all of Global Wrestling that he hated with a passion and a fury. The man who for more than half a decade had caused Brick more pain than any other individual. Salaok. The last time they'd faced one another at Wrestlecade, it was in 2003. Brick had managed to come out on top, despite a delay at the car rental, and the interference of both Quartz and Mark Kullypso. Despite the fact that he hated the man, deep in his soul he half knew he owed him. He’d never had anyone push him to that level of hate. No one ever gave him the inspiration to nurture that level of malice. No one before Salaok had ever pushed him to be... Invincible. Unfortunately, judging by tonight, Brick had returned the same favor for him tonight. When he’d been in that ring tonight he hadn’t been able to make sure that the thing Salaok prized most was taken from him, but that was ok. There hatred was about a lot of things, but a belt wasn’t one of them. The most Brick could take comfort in was the fact that he beat the hell out of Salaok like never before. Theirs had been the definition of a blood feud. From inside the arena to out there was likely no measuring of just how much of each other’s blood they’d spill in the pursuit of ending one another’s career. And despite all that, Brick had never had the pleasure of digging his hands so far into Salaok’s flesh as he had tonight. Did he win? No. Would Salaok remember who he’d crossed when he lay in bed later on and for weeks to come, aching? You God damn right. 'Hey, Brick! Brick!' 'What?' 'Salaok just lost the title! Train is the new World champion!' One would expect the news of Salaok losing a title, especially the World title, to put a smile on Brick's face. Strangely enough, this was not the case. There was just a stony silence. 'You don't look happy.' 'Yeah, I mean, Train beat Salaok. I figured you'd be dancing for joy.' Brick didn't say a word. He merely rose to his feet and, without looking at either the EMT's or the stagehand, walked away. ***** ***** ***** The glittering smell of mint wafts through the room as the dark liquid poured down from the flowery kettle, a languid cloud of steam released when the steady, opaque stream, hits the bottom of the porcelain cup. Train had only been here one time before and the time he spent was hardly significant. That’s what made it even more odd that it felt so familiar to him. It felt like home. Real home. The one that’s the stuff of legend in any black family. Big Momma’s house. It wasn’t just some half wack movie that Martin Lawrence made up so he’d have an excuse to dress up in drag. Big Momma’s was the place where the whole family returned to at one point or another. It could just be to visit the family. It could be to rest. To relax after. To regroup after life has kicked you just a bit to hard for you to pop right back up to your feet. It was home base. Most other folks would understand it, but that’s ok. It’s a black thang. For most anyway. It had been a long time since Train had been able to return home. Places to stay? They were easy to come by. Home? He wasn’t welcome there. That must be why this place felt like a good option for a replacement. Though this wasn’t his home Train knew Brick was drawn to this place. It was home base. He spent a lot of time here eating Sunday dinner. By circumstances beyond odd Train had even spent an evening here doing the same. The dining room has a warm glow that seems to originate from the luster of the luminous cherry wood tabletop of the dining room table. It glimmers under the soft white lights as the spry old woman pours the mint tea into the cup of her guest She knew he’d be coming... sooner or later. She’d raised too many children not to know what was going to happen. Children are simple and most times adults only think they’re more complicated. Any time one child gets something that the other child wants it’s bound to go badly. Bound to be jealousy and resentment. Family or not those chemicals never mixed well. Brick was her son. Train... he’d spent time at her table. Anyone that spent time at her table was family. That’s just the way it was. So...whether they liked it or not... in every way that counted... they were brothers. Now little brother had something big brother wanted. She knew big brother well enough to know he wouldn’t take that well. “How much sugar you want, baby?” Mrs. Randall asks as she sets the tea kettle back down on it’s porcelain coaster. “Three please, ma’am.” Train responds as he swipes back the few stray dreadlocks not pulled back into his ponytail. “Mmmhmmm...” the old woman mutters with the hint of a chuckle. She takes the top off her sugar bowl and grasps the tongs before gingerly removing three grainy, white cubes and dropping them into Train’s cup. “I knew it. A boy with a sweet tooth. All the grandchildren I got I can spot “em a mile away now.” “I wouldn’t waste my time trying to fool you.” Train replies with a grin. The old woman, knowing she was going to have to take on the familiar position of abritrator settled her self into her seat, emitting a groan of years, making herself comfortable for what could be a long conversation. “So what’s the problem child” You and Jamal at each other’s throats again?” “Honestly Mrs. Randall” I have no idea.” “No idea?” she responds just before sliding her lips over the edge of the porcelain cup to take in a sip of her special peppermint tea she brewed just for her guest. She takes a moment to savor it, having learned over the years to take a moment to enjoy the good things even when you’re in the midst of a bad situation. After she’d found her enjoyment she sets her cup back down also having learned you can’t avoid the bad things forever. “Now boy, how in the world could you not know if you got problems with my little boy” I mean he’s only my little boy to me. To everybody else he’s an awful big man and when he’s mad at you, you’re pretty sure to know.” “Hahaha... Yeah... You ain’t gotta tell me that, I know better than most. Thing is... I don’t know cuz he just won’t say it. Phone calls, messages, voice mails, emails.... heh... I’m thinking about sending out a carrier pigeon to get hold of him.” “Ohhhhh....” she moans with an air of familiarity. “Mmmhmmm... I’ve seen that before. Jamal was never one to talk a whole lot about what was going on in that head of his. Most times... when he gets like that” Best thing you could really do is just let him alone. I know it ain’t easy, believe me, I know. But he’s a lot like his daddy. He just needed time on his own to sort things out.” “Understood. Only thing is.... We don’t necessarily have the luxury of him doing all that thinking, you know” I mean... Like it or night Brick and me... We’re the Tag Team Champions of the World. We got a match coming up against some dudes who ain’t coming to play. They want what we got. Now if I’m the only one that’s gonna have my head in the game on this one then so be it. I just need to look him in the eye so I know what’s up.” “I understand. But just know this. When it comes right down to it” My boy... he may sulk, pout, cross his arms and stomp his feet. But when it comes to do the right thing if there’s one man in this world you can count on to do it, it’s my Jamal.” Train looked into to the tender eyes of the woman that raised the man that was supposed to be watching back and believed. That she believed. As for him? That was a whole nother thing. The “Right Thing” has a whole lot of room for interpretation. The right thing for who? To Train it was obvious the right thing was to keep reminding the world that he was the best in the world by making sure both his shoulder had hardware to haul. The Tag Team Championship of the World. The Heavyweight Championship of the World. Deny one the validity of one if you want to, but both. Only an epic delusion could help you do that. And while most of these cats in GW definitely had screws loose, they weren’t as nutty as all that. Talk shit behind his back as they may, it was a different story when they had to look him in the eye. Carrying the straps you couldn’t deny the undeniable... Train was a champion. Problem was Brick wasn’t Train. Sure fate had conspired to lock them together in some twisted form or symbiosis, but nature never takes well to things that are wholly synthetic. It knows the difference between the genuine article and a cheap knock off. This new found camaraderie was nothing if not genetically altered. A freakish test tube baby grown and harvested in the lab of Bubba McCoy’s mind. Sure, you had to hand it to him. The mad scientist that he was, McCoy had felt in his soul from the beginning latching the two junkyard dogs of GW on the same leash would produce a rabid, two headed monster and he was right. Problem was, once they’d tore everyone else to pieces... who would be left except each other? Damn the goose or the gander what was good for one was definitely not good for the other any more. Maybe what Brick figured would be good for him now was to be done with the charade. Why continue to help prop up your enemy when it would be more fun to watch him lose? Sure you lose in the process, but if it’s what you wanted, what was really lost’Yeah, he believed she believed. Now if only he could do the same. Train opened his mouth to say something, what he wasn’t quite sure, when the rubber seal around the front door made a groan of released pressure as it was pushed in. The interruption was a bit of a relief and the sound of jingling keys further extended the distraction, increasing the thankfulness that he didn’t have to burst the old woman’s bubble about what her son was and wasn’t capable of. “Hey Ma”!” rumbled the deep voice, the bass in his voice reverberating through the whole house. “I’m home! You still got some of that left over chicke.....” The thud of his heavy footsteps stopped immediately as he entered the dining room. Brick stood in the doorway dumbfounded. This was HQ. The enemy wasn’t supposed to be able to penetrate these lines. Yet there he was. His mother aiding and abetting. “Now Jamal,” his mother began to say before being quickly cut off. “Get out.” Brick stated plainly, his eye not never leaving Train. “Now.” “Jamal! Boy, have you lost yo” mind?” Train stood before her son could even respond, what had been sitting in his lap the whole time now sliding down into his hand before he lifted his arms to hoist it onto his shoulder. The GW World Title gleamed in the warm glow of the room, a gold cataract sticking in Brick’s eye. “No, no, no Mrs. Randal,” Train began trying to bring the situation down a notch. “Don’t worry about it. It’s no problem. Actually it’s good.,” Train stated as he slowly moved for the doorway, Brick’s mass still blocking the way. The two men lock eyes and Train nods, cracking the smallest of grins. “Jamal just answered all my questions.” Train lifted his eyebrows, obviously wondering if Brick was going to move out of the way long enough to let him get out. It wasn’t really a question though. This was Big Momma’s house. He wasn’t gonna disrespect that by doing what he really wanted to in the pit of his gut. Brick turned is body to open up half the archway, Train turning his to slide through the other half. Train didn’t bother to doddle, having his own respect for this place. “And don’t come back!” Brick growled the instant Train’s hand touched the knob. “Jamal!” Mrs. Randal exclaimed with a growl equal to that of her child’s. While it gave him pause he didn’t bother to turn and look. Train turned the knob, pulled the door and stepped through, closing it behind him in a fluid move. Brick could only snarl as he made his way for the stairs. He only managed to get a foot on the first one before his mother was right there behind him. “Boy!!!” his mother yelled, freezing him in place. “I ain’t even got to ask if you lost yo” mind cuz it’s clear you have if you think you so grown that you can decide who I do and don’t have in my house!” “He doesn’t belong here.” Brick finally said bringing himself around from his petrified state, restarting his climb to the top of the stairs. “I just got through telling him that when it came to doing the right thing you were the type of man that would always do the right thing. Now like it or not that boy out there is your partner and he’s depending on you to do the right thing in your next match. Now are you going to do the right thing?” Brick paused, his foot on the last step at the top of the ascending path. It was a good question. It deserved an answer. “I don’t know.” Brick said as he took the final step into the shadows of the upstairs hallway.
