An intrusion

7 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/7/05 Pain itself is not without its alleviations. It is seldom both violent and long-continued; and its pauses and intermissions become positive pleasures. It has the power of shedding a satisfaction over intervals of ease, which few enjoyments exceed. ----William Paley A diamond always looks like a diamond as it shines clearly against a darkly colored cloth. All of its creases and sharp edges shimmer forth any light that happens to hit it and beams a spectrum of color not unlike a thousand prisms put together. There are few objects that can encompass anything in comparison. However there is something that happens to be rather similar...broken glass. The full moon gleamed its bright face down through the formally picturesque skylight that is nothing more than a large hole with a handful of hanging wood fragments. As the moonlight hit the sparkling remnants of glass on the jade carpet, it partially illuminated the surroundings of the room, including the dark figure standing in the doorway. Although the light is only able to reach the leg from the knee down and shine upon the figure’s neatly polished black shoe. The figure slowly steps out from the darkness and into the light for a better view of the wonderous moon peaking out from the gray cotton clouds that would provide a relaxing image for the nature lover. Instead it drove a shiver of rage throughout his body. Little by little the man becomes more visible and his slightly arched nose reveals all that he must about his identity as Jean-Claude Dafoe. His forehead partially covers with remnants of his blonde locks tightly run into a tail behind his head. His cheeks tighten as he winces against the brightness of the glowing orb in the sky. Dafoe tilts his head downward as he decends to the ground in order to examine the damage more closely. Reaching his hand through the compiled glass, he raises a small pile of it up into the air as some trickle clumsily off to all sides, trying to escape. The remaining shards are lifted closer to his face and given a closer inspection. “A clean break,” Dafoe thought to himself as he allows the glass bits to stream out of his palm like a waterfall. “Creative. I will give him that. He has good people working for him, but not good enough to clear all traces of evidence. I’ll find something that will link them to who they were and what they work for. And when I do, they will no longer be of service to anyone ever again. I swear to that.” Clenching his fist in anger, Dafoe neglects to remember that glass tends to stick to skin fragments on occasion and the bits dig deeper into his palm. The shards break through the skin and cause a few drops of crimson to seep out through his fingers onto the carpet. However he doesn’t seem to care as he stares forward, focused on the rage. Anger management has never been his strongpoint, but it never has been a weakness in the past either. Having used the frustration in battle on several occasions has given him much success. Whether in the ring or in the corporate world, his fury has led to the downfall of many individuals. For most people it causes them to lose focus and fumble up on what they are doing. But this Icon uses it as one of his more powerful tools and crushes his foe without mercy. Time will tell if this still ring true in wrestling. “I will get you for this, Zenith,” Dafoe mutters softly, yet enough for the words to be audible. “I assure you of that.” “You’re not going to go through with this, are you?” The soft voice is enough to make Dafoe spin himself around and crouch in place as he gazes back at the woman that he loves. Michele Cordier-Dafoe, the lady herself. She is viewed leaning her left palm against the doorway while she holds closed her blue satin robe with her right. Not only does it hold her robe closed, but it also provides some slight comfort from the bruising that her lower ribs had taken from the roughness of the kidnapping just days prior. As with most injuries, these are not very visible, but they are still there beneath. Deeply beneath. Michele retracts her left arm with a slight wince of pain as she starts to walk towards her kneeling husband. “I don’t want you to go through with this,” she says with a taste of worry in her voice. Its not there often, but tonight it is there. “You have nothing to prove anymore.” “Its not about what I have left to prove,” he grumbles genuinely as he looks up at her. “Its about what he’s done to you. I’d never forgive myself if I would just let some lunatic get his hands all over you and threaten your life. Especially over something as trivial as a match!” His voice raises after every point as he lets his inner passion feed. Only so much can be held back at one time before it eventually explodes and something must be done to release the stress. “Then don’t get down to his level,” she persists as she rests her hand on his shoulder. “You of all people should know that he is just using you to gain his own means. He’s just like my uncle Eric tried. Just like Chris Davis was. And a lot like...” As she trails off, Jean-Claude looks away from her eyes and down to the left in disgrace. “Like I used to be you mean.” Michele glides her left hand off his shoulder up through his tightened blonde locks, letting a few fall straggled from the bun. But her hand is left hanging as Dafoe pulls his head away in disdain. His face muscles wince together in a slight scowl. “You’re a wonderful woman, Michele. I wake up every day knowing this and I regret it at times.” Dafoe sighs heavily as he keeps his lowly face away from his spouse. “All that I have done wrong and that has hurt you. This is why I want to do this match.” Turning his gaze back towards Michele, the Icon turns his cold blue eyes upon her. “Because NO ONE should ever hurt you again. Its not that I want to fight in this match...Its that I must fight in this match. I have to make the point that no one can touch my life. No one can even come close to you. And if I don’t, then my job does nothing but put you in danger and put our life in danger.” Raising his hand into hers, Dafoe steps to his feet and gazes down at Michele with restrained concern. “Once was enough. It’ll never happen again.” As he lifts their hands together, she notices the thickened blood going down his palm. Her face shifts into question as she turns her hand over the wound. “Jean, you’re bleeding!” “Its nothing.” Showing no expression, he shrugs off the many small gashes. His worried bride turns and opens his palm as tiny specks of glass still protrude from the creases of skin. “How did this happen?” she exclaims in a motherly tone as she attempts to clean around the wound. How most wives turn into the mother type is amazing. “I picked up some of the glass and I wasn’t paying attention. I wouldn’t worry about it.” As she tries to wash the wound with her fingers, Dafoe pulls away from her touch to keep her from concerning herself with his problems. “I can take care of this myself,” he mutters as he wrings his wrist slightly, testing the sensations in his hand. “You should be getting your rest. You were supposed to be sleeping when I came down here.” She turns to him and rubs her velvet hands against his bare torso in gentle comfort as she answers Dafoe’s objections. “Its still hard for me to sleep. I can’t lean on my right side at all or I wake up coughing. Plus, its not easy to sleep when you’re not there. I always feel it when you get up.” “Well then I should get up less, shouldn’t I?” he chuckles to her in sympathy. “That’s right,” she coos as she wraps her left arm around him in a soft embrace. The wind creeps up a bit through the broken skylight, causing Michele to shiver roughly. “Brrr! That’s cold.” Lowering his hand Jean-Claude rubs her hand at a rapid pace to give a little extra warmth, for what its worth. “Then you should get back to bed and warm up.” “Maybe I should,” she nods in acknowledgement. “Are you going to join me yet tonight?” “I’ll be there soon enough,” he says as he turns to face his bride. He lowers his head to give a soft peck to her forehead before sending her off with his arm. She turns and strides her way back into the shadows. Her right arm peaks from her shoulder as she rotates her hand around her lower ribs. Jean-Claude watches her every step letting a small grin crease across his lips. Another cold gust echoes through the air from the broken air above. Dafoe looks up slightly at the moon as it has shifted mostly out of view. I guess the world is still spinning. Some times faster than others, but it still is. The moon shines across his rippled chest as he stands twisting his wrist slightly. The cold has never bothered him much, even when he stands shirtless against nature’s weapons. There was a time when he attempted the polar bear club, but instead found there was more sense in keeping his clothes on and walked away. Dafoe turns to step out of the dim blue light and back into the darkness that his wife just passed though moments prior. She is further ahead by a room’s length, but he’s not in a chase. At least not of her. He begins to glance down and regains his thoughts on tracing the infiltrators? path. Its obvious that they passed through this hallway next since there are no adjoining rooms to the study. At least none that are visible to outsiders. There are always little tricks to mansions and they’re always put there for good reason. That night would have been a good one to use the passageways. However, the invaders were mostly silent. Even their helicopter flew as quiet as a hummingbird swarming near nectar. Its hard to believe that technology has advanced that much in so few years, but it has. The security system had been hacked through ten minutes prior to the glass breaking. They must have also gotten wind of the back-up system’s capabilities. The back-up is rather simple with mostly laser guides around the low ground and motion cameras in certain sections of the house, and its only activated if the main is shut down. Failure-proofing doesn’t always live up to the guarantees. There’s always something new to learn from as well. The assailants also had strong night vision since the hallway is nearly in pitch darkness with the windows heavily blocked by the hedges outside. Which reminds Dafoe that he must have the gardener come twice a week instead of once after heavy rain. The main hallway had a lot more light and room to deal with. Granted it was supposed to be one of the more secure rooms in the whole compound, but that doesn’t mean much. Dafoe creaks his head into the corner where he found a bloodied Raul lying unconscious later that evening. The elder manservant wasn’t badly injured, but he was left with a broken nose and a rather big knot in the back of his head. Raul held his own with two of the assailants, but the other three caught from different angles and took him down the hard way. “Rabid beasts,” he grumbles. He continues down the center hallway as the many statues and shadowed paintings stare back at him. An eerie sight for a youngster going through the darkness and still not the most comfortable feeling for an adult either. At least not for an outsider. Dafoe has learned to live with the strangeness of the art and would call them a extra watchdogs for the paranoid burglar. But they didn’t detour the kidnapping bandits at all. They simply lead to the master bedroom. And their target. Softly creaking the door open, Jean-Claude watches as Michele begins to snuggle herself back to sleep. Or as close as she can get for now. Its not just the pain she is putting up with, but some of the fear as well. It didn’t help that she was asleep when they tried to take her. And she wasn’t awake for long before they had her. A little chloroform and she was out. Dafoe gazes over at her and tries his best to hold the rage before it wells up again. “It doesn’t help to use it up now,” he says to himself. “Let it build...” Quietly stepping his way into the nearby bathroom, Dafoe closes the door before turning the light on. At least he didn’t turn on the loud fan by accident. The florescent light glimmer on in its oft-white tone, shining off the dark teal linoleum tiling and bath fixtures. Dafoe recalls that his hand is still a bit wounded, he approaches the washbasin and turns on the ivory knob labeled “H”. “Might as well clean this out while I’m here,” Dafoe says to himself as he places his injured hand under the gold faucet and winces slightly as the burning hot water strikes his tender skin. A thin fog rises up from the sink as the liquid streams around the wounds. Using his free hand, the Icon slowly begins to pick away at the larger pieces of glass still wedged beneath the reddened skin. His face contorts to different shapes of grimaces as it reflects in the mirror before him as the steam begins to collect on it. As he gets the last chunk of skylight out of his palm, he glares over at the smudged visage in the mirror. “So you’re going to do this again,” he says to the man in the mirror. “I hope you’re ready for it all.” Tucking the glass into a few strips of toilet paper, Dafoe tosses them away and crouches down underneath the sink to grab the medic kit. Unsnapping the catch, he picks up the athletic wrap and proceeds to swathe his right hand in the cloth until it completely covered the wounds. As he finished, he leans forward and watches the fog slowly evaporate around his reflection. “I need to do this. I do...” Fear has never been a factor in his career. In some ways it still isn’t. However, now it isn’t about fear for himself alone. Now...things have changed. Dafoe clenches his right fist together one last time before turning towards the bedroom door. He reaches one hand onto the doorknob, whilst the other is on the light switch. As he walks into the darkness once again, he always knows one thing in his life: “I am the Icon...” FADE TO BLACK
