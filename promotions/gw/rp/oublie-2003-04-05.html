Oublie

5 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 040503 The French call it an Oublie. The word rolled smoothly off of my tongue, my voice echoing slightly. I ran my hands over the smooth cold surface of the chamber. It is a place for forgetting. Lisha laid curled in a ball in a far corner, still woozy in her drug induced state. She listened to me, but not really listening. The mere sight of me moving probably sickened her. You will have no guard. I paced across the room. On the opposite ends, I had installed shelving. On those shelves, rations of dry non-perishable foods and bottles of water. Just these four walls. In another corner, an old mattress laid. A single bare bulb hung from the ceiling, burning. You have enough food and water for several weeks. I crossed over to the entrance of the stone chamber. I will be back in approximately one month to check on you. I stood at the doorway, looking at Lisha, still curled in her ball looking at me. She still had the look of a wild caged animal in her eye. Until then, I bid thee adieu. I pushed against the heavy stone door, my muscles straining. The heavy stone door finally began to move as I pushed against it, returning to its resting place, camouflaging itself with the rest of the wall. I moved slowly through the lower level, walking through the rubble. I made my way up a dark staircase. I had discovered the room by chance while searching for answers to still unanswerable questions. I run my hands across the fire scarred walls of the staircase. I push the memories away as I open the door at the top of the stairs. The hallways merge, dead carcasses meshing against faint memories, the new and the old clashing as I see ghosts of the past walking this way and that. I try to push it all away, but it won’t leave. I mingle with the ghosts of my mind, as the pass around and through me. I shut my eyes, moving on instinct. Straight down the hall, left, right, straight, another right. Stop. I open my eyes. The closet. I stare at the door. Ominously, it stairs back. Somehow, it had survived the carnage of the flames. I reach out, grasping the handle. Slowly, every so slowly, I turn it. The door creaked open eerily. I look inside of the small, small room. I feel it pull at me. I try to resist the urge, but I cannot. I step inside. The door slams shut behind me, moved by some unseen force. Everything flashes before my eyes. I hear screaming. I relive every moment of my childhood in the space of a few moments. The screaming still pierces the air. I realize the one screaming? Is me. FADE
