Cursed?

6 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/6/04 My father once said. “Life is what you make of it. Don’t settle for just peaches when you can have gold.” For the last year or so, I’ve been trying to figure out what that meant. Analyzing every word that was put in that sentence and still I can’t figure out what it means. But the fact of the matter is that whatever it means, I know that deep down it will come to me. Just like wrestling has come to me. Before wrestling, before all this sweat, blood, and tears I was just a girl. A girl that was looking for a path to go to. And when I met it, it was meant to be. Riding down a long path in our backyard woodlands, I could smell the morning breeze. It was fresh and a little silky in a way. Almost watery like. Riding along, passing the green leaf tree’s and the only little pond that is ours, or for that matter on our land to be exact. I could feel the rain coming in. Looking up to see the dark clouds that hovered above my pulled back long hair. My mind seemed to go in a different way. I couldn’t seem to stay focused on the task at hand. And that was to have a good time alone on a ride that I haven’t had in a while. No, I seemed to pick away at everything that I did in the ring at Overload against Freeze. It seemed that not only did I take up the moves and everything that my uncle did, I also took up his mind set as well. I remember along time ago, right after my uncle won the CWF World Title, I remember tossing him the biggest party ever. A cake that was all the way to the top of our two story house almost. Music, story telling, you name it we brought it to him. But it seemed that no matter how much stuff we bought him or how much music was playing, he kept on talking about everything that he did wrong. Was it the messed up move or the advantage that he let go of. It seemed that I had took on this feature, this messed up thinking, and I was? “Cursed,” A sharp and firm voice said to me, making me twist the horse around to see who it was. “Uncle Johnson.” I said, making a smile, yet wondering what he meant by this. He never liked riding horses. Let alone riding them in this scary place. So what was he doing here? And what did he mean by cursed? “Cursed” What do you mean Uncle?” Asking him with a confused look on my face. Still trying to figure out what he was really doing out here. “I can see it in your eyes. Your not thinking about this wonderful morning. Your thinking about what you could of done better inside that ring aren’t you?” I didn’t have the heart to ever lie to my uncle. That was our deal. We told the truth or he would stop training me. So how could I tell him no when he knew without a shadow of a doubt that I would be lying? I wiped my eyes for a second, trying to get the question, or the comment that he made away from me. “Yes,” I said. “I just can’t seem to stop. How did you ever make it stop?” “I never did. It’s still there.” Uncle Johnson said, riding up right next to me as he placed his right hand on my shoulder. This looked familiar. I remember when my Uncle lost the CWF Title. He cried and cried over and over, trying to get the title back every night he was in action. But it never did. I use to talk with him, sitting right next to him late at night when I should of be going to bed for school the next morning. I lowered my head for a moment before the two of us began riding slowly towards the other side of the woods. “I just can’t stop thinking about what I could of done to make myself look better. I know I got the win but it just didn’t feel right.” I said, looking over at his eyes as he sat there and listened. “It’s ok. Even your grandfather did it as well when he was alive.” Uncle Johnson replied. Still holding his arm on my shoulder. “That is why it’s a curse to us all.” I could still remember the times that I went over to my grandfathers house, seeing him sit there against he window as he stared out, not even responding to us being there. I often wondered what he was thinking but I never could see it or get anyone to tell me. I just shook my head as I stopped the horse. “Well I’m sorry Uncle but I’m going to beat this. I’m not going to end up like my Grandfather.” Wiping a small tear as it began to go down my soft cheek. I was strong my father said. I had my mothers bravery in a way. Though I could never see it sometimes. Maybe it was hidden deep in my soul and awaiting for the day that I would be ready to handle it. Or maybe it was already there and I just couldn’t see it. Whatever the case was, I was just going to beat this. I looked at my shocked Uncles face. Not letting him respond, I began riding up the path towards the open area that I could see ahead. Still thinking of what I said. Could I really beat it? And if I could then why couldn’t my Uncle or Grandfather. Uncle Johnson caught up with me. He seemed angry in a way as he grabbed my arm and made me stop. “Listen Elia. Don’t think that one second that you are going to beat it without help. It will consume you in every way possible.” Uncle Johnson said, lowering his head as he finished. “Trust me, I know all to well.” “What do you mean?” Responding to his angry words. But my question didn’t get a response. All it got was a look that could tell even more. Uncle Johnson’s eye locked on mine. His ice cold blue pupils almost told the words that he wanted to say. Lowering his head for a moment before riding toward the open area, I could feel a little bit of rain coming down from the dark sky above. I looked up at it, letting a few more hit my face before looking back at Uncle Johnson. “I will beat this?”
