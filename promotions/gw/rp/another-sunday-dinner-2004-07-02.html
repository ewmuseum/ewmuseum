Another Sunday dinner

2 July 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/2/04 It had been some time since Brick had spent time with his family. His mother had suggested that they meet more often for Sunday dinner. This was just one of those occasions. One thing you could definitely say about Coretta Randall: she definitely liked to cook. This week, the family was having pork chops, Spanish rice, steamed cabbage and black beans. At the head of the table sat Coretta. Brick sat to her right. To her left sat Monica and Kyle. 'Father God, we come to you in the spirit of giving thanks. We thank you for this day and for this time together. We thank for our health and for our lives. We thank you for the food we are about to receive and for the hands that prepared it. We ask that you bless this food to build the body, the mind and the soul. In Jesus' name, we pray.' 'In Jesus' name.' 'Amen.' 'Amen.' Soon after, they all started to prepare their dishes. Brick offered to fix his mother's plate. 'No, that's okay, baby. You go ahead and fix your plate.' 'Are you sure, Ma. 'Cause I can wait.' 'No, no, I'm fine.' 'OK,' Brick said, shrugging his shoulders. He then proceeded to fill his plate with two pork chops, and healthy servings of rice and cabbage. Coretta fixed her plate, then Monica fixed hers and then Kyle fixed hers. 'I would like for you two to pray for your cousin David.' 'Why, Ma? What's wrong?' 'He just filed for divorce from Natasha.' 'What? You're kidding,' said Monica. 'No, I'm serious. He's divorcing her.' 'About damn time,' Brick muttered. 'Jamal!' 'I'm sorry, Ma, but you know it's true. He ain't have no business marrying her in the first place.' 'Doesn't mean I allow cursing in my home. Yes, we all felt that your cousin shouldn't have married that girl. But I prayed on it and God told me not to say anything.' 'Yeah, which is why you tried to get me to say something.' 'So did you?' asked Kyle. 'No. I said, 'Ma, look. Everything you know about Natasha, David knows and then some. And if he's still marrying her after all that, what good is anything you or I or anybody else says gonna do?' Then we just left it at that. Even though I really wanted to say something at the wedding.' 'The few times I met her,' Kyle said, 'she seemed nice enough.' 'Seemed is right,' replied Brick. 'I remember how Kyle told me about how he and she got into arguments. How she kept trying to get him to hit her. How she was always on him about where he was, but she always went out, acting a fool.' 'What happened at their wedding is what really should have told him not to go through with it,' said Monica. 'Why? What happened at the wedding?' 'Oh, that's right. You and I weren't seeing each other, then.' 'Well,' replied Brick, 'you know when the minister asks if anyone has just cause as to why the bride and groom shouldn't be married and to speak now or forever hold your peace?' 'Yeah?' 'Right after the minister said it, one of the groomsmen passed out, right then and there.' 'You're kidding.' 'No, he's serious,' added Monica. 'Knocked over the unity candles on his way down, too.' 'Wow. Was the groomsman ok?' 'Yeah, but we kinda kept raggin' him about it, afterwards.' 'Well, I'd rather we talked about something happier,' said Coretta. 'I agree.' 'So, Monica, how's the baby doing?' 'Kyle and I just went in for the sonogram.' 'And?' 'And,' answered Kyle, 'we're having twins.' 'Twins? Oh, that's wonderful.' 'Boys or girls?' 'Both. A boy and a girl.' 'That's beautiful.' 'Have you figured out any names, yet?' “Not yet, but we’re thinking maybe we’d name the boy after Dad.” “I think he’d love that.” “That’s great. Just make sure my name ends up somewhere in there.” “Oh, Jamal.”
