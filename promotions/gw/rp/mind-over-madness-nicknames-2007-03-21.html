mind over madness: nicknames

21 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
mind over madness: nicknames Reginald Atkins Global Wrestling Promo #3050 Date: 3/21/07 Location: It’s funny how people will go great leaps and bounds to make judgments and perceptions using the littlest bit of information. Before I start off my exciting day of finally facing Nathan Boswell after my actions on Fusion and dealing with whatever dramatic thing that happens in my life almost every week, I want to talk about nicknames. Particularly my nickname. It seems that recently people have been hanging on to the nickname and using it as the only measure with which to judge me. Not my impressive technical prowess, not my actions concerning the big lug or his blind bint, but two simple words. Brit. Twit. It’s silly, really. I mean, yeah, we came up with the name, but we did it to get a message across. We thought it would be an easier way to convey my attitude to the morons who ingest this diorama of diarrhea every week. Apparently we gave the morons a bit too much credit. Let’s take a brief aside to discuss Geoffrey Chaucer. This will make sense in a mo, I promise you. Before Chaucer wrote the revolutionary piece The Canterbury Tales, English literature was not as complex as anything you’d read today. Chaucer gave us many voices, but before that everything was in a single voice, every character represented by allegory, because the writers knew that their audience would not understand anything more complex than that. So you have characters in stories named Glutton who eat and drink too much, and the Lady Reason who is logical, etcetera, so forth, all that rot. The reason the creative department came up with the nickname “The Brit Twit” was because when making a product for wrestling fans, it’s best to act as if Geoffrey Chaucer never existed. (I’m sorry to keep referring to English masterpieces I’m sure you tossers have never heard about, but it’s part of my culture and it’s how I relate to things. So if you don’t get a reference, either look it up or blindly accept that whatever I’m saying is right. Carrying on.) They called me Brit because…well…because I’m bloody British, how complex is that? But it’s the Twit thing that sends people over the edge. And at first it gave me fits. But now I’m glad that my nickname confuses people. It fits right into my plans. Because I’m a “twit,” people expect me to be a jerk, but essentially harmless. And when you see me part of little things like interviews and using staff members as shoes, it confirms that. And then everyone gets confused and upset when I try to poison and attack an innocent girl. Since when did it state you had to follow your nickname to the letter, anyway? Let’s look at some of the established legends that occupy or have occupied our sacred ground of Global Wrestling. Our lovely champion Jake Hix isn’t actually a big ‘Dawg.’ You don’t see him barking or frothing at the mouth…well…bad example. But you know what I mean. The list goes on. Eryc Morgan isn’t any more flammable than any other wrestler, you can’t put bullet holes in anyone with Ely Braxton, and I don’t expect to see Devlin Rubis performing wedding services any time soon. They don’t necessarily have to be allegorical. These are names that either send a message or sound good. Mine has the wonderful luxury of doing both. But for all of my greatness, because of the common wrestling fan I am not spending the opening minutes of thought on pressing situations, but to address something that shouldn’t be too hard to comprehend. I guess it’s what I get for moving to Texas. ---- I’ve waited all day, but he hasn’t showed up. You think after having a bombshell dropped on him like teaming him with a sworn enemy that he would come over to my house and do the usual frothing, angry “I hate you why are you doing this to me oh wait you said a few pretty words I’m immediately convinced you are right sorry so much Mr. Atkins” thing, but he hasn’t even busted in rudely once. Kind of puts me ill at ease, here. Maybe he’s learning. It’s a shame, really. Now I can’t have more fun. It’s almost a little sad to think about: my fun is dependent on Nathan. If he wasn’t around, I couldn’t do these things that get my rocks off, and I’d be bored, waiting around all day… …for the phone to ring? Ring, ring, pick up, hello. The voice is northern and gritty, and I immediately know who it is. “Hello, I’m calling for Mr. Reginald Atkins.” And the formality confirms it. James Eugene Parker. Charmed, I’m sure. Now for those of you who haven’t been paying attention to me for my entire Global run, I first want to shame on you. Then I want to explain that this man is someone who is after us for a certain…deal gone bad. He hasn’t been able to find us yet, and I knew it was only a matter of time. So I’m screwed, right? Of course not. Twits are clever, remember? I want to preface this by saying that it’s incredibly easy for us Brits to fake an American accent. Anyone seeing Jude Law’s terrible attempts to do so is getting a very limited scope. “Sorry, sir, Mr. Atkins isn’t in right now. May I take a message?” I hear a pause on the line, and I roll my eyes, waiting for the stock response. “Tell him that I’m just looking for him to touch up on our…business.” And there it is. The thing about these kinds of guys is they’re really not trying hard to find an acceptable thing to say (as saying “I’m trying to kill him for stealing my shit” isn’t exactly a good message to leave), it’s that they are trying hard to make it sound like they’re finding something acceptable to say. Talk about the showing versus telling thing. These tough guy types try to beat it into your head that they’re tough without actually saying it. I’m getting ready to hang up when I get an idea. Acting like some friendly over-confident dumb American, I let out, “He’s probably with his girlfriend at the zoo or something, if you’re in town and want to catch him.” Parker’s “Really?”, if printed, would probably have five e’s. He sounds intrigued, and he certainly is. Guys like Parker – and me, really – like to find weaknesses, and I can almost hear Parker juice himself over the phone. Parker’s attempt to sound casual is pathetic: “So Reggie’s got himself a girlfriend, huh? What’s her name?” I can’t hide my smile as I say this; I hope he’s not the kind of guy who can smell bullshit over the phone. “Arianna. It’s kind of weird, though, man.” “What do you mean, weird?” “She’s…she’s blind, dude.” I spit the word dude from my mouth like venom. I sound stupid, I sound uncultured…I sound American. Disgusting. “Fascinating,” he’s saying, and for once I know the tough guy means it. It’s a strange thing for someone to have a blind girlfriend. Everyone gets on me for being an arse, but honestly, why would anyone date a blind girl, if not out of pity? It gets weird reactions, and apparently forces British men to try to kill her. So I don’t know why Bozzy does this to himself. Parker continues. “Do they live together?” Here’s the clincher, pay attention. “No, she lives a few miles away, right outside Dallas.” Now I can hear the smile over the phone on his end; hook, line, sinker. “Dallas? Right.” Once more acting casual, the mobster finds a lame excuse to get off of the phone, and I find myself looking up at a horrified seven foot Kraut. “Wer war das?” he’s asking, his face looking very uneasy, an expression which is not fitting for him. Makes him look even uglier. “Jimmy-Gene. He knows where we are.” Lyell’s expression expands, if that’s at all possible. He shakes his head, slinging an array of German curses. When he’s quite done, he looks at me and visibly gives up. “Wir sind tot.” Honestly, it’s these kinds of moments that makes me glad I decided to keep Lyell on the sideline for this federation. He’s like an elephant jumping at harmless mice. “We’re dead.” Come on. “No, my boy. Somehow, I think we’ll be all right.” Brit Twit. A harmless arrogant man who likes to pull pranks and hurt people, but only on a small scale. All I can say is it sounded better than “The Malicious and Evil Blind Girl-Killing Englishman.”
