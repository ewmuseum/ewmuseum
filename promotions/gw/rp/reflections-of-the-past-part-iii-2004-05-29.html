Reflections of the past: Part III

29 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 29th, 2004. Thumbing through an old photo album it's sometimes awe inspiring to see where I was once was and where I am now. I see pictures of a scrawny kid getting thrown around in the ring and then I see the Evolution of that kid. I see him putting on muscle and growing larger, I see his strategy building as his mind plots feats of tomorrow. In short, I am forced to question myself with the query of… 'What have I became?' Yet this introspective question falls on deaf ears in my empty apartment. Once I was adored by the masses, they assumed that I would probably never make the big time due to my small frame and short stature but they did acknowledge my talent. Every night when I stepped into the ring they clapped, they cheered, they chanted my name and held their signs high. Yet all I was to them…is another Indy Wrestler that they paid five dollars to watch. I never failed to deliver and even to this day if a fan pays five dollars or fifty dollars to watch me I still put on an in-ring spectacle that will amaze them, inspire them and take their breath away. Yet now their adoration has vanished, their love is gone and in place is hatred. They loathe the person I've become. The underdog rose to the top…and once I was the Champion and no longer the contender they voiced their hatred for me as never before. 'You suck!' They chanted at me every night…fools, all of them. Only a year ago they had chanted my name…yet now they despise me. Flipping the page of the open album that rested across my lap, my eyes danced along the row of photos until it rested upon one in particular, the photo featured Tommy Rock and I, and it quickly captured my interest. Sitting on the ring apron after a training session we each bore a broad smile as my bluish gray eyes twinkled with happiness. His arm was thrown around my shoulders as his black moustache nearly camouflaged his smile. I was the perfect student…obedient, selfless, and submissive as well as a quick study to boot. How things have changed… My eyes darted over to the next photo a picture in which I was standing in the ring for the very first time. My eyes wide with excitement and a hidden smile of joy plastered across my face. I remember that moment with joy…yet it's the circumstances leading up to that moment that I don't recall with fondness. ---- July 10th, 1999. The clothes on my back, a backpack, four hundred-17 dollars, eighty-three cents and a swollen lip were the extent of my earthly possessions as my eyes spied the large sign that bore the words 'Rock Wrestling Academy'. I had left my Nebraska home only yesterday in pursuit of a career in Professional Wrestling and this is where I had arrived…at the training school of a Wrestling legend. Tommy Rock was a man that I had long respected for his ability in the ring as he had competed far and wide on the Indy circuit of America where he had gone unnoticed before gaining great accolades in Japan. A technical mastermind with high flying tendencies and a stubborn streak a mile long he had grown famous for being a near perfect Wrestler in the ring as well as a shrewd businessman out of the ring. So when he decided to give back to the business by training stars of the future…I knew that this was my big moment. His school had been open merely eleven months but already his students were gaining recognition throughout the Indy circuit as well as Japan and Mexico. Licking my swollen lip nervously I readjusted the backpack that hung over my shoulder and with a spring in my step I thrust my black Converse Chuck Taylor's forward as I made my way across the Colorado Springs street and to the building which resided next to an abandoned school. The building itself was an imposing; heavily built brick gymnasium and I could only picture what awaited me inside. Having made my way across the empty street I dusted off my torn up jean shorts and white wife beater before dropping my hand down to reach for the knob. I touched the knob but it seemed to jump from my grasp…and that's when I realized that I was shaking nervously. Taking a deep breath I exhaled loudly and brushed my fingers through my black locks before once again reaching for the knob. This time, with butterflies in my stomach, I turned the knob and the metal door swung on its hinges, opening me up to an all new World. 'This is it…' I muttered to myself under my breath in a muted voice. I shut the door behind me as my eyes quickly circled the contents of the gymnasium. The set up was far simpler than I had expected. In the center of the gym sat a large albeit empty ring, to each side of that ring about ten feet away from the squared circle sat four long benches. Even further yet, about five feet away from one bench was a camera and on the opposing side was a Television that rested upon a steel chair with a VCR shoved underneath. 'This is it..?' I said, rephrasing my previous statement as it now evolved into a question. For such a highly touted training facility I was expecting only the best, not something so crude as this. This was nothing more than an old basketball court with a ring and some benches in the center. My eyes still scanned the room and I noted the sign that read 'Bathrooms', looking even further I spotted a small room to the side of the Gym. The door to the room was ajar but I could only assume this was the office. My sneakers made a slight squeak as I walked across the floor to the office building. Thrusting my head inside I knocked sharply on the agape door. 'Mr. Rock?' I questioned, as I spotted the man himself sitting behind his desk, his bearded face crunched up in a scowl as he studied a set of papers in his hand. 'The one and only.' He replied without so much as looking up at me. 'And whom might you be?' He questioned. 'Josh…Josh, I'd like to train here, sir.' I said trying not to choke on the nervousness in my voice. 'Have a seat and I'll get to you in a minute.' He said, stabbing at the air with his index finger as he pointed me toward the black leather couch that sat parallel to his desk. Dropping onto the couch I swung the backpack from my shoulder and let it rest on the wood floor that seemed to be spread throughout the entire gym. As I sat in silence my eyes studied the layout even further. This office was far from being a work of art, the florescent lights that hung above Tommy's head gave off an irritating buzz and one of the lights would flicker on occasion. The walls themselves were painted a stale white color and adorned with numerous pictures of Tommy throughout his career. Such highlights as his matches in Japan decorated the wall and behind him over his head there was a picture of him smiling as he held a title aloft with blood dripping down his face as it streaked his black beard crimson. 'What's a ten letter word that is the opposite of Catholic?' Tommy inquired not even bothering to look up from the paper in his hand which I now deduced was a mere crossword puzzle. 'Protestant.' I responded with a smile, a bit amused that a man of such prestige would be playing a crossword puzzle in his office. 'Got it…' He said as he furiously scribbled down the word onto his paper before dropping both the pen and the paper back onto his desk. Taking a swallow of coffee from the heavy blue mug on his desk he looked over at me with his penetrating brown eyes. 'Now...what can I do for you?' He asked in a tone bearing a slight hint of annoyance. I avoided immediate eye contact with him as I studied his apparel, a pair of jeans, white sneakers and Gold's Gym T-shirt…he seemed down to earth by his attire alone. I answered his question slowly, hesitantly as my eyes rose to meet his. 'I'm here to become a Professional Wrestler, sir.' I stated with a proud smile my swollen lip cracking as I did so. 'You’re here to become a Wrestler?' Tommy questioned as he eyed me over. 'Yes sir, I Am.' I enthusiastically responded. 'Your kind of scrawny…are you sure you don't want to be a manager or referee? Their always in need of good referees you know.' Tommy said as he looked me over. 'No sir, I came here with the intention of becoming a Wrestler and that's what I shall do.' I flatly stated the enthusiasm gone from my voice as Tommy's questioning of my size and ability had led me to a state of despondency. 'What do you weigh, kid?' He asked with a raised eyebrow. 'One hundred and forty three pounds but I'm planning on putting on…' I began before he cut me off. 'One hundred and forty three pounds? Kid, I've thrown around turnbuckle pads bigger than you!' He said with a chuckle. 'But I'm dedicated, I have the desire to become anything I want!' I shot back in an attempt to rise from my dejected state. 'Is that what your daddy told you?' Said Tommy with an overbearing arrogant sneer. 'No…my father told me I was a fool who was throwing his life away.' I said, bowing my head in shame. 'Yet you still want to become a Professional Wrestler?' He queried with a blank stare of surprise etched on his face. 'Yes sir…' I admitted, with a humble nod. 'Well kid, maybe I misjudged you. You've got heart…I'll give you that, but you can't build a career on heart, you need the abilities as well.' He slowly stated. 'That's why I came to you…so I could become the best Wrestler alive.' I told him. 'Kid I'm a Wrestling trainer…not a miracle worker. I can't make you a star and to be honest with you…I hate to say this but…you'll probably never be a star.' He softly said in an attempt to warn me yet preserve my feeling at the same time. 'I'll be a star yet…you'll see.' I emphatically stated as my eyes rose to meet his. 'You'll see.' ---- And as I close this photo album I cant help but look back into the past and reflect on the numerous moments in the making of a star. Because that is indeed what I have became…a star. But even more than a star…a Champion, the Champion.
