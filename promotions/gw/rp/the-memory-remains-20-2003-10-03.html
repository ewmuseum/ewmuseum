The Memory Remains #20

3 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 100303 I watched in the distance as Deacon sprint across the open ground, ducking behind a small shrub and disappearing into the night. Now, onto the business at hand. I turned and looked at the small cluster of identical buildings and studied each one carefully. I had pulled a good bluff on Deacon. Offhand, I didn’t even know which building was the damn security station. But, I’ll be damned if I’m gonna go down a rat infested vent, past a giant rotating fan. I might come back missing some important pieces. And that would just not do. I referenced the LCD map on my forearm, punching the appropriate buttons to try to make out which of the three buildings was the right one. God bless technology. If it weren’t for this handy little device, it would take the rest of the night to find the right building and we would be serving chopped Deacon at the next dinner party. Strolling across the open grounds, I pulled the collar of my black jacket up to try to cover my ears. I don’t mind the rain. But, you know what I really hate? Cold rain. Especially when that shit splish-splashes on the black of my neck or right in my damn ear. Drives me crazy. As I walked, I couldn’t help but replay the song that Deac and me were listening to on the way here. Hell, how could I not? He had the music so loud that even the astronauts could here it. Ten let the countdown begin. I was born in the mind. Take the head of a snake. Behold Armageddon. Ain't no love for the rich. Only strong will survive. Cause we live by the sword. Plus sixty degrees. For the black and the blue. For the sun, Step into millennium. I pressed my back to the building. I couldn’t really go in through the front door, due to security measures. Sure, I could pick a lock, but electronic locks, uh uh, no way, no how. And its getting rather difficult now a days to fool security cameras. Once I got inside, I’m not worried, its just getting inside that’s the problem. I slid along the wall, making my way to the closest window. Reaching into my pocket, I retrieved a long tube and screwed it onto the end of the Glock in my hand. Standing underneath the window, I reached up and touched the lower pane of glass. Applying slight pressure as such an awkward angle, I pushed up, hoping that the window would be open. I was surprised when it slid smoothly up. Exposing my back to the rest of the world, I hopped up to the windowsill and pulled myself in, rolling fluidly into the hallway, landing in a crouch, my pistol up. A random person turned the corner and looked up just in time to see me crouched down before I pulled the trigger without hesitation. The gun made a slight cough and the person reached up and slapped the front of their throat before dropping to the floor in a pile. I scurry over to where the body is, doing a quick search. I found another security card, which I pocketed. Wrapped around the man’s wrist was an elastic cord with some keys on it. I took them as well. Further search turned up nothing. Looking up, I saw a small door. Walking over to it, I opened the door to reveal a closet. Smiling, I drug the body over to the closet and locked it inside. He wouldn’t be missed for a while. I raised my left arm and consulted the electronic map. It would be nice if this thing comes equipped with radar. This opening a door and seeing bad guys trying to kill me is getting a little old. Note to self, ask Il Capo about radar option. I check my position against the location of the main switchboard. According to the handy little gadget, it was located through the door in front of my and on the second door on the right. Keeping my gun extended and at a forty-five degree downward angle, I treaded carefully down the short hallway, trying not to make any excessive noise. Grabbing the first doorknob to exit the hallway, I twisted it slowly till it could turn no more. Then, pushing in, I opened the door a peak. Seeing no one, I opened the door the rest of the way and shut it silently. Knowing that Deacon was somewhere across the compound, bitching about my slowness, I quietly ran down the corridor to the second door on the right. Standing against the door that leads to the main security room, I stand with my back pressed to it. In the darkness, my black clothes help me blend in with the shadows. On the far end of the corridor, near a turning corner, I can see the light beam of a flashlight sweeping back and forth. I suck in a deep breath, waiting to see what the person was going to do. The beam swept the wall opposite of my position and started to move up and down as the person began to amble their way down the corridor. Fuck. I wiggled the knob of the door I was pressed against and it gave way. Unlocked. I could hear the person’s footstep as they got closer and closer. The smell of burning tobacco reached my nose and the sizzle of paper danced in my ears. I had no time to pre-check the room. Maybe luck would be on my side. I eased the knob to one side, leaned back on the door, and vanished into the room, shutting the door silently. I pushed up against the door and could hear the person walk by, humming some tune, unaware of my presence. Perhaps luck was one my side. Behind me, I could hear the clicking of gun safeties and chambering of rounds. I slowly turned around and saw four armed men pointing pistols at men. So much for luck. One of them gestured at me with his gun, “Drop it.” I let my silenced Glock clatter to the floor. The one that spoke moved forward carefully, almost unsure, and kicked it away from me. With the weapon on the other side of the room now, the men relaxed slightly, their prisoner now unarmed. “Get down, on you knees,” the one that spoke earlier, said. I cocked my head to one side, giving them a blank stare. “If you surrender now, I’ll spare all of you,” I said quietly. All the men started to laugh. “You’re not in a position to negotiate,” he said again. Apparently, this guy was the brain of the bunch. Or the dumbest one. The other three just kinda stood there with their weapons drawn and pointing. From their positions, I wasn’t even sure if they would hit me. The man stepped forward, releasing the two-handed grip on his gun, shifting to a single-handed grip, jamming the barrel into my forehead, angling it down. “I said, on you knees.” Slowly, I sunk down to both knees, raising my hands slightly. I focused on the guard’s grip on his gun, which was still a weak single-handed grip. When I was in my final kneeling position, his hand relaxed anymore. Amateur. “What do you think, boys” Should we kill him?” He asked sadistically, an evil gleam in his eye. The others looked at each other, unsure of if their associate was joking or not. “I don’t know, man,” a high voice came from one of them. I couldn’t see which one because I was still focused on the gun. A moment was coming. I could feel it, I could sense it. Hell, I could almost taste it. “Aww, why not?” the gunman complained. “We never get to shoot anyone,” he said, turning his head to look back at the other men. Amateur. I reached out and snatched the man by the wrist with my right arm, jerking and twisting him around, circling his gun hand to point at his chest, standing in the same fluid motion, hiding my body behind his. I snaked my finger underneath his lax finger, taking control of the gun. I looked over the shoulder of my human shield and watched his friends tense up again. “What the fuck!” one of them yelled. “Let him go!” another one put in. “SHOOT THIS MOTHERFUCKER!” my shield cried out. “That’s not a nice thing to say to your captor,” I whisper in the man’s ear. “Somebody shoot this prick,” the shield cried out again. “Now, you’ve gone and hurt my feelings,” I say flatly, pulling the gun away from him, taking hasty aim and shooting the man on the furthest left in the chest, re-aiming at the man in the center, shooting him in the head, and moving to the man on the right and shooting him in the chest as well. I shook my head sadly. Like shooting ducks in a barrel. Rather pathetic ducks in a rather small barrel. A deep burning crawled up my right arm. I looked at it and saw the only man left drawing the blade of a knife against my forearm. My arm reacted naturally, causing me to drop the gun. I pushed away from the man with my left hand, sending him tumbling. The cloth covering my arm began to soak through with blood. The remaining guard looked at me and laughed. “Look,” he sneered. “Superman bleeds.” I snarled at the man, pulling my own blade out with my left arm. No more mister nice guy. I held the dual edged blade up for the man to see and he held up his own in response, taking a few test swipes at me. I smiled. He had no clue. Tossing the blade lightly in the air, I caught the knife by the tip of the blade. Pulling my arm back and down, I threw the knife at him in an underhand motion, the knife circling once across the small distance and burying it deep in his abdomen. He looked down at the knife handle sticking out of his body in shock, dropping to his knees. Walking over to where my Glock lay, I returned to the guard. He looked back up at me with sad wet eyes. “Amateur,” I said, shooting him once in the forehead. I looked at my arm, blood still flowing freely from it. Odd, I thought to myself. I probed it briefly with my free hand for a second, noting that it was actually fairly deep. Looking to the ground, I noted a rather significant sized pool of blood that had been accumulating at my feet. I breathed in deeply. Not good. I walked over to the control panels in the room. Buttons, buttons, so many buttons. I was looking over the mass array of the large panel when I heard the door open. “What the fuck!” someone exclaimed behind me. Christ, it’s always something isn’t it? I turned around just in time to see a burly man rush towards me, catching me in a football tackle, knocking me into the panel. The counter behind me kept me from falling but made my body contort in an odd angle. Pinning me to the counter, the man brought up his fist and slammed it into the cut on my arm. I screamed out in pain, lashing out. The man pulled his body back, raising his fist again. In the fraction of a second before he struck, I flashed my arm down, unsheathing a second knife and bringing it up, burying it deep on the underside of his jaw, the blade penetrating into his mouth. I pulled the knife out and struck at an angel parallel to the ground, sinking the deadly edge into his jugular. He stood looking at me for a moment, grasping at the collar of my shirt before falling off to the side, lifeless. For emphasis, I kick the lifeless man in the side. Feeling better, I pick back up my Glock and draw the second one from my back holster. I stand in the middle of the room, and circle slowly, both weapons up. I swear, another motherfucker pop up and I’ll blow this fucking building up. Hmmm, that’s a thought regardless if someone else pops up. I folded the image away and placed it in the nearest corner of my mind. Some food for thought, so to speak. I take a deep breath and wince. In fact, I find it very difficult to breath at the moment. Looking down at the burly man on the floor, I kick him once more for good measure. Fucker. I go back to the control panel. Let’s see, where was I before I was so rudely interrupted. Ah, there it is. I flick three red switches and look at the monitor next to them, watching the fans where Deac should be begin to power down. Speaking of D, where the hell is he? “Deac, ya there?” I ask, my chest throbbing with each word. I’ll need Doc to take a look at it. That might lead to an appointment with Veronica, the lovely masseuse. And that could all too well be fun. “HELL YE” I mean hell yeah I’m here, what’s taking you so damn long!” What tha hell have you been doing runnin? marathons and shit, why are you out of breath?” Deacon whispered excitedly into my earpiece. A panel next to the monitor lit up, displaying the words Fans will resume again in? Below that, a countdown began at thirty. Oh, this can’t be good. “Thirty seconds?” my voice came out ragged. Damn. “Thirty seconds!” What tha hell you talkin? bout John?” Deacon practically screamed into my earpiece. Damnit, kid, you need to be moving your ass. I swallowed hard, forcing air into my lungs, getting my breathing under control. “Thirty seconds, you’ve got thirty seconds to get through that ventilation duct before the blades start spinning again in there!” “FUCK!” was his only reply, which must’ve been some alarm to the counter which ticked down to twenty-nine. I watched the monitor and slowly began to realize something. Deacon was not in place. This is not good. He’s quick, but can he make it? Or better yet, if he doesn’t, can I stop the fans again without catching anyone’s eye? I look at the counter again. “Twenty seconds, Deac!” I yell into the earpiece. Still no Deacon in the monitor. Come on, D, you can do it. I have faith in you. I hear the door start to squeak open. Without pulling my eyes from the monitor, I fire three rounds at the center of the door. Seconds later, the satisfying sound of a body thumping to the ground fills my ears. “TEN SECONDS!” I scream again. In my head, the numbers yell out in time with the red flashing numbers on the panel. “FIVE SECONDS, D!” I watch as the blades begin to spin slowly, picking up speed with each pass. In a blur, I see Deacon flash across the screen and dive between to of the blades, ducking and rolling onto the other side, tumbling about before landing on his back. I stare at the monitor in disbelief. Little fucker made it. I slowly let out the deep breath that I didn’t know that I had been holding. “You still alive, Deac?” I took a large block of putty colored material that was equivalent to one cubic foot. Ahh, Semtex, man’s true best friend. In matters of explosives, I’ve had several people ask me how much to use, to which my answer has always been the same. “Remember, God hates a coward. “Yeah, something like that,” came the exhausted response from the other side of the complex. I watched as he patted himself down, making sure he had all the appropriate body parts. I watched as he stood up and walked out of the camera’s view but his voice still came to me clear as a bell. “Remind me to ask Capo for a raise when we get outta here.” I left the security room to the main hall. People came running down the short corridor. Damn, do these people ever get the hint? I raise both my Glocks and let loose a volley of bullets, dropping all of them. “What the hell are you talking about now Deac?” “A raise damnit! I almost got castrated in here a few minutes ago, I’m gettin” paid extra for this shit!” “I think between that computer you blew up and all the cigars you steal out of his office you two are even.” “What!” I didn’t even touch that computer, you’re the one who gave him that disc!” “But who stole it?” “Ya’ll told me steal it! Fuck this man, we’re switchin! Next week... I’m workin” with Hughes!” exclaims Deacon as he reaches the end of the tunnel. I laughed to myself. Time to leave this building before anyone else shows back up. I retrace my steps slide out the window, landing in a crouch, both weapons drawn, the pistol in my right hand pointing straight ahead, my left arm perpendicular to my right, bracing it. Satisfied that no one was around, I re-holstered both weapons, pulling off the black over shirt I was wearing and wrapping it around my wounded right arm. I pulled a Glock free with my left hand and waited for Deac’s signal. “Time to get ghost, John,” Holmes? whispered voice comes over my radio. Get Ghost? What in the flying blue fuck does that mean. When we get done with this, I’m gonna beat him with a dictionary. “I haven’t picked up that ebonics dictionary yet Deac, you’re gonna have to put a little more suburbs in it than that for me.” “Umm, how’s this... It’s time to get tha fuck outta here!” Holmes? yells in my ear, causing me to wince. “Oh, well why didn’t you say so” One distraction coming up...” I reply calmly, holstering my weapon and taking across the lot, heading towards the car. When I get up to speed, I reach into my pocket, pulling out a small black box with a red button in the center. Pressing down on the button, I can hear an artificial thunder fill the air as the ground began to shake. Behind me, the building exploded, glass shards flying in different directions, flames licking their way out and eating at debris. The heat from the blast and the cold from the rain felt good on my skin. Reaching the main gate, I leapt forward, pulling my body up and over, my arm screaming in protest. Going over the top of the gate, I let my body drop the remaining distance to the ground. Hugging the wall, I make my way over to Deacon’s car. Reaching the car, I wait in the shadows for Deacon to appear. Not a minute or so later, I see the car lights flash and Deacon emerges, keys dangling from his fingertips. We silently get into the Porsche, and Holmes cranks the engine. He looks at me imploringly, and I give him the nod. No sooner had I done it than we were off, engine racing, tires squealing in protest, with the rear-end fish tailing as we drove off into the night.
