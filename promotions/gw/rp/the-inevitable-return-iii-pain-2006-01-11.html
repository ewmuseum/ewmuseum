The Inevitable Return III: Pain

11 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/11/2006 not a model man not a saviour or a saint imperfect in a word make no mistake but I give you everything I have take me as I am... ---- Jackson’s lips curved up in the ghost of a smile. “I wouldn’t lie to you, Gabriel. Look in my eyes, and tell me that I don’t speak with veracity. Well” look.” He widened his eyes, saying nothing more, nor did he have to. Gabriel could see the love in Jackson’s eyes; he could see no hint of falsehood. He took a step back, and that’s when Jackson finally moved, bringing his right hand down, pressing the talisman in his palm against the dark blade. There was a sizzle, and a crackle of light, and then the blade fell from Gabriel’s hand as he shouted in pain. Jackson could smell burning flesh, and a moment later, as he pushed out into the night, he realized it was his own. The hot charm had burned against the inside of his right wrist, leaving a mark. The night air was cool on his skin as he hurried across the gravel, his walk rapidly becoming a full out run, and then a gallop. The tree, he was headed for the tree? the skies overhead opened up with a mighty rush of icy water, and he was soaked to the skin in seconds, freezing? ---- That was about where we left off last time, wasn’t it? I fear my memory, like everything else is growing a bit muddy as I watch another year draw to a close. So much has come to pass in these last twelve months, in the time of my exodus from Global. I have learned much about myself, and those around me. I have come to terms with my past, such as it was? and I have had the blessing of the greatest gift of all. Life. I have been given a second chance, and now, as the year draws to a close, I am left wondering if I will squander this rare opportunity like I have everything else, or will I finally be able to transcend the boundaries that conspire to hold me back? But the greatest gift of all was love. Love of the woman who had chosen me? love of the beautiful creature who was even now sleeping soundly beside me, the bulge of her belly visible beneath the sheets. My child lay there, nestled beneath her strong, loving heart? and the thought of it filled me with a wondrous ache even as I extricated myself from her slumbering embrace. I limped over to the window, peering out at the moonlit night, at the silver dappled new fallen snow. She didn’t know, as she slept, that the world outside was still conspiring against our happiness? Gabriel was still out there somewhere, I was positive, and he would stop at nothing to reclaim the power I had stripped from him. The burn on my wrist tingled, it was still pink, still raised, a perfect imprint of the necromancer’s talisman, a twin to match the knot work on my other wrist. A symbol of the past, which I wore to this day; I could have it removed, but it served better as a reminder of the man I had once been? and if I lost sight of my purpose, the man I could very well become again... ---- FLASHBACK’Saturday, November 13, 2004 0603 hours, local time CID Safehouse, Amsterdam, Netherlands ---- The sky was that pre dawn shade of indigo, the self same shade as the man’s eyes, as he bent to the task, pulling the pegs from the frozen earth with nothing short of a Herculean effort. He could feel the muscles straining, he could feel a twinge in his back, a reminder of the last injury that should have left him dead. “I just love secret societies,” the words fell past his lips, almost unnoticed as he set to work removing the second peg, this one more stubborn than the last, “the games, the pomposity and the egos. It’s nearly as beneficial to civilization as organized religion. The piety, the pageantry, the zealotry” and really, who can forget its puritanical impact on society. Anyone up for a crusade? Who shall we burn with impunity at the stake today?” “I am so sick of all this medieval bull-? the stake came free of the earth with a sudden pop, spilling him backwards where he stumbled against the still form of Bruce McLeod, landing awkwardly on his rear in the mud. “Shit,” he muttered, stopping his mindless task long enough to inspect the damage. McLeod was ashen, his skin gone cold and waxen, it was rather disgusting to see him like this, naked and incapacitated. He found his gaze drawn to McLeod’s less than impressive family jewels, which lay shriveled against his body, a testament to the wintry cold. He dragged himself to his knees, rocking forward, letting the metal stake fall from his numbed fingers. Jackson reached out with a finger, touching the ragged gashes on McLeod’s chest that still trickled sluggishly with the rapidly cooling blood. He remembered the dream, and the crow, and for a moment, he was transported to someplace else. It was an eerie feeling, almost an out of body experience of sorts, but for a long span of seconds, he wasn’t aware of the numbing cold, or the misty, almost freezing rain that soaked him to the skin. The water rolled off his leather jacket, pooling on the dead man’s chest, washing away the clots of blood in pinkish trails. He likely would have remained like that for a long time, letting the final silence of the barren field spin out around him, amid the rain, and the blood and the mud, if not for the sudden impact that slammed into his shoulder blade, spinning him around. Pain sizzled through his nerve endings, making him gasp just as he hit the ground, just in time for a mouthful of muddy water. His hands sank into the mud, sending more fire through his shoulder, as he tried to pull himself upright, coughing and sputtering. The barbed point of a metal arrow protruded from his chest, sticky blood already running down his jacket. He looked down at the nuisance, and would have laughed at the absurdity, if not for the anger welling inside him. “Bastard!” He bellowed, dragging himself to his feet, albeit rather unsteadily. “Do you have any idea how much this jacket cost” Likely more than the pittance you make serving these foolish masters! Show yourself, fool.” He reached around behind himself, feeling the pain of the torn muscles as he raised his arm. He seized the feathered shaft, and pulled upwards, quickly, and before he could reconsider the action, pulling out the arrow. Blood gushed from the ragged hole anew, and he sagged to his knees, the blood spattering on McLeod’s face as his palm slammed into the dead man’s forehead, leaving an imprint of the talisman that still dangled from the chain twisted around his fingers. His arm hung like a dead weight, heavy, and he was unable to move it, or remove his hand from the feel of that cold dead flesh, which suddenly seemed not to be cold at all. He squinted at the shadows, trying to see past the silvery rain, seeing nothing but empty space, partially obscured by the sodden hair hanging over his eyes. “Show yourself. Don’t make me have to get up” if I have to come looking for you, I will be very, very put out!” For a long span, there was no sound other than his ragged breathing, and the hiss of the rain, and then there was an unmistakable whizzing sound as another arrow sailed past his head, lodging itself in the barren tree. He turned, looking at the arrow, watching it quiver, and then a voice came to his ears on the wind. “I didn’t have to miss.” The threat was implied, the bait there in the open, but Jackson refused to rise to it, instead sighing raggedly as he raked his sodden hair out of his eyes, leaving a smear of blood on his forehead like bizarre war paint. “I believe you,” he muttered through clenched teeth, trying to ignore the pain that was still throbbing in his torn chest, “you didn’t miss the first time, although right now I wish you would have.” “If wishes were horses” ah, well, I’m sure we both know how that ends don’t we, Jackson?” The voice was mangled, broken and garbled, as though the words were being torn unbidden from the speaker’s throat. Yet, still, all Jackson could see was the infernal rain, a mist really. “Show yourself.” It was meant to be a command, and if not for the whimper attached to the second word, it may well have been. His assailant chuckled, the sound equally as pained as his previous speech, and the wind kicked up with it, sending the heavy rain in Jackson’s face, each drop like a tiny needle piercing his numbed skin. “You don’t need to see to believe, friend” believe this, your life is now in my hands. My bow is steady, another arrow is already loaded, pointed at your heart right now? ah, even now, I can see the pulse racing, I can almost hear it. Lub-dub-lub-dub-lub-dub-lub-dub? the gallop of a horse running for his life?” ---- Lyrics credit: “Model Man” by King Crimson
