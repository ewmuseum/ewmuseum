Christmas Hell

29 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12-29-03 Silent night, holy night! All is calm, all is bright. Round yon Virgin, Mother and Child. Holy infant so tender and mild, Sleep in heavenly peace, Sleep in heavenly peace. Silent night, holy night! Shepherds quake at the sight. Glories stream from heaven afar Heavenly hosts sing Alleluia, Christ the Savior is born! Christ the Savior is born. Silent night, holy night! Son of God love's pure light. Radiant beams from Thy holy face With dawn of redeeming grace, Jesus Lord, at Thy birth. Jesus Lord, at Thy birth. ‘It had always been that time of year for me. I’d help set up the Christmas tree as I ate a cookie or two that I’d made. It was always lonely. Seeing how I had no family I had to do whatever was best for me, and that was to enjoy what I had.’ Walking into the kitchen Jesse grabbed the box of ornaments he’d brought up from the basement. As he opened it in the living room he showed a beautiful display of lights, bulbs, and other ornaments he’d collected over the years. From red, blue, white, all the way down to yellow, there was a rainbow of colors to choose from of bulbs. Jesse first looped the tree with 3 strands of lights, not wanting to make it look too bright, each set had a different color flow and some blinked, as others danced from bulb to bulb. Next came the bulbs. As he let the tree rotate on its post he put two or three of each color bulbs on certain spots of the tree. Each one with there own special shine, it was quite remarkable to see such a beautiful tree yet still not done. As Jesse went to put on the next set of ornaments, the phone rang. It was defiantly not something of Jesse’s spirit to not answer the phone joyfully on times like these. Picking up the phone Jesse answered. “Merry Christmas, this is Jesse, who am I speaking to?” joyfully states Jesse. “This is Julius, your cousin Jesse. How are you?” responds Julius who seems to be happy too. “Not too bad there cuz, how about yourself? How’s the family?” answers Jesse. “Oh not too bad, we just put up the tree yesterday, and today Samantha drew this big picture for the fridge. So not bad at all,” claimed Julius. “That’s great to hear. I’m putting up the tree right now. Just got done putting the bulbs on, and now I’m stringing the popcorn. Not to be rude, but why’d you call cuz?” questioned Jesse. “Well see, I was invited to a family gathering of our aunts and uncles, and well I wanted to see if you’d come along. I’m younger than you, and don’t know all of our relatives, so was wondering if you could come and spend some time with the family like old times. How about it?” Julius asked. ”I… uh, I guess. Where is it, and when do I have to be there?” muttered Jesse now not too happy. “My place, tomorrow night at eight o’clock. I have to go, see you tomorrow cuz!” Julius boasted as he hung up the phone in a hurry. So Jesse was to go to his cousins for a party and introduce Julius to all his uncles and aunts. *** *** *** The next day Jesse appeared at his cousins about five till eight, expecting to be the only one there. He was quickly proven wrong when this large jolly man with a gray beard and a baldhead, who seemed to be in his fifties, opened the door and shook his hand saying “Merry Christmas.” The man was just the first. For in the room we entered was a large living room with four couches and a big screen TV, along with a coffee table in the middle. Julius, his wife, and daughter sat on the one couch, as the uncle and his wife sat on another. A younger man, probably about 30 was sitting on the middle couch facing the big screen TV that had a football game on. Jesse walked in and shook the hands of Julius, and hugged his wife and child Samantha, and then went over to the wife of the uncle and hugged her, who was his aunt. Jesse sat down by the young man who he had not recognized yet but would soon know. “Hello, I’m Mark,” stated the young man with a grim face that seemed to be between happy and saddened. “I’m Jesse,” responded Jesse as he shook his hand. “I remember you! Oh my, you’ve grown so much dear, how have you been?” stated the aunt. “I’ve been great aunt Marilyn! How about yourself?” replied Jesse with a smile. “Oh you know us, living life to the fullest! We just bought a big condo down in the Bahamas, and now were just looking for a nice new car,” she boasts being the rich woman she is. Jesse looked around and noticed that the family was there. There family was quite the small one, not many would come, and in all you may have some unexpected friends, but it was unlikely. Jesse knew it was not something big, but since his parents were not alive, nor his great uncle or aunt, along with his parent’s parents, then it’d always be a small gathering of any coarse. As the family talked Julius’ wife went into the kitchen with Marilyn to set up the dinner as Mark, Julius, and the uncle talked about football. The women came back out about thirty minutes later and said dinner was ready. The seven went into the kitchen and sat down, saying they’re blessing, and then beginning to eat. Part way through the dinner the discussion of professions came up. “So what profession are you in Julius?” Uncle John questioned Julius. “Oh, I’m working the an eight hour shift down at the construction place in the middle of the city. It’s a part time job for now, but it pays well. How about you?” replied Julius not disgusted with his profession at all and proud to be an honest workingman. “I am a stock broker. Just wondering, how’d you stumble upon this beauty? Surely it wasn’t with no construction job eh?” boasted Uncle John with a smirk. “Oh, Jesse here got be a job with him at the company Lights Out Wrestli-“ Julius began but was cut off. “WRESTLING!!! No you didn’t! I knew it, oh I told you John, this boy has no respect for his grand father nor does his cousin Jesse! Oh you two should be disappointed in yourselves!” yelled Marilyn as she stood up and looked over the table now frustrated and angered. Jesse stood up and walked away from the dining room. He was not going to listen to this. For many of years had he suffered the torture of being just another wrestler, and just because his real father and grandfather had died after internal injuries sustained over there years as professional wrestlers everyone thought it was the devils work. His aunt and uncle had forbidden there children who now lived in other parts of the country from ever watching it, and told them it was a sin, as they were proud Christians. Jesse however cared little for this curse he supposed was upon the family, for he was a wrestler, and it was all he had. He didn’t care if life miss-treated him, he LOVED to wrestle, and this was unfair of his aunt to judge his career choice upon a grandfather who had passed away from a lung collapse twelve years after he retired from wrestling, or his father who’d died from natural causes only seven years after quitting because of a knee injury. Jesse would not condone it. His aunt turned to him and grabbed him by the shirt and told him not to walk away from her, but Jesse kept walking for she was not his mother, and would not be. Julius hurried out of the room and reached Jesse before he had left the house. “Hey! Why are you leaving? You know she’s only worried about us right?” stated Julius trying to get Jesse to stay. “Julius, I love you cuz, but this, this is why I don’t invite family over. I chose professional wrestling as my life goal, and now, just because a man died whom I never knew because he wrestled, don’t give her the right to condone what I do. I love my job cuz, and I will NEVER quit doing it. Now leave me alone, I’m going home where I belong. Merry Christmas to you, Melanie, and Samantha, I’ll see you sometime this week and drop off your gifts,” muttered Jesse trying to keep down his voice from being heard in the kitchen and then turned around and left through the front door. Jesse walked to the street corner and caught a cab home, as he would enjoy his Christmas from there. In the cab, we see Jesse pulling a wallet from his jeans pocket, and there he looks at a picture of his grandfather and father. Tears appears slightly running down the corner slits of his eyes, for Christmas will never be the same without these people. (Fades)
