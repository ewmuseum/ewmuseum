And Then The World Changed

1 September 2005

Date: Various Dallas, Texas Thursday, September 1st The heat hadn’t let up. For a brief instant, the change in weather from the gulf had covered the serene summer sky with dark clouds threatening, but then the gathering storm dissipated with the same sudden speed it had appeared. The weather may not have changed Texas, but the vision of nature and the psyche of the citizens had been deeply affected by the changes. Nothing would ever remain the same. Originally, tonight’s charity event was another Habitat For Humanity function. The Dallas Symphony Orchestra was pairing up with a Bachelor Auction to raise funds for the charity. The focus had switched, suddenly and radically when their neighbors to the East had been ravaged. So at the last minute, all of the proceeds and plans were redirected to raise money for the victims of Hurricane Katrina. They had even gotten together at the last moment to organize a Silent Auction in addition to the Bachelor Auction. The event would raise a lot of money for a worthy cause. For the event Jake had arranged to take one of the society darlings out for the evening. Veronica Garcia was widowed when her husband died in a tragic accident four years earlier. Now, she was a single mother who still made all of the rounds on the Dallas social scene. Her parents were wealthy with an estimated family fortune over one hundred million dollars. Her husband’s parents were worth nearly half a billion dollars, and she had the only grandchild of both families. Veronica was a highly sought after woman to date, but for Jake she was an old friend with occasional benefits. Dressed to impress, his black tuxedo featured a banded collar instead of some silly bow tie. Veronica looked stunning in an arctic blue dress that clung to her curvaceous body. Jake felt lucky to have her on his arm for the evening. They were inside the limousine, holding hands. “You know, I hear some really great things about what you’ve been doing to organize this.” Veronica said as she stroked his hand affectionately. Jake smiled at her, “People forget money only goes so far. You still need to push things along, work hard and organize to get anything accomplished. We’ll do our part money-wise tonight though.” “I’m sure we will. There are plenty of items to bid on, and even bachelors such as yourself.” She laughed. “Quit teasing me!” He protested. Veronica kept laughing, “Are you kidding me” When they make you take the catwalk down the stage I’m bribing the video guy to get me a copy!” “You think that bothers me?” Jake shrugged. “I do it every time I head to the octagon.” “Not with all the old women bidding for that special night with the Big Dawg!” She laughed again. “Next time we have a Bachelorette Auction I’m getting revenge.” Jake looked out the window. “What’s bothering you?” “I haven’t heard from everyone yet. I’m just worried some of my family and friends could still be stuck in New Orleans.” Jake sighed. “I’m sure it will be okay, but I still keep thinking something might be wrong.” “I’ll keep them and you in my prayers.” Veronica offered. Jake wondered how she would react to some of his actions as of late. Jake wondered why had ultimately decided to change course in his career. For a long time, he had longed to hear the fans cheer for him, chant his name and bark the familiar refrain he had come to enjoy. Then, when all of it had finally come to him he didn’t enjoy it like he thought he would. The fans outpouring of emotion for him seemed somehow cold and shallow when he remembered every moment that he was facing a larger crisis. The adoration of the public was not what he thought it would be, and he found himself having to act more and more instead of being himself. Jake had come to realize that the selfish man inside was about his solitude. He had never enjoyed close attachments, and that was so he never came close to disappointing anyone, especially himself. He had friends and acquaintances, but very few close friends. Those friends he had kept at arms length, until recently. Now, knowing that they could be in danger he couldn’t control he realized he had made a mistake. Sending them away, exiling them as he had, is what had put them in the greatest danger of all. He needed to ensure they were safe. The calls to Donna had so far gone unanswered, but he would bet money that was because she was working on the problem. She was the kind of woman who wouldn’t get back in touch until she knew something about his family. He shook those thoughts out of his head for the moment. The evening was also the perfect cover for a meeting with his contacts. They would be dressed up for a change, and something about them in monkey suits amused him greatly. Stephen had arranged everything, and Mr. Sensational himself had donated several items to the auction. They would all meet to determine the next steps to take in the battle against his brother. The limousine pulled in front of the Meyerson Symphony Center where the red carpet waited on them. One of the greeters opened the door, and Jake stepped out in his customer made Armani tuxedo. He reached into the limousine and gave Veronica a helping hand out of the car. As Veronica ran her arm through Jake’s the cameras flashed all around the red carpet. They began walking forward, and stepped to the side where Alexa Conomos from ABC waited on them. They stepped forward to answer her questions. “Jake and Veronica! Is this a new power couple in Dallas?” Alexa asked. “We’re good friends, and we don’t like going to these events alone.” Veronica answered with a big smile. Her lovely face absolutely beamed framed by her auburn hair and under the bright lights of the television crew. Alexa shook her head, “And I thought I had a scoop!” “I thought I had more than a friendly outing!” Jake joked. They all shared a laugh. “Jake, I understand you’re going to be one of the bachelor’s on the auction block this evening.” Alexa locked her big, brown eyes on him. “Absolutely Alexa!” Jake nodded. “I was ready to do my part to help Habitat For Humanity, but now we see an even greater need. In addition to tonight, the Hix family is donating all profits for the next week from our various business interests to the Red Cross Hurricane Relief Fund. That includes all of our manufacturing, production, and entertainment businesses. We want to be sure we are doing everything we can to help in this situation.” Alexa nodded, “Now I also understand you opened a shelter for evacuees?” “Yes. We have been working to covert the downtown warehouse to a new nightclub and retail center. We’ve halted that work temporarily to move in one hundred beds to help with relief efforts. We’ll be providing food, clothing and support to the families we house until more permanent arrangements can be made.” Jake explained. “In addition, my family will be matching the donation made by the Hix Corporations. Together, we’re going to make a difference for a lot of families affected by Katrina.” Veronica added. Jake didn’t react visibly, but the move wasn’t unexpected. She wouldn’t let him get all of the glory here. “That’s fantastic!” Alexa was impressed. “Enjoy the party!” “Thanks Alexa.” “Thanks.” They moved on down the red carpet. “Trying to outshine me?” Jake joked. Veronica leaned her head onto his shoulder, “Just supporting the cause.” They posed for more photographs and then they continued on down the red carpet. Several interviews later they were almost done, when someone motioned them over. Veronica had no clue who the man in the bad suit was, but Jake recognized him quite easily as always. Jake walked Veronica over to the man, “Chuck, surprising to see you here.” “I go where the story is.” Chuck smiled. “Who is this lovely woman on your arm this evening Big Dawg?” “Reno, this is Veronica Garcia. Veronica, this is reporter who likes to harass me over and over again all the time.” Jake’s stiffness kept a good distance between Reno and Veronica. “What can I help you with Reno?” “I wanted to talk about what you did on the last Evolution.” Reno replied, all smiles for anyone else looking on from the sides. “Chuck, I’ll be happy to discuss whatever you want, but tonight isn’t about Global. Tonight is about the millions of people ravaged by the hurricane. Surely you understand that?” Jake had turned it right back around on Chuck. He liked watching the reporter sweat it out for a change. Veronica watched on enjoying the interplay. “Well, of course tonight is about?” “That’s good of you Chuck. Now, call my office and we can arrange time to discuss those events.” Jake led Veronica up the stairs to the doors. Dallas was not always recognized as a city of the arts, thanks largely to the fact that many people had difficulty believing that Texas could support such an environment. Of course, they could not have been more wrong. In 1979, the citizens of Dallas voted in a city wide election to set aside 60 acres in downtown Dallas in which to concentrate the city’s arts and cultural treasures, and an architectural master plan was developed for the Dallas Arts District. As part of this movement, Morton H. Meyerson chaired a committee charged with developing a building program and selection of an architect and acoustician. Through the generosity of the Borden Company, several parcels of land were donated to the Dallas Symphony Association. The land and generous individual and corporate donations made it possible for the design to be established on a prime site within one of the busiest areas of the city. So as they walked into the center, the sweeping, gallant architecture and warm woods welcomed them like no other building in the city. A small string quartet in the corner composed of the back-up members of the orchestra greeted them. Jake felt some of the tension leave him. The center had been designed to relax and calm so you could better enjoy the music. Jake leaned into Veronica, “I need to go and attend to my meeting.” “What was it again?” Veronica asked, as her smile didn’t waiver at all. “Just some last minute details of the new auction. We put it together so quickly we have to make sure everything is perfect.” Jake smiled as Veronica introduced him to one of the city council members. Jake grabbed two flutes of champagne from a passing tray. He handed one to Veronica, “I promise to get back as quickly as possible.” “I’ll be fine, go.” She pushed him lightly on the shoulder. Jake nodded as he walked away. He dropped his untouched champagne on an empty tray as he headed toward the elevators. He saw Stephen there already, waiting for him. Stephen had gone for a traditional tuxedo, although a designer brand tailored to fit his body perfectly. “Just got here?” Stephen asked. “Traffic for the red carpet and such.” Jake smiled. Stephen shook his head slowly at him, “Sure, and I bet Veronica has nothing to do with it.” “A gentleman never tells.” Jake laughed as he pressed the elevator button. “Are Robert and Veston here already?” “Upstairs in the room. Veston looks very, very uncomfortable in the tuxedo. To be honest, I am enjoying watching him look uncomfortable. Robert had some trouble getting in because he’s been gone for such a long time, and a lot of the people downstairs recognized him.” Stephen explained while they waited. The elevator doors open, and they stepped inside and proceeded up into the high levels of the building. “Did they give you any idea of what’s going on?” Jake leaned against the wall of the elevator. Stephen laughed a little, “They don’t seem to trust me yet Jake. We’ll know soon enough. I made sure the conference room would not be disturbed.” “Good.” Stephen and Jake nodded at the two security guards as they proceeded through the glass doors. They walked into the large conference room and saw Robert seated comfortably in the high back chair while Veston paced around like a caged animal. “Jake, good to see you again.” Robert said with a smile. He looked better, no more uncertainty or paranoia in his demeanor. The tuxedo had done wonders for his confidence once again. “You too, and you look better than the last time we met. Veston, you look ready to snap.” Jake walked over to the hulking man who seemed completely ill at ease in this environment. “What’s the matter?” Veston pulled his tuxedo jacket off, “I don’t like restrictive clothes.” Jake watched as the bow tie followed on top of the jacket. Veston unbuttoned half the shirt and fanned it to get some airflow. “We won’t have a whole lot of time here, so let’s go over what you two have developed. The message said it was urgent.” Jake took a seat with Stephen right next to him. Veston eyed Stephen nervously, “What about him?” “I trust him, and that’s good enough for you.” Jake shot Veston a hard look. He knew better than to challenge a Hix when they were in that mode. Veston shook off the nerves he was feeling, “We have three fourths of what we need. There are instructions, revealed to people all over the world. We have the book from Europe you got in Germany, we have the book of America from the bayou and we have always had the African tomes.” Robert took over, “The books are the key to victory. The story drove each writer to madness, and together they tell the story of how to defeat the beast within your brother. If we can get them all together, we should be able to find the information and translate them. We don’t know where it will lead, but it will send us on the right path.” “Where is the final book?” Stephen asked. He was not going to be a wallflower for this operation. “We don’t know.” Veston took control again. “China was the fourth region where it was revealed. To find that book, we must work through some very ancient and powerful organizations. They are not necessarily friendly to our cause, and we do not know where the book is being held. We have to begin with a friend, and then figure out where we can pursue the book.” “That means we will be going into an environment where they do not trust outsiders. They especially will not trust someone who isn’t respectful of their culture.” Veston began explaining the issues. Jake was rubbing the side of his head. There was a small, sharp pain that seemed to come out of nowhere. He tried to shake it off as he listened to the explanation, and began trying to formulate some ideas of his own. The location of their exile had been carefully selected. The ground was holy ground, sanctified by the Lord’s presence and blessing. Now, that location had been covered in all manner of foulness. The graveyard would have been destroyed, and bodies were floating in the water even now. The sanctity of what had once been was gone, and it would never return again. Everything in his head was swirling around dangerously as he tried to make sense of the world suddenly gone mad. The hurricane seemed like the final spur to push him back out into the open. Living the life of a refugee was never his choice, but it seemed smarter to protect his family and friends. Now, with the death of his family, the idea of concealment protecting everyone he loved didn’t seem to make sense anymore. Some deep, dark part of his mind made him wonder if he had brought the punishment of the hurricane on. Of course, he dismissed that as arrogance. Finally, he was running from Sid and the rest of the world while trying to stay ahead of the destruction in the wake of the hurricane. He had been thinking everything through, and he wondered what would happen if he returned to public life? Sid would try to kill him surely, but he was trying to kill him now. Upheaval in the world was just matching up to the upheaval he felt inside. Bella was the once concern he couldn’t get over. If anything ever happened to her, he didn’t know if he could ever recover from that tragedy. He had agreed to the running, the hiding and accepting the hideout by always thinking about the woman he loved. In protecting her in this way, he began to realize he was losing his own identity. The same identity that had allowed him to battle through the forces contained within his flesh to emerge as a new being. If I turn my back on who I am, then do I even deserve to be the chosen one anymore? The old sayings were all coming back to haunt him. He had played the part, and now he was being beaten by the truth. He was the Fallen Angel. He had fallen down from heaven, and now he had a choice to make. Sid laughed, “Our relief efforts will be appreciated when we bring food and water Malum. Go in with that purpose to the world, and drag him back screaming.”
