Living the Lie, Part I: Family Tradition

19 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 19, 2006 Dear Kaden, How is everything over there in America? I hope it's everything I remember leaving oh so many years ago. I'm glad you took my advice to make your home in Michigan. It's such a wonderful place, and I'm sure you'll just love it. Your father hasn't been the same since you left. He's been spending all day out and about, doing God knows what. That's normal, but the funny thing is, he's been coming home and being the friendliest person. It actually feels like he loves me again. Much like when I first met him. We both miss you, very much, despite how your father acts towards you moving away. I know he was up to no good with your real wrestling the past few years and all, and he's upset that you are gone, but you are still his son, and he has every right to miss you. Besides catching up with you, this letter serves another purpose. Every year my side of the family gets together there in Michigan. The Jones Family Reunion. I enclosed the invitation with this letter. I figured I'd send it to you, just in case you might want to go and see what the other half of your family is like. Not sure how much you'll want to go after hearing all those things I've told you about them and all, but I sent you the invitation anyway. I suppose I'll let this be my goodbye as well Christopher. I hope your professional wrestling career is going just fine. I'd watch you on TV if I could, but I'm not sure we get anything like that here, and I wouldn't want your father seeing anyway. I love you very much, and I hope to hear from you soon. Love, Mom -------------------------------------- 'Ladies and Gentlemen, if I could have all of your attention for a moment.' An older lady said, at the top of her lungs, to the rest of the crowd that ha piled into this old looking community building. 'I know we all just got done eating, and everyone wants to talk, but just for a few minutes can I have your attention?' She said again as the crowd finally began to hush. 'Thank you all.' The lady said in appreciation. Now I was just sitting there, minding my own business, in the corner of the building. It was fairly cold inside, even with the noise the heater was making, it wasn't doing much good. I had showed up, just to see what the other half of my family was like. What was going to happen between now, and well, the time I left this place, wasn't expected. 'This better not be some family group game like you planned last year Martha. It about killed poor old Vern back there.' An older man said from the middle of the crowd, which was followed by the entire crowd laughing in unison. A smile came to my face without even knowing what had gone on, but I imagined it in my head. The older lady at the other end had shook her head, though, and continued. 'Now now everyone. I know that was all a big mistake last year, but it sounded fun at first. But that's beside the point. I'm speaking before you today because of one name that appears on this year's Jones Family Reunion guest book.' She says as she holds up the guest register, and points to a name that surely can't be seen by anyone else in the room. The silence of the crowd grew a little, obviously people thinking there might be someone here that doesn't belong. At this point in time, I was unaware of what was about to happen, I wish I had braced myself. 'Do we have an imposter?' One woman spoke out. The lady, Martha I supposed, shook her head, and the smile on her face grew even bigger. 'No, in fact this is good news to us this year. Let me read you the name signed here.' She announced as she grabbed the glasses that were hanging by a string around her neck, and placed them upon her face. 'The signature reads, Christopher K. Calix.' She said it, and then removed her glasses and brought the guest book down to her side. The gentle roar from earlier was nothing compared to this news. Then again, I wasn't paying too close attention, and almost had half a notion to ask who Christopher Calix was before it hit me. 'Oh shit!' I blurted aloud, without realizing. Everyone in the room turned their attention to me. 'Well everybody, there he is. Shall we get him up here to say a few words?' Martha had asked the family. The noise was too much to understand what anyone had said, but it didn't take long for a random family member, that I didn’t know, to grab my arm, and drag me up in front of about eighty people I had never met before. 'It's good to see that no good Jones, Mary, finally allowed someone to come over and visit the family.' Martha said, and my mouth instantly dropped. 'What was that? My mom has wanted to come back for...' I tried to stick up for her, but was cut off. I wasn't even sure where this had all come from. Martha had shaken her head, and the rest of the 'family' had quieted themselves. 'She's got you brainwashed, doesn't she? It's a wonder you made it out of her grasps after all these years. Your poor father puts up with so much.' She said, and everybody seemed to agree with her. I shook my head, and it felt as if my jaw hit the floor. 'You all have to be kidding me right? My mom is the reason I'm here.' I said in response, hoping to straighten all of this out. 'It's my father who never wanted to let me do anything for myself. He was the same way with Mom.' I continued. 'That's hardly believable.' One young man said from the crowd. Martha, who I turned my attention back to, was nodding her head in agreement before starting up. 'Your mom left the entire family for Greece, to never return. It was always said that Alec was more than happy to come and visit, but she just never wanted to see any of her relatives again. Your father is such a nice man, I can hardly make sense of him being the reason we haven't seen her in years. Or in that matter, seen you for that matter.' She finished before planting a wet one on my left cheek. I just looked around as the rest of the 'family' nodded their heads in agreement. It seemed futile to argue with them, as it was obvious the rest of the family on this side was as set in their ways and stubborn as Mom can be at time. I shrugged my shoulders, and tried walking off. That wasn't going to happen, though, as Martha saw it, and she grabbed one of my shoulders and turned me around. 'Yes?' I asked her. She smiled at me, then to the 'family', and then back to me. 'You aren't going away without telling the rest of the family how your life has been.' The 'family' responded as if they wanted to know as well. 'So what brings you to Michigan for the first time, Chris?' She asked. I looked around, and shook my head. 'You can call me Kaden.' I said to Martha, with sort of a sarcastic tone in my voice. I wasn't very happy with them believing that my mom was the one who never wanted to come here. 'I'm here because I got a contract to...' I started to say, before thinking that if they were in love with my father so much, they might just run and say why I was actually here. I couldn't let him find out; he'd hunt me down and kill me. I had to live with the lie I told him before I left Athens. Mom could be the only one who knows. 'I signed a contract with a sponsored wrestling team here in America. They travel all 50 states competing in tournaments and all. Every 4 years, they send one or two of their best wrestlers to earn spots on the Olympic team for the US.' I said, telling them the same lie I told Alec, only with a small twist in there. Then before anyone else could ask a question, I continued the lie some more. 'Only, I think that if I were to make it that far, I'd go back to Athens and make it onto the Greek Olympic team, or something like that.' I said, throwing some icing on the cake, figuring they all thought that it was my dream, not my fathers, to be on the Olympic team for Greece. The 'family' began chatting back and fourth, about me I'm sure, after I finished. I wasn't really comfortable being there anymore either, and I felt as if it was time to leave, so I started to walk away. Martha grabbed me again. 'Chri...' She cleared her throat. 'Kaden, where do you think you are going?' She asked. I threw my shoulder away from her grasped hand. 'I have to get going. I've got more important business to tend to today. I just stopped by for some free food, and to see what my family over here was like.' I finished and started walking away. Nobody was taking the hint that I didn't want to be there, or that I was upset in anyway. I heard one woman ask on the way out. 'Well, what did you think of this side of the family?' I shook my head, and before I exited the building, I stood in the front doorway, door wide open, and answered. 'Can't say I'm very impressed. Reminds me a lot of my father.' I said as I walked outside. I breathed in deep, and exhaled before taking my first steps into the parking lot to find my rent-a-car. I could hear the 'family' inside almost explode as I left. It was a great feeling.
