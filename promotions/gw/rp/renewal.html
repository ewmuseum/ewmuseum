Date: Various Present Day Texas Hill Country I miss the winter. I miss the cold breeze, and roaring fires. Such thoughts graced his mind as the fragrant blossoms of the wild flower farms penetrated his senses. The hot, arid wind graced his skin, drying the sweat from his body even as the incessant blaze of the Texas sun beat down upon his exposed flesh. Touring the southern hill country in his motorcycle, he had chosen a to go shirtless to enjoy the feeling of the inferno on his skin. The tan he normally had would become deep, perhaps even turn to burn because he failed to protect himself. Black hair whipped in the wind behind him like the passing of shadows into light. Alone, he ran through the events of the last few weeks in his mind. He had never felt the emotions roiling through his body. Jake was always the prodigal son. He had gone off, partied with wild women, drunk away half his nights and always returned home to be welcomed by the rest of the family with open arms. Now, the tides had moved out to sea carrying the safety of his home away into nothingness. He pulled off to a little corner store for gas. Filling up, he grabbed a small nectarine from the roadside fruit stand. The sweet juice ran down his chain. Slightly refreshed, he pushed on into the lingering day. The southern hill country was well known in Texas, and by a few people outside of Texas, for the bed and breakfast traffic, wineries and wild flower farms. For Jake, the break after the Crossfire event in Germany was to clear his mind. The loss of family, Sid’s agents stalking him across the Atlantic to a new continent, and his strange encounters with the vampire Corvus all required careful consideration. Of course, after driving for seven hours on black highways poorly maintained by the Texas Department Of Transportation he had done just about all the thinking he wanted. As the dusk descended down onto the day he pulled off the main roadway into one of the side roads of concrete poured over the countryside. The roads were poor, but they worked as they needed to work. Through a winding course he knew well, the asphalt turned to dirt roads. He followed almost two miles of dirt before turning into the Rose Hill Ranch. The motorcycle climbed up the slightly winding gravel road to the main house. He pulled around back and parked beneath the covered awning by the back door. Pat came walking out from inside in a sunflower dress. A portly woman with curly brunette hair and a permanently gregarious smile, she looked him up and down. “Shirtless! Now is that any way to come into my house!”!” Jake laughed. He pulled out the button-up shirt from the small bundle strapped to the back of his motorcycle. He showed the shirt to pat, “I have a shirt, and I haven’t entered your house yet.” Jake began pulling the button-up shirt on. Pat waited until it was on before he went and hugged him around the chest. “It’s been too long since you’ve come back Jake. We’ve been watching you on the satellite.” “Yeah, at least I’m on television again, huh?” Jake was all smiles as Pat led him inside. “You’re room is ready, and we’re still cooking. The chef cooks up some amazing dishes. Do you want to come and get something to eat” We just got a new shipment from all the wineries.” Pat tempted him. Pat nodded, “Sure, let’s get something to eat.” “Inside or outside?” “Are the tables set-up on the balcony again?” “Sure.” Pat knew he would like that. “I’ll find the empty spot.” He assured her. “I trust you to pick me the right meal and wine for the occasion.” The nighttime had cooled down slightly, and he enjoyed the weather he was left with. After a few minutes, Pat brought him a plate of caramelized Alaskan prawns. She also had a large wine glass and a bottle from one of the local vineyards. She set the plate down and filled his glass. The US Airways flight was on time, and the Airbus A330 had ensured the flight was comfortable. The two first class tickets they purchased, and the luck still blessing them, ensured they got the separated two seats with the curtain to cut them off from the rest of the plane. Following his saving grace thanks to Jake and Pitt, he had fled the country with Bella as an exile for his own safety. They had moved around Asia and Europe, treating it like a long vacation. Living like a fugitive sometimes, his whole frame of mind had changed. He contained dark memories of Sid and the well intentioned, but dark memories of Seth. He had a fountain of evil knowledge, and he worked to ignore it at all costs. Unfortunately, ignoring such information was not always easy. Living the lifestyle he watched everything around him, and always had three thoughts running through his mind in every situation. How could he protect himself and Bella? How could he use the situation to his advantage? And the one thought he never liked’how could he kill those around him for their souls? The thoughts crept in because his brain was worked a little differently. Luckily, he always dismissed the idea immediately, but the train of thought was like a maggot always digging through his flesh to escape. Physically, he had been able to stay in shape by following the self-workout routines he always used. The Dozens were highly effective on the road. His long hair was always worn loose, along with the full beard to better conceal himself from the rest of the world. He was still ripped, ready and dangerous when the need arose’like it had in Milan and Paris. A black tattoo from Kyoto graced his right bicep now. A reminder of the new beginning he held tightly to now. The Kanji meant hope, and he often rubbed it without realizing what he was doing. Bella knew he was doing it, even if he didn’t. Bella was just waking up when the plane touched down on American soil once again. The retirement to the chateau in France had been shattered by the attack. The subsequent running was awful, but the entire time the trails and tribulations had only succeeded in bringing her closer to Xavier. She wanted to marry him, but that was not possible at the moment. Marriage required a license, and such things could be traced to find them. Of course, that didn’t stop the Fallen Angel from buying her a diamond and platinum engagement ring and a platinum band. Xavier treated her as his wife, even if some government didn’t recognize it he did. And God’I must never forget God. Bella thought to herself. They had been blessed in so many ways, and running for protection was a small test of their love. She was with him forever, no matter what happened going forward. She couldn’t see being without him, and she knew he felt the same even if he sometimes struggled with everything he still had inside of him. She had introduced Xavier to her family, and the reunion had been short. They never approved of her chosen career, but her success had brought her some small, imperceptible measure of forgiveness. Of course, the engagement ring with Xavier had brought even more forgiveness. They were too old fashioned, seeing the possible marriage as the fulfillment of some promise she had never made to them. Of courser, the running ended all of the old concerns. How petty they seemed now. As they disembarked from the airplane, they had only their carry-on luggage with them. Speed had always been their greatest ally, and waiting for a bag on the carousel was just too much of a time investment. Xavier and Bella took their carry-on bags, and made their way through the airport to the taxi stand outside. Xavier walked up to the attendant, “Looking for a ride.” “Where to?” “Lafayette hotel.” He replied. The attendant flagged down a mini-van taxi. He walked up, had a brief conversation with the driver and motioned them over. The attendant loaded their four bags into the mini-van, and Xavier gave him three dollars tip. New Orleans was a city of legend in the states. Drive through daiquiri stands, Mardi Gras and a certain well known reputation for anything goes lawlessness had always dominated the city on the water. They both wondered why this city was selected for their arrival. Bella felt at home. There wasn’t a whole lot of France in America, especially after the war started in Iraq. Xavier had taken shots for dating a French woman, and she knew she was not always welcomed. In Louisiana though, she could fit in as someone of French-American descent without being judged. The cab driver respected their quiet nature, assuming they had been on a long trip and were too tired to speak. They pulled into the Lafayette Hotel, and the bellman came over to greet them. “Welcome to the Lafayette Hotel. Laissez Le Bon Temps Rouler!” He greeted them. Xavier and Bella simply nodded in return to the traditional greeting. Xavier passed two twenties to the taxi driver, and then handed a ten dollar bill to the attendant as he carried the bags inside. The history of the Lafayette Hotel was as rich as the interior. Marble floors, mahogany polished everyday, French doors and wrought iron accents were softened by the English botanical prints within. The staircase to the second floor was truly exquisite. They overall atmosphere spoke of the luxury and excitement of the location near all the cherished New Orleans landmarks. Opened in the 20th Century, the Lafayette was on the cutting edge of technology at the time with showers in every room, telephones and running cold water. The five-story building was a call to luxury within the city. While Bella and Xavier were oblivious to the history, the luxury could not be concealed. During World War II the hotel was leased to the U.S. Department of the Navy as a barracks for the female reservists of WAVES. The hotel rooms were out fitted with bunk beds to accommodate 135 women reservists who remained there until 1946. Following the war, the hotel turned to closure for almost forty years. There was a knock at the door. Xavier stepped out of bed, and walked over to the door. He kept his body out of the way, and leaned over looking out the peephole to see a bellman standing there holding some packages. Xavier opened the door slowly, still not trusting what was happening. “Yes?” “Mr. White, your friends sent these packages to you. I believe these are clothes and shoes for the evening.” He explained quickly, “May I hang them in the closet for you?” Xavier stepped aside, “Sure.” The bellman quickly hung the clothes up in the closet, and set the shoe boxes down at the bottom of the closet. The packages were all designer label, and Xavier knew his brother had spent money to ensure they looked the part this evening. He could feel relief wash over him. He had to lean his hand against the wall to steady himself. Such a simple gesture had almost dropped him to his knees. He felt the emotions wash over him. Could it be possible that all the running was finally over? Jake sent these gifts, and it was a sign that everything was back to being concerned about petty things such as fashion. Xavier shook it off, but he was finally feeling like everything had been taken care of permanently. Perhaps he would finally be able to settle down, start a real life with Bella. You’ve got some dresses to choose from.” Bella walked over to the closet. She pulled the door open, and looked over the dresses. Jake had sent several choices from Caroline Herrera, Versace and Chanel. He either had extremely good taste, or he had received some assistance in the selections. Bella pulled herself away from the closet to get ready. She hated to admit it, but Xavier was right. She took a long time to get ready for an event. In the end, he loved the results though. He was comfortable, but never too comfortable to not pay attention to his surroundings. . . . . . . . The pathways of Fate and Destiny had led them here. A fateful day of choices that would sever the possibilities, assuming one unending goal to move forward from them on forward into the world. Fearful of the decision he was making, he had taken to a journal for his own thoughts now. Of course, for him, the journal was no longer written by hand. He used the electrons of the world to record his thoughts, fears and prayers. Yes, prayers for himself and those he cared for. He looked to the past to try and find solace in his heart. Whenever he found a quote, or a series of quotes, he embraced the arcane and the modern.
