Memory Remains #18

22 September 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 92203 The man sits behind his desk, his face covered in shadows. A gold plated ashtray is on his desk, a lit cigar smoking slightly. He leans back in his black leather chair, looking at the two mean in front of him. The man in the shadows leans forward and grabs the small plastic disk off of his desk, twirling it around in his fingers, looking back and forth from one man to the other. “What part of stealth mission do you two not understand?” Il Capo asks, shaking his head slightly. “Well, man” You see its like this? Johnny boy here wanted to be the supervisor?” Holmes starts. Zero cuts Holmes a look. Holmes grins at Zero and continues. “Then, he made me’no’THREATEN ME, yeah, that’s it, he threatened me, saying that if I didn’t get my black ass into that vent, he’d shoot me. And I tell ya Capo, there was rats in that motherfucking vent.” Holmes holds up his left hand. “Look, one of them furry bastards even bit me.” “So you shot it.” Zero puts in. “Yeah, I shot it.” “Three times?” “He bit me. I prolly have rabies now.” Il Capo shakes his head again, picking up the cigar and taking a long slow drag off of it. He blows the smoke out in a steady stream. “OK, fine, we’ll have a doctor look at you. But that still doesn’t explain the explosion.” “Oh, that’s definitely his job in explaining,” Holmes says, pointing at Zero. “Yeah, that one’s my fault,” Zero says. “Well, not completely. You said that it was full of scientists, correct?” “Yes. It was a laboratory.” Il Capo says slowly. “So why was there so many damn guards” Armed Guards, if I might add? Trigger happy armed guards.” Il Capo lets out another audible sigh. “The reason there were so many guards, is because that you two just invaded a secret military installation. I thought that you two just might have been slick enough to go in and out while avoiding detection, but I guess I was wrong.” “The disk was wired,” Zero adds. “What?” Il Capo asks. “Correct me if I’m wrong, Deac, but the alarm did not sound until after Deacon removed it from the computer.” “Yeah, he’s right.” Il Capo looks at the disk, pressing a button on his desk. An oaken panel slides out of his desk and a computer rises to the surface. Pressing another button, the computer hums to life. Il Capo slides the disk into the computer, his fingers flying over the keys on the keyboard. Several minutes pass. An unnatural noise comes from the computer and sparks begins to fly. Seconds later, the computer is engulfed in flames. “GODDAMNIT!” Capo screams as Zero runs to a counter opposite of the office, grabbing a fire extinguisher, putting out the flames. “What the fuck was that?” Holmes asks, still amazed at the computer erupting into flames. “I didn’t know computers could just’just’combust.” “They don’t’Not without some help.” Zero adds. The three men all speak at the same time. “It was planted.” “Fuck. FUCK. Fuck fuckity fuck fuck fuck!” Holmes exclaims. “We have to go back, don’t we?” “Not there. That place is toast,” Zero puts in. “Where is the main site, Capo?” Il Capo takes out a piece of paper, and scribbles down an address. “So, how we doing this” Silent or violent?” Il Capo looks at both men. “This time, I don’t care. Just go out and do what you do. And bring me that damn disk back in one piece.” Holmes and Zero look at each other before turning and leaving Il Capo’s office. I was beginning to let my guard down, relaxing finally when three large men entered the office. All three were dressed to kill in fine suits, one sporting a necktie, another with a large black button cover, and the third in a Japanese tailored suit. “Is Hector around?” Necktie asked, his voice heavily laced with a New York accent with traces of Italian in it. I could see the bulge of a gun under his suit coat. “He’ll be back in a second,” I said, fingering the handle of the pistol under the counter. We decided to keep the gun, just in case. “We will wait,” the man said, remaining next to the counter. The man with the button cover moved to the door with his matching bulge, while the man in the Japanese tailored suit took a seat in one of the spare chairs. The toilet flushed and a door slammed, announcing Hector’s return. “I tell yeh, my boy. Nothing like an extended stay in the bathroom to clear out the old mind.” “There are some people here to see you.” Hector looked around the room, finally noticing the three suited men. “What can I do for yeh?” The seated man rose and approached the counter. Pulling an envelope from an inner coat pocket, he tossed it onto the countertop. Hector picked it up and thumbed through its contents. I caught the sight of hundred dollar bills. A lot of them. “Mr. Vedici appreciates your help in this situation. Along with the money, he will extend a wing of protection over you and your establishment.” Hector kept his eyes on the men, not once looking at me. “Hector?” “Shhh Johnny Boy.” I raised the gun and pointed it at him. “What did you do, Hector?” I felt something cold and hard press into my head. “It was coming. One way or another. And I wasn’t going let yeh take me or my girls down with yeh,” Hector said. “Mr. Vedici wants to see you,” one of the men said while lifting the gun out of my hands. “I’m sorry, Johnny boy. But all is fair in love and war.” I began to walk away. Sensing the men relaxing, I turned and leapt at Hector, knocking both of us onto the ground. I wrapped my hands around his neck. I could hear the men shouting. “Just know that I’m gonna come back for you. I could feel the men pulling me away. “In some way, shape, or form, I’m coming for you Hector,” I seethed. Something hit the back of my head and the world faded into darkness. I awoke in a room that was dimly lit by a glowing fire. I could make out the silhouette of a man standing before it. From the pale, flickering light, I could tell that the room was heavily furnished. I tried to rise, but couldn’t. The rattling of chains told me why I couldn’t move my arms or legs. Seeing a thick corded rope across my chest explained the rest. “I see you are awake,” the man said in a soft voice that almost came out like a whisper. “Yes,” was all I could manage to get out. The man turned away from the fire and walked towards me. I could make out a slight stoop as he came forward. His head was topped off with a shock of gray hair and a long thin scar ran from his left eye to his chin. “Do you know who I am?” he asked. “I can only guess.” “Then do so.” “Alessandro Vedici.” “At you service.” His voice carried a somewhat pleasant tone. “Do you know why you are here?” “Yes.” “Why?” “Because Hector double-crossed me.” “Double-crossed you?” “Yeah. I’m pretty sure that if Hector hadn’t told you what I did, I would’ve been home free.” “You’re correct. Georgio had the tendency to go missing for several weeks then show up like nothing had happened. Through Hector’s help, we were able to locate the body and identify it.” “You were still able to identify him?” “Yes.” “How?” “Scars on the back from where he took a bullet to protect me.” There was a long pause. “I hate to cut this short, but you know what I must do.” A silver pistol appeared in his hand, pointed at my head. My mind raced. The small, seemingly bottomless barrel of the gun promising eminent doom. Flashbacks of everything running laps in my head. Lily. Bryce. The bomb. The hotel. Becky’s wish of me rotting in hell. Georgio. A thought occurred to me. Maybe I could appeal to the good in the aging mobster. “Yes, I know what you must do. But, do you know why I did what I did?” Alessandro looked at me, cocking his head to one side in thought. “You killed my son. Does it make a difference?” “It might.” Alessandro lowered the pistol and pulled a chair close to me. “Tell me then.” “I was doing my job.” “So it was a professional hit?” Alessandro asked, raising his gun. “No. I wasn’t hired to kill your son. I was hired as a protector.” “A protector” A protector of what?” “A protector of women. I was hired by Hector to protect the women at his hotel. And that is how Georgio met his end. I was doing my job.” Alessandro’s gun started to lower. “He was beating her, with fists. I tried to reason with him, but he wouldn’t listen.” A little lie here and there wouldn’t hurt. Hell, I’m trying to save my life. Alessandro’s head lowered. “Georgio always had a problem with that. Even as a teenager. You wouldn’t believe how many parents I had to payoff and promise that Georgio would never look at their daughter again.” “He attacked me and his death was the result. If there were a way around it . . . I’m sorry Mr. Vedici.” I deserve an Academy Award. He raised his head and looked at me, eyes moist with forming tears. For the first time since our meeting had begun, I saw how old the mobster really was. “My son is dead,” his voice cracked as he spoke. “There is nothing that neither you nor I can do about it. However, there is still a gap that needs to be filled. Vengeance. My family name must be avenged. If word of my son’s death gets out and that I have not done anything about it, I could be ruined.” Alessandro raised his gun again. “What if I were to offer my services to you in exchange for my life?” “You” Protect me?” Alessandro burst into laughter. “I have enough protection from men twice, maybe even three times your size.” “Maybe not so much the protection, but the other aspect of my services.” “And what would that be?” “A killer.” Alessandro stopped and stared at me. “Do you know, Mr. Vedici, how many men I have killed to date?” “No.” I counted the bodies in my head. Ten. Maybe I should pad the number a little bit. “Seventeen,” I lied. “And do you know how many I have been questioned about?” Alessandro shook his head. “One.” I paused for effect. “Georgio.” He shook his head some more, muttering, “Seventeen.” “And if you don’t like my work, there is always the alternative,” I said, nodding my head at the gun. Alessandro smiled faintly and returned the gun to its hiding place with the folds of the suit that he wore.
