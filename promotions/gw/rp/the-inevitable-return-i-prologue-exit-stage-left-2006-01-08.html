The Inevitable Return I: Prologue, Exit Stage Left

8 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/08/06 ---- “Inside this room, all of my dreams become realities, and some of my realities become dreams?” ~Willy Wonka ---- Flashback, January 4, 2005 Backstage, Global Arena He sat in the shadows, his bare back against the concrete wall, sweat running down his face, his hair soaked with sweat, and the bitterly cold bottled water he’d doused himself with. The water trickled from his hair, running into his eyes, those windows to his black soul, those windows that reflected back the darkness of the void. He let out a long, shuddering breath, one that was nearly a sob and let his head hang. His hair clung to his face, cloying, almost suffocating as he gasped for breath. He exhaled sharply, spraying a fine mist of water mixed with the bitter sweat of defeat. He found his fingers moving towards the silver dog tags around his neck, he was powerless to stop them. Not that he really wanted to in his state of exhaustion. He curled his index finger around the chain, pulling it away from his neck, running the tip of his finger along the inside of the chain, feeling the comforting rub of the balls on the chain. He’d always done this, without thinking, or rather while thinking. Right now his mind was running miles ahead of him. His mind was filled with grim possibilities and the acrid smell of the bridges he’d just burned to get here. His thoughts were filled with darkness, pitch black and bleakly terrifying; the darkness of the abyss over which he stood, poised on the precipice. He let the chain drop, without noticing, and this time his rebellious fingers stole to the scar on his chest. The only flaw marring an otherwise perfect physique. Scars were a part of this business, but this one, it was different. He shook away the thoughts as they rose in his mind, physically shaking his head, making water fly from his soaked hair. Thoughts like that had no place here, in his sanctuary. Here, in this locker room, sitting on the cold floor, smelling those telltale scents; the sweat, the blood and piss, the stench of failure and the pleasant scent of victory, he felt at home. At peace. Even with tonight’s humiliation and the prospect of leaving this place behind, it still felt right. He almost didn’t want to take that last step. So, here he lingered. Lost in limbo and lost in thoughts better forgotten. He grunted and shifted his position, adjusting his cramped legs. He flipped the wet hair back from his eyes and looked up at the ceiling, muttering to himself. “You were right. I hope you realize that now. I’m willing to concede the point, and admit my weaknesses to you. Fitting, really that I choose to do it here, after hours, without a single witness.” His words are a mystery, lending no clue to his character. He pauses, considering something, chewing on the inside of his cheek as though sampling the words as one would sample a meal. His expression was bland, or rather, would have been if not for the slight curl of his lips as though tasting something slightly off. This was a perfect opportunity to study the man more closely, but the conditions were less than ideal. The light was poor, nothing more than emergency lighting close to the ceiling, lending more shadows than illumination. His face was chiseled, the jawline strong, and peppered with dark stubble. Just the hint of a cleft in the chin. His hair was dark, and unkempt, falling past his eyes, the color of which was impossible to tell in the gloom. He tossed his head, snorting in irritation as the wet hair flopped in his eyes again. “Control. It was always about control. I just never saw that until it was too fucking late. I was blind, but you didn’t even try to enlighten me. You didn’t tell me. Why” What was there to gain in misleading me? I would have given you everything if only you asked. I said it, and I’ll say it again- you were right. Does that make you feel any better? Can you sleep knowing that, if nothing else, you were right about me? I’m a failure. I tried so hard, and for what? For nothing. All my hard work has been undone in one night. They used to book loser leaves town matches. Maybe that’s what this should have been. Maybe then I wouldn’t feel like I’m letting them down by walking out that door tonight. You know who I mean, the fans.” Bitter laughter followed this thought, and a hint of self-loathing crept into the next words, giving them an acid edge. “Yeah. My millions and millions of loyal fans. Who am I kidding” They won’t care. They’ll latch on to the next big thing, the next fresh face with potential to come through that door? the next man the powers that be decide to grant that big push to. It won’t be me. Not here, anyway. I can’t stay. You saw to that. For that, and not the millions of other good reasons I should have, I hate you.” He stopped talking, suddenly falling silent as he dragged himself to his feet. He reached down in the dark, blindly snagging his duffel bag. Now that he was standing, he was able to pull the crumpled pack of Camel dead-heads from his back pocket. The lighter came from the front, catching a sliver of light, reflecting silver in a flash. A snap, and then a bluish flame flares, illuminating his tired features as he drew in the smoke as though sampling the sweetest ambrosia. He was what most would call handsome, or would have been if not for the look of dark anger that twisted his features as he exhaled the smoke towards the lights. “Just being here, one last time, still feels like home. These walls. This place. Everyone else has gone home, even you” maybe to grace someone else’s bed, maybe to sleep alone. I don’t care anymore. I can’t leave? this? this is my home.” He ran his fingers along the raised lines of the still fresh tattoo on his wrist. A reminder in Celtic knotwork- a reminder of eternity. That was what he had now. Time, pouring out endlessly, but time that was a prison of pain. He jerked, looking around furtively, suddenly paranoid. Every sound suddenly seemed to be a conspiratorial whisper; every breath of wind seemed to be the foul exhalation of some beast from the pits of hell, waiting to drag him down into their cold, eternal embrace. “Fuck it,” he muttered, crushing out the cigarette against the wall that had given him support moments ago. He strode to the door and shouldered it open with a bang that shattered the silence. He hesitated on the threshold before stepping out into the night, his features painted crimson for a moment as he passed under the exit sign. His feet found their motivation again, and he moved past that point and out into the world. His boots crunched on the gravel of the parking lot, grounding him to reality and the real world. He walked away, the door swinging shut slowly, obscuring him from view. As he faded away from this plane of existence, and into the rest of his life, he did so with only one parting thought, directed at whomever he had been talking to all this time. “And fuck you too. I hope you’re happy in knowing that I’m not...”
