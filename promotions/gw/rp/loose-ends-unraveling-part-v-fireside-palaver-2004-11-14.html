Loose Ends Unraveling: Part V: Fireside Palaver

14 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/14/2004 Monday, November 8, 2004 0159 hours, local time Fat Moe’s Bar & Grill, Sarnia, Ontario He stood in the tiny bathroom, leaning against the sink, breathing heavily. He was trying like hell to rein in his temper; killing Bruce at this juncture was not a bright idea, as much as he wanted to do it. His body was burning up, despite the chilly air that leaked in around the warped window sill behind him? his blood was like fire surging through his veins. He peered into the mirror, trying to quiet the voices in his head, the whispers of the blade that Gabriel had stolen, urging him to feed the impulse to kill. He busied himself with pulling off his heavy leather jacket, letting its weight carry it to the floor, where it puddled around his feet. The pounding in his head increased in tempo, and volume as he gripped the porcelain basin, his knuckles white. Restlessly, he raked a hand through his sweat-dampened hair before moving his hands to his waist, tearing at the heavy cotton of the black sweater, pulling it free from the confines of his worn jeans. He pulled the damp, black cotton over his head, shivering as the cool air turned the sweat soaking his white undershirt to ice. Time slowed to a crawl as he stood there, staring at his reflection, at the cold anger he saw reflected in his indigo eyes. It was obvious at this point that he was beyond pain? or death for that matter, but he still wanted to kill, that impulse hadn’t faded upon death, He tucked the sweater into the back pocket of his jeans, bending over the sink again, cranking the cold faucet on, and holding his sticky hands under the blessedly cool spray. He splashed the water on his face, welcoming the respite to his parched skin. Deep blue eyes peer out of his reflection, glittering with the drops of water on his eyelashes. More water? this slicking back his shaggy hair, smoothing the errant strands. He pulled out the elastic and tightened his ponytail. The dog tags around his neck jingle? powerful muscles bulge and flex as he runs his hands through his hair. Suddenly, he felt charged with energy? something was about to happen. No evidence of the tremors remained? his body was rock hard, steady now. With a smile, that lights his eyes, he ran a cool hand through his hair one last time, winking at his reflection. “And now, it’s time for a little,” he chuckled, the sound oozing with dark humor, “fun.” He concluded, pulling his sweater back over his head, once again concealing the muscles that rippled and bulged beneath his skin like unyielding steel. It was then that the door swung open, spilling Bruce McLeod into the tiny room, his face flushed and his eyes watery, the very picture of desperation. ---- Flashback? Sunday, October 31, 2004 time had lost all meaning? somewhere in New Mexico Gabriel withdrew the gun from behind his back, holding it in a steady hand, pointed directly at Jackson’s forehead. A lunatic grin split his lips, revealing stained teeth, bringing a trickle of blood from his chapped lips. There it was. The moment of truth. Looking into that tiny black hole? that in an instant became a gaping void? was like looking into himself. There was nothing there. Emptiness and darkness, and nothing more. “Kill me.” Jackson’s voice was flat, filled with no emotion. Bang. A shot. Stench of spent gunpowder. Fire. Pain. Then blackness. Welcome darkness? MY darkness? all for the love of a woman? the wrong woman. The light and pain faded in unison, leaving behind nothing but the numbing darkness, a darkness that seemed to be something more. It was like the great creeping nothing, devouring all of Fantasia? it was like the eternal darkness of death, and yet, it was so much more. He recognized it at once, the place was familiar. He’d been there before. Limbo. ---- I thought that was it, maybe this was my sole purpose, coming back to slaughter the CID, only to have the lone survivor kill me in a moment of anger. The irony of that wasn’t lost on me, really. I thought that was curtains for me, my hour upon the stage finally past, but it turns out I wasn’t dead- just dead wrong. Not quite as final, it would seem, but equally as fatal. ---- Darkness was everywhere, surrounding him. He could feel his life ebbing as the ichor stained the dirty floor, blood flowing down his forehead, soaking his hair at the back of his head, where the huge exit wound had blown off half of his head. Mere seconds seemed to be hours. He watched as the smoke curled up from the barrel of the gun. Everything seemed surreal, the motions blurred, like the parts were being played out underwater. Gabriel smirked, and mouthed some words that reached his ears, sounding horribly distorted. “Ashes to ashes, motherfucker?” The door was unlocked, the sound ringing with some terrible finality, and Gabriel seized him roughly, checking for a pulse. Nothing. Satisfied, he slings the lifeless body over his shoulder and carried him from the cell, dropping him inside a wheelbarrow, blood running sluggishly from his wounds, pooling inside. The world lost cohesion then? as the last strand holding him on this plane of existence snapped, sending him spiralling into the great hereafter? Or not? he came to himself suddenly, as he impacted with a rough stone floor. He dragged himself up, groaning and muttering, and then found himself sitting, quite suddenly on an icy slab, surrounded by smoke. His head was tilted back; he seemed to be contemplating the smoke as it left the smoke hole in the ceiling of the cave. Smoke? hole?” What the fuck? He felt his forehead, surprised to find nothing there. What the hell was going on? There was a chuckle from the shifting darkness that surrounded him. He tensed, his hackles rising instinctively as he glared into the darkness. “Where the fuck am I?” No response save the darkness pulling back a few feet to reveal a crackling fire. He could feel the warmth on his face and hands, chasing away that cold that was all too final. “Show yourself, I can feel you there.” Still no reply from the shifting, writhing darkness. There was a hint of crimson within the dark cloud, just a tiny flash. His eyes narrowed as he snarled, slamming his fist down on his knee. “I grow weary of these games” tell me who the fuck you are or I’ll-? “Or you’ll what” Oh please do finish that thought? you quasi-mortals amuse me so!” It was that voice again, the same one from his vision in the church. Still uncannily familiar, yet he still couldn’t place it. He snarled at the darkness, his eyes flashing with demonic ire. “I’ll fucking kill you. You wanted to hear it. Now tell me what I want to hear. Who are you” What the fuck do you want from me?” The cavern vibrated with the laughter of the other being. “How can you kill that which you cannot see, Bradley?” His eyes narrowed to tiny slits. “Listen up, asshole” I don’t fucking care who you are, nobody calls me that? you hear me? NOBODY! Bradley-motherfucking-Jackson is dead. I am no longer tied to that name. I am Namenlos. I am Duisternis! I am your worst nightmare, whoever the hell you are! You don’t-? Then, it was almost as if an invisible hand closed over his heart, squeezing, hard. He gasped, clutching at his chest, his eyes growing wide. The voice replied, in a reasonable tone, quite close to his ears, he could almost feel the cool kiss of the being’s breath there, if he concentrated on it. However, he was trying to remember how to breathe, even though he no longer needed to... so the sensation was lost on him. “I can do as I please, Bradley. Your fate, such as it is, now lies in my hands” whether you live or die is now up to my discretion. Since you have failed to live up to your part of the bargain, I am now free to do as I choose. You have been given ample time to find your purpose, yet you persist in these foolish games. Now then, do you care to disrespect me again?” The pressure waned, and he was left bent over, gasping and choking. After a moment, he looked back into the darkness, his eyes blazing with fire. His tone however, was carefully neutral. “Can you at least tell me why I’m here?” “In due time, my son” in due time, all will be answered? all shall be revealed? but that’s not the question you truly want to know, is it? Say what you mean, nameless one, and perhaps you will know.” Jackson looked away, his words coming from his mouth in a hurried gush, almost as though vomited up. “What am I” What have I become?” The darkness shifted and roiled, but remained silent. He grew irritated, almost immediately, as he slammed his fist down on his thigh, growling in vexation. “Yeah, let me guess, you’ll answer me when you fucking feel like it, and not before, right” Listen up, I’m not the type who enjoys games? I want some answers. I think you, and whoever else is in on this owes me that much. Tell me what the fuck is going on! Right fucking now! I’m sick of playing these games? I don’t want to play anymore!” The darkness rippled, a sound like laughter filling the silence. “On the contrary, nameless one. I have been watching you for some time” I know exactly how much you enjoy your? diversions.” ---- At once, the memory faded back to the present as Bruce reached out, touching Jackson’s arm with his cold hands. Jackson jerked as though electrocuted, and pulled away, slamming back against the sink. “Don’t fucking touch me,” his voice was low, almost the growl of a cornered animal as he glared at the Irishman. He reached for his fallen jacket, reaching really for the poison in the syringe that still rested in the breast pocket, alongside his gun. He held out the gleaming glass cylinder, and watched with detachment as Bruce snatched it, plunging it into his vein at the crook of his arm without any preparation. It would have been sad, if not for the feeling of strangeness that had accompanied the sudden flashback. He hadn’t remembered that, until now? and to remember it now, of all times, meant surely that it was important. He shook his head, trying to clear away the cobwebs, trying instead to focus on the man who stood before him, the broken junkie with the smile on his pasty face. “Now then,” he began, his voice trembling slightly, “let’s talk business, shall we Mr. McLeod?” To Be Continued?
