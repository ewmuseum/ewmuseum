The Precipice

17 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Week Of 10-17-05 Moving Over The Edge Hix Family Ranch East Texas Once, snow had fallen across the grounds like a fine blanket of frozen winter. During those times, men and women retreated behind closed doors to warms fires and friendly tidings. On those nights, the chill in the air was understandable, but tonight was different. The chill in the air came from hesitancy, not wanting to face what lie in the rubble. The same landscape had seen the dawn of the endless days through the eyes of eight generations of the family. In the stillness of morning, with the first glow of radiance the shadows of the family long since past seemed to rise from the land, if only for the briefest of moments. The connection to the past was never severed, but rather the strings of connection were finer, harder to discern with the passage of time. Of course, the earth had been fed with all sorts of nutrients. Cattle, the lifeblood of the ranch, had kept the gardens fertile with their natural call of dropping manure. He still remembered the worn grip of the shovel from the hours he spent cleaning up and mixing the natural fertilizer. Of course, the ground had also been fed with something else. While land disputes and arguments had caused land to go fallow, men had met with claret cutting down to feed the earth. The blood of my family? He pulled the truck to the side of the road. He stepped out into the atramentous night, letting himself draw in the redolence carried by the wind he knew so well. The smells rushed memories into his mind unbidden. He ignored them as he used his keys to unlock the multiple padlocks on the chains. Pushing open the iron gates, he returned to his truck. Small bits of rock and debris were kicked into the tepid night as he climbed up the low hill to look down at the property he had been withdrawn from for so long. He parked the truck atop the hill, looking into the lower level of what had once been desc5ribed as the country kingdom for his family. So much pain? The grass had gone to brown thanks to the lack of rainfall. Even as he came to charred earth of where the main property around the homestead had been, all he saw was roasted grass left dry and lifeless without any water to sustain the green in the St. Augustine. He looked left, knowing whatever answers he sought here tonight were within the crumbling ruins of the barn. The bonds of family had been ruptured here forever thanks to the actions of one of their own. No matter if it had been through disinterest or through a slight, the choices of his brother ended everything the family had spent decades building. The brown grass crunched beneath his boots as he approached the partial walls of the barn he had spent so many years training inside of to hone his craft. Even with the moonlight, the adumbral interior of the barn seemed to hide ghosts. He wondered if it was all mental, or if the pain and anguish was calling out to him for vengeance from beyond. “Is this what you came for?” The voice called out to him. From deep within the bowels of fire and pain, he had long ago learned the dark arts from creatures whose names had passed out of use even amongst the ancient demons. Leaving a small piece of himself to watch over the area as a guardian was not difficult. With the return of The fragment he had left to watch over the location let him know that he had returned. If he turned around, he knew that he would see him as though he was standing right in front of him. Jake wasn’t ready to face that at the moment. “Oh come on, why can’t we speak like we once did back when we were young?” The voice called out again. The shimmering silhouette of his brother Sid walked by. Jake ignored the image as he focused on the task at hand. Jake walked past Sid into the black, broken boards left of the barn. He looked at the wrestling ring, half fallen away in the center of the space. “I remember when we first built this ring. We tore down the old ring, and then we built this ring. Pitt paid for it, happy to give us something to practice on that wouldn’t break our backs. We were all so happy about that, do you remember that?” “Of course I remember that.” Sid walked up next to his brother, “We are still family after all.” “No we’re not.” Jake looked at Sid, “That never happened. Whatever was left of my brother Sid died a long time ago, and you’re just the baneful beast concealed within the flesh.” There was a shimmer, and a line of red fire flew into the night. He saw him now, not as a man but as something more. The lines across his face were darker now, and the distinct aura of his villainous soul could be seen and felt by Jake. The moment had passed away forever. “Aren’t you the clever animal?” Sid walked away. Jake kneeled down pushing aside the ash and fire baked wood until he found the ground beneath. He began digging out a small hole with his bare hand. “You know, I could destroy the entire family any time I choose. I have enjoyed our toying with one another, but now the time has come to close this battle between us.” Jake ignored him as he pulled an item from his pocket, clasping his hand around the item. He began murmuring softly, saying the prayer he knew so well. Sid was laughing, a booming laugh that echoed all around in an unnatural way. “This is how you respond” You move from trying to kill me to ignoring me?” “You are not here.” Jake turned to face Sid, “If the one who walks the earth were here I would slay him where he stood. I would remove the object of my hatred from God’s sight forever.” “How can a man of God feel hatred” That is what you’ve become, a man of God, right? How can you not love thy neighbor dear brother?” Jake squeezed tightly on the item in hand, “I love my neighbor, but I hate what God hates. He has allowed you to exist to test the faithful. My hatred is an expression of my love.” “You don’t see the stupidity in that statement Jake?” He turned, locking onto the fiery red eyes of his brother. Jake’s own blue eyes were cool and calm, the opposite of the dangerous rage behind Sid’s own eyes. “Sid, my hatred of you and everything you stand for is simply a confirmation of my love for God. I’m not a priest, but I am blessed. I’m not a divine prophet, but I know our future. I am a man, imperfect in being, chosen to do one thing. I am the man who will destroy you once and for all.” “Slay me and my followers will bring me back once again.” He had a smug sureness he didn’t deserve. “Not this time.” Jake opened his hand. The rosary fell down, dropping before him with just the end looped around his finger. “We aren’t going to run anymore Sid. I’m going to lock you away in your own little Hell, and nothing can save you.” Jake let his arm fall, and the rosary fell down into the small hole he had dug in the earth. There was a wave of light, sent out from the point of consecration. Sid’s image was obliterated like sand falling from a castle made by children thanks to the unending march of tides. He saw two beams of light coming up from the darkened hill. They were coming back, and now was the new beginning. Consequence Sid’s Lair The sudden disconnect of the connection was not like hanging up a telephone. The power of the blessing had sent Sid flying off the throne as though being slapped by an invisible force. Sid Hix, the Dark messiah, looked up at his map and scrolls on the wall. He tasted something, foul and metallic. He reached up, wiping away the short trickle coming down his mouth. He looked down at his hand as he pulled it away, seeing his own blood there on his hand. “What is this?” Sid realized he was looking at and tasting his own blood. He stood up, and found he didn’t have his balance as he fell back against the stone wall. Something had changed drastically, and the sudden clawing sensation within his dark, twisted soul was one he had not felt in a very, very long time. Fear? What has happened? What did he do? At the entryway into the throne room, someone turned away. Their fear was the greatest of all.
