Memory Remains #19 (PPV #1)

27 September 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 092703 Goddamn. Deac took us in a corner somewhere between machs two and three, and I swear I thought I heard the engine of his Porsche say “You gotta be shittin” me.” The tires put in their two cents as well, squealing at the depth of the curve and the speed that Deacon was taking it. I held fast though, a tight grip on the door with my right hand, my fingers digging deep into the leather, a even tighter grip on the silenced Tec-9 I was holding for Deac in my left, wouldn’t want to drop that puppy in this nice enclosed space, and my mouth a thin line of a combination of stress and calmness. “Goddamn!” I finally yelled at Holmes, when he took a second curve even faster that the first. “What?” he yelled back, or tried to over the bumping bass beat of the evil car stereo he had custom built into the tiny car. I reached over and grabbed the volume knob, turning it sharply to the left. “Boy!” he yelled at me. “Have you seen Rush Hour?” “No, why?” “A very, very important lesson was taught in that movie,” Holmes began. “Never, ever, EVER touch a black man’s radio. Not even in emergencies. You could get shot or something.” I cocked my head to one side, giving Deac one of my patented blank stares. “Man, don’t look at me like that. It’s true. I’ve seen that shit happen. White boy didn’t know what happened. He turned the radio station and it was like POW! He didn’t move for a coupla days. Brothas just left him lying in the street. It was sad man.” “Mhmm. Yeah, whatever you say D. Besides, need to cut the nose down,” I said, pointing off into the distance. “See, we’re here.” Deacon reached for the emergency brake to bring us to a stop. “Um” just stop us without a show. Remember, we’re trying not to be noticed.” “Yeah, sure, I knew that.” I shook my head slightly as Holmes slowly brought the car to a stop. I handed Deac back his Tec-9. “Here, take this god-awful thing.” “God-awful” Man, I borrowed it from you.” “Yeah” Doesn’t mean I like using them.” Which was true. Automatic weapons weren’t really my thing. Gimme a nice Glock, an extra clip or two, and some knives and I was a happy camper. Hell, I don’t even mind doing things the old fashioned way. Guess I’m just getting too old. I folded that last thought up into a tiny square and pushed it away into the corner of my mind. Reaching into the glove compartment, I pulled out a pair of night vision binoculars, bringing them up to my eyes. In the distance, I could see the small one room building that was the guard’s station. One lone man sat in cramp shack, reading a newspaper. “One guard, reading the paper,” I said aloud so Deacon would know what was going on. “I don’t see anyone else in the area.” I lowered the binoculars. “Ok. Here’s the game plan. I’ll run over there and subdue the guard. Grab his keys and pass card. From there, we follow the directions on the LCD map, trying not to piss off the local wildlife. We get the shit and get out, no fuss, no muss. We make as little ruckus as we can, and get the hell out. Sound cool?” “Gangsta.” Holmes replies, nodding his head up and down. Gangsta. I repeated the word in my head, letting it echo about slightly, trying to process it’s meaning. With the way he was nodding, I’m gonna take a wild ass guess and say it means cool. Gangsta. I repeated the word again, this time trying to say it like Deacon did. Gangsta. I shook my head. Yeah, I’m getting old. “Deac, remind me to buy the Ebonics dictionary when we’re through here, alright?” Deacon shook his head. “Aight man.” I eased open the car door, sliding out into the crisp night air. A small patch of gravel crunched under my feet slightly, but not enough to warm a guard in a shack some five hundred feet away. I padded my way to the wall surrounding the complex Deacon and I were to infiltrate. Flattening my back against the wall, I concentrated on being part of the wall, disappearing into the shadows; I concentrated on not being seen. I sidestepped my way to the gate, moving somewhat quickly at first, slowing down as I reached the shack. I slowly pulled out the carbon-blade knife that was in the sheath at my right hip. The black blade evaporated in the night, not giving off a glint like a normal blade would. The door to the gatehouse was ajar, and I pushed on it carefully, but it slid open without a squeak. I was standing directly behind the guard now. He folded the paper and set it down. I tightened the grip on the knife in my right hand. The guard reached over his right shoulder with his left hand scratching his back. I rushed forward, my left hand clamping on the guard’s left elbow, pulling in sharply, and trapping the guard. I flashed out with my right hand, going over the guard’s body and sinking the blade of my knife into his left armpit. I pulled out and thrust with the knife again. I kept the guard’s arm raised, the blood pouring from the two wounds. I could feel him resisting, his body easing into an eternal relaxation. Finally, there was nothing. I lower his arm, covering up the wound and the blood. Working the scene, I remove the ID clip tag from the guard’s front shirt pocket. A quick search of his other pocket’s came up with his keys. I was wrestling the guard around in the chair when Deacon walked in. “That’s fucked up, John,” he said. “What?” I asked tossing him the keys. “See if one of those has an “M” engraved on it.” Deacon searched through the keys on the key ring while I finished up with the guard. I had him leaning back, his head tilted forward and his arms crossed. From a distance, it would look like he was asleep. It would do for now. “Got it,” Deacon said, triumphantly holding up the key. “Alright, junior. Let’s go shopping.” We exit the small guard shack, and stood before the gate. We didn’t want to make any extra noise opening the large doors. Deacon looked over at me and smirked. “So, you gonna need a boost or what John?” I give Deac a sly grin, while backing up a little bit. Running, I leapt up at the large door, latching onto the iron bars a good ten feet in the air, pulling myself up. I climb the remaining five feet and fling myself over the top, landing on the ground in a kneeling position. “Nah, I got it. What about you” You need a boost, D?” Deacon glared at me, muttering something to the effect of “Fucking show-off.” “If you need me to, I’ll climb back over and give you a lift, D. Ain’t no problem,” I said, laughing slightly. “Nah, man, I got it,” he said, still glaring a little bit. I could hear him mutter. “Man, why there gotta be all this damn climbing and shit. Yo, D, get your ass in that vent. Hey, Deac, climb that gate. This is some rank bullshit.” “Think of it like this, D. At least I haven’t said, “Hey, Deac, why don’t you climb into that sewer and check the shit out for me.”” “Oh, I’m sure that’s gonna be next. Send the black man on the shit jobs. Man, if we have to go down any damn sewers, your ass is going first.” Despite his bitching and moaning, Deacon, in essence, was a good kid. He had fire in him. Sometimes, the flames got a little bit out of control, but he got what needed done, done. Climbing the fence would only talk a few moments to do, but Deac would let you hear about it for the next couple of days. When he finally touched ground, we took off in a slight jog towards the middle of the compound. Once there, we ducked behind a parked truck. “Alright, D,” I said, pointing at a smaller building in the distance. “There’s where you’re going.” “Me” Why me.” “Because you’re the thief. You’re gonna go steal the plans.” “And just where the fuck you think you’re gonna be. I’ll be goddamned if you’re gonna sit your white ass out here and call out left or fucking right!” “No, I won’t,” handing Deacon small black sleeve with an LCD panel attached to it. “This device is preprogrammed with the blueprints of that building.” I took a small black patch about the size of a square inch and placed it inside his black hoodie. I walked him through the use of the device, how to zoom in and out so that he could find the quickest routes and such. “Now for the bad news.” “Bad news?” “Yeah. Unless you have clearance, there is really only one way in and one way out of that building.” “And that is?” I laughed and sighed simultaneously. Deacon is gonna love this. “Through a vent shaft on the roof, past a giant rotating fan.” “No. Hell no. Hell muthafucking no,” Deacon yelled. “What is this, try to give Deacon claustrophobia week” I already played with the vent rats, John, try again. Or better yet, tell me where the fuck you’re gonna be.” “Well, you remember I said that there is a giant rotating fan.” “Yeah, let’s not forget to slice and dice good ole D.” “The only place to cut the fan is from the security building. I was going to infiltrate the building, shut off the fan and then create a distraction for our escape.” “Uh huh. How bout you let me take care of that and you go through the goddamn vents.” “Yeah, sure, okay. But only if you can tell me this. Which building is the security building.,” I laughed to myself. Damn, I’m an asshole sometimes. Deacon swiveled his head from left to right examining the practically identical buildings. “It’s’uh’it’s”.Man, fuck you John.” I flashed Deacon a smile. “It’s alright D. Think of it like this. At least you won’t be shot at.” “We hope I won’t get shot at. Remember last time.” I did remember last time. In fact, it was because of the after-party shenanigans that we were standing in the middle of a military complex getting ready to complete a mission for Il Capo that could land both of us in some serious shit if we got caught. But, hey, alls far in the game of greed and power. On my head, I could start to feel raindrops. “Great,” Deacon sighs. “Now it starts to fucking rain.” “No, this is a good thing. The guards will be more distracted that usual. This is good cover.” I slid my earpiece into place. “Check, Deac. You got me?” Deacon placed one hand over his left ear. “Yeah, I gotcha.” I held my closed fist out. “Let’s do it.” Deacon reached out and bumped the front of his fist to mine before turning and jogging off into the rain towards his building. I turned around and faced the security building that loomed before me. I pulled a Glock from the holster at my hip, sliding in a clip and chambering the first round. I started walking around to the side of the building towards the fire escape. Let’s do this.
