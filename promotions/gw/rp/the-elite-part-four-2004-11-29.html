The Elite (Part Four)

29 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11.29.04 The date was July 9th, 1999. The screeching of Josh's alarm clock jarred him from a pleasant slumber and he reacted by jolting upright before dropping back to the bed and slamming a fist into the pillow as he groaned unhappily. It was nine-fifteen in the morning, an hour that he usually never saw thanks to his love for the nightlife. In fact, he had to question as to what possessed him to set the alarm clock so early. 'What is today?' he mused silently to himself before he once again shot upright this time with a smile across his face. 'The ninth!' he joyfully yelled. Kicking the sheets from his nude figure, he sprung up in an instant to his bare feet, landing simultaneously on the hardwood floor. Standing erect, he bounded across the room with a smile on his face for today was the biggest day of his life, his eighteenth birthday. Reaching onto the floor, he procured a pair of black boxers and tugged them on as he proceeded to his closet. Opening the door, he saw it bare, save for a few essential items, those being the clothes that would be worn today. Pulling out the black gym shorts and the white Nike T-shirt, he stepped into the shorts and jerked the shirt over his head. Looking about for his shoes, he spotted a pair of silver trainers. Dropping back onto the bed, he pulled the trainers closer. Inside, he found a pair of socks, which he of course pulled on before stepping into and lacing the trainers. 'Today is the big day...' he half-heartedly mumbled to myself as he stood from the bed once again, dressed comfortably. Walking across the floor, his trainers made no noise as he stopped, knelt and shouldered the heavy black backpack that rested on the floor. Inside were all of the things he treasured as well as some of the items he couldn't live without. Perhaps even a trivial item or two had somehow made its way in there, but nonetheless, the heavy backpack was now responsible for everything he owned. Without even bothering to shower or brush his teeth, he was off, on his way, on his long journey to independence without a hitch... or so he assumed. The problem with assuming is bringing parents into the equation for often times they don't act as one assumes they will... such was the case in this scenario. Stepping out of the bedroom, he quickly made his way down the stairs at a quick gait, taking them two at a time, and upon reaching the bottom, he suddenly wished he was still sleeping peacefully. He sheepishly smiled at his parents as his father placed his paper on the kitchen table and peered at him over his glasses. 'Where are you going,' He curiously asked as his mother sat down her coffee and turned to look at him with a perplexed stare on her face. 'That's what I need to talk to you about,' he said in a soft tone, causing them to look at one another questioningly. 'You haven't told us of your plans,' his mother informed him as she took another sip of her coffee. Pulling up a chair at the table, he dropped the backpack onto the floor and rested his elbows. 'I haven't told you—because I'm not sure exactly how well you'll take it,' he said, punctuating the sentence with a sigh. 'Oh great,' his father moaned with his demeanor already showing signs of impatient anger. 'Mom, Dad,' he began, looking at each one of them as he spoke their name. 'I'm eighteen today, so I've decided that I can't stay around here any longer. I have to heed my life's calling,' he said, knowing full well that he had just struck a match in a powder keg. At first there was stunned silence, but he knew that wouldn't last long... not long at all, in fact. Because less than five seconds after this revelation, his father's booming voice shattered the silence. 'Your life's calling,' his father asked in a tone trapped somewhere between anger and confusion. 'I'm going to become...' he paused as he took what could very well be his last breath before he finished his sentence. '...a professional wrestler.' That's when it happened... the explosion. It started with just a hateful stare... followed by an angry twitch of the lips. Then the yelling would begin... 'A PROFESSIONAL WRESTLER,' his father screamed, seemingly outraged by his career choice. 'Yes, Dad a,' but before Josh could even form a full sentence, his father cut him off. 'What kind of idiot are you,' he screamed. 'You have a scholarship to the best college track program in the nation and you want to throw it all away for that - that - that wrestling crap,' he asked, now standing apparently enraged. 'You're not serious about turning down the scholarship to become a wrestler, are you,' his mother gasped. 'Yes, I am,' he said as he was quick to defend his chosen profession and, of course, his own freedom to be anything he so desired. 'No,' his father angrily stated in a matter of fact tone. 'What,' Josh asked, rather bewildered by his statement. 'No, you're not going to become a wrestler,' his father stated as he glared at Josh from across the table. 'Yes I am, it's my life... I can do whatever I want with it,' he said trying to keep from growing angry and make this situation worse than it already was. 'No, I forbid you to wrestle,' his father screamed as his face twisted into a mask of anger. 'You can't tell me what to do,' Josh defiantly said in retaliation. 'Josh, don't talk back to your father,' his mother stated quickly taking sides with her husband. 'What are you going to do ground me,' Josh sneered at her. Yet it was mere seconds after that had been said that he was the unsuspecting victim of a hard slap across the mouth from his father that sent him from my chair to the floor. He hit the floor hard, instantly wiping blood away from a split lip. 'What the fuck,' he demanded as he rolled over onto my back. 'Don't you ever use that kind of language in this house! As long as you live under my roof, you will abide by my rules,' his father screamed at him as he slowly stood to he feet, shouldering the backpack. 'I don't want to live in this hell hole anyway,' he shot back as he brushed by his father. Apparently he wasn't content to just let Josh go as he grabbed him by the shoulder and slammed him hard into the kitchen wall, sending dishes on a nearby shelf to the floor, where they shattered. 'What did you say,' his father demanded as he towered a full three inches over Josh and weighed significantly more than the one hundred and forty pounds the future American Youth weighed at that time. 'I said I don't want to live in this hell hole, so fuck your stupid rules and fuck you too,' Josh screamed at him as his eyes seemed to widen juxtaposed with both shock and anger. Josh tried to leave, but his father placed a hand on his chest and shoved him back into the wall with enough force that he heard the sheet rock behind him crack. That was it... Josh could take no more. He had been pushed to and beyond his breaking point and hadn't spent all of those years studying Tae kwon do for no reason at all. A situation had risen, and now it must be taken care of. Reaching up with his left hand, he grabbed his father's wrist and twisted it, firing off a snap kick that hit him in the groin at the same time. His dad slumped to his knees and with his free hand, Josh punched him hard in the mouth and then blasted him in the face with a knee that sent him onto the floor and onto his back with blood now pouring from his nose. 'Leave me alone,' Josh screamed at him, his bluish gray eyes flashing with anger. 'Leave me the fuck alone,' he repeated as he turned away from him. Storming away he walked through the living room, where he kicked over the coffee table covered in family portraits. 'Stay out of my life you worthless piece of shit! You can burn in hell for all I care,' Josh screamed as he angrily stomped across the portraits, hearing the broken glass crunch beneath his feet. Tears flooded Josh's eyes as he grasped the doorknob and threw the front door open, rushing out into the oppressive July heat. His vision was blurred by the tangles of hair that had fallen across his face and the tears that rushed from his sockets. Wiping the blood from his mouth with the back of his right hand, Josh ran across the sidewalk in front of his former residence and stepped out into the street in front of his parent's home. Secretly, he was praying that a car would hit him hard to end his misery, but with no vehicles in sight, he quickened his pace to an all out run, showing his track star speed as he ran. As he ran the problems seemed to roll off of his shoulders... He was running away from his problems, leaving them all behind. A new life would begin, a life of change... his life. Ken was so happy that Josh had finally found happiness. He was so happy that he got to be his brother, whether blood was involved or not, and he was overjoyed that his family had taken Josh in so. 'To Ken,' everyone cheered along with Josh. A smile rode across Ken's face as He saluted Josh with his glass, and Josh did the same with his Mountain Dew bottle before both downing the hatch. 'Okay,' said Ken as he sat down his glass. 'Let's cut this bird.'
