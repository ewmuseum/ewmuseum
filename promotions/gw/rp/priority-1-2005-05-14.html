Date: 5/14/05 Chapter I Women. Train would likely never have another nemesis the likes of them. Just his luck his life was full of them. Almost literally surrounded. And it never seems to matter what he’s accomplished. What foe he’s conquered. Somehow they always had a way of making him feel... small. And when talking of “women” that doesn’t refer to the occasional chick he brought home from the intermittent VIP room, who’s only reward for a night spent with him, besides a bowlegged walk, was cab fair home. Naw... Them, they were easy to deal with. Easy to discard. No, these women were the complicated sort. The kind that meant something to him. Maybe that shit was karma. He couldn’t ever remember the last time there was a woman in his life that had stayed there beyond a week or two. It escaped him how long it had been since the last time a “relationship” consisted of something beyond a 2am phone call to a name in his Blackberry’s memory with at least 3 asterisks after it. The trappings of love and affection was supposed to be candy and flowers. Train had grown past that crap to get chicks. At best they’d get a few shots of liquor and something ribbed for her pleasure. They were the definition of disposable. Maybe that’s why he seemed to be the same to women he actually needed.. It had become some kind of ugly pattern. And there were three he could think of. The first in line? His mother. Angela James hadn’t intended for her son to be a gangster. Either one of them. Leon had been the oldest. He’d been the man. The man of the house and the man period as far as his little brother Anthony had been concerned. Problem was that was a lot of pressure for a 17 year old kid. Train was man enough now to know that as much as his older brother had never shown it to anyone in the house trying to fit in the shoes of a father who’d finally lost the battle of his life to the monkey on his back and help support a family was way too much. When a 40 something year old man couldn’t handle it, what was he supposed to do? Leon had been pretty slick for awhile. Yeah mom, I’m getting all that extra money to pay the rent and buy food and keep the lights and the gas on from bagging groceries down at the store. He just forgot to mention that the store was the corner a couple of blocks away and the only thing he was bagging was H. Train had spent many a night laying awake at night wondering if it had torn Leon’s heart out making a buck slinging the same thing that they’d both watched eat their father alive for so long or whether he was just on some “a man has to do what a man has to do, John Wayne” type shit. Being grown now Train figured it was six of one, half a dozen of the other. Either way it was what it was and Leon wound up being the man to more than just him, but his whole crew. There was a time where nothing really went down in a 3 block radius without his ok. Thinking back on it Train couldn’t really see a way his mother could not have known what the deal was... what Leon was up to. But ugly is ugly. Sometimes the best way avoid ugly is not to look. Problem was... when the Cook County Coroner’s office needed someone to come down and identify Leon’s body she had no choice, but to. Angela was a strong woman, but everyone has their breaking point. Life has a funny way of finding it. Too bad there was no Dr. Phil or any of those other pop psychologist around to tell the world just what severe depression looks like. Maybe if their had been they would have been able to get her some help or, at least, the kind of help that would have actually helped her rather some pills to reduce her to a zombie. But fuck it, what’s done is done. Train was happy that she had all the strength he knew she still had to pull herself out of that tailspin a few months later. It was just unfortunate she happened to get curious as to how the gas, electric and rent was getting paid during the time she wasn’t working, knowing full well disability couldn’t cover it all. But again it was a woman that had fucked Train up the day his mother went down to the utility company to ask. If he hadn’t been flashing that wad of 100's, 50's, 20's, 10's, 5's and singles about as thick as his balled up fist for the bitch behind the pay window at the electric company to get some play, the question of where in the hell a 15 year old kid could come up with cash like that might not have come up. Chapter II It was good night for driving Earl thought to himself as he stepped outside the truck stop filling up his lungs with the night air of the Windy City. It wasn’t uncommon for these young bucks on the roads today to show up an hour or two late, it was all about what was convenient to them, not the customer. This truck stop wasn’t the type of place that would be frequented by anybody, but truckers. Over on the industrial side of the city, not far from the slaughter houses and meat packing plants this wasn’t the type of spot you just drove by.
