Calm after the Storm? (Evo RP)

21 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/21/2005 Ramirez Home; Cochella, CA August 20, 2005; 0730 hours Juan's eyes fluttered open as the alarm clock began to ring. Slowly he reached out a hand and turned the buzzer off as he began to sit up in bed. ?I still can't believe it happened,” he thought to himself. ?I hope that reporter is happy after making fools of us on television.” Looking over his shoulder, Juan noticed that Teresa’s bed was empty. “Teresa?” he said a little sleepily. At this time of year, the sun was already up and bright as the masked Luchadores normally rose for the day. But this time Juan noticed that Teresa was already up, although he did not hear the shower going... or did he? No... that was the sound of breakfast cooking; Juan could smell the faint but pleasant aroma of breakfast sausages and eggs cooking; most likely Spanish Omelets, Teresa’s favorite egg breakfast. Rising out of bed and slipping into his terry robe, the Crimson Comet stepped out of the bedroom into the kitchen. “Buenos Dias, Pap”,” said Melina pleasantly, as she padded silently toward the bathroom to wash up and brush her teeth. The child had likewise awoken from the smells of breakfast cooking. ?Buenos Dias, preciosa,” said Juan affectionately, reaching down to ruffle Melina’s hair lightly. Melina grinned a little mischievously. ?”No es su vuelta de cocinar esta ma’ana, Pap??” she inquired innocently. (*Isn’t it your turn to cook this morning?) Juan chuckled and patted Melina on the back, gently ushering her toward the entrance of the bathroom. But he knew the child was right. It was his turn to make breakfast this morning. Why had Teresa gotten up before the alarm and started to do it herself? Juan found Teresa in the kitchen, and sure enough, sausages and Spanish Omelets were busy cooking on the griddles. “Morning, Teresa,” said Juan a little cautiously. No telling if his wife still wasn’t furious at him over the fiasco during the interview. Juan’s voice startled Teresa slightly, and her head snapped over her shoulder to face him. “Oh... good morning,” she said with a small, sheepish smile. Teresa didn’t look like she was still seething over the interview... but it hadn’t faded from her mind completely. More than likely, she had pushed it to the back of her mind, waiting until the inevitability of Juan speaking to her about it. The look in her eyes showed she was clearly waiting for him to bring it up. Juan bit, though not directly. “Was my turn to make breakfast this morning,” he said with a small smile of his own. “I could have gotten that.” Teresa shrugged as if she was relinquishing a weight off of her shoulders. “I couldn’t sleep,” she said, “and I had nothing else to do... so I came here and started making omelets. I figure some of my favorite breakfast would put my mind more at ease.” ?She seems a bit more at ease,” Juan thought to himself, ?but is she really?” Juan poured himself a glass of orange juice. “We’re due at the gym at 1 PM,” he said. “Non-title match against Unite on the next Evolution.” Teresa snickered lightly. “You think Christina is still upset that Absolutely Merciless and we didn’t give the fans that cage match they all thought they’d get to see?” Juan had to laugh at the thought. “I’m sure they got a surprise,” he said. The plan to ignore the cage around them, and fight a straight-up mixed fighting bout as if the cage weren’t there, had won both Herencia and their opponents, Michael and Mindy Mercer, a rousing and lasting cheer and thunderous applause... but they knew that not all of the fans were so enthused. Some wanted a cage match, and felt cheated. Not that any of them gave a damn; Teresa had gained a whole new respect for the young and exuberant World Champion who took both her and Juan to school with her flawless Kempo technique... and the fans got quite a show. But wondering how Christina Reno reacted to seeing the cage see no use in the bout was a source of quite a bit of amusement for both the reigning GW tag-team champions, and their last opponents. But as husband and wife looked at each other, they both knew... amusement over one-upping Christina was not the foremost thing on each other’s minds. Teresa broke the silence first. “We have to be more careful what we say to Noi Tsunami, Juan,” she said. “Or else we have to start opening up with her completely.” Juan’s head snapped over his shoulder to look at his wife... his eyes showing that same gleam every time Teresa brought it up. Tears welled in Do’a Teresa’s eyes as she put her hands on Juan’s arms, squeezing lightly. “Juan, por Dios, don’t you see how much you’ve changed” Noi was right-- can’t you see what’s become of our relationship? Come back to me, Juan... the old partner I could count on. The old you who always stood beside me.” She seemed almost desperate about it now; he’d heard her cry herself to sleep a couple of nights since they’d arrived in America. And watching Teresa start to tremble, hugging herself with her arms, made tears of guilt well up in his eyes; stinging them with their salty sting. He knew that his refusal to give in on this was selfish... but it was what had made everything possible. It was what had brought them to where they were today. How could he just walk away? Juan reached out to hold his wife. “You know I’ll always be here for you, Teresa,” he began, but Teresa pulled away from him. “Will you?” her tone turned accusatory. “I always hear that from you... and it’s always just words. And day by day, I don’t feel you being here for me; I feel you slipping further away.” Teresa dropped to her knees and wrapped her arms around Juan’s knees. “I just want you as you really are,” she cried. “Don’t let me lose you the way I lost Pap”.” “You won’t,” said Juan quickly, gently trying to lift Teresa to her feet. “Teresa... we’ve restored the Ramirez family name to its former station. Mateo’s legacy is ours again. It’s taken us ten years to finally reclaim everything that was taken away from El Zorro Azul that night in the cantina. Ten years, Teresa. Now we can lift our heads proudly to any who ever dared to besmirch Mateo’s honor, and claim that he was not a pariah... he did not dishonor himself and his family.” Teresa wiped at her eyes with the back of her hand. “I don’t know if it’s worth it anymore, Juan,” she said quietly. “It’s costing me too much... and whether you care to admit it or not, it’s costing you too much as well. We always profess how family must come first... something we took from Pap” and Mam?, when they were still together. And now this legacy is tearing apart the family. I don’t think this legacy, this vow we set out to fulfill, is worth it anymore!” Juan backpedaled; looking aghast at Teresa with eyes that were suddenly angry. “And you think that by breaking the vow you made to Mateo, you will save the family?” He let out a slow breath through flaring nostrils. “And I was beginning to worry that I was selfish. You say that I’ve changed” The fault, dear Teresa, may not lie within our stars, but in ourselves.” “Juan!” cried Teresa as he stalked out of the kitchen. She heard him grab his keys off the niche in the living room entertainment center where he normally kept them, and exit the apartment, to brood on the patio. Teresa hung her head and put her face in her hand, crying openly. Her shoulders lightly shook up and down with each weeping breath, as if bowing under the weight of the burden she desperately yearned to shrug off of them. Part of her was desperate to march straight to Noi Tsunami and give her an interview like she’d never gotten before... something that would put the GW reporter’s name on the map. But she knew Juan would never forgive her if she did. How did their lives get so crazy? It seemed like such a simple, straightforward plan, when the vow was made ten years ago. It seemed like a mission that could hold only joy, not pain; an endeavor that would only bring the family closer... not tear it apart. So costly was the price of their success that she couldn’t even enjoy a family breakfast-- Breakfast! ?”Mamacita, el desayuno quema!” Melina called urgently. (*Breakfast is burning!) Teresa scrambled to thrust her hands into the protective oven mitts and snatch the griddles off of the stove top... dropping them into the sink where a quick turn of the lever doused the burning food with a spray of cold water which quickly extinguished the smoldering heat. There was no damage to any of the kitchen; the griddles were scorched but a good cleaning would make it good as new. The sausages and omelets cooking on the griddles weren’t as fortunate. No way was the food edible now. ?”Est? bien, Mamacita?” Melina asked her mother, walking up to hug her lightly around the waist. Teresa kneeled down to return the hug; grateful for the feeling of her daughter’s arms holding her. ?Tendremos que tener el desayuno en el comensal en el rinc’n,” she said. ?Diga a Juan.” (*We’ll have to have breakfast at the diner on the corner. Tell Juan.) GW Headquarters, Las Vegas August 20, 2005; 1242 hours Melina had done most of the talking during breakfast... her excited chatter about her studies and the neighborhood soccer games she and the other kids in Cochella played together kept Juan and Teresa occupied. Both of the Luchadores smiled pleasantly at Melina and offered generous praise at all her accomplishments, and this kept the child’s mind off of the tension brewing underneath the surface. Now, as they changed into workout clothes in separate locker rooms at the GW Headquarters building, the mood was returning. The tag-team champions knew Melina had overheard the unpleasantries in the kitchen, and neither of them knew how to explain things to her. Fortunately, a surprise was waiting for them in the workout room. “Hummm... how long has it been, my amigos? It is good to see you again.” The three of them beamed at the masked man in the workout room by the free weights, and Melina ran over to him right away. ?T’o Gaspar!” Melina threw her arms around him as he squatted down to give her a hug. Melina had begun calling El Serpenti “Uncle” even though they were not related. He seemed fond of the term of endearment, and he was probably Melina’s closest confidante in the fed. Juan and Teresa walked up behind Melina and greeted Serpenti warmly. “It’s good to see you again, Gaspar,” said Teresa, giving him a buss on the cheek. “How was the treasure hunting?” Serpenti made a grand sweeping arc with his arm. “I sailed all around the Spain/Portugal peninsula, into the Mediterranean Sea,” he said. “Around the horn of Mexico as well, along the northern coast of South America.” They smiled at him and at each other... the first time the masked Luchadores had smiled at each other all day. Serpenti suddenly grinned and pointed at Juan’s neck. “Do you always keep your neck so well-covered, friend?” he asked in a slightly teasing manner. He’d noted that Juan always wore some kind of turtleneck shirt as part of his street clothes... and even his workout shirts and wrestling singlets were custom-made in a way that covered up his neck. Not the usual sort of attire to wear in the brunt of the August heat, for certain, but... Juan smiled and shrugged. “You know how some of the reporters joke about GW being infested with vampires,” he said. “Teresa and I like to cook with garlic when we make our meals at home... perhaps the perspiration will make my neck smell like garlic and keep them away.” Juan and Teresa both chuckled, and Serpenti smiled. The tag-team champions clearly didn’t believe in vampires... wherever the joking amongst the reporters had gotten its origins, they didn’t know, but it made good fodder for Juan’s sly answer. Serpenti could see his friend didn’t want to share the real reasons for his attire, so he didn’t press the point. “Jos” will be along in a few minutes,” said Juan. “We’re going to prepare for a bout against Unite, a relatively new tag-team on the circuits.” Serpenti tilted his head to recollect. “I am not familiar with them,” he said. The voice that began filling Serpenti in on this tag-team called Unite, wasn’t Juan or Teresa’s. “Native-American half-brothers,” said Stuart LaPortia, walking into the workout room. “Though I think only Gray is full-blooded. They’re from the Saquamish nation. Gray is the more level headed of the two, his style is that of a power wrestler/suplexer. He’s a thinker in the ring, though he prefers not to get technical. Josh has the nickname “Fire Brand” for a reason. He’s more impulsive, and is a risk-taker in the ring, with lots of high-flying moves and innovations.” “In that case, we should make the most of the two hours we have,” said Jos? Sanchez, coming into the room behind Stuart. ?T’o Gaspar, venga esc’cheme leer,” said Melina excitedly, as she lightly tugged on his wrist. ?Maggie y yo estar’n haciendo el libro, “Curious George.”? (*Uncle Gaspar, come listen to me read. Maggie and I are doing the book, “Curious George.”) ?Ella hace muy bien,” said Teresa, smiling encouragingly at Serpenti. (*She’s doing really well.) Serpenti nodded and lightly ruffled Melina’s hair, giving her a small smile. ?Entonces, leermos,” he said. (*Then, we will read.) Melina led Serpenti toward the Commissary, smiling eagerly as she walked. Juan and Teresa smiled after the child as Jos? Sanchez finished setting up the metronome and the timer. “We’ll work on speed drills more today,” he said in his usual patient, instructor-like voice. “Speed to counter Gray Brand’s strength, and to outwit him. He’s not a technical specialist, so finesse is the best weapon against him. You won’t be able to stand toe-to-toe with the man, so don’t try it.” Juan and Teresa nodded in understanding as they began the warm-up stretching routine. Little by little, as they got into “training” mode, their minds drifted away from the events of the early morning and they focused on their usual flawless timing and coordination. Still, the thoughts hung on stubbornly to a small remote corner in the backs of their heads, and Jos? noted that their rhythm was just a little off. He noted it carefully, though he didn’t bring it up to the masked Luchadores yet. Not every training session was going to be perfect, the mentor knew, and the discrepancy was minor... minimized by the many long hours of joint training the husband-and-wife warriors had done in the ring together. GW Commissary, Las Vegas August 20, 2005; 1501 hours El Serpenti was quite pleased at how well Melina was doing with Maggie LaPortia and the new tutors she and Stuart had arranged for the child. She had taken to reading like a fish to water, he noted. She seemed fond of the Curious George series of children’s books, and Serpenti made a note to mail-order the entire set for her, as a present. Presently, Teresa came into the Commissary, tired but smiling. She’d already showered and changed after finishing the workout; she was dressed in her street clothes, but her hair was still a bit wet. Unlike most other people, she preferred to let her hair dry out naturally rather than using a blow dryer. “Hi, Teresa,” said Maggie. “Where’s Juan?” “He went to get a bite to eat,” said Teresa. “I wasn’t hungry.” Serpenti caught a glance from Teresa that told him she wanted to spend a bit of time alone with Maggie, and quickly rose to his feet, smiling down at Melina. ?Venga, unamos a tu padre,” he said. (*Let’s join your father.) Melina took Serpenti’s offered hand and went with him to the cafeteria to get a bit to eat. Teresa sat down and Margaret noted her hands were shaking. “Hey, hey...” Maggie said gently, clasping Teresa’s hands with her own. “What’s wrong” Still sore over that interview segment with Noi Tsunami?” Teresa glanced at Maggie for only a second, and then looked away. She blushed lightly under her omnipresent suntan. Maggie smiled. “Noi’s just a reporter... a media figure,” she said softly. “Stirring up controversy is just her job. She doesn’t really mean any disrespect or offense, as hard as her questions are.” “What she meant... and what she did... weren’t the same this time,” said Teresa, starting to sniffle as she lowered her head. Maggie patted Teresa’s forearm lightly. “She’s all bluster and no real bite,” she said. “Besides, we know that anything she might have been insinuating was untrue, right?” Maggie’s smile faded as she saw tears start to well up in Teresa’s eyes, spilling gradually over her bottom eyelids to track down her face. “Isn’t it?” Maggie asked again, this time with genuine concern as she touched Teresa’s chin with her hand, very lightly lifting it a few inches to get a look at her eyes. Teresa’s composure, already brittle from the argument with Juan in the kitchen that morning, crumbled under Maggie’s gentle gaze. She reached out to hold her best friend; grateful for the return hug. “Maggie... I don’t know if what we’re doing here in GW, is worth the effect it’s having on the family,” she said in between soft, muffled sobs. “Juan is so certain that fulfilling my father’s legacy is what’s keeping everything together... but he’s changed from it all. He’s not the same person I’ve always needed... who’s always needed me. Noi was right... we’re carrying a burden that’s tearing our relationship apart... and I’m starting to feel I must choose between our mission... and our family. Juan’s always been so certain that the two have always been one and the same... but it’s not that way anymore.” Maggie patted Teresa’s back gently, whispering soothing “sshhhh” sounds to try and comfort her friend. “Juan will come around,” she tried to reassure her. “Does he still really love you” And do you really love him?” “Of course I love him,” said Teresa, “but I’m beginning to feel him slipping away... as if he’s lost who I am in the identity of our tag-team.” “Do you think you’ll need some time off from GW?” Maggie asked. Teresa trembled in Maggie’s embrace. “The insult to Juan’s sense of duty... he’d never forgive me. As selfish as I feel it makes him, I couldn’t bear to hurt him in that manner. We’re the tag-team champions... the audience would forever see us as cowards if we walked away now.” Maggie carefully eased Teresa to arm’s length and looked closely into her eyes. “Can that really be worse than your marriage breaking up, unless you can work this out with him?” she asked. Teresa winced in spite of her pain. Maggie was a social worker, and along with her comforting empathy, she was capable of some pretty blunt logic. There was no way to argue her point. The question was a wake-up call Teresa couldn’t ignore. Slowly she stopped shaking and she wiped her eyes as the tears dried up. She nodded resolutely. “Thank you, Maggie,” she said. “I’ll let you know if I need your help with this... thank goodness I was able to talk to you in confidence this way. Sometimes I worry that even the walls have ears in this building...” Maggie giggled. “I know that feeling,” she said. “Stuart and I still worry and wonder when we talk here, even when the room’s empty.” As Maggie and Teresa walked out of the room to join the others at the cafeteria, they didn’t spot the reason why they had such good reason to worry and wonder. For sometimes the walls did have ears... and sometimes the room wasn’t empty. The Commissary lounge was shaped like an L... and it had doors at both ends. Maggie and Teresa had both come in, and walked out, through the same door at one end of the L, and neither had noticed the entry of another person through the other door. Nobody, including Melina and Serpenti, had seen this other person enter the Commissary to sit and relax and gaze out the nearest window. And as Maggie and Teresa exited the room, they didn’t hear the furious scribbling of pencil on notepad. Noi Tsunami never kept her pencils too sharp... the slightly blunted tips made for faster note-taking, she had always believed. And now, they also served to silence the sounds of her jotting down notes in shorthand, so she’d be able to get everything down while it was still fresh in her mind. She knew she’d really hit a big scoop this time... though she still couldn’t quite put her finger on the meat of it. But Noi was like a bloodhound in being able to sniff it out... and she wasn’t going to let this trail go cold.
