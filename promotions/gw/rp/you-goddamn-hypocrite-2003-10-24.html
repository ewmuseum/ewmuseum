You goddamn hypocrite

24 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
You goddamn hypocrite Ronnie Long Global Wrestling Promo #668 Date: 10/24/03 Location: The streets “Get the fuck up, you goddamn hypocrite…” Face turned down towards the pavement, I can’t see my attacker. But I feel his boot connect with my ribs. The impact raises me up off the ground and rolls me onto my side, where I roll over, trying to protect my face. Another boot connects with the base of my spine, and a growl of pain escapes my lips. “How dare you call yourself a hero… get your ass up, Ronnie, get the fuck up and fight back.” Something strikes me across the back. A pipe, maybe. I collapse, sprawled on my stomach in the alleyway, trying to retain consciousness. I think one of my ribs is broken, something in my side is burning as I try to force myself to my feet. “You can’t do anything, can you?” Another boot connects with my ribs, and this time I hear the crack as two of my ribs break. I can’t see what’s going on. I can’t name my attacker, although his voice sounds familiar. My trench coat, one of my personal trademarks of a sort, has become an obstacle, as my arms are tangled in it and I can’t fight back. “Get the fuck up, man! What the hell did I teach you, did that mean nothing to you? Fucking asshole! C’mon!” I feel, rather than see, this man, my unseen attacker, lift me by the neck of my coat and pull me to my feet. But this is only temporary, as he turns and runs me into a wall. I slip sideways, trying to keep my feet under me. But with my arms still caught in my coat, I fall heavily to the pavement. “Where’s your philosophy now, you dumb son of a bitch?!” Another boot to the ribs. This time, it doesn’t hurt so much. Really, it’s just the impact I feel. My whole body aches, and I’m starting to go numb. Something is tickling my wrist, and as I look, I see blood running down my arm. My palm is cut open and bleeding, from my hard landing. “I thought you came back to the GW to save the deserving. And look at you now. You’re pathetic, Ronnie. You can’t save yourself.” A tiny spark of something begins to burn in the back of my head. As I think, I notice that my latest fall had somehow caused my trench coat to unknot. I work my left arm loose as I stay lying on the ground, preparing to fight back. “You should be ashamed of yourself, Ronnie. Everything’s falling down around you, and you just lie there complacent and do nothing.” That spark? It’s burning stronger. “How much longer are you gonna do nothing? Do I have to kill you, Ron? I hear myself speak. “You can’t kill me… you aren’t strong enough.” My unseen tormenter laughs, loud and mocking. “Not strong enough? You’re lying face down on the ground. If I had a knife, I could put it in between your shoulder blades and kill you, and you couldn’t do a damn thing about it.” As the laughter continues, I slip my left arm out of its sleeve. Hiding it under my body, I get my knees under my body, preparing. “If you think I’m not strong enough, you’re a fool.” Anticipating the kick, I roll with it, rather than absorb it. The impact still rolls me all the way over… in such a way that my right arm pulls free of the trench coat. Sore and stiff, I rise to my feet, and face my attacker. The man is wearing a ski mask. Somehow, this doesn’t surprise me. Other than that, blue jeans and a black GW T-shirt. Tilting his head slightly to one side, he regards Ronnie. His expression, hidden behind the mask, is unreadable. “Finally learning to stand on your own two feet? Well, that’s progress. Unfortunately it’s too little too late.” The man pulls a gun from his pocket and points it at me. A click, and then a loud crack. A stinging pain in my chest. “RONNIE!” The voice is feminine. It cuts through the pain. And then I’m aware of someone grabbing my shoulders, shaking me. “Ronnie! Wake up!” What…? ---- I opened my eyes. Gone was my assailant. I was in my apartment, in my bedroom. Sprawled on the floor, next to a toppled nightstand. Overhead was Deanna, my girlfriend, gazing down at me with concerned eyes. “Are you OK, Ronnie? You were sleepwalking again, but you tripped over your nightstand… oh my god, you’re bleeding!” I look at my right hand. It’s cut open, presumably from the sharp corner of the dresser. My ribs also are sore, probably they took the brunt of the impact. But they’re not cracked. “It was a premonition.” Deanna looks at me. “What?” I’ll tell her about the dream later, I decide. “Deanna, do you have Christina McCoy’s phone number? I have some amends to make and a contract to renew…”
