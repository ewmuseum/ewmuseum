Going Hollywood: The Review

22 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 22, 2005 For the first time in weeks Mike felt that his career was on the right track. He had finally won the Platinum Championship and had injured Adam Myers in the process, which made the taste of success that much sweeter. The final editing of Gilligan’s Island was complete and the movie was in the middle of its limited release. The film would be in theaters everywhere on August the twelfth. Nearly overnight his popularity in the media had grown; he had appeared on several morning radio shows and late night talk shows in order to promote both the movie and himself. Mike was in such a good mood that he took his breakfast on the back porch, something he had not done in months. He had not yet showered or shaved so he still had bed-head and a scruffy face. He wore his best red silk robe that had his initials monogrammed on the right breast. As he ate his light breakfast of fruit he read the morning paper or more to the point the entertainment section. That morning’s edition contained the first review of Mike’s recent role, and it was exactly the sort of review he had hoped for: completely negative and scathing. The review was written by a man named Robert Rizzonelli and read as follows: Gilligan’s Island is the latest in a long line of horrible remakes of classic shows and movies. This movie follows the basic formula of this grotesque genre: pick a random beloved story, butcher it, add a few Hollywood stars, and then attempt to pass it off as “the funniest comedy of the year”. I can tell you right now that this poor excuse of a film is NOT the funniest comedy of the year. The movie completely does away with the character of Mrs. Howell and turns the Mr. Howell character into a young playboy that is horribly portrayed by professional wrestling star Mike Biddle. Other than that the other characters more or less remain the same, except for the fact that they all seem to have lost at least eighty points off their IQs. The plot doesn’t even revolve around the castaway’s attempts to get off the island, the only character that seems to even be concerned by the situation is the Professor (expertly played by Jason Lee). The plot is basically Gilligan trying to steal away the attention of Mary-Anne and Ginger from the horny Mr. Howell, a goal that Gilligan never achieves. The movie ends with Mr. Howell calling in his personal yacht to take him and the girls away in the middle of the night, leaving the other three castaways alone on the island. I cannot stress enough how horrible this film is, do yourself a favor and don’t see it. Mike smiled triumphantly as he glanced over on the other side of the page and saw that despite its limited release Gilligan’s Island was number seven in money made in the past week and was predicted to be number one once it was fully released. He folded the paper in half and set it down on the glass table. William’s predictions had come true. Mike’s career had nearly recovered from its brush with obscurity, his name was out in the mainstream media because of his recent film and in turn his mainstream media success had made his name more well-known. His superior name power was the reason why he was now the Platinum Champion, Myers spirit had been crushed the moment he had learned he was to face the current media darling. Now Mike only had to take that success and build upon it by advancing in the Master of the Mat tournament. He had been saddled with a worthless partner but it was only a minor inconvenience, being partnered with an idiot in a bunny suit would make second round victory that much easier. Mike leaned back in his lawn chair and took a small bite of his fruit salad. As he chewed he decided that he would spend the day at home alone, he was tired from all the running around his regained fame required of him and he just wanted to spend the day watching himself on TV. He rang the bell that summoned his servant, a few moments later the old Englishman that was his butler stood in the doorway awaiting instruction. “What can I do for you sir?” The butler asked. “If anyone asks I’m not at home,” Mike said. “Well,” he continued, “unless it’s a beautiful woman’then I am home.” “Funny you should mention that sir,” the butler said, “There is a young woman at the door saying she wishes to see you. Should I send her to you?” Mike paused a moment to consider, he wondered which one of his many female companions had come to visit him on this fine summer morning but ultimately he decided that it did not matter, the end result would be the same. “Yes,” Mike told the butler, “send her to me.” “Very well sir,” the butler said as he exited the room. Mike then set forth on finishing his breakfast while he waited for his mysterious guest, he knew what would happen next and he would need all of his strength. After he had put the last bite of his breakfast in his mouth the young lady that his butler had told him about stood in the doorway. She was a beautiful creature with pale nymph-like features, dark raven colored hair that stopped right at her elegant shoulders, and a small black dress that Mike thought would look perfect crumpled up on his floor. He didn’t recognize her at all, but at the same time there was something familiar about this woman. “You don’t recognize me do you?” the woman asked as she took a seat beside Mike. Mike looked her up and down before he responded. “Why,” he began, “should I?” The raven haired woman heaved a small sigh, an action that Mike intently watched with a great smile upon his face. “Considering everything we’ve been through you should,” she paused for dramatic effect, ?Buttermuffin.” Once she spoke the pet name he instantly recognized her. When he last saw her, her hair was blonde and she was curled up in the fetal position on his back lawn. “I should have known it was you Hillary,” he said with a disappointed voice. He continued, “After all the times that I’ve hurt you why do you insist upon coming back” What are you, some sort of glutton for pain?” “No,” she began with a defensive tone, “I was deeply in love with you and wanted you to feel the same way about me, that’s why I kept coming back.” “Why are you talking in the past tense?” Mike curiously asked. “Because I’m over you,” Hillary replied with a bright smile. Upon hearing her reply Mike responded by rolling his dark blue eyes in deep annoyance. “You came all the way over here just to tell me that” What part of I don’t care about you don’t you understand?” Mike crossed his arms and leaned back in his chair and began to wonder when this annoying woman would be out of his life. Hillary rose from her seat with a satisfied look on her nymph-like features. “I just thought that you should know that I’m not going to waste my valuable time with a jackass like you anymore,” she said as she walked away. As she left she felt more than satisfied with the way things had gone, not much longer and Mike would be begging for her company. Mike watched as she exited through the doorway and sighed in relief. He was glad that Hillary had finally gotten the hint but a small part of him was sad that he would no longer be able to break her heart on a weekly basis. “Oh well,” he thought to himself, “I have more important things to do than drag that silly woman through hell. I’ve gone Hollywood, which means that there’ll be more women just like her.” And with that Mike picked up the paper to read the review of his movie yet again. -The End-
