The Hunt Begins: Part One, Survival of the Fittest

27 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/27/2004 I sat in the shadows, not for theatrical effect, although, truth be told, I did have a gothic streak at least a mile wide. It was comfortable here; I found the last rays of the rapidly fading sun still too bright, even with the dark glasses that shielded my eyes. Another stone edifice? another part of the same city, not that you could tell. Here, the gaudiness of modern times had crept in, and as I lingered here, in the shadows where the roof of the old section met the new, with the faded old neon sign buzzing below me, I thought again of corruption. It seemed that idea kept coming back to my mind, I somehow couldn’t shake the notion that everything was tied inextricably to? her. I saw the clouds rolling in along the horizon, watching them gather overhead. Let it rain. Let the sky reflect my dark mood, frankly, I was well beyond caring at this point. But still, I couldn’t let go. There were things I needed to do? things I still had to clear up, but I was unwilling to do so. Somehow, even then, I knew she would come. I had heard the whispers, when they hadn’t known I was listening? the ripples and eddies were still fanning out from my cleverly dropped pebble. Dante hadn’t died, as I had initially suspected- and hoped, instead he had gone quite mad. He was locked up now, rather like Renfield in that Dracula movie that had starred Gary Oldman? I rather liked the image of my old friend eating insects while he muttered to himself. Quite fitting, for a bottom-feeding leech like himself. I know what people would say if they heard me now, such harsh words for my best friend? a man I had loved like my own brother. That was then, in what seemed like a lifetime ago. I was so na’ve. Still a child, really, even though I was nearly thirty. What use did I have for friends now? Compassion and loyalty meant nothing to me now? I was above it all? above the law. I could kill with impunity? I could strike them all down. I could pour out the fires of my wrath upon the earth, exacting my own brand of revenge. And that is what I planned to do. I could do that which had been denied me for so long? I could kill. Revenge, a dish best served cold? and let me tell you, you couldn’t get much colder than death. The buzzing neon tubes drew my attention for a moment. Although most of the bulbs were either broken or burned out, I still knew what it said. It was a part of my past; this place brought back memories that held no feelings for me now. It was like looking at a scrapbook of faded snapshots, the images were still there, but they meant nothing. Out of context, out of focus? faded and aged. The broken old neon sign had been here as long as I could remember? it used to read Jesus Saves. Reminded me of song lyrics, but I couldn’t place the song? some band long forgotten. What a crock of shit, to believe in an unseen deity? if God was love, why did he allow war, famine, and so much pain? I knew now, either he was a sadist, or he just didn’t give a shit? either that, or perhaps he didn’t even exist at all. Maybe He was just a lie, the ultimate good? and an ultimate evil in one? the mother of all lies to keep the cattle that populated this planet in line. A way of controlling the weak willed, and feeble minded. Feeble minds? as though taking a queue from my feverish brain, the sounds beneath me grew more orderly; the meeting of the elders was in session. Ah, now I would find the keys to the questions that haunted me? ---- Twelve chairs sat around the oblong table, ten of them filled with men, each face reflecting the anxiety that seemed to thrum through the room, charging the atmosphere. Identical faces, identical garb, each man was dressed in dark robes more befitting monks. The man at the head of the table stood slowly, and the idle chatter in the room faded away immediately as his dark eyes swept around the room, meeting the gazes of each of his assembled brethren. “Gentlemen,” he began, as he nervously raked a hand through his disheveled gray hair, “it appears that we have a problem.” A murmur of assent flowed through the room like an electric current, further charging the tension. He paused, collecting his thoughts, his attention fixed on the lanky man to his right. His name was Gabriel Delacroix? he was the CID’s most powerful psychic. The leader cleared his throat, and Gabriel looked up sharply, his gaze the only one in the room not filled with a low-simmering terror. His strange eyes flashed like a cat’s; impossibly amber, and a naked hostility seemed to ooze from those windows to the soul as he simply glared at the leader. “Gabe,” the leader prompted, “perhaps you could share with us what you know?” Gabriel shrugged his shoulders, and leaned back in the chair, propping his feet up on the lacquered surface of the table. He folded his arms across his chest, and looked down his long, aquiline nose at the leader, his expression full of disdain. “If everyone had read my report, it wouldn’t be necessary.” Another man further down the table spoke up, his voice quavering, his demeanor that of a turtle, peeking cautiously out of its shell. His thick glasses were fogged with perspiration, and his voice had the brittle quality of ancient glass. “Please, do tell us Gabriel. I, myself, didn’t have time to read it, having just returned from Amsterdam this morning.” Gabe didn’t reply, instead he turned his unsettling gaze on the bespectacled elder, causing him to duck back in his seat, the turtle returning to his shell. When he spoke, his voice was loud, full of irritation, commanding the attention of the entire room. “I wasn’t talking to you, Zacharias. Fine, if I must” I’ll enlighten you lazy bastards.” Gabriel rose, his olive complexion darkening with his sullen anger. He crossed to the large stained glass window that covered most of one wall, and looked down on the filthy street, watching the shadows lengthen as the sun sunk over the horizon, trying to quash the feeling of foreboding as they loomed like smears of blood, distorted by the colored glass. Standing here, the object of their hopeful gazes, he felt like a bug under glass, precisely like he had his entire life. He heaved a sigh, and turned back to the collective, his eyes glittering strangely as he placed his gloved hands on the tabletop and leaned forward. “To say we simply have a problem is the understatement of the year. Jackson, as we had initially believed, is not dead. The coffin we buried was empty” and that is something that after this moment shall never be spoken of again outside these chambers. We now believe,” he cast a sidelong glance at the leader, who shook his head, “ok, I know that he is alive. Moreover, he isn’t quite what he used to be” I? cannot say for certain just what he has become?” Gabriel trailed off, his expression growing troubled. A man at the opposite end of the table cleared his throat, slamming his fist down on the table, startling those around him. He shook his head, his shock of red hair glimmering in last, dying rays of sun that slanted across the room. “Then we should kill him! Now, before any more are harmed!” Gabriel glared at the man. “Have you gone mad, Artemis” We cannot kill him. For thousands of years, we have simply observed? and you would break that code now? On a whim?” Artemis roared with anger and leapt to his feet, spots of livid color forming on his cheeks, spittle spraying as he bellowed. “What about Dante”! Should that offence go unpunished?! Well, what say you, Gabriel? Has it been decided that you play God now?” Gabriel stood there for a moment, his eyes narrowed to dangerous slits, and then he pulled off one of his gloves, slowly, one finger at a time. In a voice that was low and dangerous, he addressed Artemis once again. “Sit. The. Fuck. Down. Artemis. Do not make me do something I will regret. Dante’s plight is the least of our concerns, and revenge is nothing compared to the catastrophe that will befall us if those relics are not recovered!” “Relics” You mean the ones the girl stole?” It was Gabriel’s turn to slam his fist on the table, and when his naked flesh struck the wood, it was as though a tuning fork had hit the table. A sharp sound vibrated through the room, rattling the glass in the windows, making everyone wince. “Of course I mean those relics, you imbecile! Do you have any idea what she took” Huh? Do you?” “No, Gabriel, I don’t. And neither do you” the only man who knew was Dante, and those secrets are forever lost in the sea of madness.” Gabriel shook his head, holding up his hand. “Artemis, have you forgotten, my brother, just what skills I bring to this round table” Dante’s secrets are now my own. She has the Reaver. Klinge der Schw’rzung.” Artemis blinked, taken aback as he fell back into his chair. “But, that is simply myth, is it not” It’s impossible.” The leader placed one hand on Gabriel’s shoulder, halting his hot retort. When he spoke again, it was with a tone of sadness. “Gabriel speaks with veracity. The Reaver is real. And it is missing. We believe that Jackson may have it in his possession, as it was not recovered among his belongings. If he does, indeed, have the Reaver, he poses more a threat than we ever imagined possible. Especially now that he has gone rogue.” Gabriel turned, looking at the ashen features of the leader of the council of elders. “Matthias, what will we do” What can we do?” The leader, Matthias shook his head, tears shimmering in his eyes. “We shall break the oath. He must be found” he must be killed. The Reaver must be recovered and destroyed at all costs.” “No, God no,” Gabriel shook his head violently, trying to negate the words even as they penetrated his haze of righteous anger? ---- It was right about then that the decision was made for me, I would kill? and I would start with them. ---- Gabriel saw it all, in a flash, moments before it happened, and that’s how he was able to escape. He ducked low, hiding effortlessly behind one of the tall leather chairs as the stained glass window imploded in a spray of deadly glass. Matthias went down screaming, his face torn to shreds, glass embedded in his eyes. A dark figure seemed to fly in the window, seconds behind the glass, and as he hit the floor, he was instantly recognizable. Jackson. He was dressed all in black, from head to toe, and a long leather coat covered his clothes, specked with rainbow bits of glass. The twin semi-automatic pistols he held in his hands made even Artemis reconsider attacking. “So,” he drawled, sporting his familiar lopsided grin, with the very fires of hell blazing in his eyes, “it comes down to this. Well, what are you waiting for, sheep” Kill me. Come on, take your best shot!” Not a soul moved, a pin could have dropped, and it would have been deafening. Jackson threw his head back and bellowed laughter that sounded forced. Before anyone had a chance to react, he opened fire, gunning them all down in a spray of blood and gore. Gabriel stayed on the floor, partially covered by Matthias? now limp corpse, his strange eyes wide and staring. Jackson let the guns fall from his hands, and then spoke aloud, his voice impossibly loud in the sudden silence. “I’ll tell you something that will never again leave this chamber” the rest of you. This madness stops here. Watchers no more? I am a man of action, now? a hunter. Are you satisfied, Gabriel? Your precious code is intact, none of you had to kill? keep watching, Gabriel? keep your eyes open, I’ll be coming for you soon.” And with that, he was gone, from whence he came. Gabriel pushed the corpse aside, sobbing with revulsion as he sprang to his feet, covered in blood and gore that was not his own. How? How had Jackson known he was not dead? And why hadn’t he finished the job? he had clearly meant to kill the elders, so why leave one behind? It didn’t make sense? ---- I suppose the question of the day is why didn’t I kill him? Why, when I knew he was there, alive and breathing, didn’t I put a bullet in his brain? Perhaps I ran out of bullets? maybe I grew soft, lost my will? or maybe that’s none of your fucking business. Gabriel is alive? and am I, neither of us really human. Perhaps I felt an odd kinship with him? or maybe I just wanted to know Dante’s secrets. Either way, he could come in handy? besides, I needed the practice? killing him for sport would be much more entertaining?
