Shopping takes too much time!

10 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/10/2005 City of Dallas February 10, 2005 1305 hours 'Rent is $625 a month... plus utilities and gas,' said the landlord as he showed Juan, Teresa and Melina Ramirez around the comfy two-bedroom apartment in downtown Dallas. 'No smoking in the halls, and if you have a dog, he doesn't leave the apartment without a leash. And if he makes a mess anywhere inside the building or on the curb around it, you clean it up.' 'We don't smoke, and we don't have a dog,' said Juan, flashing a slight smile at some of the seemingly frivolous terms the landlord was presenting. Still, he and Teresa understood the old tenet all too well: As long as you live in my house, you live by my rules.' They hadn't always done what their own parents wanted them to do, nor did Melina always do what they wanted HER to do. One day Melina would undoubtedly have to assert this old adage with her own children, they were certain. 'Children love dogs, though,' said the landlord, looking a little wistfully at Melina as he said it. The septuagenarian man had raised two of his own to adulthood; in fact, his eldest grandson was a little older than Melina. Both of his children and their families had at least one dog, and both dogs were treated as members of their family. Melina fidgeted uncomfortably under the landlord's gaze, even though she was able to tell that he was friendly. To the child, he was still a stranger, and she didn't want to talk to strangers. She tugged lightly on Teresa's hand. 'Mamacita, “podemos ir a casa ahora?' she asked plaintively. (*Mommy, can we go home now?) Teresa looked down in surprise and then kneeled down to give Melina a reassuring hug. 'Esto es nuestro hogar nuevo, mi querida,' she said, and smiled. 'El hombre agradable apenas le pens? quiz’s quiera un perro pronto.' (*This is our new home, darling. The nice man just thought you might like a dog soon.) The elderly landlord squatted down in front of Teresa and Melina, showing he was in surprisingly limber and fit condition for his age. '¿No quieres los perros, preciosa?' he asked in Spanish. 'Mis nietos tratan sus terrieres como otro hermano o la hermana.' (*You don't like dogs? My grandchildren treat their terriers like another brother or sister.) Juan smiled at the man. 'You speak Spanish,' he said, both surprised and impressed. Once she warmed up to him, Melina would have at least one person to talk to in the building. 'Lot of folks speak Spanish in Dallas,' laughed the man. 'You think that's a surprise here, try Miami. You don't speak Spanish in MIAMI, you've got problems.' He chuckled lightly, 'You've been very nice to us, Mr. Harrison,' said Teresa, rising to her feet. 'The apartment is nicer even than where I grew up in Mexico City, even if a little smaller. The view is beautiful.' 'And the walls are all soundproofed,' said Mr. Harrison, winking slyly. 'My Daddy made sure of it when he bought this property way back when. Radio was in its infancy then, and he had the good sense to know that people would like to listen to music without being distracted by what their neighbors were listening to.' Juan and Teresa looked at each other, each giving a small smile. Mr. Harrison had guessed they were fans of salsa music, which was usually played at an elevated volume. 'We have some paperwork that will arrange the first three months payment,' said Juan, pulling an envelope out of his jacket pocket. 'It just needs our signatures, and then yours, and you can bring them to your bank. They'll process the papers and see that you get paid the proper rent for the first three months. Our agent can answer any questions you might have.' Mr. Harrison raised his eyebrows. This was a bit unusual to him, though he wasn't adverse to it. 'Show biz folks, are ya?' he inquired conversationally. 'I've had one or two tenants in the field, although most are regular blue-collar working folks that write out good old-fashioned money orders.' He smiled. 'You could say that,' said Teresa, chuckling lightly. 'We're new employees for the wrestling promotion, Global Wrestling. The Global Arena is a twenty-minute drive from here.' 'You don't say,' said Mr. Harrison, whistling low. 'Might not want to say that too loud 'round here, young lady. Lot of kids in the neighborhood watch Evolution every Monday. You might attract more attention than you'd care for, especially when you just want to have a quiet family dinner.' Juan and Teresa both laughed. 'Quiet family dinner, we WISH,' said Juan. 'There aren't too many of those in Mexico City, when you work in Lucha Libre.' Mr. Harrison handed a small key-ring with three keys on it to Teresa. 'Small brass one is for the mailbox,' he said. 'Large silvery one opens the door to the apartment. Large brass one opens the lobby door. Two Guys Locksmiths three blocks from here will make copies of them for you, cheap. Welcome to the building.' He offered a small piece of orange candy to Melina. Teresa and Juan shook hands with Mr. Harrison, and then Teresa realized Melina was partially hiding behind her. She stepped aside and smiled, pointing to the piece of candy in the elderly man's hands. 'Pase, Melina,' she said. 'Diga gracias al Se’or Harrison.' (*Go on. Say thank you to Mr. Harrison.) Melina stretched out her hand slowly, almost timidly; before taking the piece of candy. Her expression changed at the pleasant orange taste, and she cracked her first smile at him. 'Gracias, Se’or Harrison,' she said, swirling the candy around her mouth with her tongue. Mr. Harrison chuckled and ruffled the child's hair, and for the first time, she didn't flinch. 'You can move your things in whenever you want,' he said to Juan and Teresa. 'We won't be bringing much from Mexico,' said Teresa. 'Much of what we'll be bringing in, we'll be buying anew.' She looked around the apartment and cracked a grin. 'Shopping takes too much time. The place will be spartanly furnished for a little while.' 'Ask my wife to bring you shopping; you'll need to spend the night at the local Wal-Mart,' laughed Mr. Harrison. 'I'll let you get on with your business, then. Welcome to the building, once more.' GW Arena February 11, 2005 1442 hours 'And this will be your locker room,' said Stuart, showing it to Juan and Teresa. 'Allow me.' He smiled and placed a shiny brass name-plaque onto the door... it read in bold script: LA HERENCIA DE HEREDEROS. Teresa smiled at Juan, who nodded and smiled back. 'I think that means we're open for business,' he joked to his wife. 'You debut on the Evolution for February 14th,' he said. 'You face The Connection. Have you been studying the tapes I brought you?' Teresa's face set in rigid lines as she nodded. 'From New York City... Zack Johnson and Joshua Curtis. Zack's the technician, lots of joint locks and power moves. Joshua's the high-flyer, makes daring leaping attacks and uses some submission holds. Not too dissimilar from Juan and myself, come to think of it.' Stuart grinned sardonically. 'Except in one major manner...' he paused and whispered something into Teresa's ear, and then Juan's. Both Ramirez spouses arched their brows in a bit of surprise. 'Doesn't offend you, does it?' asked Stuart. Juan and Teresa looked at each other for several long seconds before turning back to Stuart. 'A little, yes,' Juan admitted, 'because we're devout Catholics and it's condemned by our Church. Catholicism is very strong in Mexico. I would hope they don't put their hands on my person in an unfitting fashion. I am a married man, with a wife.' Stuart shook his head. 'Don't worry about that. The only people they're interested in, in that manner, is each other. Try not to preach the Bible too much to them, okay? They're here in GW to wrestle, and to win. Just like you two.' Teresa nodded. 'I don't like it much, personally, but I understand what you mean by being here to wrestle and win. We can treat them with the same respect that would be due any competitor here, and make it all business in the ring.' 'That's what I was hoping to hear,' said Stuart with a small smile. 'Be here at the arena a half hour early. I'm trying to schedule you for a debut interview with Noi Tsunami on Evolution.' 'Noi's really aggressive in pursuing a story,' grinned Jos?, coming back from the concession stand with Melina. 'Don't let her intimidate you... when she gets in your face, get right back in hers. That will show everyone you mean business.' Stuart nodded and patted Teresa on the shoulder. 'What he said. Be here a half hour early, okay? Impress Noi behind the mic, then impress the crowd inside the ring, and you're off to a good start.' Juan and Teresa nodded to each other and pressed both fists against each other's. 'Herencia de Herederos,' they said in unison; a kind of team mantra, Stuart surmised. Stuart gave them a last smile, and said, 'Okay, that's it for now. Locker room is set, your debut's set. You have free reign until Monday night.' As Stuart walked away, Jos? nodded to Juan and Teresa. 'New Yorkers,' he said, speaking of Joshua and Zack. 'They say the Big Apple has tough worms.' He grinned. Teresa's brow furrowed. 'Pardon me?' Jos? chuckled. 'New York City's called the Big Apple. You know the old clich? about worms inside apples.' 'Ah,' said Teresa, and both she and Juan smiled. 'Well, tough worms help to hook big fish.' Juan nodded to his wife, and then turned to Melina. '¿Te gustas comer las manzanas, verdad?' he asked. (*You like eating apples, right?) Melina gave a small smile and nodded. 'S?, Papa,' she said eagerly, and they all laughed. Because her parents and Jos? laughed, Melina did too.
