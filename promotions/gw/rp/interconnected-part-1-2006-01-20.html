Date: Week Of 1-20-06 Bar At Side Of Road Tuesday Night He needed a drink. Something to burn down his throat like the Texas sun over the scorched earth in the middle of Summer. He ponyed up to the bar, letting the extra weight hang over his belt and be separated by the brass rail, trying to force him away while at the same time Fat, sweaty finger pushed the stringy hair back up over the huge sign of mediocrity he tried to constantly cover with the poor comb over. “Tequila from the bottom shelf.” His voice full of need and despair. Everything for this man had gone wrong. He was alone among the other sheep, but he felt like the Taiwan pig with fluorescent skin at the moment. The wolves could smell the sweaty pork, and they wanted it more than the lambs. Mike Harris was a simple man. He went to work everyday and then went home to the dingy little town home he saved up twenty years to buy. A few simple pulls on the pud while drooling on the keyboard before some Internet goddess counted as high entertainment to Mr. Harris. Now’now he had no clue what had led him here. He was simply an accountant, a man who kept track of profit and loss for some horrible little hovels in the worst parts of town. He made deposits and kept records, nothing more. The bartender put the shot glass in front of him. The tequila smelled foul, like rubbing alcohol someone had decided to piss in for color. He threw the liquid into the back of his mouth, realizing it tasted just like what he had imagined. Mike threw two dollars down. “Did you want another?” The bartender asked. Mike looked up, “What” Shots are two dollars.” “Look, that shit is so cheap it’s only a dollar a shot man. I wouldn’t even use it to clean blood stains off the floor.” He gave the man a sour face. Mike didn’t care that he thought him pathetic. He wiped his sweaty hands on his stained white button-up. He pulled out a five-dollar bill and tossed it onto the bar. “Give me four more shots and take two dollars for your sparkling fucking attitude.” The bartender grabbed the cash. He went to pour a line of shots for the fat, ugly man. Some guys just had those days, and he guessed he was having one. Mike tried to figure out what the Hell had happened. Strip Mall In Dallas Tuesday Afternoon The temperature was back up again into the eighties, and they hadn’t seen any precipitation for twenty-eighth days. That meant it was hot and dry. It also meant that because of the budget he was forced to operate under he didn’t have the air-conditioner on even though it was too warm. Mike didn’t care. He wiped away the sweat building on his brow with the small washcloth. Relieved for a brief moment, he went right back to work on the financial records. He had just made the daily drop at the banks, spreading the money into various accounts. The money was, well, at least from a pure standpoint everything he had been provided said it was legal. He knew better, considering who his clients were, but sometimes a small lie to yourself made it all so much easier. Doris was angry with him, which was not unusual. The old, perpetually annoyed secretary was like a spider sitting on her web, waiting for someone to get taught so she could devour them. She wanted the air-conditioner turned on, but he had refused. He offered to let her go home for the day’without pay of course’and she had shut-up. Dried up old bitch should feel lucky I put up with her? He heard the front door open, and a familiar voice. “He back there?” “He can’t be disturbed right now Jamal.” Doris at least did that much of her job. “Shut up bitch.” The door was thrown open, and Jamal was standing in front of him looking very dodgy. Doris trailed behind to stop him until he spoke, “Motherfucker, where’s my money?” Doris stopped, returning to her desk to be sure she didn’t become involved in this. Mike set his paperwork down, “I’the’in the banks! I put the money’what happened?” “Nothing happened you fucking pussy.” Jamal leaned down onto the desk, “Where’s the money from last night?” “I just dropped it off to the banks.” “I need some money!” Jamal yelled. Mike looked Jamal up and down. He was tall, lanky but with quite a bit of strength. He had a shaved head and cold, black eyes. Mike noticed now he was sweating too, and there were some red spots on his white jersey. “What’s going on Jamal?” Mike braved the direct question. Jamal turned back, looking at the entrance to the office. He shut the door and locked it before speaking, “Nothing, I just need some money to get out of town for a little while.” “The accounts are all in your mother’s name. Just have her go in and make a withdrawl.” Mike explained. “That won’t work.” Jamal looked down at him. The bravado was broken when he saw the hesitation in the man’s eyes. “She’s’I thinks he’s being watched.” “The cops?” Now the sweat was coming from everywhere as his stomach twisted into a tight knot. “The cops are onto us?” “These guys weren’t Five-Oh.” Jamal shook his head as he remembered something. “Who are they?” “Shut-up!” Jamal was pacing frantically. “Just, give me some fucking money!” “I don’t have any money Jamal. I put it all in your accounts.” Jamal pulled a pistol out from his back. He pointed the gleaming steel pistol down at Mike, “Fuck you! You think I’m stupid! I know you skim from me! Give me that money!” Mike lost control, feeling the warm liquid spread out in his crotch. He was crying now, and words came out as he blubbered his response. “No, I don’t do that! Jamal’p-p-p-lease?” “Where’s my money!”!” “The b-b-b-ank.” Jamal’s eyes flashed angrily. “Quit being a faggot!” The ring of the front door froze Jamal. “Who the fuck are you meeting with?” Mike frantically shook his head, “No one!” “Fuck, I’m outta here!” Jamal ran past Mike, and took the back door to the hallway leading to the back parking lot. Mike began to bring his shaking under control, but then the office door came flying off the hinges. He looked up, seeing something that made sure rest of his bladder emptied and his ass began prairie dogging. The man in the doorframe was bigger than anyone he had ever seen. Not fat, but wide as two men with muscles on his arms bigger than Mike’s own fat legs. Long black hair hung around his face, and the green eyes looking out at him were what made the shaking start again. “Where is Jamal?” The tone made sure he couldn’t be ignored. Mike just pointed to the back door. The man pointed down at him, “You wait right here.” The huge man disappeared, and Mike was staring at the empty outer office. Doris had abandoned him, and as he sat at his desk he wondered what was happening. Mike kept looking around his office, and then he heard the pops from far away. They could have been written off to backfires, but he had lived in this part of town long enough to know better. Mike grabbed his keys and ran. Bar At Side Of Road Tuesday Night Mike was reaching for his fourth shot of tequila, when a huge hand grabbed hold of his forearm, wrist and hand. He stared down at this monster paw, afraid when he looked up he would see the face that made him wet himself in his office. “No man should have to drink that shit. Let me buy you a real drink.” The deep bass voice said. The voice was different than the one he remembered. He looked up into strong green eyes, and something familiar. The man’s angular face and shaved head seemed like he had seen it before. The blue jeans and black shirt with “TWC” surrounded by barbed wire seemed right at home in this bar. Mike pulled his hand away, “You think this is bad?” To emphasize his point, the man swept the full shot glasses away. They fell behind the bar, breaking loudly. The bartender wasn’t happy. “Hey!” The bartender came walking over. “What the Hell do you think?” “The man’s look stopped him cold. “Set up some real tequila shots, not this cheap shit and nothing you pissed in to water it down.” The man waited for a moment while the bartender stood there, shell shocked. “NOW!” The bartender quickly pulled up an unopened bottle of Patron Silver. He ripped off the plastic seal, and poured them each a shot of the premium tequila. The stranger picked his shot glass up and threw it back. Mike did the same. The premium tequila was much smoother, still powerful yet with a smoother delivery. “See, isn’t that better?” “Much.” Leave the bottle.” The man demanded. The bartender did, then walked away. He was talking frantically with one of the men at the end of the bar. “They’uh’know you here?” Mike asked, realizing he may not be in a safe place. “They know me everywhere.” “Really?” Mike watched him pour another shot. He carefully poured, making sure not to lose any of the premium liquor. “I’m a man of a certain reputation Mike.” Mike had been in mid-shot when he gagged. He coughed, spitting the liquor out on to the bar and himself. The man shook his head, “Don’t waste the tequila, it’s good stuff. I know the problem, I never introduced myself. My name is Pitt, Pitt Hix.”
