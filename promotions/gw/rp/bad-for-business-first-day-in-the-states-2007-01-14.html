Bad for Business/First Day in the States

14 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Bad for Business/First Day in the States Reginald Atkins Global Wrestling Promo #2932 Date: 1/14/07 and 1997 Location: Dallas and Connecticut We start off today’s debut episode with darkness. The video component of our feed is switched off, although the audio is running fine, as we can tell from the light tinkering noises we can hear. Maybe this offers some sort of symbolism, how the maker of this promotional video is going to be competing in a dark match for his first taste of Global Wrestling action. Or maybe it’s just a simple method with which most videos start. In any case, we hear a strong, British voice over the darkness. “Now, I’ve never been the brain of a large organization. So I’m not sure how much weight my words will hold.” It seems they’re still fiddling with the video, as we’re not getting anyt…wait, here it is. White words appear on the black screen. Reginald Atkins While this new development appears, our British voice in the background continues. “But seriously. You get this new bloke who promises you ratings and lolly, and all you’ve got to do is put him in a few matches, have him kick arse, and be done with it. “But what do I get?” The words begin to fade, slowly, and are quickly and almost rudely replaced with the face of Reginald Atkins, his lip curled in an angry sneer. “Bugger-all.” The camera takes this opportunity to pull out, focusing on his entire body. For those of you who were in the bathroom for his first appearance on Fusion, Reginald Atkins is a tall and muscular man whose body has two main spots of interest: his chest and his face. His chest is bare, and the long scar across it is a point of focus. His face is also a point of focus, and not just because the face is what you usually look at on a person. His hair is dark and slightly messy, formed into a hairstyle commonly and degradingly known as a “faux-hawk.” There is another scar along his right cheek, under his eye. The final thing noticed about him is his eyes: rather, that there are two different colors. The right is a clear blue, but the left is a murky grayish color. A pair of sharp angled sunglasses are perched on his nose. Atkins is wearing a pair of black striped slacks and dress shoes to even out the arrangement. He wipes his hand across his body, quickly. “Before I continue, I want everyone to know I understand that it’s cliché for a new grappler to get in a bate about not being recognized for his immense talent and all that rubbish. But I’m not talking about my immense talent. I’m talking about the businessman side of it. It’s just bad business.” The production assistants for this new superstar have employed an interesting tactic for the setting. Instead of showing a background in the studio, or maybe shooting this in an active or lively location, they have surrounded Reginald Atkins with complete darkness. It’s a curious move, and it makes it look weird when our focus moves suddenly, which is what he’s doing now. Throwing his arms up in the air, Atkins moves a few steps backwards and begins to pace silently. After a few seconds of this, he stops and addresses us once more. “I mean, you saw the intro. I came in with a bang, so to speak. Or whatever onomatopoeia you make when you slam staff members into walls. Hm. “Big buzz, big glory…and you don’t see me fight. Well, some of you do. The arena faithful will get to see this so-called Charity Case, as I’ve been dubbed. But you other poor buggers and bints who are slobbering over your donuts and cheese puffs at home…you don’t even get to see the big sign Ms. Sights pulled off. Not yet anyway.” Reginald Atkins shakes his head once more and lets loose a long sigh, then repeats himself. “It’s just bad business. “Not just for me, either. Just on principle. If you have a new hire, hot or no, do you just let them roast away in the untelevised part of your program? No, you showcase them. You let them test their mettle on the meat of the show, not in a setting so that only a few thousand people can tell their office mates about it the next day.” Reginald offers a small smile at this, then dusts his hands in a metaphorical fashion. “Ah well. No matter. Nothing I can do but make the best of what I’ve been given.” His smile grows wider, and opens his arms widely across his body. “And here’s what I’ve been given. A three-way match against Chris Collins and a man named Anguish. I assume that the point of this poorly-constructed idea is that the winner is given a chance to shine in front of television cameras while the losers continue to try their best while no one watches or cares. Well, I made my way in this place to do more than impress five thousand hicks. I’m here to make a mark. “So to my opponents. Collins, you didn’t defeat Tommy Osbourne. I didn’t see the match, because it wasn’t on TV.” At this, Reginald clears his throat in an obvious manner and raises his eyebrows. “But I’m just going to guess that if you’re able to be defeated, knowing my talent, I might not have too hard of a time. This Anguish person is more of a mystery, if only because he’s as new as I am. So since I know little about my opponents, I’ll talk about something I know a great deal about: myself. “I am not overly modest. I know my armament. I know what I’m capable of. And my past history has led me to believe not many blokes can compete with me. Not with my technical prowess, not with my desire to win, and not with my ability to get out of some…should I say…sticky situations with my hand raised in victory.” Lowering his hands, Atkins laughs lowly and backs off a few steps, still giving the camera an amused stare. “So that’s what I have. This is how I’ll fight back against Ms. Sights’ terrible business move. By showing her why she shouldn’t have done it.” And as he lets the pause grow, Reginald’s body begins to fade into the black background, and we are left with what we started with. But not for long. Fading in, we find ourselves going through a speedy tour of a lavish house. The time, we can tell, is sometime in the year 1997, as we can tell by the bold graphic proclaiming 1997 on the bottom. We begin in the main hallway, a room typically used by wealthy homeowners to impress the guests of theirs who were consigned to wait for their host. This home is no exception, as there are many antiques and items of note on mantels and hung on the walls. There is a large map that appears to be made of animal hide, and judging by the appearance it is quite old. There is also a large blue vase with red jewels sitting upon the far mantel against the wall. On the side of the mantel there is a large spiraling staircase, and it is here we are heading. Melting into a shot halfway up the stairs, we see some pictures hung on the wall, further designed to impress. The first is a framed dollar bill, presumably the starting profit of a company of some sort. A few feet above it is a picture of a thin-framed, slightly frazzled man with a faint smile being presented a plaque by a rotund man in a suit and top hat. The plaque is too small in the picture to make out. Another melt brings us into a hallway, where the camera cautiously pans across. We hear a soft mumbling from down the hallway, and as we approach the open door the mumbles become audible and coherent. “Yes, we’ll just offer them a buyout and use their former employees as leverage. Oh, come now, they’ll break easily when they see what we’ve prepared. Now finish the deal and get back to me in fifteen minutes. And I don’t want to hear anything but a success story, you understand? Good. Carry on.” As the doorway comes into full view, we can see the man behind the voice, sitting at a desk and hastily organizing paperwork. He is the same man from the pictures, although that must have been from a time where both the days and the metabolism were nicer to him. He still has a thin frame, we can see as he stands, but there is a paunch at his midsection drooping out. He sighs and looks in our direction, seeing the camera. Man: “What is it, Richard?” A voice from behind the camera answers loudly, made even louder because he is so near the speakers of the camera. Richard: “The two boys are pulling into the parking garage, sir.” The man on camera changes at this – it seems this news is a surprise. Man: “What boys?” Richard: “The ones you pledged to adopt, Mr. Jetters.” If the statement before surprised him, this more recent one full-on shocks him. Grabbing his desk for support, Mr. Jetters shakes his head and looks at Richard behind the camera, visibly deciding whether to be more confused or angry. Jetters: “Adopt? Explain this.” Richard: “Well, sir, you signed for it…” It seems that the angry has won out, as Mr. Jetters clinches the desk and tilts his head sideways, as if trying to process all of this information. Jetters: “I sign a multitude of things, Richard. Refresh the specifics.” Richard: “W-well, sir, last July at the annual Landfounder’s Banquet, you and three of your friends from the country club signed a form putting you on the parent list for delinquent boys from other countries. And two boys became available, and your location was considered the ideal fit for them.” Mr. Jetters pauses for a second, then looks up suddenly, hopeful. Jetters: “Wait…Brian Soundtree was on that list too. Why didn’t one go to him?” Richard risks a slight chuckle before answering. Richard: “Mr. Soundtree requested only boys from Spain. These two are from Germany and the United Kingdom, respectfully, sir.” Jetters forms his hand into a fist and shakes his head. Jetters: “Brian, you bastard…okay, let’s go down and meet them. Oh, and Richard…what’s with the camera?” The camera moves very quickly at this, coming back to focus on Richard’s face, which is a round, pudgy one with rosy cheeks and a ever-present smile, then moves it back to Jetters. Richard: “This is for memories, sir. I assume that Lyell and Reginald will want to have their first day at Jetters Estate forever at their disposal. So remember to look happy. They won’t want to see their new dad look upset at their arrival.” Jetters takes a look directly at the camera, then raises a questioning brow. Jetters: “Is it on record right now?” Richard: “No sir. It’s on standby.” At this (false) news we see our first smile from Mr. Jetters, as he claps his hands and walks towards the camera. Jetters: “Okay then, let’s go meet our troubled youths, shall we?” The camera quickly and roughly cuts to outside of the manor, and Richard uses the camera to pan over the front lawn. Richard must have come outside before Mr. Jetters, because the door opens from the house about 50 yards away and walk outside, cheery and fake smile on his face. The camera pans to the front of the circle driveway, where two teenagers stand awkwardly. As Jetters walks towards them, so does Richard, capturing it all. Mr. Jetters walks up to the two teens and matches their awkward stance. Jetters eyes the taller one, who is taller than Jetters himself. Then, reaching his hand out, he takes their hand in turn. Jetters: “Hello, my friends. Reginald Atkins, is it? And you, the big one…Lyell…Freivogel? Fair enough. My name is Yancey Jetters. And you’re going to be living here from now on.” There is no discernible response from either of the two boys. Jetters looks unsure of what to do next, so he turns to Richard. Jetters: “Turn the camera off, I’m sure that’ll be enough.” Thankfully for us, Richard doesn’t obey his boss and lets the camera rest, at an angle where we can still see most of our focuses. Jetters immediately snaps back into his previous mode, turning his nose up. Jetters: “Well then, now that that’s quite out of the way. You boys are free to roam where you want and do what you want. There’s food to eat and space to play on, and no reason to really bother Mr. Jetters at all. Understood?” The smaller one, a younger Reginald Atkins, nods dutifully. Lyell makes no move. Jetters: “Do you understand me, Lyell?” Lyell still makes no motion, and Reginald looks from his teenage counterpart to his new father. Reginald: “He’s from Germany.” Jetters: “So?” Reginald: “So he can’t speak English.” Mr. Jetters looks towards us, presumably at Richard, and shrugs his shoulders. Jetters: “Fine, you deal with the kids, I’ll finish up the paperwork. What have I gotten myself into…” And Jetters leaves our shot the same as he entered it, by mumbling. Richard immediately rights the camera and zooms towards the boys. Richard: “Well, boys, I can show you to your rooms now, if you like. Or maybe you’d like some early dinner?” Reginald and Lyell both turn to the camera and sneer, almost in harmony. Atkins shakes his head, then walks behind the house. Lyell follows him, and after a few moments of pause, so does Richard, camera in tow. Richard: “Boys, wait up! There are some things I need to show you around the house so you can get acquainted here!” As the camera rounds the corner, Richard running after the boys, we see the two of them bent over something hidden from the camera. Over his shoulder, Reginald looks up and sneers again. Reginald: “We’ve quite acquainted ourselves, thanks.” Richard: “Wha-what are you doing? We need to get your things sent in, and your rooms situated, and…what is that?” See, now Richard has come around the boys to see what they were hiding. And it is not a pleasant sight, by any means. Lyell Freivogel is holding a cat by its tail as it tries to get away, scratching at anything it can reach. The reason it can’t reach Lyell to get free is that Lyell’s other hand is holding its head down. And Reginald? Well, little Reggie has himself a pocketknife. Richard: “Children! Stop this right now!” Reginald sighs, then looks to his new brother. Reginald: “Hey, Lyell, mind taking care of our curious friend here while I hold this cat steady?” Lyell grunts in reply, then stands up and faces the camera at his full height, which is around 6 and a quarter feet. Richard gasps, then drops the camera and runs off to an unknown location. Lyell laughs, then bends down and grabs the cat again. Reginald raises his knife and laughs as well. Reginald: “Well, new friend, it seems we’re about to find out if there really is more than one way to skin a cat.” As the two begin, Lyell notices the camera is on and shakes his head. Suddenly, the last thing seen is a large fist, and the camera goes black.
