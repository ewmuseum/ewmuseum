Memory Remains #5

5 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 070503 Many men, many, many, many, many men Wish death upon me Lord I don't cry no more Don't look to the sky no more Have mercy on me John Zero stands in the middle of a large room, surround by all shapes and forms of exercise equipment. In a far corner, a stereo system plays Many Men (Wish Death) by 50 Cent through several different speakers attached to the ceiling. Zero is shirtless, wearing only a pair of black workout shorts. In his hands he holds a six-foot long staff, which he twirls in the air in front of him. Zero spins the wooden pole around and around, circling his neck with the length of the wood, his lips moving ever so slightly, in time with the words of the song. As the song comes to an end, there is a slight pause and the music restarts, and the song begins again. I left the house without incident, walking gingerly around the sleeping dogs and made it back to the car. I turned the car around and went home. When I made it to my place and within the safety of my bedroom, I started to shake. Excitement? Fear? Adrenaline? Maybe all three coursed through my body. I collapsed on my bed and didn’t move till the next day. I slept hard that night, waking early the next afternoon. Luckily, I had the day off. Besides, no one was there to wake me. No one had been there for a long time. When my mother died in a car wreck involving a transfer truck two years ago, it left me with an insurance payoff from the responsible company that was quite hefty. The payoff helped to sustain me in the apartment and other needs of mine. There was a woman, a twenty-two year old college student that lived with me for a while, but she left several months ago. That left me alone to stare at the cracking white paint on the ceiling of my bedroom. A glance at the clock on the nightstand told me I had already missed most of the day. But, considering the night’s exploits, I thought it to be a worthy sacrifice. The next day, I showered and dressed, half expecting the police to bust in and arrest me. While I drove to work, I kept watching for flashing blue and white lights and listening for the telltale siren. She looked happier than usual. Her smile was brighter and she gave off an aura that would inspire anyone. Lily immediately began to spill all the details to me. “Bryce has changed. He said he was sorry for everything and that he would never do it again. He swore on his life.” The bastard moves quick. If I couldn’t have Lily for myself, at least I could be content in the knowledge that she was safe. Unfortunately, my content lasted only a couple of weeks. Arriving at work in the morning, I saw that Lily wasn’t there. Starting and working on the project alone was not a problem. But when she didn’t arrive, I began to worry. Leaving the building and going to my car on my lunch break, I passed by some of Lily’s friends. I heard her name mentioned and the word hospital, so I decided to stop and ask where she was. “Didn’t you hear?” a lanky blonde asked. “She’s in the hospital,” a chunky brunette said. “Bryce lost control on her and put her in the hospital,” the blonde added. I felt the rage in me begin to swell. I thanked the ladies and left. It looked like I would have to follow-up with my promise to Bryce. I hurried to the hospital and rushed to Lily’s room. She was laying in bed, one eye swollen shut and her arm in a sling. Her one good eye was open and alert, watching the news on TV. When she heard me enter, the eye jerked over to the doorway. Lily couldn’t move her head due to the neck brace. She started to tell me the story of what happened, but I had already guessed some of the parts. Some guy had decided to ask Lily out on a date. Who could blame him? However, he did it in front of Bryce. Regardless of the little threat I had made, Bryce lost it. He started smacking the guy around. When Lily tried to pull Bryce off the poor shmuck, he turned on her, cussing and beating her. This was the end result. “It’s over between me and him,” she said. More than she will ever know. Many men, many, many, many, many men Wish death upon me Lord I don't cry no more Don't look to the sky no more Have mercy on me Have mercy on my soul Somewhere my heart turned cold Have mercy on many men Many, many, many, many men Wish death upon me In mid-song, the music stops. John Zero whirls around, left hand out, palm perpendicular to the ground, his right hand grasping the staff at an angle so that a portion of the pole is behind Zero, moving up and to the right. “No wonder you stay so pissed off looking all the damn time, if you listenin” to shit like that,” the words came from the mouth of Deacon Holmes, Zero’s fellow teammate in The Upper Echelon. “What does it matter to you?” Zero asks as he goes back to spinning the staff, slowly at first, then speeding up. “And put the goddamn music back on.” “What does it matter” You got to loosen up, son. You walking around like you got two of those staffs shoved up your ass. Sideways.” Deacon looks around the stereo, looking for other discs to play. “Where’s the rest of it?” “The rest of what?” “Your music, man. This can’t be all that you brought in with you.” “It can because it is.” Deacon shakes his head. “Then we will have to make due.” Deacon begins searching through the songs, trying to find the right one. “You can put it back on the song it was on, then press the repeater button,” Zero states. “No, I can’t. Like I said, you need to loosen up.” Deacon stops on a song. If I can't do it, homie, it can't be done Now I'ma let the champagne bottle pop I'ma take it to the top Fo sho I'ma make it hot, baby “This will have to do.” Deacon does a little break dance move to the bouncing beat as he walked over to where John Zero stands. “Can you stop for a second?” “No.” “Fine then.” Deacon reaches out and grabs the staff, stopping it in mid-twirl, parallel to the ground. “Let go.” “Make me.” Zero squats down and propels himself into the air, flipping over Deacon, while still holding onto the staff. Upon landing, Zero goes back to back with Holmes, using the staff for leverage, this time flipping Deacon face down on the ground. Zero drops, grinding his knee into Deacon’s back, reaching out and taking the staff from Deacon’s hand. “Thank you.” “Look, man, I’m just tryin” to help you out like you helped me. You may think you’re staying outta trouble, takin? on the low men of the totem pole, but you’re a lot deeper than you think.” “What do you mean?” “Il Capo just negotiated for you to be in the International Title Tournament.” “He what?” “I just found out. Thought you might wanna know.” “Fuckinaye I would want to know.” John Zero stands as Holmes rolls over. Reaching out, Zero grasps Holmes outstretched hand, pulling him up. “You going to let me in on the big plan that I had no fucking clue that I was a part of?” “Yeah, sure, whatever” Holmes looks around. “Come on, let’s get outta here, I’ma about to starve.” “Two minutes.” Zero walks over to a standing metal pole, with two other poles reaching out like arms. He sways to the music for a moment, finding the beat, the pulse, the life flowing from it. The pole begins to spin in Zero’s hand as if it had a life force all its own. THWACK! BANG! SMACK! In a series of fluid motions, Zero begins attacking the standing metal pole with his staff, ending with a full out baseball swing, shattering the midpoint of the staff, spraying the floor of the gym with wood splinters. “Damn son!” Holmes exclaims. “What?” “You just broke the fucking staff!” “Yeah, third one this week. Give me ten minutes to change.” John Zero wipes the fog away from the mirror, staring at himself, half-naked, a white terry cloth towel wrapped around his waist. What am I doing here? Do I have some point to prove to someone. Yes. Lily. But she’s gone. Long gone. I know, but she will still see it. Will she? Yes. I think. I don’t know. Fuck. Zero reaches back and slams his fist in the reflection, spiderwebbing the mirror, creating several hundred distorted images of himself. Much better. Walking over to his locker, Zero slips into a pair of boxers before pulling on a pair of black pants, leaving them undone. He pulls on a white stretchy tank top, tucking it into the pants and then pulling a bright red dress shirt. After buttoning the shirt, he tucks the excess into the pants. Before putting on a black sports coat, he slips on a holster with slots for two guns, which are already in place. He then slips into the coat allowing the collar of the shirt to flair out over the jacket. Walking out of the locker room, he motions to Holmes and the two walk out of the gym, with Holmes humming Big Pimpin?. “Let’s take my car,” Zero says, walking to a black Mitsubishi Eclipse Spyder, the convertible top already down. Zero opens the door, sliding in behind the wheel, as Holmes leans his back against the passenger door, spinning himself around and dropping down into the seat. Zero cuts Holmes a look. “What?” Deacon asks innocently. “Nothing,” Zero replies as he cranks the engine, slipping the car into first and sliding out into traffic. Holmes looks around, a little confused. “Where’s your limo?” Deacon asks. “The driver had a minor accident. I prefer to drive myself.” “And the body guards?” “Lower your sun visor and open the mirror up.” Zero checks the rearview mirror. “Three cars back, on the left hand side. See them?” “You ain’t worried?” “About what?” “The bad guys ganging up on you and shit. You know, Il Capo speakith and it must be done, and all that bullshit.” “I am the bad guy, Deacon, whether Il Capo realizes it or not.”
