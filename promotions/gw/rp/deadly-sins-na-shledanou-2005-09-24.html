Deadly Sins: Na Shledanou

24 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 24 September .:: ::: ::. Sequitur prima pars .:: ::: ::. 24 September Early Morning McFarlane Estate Shadow’s Room The room that used to be a guest room, furnished in rather non-descript English country elegance had long since taken on a rather different style. The heavy drapes that had once hung at the window to shut out the sun had been pulled away, in favor of a lighter cloth, layers of red and black sheer that undulated gently when there was a breeze. The bed lay unmade at the moment, and though the sleek chic of the black satin sheets was, of course still evident, the bed also bore the evidence of a rough night’s sleep from its occupant. The spread had been hastily thrown back and lay disarrayed, hanging halfway to the floor. At the writing table, there was a blank rectangle of white. Its ivory shone with contrasting purity against the ebony of the table, making all the other ebony and crimson furnishings of the room look somehow inadequate. Lia Vlaszy, known to most by her stage name of Shadow, felt intimidated by the blank papers staring up at her reverently. Every time she tried to touch her pen to the top of the first leaf, she shied away. Words at the moment were elusive; they flowed in a rushing river in her mind, but once she sat with her hand poised delicately above the silken straight seam of the top of the page, the flow was dammed, immediately and without explanation. It was impossible for her to express everything that was running through her mind right now, because there was just too much of it, and no way to say any of it without leaving out something important. The fact that words were failing her as a medium was almost an insult. Before today, she’d never had so much trouble writing a letter. Before today, she’d never had any trouble finding the right words when she wanted to walk away. .:: ::: ::. Evolution Last week “Slut!” The word had been ugly coming out of Joshua Brand’s mouth, and her lip had curled in a reflexive sneer, as all of her muscles tensed, ready to strike him. Despite the fact that she had already defeated him once in singles competition, she had no qualms with taking him on again if he wanted to test her patience. She hadn’t had the chance to lay a finger on him’the encounter had ended too quickly for her to do it. She found herself backstage, aimlessly roaming towards the Hunting Grounds locker room with a half-dazed look on her face. And then she was inside the locker room, numbly packing her things up into her bag. Packing was a loose term for it. “Shoving without rhyme or reason” would have been a more accurate way to put it. Even her ring attire, which was normally folded quite carefully into the bottom zippered compartment to preserve its shelf-life, was shoved in willy-nilly and allowed to fend for itself. It wasn’t so much one little word that had her so distracted. It was that one little word piled on top of everything else that had been thrown at her in recent weeks. First she’d been selfish, then psycho, and now a slut. But this had all started out as Lia trying as diligently as she possibly could to protect the feelings of those around her, and keep them from the pain of being rejected by someone they cared about. She had had it happen too many times to her to be completely comfortable with doing it to someone else, no matter how hard they begged her to. The door to the HG locker room opened quietly, but she paid no attention to whoever it was that entered. Right now, she didn’t feel like dealing with the larger problems of Global, or the strategy for next week. She didn’t feel like dealing with meetings, and ideas, and? “Lia, is something wrong?” She turned around suddenly to see the face of Christian Zenith, de facto leader of Hunting Grounds. With his face flashed an arena full of fans, all of them booing her for attacking Emoticon at Whiplash. She shook her head, both to clear it of those images, and to indicate that nothing was wrong. “I’m fine.” Zenith stood up against his locker, and raised a curious eyebrow at her. His arms were folded across his chest in a stance that said he meant to find out exactly what was going on, and didn’t intend to let her off easy. Her own ire was starting to rise at the thought of being forced to answer. “You really think I believe that?” Zenith was asking her, “I’ve seen you these past couple of weeks since Whiplash” Your body may be here in this room, but your head is miles away.” Lia’s face blanched in irritation. “It’s nothing, I’m just a little annoyed.” “A little annoyed doesn’t account for you practically snapping someone in half last week to get to Ambrosi after he lost his match. A little annoyed doesn’t account for how distant you’ve been this entire week.” She gritted her teeth. “I know.” Zenith’s facial muscles relaxed slightly, no longer tightly strung across his strong jawline. He knew he was getting past the barrier. “You want to talk to me about it, don’t you?” He was playing games, but he drove a hard bargain. Her head would not shake “no.” She dropped onto the sofa with a resigned sigh. “First it was Emoticon--? “I wouldn’t worry about that shrimp. He’s as good as dead,” Zenith said, his eye flickering with the impulse of violence. Usually, that would have made her feel better. But she went on, her voice flowing musically like the Vltava that ran between the halves of her home city, unaided by the prodding questions that usually accompanied any attempt at deep insight into Shadow’s mind. “It doesn’t help me that Emoticon tried to kill Virgil, and left me to deal with the aftermath. He’s not injured badly, but I don’t offer my trust lightly, and I don’t appreciate having it broken. It” it hurts, Zenith, to have someone lash out at you and yours like that, all for making a choice that they didn’t want you to make. And then there’s Brand. I mean, where does he get off?” She would have said more, only she caught herself’Zenith was looking at her as though she’d grown three heads. She hoped she hadn’t. “You’re worried” about someone calling you a slut?” He sounded incredulous, then shook his head. “Look. Put that out of your head. Insults for women are a dime a dozen in the wrestling world, and the only reason Brand said that is because he knew you’d act like this.” She didn’t look like she believed him, which was irritating in a way. She shook her head. “It’s not that, it’s just” Nevermind, I can’t explain why it’s such a big deal.” Her eyes dropped down to her hands, which were folded primly in her lap at the moment, an old habit. Zenith shook his head, turning to his locker to gather up a few last things before presumably meeting up with Vivica. He looked at her when he was done, almost sternly. He knew better than to press his compatriot at the moment, but he wasn’t going to let her off without giving her something to think about. “If you really want to stress about it, I can’t stop you. But don’t let it wreck your concentration. You know you’ll be pissed if you let yourself slip because of some jackass with a fourteen-year-old’s case of potty mouth.” That was all Zenith had to say on the matter’he never wasted words on much. He was gone by the time she looked up again, and the door banged shut with a noisy, sharp resonance. “Slut!” The word cawed in her ear like an insistent, nosy bird. The thought of it wouldn’t let her be. Was romantic love really so narrow that it could be filtered through only one lens? Was it really some unwritten code that you weren’t allowed to love more than one person? That idea did not set well with her, but she had always been willing to push boundaries. She had never believed that perception and feeling stopped with common human limits, and even now, even when she was so embattled defending her position, she still had no doubt that she was right: Joshua Brand was a tiny-minded idiot for calling her a slut. Joshua Brand could never hope to understand the world that Lia Vlaszy lived in. It was all she could do some days to understand it herself. .:: ::: ::. 24 September McFarlane Estate She knew that running away was never quite the right solution for anything. She knew that, and it wounded her desperately to think that was what she was doing. Lia had never been easy on herself when she felt like she was doing something wrong. From across the room, her medical supplies mocked her from their open, hand-carved wooden chest. One of her rolls of gauze was gone. Mentally, she noted the need to get some more, but the notation was simply a surface ploy to get her mind off of what she had used it for. Wrapped around the strong forearm of Virgil Ambrosi, those bandages reminded her only of one thing. “Emoticon betrayed you,” they said. She wondered what he’d gotten from it. She wondered if he’d already spent the two pieces of silver that were his popularity with Global Wrestling’s fans, and his jab at Virgil. She wondered if it had been worth it. “Eventually, you’ll have to choose between them,” Emoticon had told her of Virgil and Dawn, several weeks ago. It seemed like eternities had passed since then. Her entire body had revolted at the thought. Her fingers had involuntarily clenched the arm of the sofa. Emoticon’s face gazed at her impassively for a moment behind his mask. She asked him how on earth she could possibly presume that was possible. He’d poked around, asked a few questions, and come up with his glib little pronouncement. “It sounds like Virgil is the one to go with then,” he’d said, as though it were the simplest thing in the world. But it wasn’t simple. It wasn’t simple at all, because she still loved Dawn, and Emoticon knew it, too. And perhaps that had been the problem. She realized it suddenly, sitting up. Her eyes narrowed to slits. All too easy for Emoticon to tell her to choose Virgil when Emoticon wanted Dawn to himself. That had been before Emoticon thought he had a chance with Lia. And before he’d tried to force her into answering how she felt about him, which was an answer that he knew she couldn’t give. It was before Virgil had come ferociously in between the two of them, and prevented Emoticon from going any farther in his pursuit. The span of a few weeks seemed to make a lot of difference. Emoticon had pressed that one button, and she had snapped. Then Virgil had snapped. Then there had been Whiplash, and their match. It seemed so simple laid out in timeline. Emoticon had told her that she needed to be with Virgil. And then Emoticon had set Virgil on fire, and tried to kill him. Virgil’s arm was a wreck. Lia almost threw up every time she looked at it, and Lia was not a squeamish woman. She could not afford to be. The trainers wouldn’t clear him to compete until the burns started to heal. Lia knew what the trainers didn’t’it was very, very hard to heal burns on dead skin, because they should have been lethal wounds. By all rights, Virgil should have met his Final Death. She didn’t know how he’d kept his sanity long enough to put his arm out, but she supposed that animal instincts of survival often did take over, even when the Red Fear was setting every nerve on edge to the point of irrationality. Every time she looked into the marbled blue depths of his eyes, she felt like she was looking at a creature who was living on borrowed time. Yesterday, she had done the graft. She’d used her own skin, but Virgil didn’t necessarily need to know that, and her own skill at healing purposefully made wounds was great. Wherever he went though, she would be a part of him now. That would have bearing on his mental state when she left, but worrying about that right now would only remind her of what she was leaving behind. That, namely, was everything. She was leaving behind not one lover, but two. Two lovers whom she would have laid down her life for without thinking twice. She left behind friends, in Alexis, Zenith, Vivica, and anyone else from Hunting Grounds who might have asked her attention or assistance. She left behind her pets, the hellhounds Damek and Jirina, she left behind the familiar grounds of the McFarlane Estate, which had become home to her over the past few months. Her heart burned, aching with nameless protest. Her hand was arrested halfway to her bag, the black-tipped fingernails clenching around the amulet that contained two handfuls of soil from Prague’the one thing that she couldn’t leave home without. Her loyalty tugged harder and harder at her heartstrings the more she tried to leave??You’ve got a tag team tournament. You’ve got to tell Virgil and Dawn where you’re going. You’ve got to take care of the animals, and stick around to help Hunting Grounds. They’ll need you, the next few weeks look to be rough. And what if Soma comes back? You’ll need to somehow make obeisance to him too. You can’t just leave.” But she had to. In the course of only a few weeks, she had managed to knot the threads of her life into so complex a knot that she didn’t know if it was possible to unknot the mess she’d made. At the very least, if she left, she could let a few people try to live normal lives for awhile, without getting in their way. The amulet hit the inside of the bag with a quiet, but weighty thud. .:: ::: ::. Dawn, Virgil, Alexis: Please don’t be alarmed if you can’t find me on the estate grounds anywhere. I’ll save you the trouble of looking by telling you that I’m not here. I can’t explain the reasons. They all sound silly in my head, even before I put them down on paper. All that I know is that I need some time to myself, without any distractions, to figure out why I feel the way that I do right now. Virgil, I’ve left you something on the desk. Likewise, Dawn. And Dawn’if you don’t mind, please tell Vivica not to worry about the match, if you intend on telling her that I am gone. I’ll be back. I don’t know when, but don’t worry about me. I can take care of myself. L.R.V. Lia nodded with satisfaction. It had to be the fifth or sixth time she’d read through the note just since she’d come downstairs to the kitchen. As soon as the ink had dried on the page, she’d snatched up her messenger bag and darted down to the kitchen. The words were succinct, and formed a clean break with the past. That’s all that she needed. Her slender arm stretched to lay the note on the kitchen table. Reflexively, she centered it once it was there, and stared for a moment at the dark, spidery scratches across the soft paper, feeling for a moment that they were unworthy words to spoil such purity. She shook her head, clearing those thoughts, and her muscles guided her towards the door. Her cool fingers found similitude in the cool metal of the doorknob, and then? “Shadow” Where are you going?” Inwardly, she cringed. Outwardly, she put on her best face, and turned to meet the owner of that voice, who happened to be a plucky young woman with, unfortunately, very keen powers of observation. She nodded her head to Alexis Key in greeting. “Nazdar, Alexis. I have left a note on the kitchen table which I believe will explain where I am going.” Alexis? brow wrinkled. Her soft blue eyes flickered over the slim vampire’s form, noting, of course, that she carried only one small bag, and also noting that it was not time to leave for Las Vegas yet. “I’ve got to get a jump on things this week,” Shadow explained, “With Super Series coming up and all, I wanted to go out to Boston a little bit early to try and get a feel for my surroundings.” A tiny crease formed across the skin of Alexis? forehead. She folded her arms across her chest almost obstinately. “Uh-uh. No dice, Shadow. Super Series is a long enough way away, and you’ve got another Evo in Las Vegas before that. What’s going on here?” The vampire’s muscles ran taut under her skin. Ire flowed forward freely, and she was about to bite off a nasty response. But then she realized that it didn’t much matter if Alexis knew the truth’she was still leaving regardless. She held her head in her typical noble, erect bearing as she regarded Alexis with her cold, determined jade eyes; there was no shame. “You’re right. The truth is, I’m leaving because I need a little time to myself.” “Do Dawn or Virgil know about this?” Shadow bit her lip. “No, but they will know about it soon enough, after they read that note.” Alexis frowned. “That’s hardly fair to them. Don’t you think they deserve a little advance warning?” Guilt stabbed through Shadow, a sword through some chink in her armor. The most obvious chink, of course, the chink that had her constantly spilling her own sorrow like so much lost blood each time she offended someone who meant something to her. But she just couldn’t do this to herself anymore. “Fair or not, I have to go. I can’t explain the reasons in words that would satisfy any of you.” Alexis tilted her head as though she didn’t understand. Shadow hadn’t expected her to. She hadn’t expected any of them to, but then again, that’s why she didn’t mention any specifics about whether or not they should come after her; either way, she would make sure they couldn’t find her, but telling them outright not to look was only inviting a stubborn reaction from them. Human or vampire, some things would never change about innate nature. Alexis watched her slip into the garage after the lull in conversation had caused her to assume that it was over. She followed to the doorframe, watching as Shadow pulled a helmet from the peg hanging near the door to the storage room. And then, she slipped into the storage room. The first thing Alexis saw coming out of it was the front tire of a bike’Dawn’s bike. Her eyes widened. How was that possible? Dawn had wrecked that bike over a month ago when the two of them had gone out driving. She cringed as she remembered Dawn’s determination to best Alexis? speed in the Corvette, the memory in her head replaying the wreck, the line of skidding tires across the asphalt, and the task of having to get Dawn back to the Estate afterwards. But the bike had been ruined. She knew it’but obviously, it wasn’t, since there it stood, with Shadow firmly situating her bag in a metal rack, strapping it down so that it would remain in place. It wasn’t until she wheeled the bike over to the garage door and pressed the button for it to open that Alexis found her voice. “C-Can you even drive that thing?” Shadow’s head snapped towards her’she hadn’t noticed that Alexis was there. The sunlight began to spill slowly over her long, lean form as the garage groaned open. As the vampire stood looking at Alexis, a large parallelogram of sun shone on her hair, lightening it to show subtle highlights of burgundy and blue, like a raven’s wings. A small, grim smile pulled at her features. “Let us hope that I can.” Alexis? mouth opened in protest, but she was cut off by the sudden roar of the bike’s engine. She clapped her hands over her ears, wincing as Shadow slid the helmet on, and the bike slowly pulled out into the drive. The gates of the McFarlane Estate were perpetually thrown open to friend and foe alike. Shadow wound the bike carefully down the curving drive, and to the exit of the property. Once she passed the barrier separating her old world from her new one, a sudden flame rose up inside her from some deep recess. Her long form stretched lean across the length of the bike, slim fingers gripping the handlebars as the McFarlane Estate began to slip away in the background. As soon as she could no longer see it in the rearview mirror, she gunned the engine, and she flew. .:: ::: ::. Explicit prima pars .:: ::: ::.
