Trip on the Phone Lines: Just a Scare

21 February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Trip on the Phone Lines: Just a Scare Reginald Atkins Global Wrestling Promo #3005 Date: 2/21/07 Location: Dallas and CT Just a scare is the phrase of the day. In typical usage, the phrase means something that seemed important at the time, but now that the scene has settled has proved to be less than such. You can have a scare while driving, when you think your brand new Camry is going to coast right into the old beaten-up Geo Prism, but instead you swerve and miss by an inch. Just a scare. And you can have a scare when you leave your child in the living room for just a second, then return and he’s gone. You start to get worried, and then you see him in the corner, playing with his toys. Just a scare. For Nathan Boswell, the phrase is starting to take on a literal sense. Arianna returned to stable condition rather quickly. The doctors told Nathan it was because she had only inhaled a small amount through her nose, and any more might have killed her. But, as it was, she is alive and well, and it was just a scare. And Nathan’s still scared. The days after the incident, Nathan was more in shock than anything, and couldn’t think about what had happened with Reginald at his tryout. But as the days went on, his emotions did quite the rapid change regarding his friend who got him the shot in Global. Confusion was first, as he wasn’t quite sure what Reginald meant when he asked “but what if something were to happen?” a mere moments before he got the call. But as this incident seemed less and less like a coincidence, confusion became anger. Nathan wanted to go home, find Reginald, and tear him limb from limb. But after their conversation, he’s scared. “What did you do to my girlfriend?” he screamed as he burst through Reginald’s door, standing in the doorway and watching with satisfaction as Reginald, hurried and scared, put something away in a book and slid the book below the desk. After a second, however, Reginald recovered and smiled. “I didn’t do anything to your little bint, Bozzy. I was busy being choked to death in the ring by you, remember?” Snapping his fingers, Reginald looked up at the larger Boswell as if just remembering something. “Which, by the way, the scout loved. He said he wanted you to come by the show at High Octane after my match, so he can talk to you. You’re close to getting hired, man. All we need to do is grease the gears and…” Nathan slammed his hand on the desk as, in less than a second, he was out of the doorway and looming over Reginald. “I don’t give a shit about the promotion, I give a shit about my girlfriend WHO WAS POISONED! Now if you don’t tell me what you’re up to, I’m going to reach over this desk and give you a bigger beating than any wrestler has ever given you.” “Nein, du würdest nicht.” Nathan turned around to see Lyell Jett behind him, a man who matches Nathan’s height but is still bigger and more muscular. Nathan’s anger dissipated from his face as he turned back to Atkins, who motioned for him to sit down. “Now, here’s how it’s going to go,” Reginald began as Nathan slowly sat, his smile gone as his face became a stone wall. “You’re going to listen to me, and you’re going to trust me, because you and I can trust each other. I had bugger-all to do with your little bitch’s overdose. She is careless, clumsy, and do I need to state the obvious that’s she’s fucking blind?” Nathan tried to lunge at Atkins, but the big German’s hand was securely on his shoulder. Atkins continued: “I don’t give a shit about your girl. It wouldn’t have mattered to me if she would’ve died. Maybe that would’ve gotten some anger out of you, though I doubt it, since you’ve been a lifeless husk for the last three years now. But whatever problems you’ve got at home has nothing to do with me. You are my friend. You are my associate. I want you to be my wrestling partner. But you’ve got to get this silly notion out of your head that I’m a master villain here to cause pain and torture to you and your blind tart. I know I’m a bad bloke, but I prefer to do my mischief in a wrestling arena. I don’t like to do things that could actually get me arrested.” Boswell didn’t know how to act, so he remained silent, his hands clenched into fists. At the sight of this, Reginald’s smile came back. “See, Nate? This is what I want to see. I am interested in you for business purposes only. I don’t want to hurt you. After all this anger, I’m not sure I want to be your friend either. But I want to use you for your power, for your anger. And I think that you want it too. So think about it, and keep a leash on her next time, for God’s sakes. I don’t want you getting distracted again” Sneering, Reginald stood up and left the room, leaving Nathan seething in anger. And as soon as the anger subsided, Nathan became scared. Now he sits at the foot of the bed, staring at Arianna sleeping, watching her breathe slowly. He is scared for her, but he’s also scared for himself. Being the right hand man to Atkins and Lyell was easy back in the day, because all of Atkins’ anger and planning was pointed at other people. Nathan has always known that Atkins was not a good person, but when they were working together, he didn’t see any of the anger or hatred. But now that it’s pointed at him, he knows for sure what Atkins is like, knows what he’s capable of, and doesn’t like it at all. But at the same time, the idea of being in a ring, even an octagon-shaped one, gets him excited. Maybe Reginald is right: maybe three years out of the game has caused him to go soft. Nathan doesn’t want to become a monster like Atkins is, but he does want the competition. He does want the glory. And he does want the satisfaction of hurting someone. That’s why he picks up the phone. “Hey, Reggie,” he says after a few seconds, sighing. “Listen, I’m still not too happy about everything, but I’ll go in at High Octane to meet the talent scout again.” He waits, listening. “Yeah, I want to be part of Global. Thanks. I’ll see you there.” Hanging up, Nathan realizes he just sold his soul to the Devil. But sometimes, giving him your soul is the only way to get close enough to kill him. ---- A few hundred feet over, Reginald hangs up the phone with a smile. “He’s in,” he says simply, grabbing the book he had hidden when Nathan came over before. Lyell is leaning against the wall, and at this he shakes his head. “Ich weiss nicht.” “I don’t know.” Atkins looks up. “What do you mean you don’t know? He just told me he’d come to the show. He’s gonna sign, he’s gonna wrestle, and he’s gonna help us mess with everyone. Just like old days.” Before he completely slips into a nostalgic coma, Reginald notices that the large German is still shaking his head. “What?” “Er konnte versuchen, gut zu sein. Er konnte willen, Personal nicht zu schlagen.” “He could try to be good. He could not want to hurt staff members.” Atkins looks unsure, an emotion that does not fit well on his face. Looking away for a moment in thought, then looking back at Jett, he asks, “Then what do you suggest?” Jett smiles. “Wir rufen Versicherung an.” “We call insurance.” Atkins smiles widely, points at Jett in a silent “you’re right” cue, then grabs the phone again, dialing numbers quickly. After a moment, he connects. “Hey, buddy. Remember how you wanted to get back at Bozzy after he pulled you through that pane of glass back in NAFW? And remember how you told me you wanted to get back in the game? Well, I think I found a way for you to get rid of both little birdies with one big fecking rock. When can you get down to Dallas?” The response must be affirmative, because Atkins smiles wider and nods. “All right, see you tomorrow.” He hangs up. “He’s in too. Man, Global Wrestling is about to get ten times more fun.” Reginald stands up and begins to move the book he was handling before Nathan had once again burst into his house without knocking, when he is interrupted by an abrupt and loud chirping noise. Looking at his phone, he sighs. “Today seems to revolve around this little mobile, doesn’t it?” When he picks it up to take a closer look, his exasperated look melts into one of confusion and – maybe the tiniest bit – fear. “It’s Jetters.” Lyell shakes his head, moving closer to the phone. “Antwort nicht.” “Don’t answer it.” “No, no, I won’t.” It continues to chirp, and Atkins stares at the phone for a moment, then presses a button and puts the phone to his ear. “‘Lo? Oh, hey Pop. How are you do…” Interrupted, he pauses and nods. “Yeah, we’re all right. Just settling in. What’s that?” … “A package? Okay. The address?” Reginald shoots a wide-eyed look at Lyell, who shrugs. “We don’t have a permanent place yet, dad. But when we do, we’ll call you so you can send this…this package.” … “Right-o. Well, I’ve got to go. All right, g’bye.” Hanging up, he looks at Jett again. “Sweet Jeezy, dad’s looking for us. He’s trying to track us down.” Reginald starts pacing, hands behind his back. “Dad never gives a shit about us. Why would he suddenly…” Reginald stops, clapping his hands. He looks to Lyell Jett, who is still standing above the couch, leaning against it, now with a look of realization on his face. “Jimmy-Gene.” ---- “All right, Reginald. Yes, do call when everything’s settled. Bye.” Yancey Jetters hangs up the phone and looks up to his associate, shrugging. “They wouldn’t give me an address. They know.” James Eugene Parker nods, standing over Jetters’ desk and wavering, slightly, as if the whole world might come down on his head. In a literal sense, he is probably just woozy, although metaphorically if he does not get back what he needs, the world actually might fall. His world, at least. And that’s why he won’t be swayed, even when the situation isn’t as easy as getting an address and busting in, guns blazing. Plan B. “That’s okay, Yancey. See if they call back. While we wait, I’ve got some phone calls to make.” Parker begins to walk away, but Jetters grabs his sleeve. “Why are you being so patient? A few weeks ago you were going to kill me to get to them. And now when we’re close, you back off. Why?” Yancey Jetters, as stated previously, is a shrewd businessman. “Just sounds like bad business to me.” Parker laughs, putting his hand on Jetters’ shoulder. “You’re right, Yancey. This isn’t exactly a good business decision.” Parker clenches his fists. “But sometimes, when someone not only steals something that’s yours, but spits in your face while doing it, there become aspects of the situation that are no longer strictly business. See, the business side of me simply wants to find them, take what’s mine, and leave; be done with it. But the personal side…no.” Parker smiles, turning away. When he turns back, his smile is gone, and he hits one of his fists against his hand, his eyes narrowing. “On a personal side I want to back off. Make them think they lost me. Make them happy that they pulled one over ol’ Jimmy-Gene’s eyes. And then…then I go in. Then I take what’s mine. But I won’t leave. I won’t be done with it. No. This personal side makes me want to wave my victory in their face as I torture them, and make them bleed. “No, no, Mr. Jetters, there are two sides to this situation here. And the businessman side, while frugal and effective, will not compare at all to the personal side. Because the personal side,” he says, beginning to walk away, “is the reason I’m in this business to begin with.” Parker looks down at Jetters for comprehension, but gets none. Sighing, Parker begins to feel around inside his pocket for something. “I see you aren’t following me. Let me give you an example.” Parker pulls out his gun quickly and puts it to the side of Jetters’ head. Jetters sits up quickly, his eyes in fear. “What are you do –” James Eugene Parker puts his hand over Jetters’ mouth and keeps the gun held, a smile beginning to form on his face. “Here is a situation: my gun is pressed on your head. Now, the businessman side of me is telling me that this a bad move for business. You are a wealthy and well-known man, and your murder will certainly come back to me. I should not kill you, because it is a bad business move and will only get me in trouble. But,” Parker says, cocking the gun, which causes Jetters to gasp uncontrollably. “Then there’s the personal side. “On the personal side, you’re the reason those two miscreants are on the street. You didn’t raise them, you just left them to their own devices. And their own devices got them MY FUCKING PROPERTY!” he shouts in Jetters’ ear. Jetters now has his eyes closed, one tear squeezed out the corner of his eye. “So, because they’re your kids, technically, you should take responsibility, and I…well, I should blow your fucking brains out.” He stands up fully and keeps the gun pointed, his eyes gleaming. “But I won’t.” Parker lifts the gun up and begins to walk away. “Bad business decision, you know? See you around, Yancey.” And James Eugene Parker leaves the room, leaving Yancey Jetters with his hand on his head, breathing heavily. Before, Jetters thought he had everything in control. But now he is scared of his associate, and scared for his sons, as odd as that may sound. The last sound he hears of Parker is the sound of the gun uncocking. At this, Yancey Jetters sighs heavily. Just a scare. ----
