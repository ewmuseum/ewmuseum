The First Page (Comfortable Liar)

6 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/06/05 I don't think I realize the stress I put myself through sometimes. Boys, my father, my cousin, boys, taking care of Gabriella, boys, wrestling, Brad Pitt, and boys... that's a lot of things to be worried about. Perhaps I'm still too little to be dealing with all this stuff, but I guess that's why I paid seven dollars and thirty cents for this little pink diary. Now, maybe I can keep a close track of the things I go through day in and day out. I just hope I don't lose this diary like I lost my last one. 'Look here, Rose,' Luis instructed his seven year old daughter, who sat down cross legged on the comfortable blue living room couch. 'See what daddy got you?' The large man took his hands from behind his back, and in them he held out a large, old yet expensive looking book. The word 'Quaderno' was written on the cover in scribbled black handwriting, handwriting that came from anyone but her father. 'I want you to use this notebook as your journal, sweetheart,' he continued, 'you see.. my father... your grandfather got this book from his father who made it while in the Vatican. He wrote in it every day, keeping record of every important event in his life. Then, he passed it down to his son and his soon merely kept it as a birth right, until his parents both died and he decided he should also be able to write in it. And at this time, your grandfather was in America fighting in World War Two. He wrote some... interesting stories.. about his time in the United States Army, and the goings on in that country.' 'And then, little Rosalina, the day before he was on his way back home to Naples, he had been killed. Friendly fire, you see. The journal was then confiscated.. taken away.. but knowing that his time could come whilst enlisted, he left an entry that pleaded to whoever found his journal, to take it back to Naples to his wife and son. This man, Guido, didn't know your grandfather, but he did just what was asked of him in that journal. Little Guido appeared at the front door of my home, and I saw your grandmother take the book and look at it. The look in her eyes, Rose.. it made her so sad just to see it after the news of her husband being killed, so she gave it to me. I wrote about fifteen years worth of things in this journal myself. I could've wrote some entries about the war in Vietnam, but with my future plans, I didn't want to put any of that in. That future plan, was to give this journal to my son. Of course, you don't have a brother but.. you'll be just as good a recipient of this journal as any. So.. my little Rosalina... I give the journal to you.' Rose quickly snatched the book with eagerness and flipped the pages open. Tattered, torn pages filled the book, most of those pages already written in. Perhaps ten pages were left untouched by ink, no more. She frowned. 'Are you sure there's enough room in this book for me, papa?' 'Use both sides of the pages,' Luis encouraged with a confident smile. The call of a woman in the distance caused his head to turn around momentarily, before he looked at his daughter and kissed her on the cheek. 'I think your mother wants me to help her make dinner. Go on and write, darling.' With that, Rosalina's father disappeared into the kitchen, leaving her all by herself and an episode of Power Rangers on the twenty inch television. The girl inspected the book's cover, looking deeply at the ink. It was then that she noticed something else- a small bit of tarnished gold, which looked to be a letter. That letter was unattainable with the ink that covered it, but she did find it odd. Looking at the spine of the book, a faded gold icon of the Holy Cross was displayed. Rose smiled weakly at the image, drawing a momentary sense of tranquility from it, before she then shrugged it off and was bound for her bedroom. It was exactly three years later, to the very day, when she found herself in a different scenario. Same location, perhaps, but a very different scenario. 'You lost it?!' Luis yelled angrily. 'After how important I told you it was?! How could you do this, Rosalina? How could you lose our family's journal?' Rose glared back at her father, arms crossed over her chest in defiance. The look she gave her father three years ago compared to the one she gave him then spoke more than any words possibly could. 'Family journal?' Rose questioned. 'I thought it was mine. You gave it to me...' She did have a point. And yet, that was still no excuse. 'Besides,' she continued on carelessly, 'it wasn't a journal at all.' 'What?' Luis asked, stepping back from the confusion the remark had dealt him. 'Why do you say that?' 'It was a Bible, papa,' the ten year old girl confessed. 'The ink on the cover, I washed it off. It looked ugly.. not as ugly as finding the words 'Holy Bible' underneath it, however.' The mere thought of his daughter doing anything to the frail journal other than writing in it caused him to wind up and send a thunderous slap across his daughter's face. Rosalina was moved to tears almost immediately as a result. 'Why?!' she screamed, 'why hit me for what my great grandfather did?! It was wrong! Me getting rid of that stupid thing was tame compared to what he did!' Luis glared at his daughter in mere disgust. How she could say such things was a mystery to him, yet at the same time, he had sensed something of the sort coming. She was an angel, and then quickly became... another child. Another normal child. 'That book was with us for well over one hundred years... do you understand that? Does that mean anything to you at all?' 'Well, at least you know it means less to me than ripping out pages of a goddamned Bible,' Rose snivelled. Another slap later, the girl's face was buried in her hands. 'Get in your room,' her father ordered, 'the sight of you makes me sick.' She wanted to follow her father's command, if only to not deal with his yelling anymore, but she was too scared to even move. 'Do what I tell you, Rosalina! Now!' Rose wiped the tears from her eyes, and looked up pleadingly at her dad. 'Just stop... please...' 'Stop what?' Luis asked furiously, looking after Rose who stood up and slowly ambled towards her room down the hall. When she felt she was a safe enough distance away, she turned and sighed. 'Calling me Rosalina for one thing. I hate that name!' Luis started after her in a rage, but it was already too late. Young Mindy was behind her bedroom door, and in that same instant, it was locked. Luis wasn't about to break down a door to get to his daughter. Instead, he simply moved to the couch where his daughter was sitting just seconds ago, and sighed. 'Mio Dio...' Yeah, I don't think he liked that very much. Served him right, though, sick bastard. Come to think of it, every bad thing that happened to him, he had coming. Everything! God, if I still lived over there, I don't know what I would do. The pen was raised, ready to jot down another sentence, when there was a knock on the door. 'Come in,' Mindy squeaked. On the other side of the door, was her cousin Michael. 'We got maybe six hours before the show. Are we going, or what?' 'Yeh,' Mindy nodded slowly. 'Yeh, just gimme a minute, cuz.' 'I gave you a minute five minutes ago.' 'Well this one's important!' Michael attempted to peer over Mindy's shoulder, trying to see the reason why she was seated at her desk, but quickly gave up. It didn't really matter much anyway; whatever she was doing, she wasn't going to drop it until she was finished. 'Fine... I'll just be outside. Drinking.' And as swiftly as he entered the room, Michael was gone. Mindy looked back at her diary, and hastily scribbled in just a few more words. Anyway, this is the only page I'll put down for now, since my cousin's all annoying and what not. And he thinks I'm a pest... hmph. Anyway, I'll write in ya more later. XOXO~Mindy~XOXO
