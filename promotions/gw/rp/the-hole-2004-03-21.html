The Hole

21 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/21/04 'Have you ever gazed into the mirror, looked deep into your own set of eyes, and wondered where the person you once saw in that mirror has gone? Have you ever gazed into that same mirror, looked deep into those same set of eyes, and asked yourself why it is you exist? Most people who are asked those questions answer yes to at least one of them. At least three quarters answer yes to both. But then you have the low percentage of less than five percent, like myself, who don't even bother with such useless questions.' The scene fades in from black, showing Ice, in a worn and tattered pair of blue jeans, tan Doc Martin work books, and a water rusted orange colored, white tank top shirt. Slung over Ice's right shoulder is a shovel. The shovel head itself is old and rusted, and the handle looks like a piece of drift wood, that was pulled from a flowing creek, and carved into a handle. Ice slowly marches down a gravel path, leading to a small patch of trees, as the shovel head bounces up and down with each step. The gravel path stops just as the tree line begins, and Ice stops before entering the small patch of woods. The shovel remains slung over his shoulder as Ice takes in a long deep breath of fresh air, and turns to face the camera. 'Are you the type of person, who spends their time trying to find a way to make yourself a better person that someone else you know? Or are you the type of person, who believes their life is pointless and hopeless and that there is no use in trying to make anything of yourself, so you decide to do all that you can to make everyone else's life as miserable as possible, just to make up for the life that you are being forced to live?' Ice turns away and steps from the gravel path onto the soft surface of the natural ground and grass leading into the small patch of woods. On the left and on the right trees pass by slowly as Ice walks, staring straight ahead the entire time, not once looking back. The camera bobs up and down while following him, and attempting to keep up with his rather brisk walking pace. Suddenly Ice stops, spins, and swings the shovel with all of his might. The shovel head blurs by the camera lens, and a sick thud, followed by a small screech is heard. The camera turns to the left to see the shovel head up against the side of one of the trees. Slowly Ice pulls the shovel head away from the tree, and the dead carcus of a smushed squirrel, dripping with blood, slides down the side of the tree. Ice evilly glares at the creatures dead body, before turn back around, slinging the shovel back over his shoulder, and continuing on his way. The camera stays focused on the dead squirrel, for just a second longer, before quickly turning away, and once again following Ice. Again the camera bobs up and down as it hurrily tries to catch up to Ice, and his even quicker pace. Ahead in the distance the trees seem to spread out, and an opening appears. The distance grows shorter and shorter with each passing step, until finally Ice reaches the open area. The sun shines down bright on top of him. His dark, jet black hair glistens in the light. In the middle of the open area is a mound of dirt. Ice walks around the mound, and the camera follows to see a large rectangular hole in the earth. A small metal ladder stands at the bottom of the hole. Ice walks over to the ladder and drops the shovel down into the hole. He stands there gazing into the whole, almost as if in a trance-like state. 'I know what you are thinking. I've heard it all before. I know what you are going to say. It's all been said before. So before you go thinking something, or saying something foolish, please let me ask. Have you ever looked for a place just to get away from it all? Have you ever just wanted to be all alone, without any contact from the outside world, and without anyone being able to find you and bother you? You see the only time that you can really, truly be in a place where you can be all alone, without the worries of any bother from anyone, is when your time has come, and you are finally buried six feet deep. But how many of you are willing to go to the appropriate lengths just to get the peace and solitude you so desire?' Slowly Ice steps down the ladder, and into the open hole in the ground. He picks up the shovel he had carried out there, and tossed in the hole earlier, and begins to go to work, digging away at the soil beneath his feet. In a methodical motion he digs, then throws, digs then throws, digs then throws, over and over. The time slowly passes as he continues his work, of digging the hole deeper and deeper. The sun has now lowered, and the bright sky that was there once before is now a dim pink and purple and orange color as the sun begins to set. Ice tosses the shovel up and out of the hole, and follows it up the ladder. While standing on the top run of the ladder, Ice slowly glides the back of his hand across his brow, and wipes the sweat away. Ice steps up onto the solid ground, out of the hole, and picks up the shovel. Violently he slams it into the ground, so that the shovel head is not seen, and the handle stands straight up. Ice turns and walks back towards the woods, and Ice disappears in them as the scene fades to black.
