Finding The Past (Part 4)

9 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09.09.04 Since he had come out of his coma, Ken had felt like there was something missing from his life. He'd felt an emptiness consuming him and he felt as though it had been there before the accident. For some reason, he also knew that the reason it wasn't there was his fault. He knew that it was because of something that he had done... or hadn't done. However, it wasn't until now that he'd realized exactly what it was that had been missing. In a sudden rush of forgotten memories, he saw the face that was no longer warming his soul. He smelled the fragrance of what was no longer sharing his heart. He saw... her. The silhouette of Gunner Addison was forming a beautiful at the end of the hall leading from the private gym of the Global Offices to the main hall and the large front doors. She stood, laughing at a joke made by the same out-of-sight person she was talking to. She was dressed in a white pants suit that fit her absolutly perfectly and she was allowing her gorgeaous blonde hair to move at free will as it fell from her head. Ken Partridge Jr, with a towel over his left shoulder, was exiting the gym, glistening with sweat. He pulled up his balck wife beater, shaking it like a fan to cool himself off, just enough to expose his defined and chisled abs that lay just above his Banana Republic jeans. As he looked up after allowing his tanktop to fall back into place, he stopped dead in his tracks after catching an enchanting glimpse of the most beautiful sight he'd ever seen. As the memories of Gunner Addison flashed back through Ken's mind, he realized that her smile had never been so beautiful. He blushed as the made eye contact and his stomach suddenly filled with a million butterflies. If only he'd realized that her's did as well... His memories were his strength.
