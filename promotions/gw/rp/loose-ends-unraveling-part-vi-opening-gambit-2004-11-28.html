Loose Ends Unraveling: Part VI: Opening Gambit

28 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/28/2004 “No kind of sensation is keener and more active than that of pain; its impressions are unmistakable.” ~Marquis de Sade Monday, November 8, 2004 0159 hours, local time Fat Moe’s Bar & Grill, Sarnia, Ontario “And now, it’s time for a little,” Jackson chuckled, the sound oozing with dark humour, “fun.” He concluded, pulling his sweater back over his head, once again concealing the muscles that rippled and bulged beneath his skin like unyielding steel. It was then that the door swung open, spilling Bruce McLeod into the tiny room, his face flushed and his eyes watery, the very picture of desperation. Jackson ignored him, instead lifting the heavy coat from the floor and shrugging into it? he didn’t remember the onslaught of memories, or the span of seconds that he seemed about to keel over, all he remembered then, was the cold touch of a hand on his arm. His eyes snapped open, that simple touch jarring him back to reality. “Don’t touch me,” his voice was deadly as he met McLeod’s eyes in the silvery mirror above the sink. Bruce’s hand dropped to his side, flopping against his thigh like a dead fish. Something in Jackson’s eyes was less than friendly. He’d seen that look before, in the sky, before a tornado hit. Something deadly, elemental, and utterly beyond the scope of human control. Jackson turned around, pinning the smaller Irishman with a withering glare. He folded his arms across his massive chest, making the leather strain and creak. “I’ll give you the shit as soon as I’m good and ready to and not a moment before.” McLeod sighed with resignation, leaning against the door even as he fumbled to engage the lock. “Aye. I figured you might want to have a bit more discussion before handing over the goods. A man after me own heart.” His words were bland, but the fever shone bright in his slate eyes, belying the words. “Just some terms, nothing more, nothing less. They tell me you’re the best, and really, it was just Aleks who told me that. I don’t believe him, hell, I don’t even trust that cokehead, but I’m not really in a situation where I can afford to be choosy. You could say, I am a bit pressed for time. I would hunt Gabriel myself, but due to circumstances beyond my control, I am unable to take the time off to do so. I” have other obligations.” A hint of a smile flickered across his lips, fading as he cracked his knuckles slowly in a distracted gesture. “Indeed.” McLeod nodded once, perfunctorily, his eyes fastened on the bulge in Jackson’s jacket pocket. “Yes, well. I only ask one favour of you. Bring him to me alive, or, at the very least, contact me when you find him. He has something of mine I would like” returned before we send him off on a chartered cruise on the river Styx. And Highlander,” the banter dropped out of his voice, replaced with steel that matched the coldness in his eyes. He reached inside his pocket, withdrawing a small cell phone. He flipped it through the air, and McLeod just barely caught it before it smashed against the tiled wall behind him. “Don’t fuck this up, or this will be your last paying job.” McLeod would have argued, if not for the certainty with which Jackson’s threat was uttered. He could see his own death in Jackson’s cold gaze, if he looked hard enough. He gulped, nodding, not trusting himself to do more. “Aye. You have me word.” “Good.” Jackson held out his hand, and Bruce took it reluctantly, shaking it once, before the cold vial was pressed against his palm. Jackson turned to leave, saying one last thing over his shoulder as he reached for the door handle. “You have seven days, Highlander. I will await your call. Aleks has my number, if I have not heard from you before then, that phone will ring. You do NOT want that phone to ring, you hearing me, McLeod?” McLeod nodded, his eyes leaving those maddening indigo pools of Jackson’s to focus instead on the cold contours of the glass syringe he held in his hand. And with that, Jackson was gone, leaving McLeod alone in the tiny room. Alone with his thoughts, and a silence that was anything but comforting? the last place he wanted to be. Bruce heaved a sigh of relief, pushing aside the plastic cap that covered the sharp point. He pushed up his sleeve, plunging the needle into his vein without another word. His expression went from one of sickly desperation to something akin to rapture, his eyes rolling back as a smile curved his lips. The Russian still had the best crank around? the scariest friends? but that didn’t matter now. He would find Gabriel Delacroix? and he would kill him. If that spooky bastard Jackson wanted to watch it, fine? let him have his thrills? ---- November. One week before Thanksgiving weekend. Thinking about that always led to thoughts of what lay in the month ahead. One month, and then? oh yes, Christmas, Christmas time is here... time for joy... time for- oh who the hell am I kidding?! Christmas sucks. Bah humbug. Just call me the Grinch, although my heart is much, much smaller... and significantly more black. But... sometimes, Christmas depresses me. Don't get me wrong... I'm not anywhere near as disillusioned as Ebenezer Scrooge... and I certainly don't need visits from any more ghosts. I've had quite enough of those for a lifetime... yes, you heard me right... I do see dead people. And hear them... but that's not the issue right now. So here we are... in familiar territory... in the dark... alone with our dark thoughts. I remember a time, when my father tore down the Christmas tree in a fit of anger, and then threw my presents at me. I was ten at the time? the first year without my mother? and the last year we celebrated Christmas. I still hate the season, almost as much as I still hate him. Even after all this time, I guess some things are harder to forgive. I was still struggling with the desire to forgive her, although that had drifted to the back burner. Out of sight, out of mind, and as long as she wasn’t near me, I could almost forget. Almost, but not quite. This thing called love was rather maddening; it was like a cancer with no cure. One that you could cut out a million times, only to find some lingering piece still remained. Something would spark that malignant growth, and it would all come crashing back to me. A song. A scent on the wind. I had hoped her memory would fade, but it hadn’t. I could still see her face, tattooed in my memory when I closed my eyes, which would do no good at this juncture. I needed to focus. I had miles to go before I could relax, let alone sleep. Opening gambit had been played. McLeod was nothing more than a pawn? and I was gaming for a checkmate. ---- Monday, November 8, 2004 0209 hours, local time Fat Moe’s Bar & Grill, Sarnia, Ontario The bar was closed, the neon signs in the windows had been turned off, but still McLeod lingered, sitting across from Aleksander Lyashenko, nursing his last beer. The brew had gone warm and flat while he’d been in the restroom with Jackson, but he didn’t much care. The monster of a man had left, waiting outside likely, if McLeod could read him as well as he thought he could. His mind was clear now; he was riding high on the horse, untouchable. He took another sip of the foul liquid in his glass, grimacing at the aftertaste before broaching the subject again with the Russian. “So,” he began, picking his words carefully, “what do you know about this Jackson character?” The Russian chuckled, flicking his white blond hair out of his eyes before replying. “I know many things about Comrade Jackson. What is it you wish to know?” “Can I trust him?” Lyashenko simply looked at McLeod, his icy eyes twinkling with amusement. “Trust” What is this trust you speak of, Highlander? You have no reason to mistrust him; he has paid for your services, and paid well.” “I know that, fuck,” he mumbled, running a hand over his close-cropped hair, “What do you take me for, a bloody idiot” I guess I’m just not comfortable working with the CID, as opposed to the past when I was running from them. How,” he paused, downing the rest of the lukewarm beer in one long swallow, “long have you known him?” The Russian rubbed his chin, cocking his head to the side, making a big show of pondering his answer. When he didn’t reply, McLeod grew irritated, slamming his fist down on the table. The empty cup tipped over, rolling off the edge of the table, smashing on the floor with a thunderous crash. Neither man noticed, instead it was a battle of slate gray and wintry blue as they locked gazes. After a moment, Lyashenko averted his eyes, sighing. “Jackson and I have crossed paths on many an occasion. Twice, or perhaps it was three times, he has saved my life. I owe him more than I can ever repay, but I think he does not understand how alike we really are. In many ways he is like you are, Comrade McLeod. A private man, as cold as Siberia in January.” McLeod nodded, his expression screwing into a scowl. “Fine then, tell me this much then. Since when does the bloody CID hire men like me to hunt their own?” The Russian cocked an eyebrow, snickering at the question, much to McLeod’s annoyance. “You do not know?” “Know what” Why do I feel like there’s something you’re not sharing with me? Bloody hell, I can see it in your eyes. Just tell me, goddamn it!” He leaned forward, sorely tempted to wrap his hands around the Russian’s neck and just squeeze until his head exploded. “There is no more CID. Nyet. Kaput.” Lyashenko rose from the table, turning away towards the door even as McLeod was still pondering his statement with open-mouthed stupefaction. He leapt to his feet just as the Russian pushed his way out into the icy night air. “Wait,” his fingers dug into Lyashenko’s shoulder like the talons of an eagle, halting his progress. “What the fuck do you mean there’s no more CID” What the hell happened?” Lyashenko busied himself with extricating a pack of Pall Malls out of his breast pocket before cocking his head towards the black sedan that sat idling in the parking lot. Jackson was nothing more than a hulking shadow beyond the dark glass, but McLeod shivered just the same, suddenly chilled to the core. The Russian shrugged, lighting his cigarette with the panache of a lifetime smoker. “I do not know all the nitty details of the gritty. What I do know is what he has told me. Jackson was left for dead, they betrayed him” and then he betrayed them. It was, as you say, prophetic justice.” “Poetic justice. What do you mean he betrayed them?” McLeod’s face was flushed, his eyes glazed with euphoric excitement. “Is my English not good” They killed him. So he killed them. All of them? except the one he wishes you to find.” The words were bland, as though this sort of discussion was nothing more than mundane for the Russian, and perhaps it was, but it still fanned the flames of McLeod’s curiosity. “He” wait a second? you said they killed him? Obviously they didn’t or he wouldn’t be here now?” Lyashenko shook his head, still dragging heavily on the cigarette. It dangled from his lips as he replied, his tone filled with seriousness. “Ah, but now you are being simple, McLeod. Or, as you would put it, retarded. The man who hired you is dead. You, of all people should understand that, can you not feel him” Could you not see the absence of humanity in him? He is a man without a soul. A man without fear. He killed them all without a second’s hesitation? perhaps he will kill us both as well. Tell me, Highlander,” his chuckle was filled with dark humour, “is your life insurance paid?” McLeod patted his side, feeling the weight of the gun in the shoulder holster. “I have all the insurance I need right here?” ---- Saturday, November 13, 2004 0259 hours, local time CID Safehouse, Amsterdam, Netherlands The night was stereotypically dark, clouds drifting across the nearly full moon, creating a gauzy haze that did little to illuminate the night. Not that it mattered much, since McLeod was wearing night vision goggles. He looked like something out of a second rate war movie, perhaps the cutting room floor droppings of Navy Seals, but he felt as dashing as James Bond. He ran the back of his hand under his nose, wiping away the phantom tickle that had been annoying him all day. Just nerves. Bruce McLeod flicked his cigarette off into the gloom, shivering slightly as the wind picked up, penetrating the light jacket he wore with ease. So, this was the famed CID’s last bastion. The place didn’t look like much, just a huge old house surrounded by a tall concrete wall topped with barbed wire. He’d expected something more, a moat perhaps. Guard towers, even. Not this pitiful excuse for a house. He watched as the last light faded out in one of the many windows, leaving the place in shrouded in darkness. Under cover of darkness, on this moonless night, he was ready to make his move. He knew Gabriel was inside, having followed him here. He pulled out the garrotte, stretching the wire and giving it a snap, listening to the twang of the razor-sharp wire. This would be easy, like taking candy from a baby. He grabbed the lowest branch of the tree and swung himself up into its shadowy embrace. A moment later he dropped softly to the ground on the other side of the wall, pausing to listen. No alarms. No guard dogs. Just? silence. He might have considered it weird, if not for the foreknowledge of the destruction of the CID, mostly at the hands of the man who had paid him for this deed. Aleksander Lyashenko had told him about Jackson, and how he had lost himself to the darkness? finding his purpose there, after all. It was a story that Bruce could relate to, having been lost many a time himself. He lacked purpose, but then, killing for money was better than nothing, right? Jackson had slain the upper echelon, the elite council of twelve, rendering the CID powerless. That simple fact robbed the fear factor from this mission. He didn’t fear that which was all but dead. Ah, but he did fear Jackson, and that was why he was here, now? less than twenty-four hours before the deadline. He patted his pocket, feeling the weight of the cell phone that he was carrying even though he knew it was ridiculous. He shook his head, and crossed the courtyard, his boots whispering on the dead grass. The branches of the tree creaked in the wind, clattering together in a heavy gust, rattling like bones. Bones? Now where had that morbid thought come from? He paused beside a rotund shrub, catching his breath, getting his bearings. He found his eyes pulled to the sky, and what he saw there gave him pause. The moon was visible, obscured by clouds that created a gauzy orange ring. A bad omen, for sure. “Aye,” he mumbled, “but I am not a superstitious man?” ---- To be continued?
