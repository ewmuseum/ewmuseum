bring in the welcoming wagon, it's another egotist.

15 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: june 15th, 2003 It’s a one-bedroom apartment, there’s a kitchen, bathroom and a closet with a bed in it. I frown and turn to my friend Ronald; he has one foot in the home and another out, leaning against the doorway. “Don’t worry Trey,” he tells me. “It’s only temporary, you know that.” I don’t give him a response, I just keep my frown. “Look, it’s the best that you can afford for now, thanks to 360 not giving you the money they promised. In fact, you have to do things perfectly with GW this time, because you only have enough money left to keep this place for the month.” I walk away from him, giving the apartment a second look-over. All my money goes here, and now all I have for food for the rest of the month is twenty dollars. “It’s going to be tough, I know that Trey, but you’re the one who doesn’t want to stay with your parents.” I shudder at the thought of five minutes alone with Kevin Jones, my dad. So I nod at Ronny, he’s right. All I have to do is make a big impression this first month, and then they say that the contract I sign with them is going to be huge. Ronny hands me a pack of cigarettes, sans one for himself. “Try and make this last for more than the day, okay” I don’t want you starving to death this month because you spent it all on cigarettes, you have to be fresh and prime.” I pull out my Zippo, one of my last material possessions left, and light our smokes. I slump against the wall as Ronny takes a drag. “Fresh and prime. I have five days to be fresh and prime,” I smile, “At least this will be a challenge.” “There we go, a smile,” Ronny says as he drops an ash on the outside of the door. “Try not to get into too much trouble, okay friend?” He extends his hand, and I take a look at it. There’s the guy that has been there for me ever since I met him two years ago, whether I was rolling around in money or not. “Okay,” and I grab his hand. “I’ll see you after the show Friday, Trey. Take care.” Bye Ronny. He takes his foot out from the inside of the apartment and shuts the door behind him, leaving me to the empty house. Here is a list of things I own: A recliner, a bed, a fridge, a toilet, a shower, a suitcase of clothing, a pack of cigarettes, a Zippo lighter and a poster. There is no tv, no radio, no microwave, no Playstation, no weights, no ac, no fan, no table, no dresser, no laundry room, no maids to do everything for me. This is what it is like to live life without money. I take my last drag from the cigarette, and then open the door and squish it on the asphalt. Another thing I don’t have is an ashtray, nor a window. Sighing, I pick up the poster and lay down on my recliner, holding it up high about my head, examining it. I smile at the wrestler, who is holding a World Title and smiling back at me. “Hi. “My name’s Trey Reed, I’m new here. People like to call me the Last Action Hero; I don’t know why, just kind of picked it up somewhere and went with it. “I’ve heard of almost none of you, save for maybe Bubba McCoy and Jay Robson, which isn’t something to be bragging about. Just to let you all know, I’m not here to make friends...I’m not here to find out who any of you are. I don’t care about being your best friend or your worst enemy; it’s probably best that you understand that. “Do you still want to know a little more about me” Good. “I have been wrestling for eleven years now, and training for my entire life to be the best wrestler ever. I started as a spot man, wrecking my body around the ring. You know, jumping from whatever, doing whatever I could in the air...but with experience came intelligence. I found my key, and opened the door to technical wrestling, what I’m now known to be the owner of. Yeah, I own it. “My first big deal in the states came with New Era Wrestling more than four years ago, where I became an instant legend. From there, moving around wrestling in here and there. I met Mr. McCoy back in 2000, first time was in EGWA where he wrestled, and then later again in NEGWA where I wrestled. I even made a deal with Mr. McCoy in July of 2001, and I was planning on being the next GWF World Champion, or something like that. Unfortunately for all of you, the Fans Wrestling Federation called with a much more lucrative contract, and everyone knows that it’s all about the money. “So you know, from FWF to NEW back to FWF to 360 back to NEW. My career has been a melting pot of repetition and bone-breaking matches. That’s why I decided to retire six months ago, hang up my boots for good. But...Looking at me right now, you can see that things didn’t go quite as planned. I lost my money to a lawsuit, I lost my mansion to a lawsuit, and I lost my pride to a lawsuit. “I tried returning to 360, but they can’t hold things together there. I would rejoin FWF, but they’re not alive anymore. I would make a return to NEW, but I am ashamed that I told them I would leave forever. I’ve been broken by the system, the system that I used to make myself a hero to the fans. I have nothing left to myself but my nickname and my wrestling abilities, that’s why I’m in GW. “I’m in GW to get money, to get fans and to have the name Trey Reed mean something again. That’s why the McCoy’s decision was to throw me a freebie for the first one, Ace Cash. “Some green haired weirdo that sticks around dreaming of getting a win someday is what people tell me. “I guess he’ll have to just dream on.” I stand up and walk over to the wall parallel to my chair. Another thing I own: a roll of duct tape. I pull up the poster and tape it to the wall, then walk back to my recliner. I take one last look at the wrestler on the poster, and then lay back down into my chair. Rest for now.
