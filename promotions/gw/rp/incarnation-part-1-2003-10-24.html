Date: 10-24-03 The Incarnation: Part 1 Have you ever felt that you have been left out, or even discriminated? Has your life been teetered and tattered with so much that you almost broke as your blood began to boil? Have you ever felt like ending it all? End it as if you were to commit suicide? Always a few feel that way yet hide it, for they think no one cares. They sit in there own little world and hide from humanity so that they will die and not have a soul worry. Yet people do worry, and they care, so why do these people feel so depressed, so hurt, and so full of pain that they must give up when things begin to go down hill? Maybe it’s the point that people can’t stand life no more, or the hope to leave this world of torture, where when you walk around a corner a gun can be loaded waiting to be shot at your head. Is this the world anyone wants to live in? When someone dies, they do not pray to come back here, yet are forced too. This is the mere image of hell and all of it’s demons. This is where life begins and ends for those poor souls who cannot get out of the cycle. Never has it been conquered… Till now! So it begins… Shattering like glass upon the impact, the rain came down. Never had it done this before, where every drop hit and it made yet the same sound. As if something would be coming, it wanted to let whoever was out there to know. The ground covered in water puddles of mud every few steps, and the grass just drowned in its essence. It was the mere image of a hurricane. As my grand father told me when I was young, the rain meant god was crying, yet why would god cry? Why would the man who had all the power in the world cry? I never understood that… Yet in some respect to my grandfather I believed his words, and always remembered what he had said. A slapping of soaked shoes or boots and long pants had began to echo over the rains’ down poor. Slap, slap, slap, is heard as they become louder and the rain’s shattering sound begins to fade. Then the slapping stops, and with a short pause one sound is heard that designates what this something is. “Hmmm…” A man, who under the dark black rags he wears, appears to be cut. Blood trickles down the barely seen nose, and drips off the tip; mixing with the rain it forms a puddle of blood that disappears as quickly as it came. The reflection of the man’s face is seen on the stone under his feet, showing his long beard of black, brown, and gray. His eyes a deep blue almost purple, and his shaggy hair that hangs down in his face. He looks to be injured, or was in a fight, yet still he’s mysterious to the eye. The man limps up the stone path towards the house that sits on the sloped hill. It feels like eternity has passed as he walks, and the blood keeps flowing as he takes one step after the other. Limping as if hurt on his right leg, the man bends over and grabs the bandana by the sides; yanking it tight he does so, and then continues walking, for he must be in excruciating pain. Nothing can hurt me… I’ve been so weary of pain I feel as if I’m immune to it now, yet even the smallest of cuts, the tiniest scratches, do have an impact on my body, and that I have no control over. Covered in this blood I shed a path of betrayal on my body, for I am abusing it, and although it is to survive, I will pay dearly. Systematically I will grow tired, I will push the limits and if I do exceed them, I may never be able to go back… Please let me survive… PLEASE! The man falls down on the steps of the wooden porch leading up to the bright white wet house. Rain still pounds down on the roof, and slides down to the gutters where it is converged with the mud. The door creaks open on the front porch, and an old man, quite old for he has a wrinkly face and is using a cane to balance himself, walks from the door and sees the man lying there. He immediately turns around and goes inside where he gets another man, who is tall and large, very muscular in size, yet not smart from the looks of it. The large man picks up the fallen body and brings it inside. *** *** *** Minutes, hours, days, pass and yet no sign of life is seen in the man. The weather changes from bad to worse to even horrific, for the house feels like it is moving under the pressure of the winds. However that changes, and the rain begins to stop, and the walls stop rumbling, as the wind passes on and does not come back. The man’s eyes awaken, his dirty clothes lay beside his bed, and he is in a pair of cloth underwear from ankles to neck. His eyes gaze open slightly, almost as if glued together they try to stay shut. His face still ill and as pale as a ghost almost compared to a brand new painted fence, as the man turns over, and looks to his side. There a candle burns, just barely alive, and he closes his eyes and lies back down. Whatever the reason, he is not sure of himself. Moving his hands up his face, he pulls the knotted hair out of eye contact and back so it hangs over his shoulders as he begins to sit up. Not much can be seen but his eyes and how different they are. The right eye is blue and the left is almost greenish blue, a hazel to be precise. The man looks around, not caring if anyone is around for they brought him in, so why should not he be looking. He sees a mirror and takes interest in it quickly. Moving from the bed to the mirror, he gazes at himself, as if he had never seen himself before. Staring he notices the cuts upon his face, the tattoo around his neck and wrist, and the pierced ear rings. His eyes meet narrowly at his chest where he begins to unbutton the cloth. A sun with a cross in between it is tattooed around his belly button. On his right shoulder is a rose with two J’s, one in which is reversed the other way and flipped upside down. He looks around, and sees his dirty clothes, then searches through them like a beast without its food. Rummaging through he runs across a wallet, there, in which he finds no identification. He must be lost, for he does not look happy, and when the child steps to the door and knocks, he jumps with fear. “Hello?” whispers the child that is a boy. His dark hair short and bowl cut, his face slim as if he hadn’t been a big eater or was just starving, and his eyes so green, almost piercing a hole through that in which they stared upon. The man not sure of what to say replies, “Yes?” and the child enters the room a bit frightened by the man’s size, yet not afraid of him in matter of presence. “Mother and father asked, well, they…” the boy mumbled as he could not get over the man’s size and was quite frightened. “Speak,” stated the man firmly, not caring if the boy was afraid or not, for he himself had never been. “Well, they wanted me to… to check on you, and now that I have, I must ask if you are hungry sir?” blurts the boy as he hurries to end the sentence and get an answer. The man stands there unsure of what to say, then with a nod he responds. “Sure,” responded the man and he followed the child out of the room. The hallways were lit with candles, at every door there was a candle hanging from the ceiling on both sides. The electricity must have been out because of the storm, yet the people had other ways of living. The wooden floor very creaky and every step would scare the pants off any child foreign to the house. As the two entered the staircase the creaks became louder, and the echoes of each step were sent throughout the house. Clunk, clunk, clunk, the man’s feet hit the staircase and he was soon down stairs. The down stairs was much the same as a bright light, for the windows and the candles lit it up immensely. There were three open areas, one led to the living room, the other down a hallway, and the last one down to the basement. The man followed the child through the hallway into the dining room where he saw two men and a woman. The men were the ones that picked him off the porch and helped him inside. The old man on the left and the large man on the right, yet the woman was foreign to the man’s eyes. The man looked around. The dining room was quite nice. It was old style where you had a gap to see into the kitchen and a swinging door that was broken on one hinge. The carpet the man realized was brown and comfy, as if swept recently, yet damp near the entrance of the room. The man looked at the table, and seeing that three chairs were empty, he takes a seat. The two men on the other side looked at him, observing, the large man quit after a good solid ten seconds, yet the old man didn’t. He observed this new being to his home, and checked his face with his eyes, taking in every detail. The new man did not mind for he didn’t notice. He just sat there rubbing his knee and looking at the floor. “So eh, where do you pardon from?” boasted the old man without another minute of anticipation. The man looked up and turned just slightly to answer, “I’m… not quite sure sir.” “Well then what’s your name?” claims the man with a curious face, as if he’s not sure if he trusts this man but has to because he looks so bull like. “Uh… I… don’t know that either sir,” states the man still with the same face as before. The old man looks at the woman who has her head peeking over her shoulder to get some idea of who this person is. This is nuts… Who am I? What am I doing here? Where have I been? Am I okay? What’s wrong with me? What are these people doing here? Do I live here? HELP ME! I want to know who I am! The old man can realize the man’s telling the truth so he just looks around the table and grabs a plate then hands it to the child and tells him to get a plate of food for the man. The child hurries over to the woman and hands her the plate, waiting, as she gets a plate of spaghetti and hands it back to the child. The child brings it back to the table and slides it in front of the man without looking at him. The man just looks down and wonders what it is, then he realizes what it is, “Food!” yelps the man, and he digs in with a fork. “Now what can you say? He knows what it is! At least he hasn’t lost his whole memory,” mutters the old man to the woman. “Oh grandpa, please hush, he’s just not feeling well. Look at him he’s a disaster. He’s been in that room asleep for near a week… if you were that ill we’d have put a phone call in to the morgue,” laughs the woman as she begins to walk in with a bowl of spaghetti and plates. The grandpa begins to open his mouth as the man finishes the plate and excuses himself. He thanks the old man and woman and hurries back up stairs with the excuse of not feeling well. As the man enters the room he sits down on the bed and then suddenly stands back up. Looking down at the bed he sat on a sharp object, yet it was not a pin or anything like that, but a piece of barbwire that was connected to a wallet. The man opens it up and finds a credit card with the name, James Fristik. Beside that is a picture of him, and then a car licenses with James Fristik on it. Looking through the wallet he finds more, a stamp saying he works for BDW under the alias of Jesse Jamester. Then his head shifts up and he suddenly remembers something. “I’m… I’m… a… a… murderer,” mutters Jesse quietly. At the door the gasp of a boy is heard for the child was peeking on the man.
