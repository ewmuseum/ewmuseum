Bad Day (part 1)

28 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-28-03 (insert quote here) ------------------- Days may come and days may go, but days like the one I write about now will stick with you until you drive yourself up a wall, or maybe into a wall at a very high speed. Right now I'm laying in bed looking back on the day, and I realize that everything I once lived for is basically gone, and only I can get it back. I need time, but I don't have time. It all started this morning when I went to the garage my friend Troy owns and operates. Just to let you know, Troy's garage isn't your typical body shop. He customizes cars and trucks to be taken to shows and meets. Of course, this pays off not only because most of his customers pay cash, but he also puts a sticker on all of his projects, and when the vehicle is seen at the show, he gets more customers wanting to trick out their ride also. Anyway, back to the story. I walked through the open garage door and sat on the trunk lid of the blue Subaru WRX he was under. He and I were the only people in the empty shop, but the radio was blaring so loud, and he was so concentrated on the work of his personal car that he was oblivious to my entrance. After a few minutes of sitting there, tapping my finger to the guitar riffs of the Pennywise cd blaring, I kicked his foot, then laughed as I heard a solid 'thunk' sound followed by a string of obscenities. 'For fuck's sake, Derek, can't you find another way to let me know you're here?' 'Sorry, I didn't know you were going to drop your.. why do you have a lug wrench if you're working on your exhaust?' Troy glared at me and rubbed his forehead a little. 'I grabbed the first thing I could, and you best be glad I didn't slam this into YOUR forehead after you made me drop my ratchet on my head.' I laughed a little and hopped off the car, then walked over to my new beauty. 2004 Dodge Stratus R/T coupe. Black paint, black leather interior, five-speed manual transmission coupled to the 3.0 liter V6. This little beast could dash to sixty miles per hour in under 7 seconds factory, and after two weeks and a couple grand out of my pocket, she had a brand new Dreamspeed cold air intake and matching Dreamspeed supercharger, as well as a Mopar Turbo kit bolted in. Ah, the benefits of knowing people in charge of parts warehouses. My uncle called in my order for the turbo, and the guy working laughed when he found out a Stratus would become a turbo. After I got it installed, I made a trip down to the warehouse to give Mark Stretz a first hand taste of what the pure-bred Mopar could do. Never before has a parts dealer offered to help me get even the hardest to find pieces to make my custom compact show car complete. My friend David and his dad Jim helped me produce the fiberglass body kit I custom designed for the Stratus. I looked for months, and the only Stratus body kits on the market were for the first generation clouds, which aren't bad, but not as much fun to customize as something that you cannot find parts for. After Troy was done bolting on the new exhaust system, he rolled out from under his Impreza and walked over. He opened the drivers door on my car and sat down. His hand slowly crept up and down the side of the passenger seat, then he asked the all important question of the day. 'So, you ready to hit the strip and see if we can find some four-point harness racing seats to put in here?' I shook my head and laughed a little. 'Ya know, T.A., if I hadn't signed the title to this car I'd almost think she was yours as excited as you are about it.' 'Well,' he said, blushing a bit. 'I never thought I'd see the day that Derek Frost got into custom compacts. I always thought you'd be out with the 'big boys' with your Corvettes. By the way, I thought I told you never to call me T.A. I hated that nickname from the day my mom gave it to me. You know, like you with Frosty.' 'Yeah, you're right. Anyway, you can't do much to dress up a 'Vette. What there is, I've done. Besides most of those guys are snobs, who look at my blue hair and baggy jeans, and don't even bother to look under the hood or roof of my cars. If I were to show up at a compact event, I'd probably fit in, right next to your orange-haired Aussie-mobile driving ass.' 'Oh, so you wanna put your weak little two-door cloud against my street legal rally car? I've got an extra 50 stock horse in this Subaru than your Strat could ever hope for.' I smiled my laid back smile, then laid back over the hood of my car. The hood of my car. Hell yes, that will be the final touch to this project. Custom hood, with a pair of Ram Air scoops to get an extra bit of cold air to the engine, as well as an extra 15 horses to the wheels. 'Oh, so you're calling my car weak? Apparently it's not too weak for you to label. I noticed your 'The Works Custom Compact Garage' sticker on the door. I thought I told you not to start labeling my car. I'm not out putting stickers on your car.' 'Thank God. I don't need any of your stickers. Last thing I want is to be labeled as a redneck for watching your fake ass wrestling.' I kicked Troy for that one, don't worry. I knew he was kidding, and he knew I knew. We sat in silence for a few minutes, then we both started to bob our heads to the right a little, then glanced at each other and stopped abruptly, as we realized we were listening to 'Hold Me Now' by Thompson Twins. Troy runs over and changes the cd real quick. Before he gets back over to me, my cell phone rings. My hand pulls it up to my ear, flipping it open. 'Hello?' ..... ..... ..... 'Holy shit, are you serious? God damnit, where is she now? You don't know? FUCK! Ok, I'll be there as soon as I can.' I slammed my phone closed on my thigh, then jammed it back into my pocket while running across the semi-slick floor and jumping over the door over my Corvette, starting it, and slamming it into gear and burning out on the street. Back in the garage, Troy was yelling for me to stop, but I ignored him. He jumped in the Stratus and turned the engine over, backed into the street, then spun the tires making the 17 inch Konig wheels a blur of silver surrounded in white smoke. That simple phone call changed my whole day, and made it unforgettable as a repeat of the worst day ever in my life. The day my arch enemy, Mark Kullypso, kidnapped my beautiful wife, Cindy Nicole Cruse Frost.
