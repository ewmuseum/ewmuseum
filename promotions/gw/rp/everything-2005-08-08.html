Everything

8 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08.08.05 07.28.05 Michael Mercer returned home from Verona with a small victory in the battle, but a hint that he could very well end up losing the war. Michael himself could not believe that he was taking his own mother and father home with him, to a place where they would be safe, where they would no longer be tormented by the past. Michael and Mindy Mercer were both plagued by their own pasts, but the two cousins were far less similar than that. Mindy didn't have the possibility of seeing her true family ever again. Michael, on the other hand... 'These people...' In Michael's car, sat next to him a woman named Maggie. A woman in her late forties, strands of gray falling dismally in front of what were once strong yet compassionate blue eyes. To Michael, they now resembled nothing more than glass orbs. They were open, but they saw nothing. They wanted to see nothing. Behind her, sat a man who once went happily by the name of 'father', but Michael now knew him as Dante. Although slightly younger than his wife, his face was equally distressing as hers; brown eyes of anguish stared blankly into space, occasionally crumbling under the weight of their eyelids to draw a blink or two. They were brown eyes Michael inherited, but Michael's eyes still had the black to them. They always had the black, the sinister aura that surrounded them. Michael could remember his father having eyes that could paralyze if you stared into them for too long, but now they were merely eyes that lost their dark depth; their substance. 'Are these people really my parents?' To answer the question from a technical standpoint, yes, they were. Dante and Maggie Mercer, the two people who raised Michael from birth until the tender age of eleven... the two people who tried to keep Michael away from the corruption of The Bronx, to keep his head in the right places.. the two people were suddenly left Michael's life in a split second. And from that point forward, Michael considered them dead. He mourned them. Nights stacked atop eachother and took on the heaviness of ankle weights, drowning Michael in sorrow to the point where he could do absolutely nothing; he couldn't eat, drink, sleep, move... he was literally paralyzed, cut off from a life that he had truly wanted. Michael lived, but in most ways, a part of him died along with his parents. But now, they had somehow- someway- found themselves back in their son's life.. yet the part of Michael that left never returned. Maybe the next day it would come back? 'Some day I'll feel better about this. Some day...' Michael's mind lied to him; anything to keep himself from further depression. But why did he have to fight himself away from such emotional exhaustion? He had saved his very own parents- more or less given a second chance at rebuilding his life- and he felt not one ounce of weight off his shoulders. There was more work to be done, and Michael knew that. Hell, it completely slipped his mind that he still had the job of telling Mindy that her father passed, until he remembered the Italian newspaper in the backseat of the car next to his father, solely from the fact that he could hear its pages being flicked through idly. The sound grew so tiresome, Michael turned on the radio. Mom and dad always hated their son's taste in music... '...Oh well, who the hell cares?' Click. Always the same old taste, just new injury I'll wear the claws if you like that Yeah if you like that we could ride on a black horse A great, new wave, Hesparian death horse I can call you when I get back Yeah, when I get back I will call The powerful screaming set the stereo to new standards of car thumping, to which Maggie instinctively blinked her sorrowful blue eyes and slowly began to reach her hands up to cover her jolted ears. Michael sighed, and swiftly felt for the volume knob, lowering the music to a tolerable level. 'So you haven't changed much after all?' Dante's shaky voice called from the back, in a bad attempt at humor. The will to laugh was the last thing on Michael's mind. 'You have no idea.' 'I've... come to adapt to things,' Michael lied. He did more than adapt; his outlook on life was one hundred and eighty degrees different from what it was months ago. He let go of his faith in God when his mother and father left, and there he took on his faith in humanity when he was able to turn to his uncle and his wife. Now here he was, and everything was switched around on him; back to the old days where he had his parents and only his parents. And it wasn't fair. He wasn't supposed to have to deal with the roles being switched so suddenly; he wanted his wife and child, not his past coming back to bite him in the ass. Sadly, however, the past was always there when Michael didn't want it. 'Why can't things ever be simple? Why can't I be the money waving, fancy car driving wrestler that everyone presumes me to be?' The obvious answer to that question was the fact that Michael never wanted to be the famous man he was. Money wasn't something he needed; he had enough of it to be a happy middle class man for the majority of his life, depending on how long his life lasted. What he wanted more than anything, though, was a stable family. 'Look at me....' Michael sighed. 'There's something wrong if Mindy's the most reliable person I know right now.' He felt bad thinking of Mindy in a manner like that, but it was true. Michael didn't look at her as someone he'd be able to live with for more than a week, but he found that she stuck by him through everything... that she wasn't lying when she told him that he was her hero. 'A hero... let me go grab my cape and superpowers.' The thought made Michael smile weakly, an expression he couldn't so much as fake in a long time. Something inside of him wanted to smile when he found his parents alive, cowering together frightened in that dark cellar... he yearned for a smile.. but his mind blanked out, and his lips forgot their form. In that initial moment Michael appeared to his parents' eyes, he nearly seemed like one of their imprisoners. Don't speak, don't say nothing In case we ever do meet again... 'Things will return to normal, though,' Michael thought, the end of his mental note coming somewhat hesitantly, 'no matter what.. I'm going to make things right.. even if I have to be horribly wrong to do it.' The sticky feeling of blood suddenly etched itself into Michael's mind; the sense of crimson sticking to his hands and the steering wheel he clutched.. his hands were clean, but his conscience was far from it. Michael glanced at his mother once more, trying to convince himself that he was right.. that killing those people was the right thing to do. His attempt at levity failed. Something's wrong with you And I hope we never do meet again 'Home... finally.' Chino Moreno's voice left the speakers and all was silent once more, capped off with the fading engine upon Michael's turn of the key. 'Here we are,' were the first words he spoke aloud since the drive home from the airport. Maggie and Dante both climbed from the car to look at the suburbanite house, which was indeed quite the contrast to the New York home the family used to spend their time in. It was every bit as beautiful as Michael said it was in his description of it to them. As they gawked at the home, Michael reached in the back of the car and grabbed the discarded newspaper from the seat. 'Very nice for a one story home,' Maggie whispered, taking time to admire the front yard which was decorated with numerous flowers and garden gnomes; most of which were provided from the old home owners. 'It never felt scrunched up to you at all?' she questioned just in time, watching Michael slam the driver door shut and walk around to the sidewalk. 'No,' Michael said with disinterest, approaching the front door with his keys in hand. 'It's a lot bigger than it looks from here, actually.' The old couple smiled at their son, unabashedly proud of so much he had accomplished. Michael had told them everything about his wrestling career, which took hours upon hours of their time. However, it was time they were all willing to take. They certainly needed it, even though it paled in comparison to the years they would have had if none of this ever took place. 'Then again, I prolly wouldn't have been a wrestler if none of this had took place.' With a sigh, Michael turned to them and placed his hands on his hips, pages of the newspaper flapping gently in the mild wind. 'I just need to tell you before we head in...' Michael trailed off briefly, finding a way to explain, before deciding that the best way to put it was the blunt way. 'Mindy lives here as well, with her boyfriend. Mindy is... well, you know who she is.' Michael didn't mean to sound so bitter when he said those words, but the way they were stated didn't help matters. It was too far gone to apologize, however, and in the back of their minds, neither mother or father seemed to need one. Frowning, Dante gave a quick nod of his head in acknowledgement. 'Will she be alright with us here?' Michael shrugged. 'I don't know. If not... I dunno what to say. Just... offer to buy her candy or something. She likes sugar.... a lot.' After no sign of further questions, Michael turned and unlocked the front door, swinging it open and looking around with keen brown eyes. With hope, maybe Mindy and her boyfriend would still be sleeping, but that thought didn't last long- there they were, lying in plain sight on the living room couch, making out. If Michael had stepped in seconds later, he feared they more than likely would've been without articles of clothing; and that would have been a disaster all on its own. Mindy looked up from the black haired man she was kissing, to see Michael gazing back at her, arms folded across his chest. 'Um.. oh gods.. erm..' Mindy nervously scampered to her feet, pushing her untied black and pink hair away from her eyes, '...sorry, Michael,' she whispered. Michael stepped into the heart of the living room, causing Markus to sit up on the couch in embarassment. The couple glanced at Michael, and all he did was wave them off, dropping the newspaper on the coffee table before them. 'Just keep it in the bedroom next time.' Michael sternly glared back at Markus, before passing them both and entering the kitchen. Maggie and Dante nervously stepped inside, Dante closing the door behind them. The husband and wife felt like they were kids again, just getting accustomed with a new place and new people, Michael aside. The first image of the home that they let themselves see was not the admirable paint job, the elaborate entertainment system or the fancy crimson colored curtains, but the silhouette of their son popping open a bottle of Corona and taking a rather greedy swig of it in the kitchen. Mindy's eyes squinted in curiousity at the newspaper laid out before her, but her attention was soon diverted to the sight of the strangers standing just in front of the door. 'Ahem...' As if Michael felt Mindy cleared her throat so he would notice her, he turned to see Mindy looking blankly at the two middle aged people glancing solemnly back at her. 'You must be Mindy,' stammered Dante, forcing himself to greet the young woman with as friendly a smile as he could muster. Before Mindy could question who these people were and how they knew her name, Michael stormed back out into the living room to stand next to his parents. 'Shit, I'm sorry.. Mindy, this is Maggie and Dante. They're my parents.' 'No.. no way...' A fire burned inside Mindy's stomach. She wanted to scream so badly, lash out in a tearful tantrum as to why these two people did what they did, but all that came out was.. 'Hullo.' Maggie waved slightly, as Michael smiled inside. At least a half-assed greeting was better than no greeting at all. 'Markus...' his normally vibrant, now downtrodden girlfriend whispered to him, 'go to the bedroom please.. I'll be right there.' Markus opened her mouth to debate, but a small, black nail polished finger pressed to his lips prevented him from doing so. 'Please...' Markus didn't want to leave Mindy alone with those people. She told him the story Arielle explained to her.. he knew what this could lead to, and the last thing he wanted was to leave her alone with them. 'But I don't want to make things worse and not do what she asks.' 'Sure.' Markus tipped his hat to his love, and walked off down the hallway that led to Mindy's- and what had over time become somewhat his- bedroom. Seeing this, Michael knew what Mindy asked of her boyfriend, and maybe it was best that he did the same. He had other things to think about anyway. ---- 'Michael's told us how strong of a young woman you are,' Maggie complimented, sitting across from Mindy at the dining room table. Dante joined his wife next to her, and on the table between the trio, a crystal punch bowl full of- what else- gummi bears. Regardless of the fact that Mindy's favorite candy was lying in front of her, and regardless of the fact that she'd just found out her idol had complimented her, she couldn't help but only strain to rid herself of the distasteful look on her face. It didn't work. 'I can only imagine how tough it must have been on you to train to be a wrestler,' Dante continued off, not trying to pry Mindy into conversation. Hopefully, with enough praise, she would wish to speak on her own free will. 'Being World Champion.. must be great to be on top of the world. I'm very happy for you, Mindy. I'm sure your dad approves of it very much.' 'My father...' 'My dad hates me,' Mindy retorted with a sigh, halting Maggie and Dante's collective praise in their tracks. She would have changed her sentence to 'hated', if she had happened to read the obituary in that noticeable paper Michael had brought home. 'My dad hates me, which is why I came to live with my cousin.' Mindy's sugary side was just about to fall to the wayside. She felt it. 'Ugh... can't be mean to Michael's parents. That would be bad.' Quickly, she filed through for the orange gummi bears and popped a few into her mouth. As tempted as she was to lash out on the two people, she couldn't do that. It wasn't in her nature to be cruel, especially when in reality, she barely knew these people. 'Dammit, why did it have to be Mikey's parents?' Before Mindy knew it, she was presented with a rather to-the-point question. 'If you don't feel comfortable with us here,' whispered Maggie, 'just let us know. We don't mean to make things difficult for you, as Lord knows you must have gone through so much in your life. Do you really feel comfortable with us staying here?' Mindy tried desperately to keep her mind in happy places. She chewed vigorously on another gummi bear. And another. And another. And another. '...Sure,' Mindy stated, as matter-of-factly as she possibly could. 'Why wouldn't I be?' ---- 'I can't stand this, Mikey!' was the first thing Michael Mercer heard, drowning out his own drunken singing to 'No One' by Cold. The picture of him, Gabi and Jaden that he held in one hand had fluttered from his clutches and to the floor, and his other hand placed his beer on the nightstand next to his bed as he sat up. 'What?' Michael inquired lazily; he knew the answer, but in his own drunken stupidity from the numerous beers he had throughout the day kept him from remembering. 'I can't take looking at those two, Michael,' Mindy confessed, a mix of sadness and anger in her voice, 'could you please ask them to leave?' 'The hell are you talking about?' Michael shot back. Now he knew what Mindy meant. 'I can't deal with this.. they couldn't even say 'sorry'.. not that it would have made things better, but... they were responsible for...' Mindy sank to her knees before she could even finish her sentence. With a sigh, Michael rose from his bed and knelt down beside his cousin, hugging her in hopes to keep her from breaking down. 'I know,' he muttered, still having that drunk effect on him, 'I'm sorry.. I don't know what else to say cuz, I really don't. I'm sorry. But I'm not pushing them away.. not now.' Michael wished he could make things the slightest bit easier, but he simply could not. He was far from the hero Mindy thought of him as, and he certainly was no god. If he was, he would have taken the liberty to turn back time a good fifteen years so both he and his cousin could more or less start their lives from scratch, before any of this tragedy took them over and tried to become their lives. 'I don't expect you to understand...' Michael continued, 'but if you still feel wrong about it, well.. I think we have a match on Wednes, Thur- Saturday.. so you can vent to your heart's content then. Give our opponents a mighty one-eighty-fuck-you!' The last sentence in Michael's statement was heavily emphasized in his own madness of the alcohol messing with his mind. Mindy tilted her head in a confused reaction before she sighed and stood up, realizing she would not get a real resolution to the situation, no less one that she wanted. 'I'll err, try to keep them out of your hair,' Michael promised as whole heartedly as he could, and Mindy nodded. He wished that he could help more, but there was no way he was going to kick his parents out; he just couldn't do that, not after what he had to do to save them in the first place. And again, Michael had forgotten the harrowing news he needed to let Mindy know of. Before he could even think of it, however... 'Good night, Mikey.' Mindy waved to her cousin, and disappeared, quietly closing the bedroom door as she left. Oh well... there was always tomorrow. Until then, he went back to the photo he so carelessly forgot had existed for those few moments in favor of keeping his cousin happy. 'I wish there was something I could do...' Michael mumbled silently to himself. And in the back of his mind, he heard a response, as he stared into the eyes of the two dimensional Jaden Destiny James. 'I know. But you can't do everything, Michael.'
