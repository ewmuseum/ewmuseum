visiting the 3 kings market.

17 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: june 17th, 2003 Texas isn’t all it’s cracked up to be. Here I am, walking the wonderful streets of “Boonville”, where the weather stays above a hundred degrees all year round. A bus passes by; surprisingly enough on the side is an advertisement for Global Wrestling. It has the television channel, the name Global Wrestling, and Sage Sabbot (the world champion). Heh, go figure. You’d think they would have wiped his face off and placed the Last Action Hero’s on there already. “Remember Trey, no one knows you here yet,” Ronny’s voice ran through my head. No one does know me. I’ve traveled from Japan to the East Coast, they even chant for my name in Britain still. I’m one of the biggest wrestlers to ever come out of California, a legend there. I make the ladies in the Southeast scream for me, wishing I was still around. In New England there are posters on the walls of Indy feds that show my classic matches. But in Texas? Well, I’ve been to Texas before, don’t get me wrong. I’ve just never really been able to make an impact on this place before. I was here last winter, in some place called PCW New Era. Yeah, a part of NEW was there, but it wasn’t like NEW there, it was That was probably the worst decision of my life, it left me with an emotional scar against Texas. Now I have to get over that, get back into the ring, and get things going again. It makes me think about Ace Cash. If I want to make Texas remember me, I can’t have any mistakes against him this Friday. What I need to do is wrestle a perfect match, put on the old Reed charm for the fans. My train of thought is lost as I walk right into another man who seemed to not be looking either. He wasn’t exactly a small man, and he almost knocked me into the street. “Hey partner, you okay?” He asks me, offering a hand to help me up. “I’m fine.” “Where are you headed to?” He smiles and tips his cowboy hat. I cringe, “I’m just looking for a store to get a pack of non-filters at.” “Ah, a smoker,” he lets out a big bellow laugh. “Well, the Three Kings Grocery Store is only two blocks away from the next right.” I dust myself off, “thanks.” And walk away. Wiping the sweat from my brow, I take the next right. I place my hand under my shirt onto the skin of my shoulder and it burns. Three Kings. GW is a three-headed monster. Looking at it from my perspective, there is the McCoy family, there are Salaok’s druids in The Nevermore, and then there are the leftovers. Think of it like a Venn Diagram, two large circles that overlap each other, inside of a box. Where do I fit into this scheme? Am I just a mere part of the McCoy’s payroll, am I destined to join Salaok in his crusade to destroy the sun? Or, am I just going to float around, get money and have fans cheer me on? Right now, I’m walking across a parking lot, drawing a Venn Diagram inside of my mind. And that’s reality. The glass slides open, and a swamp cooler’s breeze hits the sweat on my face. A mile walk, and I am dying of heatstroke. It’s funny how someone can fight throughout the streets of New York City, but they melt under the pressure of a little sun. What can I say? I’m cold-blooded. Standing behind the counter, the rotund short woman with curly hair smiled at me. “May I help you darling?” Still, no one had recognized my physique from my number of tag team championships, my legendary J-Crown tournament victory, or anything else. I sigh, “just a pack of Marlboro’s, please.” “You look tired, do you need something to drink?” “Make sure they are not lights.” My dad always told me “it’s not what you say, it’s how you say it.” When my mom was around, she always said, “don’t use that tone of voice with me.” Everyone else tells me that my words always come with some sort of attitude, and I need to straighten up. This lady gives me a look like I just cursed her child or something, and hands me the cigarettes. “That’ll be three dollars and ninety nine cents.” Pulling the crisp twenty, I slip the bill into her hand and begin to pack the cigarettes in. She hands me my money in one-dollar bills and a pocket full of change, “thanks a lot” I mutter. I step out of the cigarette shop, and pull one out. I fumble around my pockets, but to no avail. “Damn,” the lighter is sitting on my recliner back at the house. “Need a light?” I turn to see a majestic character holding a lighter in front of him. “Thanks,” I say as the flame engulfs the nicotine tip, “you’re my hero.” “No,” the man says as he slumps down next to me on the stucco wall, “not a hero, just looking to escape the world’s grim realities.” This man has my outlook on life. I look at him, just another ordinary joe like myself walking the streets. He doesn’t wear a cowboy hat, making us the only ones in this forsaken town. I think about what I could ask the guy: his name, if he’s from around here, if he knows who Trey Reed is. I elect to enjoy the silence of the smoke since he seems to be doing the same. Five minutes pass, he snubs out his stick and stands up, then waves goodbye and walks off. And that’s that. It makes me wonder, what if our cigarettes never ran out? What if an hour passed by and we were still smoking? Would the silence continue, or would one of us have to say something? The cigarette holds a nice power within itself ? the ability to bring people together. Yeah, there’s the hold addiction, cost and cancer thing, but that’s beside the point. I’m sitting outside the three kings market, smoking what is left of my cigarette, and not worrying about what people, especially Jade, tell me I can and cannot do. Maybe I should get some water. And ice, too, it’s just to hot in Texas to not. I’m sure that would make the cashier a lot happier with me. It’s not like she had a reason to be so snooty with me in the first place, I thought the customer was always right. A walk through the racks reveals that the market refuses to carry any item of food that resembles health. So I leave it be, and grab a bag of ice and a case of water. “Will that be all?” She says, and then looks up and realizes it’s me. “Yes, unless you feel like plugging me for a pack of gum.” And that’s me. I have a dislike for this town, and it hasn’t shown much love for me either. And if that’s the way it goes, so be it. Because if I get the big contract after destroying Ace Cash, well... The first thing I’m doing is getting the hell out of this place.
