Parallel Destinies

26 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/26/2005 Alexis Key picked through the ashes of the same mansion, searching for some kind of clue as to where Gideon and Shadow might have fled after the fire. When the Den had sifted through the remains of the rather large and intricate mansion, there was no sign of any life, or unlife as the case may have been. Burned treasures, ruined structuring, and shattered glass, but no animal carcasses or incapacitated vampires... not even any suspicious ashes. That was when they'd discovered the tunnels weaving in and out of the foundation, criss-crossing to every room of the house, with a hub in the center. Of course, there was also an escape route leading out into the vineyards, which they had unfortunately not noticed until both of them had already escaped. She kicked over a wooden beam that was set atop an old-looking wine rack, before searching through the holes, finding nothing but broken glass and pieces of cork scattered around. Most of the mansion had contained things like this, and they weren't at all useful. A crow glided from a nearby tree, and lighted upon a cement wall a few feet from Alexis, peering at her. Alexis ignored it, and continued checking each of the spaces on the rack, as it was currently one of the only remaining pieces of the mansion that she'd found somewhat intact. After a few more minutes, she had run out of space, and she sat down on top of the beam, running her fingers through her hair. When she'd left Washington, she'd been so pleased with herself at conniving help out of her cousin, and finally having the resources to get to Napa Valley, that it seemed more or less assured that she'd find all the answers she needed here. The ruins surrounding her had proved much less useful than she'd hoped. All that smugness had faded, and she was once again left without a direction as to where to go. She caught movement out of the corner of her eye, only to find the crow still staring at her. Their eyes met, and she felt as though the large crow was somehow peering into her soul. She turned away, somewhat unsettled. The entire day had been spent rooting through this place, and the wine cellar had been perhaps the most substantial thing she'd uncovered. Aside from noticing that the tunnels were elaborately constructed, and provided an escape from every room in the house, she'd found nothing but dust and splinters. The ash had long since scattered on the breeze, and it wouldn't have held anything of interest anyway. It was as though there was nothing at all left of the formerly rather stunning mansion, not even any proof that it once existed here. A few cement walls, beams from the ceiling that were too thick to burn all the way through, and a few pieces of shattered glass from wine bottles or windows, which there had been very few of. She'd cut her hand open sifting through the wine rack, and tired herself extremely by moving the beams around, and had absolutely nothing to show for it. Alexis clenched her teeth, and hung her head between her knees, as she sat on the cool foundation stone. Why had she been so foolish? Coming from Washington, she'd just known that finally reaching Napa Valley would answer every question that she had. Arriving at Gideon's mansion should have, after a quick search, told her why LaBatte hadn't pursued them after burning down this mansion, why she had been removed from the Den, and all of the intricacies that were likely involved would be clearly visible to her... of course, all she arrived to was a wind-blasted pile of debris, and a stack of questions left unanswered. In fact, it seemed like everything here was mocking her horrible luck. The foundation seemed to laugh at her with its complete lack of evidence, and the dust that swirled around her was mimicking her rudely. She'd put all of her energy into coming here, finding this one place on earth, in the hope that finally, she'd find a reason for her life being turned upside down, and instead... she found absolutely nothing. What was worse, everything that lay behind her was a closed door or a burned bridge. She could never set foot in Cleveland again. Someone would kill her within days, because she knew something about the Den that they didn't want her to know. Either Den members or assassins from the outside had followed her out of Cleveland, and would probably kill her as well, if she were to backtrack to their territory. Between Cleveland and Michigan, there was nothing, until she'd reached that little town, where she was now wanted for murder, thus sealing off the surrounding area for some distance. She couldn't go back to Washington, because she'd promised herself that nobody else in her family would ever be involved in this. Her mother and father had gotten themselves killed by being involved with the Den, and she wasn't going to be responsible for any more blood on her hands. For the first time during this whole ordeal, she started to feel as though there was no way out, and that it was a sheer inevitability that someone would kill her before she ever found any truth. She couldn't go back to the way things used to be, and what lay before her was a giant void. The Den had paid for her apartment, and her college, and had provided her with employment as well... in a sense, that had been her entire life, and now, that was completely gone. Now, she didn't even have a place to sleep tonight. Despair gripped her tightly, and she felt like laying down on the cool cement, and slowly wasting away to nothing, so her broken life would match this burned-out mansion perfectly. The crow hopped down from its roost on the wall, fluttering to the ground noisily. It continued looking her over, appraising her, before walking furtively over to her, and scratching the ground next to Alexis' leg. Her head snapped up, and she swatted at the crow with the back of her hand. 'God damnit, get away from me!' The crow flapped its wings angrily, cawing at her loudly and pushing itself backward, before flying away to the north, gliding on the heavy breeze that was whipping through the valley. She pushed herself up, and ran her hands into her hair again. She couldn't just lay down and die like that... thinking that way wouldn't bring her a step closer to knowing anything, and that was all she had left right now. She had to keep fighting, and she couldn't stop until she knew all of the answers, or her assassins finally caught up with her. She hadn't hitchhiked through the middle of nowhere to get here, just to give up when she reached her destination. The wind died down, and she remembered that she'd had the idea to investigate the vineyards, in an attempt to find where the escape tunnel originated. She stepped over the charred slabs of wood, walking away from the slowly setting sun, toward the blackened vineyards, to hopefully discover something before darkness set in. ---------- The moon hung over Las Vegas drearily, as the rest of the city buzzed with light and vibrance, but the facade didn't have Virgil convinced that anything was really alive in this place. He had never understood exactly what it was about Las Vegas that the kine flocked to. Flashing lights, the promise of mountains of money, was that all these people needed to satisfy themselves? He supposed that there was really nothing different about the humans of the time when he was among them. The obsessions had been different, but they all still pathetically clung to something, if only to remove the remembrance of the fleeting nature of their existence. He would have to re-introduce one of them to the concept at some point during the night... feeding had become much more constant since his chimerstry had taken on a life of its own, poised malignantly against him. His mind had been wracked with illusions of his own creation since that miserable night when he had misinterpreted Lia's relationship with Corvus, and made the mistake of walking out on her just long enough for her to find his letter and disappear from Henderson, the town they were staying in. The letter that she had written to him in response was still in his coat pocket, and had not left it since Virgil had found it. The words pierced into him still, and every time he read it, once again he was confronted with the most foolish mistake he'd made since he had been changed from a human. That very night, he'd been plagued with horrid nightmares, interspersed with visions of scenes with her, showcasing the one pleasing thing in his existence... or perhaps mocking him with it, now that she was gone. He'd thought that would be the extent of the penance for his idiocy, but a few nights afterward, his talent in chimerstry began working against him, manifesting everything from his havens being engulfed in blazing inferno, to Lia standing next to him, to unreal creatures of his own imagination invading his privacy, which he had begun to delight in rending apart with the same talent that created them. As long as he had stayed farther away from her, in Cleveland, in Seattle, and most points in between, the nightmares became more severe, until perhaps the most frustrating experience to torture him yet finally came along. The last dream he'd had of Lia was perhaps the only illusion of his creation that he'd ever been completely taken by, perhaps because he wished so passionately that it hadn't been a falsity. They had retreated back to Spain, away from the slayers, away from all of the irritation and wasted time that wrestling brought to her, and everything was as it should be... and then he awoke. That fleeting feeling of happiness once again fell through, and plunged him into a rage. The strings of patience had snapped that day, and the usual methodical approach that came with the sense of immortality disappeared, and was replaced by a desperate need to find her as soon as possible. Las Vegas itself had been reasonably uneventful, if perhaps only because he could now see the thin veil between illusion, and what the rest of the world was seeing, perhaps developing an immunity to his own power, that might prove useful at some point. The dreams, however, had ceased, which he could only interpret as one thing; He was getting closer to her, close enough to be mesmerized by the light scent of her on the wind. He knew that this couldn't possibly be imagined. Nothing had ever affected him quite so powerfully as her disappearance, and the relaxing of the effects could only mean that he was close to ending this chase. It had been perhaps the only reason he ever considered wasting his time with a place like Global Wrestling... she drove him to perhaps the most inane thing he could possibly think of, which was a small price to pay compared against eternal bliss that only she could bring. Perhaps finding her would also explain somehow the strange behavior of his vampiric powers He was snapped from his reverie by the sound of heavy boots crunching upon the gravel-covered rooftop. Considering the fact that he owned the apartment building, and possessed the only key to the roof, aside from the mechanic, who, beside sleeping during the night, also knew better than to approach Virgil in this way. After all, offending Virgil might cause him to decide not to grace him with his powerful blood anymore... and he'd been a ghoul long enough to see what happened to one of his age when their master decided they weren't of use anymore. Virgil listened intently to the intruder, and felt a sudden twinge of primal panic fire within him... something powerful was approaching him, and he spun around, prepared to deal with the threat. He was faced with a rather thin man, or perhaps boy, no more than twenty. He wielded a crucifix in front of his face, which caused Virgil to smirk grimly. He would figure out why the Society of Leopold had found him later... first, he would deal with this insolence. 'I do hope you've brought something more powerful than that symbol of the greatest lie ever told to humanity.' The boy began praying in one of the few tongues Virgil couldn't comprehend, and the panic grew in him again, as his flesh was suddenly seared with the flames that only true faith could conjure. He growled, and took a step back, furious with himself for his overconfidence. 'You are a pox upon God's earth, vampire... in his name I will purge you and your kind from it!' The invisible flames shot through him again, but the pain was negligible without the surprise, and Virgil swiped at him with his razor-sharp fingernails, slicing the boy's throat open. He smiled as blood burst forth from the open wound, and he caught the boy as he fell forward by placing a hand on his forehead. The limp body fell to the knees, and Virgil grabbed the shoulder, pulling forward while pressing back against the head, causing a sickening snap to pierce the night. Virgil stepped back to admire his work. The head hung loosely against the back of the body, and the sharp ends of the snapped spinal column had pierced the boy's chest from the inside. A satisfied grin crossed Virgil's face, and he started to turn back toward the edge of the roof, when he heard the same noise of footsteps, and from the corner of his eye, saw the body begin to move. He turned back, and watched in surprise as the left hand reached behind the back, and shoved the head back up on to the shoulders, snapping the spine once again. Virgil launched himself at the horrid corpse desperately, plunging his hand into its chest. It dissipated as his hands gripped the heart, and he passed through the thin air that it became clumsily, smashing into one of the large air conditioning power centers that stood stoically atop the roof. Rage began to simmer within him once again, and he slammed his fist into the roof hard enough to make a crater in the concrete that composed it. At this second, a crow took light upon the roof, staring at him inquisitively. Virgil would have used his power to convince the bird it was combusting from the inside, while being assaulted by a million needles from the outside, had he not noticed the one white feather on the crow's breast, quickly recognizing it as one of his many animal retainers from his Napa Valley estate, which he had called Mobius, a reference to something he couldn't quite remember. Thoughts began to pour from the crow into his mind, as his natural connection with the animal world manifested itself. He attempted to translate the wordless thoughts of the crow into something communicable. 'Thing... burned house, move trees in.' He arched an eyebrow. Mobius flew all this way to tell him that something was foraging in the mansion? 'Why did you come, Mobius?' He attempted to divine, realizing that the crow likely wouldn't understand him completely. Thankfully for both of them, thoughts were generally much easier to interpret than words. 'Thing like you, slower. Had shiny skin parts.' There were times when he wished that he could have perhaps used a more intelligent bird, but sending anything more than a crow would probably be exceedingly conspicuous. It amused him sometimes to feel the simplistic way the crow described things in its own mind, anyway. Few things amused him in his current state. 'So...' he thought, rather interested, 'I assume he means one of the kine is rooting around my old mansion... obviously well-dressed enough to catch the crow's attention with something brightly-colored, or perhaps reflective... likely a woman, then. Perhaps he' useful for something after all.' Virgil smiled, and Mobius hopped along the side of the building, moving out of Virgil's way. 'Go back to the mansion, Mobius. Keep watching it, and if it moves, follow it.' Mobius flew away from the roof instantly, well-trained to respond to Virgil's commands. The horrifying vision from minutes ago was now far from his mind, as he pulled his phone from his coat pocket, brushing the letter with the back of his hand... always a constant reminder that she was more important, no matter what else was happening. He flipped the phone open, and dialed a number quickly. The connection to his warehouse in Seattle took a couple of seconds, but in a few moments, a voice crackled across the other line. 'Medici Logistics... we're currently closed.' Virgil smiled. All of his animals were so well trained, even the humans. 'Thank you, David, but I remember my own hours.' David gulped nervously on the other line. His boss, though often not around, always made him extremely nervous, even though he knew what Virgil was. There was still the lingering fear that he would wear out his use. 'Mister Ambrosi... what can I do for you?' Virgil dragged a fingernail across the bricks that he was currently leaning on. 'I want you or Kenji to visit my estate in Napa Valley tonight. It appears that I have a guest there, but I'm afraid I won't be there to meet them. You know how I feel about inhospitality, so I want one of you to go find whoever is there, and take them back to Seattle to be... entertained until I can speak with them personally.' David glanced over at a shipping schedule. 'Kenji is still on his run to Shanghai... but I'll leave now. I can probably make a flight down there... ' 'Good. You are always the efficient one, David. I would appreciate it if you lived up to your record on this assignment.' David broke out in a cold sweat. 'Yes sir, I won't let you down.' Virgil pressed the button to hang up, as a smile took over his usually expressionless face. Perhaps by the time he was able to finally find Lia, he would be able to introduce her to one of her would-be assassins. He certainly felt as though he would need a peace offering to make up for his mistake, and what better gift for a Tzimice than someone that had offended them, that they can gruesomely reconstruct into whatever they desire? Like clay to the sculptor... but first, he had to find her. Obligations for both of them would place them at the Global arena, and certainly he would find her there. Then, the most miserable chapter of his unlife would finally be closed.
