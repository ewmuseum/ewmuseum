Transitions (Part I)

11 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03.11.06 03.06.06 “I’m sending him up,” boomed the voice of Matthew Moore throughout Ken’s office. Ken looked back at the intercom, as if Matt could see him through it, and nodded. It had been days since he had slept and weeks since he’d slept well. To say that he was exhausted was an understatement, and it was something that he definitely didn’t want holding him down marching into High Octane. Shaking through his weariness, Ken quickly pressed the button on the system and replied with a simple “okay” back to the man he’s ordained manager of his New York nightclub. As soon, as his finger left its mark, a chime sounded as the elevator that served as the door to Ken’s office came to a stop. It was a bit dramatic for a four story building, he knew, but he couldn’t resist the flavor that it added. As the elevator doors drew open, a familiar face to GLOBAL, in the way of Eryc Morgan stepped out. Ken hit his feet and made his way from his desk to shake his hand. “Good to see you, Eryc,” greeted Ken. “Likewise,” was Morgan’s response. “These are some nice digs for an office,” he continued while looking around, coming to a stop at Ken’s glass trophy case. “There are a lot of memories in there,” said KPJ as he noticed Eryc’s gazing. “My first world title, my old Grappling God Championship belt, the Wrestler of the Year award” all there with photographic proof that it actually happened.” Ken was now gazing at the city through the large window that composed the far wall of his office. “I must say,” came Morgan, “it’s pretty impressive.” “They’re just trophies,” chimed Ken without pulling away from the skyline, “nothing more. Many of them don’t mean anything to anyone anymore. Well, of course, except for me.” Ken smiled as he turned and headed towards the glass case and the Platinum Championship contender. “However, Mr. Morgan, I don’t believe my past accomplishments are what we’re here to discuss, are they?” Eryc smiled, almost mischievously. “I can’t say as they are, Ken. They’re still impressive, though, nonetheless.” “Thank you,” replied Ken gratefully. “While your appreciation for my past accomplishments is something I’m truly grateful for, Eryc, I’d really like to get to the subject at hand.” “Yeah,” replied Morgan, stepping away from the trophy case and sitting in one of the two black leather chairs opposite Ken at his desk. When he sat, Ken noticed a bit of a grimace on his face. “No problem.” “I’m sorry, Eryc,” Ken began. “I’m just really tired. I haven’t slept in days.” “It’s okay,” he replied with a somewhat happier demeanor. “No,” Ken sighed, “it isn’t. I was being an ass and this is the first time we’ve really had the opportunity to talk outside of a GLOBAL atmosphere. Hey, speaking of which, good luck in that Four Corners match at High Octane. I think you’ve got a real chance at taking the Platinum title.” “Thanks,” Morgan replied with half a smile. “I appreciate that. Good luck to you, too. If we’re lucky, maybe we can finally face-off one-on-one for that Platinum Championship after the Pay-Per-View.” “Hopefully so,” Ken smiled. “Hopefully so.” There was a brief pause in the conversation before Ken continued. “Anyway, to the business at hand, Matt told me that he’d booked Opaque Oblivion to play here at The Elements, and I know that you came here for that, but-? “Well,” a somewhat reluctant Morgan quickly cut-in, “I’m glad you bring that up. You see, some of the guys have decided to pursue other projects and have left the band.” “Now,” Eryc quickly continued as he saw Ken’s demeanor begin a change, “I don’t want you guys to worry. I’m auditioning new members and will have the spots filled within the next month or so. The gig will still go on as planned.” After another brief air of reluctance, Ken smiled at Eryc. “That’s fine,” he said. “You’re the one we wanted anyways. You can continue discussing that with Matt, though. That’s not why I brought you here. You see, I wanted to discuss other business opportunities with you.” “Oh,” replied a somewhat confused Morgan, “such as?” “Well,” Ken continued, “namely a group known as The-? “Mr. Partridge,” the voice on the intercom quickly interjected, “Gunner’s on line three for you.” “Could you please tell her I’ll call her back, Lex,” stated a somewhat frustrated Ken. “I’m in the middle of something pretty important.” “She says it’s urgent, Ken. She said it has something to do with Josh.” “Excuse me, Eryc,” urged Ken as he picked up the phone. “This will only be a minute. He’s probably set the new sofa on fire” again.” As Ken brought the phone to his ear and gave a subtle “Hello, Sweetheart,” Eryc saw a chain of events quickly unravel in front of his face. “What?” shouted Ken. “He’s in the hospital?” Eryc saw nothing but pure terror spread across Ken’s face as he bid Gunner farewell. “I’m on my way now. I’ll take the chopper.” Ken quickly hung up the phone and grabbed his black frayed vintage Lip Service blazer from the back of his chair as he stood up. “Eryc,” he said as he rushed the Platinum contender to the door, “I’m so sorry, but we’re going to have to continue this at another time. Maybe, we can discuss it in Detroit.” “Is, is everything okay?” Morgan questioned Ken as KPJ pressed the button, prompting the elevator door to open. “I don’t know,” Ken replied honestly as they entered the cylinder to begin their descent. “I really don’t know.” The rest of the ride to the main level was filled with an awkward silence that made the forty-five seconds seem like forty-five minutes. Ken continuously checked his watch for the time. He was obviously in a hurry to get out of the elevator and to the helipad. As the device came to a stop and the doors opened, allowing them to leave, Ken extended his hand. “I’m this way,” he said shaking Eryc’s hand and motioning to the left with his head. “I’ll be in touch.” “Take care,” Eryc said as they released hands and Ken began his power walk away from him. Eryc just stood, watching the back of his head for a few seconds before chiming out, “I hope everything’s okay.” Ken raised his hand in acknowledgement of the gesture, but didn’t turn around as he made to another door and out of sight.
