Purity of Breath

19 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/19/2005 NOTE TO THE READER: This RP deals with suicide. If you are uncomfortable with that topic in any way I suggest you not continue. Despite the better part of two hours of observation, the top of Dawn’s canopy bed did not change, nor did it reveal any of the answers to the questions boiling around in her head. Her side ached fiercely, a sharp and stabbing pain that spoke of cracked bone and cartilage, forcing her mind back to the issues she was simultaneously seeking answers to and trying to avoid. It was unseasonably cool in the Greater Toronto Area, especially considering they had sweated through an absolutely disgusting wave of heat and humidity not a week before. The central air conditioning had been shut off, and the windows of the McFarlane estate were open to the summer air’except for those on the third floor of course. Directly below Dawn’s master bedroom was her guest wing, where her vampire stable mate slumbered away most of the day, oblivious to the whirlwind storm of thoughts and emotions broiling just twelve feet above her. The cool weather had let Dawn wear a pair of long, but comfortable, black pants and matching tank top, ostensibly to work out some of the week’s frustration on a punching bag. Instead she found herself sprawled out her back, staring at the black fabric that made up the ceiling of her bed, and trying to muster the energy to tackle the rest of the day. But the events of the many weeks leading up to this plagued Dawn’s thoughts, voices and images playing out in her mind in some kind of endless parade. He stared up at her from the ground, his face a mask of fury. “You’re fucking crazy you know that”! You’re a goddamn crazy cock tease!” Dawn took a step toward him and Andrew scrambled away, struggling to his feet and running out of the alley mouth. “You crazy bitch!” He screamed over his shoulder at her. “Ergh!” Dawn made an angry noise, reaching up to scrub at her eyes with the heel of her palms. “Stop thinking about it’what the hell do you care what he thinks anyway?” The answer, of course, was that she simply didn’t care’but the truth was sadly far deeper then that. The loneliness and the desire she felt had not vanished with Andrew, nor had they abated after his clumsy attempts to force her into something she was only have sure she even wanted. Instead they burned just as fiercely in the back of her mind, a constant pressure to examine a part of herself she didn’t really want to see. “It’s not like this is the first time?” She whispered, looking, once more, between her fingers to the dark fabric above her. ??so why are you making such a big deal out of it?” No answer appeared in the darkness above her, and no miraculous epiphany descended from the heavens to ease the stress upon her mind. Quite the contrary, Dawn found her mind drifting to another time’one that happened in the same place she was laying. There was a girlish giggle, followed by a playful swat on the arm. “Cut it out! You’re tickling me!” “Well how the heck am I supposed to know” I’ve never done this before.” Came the embarrassed reply, Dawn’s own voice ringing hollow in her ears. “Well it’s not like you don’t know what’oooh’mmm?” “Am I still tickling you?” “Mmm’no’that’s’much’oh?” Dawn’s face turned a bright red in the afternoon shade and she sat bolt upright in bed, clapping her hands over her ears with a frustrated noise. “Agh!” she shouted, shaking her head back and forth, as though trying to shake the sound out of her very head. Another sound pushed its way past her hands, one that sounded more urgent and more grounded in the present day. There was an insistent whining, followed by an urgent scratching at her bedroom door. Dawn stared at her door in confusion for a long moment, small tendrils of dread creeping through her chest to ensnare her heart’until she remembered that her guest had come with more then just luggage. Dawn propelled herself off the end of the bed with a kick leap, quickly crossing to her door and yanking it open. “Damek!” She scolded, waggling her finger at the enormous dog sitting outside her door. “What have I told you about scratching on my door?” Shadow’s massive hellhound lay down on his belly, staring mournfully up at Dawn as she knelt to examine the damage to her bedroom door. She had no idea what Shadow fed these two beasts, but long furrows had been dug out of the wood, leaving ribbons of the very same wood on the floor beneath. Dawn turned her green eyed glare back to Damek, sparing a glace for his partner in crime, Jirina, who was lying not far away. Damek returned her gaze evenly, showing that disconcerting spark of intelligence she had come to expect from both dogs. That familiar feeling of dread tickled the pit of her stomach as she gazed into his eyes, causing her heart to freeze seemingly in mid beat. “Is something wrong?” She asked softly, looking at the central staircase. “Something not right?” Dawn wasn’t concerned about an intruder, she was fairly certain that any erstwhile thief or murdered would have found himself set upon by both dogs in short order, and Dawn would have been alerted by the screams. Instead she was more concerned for Lia, who seemed to be still fighting off the effects of whatever it was Soma had given her, that something had happened to her. Neither dog responded however, each one just fixing Dawn with that same implacable stare, knowing what they wanted, but knowing they were unable to accurately communicate it. Dawn gazed back for a moment, before another thought crept into her mind. “Do you need to go out?” Damek sprang to his feet, Jirina not far behind him, and woofed heavily before turning to bound down the stairs, a sound not unlike an elephant tumbling down the entire staircase. Dawn slapped one hand to her face in consternation, rising to her feet to follow Shadow’s dogs. “Figures. Big Dog, Big Eats, Big Pee.” She hopped down the stairs two at a time, a little afraid they might barrel through the screen door instead of waiting, but found the canine pair lounging by the back door as though they had been there all day. Shaking her head Dawn opened the back door, following them out onto the patio and watching them romp out into the woods behind the house. Dawn shook her head with a grin as they took off, leaning against the patio rail and watching them disappear into the forest. “Okay’it’s nice to have dogs again.” As she sought to keep her eyes on the dogs she found her gaze settling instead on a simple wooden platform that was kept separate from the house by a swift running stream. She had intended to build a gazebo there, but had been interrupted in her yardworking endeavors when CW2 had rolled around, sucking her into it’s fever pitch’only to spit her back out one miserable round later. Dawn gazed at the platform for a long moment before slipping her feet into her gardening shoes and stepping off the patio, heading around her tulips toward the simple bridge her father had built that crossed the river to the small island she had intended to build her gazebo on. Removing her shoes Dawn stepped onto the smooth surface of the wood, walking to the middle of the platform and seating herself cross-legged, letting the backs of her hands rest on her knees in the classic yoga position. The voice of her instructor came to her, as it always did when she attempted meditation, the warm tones of his voice brushing over her ears. “The principle for meditation is simply, but difficult to accomplish. As you strive for perfection in the most complicated of your activities, so should you also strive for that same perfection in everything you do. Each day is started and ended with one breath, and that breath can set the tone for how you will live the rest of that day, or how you will sleep the rest of that night. Focus on achieving the same perfection in breathing as you strive to achieve in wrestling. Let each breath be more then simply breathing, let it suck in all your concerns and worries’and then expel them into the air’so that you may seem them more clearly. Focus on that Purity of Breath, and the rest will follow.” By the time she had repeated Ronin’s mantra to herself she had already closed her eyes, deep breaths being slowly inhaled and then exhaled with each moment. She didn’t block out her surroundings, but rather let the feeling of the suns warmth, the rasp of the cool breeze against her skin and the light trickle of the stream behind her feed into her mind. She struggled to push thoughts of Shadow from her mind, unwilling and unable to deal with the circumstances surrounding her new guest just yet. But with one issue repressed, the next forced it’s way to the forefront. Dawn could feel the thunder that rocked the estate, a rumbling addition to each brilliant flash of lightning that left spots dancing before her eyes. The garage floor? “was cold with the rain that had seeped under the main door, and the rough concrete bit into the soles of her feet. Her white tank top and fluffy bunny boxers did nothing to block out the night’s cold, but she hoped that in a short while’she’d never be cold again. Fighting back tears she set up her small footstool beneath the main girder of the garage, climbing it slowly and with shaking steps until she could reach the ceiling. With trembling hands she looped a small length of chain around the main girder, tossing it around and around until she had enough length to properly secure it in place. She swallowed heavily as she brought the end of the chain in front of her, the end that she had fashioned into a crude noose” Tears spilled from emerald eyes that had already been rubbed raw, but she wiped at them furiously, wiping her nose as she did on her bare wrist. Stepping to the top of the ladder Dawn slid the noose overtop of her head, shivering as the cold metal pressed against her skin. Taking the last length of chain she secured it around her wrists, tying it tightly to make sure she didn’t flail about. She paused for a long moment, looking around at her grey surroundings in despair, trailing her eyes over her midnight blue Corvette Coup and her fathers old tool bench just beyond it. “Father?” She choked out, the feelings of shame and recrimination building up in her throat. ??I’m sorry’I don’t deserve to have your name, I’m not even half the’half the athlete you were. But I’ll’I’ll see you soon right? We’ll be together again’please?” Squeezing her eyes shut Dawn rocked back and forth on the stool, starting it rocking on it’s own until she stepped off one side and it clattered to the ground. Pain seared up through her neck and she gasped with pain, the breath fleeing her body as her throat constricted forcibly. She kicked feebly against the air, her eyes fluttering as she fought instinctively for breath, each moment pure agony as her neck and lungs burned. “This is’I’m scared?” She thought, “I don’t want to live anymore’I don’t’don’t have anything left?” The door to the house squeaked open slowly, a shaft of artificial light that cut through the gloom of the garage and illuminated Dawn’s feet. Through tearing eyes she forced herself to look at the doorway, certain she had turned off every light in the? There was a little girl in the door, her hands folded primly in front of her. She was dressed immaculately in a simple white dress, and a silver crucifix hung from around her neck, seeming to almost glow with a light of its own. Long blonde hair hung in baby doll curls past her shoulders, and her eyes’the most striking thing Dawn had ever seen. Brilliant green eyes that seemed to glow with vibrancy and life, they looked so like’so like’Lia? “I don’t have anything left?” Her mind said again as her senses fogged, “I don’t want to’I don’t’want’to’die?” Dawn’s eyes snapped open as she struggled to see past the red film over everything. Her feet hung agonizingly short inches above the ground, but she could not stretch them. Her hands were bound together, so she couldn’t even reach the ceiling above her. The futility of it settled into Dawn’s mind, hot tears leaking past her eyes as the last bits of breath seemed to flee her. Then the little girl opened her mouth and shouted, in Dawn’s own voice, “I WANT TO LIVE!” Gritting her teeth Dawn tucked her knees to her chest, pulling her arms down low over her bottom and then past her legs so her hands were in front of her. She reached up, scrabbling for purchase on the ceiling beams, her fingernails tearing away as she fought for a grip. Finally she wrapped her hands around the cold metal and pulled, lifting her body up as though she was doing her exercises. The first breath was agony, and she choked and vomited as air forced it’s way back into her lungs past her wounded throat. She gulped in greedily, sucking in each breath until her lungs strained to capacity. Inch by agonizing inch she pulled herself atop the main girder, collapsing against it as her body wracked with sobs and deep breaths. She turned her head to seek out her rescuer, but the door was closed and there were no lights’there was no sign of the little girl? Dawn could feel the tears running down her face, the old shame that burned down her cheeks and dripped from her chin. She shuddered with unspent sobs, opening her eyes to the summer sky’and freezing in place, her breath caught in her throat. The little girl stood before her, hands clasped behind her back and a childish smile upon her face. She reached out one hand to Dawn’s face, gently wiping away a tear with her thumb. “You’re almost there?” She whispered, “Don’t give up yet?” The back of Dawn’s neck was cold and wet, she blinked twice at the sensation, and when she opened her eyes the second time the little girl was gone, with no trace that she had ever been there. The wet feeling on the back of her neck persisted, and she turned to find Damek staring her in the face, his wet nose having been pressed to the back of her neck. He whuffled, dragging an enormous tongue up the side of Dawn’s face, cleaning all the tears from that side before giving her another enormous kiss up the other cheek. Dawn’s lower lips trembled and her shoulders started to shake as the shame she felt burned out from her stomach and onto her face, hot tears spattering on the wood below. She threw her arms around Damek’s massive neck and sobbed into his fur, great sobs that shook her whole body as she let out some of the shame she had been holding inside.
