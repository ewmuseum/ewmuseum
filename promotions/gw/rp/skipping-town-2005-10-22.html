Skipping Town?

22 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/22/05 Last Week Before Evolution... It was rare for Virgil Ambrosi to spend any more time than he absolutely had to walking the hallways of the Global Arena. There was little to concern him, especially when there was no match to prepare for. One might take his presence out of context and make the claim that he might actually be mildly interested in one of the rivalries going on around him, but no such luck. In fact, his business here was entirely predictable; It's the only place he could find her. Had the last week not entailed a covert operation at the McFarlane estate, he still would have seemed as lost as he did right now. It wasn't as though he couldn't function without her... he just had little motivation to do so. The people he walked past seemed to pass to the side of him with a wide distance, as though some barrier forced them to keep their distance. Time spent around them had not made Virgil much more hospitable or amicable toward the human populace, and this would likely never change. There was little use in playing with one's food, after all. He ducked into a stairwell, lost in thought as he planned to intrude upon Leinad Soma's rather posh skybox once again. Lia had taken to spending time up there during the shows, something which tended to confuse Virgil slightly, as he'd never particularly noticed her to be all too fond of Soma's games or pressures, but he supposed she wouldn't be doing it without some reason. His footsteps echoed dully through the stairwell, as he slowly climbed higher and higher in the arena. Time spent alone such as this had a tendency to make him think about the state of his being more than usual. In fact, he'd been thinking about here, and he he was still lingering around such a place. The novelty of fighting humans had lost its interest for him, and the lure to shiny precious metals had never particularly exacted any kind of power over him, so what use was there in being here? It was a question that deviled him constantly. There was really no logical reason for a supernatural being, who had every capability to quickly kill anyone he stepped in the ring with, to actually enjoy sparing them... once again, he had been known to let his prey fight a little before he finished them off, but this was entirely different. It was a waste of time from a natural sense. Obviously, whatever kept him here was locked somewhere in his mind. The television exposure was probably not a good thing, considering every one of his kind probably had a horde of others who wanted him dead for some pithy grudge from hundreds of years ago. Certainly it was not for the company. He would generally rather dive into a bonfire than be around anyone he'd been forced to be around lately. When he'd started in Global, being around humans constantly was a very interesting and new experience, but he quickly tired of them. So very catty, obsessed with their own glory and appearance, and oh-so-willing to step on anyone they needed to to claw their way to the top. Even for Virgil, who had been known to pursue his own goals in life, the amount of career-related ego was staggering. Obviously, the company did not keep him here. Nor did the money, the titles, the silly rivalries, or even the opportunity to practice his martial arts skills on a living opponent. But yet, there was some reason he hadn't gotten completely, desperately bored and left. He just wasn't entirely certain what that could be. For now, though, it would have to remain a mystery, and he'd continue to wander the halls of the Global Arena, injecting his malevolent presence into the miserable lives of everyone he met. At that, he smiled slightly. What a delightful thought. He pressed the door open, and stepped into a much less utilitarian hallway. The walls here were not bare concrete blocks, and there were no massive pillars, or piles of construction refuse which always seemed to linger around arenas for some reason. The walls were papered with a florid-looking textured print, which reminded Virgil slightly of a funeral home, but he supposed it was more aesthetically pleasing than cinder blocks. He peered through a long line of glass, down at the arena, writhing with movement as though he were staring into a great bowl of insects. If the explosions and blaring music were any indication, Evolution was getting started. This, of course, was the reason for his being on the top floor in the first place. When there was Evolution, Lia would be in a place that he could find her. Finally. While he had every respect for her need for time alone, unaffected by the myriad of annoyances that accompanied everyday existence, that didn't keep him from missing her terribly, and being completely miserable. Perhaps it was slightly more bearable now that he and Alexis were no longer predator and prey, exactly, but she was still not there. He didn't want to drag her back 'home' immediately by any means, he just wanted to see her again. Everything would be in its proper place once more if he could just lay eyes on her for a few minutes, perhaps even hear the dulcet tones of her voice... perhaps he was being slightly pathetic, but there was no use denying what was there when she was not. All of that was about to be immaterial, though. He'd see her once more, speak to her for a few moments, and everything would be right and proper again. Then he'd be able to back away like she needed, and have the strength to make himself slightly less worthless than he had been lately. He pushed open the well-ornamented door to the skybox, a smile nearly gracing his face, as he knew that Soma himself was rarely up here lately. He stepped into the room, and looked around for a moment, before the look on his face turned to one of sorrow and irritation, as he found himself to be the only being in the room. Somehow, he knew; if she was not here, then she was not in the arena at all... perhaps not even in the city or surrounding areas. A sixth sense of this type had developed within him some time ago. He could practically sense her when she was anywhere nearby, but now, more than before, when he had been blinded by mindless hope, he knew she wasn't here. A despair gripped him, and he quickly turned toward the door... to find himself face to face with one of the more annoying humans he'd had the displeasure of meeting thus far; none other than his hostess, Dawn McFarlane. Dawn looked at the moment to be about as mournful as Virgil, though Virgil would have no pity party, as it were. 'She's not here, is she?' Virgil couldn't exactly put a finger on why Dawn irritated him so much. It wasn't as though she by herself were so particularly loathsome. Perhaps it could be whittled down to the fact that she wanted the same thing he wanted. Or really, he supposed 'thing' wasn't precise enough. They both wanted the same woman. 'That should be completely obvious.' Dawn's eyes narrowed. 'You don't have to be an ass about it. I just asked a question.' Virgil was in no mood to argue at the moment. 'And I just answered it. Now that we're done here...' Dawn reached out and grabbed his arm as he tried to walk past. Virgil stopped, and gritted his teeth. 'How intent are you on keeping that arm?' She didn't let go, which seemed to surprise Virgil. Usually, when he threatened to remove someone's body parts by force, they were generally compliant. The fact that he could make good on his threat didn't seem to faze Dawn. 'You're going to find her, aren't you?' The tone wasn't pleading or pathetic, as Virgil had expected. This alone managed to convince him to listen, out of curiosity. 'I refuse to interrupt her journey. I'm only following her to put my concerns about her safety to rest.' He started out the door again, shaking Dawn's hand off. 'I'm going with you, then.' 'No.' The word seemed very final, as though carrying with it the largest period in the world. Dawn's blinked at him, before her face twisted into a glare. 'Why the hell not?' Virgil turned around to point a finger at her, almost accusingly. 'You'll be nothing but a hindrance. I need no assistance nor company, and thus, I will be taking none of it.' She might have had something else to add to it, but Virgil had now tuned her out. He was usually inflexible once he'd made up his mind about something, and now would be no different. There would be no search party, only him. Somewhere in his mind, there was happiness. At last, he'd be able to shake off this involvement with all the other people that wanted to take Lia from him. Inwardly, he breathed a sigh of relief. - - - - - - - - - - Present The McFarlane Mansion Virgil had noticed something very convenient about being a vampire in his experience of being one, and that was, notably, the ability to travel with little or no baggage necessary. He simply required a small supply of blood, and a reliable method of movement, which he had purchased only recently, a black, rather swank looking Valkyrie Rune motorcycle, parked carefully outside the mansion. Both prerequisites now satisfied, he prepared to leave. After all, there was little to worry about. Cyrus was quite capable of defending himself now, and would probably get annoyed and leave for Zdice within the week, and he'd be back by then. The Den hadn't showed their faces since Virgil killed the majority of a special force sent to take care of Alexis, and with Cyrus around, Virgil wasn't entirely worried about them, anyway. After all, that was the reason he'd lingered this long in the first place. He couldn't understand the need he felt to protect Alexis. She was no less food than any other human, but something about her seemed so worthwhile that he couldn't help being protective of her. Thinking about it frustrated him immensely, because it presented him with a question lacking an answer. Thus, he resolved not to think about it, at least until he got back. Pulling his coat over his shoulders, he started for the door, and was almost immediately stopped by a voice from upstairs. 'Are you leaving?' Virgil looked up to see Alexis walking down the stairs. Had she not been halfway down, he would have assumed she'd been watching him the entire time, which would have severely irritated him. 'Yes.' She stopped, and gripped the banister rather tightly. 'Are you coming back? I don't really want to hang around here by myself...' Virgil shrugged nonchalantly. 'It may be some time, but I'll be returning. Cyrus will remain here, as will Dawn, I assume.' Her eyes widened a little. 'But I think Cyrus would rather kill me... he doesn't like me around very much.' Virgil forced a sigh. 'I must go sometime, child. What am I to do, drag you with me?' Alexis nodded. 'One of those guys got away, Virgil. They're going to come back here, and I don't want it to be when you're not around. You're the only one who gives enough of a shit about me to actually try to protect me.' At this, Virgil didn't have much to say, mostly because he realized that she was more or less right, something he was never too fond of admitting, when the one right wasn't him. 'Please just take me with you.' Virgil shook his head, getting somewhat annoyed by the almost constant delays he'd been confronting. 'No. I will not do that. It will be far too dangerous. But, I will stay here with you one more night. After this, I will make arrangements for your safety.' She blinked, rather confused. Something reminded her of the small cell he'd kept her in after capturing her near his mansion. The thought didn't make her very comfortable. '... okay, as long as that's not anything unpleasant, or anything.' Virgil raised an eyebrow. 'Beggars cannot be choosers, as they say.' He let his coat fall from his shoulders, and caught it on his forearm, before walking back down the hallway once again, casting an annoyed glance at Alexis, who turned her eyes away from him. She hated being a burden to him, especially at a time like this, but for once, she realized that she needed someone to protect her, that what she'd been able to do hadn't worked. If it meant irritating a being that could theoretically kill her in several thousand ways, it was better than certain death, at least.
