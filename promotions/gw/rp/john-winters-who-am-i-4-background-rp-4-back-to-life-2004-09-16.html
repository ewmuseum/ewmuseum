John Winters: Who am I#4 Background RP#4: Back to life?

16 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 16/9/04 Previously? “You see Johnson?” The sheriff said not taking his eyes away from Winters. “This is what these people are like. We have to stem the problem before it becomes a catastrophe.” “Okay’but I’m not watching!” The deputy finally conceded. “That’s fine,” The sheriff replied. “This ain’t for the weak of heart anyhow?” With that the sheriff proceeded to attack Winters, kicking and punching him, while still wearing the handcuffs. After a couple of minutes the sheriff was out of breath and so stopped the vicious attack. Winters lay in the ditch, beaten to a bloody pulp. “Ugh’does’that make you feel better” Beating a man while he’s cuffed?” Winters managed. The sheriff drew closer, developing a snug smile. “Hell yeah’and thanks for reminding me’I’ll take those cuffs back now.” The sheriff stood on Winters head, reached over and unlocked the cuffs. “I’ll be taking those back now’and remember if I ever see you around here again’I’ll do a lot worse.” “No,” Winters replied. “You will not’the next time you see me - if you see me coming at all - I will not be cuffed.” “I’m terrified I’m sure?” The sheriff said, walking confidently back to his car. Present Day? John Winters bolted up right in his bed, beads of sweat trickling down from his forehead. It was the nightmare again. The one with the ditch. Winters uneasily threw off his bed covers and stood, a faint glimmer of moonlight managing to penetrate the restrictive nature of the blinds, glistened against his skin. He made his way over to the kitchen area of his small box-like apartment room and poured himself a glass of water. He took a sip and then continued to remember the past? Six Months ago? It seemed as though night came much too quickly to John Winters, but when you’re lying in a ditch beaten to a pulp, slipping in and out of consciousness, time just flies by. During his brief, yet lucid, conscious states Winters had nothing to think about except the incident and the events leading up to it. Was he wrong to trust them? In hindsight, the answer was obviously yes, but at the time, before knowing what the outcome would have actually been, was he wrong to trust them? That question was not as easy to answer. First of all, the farmer found Winters in the cornfield surrounded by a couple of metres of chaos. Fair enough, Winters could understand how it looked and could understand the farmer’s initial reaction at least. Winters tried to think of the situation if the roles were reversed. A strange man in the middle of a destroyed patch of cornfield claiming have amnesia’the farmer was right not to trust him. However, surely, after the hour they spent together he had been at least a little convincing, especially when you consider that he actually did have amnesia. No. The farmer refused to believe Winters story from the start and knowing that nothing could be proven in relation to the partially destroyed cornfield he extracted his own kind of revenge. The only kind of revenge he knew how to deliver. The farmer and the sheriff appeared to be old friends at the farm and it would not surprise Winters if they were. The sheriff also knew that farmer was not one hundred percent sure that Winters did anything wrong, but money talks’as does friendship. And to believe that he only agreed to go with the farmer because he believed that the authorities would help him, almost made Winters cringe’or would’ve done if he’d been capable of moving at the time. Was he wrong to trust them? Yes’he was. Even without knowing the full extent of the farmer’s intentions he should’ve just disarmed the man and fled. Waking up one day in a cornfield and discovering that you do not know who you are is a disorientating experience to say the least. Winters figured that the decision was made with this sense of disorientation and the fear that came that emotion’if he were of sound mind and thought about it more, he figured things would’ve ended up differently. If he were thinking correctly, he would’ve realised right away that there was only one person he could ever trust’himself. Recovering from amnesia alone was not “living” by any sense of the term’it was about surviving and trusting other people blindly is not surviving’it’s like digging your own grave. With these thoughts resting heavily upon his mind, Winters slipped into an unconscious state once again and as he felt the warm comfort of the darkness take him, he wondered when - or indeed if - he would ever see the radiant glow of sunlight again?
