Answering machine

28 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/28/03 The door opens to Brick's apartment and in he walks. He's carrying a load of luggage as he has just gotten back from Texas. He drops off his luggage near the door and walks over to his answering machine to check for messages. The red light is blinking quickly, which means that there are a good deal of messages on his machine. He presses play to hear the messages. '5 new messages,' says the automated voice. 'Praise the Lord. Jamal, it's Mom. I called to tell you about the tickets to Dead Zone that I got in the mail.' These words surprise Brick. 'I was curious about it since you made such a big deal about me and the rest of the family being there, and also there was no return address and no names signed on them. Anyway, I just want you to know that I'm proud of you, and I can't wait to see you win that title.' Brick had made a big deal about his family coming to Dead Zone. More to the point, he didn't want them there. He figured it was too much of a risk, as much as he would have liked to have them there. There'd be nothing to stop them from being targets for Salaok and the Nevermore. It was something he wouldn't put past them, especially considering that a few weeks ago, Salaok mailed copies of a video he made when he infiltrated Brick's sister Monica's wedding. It would especially be precarious seeing as how he was facing Sage Sabbot for the World title in a Hell in A Cell match with win by submission only rules. Normally, Brick wasn't one to win matches with submission moves, not that he didn't know quite a few. BEEP! 'Hey, Brick, it's Aaron. I got these front row tickets to Dead Zone in the mail. I didn't know if you sent them or not, 'cause there was no return address. Call me when you get this.' BEEP! 'Hey, Jamal. Kyle and I got the tickets. We weren't sure if you sent them, because they came with no return address. If you did send them, we just wanted to let you know that we got them. Bye.' It's all a little too convenient that Brick's family and friends all receive tickets to Dead Zone. And front-row tickets at that. Brick smells a rat. BEEP! 'Mr. Jamal Randall, we would like to offer you-' Right away, Brick presses the erase button. He has no patience for telemarketers. BEEP! 'Hello, Brick.' Right away, Brick recognizes the voice and anger starts to overcome him. Somewhere in his mind, he knew that the man the voice belonged to was behind this stunt. He knew there was only one person who would be sick enough to pull off such a stunt. Salaok. “I hope your family got the complimentary front row tickets I sent them. I thought it was appropriate that they be up front to see your finest hour, or rather, see your destruction. But then again, being that they’re going to be so close to the action, one never knows what might happen. My prot’g’s do have a tendency for being, shall we say, spirited. I’d hate to think of your poor mother or your lovely sister getting hurt. Of course, Sage will do anything to prove himself to me. Just a little something for you to think about. I’ll see you at Dead Zone, Brick.” BEEP! “End of final message.” By now, Brick is seething with anger. This man has done whatever he could to make his life hell for years now. This was “Salaok, you’re a dead man. You and Quartz and Kullypso and especially that little bitch Sabbot. You’re all dead!” Brick now more than ever had a reason to fight. To obtain the prize that has so often eluded him, naturally, is his goal, but now he has another purpose: to send a message to the Nevermore. And at Dead Zone, Sage Sabbot will be his conduit.
