Purgatory of Memory: Cheating the Reaper

23 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/23/04 ??another taste of the evil I breed, will level you completely?” Friday, July 29, 2004 0457 hours, local time “the outskirts of Cielo Vista, TX The moon hung in the sky, obscured by passing clouds, surrounded with a sickly glow. A ground mist, ghostly and glowing was rising out of the parched earth on either side of the narrow two-lane road. Misty rain hung suspended in the high beams, making her shake her head, squinting through the fog on the inside of the window. She groaned, reaching down to crank on the air conditioner. It would likely clear the window about the time she made it back to Dallas. She’d only made a lousy ten-dollar tip for taking that stupid old bastard all the way out in the sticks” of course, he’d hinted at more as he adjusted himself for the millionth time. She wondered, then? as she often did, just why it was that she had the ability to attract nothing but creeps and losers. In less than a month, she would be turning forty? she sighed as she picked up her cigarettes from the dash, lighting one with a hand that shook with nerves. She was glad he’d gotten out of the cab when she’d insisted? otherwise she would have had to beat his head in. There weren’t many female cabbies in Dallas, hell, as far as she knew, she was the only one. She liked to think it made her unique? but at the same time, she was aware of what the others said about her. Freak. Lesbian. Whatever? let them talk. It paid the bills? and that was enough. The tires hissed on the wet asphalt, the vents blew a stream of tepid air around the interior of the car. Excluding the creepy weather, everything seemed peachy? and besides the last fare, Tina Contesa was having a decent night, that is, until she found the girl. Tina was solidly built, just a few inches shy of six feet. Her face was open and friendly, and her chocolate brown eyes were usually twinkling with amusement at some internal joke. The only thing that marred what would have otherwise been a beautiful face was the scar. It ran down from her hairline to the edge of her mouth, dragging her smile off center? making her seem cryptic. At the apex of the scar, her hair was white? one thick section? dead. So was the man who had done it to her? or at least he was the next best thing to the eternal state? he was in prison. Serving a double-life sentence for manslaughter. She’d been nineteen then, and he was older? wiser? or so she’d believed. Dorian “Bubba” Taylor. He’d been a senior when she was a freshman? a football player? the boy all the girls wanted. And she’d won his heart? She lost everything a few months later, when he showed up at her house, high as a kite, ranting and raving about her being unfaithful. Her parents had been home, and like any good Italian parents, they had her best interests at heart. He’d killed them both? in cold blood? and come after her. How she survived, she didn’t really know? all she remembered was the knife, flashing through the air? and the fire in her face. She woke up in the hospital? alone. She’d put him away? her parents had raised her to be an honorable girl. Honor. That was a funny thing. Thinking about honor now, after all these years? and what did she have to show for it? An empty bed, filled with cat hair. At least she had Chicken? if nothing else, he loved her. Well, as long as his dish was full, and his belly was being rubbed, he did. Her hair was sprinkled with salt and pepper gray, but in the right light, and from the right side, she looked almost normal. Maybe a few too many crow’s feet at the corners of her eyes? maybe just a bit too muscular in the arms? otherwise, passable. She yawned, squirming in the seat? another day, another lousy dollar, she thought to herself as she turned off the CB radio... followed by the meter, officially signing off for the night. Without looking, she flicked the switch, killing the light atop the car and then cranked the radio, relaxing as Alan Jackson sang about it being “5 o’clock somewhere”. She settled back in the seat, the springs creaking under her weight as she groped in the breast pocket of her faded flannel shirt for that last stick of gum. She almost reached for the cigarettes again, and then reconsidered, her fingers drummed restlessly on the wheel as she popped the gum in her mouth. She shook her head with a heavy sigh? doctors orders? quit or die? not that she really had any reason to live? but still, it was the principle of the thing? that whole raging against the dying of the light? or whatever in the hell that stupid poem she’d studied in college had said. The night crept by the fogged windows, unnoticed as the blue Crown Victoria made its way down the dark blacktop. The mist hugged the ground, swirling around the headstones in the small cemetery as she passed it, not even noticing. She shivered, suddenly creeped out? chilled to the bone. There was something strange in the air tonight? and for some bizarre reason, she found herself slowing down at a dark, narrow road, quickly turning onto its uneven surface? tonight she just felt like driving. There was nothing to go home to, really and this little detour would buy another half hour- a half hour she didn’t have to spend in the empty house? alone with that goddamn cat like some pitiful spinster. Lightning crackled across the dark sky, startling Tina from her cynical reverie. With a shaky smile, she pushed her hair back from her face and then fiddled with the radio. The visibility on this road was lousy at best. Darkness seemed to soak up all the light from her headlights? which is why she didn’t see the girl until the last minute. Suddenly, a figure loomed in her vision, causing her to stomp on the brakes, fishtailing wildly on the slick asphalt. She skidded to a stop, in a squeal and stench of burnt rubber, narrowly missing the girl standing in the middle of the road. With a shriek more suited to a hellcat, Tina leapt from the car, uttering a string of curses as she stormed towards the still figure. “What the hell is wrong with you, ain’t you got no notes”! “the fuck are you doing in the middle of the road” You could have gotten us both killed-? The rest of the verbal barrage died on her lips as she reached the girl’s side. The girl simply stared forward into the rain, her entire body trembling, and her gaze clouded and distant. Her mouth hung slackly, blood crusted on her split lip, darkening her teeth. Her hair was drenched, hanging in her face in tangled strands. Tears streamed from her eyes, mingling with the rain, trailing down her cheeks with pink tracks, cutting through the smears of blood. Both her eyes were blackened? and deep purple marks stood out on the pale skin of her neck. Finger marks. With a trembling hand, she reached out and touched the girl’s shoulder, jerking her hand away almost immediately. The girl’s skin was burning up. The shirt she wore was nothing more than a shredded rag, exposing one pale, freckled shoulder, and the bloody gouge that was there. It looked like? Tina took a step back in horror, pressing a hand against her mouth. It looked like a wild animal had bitten her. Flesh had been torn from her shoulder and neck, leaving a weeping, ragged gash. The girl moaned, and then wobbled slightly, making Tina look down. “Good Lord,” she whispered, “who did this to you?” The girl was only wearing one shoe, and she was naked from the waist down, except for a pair of blood-soaked panties. Her thighs were streaked and smeared with blood? and her bare foot was swollen and bleeding. How had she gotten here? Had she walked all this way? in this condition?” Tina stopped thinking at that point, and simply reacted, taking the girl gently by the shoulders. She looked into her eyes, praying to the Virgin for help. “Honey,” her voice cracked, she had to clear her throat before continuing. “Are you” are you ok?” Her only reply was a soft whimper, barely audible over the rising wind. With another muttered curse, Tina returned to her hack, wrenching open the back door with enough force to almost tear her own arm out of the socket. She reached up in the back window, pulling down a tattered plaid blanket. She returned to the girl’s side, taking her arm, and pulling her towards the car as the skies opened up with a mighty roar. Lightning flashed across the heavens, flickering in her lifeless gaze as Tina half lifted, and half tossed her in the back seat. Tenderly, Tina spread the blanket over the girl’s battered and soaked body and then hopped in the front seat again, shaking her head. Mamma mia? this was? she slammed her fist down on the dash, wincing at the sudden sound. What was she going to do about this? One thing was certain? if she didn’t do something? the poor girl could die out here? on a night like this. She stared out the window, thinking about Fate? thinking about the story of the Good Samaritan. She watched the rain slide down the glass, silently? and then her gaze stole to the rearview mirror? and she was watching the girl? she lay there, woodenly, staring unblinking into the night, shivering uncontrollably. Her legs were hidden beneath the blanket, but her flat belly was exposed, her mangled shirt bunched up behind her back. Uneven scratches covered her pale stomach? blood still flowed from them weakly? they almost looked like letters, carved into her skin. With a gasp, Tina wrenched her gaze away? what sort of monster would do that to a girl? She looked maybe nineteen or twenty? it was almost like looking into a mirror? at the past. D’j? vu settled over her in waves, making her feel sick. She turned around quickly, and threw the cab in gear. Dallas. She’d take her to the hospital? or maybe to Doc Bailey? he’d know what to do? ---- “Come on Mary, don’t fear the reaper?” she sang along softly, under her breath? the country had been replaced with classic rock, a few miles back, as the songs had gotten more and more melancholy. A cloud of death seemed to hang over the car, and for a moment, she felt like the ferryman on the river Styx, carrying the dead to the afterlife. The girl, however, wasn’t dead yet. Yet being the operative word. Tina hummed along to the song, her fingers drumming on the steering wheel, her jaw clenched in consternation. She was jittery, and the weirdness of the night was getting to her. She risked a look in her rearview mirror, and saw that the girl was asleep, muttering and moaning to herself. Quite suddenly, as the song reached its crescendo, the girl sat up and screamed, long and loud, the sound drilling into Tina’s head like an ice pick between the eyes. She swerved to the side of the road, slamming the cab into park before twisting around in the seat, her face ashen, her eyes wide with fearful concern. The girl sat bolt upright, her eyes wide and filled with terror. Her fist was pressed to her mouth as the wails continued, sobs wracking her slight body. Tina reached out a hand to touch her, and the girl scuttled backwards, colliding with the passenger’s side door. Blackness closed in around the car, the only sound the anguished cries of the girl, which drowned out even the sound of the rain. Tina sighed, and then spoke, her voice loud in the silence, as the girl’s cries ended suddenly, replaced with a strained silence. “Honey” Are you alright?” The girl seemed to snap out of her trance then, and she fixed her vacant emerald gaze on Tina. Her look was shattered, pain and confusion rode in those windows to her soul, and the sight cut through Tina’s tough fa’ade like a hot knife through butter. Her mouth worked, and then she spit out the first words Tina had heard from her. Her voice was husky, and soft? the screams having ravaged her vocal cords. “The darkness” I?” A fat tear rolled down her cheek as she struggled for words. Tina shook her head, grasping for a meaning, and then she had an idea? without turning from the girl’s tormented gaze, she twisted the knob on the dash, turning on the interior light. The girl relaxed visibly as a bright amber glow fills the backseat. The light is not flattering, as now Tina can better see the damage to her. Scratches mar her cheeks, purple circles of exhaustion ring her eyes? and her bottom lip was swollen, still caked with dried blood. The girl took a shuddery breath, and then looked over Tina’s shoulder, her gazing growing distant again? her voice is hollow when she speaks again. “In the darkness” his eyes? I see holes? holes like eyes. My mind is full of them? I can feel them inside me? the holes? the darkness?” Tina shivered, the words made no sense, but the haunted look on her face, and the anguish in her voice made them nonetheless real. The girl fell back against the door, her face growing ashen as she gasped as though suddenly stricken with pain, and then her eyes closed. Tina didn’t waste another second, instead she turned and threw the car in gear, leaving the interior light on to keep the girl’s terror at bay. She wasn’t cut out to be the Good Samaritan, but she had become one regardless. In for a penny, in for a pound? and she would not let this girl die?
