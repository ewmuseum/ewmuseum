Learning

1 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 1st, 2004 'Tell me, student,' asked the old monk, 'how do you feel?' 'As well as can be expected, sensei,' grunted Akodo Rokku, grimacing through his pain. Rokku had known he was in for hell when he'd asked Seikua to train him, but somehow, he had still managed to underestimate the monk. He couldn't remember the last time he had been this physically and mentally exhausted -- a scant few days of training felt like they had stretched on for weeks. Now, as Rokku continued into the third hour of a one-armed handstand, he wondered what his old sensei could possibly have in store for him next. 'You do not look particularly well, student,' said Seikua, sitting down in front of Rokku and producing an apple from his robes. 'In fact,' continued the wiry old monk, taking a large bite out of the apple, and smiling broadly, 'you look like hell. What's wrong, student? Is sensei being too hard on you?' 'N-no sensei,' grunted Rokku, trying to ignore the stinging sweat running down into his eyes, 'I find your training methods enlightening, invigorating, and not the slightest bit like ancient torture methods.' Seikua laughed raspiliy, and rose to his feet, picking up his metal staff as he did so. He began to circle Rokku, the rings on his staff jingling as he walked. 'You must understand, student,' said the old monk, 'that all I do, all I have put you through, is necessary and important. These days of physical and mental tests, pushing you to beyond the limits you thought you had, they are just a precursor to your real lessons.' 'With all due respect, sensei,' said Rokku, 'I have a fight I have to be ready for tomorrow. You've been running me ragged for days, and if this is just the introduction, I think it's time we got down to business.' Frowning, Seikua crouched down before Rokku, looking him in the eyes. 'Hmm,' said the old monk, 'yes, I think you're right. You're ready now.' With that, Seikua placed a hand on Rokku's chest and shoved. Yelping, Rokku landed flat on his back, gasping for air. Before he could even think of getting up, the spindly old monk was standing over him, grabbing him by the shoulders, and hauling him up to his feet. 'Time for the real lesson to begin, student,' said Seikua, flashing a predatory smile. Seikua's palm met Rokku's chest, and the Steel Lion suddenly found himself flying across his back yard, and crashing into a tree. 'There are points,' said Seikua, as he walked towards the fallen Lion, 'all over the body, through which chakra is channeled. They are called the tenketsu. Now, with the proper training?' Rokku struggled to his feet, trying to draw on his inner strength to push away his exhaustion. Concentrating, Rokku reached deep down, and felt the now-familiar cool blue strength rising up from within him. Then Seikua was there, driving a claw-shaped hand into Rokku's stomach. The Steel Lion screamed in pain, and suddenly, that strength was gone. '? you can strike those points, and block them,' continued Seikua, 'like I have just done to you. The amount of control you have developed over your chakra in such a short time is impressive, student. But to one like me, it is nothing. You always draw your chakra through the same points, and that leaves you vulnerable. An attacker need only strike a few of your tenketsu to cut you off from your inner strength, and leave you vulnerable.' Coughing harshly, Rokku collapsed to his hands and knees. He felt like all the air had been sucked out of him, like he was dying of thirst, like he hadn't eaten in weeks. He strained to drag himself off the ground, but to no avail, and his limbs gave out on him, depositing him roughly on the ground. 'Tch,' chided Seikua, sitting cross-legged beside his student, 'you've got so much to learn yet, and little time to learn it in. The path for your chakra may be cut off, but your chakra is still there. You must simply force it to flow around the block. Make your chakra flow from many points at once, and you greatly increase your power, as an enemy must block all of your tenketsu to separate you from it. Now, listen to sensei closely. Close your eyes. Envision your body as a vessel, an empty shell filled with tubes and valves. Lock this image into your mind, and see where I have locked those valves. From there, reach out, and force your chakra to flow through different tubes, and you will regain your strength. Do not give up. If you do, you will never be able to lift yourself from this grass, and you will never go on to the next lesson.' Unable to move or speak, Rokku forced his eyes to close. He could do this, he would do this. Forcing his pain and exhaustion aside, Rokku did as his sensei instructed, envisioning the pathways in his body through which his chakra flowed. As he fell further into the vision, it became clearer, and he could see the points within his body where that he drew his chakra through -- but more importantly, he could see the ones Seikua had blocked. Gritting his teeth, Rokku reached with all his will, struggling to grab onto something, anything that could give him strength. Seikua watched as fresh beads of sweat formed on his student's forehead. He watched as every muscle in Rokku's body tensed, though he was sure that the Steel Lion himself didn't realize he was doing it. And he watched as chakra began to find it's way into Rokku's limbs, first in a slow trickle, then, in a veritable gush. Seikua smiled as his student unleashed an epic roar, and hauled himself up off the ground. 'Very good, student,' said Seikua, standing up himself, 'you are learning quickly. Tell me, are you ready to learn more?' 'Yes,' said Rokku, excitement creeping into his voice, 'please, sensei, teach me more.' 'Very well,' said Seikua. Then he struck Rokku's forehead, and the world went dark. 'Uh, sensei,' said Rokku, his excitement quickly turning to confusion, 'I appear to be blind.' 'Yes,' said Seikua, stepping away from Rokku and beginning to circle him, 'I have blocked the tenketsu around your eyes, stealing your sight. Do not try to force your chakra to flow to them now -- I have removed all paths, and you would simply waste your energy. This is an important part of your next lesson. Now, you have told me of this sense you have developed? you receive strange feelings, find yourself 'knowing' things, do you not?' 'Yes sensei,' replied Rokku, trying to locate the source of Seikua's voice, 'but what does that have to do with you blinding me?' 'It's simple, really,' said the old monk, 'by robbing you of your sight, I will force you to develop this sense in short order. Oh, and don't expect to be able to rely on your hearing -- or any other natural sense, for that order. Confusing those is a fairly simple trick. Now, reach out with this 6th sense of yours and find me -- or die.' Taking a deep breath, Rokku reached out with his mind, trying to find that sensation, that feeling of life and energy. Unfortunately, his concentration was broken by hard metal colliding with his back. 'Come now, student,' laughed Seikua, 'you can do better than that! My blows will only come faster and harder, until you can sense my presence in the elements around you, and deflect them!' Growling, Rokku tried to block out Seikua's voice, and concentrate harder. He thought back to his meeting with the revenant Jamester? to the times he'd seen Soma and felt a chill? all the way back to that day on the Tokyo Tower, when he'd seen the world in it's true, sparkling, living state. Slowly, he began to see it again -- the spirits in everything around him, the swirl of energies in his own body, and finally, he began to feel another energy, raging strong and bright, flaring all around him? And he realized that it was his sensei. In the mundane world, the old monk moved at breakneck speed, landing blow after blow on Rokku's body, but the Steel Lion's mind was to deep into the world of spirits and magic now to even register the pain. His only concern now was how to discern his sensei's presence from the swirl of blinding light that surrounded him. Drawing on every ounce of willpower he could muster, Rokku stared into the light, forcing himself to take in every particle, every glowing ember. Everything around him began to slow. And a pattern formed. In the mundane world, Rokku's grimace became a feral grin, and his arm lashed out, hand catching the old monk's staff almost effortlessly. With a twist and a jerk, Rokku took the staff from his sensei, and sent the old monk flying across the yard. Seikua landed in a heap, but almost instantly was back on his feet, surveying his apprentice. Though Rokku's eyes were still closed, he looked straight at the monk, and smiled. Nonchalantly, Rokku held the staff at his side, and extended his free hand towards his master. The gesture was clear. Come get some. Seikua laughed joyously, and hurled himself at his student. The both of them moved at a blinding speed now, attacking and parrying as if they weren't even thinking before they acted -- which they weren't. They were both letting the flow of their energies dictate their actions, freeing them from thought, from doubt. As Yoshiko watched master and student spar from the porch, she could only come up with one word to describe them: glorious. 'You understand now!' said Seikua, as he ducked a scythe-like kick and thrust his palm into Rokku's chest, initiating another round of punches and blocks, 'Your eyes, your ears, even your body, none of those things matter! They are just tools. Weapons through which the strength of your spirit can act! And when you face this risen one, this 'Jamester' that you speak of, it is that strength that will carry you! The spirit of one such as him, who has passed through the veil of death and rejected a natural death, is twisted and corrupt. That is the 'wrongness' which you sense in his presence, and that is the weakness that you can exploit. When you reach out to the elements, he will stand out as a dark black scar upon them. Let the elements tell you what he will do, and you can counter his attacks before he even knows what they will be. Faith in yourself and the elements is your greatest weapon in this battle, student! Trust in what you feel, and even a defeat can be a victory. Do you understand?' 'Hai, sensei!' shouted Rokku, grinning wide as he turned aside a punch, 'I understand more than I've ever understood before!' 'Good,' replied Seikua, 'then it is time for the day's final lesson!' Seikua lashed out with a high kick, which Rokku quickly sidestepped, and prepared to counter with a kick of his own. But suddenly, the light of his sensei's energy flared. The intensity of the light increased so much and so suddenly, that Rokku felt his mind burn, and he staggered back, stunned. An instant later, the light crashed into him, and everything faded away. To Yoshiko's eyes, she saw Seikua vanish before her very eyes -- then reappear an instant later, driving blindingly fast palm strike into Rokku's chest that sent him rocketing back into the same tree he had struck earlier. The Steel Lion struck the tree with a resounding crack, and slumped to the ground, motionless. Immediately, Yoshiko rushed across the yard towards her lover, but the old monk was at his side in an instant. As the Silken Crane approached, she heard Seikua begin to talk. 'Never stop learning, student,' said Seikua, as he knelt down beside Rokku, and laid his hands on the Steel Lion's chest, releasing his own chakra into Rokku to heal his injuries. 'Know that there will always be one who knows more than you, who has trained more than you, who is stronger. You can never let yourself stop changing and growing, or that person, whoever they are, will find you, and defeat you. That is today's final lesson, student. Now rest.' 'Is he?' Yoshiko trailed off, unwilling to say what she feared. 'He will be fine,' said the old monk, 'though he will need to rest substantially. But don't worry, I will see to it that he is ready to fight his opponent tomorrow. I have taught him all I can in this short time, seen to it that he is as prepared as I can make him.' Yoshiko let out a sigh of relief. 'Thank you,' she said, 'before you arrived, Rokku was? confused. You've given him focus, knowledge, and peace of mind. Before your arrival, I don't know if he could have beaten whatever Jamester's become.' Seikua chuckled. 'This one? he's always been special to me. I have been more than glad to teach him again, even after some of the senseless things he said to me when we parted years ago. Such a thick skull he had then. I'd scarcely believe that this man is the same person as the boy I taught to fight all those years ago, except for one thing.' 'What's that?' asked Yoshiko. 'His spirit,' replied Seikua. 'Oh, yes, he's gained a lot of maturity in the years since I saw him last. He's certainly not the child of rage and anger that he was when I first met him, but his spirit is just as powerful and unbridled as it ever was. I dare not say it while he's conscious to hear it, but he's quite spectacular.' Yoshiko couldn't help but laugh. 'I don't want to say it while he's conscious to hear it either, but I agree. Now, Seikua-sensei? you seem to be finished with Rokku-chan for now. Would you consider turning some of your considerable talents towards training? me?' Seikua raised an eyebrow. 'You've seen my training methods, young lady,' he said, 'are you sure this is something you're ready for?' 'Are you kidding?' Yoshiko asked, 'I sleep with him. If I can share a bed with that big oaf, I'm ready for anything. But more importantly? I need to keep up with him. As much as I love him, and as proud as I am of his amazing talents, there is no way in hell I am going to let him surpass me.' Seikua grinned. 'Spoken like a true Kakita? you are a shining example of you're family's virtues, Yoshiko-chan.' Yoshiko simply returned his smile, and bowed demurely. 'Now,' continued the monk, 'let us begin?'
