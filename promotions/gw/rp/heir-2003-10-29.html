Heir

29 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 29th, 2003 Until a week ago, this three-story building, a cold construct of steel and brick, was all but abandoned. Old paperwork and documentation was stored here, and rarely if ever would the owner make a visit. It was forgotten and neglected by the one who was responsible for it. But not today. Today, the building is alive with light and noise. Dozens of workers scramble throughout it, painting, fixing, remodeling. All of them on one man's payroll, and all of them following one man's vision. That man was Akodo Rokku, and he thought they were doing a bang-up job. The place might be ready to open before even he had dreamed. Smiling to himself, the Steel Lion took a leisurely stroll around the first floor. He had a lot in common with this building. His father had abandoned both of them, left them both to rot or rise on their own -- but he had also assured they'd both be taken care of when he was gone. It was strange, thinking of his father warmly. Strange to think that if it weren't for him, none of this would be possible? *** One week ago. 'I'm sorry Mister Bowen,' said Rokku, looking as confused as he'd ever been, 'could you repeat that?' Rokku sat at his dining room table, directly opposite the lawyer. Bowen had a variety of papers spread out on the table, with charts, graphs, and legalese of all sorts. But none of them made this easier to explain. 'I'm sorry,' said the lawyer, 'I know this must be hard for you to hear. But your father is dead. A warehouse he owns burned down just a week ago, and his remains were found inside by the cleanup crew. They? they were little more than bones and ash, but dental records and DNA testing identified him. Our firm handled all your father's affairs, so we immediately searched our records for his will. It's old, but valid -- and it says very clearly that everything he owned goes to you, sir.' 'I? I just don't get it,' said Rokku, running his hands through his hair. 'How? why?' Mister Bowen stood from the table, snapping his briefcase shut. 'I'm very sorry for your loss, Akodo-san,' he said. 'I'm sure the last thing you want right now is some stranger in your house. I'll just leave the necessary documents and paperwork here, along with my card. Please don't hesitate to call me if you need anything.' Rokku nodded dumbly. 'Thank you Mister Bowen,' he said. The lawyer patted Rokku on the shoulder, and then showed himself out. The door slammed shut, leaving Rokku to his thoughts. Why would he do this? James Michaelson had been a monster, an inhuman beast of extreme cruelty and hatred. Why would he leave everything he owned to the son he saw as a mistake? It just didn't make sense? it didn't make sense at all! Rokku sighed, and let his head fall to the table. Was this some kind of game? A way of messing with Rokku's mind from beyond the grave? Maybe. Trying to make him think he'd killed his father, when all he'd really killed was a monster in his skin? Rokku sighed, and grabbed a stack of papers. He was going over them sullenly when Yoshiko appeared from the bedroom. 'Hey tiger,' she purred, strolling over to Rokku and sliding her arms around his shoulders, 'what's keeping you?' 'Memories,' said Rokku, gesturing towards the papers. Yoshiko picked a paper up off the table and read it over, a perplexed expression forming on her face. 'These are all about your father's assets. What's going on, Rokku-chan?' Rokku sighed. 'He left it to me. Everything he owned. Why would he do that, Yoshiko-chan? He hated me. He was a monster!' Yoshiko furrowed her brow in thought. 'I don't know, Rokku-chan. But we're going to find out.' Yoshiko pulled up a chair and immediately went to work on the papers, sorting and deciphering them with relative ease. 'Hmm, seems to all be pretty standard stuff,' she said, picking up another paper, 'but there isn't the slightest clue as to why he'd... oh. Oh, Rokku. You'd better look at this one.' Rokku took the paper from Yoshiko's extended hand. He thought it was odd that a legal document would be completely handwritten? Then he realized it wasn't that at all. It was a letter from his father. To him. Quietly, Rokku began to read. 'Dear son, 'I'm not sure how much longer I'm going to be with you, and that's why I'm writing this. I'm afraid that Soma? that he's selected me to be like him. I know trying to fight him or escape him would be pointless. He'd only hurt you and your mother to get to me. So I'm doing the best I can to see to it the two of you are protected. This will, though it may be ages before it comes into use, will see to it that you're taken care of. 'I? I have so much more to say, son. So many things I want to show you, to teach you? but there's no time. No time for any of that now. All I can do is try to keep you safe. I'm sorry that you'll grow up never knowing a father. I'm sorry for all the pain I know your mother will be going through. And I'm sorry for the pain I'm sure I'll inflict on you someday. Please, just know this: that isn't me. It's an animal wearing my face. Nothing it says means anything -- it's all just lies and hate. I love you. I will always love you. You're my son, my future! You and your mother are my family. My family means everything to me, and always will. Goodbye, Rokku. Love, Dad The letter slipped from Rokku's fingers, floating gracefully to the table. Immediately Yoshiko left her seat, throwing her arms around the Steel Lion. Rokku returned the hug, pressing his face into her shoulder. And for the first time in years, Akodo Rokku started to cry. *** Rokku was beginning to tear up a little when he felt a hand on his. It was Yoshiko, resplendent as ever in a black suit that matched what Rokku himself was wearing. 'It's time, Rokku-chan,' she said softly. Rokku nodded, and together they left the building. *** One car ride later? It was a beautiful day. Rokku could taste the crispness of the air, feel the moist grass crunch beneath his feet. But most importantly, he could feel Yoshiko's hand, her fingers woven in-between his. Rokku took a deep breath, then began to walk through the graveyard. He and Yoshiko passed stone after stone, until finally he stopped in front of a fresh one. Rokku exhaled slowly as he knelt down in front of it, and placed a wreath of flowers at its base. He let his eyes take in the inscription for the first time since he'd had it commissioned. James Michaelson 1950-2003 Loving Father 'My family means everything to me, and always will.' 'I love you too Dad,' said Rokku, smiling a little. Rising, he placed an arm around Yoshiko, and together they left the graves behind.
