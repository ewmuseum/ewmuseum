'Demons', part 3

6 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 6th, 2003 Sal's Bar and Grill. It wasn't so much of a grill, really. Sal had a fryer in the back, and would slap together slimy burgers when patrons occasionally wanted one. Which wasn't very often. You didn't come to a place like Sal's for a meal. The tables were small, the floor was sticky, and the service was absolute shit. No, you didn't come to a place like Sal's to eat. You came there when you wanted to drink, drink hard, and be left the hell alone. Tonight, Jack Bantam definitely wanted to be left alone. He scratched absently at his bandaged nose as he plopped down on a rickety stool, and leaned his elbows on the greasy bar. 'Ey, Jack,' said Sal, nodding to him in greeting, 'usual?' 'Make it a double,' grumbled Jack, reaching into his pocket and slapping a crumpled ten spot on the bar. Sal raised an eyebrow, but said nothing. A minute later, he placed two boilermakers on the bar, and took the ten. 'Thanks,' said Jack. He grabbed one of the two mugs and took a long swig. Jack had always liked Sal's. After a week like he'd had, getting the shit kicked out of him and being hassled by the cops, this place was exactly what he needed. Sure, everyone here was scum, but they all had an understanding. Sal's was their place. You don't make trouble, you don't mess with anyone, and everything stays peaceful. You make waves, and the whole place comes down on you. It was a strange kind of camaraderie, born of necessity and a deep desire to just relax. 'Looks like someone busted you up pretty good.' Of course, sometimes somebody had to go and test it. Jack glanced up from his drink, and scoffed. It was some long-haired prettyboy in a high-collared black coat -- probably a newly 'made' mook who figured he could do whatever the hell he wanted. 'Maybe,' said Jack, 'but you should see the other guy.' 'Guy?' said the stranger, raising an eyebrow curiously, 'I'd heard it was a little girl. No bigger than five-six, maybe a hundred and twenty pounds, tops. Kicked your ass something fierce.' Jack scowled. Either this guy was trying to diss him public, or he knew something he wasn't supposed to. 'Who the hell do you think you are, giving me that shit?' growled Jack. 'Don't go starting trouble you can't finish, kid.' 'I'm a demon,' said the stranger. 'And I've come to show you hell, Jack.' Slowly, Jack turned and really looked at the stranger for the first time. He was dressed entirely in black, from head to toe -- even his hair was ebon. A steady, cold gaze from the stranger's green eyes bored into Jack, and beneath those -- some kind of mask? Blood red, and metal, etched with a snarling animal's maw. 'Furthermore,' said the stranger, 'this is trouble I can definitely finish.' Before Jack could react, the stranger's hand struck his bandaged nose, making him howl. He nearly fell off his stool, but the stranger grabbed him by the hair and slammed his head into the bar, holding it there. 'Where is she!' shouted the stranger, as he ground Jack's head into the bar. Jack only laughed. 'You picked the wrong damn bar to pick a fight in, freak,' he said. The stranger looked up, and saw the other patrons of the bar rising from their seats, several of them pulling knives, brass knuckles, or guns. 'No,' growled the stranger, 'they did.' The stranger kicked Jack in the gut and flipped him onto his back, as the gunslinging patrons opened fire. With uncommon speed and agility, the stranger flipped over the bar, grabbing the bartender and pulling him down with him. Dazed, Jack watched as bullets whizzed over him, shattering bottles and raining glass down. Blood flowing freely from his nose, Jack dragged himself away from the bar, and slumped against the wall. A few moments later the gunfire died, and the familiar sound of empty clips clattering to the floor echoed through the bar. For a moment, things were silent again. Then the stranger sprung up onto the bar. Jack watched as shuriken flew from the stranger's hands, striking the gunmen in their hands and sending their guns spinning away. The stranger didn't waste a moment, rushing into the pack of thugs and thieves and attacking them ferociously. He wanted to run. He wanted to get out of here, to find safety, sanctuary somewhere. To be anywhere but here. But all Jack Bantam could do was sit and watch, transfixed in horror and awe. He watched as one by one, the stranger with the demon mask systematically disassembled every person in his path. Men he'd known for years, some of them the scariest lowlifes he'd ever met, fell to the floor with bleeding wounds and broken bodies. The stranger was a maelstrom of destruction, an unstoppable force with one simple goal. To get him. Jack finally snapped out of his shock, and scrambled to his feet. He watched as the stranger dropped the last of his attackers with a crushing blow to the throat. And Jack did the only reasonable thing he could do. He ran. The stranger furrowed his brow and gave chase, as Jack burst out the back door into the alley behind Sal's. Desperate to escape, he tipped over the trash cans as he ran, trying to slow the stranger down. But when he hazarded a look back, there the stranger was, leaping over the cans with ease, and closing the gap between them. Cold panic claimed Jack, and he simply ran, pushing himself as hard and as fast as he ever had. When he felt the stabbing pain in his knees, he realized it wouldn't be enough. Screaming out his pain, Jack collapsed to the ground, shuriken sticking out of the backs of his legs. The demon-masked stranger stopped over him, and crouched down, looking him in the eyes. 'Now,' said the stranger, 'you're going to answer my question. Where. Is. She.' 'What the hell are you talking about?' screamed Jack, clutching at his bleeding legs. 'Wrong answer,' said the stranger. With lightning speed, he pulled a wakizashi from within his coat, and drove it into Jack's shoulder. Jack screamed again as blood seeped out of the wound. 'There was a woman,' said the stranger, 'you were paid -- along with a dozen other SomaCorp thugs -- to attack her in her home, and help kidnap her. But you got left behind, after she beat you unconscious. I want to know where she was taken, and I want to know right now. Or I swear, I will show you hell.' 'All right, all right!' Jack sobbed, pain and fear wracking his body. 'We were supposed to help Mister Michaelson grab her, then guard her at a warehouse he owns outside the city. The address is on a card in my wallet. That's all I know, I swear!' The stranger narrowed his eyes, and pulled Jack's wallet from his pocket. 'Is this the card?' he asked, producing a black business card from the wallet. 'Yes!' squealed Jack. Nodding, the stranger yanked his sword roughly from Jack's shoulder, eliciting another scream. He wiped the blood off on Jack's shirt, slipped it silently back into its saya. Then he stood, dusted himself off, and began to walk away. 'Wait,' Jack moaned, 'you can't leave me like this!' 'Yes I can,' replied the stranger, not slowing his stride. Jack struggled to lift himself onto his hands and knees, watching helplessly as the stranger walked away. His vision started to blur, but he caught sight of something -- a golden lion's head, stitched onto the back of the stranger's coat. He knew that symbol? Weak from blood loss and fear, Jack Bantam passed out. *** With one last look at the bloody, broken man in the alley, Akodo Rokku walked away. 'Soon, father,' he spat the last word out like its flavor turned his stomach. 'Soon.'
