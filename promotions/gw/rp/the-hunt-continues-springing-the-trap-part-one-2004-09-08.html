The Hunt Continues: Springing the Trap (part one)

8 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/8/2004 My balcony led to the roof, it was part of the reason I chose the place? my private sanctuary, even before the change, I spent a vast majority of my time up here. Above it all, I could imagine myself a king, looking down on my subjects? or perhaps an eagle, soaring high above it all. I’d been a dreamer, with my head full of facts, and my feet barely touching the ground. And now, what did I have? Watch what you wish for, you may just get it. I’d always wanted absolute power? but I had never once stopped to consider the cost. ---- He crept from room to room, gun in hand, until he finally reached Jackson’s bedroom. A cold feeling of dread crept over him, settling in the pit of his stomach like a lead weight. The black metal lamp on the bedside table was burning, casting a feeble amber glow across the perfectly made bed. The linens were white and black, featuring geometric patterns that perfectly matched the rest of the room? a study in monochromatics. Shadows loomed heavily, and movement caught Gabriel’s attention as he moved towards the bed. He whirled, feeling idiotic when he realized he was pointing the gun at his own reflection in the mirrors that covered one wall. He chuckled, and then stumbled over a cast off pair of shoes. He banged against the edge of the bed, putting his hand out for support, touching something sticky. There, on the bed, cast off with evident haste, were the bloody clothes Jackson had been wearing when he last saw him. Blood-soaked, wrinkled, and damp with perspiration. Slacks, shirt, socks, underwear. Again, he detected that faint smell, the same one from the elevator, stronger here. It smelled stale and sulfurous, like spent matches. Unpleasant, and something about it made the hairs stand up on the back of his neck. A black book rested in the center of the bed, the only other thing there. Gabriel picked it up, giving the cover a cursory inspection. Necronomicon. The book of the dead. He dropped it as though it had burned his fingers, and perhaps it had. All at once, he became aware of a sound, above the noise of his labored breathing and the pounding of his head. A hiss and sizzle? almost like static. He thought perhaps that he was hearing rain on the roof, and then placed the sound for what it was. He was listening to the fall of water from the master bathroom shower. The bathroom door was open, and through the gap came wisps of steam, shimmering in the wedge of light. He used the gun to prod the door open further, rewarded with a draft of steam to the face. The bathroom matched the bedroom, another study in black and white, the countertops and walls made of black marble, while the floor was white with silver undertones. The best things money could buy. And he’d taken Jackson for a pauper, living like a monk. That had been the impression he gave, with his beat up car, and jeans worn with a leather jacket. He didn’t seem like the suit type, but now, seeing his home, Gabriel wondered if all that was an act. Clever misdirection. A beveled mirror covered most of the rear wall, clouded with condensation; it provided nothing but a warped reflection. As he moved into the room, he watched his distorted shadow loom in the glass, impossibly dark and menacing. Tendrils of steam floated in the air, caressing his face, bringing beads of sweat out along his brow. He stepped over the two sodden towels on the floor. Two dry towels still hung on the rack, folded precisely, exactly uniform. The shower was in the far right corner, a glass block and marble affair, large enough for four grown men to shower in. Two heads sprayed water from different angles. Even if the door hadn’t been shut, and clouded with steam, he couldn’t have seen into the stall from here. Still holding the gun in his trembling hand, he grasped the handle, hesitating before pulling the door open. He half expected to pull it open and find Jackson inside, transformed into some horrible monster. The jaws that bite? the claws that catch. The stall was empty. Unoccupied. A pink swirl spun like a dervish in the half inch of water, going down the drain. Blood. He leaned in, avoiding the spray, and turned off the single control, bringing a deafening silence crashing down around him. The sudden silence seemed to announce his presence as surely as if he’d triggered an alarm? or came in with full marching band fanfare. Suddenly overcome with nervousness, he turned back towards the entrance, expecting company. His senses were tingling, he felt as though something was on the verge of happening. Even though the water had been turned off, steam continued to pour over the shower wall, enveloping Gabriel. In spite of the humidity, his mouth had gone dry. Something didn’t compute. Why would a member of the walking dead come home to shower and dress in clean clothes? It was absurd, like something from a Stephen King novel. A thought came, unbidden? little red riding hood, talking to the wolf. He pushed the thought aside, and trudged back out into the bedroom, letting the cool air wash over his sweaty skin. He paused before the wall of mirrors, looking at his pallid face in the reflection. Spots of blood still spattered his cheeks in a fine mist, his eyes staring widely from amid a face lined with exhaustion. He looked terrified, and as he peered into his yellow eyes, something happened, something changed. Those eyes became blue, a deep shade of blue, nearly violet, framed with impossibly long lashes. Not his eyes. Before he had time to register this phenomena, and to step back from the silvery glass, a hand reached out from the mirror, from somewhere beyond the glass, and grabbed him by the front of the jacket, hauling him forward, off balance. He felt his skin pass through the mirror’s solid surface, suddenly gone liquid, malleable like pulling taffy. He would have screamed then, but the shock stole away his voice, leaving him mute with fear?
