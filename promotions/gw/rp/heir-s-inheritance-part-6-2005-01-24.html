Heir's Inheritance Part 6

Date: 1/24/05 FAR, we've been travelin' in FAR Without a home, But not without a star Free, only one can be free We huddle close, and hang on to a dream Ramirez Home January 16, 2005, 2340 hours 'Buena Dormida, mi chica (*Sleep well, my child),' said Teresa sweetly as she tucked her daughter into bed and kissed her good night. It wasn't common for Melina, at her young age, to go to bed this late, although it happened from time to time, especially during shows. The fact that she was tutored privately instead of attending a formal school made this not so much of an issue, as she didn't have be up and getting ready for school as early as most children. Melina snuggled into her pillow and opened her mouth in a dainty yawn. Teresa clicked off the light and closed the door, going back into the living room with Juan, who sat on the sofa, contemplatively fingering the card given him by the American agent. 'You don't believe it would be selling out on Lucha Libre, do you?' Teresa asked her husband. Juan looked up and shook his head, rubbing his fingers over an average-sized chin covered with black specks. His face was smooth to the touch but showed a shadow of growing whiskers. 'It's not that,' he said. 'Lucha Libre has been good to me, and CMLL has been good to both of us... but there are enough Lucha Libre fans who watch American wrestling venues that we wouldn't be considered sell-outs.' Teresa sat down beside him. 'So, what is it, then?' 'It's the legacy we swore to rebuild,' said Juan. 'Can we do that by wrestling in America, where virtually nobody has ever heard the name of Mateo Ramirez?' Teresa was silent. She looked thoughtfully back at Juan, her lips pursed in careful consideration. It wasn't an easy question for her to answer. 'Good question, I'll admit,' she conceded. 'Maybe we should sleep on it and talk to Jos? about it tomorrow.' Juan thought about it a few seconds further and nodded. 'Tomorrow, then,' he said. 'Go on to bed; I'm going to stay up a half hour longer to read.' 'Okay,' said Teresa. 'Good night.' On the boats and on the planes, They're coming to America Never looking back again, They're coming to America CMLL gym January 17, 2005, 1015 hours Jos? had to admit it wasn't the easiest question to answer... he could think of a few points to present, but it would be like shaving the edges off of a square peg so that it would fit into a round hole. 'Would the American audience need to know about Mateo's full story in order to appreciate the two of you trying to rebuild the family name and reputation?' he asked inquisitively. He was going to need to get into their minds even deeper than was the norm for him, as he had always accepted their reasons for training them and helping them get into the professional wrestling business, without wanting to analyze why. 'Maybe not in all its sordid details, but the less you know about a story, the less you can relate to it,' said Teresa, as she slowly executed a set of standard push-ups. 'American audiences like a good story,' said Jos?. 'I know the story of Mateo's fall from grace is a painful one, that's difficult for you to tell. But if you can tell it to the audiences piece by piece, building it up like in a book, that's how you get their attention and get them rooting for you. American professional wrestling isn't quite the same as Lucha Libre. Important as the matches themselves are, the wrestlers have to be able to carry themselves behind a microphone. They need to be able to go out as if onto a stage, and speak to the crowd to work them up. It would give you the opening to tell them your story, and explain your cause, little by little... make them relate to it. Your effort to rebuild the family name will take root there, and gain support among those fans who like to root for the underdog.' Juan looked skeptical. 'They'll be more accepting of it because their culture is different,' he pointed out between sit-ups. 'You remember that reporter on the news ten years ago, calling Mexico, 'the last proud bastion of machismo.' Here, in the local Lucha Libre shows, my wife isn't even permitted to don wrestling tights and climb into the ring against an oppo--' 'I'd hate to tell you... and especially Teresa... some of the things she'd be expected to do in a number of the larger American leagues,' interrupted Jos?, chuckling lightly at the thought. 'Don't worry, there's plenty of chauvinism in America to give yours and Teresa's fight some real merit. But... and this is a big BUT... at least you'll be able to wrestle in those inter-gender tag-team matches where the two of you truly excel. Lucha Libre won't even allow it; this GW league in America, will. Then the natural opinion of women's wrestling among much of the young-male crowd will make your fight to restore the family name, stand out.' Juan and Teresa stopped their calisthenics and looked at each other for a long moment. Then they looked at Jos?, who smiled in a way that showed he was sure he'd hit on something. The Ramirez spouses both looked thoughtful as they resumed their calisthenics in contemplative silence. Gears began to click in their minds, even as their bodies subconsciously continued their workout. Jos? began to tell them all he knew about American world-wide wrestling promotions; the story hooks, the time spent on a microphone, the media coverage, the aggressive promotion and advertising. Juan and Teresa listened with keen interest. Home...though it seems so far away And we'll travel in the light today, in the eye of the storm...in the eye of the storm CMLL Offices January 21, 2005, 0910 Hours The league President raised his eyebrows as he glanced over the forms placed on his desk. Slowly his eyes took in the sight of Juan and Teresa standing on the other side of the desk, with the short American talent agent standing beside them. 'You're sure about this?' he asked. Teresa nodded. 'Yes, sir,' she said confidently. 'Mr. LaPortia has assured us that we can be marketed well through the promotion he represents.' The CMLL President rubbed a hand across the bald spot in his hair, looking thoughtful. 'You're both hard workers,' he admitted. 'I hate to lose you.' Juan smiled appreciatively. The man sitting behind the desk was one of the few people who'd given them unconditional support in their fight to restore their family's legacy. 'Thank you,' Juan said with a slight nod. 'You gave us the head start we needed, and we won't forget that. If for any reason this doesn't work out, we'll call you right away.' The CMLL President laughed softly. 'Oh, don't you fret about that,' he said with a slight grin. 'These promotions make a lot of money... a lot of it. If the paycheque you're handed there doesn't make the league work out, then it's all the other perks you'll get because of the money they make. Two-room and three-room hotel suites... business-class and even first-class seats on airplanes... hundreds of young children smiling up at you as they wait in line for an autograph. Not to mention all the young males in the audience who will be jealous of you, Juan... and all the young women in the audience who will be jealous of you, Teresa.' Stuart LaPortia couldn't keep from smiling, because he knew all too well the truth of the words he was hearing. 'I'll take good care of them in GW... I promise,' he said, offering his hand. The CMLL President accepted the handshake and signed the forms. 'See that you do,' he said. 'I'll see that they're sent out to the GW President right away.' Juan and Teresa nodded. 'Thank you again,' said Teresa simply. 'We owe you a lot. I hope one day we can repay you.' The CMLL President tapped his chest lightly with his knuckles. 'Thank me... by being the best you can be in America. Repay me... by winning, and rebuilding your family name. I was always a fan of your father's, Teresa. I know he'd be proud to see you take this step.' Teresa wiped a tear away and bent over the desk to kiss her friend on the cheek. 'I'm sure he would,' she said, and smiled. Juan put an arm around his wife's shoulders. I'd say we have a plane to catch, then.' Stuart chuckled. 'We have five hours, yet,' he said. 'I made sure to give you that extra time. I'll meet you at the airport terminal, okay?' Juan nodded. 'We'll be there,' he said. Home... to a new and a shiny place Make our beds and we'll say our grace Freedom's light burning warm, freedom's light burning warm Ramirez Home January 20, 2005, 1224 hours Melina sniffled tearfully as she watched her parents pack her favorite doll into the suitcase. She didn't try to fight the packing of her things, but the look of unhappiness and anxiety on her young face was unmistakable. 'No quiero ir, Mamacita,' she whimpered. (*I don't want to go, Mommy.) Teresa turned around and kneeled down, gently taking Melina into her arms. 'No tienes miedo, Melina (*don't be afraid),' she said comfortingly, stroking the girl's long black hair. 'T’o Jos? estar? all? con nosotros tambi’n. Tendr’s muchos amigos pronto (*Uncle Jos? will be there with us also. You'll have lots of friends soon.) .' Juan squatted down beside Teresa and ruffled Melina's hair, smiling knowingly. '“Te gustas la pizza, verdad”' (*You like pizza, right?) Melina's brow furrowed. She looked a bit puzzled. After a second she nodded slowly. 'S?...' she said softly. A twinkle came to Juan's eye as he winked at Teresa before facing his daughter again. 'Hay muchas tiendas de la pizza en Tejas, y le necesitar’n averiguar para nosotros cuya pizza prueba el major,' he said, lightly touching Melina's cheek. (*There are many pizza shops in Texas, and we'll need you to find out for us whose pizza tastes the best.) The child's eyes slowly lit up as a smile crept across her face. Teresa pressed her fingers into the back of Juan's shoulder in a gesture of gratitude. '“As”, entonces nos ayudar’s?' she asked, smiling inquisitively. (*So, you'll help us?) Melina beamed at her parents and nodded her head vigorously. She bravely helped them finish packing, humming her favorite song under her breath. Juan and Teresa hummed along with her. Everywhere around the world, they're coming to America Every time that flags unfurled, they're coming to America Got a dream to take them there, they're coming to America Got a dream they come to share, they're coming to America Dallas/Fort Worth Airport, Texas, USA January 20, 2005, 1732 hours 'The weather in Dallas is partly cloudy, and seasonably comfortable,' said the voice of the pilot as the plane touched down lightly in Dallas/Fort Worth Airport. 'The local time is 5:32 pm. We'd like to thank you once again for flying with us this afternoon, and have a pleasant stay.' Melina's small hand tightly gripped her mother's hand as they disembarked from the plane into the massive terminal. Stuart hadn't been kidding when he talked about the size of this airport... just this airport alone was HUGE; seemingly larger than the whole neighborhood of Mexico City where they'd lived until today. Juan went on the other side of Melina from Teresa and gave her his hand to hold onto as well. The three of them stuck close together, and close to both Stuart and Jos? as the five of them went to the baggage retrieval carousel for their flight. The carousel was late in starting up and producing the baggage for the flight's passengers, causing Juan and Teresa to fidget impatiently. Melina amused herself by watching the adjacent carousel as it moved in its ongoing elliptical pattern like some metallic merry-go-round. Finally, though, the Ramirez family's luggage was produced and they hastened to claim it. They're coming to America They're coming to America They're coming to America They're coming to America 'You'll be staying at the Howard Johnson hotel for now,' said Stuart. 'The fed will put you up for a couple of weeks, so you have time to sign a lease on an apartment and have the rest of your things moved there.' Juan and Teresa took the information in with a small nod of acquiescence. 'Is it far from the GW arena where the shows are held?' Juan asked. 'About a 15 minute drive... perhaps 20-25 minutes if you take the bus,' Stuart replied. 'I'd recommend you take the time to learn your way around the local area in your off-time, to locate shopping centers, movie theaters, restaurants, and any other places you'll want to make use of. The hotel will have plenty of maps for you to use as guides.' They got on a shuttle bus to bring them to the airport's car terminal, so Stuart could claim the rental car he'd reserved. It would take another couple of weeks for the Ramirez family's car to be brought over from Mexico City. Jos? had agreed to fly back down there when the lease on their current apartment was closed, and then drive the car back up to Dallas. Today! Today! Today! Today! Howard Johnson Hotel January 21, 2005, 1900 hours Check-in having been completed and payment for the stay arranged with the GW offices, Stuart helped Juan and Teresa bring their luggage into their reserved two-room suite. The bedroom had a queen-size bed and a cot suitable for a child to sleep on. Melina let herself plop onto the cot on her backside, and bounced up and down on it a couple of times; testing its support and comfort. Teresa unpacked a few of their belongings, placing them strategically around the suite: a picture of the family on the bedroom night table; a set of free-weight dumbbells and weighted ankle straps on the living room floor between the sofa and the TV set; some books in the dresser... and her geode on the coffee table. Teresa sighed and looked a little wistfully at the geode as she set it down; glancing around quickly after she did so, and stepping away. 'You can have dinner in the hotel restaurant downstairs,' said Stuart. 'You'll find a full selection of both standard American and Tex-Mex dishes there at reasonable prices. I'll call you tomorrow so you can sign the last of the paperwork at the GW offices, and you'll be good to go. I'll let you know when your first show appearance is.' My country 'tis of thee (today) Sweet land of liberty (today) Of thee I sing (today) Of thee I sing (today) Juan and Teresa looked at each other and let out a slow breath in unison; nodding resolutely to each other before both nodding to Stuart. 'We'll be ready,' said Juan. 'Good,' said Stuart, and smiled. 'Have a good night... and welcome to America, and the GW.' He closed the suite door behind him and took out his cell phone to make a call. If these two were as good as he'd heard... he was sure he could negotiate a signing bonus to his commission. Today!
