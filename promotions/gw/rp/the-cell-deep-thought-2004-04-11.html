The Cell: Deep Thought

11 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-11-04 Prologue: In the last part of the Cell Jesse had been interviewed by a man, someone who he’d not been too sure was a guy he could trust. So after telling him about his opponent Train he knocked him out and took his paper and pen. When asked what happened to the man, Jesse stated he tripped over his own two feet and hit his head off the bars. They bought the whole story and Jesse was happy, for he finally had a pen and paper to express his self. The day after though the suspension was lifted and Jesse could leave the cell when time permitted itself. So Jesse finally was allowed to roam free, but what he noticed was the cell was much more like a retarded prison than a facility of what he was brought in for. Walking around he ran into many people, some who gave him ideas, others who told him to get away, and few who didn’t know what they were saying. Will Jesse be able to clear his head of the negative thinking? Will he get past the illusions of sick torment he wants to apply to the killer? Or will his fists do all the talking for him? Story Continues: ’Clearing my mind was something I could never do so easily as this. This place drained me of energy, it’d taken the joy out of life for me, and it’d bottled it up and tamed it to become a monster of wrath. When I’d finally been able to get out of my cell I just stepped out of the cell and stood there. It wasn’t the cell enclosing me that bothered me, it was the boxed up freedom I was presented with inside it. Everything in the cell was so confined you could almost feel like you were being suffocated. The walls so close together, the bars so well used that they were like pit bulls stopping you at the fence to a large mansion late at night, and then their was the air… The room stunk. I could sit their and smell myself rot away in that cell. It was unnatural to me. If I’d stayed in that cell I’d have died of the stench… God bless fresh air I say, god bless..’ Jesse stood there on the outside of the cell for a while before he actually moved and left the side of the cell to go wherever he pleases. Walking down the hallway Jesse turned and went into a room where three men sat on a couch watching a horse race. The one man a rather fat and large man, who had brown short hair with a bag of cheese puffs beside him. The man beside him was very old and had gray hair only around his ears and the top of his head was completely bald. Then finally the last man was a younger man, in his early twenties, and showed a little flare at Jesse when he sat down. The kid stared at Jesse for a moment, as if not caring whether he noticed or not. Then he looked at the horse race. The kid wasn’t patient, he disliked the horses racing for his eager mind wanted the fast, flashy, type things in life as he showed when he clicked the remote to change the channel. ’Hey… Turn that back.’ Stated Jesse without looking at the young kid. The kid turned it back but then looked at Jesse. He noticed who he was, and his eyes immediately widened. ’You’re that wrestling guy who killed his girl-‘ But Jesse clamped his hand over the kids mouth and shook his head no. Getting up he left the room and started to walk down the hallway to his cell, but the kid was right behind him. ’Hey! Man, hold up! I just want to talk.’ The once impatient and annoying kid now was a bright alive vivid spark plug as he chased Jesse down the hallway. Jesse turned and went into his cell closing the gate as the kid stopped in front of it and began talking to him. ’You’re Jesse Jamester right? I saw you wrestling once man, you’re good!’ Jesse laid on his bed trying to ignore the kid. All he wanted was peace and quite, he had been so use to it with his girlfriend gone that now he didn’t want to care about anyone but himself. ’So you still wrestle?’ However as much as Jesse ignored the kid, he knew he wouldn’t go away. People were attracted to celebrities, and Jesse was ranked as a celebrity for he’d been on Television, something most people don’t get to do. ’Yes, I’m still wrestling.’ ’Where at? ’Global Wrestling, in Dallas Texas.’ ’You a champion or something? Jesse was hesitant to say. He didn’t want to bring much attention to himself, for this kid had already begun to annoy him. Furthermore the time he had left before he’d snap and do to the kid as he did to the other man was short. ’No…’ That’s when the kids’ face dropped to a droopy dog like face. He looked disappointed, like he hadn’t been given his bone. Jesse hated when people thought of him as something he wasn’t, but this time it was for the better. ’Why you in here kid?’ ’I… I’m a cocaine addict.’ Jesse looked over at him. Raising his eyebrows he wondered how a kid or man as he really was could get hold of cocaine. ’How’d you manage to get that?’ ’A friend hooked me up. He supplied me until just last month when I was busted and sent here. The police said I’d only been a junkie and that I could probably get clean if I worked for it. ’So are you working hard to stop?’ Now the kid began to get interesting to Jesse. Never had Jesse heard of people getting off so easy from drug use and never did had he heard a story so screwed up it that it compared to his. After the kid answered the question and told Jesse how he became hooked, the kid had something to ask Jesse. ’So, did you really kill her?’ Jesse’s body tensed, he knew he couldn’t answer that without really bothering to show anger, but tried, and tried in a way he had yet to do… ’No.’ With a simple answer that is. ’Come on, tell me what happened?’ Nevertheless the kid wouldn’t take that for an answer and so Jesse rearranged himself so he could say it more clearly to the kid. ’I was at the Global Wrestling Pay-Per-View Clash of the Titans when I’d been told something happened to Nikki, my girlfriend. I ran to the area she was found and was told she’d been attacked. So I left the PPV with her to go to the hospital. In the hospital she died, and I was said to be the #1 suspect because of my list of past offenses. So that’s why I’m here.’ ’So you’re innocent then. Aren’t cops stupid?’ However Jesse didn’t get to reply for two guards came up and grabbed the kid and escorted him down the hallway back to the room. Apparently he wasn’t allowed to be talking to anyone outside of the room. Jesse felt bad but he had his peace back so it was a loss he could cope with. ’How typical… The man who has the most power suffocates the people with less. Just like I am suffocated everyday by this greed inside of me. I never can understand it but I always want more. In Global Wrestling it’s more respect, in life it’s more peace, and in me it’s more answers… Just like life I’m very complicated. Whether it be in the my head or in the squared circle, I’ve proved to be a compelled man in whatever I do. I wonder sometimes, whether wrestling can cause me to be this way. Can the ropes and the mat make my head spin like it does? Or is this just how I am? I’ve never really lead myself into a normal life. I’ve always been given something to do or use to take myself in many different directions. I think at times whether this is worth it. All this stress, all this pain, and past that the hate I develop for some men in that ring such as Ice and Train. The men I have to beat up to gain respect and the men I sometimes injury to ensure my existence as one of the top men. Then I ask myself is it worth it? Then I remember the good things, the championship belts I win, the women I’ve met, the fans chanting into my ears as I am rising up from that devastating move that my opponent delivers to me. I remember the finest moments of this business… After that I know that this is where I belong.’ Jesse rolls over on his stomach and leans on his pillow. Pulling the paper and pen he had gotten off the reporter yesterday he begins to scribble more of how he feels down on the paper.
