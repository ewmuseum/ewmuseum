Meeting (High Octane RP)

23 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/23/04 Christina McCoy was under a lot of stress. High Octane, the second pay-per-view event of the year was in a few days, and she had to make sure that everything went off without a hitch. The one match that had her stressed out the most was the most controversial and anticipated match on the card: Brick vs. Mark Makonie for the Platinum title. The emotions are going to be the highest they’ve ever been. A huge factor in the match is Makonie’s own blatant racism. It’s been a problem since he arrived in the company. Christina is afraid of the damage that could come as a result of the emotions involved. Brick has already thrown Makonie out a window. Makonie just pushed the issue further on a previous Evolution when he ambushed Brick and attempted to spray paint a racial slur on his back. Suddenly, the intercom buzzed. “Ms. McCoy, Brick is here.” “Send him in.” The door opened and Brick walked in, carrying the Platinum title on his right shoulder. “You wanted to see me?” “I did. Have a seat.” As Brick sat, there was a silence between the two. “You look tired,” Brick remarked. “I am. Putting this card together had drained me. Not to mention keeping Train in check.” “I know. I read about his little “incident” with the Cowboys. Plus, Ely told me about what happened when you showed up at his place.” “Yeah, that was not a pleasant experience.” “I can’t imagine.” “Plus I’ve got the NAACP breathing down my neck now.” “Because of Makonie.” “Yes.” “You need to fire his ass.” “Believe it or not, I actually have considered that.” “So why didn’t you?” “Because I figured that anything I do to him would be nothing compared to what you might have in mind.” She was right. Brick could be unusually cruel when he wanted to be. Christina herself knew that better than anyone. Derek Draven was also a perfect example. “You know, Al Sharpton actually called me up and wished me well on my match?” “Did he?” “I didn’t really believe it was him at first. I thought it was Aaron playing a game with me. I’d probably vote for him if he went all the way. Anyway, he told me that a victory would basically be a slap in the face of every racist in the country, in the world.” “Speaking of which, Brick, that's why I wanted to see you. I don’t know if you’ve heard, but word is, Makonie’s been in contact with some of his White supremacist buddies. They might show up at High Octane and disrupt the match.” “Might” I guarantee you, Christina. Those crackers see me beating up on a white guy, I assure you they’ll get involved.” “Plus, there’s the possibility that the Syndicate might get involved, since they’ve been running with Makonie as of late. That’s why I plan to beef up security. Plus, Ely and Jesse are going to be on standby.” “You know, it’s typical. Makonie wants to talk about how perfect he is, how perfect his race is, then he proves what a chicken shit he really is and he calls his boys in. Well, I know people, too.” “That might be what he was counting on.” “Might be, but I seriously doubt it. Makonie’s a little bitch. Can’t take me without his boys.” Brick paused for a minute. “Christina, I need to win this match. It’s not just about the title. I need to prove cats like him wrong once and for all. Just like how Jesse Owens proved Hitler wrong in 1939.” “Well, I hope you do it. A lot of people are rooting for you.” “I kinda figured. Well, at High Octane, I’m gonna give them a reason a root. And I’m gonna give Mark Makonie a reason not to underestimate my people.” Brick then rose out of his seat, about to leave Christina’s office with renewed purpose. “Oh, and Christina?” “Yes?” “If you see Tyler Preston, tell him he just might see me knock Makonie’s teeth in after all.”
